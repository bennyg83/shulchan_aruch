11) [Seif 3] Any internal wound, meaning from the teeth, etc. This applies specifically when the pain is severe enough to be called a wound. Shabbos is not desecrated for a mere toothache. Bach. Nevertheless, if his entire body becomes ill from it, treatment through a non-Jew is permitted. An actual wound in the teeth is like an internal wound, and ordinarily Shabbos is desecrated for it through a Jew even by performing Torah-prohibited labor. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. See below, paragraph 17.
12) There: Meaning from the teeth inward, etc. Even if it is not tzafidna, a disease of the teeth and gums that begins in the mouth and ends in the intestines, whose sign is that blood flows from the row of teeth when something is placed upon them and that develops from eating excessively hot bread or very cold wheat food in winter, as stated in Yoma 84a and Avodah Zarah 28a, Shabbos is desecrated whenever there is an actual wound. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, writes this, contrary to Olas Shabbos, who was imprecise; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, and Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, write the same. Similarly, when the pain is severe enough to be called a wound, Shabbos is desecrated, as stated in the preceding paragraph in the name of Bach. Tosefes Shabbos there writes the same.
13) A distinction exists regarding tzafidna: even if the patient and physician say treatment is unnecessary, their words are treated as false, because the Sages received a tradition that it is dangerous. Regarding other tooth pain that is ordinarily considered an internal wound, Shabbos is desecrated unless the physician or patient says treatment is unnecessary. Regarding a noninternal wound, Shabbos ordinarily is not desecrated, but if the physician or patient says treatment is necessary, it is desecrated. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. See below, seif 10.
14) There: Meaning from the teeth inward, etc. If the place in which the teeth sit, namely the gums, is afflicted, this too is included among internal wounds. This is implied in Avodah Zarah 28a, which states 'from the lip inward'; see there.
15) There: And the teeth themselves, etc. See Issur VeHeter, klal 59, law 18, which writes that certain teeth are connected to the eyes and that such a patient is considered dangerously ill. See also law 24 there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 5. Pesachim 113a likewise relates that Rav told his son Chiya not to extract a tooth. Rabbeinu Chananel explains: If your tooth hurts, do not extract it, lest your eyesight weaken; see there.
16) There: And the teeth themselves, etc. This refers to teeth that have decayed. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3.
17) There: But discomforts are not called a wound. Shabbos is not desecrated for them even if the pain is very severe. If he suffers so greatly that his entire body becomes ill, full-fledged labor may be performed through a non-Jew. If he does not suffer to this extent, any rabbinically forbidden act may be performed through a non-Jew. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
18) There, gloss: Nevertheless, one who has a toothache and suffers, etc. The text must mean that his entire body became ill, as stated in seif 17, and this is also implied by Darkei Moshe. Extracting a tooth is Torah-prohibited labor because it causes a wound for a therapeutic purpose. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. It may therefore be permitted only when his entire body became ill and only through a non-Jew, as stated in seif 17. According to those who hold that labor not needed for its own purpose is prohibited only rabbinically, however, the tooth may be extracted through a non-Jew even when his entire body has not become ill. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. See above, siman 278.
19) There, gloss: He may tell a non-Jew to extract it. This follows the rule that for any non-dangerous illness one may instruct a non-Jew to act. Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim. Maharshal writes that there is no proof that a tooth may be extracted on Shabbos through a non-Jew because of danger to life. Only applying a substance, which is a definite treatment, permits desecrating Shabbos even through Torah-prohibited labor such as grinding medicinal ingredients, whereas extracting the tooth itself also presents danger to life. These are his words, cited by Bach. Taz, paragraph 1, forbids it for another reason: the Jew assists the non-Jew in extracting the tooth; see there. Shach in Nekudos HaKesef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, however, writes that specifically one receiving a haircut is liable for assisting by positioning his head, because Scripture expressly includes him. Elsewhere we maintain that assistance has no substance; see there. Bach also appears to permit it like Orchos Chaim and Rema. This is likewise the view of Chemed Moshe, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4. The latter writes that although Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 4, records a prohibiting custom, one may nevertheless be lenient; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, Issur VeHeter, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, and Chayei Adam, klal 69, paragraph 11, write the same. This is the view of the later authorities. See below, paragraph 115.
20) There, gloss: He may tell a non-Jew to extract it. If a person has facial swelling and its traditional remedy is to mark the swelling with the shape of a seal, may one tell a non-Jew to do so on Shabbos? See Admas Kodesh, volume 1, siman 5, which concludes that it is permitted; see there. Zechor LeAvraham, laws of Shabbos, paragraph 8.
21) Mishbetzos Zahav writes in paragraph 1: It is obvious to me that a man and woman are equal regarding danger to life. Shabbos is desecrated even for a one-day-old infant whose full term is uncertain, as with bris milah, because we follow the majority and presume the child viable. Any doubt overrides Shabbos in all such matters. A child born to a non-Jew or female slave follows the mother's status, and Shabbos is not desecrated for a non-Jew. It is uncertain whether Shabbos is desecrated for a fetus when the mother is not endangered. Nevertheless, one can say that a miscarriage would endanger her, so Shabbos is desecrated for her. See there. Shabbos is also desecrated for a Canaanite slave, since he is obligated in mitzvos like a woman. Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 2. See also below, paragraph 91, and siman 329, paragraph 1.
