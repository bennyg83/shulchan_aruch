[3] From the teeth inward, etc. Even if it is not the disease called tzafidna, whenever there is a wound one desecrates Shabbos, as stated by the Magen Avraham. The Bach implies that whenever the teeth cause severe pain it is considered a wound. What he later writes, that one tells a non-Jew and he extracts the tooth, means that tooth extraction is not a recognized cure and therefore was not permitted through a Jew.
[4] [Levush] And he suffers greatly, etc. One must say that his entire body has become ill from it, as stated in seif 14. The Taz extensively argues that extraction by a non-Jew is prohibited, comparing it to being shaved by another, as discussed in the final chapter of Makkot, where the person being shaved is liable. See Yoreh Deah siman 181, seif 4, which says that the person being shaved is liable if he assists by inclining himself toward the one shaving him. This implies that shaving is possible even without his inclining himself, in which case he is not liable. In Nekudot HaKesef, siman 198, it is written that the prohibition of assisting applies specifically to the shaver and the person shaved because the verse implies both parties. See there at length. Nevertheless, Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that the custom is to prohibit. Even so, there is room to be lenient.
[5] Everything that is ordinarily done, etc. This implies that it is permitted even where withholding that particular thing would not endanger him, as stated by Mateh Moshe and the Beit Yosef. There is a scribal error in the Magen Avraham; see there.
