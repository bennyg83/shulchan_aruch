(א) They tell a non-Jew. Specifically when he needs it on Shabbat itself. Radbaz part 2 chapter 7:

(ב) Even if there is not. It implies all the more so when there is danger to a limb — it is permitted even if he is not a patient comprising the whole body; and this is Maggid Mishneh's view in what he concluded; but in one limb, since there is no danger — forbidden; and this is why they did not permit kohling except from a non-Jew end of his words — meaning there is no danger to the limb; not like Beit Yosef; and so Maggid Mishneh afterward; but complete melacha through an Israelite was not permitted even with danger to a limb end of his words; and it implies shvut is permitted with danger to a limb; and if there is no danger to a limb — permitted through a non-Jew; and this is Ameimar's case, as written siman 327 seif 5: shvut of shvut is permitted for partial illness; and ordinary discomfort — even through a non-Jew forbidden, as written seif 1; and see siman 396:

(ג) Adjacent to melacha. Such as kohling which is like writing, or making healing with a drug where there is concern of grinding spices; and this law Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote from his own view; and Bach challenged him, for Rosh and Ran proved from the case of a rebellious eye that it is permitted to kohle it even though it is adjacent to melacha; see there:

(ד) The third rationale. Meaning they do with a change, etc.; and examine the poskim and you will find he is among the great poskim; and Bach's words require further study:

(ה) To make a stew. And permitted to feed him muktzeh with hands if impossible otherwise (Orchot Chayyim):

(ו) To assist him a little. Meaning that otherwise too it is done, only he assists a little — for example a non-Jew kohls the eye and an Israelite closes and opens the eye so the kohl enters; but if he cannot do it without the Israelite — forbidden [Rashi; and so Tosafos Shabbos daf 93]; and it implies there that even in d'oraisa melacha assisting has no substance:
