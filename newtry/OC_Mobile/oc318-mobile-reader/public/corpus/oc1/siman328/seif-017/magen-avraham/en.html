One tells a non-Jew. This applies specifically when the patient needs it on Shabbos itself. See Radach, part 2, section 7.
Even where there is no. This implies that where there is danger to a limb, it is certainly permitted even if the illness does not affect the entire body. This is the view of the Maggid Mishneh. His concluding statement, “But regarding one limb, since there is no danger, it is forbidden,” means that there is no danger to the limb, contrary to Beit Yosef. The Maggid Mishneh subsequently writes likewise that a complete melachah performed by a Jew was not permitted even where there is danger to a limb. This implies that a shevus is permitted where there is danger to a limb. Where there is no danger to the limb, it may be done through a non-Jew. This is the incident involving Ameimar, and accords with siman 307, seif 5, where a double shevus is permitted for a partial illness. For a mere discomfort, even through a non-Jew it is forbidden, as stated in seif 1. See also what I wrote in siman 496.
It resembles a melachah. Such as applying eye paint, which resembles writing, or preparing a remedy from medicinal ingredients, where there is concern that one may grind them. Beit Yosef wrote this ruling on his own authority. Bach challenges him, since Rosh and Ran prove from the case of a rebellious eye that one may apply eye paint even though it resembles a melachah; see there.
The third opinion. This is the view that the act is performed with an unusual method, etc. Examine the poskim carefully and you will find that this is the view of major authorities. The words of Bach require study.
To prepare a cooked dish. It is permitted to feed him muktzeh directly when no other method is possible. See Issur VeHeter.
To assist him slightly. This means that the act would be accomplished without the Jew, and the Jew merely assists slightly. For example, a non-Jew applies eye paint and the Jew closes and opens the eye so that the paint enters. If the non-Jew cannot perform the act without the Jew, it is forbidden. See Rashi and Tosafos, Shabbos 93a. It is implied there that even regarding a melachah according to the Torah, mere assistance has no halachic substance.
