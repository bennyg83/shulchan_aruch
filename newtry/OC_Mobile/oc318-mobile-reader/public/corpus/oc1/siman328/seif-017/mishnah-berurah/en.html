(א) And there is no danger — meaning danger of death, whether there is danger to a limb or not:
(ב) They say, etc. — and specifically when the remedy is needed on Shabbat itself; for when not needed on Shabbat — wait until Motza'ei Shabbat; but when there is danger — forbidden to wait [Peri Megadim]:
(ג) To do healing for him — even in melacha d'oraisa; and the same for his other needs such as to bake and cook and the like if needed for this:
(ד) Even, etc. — for a Jew was not permitted to transgress Torah prohibition as long as it does not touch pikuach nefesh directly:
(ה) Limb — see above in seif 6 and 9 that there are limbs touching pikuach nefesh; and the same regarding other limbs — if the doctor says that if they do not heal the limb he can come to pikuach nefesh — behold he is like other illness with danger:
(ו) In rabbinic prohibition — meaning all shevutin, even without change; and because they hold that in a place of illness they did not decree:
(ז) Even, etc. — for if there is danger even only to one limb — for all views [except the last view see there] permitted for a Jew to desecrate for him in all shevutin for his healing, even if he is not ill in the entire body at all. Chayei Adam wrote: anything permitted to do through a Jew for a sick person who has no danger — even if he can do through a gentile — permitted:
(ח) Limb — and regarding above at beginning of seif it deals where he is a sick person who fell to bed or suffers greatly and his entire body became ill from it; but if his entire body is not ill at all and he is only ill in one of his limbs and also in that limb there is no danger for all — not permitted to desecrate except through a gentile; and also specifically in a matter that is only shevut — for then through a gentile it becomes shevut of shevut and permitted in partial illness, as above in siman 307 seif 5:
(ט) And if there is not, etc. — even though he is a sick person encompassing the entire body — some hold forbidden to do through a Jew; and according to this second view even through change forbidden to do himself unless impossible through a gentile — they agree to the third view that permitted through a Jew with change:
(י) They do through change — meaning rabbinic prohibition; and need not search for a gentile:
(כ) Attached to melacha d'oraisa — such as to put kohl in the eye [at end of illness without danger, as above in seif 9] which is like writing:
(ל) That are not in them, etc. — such as those explained in Semag — see there:
(מ) The third reasoning — meaning permitted to do even all shevutin, only through change if he is illness of the entire body and there is no danger to a limb; and if there is danger to a limb — no change needed at all; and so Taz and Magen Avraham; and so Gra wrote that this view is essential — it is the view of most poskim:
(נ) Permitted — and if the infant does not wish to eat except through his mother — permitted for the mother to feed him even her gentile milk and what she cooked, even though it is muktzeh movable; and see in Peri Megadim who somewhat leans that change is required. Chayei Adam wrote: one must be careful not to put the food itself into the pot — for one gives water and one pours into the pot — the first is exempt but forbidden:
(ס) Permitted for a sick person — not specifically a sick person — the same for another; rather customary speech was used:
(ע) To assist him somewhat — meaning where it would be done anyway but he assists a little — such as a gentile puts kohl in the eye and a Jew opens and closes the eye so the kohl enters; but if he cannot do it without a Jew — forbidden; and it implies even in melacha d'oraisa mesayei ein bo mamash [Magen Avraham]:
