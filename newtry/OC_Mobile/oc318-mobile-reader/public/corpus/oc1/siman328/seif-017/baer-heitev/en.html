(א) They tell a non-Jew. Specifically when he needs it on Shabbat itself. Radbaz. Magen Avraham:

(ב) They do with a change. And this is the third rationale whose words appear; and see this seif in Beit Yosef and Acharonim and Yad Aharon:

(ג) Stew. And permitted to feed him muktzeh with hands if impossible otherwise. Orchot Chayyim:

(ד) For a patient to assist him. Meaning that otherwise too it is done, only he assists a little — for example a non-Jew kohls the eye and an Israelite closes and opens the eye so the kohl enters; but if he cannot do it without the Israelite — forbidden; and it implies there that even in d'oraisa melacha assisting has no substance. Magen Avraham; and Taz already wrote that assistance should be specifically when the non-Jew is engaged in it, and not that he precede him at all; see Magen Avraham s.k. 2; and he further wrote that from this language it implies specifically for the patient assistance a little was permitted but not for another — and I do not know the reason to distinguish; and it appears he does not speak specifically of a patient but the same applies to another; see there:
