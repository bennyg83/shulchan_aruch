A sick person who fell to bed from illness and there is no danger.Or one who has pain that distresses him and his whole body became ill from it, that then even though he walks he is considered as one who fell to bed [HaMagid chapter 2].} They tell a non-Jew to do healing for him; but they do not desecrate Shabbat for him with a d'oraisa prohibition, even if there is danger to a limb; and for a Jew to desecrate for him a d'rabbanan prohibition by hand — some permit even if there is no danger to a limb; and some say if there is danger to a limb they do, and if not they do not; and some say if there is no danger to a limb they do with a change and if there is danger to a limb they do without a change; and some say even if there is danger to a limb they do not do for him something that is ancillary to d'oraisa melacha, and things with no ancillary melacha connection they do even without danger to a limb; and the words of the third reasoning seem correct. {Rama: It is permitted to tell a non-Jew to cook a dish for a child who has nothing to eat, for the needs of a child are considered like a sick person without danger (R' Yerucham ni"b chapter 9 and Rambam and Rashba);
