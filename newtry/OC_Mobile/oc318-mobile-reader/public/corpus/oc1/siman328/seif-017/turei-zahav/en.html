(א) A patient who fell, etc. First we will cite and explain Tur's words per my humble opinion, as written in name of Rambam, to resolve Rosh's doubt — namely Rosh was uncertain regarding a patient without danger whether shvut through an Israelite is permitted; and thus Rambam: a patient without danger — they do for him all his needs through a non-Jew; how so? Tell a non-Jew to do and he cooks for him, bakes for him, etc.; and so kohling the eyes from a non-Jew on Shabbat even though there is no danger; and if they needed things without melacha — an Israelite may do them; therefore they may set ears, etc. end of his words; and Tur learned from Rambam that "things without melacha" means d'oraisa, but there is shvut in them; and Beit Yosef challenged: how did Tur resolve his doubt leniently from Rambam's words that it is permitted for a patient without danger through an Israelite even in a matter forbidden to an Israelite on account of shvut — nevertheless an Israelite may do this act since it is for the need of a patient without danger from what Rambam wrote "if they needed things without melacha," etc.; for if so it is difficult what he wrote regarding kohling — that it is permitted only through a non-Jew, for kohling is among things with only shvut, as in mishnah end of chapter Hametzni'a regarding kohling and poulticing, etc.; and Beit Yosef brought to answer per the Maggid's words who explained Rambam's words: initially he said regarding a patient with illness of the whole body it is permitted through a non-Jew; and regarding kohling where there is not illness of the whole body but of one limb, even without danger — then too permitted through a non-Jew; and in the conclusion he said regarding illness of the whole body — namely onkeli and setting ears — permitted through an Israelite since the whole body is ill in it; this is the essence of the explanation even though this is not its language. And accordingly Tur's words are settled per Rambam's explanation from the law of kohling that permission is only through a non-Jew; and Beit Yosef challenged the Maggid that no hint is found in Rambam of distinguishing illness of the whole body; and the explanation is Rambam's words in this manner: there is no distinction between illness of the whole body; rather the distinction is between shvut that has an essential melacha adjacent to d'oraisa melacha like kohling where there is concern of grinding spices — this is permitted only through a non-Jew; but shvut not adjacent to d'oraisa melacha is permitted even through an Israelite; and for this reason they permitted setting ears and onkeli — for these things were not forbidden because they resemble melacha, and also there is no decree on them on account of grinding spices, for these things are not made with spices; and this is what he said "if they needed things without melacha-making," etc. And from now Rosh's doubt is not resolved from Rambam leniently or stringently; and moreover even what is clear to Rosh and Ran that with danger to a limb shvut done through an Israelite is permitted — for Rambam, whatever resembles melacha or regarding which there is decree on account of grinding spices is forbidden through an Israelite, and only something that does not resemble melacha and is not made with spices is permitted end of Beit Yosef's words. And I stood astonished at Rosh's and Tur's words here in what they were uncertain, for it is stated explicitly in chapter 2 of Beitzah plainly regarding Ameimar kohling the eye — that it is forbidden, for there they said: Ameimar permitted kohling the eye on Shabbat through a non-Jew; Rav Ashi said to him: what is your reasoning? For all needs of a patient without danger — tell a non-Jew and he does; but you master assist — he squeezes and opens; he answered: assisting has no substance — learn that through an Israelite it is forbidden; and so too Rosh himself there, and so Tur siman 396; if so how can we say here regarding a patient without danger and without missing limb that it is permitted through an Israelite? And that which they said in chapter 8 of Sheratzim — Rav said: a rebellious eye may be kohled on Shabbat — they thought from this it deals when spices were ground from erev Shabbat, etc., as Beit Yosef wrote — learn that without pikuach nefesh it is permitted when spices were ground from erev Shabbat, and even though there is d'rabbanan prohibition — Ran already answered that there was at least danger to a limb, unlike Ameimar's case where there was not even danger in the eye but only illness; but Rosh who wants to say on one side that it is permitted even without danger to a limb through an Israelite — why did Ameimar need a non-Jew? And he answered that there it was not even illness but only ordinary discomfort — this is not so, for explicitly the Gemara compares it to a patient without danger; and if so, whatever is in kohling an eye is like a patient without danger; and furthermore, if they differ — how does the Gemara derive the law of kohling from a patient without danger? If so, Beit Yosef's answer to resolve Tur's words per the Maggid, distinguishing illness of the whole body — namely a patient without danger — from kohling — is hidden; and also that Ameimar deals with grinding spices on Yom Tov, for there they said afterward: Ameimar permitted kohling the eye on the second day of Rosh Hashana — and this certainly deals with grindings before Yom Tov; and so Rashi explained there explicitly "to kohle the eye" — to put kohl in it for healing, meaning it was only application; and if so it ceteris paribus even though ground from erev Shabbat — specifically through a non-Jew; and Rambam's words are also explained thus, as Beit Yosef wrote in explaining Rambam's words — and this is the true explanation that Rambam permitted only something without decree of grinding spices like setting onkeli as Rambam himself wrote in chapter 1; if so, still requires study what Beit Yosef wrote on Rosh's ruling: and Rashba ruled this doubt leniently, who wrote in responsum: a patient without danger or with danger to a limb — things with only d'rabbanan prohibition are done even through an Israelite end of his words — it is more difficult from Ameimar's case why a non-Jew was needed; and one may say this deals with something without decree of grinding spices, like Beit Yosef explained in what is written "if they needed things without melacha," etc.; and see further below nearby; but Rosh's and Tur's words I do not know how to reconcile until one whose heart is complete in the matter comes and instructs me:

(ב) In d'rabbanan prohibition with hands. It implies even in something where there is decree of grinding spices — and nevertheless some permit; and this is Rashba whom I mentioned in name of Beit Yosef — for otherwise all agree it is permitted through an Israelite; and I already wrote the astonishment on this from Ameimar's case:

(ג) And some say that if there is, etc. This is Ran's view whom we mentioned nearby, who distinguishes between that of chapter 8 Sheratzim regarding a rebellious eye and Ameimar's case; and what he wrote "and if there is no danger to a limb" — meaning it is illness of the whole body only without danger — then they do specifically through a non-Jew:

(ד) They do with a change. This is Ramban's view in Hagahot HaHagahot: for a patient without danger, even for a limb — forbidden through an Israelite except shvut done with a change as not the usual way; and his proof from chapter R' Eliezer of Milah: one does not crush wine and oil for a patient on Shabbat, etc.; and the Maggid concluded: Rashba's view is like Ramban; and from this I am greatly astonished — how did Beit Yosef write in name of Rashba in responsum to permit for a patient without danger like with danger to a limb through an Israelite — and this is the first "some say" here; and if so Rashba's words contradict each other; and as it appears to my humble opinion — that responsum is not reliable, or there is some error in it, for on the responsum not Mar bar Rav Ashi signed; and here the Maggid testified on Rashba that he wrote thus; if so there is no source for the first "some permit" that he wrote — for on the good he alludes that he derives thus from Rambam leniently, which Beit Yosef already rejected; and all the more so per what we challenged from Ameimar's Gemara:

(ה) And some say even if there is danger to a limb. This is Rambam's view per Beit Yosef's explanation; and one may say on this that in chapter 8 Sheratzim they said: a rebellious eye may be kohled on Shabbat — they thought from this it deals when spices were ground from erev Shabbat; but if one grinds and brings — they told him: that rabbi explained to me from Rav Yehuda that even if one grinds and brings — because the eye's cords depend on the heart there is pikuach nefesh; and from this Rosh and Ran learned: since initially it was in their thought regarding rebellious eye that there is no pikuach nefesh, and nevertheless permitted with ground spices from erev Shabbat even though Rabbanan forbade on account of healing — learn that with danger to a limb it is permitted even through an Israelite; and from this it is difficult on Rambam who forbids even with danger to a limb — we require specifically that there not be melacha adjacent to d'oraisa prohibition, namely grinding spices — then it is permitted; and one may say Rambam's words deal with kohling an eye not in a place of danger to a limb, as Ran explained — and not as Beit Yosef wrote, for if so Rambam should have written the law of danger to a limb that they desecrate through an Israelite end of his words; therefore it appears certainly the truth is per Beit Yosef's explanation that for Rambam there is no distinction between danger to a limb or not — all are like a patient without danger alone; and that of chapter 8 Sheratzim — it appears Rambam holds that initially he thought it deals with spices ground from erev Shabbat, and then it must be said that even though there is no pikuach nefesh it is permitted through an Israelite even where there is d'rabbanan prohibition on account of grinding spices; but in the end when it deals with pikuach nefesh and permitted even grinding of that day — if so this law is not hinted at all where there is only danger to a limb; rather everything depends on pikuach nefesh — then it is fully permitted; and where it is not pikuach nefesh — forbidden where there is adjacency to Torah prohibition — so it appears correct per Rambam's words:

(ו) And the words of the third rationale appear. It implies he agrees with Ramban's view which is the third opinion; and my teacher z"l explained it refers to Rambam — which is the third "some say"; and Levush too; and I do not know why he explained thus and did not explain with the first opinion — namely "some permit," etc.; rather on three "some say" he refers; and furthermore if so he should have said "and the words of this rationale appear," since it refers above; therefore it appears to me per the plain sense it refers to Ramban:

(ז) To assist him a little. See in seif 3 that assistance should be specifically when the non-Jew is engaged in it — not that he precede him at all; and it implies from this language that specifically for the patient assistance a little was permitted, but not for another; and I do not know the reason to distinguish, for we did not find greater permission for a patient except regarding if he says "I need"; and it appears here too he does not speak specifically of a patient but the same applies to another:
