And some say that if possible, etc. This view is in Shulchan Gavoah in name of Riaz; and it seems to me this matter is astonishing, for just as there is prohibition to do through a non-Jew if one must be concerned he will be lazy, as written afterward here too — so too there is concern lest you cause them to stumble in the future: if they see now that they do only through a non-Jew, they will think there is prohibition through an Israelite; and sometimes no non-Jew will be available, and through this the patient will be endangered by waiting for the non-Jew; and many times we find in the Gemara "you cause them to stumble for the future"; and in this too they said in Yerushalmi: the asker — this one is a wicked person, and the one asked — this one is disgraceful, as Tur wrote in this siman, because the one asked should already have taught this publicly that pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat, as Beit Yosef in name of Terumat HaDeshen — all the more so one should not make a stumbling block and give place for error; and furthermore, for they said in this "and whoever is zealous — this one is praiseworthy" — if so, why should not the Israelite be zealous before the non-Jew? Accordingly it appears to me what two batei midrash wrote in the Gemara last chapter of Yoma: these things are not done neither through a non-Jew nor through minors, but through adult Israelites; and it is impossible to do these things through women and not Kutim, etc.; and Beit Yosef already mentioned reasons for distinguishing the two batei midrash; and per this view it is stronger: first he said there is prohibition to do through a non-Jew lest they say there is prohibition through an Israelite; and afterward he said it is a mitzvah to do through adult Israelites, and impossible through women and Kutim — meaning even if they are available before us and Shabbat observers — if so the mentioned stumbling block does not apply; nevertheless they should do specifically through adult Israelites in order to teach practical halacha to the public; and Beit Yosef wrote: these "adult Israelites" does not mean adults in wisdom, as Rambam's words imply "adult Israelites and their sages" — for if so, why specify women and Kutim? Even other people too; rather certainly "adults" to exclude minor Israelites; and Rambam added "and their sages" — meaning they are people of intellect, to exclude women and slaves whose intellect is light end of his words; and how much effort the rabbi expended to depart from the plain sense and explain with great strain — for if as his words, he should have said "adult Israelites" as precise language; rather "adult Israelites" [also in Ran chapter 8 of Sheratzim he wrote: from this you learn that for a dangerously ill patient his needs are done through adult Israelites end of his words — and there it is not applicable to say to exclude women and slaves, for he did not mention this at all, and he entered this in vain] — certainly in what is written "adult Israelites" he does not intend to distinguish prohibition in an ordinary Israelite, but rather to state the preferred mitzvah; and the intent is lest you say to do through a non-Jew and forbid through an Israelite — rather on the contrary there is mitzvah in the matter and adult Israelites should do it; and it turns out there is prohibition through a non-Jew, and among Israelites themselves there is virtue for the one who does this — so it appears correct to me; and one who comes to do through an available non-Jew should at least reveal to the rabbis at that time that there is permission for the Israelite himself, but that the non-Jew is available here; but the essence of the matter is not to pay attention to the non-Jew at all — if he sees clear danger and even doubtful rescue, "whoever is zealous — this one is praiseworthy" if he does it himself; for even though the non-Jew is before us and does the rescue — nevertheless the Israelite should do it with greater zeal; and even though Rama wrote "and so they practice" — from this there is no proof, for it is not an established custom; also from Ran's words that Beit Yosef brings it implies so, for he wrote "lest the matter come," etc. — implying he was concerned for the future even though now there is no concern; and what is written here in Maggid Mishneh "Tosafos and Ran" is a copyist's error and is not true in the essential law that Rama mentioned in this — so it appears clear to me:
