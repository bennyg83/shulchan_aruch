(א) (s.k. 12) Even, etc. — it implies all the more, etc. — from the language "even," etc. — also for all the views he brought afterward — they hold there is more reason to be lenient with danger to a limb than with illness comprising the whole body; and this is the opposite of Rabbeinu Yehuda's view who brought Maggid Mishneh's words that Rambam wrote chapter 2: a patient without danger — they do all his needs through a non-Jew; how so? Tell a non-Jew, etc., to cook and bake, etc.; and so kohling his eyes from a non-Jew on Shabbat even though there is no danger; and if they needed things without melacha — an Israelite may do them; therefore they may set ears and onkeli, etc. (and this is explained in Semag); and Magen Avraham wrote nevertheless; and it was already explained to you in our rabbi's words that all shvut is permitted for a patient without danger since it is illness comprising the whole body — whether shvut of telling a non-Jew or shvut done through an Israelite; but in one limb, since there is no danger — not permitted to an Israelite even complete shvut; and this is why they did not permit kohling except from a non-Jew even though in kohling there is only shvut; but complete melacha through an Israelite was not permitted even with danger to one limb except with pikuach nefesh end of Maggid Mishneh; and Rabbeinu Yehuda understood what Maggid Mishneh wrote "but in one limb, since there is no danger," etc. — meaning there is no pikuach nefesh even if it is danger to a limb — nevertheless not permitted to an Israelite even shvut; and Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote from this that Rambam holds there is more reason to be lenient with a patient comprising the whole body than with danger to a limb — and this is the opposite of what Tur wrote in name of Rambam; but Rabbeinu Yehuda himself challenged Maggid Mishneh's view too and explained that Rambam holds to distinguish between adjacent to d'oraisa melacha — and this is the third opinion brought in Shulchan Aruch; therefore Magen Avraham wrote Maggid Mishneh's intents are not so; and also Maggid Mishneh holds that with danger to a limb one is more lenient than with illness comprising the whole body; and what Maggid Mishneh wrote "but in one limb, since there is no danger" — meaning there is no danger to a limb either; but if there is danger to a limb — permitted to an Israelite to do shvut; and proof: afterward Maggid Mishneh wrote that with danger to a limb complete melacha was not permitted — implying shvut is permitted; and if there is no danger to a limb and also not illness comprising the whole body — likewise; and this is Ameimar's case who permitted kohling through a non-Jew where there was no danger to a limb and also not illness comprising the whole body — nevertheless since in kohling there is only shvut — if so through a non-Jew it is shvut of shvut and permitted even for partial illness:

(ב) (s.k. 12) And with ordinary discomfort, etc. — and if so per this view there are three distinctions: (1) ordinary discomfort — even shvut of shvut forbidden; (2) partial illness — shvut of shvut permitted; (3) illness comprising the whole body — all the more danger to a limb — permitted to an Israelite to do shvut, and all the more to tell a non-Jew to do d'oraisa melacha; and see siman 396; and see there in Hagahot HaHagahot:
