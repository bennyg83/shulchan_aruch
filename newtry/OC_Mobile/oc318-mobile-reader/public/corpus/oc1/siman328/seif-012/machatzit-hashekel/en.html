(s.k. 12) Even, etc. It implies, all the more so, etc., since he used the expression even, etc. Also, according to all the opinions he brings afterward, they hold that one should be more lenient regarding danger to a limb than regarding an illness that includes the whole body. This is the opposite of the view of the Beit Yosef, who brought the Rambam's words, who wrote in chapter 2: For a sick person who is not in danger, all his needs are done through a non-Jew. How so? One tells a non-Jew, etc., to cook and bake, etc. Likewise, he has his eyes painted by a non-Jew on Shabbos, although there is no danger there. If he needed things that involve no melacha, even a Jew may do them; therefore one may raise the ears and raise the unkeli, etc. This is explained in the Semag. The Maggid Mishneh wrote: It has already been explained to you in the words of our master that every shevus is permitted for a sick person who is not in danger, since it is an illness that includes the whole body, whether it is a shevus of telling a non-Jew or a shevus performed by a Jew. But with one limb, since there is no danger, even a full shevus was not permitted to a Jew. Thus they permitted painting the eye only through a non-Jew, although painting itself is only a shevus. But a full melacha by a Jew was not permitted even for danger to one limb, only for danger to life. End quote. The Beit Yosef understood that what the Maggid Mishneh wrote, but with one limb, since there is no danger, means that there is no danger to life, even though there is danger to a limb; nevertheless even a shevus was not permitted to a Jew. The Beit Yosef wrote that this proves that according to the Rambam one should be more lenient with an illness that includes the whole body than with danger to a limb, which is the opposite of what the Tur wrote in the name of the Rambam. But the Beit Yosef himself also challenged the approach of the Maggid Mishneh and explained that according to the Rambam the distinction is between something attached to a melacha according to the Torah, and this is the third opinion brought in Shulchan Aruch. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote that this was not the intent of the Maggid Mishneh, and the Maggid Mishneh also holds that regarding danger to a limb one should be more lenient than regarding an illness that includes the whole body. What the Maggid Mishneh wrote, but with one limb, since there is no danger, means that there is also no danger to the limb. But if there is danger to a limb, a Jew is permitted to perform a shevus. The proof is that the Maggid Mishneh wrote afterward that for danger to a limb a full melacha was not permitted, which implies that a shevus is permitted. And if there is no danger to a limb and it is also not an illness affecting the whole body, this is the case of Ameimar, who permitted painting the eye through a non-Jew, for there was no danger to a limb and also no illness affecting the whole body. Nevertheless, since painting is only a shevus, through a non-Jew it is a double shevus and is permitted even for partial illness.
And with mere discomfort, etc. If so, according to this opinion there are three distinctions. First, mere discomfort, for which even a double shevus is forbidden. Second, partial illness, for which a double shevus is permitted. Third, an illness that affects the whole body, and all the more so danger to a limb, for which a Jew may perform a shevus, and all the more so one may tell a non-Jew to perform a melacha according to the Torah.
