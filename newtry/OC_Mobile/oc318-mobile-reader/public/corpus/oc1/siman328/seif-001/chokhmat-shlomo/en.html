Seif 1. NB: We have the girsa in tractate Shabbat: we learned [regarding fathers who became liable for their sons' acts] until he knows it regarding techumim, according to R' Akiva. It is difficult to me — why does the gemara not answer that he erred regarding measures, for every melacha of Shabbat requires a measure, and thus one could say he thought he is not liable for them except at a large measure and he performed a small measure. And if we say according to those who hold that on Shabbat chatzi shiur is permitted from the Torah, he is considered shogeg — also according to those who hold chatzi shiur is forbidden from the Torah on Shabbat, we still find it: he did not know that chatzi shiur is forbidden from the Torah on Shabbat; and even if he knew, we see one holds that even if he was intentional regarding a negative commandment he is shogeg. Therefore it is that he holds we require that he be shogeg regarding the negative commandment as well; nevertheless one could say he admits that if he was intentional regarding chatzi shiur, which is neither negative commandment nor positive commandment but only a general prohibition, he admits he is shogeg; and behold without the verse "which you shall not do" he would admit that even if he was intentional regarding a negative commandment it is shogeg, only that the verse "which you shall not do" was revealed and from it we learn shogeg applies even to a negative commandment; but in chatzi shiur where there is not even a negative commandment and it is lighter even than an act, as Tosafot wrote in Shevuot — therefore properly such a mezid can exist, and thus properly we find he erred regarding measures. And one cannot say: if he knew the prohibition of melachot but erred regarding measures, then it is obvious he is liable for each and every one — what does it teach us. However according to this one could again say: for all the melachot he did not know the prohibitions at all and thought they are entirely permitted, and he could desecrate Shabbat in one melacha from them; and in that melacha he erred regarding the prohibition of how much the measure is, for he thought the measure is the greater one and thought he did not do the measure — thus he is also liable for it a sin-offering, and nevertheless he could desecrate Shabbat since he knew this melacha is forbidden only he thought we require the greater measure; and for all the melachot he did not know at all they are forbidden — and it teaches us that nevertheless he is liable for each and every one; and requires study in my opinion. And likewise difficult in what it asks afterward from a false oath regarding the past: behold eating is in an olive-bulk, and perhaps he ate less than an olive-bulk and thought eating is in a kav, and swore he ate and in truth it was less than the measure, or for another reason it is not eating — requires study. And behold after I wrote this I saw in gemara Shabbat daf 73, where it says: granted according to Abbaye who says in such a case he is liable, etc., that he erred regarding measures; but according to Rava who says exempt — how do we find it, etc. — and most wondrous: for Rava exempts only if he intended to throw two and threw four, which is mis'asek; but if he intended to throw four only he thought four is not a measure of melacha but only more, and afterward it became known to him that in four cubits there is a measure of melacha — certainly liable, for it is not mis'asek since he intended for this melacha; and thus even for Rava it is answered he erred regarding measures, namely he thought every melacha is at a measure greater than it is in truth and thought he did less than the measure and in truth did the measure — thus he is shogeg in this and not mis'asek; requires study in this. And behold when I examined the sugya on that page, it seems to resolve from this what Peri Megadim was uncertain in Yoreh De'ah siman 62 regarding intent of chazi le'itztarufi — whether the intent is that if he does so again it will join to the measure, or the intent is that if he did a full measure he would be liable, therefore this too is forbidden. And from this sugya one may resolve this doubt in what it says there "and it is necessary": if we had taught the first case, in that case Rava says he does not intend for a forbidden slice; but he intended to throw two and threw four — four without two is not thrown, I would say he agrees to Abbaye, etc.; and Rashi there: this one intended to throw two and threw four — his thought is fulfilled, for within four are two, thus his thought is fulfilled and it is not mis'asek, end of his words. It is difficult why Rashi needed to explain thus; why did he not explain simply, since Rashi holds chatzi shiur is forbidden from the Torah on Shabbat as Maharshal wrote in his name in chapter 18 of Hilchot Shabbat regarding "is it permitted to bake less than the measure" — thus also here, since in any case two cubits are chatzi shiur, thus forbidden from the Torah and he intended for prohibition; but in intending to lift the detached he did not intend for prohibition — why did Rashi need to extend with another reason. True, the language of the gemara compels him, for otherwise the gemara should have said briefly: here he intended for a forbidden throwing since chatzi shiur is forbidden from the Torah — why extend that four without two is not found; necessarily the intent is as Rashi explained. However it is difficult on the gemara according to Rashi's view that chatzi shiur is forbidden from the Torah — why does it not say so. Necessarily it is proven that chatzi shiur forbidden from the Torah is when he afterward does another chatzi shiur it joins; thus it works for other melachot; but in throwing, if he threw two and it rested, even if he throws another two afterward he is exempt — thus chazi le'itztarufi does not apply here, and therefore one must say another matter. But if we say chazi le'itztarufi means if he were doing a full measure he would be liable — thus also in throwing two it would be so, and it should have said so; necessarily it is proven as stated; examine well. And behold I sat and saw one may bring proof to the essence of the law that chatzi shiur is permitted from the Torah on Shabbat from what is stated in tractate Shabbat daf 74: there were before him two types of food; he selected and ate, and selected and left; R' Eliezer teaches exempt and R' Yirmiyah of Difti teaches liable; it asks R' Eliezer teaches exempt and a baraita teaches liable, etc. It is difficult to me — what does it ask; behold it is clear in gemara chapter 3 of Pesachim that Abbaye holds hatar mitztaref le'issur in all prohibitions, and thus one could say R' Yirmiyah of Difti also holds so, and it deals thus: for the gemara asked above that selecting is forbidden even less than the measure, and thus one could say they deal where he selected chatzi shiur to eat immediately and chatzi shiur to leave, and therefore R' Eliezer exempts since there is no measure in liability, and R' Yirmiyah of Difti holds like Abbaye that hatar mitztaref le'issur in all the Torah, and thus also on Shabbat it is so, and therefore what he selected to eat immediately joins to what he selected to leave and therefore he is liable; and the baraita deals where he selected a measure to leave and therefore certainly liable; and necessarily it is proven according to those who hold chatzi shiur is permitted from the Torah on Shabbat; and there is proof from this to what I wrote in my work that hatar mitztaref le'issur applies only in a matter where chatzi shiur is forbidden from the Torah — then since it is within prohibition one may say hatar mitztaref le'issur; but if chatzi shiur is permitted from the Torah one cannot say hatar mitztaref le'issur, since it too is permitted from the Torah; and thus it is impossible to explain from combination — for then why would R' Yirmiyah of Difti be liable; and it comes out well; examine well.
