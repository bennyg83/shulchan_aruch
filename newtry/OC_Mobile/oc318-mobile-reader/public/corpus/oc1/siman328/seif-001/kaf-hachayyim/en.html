1) [Seif 1] One who has a mere discomfort, etc. If he is bedridden or suffers such severe pain that his entire body becomes ill, although there is no danger, treatment may be performed for him through a non-Jew, even if it entails full-fledged labor, as stated below in seif 17; see there.
2) There: One may not perform any treatment for him. Even things permitted to healthy people are forbidden to him whenever his actions demonstrate that his intent is medicinal. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1, in the name of Ran; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
3) There: Even through a non-Jew. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, halachah 10, implies that even a rabbinically prohibited act performed through a non-Jew is forbidden; see there. Beis Yosef cites him.
4) There: As a decree lest one grind medicinal ingredients. Grinding is a primary labor. Bach.
5) There: As a decree lest one grind medicinal ingredients. If a person has swelling on one side of his face caused by cold, the treatment is to immerse a cloth in beer, place it upon the swelling, warm it, and thereby cure it. May this be done on Shabbos in a manner that avoids squeezing, by not soaking the cloth in beer but thoroughly rubbing the swelling with beer by hand and then placing a dry cloth upon it to preserve the warmth? One might say that it is permitted because no medicine is involved, so the decree against grinding ingredients does not apply. Alternatively, all treatment may have been forbidden even when grinding does not apply, lest one perform another treatment that does require grinding. See Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, who discusses this matter and permits it specifically when the suffering is sufficiently severe, because the Sages did not impose their decree in a case of suffering; see there.
