(א) Dangerous sick person — Or Taz, Beitzah 22: Amemar permitted, etc.; see in Rosh chapter 8 of Sheratzim regarding an eye that rebelled — permitted to put kohl on Shabbat; he brought proof for permission in danger to limb that shevut through a Jew is permitted from "an eye that rebelled — permitted to put kohl"; he thought at first that ground spices from erev are meant; he said even to grind today for danger to the entire body it is permitted; and according to this conclusion there is only danger to limb that shevut is permitted through a Jew even if attached to Torah melacha of grinding spices; and in danger to limb — only through a gentile from Beitzah, etc.; and per the fourth view mentioned in Shulchan Aruch to distinguish — in attached Torah melacha it is forbidden even in danger to limb; and this is difficult from "eye that rebelled" for that conclusion; and as Bach wrote. It turns out for view 1 it is difficult from Amemar; and for view 4 difficult from "eye that rebelled" for that conclusion; and for views 2 and 3 — no difficulty except Shulchan Aruch ruled like Ramban's third view to be lenient through change at least; and no difficulty from Amemar, etc.; and as Morus in siman 324 is not bound bread d'rabbanan permitted through change — so too here every rabbinic prohibition for some need is permitted through change, at least not for such a case in a sick person who has no danger. Also Rambam's words chapter 2 halacha 10 do not contradict this; and Or Taz and Magen Avraham 12, 14 explain there; and I will explain further in Magen Avraham sign 95 and Avnei Nezer sign 95 — see there. And know I am uncertain regarding a sick person with danger to one limb whether he may eat rabbinic prohibition — one can say it is lighter than illness of the entire body where without danger to limb it is forbidden to eat rabbinic prohibition like siman 155 in Yoreh De'ah; unlike danger to limb; and it is reasonable he may eat rabbinic prohibition with danger to limb; and I am uncertain with danger to limb whether permitted to heal through Torah prohibition by way of eating and enjoyment; and if they force him with danger to limb to cut if he will not do Torah prohibition — it is explained in Shach Yoreh De'ah 14 siman 157 sign 3 he leans to leniency — see there; and one can say this in other negative commandments; but Shabbat is more severe and not from them; and in other negative commandments with danger to limb one can say permitted to heal through Torah prohibition; and Shabbat is different and is severe; and I the poor one found one responsum in Radbaz third part printed newly in Furth 496 — there is much to learn many novel laws; I will copy it here and this is its title siman 245:

(ב) Question: An official said to a Jew: Leave me to cut off one limb that you will not die from, or I will kill your Jewish fellow — it appears a fortiori: if danger to one limb does not override Shabbat (in Torah prohibition) and Shabbat is overridden for pikuach nefesh — how much more so danger to one limb overrides pikuach nefesh of his fellow; and he answered: we do not punish through inference; and further on Shabbat it is from Heaven; but shevi is different duress — we did not hear to desecrate Shabbat for pikuach nefesh; and also sometimes there is danger to life if he will lose much blood (or fear) and his doubt is preferable to his fellow's certainty — end in brief; see Yevamot 7: there whether he dies on Shabbat or afterward. And nevertheless this was when he said cut for you or I kill your fellow; but when he says you kill your fellow and he said cut off one limb for you — it appears he is obligated to be killed and not transgress; unless one can say in such a case he is liable to death as intentional murder; and nevertheless when they say leave to throw you in such a case — requires study. And in the index there it is written "cut off a limb for you and if not you kill your fellow" — it appears it should be "kill" in the text. And from there we learn if they want to cut him a limb — if he will transgress a negative commandment there is room to be lenient; and even in desecrating Shabbat as safek nefashot lest much blood exit or fear; and specifically in active violation with danger to limb we do not permit Torah prohibition — all the more in melacha that certainly duress permits; and all the more in other negative commandments as we said; and a fortiori conversely if danger to limb overrides his fellow's pikuach nefesh in such a case — all the more Shabbat should override in such a case; and a gentile who wanted to cut an ear if he will not drink stam wine — there is room to be lenient; but wine poured as a libation is certainly included in gentile like siman 154 in Yoreh De'ah Shach sign 14:
