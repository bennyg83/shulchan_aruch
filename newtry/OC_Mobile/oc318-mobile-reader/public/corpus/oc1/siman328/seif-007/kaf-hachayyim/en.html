33) [Seif 7] Shabbos is desecrated for any wound, etc. This applies even to external flesh not upon the back of the hand or foot. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 7.
34) There: Caused by metal. See Tevuos Shor, Laws of Tereifos, siman 44, paragraph 4, who writes that even a gentle cutting motion presents danger; see there.
34) There: And a boil at the anal opening. If one has hemorrhoids or heat at the anal opening and wishes to drink a laxative medicine called rabinett, which cools the blood, it is forbidden. This beverage is taken only medicinally, and anything drunk for treatment is forbidden, as stated in seif 37; see there. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1.
35) There: And one who has a fever, etc. Issur VeHeter, klal 59, siman 26, writes that bloodletting through a non-Jew on Shabbos is permitted for fever. For all other internal illnesses, it is permitted even through a Jew upon the instruction of an expert physician. If a person falls or is struck and blood collects at the place of the fall or blow, however, he is not considered internally ill. Since no danger exists, cupping is forbidden even on Yom Tov and even through a non-Jew. These are his words. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 11; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 7. If a person with fever was given an herbal amulet to wear for several days and was instructed to throw it behind him into a river when that time ended, and the time ends on Shabbos, he may throw it because the river is a karmelis. Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, siman 50.
36) There: Or with chills. The term derives from the verse, 'the hair of my flesh stood on end.' This does not refer to common fever, which is known not to endanger life and begins with cold and ends with heat. It refers to heat and cold striking simultaneously. Taz, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 7; Chayei Adam, klal 68, paragraph 5. All the needs of one with common fever may nevertheless be performed through a non-Jew. Chayei Adam there. Common tertian and quotidian fevers are not dangerous, but quartan fever is dangerous. Mor U'Ketziah states this based on the conditions in his locale. In truth, if a physician says danger exists and the case differs from ordinary common fevers, Shabbos is desecrated. This is not an absolute rule and is obvious. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2. An expert is required to determine whether a fever is extremely hot before Shabbos may be desecrated. Rabbi Yosef Molcho, responsa manuscript, siman 119; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3.
37) There: Or with chills. It appears that regarding everything listed in this seif from the Gemara, we do not listen even if a physician or patient says treatment is unnecessary. Their words are treated as false, since the Gemara states that these conditions are dangerous, and we do not say that circumstances have changed. Regarding an internal wound, for which Shabbos is ordinarily desecrated because danger is presumed, if the physicians and patient say treatment is unnecessary, Shabbos is not desecrated. A dangerously ill person may use items from which benefit is otherwise prohibited. For a non-dangerous illness, using them in an unusual manner of benefit is permitted. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 155, seif 3.
