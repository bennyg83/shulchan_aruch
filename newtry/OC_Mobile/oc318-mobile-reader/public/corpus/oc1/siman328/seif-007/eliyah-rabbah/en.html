[7] [Levush] Fever, etc. Issur VeHeter writes that it is permitted to have blood let by a non-Jew on Shabbos for fever and for all other internal illnesses. Even through a Jew it is permitted on the instruction of an expert physician. However, a person who falls or is struck and blood collects at the place of the fall or blow is not considered internally ill. Since there is no danger, it is prohibited to let blood, called cupping, even on Yom Tov and even through non-Jews. End of his words. Regarding a leech called a niglin, the Magen Avraham at the end of this siman is uncertain whether it constitutes a melachah because of wounding or only a rabbinic prohibition, in which case it would be permitted even where there is no danger. One should be stringent. Sefer Zichronot writes that those who have a fontanel are prohibited from changing the cloth or paper covering it, because pressure sometimes causes blood to emerge. The Magen Avraham, seif katan 33, is uncertain whether, if the opening becomes sealed, one may place a legume into it to reopen it. Regarding changing the cloth, his view appears to permit it. If one knows that wiping it will cause blood to emerge, however, he may not wipe it, because that is an inevitable consequence.
[8] Or if chills, etc. This means that heat and cold come upon him simultaneously. The ordinary fever, may we be spared, that begins with cold and ends with heat is not dangerous, as stated by the Taz and Magen Avraham.
