Shivering. From the language "the hair of my flesh bristles"; and it appears this is what they call in foreign tongue shuddering; and in any case this is not the common fever known to us, for it is known that this is not pikuach nefesh, and its beginning is cold and its end hot; rather this deals when at one time heat and cold come upon him — Taz; and he wrote in Orchot Chayyim that it is permitted to let blood on Shabbat for fever through a non-Jew, and for all other internal illnesses — even through an Israelite it is permitted according to an expert doctor; one who became ill with fever illness and they made for him a healing amulet of herbs to hang on him for a number of days — when the appointed time of days is completed they commanded to throw it into the river from behind him, and it was an expert amulet; and when the time was completed on Shabbat he may throw it, since the river is a karmelit.
