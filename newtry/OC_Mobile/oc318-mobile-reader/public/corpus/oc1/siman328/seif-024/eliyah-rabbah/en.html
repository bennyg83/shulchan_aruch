[25] Grape leaves. The same applies to any leaves that have medicinal effect, as stated by the Magen Avraham. The Raavan, siman 357, writes that placing vegetables over the eye is not considered medical treatment, for Shmuel said regarding them that they involve no issue of healing.
[26] Reed grass, etc. In the Gemara, Shabbos 137, the text reads 'dry,' but the Tur and Rama do not write 'dry.' Perhaps this is because with moist grass it is obvious that one would not place it there, since it becomes repulsive and is not medicinal. The Rambam omits this law entirely. This requires study, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov. I say that the Tur learned it from an explicit Mishnah at the end of Eruvin: a kohen who injured his finger may wrap reed grass around it in the Temple but not outside the Temple. Rashi explains that the prohibition is because it heals. Thus the Tanna also states the law without qualification. The Gemara's suggestion that it used this wording because of the first clause should be understood accordingly. Examine carefully. Alternatively, moist grass is obviously medicinal. Malbushei Yom Tov also overlooked the Rambam's statement in chapter 21, halachah 25, that one who injured his finger may not wrap reed grass around it for the purpose of healing.
