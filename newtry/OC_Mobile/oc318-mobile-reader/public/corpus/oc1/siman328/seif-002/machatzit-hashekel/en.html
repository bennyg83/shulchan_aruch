(א) (s.k. 2) From the teeth, etc. — even if not tzefidanit. Tzefidanit is explained explicitly in mishnah Yoma as danger beginning in the mouth and ending in the intestines; but other illnesses — "from the teeth and inward" was asked and not resolved in Avodah Zarah daf 28: they said regarding a soldier's wound — namely from the teeth and inward — and they asked whether teeth and inward themselves — if they are included in a soldier's wound; and they sought to resolve from what we taught in Shabbos: one concerned about his teeth should not suck vinegar in it — "concerned" yes, but if it hurts greatly — permitted (and perforce it must be said it is a soldier's wound, for otherwise it would be satisfactory to the Gemara that it is ordinary discomfort and not called a patient at all, and even to suck which is very light as will be explained below in name of Ran — it would be forbidden); and he rejected: perhaps this tanna even when it hurts greatly calls him "concerned"; and Rosh wrote: since the question was not resolved — it is a safek nefashot and we go leniently and desecrate Shabbat for him:

(ב) (s.k. 2) And so Ran, etc. — "was asked and not resolved" — until here Ran's language; and what Magen Avraham wrote "and we rule leniently" are Magen Avraham's words; and it follows automatically that since it was not resolved — it is safek nefashot:

(ג) (s.k. 2) And what is written regarding a rebellious eye, etc. — thus Ran: and it is reasonable that when we permit through a non-Jew — this applies to a patient who falls to bed from his illness; but with discomfort where a person strengthens in it and goes like a healthy person — not; and not only that, but even things permitted to healthy people are forbidden to him whenever his actions prove he intends healing; and this is what we learned: one concerned about his teeth should not suck vinegar; and they also said in chapter Eizehu Mekoman: even when it hurts him greatly they call him "concerned," because this thing is not illness but ordinary discomfort end of Ran; and Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote on this: it appears he rules thus; and since pikuach nefesh was stated leniently — and it is possible Ran did not say it to rule thus but only to explain the reason for this rejection end of his words; and Bach rejected Rabbeinu Yehuda's words, for per Rabbeinu Yehuda's view it is not relevant to the beginning of Ran's words; and Magen Avraham preceded to challenge, for Ran himself wrote there that the question was not resolved — and perforce he holds pikuach nefesh was stated leniently; nevertheless from him we learn for another place that where there is only discomfort — even things permitted to healthy people are forbidden to him — all the more forbidden to tell a non-Jew to do melacha, for we see such is the view of those who reject, even though we hold for safek nefashot where it hurts him greatly "from the teeth and inward" — even for an Israelite melacha is permitted; but where there is only discomfort — forbidden even healthy people's food; in this the rejecters' view remains, and even though from the mishnah itself we learn it teaches "one concerned," etc. "should not suck" — if so — nevertheless he brought this rejection to teach us that even this discomfort where it hurts him greatly — only that he strengthens and goes like a healthy person — nevertheless forbidden:
