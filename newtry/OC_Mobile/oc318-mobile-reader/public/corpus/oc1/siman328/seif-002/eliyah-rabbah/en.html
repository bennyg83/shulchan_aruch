[1] And one who asks, etc. The one who is consulted is considered blameworthy. This means that he should already have taught publicly that it is permitted. The Perishah explains that it refers to one who is concerned for his own honor and is upset with a person who permits himself to act without first asking him. Even when two people are able to perform a melachah together in the same manner as on a weekday, it is permitted, as stated in a responsum of the Rama. Even where the ill person himself could perform it with difficulty, a healthy person may perform it for him, as stated in the gloss to Issur VeHeter.
[2] [Levush] Meaning, that he shall live, etc. Rashi's wording in Yoma 85 is: 'which a person shall do and live by them,' meaning the mitzvot by which he shall certainly live and not come through them to possible death. End of his words. Since Rashi does not write 'and he shall live,' which would clearly refer back to the person performing the mitzvah, but instead emphasizes 'and live,' this implies life of another person as well. That is, you are obligated by the mitzvah to act when another person will certainly live, whereas if another is in possible danger of death, do not perform the mitzvah but desecrate Shabbos instead. So it appears to me. The words of the Levush are strained, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov.
