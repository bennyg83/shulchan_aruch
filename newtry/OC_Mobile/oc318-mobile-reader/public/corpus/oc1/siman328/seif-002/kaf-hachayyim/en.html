6) [Seif 2] It is a mitzvah to desecrate Shabbos for him. The verse states, 'which a person shall perform and live through them,' meaning he shall live through them and not die through them. Yoma 85b; Rambam, chapter 2, halachah 3. Even a possible danger to life overrides Shabbos. This is stated there in the Gemara and in the Mishnah on page 83, and Rambam rules accordingly there in halachah 1, as does Shulchan Aruch below in seif 10.
7) There: One who acts promptly is praiseworthy. Even two people may perform one labor in the ordinary manner. Responsa Rema, siman 76. Even if the ill person could perform the acts himself with difficulty, a healthy person may do them. Hagahos Issur VeHeter; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 12; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1.
8) There: One who acts promptly is praiseworthy. See Responsa Kol Yehudah, siman 7, concerning a person who publicly desecrated Shabbos and became dangerously ill, whether one may desecrate Shabbos for him. After lengthy discussion, he concludes that one may not desecrate Shabbos for him unless he violated only a rabbinic prohibition; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 4.
9) There: One who asks, etc. The sage who is asked is blameworthy. Tur in the name of Yerushalmi. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 58, explains that he should have taught this law to the residents of his city before they needed it. Beis Yosef cites him. See Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 155, which requires the treatment to be established or prescribed by an expert; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 68, paragraph 1. When the treatment is established, Shabbos is desecrated even if it is uncertain whether this patient will be cured by it. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See Beis Yosef at the end of siman 134, who writes that ordinary fever is not dangerous. Magen Avraham there. Regarding seif 7, which states that Shabbos is desecrated for one who has a fever, Taz, paragraph 2, writes that this does not refer to the fever common among us; see there. Machatzis HaShekel.
10) There: One who asks, etc. A halachic authority must have these laws fluent in his mouth so that he will not need to delay while consulting books. If a question arises and the authority must pause to consult books, while another learned and knowledgeable person present knows the law, the latter should not defer to the rabbi's honor but should tell the questioner the ruling. Mateh Ephraim, siman 618, paragraph 16.
