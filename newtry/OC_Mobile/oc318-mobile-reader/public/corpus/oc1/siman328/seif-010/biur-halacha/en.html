(א) And if one doctor — and several Acharonim agreed [Magen Avraham in name of Bach and Avnei Nezer — see there] that this is specifically when both are equal; but if one of them is superior in wisdom we heed his words whether to be lenient or stringent — for since in number they are equal, we follow majority of wisdom:

(ב) And one doctor said it is not needed — see in Mishna Berurah who wrote in name of Radbaz vol. 4 siman 15 that if the sick person says "I need such-and-such remedy" and the doctor says it is not needed — we heed the sick person, as we say regarding fasting on Yom Kippur; and in my humble opinion these words apply only when he says he feels weakness in such-and-such limb — therefore they should make him such-and-such remedy that helps for illness of that limb — in this we certainly heed him, for "the heart knows the bitterness of its soul" applies in all matters; and even if the doctor says no remedy is needed we do not heed him; but if the illness is known and the sick person says this remedy helps for this illness and the doctor says it does not help for this — there is no reason to heed the sick person to desecrate Shabbat in vain [and nevertheless it is possible to say where the sick person says it is known to him that his body's nature is to heal from this illness when taking this remedy — it is possible we heed him, for here too there is some applicability to say a person knows his body better than a hundred doctors]; unless there is concern lest his mind be unsettled toward them if he sees they do not act according to his words. And what we say regarding an animal that until seven days we desecrate when she said "I need" — and it is explained in Rashba's chiddushim that even if the doctor said it is not needed we heed her — this too deals in this manner: she says she needs hot water to drink or for bathing that causes weakness in her limbs and the like, and the doctor says that according to her body's strength it is not needed — therefore we do not heed his words, for "the heart knows," etc.; but when she disagrees about which remedy will help — there is no reason to heed her [and see in Shiltei HaGibborim chapter Mefinin s.v. "HaShe'elot" and examine]; and so it appears in Rashba's responsum vol. 4 siman 284 as we said:
