[35] He swallows a large amount of oil. So writes the Rambam, and this is missing from the Shulchan Aruch, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov. The Bach objects to the Shulchan Aruch, saying that the permission applies specifically together with anigron, meaning vegetable broth, but not to oil alone, and that the Rambam is a lone opinion. I am astonished, for the Beit Yosef himself cites Tosafot, who explain that according to Rashi, oil alone is permitted if swallowed immediately, whereas with anigron it is permitted even if held in the mouth for a while. The Rif's words can also be explained this way, and the Hagahot Maimoniyot rules likewise in the name of the Rashba. The Magen Avraham, however, writes that everything depends on place and time. If healthy people do not ordinarily swallow oil, it is prohibited, as stated in seif 37. All this concerns only a minor discomfort. If he has severe pain that weakens the rest of his body, all forms of treatment are permitted. It appears to me from the unqualified wording of the Shulchan Aruch and Rambam that even with anigron the permission applies only if he swallows immediately. They do not accept Rashi's explanation in this respect. So too it appears from the Beit Yosef, Magen Avraham, Shiltei Giborim, Tosafot, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Rabbenu Yonah, unlike Olat Shabbos.
