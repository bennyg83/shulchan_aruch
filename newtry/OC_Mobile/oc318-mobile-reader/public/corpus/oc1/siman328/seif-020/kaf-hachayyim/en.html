44) [Seif 10] Any illness concerning which physicians say, etc. Hagahos Mordechai, chapter Mefanin, discusses these laws at length. It writes that the patient need not say he will die if these things are not done. Whenever the patient knows it is Shabbos and says that he needs something because he cannot endure the illness, Shabbos is desecrated. This is also implied below in siman 618. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1.
45) There: Any illness concerning which physicians say, etc. If the patient refuses food or treatment as an act of piety, he is compelled to accept it. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 66; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, paragraph 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 11; Chayei Adam, klal 68, paragraph 8.
46) If the patient says, 'I need a particular treatment,' while the physician says he does not need it, we listen to the patient. Radbaz there; Knesses HaGedolah there; Magen Avraham there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 12; Chayei Adam there, paragraph 6.
47) If the patient says, 'I need a particular treatment,' while the physician says that treatment will harm him, we listen to the physician. Radbaz there; Knesses HaGedolah there; Magen Avraham there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Chayei Adam there. This applies specifically when the physician says that this treatment will harm him. If the patient says he needs to eat, however, we listen to the patient even when the physician says the food will harm him, as stated in siman 618, seif 1, and Magen Avraham there, paragraph 3.
48) There: Any illness concerning which physicians say, etc. This includes non-Jewish physicians who regularly treat everyone in the city. Issur VeHeter, klal 59, siman 6; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 5. This is also stated below at the beginning of siman 618. We rely upon a non-Jewish physician to desecrate Shabbos even for a condition that the language of the Gemara implies is not dangerous, because our own eyes see that circumstances have changed in all such matters. Responsa Besamim Rosh, siman 259; Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 12.
49) There: If one physician says, etc. The same applies when two physicians oppose two others, as stated below in siman 618, seif 2; see there.
