[46] With oil and salt, etc. It appears to me that this means either one or the other, not that they are mixed. This explains why the Tur and Shulchan Aruch did not write salt. Although the text was emended, it nevertheless means either one or the other, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov. In my humble opinion, however, the Gemara, Shabbos 66, says that one may anoint with oil and salt on Shabbos, as in the incident of Rav Huna. It then describes bringing oil and salt and saying, 'Just as this oil is clear, so may the wine be clear,' and does not separately explain the salt. Rather, they mixed oil and salt, and the mixture was called oil.
[47] It is permitted to anoint it, etc. The Magen Avraham explains the reason, based on the Semag, that these things are not generally prepared through medicinal ingredients. This is strained. It appears to me that this simply is not considered medical treatment, as above in seif 36 and seif 38, and so too it appears from the Taz. According to this, one may reject what Malbushei Yom Tov writes, that in a place where oil is not commonly used, as discussed at the beginning of siman 224, it should likewise be prohibited here. The present case is different, as explained. The Taz writes that one cannot infer from this permission for the custom of placing into the nose powder made from crushed herbs to dispel drunkenness, because that powder is also used medically for other conditions in people who are not drunk, so the decree concerning medicinal ingredients applies. As for the herb called snuff tobacco that people in these lands use even on Shabbos, one must say the reason is that healthy people also use it. This requires study.
[48] [Levush] That it is prohibited for a patient, etc. This implies that for a healthy person it is permitted. See siman 324, seif katan 3. However, in the Rambam, chapter 21, I saw that the word 'patient' does not appear. He merely writes that it is prohibited to exert oneself. The word 'patient' was added in the Tur. According to this, it is prohibited even for a healthy person. Nevertheless, it appears that this applies specifically when he intends to perspire for medical purposes.
