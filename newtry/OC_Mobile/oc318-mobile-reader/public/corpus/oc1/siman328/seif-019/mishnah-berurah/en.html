(63) Permitted in bishul akum — and even though other rabbinic prohibitions are forbidden for a sick person who has no danger to eat and drink, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 155 — bishul akum is different since its prohibition is not on account of itself [Peri Megadim]; and the sick person also blesses on it, for he eats in the permitted manner; and what remains for Motza'ei Shabbat is forbidden even for the sick person himself since it is possible to cook for him then through a Jew. And vessels of bishul akum require kashering, even earthenware vessels — since its essential prohibition is only d'rabbanan, it suffices if he hag'alas three times; and b'dieved if he cooked without hag'alah and there is majority in the dish — permitted; and some are lenient that they need no kashering at all; and one who relies on them regarding vessels in which they cooked for a sick person on Shabbat did not lose:
