(א) (s.k. 43) Discomfort, etc. — in Tur it is written, etc. — it implies, etc. — not like Rabbeinu Yehuda who wrote not specifically for hunger and thirst but ordinary manner of speech; for this Magen Avraham wrote: Tur specifically stated and for precision: if he does for healing — forbidden; and if so there is no distinction here between healthy and patient; and it must be said here ordinary manner of speech — for ordinarily a patient with discomfort does for healing, and ordinarily a healthy person his intent is only hunger and thirst:

(ב) (s.k. 43) For one who bathes, etc. — he is healthy; and even so forbidden if he soaks, since evident; and here too since it is not healthy people's food and evident he does for healing — forbidden even for a healthy person:

(ג) (s.k. 43) And furthermore for they said in the Gemara daf 109: even though he has boils — it is stated there: they bathe in mei garar and Tiberias waters and mei mishra even though he has boils on his head — in what case? When he did not soak; but if he soaked — forbidden end of his words; and from "even though he has boils" it implies the main braita deals without boils and teaches a leniency: even if he has boils; and on this it said in what case when he did not soak, etc. — but if he soaked forbidden — implying in all cases even without boils — namely healthy; and nevertheless forbidden since evident; and soaking too must be something not healthy people's food, for they said he does for healing:

(ד) (s.k. 43) Forbidden to stand on diyonis ground, etc. — from that it stated simply forbidden to stand — implies any person even healthy; and the reason: evident he does for healing, for Rashi wrote there it toils, heats and heals — the mud is salty; and perforce standing in mud; and likewise the language "forbidden to stand on diyonis ground," etc. — and not everyone's way to stand in mud, and evident he does for healing; and healing on Shabbat — who permitted? They said soft drink — Rav said one blesses shehakol since he does for healing; Rav Yosef challenged: but we taught "and both agree one may concern the drink on Shabbat" (and explained above siman 321 seif 14) — healing on Shabbat who permitted? Abaye said per your reasoning — behold we taught: all foods a person may eat for healing on Shabbat, etc.; if so a person intends for eating and healing comes automatically — here too a person, etc.; and why not establish with a healthy person? Therefore permitted — perforce even for a healthy person forbidden where evident; and soaking too must also be something not healthy people's food, for he said he does for healing; and so Rambam chapter 21: one who needs to crush pepper, etc. — but not in a mortar because it is tochen; therefore forbidden for a healthy person to heal on Shabbat — decree lest he grind spices; how so? He should not eat things that are not healthy people's food; and Maggid Mishneh wrote on this: mishnah "one does not eat," etc. — meaning all these things for a healthy person who does not fall to bed and is not ill end of his words; behold for something that is not healthy people's food where evident — even for a healthy person forbidden if he intends healing:

(ה) (s.k. 43) And so in Tosafos daf 18 — these are Tosafos' words Magen Avraham brought in s.k. 22 who wrote: this soaking cucumbers of daf 118 deals with a healthy person and he does not leave it for healing but for pleasure — behold for healing forbidden even for a healthy person; and it must be said there too evident:

(ו) (s.k. 43) Ravin, etc. — R' Yehuda's rope of Sodom sea — he said to him: what about using those waters on Shabbat? He said: permitted; he said: what about opening and closing? He answered to forbid; and it implies he asked about himself, for if about a patient he should have said "what about using for a patient"; and he was healthy, for he walked after R' Yirmiyah:
