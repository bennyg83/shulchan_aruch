(s.k. 18) And closing, etc. — for they say for washing, etc.; and they challenged in Sefer Or Sameach and afterward in Sefer Teshuvah VeHanhagah: what is written in siman 321 regarding soaking cucumbers — there too there is no concern of marit ayin for others who say he intended for washing; and nevertheless requires hakarah for himself who knows he does for healing — namely what was not permitted except from erev Shabbat; if so here too — for himself he has hakarah for what was not permitted to open and close — namely here when he puts wine into the eye whose weekday way is to open and close so the wine enters the eye, for this is the essence of his healing; therefore on Shabbat when forbidden to open and close — this recognition suffices; unlike cucumbers where even on a weekday his way is not to open and close the eye, for the essence of healing is the cucumber placed on the eye; and even though when he opens and closes it heals more — therefore forbidden to open and close, for then observers know his intent is healing and not washing, and there is marit ayin; but hakarah is not called since also on weekdays most times he does not close and open; therefore we require another recognition, as Rashi wrote siman 326 — it should read: in a place where his way is not to anoint with oil except for healing — forbidden to anoint on Shabbat; and see s.k. 22:
