(א) (s.k. 9) They slaughter — for Shabbat was permitted, etc. — for in Yoma daf 6 they disagree: R' Nachman and R' Shimon regarding what the Torah permitted if the community is impure to offer sacrifice in impurity — do we say impurity is overridden in the community and therefore if possible to do in purity must do in purity; or perhaps impurity was permitted in the community and there is no impurity law at all in the community; and Rosh holds like those who say impurity was permitted in the community; and so this law applies regarding what the Torah permitted to desecrate Shabbat in place of danger — is Shabbat overridden regarding a patient with danger, or was it permitted; and Rosh holds it was permitted, and Shabbat regarding a patient with danger is like ordinary weekday; therefore better to slaughter, for there is no Shabbat desecration in feeding him neveilah; and even though just as Shabbat was permitted regarding a patient — because it is written "and live by them" and not "that he die by them" as stated in Yoma daf 85 side 2 — from this also neveilah and all prohibitions were permitted regarding a patient; if so still why is slaughtering better than feeding neveilah? This is resolved per Rosh's words who wrote: and R' Meir answered in responsum on this (meaning permitted to slaughter and not feed neveilah); and he brought a comparison from ochel nefesh on Yom Tov — they slaughter on Yom Tov where there is positive commandment and Torah prohibition, etc.; or say tell a non-Jew to slaughter birds where there is only d'rabbanan prohibition, for there is no Torah slaughter for a bird; but since the Torah permitted ochel nefesh on Yom Tov — for us all food on Yom Tov is like weekday; and here too since the Torah permitted pikuach nefesh — all melacha done on Shabbat for a patient with danger is as if done on a weekday; and where there are two prohibitions — they feed him the lighter one; and slaughtering the food is permitted, but neveilah the food itself is forbidden and violates a quarter-log end of his words; but, etc. end of Rosh's words:

(ב) (s.k. 9) And it implies, etc. — even a d'rabbanan prohibition, etc. — meaning Rosh's words above who brought proof from slaughtering a bird by a non-Jew on Yom Tov — better to slaughter even though in slaughtering by stabbing there is per R' Meir who rules like those who say there is no slaughter for a bird — nevertheless only d'rabbanan prohibition; however to my humble opinion one may question: granted d'rabbanan slaughter prohibition was not permitted at all, and ochel nefesh slaughter was permitted on Yom Tov from the Torah to eat d'rabbanan prohibition where possible to slaughter permissibly and not do any prohibition at all; unlike regarding a patient where all prohibitions were permitted for him — why permit slaughtering to transgress Torah prohibition and not feed d'rabbanan prohibition? Consider yourself: if before us meat slaughtered from erev Shabbat and it is as good as if slaughtered today — would we permit slaughtering for a patient? Here too with d'rabbanan prohibition — from the Torah there is no prohibition, and it would be forbidden to slaughter only to feed that thing; and if Rabbanan forbade that thing — did they come to be lenient on Torah's view? In this Rosh's reasoning does not apply that the food is called the more severe prohibition since it violates a quarter-log, for there is only d'rabbanan prohibition — requires study:

(ג) (s.k. 9) But Ran wrote, etc. — he follows their reasoning who rule impurity is overridden in the community, as Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote in his name; and so Rambam ruled; and Rosh's reason is unnecessary:

(ד) (s.k. 9) And they are more severe than one Shabbat prohibition; and it is possible for this reason: if there is food with d'rabbanan prohibition — they do not permit slaughtering; further reasons: lest when the patient knows he be disgusted by neveilah, unlike if slaughtered on Shabbat he is not disgusted; written in Sefer Darkei Moshe who came to reconcile what is written that they permit slaughtering and do not permit an Israelite to cook lest the wine be poured as libation — on this he wrote: since its prohibition is from their words — namely their wine libation; unlike neveilah which is Torah prohibition; and even per Rosh's view that even d'rabbanan prohibition they do not feed, as Magen Avraham wrote — with this he concluded: and also its prohibition is very light and lighter than other d'rabbanan prohibitions; and for the reason the patient be disgusted by neveilah — he wrote: and also the patient is not disgusted by it:

(ה) (s.k. 9) And also in responsum he permits, etc. — but we do not hold thus, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 255 specifically to make a bath from their ordinary wine — permitted even though it is in the manner of their pleasure, provided he does not drink, since there is no danger; see there; nevertheless regarding Shabbat he made it a branch:

(ו) (s.k. 9) And see Yoreh De'ah, etc. — possible without libation — meaning if the vessel is wide and not full — no concern lest he touch the wine itself since it is not full — impossible to touch with a spoon if an Israelite does not see him; and even though since he places it by the fire he raises it and also while moving it they shake it, and we hold where there is raising and shaking it is forbidden even if he did not touch the wine — nevertheless since the vessel is wide and with a spoon the wine spills through shaking — the non-Jew is not pleased with shaking and does not forbid; and if the Israelite stands near the non-Jew — even if the vessel is full and easily touches — nevertheless no concern for touching that trembles since the Israelite stands beside him:
