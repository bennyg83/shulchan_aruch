And he is not concerned if it closes again immediately [Rashi]; therefore good to do through a non-Jew [Agudah siman 121]; permitted to remove a thorn with a needle (mishnah chapter 17 and Rambam chapter 25) provided he beware not to draw blood, for he makes a wound as stated in the first chapter of Sanhedrin:

(ב) This is permitted. For melacha she-einah tzerichah legufah is d'rabbanan, and in a place of pain they did not decree; and for the one who says he is liable — it appears when he does to extract discharge it is not completion of melacha and impossible to come to liability for striking the final blow in any manner; or it is davar she-eino mitkaven (nevertheless); and those who have a hole in the arm called fontanel — if the hole closed somewhat — requires study whether permitted to put a lentil inside to open it — here certainly his intent is that it remain open, or perhaps since it was already open it is permitted, as written siman 314 seif 3; and the same for a hole in a wound already opened — requires study likewise; and a poultice is permitted to place on the fontanel, as written siman 327; and from Shulchan Aruch's language it implies even if there is a hole and he comes to widen it — forbidden; and in Sefer Ra'avan it implies forbidden to place a poultice on it; if he knows it draws blood when he wipes it — he should not wipe it, for it is psik reisha; but permitted to change to another garment, for if he does not change it will rot — great is human dignity, etc.; and see siman 13; and also they have pain; see siman 317 in Eruvin; and in Sefer HaTorah parashat Mifanim he wrote permitted to extract discharge from one's burn called quaitra in foreign tongue, even though there is blood in the burn, because it is deposited and entrusted like a boil; and so written in name of Sefer HaZikhronot daf 52 that forbidden to change the garment or paper that draws discharge; and also concern lest blood come out and dye the garment; see above seif 46 what is written; and it appears to me here is different, since there is a hole in it — all the more so one should not place zinc oxide on a wound that has no hole; and it appears forbidden to suck scabs that draw blood; and one may say regarding discharge that it is deposited and entrusted; but blood that comes through sucking connects and is absorbed in the flesh and makes chibur in drawing blood:
