(א) One who splits a boil — meaning he splits it:

(ב) To extract, etc. — and he is not concerned if it returns and closes immediately; and better to do through a gentile. Permitted to take the thorn with a needle provided he is careful not to extract blood, for he makes a chaburah; and even though he damages — nevertheless forbidden for all [Magen Avraham]; and even though for a boil they permitted rabbinic prohibition on account of pain, as below here — since it is possible to extract the thorn without extracting blood one should not transgress prohibition for this:

(ג) From it moisture — and even if there is blood together with the moisture — permitted, for that blood and moisture enclosed there contain no chaburah concern; only he should be careful not to press the wound to extract blood anew [Peri Megadim]:

(ד) This is permitted — and even though automatically an opening is made — melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah and d'rabbanan; and in a place of pain they did not decree; and even per the view that melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah is liable — one can say when he does only to extract moisture it is not completion of melacha and impossible to come to liability of striking with a hammer; and those who have an opening in the arm called apitora — if the opening closed somewhat requires study whether permitted to put beans inside so it remain open — for here certainly his intent is it remain open; or perhaps since it was already open — permitted; and the same for an opening in a wound already opened and closed somewhat — requires study whether permitted to open more on Shabbat; and from Shulchan Aruch's language it appears even if there is an opening and he comes to widen it — forbidden. And a bandage permitted to place on apitora as stated s.k. 27; and in Sefer Raavan it appears forbidden to place a bandage on it and permitted to wipe and extract moisture inside; and if he knows it extracts blood when he wipes — he should not wipe on Shabbat, for it is psik reisha; but permitted to exchange another piece of cloth on that place — for if he does not exchange it will putrefy, and great is human dignity; and he also has pain from this; and see above siman 301 in what we wrote there in Mishna Berurah.
