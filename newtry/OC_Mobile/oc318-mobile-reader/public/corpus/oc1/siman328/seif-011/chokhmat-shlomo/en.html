There seif 11, a sick person with danger. NB: Behold this is agreed that pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat, and the same for other negative commandments; and see in Chiddushei Torah parashat Emor year 5597 what I was uncertain there: whether several negative commandments together also override pikuach nefesh, or only one negative commandment is pushed aside for pikuach nefesh and not two negative commandments; and see there what I brought from gemara Yoma regarding feeding him the lighter, etc. And behold incidentally I will explain here the words of Rosh in chapter 8 of Yoma section 14 that are wondrous, thus: they asked Raavad: a sick person with danger, etc., and he answered the words of "some say" are intended [for another case], but one could say that since the prohibition of Shabbat was already given to be pushed aside for kindling and cooking and heating water for him — thus it is impossible there not be one minor at the end of the world, etc. And behold this "or also" has no understanding; and I saw in responsum Chasam Sofer Orach Chayim part 1 siman 25 who strained to explain the intent regarding a minor to circumcise him, and this explanation is not tolerable at all, for then it should have said it is impossible there not be one circumcised today. Therefore it appears in my humble opinion: since we establish regarding a minor who slaughtered and others see him — his slaughter is valid; and since we establish a minor eats neveilot — bet din is not commanded to separate him, only by hand it is forbidden to give him. And behold it appears from gemara Yevamot daf 114, where it says do not feed him by hand — it is proven specifically by hand it is forbidden to give, but to tell him by mouth is permitted; and even if we say also by mouth forbidden to tell him — see in Kinnat Sofrim at its end. Nevertheless it appears this is only rabbinically, and from the Torah permitted to tell him at least by mouth; and thus since if there were a minor here it would be permitted to slaughter through a minor — thus slaughter has permission for its prohibition through a minor; but neveilah for eating — there is no permission to give a person to eat except through pushing aside; therefore better to slaughter for him; and see similar reasoning in Beit Yosef on Tur Orach Chayim siman 11, who wrote that what we establish "if impossible to fulfill both" — a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment — this is specifically when impossible in any manner to fulfill both through permission; but if possible in some manner, even though now impossible, it does not override. Behold what is possible sometime to fulfill through permission, even though now impossible, is considered possible; one could say likewise regarding a sick person it is the reverse: since it is possible sometime through a minor, it is considered possible through permission; therefore it is lighter than a matter impossible in any manner to be through permission; examine.
