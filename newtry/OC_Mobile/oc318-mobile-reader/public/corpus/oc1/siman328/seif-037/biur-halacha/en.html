(א) But if, etc. — see Mishna Berurah what is written in name of Avnei Nezer; and his words flow from the implication of Tur's words, and his source is Rashi in the mishnah explaining what he explained "for his thirst — if he is not sick"; and it appears that for a sick person even for his thirst it is forbidden; however without his words the plain mishnah implies that for his thirst even for a sick person it is permitted since he does not now drink for healing but for thirst — they did not decree on this on account of grinding spices; and behold Rosh who copied all of Rashi on this mishnah omitted these words and did not copy them — it appears he does not hold like him in this; and afterward I found explicitly in Az Nidberu in Pirkei Chagigah end of chapter 8 of Sheratzim who wrote there: "Therefore even though rose oil — if he anoints in his usual manner and does not intend for healing, even though he is healed — whether in a place where rose oil is common or uncommon — permitted," see there; and the same applies in our matter — plainly he holds that even where he has discomfort and drinks things that heal him, since he does not intend for healing but for his thirst — permitted; and even though regarding rose oil Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rif and Rambam — this is because they explain another explanation in the sugya; but on the essence of his reasoning we do not find they argue on him in this; and it is possible they too agree that if he does not intend in anointing for healing — permitted everywhere; and the same in our matter even if he has discomfort, since he does not intend for healing — permitted; and so I lean in my humble opinion from the plain words of Rambam who wrote there halacha 20: "It is forbidden for a healthy person to heal on Shabbat — a decree lest he grind spices; how so — he may not eat things that are not food for healthy people," etc. [and "healthy" necessarily means a healthy person who has discomfort, as is evident in Maggid Mishneh end of s.v. "therefore" and above chapter 2 in his words — see there well; and he said "healthy" to exclude a sick person who has no danger who is permitted these remedies]; and afterward in halacha 22 he wrote: "A person eats foods and drinks that healthy people are accustomed to eat and drink," etc., "even though they heal and he eats them in order to heal — permitted since they are food for healthy people," see there — it appears from this that in the first part where he forbade he also deals in this manner that he eats them in order to heal and therefore forbidden; but if for his hunger — permitted, as is implied from his language that he wrote "forbidden for a healthy person to heal on Shabbat — a decree lest he grind spices" — it implies if he does not intend to heal automatically the decree lest he grind spices does not apply. And behold what compelled Rashi to explain thus — in my humble opinion it emerges for him from the sugya Berachot 38a where Abbaye answered why all who eat eat for healing — a person intends for eating and healing comes automatically; and nevertheless the mishnah concludes except for palm wine — meaning even if he intends to drink it is forbidden because a person does not do so except for healing; and if so why did the mishnah conclude "but he drinks" for his thirst — therefore Rashi explained this is when he is not sick, for then the decree of grinding spices does not apply at all; and the other poskim cited above — in my humble opinion they will explain that the intent is that for all it is not discernible that one will say "a person intends for eating," etc.; unlike palm wine where there is no manner at all for drinking purpose except healing — forbidden; but all this when he truly intends for healing — unlike when he truly intends for his thirst — permitted. The outcome is that in my humble opinion this law depends on a dispute among Rishonim — requires study for practical halacha:

(ב) And likewise if he fell to bed — see in Mishna Berurah where I explained Rama's words; and as Radbaz wrote in responsum at length — see there — that so is implied in Ramban and his supporters; and in Rambam chapter 21 explicitly they wrote that all these of the mishnah which are matters without trace of melacha, which are on a lower level than melachot done through a gentile — are permitted for a sick person; and the mishnah that forbade deals only with a healthy person who has discomfort; however in truth the essential words of Ramban and his supporters who divided this into three levels are not agreed by all; and as we will explain; and from the gemara too it appears at first glance to be stringent like them — behold in Shabbat 109 it states: Ravina found R' Elazar, etc., who was smeared with wine; he said to him "it does not seem right to the Master," etc., and he answered "the back of the hand and back of the foot are different," etc.; and from the plain sugya it appears there are only two levels — either a sick person who has no danger or a sick person who has danger; and so Rashi "his back is endangered and they desecrate," see there; and presumably in this matter it is "it hurts him greatly," and this too is included in "sick person," as is explained in the gloss in seif 17; and according to Ramban and his supporters even if there were no danger here too it would be permitted to fast, for there is no trace of melacha here [and perhaps for clarity he said even if there is danger here; but otherwise even for a mere sick person it is permitted; or perhaps there it is not "it hurts him greatly" until he suffers in his entire body from this — therefore if not for concern of danger through this limb itself it would be forbidden]; and thus R' Chananel there: "and if he is pampered — even wine is forbidden unless there is a wound of the hollow for which they desecrate Shabbat," see there — and from his words too it appears at first glance that no remedy was permitted unless he is a dangerous sick person; and so in Yerushalmi in the sugya it also appears similarly — it states there: "What of drinking kroratin on Shabbat — if for pleasure permitted, if for healing forbidden"; and it challenged "but is it not obvious"; and it resolved "only a matter of danger," end Yerushalmi — it appears we permit drinking a remedy only for a dangerous illness; and so it appears in several Rishonim, namely Meiri 134b "one does not place hot water and oil on the wound in a place where there is no danger — a decree on account of grinding spices" [and so further 128 "royal sons anoint with oil even though there is no danger in them," and so further 148]; and so in Shibolei Leket siman 217: "Foods and drinks that are customary to eat," etc., "permitted; but these that are not customary," etc., "forbidden unless there was danger — so explained R' Yeshaya," see there; and so it appears in Mordekhai chapter 8 of Sheratzim: "A woman who has a wound for several years — if she can place kiln ash," etc., he wrote that on Shabbat it is forbidden if there is no pikuach nefesh — a decree on account of grinding spices, see there — there is none to reject, for kiln ash is worse since it also brings anointing with oil, see there — behold he does not disagree at all; and further more explicit there in Mordekhai on that page regarding drinking urine, see there; and so in Rokeach end of siman 109 he wrote: "And wherever the healing is discernible and there is no danger — forbidden," see there; and behold the main support for those who are lenient is from what is stated in the gemara regarding needs of a sick person who has no danger — they do through a gentile; and behold even if the gentile prepares the remedy for him — the drinking of the remedy itself the Jew does himself and nevertheless permitted; and behold the poskim we brought to be stringent will necessarily answer that here since the gentile prepares the remedy and hands it to him there is a recognizable sign lest the Jew afterward come to grind spices [and see in Beit Yosef]; unlike generally where the Jew takes ground spices prepared from erev Shabbat and there is no recognizable sign — forbidden. I expanded on all this to explain that according to Ramban and his supporters [and according to Maggid Mishneh's words Rambam too holds thus] it is not agreed by the other poskim I mentioned; and nevertheless we cannot protest those who are lenient after Shulchan Aruch ruled like them; and it is a rabbinic matter; and the plain sugya of "they do the needs of a sick person through a gentile" appears more like them:
