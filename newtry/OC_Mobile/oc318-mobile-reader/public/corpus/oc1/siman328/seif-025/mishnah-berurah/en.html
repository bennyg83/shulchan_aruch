(א) He should not return it — lest he smear on it to smooth the bumps in it; and smearing a bandage is melacha d'oraisa on account of memachek; but on account of grinding spices there is no decree since it was on it from yesterday:
(ב) He may return it — for as when the bandage was smoothed from its place it is considered — certainly permitted to return it to its place; and when returning it he should not tie on Shabbat a permanent knot but a bow. And all this when the bandage fell by itself; but if he removed it intentionally — forbidden to return it; and some disagree and permit; and it is possible when he removed it intending to fix — one may rely on them to be lenient:
(ג) And through a gentile, etc. — and it deals when he was somewhat ill from the wound; for if it was only mere discomfort — even through a gentile forbidden, as above at beginning of this siman:
(ד) And permitted, etc. to make a bandage — meaning even though making a bandage is melacha d'oraisa of memachek — nevertheless permitted through a gentile; but this is not permitted except when his entire body became ill from the wound, as above in seif 17 in the gloss, or there is danger to a limb; for if only partially ill — not permitted to do melacha d'oraisa through a gentile:
(ה) And forbidden to place, etc. — even though it is only rabbinic prohibition — nevertheless through the Jew himself forbidden without change, even if his entire body became ill, since he is a sick person who has no danger, as above in seif 17 according to Shulchan Aruch's conclusion there:
