And made tzitzis for it. This is from the Tosefta cited by Beit Yosef. Beit Yosef asks why Tur did not write the same concerning tefillin, since Rambam indeed writes it there as well. He answers in the name of Mahari Abuhav that Tur maintains Shehecheyanu is recited only over a mitzvah that comes at fixed intervals, as implied in the laws of Pesach, siman 432. The reason it is mentioned here concerning tzitzis is that he purchased a tallis, and the blessing is therefore like that recited over new utensils. Beit Yosef writes the same in the name of Baal HaIttur. This is why Tur writes here, “One who purchased a tallis and made, etc.” Tur's wording, however, does not indicate this, because in the core law he mentions only tzitzis and not the tallis. It appears that Tur intended that we should understand the same rule regarding tefillin automatically. What difference is there between them? The Tosefta itself mentions only tzitzis in this passage for this purpose, because tzitzis precedes the mitzvah of tefillin. My father-in-law, Bach, cites Maharshal, who writes upon Tur's ruling that the practice is not to do so. This appears to be because Tosafos in the chapter Lulav VaAravah assume simply that one does not recite Shehecheyanu over either tzitzis or tefillin, and doubtful blessings are treated leniently. Yet according to Tosafos there, one should seemingly not recite Shehecheyanu even over new utensils. They cite Rav Sherira Gaon that we do not rely upon the passage in HaRoeh that requires a blessing over new utensils, because the conclusion in chapter BaKol Me'arvin requires something that comes at fixed intervals. Tosafos then find this difficult from redemption of the firstborn. The settled halachah below is that Shehecheyanu is recited over new utensils, based upon Beit Yosef's explanation in the name of Terumas HaDeshen that this too resembles something that comes at fixed intervals. Nevertheless, both regarding tzitzis and tefillin, the first time one recites the blessing over them he should also recite Shehecheyanu. This follows Rama in Yoreh Deah, siman 28, concerning covering the blood, from which we learn that whenever a person performs a mitzvah for the first time, he recites Shehecheyanu. This appears correct.
