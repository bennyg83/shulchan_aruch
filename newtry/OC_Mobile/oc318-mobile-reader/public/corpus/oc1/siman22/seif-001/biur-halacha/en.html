One who purchased, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 2, and Beit Yosef, who explains that this entire ruling follows Baal HaIttur. Know that although one should certainly follow this view initially, since Shulchan Aruch and many later authorities rule like Baal HaIttur, the matter is not entirely settled. Olas Tamid and Taz rule like Rambam, and Eliyah Rabbah cites them. The Gra likewise rules like Rambam, that one must recite Shehecheyanu even over new tefillin. All this applies when he made them himself. If he purchased the tefillin from a scribe, or if another person attached the tzitzis to his garment, the Gra implies that even according to Rambam he need not recite Shehecheyanu, unless he has now purchased a new tallis, in which case he recites it because of the new garment. It is therefore proper that he create another obligation to recite Shehecheyanu and intend to exempt this item as well. This is especially important if he is now wearing tefillin for the first time in his life, for some poskim maintain that whenever a person performs a mitzvah for the first time in his life, he recites Shehecheyanu. See Tevuos Shor in Yoreh Deah, siman 28, Peri Megadim there, and Eliyah Rabbah in this siman. The Gra in this siman appears not to accept the view of Rokeach on this point.
At the first wrapping. See Peri Megadim, who writes that this is not necessarily exact and refers to Levush in siman 225. His intent is to the ruling in seif 3 there that if one did not recite the blessing upon first seeing the item, he may recite it upon seeing it a second time. Levush explains that the obligation to recite the blessing still rests upon him. The same should apply here. At first glance, it is surprising that Peri Megadim did not refer to Magen Avraham there, who disagrees with Rama's gloss and himself agrees with the dissenting view; see there. One may answer that Peri Megadim's intent concerns Rama: according to what Rama writes there, his wording here, “the first wrapping,” is not exact. Know further that the Gra there appears to agree with Rama's gloss, and Eliyah Rabbah writes there that this is the halachah. Accordingly, in our case one may apparently rely after the fact upon reciting the blessing even later. This also appears, in my humble opinion, from Rambam's wording. He writes that if one did not recite Shehecheyanu over sukkah, lulav, and similar mitzvos at the time of making them, he recites Shehecheyanu when he fulfills his obligation with them.
