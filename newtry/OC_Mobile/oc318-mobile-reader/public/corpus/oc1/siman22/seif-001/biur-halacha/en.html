One buys, etc. — See Mishnah Berurah, se'if katan 2; see Beit Yosef — all this is the view of the author of the Ittur. Know that although lechatchilah one should certainly practice thus, since Shulchan Aruch and many later authorities ruled like the Ittur, nevertheless it is not a decisive matter — Olas Tamid and Taz ruled like the Rambam; Or Zarua and Gra in their commentaries also rule like the Rambam, that one must bless Shehecheyanu on new tefillin too [and all the more so if he made them himself; but if he bought tefillin from a scribe, and likewise regarding tzitzis if another attached them to his garment — it seems from Gra there that even per the Rambam one need not bless Shehecheyanu unless he now bought the tallis anew, for then one blesses because of new vessels]. Therefore it is proper that one include oneself in obligation of Shehecheyanu from another side, and intend to exempt this too — especially if he is now wearing the tefillin for the first time in his life; he should certainly consider this advice, for there are poskim who hold that for every mitzvah a person performs for the first time in his life, one blesses Shehecheyanu. See Turei Zahav in Yoreh De'ah siman 28 and Pri Megadim there, and Or Zarua in this siman. From Gra in this siman it seems he does not hold like the Rokeach in this.

At the first wrapping — See Pri Megadim, who wrote it is not precise; he directed one to examine Levush siman 225 — his intent is to what se'if 3 states there: if one did not bless at the first sighting, one may bless the second time. Levush explained there the reason: the obligation of the blessing still applies — see there. If so, the same in our matter. It is surprising on Pri Megadim: why did he not hint to examine Magen Avraham there, who disagrees with the Rama gloss there and himself agreed with it — see there. One may answer: his intent was on Rama — for according to what he wrote stated in the gloss, his words here in the gloss that "first wrapping" is not precise. Know further: from Gra's words there it seems he agrees with the Rama gloss there; Or Zarua also wrote halacha is thus. If so, in our matter one may rely bedieved to bless even afterward; and so it seems in my humble opinion from the Rambam's wording in our matter: if one did not bless Shehecheyanu on sukkah, lulav, and the like at the time of making, one blesses Shehecheyanu when fulfilling one's obligation with them.
