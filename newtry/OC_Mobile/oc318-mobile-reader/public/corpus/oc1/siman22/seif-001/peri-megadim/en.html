And makes — see Taz. The principle: one blesses Shehecheyanu only if one has joy — even if not from time to time, one blesses for joy of heart that God revived him and he acquired, etc. Therefore new vessels — one blesses, as Berachos 37; mitzvos, even though they are royal decree and one does not have so much joy — any that come from time to time, one blesses Shehecheyanu that God revived him for this time. Pidyon haben — Bach wrote the child must be thirty-one days; nevertheless, anything with joy one blesses, like new vessels that bring joy — so Levush explained here. At milah, Yoreh De'ah 265, se'if 6 — one does not bless Shehecheyanu; I thought to say because of the infant's pain; but I saw Levush there: if so, when one's father dies, why bless Shehecheyanu (Orach Chayim 223, se'if 2 — if he has inheritance)? He answered: milah is common and pidyon haben applies only to a firstborn — see there. Bach's words also do not help for milah, that one must wait eight days — what difference between thirty-one days or eight days? See Ginas Veradim, chapter 3 of Milah, letter 4 — he wrote what Levush wrote — see there.

And what Taz wrote — on a mitzvah one is educated in for the first time, one blesses Shehecheyanu, like one who became bar mitzvah, and a minor who reached education in tzitzis — see Yoreh De'ah 28 in Shach, letter 5, who left this in doubt. If one bought a garment of fox skins, etc., one blesses Shehecheyanu; no comparison to Yoreh De'ah 28, se'if 2, on slaughter — one does not bless Shehecheyanu, for it harms a creature with hands, unlike here. No comparison to 223, se'if 6, in the gloss — one does not say "wear out and renew," for they would need to kill again — see there in Magen Avraham; unlike here, in my view.
