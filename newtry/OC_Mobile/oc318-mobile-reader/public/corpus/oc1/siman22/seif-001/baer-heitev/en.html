New. He does not recite Shehecheyanu over the mitzvah itself, since it does not come only at fixed intervals. Accordingly, if he made tzitzis for an old garment that he had already purchased and over which he had already recited Shehecheyanu, he does not recite the blessing. The same applies to tefillin: he does not recite Shehecheyanu. This is why the text says, “One who purchased a tallis and made tzitzis for it.” One should not ask that the blessing over new utensils is recited at the time of purchase, and therefore why the text mentions that he made tzitzis for it. The answer is that when one purchases a finished garment ready to wear, he recites Shehecheyanu at the time of purchase. If he purchases fabric from which garments will be made, however, he recites it at the time of wearing. Here, because he is attaching tzitzis to it, he recites the blessing incidentally at the time he performs the mitzvah. This is the view of Beit Yosef, Bach, Maharshal, Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah, and Magen Avraham. Rambam, however, maintains that one recites Shehecheyanu because of the new mitzvah being performed. He therefore writes this law regarding both tzitzis and tefillin; see there. Olas Tamid writes that one should follow Rambam, and Taz writes similarly: both for tzitzis and for tefillin, the first time one recites the blessing upon them he should also recite Shehecheyanu. This follows Rama in Yoreh Deah, siman 28, concerning covering the blood, from which we learn that whenever a person performs a mitzvah for the first time, he recites Shehecheyanu. See there, and see Yoreh Deah there, seif katan 2, as well as Bnei Chayei and responsa Chavos Yair, siman 237.
At the first wrapping. Meaning, he first recites Shehecheyanu and afterward the blessing “to wrap oneself in tzitzis.” Bach and Olas Tamid.
