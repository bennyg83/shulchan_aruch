[1] He blesses at the time of wrapping, etc. Olas Tamid explained: one blesses Shehecheyanu and then "to wrap oneself." See my book on Yoreh De'ah siman 28, where I wrote one blesses Shehecheyanu after covering the blood; and the same law for pidyon haben in siman 305. Also, the Tosefta compares it to sukkah and lulav, who bless Shehecheyanu when fulfilling the mitzvah with them; and there we establish one blesses first on sukkah and lulav and then Shehecheyanu — this requires investigation. Levush wrote: but one who makes tzitzis on an old tallis, even if it never had tzitzis, need not Shehecheyanu — for there is no set time for making tzitzis, and there is no joy in tzitzis without a new tallis. Bach wrote: Rambam and Tur hold that even on an old tallis, since making tzitzis renews it, one blesses; and likewise tefillin. Shach on Yoreh De'ah siman 28 wrote the Rambam's reason: because they are his acquisition — see there. Taz and Olas Tamid rule like the Rambam to bless even on tefillin. Taz concluded — and this is his wording — nevertheless both for tzitzis and tefillin one should bless Shehecheyanu the first time, like covering blood; and from this, for every mitzvah a person does the first time, one blesses Shehecheyanu — so far his wording. It implies all agree one blesses the first time one is educated in it — unlike Shach there and Chavot Yair siman 237, who wrote plainly one does not bless. It also seems to me one may prove from Bach siman 29 that we do not say safek berachos lehakel regarding Shehecheyanu. Evidence from Berachos 60a regarding one who acquired new vessels: he asked, let us distinguish between acquisition and re-acquisition, to inform you Rabbi Yehuda's strength that one must bless; they answered: the strength of permitting is preferable to him. One might say: Rashi at the beginning of Beitzah explains the strength of permitting is preferable because the strength of forbidding is not proof — everyone can be stringent even in permitted matters — see there. If so, in Berachos the opposite — the strength of obligation to bless should be preferable, since safek berachos lehakel applies so as not to violate "do not take in vain." Rather, in my humble opinion, Shehecheyanu blessings are different, for one does not violate "do not take in vain" — and consider well. This also resolves what Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah wondered below, siman 219, in the name of Maharash Algazi, on the Tur who wrote we are stringent not to bless Birkat HaGomel except on twelve — he wondered on the Tur from the Gemara cited above, which calls it "strength of permitting" if one does not bless — implying not blessing is leniency — see there. According to what I wrote, there is no difficulty — Shehecheyanu blessings are different. Below I wrote more on this, be-siyata di-shmaya.
