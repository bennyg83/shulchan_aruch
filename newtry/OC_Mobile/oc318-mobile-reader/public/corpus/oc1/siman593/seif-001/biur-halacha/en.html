Prevent fulfillment, etc. This means that if he does not know all of them, he should not say even one of them. The Pri Megadim wrote: this requires analysis, why should he not say the first three blessings, the last three blessings, and the sanctity of the day in the middle? For the Tur wrote in the name of Rav Yitzchak ibn Ghayyat in siman 591 that the congregation prays silently only seven blessings. Although we do not rule so, nevertheless where he does not know, etc. Perhaps indeed this is so; only he should not say the ten verses. End quote. That is to say, the Gemara states: What is the reason? Rabbah said: The Holy One, blessed is He, said: Say before Me kingship verses so that you make Me King over you, remembrance verses so that your remembrance rises before Me for good, etc. The intent of the Gemara would be that if he will not say remembrances and shofaros, he should not say the ten verses of kingship, but the blessing itself he should say. In truth, however, this is very forced, for the language of the Gemara, the blessings prevent fulfillment, implies that if he omitted one blessing he should not say the second. Indeed, his question is not difficult at all, for according to Rav Yitzchak ibn Ghayyat, who holds that the congregation silently says only kingships, the Gemara that mentions kingships, remembrances, and shofaros must refer to the shaliach tzibbur, and with him they certainly prevent each other, as Rabbah states the reason. According to us, who hold that the congregation too is obligated to pray nine blessings, the beraisa refers also to the congregation, and the reason is as Rabbah said.
One another. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote in the name of the Magen Avraham. Know that in Sefer Nehar Shalom he disagreed with him, as follows: If his intent is the six blessings added on a fast day, this is astonishing, for according to the explanation of the Rosh and Ran this is explicit in the Gemara; see there. Why then did he say that so is proven in the Gemara? And if his intent is the eighteen blessings of the whole year, this too is astonishing, for they were instituted only adjacent to one another, which is why they do not have an opening formula, and especially the first three and last three are considered like one. Also, there is no implication of this in the Gemara. End quote. So too wrote the Eliyah Rabbah. In my humble opinion it appears simple, as I wrote inside, that even according to the Magen Avraham this is only a general plea for mercy, and in any event he thereby fulfills the Torah mitzvah of prayer, for one request is enough, as the poskim wrote. The proof of the Magen Avraham is from the language of the Gemara, which says, the blessings of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, implying that on the rest of the days of the year they do not prevent fulfillment. It must refer to the manner that I wrote.
