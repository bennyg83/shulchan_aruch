So too it appears from the Gemara. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. However, Nehar Shalom, os 1, challenged the aforementioned words of the Magen Avraham. If his intent is the six blessings added on fast days, this is astonishing, for according to the explanations of the Rosh and Ran, this is explicitly stated in the Gemara. If his intent is the 18 blessings recited throughout the year, this is likewise astonishing, for they were instituted only in sequence with one another. For this reason they do not have opening formulas, and in particular, the first 3 and the last 3 are considered as one. Furthermore, this is not implied by the Gemara, see there. Eliyah Rabbah, os 2, likewise challenged the aforementioned words of the Magen Avraham and left the matter requiring further study. The Mishna Berurah, os 2, wrote that even according to the Magen Avraham, this is merely a prayer for mercy, but he does not fulfill his obligation at all through it. For even if he omitted only one blessing of the 18, we rule above, siman 119, seif 3, that he must return and recite all of them in the order in which they were instituted. It can therefore be said that he does not fulfill the obligation of prayer through this, and all the more so in this case. It also appears that he should attach it to some blessing, such as "Who formed light" or the like, so that it will be connected to the blessing adjoining it, end quote. Meaning, he nevertheless fulfills through this the Torah commandment of prayer, for one request is sufficient, as the poskim wrote, and as written in the Biur Halacha there, see there.
