(s.k. 2) Prevent fulfillment, etc. So it is also implied in the Gemara, folio 34b: The Sages taught: the blasts of a fast day do not prevent one another; the blessings of a fast day do not prevent one another. The blasts and blessings of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur do prevent fulfillment. The Ran explained, unlike Rashi, that ordinary blasts and blessings, such as those of a fast day, do not prevent one another; if one knows one of the blessings, or knows one of the blasts but is not expert in the others, he blows or blesses the one he knows and in which he is expert.
The blasts and blessings of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur prevent one another, such that if one knows one of the blessings, or knows how to blow one of the blasts but is not clear about the others, he does not bless and does not blow even those in which he is expert. End quote. So we rule like the Ran. Thus, on a fast day the blessings do not prevent one another, and the same applies on the rest of the days of the year. Although it is possible that what it says, that on a fast day they do not prevent, refers to the six blessings added on a fast day to the eighteen blessings recited all year, as written in siman 575, seif 4, similar to the blessings of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, where certainly the intent is only kingships, remembrances, and shofaros, and not the other seven blessings; and see above at the beginning of siman 591. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham understands that one should not distinguish between the added blessings on a fast day and the other blessings.
