Seif 4: One must lengthen. This is the language of the Tur, and it implies from their words like the first formulation, that this refers to its writing, since he wrote it in this siman among the laws of writing.
In its writing, and some say. The Rosh and the Ran.
And it requires, and so on. In the Yerushalmi there, Rabbi Yirmiyah bar Bun said: one must have “ish” at the beginning of the page and “ve'es” at its end, descending like this strap. This means like two straps of a pouch, equal on both sides like a builders? plumb line. See Maseches Sofrim there. What it says, “at the beginning of the page,” means at the beginning of the line and at the end of the line, not like those who explain it as the beginning of the column. For otherwise, what did Rabbi Yirmiyah bar Bun come to add to what Rabbi Yochanan said in the name of Rav, who said there that the ten sons of Haman and the song of the kings of Canaan are written brick over brick, and so on? From this came the practice of writing the ten sons of Haman in their own column and in large letters, but this is a mistake, for it was not transmitted to us to write the ten sons of Haman with the letters of the large alphabet. Rather, the main explanation of “at the beginning of the page” is at the beginning of the line, as above. Rabbi Yirmiyah bar Bun teaches this very point: if we had only Rabbi Yochanan in the name of Rav, I might have said that since the ten sons of Haman were separated from all other songs and equated with the song of the kings of Canaan, in that both are written only brick over brick, and so on, as above and as in the Gemara, then we should equate them entirely, and not write within the ten sons of Haman the word that precedes them, just as the song of the kings of Canaan begins with “king,” and so on. Therefore Rabbi Yirmiyah bar Bun said that “ish” must be at the beginning, meaning as above, at the beginning of the line, and “ve'es” at the end of the line, like a pouch and two straps, as above, and like the other letters of the Megillah.
