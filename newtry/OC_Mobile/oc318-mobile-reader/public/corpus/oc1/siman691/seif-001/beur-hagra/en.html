Seif 1: On gevil or, and so on, like a sefer Torah. There it says “on a sefer,” and in the Gemara there: from where do we know this? It is derived by the word “writing” from “writing,” and so on. This follows his own view, for he ruled in chapter 1 of the laws of tefillin that a sefer Torah may be written only on gevil or parchment. So too the Shulchan Aruch ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, seif 3. This is as written in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah, that one must write on gevil on the hair side and on parchment on the flesh side, and if one changed this it is invalid. This implies that only these are valid. So too they said in the first chapter of Maseches Sofrim, and in Bava Basra 14a: He said to him, with gevil it is six, with parchment how much, and so on. What is written in Shabbos 79b, that the academy of Menashya taught, and so on, and they established it as referring to a sefer Torah, is an individual opinion.
And if he wrote it, and so on. So too he ruled regarding a sefer Torah. This does not mean with each one by itself, for kankantum is invalid in the Mishnah, and it implies that it is not ink; the same applies to a sefer Torah, as written in Shabbos 103b, “if he wrote it not with ink,” and so on. In the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Megillah, it is a halachah to Moshe from Sinai that they be written with ink. Likewise, gall-nut water in Gittin 19a implies that it is not ink except when both are together, for then it is ink. It teaches us that although it cannot be erased, as written in Eruvin 13a, nevertheless it is permitted. He ruled like Rabbi Yehudah in the name of Rabbi Meir, who said: into all of them one may place, and so on, as written in chapter 1 of the laws of tefillin. This is their language: How is ink made? They gather the soot of oils, and so on, soak it in gall-nut water or the like, and write with it, so that if one erases it, it is erased. This is the ink with which it is the choicest mitzvah to write a sefer Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos. If one wrote all three with gall-nut water and kankantum, which endures and is not erased, they are valid. If so, what did the halachah said to Moshe at Sinai, that they be written with ink, exclude? It excludes other kinds of colors, such as red, green, and the like; if one wrote even one letter in a sefer Torah, tefillin, or mezuzah with other kinds of colors or with gold, they are invalid. So too the Rosh wrote in Hilchos Ketanos. This is what is meant here by “if he wrote it with other,” and so on. This is his language in his commentary to the second chapter of Sotah: I saw in Eretz Yisrael that they distance themselves from writing a sefer Torah with one type of ink called in Arabic chibr, and write only with actual ink. He erred in the halachic ruling from the Gemara, for the last statement said in the Gemara on this matter is that one may place kankantum into all of them except the Sotah passage in the Mikdash. The copyist wrote there: I, the copyist, saw fit to explain what chibr is, as the Geonim explained. Chibr is gall-nut ink, into which they place kankantum, a kind of blackening earth that is not erased. So too wrote Rabbeinu Chananel, of blessed memory, and so on; see there.
And it requires ruling. On each and every line, unlike the Levush and Magen Avraham. They erred in this, for regarding tefillin, which do not require ruling, we say so, as written in Tosafos, Gittin 6b, s.v. “Rabbi Yitzchak said,” and so on: “And if you say,” and so on, and Rabbeinu Tam says, and so on, and Rabbeinu Tam says, and so on; and likewise in Agudah. But a mezuzah and anything that requires ruling must necessarily be ruled on every line. See there and in the Rosh.
And the hide is not, and so on. This follows his own view, for he ruled that even a mezuzah does not require processing for its own sake. Although regarding a sefer Torah we hold that it requires processing for its own sake, as they said in Gittin 54b, and so he ruled in chapter 10 of the laws of sefer Torah and in chapter 1 of the laws of tefillin, they hold that this was said only regarding a sefer Torah, and likewise tefillin, which are called Torah, as written in Shabbos 28b and 108a. But other things, such as books and mezuzah, although their law is like a sefer Torah, do not require this. He learned this from what is written in Menachos 42b: tefillin have, and so on; books and mezuzos, and so on, according to Rashi's explanation there, s.v. “tefillin,” and s.v. “and they are purchased,” and so on. We have no “books” in this context except Megillah. See Tosafos there, s.v. “tefillin,” and so too the Rambam explained, and so on; see there. He learned further from what is written in Sanhedrin 48b: it is a dispute of Tannaim, as it was taught, and so on. He ruled like the first Tanna and like Rava, according to Rashi's explanation there, and as written in chapter 2 of the laws of tefillin. Although they have a shin, as they said in Shabbos 28b, “but for their hide,” and did Abaye not say “the shin,” and so on, the same applies here regarding Megillah and likewise regarding mezuzah. Although in Menachos 42b Rabbi Yehudah said in the name of Rav that if he made it, and so on, it is a dispute of Tannaim, he ruled like Shmuel, as they said in chapter 1 of the laws of tzitzis; he ruled like the sugya in Sukkah 9a, that Beis Hillel do not accept Rabbi Yehudah, and so on, because there it is different, and so on, while the sugya in Menachos follows Beis Shammai.
And some say that it requires. This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam, who wrote in Menachos there, s.v. “until,” and so on, that the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for the sugya in HaNizakin follows him. Regarding the chapter Nigmar HaDin, where Abaye and Rava disagree, and so on, Rabbeinu Tam and Rabbeinu Chananel explained that Rava follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who requires processing for its own sake. Necessarily, designation is not effective; for otherwise, it could be done through cutting the hide and small repairs, and so on. So too he wrote in Sanhedrin there; see there. So he ruled regarding mezuzah. See siman 32 and Yoreh De'ah, siman 288.
