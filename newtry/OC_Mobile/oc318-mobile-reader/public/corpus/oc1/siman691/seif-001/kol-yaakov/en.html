1) Seif 1, One does not write, etc., on gevil or on parchment like a Torah scroll. If he wrote it on dukhsustus it is invalid, as with a Torah scroll, for the Mechaber ruled in every place like the Rambam, who wrote in Hilchos Tefillin, chapter 1, halacha 9, that it is forbidden to write a Torah scroll on dukhsustus. Beur HaGra, letter 1. See Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, letter 1. There are those who validate it; see Responsa Radbaz in the language of the Rambam, siman 125; Erech HaShulchan, letter 2; Beis Oved, in the laws of writing a Megillah, letter 2. Likewise, if he wrote half of it on gevil and half on parchment, it is valid after the fact, Radbaz there, Erech HaShulchan there, Beis Oved there. Beis Oved there wrote that the same applies if he wrote half on gevil or on parchment and half on dukhsustus, that it is valid.
2) There, And if he wrote it with gallnut water, etc. Nowadays the scribes are accustomed to make ink, even initially, from gallnut water and vitriol. See our words in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, letters 15 and 17.
3) A Megillah. A printed Megillah is invalid. Bach; Pri Chadash, letter 2; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 5; Keset HaSofer, siman 21, letter 7; Lishkas HaSofer; Shaarei Ephraim, gate 6, letter 59. If they already printed Megillos on parchment, they must be placed in genizah, because one who sees them on parchment in the form of written Megillos will think they are valid, read from them, not fulfill his obligation, and recite a blessing in vain. But to print their equivalent on paper is permitted, for people will not err regarding them. Birkei Yosef, letter 1; Zecher LeAvraham, letter mem; Beis Oved, in the laws of writing a Megillah, letter 18. See our words in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, letter 24.
4) There, And it requires scoring lines, etc. One must score every line, as with a Torah scroll. Darchei Moshe, letter 1; Chacham Tzvi, siman 97; Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 1; Beurei HaGra, letter 3; Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger there; Keset HaSofer, siman 28, letter 1, and Lishkas HaSofer there; Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 1 in Chochmah, and letter 3 in Binah. See Erech HaShulchan, letter 4, who wrote that this is the view of the Mechaber, of blessed memory. So too Beis Oved wrote there, letter 3, and so too in Sefer HaKavanos, gate 6, in the discourse for Purim, discourse 1, that its law is like a Torah scroll. It is explained in Pri Etz Chaim, gate of Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, and Purim, chapter 6, regarding scoring lines and crowns. So is the practice here in Baghdad, may the Almighty protect it, to score all the lines, as with a Torah scroll. If he did not score all the lines, even after the fact it is invalid, Keset HaSofer there; Lishkas HaSofer; Meleches Shamayim there in Binah. If he has no scored Megillah, but only one with the first line scored, Beis Oved wrote there that he should read from it, but not bless over it; and when a scored Megillah later becomes available to him, he should read it again with a blessing, since most authorities hold that a Megillah not fully scored is invalid. If, when he reads it again from a valid Megillah, he does not wish to bless out of concern for the view of Orchos Chaim and Levush, who hold that the first line alone suffices, we do not allow him to refrain. These are his words. However, it appears that he should not bless, for Zecher LeAvraham, part 1, Hilchos Berachos, letter samech, and part 3, letter samech, wrote that with respect to leniency in a doubtful blessing, we do not follow the majority. Even if the doubt is not about the blessing itself but about performance of the mitzvah, nevertheless one should not bless. So too wrote Divrei Avraham, siman 15, letter 18.
5) There, And its hide does not require processing for its required purpose, etc. The later authorities wrote that one should not rely on this reasoning except in pressing circumstances, when he has no other Megillah, and even then he should not bless over it. Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, letter 2; Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 1 in Chochmah and letter 2 in Binah. So Ruach Chaim, letter 1, ruled as practical halacha in accordance with the view cited as yesh omrim, and Keset HaSofer, siman 28, letter 1, ruled likewise, unlike Beis Oved there, letter 4. And so is the practice here in Baghdad, may the Almighty protect it, to process the hide of the Megillah for its required purpose.
