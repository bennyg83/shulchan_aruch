(*) Seif 1 in Shulchan Aruch: One may not write, etc. See Birkei Yosef, who brought in the name of Mahar"m Provenzal in a manuscript responsum that in an incident where megillot were printed on parchment, he required them to be placed in genizah, for they are invalid because of chak tokhot, and perhaps people would come to err and validate them; but on paper people will not come to err; see there. See Pri Chadash, that if most of it is printed it is invalid, but if most of it is written it is valid, unlike Sefer HaZikhronot, etc. See Korban Netanel, who challenged him, for he compares it to a scribe omitting text, etc.; but this, where it was written improperly, is worse, as Rashi wrote on page 9. Birkei Yosef defended Pri Chadash, explaining that he holds Tosafot disagree with Rashi on this; see there. Regarding whether printing is chak tokhot, several responsa hold that it is considered writing. See above siman 284 and in Yoreh Deah, Laws of a Sefer Torah. See Tashbetz, vol. 1, siman 8, that a megillah written in mashet script, which is like our meshuta, is invalid. Kenesset HaGedolah cites this and wondered at the poskim for omitting it, since our Mishnah teaches that it must be written in Ashurit; see there. I have been brief here.
(a) Ruling lines. See Baer Heitev. What he wrote in the name of Chacham Tzvi, see Birkei Yosef: Kenesset HaGedolah wrote that one who trembles at the word of God should seek a megillah ruled throughout. Nevertheless, if only the first line is ruled it is valid after the fact, but if it is not ruled at all it is invalid even after the fact.
