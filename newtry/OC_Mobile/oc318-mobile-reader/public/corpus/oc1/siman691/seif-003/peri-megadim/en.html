In the Rosh it implies that one should lengthen it in writing, so that the vav is among the large letters; see there. I did not understand the explanation of the Ran, of blessed memory. If he means that the vav should be only a straight line without a bent head, this is not the form of a vav, as the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 36 in the name of HaManhig, that it must be like hooks, like pegs for posts, so that the curtains should not fall. If he means that the somewhat bent head should stand upright diagonally upward, nevertheless I have not seen the scribes of our time do this; they only write it among the large letters. This requires analysis. Regarding missing and extra letters, see Eliyah Rabbah, who elaborated and brought different versions. Although after the fact it is valid immediately, for it is like where the scribe omitted up to half of it, nevertheless initially it is proper and correct to write according to the law. In Shulchan Aruch, seif 8: publicly, one does not fulfill the obligation even after the fact with a Megillah written among the Writings, while an individual reads from it. This implies that even initially, even if he has a Megillah written separately, he may read from this one. But in the Tur he wrote: “but an individual who reads from it has fulfilled the obligation,” which implies after the fact. See the Rosh, chapter 2, page 19. From there it also implies that if it is missing or extra, the public fulfills with it after the fact, though perhaps not initially. The Mechaber ruled like the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 2 of Megillah, halachah 1, that publicly one has not fulfilled the obligation from it even after the fact, while for an individual, even initially he reads from it. Perhaps what the Tur wrote, that an individual fulfills after the fact, is because initially an individual is not common, for a Megillah in its proper time initially requires ten. Nevertheless, he should have explained that if there are not ten and he has a Megillah written separately, he may initially read from one written among the Writings. See Megillah 19a, regarding the one who read from a Megillah written among the Writings; see there. See the Beis Yosef regarding what the Mordechai wrote, that it is not comparable to a Megillah written among the Writings. From the fact that it is not comparable to a Megillah written among the Writings, there is no proof that an individual may read initially; therefore the Mordechai needed to distinguish between them regarding the initial law. In the Rema, seif 11: one does not recite a blessing over a stolen Megillah. See siman 649, seif 1, that even if he acquired it through a physical change and the like, nevertheless he should not bless over it. The same applies here. Although there is no theft of sound, nevertheless there too he acquired it and still does not bless, since it came through theft; the same applies here. The same applies to shofar, that he does not bless; see siman 586 in the Magen Avraham there. With Hashem’s help, it will be explained further in the Magen Avraham here. What is written that with a stolen Megillah he fulfilled the obligation because there is no theft of sound: were it not for that reason, even though “yours” is not written, nevertheless it would be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, like shofar. If not for the fact that there is no theft of sound, he would not fulfill the obligation. See the Beis Yosef here and in siman 586. According to the Mechaber in siman 649, seif 5, which is the view of the Rambam, of blessed memory, that in Rabbinic matters one fulfills even with a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, one must say that Megillah is words of Kabbalah and is like Torah law, and therefore the reason that there is no theft of sound is needed. See the Beis Yosef, siman 686, in the name of the Baal HaMaor; see the Beis Yosef, siman 649, and here, regarding what he wrote in the name of the Rashba, of blessed memory. If one wrote a Megillah on something from which benefit is forbidden, he has fulfilled the obligation, for mitzvos, even Rabbinic mitzvos, were not given for benefit; see Rosh Hashanah 28 in the Ran, and see siman 649. I am uncertain if he wrote it on hide of idolatry or of an idolatrous city, which must be burned. Regarding shofar in siman 586, since it requires a minimum measure, he does not fulfill with it. Regarding Megillah, does he fulfill, or perhaps he does not, because it is like reading by heart? See Even HaEzer regarding a get written on items forbidden for benefit and idolatry. With Hashem’s help, it will be explained further in the Magen Avraham.
