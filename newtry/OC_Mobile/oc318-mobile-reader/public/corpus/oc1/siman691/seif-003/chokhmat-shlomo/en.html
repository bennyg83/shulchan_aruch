(there seif 8) One does not read in congregation from a megillah written among the Writings, etc. NB: thus is Rambam's language Megillah ch. 2 and Tur's language: if the individual read, etc. And it appears their words contradict: from "one does not read in congregation" perforce the intent is to discharge the many — yotzei; if so it implies that whoever is not congregation, even nine and one reads for all — valid; and afterward they wrote but the individual reads and fulfills yotzei — implies specifically the reader himself fulfills yotzei but to discharge his fellow even not in congregation it does not help — these contradict. In Rambam and Shulchan Aruch one could explain: since they wrote but the individual reads — implies even l'chatchila; if so one can say three distinctions: in congregation even b'dieved he does not fulfill; individual for himself reads l'chatchila; individual to discharge others initially he should not read lest we decree because of congregation, but b'dieved he fulfills since they are not congregation. But this fits Rambam who holds individual reads l'chatchila. But what of Tur who wrote: if it was bound with them and the individual read — he fulfilled — he used b'dieved language implying l'chatchila even the individual alone does not fulfill; if so his words contradict: first he wrote individual reads implying specifically for himself but not to discharge his fellow, and afterward only congregation implying in individuality one discharges, etc. — requires study. What seemed to me to settle this: from here will be proven what Magen Avraham disputes above siman 689 s.k. 10 whether one individual discharges his fellow even if expert or specifically if not expert but expert does not discharge — see there. From here will be proven Tur also holds one does not discharge his fellow if he is expert. Per this one can explain. In Yerushalmi ch. 2 halacha 4: R' Chelbo, etc. — if written in chumashim one does not read in rabim; R' Tanchuma said: because of amateurs — till here. Knesset HaGedolah strained there: amateurs means they will say it is exempt, etc. — see there — strained; especially per Ran's explanation it is only from honor of congregation — certainly not Knesset HaGedolah's intent. In my humble opinion the intent is thus: in our Talmud the reading is "congregation" but Yerushalmi says "rabim." Congregation per Beit HaLevi is specifically in synagogue; Tur wrote every ten is called congregation but less than ten all agree not called congregation; but rabim even three are called rabim as known in Nedarim 34b and many places. Therefore Yerushalmi teaches even with less than ten only in rabim one does not read. Therefore it was hard: it implies if not written in chumashim permitted in rabim — yet one does not discharge his fellow with less than ten; and if dealing with ten should have said congregation — therefore he says because of amateurs who are not expert to read — by this the individual discharges his fellow — therefore teaches in any case if written among Writings he does not fulfill. Also intent simply: in Talmud it says a foreigner who heard Assyrian fulfills and challenges he did not know what is said and answers like men and am ha'aretz; afterward: are the Achashteranim sons of the Ramachim known to us — only mitzvah of reading and publicizing miracle; so too one who does not understand fulfills only from reading and publicizing miracle. Therefore Yerushalmi said because of amateurs — certainly those who understand the language since they hear it is megillah fulfill even in chumashim; only because of those who do not understand who fulfill only from reading and publicizing miracle. This fits if they see recognition; but if no recognition there is no knowledge — if so both explanations exist; and Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch hold: in congregation even if all know and understand nevertheless they do not fulfill in writing among Writings; and individual reading for himself who is the reader — if so knows from here how to read — therefore fulfills even if he does not know the language; but to discharge others with less than ten — if they are not expert even with less than ten they do not fulfill since they do not understand — no publicizing miracle; and if with ten they understand — nevertheless if written alone one does not discharge his fellow with less than ten when he is expert. Therefore Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch did not cite from either side. This fits me better than answer one: difficult they should explain b'dieved discharges in writing among Writings and not l'chatchila; but per what I said now there is no either/or and proven from all one does not discharge his fellow with less than ten only if not expert — understand and examine well.
