Circumference. See Birkei Yosef in the name of the author of Keser Kehunah in manuscript: whenever there is enough parchment in the sheet to write three columns, even if only one column was written, it is valid after the fact in a sefer Torah, and all the more so in a Megillah; see there and in Yoreh De'ah, siman 272. See Sanhedrin, page 20, that when they reached Megillas Esther they said: this does not require a cloth; see there. In Moed Katan he wrote that it requires a cloth, apparently in Chelek, page 20; see there. In truth, the Talmud says the opposite. Only in She'eilas Yaavetz he wrote that it was difficult to him: why should we say it does not require one, since it renders the hands tamei? The main reason for tumah of the hands is because of Rabbi Parnach's teaching, so that one should not hold them bare, without a cloth. Perhaps for this reason Rabbi Yaakov was particular about them, to say that it is not so, but rather it requires a cloth; see there. The custom is not like his words, and people are not careful at all to take the Megillah with a cloth. This follows the simple meaning of the Talmud's words: regarding this we say that since it is called a letter, it is enough without a cloth. Although it is also called a book, that is regarding matters that qualify the writing, such as ruling the lines and parchment circumference. But regarding matters after the writing, it is like a letter. Even though it renders the hands tamei, it may be taken bare, like tefillin. See Magen Avraham, siman 147, whose view is that one should be careful with a Megillah written according to its law. In Pri Megadim, volume 1, siman 76, he wrote that people are not careful, and he brought there from the Talmud in Sanhedrin. So too in Shevus Yaakov, siman 11, his view is to permit even with a Megillah; see there. He wrote that for this reason they instituted two poles for a sefer Torah, because it is forbidden to hold it bare, unlike other sacred writings; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 147, who explains that what is written there in the gloss, that it is good to be stringent if he did not wash his hands, refers to Megillah, even with ordinary hands. When one's hands are known to be dirty, one should be stringent even with other sacred writings; see there. People are not careful to wash ordinary hands before taking the Megillah in their hands, but it is good to be stringent, as the Eliyah Rabbah wrote.
