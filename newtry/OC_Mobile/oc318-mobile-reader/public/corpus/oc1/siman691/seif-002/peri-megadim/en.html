Also. See the Taz. I did not understand this, for they leave blank space equal to its circumference at its beginning. Just as there is a dispute about the pole, meaning the wood attached to it at its beginning or at its end, whether it belongs at the beginning or the end, so too there is a dispute about the blank space. Since this is so, one should have left blank space at both the beginning and the end. Furthermore, in practice they roll it from its end to its beginning. It would also have been possible to satisfy both opinions regarding the pole by making a pole at both the beginning and the end. What he wrote in the name of the Rashba is explained in siman 690, Magen Avraham, letter 15: the listener must hear each and every word, except that the listener does not need to have intent for each word; see there. There I had difficulty with the language of the responsum, but here is not the place to elaborate.
And behold. The Mechaber wrote that it requires parchment surrounding each letter, so every letter must be surrounded by parchment; the humps of the ches like two zayins, and so on; and the hanging of the heh and kuf, all like a sefer Torah, according to Rabbeinu Tam, except for what is explicitly mentioned as not being equal to a sefer Torah. But regarding making the tagin of Shaatnez Getz, he brought two opinions. There is no difficulty from processing the parchment, for the proof is from a Megillah among the Writings, as I wrote above. In the Beis Yosef, regarding what the Tur wrote that the Baal Halachos Gedolos wrote that one need not make tagin on it, meaning unlike Rabbeinu Tam, perhaps his intent is not specifically tagin; the same applies to all laws of a sefer Torah, that it has none of them except what is explicitly mentioned as equal to a sefer Torah. This is difficult here, as we wrote. Also, regarding what he wrote about the humps of the ches letters, scribes are not accustomed to make humps even in a sefer Torah, all the more so in a Megillah. See siman 32, that exacting scribes wrote humps, not ordinary scribes. There is something like proof from what is written that one should not make heh letters into ches letters. In Yoreh De'ah 274, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch use the term humps there; see Magen Avraham, siman 36, letter 3.
Further. I will explain to you: in Yoreh De'ah 281, seifim 3 and 4, regarding a sefer Torah, a slave, a woman, and a minor are invalid, as is learned from tefillin; see there. Regarding Megillah, a woman and a slave are obligated at least in hearing it, and therefore they can write it. But a minor, even if he has reached the age of chinuch, is not comparable to an adult, as explained in siman 689, and is invalid to write it. Regarding a mamzer and a convert, some invalidate them for writing a sefer Torah. See the Shach there, letter 8, who gives the reason in the name of the Mordechai, that a mamzer cannot discharge the public obligation and is therefore invalid to write. According to this, the same applies to Megillah, and it appears that it should be written by someone who can discharge others through performance of the mitzvah. Know that what the Mordechai wrote, that a mamzer cannot discharge the public obligation, is astonishing in my eyes, for a mamzer is a full Jew and is obligated in the matter. To say that those invalid to enter the congregation are not included in mutual responsibility, but only those who are valid, would apply also to an Egyptian and an Edomite. However, perhaps a fourth-generation Egyptian has no deficiency on the side of his soul; see the Tosafos Yom Tov in Rosh Hashanah, that nowadays one discharges others through mutual responsibility. If so, this can be resolved, but it requires analysis, for I found this in no authority. See above, simanim 53 and 55, regarding a mamzer serving as shaliach tzibbur; see there. Also, hearing is like responding. Know that the Shach in Yoreh De'ah there wrote the reason that a mamzer and a convert are not obligated in all mitzvos, and therefore they are invalid; see there. This means a mamzer is permitted to a maidservant; see Even HaEzer, siman 4, and the Beis Shmuel there. A convert is permitted to a mamzeres, an Egyptian, and an Edomite. But if not being fit to be a king or a judge for chalitzah were enough, then a person blind in one eye should also be invalid for writing a sefer Torah. However, in the Shulchan Aruch there it says ger toshav, meaning one who accepted not to worship idolatry in partnership; I explained the matter of partnership in my Pri on Yoreh De'ah. This implies that an ordinary righteous convert is valid for a sefer Torah. If so, according to the Taz there, letter 4, a mamzer is valid for Megillah, while according to the Mordechai he is invalid. See Magen Avraham 39, letter 9, in the name of Darkei Moshe, that a mamzer and a ger toshav are valid for tefillin. This is difficult: tefillin require writing for their own sake, so how can this be valid? See Magen Avraham 32. In the Fuerth printing of Darkei Moshe, the word toshav was omitted; see there. Again Hashem enlightened my eyes, and I examined the Mordechai, Krakow edition, at the end of Bava Kamma, page 56. In the print there is an error; see the lower number there, 7 and 8. He brought from Maseches Sofrim the reading “convert” without qualification, and the Mordechai, according to his approach, explains it as a ger toshav. But according to the reason that he cannot discharge the public obligation, as the Mordechai wrote there in the name of Rabbeinu Simchah, who did not have the reading mamzer, for with a sefer Torah whoever can discharge the public writes it, and a mamzer can discharge, while the Mordechai does read mamzer and learns that a mamzer cannot discharge the public obligation, either from Torah law or from the words of the Sages. According to this, the same applies to a righteous convert, based on what the Rosh wrote in Berachos, that women are not included in mutual responsibility; see there, page 20b. Also according to the Shach’s reason, the same applies to a righteous convert. In Maseches Sofrim the reading is simply convert, as we wrote. See what I wrote in simanim 39, 53, and 55. This requires analysis.
