It also must be written from a written text. See the Mishnah Berurah regarding what we wrote about the law after the fact. This is from the Darkei Moshe in the name of the Ran. The reason the Rema did not bring this in his gloss appears to be that this matter was not clear to him, and his proof from the Yerushalmi can be rejected simply. The Yerushalmi gave two answers regarding the case of Rabbi Meir: one, that a pressing circumstance is different; and one, that Rabbi Meir wrote two Megillos, and buried the first. From the fact that he buried it, it follows that even after the fact one may not read from it. But the Bavli answered only that a pressing circumstance is different. We can simply say that in a pressing circumstance Rabbi Meir permitted himself to write it and read from it initially, while not in a pressing circumstance he would not have permitted himself to do so. However, after the fact, perhaps it is permitted to read from it and it need not be buried. Know further that what he wrote, that it must be written from a written text, applies even if it was very fluent in his mouth. What they permitted above in siman 32, seif 29, regarding tefillin when they are fluent in his mouth, is because there it is common for everyone to have them fluent, unlike Megillah. So it is proven in the Gemara.
