If it was written on paper, or on hide that was not processed, or if a gentile or heretic wrote it, it is invalid. Its law is like a sefer Torah regarding parchment surrounding each letter, the humps of the letter ches, the hanging of the letters heh and kuf, all the bodies of the letters in their proper form, and missing and extra letters. Gloss: It must also be written from a written text, as the Ran writes, and the scribe must utter every word with his mouth before writing it, as with a sefer Torah, as the Maharik writes. All its sections are made closed. If he made them open, it is invalid, as in Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 2, and Piskei Maharai, siman 93. After the fact, one should not invalidate a Megillah because of missing or extra letters, for it is no worse than if the scribe omitted letters from it, where it is valid, as in Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 2, and Or Zarua, as was explained in siman 690, seif 3. It requires a pole at its end and blank space at its beginning with which to wrap it. Some say it requires tagin, and some say it does not require them. Gloss: The custom is to make tagin on it. They also had the custom not to make any pole for it at its end at all, as the Maharil writes.
