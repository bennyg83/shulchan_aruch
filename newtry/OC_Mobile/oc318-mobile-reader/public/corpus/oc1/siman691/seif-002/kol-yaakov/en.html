6) Seif 2, If it was written, etc., or if a non-Jew wrote it, etc. If it was written by a woman or a minor, or was written with the left hand, some invalidate it. Maaseh Rokeach on the Rambam, Hilchos Megillah, chapter 1, and so is the view of Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 3 in Chochmah. Others validate it, Pri Chadash, letter 3; Mateh Yehudah, letter 2; Machazik Berachah, letter 2. Beis Oved, letter 6, wrote as follows: therefore, the essential practical ruling appears to be that if a woman wrote it, it is valid to read from it even initially, since she too is obligated in its reading. But if a minor wrote it, one should not initially fulfill his obligation with it; however, after the fact, if he already read from it, or if he has no other Megillah, it is valid. These are his words. But Shaarei Teshuvah wrote that even regarding a woman’s writing, if another can be found easily, he should read from the second without a blessing.
7) And its law is like a Torah scroll, etc. The same applies for every matter: the number of lines, the width of the column sufficient to write three times the word le-mishpechoseichem, the space between one line and another the fullness of a line, between one word and another the fullness of a small letter, the measure of the margin above, three fingerbreadths, below, four, and between one column and another the width of two fingerbreadths, and he should make its lines even. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 2 of Hilchos Megillah, letter nun; Magen Avraham, letter 3; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Chayei Adam, rule 155, letter 10; Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 8 in Chochmah. Some say that one need not be particular about the number of lines, Keset HaSofer, siman 28, letter 4, in Lishkas HaSofer. In any event, he should not make fewer than eleven lines, so that ish is at the head of the column and aseres at the end of the column, Meleches Shamayim there, Binah, letter 4. Some say that when we derive Megillah from a Torah scroll, this refers to matters that invalidate a Torah scroll, but matters that do not invalidate a Torah scroll, such as the length of the lines, the arrangement of the columns, and the like, are valid in a Megillah even initially. Maaseh Rokeach on the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Megillah; Mateh Yehudah on seif 2, s.v. Ve-da; Erech HaShulchan, letter 8; Zera Avraham, part 1, siman 100. On this they rely here in Baghdad, may the Almighty protect it, that they are not particular in the Megillah about the measure of the upper and lower margins, the space between columns, the number of lines, and the measure of the sheet to make it like a Torah scroll, since all these matters do not invalidate a Torah scroll after the fact. See Yoreh Deah, siman 273, seif 5, and Taz, siman 272, seif katan 4.
8) There, And its law is like a Torah scroll, etc. A Megillah in which he wrote only three or four lines in the final column, leaving the rest blank, and he also did not finish in the middle of a line but at its end, is valid to read from even initially. Zera Avraham, part 1, siman 100; Machazik Berachah, letter 4; Erech HaShulchan, letter 8; Shaarei Ephraim, gate 6, letter 56; Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 10 in Binah; Emes L’Yaakov, rule 22, letter 3; Beis Oved, letter 14. What Zera Avraham wrote there at the end of the siman, and Beis Oved cited there, that if it is impossible to finish in the middle of the line at the end of the column except by stretching the letters, it is not proper to do so, this is not the practice here in Baghdad, may the Almighty protect it. Rather, the practice is to stretch the letters and finish at the end of the column and in the middle of the line. See our words in Yoreh Deah, siman 272, letters 11 and 13, and see above, letter 7.
9) There, Regarding surrounded by blank parchment, etc. If the letters touch one another, it is invalid according to all opinions. Kenesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur, end of the siman, and so wrote the later authorities. So too wrote Mateh Yehudah; and even in a minority of it, it is invalid, as with a Torah scroll. Pri Chadash, letter 2, wrote that if most of it was written properly it is not invalid, and so wrote Levushai Serad in Gilyon HaMagenim, section 1. See Birkei Yosef, letter 2.
10) There in the gloss, One must also write it from a written text, etc. If he wrote it not from a written text, he does not fulfill his obligation with it except in pressing circumstances, Darchei Moshe; Erech HaShulchan, letter 9; Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 2 in Chochmah; Beis Oved, in the laws of writing the Megillah, letter 8. But it is permitted to copy the Megillah from that one which was written by heart. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2; Meleches Shamayim there. See the Gra, letter 15. Meleches Shamayim wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi of Megillah, which the Gra cited, that after copying, one should place in genizah the one written by heart. This is specifically if it is written on hide, but if it is written on paper, it need not be placed in genizah. See above, letter 3.
11) There in the gloss, And to articulate every word with his mouth, etc. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2, who disagrees with the Magen Avraham and rules that even one who copies from a book must articulate every word with his mouth. Tefillin are different, as the Magen Avraham himself wrote in siman 32, seif katan 31. The Gra, letter 16, also disagrees with the Magen Avraham, brings proof from all the authorities, and writes that the authorities stated this as a greater novelty, that even when another reads it to him he must articulate it with his mouth, and certainly when copying from a book. See there. Meleches Shamayim, rule 24, letter 8 in Binah. See our words in Yoreh Deah, siman 274, letter 7.
