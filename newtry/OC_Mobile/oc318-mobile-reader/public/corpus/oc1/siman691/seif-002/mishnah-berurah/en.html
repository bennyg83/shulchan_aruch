(8) Regarding parchment surrounding each letter. This applies to every letter. The same applies to every matter, and therefore we require that its lines be even, and that the width of the column be three times “lemishpechoseichem,” and all the laws of a sefer Torah.
(9) From a written text. The same applies if someone else recites it to him by heart so that he not err. If he transgressed and wrote it by heart, not from a written text, some say it is forbidden to read from it except in a pressing circumstance.
(10) And to utter every word, and so on. This is initially; after the fact, one should not be stringent if he did not err.
(11) As with a sefer Torah. This means even if he writes it from a written text.
(12) Closed. For it is called a letter.
(13) If they are open, it is invalid. In Responsa Rabbi Moshe Mintz, siman 12, din 5, he questions this. Therefore it appears to me that one may rely on him in a pressing circumstance, as the Magen Avraham writes.
(14) And after the fact. Meaning, where he already read from it. And when he has no other Megillah, he may read from it initially.
(15) They also had the custom not to make, and so on. But a blank space is left at its beginning, and he rolls its end toward its beginning.
(16) At all. The Gra in his commentary greatly questions this.
