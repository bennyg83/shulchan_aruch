There, seif 8. One does not read publicly from a Megillah written among the Writings, and so on. Note: this is the language of the Rambam in chapter 2 of Megillah, and so too is the language of the Tur: if an individual read from it, and so on. At first glance, their words contradict each other. From “one does not read from it publicly,” the intent must be to discharge the public obligation, implying that whenever it is not a community, even if there are nine and one reads for all, it is valid. But afterward they wrote that an individual reads from it and fulfills his obligation, implying only the reader himself fulfills, but not that he can discharge another even outside a public setting. Regarding the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch one could say there are three distinctions: publicly, even after the fact one has not fulfilled; an individual reading for himself may read initially; but an individual reading to discharge others should not initially do so, lest we decree because of public reading, though after the fact they fulfill because they are not a community. This can be said for the Rambam, who holds an individual may read initially. But the Tur wrote, “if it was attached with them and he read from it as an individual, he fulfilled,” using after-the-fact language, implying that even an individual himself should not do so initially. His words are therefore difficult: first he implies only the reader himself, not one discharging another, and afterward he implies only public reading is invalid, while private reading for others works. It seems to me that from here there is proof for the Magen Avraham’s discussion above in siman 689, seif katan 10, whether when alone one can discharge another even if the other is expert, or only if the other is not expert. From here it is proven that the Tur also holds that one cannot discharge an expert fellow when fewer than ten are present. In the Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halachah 4, it says that if it was written as Chumashim, one does not read from it before the many, and Rabbi Tanchuma said this is because of the unlearned. The Korban HaEdah’s explanation that the unlearned will say it is a haftarah is forced, especially according to the Ran, who explains it as a matter of public honor. Rather, our Talmud says “with a community,” while the Yerushalmi says “before the many.” A community means ten according to the Tur, while fewer than ten is not a community; but “many” can mean even three. Thus the Yerushalmi teaches that even fewer than ten may not read from such a Megillah. The difficulty is that if it were not written as Chumashim, it would imply that one could read for many, though one cannot discharge another with fewer than ten. If it were speaking of ten, it should have said community. Therefore it says because of the unlearned: where they are not expert, one individual can discharge another, and nevertheless if it is written among the Writings he does not fulfill. One can also explain simply: our Talmud says a foreign-language speaker who heard it in Ashuris fulfilled the obligation, even though he does not know what is being said, because the mitzvah is reading and publicizing the miracle. Thus one who does not understand fulfills only through reading and publicizing. The Yerushalmi says “because of the unlearned,” meaning those who understand the language fulfill when they hear it is the Megillah even from Chumashim; but those who do not understand fulfill only through reading and publicizing, and if there is no visible distinction, there is no awareness of the miracle. Thus both explanations can be true. Publicly, even if all understand, they do not fulfill with a Megillah among the Writings. An individual reading for himself knows what he is reading and fulfills even if he does not understand the language. But to discharge others with fewer than ten: if they are not expert and do not understand, there is no publicizing; if they are expert and understand, then even with a separately written Megillah one person does not discharge an expert fellow with fewer than ten. Therefore the Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch did not mention this case, because either way it does not apply. This answer sits better, and proves that one person does not discharge another with fewer than ten except where the other is not expert. Examine this carefully.
