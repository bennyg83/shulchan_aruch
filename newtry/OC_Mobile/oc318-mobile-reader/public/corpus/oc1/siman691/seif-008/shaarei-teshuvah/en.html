Seif 11 in the Shulchan Aruch: Regarding a stolen lulav. This is how it should read. See Pri Chadash, siman 586, who wrote regarding a stolen shofar that one recites a blessing. According to him, the same applies here, for the law of Megillah is learned from the law of shofar in this matter. But in Birkei Yosef he wrote in the name of the Meiri that some are stringent regarding Megillah even though they validate shofar; see there. According to this, even those who validate a Megillah would at any rate hold it worse than shofar, so that one should not recite a blessing. However, the Birkei Yosef wrote that a reason is needed for why Megillah should be worse than shofar. Nevertheless, it appears that one should recite a blessing neither over a stolen Megillah nor over a stolen shofar, for in a doubt about blessings we are lenient. But if the owners of the shofar or Megillah are there in the beis haknesses and fulfill their obligation through this hearing or reading, it appears that he may properly recite the blessing, since for the owner there is no theft, and he too intends to fulfill the obligation with the blessing; there is no issue here of “the robber blesses” at all.
