[7] Levush: One should not write on the page, and so on. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of the Raavyah, and it seems that the custom is to be lenient initially. I later examined Sefer Amarkal, which wrote that the authorities disagree with the Raavyah. That which we say, lest people say the blessings are written in the Torah, applies when they are between one section and another; but if he wrote them at the beginning of the sefer Torah or at the end, it is not invalid. This is the end of his words. If so, regarding Megillah perhaps even initially it is permitted. Nevertheless, it is good to be concerned initially. It appears that the illustrations people make in the margins are also not correct, for initially we require it to be like a sefer Torah, and in a sefer Torah it is obvious that this is forbidden. It is also similar to what is written in siman 90, seif 23, that it is forbidden to make illustrations in books from which people daven. However, one can distinguish that in davening one especially needs concentration.
