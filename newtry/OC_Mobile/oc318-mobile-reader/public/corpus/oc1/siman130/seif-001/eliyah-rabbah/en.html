(1) One who saw, etc. — to exclude those who say it every day; and it also does not help unless he says it on the day he saw the dream — Torat Chayim end of Bava Kamma chapter 5. And it does not seem to me that if so, why do we all say it on the festival — are we to say all saw a bad dream that day? Rather perforce they say it for dreams seen on other days, as Magen Avraham wrote. And I wonder, for I saw Torat Chayim himself wrote this custom that all Israel say on the festival, and wrote they did not act well — only one who saw should say; and so it seems to me from the Gemara and poskim, for he said to finish when the congregation answers Amen — it implies he alone said it, not the congregation. And further, Torat Chayim brings proof that it is similar to fasts that it is on that day; also the language "a dream I dreamed" implies thus; also Magen Avraham concluded it is not proper to say every day; and Taz wrote except if a dream came the night before; nevertheless on the festival at the duchan that their intent is all should say requires study. He wrote in Magen Avraham in the name of Pitron Chalomot, who disagrees with the Geonim's responsum whether one may fast on Shabbat — a fortiori to say Ribono — until here. Magen Avraham challenged him: fasting nullifies a dream like fire to flax, which is not so with Ribono — until here. And per my humble opinion, proof for Pitron Chalomot from responsum of R' Meir of Rothenburg siman 243, who wrote a preacher may fall on his face on Shabbat if he needs mercy — a fortiori for fasts, see there; and in any case certainly one does not say on Shabbat except when he dreamed a bad dream.
