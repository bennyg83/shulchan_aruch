(Ribono shel olam, I am Yours.) Rashal wrote and I accepted to add at the end "and may the kohanim's blessing come upon me and satisfy me" — until here. And I say one should finish at the end of a verse with a word like that verse: at the end of the first verse yishmerecha — v'tishmereni; at v'yichuneka — v'tichaneni; at shalom — v'teratzenu b'shalom; and it is fit to say per Rashal's words — the first time "and may the kohanim's blessing come upon me and v'tishmereni," and so afterward.

(And if not he should say thus.) Meaning: if he finished Ribono before the kohanim finished, he says this, for with it he finishes with the kohanim; perforce no place for Adir bamarom if he finished with the kohanim. Now they practice to say it every time at the end of the shatz's prayer when he says "and may it be good in Your eyes," etc. And so is Rif's language: "and when the kohanim turn their faces — he says Shochen b'gevurah," etc. Duchan means the place kohanim ascend at nesiat kapayim.

(When the shatz says place peace.) I have not seen this practiced except by some individuals who say it every day; and to me one should not do so, for these matters are from R' Yitzchak in chapter HaRo'eh, referring to the Talmud's words that one who saw a dream and did not know what — he says thus at the time of the duchan; and this applies also when the shatz says place peace. But to say thus every day does not seem proper, for perhaps he did not dream at all — how can he say "a dream I dreamed" when he has no dream? Granted at the time of the duchan it is proper, for certainly he dreamed sometime between festival and festival — therefore he may say without specification; unlike saying every day, when he says for what he dreamed today, and perhaps he did not dream at all — therefore it is proper not to say Ribono every day except if a dream came the night before; unlike festival time when they ascend to the duchan, when he may say even without specification, for it refers to what happened in the interim — as mentioned, correct.
