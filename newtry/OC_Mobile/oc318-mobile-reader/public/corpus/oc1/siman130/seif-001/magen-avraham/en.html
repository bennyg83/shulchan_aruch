(One who saw a dream.) To exclude those who say it every day; and it also does not help unless he says it on the day he saw the dream — Torat Chayim Bava Kamma end of chapter 5. And it does not seem that if so, why do all Israel say it on the festival — are we to say all saw a bad dream that day? Rather perforce for dreams seen on other days; nevertheless it is not proper to say every day.

And your dreams — between dreams I dreamed about others and dreams I dreamed about myself, etc., one must request for one's fellow first [Berakhot, Maharam]; and conclude: u'shmorani against v'yishmerecha, v'choneni against v'yichuneka, u'seratzeni meaning shalom [Mateh Moshe and so the custom, not like Maharshal]:

(And if not.) Meaning the kohanim have not yet finished — unlike people who cannot say it except at the end; and this is not correct; and Orach Tzedek did not look at end of siman 128.

(Place peace.) And if he sees he cannot finish with the shatz, he begins when the shatz says yevarechecha — 38; and in Zohar Terumah 128: when they reach place peace, etc., a person need not be there nor ask questions but must fall on his face, etc.; and it seems this is after they finished place peace, for we say "and He is merciful" twice — etc.
