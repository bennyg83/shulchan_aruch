1) [Seif 1] One who saw a dream, etc. This, until the end of the siman, is all in Berachos, folio 55b, and the Tur and Levush cited it. And it is written in the Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 14 of Hilchos Tefillah, that the Maharam was accustomed to say, at the time that [the kohanim] say 'v'yishmerecha, viychuneka, shalom,' the prayer 'Master of the world, I am Yours,' etc. And it is written in the Terumas HaDeshen, siman 27, concerning this, that one should be precise to say it exactly at the time that they prolong the kaf of 'v'yishmerecha' and 'viychuneka,' as we say in the chapter 'Eilu Ne'emarin' (Sotah, folio 40a): there is a servant whose master blesses him yet does not show him a pleasant countenance. And although at the end of the third verse, namely the word 'shalom,' it is impossible to wait until they finish the word, since they prolong the melody before they complete the mem of 'shalom,' nonetheless we do what is possible to arrange and to act according to the halachah. Until here. And the Beis Yosef cited it in this siman; Levush in siman 128, seif 26, and in this siman; Olas Tamid there, ot 60, and in this siman, ot 2; Magen Avraham there, ot 73. However, in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 51c, in the text of the tefillah, he wrote, and this is his language: When they reach 'v'yaseim lecha shalom,' the dreamer should say 'Master of the world, I am Yours,' etc., as written in the siddur; and he should complete this prayer with the conclusion of the Priestly Blessing, so that the congregation answer Amen after his prayer as well, etc. End quote. And it was cited above in siman 48; see there. If so, it implies that one should not say it until they reach 'v'yaseim lecha shalom,' and also that one should say it only one time. See below, ot 4.
