(14) He should not call a name - That is to say, if he wishes to separate a small cake for challah he must be careful not to call upon it the name challah before its baking; rather he should call it a cake or matzah [and also that it not be in his mind that the sanctity of challah take effect upon it until after its baking; for the sanctity of terumos and maasros is through speech or thought alone, as clarified in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, siman 331]. And if he does not wish at all to bake a special cake for challah, but rather bakes ordinarily and afterward separates one matzah or one piece from a matzah upon all of them for challah, that too is proper. And the manner of separating after baking was already clarified above in s.k. 7.
(15) He is not permitted to bake it - Since in this baking there is no need of ochel nefesh; and specifically when its owners called a name upon it; but regarding another, even though he calls a name upon it, he has no [effect] in it, for another has no authority to separate terumah. And regarding a maidservant in the house, see Yoreh De'ah, siman 328, in the later authorities there.
(16) It will become chametz - And even though it is hekdesh and not his, nevertheless he transgresses bal yera'eh, since if he wished he could ask [to annul] concerning it, and it would be chullin.
(17) And he is not permitted, etc. - Meaning: for to burn immediately before it becomes chametz he also cannot, for one does not burn kodshim on Yom Tov.
(18) He should cast it, etc. - But l'chatchilah he should not rely on this advice: to call the name challah while it is still dough and cast it into cold water; for we are concerned lest he not be careful enough that the waters be cold.
(19) Into cold water - And when he sees that the water warms a little as it stands in the house, he should be careful to exchange it for cold water [later authorities].
(20) And if there is a kohen, etc. - The Mechaber deals with challah of Eretz Yisrael, which is not eaten by kohanim nowadays since we are all tamei meis, and the challah became tamei and it is forbidden to feed it even to a tahor kohen; therefore it is forbidden to bake it on Yom Tov in every case, and for that reason he did not mention these details. And Rama deals with challah of chutz la'aretz, which is forbidden only to one from whom tumah issues from his body, such as a zav or a baal keri; but a kohen who is tahor from zav and from keri, such as a minor kohen less than nine years and one day old who does not become tamei through keri, and also presumably did not see zivah since he is less than nine, or an adult who immersed for his seminal emission, is permitted to eat challah of chutz la'aretz, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 322. Therefore Rama wrote that if there is a minor kohen, etc.: even though he called upon it the name challah, he is permitted to bake even on Yom Tov, for it is fit for eating.
(21) Or an adult who immersed for his seminal emission - That is to say, even though his sun has not yet set; and it is good to be careful that he eat immediately after his immersion and not urinate between immersion and eating, lest he emit split or cloudy water concerning which there is concern of keri, and he would be forbidden to eat this challah until he immerses again.
(22) That we do not feed, etc. - For we do not regard him as a certain kohen, lest one of his maternal foremothers were chalalah. And nevertheless as a matter of law the Rav's view is like the first opinion: that we give to a minor or to an adult who immersed for his seminal emission [Shach in Yoreh De'ah, siman 322, letter 9]. And Peri Megadim wrote: and nevertheless I have not seen them practice thus even on Pesach. And in our countries there are some places that give to an adult kohen who immersed for his seminal emission on Pesach; and see Magen Avraham who wrote a reason why specifically on Pesach the custom is to give.
(23) On Yom Tov - Of Pesach. And in the commentary of the Gra there is doubt concerning this.
