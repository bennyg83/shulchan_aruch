For one does not burn kodshim on Yom Tov; and Tur concludes here: therefore he should not call it by name until after baking, and then combine everything in the basket and separate one for all — end quote. And requires study: behold initially there was in the dough the measure for challah, for he kneaded only one dough; if so basket-combination is not required, only plain surrounding, as Tur himself wrote before this — basket-combination is required only when there was not the measure for challah, then the basket combines everything for challah; not so in obligation of surrounding when there is the measure for challah; and it appears Tur cited combination here but intended they be close to one another; and if so, even when placed adjacent one to the other, even on the table, it suffices; and there is room to study in what is implied that there is obligation to surround when there is the measure for challah, as Tur wrote before this — rather he brings all the loaves together, etc.; and in Be'er Sheva first chapter of Beitzah it is written: however surrounding we certainly do not require, since he permits in challah of chutz la'aretz: eat and go and afterward separate; and what they practiced on Pesach to place all the matzot in one vessel — not because of surrounding, etc., as written nearby; and if so why did Tur write in this siman that we require surrounding, and he also brings the law of challah of chutz la'aretz eat and go — it is implied even l'chatchila we do not require surrounding, for when he separates afterward the challah on what he eats, behold there is no surrounding; and in truth one may say so also on Rosh, who wrote in chapter 1 of Overin: when there is the measure for challah, basket-combination is not required, only plain surrounding — from which we learn surrounding is in any case required here; one may say there he deals with explaining R' Eliezer's words who said the basket combines them for challah, and the tanna in chapter 1 of Overin deals that we require surrounding — and he does not deal there with challah of chutz la'aretz; but on Tur it is difficult, for he required surrounding — it is implied even in chutz la'aretz; and this contradicts what he wrote afterward eat and go, etc.; and one may explain Rosh's words without Tur: in chapter 1 of Overin it deals when there are two doughs where each has the measure for challah and he wishes to separate from one for its fellow — in this surrounding is required; for this is not in the category of challah of chutz la'aretz eat and go, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mordechai: where he forgot on Yom Tov and did not separate their challah — in this there is no permission to rely on leaving matzah to be eat and afterward separate; for this applies only if the dough was kneaded together; not so in matzot where each dough was kneaded alone — they do not have this law; nevertheless Rosh first chapter of Beitzah that surrounding is not required deals when he baked only one dough that has the measure and divided it into matzot — in this surrounding is not required; but in Tur one cannot say so, for he wrote: and if it is known there was in the dough the measure for challah and he forgot to separate challah until after baking, etc. — behold he does not deal with separating from one dough for its fellow — for otherwise why mention forgetting; rather it is simple he deals only with repairing that dough that he forgot to separate; and he says about this that he must bring close — meaning surrounding; and afterward he said challah of chutz la'aretz eat and go, etc.; and I have no way to settle Tur's words except in this way: he does not hold what Rosh wrote first chapter of Beitzah that surrounding is not required — rather surrounding is required even in challah of chutz la'aretz; and one may explain that law of challah of chutz la'aretz eat and go and afterward separate: there, when he begins to eat those doughs he sets his eyes to separate afterward on everything, and it is like separating at that very time — and for him it is surrounding; not so when he forgot to separate and divided the dough into loaves — when he comes to separate he must surround; and so one who kneads dough on Pesach Yom Tov — he does not call a name until after baking, and then it is his beginning to separate and he must be from the surrounding batch.
