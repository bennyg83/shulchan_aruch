Seif 2. "One who kneads dough on Yom Tov of Pesach should not designate it by name until it is baked, etc." NB: We read in Pesachim, chapter 5, fol. 62, that Rav Chisda held that an uncircumcised person is also subject to obligation and is subject to atonement, since if he wished he could correct himself. The Gemara asks whether Rav Chisda accepts the principle of ho'il, for it was stated regarding one who bakes on Yom Tov for a weekday, etc. It answers that Rabbah against Rabbah is not difficult: here an act is lacking, there no act is lacking; but Rav Chisda against Rav Chisda is difficult. They answered that Rav Chisda does not accept ho'il leniently, but he accepts it stringently. It is proven there that where no act is lacking, and it is stringent, everyone says ho'il. This requires analysis from Makkot chapter 3, 13b, in the sugya whether those liable to karet receive lashes. It says there: according to Rabbi Akiva, if so, those liable to karet too; what will you say, perhaps they repented? At present they have not repented, etc., until fol. 14, where it says: what will you say, they did not repent? This is not certain. See there. This is difficult: why did it not answer simply by way of ho'il, since if he repents it is not considered two punishments, and therefore he receives lashes? This ho'il would be stringent, and no act is lacking, as is proven in Kiddushin regarding one who says, "on condition that I am righteous," where even if he is wholly wicked, perhaps he contemplated repentance in his heart. If so, why do we not say ho'il here, and what is the Gemara asking? This requires great study. Examine carefully. In the above sugya of ho'il it is also explained that Rabbi Yehoshua holds that tovat hana'ah is not money. The Shaagat Aryeh, in Hilchot Chametz U'Matzah, asked from Bava Metzia chapter 1, where it is proven that Rabbi Yehoshua holds that tovat hana'ah is money. I have already discussed this at length in several places. Now it appears to me simply resolved: the dispute of Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua whether tovat hana'ah is money is not based on logic alone; rather, they dispute whether the Torah made it like money or not. One holds that since the Torah gave him the right to separate, the Torah made it like money, and the other holds that the Torah did not make it like money. Now regarding chametz we hold like Rabbi Eliezer, that chametz is not in a person's domain, but Scripture made it as though it is in his domain. This is well for actual money: the Torah made it as though it is in his domain. But tovat hana'ah is not money except that the Torah made it like money. During the whole year, where even actual money is not in his domain except that the Torah made it as if in his domain, this applies to actual money. But something that in truth is not money, and only the Torah made it like money, the Torah made it like money only to acquire now; it did not also make it as though it is in his domain. The matter remains in its simple state: tovat hana'ah is not truly money in itself. This resolves it. Examine very carefully.
There, seif 2, in the gloss. "And if there is a minor kohen who has not seen keri, or an adult who immersed from his keri, they may bake the challah for him, etc." NB: See the Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Darkei Moshe that this means below the age of nine. He wrote that this is because we assume he has not seen keri, but if he did see, even if he is one day old he becomes tamei. The Chok Yaakov objected that below nine years old, even if he saw keri he does not become tamei; see there, and see what I wrote in my work Mei Niddah, fols. 35 and 41. Now I have seen that the Magen Avraham's words are clearly implied by the Rambam in Hilchot Terumot chapter 7, who wrote: "Therefore, a minor kohen who has not yet seen keri, and a minor girl who has not yet seen niddah blood, may always eat it without inspection, for their presumption is that tumah has not yet come out upon them." Since Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Darkei Moshe gave the measure as until nine, perforce the Rambam speaks of a minor below nine, and nevertheless he wrote that their presumption is that tumah has not yet come out upon them. This implies that if we knew tumah had come out upon them, they would be forbidden to eat terumah like an adult who is tamei. This proves the Magen Avraham's view. Perforce what the Rambam wrote, brought by the Chok Yaakov to the contrary, must be as the Chok Yaakov himself wrote: there is a distinction between making others tamei by contact, where he does not make them tamei, and his own body, from which tumah issues, where he himself is tamei. This is a clear proof in my humble opinion. Examine carefully. Now I saw in the Rambam, Hilchot Tumat Met 1:14: "Both an adult and a minor become tamei with all tumot, etc." From his language, "all tumot," it implies even tumat keri. In particular, above this in the same halacha he wrote: "Not only with tumat met, but with all tumot, neither non-Jews nor animals become tamei," and from the Rambam's words it seems that the Sages decreed that non-Jews be like zavim in all respects, etc. What relevance does mentioning zivah have here if he speaks only of tumat met? Perforce he was careful: since he first included that a non-Jew is not tamei with all tumot, which would include tumat zivah, he returned to say that nevertheless rabbinically they are like zavim. It is thus proven that his phrase "all tumot" includes even tumah that comes from one's body. If so, when afterward he wrote that both an adult and a minor become tamei with all tumot, this too includes tumah that comes from the body, proving the view of the Magen Avraham. It is forced to say that it speaks of a nine-year-old minor, since with all tumot even a one-day-old infant becomes tamei; since he included here all tumot, it speaks even of a one-day-old, and since he wrote all tumot, it proves that even with keri a one-day-old becomes tamei, as the Magen Avraham held. Examine carefully. Now, while learning Bekhorot, I saw that the dispute between the Magen Avraham and the Chok Yaakov whether a minor below nine becomes tamei through keri depends on the two versions in Rashi there, Bekhorot 27, s.v. kohen katan: "and not an adult, because tumah comes out upon him from his body, but if he immersed from his keri he is like a minor kohen. Another version: therefore it mentioned a minor kohen who has not seen keri and tumah has not come out from his body; and in the She'iltot of R. Achai it explains this way." Perforce the two versions differ in the dispute of the Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov. The first version holds like the Chok Yaakov, that even if a minor saw keri he is not tamei; the second holds like the Magen Avraham, that specifically we assume he has not seen keri, but if he saw, he becomes tamei. Examine carefully. See also my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5616, in novellae to Sukkah 21a, where I showed that this is explicit in Rashi in the name of the Tosefta, like the Magen Avraham; see there. See also my work on Hilchot Pesach, first edition, where we discussed this at length; see there and examine carefully.
