Se'if 2. One who kneads dough on Yom Tov of Pesach should not call a name upon it until it is baked, etc. Marginal note: We learn in the Talmud, chapter 5 of Pesachim, folio 62, and Rav Chisda held: that uncircumcised one as well, since he is a person of obligation, is a person of atonement, since if he wished he could remake himself; and it asks: and does Rav Chisda have "since [ho'il]"? And it was stated: one who bakes from Yom Tov for a weekday, etc. Granted regarding Rabbah against Rabbah there is no difficulty: here it lacks an act, there it does not lack an act; but Rav Chisda against Rav Chisda is difficult. They say: when Rav Chisda does not have "since," it is for leniency; but for stringency he has it, etc. And behold it is proven there that where it does not lack an act, and for stringency, according to everyone we say "since." And there is room for inquiry from what we learn in chapter 3 of Makkos, folio 13b, in the sugya that those liable to kares are flogged, and it says there: and according to R' Akiva, if so those liable to kares as well: what will you say: perhaps they did teshuvah? Now at least they have not done, etc., until folio 14: what will you say: they have not done teshuvah: it is not decisive for him, etc.; see there. And it is difficult: why did they not answer a handsbreadth [further] from "since": since if he does teshuvah he is not considered of two wickednesses, and therefore he is flogged; and this "since" is for stringency, and also it does not lack an act, as proven in Kiddushin regarding "on condition that I am righteous," etc.: even if he is utterly wicked, perhaps he contemplated teshuvah in his heart. If so, why should we not say "since" here, and what does it ask? And this requires great further study; and examine carefully. And behold in the sugya of "since" mentioned above it is clarified that R' Yehoshua holds that the benefit of goodwill (tovas hanaah) is not money; and Shaagas Aryeh asked from the laws of chametz and matzah that in the first chapter of Bava Metzia it is proven there that R' Yehoshua holds that tovas hanaah is money. And I have already lengthened on this in several places; and now it appears to me simply that it is well: for that which R' Eliezer and R' Yehoshua disagree whether tovas hanaah is money or not is not from reason that they disagree, only that they disagree whether the Torah made it like money or not: one holds that since the Torah granted him to separate, the Torah made it like money; and one holds that the Torah did not make it like money. And therefore regarding chametz we hold like R' Eliezer that chametz is not in a person's possession, only that Scripture treated it as if it is in his possession. And if so, fine for what is actual money: the Torah treated it as if it is in his possession; but tovas hanaah, which is not money, only that the Torah treated it as like money: then fine the whole year, when even actual money is not in his possession, only that the Torah treated it as if it is in his possession: so fine for actual money; but for what is in truth not money, only that the Torah treated it as like money: then fine, now it is acquired; but to make it further as if it is in his possession the Torah did not treat it so, only left it on its simple [status], and tovas hanaah is not money in truth of itself; and it is well; and examine carefully. (Same place, se'if 2) In the gloss: and if there is a minor kohen who has not seen a seminal emission, or an adult who immersed for his seminal emission, they are permitted to bake the challah for him, etc. Marginal note: see Magen Avraham who wrote in the name of Riv and Darkei Moshe that this means less than nine years old; and he wrote upon it that this means we say presumably he did not see a seminal emission, but if he saw, even a one-day-old conveys tumah. And Chok Yaakov challenged him that one less than nine years old, even if he saw a seminal emission, does not convey tumah; see there. And see what I wrote in my work Mei Niddah, folios 35 and 41; see there. And now behold I have seen that like Magen Avraham's words it is explicitly implied from Rambam in the laws of Terumos, chapter 7, who wrote, and this is his language: therefore a minor kohen who has not yet seen a seminal emission, and a minor girl who has not yet seen dam niddah, always eat it without examination, for their presumption is that tumah has not yet issued upon them, end quote. And behold, since Riv and Darkei Moshe gave the measure up to nine years old, necessarily Rambam deals with a minor less than nine years old; and even so he wrote that their presumption is that tumah has not yet issued upon them: it is implied that if we know that tumah issued upon them they would be forbidden to eat terumah, like the law of an adult who is tamei; and it is proven like Magen Avraham's view. And therefore necessarily what Rambam wrote that Chok Yaakov brought to the opposite: that is as Chok Yaakov wrote, that there is a distinction between conveying tumah to others by contact, which he does not do, but he himself, from whom tumah issues from his body, is tamei; and this is a clear proof in my humble opinion; and examine carefully. And behold now I have seen in Rambam, chapter 1 of the laws of Tum'as Meis, halacha 14, who wrote, and this is his language: both an adult and a minor become tamei with all the tumos, etc. And behold from his language that he wrote "with all the tumos" it is implied even with tumah of keri; and particularly that above this in that same law he wrote, and this is his language: and not with tum'as meis alone, but with all the tumos, neither a non-Jew nor an animal become tamei with them; and from Rambam's words it is implied that they decreed that non-Jews be like zavim for all their matters, etc. And what is the relevance of mentioning zivah here, for here he deals only with tum'as meis? Necessarily he was careful: since he first generalized that a non-Jew does not convey tumah with all the tumos, and if so even tumah of zivah is included, therefore he returned to say that nevertheless by their words they are like zavim. If so it is proven that his language "with all the tumos" includes even tumah that issues from his body; and if so afterward when he wrote that both an adult and a minor become tamei with all the tumos, that too includes even tumah that issues from his body; and it is proven like Magen Avraham's view. And it is forced to say that he deals with a minor nine years old, since regarding all the tumos even a one-day-old infant conveys tumah; and if so, since he generalized here all the tumos, he deals even with a one-day-old; and since he wrote all the tumos it is proven that also with keri a one-day-old conveys tumah, like Magen Avraham's view above; and examine carefully. And behold now in my studying tractate Bechoros I saw that the dispute of Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov whether a minor less than nine years old conveys tumah through keri is [reflected in] two wordings in Rashi there in tractate Bechoros, folio 27, s.v. "kohen katan," and this is his language: and not an adult, because tumah issues upon him from his body; and if he immersed for his seminal emission he is like a minor kohen. Another version: for this reason he stated "a minor kohen who has not seen a seminal emission," and tumah has not issued from his body; and in the She'iltos of R' Acha he explains thus, end quote. And in what do these two wordings differ? Necessarily in the dispute of Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov: the first wording holds like Chok Yaakov that a minor even if he saw a seminal emission does not convey tumah; and the second wording holds like Magen Avraham: specifically that he has not seen a seminal emission as a presumption, but if he saw he conveys tumah; and examine carefully. And see in my work on Orach Chaim, year 5616, in my novellae on tractate Sukkah, folio 21a, I showed that so it is explicit in Rashi in the name of the Tosefta like Magen Avraham's view; see there. And see in my work on the laws of Pesach, first edition: there we lengthened on this; see there; and examine carefully.
There, seif 2, in the gloss. "And if there is a minor kohen who has not seen keri, or an adult who immersed from his keri, they may bake the challah for him, etc." NB: See the Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Darkei Moshe that this means below the age of nine. He wrote that this is because we assume he has not seen keri, but if he did see, even if he is one day old he becomes tamei. The Chok Yaakov objected that below nine years old, even if he saw keri he does not become tamei; see there, and see what I wrote in my work Mei Niddah, fols. 35 and 41. Now I have seen that the Magen Avraham's words are clearly implied by the Rambam in Hilchot Terumot chapter 7, who wrote: "Therefore, a minor kohen who has not yet seen keri, and a minor girl who has not yet seen niddah blood, may always eat it without inspection, for their presumption is that tumah has not yet come out upon them." Since Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Darkei Moshe gave the measure as until nine, perforce the Rambam speaks of a minor below nine, and nevertheless he wrote that their presumption is that tumah has not yet come out upon them. This implies that if we knew tumah had come out upon them, they would be forbidden to eat terumah like an adult who is tamei. This proves the Magen Avraham's view. Perforce what the Rambam wrote, brought by the Chok Yaakov to the contrary, must be as the Chok Yaakov himself wrote: there is a distinction between making others tamei by contact, where he does not make them tamei, and his own body, from which tumah issues, where he himself is tamei. This is a clear proof in my humble opinion. Examine carefully. Now I saw in the Rambam, Hilchot Tumat Met 1:14: "Both an adult and a minor become tamei with all tumot, etc." From his language, "all tumot," it implies even tumat keri. In particular, above this in the same halacha he wrote: "Not only with tumat met, but with all tumot, neither non-Jews nor animals become tamei," and from the Rambam's words it seems that the Sages decreed that non-Jews be like zavim in all respects, etc. What relevance does mentioning zivah have here if he speaks only of tumat met? Perforce he was careful: since he first included that a non-Jew is not tamei with all tumot, which would include tumat zivah, he returned to say that nevertheless rabbinically they are like zavim. It is thus proven that his phrase "all tumot" includes even tumah that comes from one's body. If so, when afterward he wrote that both an adult and a minor become tamei with all tumot, this too includes tumah that comes from the body, proving the view of the Magen Avraham. It is forced to say that it speaks of a nine-year-old minor, since with all tumot even a one-day-old infant becomes tamei; since he included here all tumot, it speaks even of a one-day-old, and since he wrote all tumot, it proves that even with keri a one-day-old becomes tamei, as the Magen Avraham held. Examine carefully. Now, while learning Bekhorot, I saw that the dispute between the Magen Avraham and the Chok Yaakov whether a minor below nine becomes tamei through keri depends on the two versions in Rashi there, Bekhorot 27, s.v. kohen katan: "and not an adult, because tumah comes out upon him from his body, but if he immersed from his keri he is like a minor kohen. Another version: therefore it mentioned a minor kohen who has not seen keri and tumah has not come out from his body; and in the She'iltot of R. Achai it explains this way." Perforce the two versions differ in the dispute of the Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov. The first version holds like the Chok Yaakov, that even if a minor saw keri he is not tamei; the second holds like the Magen Avraham, that specifically we assume he has not seen keri, but if he saw, he becomes tamei. Examine carefully. See also my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5616, in novellae to Sukkah 21a, where I showed that this is explicit in Rashi in the name of the Tosefta, like the Magen Avraham; see there. See also my work on Hilchot Pesach, first edition, where we discussed this at length; see there and examine carefully.
