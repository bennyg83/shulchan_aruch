He should not call a name. Rather he should call it kuchin in the vernacular (Maharil). And it appears to me that there is no concern except when its owner calls a name upon it; but regarding another person, even though he calls a name upon it, he has no [effect] in it, for he has no authority to separate terumah.
It will become chametz. And even though it is hekdesh and not his, he transgresses bal yera'eh, since if he wished he could ask [to annul] concerning it, and it would be chullin.
He should cast it into cold water. This requires further study, whether one may rely on this nowadays, when even cold waters that stand a little in the house warm up; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 36, and in Shach; and see above, siman 459, se'if 4.
A minor. Less than nine years (Darkei Moshe, citing Riv). Meaning we say that presumably he did not see a seminal emission; but if we know that he saw a seminal emission, even if he is one day old he is tamei for his emission.
Who immersed for his seminal emission. It appears to me that if afterward he saw split or cloudy water, he is tamei, as stated in chapter 8 of Mikvaos; therefore he should eat immediately after the immersion and not urinate in between. And see siman 606 regarding the laws of immersion for a seminal emission, and see there, chapter 8.
That we do not feed. For we do not regard him as a certain kohen, lest one of his maternal foremothers were chalalah; and so ruled Maharshal and Maharil. And Shach in Yoreh De'ah, siman 322, wrote: a reason is needed why on Pesach they practice to give challah to a minor, and on the other days of the year they do not practice to give to a minor, end quote. And perhaps it is because the challos are numerous, and if he will not eat it they will not regard him as a kohen, as Rashi wrote in Chullin, chapter 18, regarding the priestly gifts.
