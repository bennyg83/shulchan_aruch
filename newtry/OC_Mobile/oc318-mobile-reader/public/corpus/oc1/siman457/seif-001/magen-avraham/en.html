It is good to bring close. And some place the dough folded at the time of separating challah, and this is not correct (Shlah). The custom is to separate from each dough by itself (Darkei Moshe). And nevertheless one blessing suffices for all of them if he did not interrupt between them with speech that is unrelated to the kneading; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 19, and see what I wrote in siman 432. And regarding the blessing there is no difference between Pesach and the other days of the year (Darkei Moshe 38). And in Derishah, Yoreh De'ah, siman 328, he wrote a reason for those who practice saying on Pesach "terumat challah matzah from the dough"; see there. And he also gave a reason why they practice making it first like matzah and afterward throwing it into the fire; and he concludes that in the house of Maharshal they practiced burning it without [prior] preparation; and so wrote Mateh Moshe.
And if it is impossible, etc. For l'chatchilah one must separate while it is still dough, for it is written "the first of your kneading" (Beis Yosef and Shlah).
Not required. But both must be placed before him; for even though we hold that challah of chutz la'aretz may be eaten continuously and afterward one separates, nevertheless l'chatchilah we require that it be from the surrounding batch (so it is implied in the Tur, and according to the view of the Rosh in the third chapter of Pesachim). And the Bach asked, for in Tur Yoreh De'ah he wrote that it is permitted l'chatchilah to eat and afterward separate; and he wrote to distinguish between two doughs; and the Rosh in the third chapter of Pesachim deals with one dough, for he disagrees with Rashi; see there. And it appears to me to distinguish: in any case the challah together with that from which one separates requires from the surrounding batch (see Nahach). Maharil wrote that it is permitted to burn it in a Jew's bonfire, for Rashi ruled that a Yisrael is permitted to eat it through nullification in the majority, and it is fit to rely on him to benefit from it; and see Yoreh De'ah, siman 323; and some are stringent.
