(1) Not to increase it, etc. - As is clarified in the previous siman.
(2) To bring the doughs close - That is to say, after he kneaded the first dough, it should be placed on the table and one should work with it until they knead another dough [or knead two doughs at once in two troughs], and between the two of them there will certainly be the measure of challah.
(3) That they kiss one another - That is to say, that the doughs adhere one to the other until if they separate, one tears a little from its fellow; and adhesion like this counts them as one dough, and when challah is taken from one it suffices. And the later authorities wrote that if the doughs are kneaded hard it is impossible afterward to adhere them well together, and there is no combination here; therefore one should combine them by a vessel or by covering with a cloth.
(4) For perhaps there is, etc. - That is to say: and if so, when he separates challah with a blessing his blessing will be in vain; and further we are concerned, if some of the doughs do not have the measure of challah, then their separation is nothing; and perhaps afterward after baking he will put the matzos into one vessel and they will combine and become obligated in challah by law, and he will be found eating tevel. And in a place where people customarily knead large doughs of the measure of challah, as brought at the end of the previous siman in Mishnah Berurah, they separate challah from each dough by itself, and they practice that from each dough a different woman separates and recites the blessing for herself. And if one person separates for all the doughs, one blessing suffices for all if he did not interrupt between them with speech unrelated to kneading. The later authorities wrote that the custom some practice to fold the dough at the time of separation in order to separate the challah from its two ends is a custom that is not correct; it suffices to separate from one end.
(5) And if it is impossible, etc. - For if it is possible, it is better to separate from the dough, as it is written "the first of your kneading": behold, the main mitzvah l'chatchilah is while it is still dough.
(6) After baking, immediately when he places, etc. - And so too regarding challah of chutz la'aretz; and even though regarding challah of chutz la'aretz of eating continuously and leaving some for challah, that is for one large dough, even though afterward divided into several loaves, since at first they were joined from one dough they were lenient in chutz la'aretz that we do not require surrounding and connection at the time of separation; which is not so here, where they are from separate doughs: even though they were placed one by one in a basket, nevertheless it is not like complete joining so as to be lenient to eat before separation. And even if each dough had the measure of challah, we also require that it be from the surrounding batch at the time of separation, since they are not from one dough, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 323, se'if 1, in the gloss.
(7) In a basket - And if the basket is large, he must bring them close so that they touch one another. However, if each dough has the measure of challah, concerning which Rama rules below that basket-combination is not required, likewise touching one another is not required, whether they are placed in a basket or placed in the house, for the house combines them, and he may separate from this upon that. And this is specifically when they are placed without vessels; but if they are placed in vessels, he must bring the vessels close to one another and they must be open above [for if they are closed, even if they are close it does not help]. And all this is when each dough has the measure of challah; but if each dough does not have the measure of challah and we need combination, one should not be lenient even if the vessels are open and close to one another, unless he places them inside one basket or covers them with a cloth; and see Biur Halacha.
(8) And he forgot to separate - That is to say, while it was still dough; for l'chatchilah it is more proper to separate from the dough, as above in s.k. 5.
(9) Afterward - That is to say, after the baking.
(10) And even basket-combination, etc. - Since each one has the measure, he may separate from the matzos of one dough upon all; and we require only that everything be placed before him in the house, as above in s.k. 7. And if he had only one dough, even though it was divided into several matzos, since it was kneaded together from the outset he may separate from one upon the other matzos even if they are not before him; for in such a case from the surrounding batch is not required for challah of chutz la'aretz, as above in s.k. 6; see there. If he forgot to separate challah on erev Yom Tov and remembered on Yom Tov: if there was not the measure of challah in each dough and they were combined in a vessel together on Yom Tov, so that the obligation of challah arose on Yom Tov, the view of several later authorities is that it is permitted to separate challah on Yom Tov. But if they were combined in a vessel before Yom Tov, or each dough had the measure of challah, so that the challah obligation arose before Yom Tov, it is forbidden to separate challah on Yom Tov. Rather, if the matzos of each dough are recognizable by themselves, it is permitted to eat all the matzos of that dough and leave from one of its matzos a little, relying on separating from it on Chol HaMoed a little as challah and burning it. But if the matzos became mixed, he must leave a little from each and every matzah, and after Yom Tov combine all the pieces in one vessel and take challah from one piece (that is, separate a little from it) upon all of them; or he should proceed in this way: knead and bake a small dough less than the measure on Yom Tov and combine them with the matzos in one basket so that it become obligated in challah, and afterward he may separate from this dough upon all of them, since the obligation of this dough arises now on Yom Tov, he is permitted to separate from it on Yom Tov. And even if each dough did not have the measure and all the matzos were combined in one vessel before Yom Tov, it is also proper to make this remedy. And l'chatchilah, whenever he remembered before night, he should separate challah immediately and not leave separating for Yom Tov.
(11) From each one individually - For if he takes from one, there is concern lest this is from that which was already exempted, and then it would be from the exempt upon the obligated.
(12) And he takes from it, etc. - That is to say, that in his separating he intend to exempt all the obligated doughs, and all of them must be placed before him in one house, as above in s.k. 7.
(13) And if he recognizes, etc. - That is to say, that it is from dough from which its challah has not yet been separated.
