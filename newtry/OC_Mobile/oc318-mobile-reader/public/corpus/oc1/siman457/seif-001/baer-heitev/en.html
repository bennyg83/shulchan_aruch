To increase. Even if he has many helpers and also two ovens, nevertheless he should not increase the measure of the dough; and if it was more by five or six eggs there is no concern, even for matzah of the mitzvah (Maharil; Chok Yaakov).
To bring close. For l'chatchilah one must separate from the dough, for it is written "the first of your kneading" (Beis Yosef).
In a basket. And a vessel that has no receptacle: it is a doubt whether it combines; and if one covers the bread with a cloth it is considered like a vessel and they combine; and so one should practice (Taz). The Shlah wrote that one should protest those who fold the dough and take challah from two ends; rather she should take from one end, and one should arrange with the rolling-pin at the other end so that it not be left idle. The custom is to separate from each dough by itself (Darkei Moshe). And one must say that this follows the custom of making large doughs; and Magen Avraham wrote that one blessing suffices for all of them if he did not interrupt between them with speech unrelated to kneading. And now they mostly practice that from each dough a different woman separates and recites the blessing for herself. And regarding the blessing there is no difference between Pesach and the other days of the year (Darkei Moshe; and Taz, Yoreh De'ah, siman 328; see there). And in Derishah, Yoreh De'ah there, he wrote a reason for those who practice saying on Pesach "terumat challah matzah from the dough"; see there; and he also gave a reason why they practice making the challah first like matzah and afterward throwing it into the fire; and he concludes there that in the house of Maharshal they practiced burning it without preparation; and so wrote Mateh Moshe. And if the challah fell into a boiling cooked dish: besides the law of a mixture of challah that is clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 324, one must discuss here the law of the prohibition of chametz; see siman 454, se'if 3. And Chok Yaakov in his father-in-law's name wrote that it is permitted for benefit, or to keep it until after Pesach; see there.
Not required. But both must be placed before him; even though we hold that challah of chutz la'aretz may be eaten continuously and afterward one separates, nevertheless l'chatchilah we require that it be from the surrounding batch (Magen Avraham). And Chok Yaakov wrote that from the surrounding batch is not required, for challah of chutz la'aretz may be eaten continuously and afterward one separates; and see the later authorities. He wrote in Mateh Moshe and the minhagim and Levush: if he forgot to separate from the doughs of the matzah of the mitzvah, he may wait until night so that he place them thus whole on the plate; and when he breaks the second matzah into two to keep half for afikoman, then he should place the two whole matzos and the two broken pieces in a vessel to combine them and cover them, and take a small piece from the broken one and recite the blessing to separate challah, and keep it for Chol HaMoed (it is permitted to benefit while burning it, Maharil; but he concluded that nevertheless it is proper to make a separate bonfire), and they burn on Chol HaMoed; and it appears to me that it is better that he first break from it a small piece immediately by day, lest he again forget, for in any case he will break it without a blessing, end quote. And Chok Yaakov wrote that so it is proper to practice: not to wait until night, for in any case dough kneaded from erev Yom Tov it is forbidden to separate from it on Yom Tov, as written in siman 506; see there. (And if it did not have the measure, so that on erev Yom Tov he was not obligated in challah at all, but on Yom Tov through combination they became obligated: certainly he separates from it on Yom Tov.)
