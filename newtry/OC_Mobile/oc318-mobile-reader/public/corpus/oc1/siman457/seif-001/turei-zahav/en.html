It is good to bring the doughs close. Beis Yosef wrote that the doughs should touch one another until a little of this adheres to that; and so wrote our master in Yoreh De'ah, siman 325, end quote. And in the Gemara the language is "they bite one another." And in Yoreh De'ah, siman 325, I brought the words of Terumat HaDeshen that it is required that if they separate, one tears a little from its fellow; and if so, any dough kneaded hard, even though they are placed one upon the other, does not adhere together, and there is no combination here; therefore one must act through vessel-combination. And Beis Yosef wrote that a vessel that has no receptacle: Rambam wrote that it is a doubt whether it combines; and if one covers the bread with a cloth, it is considered like a vessel and combines them; therefore one should so practice.
For perhaps there is among them one, etc. And we are concerned lest afterward he come to obligation through combination and be found eating tevel; so wrote the Rosh in the first chapter of Beitzah, as written nearby. And my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote: at first glance it is implied from the language of our master that basket-combination helps even if they do not touch; and also in Yoreh De'ah, siman 325, he wrote simply that the basket combines them; but in Semak, laws of chametz, he wrote specifically that they touch together; and so wrote Tosafot in chapter Eilu Ovrin, folio 46, s.v. "ho'il"; and from the words of the Rosh in the laws of challah it appears that he agreed with this, for he wrote, and this is his language: R' Eliezer does not require biting, but touching in a vessel he does require, etc.; and one must say that our master abbreviated in this, end quote. And this is the language of Tosafot there in chapter Eilu Ovrin: however it appears to me that if it is placed in a basket we also require touching one another because of taking challah from the surrounding [batch], etc., end quote. And if so, they required the touching only so that it be from the surrounding batch; but for combination of the measure of challah, basket-combination alone suffices even if they did not touch one another. Therefore the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch did well not to require touching here; for we hold that we do not require from the surrounding batch, as Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 323: dough that became obligated together with this challah he can exempt not from the surrounding batch; but dough kneaded separately, one may not separate challah from another dough not from the surrounding batch. Therefore touching one another in a basket is not needed except where there is obligation in each one alone and he wishes to separate from this upon that, for then surrounding is required; which is not so where there is no obligation in each one, only that it becomes obligated through combination: surrounding is not required. And the Rosh who wrote that biting is not required, only touching in a vessel: he does not mean touching one another, but that touching by means of a vessel suffices for the measure of challah; and so he wrote explicitly in the first chapter of Beitzah, and this is his language: and that which they practiced on Pesach to place all the matzos in one vessel at the time of separating challah is not because we require surrounding, but because they practiced to bake measure by measure on Pesach because of chimutz; and if he separated challah before he combined them in a vessel they would not thereby be exempted; and if afterward they come to combination, he becomes obligated in challah and eats tevel, end quote. Behold, then, it need not be from the surrounding batch. And in chapter Eilu Ovrin he wrote that basket-combination is not needed except where it does not have the measure of challah; but if from the outset it had the measure of challah, basket-combination is not needed, only surrounding in general in order to take terumah from the surrounding batch, end quote. Behold, then, it must be from the surrounding batch; rather, necessarily, since there is obligation for each one separately and he wishes to take terumah from one upon its fellow, then surrounding is required; and for this Tosafot in the name of R' Yitzchak, and also Semag, required because of surrounding that they touch one another even in one basket. And this is unlike the view of the Rosh and the Tur, that for surrounding one need only bring them close together, and not a basket. And it is possible that R' Yitzchak also did not mean here that a basket and touching are required; rather this is what he means: even though they are in a basket, touching is required; and touching alone [in that sense] suffices, for for surrounding a basket is not needed; but here, where he wishes to combine them for the measure of challah, touching one another is not needed at all, only vessel-combination. And nearby we will mention [how] to reconcile the words of the Rosh and the Tur even where there is the measure of challah, regarding surrounding.
And if it had the measure, etc. In the Tur he wrote: and even if he forgot and ate some, he may separate upon the remainder; for challah of chutz la'aretz: one eats continuously and afterward separates. And at first glance it is difficult, for Shmuel permits even l'chatchilah; and why did he write "forgot and ate"? And this my father-in-law of blessed memory asked, and he lengthened with answers. And it appears to my humble opinion that there is no difficulty; for certainly it is permitted l'chatchilah to rely and eat with intent to leave over afterward, since there is the measure of challah at the time he begins to eat, for it is as if he separated at that time. But if he forgot to separate and afterward what remains is less than the measure, one might have thought that at that hour when he recalled, since there is not the measure there, he must combine another dough: it teaches us that he need not, since at first it had the measure. And in Eretz Yisrael one must indeed combine that [other dough]; this is simple and clear.
