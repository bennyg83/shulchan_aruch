These hours. That is from sunrise until the end of its setting, and it is like a quarter of an hour before tzeis hakochavim — see Magen Avraham:

To 12 hours. Meaning that the day we calculate into 12 parts even if it is long 18 hours and each part is called an hour. And on this siman 89 seif kaf gimmel:

Always as one of them. Explanation that one should not conduct himself sometimes this way and sometimes that way, and all the more so that he should not pray on one day Mincha after plag hamincha and Maariv before tzeis hakochavim. Magen Avraham:

Tzeis hakochavim. Thus wrote D.M. — Maharil would pray Mincha at a time of pressure close to tzeis hakochavim and said: even though it appears in the city like night, nevertheless it is still day — end of his words. And Magen Avraham wrote: and this is specifically in a city whose houses are tall or sits in a valley. And now the custom is to pray Mincha and Maariv close to each other because they hold like the Rabbis; and that they pray Maariv before night is because of the pressure, for it is trouble to gather the congregation again. And an individual who did not pray in the synagogue should wait until night. And one must be careful not to pray Maariv before plag hamincha. Magen Avraham:
