1) [Seif 1] One who prayed Minchah, etc. This implies that only after the fact did one fulfill his obligation if he prayed from six and one-half seasonal hours onward. Initially, however, one should pray only from nine and one-half seasonal hours onward. This is the Rambam's view in chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillah. He explains there that since the Minchah prayer was instituted corresponding to the daily afternoon offering, its time follows that offering. Since the daily offering was brought each day at nine and one-half hours, the Sages established Minchah's time from nine and one-half hours. This is called Minchah Ketanah. Since on the eve of Pesach that fell on Friday they slaughtered the daily offering at six and one-half hours, the Sages said that one who prays after six and one-half hours fulfills his obligation. Once this time arrives, the time of its obligation has arrived. This is called Minchah Gedolah. Beis Yosef cites this; see there. Beis Yosef nevertheless writes that the words of the Tur and Rosh imply that Minchah's initial time is from six and one-half hours onward; see there. Therefore, some practice leniently when necessary and pray initially from six and one-half hours onward. Without a need, however, one should pray only after nine and one-half hours, in accordance with the Rambam and Maran, of blessed memory. See below, paragraph 3.
2) There: And onward, he fulfilled his obligation. This implies that before then he did not fulfill his obligation. Although its true time begins after six hours, as stated in Yoma 28, since we are not expert in determining the time, he did not fulfill his obligation. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Pri Chadash in his glosses to this siman likewise concludes this way. Mateh Yehudah similarly proves it and writes that he must pray again at the proper time; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, rules likewise, contrary to Beis Yaakov, siman 91, who rules that he fulfilled his obligation; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, cites this, and Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, rules the same. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, however, agrees with Beis Yaakov that Minchah's time begins at the start of the seventh hour; see there. Zichron Yosef, folio 100a, holds the same view, cited by Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, who writes that this is likewise implied by the Yerushalmi in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar; see there. Machazik Berachah further writes in Kuntrus Acharon that this also appears from Responsa Rif, siman 320, and the words of Rabbeinu Tam in Sefer HaYashar; see there. Nehar Shalom, siman 286, paragraph 5, writes the same; see there. Therefore, since the matter is disputed, and we rule leniently in rabbinic cases of doubt concerning blessings, while most authorities hold prayer is rabbinic, and even according to those who hold it is of Torah origin, neither the number nor the text of prayers is from the Torah, as stated above in siman 106, paragraph 7, if one erred and prayed Minchah from the beginning of the seventh hour, one may say he need not pray again.
