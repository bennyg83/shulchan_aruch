The time of the Minchah prayer. It contains two se'ifim.
One who prayed the Minchah prayer after six and a half hours and onward has fulfilled his obligation; but the primary time for it is from nine and a half hours and onward until night, according to the Rabbanan; and according to Rabbi Yehudah, until plag haMinchah, which is until the end of eleven hours less a quarter. {Rama: And we calculate these hours according to [the length of] the day; and even if the day is long, we calculate it as twelve hours, and these are called sha'os zemaniyos [seasonal hours]; and likewise, everywhere that the Sages measured in hours, we measure in these hours. (Rambam in his Commentary on the Mishnah, first chapter of Berachos.)} And we conclude that one who acts in accordance with one master has acted [properly], and one who acts in accordance with the other master has acted [properly] — provided that he always acts in accordance with one of them: for if he acts in accordance with the Rabbanan and prays Minchah until night, he may no longer pray Ma'ariv from plag haMinchah and onward; and if he acts in accordance with Rabbi Yehudah and prays Ma'ariv from plag haMinchah and onward, he must be careful not to pray Minchah at that time. And now that the custom is to pray the Minchah prayer until night, one should not pray the Ma'ariv prayer before sunset; but if bedieved he prayed the Ma'ariv prayer from plag haMinchah and onward, he has fulfilled his obligation; and in pressing circumstances he may pray the Ma'ariv prayer from plag haMinchah and onward. {Rama: And for us, in these lands, where the custom is to pray Ma'ariv from plag haMinchah, one should not pray Minchah afterward; but bedieved, or in pressing circumstances, he has fulfilled his obligation if he prays Minchah until night — that is, the emergence of the stars. (Beis Yosef in the name of Ohel Mo'ed and the Rashba.)}
