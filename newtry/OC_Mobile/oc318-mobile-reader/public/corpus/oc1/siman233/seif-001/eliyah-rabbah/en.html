[Levush] Until the time of sunset, etc. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 1, writes that the day begins at dawn and ends at tzeit hakochavim. Maadanei Melech, page 7, however, writes that Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah in Pesachim chapter 2 supports the Levush, and Tosafot Yom Tov there rules accordingly. See Yerushalmi Berachot, page 1c; this requires study. It is obvious that the Levush means the beginning of sunset, as Maadanei Melech states explicitly and as the Levush himself writes clearly in siman 267. This excludes Magen Avraham's claim that he means the end of sunset, about a quarter hour before tzeit hakochavim; that is not precise.
[Levush] According to the offering of the daily tamid, etc. Beit Yosef cites Rambam for this reason. In siman 232, however, the Levush gives another reason of his own, that at that time the limbs were being brought up, etc. This requires reconciliation. Magen Avraham writes that if one wishes to eat or set out on a journey, he may initially pray Minchah Gedolah. See Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 91.
