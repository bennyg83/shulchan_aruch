(1) Who prayed etc. — for Mincha prayer corresponds to the afternoon tamid they fixed it; and the time of its slaughter is d'oraisa from after six hours and a half, that is half an hour after midday; and only because in order that they be able to offer individual offerings beforehand [for after the afternoon tamid it is forbidden to offer any offering] therefore they would delay its offering each day until 9 hours and a half except on erev Pesach that fell on erev Shabbat when they would slaughter the tamid at six hours and a half; and therefore the primary time of Mincha prayer l'chatchila is from 9 and a half and onward corresponding to the tamid they would offer each day; and nevertheless if he prayed from six and a half and onward he fulfilled, for the primary time of the tamid is d'oraisa from that time; and there are among the early authorities who are lenient l'chatchila from six hours and a half and onward; and in any case if he wants to eat or go on a journey or it is its time he can pray with the congregation; and if he waits for Mincha Ketanah he will not have a minyan — according to all it is permitted to pray l'chatchila from six hours and a half and onward:

(2) And a half and onward he fulfilled — it implies that beforehand he did not fulfill even b'dieved [likewise Magen Avraham and Derech HaChayim and Birkei Yosef]; and there are among the later authorities who hold that although l'chatchila it is forbidden to pray before the half hour after midday is completed, nevertheless b'dieved if he prayed in that half hour he should not pray again. And know that what is written until night per the Rabbis is not precise, for approximately a quarter of an hour before tzeis hakochavim is included according to all in bein hashemashos as below in siman 261; and one should not pray Mincha then; and see below in the second gloss what we wrote on this matter in practice:

(3) Which is until the end etc. — for Mincha Ketanah time is two hours and a half [for it is from 9 hours and a half until the end of 12 hours] and it turns out that plag hamincha is a full hour and another quarter:

(4) And we calculate these hours etc. — meaning all the above hour calculations both for Mincha Gedolah, both for Mincha Ketanah and plag hamincha — we calculate in the hours of the day: we divide the day whether long or short into 12 parts and each part is called an hour; and according to these hours we establish Mincha time for all the above. And the measure of 12 hours of the day that we calculate — there is a dispute among the poskim: some say we count from dawn until tzeis hakochavim, and automatically for R' Yehuda it is permitted to pray Mincha until an hour and a quarter before tzeis hakochavim, and for the Rabbis until night — as above in seif kaf bet; and so the Mechaber stated plainly in this siman and below in siman 443; and some say we count the 12 hours from sunrise until sunset, and automatically for R' Yehuda it is not permitted to pray Mincha except until an hour and a quarter before sunset, and for the Rabbis until sunset:

(5) One who acts like one master etc. — and each of them has leniency and stringency; meaning that regarding Mincha, R' Yehuda's view is only as a stringency that one should not pray Mincha except until plag hamincha; but automatically there is leniency regarding Maariv, for from then and onward is considered night for the matter that he can pray Maariv [meaning only the prayer itself, for the time of Shema of Maariv according to all is at tzeis hakochavim as below in siman 235]; and for the Rabbis there is leniency regarding Mincha prayer for they pray until evening; and automatically stringency regarding Maariv prayer for one should not pray before darkness:

(6) Always as one — explanation that one should not conduct himself sometimes this way and sometimes that way because it would be two contradictory practices even for tomorrow; and all the more so that he should not pray on one day Mincha after plag like the Rabbis and Maariv before tzeis hakochavim:

(7) At that hour — rather specifically before plag:

(8) Until night — meaning until bein hashemashos which is doubtful night:

(9) Before sunset — meaning the end of sunset which per the Mechaber in siman 261 is a short time before tzeis hakochavim; and even though also then one should not pray until tzeis hakochavim precisely, for beforehand it is only doubtful night — as below in siman 235 in this small amount he was not precise:

(10) And onward he fulfilled — but before plag even b'dieved he did not fulfill; and above is explained that there are views among the poskim when is plag hamincha; and therefore one may rely to be lenient not to return and pray even if he prayed an hour and a quarter before sunset:

(11) And at a time of pressure etc. — meaning that even if his way is always to pray Mincha after plag, nevertheless he can pray Maariv also at this time; and nevertheless one should not be lenient in this except if at least on that day he prayed Mincha before plag; but if on that very day he prayed Mincha after plag, again it is forbidden for him to pray Maariv before night for it would be two contradictory practices on that very day; and all this if he prays individually; but a congregation that prayed Mincha and when they go home it will be trouble to gather them again for Maariv prayer and the congregation's prayer will be completely nullified — the later authorities were lenient that it is permitted to pray Maariv close to Mincha; and see below in siman 235 seif 61:

(12) Mincha afterward — meaning even on another day he should not pray Mincha after plag, since he always holds that time for Maariv prayer time, even if in his mind on that day to pray Maariv after tzeis hakochavim:

(13) Or at a time of pressure etc. — meaning that since it is a time of pressure it is permitted for him l'chatchila to pray Mincha at the hour he is accustomed until today to pray Maariv; and I already wrote in Sha'ar HaTziyun that he must be careful not to pray Maariv then except after tzeis hakochavim unless he is with the congregation and there will be trouble to gather them again when each goes to his home:

(14) That is, until tzeis hakochavim — not precise, for approximately a quarter of an hour before tzeis hakochavim is certainly bein hashemashos according to all and one should not pray at that time; but rather meaning close to this; and so in D.M. in siman 232. And know that even though from the Mechaber and Rama it implies that for us who practice to pray Maariv after tzeis hakochavim it is permitted to pray Mincha even after sunset until close to tzeis hakochavim — there are many poskim who disagree and their view is that Mincha prayer is only before sunset; and therefore l'chatchila every person must be careful to pray specifically before sunset, meaning that he finish his prayer while the sun is not yet hidden from our eyes; and it is better to pray at its time individually than to pray afterward with the congregation; and b'dieved one can rely on the view of those lenient to pray after sunset until a quarter of an hour before tzeis hakochavim [but all that he can advance he is obligated to advance so as not to enter the doubt of bein hashemashos; however if stars were already seen, Mincha time has certainly passed, for this is a sign of night as explained all this in siman 293 — see there in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halacha]; but all this is b'dieved and great time of pressure; but l'chatchila certainly one should not delay Mincha time until after sunset; and all the more one must be very careful not to delay until close to tzeis hakochavim; and already they said in the west: they curse one who prays with the dimming of the sun, lest the hour be confused for him:
