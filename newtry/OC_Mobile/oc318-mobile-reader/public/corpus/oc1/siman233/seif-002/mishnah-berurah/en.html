(15) He must wash, etc. This applies both before Mincha and before Maariv, for it is a mitzvah to wash one’s hands before prayer.
(16) Although he does not know, etc. If he washed his hands for Mincha and did not divert his attention from guarding them, he need not wash again for Maariv.
(17) See above, etc. Meaning, as explained there, that even if his hands were dirty and he washes them for prayer, he does not recite a blessing. See above, siman 6, seif 1, in Mishnah Berurah.
(18) From his study. Presumably he did not touch an unclean place while studying, yet it remains a mitzvah to wash for prayer. Magen Avraham writes that if he washed for prayer and then interrupted with study, he need not wash again. On Shabbos, when the congregation interrupts for the sale of mitzvos, they must wash again before Mussaf unless they maintained awareness of guarding their hands. Eliyah Rabbah writes that as long as they remain in the beis midrash, there is presumably no lapse of awareness. This is the accepted practice.
(19) For prayer. Even if he washed for eating, ate, and did not divert his attention from guarding his hands, he must wash again for prayer, since washing for eating does not count for prayer. Morning washing performed when rising from bed, however, counts for prayer if he did not divert his attention from guarding his hands.
(20) He need not wash. Rather, he should clean his hands with any cleansing substance. This applies to hands of ordinary status. If he touched an unclean place or relieved himself, he must seek water even for Mincha or Maariv, just as for Shacharis. The required distance is explained above in siman 92, seif 4. See there in Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah for the laws relevant here.
