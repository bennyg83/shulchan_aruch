(15) Must wash etc. — both for Mincha prayer and for Maariv prayer, for it is a mitzvah to wash hands for prayer:

(16) Even though he does not know etc. — and if he washed his hands for Mincha prayer and did not distract his mind from guarding them, he need not wash his hands for Maariv prayer:

(17) And see above etc. — meaning what is explained there that even if his hands were dirty and he washes his hands for prayer he does not recite a blessing; and see above in siman 4 seif alef in Mishna Berurah:

(18) From his study — meaning that presumably he did not touch in the middle of study an unclean place; nevertheless it is a mitzvah to wash for the sake of prayer; and Magen Avraham wrote: nevertheless if he washed for prayer and interrupted in study he need not wash again; and on Shabbat when they interrupt for selling matzot they need to wash again for Musaf prayer unless he did not distract his mind from guarding his hands; and Eshel Avraham wrote that all this when they are in the synagogue — presumably there is no distraction of mind; and so is the custom:

(19) For prayer — and even if he washed his hands for eating and ate and did not distract his mind, he requires washing for prayer, for washing for eating does not suffice for prayer; but morning washing that he washed when rising from his bed and did not distract his mind — suffices for him for prayer:

(20) Need not wash — rather he cleans his hands with anything that cleans; and all this with ordinary hands; but if he touched his hands in an unclean place or relieved himself he must seek water again even in Mincha and Maariv prayer, as in Shacharit prayer; and as explained its measure above in siman 92 seif 4 — see there [later authorities in siman 92]; and see there in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halacha what applies here:
