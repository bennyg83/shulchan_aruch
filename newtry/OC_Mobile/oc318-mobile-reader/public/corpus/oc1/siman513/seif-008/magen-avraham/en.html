Forbidden on the Yom Tov after it. Because of preparation — meaning when it was born it finished, and if so Shabbat prepared for Yom Tov. And Avodat HaKodesh raised many difficulties, and it appears to me a fortiori: certainly when born on Yom Tov after Shabbat it is permitted d'oraisa on that day, for it did not finish yesterday — for an egg there is no such thing, and one cannot decree that if one eats what was born on Yom Tov he will eat what was born on Shabbat, for Shabbat on Yom Tov is not interchangeable, as is written in siman 507 seif 2. But regarding an egg we decree that if one eats an egg born on Yom Tov when Yom Tov falls on Sunday he will also eat the egg born on that day. And the Ran wrote that a chick born on Shabbat is permitted d'oraisa on Yom Tov since it is possible to slaughter it for a sick person on Shabbat — if so Shabbat prepared for itself — see there regarding a calf. If so, a fortiori: it itself is a decree and we do not decree a decree upon a decree. And in Yeshuot Shalom he disagrees with Tur and holds that a chick is not considered preparation, as the Rosh wrote regarding a calf that its birth is not preparation.
