And if born on Shabbat, etc. In Tur this is written in the name of his brother R' Yehuda: my brother R' Yehuda wrote that it is forbidden on the Yom Tov after it because of preparation — certainly preparation applies, for while still in its shell it is fit for nothing — until here. It appears his intent: even though above regarding born on Yom Tov he wrote that some permit and the Geonim forbid — they disagree as to whose halacha: if like Rav who forbids because of muktzeh, or like Shmuel and R' Yehuda who permit, for there is no muktzeh here since it permits itself through slaughter today — this is regarding born on Yom Tov. But born on Shabbat is forbidden even per those who permit born on Yom Tov; nevertheless here on the Yom Tov after it is forbidden d'oraisa, for Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov, as with an egg born on Yom Tov forbidden because in its mother's womb it finished yesterday on Shabbat, only that birth alone did not make the completion there — likewise here with a chick forbidden on the Yom Tov after it because of preparation, and here birth alone completes; unlike an egg that was also fit in its mother's womb for eating after finishing if they would slaughter the hen, only Shabbat prohibition rests on it; but a chick while in its shell is fit for nothing — therefore birth is completion, and therefore it is forbidden from the Torah on the Yom Tov after it because of preparation which is d'oraisa. It turns out there is a practical difference in R' Yechiel's ruling: even for those who forbid on Yom Tov itself — whether because of muktzeh or "born" which are rabbinic — but here wanting to eat it on the Yom Tov after it, and likewise the reverse born on Yom Tov forbidden on the Shabbat after it d'oraisa because of preparation. However as it appears there is a distinction between this preparation and egg preparation: even though both are forbidden because of preparation, nevertheless in egg preparation born on Yom Tov — it finished yesterday on Shabbat — forbidden d'oraisa, and on ordinary Yom Tov permitted d'oraisa since it finished on a weekday, only because of a decree because of Yom Tov after Shabbat; but here they did not decree on ordinary Yom Tov at all because of Yom Tov after Shabbat, for in an egg there is room to err — one seeing this person eat the egg born today on ordinary Yom Tov will also eat what was born on Yom Tov after Shabbat, since both were born on Yom Tov; not so here with a chick — if born on Yom Tov after Shabbat it is truly permitted even rabbinically per Shmuel and R' Yehuda, and even per Rav permitted d'oraisa; only he wanted to decree because of born on the Shabbat before Yom Tov — we do not find such a decree at all in the words of the Sages; this is not an equal decree on the day of birth, only born on ordinary Yom Tov, and likewise Yom Tov after Shabbat depends on the dispute, and per the lenient who rule like Shmuel and R' Yehuda it is permitted also born on Yom Tov after Shabbat; but born on Shabbat is forbidden for everyone d'oraisa on the Yom Tov after it. And Beit Yosef wondered: one should wonder at R' Yehuda — behold Rav who forbids born on Yom Tov is because of muktzeh, not preparation; he entered a difficulty for this and likewise I saw Maharshal questioned thus, and I wonder greatly at these great men — there is no beginning to the difficulty: Rav discusses born on Yom Tov itself, when preparation does not apply, for certainly Yom Tov prepares for itself on that day; rather Rav forbade because of muktzeh. Not so here regarding born on Shabbat for the Yom Tov after it — certainly there is a preparation prohibition. Beit Yosef further questioned: if because of preparation Rav forbade, perforce forbidden on Yom Tov after Shabbat — for an egg born on Yom Tov is forbidden only because of a decree because of Yom Tov after Shabbat; if so what did R' Yechiel teach, etc. Maharshal likewise wrote that per R' Yechiel's view this decree applies on ordinary Yom Tov because of Yom Tov after Shabbat like an egg — but this is not so at all in my humble opinion: here it is completely permitted regarding born on Yom Tov after Shabbat per the lenient like Rav, and this decree from the reason we mentioned does not apply — it is not an equal decree. And Maharshal z"l wrote to distinguish between this preparation and an egg — here preparation is only rabbinic. And all this is not equal to me; rather there is a d'oraisa prohibition per the view that holds Rabba's preparation.
