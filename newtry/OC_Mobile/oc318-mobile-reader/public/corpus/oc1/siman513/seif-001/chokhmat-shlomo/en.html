(Seif 1) An egg laid on Yom Tov, etc. Note: It is known the view of the Ran that regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim we do not follow the majority. And see in my novellae on the laws of vows siman 213 what I wrote that one can question his words from the sugya in Nedarim 73b — in that he distinguishes there between terumah which is rabbinic and vows which are d'oraisa, and he does not distinguish that vows are a davar sheyeish lo matirim and we do not follow the majority, and therefore we are concerned for a minority; not so with terumah which is a davar she'ein lo matirim and therefore we are not concerned for a minority. And from this I proved that even the Ran was stringent only in Torah law or something as stringent as Torah law, but in other rabbinic prohibitions the Ran agrees — even though its doubt is rabbinically stringent in a davar sheyeish lo matirim — nevertheless we follow the majority and it is permitted — see there and understand, for I was brief. And see in my composition on Avodah Zarah fifth edition siman 149 what I proved that there is a distinction between d'oraisa and d'rabbanan: in Torah prohibition we do not follow the majority and presumption in a davar sheyeish lo matirim, and in d'rabbanan we follow the majority in a davar sheyeish lo matirim — see there what I proved from the Talmud chapter 1 of Chullin — see there and understand.
