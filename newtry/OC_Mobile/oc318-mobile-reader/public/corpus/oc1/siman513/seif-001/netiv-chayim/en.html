(Magen Avraham, small seif 3.) For it is davar she-yesh lo matirin. See in responsa Tzemach Tzedek what he challenged from the Gemara in Beitzah daf 3, see there; and apparently it arose in my mind to distinguish, for per the settled view he understood that in a rabbinic matter we do not say davar she-yesh lo matirin, for R' Eliezer in the conclusion innovated for us this law; and my teacher the Gaon, z"l, told me: whoever confronts us, confronts us properly; for according to truth, why did Rav Ashi need to say 'doubt Yom Tov doubt weekday' — even in a rabbinic doubt we say davar she-yesh lo matirin is not batel; R' Eliezer should only have needed to [teach] in a certainty of rabbinic [law] that davar she-yesh lo matirin [applies], and he taught: and a doubt is forbidden — an egg born on Yom Tov, doubt of treifah:
