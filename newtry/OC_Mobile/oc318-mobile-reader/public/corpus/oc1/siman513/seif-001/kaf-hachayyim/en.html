1) [Se'if 1] An egg laid on Yom Tov may not be touched. This applies even when it came from a hen designated for eating. On a Yom Tov that falls on Sunday, the egg is forbidden under Torah law, because every egg laid today was completed the preceding day, and Shabbos may not prepare for Yom Tov. The Sages therefore decreed regarding an ordinary Yom Tov because of a Yom Tov that falls on Sunday. It is forbidden to move the egg because the Sages made no distinction. Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 5; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. This follows Beitzah 2b. The phrase 'the Sages made no distinction' means that no distinction is made between an egg laid by a hen designated to produce eggs and one laid by a hen designated for eating. Since both are forbidden to eat, both are also forbidden to move, as Yam Shel Shlomo writes there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, cites him. Rambam, chapter 1, law 20, likewise writes that just as it is forbidden to eat the egg, it is forbidden to move it. Rashba writes likewise in Avodas HaKodesh, gate 5, subsection 4. Erech HaShulchan there also writes in the name of several early authorities that an egg laid by a hen designated for eating is forbidden to move, unlike Tosafos, Shabbos 45b. See there.
