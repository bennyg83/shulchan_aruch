1) [Se'if 1] An egg laid on Yom Tov may not be touched. This applies even when it came from a hen designated for eating. On a Yom Tov that falls on Sunday, the egg is forbidden under Torah law, because every egg laid today was completed the preceding day, and Shabbos may not prepare for Yom Tov. The Sages therefore decreed regarding an ordinary Yom Tov because of a Yom Tov that falls on Sunday. It is forbidden to move the egg because the Sages made no distinction. Yam Shel Shlomo, first chapter of Beitzah, siman 5; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. This follows Beitzah 2b. The phrase 'the Sages made no distinction' means that no distinction is made between an egg laid by a hen designated to produce eggs and one laid by a hen designated for eating. Since both are forbidden to eat, both are also forbidden to move, as Yam Shel Shlomo writes there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, cites him. Rambam, chapter 1, law 20, likewise writes that just as it is forbidden to eat the egg, it is forbidden to move it. Rashba writes likewise in Avodas HaKodesh, gate 5, subsection 4. Erech HaShulchan there also writes in the name of several early authorities that an egg laid by a hen designated for eating is forbidden to move, unlike Tosafos, Shabbos 45b. See there. See further above, at the beginning of siman 322, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
2) There. It may not be touched. Although one may touch a muktzeh object so long as one does not move it, as written above, siman 308, se'if 3, in the gloss, an egg is different. Since it rolls, we are concerned that touching it might cause it to move. Beis Yosef; Bach. This is the reason for Rama's statement in the gloss, 'that is, to move it.' He means that touching is forbidden because the egg rolls and touching it constitutes moving it, as Magen Avraham, subsection 2, writes. One may touch muktzeh that does not move, as written above, siman 308, se'if 3, in the gloss. Accordingly, if an egg is resting in a place where it will not move when touched, it may be touched. According to Shulchan Aruch, however, one may not touch muktzeh even when it does not move, unless this is necessary for a permitted object, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Terumas HaDeshen. See there. Magen Avraham there writes likewise. We also wrote this above, siman 308, subsection 30, and siman 310, subsection 31. See there.
3) There. It may not be touched. This applies specifically when there is no need. If a movable object is resting upon the egg, one may take that object, and there is no concern if the egg rolls as a result. Chemed Moshe, subsection 1. See above, siman 308, subsection 31.
4) There. If it became mixed even with one thousand, etc. This is because it is an item that will become permitted. If it is left until after Yom Tov, it will become permitted by itself. The Sages ruled stringently that an item which will become permitted by itself is not nullified even in one thousand. It is better to wait to eat it until the time of its permissibility arrives than to eat it while forbidden by relying upon nullification. Levush, se'if 4; Taz, subsection 2; Magen Avraham, subsection 3; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4; Chayei Adam, rule 101, subsection 4; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 7, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4. For this same reason, the doubtful case in se'if 2 is also forbidden. Taz there; Chemed Moshe there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Chayei Adam there; Mikraei Kodesh there; Mishnah Berurah there. See Yoreh Deah, siman 86, se'if 3.
5) There. They are all forbidden. They are forbidden both to eat and to move. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Mikraei Kodesh there; Mishnah Berurah there.
