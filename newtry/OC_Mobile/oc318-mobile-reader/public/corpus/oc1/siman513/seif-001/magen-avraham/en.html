Forbidden to touch, etc. Even from a hen designated for eating, for on Yom Tov that falls on Sunday it is forbidden d'oraisa, for every egg born today finished yesterday, and Shabbat does not prepare for Yom Tov, and we decree on ordinary Yom Tov because of Yom Tov that falls on Sunday. And that it is forbidden in tilting is because we do not distinguish (Yeshuot Shalom). And see siman 515.

That is, to move it. He holds that the reason for the prohibition of touching is because the egg rolls and it is tilting — not like Terumat HaDeshen who forbids touching muktzeh when it is for the need of the muktzeh. And see in Bach and Mordechai, and likewise siman 477 seif 3 and siman 308 seif 3 in the gloss where he stated it without distinguishing; however it is difficult in siman 265 seif 3 where he wrote that it is forbidden to touch a lit lamp when it is hanging lest it sway somewhat from his touch and tilt — it is implied that when it is not lit it is permitted to touch even though it sways from his touch. And in Darkei Moshe it is written in the name of Or Zarua: and from the question of muktzeh there is none, for tilting from the side — until here; it is implied that it discusses when a permitted item rests on the lamp, then it is permitted to take it from it, but to touch it directly is forbidden. And from the words of Shulchan Aruch in siman 510 seif 6 it is implied that he holds like Terumat HaDeshen that all muktzeh is forbidden to touch when touching is for the need of muktzeh, even if it is not round like an egg — for he wrote it explicitly without distinguishing. And it requires study on Rama who did not distinguish there, and siman 521 seif 2.
