Forbidden to touch, etc. Even from a hen that stands for eating; for on Yom Tov that falls on Sunday it is forbidden according to the Torah, for every egg that is laid today finished yesterday, and Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov; and we decree on ordinary Yom Tov on account of Yom Tov that falls on Sunday. And that it is forbidden in moving is because we do not distinguish (Yam Shel Shlomo). And see what he wrote in siman 516.
That is, to move it. He holds that the reason for the prohibition of touching is because the egg rolls and it is moving; not like Terumat HaDeshen, who forbids touching a muktzeh item when the touching is for the need of the muktzeh. And see in Bach and Mordechai, and likewise in siman 277, seif 3, and siman 308, seif 3 in the gloss, where he stated it without distinguishing; however it is difficult, for in siman 265 at the end of seif 3 he wrote that it is forbidden to touch a lit lamp when it is hanging, lest it sway somewhat from his touch and tilt; end quote; it is implied that when it is not lit it is permitted to touch even though it sways from his touch. And in Darkei Moshe it is written in the name of Or Zarua, and this is his language: and as for muktzeh there is none, for it is moving from the side; end quote; it is implied that it discusses when a permitted item rests on the lamp, for then it is permitted to take it from it, but to touch it directly is forbidden. And from the words of Shulchan Aruch in siman 310, seif 6, it is implied that he holds like Terumat HaDeshen, that all muktzeh is forbidden to touch when the touching is for the need of the muktzeh item, even if it is not round like an egg, for he wrote it without distinction; and this requires further study on Rama, who did not distinguish there; and see siman 521, seif 2.
All are forbidden. For it is a davar sheyeish lo matirim.
