{6} Unwittingly: for if it had been intentional, nullification would not help him, even though it is a rabbinic prohibition, as is explained in Yoreh Deah siman 99, seif 6.
{7} All is permitted: whether the egg was whole and peeled [for if it was not peeled, it would be permitted even without sixty against it, whether cooked with meat and cooked food or cooked with other eggs, for it does not give taste when in its shell, as is explained in Yoreh Deah siman 86], or whether it was beaten and mixed into the cooked food; for that which we hold that a davar sheyeish lo matirim is not nullified is specifically when it is the same kind in same kind, but not when it is a different kind.
{8} Except for the egg: it is implied that the egg is still whole, and nevertheless [it is permitted] specifically when cooked with meat; but with other eggs, even if he recognizes that egg, nevertheless all the eggs are forbidden, for it is the same kind and is not nullified in a davar sheyeish lo matirim. But Rama's view in Yoreh Deah siman 102, seif 4 in the gloss is that since it is only its taste and not the substance of the prohibition, it is nullified even in a davar sheyeish lo matirim; and where joy of Yom Tov would otherwise be prevented, one may rely on this.
{9} Something done for appearance and taste, etc.: Magen Avraham explains that we specifically require both appearance and taste; but in Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 102, s.k. 106, and likewise in Biur HaGra there, they explain that the intent is appearance or taste.
{10} It is not nullified: for thereby it is considered like same kind in same kind, which is not nullified in a davar sheyeish lo matirim.
