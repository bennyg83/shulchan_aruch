(א) That was cooked unwittingly. Kol Bo wrote on this: everything is permitted except the egg that one leaves until tomorrow, for we hold peeling of eggs is permitted, for it is merely the moisture of eggs [in general] and there is no taste at all; and he wrote that this was an incident in Luniel with an egg born on Yom Tov that was placed in a stew called fnadeh, and they permitted everything except the egg — end quote. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: and according to what our master wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, even without taste it is permitted, for the taste is batel in 60 — end quote; explanation of Rabbeinu Tam: he holds we are not stringent regarding davar she-yesh lo matirin except when it is in substance and not taste alone. And Beit Yosef's words here are astounding, for according to Kol Bo's taste one does not need 60 to be batel, for there is no prohibition at all; and his words in Shulchan Aruch here are most astounding — why did he require 60, for he himself ruled in Yoreh Deah siman 86, seif 5, this view that mere moisture of eggs in general does not forbid at all; and it is strained to say here it deals with a pierced egg, for he should have mentioned this matter; and furthermore, Kol Bo explicitly deals with an unpierced egg, and since he refers to his words, automatically it also deals with an unpierced egg:
