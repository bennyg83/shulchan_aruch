A doubt, etc. In the Tur it is written the opposite: a doubt, etc., is forbidden, and if it became mixed in a thousand all are forbidden. From the fact that Beis Yosef changed his wording it is implied that he holds that a doubt that became mixed, all are permitted, for each one is a double doubt, as Maggid Mishneh wrote; and this requires further study, for in Yoreh Deah siman 102 he ruled to forbid; and perhaps there it discusses (as he wrote) Sunday, which is according to the Torah and a davar sheyeish lo matirim, but here which is rabbinic; and nevertheless it appears to me to be stringent, for even a double doubt where both [doubts] are in the item itself some forbid in a davar sheyeish lo matirim, as is written in siman 497, seif 4; and a fortiori when one doubt is in the item itself and one through mixture, which is worse, as is written in Yoreh Deah siman 110; and so he ruled in Darkei Moshe and in Toras Chatas according to the view of the Tur.
