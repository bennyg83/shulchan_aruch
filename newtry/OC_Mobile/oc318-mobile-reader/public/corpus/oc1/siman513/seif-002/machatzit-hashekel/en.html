(Seif katan 2) That is, etc. — that the prohibition of touching is from Yerushalmi on this that we teach on page 4 of Beitzah, etc., but one may invert a vessel over it so it does not break. Yerushalmi concludes: only that one should not touch it. They brought this in Beit Yosef in siman 510, and Maggid Mishneh chapter 25 of Laws of Shabbat wrote: I do not know the reason, etc. And perhaps the egg will sway through the vessel (meaning because the egg is round it sways through touching). Until here. And in Terumat HaDeshen he wrote that this reason is strained. And perhaps Terumat HaDeshen holds that there is no reason to forbid touching from this, for it is a davar she'eino mitkaven and also not a psik reisha. And even if it is a psik reisha one can say Terumat HaDeshen follows his view that in a rabbinic matter a psik reisha is permitted (as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 514 seif katan 5), and Terumat HaDeshen wrote: rather it appears to distinguish — there, since placing the vessel on the egg is for the need of the egg which is muktzeh, therefore touching is forbidden; but when the placement and use is for the need of something that is not muktzeh, when one touches through the placement on muktzeh it is permitted. Until here Beit Yosef above siman 514.

And likewise siman 477 seif 3 — there Rama wrote: nevertheless it is permitted to touch a tray, etc., and likewise it is permitted to touch a lamp, etc., only that one should not cause movement, etc., and he stated it without stipulating specifically when touching is for the need of a permitted matter — therefore even for the need of muktzeh it is permitted, not like Terumat HaDeshen. And likewise in siman 308 seif 3 in the gloss he wrote: all muktzeh, etc., but touching alone when it does not sway is permitted — and likewise he stated it without writing specifically when touching is for the need of a permitted matter; perforce he holds that when it does not sway in every case it is permitted even for the need of muktzeh, not like Terumat HaDeshen.

However it is difficult, etc., even though it sways from his touch. And to resolve the Yerushalmi that wrote "only that he should not touch" — which implies touching is forbidden — perforce it must be as Terumat HaDeshen wrote to distinguish whether touching is for the need of a permitted matter or not; and if so the words of Rama contradict each other. And in Darkei Moshe he wrote in the name of Or Zarua, etc. — meaning per the words of Darkei Moshe the words of Rama are resolved.

It is implied that it discusses, etc. — meaning Or Zarua discusses when a permitted item rests on the lamp and therefore it is permitted to take a permitted item from the lamp even though thereby the lamp is caused to sway; nevertheless since he does not touch the lamp itself, only through a permitted item resting on it, it is only tilting from the side and therefore permitted. And with this one resolves the words of Rama in siman 265 — certainly also in siman 265 he holds not like Terumat HaDeshen, and there is no distinction between touching for the need of a permitted matter or for the need of muktzeh. And when it sways in any case it is forbidden. And that he wrote "and forbidden to touch a lit lamp," etc. — he stated a psik reisha matter: when the lamp is lit it is forbidden even if one touches it through a permitted item resting on it, for it is not only tilting from the side; but when it is not lit the prohibition is not a psik reisha matter, for sometimes it is permitted — that is, when he does not touch it, only through a permitted item — that is, tilting from the side. And even though in Yerushalmi it wrote regarding inverting a vessel over it and only that he should not touch the vessel — as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 514, the wording of Yerushalmi, and as is written here seif 4 — the reason is that Rama holds that swaying is also called tilting muktzeh; nevertheless we establish in siman 511 that tilting from the side is permitted — that is, specifically when tilting is for the need of a permitted matter; therefore Magen Avraham wrote that a permitted item rests. But in Yerushalmi that discusses inverting a vessel, which is for the need of the muktzeh egg — in such a case even tilting from the side is forbidden.

For he wrote explicitly the Mechaber, etc., that it is forbidden to tilt it, etc., but permitted to invert a vessel over it and only that he should not touch it — until here Beit Yosef. And he stated it without writing that it discusses a round thing that sways; perforce even if it does not sway it is forbidden since there touching is for the need of muktzeh, as Magen Avraham wrote there — that is, per the view of Terumat HaDeshen. And if so Rama, who does not hold like Terumat HaDeshen, should have distinguished there specifically when it sways it is forbidden.
