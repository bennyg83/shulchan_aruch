Magen Avraham seif katan 15: if so, a fortiori — it itself is a decree; otherwise it is fine that if a chick born on Yom Tov were not forbidden because of muktzeh, also one born on Shabbat would be permitted on the Yom Tov after it, for it prepared for itself — if they slaughter it for a sick person it would be permitted for a healthy person in its entrails. And even rabbinically it is not preparation; but in truth a chick that was born is muktzeh — born on Shabbat is forbidden on the Yom Tov after it as rabbinic preparation, for there is no preparation for itself; for if they slaughter it for a sick person it would be forbidden for a healthy person because of muktzeh. And even if we say that also R' Yehuda who permits a chick born on Yom Tov — since he permits it through slaughter, this helps for the muktzeh that was until the time of birth, not to judge from the fact it was muktzeh during twilight; but on Shabbat nevertheless after birth it is muktzeh from Shabbat prohibition. And if so, since the muktzeh of twilight and the muktzeh from the time of birth onward — if they slaughter for a sick person it is permitted for a healthy person, for there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbat — nevertheless the preparation itself for a sick person: at the time of birth it is permitted for a healthy person in its entrails; if so it is not preparation even rabbinically. And I saw to resolve Yeshuot Shalom that per R' Yehuda a chick born on Shabbat is permitted to tilt, since it permits itself through slaughter; and in my humble opinion this is not so, for nevertheless after birth it is muktzeh from Shabbat prohibition.
