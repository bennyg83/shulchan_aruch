{13} It is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov: that is, on the night of the second day of Yom Tov, for either way one of them is a weekday.
{14} Of Rosh Hashana: because the two days are holy and are considered like one long day; and in a case of doubt whether it was laid on the first day of Yom Tov, it is permitted even on the second day of Yom Tov of Rosh Hashana; so wrote Kenesses HaGedolah; and Olat Shmuel is stringent.
{15} Adjacent to each other: whether it is Yom Tov after Shabbos or Shabbos after Yom Tov.
{16} It is forbidden on this one: until evening; for through this laying the egg becomes prepared for eating, and if we permit it, it turns out that one prepared for the other; and we hold that Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov, and Yom Tov does not prepare for Shabbos.
{17} On Sunday and Monday: to exclude if Yom Tov was on Shabbos and on Sunday; [then] it is forbidden on the first day even though it is the second day of Yom Tov, lest today be holy.
{18} It is permitted on Monday: for either way, if today the first day was holy, today is a weekday; and if today is holy, yesterday when it was laid was a weekday.
{19} Of the diaspora: and to exclude Rosh Hashana, for both of them are like one long day, so even on the second day it is forbidden; and the same law applies if Rosh Hashana falls on Thursday and Friday and it was laid on Thursday: for this reason it is forbidden even on Shabbos.
