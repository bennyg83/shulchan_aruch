[5] Permitted on the second day, etc. So wrote Tamim De'im siman 127, and so is implied from the Rif at the beginning of Beitzah who wrote: it makes no difference — Shabbat to the second day of Yom Tov, such as when Yom Tov falls on Shabbat, etc. — it is implied that when the first day of Yom Tov is on the first day it is permitted on the second. And it appears to me that this is how he explained what Maharil wrote: an egg laid on the first day of Yom Tov is permitted on the second — only regarding Yom Tov that falls after Shabbat, until here — meaning that the second day of Yom Tov falls on the first day and the first day of Yom Tov is on Shabbat. And this is the intent of Darkei Moshe who wrote to permit in the name of Maharil — not like Bach who wrote in his name to forbid. And see Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah.
