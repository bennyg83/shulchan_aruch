And likewise on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And if you ask: when it was born on Shabbat, behold it was completed on day six, which is a weekday, and it should be permitted — one can say: since it was concealed on Shabbat due to rabbinic [law], if you permit it on the Yom Tov that follows it, it turns out Shabbat prepared for Yom Tov and not for itself; and this prohibition is only d'rabbanan, for by Torah law even on the first [day] it is permitted, and if so [Shabbat] prepared for itself — end quote Ran. And in Yeshuot Shalom he wrote: according to what Tosafot wrote in the first chapter, that birth is considered preparation — if so it is forbidden d'oraisa on the day after it — end quote. And it seems he did not see the words of Ramban in Milchamot, who wrote that birth is considered preparation and nevertheless it is permitted d'oraisa on the second day, for the first day prepared for itself — end quote. And so in Sefer HaChinuch, that you do not find preparation d'oraisa except regarding an egg that was born on Shabbat after Yom Tov or on Yom Tov after Shabbat; and see there siman 366 and siman 507, seif 2:
