(s.k. 4) A doubt, etc. And from the fact that Beis Yosef changed, etc.: that is to say, the Tur who wrote first "and its doubt is forbidden," and afterward wrote "and if it became mixed," etc., it is implied that this "and if it became mixed" also refers to the doubt, and he holds regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim that even a double doubt is forbidden. But Beis Yosef changed and wrote first "and if it became mixed," etc., and afterward wrote "and its doubt," etc., it is implied that this "and if it became mixed," etc., refers specifically to one certainly laid on Yom Tov; but a doubt that became mixed is permitted.
And perhaps there it discusses Sunday, as it should read; that is to say, the egg is in doubt whether it was laid on Sunday or laid on erev Shabbos; for if it were certainly laid on Sunday it is forbidden according to the Torah because of preparation; therefore they were stringent in doubt, that even if it became mixed it is forbidden. But here, where the doubt is whether it was laid on Yom Tov: for even if it were certainly laid on ordinary Yom Tov it is forbidden only by rabbinic law, for they decreed on account of Yom Tov after Shabbos; therefore they were lenient regarding its doubt through mixtures.
And as is written in siman 497, seif 4. That is to say, Beis Yosef in his long composition brought the words of the Ran, who wrote that there are those who disagree with Raavad of Lunel, who permitted a doubt of something prepared on the second day of Yom Tov by reason of a double doubt.
A fortiori when one doubt is in the item itself, etc., which is worse, etc.: that is to say, the Mechaber wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 110, seif 9: an egg of doubtful terefah that became mixed with others, all are forbidden, etc. Since the first doubt was in the item itself, one may not permit it through a double doubt. And on this Rama wrote: and some say the reason it is forbidden is since the doubt is a Torah prohibition, and we cannot say further a doubt that there is no prohibition here, only that it became mixed with others: this is not called a double doubt. And Shach wrote that there is a practical difference between the two reasons if the first doubt was rabbinic: per Rama it is permitted, and per Beis Yosef forbidden; and in the end Shach inclined that one can say even the Mechaber agrees where one doubt is rabbinic, it is permitted through a double doubt, namely when it became mixed, even though the first doubt is in the item itself and the second through mixture; see there. And if so here, which discusses ordinary Yom Tov whose egg prohibition is rabbinic: if so, even one doubt in the item itself and one through mixture is a double doubt and permitted. Nevertheless such a case, where one is in the item itself and one through mixture, is worse than other double doubts, for in such a case in Torah law it is not a double doubt, and regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim there are poskim that even a double doubt where both are in the item itself is forbidden. In any event one should be stringent regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim with one doubt in the item itself and one through mixture, for it is not a double doubt; and three mixtures in a davar sheyeish lo matirim: see above in siman 496 what Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Shach.
