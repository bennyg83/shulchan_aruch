An egg that emerged mostly before Yom Tov and then went back in and was afterward laid on Yom' Tov — it is permitted. Therefore, even if one checked a hen's nest just before nightfall on the eve of Yom Tov and found no egg, and the next morning got up early and found an egg — it is permitted, since' hens do not lay at night and we assume the greater part had already emerged the day before and went back in. This applies when there is a rooster within sixty' houses with no river without a bridge separating them, for otherwise it is possible she laid at night and it would be forbidden. But if one did not check before Yom Tov, even if there is no rooster with her it is permitted, since we assume it was laid the day before — and without a rooster most hens lay during the day. Therefore it is permitted to buy eggs from a non-Jew on the first night of Yom Tov, as we assume they were laid on the eve of Yom Tov. Similarly on the second night of the two Diaspora Yom Tov days — but not on the second night of Rosh Hashanah, nor on the night of Yom Tov that follows Shabbat.Rema: A non-Jew who casually mentions that the eggs were laid yesterday may be relied upon. (Mordechai, beginning of Beitzah) This applies specifically to Yom Tov that falls in the middle of the week, but Yom Tov that falls on Sunday — where there is concern the egg was laid on Shabbat and is Biblically forbidden on Yom Tov — a non-Jew speaking casually is not believed. This applies only to the first day of Yom Tov, but on the second day, even Rosh Hashanah, he is believed since the prohibition is only Rabbinic. (Terumat HaDeshen §79)]:
