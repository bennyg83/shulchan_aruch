(6) [seif 2] If there is a doubt whether it was born on Yom Tov or on a weekday, it is forbidden; and even if it was mixed among a thousand, all of them are forbidden. Tur. And the reason is because it is a davar she-yesh lo matirin, and anything that has a permissible use is not batel. Beit Yosef. However, nevertheless, Maggid Mishneh chapter 1 law 80 wrote that regarding its doubt when it was mixed with others, there are those who ruled that all of them are permitted; and when it teaches that it was mixed among a thousand, all of them are forbidden — it certainly refers [only] to a case of certainty, and there are those who forbid. And regarding the view of those who permit, the Rashba, z"l, agreed (in Avodat HaKodesh, gate 5, section 4); and so it appears from the words of the Rambam, end of his words; and Bach agreed with Beit Yosef, and Taz. And so is the view of Maadanei Kodesh and Bigdei Yesha, s.k. 24. However, in Yoreh Deah siman 102, seif 1, the Shulchan Aruch ruled there to forbid; and so too Magen Avraham, s.k. 24, that one should be stringent. And so R' Zvi, section 4, Chayei Adam general rule 101, section 4, Machatzit HaShekel siman 7, section 2. However, R' Zvi wrote there that on the second day of Yom Tov one may be lenient if eggs are needed — see there. And in Chayei Adam general rule 53, section 19, he wrote that on an ordinary Yom Tov that is not after Shabbat one may permit for great need; and it implies even on the first day of Yom Tov, as long as it is not after Shabbat.
