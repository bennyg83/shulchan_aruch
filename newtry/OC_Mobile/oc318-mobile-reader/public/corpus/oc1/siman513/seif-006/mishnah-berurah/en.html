{20} It is permitted: for since most of it emerged, it is as if born.
{21} And the next day he arose early: that is, before dawn; for if afterward we attribute that today she laid, for most of it emerged and returned, which is not common.
{22} She does not give birth at night: for whatever mates by day is born only by day, and a rooster mates by day; and even though if the hen warms herself from the ground she can sometimes give birth even at night, we do not attribute to this, for since there is a male even in another house far from her [that is, up to sixty houses], and she hears the sound of his crowing, she does not wish to warm herself from the ground and she pairs with the rooster; and that is why he wrote that there is no bridge in it: for if there is a bridge she would cross on the bridge.
{23} It is possible that she give birth at night: and even though most of them, even those that warm from the ground, do not give birth at night, here we follow the minority, since he checked yesterday and there was no egg there, and today he arose early before dawn and found [one], and we do not attribute that most of it emerged yesterday and returned, for that is a minority of a minority; and it is more satisfactory for us to attribute that she gave birth at night.
{24} But if he did not check, etc.: meaning, he arose early before dawn and found [it]; but if after dawn he found [it], it is forbidden out of doubt; and even though it is a rabbinic doubt, regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim we are stringent even in a doubt, as above.
{25} And without a male, most of them, etc.: that is to say, even without a male, in any event most of them give birth by day, and it is not an equal doubt.
{26} And likewise on the second night, etc.: for even if we say it was laid on the first day of Yom Tov, behold what was laid on this is permitted on that, as above in seif 5; but if he brought it by day, even on the second day of Yom Tov of the diaspora it is forbidden, lest it was laid today.
{27} Of Rosh Hashana, etc., that after Shabbos: for even if we say it was laid on the first day of Rosh Hashana or on Shabbos, it is likewise forbidden, as above in seif 5; and even if the second day of Yom Tov falls after Shabbos; and it discusses when the non-Jew does not recount at all when it was laid, or recounts that it was laid earlier and not of his own accord, such as after Jews asked when it was laid, and he answers that it was before Rosh Hashana or before Shabbos.
{28} And speaks casually, etc.: and it discusses when the non-Jew does not know that an egg laid today is forbidden to a Jew; for if he knows, even though he speaks casually he is not believed, for he intends to improve his merchandise so they buy from him, since the eggs are his. But if the eggs belong to a Jew and the non-Jew speaks of his own accord that they were laid on erev Yom Tov, even though he knows that an egg laid today is forbidden to a Jew, nevertheless since the non-Jew himself gains no benefit from lying that it was laid earlier, one may rely on him since he speaks of his own accord.
{29} One may rely on him: since it is a rabbinic matter, and also a prohibition was not established.
{30} Lest it was laid on Shabbos: and Magen Avraham's view is that even if it was laid on Shabbos there is no Torah prohibition to eat it; rather the main concern is lest it was laid today, and if so it was finished yesterday in her insides, for every egg that is laid today finished yesterday, and it turns out that Shabbos prepared for Yom Tov, and it is forbidden according to the Torah. Accordingly, if he brought it on Sunday before dawn, where there is no concern lest it was laid at night, and a non-Jew speaks casually that it was laid on erev Shabbos, it is permitted; for even if you say it was laid on Shabbos, its prohibition is only rabbinic, and in a rabbinic matter one who speaks casually is believed.
{31} A non-Jew who speaks casually is not believed: and in our countries, where eggs are very common and they bring many at once, certainly most of the eggs they bring to sell are from those already laid; therefore the non-Jew is believed when he speaks casually and says they were laid before Shabbos. And all this is when the non-Jew brings the eggs to a Jew's house; and if a Jew goes to the non-Jew's house to take eggs, see in the Ran and in Machatzit HaShekel what he brought in the name of Shitah Mekubetzet.
{32} On the second day of Yom Tov, even Rosh Hashana: that is to say, when Rosh Hashana falls on Shabbos and on Sunday, and the non-Jew brought on Sunday, he is believed when he speaks casually, for this too is only rabbinic, since we are expert in fixing the month and the main day is the first day; and in a rabbinic matter the non-Jew is believed when he speaks casually.
