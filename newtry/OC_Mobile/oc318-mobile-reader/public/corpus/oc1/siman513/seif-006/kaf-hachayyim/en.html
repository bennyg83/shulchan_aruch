6) [Se'if 2] If it is uncertain whether the egg was laid on Yom Tov or on a weekday, it is forbidden. Even if it became mixed with one thousand eggs, they are all forbidden. Tur. The reason is that it is an item that will become permitted, and any item that will become permitted is not nullified. Beis Yosef. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1, law 20, however, writes that some rule that if an egg of uncertain status became mixed with others, all are permitted. According to them, the statement that if it became mixed with one thousand all are forbidden refers to an egg certainly laid on Yom Tov. Others forbid the mixture. Rashba agrees with those who permit it in Avodas HaKodesh, gate 5, subsection 4, and this also appears from Rambam's words. These are his words. Beis Yosef and Ateres Zekeinim cite him. This is also the view of Mor Uketziah and Bigdei Yesha, subsection 4. Shulchan Aruch, however, rules stringently in Yoreh Deah, siman 102, se'if 1, and Magen Avraham, subsection 4, likewise writes that one should be stringent. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4; Chayei Adam, rule 101, subsection 4; Mikraei Kodesh, siman 7, subsection 2, rule accordingly. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there, however, that on the second day of Yom Tov one may be lenient if he needs the eggs. Chochmas Adam, rule 53, subsection 19, writes that on an ordinary Yom Tov that does not follow Shabbos, one may permit them in a case of great need. This implies even on the first day of Yom Tov, so long as it does not follow Shabbos. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 5, likewise writes that in a case of loss one may rely upon those who permit it.
