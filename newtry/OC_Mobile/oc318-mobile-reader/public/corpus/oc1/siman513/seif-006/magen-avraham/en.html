In Terumat HaDeshen he emended: perhaps it was laid on Sunday; and Darkei Moshe brings it; it is implied that if he brought it on Sunday at dawn, where there is no concern that it was laid today, and he speaks casually that it was laid on erev Shabbos, it is permitted; for even if you say it was laid on Shabbos, it is forbidden on Yom Tov only by rabbinic law, as he wrote in seif 5; therefore it appears to me that here one must emend: perhaps it was finished on Shabbos. And Yam Shel Shlomo wrote, and this is his language: Mahariv, siman 6, [wrote that] even on Yom Tov after Shabbos he is believed, since he does not improve his merchandise; on the contrary he diminishes it, for old eggs are inferior, and we are not concerned that he acted to cause a Jew to stumble (as is written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 118). And even though one may question his words, for there we say that he does not do an act to cause stumbling since he gains nothing, but we never believe him in what is a Torah doubt; nevertheless it appears to me to permit, for most sellers of eggs [sell] from those already laid, and although we do not permit because of a majority since it is a davar sheyeish lo matirim, nevertheless according to the Torah we may rely on the majority, only that it is forbidden by rabbinic law; therefore a non-Jew who speaks casually is believed; end quote of Yam Shel Shlomo. And Mordechai wrote the reason that we do not follow the majority: because eggs, as soon as they are laid, are eaten, and it is like something that becomes important; see there; if so they are forbidden according to the Torah, as is implied in the Gemara in Bava Metzia, chapter 2. However it appears to me that in our country eggs are very common and they bring many at once, and certainly the majority of what they bring to sell is from those already laid. And see in Responsa Rashba at the beginning of siman 243, and in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 15. And Mahariv's words, that we are not concerned that he acts to cause stumbling, imply that he knows that one laid [today] is forbidden; and if so, what is this that he wrote that he diminishes his merchandise? On the contrary he improves it, for he knows that a Jew will not buy it if he says it was laid today, as he wrote above in the name of Mordechai.
