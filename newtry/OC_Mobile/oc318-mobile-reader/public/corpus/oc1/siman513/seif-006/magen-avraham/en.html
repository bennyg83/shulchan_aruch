And if the eggs belong to an Israelite and the gentile is mistaken that they were born yesterday, and it is known they were born — it is forbidden to an Israelite; Yoreh Deah siman 69, seif 10 — the one who separated and Bach forbid, and Shach and Taz permit, see there:

Perhaps it was born on Shabbat. In Terumat HaDeshen he emended: perhaps it was born on Sunday, and Darkei Moshe brings it — it is implied that if he brought it on the first day at dawn there is no concern it was born today, and one who speaks loosely that it was born the day before is permitted, for even if you say it was born on Shabbat it is not forbidden on Yom Tov except rabbinically, as is written in seif 5; therefore it appears to me that here one must emend: perhaps it finished on Shabbat. And Yeshuot Shalom wrote: Mahariv siman 6 — even on Yom Tov after Shabbat he is believed, since he does not improve his merchandise; on the contrary he diminishes it, for old eggs are inferior and we are not concerned he acted to cause a Jew to stumble (as is written in Yoreh De'ah end of siman 118). And even though one can question his words — for there they say he does not do an act to cause stumbling since he gains nothing; but we never believe him in what is a Torah doubt — nevertheless it appears to me to permit, for most sellers of eggs are from those already born, and although we do not permit because of majority since it is a davar sheyeish lo matirim, nevertheless d'oraisa we may rely on the majority, only it is forbidden rabbinically; therefore a non-Jew speaking loosely is believed — until here Yeshuot Shalom. And Mordechai wrote the reason we do not follow the majority: because eggs are eaten immediately when born and it is like something that becomes important — see there; if so they are forbidden d'oraisa as is implied in the Gemara in Bava Metzia chapter 2. However it appears to me that in our country eggs are very common and they bring many on one day, and certainly the majority they bring to sell are already born. And see in responsa Rashba 243 and Yoreh De'ah siman 15. And Mahariv's words that we are not concerned he acts to cause stumbling imply that if he knows it was born it is forbidden; and if so what is this that he wrote that he diminishes his merchandise — on the contrary he improves it, for he knows a Jew will not buy it if he says it was born today, as Mordechai wrote.
