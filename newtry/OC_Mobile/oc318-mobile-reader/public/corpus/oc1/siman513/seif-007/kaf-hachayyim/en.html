If it is uncertain whether it was laid on Yom Tov, etc. If it became mixed with other eggs, and some of those then became mixed with still others, all the eggs in the second mixture are permitted, because there is a compound doubt arising through mixtures. All authorities rule leniently regarding these doubts. Avodas HaKodesh there; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4. This is also the view of Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh Deah, siman 106, se'if 8. Rama there in the gloss, however, is stringent except in a case of need, as Shach writes there, subsection 57. Pri Chadash there, subsection 43, agrees with Shulchan Aruch, as do Beis Lechem Yehudah there, subsection 46; Lechem HaPanim there, subsection 60; Pleisi there, at the end of subsection 28; and Erech HaShulchan there, at the end of subsection 18. Pri Chadash writes there that even according to Rama, who is stringent, this applies specifically to a Torah prohibition, such as an egg laid after Shabbos. Regarding a rabbinic prohibition, such as an egg laid on the second day of Yom Tov, even Rama permits it in this case. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav in this siman, subsection 4, writes that permission in this case applies only on the second day of Yom Tov, not on the first. This implies that even if the first day does not follow Shabbos, it may not be permitted. Nevertheless, we have already written that even according to Rama it is permitted in a case of need.
