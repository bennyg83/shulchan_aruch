If there is a doubt whether it was born on Yom Tov, etc.; and if it was mixed with others and from these with others, any mixture of the second group is permitted, because there is here a safek safeka arising from the mixtures, and all of them rule in these doubts to permit. Avodat HaKodesh there. Eliyah Rabbah, section 4; and so is the view of the Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh Deah siman 106, seif 8; however the view of our teacher Rama, z"l, there, in the Hagahah is to be stringent unless for a time of need, as Sha'ar HaTziyun wrote there, s.k. 57 — see there. And so is the view of Peri Chadash there, section 43, like the words of the Shulchan Aruch; and so is the view of Beit Levi there, section 46; Likutei Halachot Peri Megadim there, section 60; Peleiti there at the end, section 28. Eliyah Rabbah there at the end, section 18; and Peri Chadash wrote there that even according to our teacher Rama, z"l, who is stringent, this applies to a d'oraisa prohibition, such as an egg that was born after Shabbat; but in a d'rabbanan prohibition, such as an egg that was born on Monday, even according to our teacher Rama, z"l, it is permitted in such a case — see there. And R' Zvi in this siman, section 4, wrote that one may not permit in such a case except on the second day of Yom Tov, but not on the first day of Yom Tov; and it implies even if it is not after Shabbat one may not permit on the first day of Yom Tov; however, we already wrote that even according to our teacher Rama, z"l, for a time of need it is permitted:
