(א) (s.k. 7) For appearance, etc. — even though it came to improve, etc.; for at the end of tractate Beitzah we learn: a woman who borrowed water and salt for her dough (it was brought above in Shulchan Aruch siman 327, seif 12) — the dough goes per the feet of both of them, and they cannot bring the dough except to a place where both women can walk, for all things go per the feet of the owners; and the Talmud challenges: let the water and salt be batel relative to the dough and the dough should go per the feet of the owner of the flour; and Rav Ashi answers: because it is davar she-yesh lo matirin and it is not batel. And Tosafot challenged: here it is a different type in a different type, and also davar she-yesh lo matirin is batel as written in Yoreh Deah siman 102. And they answered: in their boundaries they were further stringent, etc., since water and salt come to improve the dough it is called the same type in the same type (and Taz and Peri Chadash in Yoreh Deah wrote: specifically regarding davar she-yesh lo matirin it is called the same type in the same type where it came to improve and not in another place); and therefore Magen Avraham wrote: since the Mechaber wrote 'for appearance and taste' it implies he requires both. But if he only did it for taste and it is not recognizable in appearance it is batel (that is, this is not difficult, for we hold every taste that was done only for taste is not batel, as Sha'ar HaTziyun already wrote in siman 98, s.k. 28, where the taste is not sensed it is batel, and so Peri Chadash); nevertheless one must say the Mechaber does not hold like the second answer of Tosafot mentioned above, but even when it came to improve it is called a different type, and therefore even davar she-yesh lo matirin is batel, and therefore he requires both appearance and taste:

The words of Maharai: where one comes to repair it is called a rabbinical court.

(ג) And see there in Shach, for he holds and explains thus the words of Shulchan Aruch there, that one suffices — either he did it for appearance or he did it for taste, it is not batel; and Peri Chadash disagrees regarding appearance, since it is an unresolved question that was not decided in Bava Kamma; therefore in a d'oraisa matter — namely an egg that was born on Yom Tov after Shabbat — we go stringently that appearance is a significant matter, but on an ordinary Yom Tov, which is d'rabbanan, we say appearance is not a significant matter; and see in Minchat Yaakov, general rule 74, s.k. 5:

If it is not peeled, etc. — even sixty are not needed, etc., and likewise Terumat HaDeshen — it is implied from Magen Avraham's words that even where there is no loss it is permitted and sixty are not required. And Beit Yosef brought here in the name of Kol Bo: an unpeeled egg laid on Yom Tov that was cooked with the stew — the stew is permitted because it is like ordinary eggs, etc. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: for those who say taste is nullified even in a davar sheyeish lo matirim, Kol Bo did not need the reason that it is like ordinary eggs. And Terumat HaDeshen ruled generally per the law of three differences of opinion regarding taste Kol Bo — even sixty are not needed. And he wrote further: even for one who disagrees with Kol Bo and holds that even an unpeeled egg forbids — nevertheless on ordinary Yom Tov which is only rabbinic he agrees that an unpeeled egg does not forbid. And even if one says he disagrees even in a rabbinic prohibition — nevertheless we have whom to rely on at least, that in a rabbinic prohibition — that is, ordinary Yom Tov — an unpeeled egg does not forbid as of now. And it is implied that even where there is no loss one may permit in such a case. However in Yoreh De'ah siman 86 seif 6 Rama wrote: and some say that all eggs, etc., and they forbid even when they are not peeled, and so one should practice where loss is not great, etc. — see there in Terumat HaDeshen in Minchat Yaakov. And from Magen Avraham's words it is implied he ruled like Terumat HaDeshen.
