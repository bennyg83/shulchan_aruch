(s.k. 7) For appearance, etc. even though it came to improve, etc.: for at the end of tractate Beitzah we learn: a woman who borrowed water and salt for her dough (brought above in Shulchan Aruch siman 397, seif 12): the dough is according to the feet of both of them, and they cannot carry the dough except to a place where both women can go, for all things are according to the feet of the owners; and the Gemara challenges: let the water and salt be nullified relative to the dough, and the dough should be according to the feet of the owner of the flour; and R' Ashi answers: because it is a davar sheyeish lo matirim and is not nullified. And Tosafos challenged: but a different kind, even a davar sheyeish lo matirim, is nullified, as is written in Yoreh Deah siman 102. And they answered: regarding techumin they were more stringent. Another answer: since water and salt come to improve the dough, it is called same kind in same kind (and Taz and Peri Chadash in Yoreh Deah wrote: specifically regarding a davar sheyeish lo matirim it is called same kind in same kind where it came to improve, and not elsewhere). And therefore Magen Avraham wrote: from the fact that the Mechaber wrote "for appearance and taste," it is implied that we require both. But if it acts only for taste and is not recognizable in appearance, it is nullified (that is, this is not difficult, for we hold that whatever acts for taste is not nullified: Shach already wrote in siman 98, s.k. 28, that where the taste is not sensed it is nullified, and so Peri Chadash). Nevertheless one must say that the Mechaber does not hold like the second answer of Tosafos mentioned above, but even when it came to improve it is called a different kind, and therefore even a davar sheyeish lo matirim is nullified; and therefore we require both appearance and taste.
The words of Maharai: where it came to improve, it is called same kind in same kind.
And see there in Shach, who holds and explains thus the words of Shulchan Aruch there: that one suffices, either that it acts for appearance or that it acts for taste, it is not nullified; and Peri Chadash disagrees regarding appearance, since it is an unresolved question that was not decided in Bava Kamma; therefore regarding Torah law, namely an egg laid on Yom Tov after Shabbos, we go stringently that appearance is a matter, and on ordinary Yom Tov which is rabbinic we say appearance is not a matter; and see in Sefer Minchat Yaakov, klal 74, s.k. 5.
If it is not peeled, etc. even sixty are not needed, etc.; and so Toras Chatas ruled: it is implied from Magen Avraham's words that even where there is no loss it is permitted and sixty are not required. And Beis Yosef brought here in the name of Kol Bo: an unpeeled egg laid on Yom Tov that was cooked with the cooked food: the cooked food is permitted, for it is merely like egg-water, etc. And Beis Yosef wrote on this: for those who say that taste is nullified even in a davar sheyeish lo matirim, Kol Bo did not need the reason that it is merely like egg-water. And Toras Chatas, klal 74, din 3, wrote that there is a practical difference per Kol Bo's reason: even sixty are not needed. And he further wrote: even according to one who disagrees with Kol Bo and holds that even an unpeeled [egg] forbids, nevertheless on ordinary Yom Tov which is only rabbinic he agrees that an unpeeled [egg] does not forbid; and even if you say he disagrees even regarding a rabbinic prohibition, nevertheless we at least have upon whom to rely, that regarding a rabbinic prohibition, namely ordinary Yom Tov, an unpeeled [egg] does not forbid; end of his words. And it is implied that even where there is no loss one may permit in such a case. However in Yoreh Deah siman 87, seif 6, Rama wrote, and this is his language: and some say that all eggs, etc., and they forbid even when they are not peeled, and so one should practice where there is no great loss, etc. And see there in Toras Chatas in Sefer Minchat Yaakov. And from Magen Avraham's words it is implied that he ruled like the view of Toras Chatas.
