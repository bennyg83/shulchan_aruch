Another to blow. He stands in his place, and need not stand on the bimah [Darkei Moshe, siman 586].
It is proper to do so. Because one should not pass the mitzvah away from him; see what I wrote at the end of siman 174. It seems to me that all this refers to their times, when they would give him the shofar-blowing without specifying otherwise, and therefore he immediately acquired the right to all the blasts, as written in siman 174. But now, when in some places the custom is to give the orders of blasts to another person, the first one never acquired the right to all of them, and it is permitted. This is what he means by, 'another may blow.' You should know this, for with the tamid offering, nine kohanim acquired roles in it, because 'with a multitude of people,' etc.
