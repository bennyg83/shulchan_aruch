The Rif and Rosh wrote there that strictly speaking they should sound during the order of the blessings in the same sequence as they sounded while seated. However, since the blessings are not indispensable to the blasts, and they already fulfilled their obligation through those sounded while seated, it is sufficient to sound tashrat, tashat, and tarat once during the order of the blessings, so as not to burden the congregation. This is the custom throughout the world. However, the Rosh wrote there that Rabbeinu Tam wondered at this custom, namely, tashrat for Malchuyos and tashat for Zichronos, etc. For if the proper teruah is groaning followed by wailing, all should be tashrat; if it is groaning alone, all should be tashat, etc. Rabbeinu Tam therefore instituted sounding tashrat for Zichronos and Shofaros as for Malchuyos. In this manner one emerges from all doubts, and there is only a possible interruption. In order not to alter the custom too greatly, we are not concerned for the interruption, for we rule that one who heard nine blasts over nine hours of the day has fulfilled his obligation, see there. The Tur and Beit Yosef cited his words. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch ruled without qualification in accordance with the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. The words of the Rama in the gloss follow Rabbeinu Tam's view, as stated. However, Rabbeinu Yeshayah the First in his manuscript rulings, the Riaz, the Radbaz, siman 29, and the Shelah agreed that the primary custom is to sound tashrat, tashat, and tarat in each section, see there. The Erekh HaShulchan, os 1, cited their words. This is also the view of the Aruch and Sefer HaManhig, as written above, os 1, see there. See further in the following os.
