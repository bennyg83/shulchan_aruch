Neither the one blowing nor others should speak, etc. In the Tur it is written that he need not go back and recite the blessing, but nevertheless one should rebuke one who speaks, etc. The Ran likewise wrote in the name of the head of the yeshivah that it is proper to rebuke him. The Ran wondered about this, for this speech is not an interruption, just like one who speaks in the middle of a meal, who need not go back and recite Hamotzi. In my humble opinion, the answer is that this is like one who has not yet begun the mitzvah, such as where he has not yet eaten at all; for at the time of the blessing he intends for the main fulfillment of the mitzvah, which is according to the order of the blessings, as written in siman 575: Why do they blow when sitting and then return and blow during Mussaf? In order to confuse the Satan. This means that through the first shofar blast he is confused before the prayer, so that he not prosecute during the prayer, etc. Thus, the primary shofar blast of the mitzvah is the one according to the order of the blessings; the sitting blasts are only to confuse the Satan at the time of fulfilling the mitzvah that comes afterward. Granted, he need not go back and recite the blessing, since in any event he began performing the mitzvah and its need; nevertheless, as long as he has not yet fulfilled the main part, he cannot interrupt. Moreover, in any mitzvah in which he is engaged and is not permitted to leave from there until he has fulfilled it entirely, there is a prohibition in interrupting it; and with bedikas chametz too we find such a prohibition, as written in siman 432. This is not comparable to one who speaks in the middle of a meal or while sitting in a sukkah, for there, if he wants, he may stop and leave, unlike a place where he has not yet completed the mitzvah, as here, where the main mitzvah is still before him. It is obvious that there is a prohibition to speak. What difference is there between speech between the sitting blasts themselves, where there is certainly a prohibition to interrupt with speech, and speech after them, as long as he has not yet fulfilled what belongs to this mitzvah? In siman 432 I wrote further about this; see there. So it seems to me, that there is a prohibition in this interruption by speech according to the law. A parallel to this we find in chapter Arvei Pesachim, folio 120, regarding the blessing on matzah, as follows: 'Even if the main mitzvah is with the last one, it is no wonder if we recite the blessing on the first one in order to exempt the last one,' etc., end quote. Although there it is permitted to speak between the first eating and the last, there they are one matter, except that one of them is primary; unlike here, where the first and the last are each a distinct mitzvah.
