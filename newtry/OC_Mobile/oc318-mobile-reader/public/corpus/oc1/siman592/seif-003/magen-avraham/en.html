He should not speak. During the shofar blasts themselves it is obvious that it is forbidden to speak; even to spit is forbidden [Olas Hamid].
And the prayers are not an interruption. Although in siman 167, seif 6, he wrote that ab initio one should not speak at all, that refers to between the blessing and Hamotzi. But between one shofar blast and another it is permitted ab initio to speak about matters related to them.
He need not go back. This is not comparable to one who spoke between the tefillin of the arm and the tefillin of the head, for there they are two mitzvos, unlike here, where all of it is one mitzvah [Rif]. Therefore, even if he spoke between the sitting blasts themselves, he need not go back and recite the blessing [Hagahos Maimoniyos and Rosh]. See what I wrote in siman 590, seif 8.
About matters of the shofar blasts. This requires analysis, for in siman 167, seif 6, he ruled that ab initio one should not interrupt at all. Perhaps here it is discussing a case where he needs to speak, such as telling someone to bring a shofar and the like. It is obvious that if he spoke between the blessing and the shofar blast about matters of prayer, he must go back and recite the blessing. This is not comparable to what I wrote above, that if one spoke about needs of the meal, even though it is not a need of Hamotzi, he need not recite another blessing, for there Hamotzi is also a need of the meal. Here, however, what connection is there between prayer and the shofar blast? This is also implied in Mordechai. What the Rema wrote, that the prayers are not an interruption, refers to between one blast and another; but between the blessing and the blast it is an interruption, and he must go back and recite the blessing. It is obvious that even a listener, if he spoke between the blessing and the blast, must recite the blessing. See what I wrote in siman 167, seif 6, in the gloss.
