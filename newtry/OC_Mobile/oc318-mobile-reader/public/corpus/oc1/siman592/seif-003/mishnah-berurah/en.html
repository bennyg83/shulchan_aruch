(8) He should not speak, etc. For the blessing that he recites before the shofar blasts also applies to the standing blasts.
(9) Nor the congregation. For they fulfilled their obligation through his blessing, and it is as though they recited the blessing themselves.
(10) Between, etc. During the shofar blasts themselves, it is obvious that it is forbidden to speak; even spitting is forbidden, because one must hear the entire blast from beginning to end, even if it is very long, as written in siman 590. All the more so, it is forbidden to make a sound by yawning or by clearing the throat, which also confuses the other listeners.
(11) For the standing blasts. The same applies between the sitting blasts; and also within the standing blasts themselves, it is likewise forbidden to interrupt.
(12) And the prayers are not an interruption. That is to say, between the sitting blasts and the standing blasts it is permitted ab initio to speak about matters related to them. See Derech HaChaim, whose view is that from the blessing until the end of the sitting blasts it is forbidden to interrupt even with prayers. Therefore one should not say the Yehi Ratzon printed in the machzorim between the sitting blasts; rather, he should think it in his heart and not articulate it with his mouth, or say the Yehi Ratzon after the completion of the sitting blasts.
(13) He need not go back and recite the blessing. This is not comparable to one who spoke between the tefillin of the arm and the tefillin of the head, for there they are two mitzvos, unlike here, where all of it is one mitzvah. Therefore, even if he spoke idle words between the sitting blasts themselves, he need not go back and recite the blessing, just as one who speaks after tasting a little from the blessing of Hamotzi need not go back and recite the blessing, because it is all one meal. Nevertheless, the later authorities incline to say that one who spoke in tashrat between shevarim and teruah, even about matters of the shofar blast, although he need not go back and recite the blessing, nevertheless has not fulfilled his obligation and must go back and blow that order.
(14) About matters of the shofar blasts. Although in siman 167, seif 6, he ruled that ab initio one should not interrupt at all, here it is discussing a case where he needs to speak, such as telling someone to bring a shofar or to soak it in water because he cannot blow, and the like. It is obvious that if he spoke between the blessing and the shofar blast about a matter of prayer, he must go back and recite the blessing. What the Rema wrote, that prayer is not an interruption, refers to between one blast and another, as above; but between the blessing and the blast it is an interruption, and even a listener, if he spoke between the blessing and the blast, must recite the blessing.
