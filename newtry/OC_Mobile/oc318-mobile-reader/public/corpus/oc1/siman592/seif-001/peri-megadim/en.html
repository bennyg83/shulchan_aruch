And some say. See Taz and Beis Yosef on this, and siman 590, seif 8. That if one interrupted with another sound he must repeat even after the fact is difficult, and one must say that since they already fulfilled, with the sitting blasts, all the uncertainties, and there is also a double uncertainty: perhaps tashrat is the primary form, and perhaps another sound is not a concern. Rabbi Avahu instituted this ab initio, and even if one repeats and blows, that too is considered like ab initio. Therefore, because of the burden on the congregation, they instituted in the orders three times tashrat, according to the custom of Rabbeinu Tam. See Shelah, who wrote: Praiseworthy is one who blows thirty sounds in the orders; but our custom should not be changed. See siman 595, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there further.
