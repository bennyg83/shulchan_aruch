1) [Seif 1] The shaliach tzibbur repeats the prayer, and they sound the shofar, etc. However, during the silent Mussaf prayer they do not sound the shofar. This appears from the words of the Tur. So too wrote the Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah above, siman 591, os 2. Since the Shulchan Aruch did not mention above, in siman 591, the order of blasts sounded during the blessings in the silent prayer, as he mentioned in this siman regarding the repetition of Mussaf, infer from this that they did not sound the shofar during the silent Mussaf as we do. He wrote that this is also implied by the silence of virtually all the poskim, who mention blasts during the repetition but not during the silent Mussaf. It is also implied by the Mishnah and Gemara in the second chapter of Rosh Hashanah, 32b, see there. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur, wrote that some are accustomed to sound the shofar during the silent prayer, but that the more correct custom is not to do so, for several reasons, see there. The Magen Avraham cited his words at the beginning of the siman. However, the Aruch wrote: "Those who are stringent sound 30 while seated, 30 during the silent prayer, and 30 during the ordered blessings, corresponding to the 100 cries uttered by Sisera's mother. The remaining 10 are sounded when the entire prayer is completed, for the blasts of individuals must total 10, namely tashrat, tashat, and tarat, and together they are 100," end quote. Tosafos, Rosh Hashanah 33b, the Tur, and the Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah there cited his words. The Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote there that Sefer HaManhig also cited the words of the Aruch and wrote that this was their custom, see there. The Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah further sought to infer from responsa Rashba, siman 295, that their custom was likewise to sound the shofar during the silent prayer. He also cited the aforementioned words of Knesset HaGedolah, and concluded that although this custom of sounding the shofar during the silent prayer is not universally accepted, for each river follows its own course, nevertheless, one should not abolish the custom in a place where it is practiced, and one who abolishes the custom acts astonishingly, see there. We have already written above, siman 585, os 28, that according to the words of the Arizal the shofar must be sounded during both the silent prayer and the repetition. This is the custom of Yeshivas Beit El, may it be firmly established, in our holy city of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established speedily in our days, whose members follow the teachings of the Arizal, see there. Therefore, the custom has now spread in many places to sound the shofar during the silent prayer and the repetition, in accordance with the Aruch, Sefer HaManhig, and the Arizal.
2) When they sound the shofar during the silent prayer, they also say "Hayom Haras Olam" in the silent prayer. Meaning, even according to the Ashkenazic custom not to say "Hayom Haras Olam" during the silent prayer, as written above, siman 591, os 33, and below, os 7, in a place where the custom is to sound the shofar during the silent prayer, they do say it. However, they do not say "Areshet" during the silent prayer. Individuals who have reached the end of the Malchuyos section wait until the shaliach tzibbur reaches that point and sounds the shofar, and similarly for the other sections. If some individuals pray more slowly than the shaliach tzibbur and have not yet reached the end of the section when he begins sounding the shofar, they should remain silent and direct their ears to the blasts of the shaliach tzibbur. They should not say "Hayom Haras Olam" at the point where they are standing, but only when they reach the conclusion of that section's blessing. If the shaliach tzibbur errs in sounding the shofar, it is forbidden for them to speak or rebuke him. Even if they did not hear the blasts sounded while seated, after the prayer they should repeat only the series in which he erred, in the manner explained in siman 590, and that is sufficient. Mateh Ephraim, siman 591, os 13. See above, siman 591, os 6.
3) Ibid. They sound the shofar during the order of the blessings, etc. The shofar blower and the listeners must stand. Levush, siman 585, seif 1; Magen Avraham, beginning of this siman; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2; Mishna Berurah, os 2. After the fact, if he sounded or heard while seated, he has fulfilled his obligation. Eshel Avraham, beginning of the siman; Mishna Berurah, ibid. We have already written concerning this above, siman 585, os 2 and os 5; take it from there.
4) Ibid. For Malchuyos, tashrat once; for Zichronos, tashat, etc. So wrote the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. The Rif and Rosh wrote there that strictly speaking they should sound during the order of the blessings in the same sequence as they sounded while seated. However, since the blessings are not indispensable to the blasts, and they already fulfilled their obligation through those sounded while seated, it is sufficient to sound tashrat, tashat, and tarat once during the order of the blessings, so as not to burden the congregation. This is the custom throughout the world. However, the Rosh wrote there that Rabbeinu Tam wondered at this custom, namely, tashrat for Malchuyos and tashat for Zichronos, etc. For if the proper teruah is groaning followed by wailing, all should be tashrat; if it is groaning alone, all should be tashat, etc. Rabbeinu Tam therefore instituted sounding tashrat for Zichronos and Shofaros as for Malchuyos. In this manner one emerges from all doubts, and there is only a possible interruption. In order not to alter the custom too greatly, we are not concerned for the interruption, for we rule that one who heard nine blasts over nine hours of the day has fulfilled his obligation, see there. The Tur and Beit Yosef cited his words. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch ruled without qualification in accordance with the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. The words of the Rama in the gloss follow Rabbeinu Tam's view, as stated. However, Rabbeinu Yeshayah the First in his manuscript rulings, the Riaz, the Radbaz, siman 29, and the Shelah agreed that the primary custom is to sound tashrat, tashat, and tarat in each section, see there. The Erekh HaShulchan, os 1, cited their words. This is also the view of the Aruch and Sefer HaManhig, as written above, os 1, see there. See further in the following os.
