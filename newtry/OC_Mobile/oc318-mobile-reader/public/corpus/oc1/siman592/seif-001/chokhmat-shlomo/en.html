Seif 1. The shaliach tzibbur repeats the prayer and they blow according to the order of the blessings, etc. Note: In our Talmud the reason is explicit for the second set of blasts: they taught this because of a governmental decree. See the Yerushalmi, which Tosafos there cite in another wording. The Yerushalmi asks: should it be so with Hallel as well? It answers: not all the people are there. It then says: with the shofar blast too, not all the people are there. Rabbi Yonah said: It is written, 'They seek Me day by day'; this refers to shofar-blowing and the aravah, end quote. This is astonishing and not understandable at all; see Yefeh Mareh, who was greatly pressed there. In my humble opinion one may explain it in this way. Rashi in Pesachim, folio 58, s.v. 'Rabbi Yehoshua holds,' explains that the regular time for the Mussaf offerings is at six hours, for regarding them it is written 'day' and it is not written 'morning,' as it is written, 'the matter of each day on its day.' And we hold, according to the order of the day in the chapter 'The appointed officer said to them,' that 'morning' means earlier and 'day' means later; see there. If so, it is difficult: why did the Talmud need the reason of the decree, and why did it not say the reason is because it is written 'a day of teruah,' and 'day' means later, so it belongs in Mussaf, just as with the Mussaf offerings themselves, where because it says 'on the day' their time is at six hours? However, it seems that this is proper, for one who examines chapter 3 of Megillah, regarding 'the entire day is valid,' etc., asks: From where do we know this for shofar-blowing? Because it is written, 'it shall be a day of teruah for you.' If so, it implies that without that verse it would not be valid the whole day. Therefore there is no proof from the verse 'a day of teruah' regarding the ab initio time, for one can say that the verse was needed for after the fact, that if he transgressed and delayed, it is valid the whole day; but ab initio one could say that its mitzvah is in the morning. Therefore the reason of the decree was needed. But it is difficult: if so, how did Rashi write that Mussaf is therefore at six hours because it is written 'the matter of each day on its day'? There too, regarding Mussaf, they also derive from that verse, 'the matter of each day on its day,' that it is valid the whole day. If so, let us also say that 'the matter of each day on its day' refers to after the fact, and from where do we know that even ab initio it is later? However, the answer is that with 'a day of teruah' the word 'day' is written only once; therefore one may say that it comes for after the fact. But with Mussaf, where it is written 'the matter of each day on its day,' 'day' is written twice, so the verse proves that even ab initio it should be later. With this the words of the Yerushalmi are understandable. It too found it difficult: why do they blow during Mussaf? It did not wish to learn from the verse 'a day of teruah' that it implies lateness, because one can say the verse comes for after the fact. Therefore it answered based on the decree. But Rabbi Yonah answered that since there is another verse, 'They seek Me day by day,' there is therefore another 'day' regarding teruah; therefore it again resembles the Mussaf offerings, where it is written 'day on its day,' and it implies that ab initio they are delayed until Mussaf. Only, lest you ask that if so there are three instances of 'day' regarding teruah, and if you expound all of them, why do I need the third 'day'? Therefore he said that the second occurrence of 'day' comes to include the day of aravah, leaving two occurrences of 'day' regarding teruah: one for after the fact, and the second even for ab initio. Accordingly, one may explain what is written in the verse, 'It shall be a day of teruah for you,' according to what I wrote in my Torah novellae this year, Parashas Nitzavim, that the reason we blow during Mussaf and not during Shacharis is as follows. Although throughout the year Yisrael is judged first, before the nations of the world, before anger increases, nevertheless on Rosh Hashanah the nations of the world are judged first, so that the Satan will already be confused and will not prosecute Yisrael. Therefore the nations of the world are judged first, so that the Satan is still confused. If so, the main point of the shofar blasts being delayed is only so that the blasts remain only for us and the nations of the world have no share in them. This is what the verse says: 'It shall be a day of teruah for you' - that the teruah is done later in the day, implying later and not in the morning, so that the day of teruah will be 'for you' and not for the nations of the world, just as we expound 'that which may be eaten by every soul, that alone may be done for you' as 'for you' and not for idolaters. So too here, 'a day of teruah,' implying delay, 'shall be for you' and not for idolaters, so that idolaters will not have a share in it. This is proper. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, who wrote in the name of Baal Halachos Gedolos that some blow during the silent prayer and one should not follow this practice ab initio. See my novellae to the laws of Rosh Hashanah, siman 584, which is in my work on Orach Chaim from the year 599, where we proved that there is such a dispute in the Yerushalmi, and there I ruled halachically that one should not blow during the silent prayer, but only during the repetition, and that it has a source in the Yerushalmi and in verses; see there. The custom of Yisrael is Torah. There in the gloss: They are accustomed to say each time after they blow, 'Today the world was conceived' and 'Areshes,' and even on Shabbos, etc. Note: As for their custom to say it specifically after they blow, one can explain it by understanding the statement 'Today the world was conceived.' Tosafos already noted the difficulty that we hold that the world was created in Nissan. It seems that the meaning is as follows. We hold that the final act is first in thought, and repentance preceded the creation of the world. That is, the world would not have been fit to be created if, Heaven forbid, repentance were not effective; only because the thought of repentance preceded it was the world created. The time prepared for repentance is Tishrei. Therefore he says, 'Today the world was conceived,' for it is considered as though it were now created; without this day, when repentance is effective, it would not have been created. Only because the Holy One, blessed is He, foresaw this day did He create it, and therefore it is considered as though it were created today. This is connected to the shofar, because the shofar alludes to repentance, as the Rambam of blessed memory wrote. Therefore, once they blew the shofar, which alludes to the fact that repentance is effective, one can then say, 'Today the world was conceived.' One can say that this is the dispute between Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua, whether the world was created in Nissan or in Tishrei. How could one think that they disagree about a fact? Rather, it seems that they agree about the actual time of its creation, as we say in prayer, 'Today the world was conceived.' They disagree about what is considered the main creation, and their dispute follows the dispute in Perek Chelek whether a baal teshuvah is superior or a completely righteous person is superior. Nissan indicates a completely righteous person, for the commentators wrote that matzah indicates a righteous person, while chametz indicates a wicked person, 'the abomination of Hashem is every haughty heart.' Nissan therefore indicates a completely righteous person, for there never was chametz. Tishrei indicates a baal teshuvah. Therefore the one who says that the world was created in Nissan holds that a completely righteous person is superior, and the purpose of creation is for Nissan; this is what he means by saying 'in Nissan the world was created,' that it was created for the sake of Nissan. The other opinion says that a baal teshuvah is superior, and therefore it was in Tishrei; this is what he means by saying 'for the sake of Tishrei the world was created.' They disagree about what is the purpose of creation. This is proper; analyze it. See Magen Avraham, who brought the words of Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah that in Tishrei the world was created only in thought and was not created until Nissan. See my sermons from the year 609, in my work Koheles Yaakov, in the sermon for Parashas HaChodesh, part 2, where I wrote the opposite, because interpreting 'this is the beginning of Your works' according to them is forced, and it also contradicts the words of the Talmud, which says that this follows Rabbi Eliezer; see there what I wrote to the contrary, and analyze it. Now, the custom in some places is that when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbos, they do not fall and bow during Aleinu, but on Yom Kippur they do fall. There are some who even on Yom Kippur fall during the Avodah, but during Aleinu they do not fall. At first glance this has no reason at all: what connection is there between falling and bowing in Aleinu and a distinction between Shabbos and weekday? But after I settled the matter in my mind, I said: the custom of Yisrael is Torah, and this is the proper practice; it has two reasons. The main reason that on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur we fall on our faces, unlike the rest of the year, is so that we not be like those who speak falsely before Him, blessed is He, saying 'and we bow' while not doing so. During the silent prayer this cannot be done, for it would be an interruption in the prayer and they would need to leave their places. Therefore they instituted that they fall during the shaliach tzibbur repetition, and at least it will not appear as falsehood, since they do it once. Throughout the year we are not particular about this, but on Rosh Hashanah, when the judgment is severe, without giving the Satan an opening of the mouth, just as we must be precise in the text of the prayer and are more careful about this on Rosh Hashanah than during the rest of the year, so too with this. This works when it falls on a weekday, when the Satan has permission to prosecute and his power is strong then; therefore extra care is needed in this. But on Shabbos, since we do not blow the shofar, for how could Chazal not be concerned to confuse the Satan? It must be that on Shabbos, without anything else, his power is not strong and he does not have the ability to prosecute, as the Yaaros Devash also wrote, and as I wrote in a sermon for the year 598. Therefore one need not be careful about this more than during the rest of the year. But on Yom Kippur, regarding the Avodah, there they truly bowed, and not because of the concern that it appears false; therefore what difference is there to me whether it is Shabbos or weekday? Just as they did then, we also do now. But Aleinu, where throughout the year we do not fall and only on Rosh Hashanah do so because of the concern that it appears false, on Shabbos we are not particular about this. Another reason for the matter may be said. Bowing and prostration are the level of a servant. Even if a king son bows to his father, it is only a slight bow, not kneeling all the way to the ground; therefore this is the level of servitude. Throughout the weekdays we are in the level of servants to the Omnipresent, but on Shabbos we are in the level of children. If we were in the level of servants, we could not observe Shabbos, for a non-Jew who rests on Shabbos is liable for death, based on what is written in the Midrash with the parable of a king and matron: if a servant inserted his head between them, he is liable for death, but the son may insert his head between them. Therefore, from the fact that we observe Shabbos, it is proven that we are in the level of children. For this reason one should not then fall in bowing, for it would be two contradictory matters. On Yom Kippur, since they bowed then, we disregard this; and especially on Yom Kippur, there is no concern if we portray ourselves as servants by way of humility. Also, since on Yom Kippur they truly bowed then, we are not now bowing for ourselves, but only making a remembrance of the Mikdash. Therefore what difference is there to me whether it is Shabbos or weekday? We make a remembrance of the Mikdash, of what they did then; and they, because they heard the explicit Name, were obligated to bow. But Aleinu is not a remembrance of the Mikdash, and we bow for ourselves; in this there is a distinction between Shabbos and weekday. On Rosh Hashanah, since we blow the shofar and are concerned to confuse the Satan, it must be that on a weekday we are in the level of servants. The prosecutor is not afraid to prosecute a servant and to persist in his prosecution; therefore above as well the Satan prosecutes greatly, and we need the shofar to confuse the Satan. But on Shabbos we are in the level of children, for otherwise we would not observe Shabbos, as explained above. The prosecutor is afraid to inform extensively against the son, lest his father be angry with him; therefore the Satan does not prosecute so much, and we do not need shofar. If so, if we fall in bowing, it appears like the level of servitude, and there will be two contradictory matters, giving the Satan an opening of the mouth to strengthen himself. Therefore we do not fall. However, the shaliach tzibbur alone falls so that it should not be like speaking falsehood; nevertheless there is no concern for the prosecution of the Satan, since the entire congregation does not fall. This seems correct in my humble opinion as the reason for this custom, and analyze it.
