Seif 1, the shaliach tzibbur repeats, etc. And they blow the shofar. Rosh Hashanah 34b, according to the explanation of Rav Hai, that all of them are valid, whether shevarim or teruah. See Tosafos 33b, 'and it seems that there is some reason to give,' etc.; and see Ran.
For Malchiyos, etc. Rif and Tosafos. The matter is as Rav Hai wrote, that all of them are true as halacha. See the Rosh, and the Ran also wrote this. See Milchamos, who refuted the words of Rav Hai Gaon.
And now, etc. At first glance it would seem that for each one he should blow tashrat, tashat, tarat, as Tosafos wrote there, and so it is according to the basic law. But because Rabbi Yoel wrote that whenever he interrupted with another sound he lost everything, as written at the end of siman 590, this cannot be done; for if so, there would be tashat and tarat between the first tashrat and the second, and we hold that one must hear them in order, as I wrote above in siman 588, seif 2. This is the reason of the early authorities, who blew only one order and not three tashrat blasts, so that there should not be an interruption. The Tosafos cited above hold as the Ramban and as the Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 590, that he has not lost.
And some say, etc. This is how Rabbeinu Tam instituted it, following his view that another sound does not ruin the order as an interruption; only a delay is a concern ab initio. Although Rabbi Avahu instituted tashrat, tashat, tarat ab initio, after the fact one fulfills his obligation with tashrat, for we hold like Rabbi Yochanan that even over nine hours he fulfills his obligation. Rabbeinu Tam holds that the Gemara question, 'perhaps it is a wailing sound,' etc., is not because of an interruption, but because of an ab initio delay, for only after the fact did Rabbi Yochanan say that he fulfills his obligation; an interruption does not invalidate it. This is like the reading in the Tosefta, according to our text: he blew a tekiah, sounded a teruah, blew a tekiah, sounded a teruah, blew a tekiah, etc. This is what the Yerushalmi says, 'provided that he hears them in order,' excluding a case like this. But the Raavyah, the Ramban, and other poskim hold that it is because of an interruption. See Ran and Rosh. In any event, the practice of Rabbeinu Tam is more correct, because the sets do not contradict one another, unlike the practice of the Rif. See the Ran and Rabbeinu Tam, etc., who said: It is better that we be concerned for the uncertainties and not be concerned for interruptions.
