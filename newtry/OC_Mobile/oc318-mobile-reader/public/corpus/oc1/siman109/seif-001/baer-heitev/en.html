For Kaddish. Meaning, until the chazzan reaches yehei shemei rabba, as stated by Mordechai and Agudah. This implies that he may continue praying until the congregation answers yehei shemei rabba, but he should not answer Amen, since he did not direct his attention to that which the Amen addresses, and it would therefore be an orphaned Amen. If one comes to the synagogue immediately after Kedushah, and if he waits until after Modim he will be unable to answer yehei shemei rabba after the Kaddish following the Amidah, while if he waits until after Kaddish the time for prayer will pass or he will be unable to pray Ma'ariv with the congregation, it appears to me that he should begin immediately. It is preferable to forgo Modim rather than yehei shemei rabba, as written in siman 56 in the name of Beit Shmuel. Moreover, he can bow in the middle of a blessing for Modim. If he arrives close to Kaddish, and by praying immediately he will lose yehei shemei rabba, while by waiting until after Kaddish he will be unable to pray Ma'ariv with the congregation, communal prayer is preferable. If he arrives close to Kedushah, and by beginning immediately he will be unable to recite Kedushah, while by beginning after Kedushah he will be unable to recite Modim, and by waiting until after Modim the time for prayer will pass, it appears to me that he should begin after Kedushah. It is preferable to recite Kedushah with the congregation, and Modim can be fulfilled by bowing in the middle of a blessing. Magen Avraham.
For Modim. According to the Rama, he must finish before the Amen following Shome'a Tefillah. Otherwise, he should not begin until after Modim. Magen Avraham.
For prayer. Meaning, he erred and continued until Ga'al Yisrael. Ideally, he should wait at Shirah Chadashah, as stated in siman 66.
He should bow with them. Ideally, he should not begin with the intention of bowing in the middle of a blessing, lest he forget to bow, since he is not independently obligated to bow there. Darchei Moshe; Magen Avraham.
