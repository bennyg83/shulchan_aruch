(1) One who enters the synagogue — and if he wishes to go out to pray in the courtyard outside the synagogue, he prays and need not wait.

(2) "The congregation is praying..." — this seif deals with Minchah prayer, and the same applies at Shacharit if he was standing in Shema and its blessings at the time the congregation began Shemoneh Esrei: if he can begin and finish the prayer [until "Elohai Netzor"] before the prayer leader reaches Kedushah, he should pray; and if not, he should not pray, but rather wait in Shirah Chadashah. And see what we write below in the name of Chayei Adam on this matter.

(3) "The prayer leader reaches Kedushah" — for Kedushah is forbidden to recite alone, and similarly Kaddish and Modim; the reason is that one must bow with the congregation so that one should not appear as a heretic toward One to Whom the congregation bows.

(4) "Or for Kaddish" — it would seem that since he must finish his prayer before Kedushah, all the more so he must hear the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei; one can say this deals with Maariv where there is no Kedushah. Alternatively, it deals with when he already heard Kedushah, that he need not wait on account of Kedushah. And as we will write below, nevertheless he must finish before the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei — that is, he must finish at least before the prayer leader reaches "Yehai Shmei Rabba," and he should not answer Amen then, but only respond "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." in his heart, because since he does not intend then for what to say, the Amen is called an orphaned Amen; and if he gives his heart and knows then for what he answers Amen, he is permitted. And know that "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." is preferable to Kedushah; therefore if he arrives near Kedushah, and if he waits until after Kedushah he will not finish his prayer until after Kaddish, and he cannot wait until after Kaddish because the time for prayer is passing, it is better that he begin praying before Kedushah and be able to answer "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." Kedushah is preferable to Modim; therefore if he arrives near Kedushah, and if he begins immediately he will not be able to say Kedushah, and if he waits until after Kedushah he will not be able to say Modim with the congregation, and he cannot wait until after Modim because of concern that the time for prayer will pass — it is better that he begin praying after Kedushah, so too Magen Avraham. And according to what we explained below in comment 14 in the name of Peri Megadim, it is better in this case to begin praying together with the prayer leader, and he will have Kedushah and Modim; and all the more so that "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." is preferable to Modim. Therefore one who comes to the synagogue immediately after Kedushah — if he waits until after Modim he will not be able to answer "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." after the Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei, and if he waits until after the Kaddish the time for prayer will pass — he should begin immediately, for it is better to forfeit Modim than to forfeit "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..."; and furthermore, Modim can be bowed in the middle of it. And similarly "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." is preferable to praying with the congregation; therefore if he arrives near Kaddish, and if he prays immediately he will forfeit "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..."; and if he waits until after Kaddish he will not be able to pray Maariv with the congregation — "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." is preferable; and immediately after this he should begin Shemoneh Esrei for Minchah, and he need not be concerned that the congregation is beginning Maariv since it is still day. And see what we wrote further in Biur Halacha in the name of Peri Megadim.

(5) "And the same applies to Amen..." — it would seem that, granted, regarding Amen of "Who Hears Prayer" there is a difference that he would need to wait from praying even after Kedushah until he knows he will finish before the blessing of "Who Hears Prayer"; but regarding Amen of "The Holy God," for what difference does it make? Since he must finish praying before Kedushah, automatically he will answer Amen after "The Holy God." One can say there is a difference even if he heard Kedushah, that nevertheless he must wait on account of Amen of "The Holy God" that he has not yet heard. And also regarding not standing to pray immediately after Kedushah, but rather after he answers Amen on the blessing — for this too applies to Kedushah. And the same if he knows the congregation will say Barchu and he has not yet heard — if he can finish his prayer before the congregation says Barchu, he prays as usual; and if he knows he will not be able to finish his prayer before Barchu, he must wait in Shirah Chadashah until he hears Barchu. And if he already heard Kedushah or Barchu, or knows he will hear afterward, he need not wait. However, for Modim, even if he heard Modim many times with the congregation, he must wait, for the reason is so he should not appear as a heretic if he does not bow [achronim]. And similarly for Kaddish he must wait, for it has no fixed measure. And it is possible that the Kaddishim from "Titbarach" onward are not included in this, so too Magen Avraham and other authorities; and Darkhei Chayyim and Chayei Adam wrote that for all Kaddishim, if he already heard and answered "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." he need not wait again.

(6) "After Kedushah" — not specifically; the same applies if he entered before Kedushah and waited from praying until after Kedushah — he must also estimate for himself whether he can finish before the prayer leader reaches Modim.

(7) "The prayer leader reaches Modim" — and according to Rama above in his gloss, he must finish before Amen of "Who Hears Prayer," or estimate for himself that he can reach in his prayer the blessing of "Who Hears Prayer" together with the prayer leader; and if not, he should not begin until Modim. And so the later authorities ruled for practical law.

(8) "That they bow in them..." — even though he cannot say Modim Derabbanan that the congregation says, it is of no consequence since he bows with them, and this is permitted even l'chatchila; but he should shorten so that the blessing "Who Hears Prayer" he says together with the prayer leader, as below in seif 2.

(9) "In order to juxtapose" — meaning he erred and said through "Ga'al Yisrael," from which he is obligated to juxtapose to it the prayer immediately; and the same if because the hour is passing he is obligated to stand immediately to pray, for otherwise he would need l'chatchila to wait on account of Kedushah in Shirah Chadashah, as written in siman 66 seif 9.

(10) "He should bow with him" — and he does not then say Modim; and the bowing is so he should not appear as a heretic toward One to Whom the congregation bows. Nevertheless, if he is not compelled to begin immediately, it was not permitted for him to begin the prayer on condition that he bow in the middle of the blessings, even though it is permitted to bow in the middle of all blessings, as below in siman 113, because perhaps he will forget to bow since he is not obligated there to bow; and if he gives his heart to this he will be troubled and not concentrate in his prayer.
