The chazzan reaches Kedushah. Kedushah may not be recited by an individual, and the same applies to Kaddish. Regarding Modim, the reason is that one must bow with the congregation so that he not appear to deny the One before whom they bow.
When he is in the middle of one of the blessings. In siman 113, Beit Yosef cites Mahari Abuhav's question: why does the Tur write there that one may bow in the middle of a blessing in every case, while here he permits it only when one must join redemption to prayer? He gives one answer, and Beit Yosef gives another. It appears to me that there is no difficulty. If one wishes to bow in the middle because he considers bowing appropriate at that point, it is permitted in every case, since he is in the middle and there is no interruption. Here, however, he must bow only because the congregation is bowing, not because of the prayer itself. That is considered somewhat of an interruption, and therefore one should not rely upon it ideally. This appears correct to me.
