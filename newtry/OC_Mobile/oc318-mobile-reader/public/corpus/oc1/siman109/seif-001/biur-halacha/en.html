One who enters the synagogue and finds a congregation, etc.—I was in doubt: if by nature he lengthens in prayer and cannot finish before Kedushah, is he permitted to begin? From this there is no proof to prohibition, for perhaps this is not called complete public prayer since he did not begin with them equally, as Pri Megadim wrote; but if he begins equally, perhaps since the law applies to him to pray with the congregation and the obligation to answer Kedushah for now is not upon him, he need not be concerned at all that afterward he will not be able to fulfill a mitzvah that is more important than this; and also that then he will be compelled and exempt from answering. Nevertheless one should not bring a proof to be lenient from what the Gemara stated "and finds a congregation praying" and did not state a greater matter—the case we depicted—for perhaps the Gemara stated a common matter; and one must examine the general rule: we do not pass over mitzvos.

For Kedushah or Kaddish—see Pri Megadim, who considers the order of rankings which is preferable to its fellow, and practical difference whether one hears both as one in two minyanim which is first, or if one cannot fulfill all of them; and he wrote there their order thus: Amen yehei shemei rabba—for Kaddish is preferable to Kedushah, and Kedushah preferable to Modim; and Modim, and also Barekhu, preferable to the two Amens, namely HaEil HaKadosh and Shomeah Tefillah; and these Amens preferable to others. But he is uncertain there regarding Modim and Barekhu which is preferable, and leans there that Modim is preferable [and Biur Halacha wrote that sometimes Barekhu is preferable to Modim, for regarding Modim one can intend to arrive in the middle of a blessing and skip—see there]. Further he wrote there that answering these two Amens is preferable to prayer with the congregation, for we push him from praying with the congregation on account of answering these Amens [but he wrote there in one comment that this congregation is inferior in part to others because he came in the middle]. And juxtaposing Geulah to prayer in the morning is preferable even to Amen yehei shemei rabba; and know, for one does not answer Kaddish and Kedushah between Geulah and prayer, as above siman 66 note 9. And if it happened that he should answer Modim after he said Ga'al Yisrael, he should skip and not say anything; and at night even prayer with the congregation is preferable to juxtaposing Geulah to prayer, as below siman 236 in Magen Avraham note 3.
