One who enters the synagogue and finds the congregation, etc. I am uncertain regarding one who naturally prays at length and cannot finish before Kedushah, whether he may begin with the congregation. No proof to prohibit this can be brought from here, because perhaps beginning after the congregation has already started is not considered full communal prayer, since he did not begin together with them, as Peri Megadim writes. If, however, he begins at the same time as they do, perhaps the present obligation of communal prayer rests upon him, while the obligation to answer Kedushah has not yet arisen. He should therefore not be concerned that later he will be unable to fulfill another, more important mitzvah, especially since he will then be unavoidably prevented and exempt from answering. Nevertheless, one cannot prove leniency from the Gemara's wording, which speaks of finding the congregation already praying rather than presenting our more novel case, because the Gemara may simply have discussed the more common situation. The general rule of not passing over mitzvos requires study in this context.
For Kedushah or for Kaddish. See Peri Megadim, who lists the hierarchy of these responses. This is relevant when one hears two of them simultaneously from two minyanim, or cannot fulfill them all. He orders them as follows: yehei shemei rabba of Kaddish is preferable to Kedushah; Kedushah is preferable to Modim; Modim and Barechu are preferable to the two Amens after HaKel HaKadosh and Shome'a Tefillah; and these two Amens are preferable to other Amens. He is uncertain whether Modim or Barechu takes precedence, but inclines to Modim. Derech HaChaim writes that Barechu is sometimes preferable, since for Modim one may arrange to reach the middle of a blessing and bow. Peri Megadim further writes that these two Amens take precedence over communal prayer, since one delays praying with the congregation in order to answer them, although he notes in Eshel Avraham that the communal prayer in this case is somewhat deficient because one arrived in the middle. Joining redemption to prayer at Shacharis takes precedence even over yehei shemei rabba, as shown by the prohibition against answering Kaddish or Kedushah between redemption and prayer in siman 66, seif 9. If Modim occurs after one has said Ga'al Yisrael, he should bow without saying anything. At night, however, communal prayer is preferable even to joining redemption to prayer, as stated below in siman 236, Magen Avraham, seif katan 3.
