(Siman kaf alef) Or to Kaddish — meaning Kaddish, etc. This was difficult for Magen Avraham: since one must finish one's prayer before Kedushah and hear Kedushah, all the more so one should hear the Kaddish after [the eighteen blessings]. And why did [the Mechaber] write "or to Kaddish"? Meaning: if one came after Kedushah and also after Modim, or before Modim, and knows that he too will reach Modim together with the shaliach tzibur — nevertheless he must finish before the Kaddish after [the eighteen blessings]; for from this angle we do not deal thus, but rather afterwards. But here we deal with one who entered before Kedushah; therefore [the Mechaber] had to say that what he wrote "or to Kaddish" refers to Maariv, where there is no Kedushah — nevertheless one must finish before the Kaddish after [the eighteen blessings]. However, what Rama wrote — "and the same [applies to] Amen of HaEl HaKadosh" — requires explanation: how do we find this? Since one must finish before Kedushah, automatically he will answer Amen after HaEl HaKadosh. And it must be said that there is a difference from what is written in seif 2: if he began praying together with the shaliach tzibur, the law is that he says together with the shaliach tzibur until the end of Kedushah; and one might think that with this it suffices, and after Kedushah he may pray on his own — Rama teaches us that he must also say "l'dor vador," etc., until HaEl HaKadosh together with the shaliach tzibur word for word, because Amen of HaEl HaKadosh is also the law of Kedushah, one could say. Therefore if he says the end of the blessing together with the shaliach tzibur word for word, then he is not permitted to answer Amen — for he would be answering Amen to his own blessings and would fulfill [his obligation] thereby; and so is explicit in Tosafot there. And the same [applies to] what Magen Avraham explained: that what [the Mechaber] wrote "or to Kaddish" refers to one who already heard Kedushah but has not yet heard Kaddish — for because of Kedushah one need not wait, as Magen Avraham wrote siman 3. But since nowadays this was not mentioned in the explanation in Shulchan Aruch — that if one already heard Kedushah one need not wait — even though the law is true, nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote a simpler comment.
