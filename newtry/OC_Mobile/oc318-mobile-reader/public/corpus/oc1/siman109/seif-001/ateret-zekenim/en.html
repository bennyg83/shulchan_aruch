One who enters the synagogue, etc.—and if he finds a congregation that has already begun answering Kedushah or Amen yehei shemei rabba, whoever has not finished [his Amidah] may answer Amen with them (Responsa of Maharar Ma..., siman 102).

The same law applies to Amen to HaEil HaKadosh and Shomeah Tefillah; we judge them like Kaddish and Kedushah, for one must answer Amen after HaEil HaKadosh and after Shomeah Tefillah. And ab initio one must answer Amen after each and every blessing; therefore it is better that one not occupy oneself with anything during the prayer leader's repetition and that one intend to answer Amen (Responsa of Maharar Ma..., siman 102). And if the time is passing, let him pray word by word with the prayer leader; and the same if he can reach Modim. The reason is that we are concerned lest he not be able to compress [his prayer] so that he be in the middle of the blessing when the prayer leader reaches Modim, but rather at the beginning of a blessing in which one does not bow, or at its end (Beit Yosef siman 113). Or one can say the reason: because in this bowing of permission he does not exempt ab initio the bowing of Modim, which is of a mitzvah; therefore he should not begin praying ab initio unless he knows he can reach Modim or one of the blessings in which one bows when the prayer leader reaches Modim—for then this bowing of a mitzvah also exempts the bowing of Modim (Ramaz). And some say that even if he reaches [Modim] with the prayer leader, it is forbidden for one who began [late], because he cannot say Modim deRabbanan (Or Zarua).
