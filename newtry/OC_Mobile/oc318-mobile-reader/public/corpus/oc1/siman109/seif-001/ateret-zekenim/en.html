One who enters the synagogue, etc. If he finds the congregation already beginning to answer Kedushah or Amen, yehei shemei rabba, so long as they have not completed it, he may answer Amen with them. (Responsa of Rabbi Menachem Azariah, siman 102.)
The same applies to Amen after HaKel HaKadosh and Shome'a Tefillah, whose law is like Kaddish and Kedushah, because one must answer Amen after HaKel HaKadosh and after Shome'a Tefillah. Ideally, one should answer Amen after every blessing. Therefore, it is proper not to occupy oneself with anything during the repetition of the Amidah, but to concentrate on answering Amen. (Responsa of Rabbi Menachem Azariah, siman 102.) If the time for prayer is passing, he should pray word for word with the chazzan. The same applies if he can reach Modim together with him. The reason is that we are concerned that he may not calculate precisely enough to be in the middle of a blessing when the chazzan reaches Modim, but instead may be at the beginning or end of a blessing where one does not bow. (Beit Yosef, siman 113.) Alternatively, the reason is that this optional bowing should not, ideally, replace the obligatory bowing of Modim. Therefore, he should not begin unless he knows that when the chazzan reaches Modim he can himself reach Modim or one of the blessings in which one bows. In that case, his bowing is also obligatory and may replace the bowing of Modim. (Ramah MiPano.) Some write that even if he reaches Modim together with the chazzan, he should not begin, because he will be unable to recite Modim D'Rabbanan. (Eliyah Zuta.)
