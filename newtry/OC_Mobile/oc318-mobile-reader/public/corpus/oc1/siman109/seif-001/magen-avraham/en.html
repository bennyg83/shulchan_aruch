Or for Kaddish. Meaning, the Kaddish after the prayer, such as at Ma'ariv, unlike Olas Tamid.
For Kaddish. Meaning, until the chazzan reaches yehei shemei rabba, as stated by Mordechai and Agudah. This implies that he may continue praying until the congregation answers yehei shemei rabba, but he should not answer Amen, because he did not direct his attention to that which the Amen addresses, and it would be an orphaned Amen, as stated in siman 124. See the gloss at the end of siman 56. If one comes to the synagogue immediately after Kedushah, and if he waits until after Modim he will be unable to answer yehei shemei rabba after the Kaddish following the Amidah, while if he waits until after Kaddish the time for prayer will pass or he will be unable to pray Ma'ariv with the congregation, it appears to me that he should begin immediately. It is preferable to forgo Modim rather than yehei shemei rabba, as stated in siman 56 in the name of Beit Shmuel. Moreover, he can bow in the middle of a blessing for Modim. If he arrives close to Kaddish, and by praying immediately he will lose yehei shemei rabba, while by waiting until after Kaddish he will be unable to pray Ma'ariv with the congregation, communal prayer is preferable. If he arrives close to Kedushah, and by beginning immediately he will be unable to recite Kedushah, while by beginning after Kedushah he will be unable to recite Modim, and by waiting until after Modim the time for prayer will pass, it appears to me that he should begin after Kedushah. It is preferable to recite Kedushah with the congregation. Although the Amoraim dispute Kedushah while all agree regarding Modim, that is because they maintain that an individual may recite Kedushah. According to our ruling that an individual may not recite Kedushah, Kedushah is preferable. Moreover, one may bow in the middle of a blessing for Modim.
The same applies to Amen. It appears to me that the same applies to Barechu, since an individual does not recite Barechu. If he has already heard Kedushah or Barechu, or knows he will hear it later, he need not wait, for according to the view that an individual may recite Kedushah he need not wait. For Kaddish, however, he must wait, since there is no fixed limit. It is possible that the Kaddish recited from Aleinu onward is not included.
Before the chazzan reaches Modim. According to the Rama, he must finish before the Amen following Shome'a Tefillah. Otherwise, he should not begin until after Modim. See seif 2.
In order to join. Meaning, he erred and said Ga'al Yisrael. Ideally, he should have waited at Shirah Chadashah, as stated in siman 66.
In the middle he should bow with them. Ideally, however, he should not begin intending to bow in the middle of a blessing, lest he forget to bow, since he is not independently obligated to bow there. Darchei Moshe, siman 113. See Beit Yosef there for further answers.
