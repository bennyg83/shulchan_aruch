Reaches. See Taz. Berachos 21b teaches that Kedushah and Kaddish may not be recited by an individual, while Modim is required so that he not appear to deny. Communal prayer is highly important. At Minchah, he may recite Ashrei afterward. See Magen Avraham 108:5. At Ma'ariv, even if he has not yet recited Krias Shema, since joining redemption to prayer at night is not as obligatory, he should pray with the congregation if he can finish before the Kaddish and yehei shemei rabba. See Magen Avraham 236:3. At Shacharis, if he has already recited Krias Shema and comes to the synagogue, he should pray if he can finish before Kedushah or Kaddish, according to our custom of reciting a responsive Kedushah after the silent Amidah. See siman 69. The Rama's reference to Amen after HaKel HaKadosh concerns one who arrived after Kedushah. The Yerushalmi, however, implies that he should not begin merely because he can finish in time. This is somewhat difficult, for he should already be prevented by Kedushah. It is strained to say that Kedushah has the solution of praying word for word with the chazzan as in seif 2, whereas Amen does not, corresponding to Terumas HaDeshen's question in siman 11. We shall discuss this below, with Hashem's help. I have seen fit to preface several matters here.
Know that the hierarchy is: yehei shemei rabba of Kaddish; Kedushah; Modim; Barechu; the two Amens; and other Amens. Yehei shemei rabba is preferable to all, as is evident from Berachos 21b, where the Gemara asks whether one interrupts for it. See Kesef Mishneh, chapter 10 of Hilchos Tefillah, Taz, and Peri Chadash here. Do not challenge this from siman 66, seifim 7 and 9. The Rama is silent in seif 9, while in seif 7 he glosses that one interrupts for Amen, even though Amen is the least of them. One may answer that Amen after Ga'al Yisrael is treated as part of the blessing and is therefore not an interruption, as in siman 68, seif 3, whereas other Amens, even after HaKel HaKadosh, are not. Kedushah is preferable to Modim, for Rambam in chapter 10, halachah 16, omits Modim, apparently holding that Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi disputes Rav Huna. Thus, even according to our ruling, Kedushah is preferable to Modim. Modim is preferable to the two Amens, since the Tur and Mechaber omit those Amens. Barechu is preferable to the two Amens, since the Mechaber in siman 66, seif 3, mentions Barechu but omits them. The Rama, who adds them, certainly agrees that Barechu is preferable. This is what Magen Avraham means when he writes that the same applies to Barechu, and it is all the more so. See Eliyah Rabbah 7. I had thought that Modim is preferable to Barechu, since the Mechaber omits the two Amens and Barechu but retains Modim, implying that Modim is preferable. Yet the order in siman 66, Kaddish, Kedushah, Barechu, and likewise Modim, implies that Barechu is preferable. One may answer that Barechu is simply not relevant here. The practical difference is when one hears two responses simultaneously from two minyanim. At Shacharis, the order is: joining redemption to prayer; then Kaddish through the two Amens; then communal prayer, as shown in siman 66, seif 9, here in seif 1, and siman 111, seif 3. At Ma'ariv, the order is Kaddish through the two Amens; then communal prayer; then joining redemption to prayer, as in siman 236, Magen Avraham 3. It appears to me that between Ga'al Yisrael and Hashem sefasai tiftach he should bow for Modim, according to Rabbeinu Tam's practice, but say nothing. See Magen Avraham here. Rambam's statement, “and needless to say regarding the other blessings,” appears to refer to the two Amens. See Lechem Mishneh there. This will be explained further, with Hashem's help.
