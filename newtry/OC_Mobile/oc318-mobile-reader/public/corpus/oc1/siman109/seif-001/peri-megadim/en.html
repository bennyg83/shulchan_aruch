"When he reaches" — in comment on Berachos 21b: Kedushah and Kaddish an individual does not say, and Modim — so that he should not appear as a heretic. And behold communal prayer is very good; and at Minchah he says Ashrei afterward (Magen Avraham 108:5). And Maariv — even if he did not yet recite Shema, since at Maariv there is not so much mitzvah to juxtapose Geulah to prayer, he prays with them if he finishes before Kaddish "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." (Magen Avraham 236 note 3). And Shacharit — if he recited Shema and came to the synagogue, if he can finish before Kedushah or Kaddish (for our custom that they spread [the Amidah] after Chazaras Hashatz quietly) — see siman 69; and what Mishna Berurah [says] on Amen of "The Holy God" means if he came after Kedushah. But in Yerushalmi it implies he should not begin and finish (Beit Yosef) — it is somewhat difficult; let it be resolved on account of Kedushah, and it is strained to say Kedushah has a remedy — he says word by word as in seif 2, unlike Amen like the difficulty of Terumat HaDeshen siman 11; and as we need to say below with God's help. And behold I saw to precede here several matters:

Know that the order of ranks is: "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba...," Kaddish, Kedushah, Modim, Barchu, two Amens, and other Amens. Behold "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." is preferable to all of them, as proven in Berachos 21b that "Amen, yehei Shmei Rabba..." is discussed whether it interrupts; therefore Taz and Peri Chadash so ruled. And do not answer me from siman 66:7 and 69 that Mishna Berurah was silent to him in seif 9 and in 67 emended that Amen interrupts (and Amen is lightest of all) — one can say Amen after the blessing Ga'al Yisrael is akin to the blessing and is not an interruption, as siman 68:3 in the gloss (and other Amens, even of "The Holy Heaven," not). And Kedushah is preferable to Modim, for Rambam in the first chapter, Mahadura Basra, Taz omitted Modim (holding R' Yehudah disputes R' Chanina); if so, for us too Kedushah is preferable to Modim, and Modim is preferable to two Amens, for Tur and Mechaber omitted two Amens. And Barchu is preferable to two Amens, for Mechaber says so in siman 66 seif 3 — he omitted Amens; and Mishna Berurah who emended so too regarding two Amens, all the more Barchu; and so Magen Avraham note 3 — so it appears to me that Barchu too, and all the more it and Ateret Zekenim. And I thought Modim is preferable to Barchu, for Mechaber omitted two Amens (Bavli disputes, for it said until he reaches Modim and not "Who Hears Prayer"; one can say Modim even on Shabbat and Yom Tov) and omitted Barchu — evidently Modim is preferable to Barchu. And from the order of the wording in siman 66 — to Kaddish and Kedushah and Barchu and similarly Modim — implies Barchu is preferable; one can say here the wording is Barchu. And there is a practical difference in all this if hearing both as one in two minyanim — which comes first. And behold at Shacharit the ranks are thus: juxtapose Geulah to prayer, then Kaddish until two Amens, then communal prayer, for you see here siman 66:9 and here 61 and 111:3; but at Maariv the order is Kaddish until two Amens, then prayer with the congregation, then juxtapose Geulah, as siman 236 Magen Avraham 3. And it seems to me between Ga'al Yisrael and "Heaven, open my lips" he bows Modim per Rabbeinu Tam's custom (here in seif 1) and says nothing, and Magen Avraham on this.
