If he begins to pray, etc. See what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 14, in the name of Eliyah Rabbah, who maintains that this is permitted even ideally. If, however, he began praying before the chazzan and, because of his intense concentration or another reason, reached Kedushah together with the chazzan, or conversely began after the chazzan but reached Kedushah because the chazzan prolonged his prayer, Eliyah Rabbah implies that even he permits this only after the fact. It appears to me that the same applies when there is no other way for him to hear Kedushah.
But ideally, etc. See Magen Avraham, who explains that he must remain silent and listen, as written at the beginning of siman 125. Meaning, how could he himself recite Nakdishach? According to our custom, however, which follows the Taz below in siman 125 and recites Nakdishach, there would seemingly be no basis for stringency. In truth, however, the primary halachah does not follow the Taz, as we explain there.
