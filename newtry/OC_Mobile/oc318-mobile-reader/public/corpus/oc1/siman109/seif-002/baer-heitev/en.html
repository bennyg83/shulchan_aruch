Word for word. Meaning, he should recite with the chazzan, Nakdishach, Ledor Vador, and so forth, until HaKel HaKadosh. He may then continue praying independently if he can finish before the chazzan reaches the conclusion of Shome'a Tefillah. So it appears from Terumas HaDeshen and Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv. On a public fast, he should not recite Aneinu with the chazzan as a separate blessing, but should insert it in Shome'a Tefillah as an individual does. Mateh Moshe, Maharil, Magen Avraham.
Prayer. Meaning, he should intend to conclude these two blessings together with the chazzan. See Magen Avraham.
He should begin, etc. The Magen Avraham writes that the same applies to Barechu, since an individual does not recite Barechu. If he has already heard Kedushah or Barechu, or knows that he will hear it afterward, he need not wait. For Kaddish, however, he must wait, because there is no fixed limit to the number of Kaddish recitations. It is possible that the Kaddish recited from Aleinu onward is not included in this rule. Magen Avraham.
