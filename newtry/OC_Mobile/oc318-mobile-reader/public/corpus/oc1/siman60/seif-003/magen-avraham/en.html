And even though it does not open with Baruch, and every blessing that has no malchut is not a blessing—nevertheless, since it was enacted adjacent to its fellow, even when one recites it separately it has no [mention of] name and malchut [Kesef Mishneh]. And in Rashba's responsum siman 317 he wrote the reason: because it is a short blessing—and requires investigation. See in Tur siman 6: it is found in Kavanot and in writings that these remembrances are positive commandments; therefore when one says "and You chose us" one should remember the giving of the Torah; "and You brought us near"—the standing at Mount Sinai; "for Your great name"—the act of Amalek, for the Name is not complete; "with love, to thank You"—the mouth was not created except to give thanks and not to speak lashon hara, and this is remembrance of the act of Miriam; "and you shall remember all the commandments of the Omnipresent"—this is Shabbat, which is equivalent to all the mitzvot. And when saying "and You brought us in peace from the four corners of the earth," one should lower the wings of the tallit on his shoulders to fall downward, end quote. And in Yalkut parashat Bechukotai he also brings "remember how you provoked the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent in the desert," etc.—"remember" with the mouth, in the name of Sifrei. And it seems one should remember this when saying "with love"—to exclude that at that time they were not lovers of the Name. And a reason is needed why they enacted to read the parashah of Amalek, which they did not enact with other remembrances—on siman 685. And one may say: for the giving of the Torah we have the festival of Shavuot, and likewise Shabbat; but the act of Miriam and the act of the calf they did not enact, because it was a disgrace to Israel.
