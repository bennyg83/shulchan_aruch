Although this blessing does not begin with ‘Blessed,’ and any blessing that does not mention kingship is not a blessing, nevertheless, since it was instituted adjoining another blessing, even when one recites it independently it need not contain Hashem's name and kingship. This is the implication of the Kesef Mishneh. In responsum 317, the Rashba explains that this is because it is a short blessing, but this requires study. See the Tur in siman 6. It is stated in the kabbalistic intentions and writings that these remembrances are positive mitzvos. Therefore, when one says, ‘And You chose us,’ he should remember Matan Torah; when saying, ‘And You brought us near,’ he should remember the assembly at Har Sinai; when saying, ‘To Your great name,’ he should remember the episode of Amalek, because Hashem's name is not complete; when saying, ‘To thank You,’ he should remember that the mouth was created only to thank and not to speak lashon hara, which is the remembrance of the episode of Miriam; and when saying, ‘And you shall remember all the mitzvos of Hashem,’ he should remember Shabbos, which is equivalent to all the mitzvos. When he says, ‘And bring us in peace from the four corners of the earth,’ he should allow the corners of the tallis resting on his shoulders to fall downward. The Yalkut on Parashas Bechukosai likewise cites, ‘Remember how you provoked Hashem your God in the wilderness,’ etc., and explains in the name of the Sifrei that ‘remember’ means verbally. See there. It appears to me that one should remember this when saying, ‘with love,’ in contrast to that occasion, when they did not love Hashem. A reason is required why the Sages instituted the reading of the section of Amalek but did not institute readings for the other remembrances. See the end of siman 685. One may answer that for Matan Torah we have the festival of Shavuos, and likewise Shabbos; the episodes of Miriam and the Golden Calf were not instituted because they recount the disgrace of Israel.
