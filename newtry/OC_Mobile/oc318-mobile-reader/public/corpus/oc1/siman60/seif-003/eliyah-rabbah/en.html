[3] [Levush:] Therefore, one who reads, etc. The wording of Levush implies that according to the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent, one fulfills the obligation even if he lacked intent during the first verse. Olas Tamid, however, writes that according to all opinions the first verse requires intent, because it constitutes acceptance of the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven, and he does not mention the words of Levush. The Tur likewise appears to support Levush. This is difficult, however, because in siman 63, seif 4, Levush himself writes that according to all opinions the first verse requires intent. Shulchan Aruch rules likewise, based on Rashba as cited there by Beit Yosef. It appears that one must distinguish as follows: there, the discussion concerns concentration upon the meaning, whereas here it concerns intent to fulfill the obligation. Such intent is unnecessary according to the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent. This also appears from the novellae of Rashba, and I found the same in Rabbi David Abudraham, page 31. See below, siman 213, what I wrote concerning this. Radbaz writes that D'Rabbanan mitzvos do not require intent.
