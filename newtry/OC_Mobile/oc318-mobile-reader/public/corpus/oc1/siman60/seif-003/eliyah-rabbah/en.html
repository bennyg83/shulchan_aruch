(3) "[Levush] Therefore one who reads," etc. The Levush's language implies that per the opinion that mitzvot do not require intention, if one did not have intention in the first verse one has fulfilled. But in Olat Tamid he wrote that everyone requires intention, for this is acceptance of the yoke of the kingdom of heaven, and he did not mention Levush's words; also in the Tur it implies like Levush's words. But it is difficult—for in siman 63 se'if 4 the Levush himself wrote that everyone requires intention in the first verse, and likewise Shulchan Aruch wrote, and it is Rashba's words that Beit Yosef brought there. It appears one should distinguish: there it refers to intention of the heart, but here it refers to intention to fulfill, for we do not require this for one who holds mitzvot do not require intention; and so it appears from Rashba's novellae, and so I found in R. David Abudraham folio 31; and see below siman 213 what I wrote from this. Radvaz wrote: Rabbinic mitzvot do not require intention.
