The blessings. And even if he did not read them at all, he has fulfilled the obligation of Keriat Shema; see siman 58 seif 6. He wrote in Sefer HaKavanot that there are four remembrances. They are: (1) remembrance of the giving of the Torah, as it is written, "Remember the day you stood before Hashem your God at Chorev," etc.; (2) "Remember what Amalek did to you"; (3) "Remember what Hashem your God did to Miriam," etc.; (4) remembrance of Egypt. These four remembrances one should intend when he says "u'vanu vacharta"—he should intend the standing at Mount Sinai, when He chose us from every nation and brought us near to receive His Torah; "l'shmekha haGadol"—he should intend the act of Amalek, for the Name is not complete until the name of Amalek is blotted out; "l'hodot lekha"—he should intend that the mouth and tongue were created to give thanks and praise His name and not to speak lashon hara, and thereby he remembers the act of Miriam. Remembrance of Egypt he should intend in the section of tzitzit on the verse "Ani Hashem Elokeikhem asher hotzeiti etchem me'eretz Mitzrayim," end quote. And Magen Avraham wrote that one must also intend "Remember how you provoked Hashem your God," etc., and he should intend this when he says "b'ahavah"—to exclude that time when it was not with love, end quote. And before Keriat Shema he should intend to fulfill the positive commandment of Keriat Shema. One who was blessing the berakhah of Keriat Shema and found himself at "haEl haGadol" and was in doubt whether "b'shem kodshekha haGadol" refers to "betachonenu sheb'ahavat olam" or to "haGibbor vehaNora" in "Kadosh" in Birkat Yotzer Or—he returns to the first berakhah. Pri Eretz, part 1, siman 3. But in the responsum of Ginat Veradim, part 1, kelal 1, siman 11, he ruled that he does not return to the first berakhah, for doubt in berakhot is lenient, and from "mehuyot tov" he returns to the head of the second berakhah, end quote; and so too Yad Aharon, end quote, and see above siman 67.
