[2] The second before the first, etc. The Tur states: “Rav Hai explained that certainly, if one did not recite the blessings at all, their omission is indispensable. Rather, when the Yerushalmi says that their order is not indispensable, it means that if one reversed the order, reciting the first at the end and the second first, this does not invalidate.” This is the end of his words. The phrases “the first at the end” and “the second first” appear redundant, since either one alone would have sufficed, as in the wording of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush. One must also wonder at the Beit Yosef's objection, for the Yerushalmi derives this from the Mishnah concerning one who was reading, yet that Mishnah provides no proof regarding the order of the blessing Yotzer before Ahavah Rabbah. See there also his other difficulty. Bach wrote at length to resolve the Beit Yosef's difficulty, but his words are strained and do not satisfy the careful reader. He also based his position on our Gemara's statement that the order of the blessings is not indispensable, and asked how Rabbeinu Chananel would explain it. See there. It appears to me that he overlooked the words of Rashba in his novellae, who resolves this and writes that the Gemara's statement is merely a deflection. See there, as I have abbreviated. In my humble opinion, the Tur should be understood according to what I found in the Rashba's novellae on page 11, where he concludes: “The Yerushalmi appears to me to mean that the blessings are not indispensable as prerequisites to Krias Shema, but one must return and recite them after Krias Shema.” This is the end of his words. It would seem that the same applies if one recited the blessings following Krias Shema before Krias Shema, for their order is not indispensable. This is evidently the Tur's meaning when he says that one reversed the order and “recited the first at the end,” meaning that he recited the blessings preceding Krias Shema after Krias Shema. What he writes, “and the second first,” means the blessings following Krias Shema. These are precisely the words of Rashba. At the outset, the Tur merely cites Rav Hai's view that the blessings themselves are indispensable if they were omitted entirely. The Yerushalmi can now properly derive this from the Mishnah, which teaches that one who was reading and directed his heart fulfilled his obligation. Evidently, the order of the blessings, whether before or after Krias Shema, is immaterial. With Heaven's help, this resolves everything, and it is clear. Taz wrote at length to emend the Tur. See there. He has left room for my explanation.
