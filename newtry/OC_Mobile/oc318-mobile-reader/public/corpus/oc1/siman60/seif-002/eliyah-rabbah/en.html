(2) "The second before the first," etc. The Tur and Rav Hai Gaon explained: certainly if one did not say the blessings at all they are indispensable; rather, regarding this matter he speaks from the Yerushalmi that they are not indispensable—if one changed the order to say the first at the end and the second at the beginning it does not hold one back, until here is his language. And he duplicated the language "first at the end and second at the beginning," whereas one expression suffices, as the language of Shulchan Aruch and Levush. Also it is astonishing what Beit Yosef wondered—for the Yerushalmi indeed infers this from the mishnah "he was reading"—yet from there there is no inference at all to the order of Yotzer after Ahavah Rabbah; also his other questions, see there. And the Bach elaborated to resolve Beit Yosef's question, and his words are strained and not for investigation. Also what he built his foundation from our Gemara that says the order of blessings is not indispensable—how does Rabbeinu Chananel resolve it for him, see there; it seems to me he omitted Rashba's words in his novellae, who resolves and writes that this is only a mere deflection, see there for I abbreviated. What appears correct in my humble opinion is like the Tur's explanation, according to what I found in Rashba's novellae folio 11 at the end of his words—the Yerushalmi, it seems to me, is to be explained: what "they are not indispensable" means is not to precede them to its reading, but rather one returns and recites them after the reading of Shema, until here is his language. And it appears the same law applies if one said the blessings after Shema before reading Shema—they are not indispensable; and this is the meaning of the Tur's explanation that if one changed the order to say "the first at the end," he means to say the blessings before Shema after Shema. And what he wrote "the second at the beginning" means to say the blessings after Shema; and these are Rashba's words—only at the outset he cites Rav Hai Gaon's opinion that blessings are indispensable if one did not say them at all. And now the Yerushalmi's inference is well explained from the mishnah that teaches "he was reading"—and he directed his heart—he fulfilled, showing he does not care about the order of blessings before Shema or after Shema; and with this everything is resolved, be"H, and it is clear. The Taz elaborated to emend the Tur, see there, and left me room.
