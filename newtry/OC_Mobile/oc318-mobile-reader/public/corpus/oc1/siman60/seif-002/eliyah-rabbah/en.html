Rather, the Yerushalmi says only in this respect that they do not prevent fulfillment: if one changed the order, reciting the first at the end and the second first, this does not prevent fulfillment." End quote. The phrases "the first at the end" and "the second first" are redundant, for either one alone would have sufficed, as in the wording of Shulchan Aruch and Levush. One should also question Beis Yosef's difficulty. The Yerushalmi derives this from the Mishnah concerning one who was reading, yet that Mishnah provides no inference at all concerning placing the blessing Yotzer after Ahavah Rabbah. See there also his other difficulty. Bach wrote at length to resolve Beis Yosef's difficulty, but his words are strained and do not satisfy one who examines them. He also built his foundation upon the statement of our Gemara that the order of the blessings does not prevent fulfillment, and asked how Rabbeinu Chananel would explain it; see there. It appears to me that he overlooked Rashba's words in his novellae, where Rashba resolves this by writing that the Gemara's statement is merely a rejection of the proposed proof. See there; I have abbreviated. In my humble opinion, the Tur should be explained according to what I found at the end of Rashba's novellae on page 11: "The Yerushalmi appears to me to mean that the blessings do not prevent fulfillment when they are not recited before the reading, but one must return and recite them after Krias Shema." End quote. It would appear that the same applies if one recited the blessings that follow Krias Shema before Krias Shema: their order does not prevent fulfillment. This is evidently the Tur's meaning when he says that one changed the order and recited "the first at the end": he means that the blessings preceding Krias Shema were recited after Krias Shema. His words "and the second first" refer to the blessings following Krias Shema being recited first. These are precisely Rashba's words. At the outset, the Tur merely cites Rav Hai's view that the blessings prevent fulfillment if one did not recite them at all. The Yerushalmi can now properly infer this from the Mishnah, which teaches that one who was reading and directed his heart fulfilled his obligation. Evidently, it is immaterial whether the blessings are recited before or after Krias Shema. With Heaven's help, this resolves everything, and it is clear. Taz wrote at length to emend the Tur; see there. He has left room for my explanation.
