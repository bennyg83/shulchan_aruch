“It appears to me that it is preferable, etc.” See Peri Megadim, who writes that the Shulchan Aruch’s intent here is to take into account the view of Rav Hai, who rules that one does not properly fulfill his obligation without the blessings. This is surprising, for even according to Rav Hai the order is not indispensable at all; since he recites the blessings after Keriat Shema, he has fulfilled the obligation of Keriat Shema properly. See Beit Yosef. It would be strained to say that this applies specifically when, at the time he recited Keriat Shema, he intended to recite the blessings immediately afterward, whereas here he did not have this in mind. Furthermore, the Beit Yosef holds that even according to Rav Hai an individual fulfills his obligation, and the Shulchan Aruch makes no distinction in his ruling. The correct explanation is as I cited in the Mishnah Berurah in the name of the Gra. Accordingly, even if he recited Keriat Shema within the first three hours but recites its blessings after three hours, he must nevertheless recite Keriat Shema again, as the Gra derives from the Jerusalem Talmud; see there. This is unlike the Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, se’if katan 1.
