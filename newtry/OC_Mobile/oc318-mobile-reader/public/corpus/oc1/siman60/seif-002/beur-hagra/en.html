Siman 62—one who read, etc. Mishnah end of chapter 2: he was reading, etc., and he said upon it in the Yerushalmi: Rabbi said, this teaches that berakhot do not impede; and even Rabbi Hai Gaon wrote specifically for an individual, but in a congregation they impede, as written 12a in their order—specifically it does not impede, and there in a congregation as written. Nevertheless Rashba agreed to the first book that the berakhot do not refer to Keriat Shema—for otherwise how would we be lenient to interrupt between the perakim, and we are more lenient in them than in the middle—Kaf HaChayyim. And see Magen Avraham siman 206 s.k. 4.

And he returns, etc.—meaning: even though they do not impede for Keriat Shema and he fulfills Keriat Shema even if he did not bless at all, nevertheless the eighteen berakhot he did not fulfill, and he may bless without Keriat Shema, for they were not instituted upon Keriat Shema for one who does not bless, and "venu tzivanu"—as written at the end of chapter 3: if he can ascend, etc., even though there is no time to bless and he will bless afterward; and at the end of chapter 10 he did not lose the berakhot even though he lost Keriat Shema, and it is only like one reading from the Torah—and as written above, their order does not impede and he blesses after Keriat Shema. Greater than this they said: whoever did not say emet veyatziv in the morning, etc.

And it seems to me, etc. In order to stand in prayer from words of Torah, as written in the Yerushalmi chapter 1: Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Eliezer went out to fast, etc., as written above siman 58—even though one must erase them on account of having read out of time, as written: the order of berakhot as above.
