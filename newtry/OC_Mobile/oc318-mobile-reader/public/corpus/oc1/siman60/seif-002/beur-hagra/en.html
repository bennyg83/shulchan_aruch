Seif 2, ‘he recited,’ etc. The Mishnah at the beginning of the second chapter states, ‘If one was reading,’ etc. The Yerushalmi comments there in the name of Rabbi Abba: ‘This teaches that the blessings are not indispensable.’ Although Rav Hai Gaon wrote that this applies specifically to an individual, whereas for a congregation the blessings are indispensable, his meaning is only that their prescribed order is not indispensable, as stated in Berachos 12a. That passage concerns a congregation, as indicated by the wording, ‘The appointed official said to them.’ The blessings themselves, however, are indispensable. Nevertheless, the Rashba agreed with the first view, that the blessings are not attached to Krias Shema itself. Otherwise, how could the law be more lenient regarding interruptions between the sections, and more lenient there than in the middle of a section, as is evident in several places? See Magen Avraham, siman 206, seif katan 4.
And he returns, etc. Meaning, although the blessings are not indispensable to Krias Shema, and one fulfills the obligation of Krias Shema even if he recited no blessing at all, he has nevertheless not fulfilled the obligation of the blessings. He may recite them without Krias Shema, because they were not instituted as blessings over Krias Shema, since one does not say, ‘Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us.’ This is what is stated at the end of the third chapter: ‘If he can ascend,’ etc. Even when there is no time to recite the blessings, he may recite them afterward. Likewise, at the end of the first chapter, ‘he has not lost the blessings,’ even though he has missed Krias Shema and is merely like one reading from the Torah. This is also the meaning of the statement above that their order is not indispensable, for he may recite the blessings after Krias Shema. They said even more than this: ‘Anyone who did not say Emes VeYatziv in the morning,’ etc.
And it appears to me, etc. This is so that one may stand in prayer after words of Torah, as stated in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of the first chapter regarding Rabbi Yose and Rabbi Acha, who went out for a fast, etc., as I wrote above at the beginning of siman 58. This is so even though they wished to protest against them because they recited it outside its proper time, as stated there. ‘The order of the blessings,’ etc., is as explained above.
