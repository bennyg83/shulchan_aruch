Seif katan 2, ‘That if,’ etc. ‘And every blessing,’ etc. These are two separate questions. Granted, when one places Ahavah Rabbah immediately after Yotzer Or, the blessing of Ahavah need not begin with ‘Blessed,’ in accordance with the rule that a blessing adjoining another blessing does not begin with ‘Blessed.’ Even its conclusion contains no mention of kingship, and any blessing lacking kingship is not a blessing. When he adjoins it and recites it after Yotzer Or, this presents no difficulty. Now, however, when he recites Ahavah Rabbah by itself, it should seemingly begin with a blessing formula and should also mention kingship at its opening.
For it is a short blessing. This requires study. His meaning is that its formula is in fact lengthy, so why did he call it short? See the Tur in siman 6. His meaning is that the Tur writes that one should place Elokai Neshamah immediately before Bircas HaTorah, because Elokai Neshamah does not begin with ‘Blessed,’ as it adjoins Bircas HaTorah, and therefore one should not interrupt between them. Although according to the Magen Avraham's words, once a blessing was instituted adjoining another blessing, it need not begin with ‘Blessed’ even when recited independently, it is certainly preferable initially to adjoin it. Here the discussion concerns one who is prevented from doing so.
It is stated in the kabbalistic intentions, etc. ‘A reason is required,’ etc. See the end of siman 685. His meaning is that there the Magen Avraham writes that one certainly fulfills the Torah obligation of remembering Amalek when reading its passage in Parashas Beshalach or Parashas Ki Seitzei; it is only a Rabbinic enactment to read it before Purim. If so, the same should apply to the remembrances the Magen Avraham mentions here: one fulfills them when those matters are read on the Shabbos on which their respective parashah is read. Nevertheless, it remains somewhat difficult, for the Sages should have instituted a special reading for them.
