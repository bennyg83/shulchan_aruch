(Note 2) "That if," etc.—and every blessing, etc.—these are two questions. Namely, when Ahavah Rabbah is adjacent to Yotzer Ohr, the blessing of love need not open with Baruch, as is the law of every blessing adjacent to its fellow that does not open with Baruch; and also even in its conclusion it has no malchut, and every blessing that has no malchut is not a blessing; and if it is adjacent and one says it after Yotzer Ohr, what need is there for it? Nevertheless, now that one says Ahavah Rabbah by itself, it would have been fitting that it begin with a blessing and also mention malchut in its opening.

For it is a short blessing—requires investigation; the meaning is: behold it is of long nature, so why did he call it short? And see in Tur siman 6. The meaning is that he wrote that one should adjoin Elohai Neshamah to the blessing on the Torah, for it does not open with Baruch since it is adjacent to the blessing on the Torah; therefore one should not interrupt between them. And even though according to Magen Avraham's words, since it was enacted adjacent to its fellow, even if one said it by itself it need not open with a blessing—nevertheless ab initio it is certainly preferable to adjoin it; and here we speak of one who is compelled.

"It says in Kavanot," etc.—and a reason is needed, etc., on siman 685. The meaning is: there Magen Avraham wrote that certainly from the Torah one fulfills with the reading of parashat Zachor when reading it in parashat Beshalach or parashat Tetzaveh; only that the Sages enacted to read it before Purim. If so, likewise with these remembrances that Magen Avraham wrote here—one also fulfills with what one reads on that Shabbat where they are written in that parashah. Nevertheless it is somewhat difficult—for it would follow that the Sages enacted for them a special reading.
