Seif katan 1, ‘And he returns,’ etc. ‘He has fulfilled the obligation of Krias Shema,’ etc. In truth, the Beit Yosef writes as follows: ‘Their words imply that, according to the opinion that the blessings are indispensable to one another, meaning that if he recited only one blessing, he has not even fulfilled the obligation of the blessing he recited, the same applies if he recited Krias Shema without its blessings: he has not fulfilled the obligation of Krias Shema in its proper form. According to the opinion that the blessings are not indispensable to one another, if he recited Krias Shema without its blessings, he has nevertheless fulfilled the obligation of Krias Shema.’
Since we rule that the blessings are indispensable to one another, although their order is not indispensable, as stated in seif 3, it follows that he has also not fulfilled the obligation of Krias Shema in its proper form. We must therefore say that the Magen Avraham's statement that he has fulfilled the obligation of Krias Shema means that he has fulfilled its D'Oraisa obligation. He has not, however, fulfilled Krias Shema in its proper instituted form.
