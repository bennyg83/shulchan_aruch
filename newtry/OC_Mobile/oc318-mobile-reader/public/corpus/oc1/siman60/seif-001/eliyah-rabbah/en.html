[1] With abundant love, etc. The Shulchan Aruch of Rabbi Yitzchak Luria states as follows: At “from the four corners of the earth,” take the four tzitzis in your left hand and place that hand on the left side opposite the heart. Keep them in your hand until “and desirable forever.” This is the end of his words. It also appears there that when one reaches the passage of tzitzis, he should take them in his right hand, so that they are held in both hands until “and desirable.” He should then kiss them, place them upon his eyes, and release them from his hand. The Kavanot states that when saying “and You chose us,” one should recall the Giving of the Torah; at “and You brought us near,” the assembly at Mount Sinai; at “to Your great Name,” the episode of Amalek; at “to thank You,” that the mouth was created to thank Hashem and not to speak lashon hara, which recalls the episode of Miriam; and at “and you shall remember all the mitzvos of Hashem,” Shabbos. When saying “and bring us in peace,” one should allow the corners of the tallis that are upon his shoulders to fall downward. This is the end of his words. Magen Avraham writes that when saying “with love,” one should recall that in the wilderness they provoked Hashem and did not love Him, as it is written, “Remember how you provoked.” A gloss to Magen Avraham states that one should say “so, our Father,” rather than “and grant us favor and teach us,” and that one need not say “the mighty,” but only “the great and awesome.” This is the end of his words. However, I saw in Abudraham the text, “so grant us favor, our Father, etc., the great, mighty, and awesome.”
