(1) Ahavah Rabbah, etc. It is written in Shulchan Aruch: Mahari Luria—his words: "From the four corners of the earth," etc.—take four tzitzit in your left hand and place the hand on the left side against the heart, and they shall be in your hand until "unechmadim le'ad," end quote. It further appears there that when he reaches the section of tzitzit he should hold them in his right hand, and they should be so in both his hands until "unechmadim," and then he kisses them and places them on his eyes and removes them from his hand. He wrote in HaKavanot: when he says "u'vanu vacharta" he should remember the giving of the Torah and our being brought near at the standing at Mount Sinai; "l'shmekha haGadol"—the act of Amalek; "l'hodot lekha"—the mouth was created to give thanks and not to speak lashon hara, and this is remembrance of the act of Miriam; "v'zikhartem et kol mitzvot Hashem"—this is Shabbat; and when he says "v'havi'anu l'shalom," etc., he should lower the wings of the tallit on his shoulders to fall downward, end quote. Magen Avraham wrote: when he says "b'ahavah" he should remember that in the desert they provoked and were not lovers of Hashem, as it is written, "Remember how you provoked," etc. He wrote in the margin of Magen Avraham: one must say "ken Avinu" and not "chanenu ve'talmadenu," and one need not say "HaGibbor" but "HaGadol" and "HaNora," end quote. But I saw in Avudraham: "ken chanenu Avinu," etc., "HaGadol HaGibbor vehaNora."
