Kedushah of the sidra—that is, "Uva letzion," siman 132; its law is like the kedushah of Yotzer; see there. And one does not say it in a loud voice but in a whisper; and even in a congregation they say in a whisper, as the Zohar wrote: every matter of targum one says in a whisper. And "Uva letzion" has two kedushot: one in Hebrew and "Kadosh Kadosh Kadosh," etc.; and some say in a loud voice the targum of this verse and "Mekabelin din min din" and "Amrin kedish," etc.—this is the targum of the Hebrew; this they say in a whisper. And so too it is proper on Shabbat at Minchah when the shaliach tzibbur chants—they say the verse and "Mekabelin kedish," etc., three times in a whisper; see Ohr Zaru'a here and Levush there. And see Beit Yosef there—it will be explained there; here there is no room to elaborate.

The author ruled Ahavat Olam and not Ahavah Rabbah; nevertheless only the beginning differs, and from here onward there is no difference. Beit Yosef and Rashi. And that which they chose specifically in the morning Ahavah Rabbah and in the evening Ahavat Olam—"Renewed each morning—great is Your faithfulness" in the morning, and "Ahavat Olam—You have loved us," etc., in the evening. And the Sephardic wording—morning and evening Ahavat Olam—Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema halakhah 6.

Order of Tur. Beit Yosef and Bach and Perishah and see in Magen Avraham 1; and I will explain a bit regarding Rav Hai: if one read Keriat Shema without berakhot—even though he fulfills Keriat Shema like all mitzvot, for we hold berakhot do not impede—nevertheless he has not fulfilled the mitzvah as enacted; and automatically when he returns and says the berakhot afterward he must return and read Keriat Shema as well. And that which Rabbi Pappa was reading—if he intended he fulfilled and need not return and read; in an individual this is discussed, but a congregation reads Keriat Shema again with its berakhot. Second, Rav Hai holds that berakhot impede one another: if he said only one and did not say the second at all, even for the one he said he has not fulfilled, and it is a berakhah in vain, for the Sages enacted to say specifically both; and like that of the twelve [men of the] ma'amad who would say "Amein"—and when the time of Yotzer Or arrived they would say it, and that is rejected. And so Rambam z"l in chapter 6 of Temidin uMusafin halakhah 4: if he said "Amein" they say (see in Nekudot HaKesef); and there is a great practical difference if he delayed reading until the end of the fourth hour and there is no time to say both berakhot with Keriat Shema and there is time to say one—for Rav Hai he should not say, for they impede one another (Beit Yosef and Hagahot in Berakhot 11b and see Perishah); and one berakhah would be in vain. And accordingly the author in seif 2 ruled like Rashba, not like Rav Hai lehalachah, that one need not return and read Keriat Shema; nevertheless one who sees is concerned for Rav Hai's words and reads Keriat Shema again, etc. And in the order of berakhot he ruled that they do not impede—if he preceded the second to the first he fulfills the obligation of berakhot as well; and "venu tzivanu"—"Amein" is not in it malkhut (Magen Avraham 2); and this is Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema halakhah 8, and he derives it from Berakhot 12, as we said—it implies that berakhot impede one without the other, as we said (Ephraim Zalman Margoliot); and there is no decisive ruling—one could say he speaks as a stringency that order does not impede, and a fortiori berakhot one without the other do not impede; nevertheless one must say the berakhot—even an individual who said Keriat Shema; Beit Yosef—and there is no room to elaborate.

And incidentally—a tangent from what is written in Tur: and that which we learned "if he intended," etc.—for one could explain per the view that "he intended" means to read without omissions and additions—that he did not read mezuzah tefillin, etc.; if so, there is no proof at all to the Yerushalmi. This [sugya] teaches perhaps as explained above; but this is not relevant to Rav Hai—and behold the Yerushalmi that challenges thus from the Mishnah holds that without berakhot too one fulfills.
