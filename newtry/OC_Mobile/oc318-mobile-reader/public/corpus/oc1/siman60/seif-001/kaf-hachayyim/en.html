1) [Se'if 1] The second blessing is Ahavas Olam, both in the morning and in the evening. Beis Yosef cites Rif and Rambam and writes that this is the custom of Sephardim. The Rav likewise writes in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the intention of Yotzer, folio 19a: It is improper to say Ahavah Rabbah, because great love is in the attribute of Chesed in the world of Atzilus, while we are now in the chambers of Beriah. One must therefore say Ahavas Olam, which is the lesser love of Malchus, called Olam, that is still below in the world of Beriah. It has thus been explained that Ahavas Olam is the mystical meaning of the Chamber of Love, which is the Chesed of Beriah. Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 3, writes likewise. There is no distinction between Shabbos and Yom Kippur and the other days of the year, because all are within the chambers of Beriah, as explained in Etz Chaim, gate 46, chapter 4, and as is known to those versed in the hidden wisdom. This is unlike Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 92a, and Shaarei Teshuvah; see there and consider carefully. The custom of the pious members of Beis El, may it be built and established speedily, within our holy city of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, is to say only Ahavas Olam, whether on a weekday, Shabbos, or Yom Kippur. This is also the general custom.
2) There: The second blessing is Ahavas Olam. See what we wrote above in siman 48, paragraph 1, s.v. Ahavas Olam, and siman 24, paragraph 8, regarding what pertains to this blessing from Shaar HaKavanos. Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 3, says that the text should read "le'hodos lach uleyachedcha ule'ahavah es Shemecha," etc. See Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 92b, who writes that he found in a manuscript of Rabbi Chaim Vital that the text should read "leyirah ule'ahavah." Yafeh LaLev writes that love should seemingly precede fear because love corresponds to the yud of the Name and fear to its final hei. See, however, Shaar HaKavanos, folio 59a, which writes that fear corresponds to the yud of the Name and love to the first hei. See also Shaar Maamarei Rashbi, Parashas Naso, folio 38c in the new edition; Etz Chaim, gate 38, beginning of chapter 3; and below, paragraph 11. Consider this carefully.
3) When saying, "and to fulfill all the words of Your Torah with love," one should include the mitzvah of supporting those who study Torah. The Yerushalmi in Sotah, chapter 7, and Midrash Rabbah, Parashas Kedoshim, chapter 25, say that one who studied, taught, observed, and performed, and had the means to support Torah but did not, is included in "Cursed is one who does not uphold the words of this Torah." Thus, after we pray, "Place in our hearts to understand and discern, to hear, study, and teach, observe, and perform," if one has the means to support Torah but does not, he is included in "Cursed is one who does not uphold all the words of this Torah." One must therefore pray, "and to fulfill all the words of Your Torah with love." Ruach Chaim, paragraph 3. When saying, "Enlighten our eyes in Your Torah," one should intend that Hashem illuminate his eyes in the Torah's secrets, as the pious Rabbi Yosef Yavetz writes. Rabbi Chaim cites this in his introduction, folio 4b; see there. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 4, cites it.
3) "And we shall not be ashamed, nor humiliated" is the correct text, not the version in some siddurim, "so that we shall not be ashamed." While reciting these words, one should pour out his soul in prayer for his descendants, that, G-d forbid, they not cause him shame and humiliation in this world or the World to Come.
3) Also, when saying, "And we shall not be ashamed, nor humiliated," one should intend to request that he merit the wisdom of Kabbalah and the Torah's secrets, and that he merit completing the rectification of his nefesh, ruach, and neshamah. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3.
4) There: The second blessing is Ahavas Olam. In this blessing one should recall three remembrances that are positive commandments, and a fourth in the passage of tzitzis. They are as follows: when saying "You brought us near, our King," he should intend to remember the revelation at Mount Sinai; at "to Your great Name," the remembrance of erasing Amalek; and at "with love, to thank You," the remembrance of the incident of Miriam, for the mouth was created to thank Hashem and proclaim His unity, not, G-d forbid, to speak lashon hara. In the passage of tzitzis, when saying, "Who brought you out of the land of Egypt," he should remember the Exodus from Egypt. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the intention of Yotzer, folio 19b; Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 3; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Yad Aharon, Hagahos on the Tur; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Kesher Gudal, siman 10, paragraph 14; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 6; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Shemos, paragraph 5.
4) Many poskim, however, hold that ten remembrances must be recalled and that they must be articulated verbally, remembrance in the heart being insufficient. It is therefore proper for every G-d-fearing person who delights in His mitzvos to recite the verses of the Ten Remembrances printed in the siddurim, or to say the following each day:
4) For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed be He, and His Shechinah: Behold, I fulfill the mitzvah of the Ten Remembrances: the Exodus from Egypt; Shabbos; the manna; the deed of wicked Amalek, may his name and memory be blotted out; the revelation at Mount Sinai; how our ancestors angered the Holy One, blessed be He, in the wilderness, especially with the calf; what Balak and Bilam counseled to do to our ancestors, "in order to know the righteous acts of Hashem"; the incident of Miriam the prophetess; the remembrance of Yerushalayim; and the mitzvah, "You shall remember that He is the One Who gives you strength to make wealth." The essence of the mitzvah of these remembrances is to take to heart the benefit that follows from them, to guard them carefully, and to act accordingly; one who examines them closely will find that they encompass much. Chesed LaAlafim there. See Shaagas Aryeh, siman 13. One may recite these Ten Remembrances before or after prayer. He should also remember the four remembrances alluded to in the blessing Ahavas Olam and the passage of tzitzis. Since the Arizal, of blessed memory, stated that they must be recalled at that place, one should not change it; rather, he should do both. This is proper.
