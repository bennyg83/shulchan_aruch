4) There: The second blessing is Ahavas Olam. In this blessing one should recall three remembrances that are positive commandments, and a fourth in the passage of tzitzis. They are as follows: when saying "You brought us near, our King," he should intend to remember the revelation at Mount Sinai; at "to Your great Name," the remembrance of erasing Amalek; and at "with love, to thank You," the remembrance of the incident of Miriam, for the mouth was created to thank Hashem and proclaim His unity, not, God forbid, to speak lashon hara. In the passage of tzitzis, when saying, "Who brought you out of the land of Egypt," he should remember the Exodus from Egypt. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the intention of Yotzer, folio 19b; Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 3; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Yad Aharon, Hagahos on the Tur; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Kesher Gudal, siman 10, paragraph 14; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 6; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Shemos, paragraph 5.
4) Many poskim, however, hold that ten remembrances must be recalled and that they must be articulated verbally, remembrance in the heart being insufficient. It is therefore proper for every God-fearing person who delights in His mitzvos to recite the verses of the Ten Remembrances printed in the siddurim, or to say the following each day:
4) For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed be He, and His Shechinah: Behold, I fulfill the mitzvah of the Ten Remembrances: the Exodus from Egypt; Shabbos; the manna; the deed of wicked Amalek, may his name and memory be blotted out; the revelation at Mount Sinai; how our ancestors angered the Holy One, blessed be He, in the wilderness, especially with the calf; what Balak and Bilam counseled to do to our ancestors, "in order to know the righteous acts of Hashem"; the incident of Miriam the prophetess; the remembrance of Yerushalayim; and the mitzvah, "You shall remember that He is the One Who gives you strength to make wealth." The essence of the mitzvah of these remembrances is to take to heart the benefit that follows from them, to guard them carefully, and to act accordingly; one who examines them closely will find that they encompass much. Chesed LaAlafim there. See Shaagas Aryeh, siman 13. One may recite these Ten Remembrances before or after prayer. He should also remember the four remembrances alluded to in the blessing Ahavas Olam and the passage of tzitzis. Since the Arizal, of blessed memory, stated that they must be recalled at that place, one should not change it; rather, he should do both. This is proper.
