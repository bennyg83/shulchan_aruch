(7) Some say—that is, as explained from the poskim, there are two intentions for one mitzvah: (1) kavanah of the heart for the mitzvah itself, and (2) intention to fulfill thereby—that is, that he intend to fulfill thereby as He commanded, as Bach wrote siman 8. And the kavanah of the mitzvah mentioned in this seif does not depend at all on kavanah of the heart for the mitzvah itself—that he intend in his heart what he utters from his mouth and not let his heart wander elsewhere—such as in Keriat Shema and tefillah and Birkat HaMazon and Kiddush and the like; for this, all agree lekhatchilah it is a mitzvah to have kavanah in one's heart, and bedi'eved if he did not intend he has fulfilled, except for the first verse of Keriat Shema and Avot of tefillah, as explained below; only they dispute whether he must intend to fulfill before beginning the mitzvah. And for a mitzvah it is preferable—all agree that kavanah is required, as stated in Nedarim: Rabbeinu Tam says "Do things for the sake of their Maker," and it is said "Let your fear of Me be the command of people"—mere learning, as the Gra wrote on that which is stated siman 8; see there.

(8) That they require kavanah—and if he did not intend to fulfill his obligation in doing the mitzvah, he has not fulfilled from the Torah and must return and do it. And even if he doubts whether he intended—if it is a Torah mitzvah, doubt is stringent; so too wrote Pri Megadim siman 489; see there. And it seems to me one should not bless then on the mitzvah, for without [kavanah] there are several views on the matter of berakhah, even if he certainly did not intend the first time.

(9) To fulfill—therefore one who sounds to teach, or one who blesses Birkat HaMazon with children to educate them regarding mitzvot, and he too was obligated in Birkat HaMazon and then forgot to intend to fulfill thereby for himself as well—in every mitzvah he did for some other purpose he has not fulfilled his obligation; see Taz siman 489, from whose words it is proven that one who blesses with the aforementioned children has not fulfilled even per the view that mitzvot do not require kavanah, for this is like one who intends explicitly not to fulfill; but if his intention in doing the mitzvah is for some purpose and also to fulfill the mitzvah thereby—he has fulfilled.

(10) And such is the halachah—Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Radbaz that this is specifically for a Torah mitzvah, but for a rabbinic mitzvah one need not have kavanah. Accordingly, all berakhot which are also rabbinic—except Birkat HaMazon—if he did not intend therein to fulfill, he has fulfilled bedi'eved; but from many places in Shulchan Aruch it implies that he disagrees with this, and so too from the Gra's explanation siman 489 it seems there is no distinction between Torah and rabbinic mitzvot. Know that Magen Avraham wrote below siman 489 sk 8: even though Shulchan Aruch ruled lehalachah that mitzvot require kavanah—and if so, where he did not intend the first time he must return and do the mitzvah—nevertheless he should not bless again on it, for regarding berakhah one must be concerned for the view of "some say" that one need not have kavanah; see Bach. Know further that Chayei Adam wrote in klal 68: that which we require him to return and do the mitzvah is where it can be presumed that the first act was not for the sake of the mitzvah—such as in tekiah that was to teach, or Keriat Shema that was through his study and the like; but if he reads Keriat Shema in the way we read in the order of tefillah, or ate matzah or took the lulav or sounded the shofar—even though he did not intend to fulfill, he has fulfilled, for he does this in order to fulfill even though he does not intend explicitly; so too Chayei Adam. Meaning: where it is evident from the matter that his act is in order to fulfill even though he did not intend explicitly—he has fulfilled; but stam, certainly he has not fulfilled, as Tosafot Sukkah 39a s.v. oveir; see there. All this is regarding bedi'eved; but lekhatchilah he certainly must be careful to intend before every mitzvah to fulfill the obligation of the mitzvah—and so copied all the acharonim in their books; see Chayei Adam klal 21 and in Sha'ar HaTziyun Laws of Keriat Shema and Ma'aseh Rav.
