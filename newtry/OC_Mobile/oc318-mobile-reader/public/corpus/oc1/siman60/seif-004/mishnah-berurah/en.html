(7) Some say. Know that, as emerges from the poskim, a mitzvah involves two forms of intent. The first is concentration on the mitzvah itself, meaning that he should direct his heart to what he is uttering and not think about something else, as in Krias Shema, the Amidah, Bircas HaMazon, Kiddush, and the like. Regarding this, all agree that initially it is a mitzvah to concentrate, but after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation without such concentration, except for the first verse of Krias Shema and the first blessing of the Amidah, as explained below. The second is intent to fulfill the obligation, meaning that he intends through this act to fulfill what Hashem commanded, as the Bach writes in siman 8. The intent discussed in this seif concerns this second form. For the preferred fulfillment of a mitzvah, all agree that intent is required, as stated in Nedarim: Rabbi Eliezer son of Tzadok says, perform deeds for the sake of the One who made them. It is also stated, ‘Their fear of Me has become a commandment of people learned by rote,’ as the Gra writes regarding siman 8. See there.
(8) That they require intent. If he did not intend to fulfill his obligation when performing the mitzvah, he has not fulfilled it on a Torah level and must perform it again. Even when he is uncertain whether he intended, if it is a Torah-level mitzvah the doubt is treated stringently, as the Peri Megadim writes in siman 589. It appears to me that he should not recite the blessing when repeating the mitzvah, because there are several opinions concerning the blessing even where he certainly lacked intent the first time.
(9) To fulfill the obligation. Therefore, one who sounds the shofar for practice, or one who recites Bircas HaMazon with children in order to train them in mitzvos while he himself is also obligated in Bircas HaMazon, but forgets to intend to fulfill his own obligation, does not fulfill it. The same applies to all comparable mitzvos performed for some other purpose. See the Taz in siman 489, whose words indicate that one who recites Bircas HaMazon with children as described does not fulfill his obligation even according to the view that mitzvos do not require intent, because it is as though he expressly intended not to fulfill it. If his intent in performing the mitzvah is both for another purpose and to fulfill the mitzvah, he does fulfill his obligation.
(10) And such is the halachah. The Magen Avraham writes in the name of the Radbaz that this applies only to a Torah-level mitzvah, but a Rabbinic mitzvah does not require intent. Accordingly, regarding all blessings, which are Rabbinic except for Bircas HaMazon, one who failed to intend to fulfill the obligation has nevertheless fulfilled it after the fact. However, several rulings in the Shulchan Aruch indicate that it disagrees with this distinction, and the explanation of the Gra in siman 489 likewise indicates that no distinction is made between a Torah-level and a Rabbinic mitzvah. Know also that the Magen Avraham writes below, siman 489, seif katan 8, that although the Shulchan Aruch rules that mitzvos require intent, so that one who lacked intent the first time must perform the mitzvah again, he nevertheless should not recite another blessing, because regarding the blessing one must take into account the opinion that intent is not required. See Biur Halachah. The Chayei Adam writes in klal 68 that the requirement to repeat the mitzvah applies where there is reason to attribute the first act to a purpose other than the mitzvah, such as sounding the shofar for practice or reciting Krias Shema as part of one's studies. But where one recites Krias Shema in the normal order of prayer, or eats matzah, sounds the shofar, or takes the lulav, he has fulfilled his obligation even without explicit intent, because this is plainly why he is performing the act. His meaning is that where the circumstances demonstrate that the act is being done to fulfill the obligation, explicit intent is unnecessary after the fact. In an unqualified case, however, he certainly has not fulfilled his obligation, as stated in Tosafos, Sukkah 39a, s.v. over. All this concerns the law after the fact. Initially, one must certainly be careful before every mitzvah to intend to fulfill the obligation, as all the later authorities record in their works. See Chayei Adam, klal 21; Derech HaChaim, laws of Krias Shema; and Maaseh Rav.
