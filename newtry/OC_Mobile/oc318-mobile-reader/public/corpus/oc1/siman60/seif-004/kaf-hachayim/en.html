(א) There—the second blessing Ahavat Olam. In this blessing one should remember three remembrances that are positive commandments, and one in the section of tzitzit; and these are: when saying "and You brought us near, our King," one should intend to remember the standing at Mount Sinai; "for Your great name"—remembrance of blotting out Amalek; "with love, to thank You"—remembrance of the act of Miriam, that the mouth was created to thank God and to unify Him, and not to speak in it lashon hara, God forbid. And in the section of tzitzit, when saying "that I brought you out of the land of Egypt," one should remember the Exodus from Egypt. Sha'ar HaKavanot in the discourse on the intention of Yotzer folio 19 side b; Pri Etz Chaim gate of Kriat Shema ch. 3; Magen Avraham note 2; Yeshuot Yaakov in Hagahot Taz; Avnei Nezer note 1; Razah note 4; Kaf HaChayyim siman 10 note 14; Chida note 6; Ben Ish Chai parashat Shemot note 5.

(ב) However, many poskim hold that one must remember the ten remembrances and that one must remember them with the mouth and the heart alone does not suffice; therefore it is fitting for everyone who fears God and desires His commandments to say the verses of the ten remembrances as brought in the siddurim. Or one may say each day thus:

(ג) "By the merit of the holy ones who are in the earth," etc.—"Behold I fulfill the mitzvah of the ten remembrances: the Exodus from Egypt; Shabbat; the manna; the act of wicked Amalek, may his name be blotted out; the standing at Mount Sinai; that our fathers provoked the Holy One, blessed be He, in the desert, and especially at the calf; that Balak and Balaam counseled to do to our fathers; and "in order that you know the righteousness of God"; the act of Miriam the prophetess; remembrance of Jerusalem; the mitzvah of "and you shall remember that it is God who gives you power to be valiant." And the essence of the mitzvah of the remembrances is that one set upon his heart the benefit that flows from them and guard them greatly and do them, for one who is exacting in them finds that they include much. Chida there. And see Sha'agat Aryeh siman 13. And one may say these ten remembrances before prayer or after; and one should also remember the four remembrances alluded to in the blessing of Ahavat Olam and in the section of tzitzit—for since they issued from the mouth of the Ari, may his righteous memory be for a blessing, that one must remember them in this place, one should not change but do both—and such is proper.
