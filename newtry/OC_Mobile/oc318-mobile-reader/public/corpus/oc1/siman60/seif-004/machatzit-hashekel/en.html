Seif katan 4, ‘He has fulfilled,’ etc. See the Beit Yosef, etc., in the name of the Rashba, who also writes this reasoning. ‘Although we say,’ etc. His meaning is that at the beginning of the chapter Hayah Korei the Mishnah teaches: If one was reading from the Torah and the time for Krias Shema arrived, if he directed his heart to fulfill the obligation, he fulfilled it; otherwise, he did not. The Gemara infers from this that mitzvos require intent, because the Mishnah states that if he did not direct his heart, he did not fulfill the obligation. The Gemara rejects this by explaining that the case concerns one reading to proofread. Therefore, if he did not direct his heart, he did not fulfill the obligation. Thus, even according to the view that mitzvos do not require intent, one reading to proofread does not fulfill the obligation. Certainly according to us, who rule that mitzvos require intent, one reading to proofread does not fulfill it. Why, then, does the Shulchan Aruch write that even if one was reading from the Torah or proofreading the sections, he fulfills the obligation? This is the Kesef Mishneh's question on the Rambam in chapter 2 of Hilchos Krias Shema, for the Rambam writes in the same manner as the Shulchan Aruch. The answer is that the Gemara concerns one who does not read according to the proper rules, as Rabbeinu Yonah writes, and Tosafos likewise explain. Tosafos write regarding Rashi's explanation, that he is proofreading the scroll and did not even intend to read. Tosafos object that, since mitzvos do not require intent, why should he not fulfill the obligation, given that he did read? They therefore explain that he does not pronounce the words according to their proper vocalization, but reads them according to their written form in order to identify deficient and plene spellings, such as the forms of ‘totafos’ and ‘mezuzos.’ ‘If he directed his heart’ is not literal; it means that he intended to read in the proper manner. Rabbeinu Yonah writes similarly, though in a different formulation. In any event, the phrase ‘reading to proofread’ in the Shulchan Aruch does not have the same meaning as ‘reading to proofread’ in the Gemara. In the Gemara it means that he does not read according to the proper rules, whereas in the Shulchan Aruch it means that he reads correctly with the vocalization, but his purpose is only to proofread, while also intending to fulfill the obligation.
And in the Kesef Mishneh, etc. ‘Even if he did not intend,’ etc., ‘he fulfilled the obligation.’ The Kesef Mishneh raises the Magen Avraham's question against the Rambam, as explained above, and answers that the Gemara's establishment of the case as one who reads to proofread follows only the initial assumption. According to our ruling, however, we rule like Rabbi Meir on page 14, that only the first verse requires concentration of the heart and no more.
The first verse, however, requires full concentration. Once there is full concentration during the first verse, it is of no consequence if he reads the remaining sections merely to proofread. These are the words of the Kesef Mishneh. Accordingly, the Mishnah's statement, ‘If he directed his heart, he fulfilled the obligation,’ refers to directing his heart during the first verse, even if he read the remaining sections to proofread. If he did not direct his heart during the first verse, he did not fulfill the obligation even if he read all the sections not for proofreading. This is the substance of the Kesef Mishneh's words. The reason according to the Kesef Mishneh may be that for the other sections he fulfills the obligation even without intent to fulfill, because he follows the Rashba's view, cited by the Magen Avraham in siman 63, seif katan 8, that only the first verse is D'Oraisa, whereas everything thereafter is D'Rabbanan. He also follows the Magen Avraham's statement in seif katan 3 that mitzvos D'Rabbanan do not require intent. The Magen Avraham may have intended this when he concluded this seif katan, ‘See what I wrote in siman 63, seif 5.’
And this is also implied by the Rambam's wording. The Rambam's wording is the same as that of the Shulchan Aruch. In truth, his intention is also that the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies the Kesef Mishneh's explanation. He cites the Rambam because the Kesef Mishneh's comments were written regarding the Rambam. According to the Magen Avraham's explanation, however, one must say that ‘reading to proofread’ in the Gemara and ‘reading to proofread’ in the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch use the same wording but have different meanings, as explained above, and this is strained.
