(Note 4) "He fulfilled," etc.—Abudraham, etc., in the name of Rashba. The meaning is that he also wrote this view. And even though we say, etc.—the meaning is in the beginning of the chapter He Was Reading we learned: "he was reading in the Torah and the time of Shema arrived—if he directed his heart to fulfill, he fulfilled; if not, he did not fulfill." And we say on this: learn from this that mitzvot require intention—from "if he did not direct his heart he did not fulfill." And Beit Yosef deflected "one who reads for proofreading"; therefore if he did not direct his heart he did not fulfill—behold even per the opinion that mitzvot do not require intention. Nevertheless, one who reads for proofreading did not fulfill—all the more so for us who hold mitzvot require intention, that one who reads for proofreading did not fulfill. If so, why did Shulchan Aruch write "even if he was reading from the Torah or proofreading the sections," etc., "he fulfilled"? And this is the Kesef Mishneh's question ch. 2 of Hilchot Kriat Shema on the Rambam, who wrote in the language of Shulchan Aruch—namely when he does not read as required, as Terumat HaDeshen wrote; and likewise Tosafot, who wrote on Rashi's explanation that explained "one who reads for proofreading" the book—even for reading he did not intend. And Tosafot asked: why did he not fulfill, since mitzvot do not require intention, and in any case he read? Therefore Tosafot explained that he does not read the words as required with their vowels but as written, in order to understand omissions and additions like totafot and mezuzat. And if he directed his heart—not specifically, but meaning to read as required, etc., end quote. And likewise Terumat HaDeshen wrote on this matter in another manner. In any case, "one who reads for proofreading" that Shulchan Aruch wrote is not the same meaning as "one who reads for proofreading" in the Gemara—for in the Gemara the meaning is that he does not read as required, and in Shulchan Aruch the meaning is that he reads as required with their vowels, only that his intention is solely to proofread but he intends to fulfill.

And in Kesef Mishneh, etc.—that even if he did not intend, etc., "he fulfilled"—for he raised Magen Avraham's question on the Rambam as above, and explained that the Gemara's words that establish "one who reads for proofreading" are according to what was thought. But for us who hold like R. Meir folio 14 that the first verse requires intention of the heart and no more:

But the first verse requires full intention; and since there is full intention in the first verse, for the rest of the sections even if he read for proofreading we have no concern—end quote Kesef Mishneh. And now what we learned "if he directed his heart he fulfilled"—this means in the first verse; even if he read the rest of the sections for proofreading. And if he did not direct his heart in the first verse, even if he read all the sections not for proofreading he did not fulfill—such is the essence of Kesef Mishneh's words. And the reason may be said according to Kesef Mishneh's view: in the rest of the sections, even if he did not intend to fulfill, he fulfilled—for he holds like Rashba (brought in Magen Avraham siman 63 note 8) that the first verse alone is from the Torah and from here onward is rabbinic. And he holds as Magen Avraham wrote note 3 here, that in rabbinic mitzvot intention is not required. And perhaps for this Magen Avraham intended in what he wrote in this note here; and see siman 63 se'if 5.

And in Kesef Mishneh on the Rambam's words—and this is Shulchan Aruch's language; and in truth Shulchan Aruch's intent also appears as Kesef Mishneh wrote. And the Rambam cited it since Kesef Mishneh's words are said about the Rambam—his meaning is possible that according to Magen Avraham one must say that "one who reads for proofreading" in the Gemara with "one who reads for proofreading" in the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch are equal in their language and differ in their explanations as above—and this is strained.
