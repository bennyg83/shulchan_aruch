Siman 64—some say, etc. As written in Rosh Hashanah 28: they sent to him, etc.; Rabbah said this teaches, etc.; Rava said to fulfill, etc.; and Rabbi Zeira is alone against them; and Rabbi Yosi is an individual, for the Tanna argues on him as written in the chapter "al efes" 114b, and so is the conclusion of the last chapter of Eruvin, and so we rule like Rav in chapter 2 of Chullin: a niddah who was compelled and immersed—Rav Nachman holds with him, as the poskim wrote.

And some say, etc. Like Rabbi Zeira in Rosh Hashanah there—and even though they sent to him and Rava and Rabbah argue on him, we rule like Rabbi Zeira, for Rabbi Zeira infers the Mishnah in Pesachim there with him, and Rabbi Yosi is explained with him; and in the Yerushalmi on the Mishnah in Rosh Hashanah there: "he was passing," etc.—he infers the beraita "mitzvot require kavanah," and the Tanna taught: if the listener intended and the one who sounds did not intend, etc.; and even though in the Gemara we learned them as forced readings, and also that which they sent to him—the Rambam already explained the chapter, even though he holds like Rabbi Zeira, as the Ran wrote, for according to Rabbi Zeira we say as written there "according to all," etc., not like Rava; and also according to Rabbi Zeira he also holds that one need not have kavanah, and that which he said "if he intends," etc.—for the sake of tekiah, as some commentators explained, for he establishes it like Rabbi Yosi, and Rabbi Yosi holds that orally they require kavanah; and Tosafot there 15a s.v. mitkayem, etc.

And such is the halachah. The plain meaning of the Mishnah and beraitot of chapter 3 of Rosh Hashanah—Kaf HaChayyim—and the changes in the Gemara are forced readings, as above; and so too in the Yerushalmi cited above.
