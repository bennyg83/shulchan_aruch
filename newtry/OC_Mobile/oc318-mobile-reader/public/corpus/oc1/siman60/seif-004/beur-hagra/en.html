Seif 4, ‘Some say,’ etc. This follows what is stated in Rosh Hashanah 28a: ‘They sent to him,’ etc.; Rabbah said, ‘This teaches,’ etc.; and Rava said, ‘In order to fulfill,’ etc. Rabbi Zeira is therefore a lone opinion against them. Rabbi Yose is likewise a lone opinion, since the first Tanna disagrees with him, as stated in Pesachim 114b. This is also the conclusion of the discussion at the beginning of the final chapter of Eruvin. Likewise, we rule like Rav in the second chapter of Chullin regarding a menstruant who was compelled to immerse, since Rav Nachman agrees with him, as the poskim explain there.
And some say, etc. This follows Rabbi Zeira in Rosh Hashanah there. Although they sent a ruling to him, and Rava and Rabbah disagree with him, we rule like Rabbi Zeira. Reish Lakish derives support for his view from the Mishnah in Pesachim there, and Rabbi Yose's reasoning accords with him. The Yerushalmi, commenting on the Mishnah in Rosh Hashanah concerning one who was passing behind a synagogue, etc., likewise infers: ‘This means that mitzvos require intent.’ It was also taught: ‘If the listener intended but the one sounding did not intend,’ etc. Although the Bavli offers answers to these proofs, they are strained answers. The ruling that was sent to Rabbi Zeira was already explained by the Rambam in a manner consistent with Rabbi Zeira, even though the Rambam rules like him, as the Ran writes. According to Rabbi Zeira, the case is explained as stated there, concerning one who says, ‘Eat,’ etc., unlike Rava. Nor can one say that Rabbi Zeira also holds that mitzvos do not require intent, and that his statement, ‘intend,’ etc., means only to intend to produce a shofar blast, as some commentators explain. The Gemara establishes his view according to Rabbi Yose, and Rabbi Yose holds in Arvei Pesachim that mitzvos require intent. See Tosafos there, 115a, s.v. Maskif, etc.
And so is the halachah. The straightforward meaning of the Mishnah and the baraisos at the end of the third chapter of Rosh Hashanah indicates this. The answers offered by the Gemara are strained, as explained above, and the Yerushalmi cited above also rules this way.
