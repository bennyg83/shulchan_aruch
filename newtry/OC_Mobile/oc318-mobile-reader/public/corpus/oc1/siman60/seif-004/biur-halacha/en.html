Some say that mitzvot do not require kavanah—whether he does it himself and does not intend to fulfill his obligation thereby, or whether he hears from another, such as megillah and shofar, and did not intend in the sounding to fulfill the obligation of the mitzvah (Beit Yosef, siman 213 and 219). And likewise in all mitzvot that depend on speech, such as Keriat Shema and Birkat HaMazon; and some say that in a matter that consists only of speech, all agree it requires kavanah. Beit Yosef siman 489. And the Magen Avraham [means]: that he intends for this action that he is doing, only that he does not intend to fulfill the mitzvah thereby—such as in the matter of tekiot that he sounds to sing or to teach, and the like in other mitzvot; but if he is dealing casually and the mitzvah comes about in his hand, such as one who blows into a shofar and a tekiah comes out in his hand, and the like in other mitzvot—all agree he has not fulfilled. Ramban in Milchamot on Rosh Hashanah, and so in Berakhot 13a. And the like is proven from Terumat HaDeshen in what he wrote regarding one who reads to correct—see there. And know further: specifically if he knows that he is still obligated in this mitzvah that he is doing; but if he thinks he is exempt from it, such as one who took a lulav on day one of Sukkot and thinks it is erev Sukkot, or thinks this lulav is invalid—all agree he has not fulfilled. And proof from what is written in siman 475 seif 4: if he thought it was chol or that this was not matzah, he did not fulfill his obligation—and behold in a matter of eating, one who does not intend is like one who intends, as Magen Avraham wrote there; and nevertheless he did not fulfill, and one could say to reject: that there it is according to what we rule in general that mitzvot require kavanah, and only in matters of eating, on account of the benefit that he enjoys, we cannot cancel his act as if he did not do it, and the matter is considered as if he intended; and therefore we say that if he did not know that today is Pesach we cannot say about him as if he intended—unlike if we say that mitzvot never require kavanah, for then even in such a case he would fulfill; and this is the meaning of what the Ran in Rosh Hashanah brings as proof for this law, for otherwise why would the father of Shmuel need to tie him when Persians compelled him—to teach us a greater matter, that even in one of these ways he fulfills; and if so, it is still difficult according to what Rava said there, etc., who holds that mitzvot do not require kavanah; but certainly even if they do not require kavanah, in such a case he does not fulfill—and it is astonishing on the rabbi, author of Levush, who concludes there a reason for this law that mitzvot require kavanah. And one who intends explicitly not to fulfill—all agree he does not fulfill. Beit Yosef in siman 489.

And some say that they require kavanah—meaning: from the Torah; thus Rashbam wrote explicitly in Pesachim 114b s.v. af al gim, and so the plain meaning of the sugya in Rosh Hashanah 28b in the Gemara: "but remembrance of teruah is written," etc.—it implies that their dispute is from the Torah; and so wrote Levush and the other acharonim—not like Pri Megadim, who is in doubt about this. And know that all this is in other mitzvot; but a mitzvah that depends on eating, such as a kezayit of matzah on Pesach, and likewise eating a kezayit in the sukkah on the first night—the view of Shulchan Aruch later in siman 475 seif 4 is that he fulfills bedi'eved even if he did not intend, and Bach is stringent there as well; see there. And see in Bach and Pri Megadim in siman 8 and siman 625, for it appears from their words that mitzvot of tzitzit and sukkah—the kavanah in them is for impeding like other mitzvot; and accordingly, if they called him to the Torah and he takes his tallit or the congregation's tallit to ascend to the bimah—then its time is passing and presumably he does not then intend in putting it on to fulfill the positive commandment of tzitzit—he thereby transgresses the positive commandment if he does not intend for the sake of the mitzvah, and then he may also bless; and the world is not careful about this, and perhaps their reason is that since he does not wish to wear the tallit then and wears it only for honor of the Torah for a short time, this is not a wearing that obligates him in tzitzit—similar to one who wears to show the buyer his size, who is then exempt from tzitzit. And the advised counsel is to act as Sha'arei Efraim wrote—I brought his words above in siman 14 seif 3 in Biur Halachah; see there.

To fulfill—see in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 9 what he wrote: one who blesses with children does not fulfill. And nevertheless it seems to me that if he did not eat enough that his obligation of Birkat HaMazon is rabbinic, it is possible to rely bedi'eved on the view of Radbaz, whom some acharonim agreed with, that for a rabbinic mitzvah one need not have kavanah to fulfill, and one need not return and bless—even though Taz wrote that this is like one who intends not to fulfill, and he brought proof for this from the words of the Rosh—it is astonishing, for the Rosh and Rif, according to their view, hold in general that mitzvot require kavanah, as the Rosh wrote in Rosh Hashanah; but per one who holds that mitzvot do not require kavanah, I would say that here too he fulfills, like one who sounds to teach; and in any case his proof is astonishing, as Eshel Avraham and Avnei Nezer questioned—see there. Lechat'chila, one who is obligated in Birkat HaMazon and wishes to bless with children to educate them should act in one of two ways: either he should intend to fulfill thereby for himself as well, and he fulfills thereby, as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Avnei Nezer; or he should intend explicitly not to fulfill and bless afterward for himself.

And such is the halachah—see in Mishnah Berurah what he wrote, and so from the explanation of the Gra, etc.; see there in seif 4 in Shulchan Aruch in Biur HaGra, that he strains himself to establish the ruling of Shulchan Aruch per the view that mitzvot do not require kavanah; but in truth Shulchan Aruch himself ruled in siman 60 that they require kavanah. It would be better to resolve it because counting is rabbinic, as Avnei Nezer resolves—or that he holds there is no distinction in this. And regarding whether one must return and bless when he did it the first time—even for a Torah mitzvah without kavanah—we wrote in the name of Magen Avraham that one need not return and bless; and I found that Shakh also wrote so in siman 489—see there; and see in Pri Megadim in the Petichah, that he is in doubt whether that which Shulchan Aruch rules lehalachah that they require kavanah is from certainty or because a doubt in Torah [law] is stringent—and there is a practical difference regarding rabbinic [mitzvot]; and in his composition in siman 489 and siman 489 he wrote a practical difference regarding berakhah as well—he contradicts what he wrote in the Petichah, see there—and he is not the first in this, for also Or Zarua wrote that the reason of Shulchan Aruch is on account of doubt, as brought in his name in Avnei Nezer siman 213; therefore it was difficult for him in several places—see there in Avnei Nezer. And it appears from the words of Pri Megadim in siman 489 s.k. 8 that he wishes to side also with the view of Magen Avraham in this, and therefore Magen Avraham holds that one need not return and bless on account of doubt; but when we examine Magen Avraham well, there is no proof for this—that his intent regarding berakhah is that one must be concerned for the view of these rabbanan not to bless again. Also in Avnei Nezer siman 213 he rejected the words of Or Zarua and holds that Shulchan Aruch is not in doubt at all. Also in Biur HaGra in this siman seif 4 s.v. vekhen halachah—it is proven explicitly that he holds like Avnei Nezer. And know that what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah "where it is evident," etc.—such as in Keriat Shema through the order of tefillah, and in other mitzvot through the berakhot or through preparation for the mitzvah, such as what they said in the Yerushalmi chapter 10 halakhah 3 regarding matzah: whether he intended or did not intend, since he reclined, there is a presumption that he intended; and in the Yerushalmi of Rosh Hashanah regarding "he was passing behind the synagogue and heard the sound of a shofar or the sound of a megillah," etc.—one might say when passing; but when standing, there is a presumption of intent, and he explains: specifically if he stands outside the synagogue, presumably it does not refer to a mitzvah; but if he stands inside the synagogue and hears the sound of a shofar, presumably he stands for the sake of kavanah, as written in Yeshuot Ya'akov in this siman; and all such cases we can find in all mitzvot. And know further: even according to Chayei Adam, in the Keriat Shema of Shabbat night—according to our custom that we read it in the synagogue with its berakhot before its time and rely on reading it again at its time—then even if it happened that he read it in the synagogue at its time but without intent to fulfill, it appears that he must return and read it, for the law here is not evident from reading it in the order of tefillah, for he always reads it in the order of tefillah even not at its time in order to juxtapose redemption to tefillah.
