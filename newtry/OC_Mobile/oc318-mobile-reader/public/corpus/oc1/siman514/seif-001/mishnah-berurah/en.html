{1} To extinguish a fire on Yom Tov: for extinguishing on Yom Tov was not permitted because of monetary loss.
{2} His house that burned: that is, even if he has no other house to dwell in and he will be prevented from joy of Yom Tov, according to this view it is forbidden, for nevertheless this is not considered a need of ochel nefesh.
{3} And even in order to, etc.: that is to say, and all the more so if his intent is to extinguish because he cares for it that it not burn entirely, even though he intends that it remain to cook with again that same day, it is forbidden.
{4} Or the pot: and this extinguishing is not considered ochel nefesh even if he has no other place to set the pot, because extinguishing the log does not assist ochel nefesh at all; rather it only prevents damage by extinguishing it. And although in any case this is no less than machshirei ochel nefesh that cannot be done from erev Yom Tov, which we hold are permitted as below in siman 509 in the gloss, according to this view not every machshir of ochel nefesh was permitted, but only those that are closer to the preparation of the food.
{5} Or in order to have marital relations, etc.: for it is forbidden to have marital relations before a lamp; and it is stated in the Gemara that even if he has no other house to take the lamp out to, and no vessel to overturn upon the lamp, it is nevertheless forbidden, and even if it is mitzvah relations; and see Biur Halacha.
{6} If it is possible to save the pot: from smoking, for example if he can make a fire elsewhere and set it there, or if he at least has a place to throw the log outside and not extinguish it literally.
{7} It is permitted to extinguish: he holds that this is an actual need of ochel nefesh, and extinguishing is permitted just as the Torah permitted baking and cooking; and see Taz, siman 502, that if the fire is large and burns the dish, and he needs to remove some of it, even though thereby the fire will certainly go out from those [pieces], it is permitted.
{8} And as above: and so the Acharonim copied it; and also regarding the law after this they copied like the Rama.
{9} If the house will burn: from the fact that the Rama skips and did not write his ruling also regarding the house filling with smoke, it implies that in this he agrees with the Mechaber that even if he has no other house it is forbidden to extinguish.
{10} He will not have a place: but if he will find a place to eat, even in a gentile's house, it is forbidden to extinguish (Magen Avraham); and Eliyah Rabbah is uncertain about this.
{11} And he will lose his meal: it does not mean that his prepared meal will be ruined by the fire, for in that case even if he would afterward find a place to eat it would also be permitted to extinguish for the meal itself; rather the case is that he can save the meal itself without any extinguishing of the fire at all, and only because he will have no place to eat, his Yom Tov meal will be nullified for him, and even so it is permitted, for this is included in extinguishing for the need of ochel nefesh; and the same law applies when eating utensils will burn and he cannot obtain other utensils for his meal, it is likewise permitted to extinguish.
{12} It is forbidden to extinguish: and see above in siman 334, seif 26, that nowadays when we dwell among the gentiles and there is concern of danger to life, it is permitted to extinguish even on Shabbos.
