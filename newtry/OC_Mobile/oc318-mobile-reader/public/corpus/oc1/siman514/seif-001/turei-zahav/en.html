Or the pot. Although it was for food for souls need, nevertheless here there are only preparers for food for souls, and permitted only for R' Yehuda who expounds "for all your needs"; but we hold like the Rabbis who expound "it" and not its preparers, as in the Gemara chapter 2 of Beitzah; only it is difficult: in chapter Ein Tzadin they say the halacha is like R' Yehuda; and Tosafos answered this is in what is written there "halacha and we do not instruct thus"; and therefore Rif and Rambam's view is the first view that they forbade here; but those who permit that Rama brings are Rosh who wrote: that which is forbidden to extinguish so the pot not become smoky is when it is possible to save it from smoke without extinguishing, such as removing it from this fire and placing it to cook on another fire; and if he has no fire and must cook on this fire, and if he does not extinguish the pot will become smoky — permitted to extinguish for food for souls need as permitted to bake and cook; however it is difficult, for this is like the Rabbis, and in chapter Ein Tzadin ruled like R' Yehuda; and perhaps because we do not instruct thus, or in a matter that is not actual food for souls they were stricter — end of his words; also Ran wrote they distinguished among preparers according to what they are; and Mahari Chagiz challenged on this that when it is possible to save it, even R' Yehuda agrees forbidden, like scraping an oven siman 507 — if possible to bake without scraping, forbidden to scrape even for R' Yehuda as Rashi implies there as I wrote; therefore he rejected these words of Rosh; and the resolution: certainly regarding scraping the oven R' Yehuda permits from the Torah in every case even when possible to bake without scraping, only because of moving the clay they forbade there as Rosh wrote explicitly there the reason is because of moving, as I wrote siman 506 seif 4 in our words — see there; and that here it is permitted for R' Yehuda even when possible to save, and there is no prohibition of moving, for there is no muktzeh, only for the Rabbis forbidden unless impossible to save it; and in this permitted also if he takes the burning wood from there, for wood is permitted in moving on Yom Tov; and Mordechai wrote that the case of the house not becoming smoky applies specifically like R' Yehuda, but for the pot even for the Rabbis permitted, for it is actual food for souls.

Or in order to have marital relations on his bed. In the Gemara daf 22b Abba Mari bar Martha from Abaye: what is the law to extinguish a lamp because of another matter (explanation: relations)? He said: possible in another house? He has no other house — what is possible? Make a partition (Rashi: with a sheet). He has none to make a partition — possible to turn a vessel over it? He has no vessel — what? He said: forbidden. He sat. One does not extinguish a chip of wood to pity it; but if so the house not become smoky — permitted; thus for Yom Tov need to enjoy the day it is permitted. He said: this is R' Yehuda's, who said preparers permitted, who expounds "for all your needs," and this too is your need; and relations too, for I speak like the Rabbis — Rashi: and I who forbade like the Rabbis who expound "it" and not preparers. And it is difficult: what did he answer him first "possible in another house" and did not answer immediately forbidden, as every answerer to a questioner for what purpose does he advise going to another house — this is obvious permitted; the advisor gave it to him; and it appears since the halacha is like R' Yehuda only we do not instruct thus, therefore he did not want to say forbidden explicitly, for in truth it is permitted from the law whenever impossible otherwise; therefore he said "possible in another house," and did not say go to another house, but meant: since possible in another house, forbidden here; and so the intent afterward with partition and turning a vessel — we automatically know if he lacks all these, permitted; and after they asked if he lacks all these how shall he act, he said forbidden — explanation: he did not want to rule explicitly permitted but forbade him after he already revealed there is permission whenever impossible otherwise; he will know there was halacha and we do not instruct thus; and now it is correct that he said "for I speak to the Rabbis" and did not say "and I hold like the Rabbis," but certainly what I told you is per the Rabbis, since we do not instruct thus like R' Yehuda; and this sugya is like the sugya of Ein Tzadin: R' Nachman stood before Rava and was passing a knife at the mouth of a basket; he said: is the master sharpening or removing fat? He said: removing fat; and he saw he intended to sharpen and thought halacha is like R' Yehuda and we do not instruct thus — Rashi: every person may do, but we do not instruct thus to the public lest they treat lightly even preparers that can be done erev Yom Tov; if so, Abaye intended this way not to reveal permission explicitly; and it appears this was Rokeach's view that Beit Yosef brought in his name: they extinguish a chip so the house not become smoky when he cannot sit, or for marital relations; but if he can turn a vessel on the lamp or take the lamp to another house, not extinguish as we say in chapter Yom Tov — end of his words. And it is difficult, for in chapter Yom Tov Abaye said explicitly forbidden — except as said, that answer was not per true law, otherwise he should have answered immediately forbidden. I wrote this although elsewhere they wrote to forbid in general — that is the manner of ruling for the public although not so explicitly; nevertheless they relied on what is written regarding sharpening a knife, and one reason is for R' Yehuda; but Ramban distinguished between them as explained in Beit Yosef; and so I saw Rashal in chapter 2 that Abaye's words are the way of "we do not instruct thus"; and he also was precise in Abaye's wording that he did not say "and I hold like the Rabbis" as I wrote; and the difference: if he did one act of permission in a matter where he has no partition and no turning a vessel, there is no sin upon him, for Rava and Abaye in chapter Ein Tzadin regarding a knife also did permission that we do not instruct thus; and what Maharam wrote in Mordechai chapter Kirah to make a partition specifically before Shabbat before books so he does his needs on Shabbat — it is a stringency l'chatchila; and it fits what we hold in what Abaye said possible to make a partition, for it is explained siman 515 forbidden to make a partition on Shabbat for marital relations; and if one thought to say here forbidden by law for relations, it would be Yom Tov on Shabbat; if so, why did Abaye permit making a partition here; but per our explanation it is correct: by law permitted to extinguish for relations, only we do not instruct thus; if so, no complete benefit in making the partition, and it is like making a partition for modesty generally that is permitted as siman 515; so it appears to me; likewise turning a vessel permitted for relations as siman 276 at the end — see there.
