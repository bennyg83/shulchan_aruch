1) [Se'if 1] It is forbidden to extinguish a fire on Yom Tov. The Torah permitted only kindling, without which food preparation is impossible. Regarding kindling we apply the principle that since it was permitted when needed, it was also permitted when not needed for food preparation. See above, at the beginning of siman 511. Extinguishing, however, is a primary category of melachah and remains forbidden as on Shabbos. Levush. See above, siman 278, subsections 1 and 2, regarding which forms of extinguishing incur liability. See also siman 334, se'if 26, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
2) There. It is forbidden to extinguish, etc. Responsa Shav Yaakov, Orach Chaim, siman 15, provides justification for the practice of having a non-Jew come to the synagogue and extinguish the lamps on the night of Shabbos and Yom Tov. Machazik Berachah, subsection 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 13. I already cited his words above, siman 334, subsection 125, and siman 307, subsection 38. See there.
3) There. It is forbidden to extinguish, etc. Regarding smoking on Yom Tov, we have already written about it above, siman 511, subsections 35, 36, and 37; take the ruling from there.
4) There. Even if he sees his house burning. Even if his house will burn and he will have no other house in which to eat, extinguishing is forbidden. This is the view of Shulchan Aruch, which rules according to Rif and Rambam, as explained in Beis Yosef. See there. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 2 of Beitzah, siman 30, rules likewise. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, also writes this according to Shulchan Aruch. See there. Rama, however, in Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, disagrees with the aforementioned words of Beis Yosef and permits extinguishing if one will have no other place in which to eat. This is why Rama presents this permission here in his gloss to Shulchan Aruch as an alternative opinion. See below, subsection 23.
5) There. If there is no danger to life. When danger to life is present, extinguishing is permitted even on Shabbos. Beitzah 22a; Beis Yosef; Levush; Olas Shabbos there. See above, at the beginning of siman 329, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
6) There. A piece of wood may not be extinguished, etc. The Sages did not permit every preparatory act needed for food. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, subsection 1. See above, siman 509, subsection 1.
7) There. A piece of wood may not be extinguished, etc. If one has a place where he can throw it outside, he should throw it there. If it goes out, it goes out, because he does not intend to extinguish it. Levush; Ateres Zekeinim.
8) There. A piece of wood may not be extinguished, etc. From here it follows that women should be admonished not to cover a fire on Yom Tov. Tosafos, Beitzah 22a. In the name of Sefer HaAgudah, however, I found it written that one may cover the fire with earth at night so that it will be available the next day on Yom Tov. Beis Yosef; Ateres Zekeinim. We have already written about this law above, siman 502, from subsection 12 through subsection 18; take the ruling from there.
9) There. Even so that the house will not become smoky, etc. It is certainly forbidden if one is concerned that the piece of wood might burn. Shitah Mekubetzes writes that this applies even if one intends to put it away for another meal on Yom Tov. Since he does not presently need to extinguish it, we are concerned that he might not derive benefit from it. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 3.
10) There. Or the pot. This extinguishing is not considered food preparation, even when he has no other place to set the pot. Extinguishing the piece of wood does not contribute anything to the food but merely prevents damage. Although this appears no less than preparatory work for food that could not have been performed before Yom Tov, which we rule is permitted as stated above, siman 509, in the gloss, this opinion holds that not all preparatory work for food was permitted. Only acts more directly connected to preparing the food were permitted. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 4.
11) There. Or the pot. This implies that even if he has no other fire to which he can move the pot, and if he does not extinguish the wood the pot will become completely smoke-blackened, it is nevertheless forbidden. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 4, law 4, writes likewise according to Rambam, as does Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. This follows Shulchan Aruch, which rules according to Rif and Rambam, as written above, subsection 4. According to the alternative opinion cited by Rama in the gloss, however, extinguishing is permitted in such a case. Maggid Mishneh there writes likewise.
