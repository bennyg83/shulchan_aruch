[11] [Levush] Some say there is no permission, etc. So Beit Yosef and Rashal wrote per Rosh's view that I wrote in seif katan 7 that all who touch the lamp itself is extinguishing; but Bach wrote that Rosh agrees it is permitted to place something that prevents, and therefore he permits also to insert it in sand on a weekday or to place a knife or other Yom Tov-movable object on a lamp so it burns only the measure he wants; and he wrote so in the name of Rashal; and I wondered in Eliyah Zuta in Yam Shel Shlomo who forbids l'chatchila; and now I found Rashal wrote so in his explanation to Semag daf 52 and contradicts himself; also it appears to me a proof for Bach's explanation in Tur — it is not specifically "he wants to light" that he said, for I saw in Sefer Tzidah LaDerech he did not write at all "he wants to light"; and so Taz and Magen Avraham ruled to permit in all mentioned above — for Rosh forbids only taking from the body of the burning object; but when he places something outside it, permitted; and it is implied from Magen Avraham's words that even into water it is permitted to place the piece of candle; in this he was stringent unless in slight need; but requires study on Maharil mentioned above who forbids when sticking; and perhaps Or Zarua has his reason who forbids in Hagahot Asheiri also to cut with fire, and we who hold it is permitted — also permitted to stick; but I saw in Mordechai's margin on parchment that causing extinguishing on Yom Tov is forbidden, such as sticking until half the candle in the wall so it falls and extinguishes — if the light catches he puts water on it, for this causing extinguishing is permitted from piety Semak — until here his wording; and perhaps when sticking it is like taking from the body; and per this it appears to me one should not place a candle in a vessel called zwengel unless before lighting, for it goes out when it reaches there (and in Gan Netoa he erred as is his way in Eliyah Zuta books and thought Maharil wrote thus); and at the end of siman 488 I wrote more on this.
