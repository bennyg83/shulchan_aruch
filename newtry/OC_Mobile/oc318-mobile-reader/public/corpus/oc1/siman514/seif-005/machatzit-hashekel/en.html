Permitted even for Rosh, etc. For in tractate Beitzah daf 22a it is taught: one who removes oil from a burning lamp is liable because of extinguishing. And Tosafos challenged: behold removing oil is only causing extinguishing that causes the lamp to extinguish faster, and we hold causing extinguishing is permitted. And Tosafos answered: removing is actual extinguishing, for immediately when he takes from the oil it dims the lamp's light and it does not illuminate as much as when there was much oil in it; and Rosh challenged on this — if so, the mishnah in Shabbos daf 30 that one should not pierce an eggshell and fill it with oil and place it on the mouth of the lamp so it drips, etc., and we say the reason is we decree lest he remove from oil in the tube, since the oil is not in the lamp itself he would not think to do a prohibition; and with a tube even if he removes from the oil it does not dim the lamp's light, for even if he takes from oil in the tube, nevertheless it drips from the egg into the lamp as before; and in the lamp itself there is not less oil than before he removed; and nevertheless we decree lest he remove; therefore the reason is because it causes faster extinguishing. And it is not comparable to causing extinguishing, which is making a partition in an earthenware vessel before the flame as above — he does not touch the burning object but only does something outside it that causes extinguishing when the flame reaches there; but here oil and wick both cause lighting, and one who diminishes from one of them and hastens extinguishing is liable — end of Rosh. And the difference between Tosafos and Rosh: for Tosafos it is permitted to cut from a wax candle (by fire in the middle of the candle) even while burning, for in such a case it does not dim its light; for Rosh it is forbidden, for in any case it causes faster extinguishing and acts on the lamp itself, not outside, as the dispute is explained in seif 3 (and see Magen Avraham's explanation of Rosh's words). And Yeshuot Shalom wrote it is permitted to take one of the distant tallow pieces; in my humble opinion per Tosafos it is permitted, for in such a case it does not dim its light, and it is no worse than cutting a wax candle permitted per Tosafos as above; but even per Rosh who forbids on a wax candle, nevertheless to take a tallow piece he agrees it is permitted, for specifically on a wax candle when he takes from the body of the burning object — for the whole candle is one connection, unlike a tallow piece that he does not take from the body of the burning object, for the pieces have no connection to each other — also permitted for Rosh. And Taz wrote in seif katan 4: nevertheless it is permitted to distance some oil so he can grasp the wick and take it out somewhat so it will light better afterward; and in Or Sameach and Chemdas Moshe they disagree, for in any case when he distances it he does a prohibition — what help is it that afterward the oil returns to the wick and lights better. Taz further was lenient in seif katan 6: if he wants to tilt a wax or tallow lamp downward a bit so the wax or tallow melts and he returns it upward, and does this many times so quickly all the candle burns — permitted even for Rosh, for through this tilting he kindles more and it is comparable to increasing wicks that Rama permits in the gloss; and on this too Or Sameach and Chemdas Moshe disagreed, for specifically in increasing wicks although it causes quick extinguishing, nevertheless all the oil is burned, unlike Taz's case where that wax or tallow melts and drips to the ground before being lost and is not burned — and one should forbid — see there.
