An unnecessary light. Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 35, writes that even after one has recited the blessing over the candles, one may add further lights in honor of Yom Tov. A light is considered unnecessary only when one kindles it in order to display his wealth and generosity before others. People are not careful even about this, but they have an authority upon whom to rely, for Rambam permits it.
A synagogue light. Rashba questions this permission and tends to forbid it, because the Torah permitted only food preparation or something enjoyed by everyone, whereas this light is only for honor. See Beit Yosef there. Although the accepted ruling is that it is permitted, the recent custom of igniting various kinds of gunpowder on Simchas Torah is plainly forbidden. Even though it is done for rejoicing, it is not something enjoyed by everyone and is no better than mugmar, which is forbidden. See what I wrote in siman 669. Nevertheless, one may attach gunpowder to a candle even though it causes the candle to go out, as stated in seif 3.
There is a mitzvah. This implies that it is permitted for this reason even when no one is present in the synagogue. If it is close to nightfall, one may light even in his own house, because he needs the light at that time. Yam Shel Shlomo; Beit Yosef.
