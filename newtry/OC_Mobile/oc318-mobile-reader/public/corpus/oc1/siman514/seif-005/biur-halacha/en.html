(א) A candle of betalah — see Mishna Berurah; and see Meiri on Beitzah daf 12, who wrote: a candle of betalah — meaning a candle they make not for need, but they do not wish to be without a candle even at the hour when they sleep erev Shabbat; the reason is that this is not a Yom Tov need at all. However, people who are afraid to sleep if there is no burning candle — it is reasonable that it is permitted, for it is included in bodily need, and as it is permitted to heat hot water for one's feet; and as we find in the Gemara daf 25: Bilta, wife of R' Nachman — they permitted her to go out with a torch because of fear. And see siman 522 in Magen Avraham there. And know that Magen Avraham brought in the name of Rabbi Shlomo Luria that the world is not careful about a candle of betalah, and they have upon what to rely, for Rambam permits erev Shabbat; and we did not copy this because most poskim are strict in this, and as a safek in the Jerusalem Talmud — see Rashba in his chiddushim, and in Avodat HaKodesh, and in Ra'ash, and Tur, and Rivash, and Shulchan Shel Lavan in the name of Rabbeinu Yeshaya, who permitted only candles of the synagogue; and also the Gra HaZ omitted this leniency; therefore one should not be lenient in this. And know further: a yahrzeit candle — if they did not light it on erev Yom Tov, they should light it in any case in the room where they eat, for it adds light in the room; and it is better that they light it in the synagogue and thereby escape the concern of a candle of betalah. And in a time of pressing need it is possible that there is permission in every manner, for it is like a candle of mitzvah that is for honoring one's ancestors — so it is explained in Kesav Sofer.

For behold in lighting it there is a mitzvah — see Mishna Berurah that even if there is no person there — and Magen Avraham. And in truth, although so it appears from Berachos 53 that where there is no attendant it is for honor, and it is brought also above siman 288 — see there — nevertheless it is not proven from there except that at night they lit a lamp in the synagogue for honor; but by day and not at prayer time, and also when the congregation already went home, we do not find that it is honor to light; and see Beit Yosef in explaining Rosh and Rashba's words — it is proven that even by day when they are not praying, for Rosh there is still mitzvah; but nevertheless perhaps it deals when there are at least some people there and there is honor in the synagogue; and so appears from responsa of R' Yeshaya brought in Shevut Yaakov that honor of the sanctuary is also for honor of creatures — see there, i.e. when there is a person there. And in truth it is explained in Avodat HaKodesh to Rashba that he is in doubt on the essence of synagogue lamp law by day, and even at prayer time, all the more not at prayer time, and also when no person is there — I do not know if one may be lenient; requires study. And know further that in Be'er Heitev he tried to find merit for what was practiced in his time to light by a non-Jew on Shabbat after Mincha when Yom Tov follows; and many Acharonim cried out against this; and mainly the matter is not understood — for what need did those who practice enter a narrow breach to permit lighting on Shabbat for Yom Tov need, when on Yom Tov itself one may light; and if because at twilight there should be light in the synagogue to enter Yom Tov with joy — better they should tell the non-Jew to light at twilight, which is permitted at least for some poskim in a place of mitzvah as explained above siman 276 in Mishna Berurah seif katan 24 — see there; but in truth here too it is hard to be lenient, for what mitzvah is here; and regarding Ne'ilah it will be explained in its place.
