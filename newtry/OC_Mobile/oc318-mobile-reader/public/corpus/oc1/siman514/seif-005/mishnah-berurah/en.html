{30} That is, that he has no need of it: and needless to say if he has no need at all in his kindling it is forbidden [and according to several poskim there is liability of lashes in this; see siman 518]; but even if he does this for some purpose, such as to display his wealth and his ornaments, since he does not intend thereby for a Yom Tov need or for a bodily need at all, it is forbidden. However, if he does this for the honor of Yom Tov, it is obvious that it is permitted, and there is also a mitzvah; and even though he has already lit lamps of blessing, when he adds lamps to increase light in his house there is joy of Yom Tov; and specifically at night, but by day it is certainly forbidden in his house. And if there is a bris milah in the house where they are accustomed to light lamps, it is reasonable that it is permitted, for all this is honor and affection for the mitzvah.
{31} But [lamps] of the synagogue are not considered, etc.: and needless to say at night, for there is here a need of the mitzvah of the day, that if lamps are not burning the people will not gather there to pray; but even by day it is likewise permitted, for it is honor of the Place, as it is said, "Honor Hashem with lights."
{32} After Mincha: and all the more so before Mincha it is permitted, for that too is for the honor of the prayer.
{33} There is a mitzvah, etc.: that is to say, even if he has no need of the light, for the day is still great, nevertheless there is a mitzvah to light because of the honor of the synagogue. And if it is already near nightfall, even in his house it is permitted to light, for behold he needs it at that hour.
{34} For that hour: and it implies that even if there is no person in the synagogue it is permitted for this reason; and see Biur Halacha.
{35} And to prepare the wicks, etc.: and even to set wax candles for the need of the night is forbidden, for even mere effort is forbidden because of preparation, and even from the first day of Yom Tov to the second.
