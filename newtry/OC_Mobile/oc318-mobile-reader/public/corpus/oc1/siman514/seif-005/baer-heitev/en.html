Purposeless. And all that one lights and does for the honor of Yom Tov is not called a purposeless lamp (Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 34); see Magen Avraham.
Hour. It implies that even if there is no person in the synagogue it is permitted for this reason. And if it is near nightfall, even in his house it is permitted to light, for he needs it at that hour (Yam Shel Shlomo, Beis Yosef, Magen Avraham). (Says the glossator: I was always astonished at what I saw, lighting lamps in the synagogue through a gentile from Shabbos to Yom Tov while the day is still great after Mincha, for seemingly it is a complete prohibition and one is obligated to protest the gentile, as the law of a gentile who does melacha for a Jew, as explained in the poskim; but Hashem, may He be blessed, favored me to learn a permission: for according to what Beis Yosef wrote in siman 276, the reason that a fixed hire does not help for a lamp is because the Jew's body benefits from it; if so, here this does not apply, for one does not see by its light except at night, and it is like other matters such as a gentile making a garment, which is permitted with a fixed hire in siman 252; and even according to what they wrote that in a Jew's house a fixed hire does not help, that reason is because of suspicion, as explained there, and for a public [matter] there is no suspicion, and this is clear there in the poskim. And several more poskim, the Ran and those who follow him, permit also with a lamp with a fixed hire, and it is proper to rely on them in a place of mitzvah and joy of Yom Tov; and several poskim permit even one shevus in a place of mitzvah; if so they have upon what to rely. The custom of Israel is Torah. And upon this permission one may also rely regarding what is practiced on Yom Kippur, that the gentile lights lamps in the synagogue before Ne'ilah and they pray by their light; for seemingly we have no proof to permit, but according to these matters there is support and firm basis to permit. As it appears to me, to judge favorably the custom of Israel.)
The wicks. It appears to me that even to set wax candles for the need of the night is forbidden; see siman 503 (Magen Avraham).
