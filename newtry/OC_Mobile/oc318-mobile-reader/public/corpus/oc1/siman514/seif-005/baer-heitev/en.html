(1) Purposeless. And all that one lights and does for the honor of Yom Tov is not called a purposeless lamp — Yeshuot Shalom siman 34 see Magen Avraham.

(2) Hour. It implies even if there is no person in the synagogue it is permitted for this reason. And if he is near dark, even in his house it is permitted to light, for he needs it at that hour — Yeshuot Shalom, Beit Yosef, Magen Avraham. (The editor said: I was always astonished at what I saw — lighting candles in the synagogue by a non-Jew from Shabbat to Yom Tov while the day is still great after Mincha — for seemingly it is a complete prohibition and one must protest the non-Jew as the law of a non-Jew doing work for an Israelite, as explained in poskim; but Heaven graced me to learn permission: for per what Beit Yosef wrote siman 276, the reason that cutting a lamp does not help is because the Israelite's body benefits from it; if so, here this does not apply, for one does not see its light except at night, and it is like other matters such as a non-Jew making a garment that is permitted with cutting siman 252; and even per what they wrote that in an Israelite's house cutting does not help — the reason is suspicion as explained there, and for the public there is no suspicion, and this is clear there in poskim. And several more poskim — Ran and his supporters — permit also with a lamp with cutting, and one may rely on them in a place of mitzvah and joy of Yom Tov; and many poskim permit even one rabbinic prohibition in a place of mitzvah; if so they have upon what to rely. The custom of Israel is Torah. And on this permission one may also rely on what is practiced on Yom Kippur — the non-Jew lights candles in the synagogue before Ne'ilah prayer and they pray by their light; for seemingly we have no proof to permit, but per these matters there is support and firm basis to permit. As it appears to me, to judge favorably the custom of Israel.)
