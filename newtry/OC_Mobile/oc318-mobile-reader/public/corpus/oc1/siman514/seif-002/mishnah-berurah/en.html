{13} In order to distance: that is to say, and it will go out quickly.
{14} It is considered extinguishing: and needless to say it is forbidden to take oil from the oil in the lamp while it is burning, for since he removes a little oil from the lamp, thereby its light dims somewhat, and behold this is like extinguishing.
{15} And it is forbidden: and pieces of tallow or wax lying in the lamp, it is permitted to take one of the pieces that are far from the wicks, since the wick is not yet drawing from them at all (Magen Avraham and other later authorities).
{16} Immediately he extinguishes: that is to say, that its light is at least diminished, and it is considered extinguishing; and what does it help that he will light it afterward? When is this said? When he removes it entirely from the oil; but it is permitted to draw out its upper portion outside the oil so that the flame take hold on that portion that he drew out of the oil and the wick burn well. And if he cannot grasp the wick to draw out a portion of it outside the oil because the oil is very close to the burning flame, it is permitted to raise the lamp a bit so that the oil retreat somewhat backward and he can grasp the wick and draw out a portion so that the flame take hold on it, and afterward he again bring the oil near the fire as it was.
{17} To add wicks: and likewise it is permitted to fold a burning wax candle and also light the lower end so that it burn quickly.
{18} And it will go out quickly: see Biur Halacha that if he intends that its light go out, it is not for all opinions, and is only according to the view of those who permit below in seif 3; and according to what is written there that it is proper to be stringent in this, the same law applies here. It is forbidden to heat a wax candle and stick it to a wall or a menorah, as a decree lest he smear, that is, rub it to smooth it upon the surface of the wall, and that is a toldah of memachek.
