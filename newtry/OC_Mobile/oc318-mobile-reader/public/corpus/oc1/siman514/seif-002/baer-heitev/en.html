To distance. This deals with a case where it will remain so and he will not again tilt the oil back upon it. And it appears to me from this that if he tilts and draws the oil a bit away from the wick so that they can grasp the wick and draw it out a bit more than it was at first outside the oil, and afterward he again tilts the oil toward it, and this is so that it burn well, it is permitted, and we do not say that at the moment he first drew the oil he is like one who extinguishes (Taz); see there.
Wicks. And likewise it is permitted to fold a wax candle so that it burn quickly; see there in Taz. And likewise it is permitted to tilt the wax candle with the fire a bit downward so that the wax melts, and return it upward, and so he may do many times so that the whole candle burn quickly; and likewise with a tallow candle it is permitted, although he causes extinguishing through the body of the candle, nevertheless it is permitted, for through this tilting he kindles more, and it resembles increasing wicks (Taz); see there. (But in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he disagrees and forbids; see there.) It is forbidden to heat a wax candle in order to stick it to a menorah, because it is smearing, as he wrote in siman 314; and Sefer HaZichronos wrote that it is forbidden to touch the wax lest he smear (Kenesses HaGedolah, siman 314). And Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that when he sticks it to the menorah there is no [actual] smearing, for smearing is a toldah of memachek, so that if he does not rub it there is no smearing; rather it is forbidden to stick it as a decree lest he smear; but it is permitted to move it about, for we do not decree lest he smear; end of his words.
