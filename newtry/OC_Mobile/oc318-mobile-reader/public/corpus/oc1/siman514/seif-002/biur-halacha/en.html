(א) In another candle, Dr. Demmela took and directed him a bit with the ark and prepared for his oil to beware that the radar should not be completely removed from the oil, and that he should not be able to know that he should not be completely removed from the oil, and that he should not even be able to know that he is in this manner As we wrote at the time, there is still another last minute in his method, and there is no way to protest in the hands of the Macin, and we have made it easy for the High Court to make it impossible for them to make sure that the oil is not entirely protected, and that it is not strictly forbidden to rely on it

(ב) Immediately he extinguishes — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote: if one cannot hold the wick, etc.; this is from Taz. And there are those who wish to rule from this a leniency regarding our lamps at the time of lighting on Yom Tov as on a weekday, whose way is to remove the wick and light it and afterward push the wick into it by means of the screw made for this and place the glass chimney on it, and afterward remove it outside. Their reasoning: although when pushing the wick into it he diminishes the light and this is included in extinguishing, since it is for benefit that through this he can afterward increase the light — but in truth it is not so, for it is literally comparable to the ruling brought inside. And what they said that it is for benefit — cannot one limit himself initially to put the light into the lamp chimney and light the wick there, and not remove the wick outside to light it and afterward lower it and push it down, which is included in extinguishing? And so I practiced myself. And see Shabbat 134, where the Gemara answers there it is impossible, here it is possible.

And it will extinguish quickly — see in Or Sameach and Nehar Shalom who question Rama in that he added "so that it burns much and extinguishes quickly" — in this it is not found in Ran; and in Avodat HaKodesh to Rashba it is explained as forbidden if he intends to extinguish, and it is not permitted unless he intends that it shine much even though it will extinguish quickly by itself; and so Or Sameach brought in the name of Sefer HaManhig; and so they proved from Ran's wording itself that it deals when he intends to illuminate; and so I found in Meiri that if he intends to hasten its extinguishing it is forbidden; only it appears this depends on the dispute of Tosafos and Rosh — for Tosafos, i.e. those who permit below seif 3 to light a burning wax candle in the middle even though he intends to shorten it and hasten extinguishing, the same here; but for Rosh who forbids there, the same here; and so is proven from Or Sameach and Biur HaGra that this law is per those who permit below; and behold it is explained below in Mishna Berurah that one should be concerned there for Rosh's view; if so, here too one should be concerned to forbid if he intends it will extinguish; and especially many Rishonim wrote explicitly to forbid when he intends to extinguish, as above. And know further that Taz who derived from this law that it is permitted to tilt a burning lamp slightly so the wax melts and he returns it upward, and does this many times until it is consumed and burns quickly — he learned this from Rama's permission to increase wicks; if so, here too when he intends it will be consumed quickly it is forbidden as above; and I also saw in Or Sameach, Nehar Shalom, and Chemdas Moshe who rejected his words — see there — they understood his words that when he tilts the lamp wax drips to the ground, which is certainly forbidden for Rosh who diminishes wax and extinguishes; Magen Avraham sides to forbid even per Tosafos because beforehand it dims the light through tilting and melting to the ground, only afterward it kindles more; but even if we explain his words that it does not drip to the ground, only through tilting and melting the flame rises and it burns quickly (as found in Magen Avraham in the name of some explainers) — likewise forbidden since he intends it will be consumed quickly, as above.
