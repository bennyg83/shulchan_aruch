To tilt the lamp, etc. Where pieces of tallow are placed in a lamp, one may remove one of the pieces that is distant from the wicks. Although indirectly causing extinguishing is permitted only to prevent a loss, as stated in siman 334, seif 22, that case is different, because when the fire reaches that point it is actually extinguished. Here, however, he merely prevents the fire from consuming additional fuel, and this is permitted even according to the Rosh. Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 28. Although the Bach cites Maharshal as prohibiting it, we follow Maharshal's later work. This is also implied by the Rama in seif 3 and by siman 502.
To add wicks. The same applies that one may fold a wax candle so that it will burn more quickly; see seif katan 9. It is forbidden to heat a wax candle in order to attach it to a candlestick, because this constitutes smoothing, as stated in siman 314. Sefer HaZichronos writes that it is forbidden even to touch wax, lest one smooth it. Knesses HaGedolah, siman 314. It appears to me, however, that attaching it to a candlestick does not itself constitute smoothing, because smoothing is a derivative of scraping, and if he does not rub it there is no smoothing. Rather, attaching it is forbidden as a decree lest he smooth it, as stated in the Mishnah in chapter 23 of Shabbos. It is nevertheless permitted to move the wax, for the Sages did not decree against moving it lest one smooth it. The Mishnah states, 'He may not place wax upon it,' which implies that moving it is permitted.
