A lamp. See Taz, who elaborated; and see letter 9, Yoreh Deah; and in Magen Avraham letter 7; and it will be explained nearby, God willing. And here I will mention what some do with a wax-stick (or they call it Statzik): they wrap it in a metal utensil, and in the utensil there is something like scissors; they cut in the wax and prevent burning further. At any rate, to do this before lighting: for after it has been lit, according to the Mechaber, and that is the view of Rosh, one should forbid this; however, according to the Rav [Rama] one may say this is permitted; see Magen Avraham letter 8. And nevertheless many hold like Rosh, with a proof from a tube; and see Olas Shabbos. And because of fixing [a vessel] and weekday labors with a utensil one should not forbid, so long as he does not cut the wick but only the wax alone; see seif 8.
And behold. The Mechaber's wording is exactly the wording of Tur; however, Tur concluded that cause of extinguishing is permitted, and the Mechaber deliberately omitted it, explaining Tur's words as specifically before it was lit (from Tur's writing "that he wants to light it"); if so, cause of extinguishing does not apply here at all; on the contrary, once it has been lit there is no repairing even only placing something that prevents upon it, such as a knife and the like; and even on a weekday one may say it is forbidden: behold, cause of extinguishing is forbidden. And from the careful reading of our master Maran HaMeshbir, of blessed memory, in Beis Yosef, it appears that according to Rosh it is simply obvious to him that to cut wax by fire after it is lit is forbidden like a tube; but to place upon it something that prevents outside it is a doubt; and from Tur it appeared to him that this too is forbidden; and therefore Beis Yosef wrote again, "and so it appears from Rosh," that even this is forbidden once lit. And the meaning of the word "outside it" that he wrote is: namely with a tallis that has caught fire, although [the fire] will pass and reach the whole tallis, nevertheless the tallis was not set aside and made for kindling; rather fire fell upon it by duress; therefore such cause of extinguishing is permitted, that he places something that prevents outside it, meaning outside the place of the fire; which is not so with wax: even if he places upon the wax a preventer outside the wax, nevertheless the wax and the wick were both prepared for lighting; so long as he diminishes from either of them after lighting, such cause of extinguishing is certainly forbidden. This [follows] from what Rosh, of blessed memory, wrote: that he does not touch the burning object but does something outside it, etc., when the fire reaches there; and he did not write that he performs no act: consider as aforesaid, and understand this.
And in the holy words of our master Taz, of blessed memory, this requires further study for me, for it implies from his words that after it has been lit it is permitted to place around it, and not by means of fire; and one must say "outside" means that he performs no act but only places upon it. And how did he write that therefore Taz wrote "to light it," for if it were already burning it would be forbidden to shorten it: behold, to place around it is permitted? And one may say with difficulty: like what they do with scissors or with a zwengel that Ohr Zarua brought; therefore specifically before it was lit, for he cuts in the body of the wax; and this requires further study. And what he wrote to reconcile the tube: see siman 265 and in Darkei Moshe there and here. And in Olas Shabbos: that even if he takes the whole tube, he still does not extinguish; and Eshel Avraham. And see Pesachim 11 and Shabbos, Bava Metzia, the necessity [of both teachings]; and God willing in Magen Avraham letter 7 it will be explained further. And in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 2, law 34, it implies that he holds like Beis Yosef, that according to Ra'avyah and Tur, after it has burned even on a weekday it is forbidden to place [a barrier], for they are not barriers but only diminish the wax; and cutting tallow at a distance is what is permitted, for it is not attached to the wick, which is not so with a candila; but Taz, of blessed memory, holds to distinguish between performing an act in the body of the wax, cutting in the wax with a zwengel; see Ohr Zarua in Levush, and it has already been explained. If so, one who wishes to be stringent ideally like Yam Shel Shlomo in Rosh's view and according to Beis Yosef's understanding of Tur and the plain implication of the Mechaber: once it has been lit, even only to place upon the wax something that prevents is forbidden, and even to insert into sand, and all the more so with a zwengel or scissors to cut some of the wax is forbidden according to this, as I have written.
