(א) (s.k. 7) Before, etc. — for the Rosh wrote, etc., on this that was taught in tractate Beitzah daf 22: one who touches oil in a candle while it is burning is liable because of extinguishing. These are the words of the Rosh, and as was written above in s.k. 5 his words and the words of Tosafot erev Shabbat.

(ב) That is why it is forbidden. This is the words of the Rav, who understood that the portion of the Rashi between the oil and the cause of the fire to the pelvis was cut into the gas. Dov does not do anything in the body that is divided, and therefore the ministers of the oil are doing something in the body, which is therefore forbidden to do so in the body that the rabbi of the occupants of the so-called “Sham” wrote:

And requires study what he wrote regarding a cloth, etc. — per Rabbeinu Yehuda's understanding of Rosh's words, behold with a cloth too he acts on the body of the burning object.

For Rosh holds, etc. — otherwise there would be no difficulty at all from causing extinguishing. And per the simple understanding of Magen Avraham's intent, therefore Rosh disagrees with Mordechai whom Rama brought siman 514, and Rosh holds even where there is no loss causing extinguishing is permitted; otherwise what is his difficulty from the outset on one who removes being liable because of extinguishing — behold causing extinguishing is permitted. And Chemdas Moshe understood thus: in my humble opinion there is not much difficulty here; nevertheless Rosh properly challenged, for if the Rabbis forbade when there is no loss, it is rabbinic prohibition without liability for a sin-offering; if there were liability for a sin-offering they would not permit in a place of loss; and since so, why does it teach one who removes is liable, etc.; therefore Rabbeinu Yehuda's words are primary, etc. — end of his words. And in my humble opinion Magen Avraham did not intend Rosh's first difficulty but his second difficulty rejecting Tosafos' reason that wrote why liable in removing — because it dims the light. And on this Rosh challenged from the mishnah one should not pierce a tube, etc., as above seif katan 5. And what is the difficulty — perhaps in removing he is liable as Tosafos said because it dims, and with a tube although it does not dim, nevertheless forbidden because of causing extinguishing, and although causing extinguishing is permitted, that is only in a place of loss specifically. And here with a tube it does not teach liable but forbidden language, and it may be said rabbinically because of causing extinguishing. And one may answer: if so, why did they forbid with a tube because lest he remove — behold it would be a decree for a decree: one, lest he remove; and even if he removes, there is only rabbinic prohibition of causing extinguishing. And Magen Avraham brought in seif katan 7 in the name of Or Sameach who challenged for us who hold like R' Shimon that melacha she'einah tzricha le-gufah is exempt and is only rabbinic prohibition — therefore in extinguishing he is liable only when he extinguishes to make charcoal or to make it flame; if so, why decree lest he remove — behold even if he removes there is only rabbinic prohibition, and it would be a decree for a decree. And Magen Avraham answered: many times they decreed a decree for a decree, and all is one decree; if so, here too; and one may answer: here it is worse, for it is three decrees: one lest he remove; and also if he removes, it is only causing, which is forbidden only rabbinically; and also if actual extinguishing, there is only rabbinic prohibition of melacha she'einah tzricha le-gufah — end of his words. And from Or Sameach's challenge, his challenge is only for us who hold like R' Shimon; and we hold like the mishnah of the tube; but on the mishnah itself it may be said it is like R' Yehuda who holds melacha she'einah tzricha le-gufah is liable; and although R' Yehuda disagrees in the conclusion and we do not decree lest he remove — if so, the first part is not R' Yehuda; this too is not so, for the first tanna also agrees with R' Yehuda that melacha she'einah tzricha le-gufah is liable like R' Yehuda; if so, even if we say causing extinguishing of the tube has only rabbinic prohibition, nevertheless we decree lest he remove, for there are only two decrees, and such we decree here. But when he places something outside it, even sticking it to the lamp itself even while burning — nevertheless permitted, since he does not take anything from the burning lamp; and so from his wording there who wrote "and one who diminishes from one of them," etc., as his wording above — implying the prohibition is because he diminishes, i.e. takes from it; and what he wrote at the beginning "when he does not touch," etc. — is not specific, for even if he touched, since he does not take anything, it is permitted; only truthfully he stated it regarding making a partition before the flame where he does not touch, etc.

And from Tur's wording who wrote: a wax candle he wants to light on Yom Tov, etc., he may place around it something that prevents, etc., and since he did not write "before he lights it" — it implies even after lighting it is permitted to place around it something that prevents; for permission and prohibition do not depend on this, only on not taking from the burning object. And what Tur wrote "he wants to light it" — from this Rabbeinu Yehuda inferred specifically before lighting it is permitted, not that he pities; for one may say as Bach wrote that Tur stated the usual manner that generally one does so before lighting, but permission does not depend on this; for if permission depended on this, he should have explained explicitly and written "before he lights it"; and see Bach and Yeshuot Shalom.

(ו) There was no end between the suns. Dea Miryi was a drink that lit in the High Court. And then, he said, “There is no end to the Shabbat.”

(ז) And furthermore, that he could have, etc. — meaning: even if it was lit from erev Yom Tov, nevertheless it is not muktzeh per the view of Magen Avraham, who argues on Rabbeinu Yonah and holds that even while burning one may place around it something that prevents burning.

That was lit on Yom Tov. Explanation: that it was lit for the honor of Yom Tov and thereby set aside for its mitzvah; but in any case it deals with what was lit from erev Yom Tov, for otherwise it would not be set aside from the synagogue; and if so, there is no muktzeh for half Shabbat, as Magen Avraham concluded the reason to forbid — because we hold: since it was set aside for the synagogue, etc.; forbidden to benefit from it and eat it; but moving it is certainly permitted: one, that even while burning it was not set aside from moving, for on Yom Tov one may move a burning lamp; and also on Shabbat one may move oil dripping per R' Shimon, for setting aside for its mitzvah does not apply to moving, for there is no nullification of mitzvah in moving it — see there.

After it went out — as the Gemara says there, there is no muktzeh for R' Shimon except oil in the lamp while it burns.
