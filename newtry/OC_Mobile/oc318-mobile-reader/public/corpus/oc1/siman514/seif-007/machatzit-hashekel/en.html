(s.k. 7) Before, etc. For Rosh wrote, etc., upon that which was taught in tractate Beitzah, folio 22: one who takes oil from the oil in a lamp while it is burning is liable because of extinguishing; upon this Rosh's words are said, as I wrote above in s.k. 5 his words and Tosafos' words; see there.
And therefore it is forbidden, etc. These are Beis Yosef's words, who understood that Rosh distinguishes between one who takes from the oil and cause of extinguishing by making a partition before a fire. For one who makes a partition does nothing to the body of the burning object, therefore it is permitted; which is not so with one who takes from the oil: he does something to the body of the burning object, therefore it is forbidden. Therefore Beis Yosef, who rules like Rosh, wrote that if he cares for the candle, etc., he must place [the barrier] before he lights it; but if it is already burning it is forbidden, since he performs an act on the body of the burning object.
And this requires further study: what differs from a tallis, etc. According to Beis Yosef's understanding of Rosh's words: behold with a tallis he also performs an act on the body of the burning object.
For behold Rosh holds, etc. For if not so, there would be no difficulty at all from cause of extinguishing. And according to the plain understanding of Magen Avraham's intent, necessarily Rosh disagrees with Mordechai whom Rama brought in siman 334, and Rosh holds that even where there is no loss, cause of extinguishing is permitted; for if not so, what was his initial difficulty on that one who takes [oil] is liable because of extinguishing: behold cause of extinguishing is permitted? Behold cause of extinguishing was permitted only where there is loss, and regarding one who takes [oil] there is no loss if he does not take. And so Sefer Chemdas Moshe understood, and he wrote upon this as follows: and in my humble opinion there is not even a partial difficulty here, for nevertheless Rosh challenged properly: if the Sages forbade where there is no loss, that is only a rabbinic prohibition and there is no liability of a chatas for it, for if there were liability of a chatas in this they would not permit where there is loss; and since that is so, why does it say "and one who takes from it is liable to a chatas," etc.? Therefore Beis Yosef's words appear primary, etc.; end of his words. And in my humble opinion Magen Avraham did not intend Rosh's first difficulty but the second difficulty, where he rejected Tosafos' reason who wrote that for that reason one who takes [oil] is liable, because he extinguishes the fire, for immediately upon taking from the oil he dims its light. And upon this Rosh challenged: according to this the Mishnah "a person should not pierce a tube," etc., is difficult, as I wrote above in s.k. 5. And what is the difficulty? Perhaps forever one who takes [oil] is liable as Tosafos wrote, because he dims its light; and with a tube, which does not dim its light, nevertheless it is forbidden because of cause of extinguishing; and although cause of extinguishing is permitted, that is specifically where there is loss. And behold regarding the tube it does not teach "liable" but the language of "forbidden," and one may well say it is rabbinic and because of cause of extinguishing. And one cannot say: according to this, why did they forbid with a tube lest he take [oil], for that is like a decree upon a decree: one, lest he take [oil], and even if he takes there is only a rabbinic prohibition, for cause of extinguishing where there is loss is permitted as above? That is not so, for independently Magen Avraham at the end of s.k. 7 brought in the name of Eliyah Rabbah who challenged for us who hold like R' Shimon that a melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah is exempt and is only a rabbinic prohibition, therefore regarding extinguishing one is liable only when extinguishing to make charcoal or to brighten [a wick]; and if so, why do we decree lest he take [oil], for even taking [oil] is only a rabbinic prohibition, and it is a decree upon a decree? And Magen Avraham answered that several times they decreed a decree upon a decree and it is all one decree. If so, the same law applies here. And one cannot say that here it is worse, for there are three decrees: one, lest he take [oil]; and also if he takes it is only cause, which is forbidden only rabbinically; and also if he extinguishes literally there is only a rabbinic prohibition, for it is a melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah. That is not so, for necessarily Eliyah Rabbah's challenge applies only for us who hold like R' Shimon that a melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah is exempt, and we hold like the Mishnah of the tube; but on the Mishnah itself there is no difficulty, for one may say it comes like R' Yehudah who holds a melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah liable. And although R' Yehudah disagrees in the latter clause and permits, for we do not decree lest he take [oil], if so necessarily the former clause is not R' Yehudah. This too is not so, for necessarily the first opinion disagrees with R' Yehudah only on whether we decree lest he take [oil]; but the first opinion also agrees that a melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah is liable like R' Yehudah. And if so, according to this, even if we say that cause of extinguishing of a tube is only a rabbinic prohibition, nevertheless we decree lest he take [oil], for there are only two decrees, and of this sort we decree here. But when he places something outside it, even if he sticks it to the lamp itself even while burning, nevertheless it is permitted since he does not take anything from the burning lamp. And so it is implied there from his wording: "and one who diminishes from either of them," etc., as I wrote his wording above: it implies that the prohibition is because he diminishes, that is, he takes from it, and upon this the prohibition depends. And what he wrote at the beginning of his words, namely when he does not touch, etc., is not precise, for even if he were touching, since he does not take anything it is permitted; rather he stated the truth, for there regarding making a partition before a fire he does not touch, etc.
And from Tur's wording who wrote "a wax candle that he wants to light on Yom Tov," etc. He may place around it something that prevents, etc. And from the fact that he did not write "before he lights it," it implies that even after he has lit it it is permitted to place around it something that prevents, for the permission and prohibition do not depend on this but only on that he does not take anything from the burning object. And what Tur wrote "that he wants to light it," from which Beis Yosef inferred that specifically before lighting it is permitted: Magen Avraham was not concerned for that inference, for one may say as Bach wrote, that Tur stated the usual manner of the matter, that ordinarily one does so before lighting, but the permission does not depend on this. For if the permission depended on this, he should have explained explicitly and written "before he lights it." And see Bach and Yam Shel Shlomo.
That it was not muktzeh at bein hashemashos. For behold we deal now with that which was lit on Yom Tov. And we hold in siman 310 that there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos; see there.
And further, that he could, etc. That is to say: even if it was lit from erev Yom Tov, nevertheless it is not muktzeh according to Magen Avraham's view, who disagrees with Beis Yosef and holds that even while burning one may place around it something that prevents it from burning.
That was lit on Yom Tov. Meaning: that was lit for the honor of Yom Tov, and thereby it was set aside for its mitzvah. But in any case we deal with that which was lit from erev Yom Tov, for if you do not say so it would not be muktzeh at bein hashemashos. And if so, there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos, and as Magen Avraham concluded the reason to forbid: because we hold "since it was set aside for bein hashemashos," etc., it is forbidden to benefit from it and to eat it. But moving is certainly permitted: first, even while it is burning it was not set aside from moving, for on Yom Tov it is permitted to move a burning lamp; and also even on Shabbos it is permitted to move dripping oil according to R' Shimon, for regarding moving, "set aside for its mitzvah" does not apply, for there is no nullification of a mitzvah in moving it; see there.
After it has gone out, as the Gemara says there: there is no muktzeh according to R' Shimon except oil in a lamp while it is burning.
In s.v. "in the lamp," etc. Whether a condition helps, and also what he wrote to distinguish between a Shabbos lamp and a Chanukah lamp, what Eliyah Rabbah wrote, etc., has already been explained above.
