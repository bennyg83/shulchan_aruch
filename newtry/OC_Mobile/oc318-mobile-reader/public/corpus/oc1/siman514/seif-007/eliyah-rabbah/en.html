[7] [Levush] Rather he does outside, etc. His words are not precise in this, for at the beginning of seif 3 he wrote Tosafos' reason on Beitzah 22 that he immediately extinguishes; and Rosh challenged them from a tube at the beginning of siman 265 that constantly drips to the lamp's light: why is it extinguishing when taking a little? Therefore he wrote another reason, namely specifically when he does outside, etc. And if so, he should not have written in the gloss what he wrote in the body, or he should have written them as a dispute. And behold, Bach at the beginning of siman 265 challenged why it is forbidden with a tube and answered like Tosafos' reason mentioned above that he immediately extinguishes, etc.; and I wonder: behold Rosh challenged Tosafos' reason from a tube. And perhaps he holds like what Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 21, wrote to answer Rosh's difficulty: we are concerned with a tube that he take all the oil so that the drip by drip cease, and that is like extinguishing; or like Taz's answer that we decree: when he takes from a tube he will also take from the oil in the lamp itself. And in Magen Avraham s.k. 19 he challenged those who permit cutting the candle by fire from the [law of the] tube; see there; and there is no difficulty at all, for he can hold the answers mentioned above and like Tosafos; and further, even according to Rosh one may say it is permitted in this like increasing wicks to kindle, as he himself wrote in s.k. 6; but nearby I will clarify also on this toward prohibition. And in Olas Shabbos he wrote: I do not understand Maharshal's words: what of it if he takes everything? Behold the oil in the tube is not attached to the oil in the lamp, and when he takes the tube he does not extinguish the light; until here. And in my humble opinion Maharshal's words are plain: since it constantly drips drop by drop, the lamp constantly burns with the drip, and so long as he does not take everything it still drips and the light remains clear as when he does not take; but when he takes it all, the drip stops and he extinguishes the light. But I have a difficulty on Maharshal's answer from Pesachim 11, where they challenge R' Yehudah who permits with a tube from R' Yehudah who decreed lest he eat from chametz when checking during Pesach: perhaps a tube is different, for they did not decree so far that he eat it all, which is not so with chametz, where even a part is forbidden; and according to Taz's answer mentioned above it is obviously difficult; and examine well.
