(s.k. 10) And it is permitted, etc. It appears to me that he inferred, etc.: that is to say, Maharil, who is the master of this law, inferred thus.
And this requires further study, for with a bonfire, etc. That is to say, on the contrary the opposite is proven: here where we deal with a lamp, even when there is no wind it is forbidden. For with a bonfire, by law even when there is a common wind it is permitted, for a common wind does not extinguish a bonfire; and even so it is forbidden when there is a common wind, for we decree on account of an uncommon [wind], even though it is uncommon; all the more so with a lamp, where when there is a common wind it is forbidden by law, for a common wind also extinguishes a lamp: there is [reason] to forbid even now when there is no common wind, decreeing on account of a common wind, for that is common; and see Shabbos 120.
And what he wrote in siman 277, seif 2, "and even if there is no one there, etc.": that is Maharil's proof as above.
And therefore when there is no one there, etc.: for it is like a decree upon a decree.
And the custom, etc. And the opposite is the case: carrying home is forbidden even when there is no common wind, as above; and inserting it into sand is permitted according to Rama who wrote upon "and some permit to cut, etc." and "so is the custom, etc."; and that is Tosafos' view, and according to them inserting into sand is also permitted even while burning, as Magen Avraham wrote. And in truth according to Magen Avraham's view in s.k. 7, even according to Rosh it is permitted since he does not take anything from what is burning. And all this is specifically when he does not make a hollow, that is, the sand is not hard but soft and loose, or the hollow was already made from erev Yom Tov, as Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 498, s.k. 32.
