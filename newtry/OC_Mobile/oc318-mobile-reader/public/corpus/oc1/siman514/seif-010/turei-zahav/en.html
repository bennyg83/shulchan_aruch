Permitted to remove the charcoal. In the Gemara they said: one trims the wick on Yom Tov — what is trimming? To remove darkness — Rashi explained: when charcoal formed on its head, he removes it; and Rosh wrote: removes the head of the wick that burned and became charcoal and darkens the light, even though he drops it to the ground and it is extinguished — permitted, as he is permitted to light the lamp to use its light, so permitted to extinguish what prevents the lamp's lighting; and Rambam forbids removing it with a utensil, and perhaps he considers it fixing a vessel, and it is not reasonable there is no fixing a vessel; also since it says trimming without specification, it implies permitted in every manner — end of his words; and Beit Yosef wrote: from Rashi and Rosh's words it appears permitted to remove only the head of the wick that became charcoal, but to cut the body of the wick — no — end of his words. The entire reason: what became charcoal inhibits the light — that specifically is permitted, for there is no fixing, only removing something defective, and it is as if something else stuck to the lamp that does not burn at all; but to cut the head of the wick that did not become charcoal — that is called fixing the vessel itself, for it too burns, only not shining so much at its head; therefore Rosh permitted removing charcoal even by utensil, since there is no fixing a vessel; and ruled here like Rambam; and Shulchan Aruch's wording here is unclear — he began with charcoal and ended with the head of the wick; and it should have written "but not by hand"; and it must be he follows Rambam's words who wrote: and he does not cut the head of the wick with a utensil, but flicks its head with his hand — end of his words; and Beit Yosef wrote: he did not write "but he cuts its head by hand" as said at the beginning — implying: he does not cut the head of the wick, i.e. the body of the wick, even by hand, because it is something usually done with a utensil and is fixing a vessel; but flicks its head by hand, i.e. removes charcoal on its head, even with a needle, because the manner is to remove it by hand — end of his words; if so, here too he did not intend to distinguish except between charcoal and the body of the wick; at the beginning permitted even with utensil, and at the end forbidden even by hand and with utensil, since he stated the manner is with a utensil; and in my humble opinion this is a very strained explanation in Rambam's words; rather his words are: he certainly holds permission is only for charcoal alone, not the wick; therefore he distinguished in charcoal itself between hand and utensil; and when cutting with a utensil, impossible to limit so he cuts only charcoal with the wick that did not become charcoal — forbidden; unlike flicking charcoal by hand, then certainly he will not touch the wick body, for flicking is placing his finger on them and tilting to the side and it falls, for it has no attachment to the wick, as the language of flicking in salting meat in Yoreh De'ah — he shakes the meat; likewise he shakes his finger on the charcoal and it falls by itself; but if he wants to take the charcoal between two fingers — certainly forbidden, for then very likely he will not limit to take charcoal alone without a little wick; therefore he did not say at the end "but cuts its head by hand," which would imply cutting between his fingers — forbidden; and at the beginning where he wrote he does not cut the head of the wick with a utensil — he teaches that with a utensil one can limit more to separate charcoal from wick because it is thin, unlike between fingers that are thick and easily catch also from the wick; and he teaches at the beginning that nevertheless forbidden, and permission is only flicking by hand, not cutting — all this is a very fine explanation of Rambam's words in section 4. And the conclusion for practice: also for tallow or wax candle, no permission at all to cut the charcoal on the candle between fingers or by the scissors utensil belonging to this; and flicking too does not apply here, for it applies only to a wick burning in oil where it is known charcoal forms on its head and when touched it separates and falls — this does not apply to tallow or wax; nevertheless there is some benefit in tilting the head of the wick with his finger so it lights well, as Mahari Chagiz wrote to distinguish between flicking and removing between fingers; and Rashal wrote in conclusion one should not enter doubtful primary melacha, and nowadays people are careful not to trim on Yom Tov at all, and one should not be lenient before them lest they come to trim with a utensil which is a primary melacha — end of his words; and per him permitted by law to cut charcoal even between fingers; but per what is written there is complete prohibition; nevertheless it appears one should not be stringent against tilting the head of the wick slightly with his finger so it kindles well, in the manner of flicking we mentioned in oil; and from this observers learn there is prohibition to trim the lamp even between fingers, since they see this is not trimming in the usual way but only tilting alone — so it appears correct to me. Tosafos chapter 2 wrote: one should protest to women not to cover fire on Yom Tov; however Agudah wrote to cover fire with ashes at night so tomorrow they find on Yom Tov; and Rashal wrote: and so it appears to be lenient, for it is davar she'eino mitkaven; and all the more per what we hold like R' Shimon that all extinguishing is melacha she'einah tzricha le-gufah, etc.
