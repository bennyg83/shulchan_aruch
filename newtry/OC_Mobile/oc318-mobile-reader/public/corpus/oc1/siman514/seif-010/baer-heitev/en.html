The charred wick (pacham). See Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham and Taz, who wrote that the explanation of the matter is thus: he holds that permission is only for the charred part alone, but not for the wick; therefore he distinguished within the charred part itself between by hand and by utensil — for when one cuts with a utensil it is impossible to limit oneself so that he will not cut from the body of the wick what has not yet become charred with it, and this is forbidden; whereas if he flicks off the charred part by hand, then he certainly will not touch the body of the wick, for flicking is that he places his finger on them and tilts it toward the ground and it falls, because it has no adhesion with the wick and it falls by itself. But if he wants to take the charred part between his fingers, it is certainly forbidden, for then it is very close that he will not limit himself to take the charred part alone without a bit of the wick; therefore he did not say at the end "but he cuts its head by his hand," for that would imply that he does so by cutting between his fingers, and this is forbidden. And what he wrote "and he does not cut the head of the wick with a utensil" — a great novelty is taught: by means of a utensil one can limit oneself more to separate the charred part from the wick, because it is a thin thing, whereas between his fingers, which are thick, it grips easily also from the wick — it is taught that even so it is forbidden, and permission is only flicking by hand and not cutting at all. This is a very fine explanation. And what emerges from this in practice also for a candle of milk or of wax: there is no permission at all to cut the charred part on the candle between his fingers or by means of the scissors belonging to this; and flicking is not relevant here, for it is relevant only for a wick burning within oil, where it is known that at its head charred matter forms and when one touches it it separates and falls to the ground — this is not relevant for milk or wax; nevertheless there is some benefit in that he tilts the head of the wick with his finger so it will burn well — and likewise Maharam Chalavah, to distinguish between flicking and removing between his fingers. And Rabbi Shlomo Luria wrote: the world is careful not to trim at all, and one should not be lenient with them lest they come to trim with a utensil like tweezers, and permission is only to flick with a finger, end of his words. It implies that according to him it is permitted by law to cut the wick even between his fingers, but according to what is written above there is a complete prohibition in this; nevertheless it appears one should not be strict against tilting the head of the wick slightly with his finger so it will burn well, in the manner of the flicking we mentioned in oil — hence the wording of Taz. And see the responsum of Darkei Noam, Orach Chayim part 1 siman 13, where he ruled there to permit when it is by hand erev Shabbat. And likewise in Shakhna Hagoz that in our places they practice to permit erev Shabbat, and one should be careful like Rabbi Shlomo Luria and Taz. (But in the book Eliyah Rabbah he argued to permit cutting the charred part between his fingers erev Shabbat.) Tosafot wrote: one should protest to women not to cover the fire on Yom Tov. Indeed Agudah wrote [it is permitted] to cover the fire with ashes at night so they will find it tomorrow on Yom Tov; and Rabbi Shlomo Luria wrote likewise to be lenient, for it is davar she'eino mitkavein.
