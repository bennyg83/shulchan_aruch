{19} To place around it, etc.: for to cut it with a knife is forbidden, because what he cuts into two and makes of each one a separate lamp is included in fixing a vessel; and to cut it by fire before lighting, which is permitted according to all as below in seif 8, he does not want, for his intent is that a piece of candle remain for him to light yet another time, and if he lights it with fire both will burn and nothing will remain.
{20} Before he lights it: for although we hold in siman 334, seif 22, that cause of extinguishing is permitted, that is there where he does not touch the burning object; see there; but here he touches the wax that is entirely designated for lighting, and therefore whether he cuts it and shortens it or does something around it so that the wick go out when it reaches there, it is forbidden: this is the Mechaber's reasoning. But several Acharonim disagree with this and hold that even after he has lit it, it is likewise permitted to place something that prevents burning, since he does not perform an act on the body of the thing that is burning.
{21} Something that prevents burning: for example placing sand around it and the like; and likewise it is permitted to insert it into soft, loose sand in a manner that there is no [prohibition of] making a hollow, as we wrote in siman 498, seif 16, in Mishnah Berurah, so that when it reaches the sand it will go out. And according to what we explained in the name of several Acharonim that something that prevents may be placed even after lighting, it will be permitted to insert it into sand even after lighting. But he should not place the candle in a zwengel after lighting, or stick the candle to the wall in a manner that it will go out when it reaches there, for that is as if he cuts from the candle, and this is forbidden according to all according to the first view, since he performs an act on the body of the burning object in order to hasten its extinguishing: it is included in extinguishing.
{22} And some permit to cut, etc.: that is, even after lighting; and their reason is that they forbade because of extinguishing only regarding one who takes oil from the oil in the lamp while it is burning, because at the moment he takes from it he dims its light somewhat; which is not so with a wax candle, for when he cuts below, this does not touch the light at all, and only afterward it will hasten to go out, and this is only included in cause of extinguishing, and it is permitted.
{23} And so is the custom: and several Acharonim wrote that it is proper to be stringent like the first view that forbids in this.
{24} By means of a knife it is forbidden: for by cutting it he makes another wick to give light, and it is included in fixing a vessel, even though he does not intend for that.
{25} In a place where the wind can prevail: that is to say, that it can prevail when it comes; and the reason for the permission is that it is only mere cause of extinguishing. And some of the Acharonim are stringent in this, for at every moment the wind is blowing; and for this reason the custom on Yom Tov night is that they do not take the lamps from the synagogue to their homes as they do on Motzaei Shabbos, but rather insert them into sand, as above in s.k. 21.
{26} The wind is blowing: even if it is only a common wind, for then it is considered like actual extinguishing.
