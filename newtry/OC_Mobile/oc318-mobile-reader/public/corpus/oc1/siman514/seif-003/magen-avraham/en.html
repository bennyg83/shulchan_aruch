Before he lights it. For Rosh wrote: although causing extinguishing is permitted as written siman 514 seif 22 — that is when he does not touch the burning object; but oil and wick both cause lighting, and one who diminishes from one of them hastens extinguishing; therefore forbidden to place around it something that prevents after he lights it — end of his words, Beit Yosef; and requires study what he wrote regarding a cloth that caught fire — permitted to put water on it as written siman 514 seif 24; and one may answer: there because of loss, for Rosh holds that even without loss it is permitted, otherwise there would be no difficulty from causing extinguishing — see there; therefore it appears to me Rosh forbade only taking from the body of the burning object; but when he places something outside it, permitted; and so from his wording there and from Tur's wording, who did not write the words "before he lights it" — implying even after lighting permitted; and the remaining piece is not forbidden due to muktzeh, for it was not muktzeh from erev Shabbat; and also he could have caused it extinguishing — if so he did not remove his mind from it — Arsi"r Ra"t; and nevertheless it appears to me a mitzvah candle lit on Yom Tov — even though it went out — forbidden to benefit and eat, since set aside for its mitzvah like sukkah decoration, as Tosafos wrote Shabbos 42b s.v. ve'ein ne'osin mimenu; and even for R' Shimon there is no muktzeh, nevertheless oil dripping from the lamp is forbidden to benefit, for it was set aside for its mitzvah — end of his words; and although for R' Shimon permitted to benefit after it went out — for us forbidden the whole day, for we hold: since it was set aside for the synagogue, it was set aside for the whole day; and see daf 44a s.v. she-bi-nar u-vi-k'arah; what Or Sameach wrote here — we do not decree a decree for a decree — see there: we find this many times and we say all is one decree.

That is, that they light. Explanation: specifically in order to light both as one; but if he acts to disconnect the first part so the other side will not light — forbidden (Yeshuot Shalom); but from Rama's wording it implies even if he does not need both, only that he acts to shorten it — permitted; and do not say in seif 8 his intent is to make one wick into two, therefore forbidden unless he needs both — that is fixing a vessel; but here where he intends only to shorten so it extinguishes quickly — permitted, for that is causing extinguishing; and per this view it is obvious it is permitted to insert a candle in sand even on a weekday when burning; therefore Rama wrote "and some permit," etc., that he argues on the first reason; and so Bach; and as he wrote afterward, even for the first reason it is permitted; and see seif 2 and seif 9 and siman 509 seif 3.
