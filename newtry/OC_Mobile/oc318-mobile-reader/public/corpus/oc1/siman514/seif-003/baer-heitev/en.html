That he light it. And therefore one should caution those who have a zwengel, such that the candle burns only until it reaches the zwengel, not to place [it] into it on Yom Tov unless before they light (Maharil). But Magen Avraham wrote as follows: for Rosh wrote that although cause of extinguishing is permitted, as written in siman 334, seif 22, that is when he does not touch the burning object but only causes something outside the lamp, namely as written here that he places around it something that obstructs the kindling; but he should not diminish the kindling by taking any part from its body, such as the oil and the wick, for both cause the fire, and one who diminishes from either of them hastens the extinguishing; therefore it appears to me that Rosh forbade only taking from the body of the burning object; but when he places something outside it, it is permitted; and so is implied from his wording and from the wording of Tur, who did not write the word "before he lights it," implying even after he has lit it is permitted; therefore Rama wrote "and some permit, etc.," who disagree with the first reason. And the remaining piece is not forbidden because of muktzeh, for it was not muktzeh at bein hashemashos; and further, he could have caused it to extinguish, and if so he did not remove his mind from it; see siman 279. And nevertheless it appears to me that a mitzvah candle that was lit on Yom Tov, even though it went out, it is forbidden to benefit from it and to eat it, since it was set aside for its mitzvah like the adornment of a sukkah; end of his words (Magen Avraham).
That they light. Meaning: specifically in order to light both as one; but if he acts so as to detach the first part so that the other side not light, it is forbidden (Yam Shel Shlomo). But from Rama's wording it implies that even if he does not need both, only that he acts to shorten it, it is permitted; and according to this view it is obvious that it is permitted to insert a candle into sand even while it is burning (Magen Avraham); and so wrote Taz; see there. And see siman 498, s.k. 18: I wrote in the name of Magen Avraham that it is forbidden to insert into sand because he makes a hollow; see there; and so is the view of Shelah. And Taz permits, when there is some need, to insert the candle even into water; see there.

The wind. Magen Avraham wrote: and the custom on Yom Tov night is that they do not take the lamps from the synagogue home as they do on Motzaei Shabbos, for at every moment the wind is blowing and it is impossible to discern; not like Shelah, who forbids inserting it into sand and would have one carry it home, and [rather] the opposite is the case; end of his words; see there.
