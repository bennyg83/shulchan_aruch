Something that prevents burning — see in Mishna Berurah as we wrote, such as through sand and the like; and regarding inserting it into water — Taz's view that in slight need one may be lenient; see Chemdas Moshe who disagrees that in water it does not enter the category of preventing burning but of hastening extinguishing, as explained in siman 265 in Acharonim — see there; and Chayei Adam in general 95 likewise did not permit this except for great need, and on the second day of Yom Tov in exile — see there; and Gra"z in siman 265 in Kuntres Acharon concludes there to forbid because his words are against many poskim — see there — only if it is great need he permits there — see there his reason [and in Or Sameach it implies Magen Avraham is lenient here, and I searched and did not find in him any hint to this]. And even per those who permit, one should be stringent.

That they light also from below to shorten it — behold from Magen Avraham's words [and as Peri Megadim explains] it implies that per this view it is permitted even when the lower part does not light afterward at all [such as it flickers slightly in fire and then he disconnects one from the other] — and it is not comparable to seif 8 where his intent is to make one wick into two, therefore forbidden unless he needs both; but here where he intends only that it extinguish quickly, it is permitted even in such a case; but from many Acharonim it implies the intent of those who permit is when both parts light — see Taz below seif katan 10; and so Biur HaGra; and so Chemdas Moshe that we require the second part also lights; and so in Ma'amar Mordechai, for those who permit derive from Tosafos and their supporters, and the source is the law of seif 8; and Chayei Moshe also grounded his words because if he does not light the lower part, even though he does not intend to make another wick, it is psik reisha; and Machatzis HaShekel reconciles somewhat.

And it is permitted to place a lamp, etc. — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote that some are stringent, namely Magen Avraham; and see Mateh Yehuda who rejected his words; and in my humble opinion the law is with him, from the reason explained above siman 265 — forbidden to place a vessel with water under sparks lest he raise the vessel with water against the sparks and extinguish them; and likewise here, since at every moment wind may come, he may bring the vessel against the wind. And what he further wrote that the world is accustomed not to carry lamps from the synagogue — in Halacha Berurah he wrote that per his view it is permitted in this even if we are stringent in his first law, for here he does not intend to extinguish it and on the contrary wants it to illuminate; if so, all where there was no wind it is not psik reisha that it will extinguish — permitted; and in my humble opinion it appears the opposite: even if we are lenient in the first law, here one should be stringent, for carrying certainly causes extinguishing.
