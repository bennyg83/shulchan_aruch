One does not cut the wick. For he fixes a vessel; and if he places both ends in the mouth of two lamps, etc. — then it is not proven he fixes a vessel, only he intends for lighting — so Rashi explained.

(ב) Provided that he needs both of them. Rabbi Shlomo Luria wrote in the chapter HaMavi, and these are his words: if so, the essence of the permission depends on this. Likewise it is permitted to cut the wax candle by fire in order to light both of them as one. But a candle that is burning — it is forbidden to cut, even by fire, in order to detach the first part from it so it will not ignite on the second side; and as was written in chapter 19 siman 28, end of his words. And I did not understand his words: how will he cut by means of fire and the second part will not ignite? And how is this possible unless the second part is extinguished after he ignites there in order to separate them? And if it is possible to separate them by means of fire without lighting — it is obvious that it is forbidden, like with a knife. And what he wrote as proof from chapter 19 siman 28 — there was mentioned only the matter of one who wants to cut by fire in order to hasten the extinguishing, which he holds is forbidden per the Rosh because he diminishes the body of the wax; but it does not appear to me thus, for accordingly we would not have needed here "that he needs both of them" except so it should not appear as repairing a utensil, since the wick was not yet lit that he is making it into a utensil now. But if he wants to light a wax candle that is already burning and he intends that the candle will burn quickly — even per the Rosh above, there is no prohibition, for this is like adding wicks so it will burn quickly, which Rama in seif 2 mentioned is permitted; for what difference is there whether he adds burning by means of another wick or by means of that same wick itself in a wax candle? And the Rosh does not argue on Tosafot above except when he diminishes without lighting. And I heard from Hagahot Ashiri chapter 2, who wrote and these are his words: but if a small cut in a burning candle, and he takes one of the pieces from those far from the wick — it does not appear that he would be liable; nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden to do, even on Yom Tov; and likewise to cut from a wax candle while it is burning, end of his words. It implies that even by fire it is forbidden; it appears we do not hold like this hagahah, for Rabbi Shlomo Luria himself permitted taking a piece of milk, as was written in his name in seif 3; likewise in this matter, so the wax candle will not ignite at the first end — he does not hold like Ran, who permits adding wicks that Rama mentioned; but we do not hold like my view. And Rabbi Shlomo Luria himself brought proof also for this that adding wicks is permitted; and the reason is that all lighting is the same — likewise in this, that he ignites it at one end; it appears to me to be lenient, since Tosafot wrote explicitly that it is permitted in a wax lamp to cut by means of fire, and the Rosh did not bring this to forbid in his dispute but listed the prohibition of taking from the body of the candle.
