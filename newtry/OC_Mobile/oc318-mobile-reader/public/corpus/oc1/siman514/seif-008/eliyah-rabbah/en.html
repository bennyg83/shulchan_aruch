[8] In another lamp, etc. And the same law applies to this lamp itself. Taz challenged what is written in siman 502, seif 2, that it is permitted to take wood from a bonfire, etc.; and it seems that whoever ruled there leniently, here too with a wick, requires further study regarding Rama and Maharshal, who ruled for both of them, one lenient and one stringent; until here. And in truth Tosafos on folio 22 wrote only not to take a wick and place it on the ground, implying that for another lamp it is permitted; and therefore I wonder at Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 29, who wrote as follows: Tosafos and Rosh after them wrote that it is forbidden to take, etc., and place it in another lamp; until here: for that is not so, for only Rosh wrote thus, but from Tosafos the opposite is implied. Later I saw that Rabbeinu Yeruham, folio 29, also wrote likewise in the name of Tosafos, and perhaps before him there was a different Tosafos. And regarding Taz's wonder at Rama and Maharshal, I learned a proper reconciliation from what Maharil wrote as follows: it is forbidden to take a wick from this lamp and place it in its fellow, for since he diminishes a drop of oil from it he is liable because of extinguishing; until here. And it appears his meaning is that in taking the wick it is impossible that a drop not drip to waste or that [oil] stick to his hands, therefore it is forbidden; and if so, since with a bonfire this does not apply, therefore they permitted. And what Olas Shabbos wrote is forced.
