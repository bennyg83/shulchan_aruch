(Seif katan 8) That is, etc. — that the other side not light — forbidden because of fixing a vessel; and as written seif 8, and it is when he needs both.

(ב) And in the press, how can we be circulated by the light and will not reflect the other part if it does not lie down. And he said, “It’s a little bit to him that he’s not going to get away.”

(ג) But here, where he does not intend except to shorten, etc. And he wrote on this in Choshen Mishpat: behold nevertheless it is psik reisha. And in my humble opinion it requires further study, according to what Magen Avraham explained above in siman 348 s.k. 36 in the name of Maharam Muhlhausen on the matter of making a utensil, and these are his words: it appears that anyone who does not intend — it is not fit to call it psik reisha and to obligate him, because when he intends he does melacha, and when he does not intend he does not do melacha at all, since he does not want to make a utensil from it. It is similar to breaking a splinter: whoever does not break it to wedge it in his teeth is exempt, for all that is due to repairing a utensil — one who is not repairing is exempt; even according to Rabbi Yehuda, who obligates in davar she'eino mitkavein, end of his words. And it implies there that even a rabbinic prohibition there is none; and the same applies here. And that which it is forbidden by means of a utensil because of repairing a utensil, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 9 — it must be said: since its way is by means of a utensil, they were strict. And see in Magen Avraham s.k. 20.

And per this view, etc. — it does not refer to what he wrote before that Rama argues on Yeshuot Shalom, but a matter in itself; and it refers to Shulchan Aruch's words; and he means: per this view, i.e. per those who permit that Rama wrote — do not err that Rama's intent was to add to the Mechaber's view who permits to place around it something that prevents burning — Rama added that it is also permitted to cut it with fire; but Rama agrees that around it must be placed specifically before lighting; therefore Magen Avraham wrote this is not so; for the view of those who permit that Magen Avraham wrote is Tosafos' view who hold prohibition of removing is because it dims the light; and per them, even while burning it is permitted to cut with fire, all the more to place around it something that prevents burning, even while burning.

And as he wrote afterward: even for the first reason, i.e. Rosh whom the Mechaber ruled like — it would be that for Rosh, while burning, forbidden to cut by fire; nevertheless to place around it something that prevents burning is permitted even while burning, since he does not take from the burning object — permitted; not like Rabbeinu Yehuda's understanding of Rosh's words, as above seif katan 7.
