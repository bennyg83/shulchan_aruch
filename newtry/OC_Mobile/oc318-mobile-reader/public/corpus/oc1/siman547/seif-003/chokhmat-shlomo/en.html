Seif 3. All thirty days before the festival it is forbidden to eulogize for a dead person who died for him from before thirty days before the festival, etc. Note: Now the source of this distinction between one who died within thirty days of the festival or earlier grew from the Yerushalmi that the Beis Yosef cited there; and this is the language of the Yerushalmi: one should not arouse [grief] over his dead, etc.; one should not eulogize him before the festival for thirty days: this is one who makes a eulogy for him independently. This refers to an old [death], but for a recent [death] it is permitted. What is recent? Within thirty days. Old: after thirty days. End quote of the Yerushalmi. Now the Beis Yosef understood that its intent is that "within thirty days" and "after thirty days" refer to the festival: whether he died within thirty days of the festival or not. And from this he was forced to the reason that all thirty days [beforehand] the bitterness is in any case in his heart, etc.; see there. But in my humble opinion, in my poverty, it does not appear so. For what of it? Ultimately, if his thirty days end within the festival, then at least he can fulfill the mitzvah of "and you shall rejoice in your festival" at the end of the festival days, and especially on the festival of Sukkos, where there is an independent mitzvah to include even the night of the last Yom Tov for joy; and if he eulogizes him, his thirty days will end only after the entire festival, and if so he still nullifies the mitzvah of joy from the last Yom Tov; so why should he not be concerned to fulfill what is possible? Therefore it appears to explain the intent of the Yerushalmi that it does not refer to within the festival, but only to when the eulogy was done: if the eulogy is for a recent dead person within thirty days of his death, there is no concern that this is within thirty days of the festival, since we learn in the Gemara in chapter 2 of Sukkah that one who is occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from [another] mitzvah, from the verse "and there were men who were impure through a human corpse and could not make the Pesach offering on that day"; and if so they rendered themselves impure even though they knew they would be unable to make the Pesach offering; nevertheless they rendered themselves impure and were not concerned for the loss of making the Pesach offering. And Rashi explained there that they were not literally a meis mitzvah, but only of the seven dead for whom one becomes impure; and it says there that both [verses] are needed, for if from Pesach alone one might have thought that since the time of the Pesach obligation had not yet arrived [it is different], and if from the a fortiori alone one might have thought that it is specifically regarding a matter that has no liability of kares, etc. And if so, at any rate it is proven that one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from [another] mitzvah, and especially where the time of obligation has not yet arrived, he is certainly exempt. And if so, here too, since there is an obligation to eulogize, what need have we to be concerned that he will nullify the joy of the festival? For one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from [another] mitzvah, and especially since the time of the obligation of festival joy has not yet arrived. However, the main reason that one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from [another] mitzvah applies only where it is impossible to fulfill both; but if it is possible to fulfill both, he is not exempt. And if so, regarding a eulogy, he can eulogize him after the festival; therefore it does not override the joy of the festival. But according to this, it is well known that the main eulogy is at the time of [emotional] heat, and the main heat is within thirty days of mourning; but after thirty days there is not so much heat. And therefore one is exempt from mourning upon hearing after thirty days, for there is no time of heat, and therefore he is exempt even from tearing. And if so, this eulogy that would be done within thirty days would not be done to the same degree after thirty days, and therefore it is like a case where it is impossible to fulfill both; and therefore it is permitted to eulogize him, and he need not be concerned for the joy of the festival. And even though during the festival itself it is certainly forbidden to eulogize him, that is because a eulogy is rabbinic and the joy of the festival is according to the Torah; a rabbinic mitzvah does not override a positive commandment according to the Torah. But that is specifically where the time of the Yom Tov obligation has arrived; then uprooting a matter of Torah by active doing is not within their power. And even if it is considered as "sit and do not act," nevertheless the Re'em so wrote, cited in Sha'ar HaMelech, that it is not within their power to uproot a matter of Torah even by "sit and do not act," except where it is not "in its time"; but "in its time," it is not within the power of the Sages to uproot a matter of Torah even by "sit and do not act." And if so, here too, since the joy of the festival has already taken effect upon him, it is not within their power to uproot a matter of Torah, even if it would be considered "sit and do not act." But before the festival, when the time of the Yom Tov obligation has not yet arrived, and as the Gemara of Sukkah mentioned above says regarding Pesach, then in this it is within the power of the Sages to uproot by "sit and do not act." If so, now he eulogizes him permissibly, and afterward that he will not fulfill the joy of Yom Tov is only by "sit and do not act"; in this it is properly within the power of the Sages. And therefore the Yerushalmi said: when is this said? Regarding an old [death], namely that the eulogy is for an old dead person who is already after thirty days from his death; if so, such a eulogy can be done afterward, and in this it is forbidden to displace the joy of the festival through it. But regarding a recent [death], where the eulogy is for a recent dead person within thirty days of his death, in this there is no concern for nullifying the joy of the festival, for one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from [another] mitzvah, and it is like a case where it is impossible to fulfill both; and since the time of obligation has not arrived, even one occupied with a rabbinic mitzvah is exempt from the other. And this is clear in the intent of the language of the Yerushalmi, in my humble opinion. After writing this I saw in a responsum of the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Rabbi Azriel Margoliyos, of blessed memory, that the Ramban already sensed the explanation of this Yerushalmi; but he was forced to distinguish on the basis that within thirty days [the bitterness] in any case does not leave his heart, and no new mourning is newly created for him; he was also forced to reconcile the Yerushalmi even according to Rav, that the reason is on account of pay, because in this it would not add pay; see there; and all this is forced. But according to what I wrote it comes out properly with its reason, and there is no contradiction at all to Rav, for the intent is only that one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from [another] mitzvah. And therefore, since the halachah follows Rav in prohibitions, only here that they ruled like Shmuel is for the reason that they bring a proof from the Yerushalmi; and since according to us there is no proof from the Yerushalmi, we therefore return to the general rule that the halachah follows Rav in prohibitions; and consequently it is forbidden only for pay, but for free it is permitted. And upon this I relied and made a eulogy for a certain scholar in the year 5596 who died before thirty days before the festival, and the eulogy was within thirty days of the festival, together with all these reasons; and so ruled the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Rabbi Azriel, of blessed memory, and I strengthened his words further; and this is clear in my humble opinion. And from the words of the Ramban himself there is a proof for my words, for he holds that within thirty days mourning is not newly created through the eulogy; and necessarily he holds that within thirty days it is considered like the very time of death itself; and certainly the heat of the time of death is not comparable to afterward; and therefore it is not considered possible to fulfill both by eulogizing him after thirty days; and examine this well.
