7) [Se'if 2] Thirty days before the festival. The reason is that the deceased is not forgotten for thirty days; if one eulogizes within thirty days of the festival, he will come to grieve during the festival. According to Shmuel, even a free eulogy is forbidden. Rav prohibits only a paid eulogy, because one thereby spends festival funds, but permits a free eulogy. The halachah follows Shmuel. Taz, paragraph 1. There is no difference whether the eulogizer is an outsider or one of the mourners. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1; Mikra'ei Kodesh, siman 58; Chayei Adam, rule 3; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 2. If the person died within thirty days of the festival, even a paid eulogy is permitted, for the halachah follows Shmuel. Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 5.
8) There: For one who died before then. Even for a Torah scholar who died more than thirty days before the festival, it is forbidden to eulogize him within thirty days of the festival. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 4; Yeshuos Yaakov, paragraph 1. Responsa Olas Shmuel, siman 77, permits eulogizing a great man even upon distant tidings, when there had been no prior opportunity to eulogize him. Pischei Teshuvah.
9) There. If one died more than thirty days before the festival, even though the eulogy would still be within thirty days of the death, it is forbidden once the interval from the eulogy to the festival is less than thirty days. Mishnah Berurah, Biur Halachah; Pischei Teshuvah to Yoreh Deah, siman 347.
10) There. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, remains uncertain whether Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur have the status of a festival for this law. Yeshuos Yaakov is lenient. Igeres Ezov, in Alon Bachus, discourse 6, also writes that they do not have that status. Pischei Teshuvah to Yoreh Deah, siman 347; Mikra'ei Kodesh, siman 58; Chayei Adam, Likkutei Rima, paragraph 1.
11) There: One may eulogize him. Since the loss is fresh, his bitterness is already fixed, and the eulogy does not increase it. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. This applies even if the deceased was not a Torah scholar. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 4; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 5. It applies even to a paid eulogy, as explained in paragraph 7 above.
12) There: Even if he died on the eve of the festival. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 4, is uncertain about a death after midday. Although Tzidduk HaDin is less severe than a eulogy, it is not recited on Erev Shabbos after midday, and perhaps the same applies on Erev Yom Tov. Certainly on Erev Pesach after midday, when the Pesach offering was brought, there should be no eulogy except for a Torah scholar, whom one may eulogize on Erev Shabbos and Erev Yom Tov, and even on Erev Pesach after midday, since a Torah scholar may be eulogized even on Chol HaMoed, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 401. See above, siman 420, paragraph 2.
