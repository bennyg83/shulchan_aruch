Discreetly. And if it is impossible discreetly, they do it publicly; however, specifically publicity before Jews, but not publicity before non-Jews, even when it is impossible otherwise (Tumas Yesharim; Knesses HaGedolah; Magen Avraham); and see siman 526, s.k. 14.
A tree. But it is permitted to cut myrtle, for everyone knows it is for the need of the dead; which is not so of cedars, for they will suppose that they cut them for building. And it implies even for a well-known person, and even if they have no other wood, it is forbidden. However, through a non-Jew there is reason to permit, since it is a need of a mitzvah; see the beginning of siman 543. And in Knesses HaGedolah he wrote in the name of Tumas Yesharim that where it is impossible otherwise, it is permitted to cut from that which is attached.
