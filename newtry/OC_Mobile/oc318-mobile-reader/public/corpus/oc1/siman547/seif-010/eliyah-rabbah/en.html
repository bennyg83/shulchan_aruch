[10] One may not cut, etc. This means even when the matter is public knowledge. It is permitted through non-Jews. Magen Avraham. Where no other option exists, it is permitted to cut wood from that which is attached to the ground, even when the matter is not public knowledge, if no other wood is available. So it appears to me from Tumas Yesharim, siman 117, in the name of Rabbi A. Yerushalmi. In Knesses HaGedolah the name “Rabbi A.” was omitted, and Magen Avraham therefore thought that the source was the Jerusalem Talmud, but this is incorrect.
