[10] One may not cut, etc. This means even publicly; by means of non-Jews it is permitted, Magen Avraham. Where it is impossible, it is permitted to cut from attached growth even when it is not public, if he has no other wood, as it appears to me. So it is implied in Tumat Yesharim, siman 117, in the name of R. Aharon Yerushalmi; in Knesset HaGedolah the name R. Aharon was omitted, and Magen Avraham thought it was from the Yerushalmi Talmud, but that is not so.
