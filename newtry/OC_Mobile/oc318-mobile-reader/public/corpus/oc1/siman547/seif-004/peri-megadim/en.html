And there are those who disagree; see the Taz; and in Yoreh Deah 340, letter 18, and Shach 47: other relatives, [if] a recent report [comes] on Chol HaMoed and after the festival it would be distant, one tears on Chol HaMoed even according to the Ashkenaz custom; and for one's father or mother, if one heard a distant report on Chol HaMoed, one tears after the festival; see there. See Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the responsa of Maharam of Lublin: if one's father or mother died and was buried on Yom Tov, they do not tear even on the second day of Yom Tov; one should not tear on Chol HaMoed, only after the festival; see there.
It is implied from the silence of the poskim that even a Torah scholar who died before thirty days, one should not eulogize him within thirty days; but if he died within thirty days, even on the eve of the festival, even an ordinary person, it is permitted to eulogize him on the eve of the festival. And if he died after midday, it requires further study, for one may say that Tziduk HaDin is lighter by law than a eulogy; see siman 408, and see there that after midday they do not say Tziduk HaDin; and one may say that the same applies on the eve of a festival after midday, and all the more so on erev Pesach after midday, which is the time of slaughtering the Pesach offering, that they likewise do not eulogize, except for a Torah scholar, whom they eulogize on erev Shabbos and on the eve of a festival, even on erev Pesach after midday, for it is no better than Chol HaMoed, when they do eulogize him; see Yoreh Deah 401, and see siman 420 and the Magen Avraham there.
