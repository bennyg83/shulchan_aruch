Seif 3: For all thirty days before the festival it is forbidden to deliver a eulogy for a deceased who died for him more than thirty days before the festival, etc. — note: behold, the source of this distinction between a deceased who died within thirty days of the festival or before sprouted from the Yerushalmi that the Beit Yosef brought there, and this is its language: the Yerushalmi — one should not awaken eulogy for his dead, etc., one should not eulogize him before the festival thirty days — this applies to one who makes him a separate eulogy; this is regarding an old [deceased], but regarding a new one it is permitted. What is new? Within thirty days; old — after thirty days — end of Yerushalmi language. And behold the Beit Yosef understood that its intent is whether death was within thirty days or after thirty days relative to the festival. And from this he needed the reason that all thirty days in any case there is bitterness in his heart, etc., see there. In my humble opinion in this matter it does not seem so — for what of it? After all, if his thirty days end within the festival, then in any case he will fulfill 'and you shall rejoice in your festival' at the end of the festival days, and especially on Sukkot which is a mitzvah in itself to include the night of the last Yom Tov in joy; and if one eulogizes him he cannot complete thirty days until after the entire festival, and thus he still nullifies the mitzvah of festival joy from the last Yom Tov — so why should he not be concerned to fulfill what is possible? Therefore it seems to interpret the Yerushalmi's intent: it does not refer to within the festival, only to when the eulogy is performed — if the eulogy is for a new deceased within thirty days of his death, one need not be concerned that it is within thirty days of the festival, since we derive in the Gemara chapter 2 of Sukkah that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah from the verse 'And there were men who were impure through contact with a human corpse and could not make the Pesach on that day' — if so, they became impure even though they knew they could not make Pesach, nevertheless they became impure and were not concerned about losing the performance of Pesach; and Rashi explained there they were not actually a met mitzvah, only from the seven [relatives] that defile him; and it says there both were needed — for if from Pesach one might think since the time of obligation of Pesach has not arrived; and if from [the verse] 'they could not' one might think only regarding a matter that has no karet obligation, etc. — if so, in any case it is proven one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah, and especially where the time of obligation has not arrived he is certainly exempt; if so here too, since there is an obligation to eulogize, why should we be concerned that festival joy will be nullified? Behold one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah, and especially since the time of obligation of festival joy has not yet arrived. But the main reason that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah is only when it is impossible to fulfill both; but when it is possible to fulfill both he is not exempt; if so regarding eulogy, behold he can eulogize him after the festival — therefore it does not override festival joy. But per this, behold it is known that the main eulogy is at the time of heat [of mourning], and the main heat is within thirty days of mourning; but after thirty there is not so much heat — therefore one is exempt from mourning upon hearing after thirty days because there is no time of heat, and therefore exempt also from tearing; if so, a eulogy that would be performed within thirty would not be performed so much after thirty — therefore it is as if impossible to fulfill both, and therefore permitted to eulogize him and he need not be concerned for festival joy. And even though during the festival itself it is certainly forbidden to eulogize him — namely, since eulogy is d'rabbanan and festival joy is d'oraisa, a d'rabbanan mitzvah does not override a positive d'oraisa mitzvah. But this is only where the time of obligation of Yom Tov has arrived — then it is uprooting Torah in a positive and negative command, and they have no power to uproot; and even if it is considered she'as hadechak — nevertheless, so too the Ra'avad brought in Shulchan Aruch that they have no power to uproot Torah even in she'as hadechak except where it is not in its time, but in its time the Sages have no power to uproot Torah even in she'as hadechak; if so here too, since festival joy has already taken hold upon him, they have no power to uproot Torah even if it would be considered she'as hadechak; but before the festival, when the time of obligation of Yom Tov has not yet arrived — as the Gemara says in Sukkah there regarding Pesach — if so, in this the Sages have power to uproot in she'as hadechak; if so, now when permitted to eulogize him and afterward he will not fulfill Yom Tov joy, it is a doubt in she'as hadechak — in this the Sages have power properly; therefore the Yerushalmi said 'in what case is this? regarding old' — namely, that the eulogy is for an old deceased who is already after thirty from his death — if so, such a eulogy can be done afterward, and in this it is forbidden to defer festival joy through it; but regarding new, where the eulogy is for a new deceased within thirty of his death — in this one need not be concerned for nullifying festival joy, for one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah, and it is as impossible to fulfill both; and since the time of obligation has not arrived, even regarding one engaged in a d'rabbanan mitzvah he is exempt from the other — and this is the intent of the Yerushalmi's language in my humble opinion. After writing this I saw in the responsum of Chanaan, our teacher R' Azriel of Verona, may his righteous memory be for a blessing, that the Ramban already explained this Yerushalmi, but he strained to distinguish based on that within thirty in any case it does not leave his heart and no new mourning was renewed for him; he also strained to reconcile the Yerushalmi even according to Rav, that the reason is because of payment, because in this he will not add payment, see there — and all this is strained. But per what I said it fits well on the correct reason and there is no contradiction at all to Rav, for the intent is only that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah. Therefore, since the halakha is like Rav in prohibitions, only here they ruled like Shmuel because they bring proof from the Yerushalmi; and since for us there is no proof from the Yerushalmi, we revert to the general rule that halakha is like Rav in prohibitions, and automatically it is not forbidden except with payment, but for free it is permitted. On this I relied and conducted a eulogy for a certain scholar in the year 5596 who died before thirty to the festival and the eulogy was within thirty to the festival, with the combination of all these reasons; and so ruled the gaon our teacher R' Azriel, may his righteous memory be for a blessing, and I upheld his words more — and this is in my humble opinion; and from the Ramban's own words there is proof to my words, for behold he holds that within thirty mourning was not renewed through the eulogy, and as if he holds that within thirty is considered like the actual time of death — and certainly the heat of the time of death is not comparable to afterward; therefore it is not considered possible to fulfill both what would eulogize him after thirty days — examine well.
