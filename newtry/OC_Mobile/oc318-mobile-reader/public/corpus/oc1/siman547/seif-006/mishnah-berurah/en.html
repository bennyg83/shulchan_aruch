(11) Except for his relatives - But one who is not obligated and wishes to tear for the sake of honor is forbidden. And even for his relatives, this is specifically if he died on Chol HaMoed; but if he died on Yom Tov, one should not tear on the festival until after Yom Tov, for that time is neither a time of emotion nor a time of hearing the report.
(12) For a scholar - Whom they ask a matter of halachah in every place, and he answers, even if he does not know how to give and take in it. And there is no such Torah scholar in our time (Magen Avraham).
(13) And for a relative, etc. - Now he proceeds to explain his preceding words.
(14) If it is within thirty days - But if it is after thirty days, he does not tear on the festival, even for his father or mother (Yoreh Deah, siman 340, seif 31, in the Shach there).
(15) And for an upright person, etc. - And if he was present at the moment the soul departed, he is obligated to tear even if he is not an upright person; and see in Yoreh Deah, siman 340, seif 6, in the gloss, that they customarily are lenient even regarding an upright person, unless one is present at the moment the soul departs.
(16) And there are those who disagree - Who hold that there is no tearing at all on Chol HaMoed; and the people of Ashkenaz customarily made a compromise between these two views, and distinguish between father and mother and other relatives. And regarding this the Rama concluded that where there is no clear custom, one should tear for all of them, for according to the main law the halachah follows the first opinion.
(17) The people of Ashkenaz - That is Ashkenaz proper; but in Poland they customarily tear for all of them (later authorities).
