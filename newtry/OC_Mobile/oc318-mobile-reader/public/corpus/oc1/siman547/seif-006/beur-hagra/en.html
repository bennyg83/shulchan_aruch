Seif 6: They do not tear, etc. A mishnah; and it speaks of Chol HaMoed, as is written in the Yerushalmi, and Tosafos there and the Rosh cited it; see there.
That they, etc. Yerushalmi and the aforementioned Tosafos.
And likewise, etc. There in the Gemara, from the fact that it challenges the mishnah, it teaches that [this applies] even on Chol HaMoed.
And for a relative, etc., even. From its saying "a scholar," etc., [it follows that] even for an upright person one is obligated even if he was not present at the moment the soul departed; and one must therefore say that for an upright person it is specifically when one knew between death and burial, and for a scholar even upon hearing the report; and from its saying "all are like his relatives," it teaches that the same applies regarding relatives; and that is what it means by "and for a relative," etc., "and for a scholar," etc., "and for an upright person," etc.
And there are those who disagree. For they hold that the mishnah speaks of a weekday.
And they customarily, etc. They hold like the first reasoning; but because regarding others they are lenient even on a weekday, as is written in Yoreh Deah, siman 340, seif 6 and seif 8 in the gloss, therefore on Chol HaMoed it is forbidden, for it is like [tearing] for others, for whom according to the lenient opinion they do not tear; and see there.
