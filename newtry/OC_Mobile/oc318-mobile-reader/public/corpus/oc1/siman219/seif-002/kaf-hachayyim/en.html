2) A minor under thirteen does not recite Birkas HaGomel. It is inappropriate for him to say, 'Who bestows goodness upon the guilty,' because he is not subject to punishment. If he intends 'the guilty' to refer to his father, this is forbidden. If he omits the word 'guilty,' one may not alter the formulation established by the Sages. If one says that 'guilty' refers to him because suffering comes upon him due to a previous incarnation, how does he know this? Perhaps it comes because of his father's sin. Therefore, he should not recite the blessing. Maharam Mintz, siman 14; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Responsa Lachmei Todah, siman 5, however, disagrees and concludes that even a minor recites it. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that the custom in their regions is for a minor to recite it; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, however, writes that in their country the practice follows Maharam Mintz and a minor does not recite it; see there. Likewise, throughout our regions in Eretz Yisrael we have neither heard nor seen that a minor recites it. Therefore, wherever there is no established custom, he should not recite it, because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. All the cited authorities imply that after thirteen, all agree he recites it. Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman writes the same; see there. See further below, paragraph 29.
