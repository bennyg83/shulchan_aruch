He blesses: Blessed are You, Hashem, etc. Rambam writes that he stands among them and blesses. Kesef Mishneh says the source requires study, and therefore Shulchan Aruch omitted this requirement. Bach writes that Rambam derived it from the verse, "and in the assembly of elders they shall praise Him": the elders are seated, implying that those praising stand. This is strained. In my humble opinion Rambam derived it from Scripture calling it "praise," making it like Hallel, which we rule below in siman 422 must be recited standing, as it says, "Praise, servants of Hashem, who stand." This is the practice and should not be changed. Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 14, writes that a boy who is not yet bar mitzvah should not recite this blessing at all, because saying "Who bestows good upon the guilty" would appear to call his father wicked. Omitting the word "guilty" would alter the fixed text of the blessing.
[Levush] The wicked, etc. Perishah asks that Chazal say whoever claims that the Holy One, blessed be He, overlooks sin will have his life overlooked. He answers that this applies when one says Hashem does so without reason, merely out of indulgence. Here, however, one says He acts because of recompense and kindness.
