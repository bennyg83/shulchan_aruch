(s.k. 2) And those who hear, etc.: and if they did not say amen it is not an impediment, for in our gemara text it is not that the listeners respond but Rambam wrote it and Tur translated; and therefore even though l'chatchila one is concerned for Rambam and Tur's view, nevertheless b'dieved we rely on our text.

Good to bless standing: Rambam, and Kesef Mishneh, requires study from where; and Bach wrote why Beit Yosef omitted it in Shulchan Aruch; nevertheless Bach wrote: however all Israel's custom is to bless standing and one may not change, and such is the implication of the verse "let them exalt Him in the congregation of the people and in the assembly of elders let them praise Him" (for we derive that ten are required: this verse is written at the end of the chapter of Tehillim in which these four who must give thanks were mentioned, and they expound "congregation of the people" means ten and "assembly of elders" means Torah scholars; and we say that within these ten both must be elders, meaning Torah scholars: minority of elders is two); and since it says "and in the assembly of elders let them praise Him": implies elders are sitting, from which it is implied that those praising are not sitting but standing, end of Bach's words; and why Bach wrote Magen Avraham wrote "good to bless standing": in Rambam it was not written "good" for for Rambam it is by law; only since in many places he left the law in requires study and Shulchan Aruch omitted it, it implies he does not hold like Rambam: therefore Magen Avraham wrote "good," meaning l'chatchila.
