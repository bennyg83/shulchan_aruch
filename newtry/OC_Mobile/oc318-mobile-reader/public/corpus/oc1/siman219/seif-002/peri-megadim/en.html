He blessed. See Taz and Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. He himself did not fulfill his obligation with fewer than ten, and the matter depends upon the dispute. Regarding one’s teacher or father, however, the blessing is optional, and it may be recited even before fewer than ten.
Know. Initially I found the following difficult. Rabbeinu Yonah, who writes this, holds that after the fact one has not fulfilled his obligation before fewer than ten. The Gemara’s question, “But does he not require ten?” is directed at Rav Yehudah: how did he fulfill his obligation? According to the opinion that after the fact he has fulfilled it, however, what is the question? It must then be directed at the others: how did they recite the blessing before fewer than ten? Accordingly, even according to the opinion that the rescued person has fulfilled his obligation, there is still a practical stringency that another person may not bless before fewer than ten. I resolved this as follows. The question concerns Rav Yehudah. He should not have intended to fulfill his obligation by answering amen. Although he was compelled to answer amen because it is obligatory, as stated in siman 215, seif 2, he should not have intended to fulfill his obligation, since initially ten are required. This passage proves that the listener must intend to fulfill his obligation. The one reciting the blessing, however, apparently need not intend to discharge him, for they did not know that they would discharge Rav Yehudah, as the Gemara implies. See there. I later saw that Eliyah Rabbah, seifim katanim 9 and 10, noticed this point. See also Magen Avraham, seif katan 4.
