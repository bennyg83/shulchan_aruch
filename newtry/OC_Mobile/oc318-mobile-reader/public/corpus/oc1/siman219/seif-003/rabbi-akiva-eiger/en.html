Seif 3. Before ten, two of whom are Torah scholars. Ranach writes in his derashos on Parashas Emor that these people must certainly be in addition to those giving thanks and those who experienced the miracle. Therefore, if they are traveling and there are not ten plus two scholars besides them, then even if they are numerous, since all of them experienced the miracle they may not recite the blessing.
There. The sugya states: Abaye said, “He must give thanks before ten.” Mar Zutra said, “Two of them must be Torah scholars.” The Gemara asks: perhaps there must be ten ordinary people and two scholars. Tosafos writes that we act stringently, and even if two scholars are unavailable, he nevertheless recites the blessing. Tur writes in the name of Tosafos that since the matter was not resolved, we act stringently and require both. According to our version of Tosafos, this is difficult. How can there be a stringency to recite a blessing? Everywhere the rule is that in a doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently and do not recite it because of the prohibition of taking Hashem’s name in vain. Perhaps Tosafos means that the expression “he must give thanks” applies only initially, but if he cannot find ten he recites it even before fewer than ten; see Tur and Beit Yosef. If so, the difficulty is even greater. Since the entire discussion concerns whether initially he must exert himself to find ten and an additional two scholars, the ruling should have been that he must make the effort to obtain ten plus two scholars. Tur’s version is therefore understandable: we act stringently and require both. Yet even this initially appears difficult. What stringency is involved? He does not bless until both are present, which is usually termed a leniency, as we say that a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently. It appears, however, that Tur means the entire discussion concerns only the preferred practice. After the fact, if he cannot find ten, he nevertheless recites the blessing, as appears to be Tur’s view upon careful examination. Thus, he correctly writes that we act stringently, meaning that one must make the effort to find ten and two scholars. Our version of Tosafos, however, remains difficult.
