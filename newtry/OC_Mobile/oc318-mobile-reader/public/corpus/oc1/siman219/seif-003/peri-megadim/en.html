Over. See Taz. Regarding one’s father or teacher, one may recite “Who has bestowed every good upon you.” The phrase “Who bestows good upon the culpable” refers to the person reciting the blessing. Regarding a woman after childbirth, Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 5, writes that the husband should intend during Bircas HaTorah, when he says “Bless Hashem, the blessed One,” to discharge his wife’s obligation, and she should answer amen, as it is written, “Give thanks to Hashem; bless His name.” In seif katan 12 he writes that she should recite the blessing herself privately. It may be argued that this creates a possible blessing in vain. The third method, however, that the husband say, “Who has bestowed good upon you,” and that she answer amen, presents no concern. See there. The expression “the culpable” does not apply to a woman, just as it does not apply to a minor, because a woman’s principal punishment comes on account of her husband. See there. This too requires study, for she also has punishments of her own. I, in my poverty, have a further difficulty here. How can one say over a person who is not beloved, “Blessed is the Merciful One, Who gave you to us and not to the dust”? The word “Merciful One” is a Divine name, as stated in siman 167 and in Taz, seif katan 11, and it is forbidden to mention even a Divine name alone. One might answer that the prohibition applies only when one uses the actual Name, but not when saying “the Merciful One.” If so, one could recite the entire blessing in Aramaic: “The Merciful One, King,” and so forth. One might answer that mentioning kingship is prohibited, but that is not so. See Even HaEzer, siman 34, Chelkas Mechokek and Beis Shmuel, seif katan 6, which appears to imply that the principal concern is the Divine name, not kingship. If it is nevertheless permitted, first, Taz here appears to forbid it, and second, why were the authorities so deeply concerned to rule leniently in cases of uncertain blessings because of the prohibition of taking Hashem’s name in vain? One could simply recite the blessing in Aramaic, since blessings may be said in any language, and there would be no prohibition. We must therefore say that when one says “the Merciful One” alone there is no concern, but when he adds “King of the universe,” it clearly indicates that “the Merciful One” means Hashem, and it is forbidden.
