Before ten, etc. Initially one should be concerned for Tosafot and the Tur, who rule stringently that both conditions are required, and should seek two Torah scholars in addition to the ten, making twelve in total. Rokeach rules likewise. Lechem Chamudot, page 68, wonders at the Tur: requiring twelve would seemingly be a leniency, because if twelve are unavailable he would not bless, and doubtful blessings are treated leniently. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah discusses this at length. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. The Tur follows his own ruling that after the fact one fulfills the blessing even with fewer than ten. Thus the discussion of ten plus two is only an initial enhancement of the mitzvah, and calling the larger requirement a stringency is entirely proper. Rabbenu Yerucham, page 104, writes that one must give thanks before ten and two of them must be Torah scholars, whereas Rif mentioned two Torah scholars only within the ten. This is surprising, because Rif explicitly writes "and two of them are rabbis." One would have to say Rabbenu Yerucham meant two scholars in addition to the ten, but this is strained. Regarding the Levush's statement that the practice is to bless after Torah reading, I found an additional reason in Torat Chaim, chapter Chelek: when one says "Bless Hashem," this itself resembles thanksgiving, as it says, "Give thanks to Hashem, bless His Name." For a woman after childbirth, her husband should intend when he says Barchu to give thanks on her behalf, and she should answer Amen to his blessing. See above, seif katan 12.
[Levush] And another person, etc. Meaning that another person may say it even when ten are not present, although the sick person is not thereby discharged even if he answers Amen. This is from Rabbenu Yonah as cited by Beit Yosef. The reason must be as explained in seif 4.
[7] Some say that he has fulfilled, etc. Tur writes this because the wording “he must give thanks before ten” implies only an initial requirement. It is surprising that in Yoreh Deah, siman 274, concerning the crowns on the letters shaatnez getz, Tur rules oppositely, that the word “must” applies even after the fact.
And it is good to repeat, etc. Bach rules that he should not bless again at all, because the word "must" denotes only an initial requirement. Be'er Sheva, page 100, rules likewise. In my humble opinion, Bach's proof from the beginning of chapter 2 of Gittin contradicts his proof from Berachot 15 and Eruvin, because in Gittin the Gemara initially understood the term as an initial requirement. In truth, it appears that both meanings are possible in the word "must." Therefore the ruling of Shulchan Aruch and Levush appears primary. Moreover, I saw that Rabbenu Yerucham rules he has not fulfilled his obligation, and so appears from Mordechai and the poskim.
