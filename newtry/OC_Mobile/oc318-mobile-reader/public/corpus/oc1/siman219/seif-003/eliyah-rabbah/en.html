(3) He blesses "Blessed are You, Heaven," etc. Rambam's language: he stands among them and blesses; and Kesef Mishnah wrote it requires study — from where does he have this? — and therefore they omitted it in Shulchan Aruch. And Bach wrote that he learned from the language of the verse, "and in the seat of elders they shall praise Him" — apparently elders in session, from which we infer they praise standing — end quote; and this is strained. And in my humble opinion Rambam derives from that the verse calls it language of hallel, comparable to hallel that we maintain below siman 422, that hallel is standing, as it is written "praise, all servants of Heaven who stand," and so is the custom and one should not change. R' Moshe Minz wrote in responsum siman 14: a youth who is not bar mitzvah should not bless at all, for it appears as if he calls his father wicked; and if he skips to the obligated ones, it is changing the nature of the blessing.
