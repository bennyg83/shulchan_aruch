And two of them are rabbis — See Peri Megadim, who is uncertain in the Mechaber's words, that he omitted the view of Tosafot that we require two rabbis besides the ten, since Rif and Rambam also agree to this method; and in truth he followed in error the words of Beit Yosef, and Prisha already wrote that it is a printing error in Beit Yosef, and on the contrary Rif upholds Rambam's method, who holds the opposite of Tosafot; and so in Eshkol and in Hilkhot Ravyah [now published] he also copied thus; and I found that the commentator on Chiddushei HaRa'ah already noted this — see there. And in the main matter there is no wonder at all about them, for it is known that many Rishonim hold that wherever the Gemara did not conclude with a refutation, the law of the Amora is not rejected [and Tosafot possibly uphold Rashbam's method in perek Chazakat HaBatim (Bava Batra 52b) s.v. kashya, that he does not hold like these Rishonim — afterward I found that the Gra noted all this]. And one can say also in our matter that Mar Zutra holds there is no reason to require two besides the ten — for where do we find in all the Torah to require a number of twelve people? And certainly these two are from within the ten. And know further that Tosafot's language here as it is before us is very abbreviated; see in the Achronim who strained to understand it, and in Tosafot of Rabbeinu Yehuda the language is very clear, and this is what he says: "I will say ten and two rabbis — difficult; and since it was not resolved for us, we act stringently; and if there are none, he should bless; even if there are not two rabbins" — end quote; and it is explicit that these are two matters; and the Gra in his explanation also intended this per his broad view — see there.

They are not common, rabbis, etc. — See in the Gra's explanation, that it implies from him that per the Mechaber, who ruled initially that only ten are required, there is no source for this law [and so Chemed Moshe questioned this — see there]; and nevertheless in practice it seems as the Mechaber ruled; and it is possible that even the Gra admits here that it is not invalidating, for one who examines Beit HaGadol sees that he did not copy at all the statement of Mar Zutra that we require two rabbis; and it is possible he holds that since Rav Ashi raises on it difficulty after difficulty, presumably he did not pay attention to it at all; and he holds that since the early Amoraim like Rav Yehuda and Abaye did not mention this at all, he holds the verse cites the ordinary manner of the matter, that one seeks after elders in order that they bless him, as Rambam wrote that the listeners say "He who bestowed good upon you" etc. — but not that the law is so; and as with the blessing of bridegrooms for R"N, who derives [Ketubot 7b] from "And Boaz took ten men of the elders" etc. — certainly "elders" is not precise, and he did not take them except so that they bless him, as is explained there truly in the verse "and they blessed him" [and therefore one must say thus for R"N, that he does not hold like the other view that he took elders to teach Ammonite etc. — see there well]. So too here; and so in the version of Rif that was before Rabbeinu Yeruham — he also did not bring this of Mar Zutra; and so Ra'ah in his Hilkhot wrote that Rif did not bring this of Mar Zutra, and wrote on this, and it implies because in the Gemara they challenge on it "I will say" etc. — see there; and so wrote after him in Shitah Mekubetzet — see there; and as we also wrote the reason in Beit HaGadol — and therefore even though we rule that we require two rabbis, nevertheless if rabbis are not common it is reasonable that it is not invalidating, since Beit HaGadol and Rif [per the girsa of Ra'ah and Rabbeinu Yeruham] did not copy this statement at all.

And some say he has not fulfilled — See in Tur, who brought proof from that Abaye said in the language "need" — it implies it is only a general mitzvah; and in Beit HaGadol it implies there is an obligation in this — see there. However Ra'ah's view in his Hilkhot is like Tur — see there. And know that from Machatzit HaShekel's words in the introduction to the siman it implies that per the last view, if he blesses without ten it is a blessing in vain, for it was not enacted at all without ten; and therefore per his words, since the Mechaber did not decide this dispute, therefore automatically he is not permitted to bless without ten. But R' Akiva Eiger's view in his chiddushim is that even per the last view it is not forbidden to bless without ten as praise and thanks, for it is not less than an ordinary person who blesses this blessing on his fellow even though he was not obligated at all, as written in the gloss in seif 4; and how much more so he himself — only that if ten become available to him afterward he must bless again as an obligation per the last view. And nevertheless l'chatchila one should be careful not to bless without ten, even as praise and thanks, for if ten become available to him afterward he will not be able to bless a second time as an obligation, lest we hold like the first view and he already fulfilled — see there [but if he intended explicitly not to fulfill with the blessing, it is possible that per all he can return and bless, for even for one who holds a mitzvah does not require intent — that is in general; but not with explicit intent not to fulfill — see].
