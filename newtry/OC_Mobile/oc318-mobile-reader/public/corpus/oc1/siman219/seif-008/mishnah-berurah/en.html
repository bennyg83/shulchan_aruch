(24) All who took to bed — but if he did not take to bed at all, only that he has some mere ailment in his head or throat and the like — he does not bless even per this view.
(25) Because it is comparable to one raised [to judgment] etc. — that we do not know how his judgment will come out; similarly for a sick person — since he fell to bed we do not know his end, for many times even though at first there was no danger, in the end the illness intensifies and comes to danger.
(26) And there is no difference between etc. — meaning you should not say that an ailment that comes from time to time is already known that there is no danger in it — this is not so; on the contrary, whatever is fixed intensifies more; and even though a miracle was done for him many times and he was saved from it by Heaven's mercy — a miracle does not occur at every moment, end quote Rashba in responsum.
(27) And whether it is not fixed — but in every case, all who took to bed on account of it at the time he feared it he is obligated to bless afterward.
(28) Such as a wound in the hollow of the body — or other illness that has danger such as fever of the whole body (that they call shvayderin) as explained in siman 328; and see Biur Halacha. And so they practice in Ashkenaz — and Magen Avraham wrote some practice per the Mechaber's view and so is Eliyah Rabbah le-dinah; and so wrote in Magen Giborim that whoever became ill in his whole body such that for such they desecrate Shabbat through a gentile — he blesses HaGomel [and brought thus in name of Radbaz]; and similarly Chayei Adam wrote; but he wrote that nevertheless he does not bless unless he fell to bed not less than three days; and see Biur Halacha that if his illness is a matter that has danger, even in less than three days he must also bless.
