4) There: Seafarers, when they came up from the sea. This means that even if a miracle occurred to him at one point, where he was in danger and was saved, he need not recite the blessing immediately. Rather, he waits until he comes up from the sea. Likewise, if one travels by ship and occasionally enters a port and remains there for one or two days, he need not recite the blessing, since he is still traveling and intends to return immediately to the sea. If he remains a long time, however, whether he should recite it requires consideration. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, cites it and writes that he found it recorded that one should not recite the blessing until he has completely emerged from the danger; see there. Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 2, writes the same. Accordingly, even if he remains a long time, he should not recite it until reaching his destination. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, and Machazik Berachah, paragraph 5, likewise write in the name of Responsa Kol Yaakov, siman 3, that one traveling from place to place who passes through large cities and rests there for a day or two should not recite HaGomel there, but only upon reaching his destination; see there.
