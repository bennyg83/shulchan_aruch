(10) If another blessed — from the Mechaber's unstated wording it implies that even another who is not his relative may bless for his fellow's good, whoever rejoices in it; and see in Biur Halacha s.v. and this is not etc.

(11) And he answers amen — and if he did not answer amen he has not fulfilled; and it deals with one who heard the blessing from beginning to end and also intended to fulfill through it his obligation of Birkat HaGomel that he became obligated in.

(12) He has fulfilled — and the same applies that l'chatchila he can exempt himself thus; and specifically when there are ten there — but if there are not ten certainly he should not intend to exempt himself; and b'dieved if he intended, whether he must return and bless depends on the dispute in seif 3, and he blesses without shem u'malchut.

(13) di-havakh lan — in the gemara the text is di-havakh lan and not yehavakh le-afra.

(14) And this is not etc. — meaning even if there were not ten people there; and per the first view mentioned in seif 3 who holds he does not fulfill with fewer than ten — if so the sick person does not fulfill through this blessing; nevertheless this is not called a blessing in vain regarding the blesser, who is permitted to say this blessing per all opinions even with fewer than ten [Gra].

(15) Even though he was not obligated — for obligation in this blessing is not mentioned in the gemara regarding the four who must give thanks except in themselves, and not that another blesses on them.

(16) Only as praise — meaning not as an obligation.

(17) For his fellow's good — and all the more so for his wife who is like his body he is permitted to bless; and from this some practice that when their wives give birth and return to health they stand and bless Birkat HaGomel, and when she answers amen on his blessing she fulfills through this; and the blessing formula: he says "Who bestows good to the undeserving" that He bestowed upon you all good. And if he blessed not in her presence he says that He bestowed upon my wife all good; and if he blesses on his father [or rabbi] he says "Blessed are You Heaven our Heaven King of the world Who bestowed upon my father [or rabbi] all good"; and if in his presence he says that He bestowed upon you all good.

(18) That he rejoices in it — and specifically regarding his relative or beloved that at least he rejoices in his healing or his salvation; but if he is not so happy in his heart but says so for the sake of peace — he should not bless with shem u'malchut for in this his blessing is in vain; rather he says "Blessed is Heaven Who bestowed good upon us"; and see in Biur Halacha that some dispute the fundamental law of this Rama gloss; and therefore it is proper to be careful l'chatchila not to bless Birkat HaGomel even on his relative and beloved except only on his father and rabbi.
