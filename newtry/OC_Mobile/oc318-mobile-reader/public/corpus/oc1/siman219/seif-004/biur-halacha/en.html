And he answers amen — See Mishna Berurah; and even though everywhere we hold that the listener is like the one who answers, and even without answering amen, as explained in siman 213 — here is different, for the blesser too was not obligated in this blessing but blesses as praise and laudation on his fellow's deliverance — therefore whoever did not answer amen has not fulfilled [Tur in name of Ra'ash]. But all this is only per his view that it is permitted to bless as praise and laudation on his fellow's deliverance even though he was not obligated in this; and see below in Biur Halacha s.v. ve'ein etc., and in Shaarei Kavanah he wrote another reasoning: since the blesser does not intend to release him with it, therefore we require at least that he answer amen within k'dei dibur of the blesser — see there. And behold per this reasoning, it would seem that everywhere too, if he answered amen we do not require that he intend to release him — and in truth this is not so; see at the end of siman 213; and it is possible that here it is preferable more, because it is not possible to intend to release him in this formula, as we wrote in Shaarei Tziyon — see there; and in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger he wrote another reason: here "the listener is like the answerer" does not apply, for even if he would say explicitly himself a blessing in this language it would not help, for the blessing was said facing the one delivered and is not applicable except to a foreign person who gives thanks for his fellow's good — and therefore we require that he answer amen on his fellow's blessing; if so, at least he also admits to Heaven that He bestowed good upon him; but without this it does not help at all — and this is very correct.

And this is not a blessing in vain — And so agreed Magen Avraham, and wrote that so too seems the Mechaber's view, who ruled in general on this, and not as Beit Yosef wrote that one should rebuke one who blesses Birkat HaGomel on his fellow; but in Avnei Nezer he wrote that the main thing is as Beit Yosef's words, that besides a son or student they are not permitted to bless, and so wrote in Eretz Tzvi that such is the view of most poskim — see there. And so it seems that in truth the words of the gloss, which are Tur's words in name of Ra'ash, are difficult: where do we find blessings that one is not obligated at all and nevertheless may bless? And even though they said about Shehecheyanu that it is optional — they do not mean complete option, but that it is not so much an obligation; but there is a mitzvah to bless. And further: what is the difference between this blessing and Birkat HaNes in siman 218, where in the entire siman there they investigated on which matter one may bless and on which one may not, until it was decided that even for a miracle done to tribes one may not bless unless a majority of Israel — and no posek descended there to say that in any case even without obligation one may bless, rejoicing in the deliverance of thousands of Israel; and therefore they do not hold like Tur's reasoning, and Rav Chanan and the rabbis did not bless Rav Yehuda except because he was their rabbi, as Ramban and Rashba and Ra'ah wrote at the head of the sugya; and because a rabbi too is obligated to bless Birkat HaNes; and even though Ra'ah at the end of the sugya wrote another reasoning, that on one who rejoices in him one must bless Birkat HaGomel — see there — nevertheless he himself also wrote that on all who rejoice in him one may also bless Birkat HaNes, and they did not require father or rabbi except specifically when reaching the place where the miracle was done, and not when seeing him himself; if so, for us, who did not rule thus in siman 218 seif 6 in the gloss, and it is not preferable to the place of the miracle where we require specifically his father or rabbi or majority of Israel, as written there — if so, also for Birkat HaGomel we have no [basis] to rule like this method except specifically for his father and rabbi and the like, for there is no reason to prefer Birkat HaGomel over Birkat She'asah Nissim, which was enacted on a miracle; and therefore it seems that without his father and rabbi it is not correct to bless, even a father on his son and the like [and see what he wrote in Eretz Tzvi in name of Birkei Yosef, and perhaps their reason is as I wrote]; and see in Avnei Nezer sk 2, that his view is to rely on the gloss's words regarding a husband on his wife, because his wife is like his body — see there; and this too requires study, for just as in Birkat Al HaNissim a husband does not bless on his wife, as we wrote in siman 218 — so too here; but on his father and rabbi it is reasonable that he must bless HaGomel, as he must bless on his miracle, and as the act of Rav Chana of Gadya brought in the Gemara, who blessed Rav Yehuda his rabbi. And behold I saw in Sefer Avnei Nezer that he brought in name of Ramban that Birkat HaGomel is inferior, that even for his father and rabbi it is only optional, for the blessing of healing applies to him himself and not to others [meaning this is like other human needs for which a person blesses on them every morning, such as "Who clothes the naked" and "Who releases the bound" and the like — it is possible his student or son will need to bless because his father has clothing and the like; not so when a complete miracle was done for him, his son and student must bless on it]. And even though we do not find in other blessings that the Sages permitted to bless where one is not obligated for this — it is possible because when a complete miracle was done for his father and rabbi there is an obligation to bless on it, therefore they permitted him also in this to bless on it. And in Rambam he omitted entirely the incident of Rav Chana — and it requires study in his reason.
