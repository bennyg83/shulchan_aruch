(10) [Levush] And that he said, etc. But Olat Tamid wrote that even in this he has fulfilled and concluded; and in Atteret Tzvi he did not write thus and was not precise — end quote. And he did not know that Atteret Tzvi is taken from Levush's words; and in truth from Be'er Sheva it implies Rabbeinu Yonah, who wrote thus, holds that even if he blessed with fewer than ten he has not fulfilled; but "some say" that he fulfilled above — the same law applies here that he fulfilled; and so it implies in Shulchan Aruch and Magen Avraham. And perhaps the intent of Levush and Atteret Tzvi too is that if he does not say it before ten, he blesses without God's name and kingship, as above. But it is difficult for me: the Gemara challenges on that which R' Yehuda said "I have exempted myself from giving thanks," and Abaye said one must give thanks before ten etc. — what is the challenge? Abaye admits that b'dieved he fulfills! And one can strain and answer that he challenges R' Yehuda, that he should not have answered amen l'chatchila; and even though the Gemara answers afterward that he answered amen — one can say that the questioner also knew this, but it did not enter his mind that answering amen is like giving thanks himself — and it requires study. Or alternatively, he challenges thus: R' Yehuda should not have intended to fulfill, for if he does not intend, even if he answers amen he has not fulfilled.
