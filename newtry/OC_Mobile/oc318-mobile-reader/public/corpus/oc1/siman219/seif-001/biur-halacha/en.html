Those who descend the sea — Is it specifically the sea, or the same applies to a river on which they travel in ships or in rafts of wood as is customary on great rivers? It would seem that it depends on the custom of Spain and Ashkenaz explained below by the Mechaber in seif 67: per the view there, even on an ordinary road they also bless — so too here a great river is not less than an ordinary road. However, per the custom of Ashkenaz that on a road they do not bless, it is possible that on a river as well they do not bless, for danger is not common as with a full sea, as explained above. And know further: it is obvious that per all opinions, both on the sea and in the desert they bless even if no danger occurred to him — such as if he crossed the sea and there was no storm wind, and the like; or he went in the desert and did not lose his way and did not lack water, and the like — nevertheless the Sages enacted to bless; and even though in Scripture it is written "they lost their way in the desert, in desolation" etc., and likewise regarding those who descend the sea "and he said, and a storm wind stood" etc. — it is not specifically that it happened to him, but since such events are liable to happen to him he must give thanks that he was saved from this.

Imprisoned — See what Mishna Berurah wrote in the name of Magen Avraham, that it speaks of one imprisoned on capital matters; and it seems from his words here that even an imprisoned person is not obligated to give thanks except because he is in danger due to his imprisonment — therefore for imprisonment due to money that they sue him for, certainly in any case the matter will not come to danger of life, for even if he cannot pay they will not kill him; therefore they do not bless. Not so for imprisonment because there is a capital charge against him — if so, behold he stands in danger lest his judgment be concluded to execution and the like, and therefore he blesses. And per this reason, also for imprisonment due to capital matters that they bless — it would seem this is specifically when imprisoned until his judgment is concluded, for then he stands in danger, for he does not know whether his judgment will come out to acquittal or mercy; and then if he is released he must bless. Not so when his judgment was concluded that he sits in prison, as in these countries where sitting in prison itself is the punishment — even if the charge was capital, nevertheless when he leaves prison he does not bless, for there is no danger after it is known that this is his entire punishment [unless the stay itself is dangerous, as is known that there are prisons for great crimes where staying in them a long time is danger of life, and certainly he must bless when he leaves from there]. However, one can reject [Magen Avraham] that he is obligated to give thanks and bless Heaven that his judgment was not concluded from the outset only to sitting in prison and not to capital punishment. And behold Avnei Nezer and Birkei Yosef and other Achronim disagreed with him and proved from many Rishonim that even for imprisonment due to money where there was no danger at all he also blesses, since in any case he was imprisoned and was not master over himself. And there are Achronim who wrote that Magen Avraham too did not write his words except per the custom of Ashkenaz brought in seif 7 and seif 8, that specifically on a road where there is danger or on illness in the hollow of the body — not so for the custom of Spain, where even for any illness or even on an ordinary road they bless — so too here regarding imprisonment, even for monetary matters; and these early authorities are holders of this method of the custom of Spain. But in truth from Magen Avraham's conclusion it seems that even for the custom of Spain we require specifically imprisonment due to capital matters. And further proof: Magen Avraham himself inclined in sk 8 to the Mechaber's view that even without a wound in the hollow of the body; if so, how did he rule here in general that specifically due to capital matters? And it seems Magen Avraham's view is that these early authorities whom Birkei Yosef brings follow the method of the Aruch, that even for any slight illness they bless; and therefore the same applies to imprisonment due to money, since in any case he is imprisoned and cannot leave as he wishes — he is obligated in the blessing. Not so for the Mechaber in seif 8, who did not fully grasp the Aruch's method but required at least that he took to his bed; and the reason: since he took to his bed, even though he is not dangerous for now, nevertheless it often happens that since he fell to bed the illness overcomes him and he comes to danger — and for this he is obligated to give thanks, as the Mechaber truly wrote this reasoning in what he concludes and compares to one raised to the gallows to be judged, who does not know how his judgment will come out. If so, per this method, for imprisonment due to capital matters that in any case they will raise him to the gallows he is obligated to bless; not so for imprisonment due to money, for they will never raise him to the gallows to judge him because he has nothing to pay — he is not obligated to bless. And nevertheless, even if we grant their words to reject Magen Avraham's words, this is only for the custom of Spain and per the Mechaber's conclusion in seif 8; not so for Rama's view and the custom of Ashkenaz and one who practices thus — certainly Magen Avraham's words were correct, that where there is no danger they do not bless; and what does it matter that he is not master over himself [unless in a place where even for imprisonment for monetary matters he is not entirely secure from danger, as seems from the language of Mahariv]. And it is astonishing on Avnei Nezer that he did not descend to this. And know further: even according to those who disagree with Magen Avraham and their method — if they judged him to sit in prison, certainly he is obligated to bless when he leaves afterward, even though he had no danger at all; nevertheless it seems all this is when he sits at least in a complete prison for some time — but not when they judged him to sit a day or two in the police house as is customary in our time for light sins against the laws of the government, may its glory be exalted — for in this per all opinions they do not bless, for there is no danger here, and also the reason of Mahariv Migash that Birkei Yosef brought, because he is not master over himself, does not apply at all to the matter of the verse that writes in it "sitters in darkness and shadow of death" etc. "and they are afflicted through toil of their heart" etc. "and they stagger and there is none to help" etc. And greater than this I found one who wrote that an imprisoned person blesses only when he was bound in iron chains — and even though for the law it does not seem so, for it appears he followed the language of Scripture that writes "prisoners of poverty and iron," and we already wrote above in Biur Halacha that we do not require similarity to the verse, and even without "they lost their way in the desert" or "a storm wind did not stand" they also bless — and the same here; and so it seems from other poskim who did not mention this condition. But in any case for sitting a day or two it is certainly not the matter of the verse at all, and one does not bless.
