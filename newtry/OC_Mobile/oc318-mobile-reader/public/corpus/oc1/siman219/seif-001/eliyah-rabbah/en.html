When he comes up from it. Meaning, even if a separate miracle occurred to him at some point during the journey, he need not bless immediately; he waits until he emerges from the sea. Similarly, if he traveled by ship and they occasionally enter a port and remain there a day or two, he need not bless yet. If they remain a long time, the law requires study, as Olat Tamid writes. I found it written that one should not bless until completely delivered from the danger. This is why "it was good" was not said on the second day of creation, because the work of the waters was not completed; the word good indicates a benefit completed fully and well. Likewise, a sick person waits until he returns to health. Shelah, page 202, writes that one should recite the thanksgiving passage in Parashat Tzav, from "This is the law of the peace offering" through "he shall not leave any of it until morning," and say a prayer that it be considered as though he had offered a thanksgiving offering in its proper time.
