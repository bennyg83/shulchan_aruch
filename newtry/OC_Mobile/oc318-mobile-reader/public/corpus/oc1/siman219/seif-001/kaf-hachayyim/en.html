1) [Seif 1] Four must give thanks, etc. One who became obligated for all four circumstances recites only one blessing for all of them. Pri HaAretz, Orach Chaim section, siman 7; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur. Likewise, one who was imprisoned two or three times requires only one blessing. Kol Ben Levi, siman 1; Ginas Veradim, rule 8, siman 48, writes the same.
2) A minor under thirteen does not recite Birkas HaGomel. It is inappropriate for him to say, 'Who bestows goodness upon the guilty,' because he is not subject to punishment. If he intends 'the guilty' to refer to his father, this is forbidden. If he omits the word 'guilty,' one may not alter the formulation established by the Sages. If one says that 'guilty' refers to him because suffering comes upon him due to a previous incarnation, how does he know this? Perhaps it comes because of his father's sin. Therefore, he should not recite the blessing. Maharam Mintz, siman 14; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Responsa Lachmei Todah, siman 5, however, disagrees and concludes that even a minor recites it. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, cites this and writes that the custom in their regions is for a minor to recite it; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, however, writes that in their country the practice follows Maharam Mintz and a minor does not recite it; see there. Likewise, throughout our regions in Eretz Yisrael we have neither heard nor seen that a minor recites it. Therefore, wherever there is no established custom, he should not recite it, because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. All the cited authorities imply that after thirteen, all agree he recites it. Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman writes the same; see there. See further below, paragraph 29.
3) Women are obligated in Birkas HaGomel. Regarding the requirement to give thanks before ten and the impropriety of a woman standing before men, she may stand in the women's section of the synagogue and recite the blessing while the men hear in their section. At minimum, she should recite it before one man or before women. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, who writes that the pious R' Yosef Molcho agrees in a handwritten responsum, siman 141. Knesses HaGedolah's statement that she may recite it before one man or women follows the view that one who recited it before fewer than ten fulfilled her obligation, as stated below in seif 3. According to the view that she did not fulfill her obligation, however, this would be a blessing in vain. Therefore, since we are concerned about doubtful blessings, a woman should recite it only before ten, like a man. Since it is improper for a woman to stand before men, she should recite it in the women's section while ten men hear in their section, or before ten male relatives and acquaintances. Otherwise, she should recite it without the Name and kingship.
4) There: Seafarers, when they came up from the sea. This means that even if a miracle occurred to him at one point, where he was in danger and was saved, he need not recite the blessing immediately. Rather, he waits until he comes up from the sea. Likewise, if one travels by ship and occasionally enters a port and remains there for one or two days, he need not recite the blessing, since he is still traveling and intends to return immediately to the sea. If he remains a long time, however, whether he should recite it requires consideration. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, cites it and writes that he found it recorded that one should not recite the blessing until he has completely emerged from the danger; see there. Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 2, writes the same. Accordingly, even if he remains a long time, he should not recite it until reaching his destination. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, and Machazik Berachah, paragraph 5, likewise write in the name of Responsa Kol Yaakov, siman 3, that one traveling from place to place who passes through large cities and rests there for a day or two should not recite HaGomel there, but only upon reaching his destination; see there. Ledavid Emes, siman 23, paragraph 5; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1; Lev Chaim, volume 3, siman 54.
5) Maamar Mordechai writes there that emissaries from Eretz Yisrael practice reciting Birkas HaGomel in every place they visit. He explains that their lengthy stays there are the reason for this practice. He further writes that he saw people traveling from one place to another recite HaGomel upon reaching their destination and recite it again when they returned home safely; see there. Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, section Berachos, siman 2, paragraph 11, cites this. There he writes in the name of R' Yosef Abulafia that those who travel to prostrate themselves at the graves of the righteous practice reciting it at each place. The reason is that those setting out for the graves of the righteous desire and intend each separate location, and they depart with this intent. He writes that he himself acted accordingly and recited the blessing; see there. It appears that this applies when one intends to remain and rest there for several days. If he intends only to prostrate himself and return immediately that day or the next, he should not recite the blessing until reaching the place where he intends to rest for several days or until returning home safely. We acted accordingly in the year 5668, when we traveled from Yerushalayim to Chevron to prostrate ourselves at the graves of the Patriarchs. Since we did not intend to remain but to return on the second day, we did not recite HaGomel there, but only upon returning to Yerushalayim.
6) There: When they came up from the sea, etc. Regarding one who traveled by ship upon the open sea or traveled overland and experienced no mishap, Amudei Esh, siman 2, paragraph 23, writes that it is obvious to him that he must recite the blessing, that this is the implication of the Talmud and halachic authorities, and that this is universal practice; see there. Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, under heh, paragraph 38, agrees; see there.
7) There: And one who was ill, etc. He does not recite the blessing until he has fully recovered. Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1; Ledavid Emes, siman 23, paragraph 5. Accordingly, a woman after childbirth does not recite HaGomel until after seven days, for she has then fully recovered and is like any other person, as stated below in siman 617, seif 4; see there. See below, paragraph 27.
8) There: And one who was ill, etc. Regarding a patient who recovered but whom physicians assessed as having become a tereifah in his internal organs because of the illness he suffered, Petach HaDevir, paragraph 3, discusses the uncertainty and concludes that he should recite the blessing without the Name and kingship; see there.
9) A patient who recovered during mourning should recite HaGomel before ten but should not receive an aliyah to the Torah. Responsa R' Avraham Pala'gi, volume 3, siman 479; Kerem Shlomo; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 5; Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, section Mourning, paragraph 52. It appears that if fewer than three days of mourning remain, he should wait until completing his mourning and then recite the blessing. See below, seif 6. The blessing, 'Who performed a miracle for me,' however, should not be recited during mourning. Sedei Chemed there in the name of Chaim BeYad, siman 122; see there.
10) If a bone lodged in one's throat and he was saved, whether he must recite HaGomel was asked in Responsa Divrei Nechemyah, which concludes that the practical ruling requires consideration; see there. Sedei Chemed, Asifas Dinim, section Berachos, siman 2, paragraph 14, cites this. Therefore, he should recite it without the Name and kingship. Likewise, if one fell from a ladder and suffered pain in his limbs but was not bedridden and did not become ill, he should recite it without the Name and kingship. Rivash, siman 336; Radbaz, volume 3, siman 572; Ikarei HaDinim, siman 10, paragraph 52.
