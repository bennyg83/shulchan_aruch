Seif 1. A non-Jew who brought a lulav from outside the techum, etc. Note: Regarding the law whether it is permitted to buy an esrog on Shabbos and Yom Tov where he has no other esrog, we already alluded to this above. Now I will speak about this at length, with Hashem's help. The Magen Avraham above in siman 306 wrote simply that it is prohibited to buy an esrog on Yom Tov, and he wrote that in the synagogue he does not acquire it, but only at the time of its mitzvah, etc. Seemingly there is a proof for his words from what is written in the Yerushalmi, chapter 3, halachah 10, as follows: Rav Nachman bar Yaakov gave an esrog as a gift to his son. He said to him: when you acquire it and its mitzvah, return it to me. Now, one should be precise: why did he say, 'when you acquire it'? Since he had already given it to him, he had acquired it. What is the meaning of 'when you acquire it'? He should have said only: when you acquire its mitzvah, return it to me. From this it implies that although he gave it to him as a gift, he cannot acquire it before the mitzvah, for if so, it would be like acquiring an acquisition on Shabbos and would be prohibited. Therefore he can acquire only at the very time of the mitzvah. The matter can be explained further according to what the Re'em wrote, brought by the Shaar HaMelech in Hilchos Avel, that Chazal have power to uphold their words only where it is not at the same time, but if it is at the same time, Chazal do not have power to uphold their words against Torah law. Therefore this is understandable: regarding acquiring an acquisition before fulfilling the mitzvah, Chazal upheld their words; but at the time of fulfilling the mitzvah, he can intend to acquire it, for at the very time of fulfillment they did not uphold their words. Therefore he said: now you are still prohibited to acquire it; only when you acquire it and its mitzvah together, then return it to me. From this it also appeared to me to derive a novel law: although we hold in siman 658 that one who gives a gift on condition to return may give it to another, and that other to another, and in my work there I wrote that the Mechaber stated a novelty that after he fulfilled the obligation with it he may give it to another, and all the more so before he fulfilled the obligation with it, now I saw that one may say the opposite. Before he has fulfilled the obligation with it, even though the first person gave him the esrog as a gift, he has not yet acquired it, since he is not permitted to acquire an acquisition on Yom Tov. If so, since he did not acquire it, his transfer of ownership to another is nothing. Only after he has fulfilled the obligation with it has he already acquired it and it has become his, as is proven from the above Yerushalmi, and then he may give it to another. However, one can prove the opposite: anything in which he has no need except for a mitzvah is not considered an acquisition at all, and it is permitted to acquire it even if he has no need for it today but only tomorrow, and all the more so if he will need it today afterward. This is from what is written in chapter 3 of Sukkah regarding Rabban Gamliel and R. Yehoshua, etc., who were coming on a ship, etc. It says there: why does it say 'and he returned it to Rabban Gamliel'? This teaches us that a gift on condition to return is called a gift. This is difficult: how was he permitted to return it on Yom Tov? The Rosh proves that every gift on condition to return requires a new transfer, for he is obligated to return and transfer ownership to him. If so, how was it permitted to return it to Rabban Gamliel on Yom Tov? It is prohibited to acquire an acquisition on Yom Tov. How did he return and transfer ownership to him on Yom Tov? In particular, Rabban Gamliel did not need it again that day, only the next day, as the Kapos Temarim wrote there; if so, how did he transfer ownership to him that day? Perforce, it is proven that this is not considered acquiring an acquisition, since he needs it only for a mitzvah. If so, by the same law, it is permitted to acquire it, for what difference is there between a gift and a sale? It is explicitly proven from the Rosh's view that it is permitted to sell and buy an esrog on Yom Tov even for the need of tomorrow. However, one may reject this according to what I wrote in my work, siman 323, regarding the law of giving a gift to a non-Jew on Yom Tov. I wrote there that the main prohibition of a gift is only from the side of the recipient and not from the side of the giver, for the giver does nothing. I found this in HaMakneh, siman 65; see there. If so, it is understandable. One may say that even according to the Rosh's view, that a gift on condition to return requires a new transfer, this means only that the recipient must return and transfer ownership to him, and another person's intent to transfer ownership to him is required, that the recipient intend to give it to him as a new gift. But nevertheless, we do not require the recipient's own intent to acquire, that he intend to acquire it and perform with it an act of acquisition required for one buying a new object. In this the Rosh agrees that a new act of acquisition is not required, only that the recipient intend to transfer ownership to him. The practical difference is if he received it on his own without the giver's knowledge, or if another returned it without the giver's knowledge; this does not help. Therefore, automatically, it is impossible to transfer ownership this way to a minor, for a minor cannot transfer ownership, and his return is like without his knowledge, as is known, for every act of a minor is considered without knowledge. Therefore it does not help. But if there is the knowledge of one transferring ownership, we do not require the knowledge of the acquirer in this. Know that the main proofs of the Rosh are from what we say: a person should not transfer ownership to a child, etc. If so, from there there is proof only that we require the knowledge of the acquirer, that he give it to him as a new gift and intend to transfer ownership to him. But there is no proof that we require the giver to perform a new act of acquisition; rather, with the giver's intent alone the esrog is acquired to him. So too the wording of the Rosh indicates; see there. If so, this is understandable, for whichever way you look at it, there is no prohibition here. From the side of the giver there is no prohibition, for he does nothing, and in every gift on Shabbos there is no prohibition from the giver's intent. From the side of the recipient, namely the first giver, since no act and no new acquisition are required, there is also no prohibition. They prohibited gifts and acquisitions only where an act of acquisition is required in the manner of all acquisitions, unlike here. According to this, the Magen Avraham's law is strengthened again: to buy a new esrog, which is acquired only through an actual acquisition, one may say is prohibited on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and it is permitted only at the very time of the mitzvah, like the view of the above Yerushalmi that I brought. Examine this carefully. However, I am still not satisfied with the language of the Talmud there in Sukkah, which says that the reason it teaches 'and he returned it to Rabban Gamliel' is to teach that a gift on condition to return is called a gift. Why did it not say that it teaches us that in such a case it is permitted to return it on Shabbos and Yom Tov? According to the Rosh, who requires a new transfer, one might have thought it would be prohibited to return it on Shabbos and Yom Tov; therefore it teaches that it is permitted, for the reason that I wrote. From this it would be proven against the Magen Avraham. Therefore the Gemara found it properly difficult: if this is what it comes to teach, why specifically a gift on condition to return, where he returns what is his? It should teach us a greater novelty, that even a new acquisition is permitted, and one may buy an esrog on Yom Tov even for the need of tomorrow. It would be necessarily proven from here against the Magen Avraham. This requires investigation for practical halachah at present. See my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5587, in Hilchos Lulav, siman 658, what I wrote about this, and examine it well.
