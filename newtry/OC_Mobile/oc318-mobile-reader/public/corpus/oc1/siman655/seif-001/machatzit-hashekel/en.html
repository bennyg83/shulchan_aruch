Rashal wrote in responsum, siman 8, etc. That one must explicitly say that they should bring it on Yom Tov, etc. See above, siman 247 in Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. Although Maharshal holds that nowadays there is a public domain, if so telling a non-Jew to bring it through a public domain involves only one rabbinic restriction, and above in siman 307:5 telling a non-Jew to perform a rabbinic act, which is a double rabbinic restriction, was not permitted, except for the Baal HaItur, brought in siman 276:2, who permits even a single rabbinic restriction in the place of a mitzvah. See what the Rema wrote about him there, and Magen Avraham in seif katan 8 and 9. Nevertheless, he holds that it is permitted for the need of such a mitzvah, meaning that it is different from other mitzvos because it is a passing mitzvah, or because of the reason the Taz wrote, that it is a public positive commandment. However, from the words of Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 446 it appears that this is not called a public positive commandment, since one does not discharge the many. Or there is some other reason. Even according to the one who says that we have no public domain because we require that six hundred thousand pass through it, similar to the flags of the wilderness...
It is prohibited for the need of the second Yom Tov, for so was the incident in the above responsum, and this strengthens the prohibition, that on the second Yom Tov everyone agrees it is prohibited. But Rashal, according to his own view, prohibits even on the first Yom Tov.
And it requires investigation whether it is permitted to send, etc. Meaning, even in a place where it is permitted to send, that is where the Jew who needs it may send a non-Jew to bring it to him from another non-Jew or from a Jew, and the non-Jewish messenger will not inform the Jew that it is for the need of a Jew. In such a case it is permitted so that he fulfill the mitzvah. But perhaps another Jew is not permitted to send to another Jew, since the sender, who performs the prohibited sending, has no need in this to fulfill the mitzvah, only that the Jew to whom it is sent should fulfill a mitzvah; and then we say that a person may not sin, etc. See the end of siman 306, seif katan 28. 'And perhaps, since,' etc., returns to Rashal's words, that he prohibits for the need of the second Yom Tov because there is only a rabbinic mitzvah. About this the Magen Avraham wrote that for us, who do not have a public domain, even for the need of a rabbinic mitzvah it is permitted. See what I wrote in siman 307:6, which implies that even in the place of a rabbinic mitzvah it was permitted.
And it appears to me, etc. See siman 515, where it says that if a non-Jew brought a gift to a Jew from something whose type is attached to the ground, and it was detached on Yom Tov, it is prohibited to the Jew that day, and after Yom Tov it remains prohibited for the time it would take to prepare. Rashi wrote that the reason is so that he not benefit from labor of Yom Tov. If so, if it was brought on the first Yom Tov, it is permitted after the first Yom Tov once he waited the time it would take to prepare, since whichever way you look at it, one day is weekday, and he did not benefit from labor of Yom Tov. But according to Rabbeinu Tam, he must wait until after the time it would take to prepare after the second Yom Tov, for he holds that the reason one must wait the time it would take to prepare is that we decree lest one tell a non-Jew to bring them to him on Yom Tov so that it will be available to him immediately after Yom Tov; see there. Therefore here, not only according to Rashi is it permitted on the second Yom Tov, as above, but even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who elsewhere prohibits also on the second Yom Tov, here he agrees that there is no decree. And if it was brought from outside the Land, regarding moving it outside four amos, see above siman 515:5.
