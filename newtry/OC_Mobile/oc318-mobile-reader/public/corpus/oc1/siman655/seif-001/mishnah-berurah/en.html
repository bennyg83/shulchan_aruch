(1) It is permitted to take it, etc. - Nevertheless, it is prohibited to move it outside four amos if the city has no eruv; he must go to the place where the non-Jew left it and take it there. A house, even a large one, is considered like four amos. For an elder or sick person who cannot go, one should tell a non-Jew to bring it to them. But if there is an eruv there, he may move it throughout the city; only outside the place of the eruv may he not move it [Acharonim].
(2) For him - Whether on the first Yom Tov or the second Yom Tov, and even if it came by his command [Shaarei Teshuvah].
(3) And see above, siman 586:21 and siman 307, etc. - This means that in siman 307 the Mechaber also brings the view that prohibits a double rabbinic prohibition in the place of a mitzvah. However, in siman 586:21 he states simply to be lenient in this, and does not bring the prohibiting view at all. According to this, it is permitted even ab initio to tell a non-Jew on Yom Tov to bring a lulav from outside the techum. What he wrote at first, 'a non-Jew who brought,' etc., means that in this case it is permitted even if he brought it from outside a techum of twelve mil, which according to several poskim is a Torah prohibition for a Jew. If so, ab initio it is prohibited to tell a non-Jew to bring it even in the place of a mitzvah. Nevertheless, since he already brought it, it is permitted to take it and fulfill the obligation with it, even for the person for whom it was brought. The reason is that since moving it is permitted for everyone, it is automatically permitted also to fulfill the obligation with it, for it is prohibited only to benefit from it, and a mitzvah is not called benefit. Nevertheless, in pressing circumstances, when there is no other lulav in the city with which to fulfill the obligation, the Acharonim agreed that one may be lenient and tell a non-Jew to bring it even from outside twelve mil, in order to fulfill the positive commandment of lulav. All this is specifically if he can return that day and fulfill the mitzvah immediately; but to send him on the first day so that he will come on the second Yom Tov is prohibited if he sends him outside twelve mil. Know further that this is specifically from outside the techum, where there is no prohibition of moving it, as above. But an aravah that was detached on Yom Tov, even not for the sake of a Jew, whether on the first Yom Tov or the second Yom Tov, is prohibited to move even if he has no other with which to fulfill the obligation, for it is muktzeh, and the Sages prohibited a rabbinic restriction even in the place of a mitzvah. However, if it was detached on the first Yom Tov, it is permitted on the second, unless the first Yom Tov occurred on Shabbos, in which case it is prohibited to fulfill the obligation with it also on the first day, as we hold in siman 513 regarding an egg, that when Shabbos and Yom Tov are adjacent, if it was born on this one it is prohibited on the other [Shaarei Teshuvah]. If his fellow has no lulav and is outside the techum, if he has no money to buy one for himself, this person may send it to him; but if he is able to buy and was negligent and did not buy, we do not tell a person to sin so that your fellow may merit.
