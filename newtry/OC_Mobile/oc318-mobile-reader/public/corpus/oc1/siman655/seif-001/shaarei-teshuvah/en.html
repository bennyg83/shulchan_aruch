To a non-Jew; see Baer Heitev. In Responsa Pnei Yehoshua manuscript he wrote: an aravah cut on the first Yom Tov of the festival is prohibited to move and place in one's lulav that day. This is not comparable to a lulav brought from outside the techum, etc., for there it is not muktzeh, since it is fit for another Jew; only because it was brought from outside the techum is it prohibited for eating and benefit, and mitzvos were not given for benefit. But something detached from the ground, which is prohibited to everyone, is complete muktzeh to move, and the Sages upheld their words in the place of a mitzvah through sitting and not acting, etc.; see there. In the question there, he was uncertain whether, if you will say it is prohibited, even if the first Yom Tov occurred on Shabbos, it is permitted on the second Yom Tov; but the conclusion of the responsum, what his opinion was in this, is not there. Now, the Magen Avraham wrote that if it was detached on the first day, it is permitted on the second. But it appears that this means where Yom Tov occurred on a weekday; if the first day was Shabbos, it is prohibited, for we hold in siman 513 regarding an egg that when Shabbos and Yom Tov are adjacent, if it was born on this one it is prohibited on the other. The same applies here. But if it was detached on Shabbos, with Yom Tov on the first and second days, it is permitted on the second, as explained there in the gloss; see there. See Devar Shmuel, siman 228, who permits, regarding a lulav brought from outside the techum for a Jew and by the Jew's command, that another Jew may take it where they have other lulavim; see there. It implies that for this Jew, one should be stringent where there are other lulavim, at least in such a case, where it came for him and by his command.
