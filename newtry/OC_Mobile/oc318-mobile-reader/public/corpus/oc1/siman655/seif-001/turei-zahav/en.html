And see above, siman 586. There, regarding shofar, he permitted it; in siman 307 he wrote that there is a dispute about this. Rashal in a responsum wrote to prohibit where one did not have hadas and the first day was Shabbos, that one should not send a non-Jew even on Friday after hadas to another place, since after the first day it is rabbinic. In the Mordechai, chapter Hadar, it is stated that Rabbeinu Yoel wrote to permit telling a non-Jew to bring an esrog even from far away on Yom Tov where there was unavoidable circumstance and they had no esrog, because techumin are rabbinic according to the Sages who disagree with R. Akiva; even twelve mil is not prohibited from the Torah. In Hagahos Minhagim he brings these words and wrote about this: however, this is difficult for me from what the Semag brings in negative commandment 57, that the shaving of a metzora is not done by a non-Jew, although there is only a rabbinic prohibition; examine well, for one can distinguish. End quote. It appears to me to distinguish in this way: the mitzvah of lulav has greater strength, for it is a public positive commandment. As we distinguish in Moed Katan, brought by the Rif in chapter Mi SheMeiso, that a public positive commandment is different, namely the festival, before which the individual positive commandment of mourning is pushed off. Here too, it is proper to push off telling a non-Jew, which is a rabbinic restriction, before the mitzvah of lulav, which is a public positive commandment. Even after the first day, when it is rabbinic, one may push off telling a non-Jew, which is rabbinic, before rabbinic lulav, which is a public mitzvah. So is the custom in this country, to send after an esrog on Yom Tov. Nevertheless, on Shabbos one should not send for the sake of the Yom Tov after it, as it appears to my humble mind.
