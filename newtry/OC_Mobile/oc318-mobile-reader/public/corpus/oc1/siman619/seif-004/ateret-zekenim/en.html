Nevertheless, the three should say it together, and the entire congregation should also say it softly with the shaliach tzibbur. In our text, it begins in the singular and then uses the plural; therefore it appears that it is corrupt, and it should read: 'and that I have forbidden upon myself,' and at the end he should say, 'my vows are not vows, my prohibitions are not prohibitions, and my oaths are not oaths' (Rema of Lublin). This is very astonishing, for even according to the view of the Rema of Lublin, what concern is there if he says at the end the plural form even regarding an individual? As for 'our souls,' there is certainly no difficulty at all, because the souls are many: nefesh, ruach, and neshamah. And even though the beginning is in the singular, what of it? What he wrote, that every individual must stipulate for himself, is not correct. Even if you say that one cannot stipulate through an agent, nevertheless, when many agreed to a certain condition and one of them says explicitly, 'such and such we stipulated and accepted upon ourselves,' it is effective by the law of communal agreements and not because of agency. It further appears that the main text is to say 'denadarna' and 'de-ishtabana,' as the Rosh, Rabbeinu the Semag, and the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote (Bach). Recently some printed machzorim were emended to 'de-indarna' based on the Minhagim, which is singular, but this does not seem correct. Alternatively, even the author of the Minhagim holds that 'idarna' is plural, and the alef does not indicate anything except the future, while the nun indicates the plural. The rule of the matter is that one should not change the language of Kol Nidrei at all, for all of its language is plural, and the shaliach tzibbur releases the vows for the future by the law of stipulation, as it seems to me. 'And it shall be forgiven to the entire congregation of the Children of Yisrael' is customarily said aloud (Mordechai). One should not say 'as it is written, and it shall be forgiven,' etc.; Hagahos Maimoniyos according to the view of Rabbeinu Tam, and so wrote Maharil and Abudraham.
