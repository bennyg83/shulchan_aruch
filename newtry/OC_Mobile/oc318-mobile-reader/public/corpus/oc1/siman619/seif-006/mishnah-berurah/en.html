(14) To spend the night in the synagogue. The Levush writes that it is preferable to go home to sleep, because one who remains awake at night will sleep during the day and will not pray with proper concentration. If the purpose is to guard the candles, a guard should be hired. Shelah writes that before sleeping one should recite the first four chapters of Tehillim, which protect against a nocturnal emission, in addition to the regular Krias Shema recited each day. It is good not to wrap oneself in warming pillows and blankets, and at the very least one should not cover his feet.
(15) Far from the Aron. Lest he pass wind.
(16) And one who is not, etc. The reason sleeping there is permitted is that he stays there for the sake of a mitzvah, and therefore he may sleep, as explained by the Gra. It is good to study the Mishnayos of tractate Yoma on Yom Kippur, and also to study the teachings at the end of Yoma that discuss teshuvah. A Torah scholar should not occupy himself with Torah study in a way that causes him to omit the prayers and Selichos recited with the congregation, as stated by the acharonim.
