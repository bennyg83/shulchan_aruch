That he says. See the Taz. See above, siman 55, se'ifim 11 and 14, and siman 334 regarding when such a person joins or does not join. Above, siman 25, se'if 12, if they explicitly stipulated stringently not to pray with him in the synagogue, even if there are ten besides him, they are permitted to do so, and afterward he needs release. Therefore on Yom Kippur, because any fast that does not include the sinners of Yisrael with them is like a lulav bundle without all its species and is not a fast, and otherwise there is mutual responsibility, they release them even if there is a minyan without them. This is because perhaps they stipulated initially not to pray while they are in the synagogue. Three are not necessary, because this is only to annul an agreement that existed initially; therefore the shaliach tzibbur, as agent of the congregation, is sufficient. And if you say that three are required, it is so that they appear like agents of the congregation. What is written by the Maharil, that he or the great man says it, is not precise: the great man begins and the two with him also say it, or their standing there is sufficient, for they are like agents of their congregation. See the Magen Avraham, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. There is also another reason for three on a public fast, corresponding to Moshe Rabbeinu, Aharon, and Chur, and it would have been proper for them to stand there the entire day. But regarding Kol Nidrei, we rule like Rabbeinu Tam, that it is a stipulation for the future and not for the past, and three are not needed. See letter 2, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there.
