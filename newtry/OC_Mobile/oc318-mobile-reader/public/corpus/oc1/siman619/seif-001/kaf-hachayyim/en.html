1) [Seif 1] The night of Yom Kippur, etc. Before sunset, prior to beginning, "By authority of the Heavenly Court," etc., each person should say privately: For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah, etc., behold, I come to fulfill the positive Torah commandment to fast on Yom Kippur, on the 10th day of the month of Tishrei, and also not to perform melacha on this day, as it is written, "On the 10th day of this seventh month, there shall be a sacred convocation for you, and you shall afflict your souls; you shall not perform any melacha." I also hereby accept upon myself the additional sanctity of Yom Kippur, to add from the weekday onto the sacred, in order to rectify the root of all these mitzvos and all the mitzvos dependent upon them in the supernal realm. "May the pleasantness," etc. Before this, he should say what is written above in siman 606, sec. 45, see there.
2) Ibid. The night of Yom Kippur, etc. The custom is to wrap oneself in a tallis while it is still day and recite the blessing over it. Levush; Bach; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur. There are places where the custom is not to wrap oneself in a tallis. Levush; Bach; Ateres Zekeinim. If he forgot until night, he should not recite the blessing, for we do not rule that a daytime garment is obligated even at night. Levush; Yad Aharon, ibid.; Eshel Avraham, sec. 4. Likewise, if it is bein hashmashos, he should not recite the blessing. Mateh Ephraim, sec. 7. See further above siman 18, seif 1 in the gloss, and our words there, with the help of Heaven. A fringed tallis that is decorated with gold may be worn on Yom Kippur. Ritva, Rosh Hashana; Pischei Olam, sec. 1.
3) Ibid. The night of Yom Kippur, etc. Every person should come early to the synagogue so that they may ask forgiveness from one another before the shaliach tzibbur descends before the lectern. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Yom Kippur. See above siman 606, sec. 43.
4) Ibid. The custom is that the shaliach tzibbur says, etc. In the glosses to Derashos Maharil there it is written that the leading authority of each city says, "By authority of the Heavenly Court," etc., and he wrote that this is indeed the common practice, see there. The Bach, Taz, s.k. 1, and Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, cited his words. The Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur, sec. 4, wrote that the custom is for the leading authority of the city or the leader of the congregation to say it. Accordingly, each place should follow its custom.
5) Ibid. The custom is that the shaliach tzibbur says, etc. He did not mention here that another two people should stand with him. In the Minhagim it is written that another two must join him. The Taz, s.k. 1, cited these words and wrote that specifically when the Rav or the leading member of the congregation says it, two others must stand with him. However, a shaliach tzibbur, who is appointed by the congregation, does not need two others with him, see there. So too wrote Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1. However, Nehar Shalom, sec. 1, wrote that there is no basis to distinguish between whether the shaliach tzibbur or the Rav says Kol Nidrei, and he challenged the aforementioned words of the Taz, see there. This is also the view of Shaarei Teshuvah, sec. 2, that even when the shaliach tzibbur says it, two people must stand with him, see there.
6) And the Levush wrote that two distinguished members of the congregation go and stand beside the chazzan, because this is needed to permit praying with transgressors. However, because of Kol Nidrei, three are not needed, since it is only a stipulation concerning the future, etc. Nevertheless, the Taz there wrote that they must remain until the conclusion of Kol Nidrei, see there. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah, secs. 2 and 4. So too wrote Mateh Ephraim, sec. 10, that the two remain standing until after the chazzan says Barechu, etc., and Einayim Beruch, etc., and then they return to their places. If Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, when Mizmor Shir L'Yom HaShabbos is said before Barechu, they may return to their places at the beginning of Mizmor Shir L'Yom HaShabbos, end quote. See below, seif 4.
