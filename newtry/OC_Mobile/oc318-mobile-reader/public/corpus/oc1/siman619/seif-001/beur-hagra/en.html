Se'if 1, The night of Yom Kippur, etc. Kerisos 6b.
And the custom is that he says, etc. According to Rabbeinu Tam's version, this is because of Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov in chapter 3 of Nedarim, and Yom Kippur is called Rosh Hashanah, as stated in Arachin 12a. According to the version of the early authorities, it is because regarding release of vows it is written, 'And if he restrained,' etc., 'and Hashem will forgive her,' and today is a day of forgiveness. See what I wrote below, and see the Ra'avan at the end of chapter 3 of Eruvin.
And afterward he says, etc. See Be'er HaGolah.
And the custom, etc. According to the version of the early authorities, because one asks release from vows only for the need of the day, as written at the end of Shabbos. The version of the early authorities is primary, as the Rosh wrote. See the Magen Avraham.
And he continues, etc. See the Magen Avraham.
And they say it three times, etc.; and so the shaliach tzibbur says, etc. According to the version of Rabbeinu Tam, this is because the way of the words of the Sages is like this: 'this sickle,' etc. They say 'and it shall be forgiven' even though he annulled them, lest he transgress and not know that he annulled them, as stated in Nazir 23 and Kiddushin 81. But according to the version of the early authorities, 'and it shall be forgiven' refers to the fact that he vowed, as written in Nedarim 22, and there in Avodah Zarah, and certainly if he transgressed. See Rambam in his commentary at the end of chapter 10 of Nedarim. He also says as written in Tur and Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, siman 228, se'if 3. See Yerushalmi, Nedarim chapter 11, halachah 10: the elder says, 'There is no vow here; there is no oath here'; see there. Rabbeinu Tam's version is difficult to understand, that he should request forgiveness for sins that he will commit in the future. See the Rosh.
And one should not change, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia chapter 3, and in the Yerushalmi there: this teaches that custom can override halachah. And there in chapter 6: a person should never change, etc. And in Pesachim chapter 4, even to be lenient against the halachah. See the Rif in Pesachim chapter 4, in the name of the Yerushalmi: all matters were made dependent on custom, etc. This applies specifically to an important custom, as written in the Yerushalmi there regarding what is written after the order is completed, that it is not a custom; and as stated in Maseches Sofrim and in the Gemara at the beginning of chapter Arvei Pesachim: what is different, etc.; there it is a sign of blessing, etc. The Ran explained that since the prohibition is not severe, one cannot say regarding Erev Shabbos and Erev Yom Tov that 'a place where they practiced' applies, for a custom in this matter is nothing. See the Ran there, and the Rosh, and Bava Kamma 116b, and the last chapter of Ta'anis 28b, and Menachos chapter 3, and Yevamos chapter 12, 'if Eliyahu comes,' etc., and Maseches Sofrim chapter 14: the people practiced this way, for halachah is not fixed until there is a custom, etc.
