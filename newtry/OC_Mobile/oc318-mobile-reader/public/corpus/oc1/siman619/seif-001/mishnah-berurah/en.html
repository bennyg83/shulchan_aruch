(1) That the shaliach tzibbur says - Nowadays the greatest person in each city says it. Another two must be joined with him, and they say it while seated, etc., for the yeshivos agree to join the view of the Omnipresent to the view of the congregation to release the transgressors from their transgressions. A transgressor means one who violated a communal decree or committed a transgression. It is explained above in siman 55 that if they excommunicated him, he is not counted for any matter of sanctity, and they can also stipulate stringently not to pray with him in the synagogue even if there are ten there. Perhaps they stipulated this; therefore we release them, so that we can pray with them. For any public fast that does not include the sinners of Yisrael is not a fast, as the chelbenah has a foul smell and yet Scripture counted it among the ingredients of the incense.
(2) And the custom is that he says Kol Nidrei, etc. - The ancient custom was to say, 'from last Yom Kippur until this Yom Kippur,' and the intention was to release the vows and oaths that they had already vowed and sworn, lest someone had transgressed them. Rabbeinu Tam challenged this, and therefore amended the text to say, 'from this Yom Kippur until the coming Yom Kippur, upon us for good.' The intention is to stipulate regarding vows that he will vow from now on, that they should not take effect. This stipulation helps if he does not remember the stipulation at the time that he later vows. But if he remembers the stipulation and nevertheless vows, he uproots the stipulation. Our custom follows Rabbeinu Tam. According to this, one should say 'di nedirna' and 'de-mishtab'ina' in the future tense. The Magen Avraham concludes that even according to Rabbeinu Tam one may also say in one word 'denadarna,' because this wording also implies the future. The congregation should say it quietly with the shaliach tzibbur, for the stipulation of the shaliach tzibbur does not help for the congregation.
(3) And afterward he says Shehecheyanu - The congregation too should each say it quietly. It is good that they hurry to finish the blessing they recite for themselves, so that when the shaliach tzibbur finishes the blessing they can answer amen. Nevertheless, the shaliach tzibbur must be careful to intend to discharge any of the congregation who want to fulfill their obligation with his blessing.
(4) Without a cup - For since he recites Shehecheyanu, even if it is still before day ends, he has accepted upon himself the sanctity of Yom Kippur and it is forbidden to drink. If we were to give it to a child, he might come to be drawn after drinking on Yom Kippur even after he reaches his thirteenth year. One should wrap himself in a tallis while it is still day in order to recite the blessing, because at night one does not recite the blessing even over a garment designated for daytime use. See above, siman 18, se'if katan 7 in the Mishnah Berurah.
(5) While it is still day - Although we hold like Rabbeinu Tam and make a stipulation for the future that the vows should not take effect, nevertheless it is somewhat similar to annulling vows, and one may not annul vows on Shabbos and Yom Tov; the same applies to Yom Kippur.
(6) Until night - In order to pray Ma'ariv at night. If the time of Kerias Shema has not yet arrived, they should say some chapters of Tehillim before Ma'ariv.
(7) Even with melodies - Because through this he confuses the concentration of the congregation. We do not say 'be'ahavah' before 'mikra kodesh.' In places where they have the custom to say 'be'ahavah mikra kodesh,' one should not change. If it falls on Shabbos, one does not say 'Elokeinu ve'Elokei Avoseinu, retzeh vimnuchaseinu,' because since it is a fast day there is not so much 'favor in our rest.' But we do say, 'Grant us, Hashem our Elokim, with love and favor, Your holy Shabbos, and may they rest on it,' etc., and he concludes, 'for You are forgiving,' etc., and should not say, 'and Your word is true and enduring forever.'
