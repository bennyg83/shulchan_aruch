(In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 1) And the custom is that he says Kol Nidrei. The She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 145, wrote that the text of the early authorities is to say 'di nedarna,' which refers to the past; but according to Rabbeinu Tam, where it refers to the future, one must answer and say 'de-nedor' or 'de-nidar,' properly with a chirik and a dagesh in the dalet, and similarly in all of them, 'de-nishtaba,' etc. He wrote that out of concern for the words of Rabbeinu Tam, he practices saying both forms. In Poland the text is 'denadarna ve-denidar, de-ishtabana ve-de-nishtaba, de-asarna ve-de-neisar,' etc., 'from the past Yom Kippur,' etc., 'and from this Yom Kippur,' etc., to satisfy everyone; see there. It seems that an individual may say this quietly on his own, but the shaliach tzibbur in public should not change from the printed text that is familiar in everyone's mouth.
