That the shaliach tzibbur says. Nowadays the great man says it alone [Bach].
Kol Nidrei, etc. 'Di nedarna' is the correct version (Darkei Moshe, Tosafos Yom Tov, Nedarim). Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote to say 'di nedirna' and 'di mishtab'ina,' etc., 'icharatna behon.' So too in Hagahos Minhagim it says 'denadarna,' which also implies for the future; therefore not like the Levush, who wanted to declare the old books corrupt. The Bach wrote that he received the tradition to say 'de'icharatna,' meaning: when I regret them, they shall be null. One should not move from the old versions.
He says Shehecheyanu. The Kol Bo wrote that it is better for each person to recite the blessing for himself. End quote. This is not the custom; rather, everyone relies on the shaliach tzibbur (Darkei Moshe). The Shelah wrote that it is permitted to rely on the shaliach tzibbur, as written in siman 298, se'if 14, and the same applies to the blessings over Hallel and lulav. End quote. But it seems to me that nowadays, for the most part, the shaliach tzibbur does not intend to discharge others, and therefore each person should recite the blessing for himself. So is the custom regarding Hallel and lulav.
Without a cup. Because since he says Shehecheyanu, he has accepted Yom Kippur upon himself and it is forbidden to drink. And if we give it to a child, he may come to be drawn after it; see siman 559, se'if 7. One should wrap himself in a tallis (writings); see siman 18.
While it is still day. Although we make a stipulation regarding the future, nevertheless it is somewhat comparable to annulling vows, which may not be annulled on Shabbos and Yom Tov.
Until night. In order to pray Ma'ariv at night. The congregation says Kol Nidrei quietly with the shaliach tzibbur, because the stipulation of the shaliach tzibbur does not help for the congregation (Darkei Moshe, Rosh, Mordechai, Levush, unlike the Bach).
With melodies. Because they confuse the congregation's concentration. One should say 'mikra kodesh zecher,' etc., and one should not say 'be'ahavah.' If it is Shabbos, one does not say 'Elokeinu,' etc., 'Retzeh vimnuchaseinu,' and he concludes, 'for You are forgiving,' etc. (Levush).
