Who stand, meaning at the time of the Ma'ariv prayer; for otherwise they would not sleep the entire night and would be unable to concentrate properly in their prayer during the day (Beis Yosef). See above, siman 614, whether it is permitted to stand on top of a leather lamp-cover or cushion. One who is standing may lean on something when he becomes weak (Maharil). He should be careful not to hold back from relieving himself, because of 'do not make yourselves detestable' (Mahariv). This applies all the more to those young men who fill their bellies so that they should not sleep in the synagogue, lest they come to flatulence. One who sleeps in the synagogue must recite Kerias Shema before his bed as on any other night (Maharil). Some have the custom to pray this request: 'May it be Your will, Hashem my Elokim and Elokim of my fathers, to purify me from the arrangements of my thoughts,' etc. Although the custom is not to say it, it is good to say it quietly (Kol Bo). The custom is to say all of Tehillim on Yom Kippur until the end; see above, siman 579. Before Tehillim they say the familiar Yehi Ratzon, and that Yehi Ratzon is commonly found in our country.
