Before. See Turei Zahav. If he kindled all the lamps, it appears that he should not bless afterward. In all matters, a mitzvah that has no continuing duration is not blessed afterward. The blessing of time he says on the second night; but after lighting on the first night, no. Whenever he remembers means on the third night and onward, for these blessings were instituted only on the lighting. This is unlike the blessing of time for a Festival, which he says even in the marketplace. See Pri Chadash here and Magen Avraham 473:1. However, one may say that the blessings She'asah Nissim and Shehecheyanu are also recited by one who sees, as in section 3; the same applies here. Although he kindled, so long as he did not bless at the lighting, he blesses upon seeing. But the blessing to kindle, no, for that has already passed. However, if he kindled one lamp, one may say that he blesses on the remaining lamps on the second night and onward. Although one lamp is the mitzvah and the rest are only mehadrin, as in siman 671, nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote in paragraph 1 in the name of Beis Yosef, siman 673, that the blessing applies to all of them. This implies that if he did not intend to bless, he blesses on the rest. See Eliyah Rabbah; with Hashem's help this will be explained there. Nevertheless, this requires examination, since there is no mitzvah there except beautification.
