He does not go back, etc. But if his wife kindled for him on the first night, he must bless Shehecheyanu on the second night, because his wife exempts only his house and not his body. Bach. To me it seems obvious that according to the authorities who hold that when his wife kindles for him he need not bless upon seeing, the same applies that he need not bless Shehecheyanu when he kindles. So it also seems from Rabbi Zeira, who relied on his wife. If you would think that his body was not exempted, then he should have participated with coins to exempt his body. Rather, perforce, he is entirely exempt. So it also seems from Maharil, siman 157. See what I wrote in siman 677. So wrote Olas Shabbos in the name of Knesses HaGedolah, second edition.
