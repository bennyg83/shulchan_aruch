Shelchanukah is one word. Maharshal. See the reason in Shnei Luchos HaBris, and so wrote Mateh Moshe. One says bazman hazeh, and not uvazman; lazman hazeh has a chirik under the lamed. Maharshal, Mateh Moshe. If he forgot and kindled one lamp fewer than proper, he should kindle another and not bless, because the blessing that he initially made was made over the obligation of all the lamps. Beis Yosef, siman 672, Orchot Chayim, Darkei Moshe. See what I wrote at the end of siman 651. They do not kindle for him. Maharshal ruled that even though they kindled for him, he must bless, but one who participates with coins and hears the blessing from the homeowner need not bless. Bach, and so it seems in Rosh. Nevertheless, it seems to me that where there is doubt about blessings, we are lenient. So it also seems from Tosafos, Sukkah 46, who asked why they instituted on Chanukah a blessing for one who sees. Why did they not answer that the Chanukah lamp is different because there is a mitzvah in seeing it, unlike lulav and sukkah, where there is no mitzvah in seeing them, only in sitting in the sukkah?
