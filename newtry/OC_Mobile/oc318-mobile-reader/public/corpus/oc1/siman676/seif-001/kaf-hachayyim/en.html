1) [Seif 1] To kindle a Chanukah light. This is the principal, precise wording, and the wording of the Gemara, the Rif, and the Rambam, in which it is written, "to kindle a light of Chanukah," is not the principal wording. Pri Chadash; Shulchan Gavohah, letter 2. So too wrote Halachos Ketanos, vol. 1, siman 3, that it is better to recite, "to kindle a Chanukah light," and not "of," because according to the hidden meaning its initial letters spell nachal. Yad Aharon cited his words in Hagahos HaTur. So too wrote Yafeh LaLev, vol. 2, letter 2; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Vayeishev, letter 2, and see there, where he wrote several reasons for this. See also the following letter.
2) Ibid. To kindle a Chanukah light. Such is our custom, and such is the wording of our master the Arizal, of blessed memory, in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 108, column 4. However, on Friday night the wording of the blessing is, "to kindle the light of Shabbos." The reason is that it is forbidden to make use of the Chanukah light. He therefore says, "a Chanukah light," to indicate that the light is only for the mitzvah of Chanukah. This is not so with the Shabbos light, whose purpose is a light that we use, and whose illumination benefits us. This illuminating light is "of Shabbos." Birkei Yosef, letter 1. In Machazik Berachah, letter 1, he wrote another reason: On Chanukah there is no act other than the lights, and no other practical matter at all. It is therefore called a "Chanukah light," for Chanukah is specifically the light. This is not so with Shabbos, which has several practical mitzvos, such as the meal, Kiddush, fine garments, and the like, with the light being one of the mitzvos. This is the meaning of "the light of Shabbos," for Shabbos has several matters and this is one of them, namely, the familiar light of Shabbos among its many matters. This is not so with Chanukah, which has no practical act other than the light, end quote. Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 1, cited his words. See also below, letter 9.
3) Ibid. To kindle a Chanukah light, Who performed miracles, and Shehecheyanu. A mnemonic for the matter is: "Make for yourself a fiery serpent and place it upon a pole, and it shall be that whoever sees it shall live." Mateh Moshe, siman 980; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1.
4) Ibid. Who performed miracles. A convert may say, if he wishes, "Who performed miracles for our forefathers." If he wishes to alter it and say, "Who performed miracles for Israel," he may say so. Igros HaRambam, siman 9; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur; Olas Shabbos, letter 1; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2; Pri Chadash.
5) In the wording of the blessing "Who performed miracles," he should say, "in those days, at this time." Its meaning is that He performed miracles in the days that actually passed, at this time of year, and not, as some say, "in those days and at this time." Maharshal, responsa, siman 64; Knesset HaGedolah, ibid.; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Pri Chadash; Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1; Birkei Yosef, letter 2; Ruach Chaim, letter 3; Yafeh LaLev, vol. 2, letter 4; Mishna Berurah, letter 1.
