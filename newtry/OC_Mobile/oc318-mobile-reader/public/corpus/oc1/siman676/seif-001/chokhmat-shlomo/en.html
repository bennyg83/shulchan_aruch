Section 1. One who kindles on the first night blesses three blessings, etc. Note: regarding whether it is permitted to interrupt between one lamp and another to inquire out of honor or fear, see what I wrote at some length in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5604, in the laws of Rosh Chodesh, siman 422; examine it. For we read in Shabbos, chapter 2, page 23: what does he reduce? He reduces the blessing of time. Let him reduce the blessing of the miracle. It answers: the miracle exists on every day. If the meaning were simple, one should examine: what was the question, let him reduce the miracle? What superiority does the blessing of time have over the blessing of the miracle? Even according to the initial assumption, before he knew that the miracle is preferable, at any rate it is no worse than the blessing of time. And know this: if there were room to say that the blessing of time has a superiority over the miracle, then even though it answers that the miracle has a superiority because the miracle exists on every day, still the blessing of time also has a superiority according to the initial assumption, and one should still say all of them. Perforce, even the questioner knew that time has no stronger connection than the miracle. If so, what did he ask, let him reduce the miracle? Is that enough to make time superior? It is forced to explain that the intent was, let him also reduce the miracle; but if so it should have said this explicitly, let him also reduce the miracle. There is another difficulty: why regarding Megillah do we bless Shehecheyanu also in the morning and not say that the nighttime blessing is enough, since once He brought us to the beginning of the time, He brought us, as Rashi explains here by Chanukah? Therefore it seems that from here there is proof for what I wrote in my work Sefer HaChayim, and afterward I also found that Binah LaItim wrote this as well: the first lamp commemorates the essential miracle of the salvation, and the other days commemorate the miracle of the oil. If so, it is well understood. Certainly it would have been relevant to bless all three blessings on all the days, but they intentionally instituted omitting one blessing on the remaining days to show that the remaining days differ from the day before them, and that the first day has an advantage over the other days. Therefore, since this is only for remembrance, to distinguish the first day from all the days, the Gemara properly asks: let him reduce the miracle and say the blessing of time, and there will be a greater distinction for the first day over the other days. It therefore answers that the miracle also exists on every day, and therefore it is more fitting to bless over the miracles, with the distinction being made through the blessing of time, rather than doing the opposite, blessing the blessing of time and making the distinction through the miracle. This is well understood; examine carefully.
