He will begin to kindle, etc. In Tur HaShulchan siman 106 wrote the people of the Rhine begin at the candle further left and turn right because of all corners, etc., and so Maharam; the people of Austria begin on the right and kindle as Bnei Brit write; he wrote to reconcile these views: where there is no mezuzah in the room one must kindle on the right of the entrance at the tefach next to the door as Mordechai — and the candle opposite his right is always next to the door and he begins with it, essence of mitzvah; by itself suffices if not mehaderin. Rhine people speak where he has mezuzah — then left of entrance, candle next to door always opposite his left. If you ask why Maharam needs all corners — resolve from beginning at tefach next to door; one may say difference if candles arranged from door toward wall opposite — door east, candles east to west, turn south, begin candle next to door, not north — because of all corners — end. Beit Yosef wrote it appears Rhine custom is correct, kindle every night on additional candle as Shulchan Aruch, no distinction right or left of entrance — so we practice — end. Rashal the meticulous places lengthwise in tefach next to door so all equal near door opening; then kindle left to right, always begin leftmost and add rightward; in synagogue where menorah faces south, placed east-west, turn left to right — end. Levush wrote: I say the explanation of all corners is not as they explain but as our writing way, etc., as from encircling the altar the kohen ascended south and began southeast corner, etc.; if kohen could stand in place to throw — he is as said, etc.; he extended on human nature turning — so too here l'maaseh like Austria — end. It appears his words are correct: what Chazal said all corners means beginning the corner — you have right and left, whenever you begin turn right; first time with two or more before you, kindle the rightmost; if we explain kindle leftmost first to then turn right — why is the end preferable to the beginning which is primary? Rather certainly explain on the beginning: with two sides take the right, then go to its left; therefore I practiced arranging candles lengthwise near door as Rashal so all in tefach next to door (kindle first night at wall candle, second night add second toward room interior, begin additional, finish wall candle; each night add toward interior, begin additional on his right, finish wall candle; if impossible arrange on wall in row from right of door — even if later additions not all in tefach next to door no concern since first is in that tefach; each night begin additional on his right and kindle to wall candle — correct; in synagogue arranged menorah east-west kindle first night easternmost, add westward, always begin additional — end siman 671 regarding lulav). One more reason to begin at leftmost which is person's right — otherwise he passes over mitzvah moving hand toward candles before him when he could kindle left candle first then those before him — end, correct.
