The blessing. See Baer Heitev. What he wrote, and as long as he is occupied, etc., slightly implies that even after he kindled all of them, as long as he is still occupied there with fixing the wicks or the like, he may bless. This is not so, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 19: according to Shach, he should not bless after slaughtering at all if he did not bless before, while according to Pri Chadash he may bless within the time of speaking after slaughtering. So too it is explained above in siman 432 that after finishing the search one does not bless. See siman 167, section 8, siman 640, paragraph 12, and siman 651, paragraph 18; see there, where he wrote that if one forgot to bless before taking, he still blesses. See Pri Megadim there, who wrote that it has a continuing duration because he must wave it, and Eliyah Rabbah wrote that according to many authorities this is called before performance, since as long as it is in his hand he is still occupied with his mitzvah. See there. That has no relevance here, for there the form of the blessing is over taking, and as long as it is in his hand it is one taking. This is not so here, where the form of the blessing is to kindle, and once he kindled, his mitzvah is immediately completed. It is possible, however, that here too, if he is occupied with something such that if he did not fix it, it would immediately go out, he can bless, for it is as though he had not yet kindled at all, since from this lighting it was destined to go out. In such a case, certainly if it went out he is required to tend to it. It is possible that even Knesses HaGedolah and Turei Zahav, brought in siman 673, paragraph 11, agree that in such a case he goes back and kindles with a blessing. There Turei Zahav wrote about one who kindles in a place where wind is present, that perhaps it is like oils that are not drawn after the wick; see there. There too it is not certain, only a concern that it may not draw well and will go out or will not withstand the wind. But at least the lighting itself was proper. This is not so here, where at the time of lighting itself it is known that it will not remain lit without this fixing, and it is a blessing in vain if he does not fix it. In such a case it is reasonable that he may bless after the lighting before the fixing, for this fixing certainly belongs to the lighting, and it is as though he did not kindle. However, in Halachos Ketanos there he wrote as follows: Question: if he kindled the first lamp and did not bless, should he bless on the rest? Answer: perhaps as long as he is occupied with the beautification of the mitzvah, he may bless. Until here. This means that as long as he is occupied with kindling the other lamps, which are the beautification of the mitzvah, as we hold that the mehadrin add progressively, he may properly bless. But he is not speaking after he completed kindling all the lamps, for then he should not bless even if he is still occupied with them, unless he is occupied with fixing the wicks in a case where it is known that it cannot remain lit without this fixing, as I wrote.
