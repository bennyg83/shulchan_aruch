The blessing. See Baer Heitev. What he wrote, "as long as he is occupied," implies somewhat that even after he lit them all, if he is still occupied there with fixing the wicks and the like, he may bless. This is not so, as is clear in Yoreh Deah siman 19, that according to the Shach one should not bless at all after slaughtering if he did not bless beforehand, and according to Pri Chadash he may bless within tokh kedei dibbur of the slaughtering. So too it is clear above, siman 432, that after completing the search one may not bless. See siman 167 seif 8, siman 640 seif katan 12, and siman 651 seif katan 18. See there, where he wrote that if one forgot to bless before taking the lulav he may still bless; and see Pri Megadim there, who wrote that it has an extended duration because one must wave it, and Eliyah Rabbah wrote that according to many poskim this is called before performance, because as long as it is in his hand he is still occupied with the mitzvah. See there. This is not comparable to here, for there the blessing formula is on taking, and as long as it is in his hand it is one act of taking. Here, however, the blessing formula is "to kindle," and once he kindled he immediately completed his mitzvah. Perhaps even here, if his occupation is such that without fixing it the flame would immediately go out, he may bless, for it is as though he had not yet kindled at all, since from this lighting it was destined to go out; in such a case certainly if it went out he is bound to relight it. Perhaps even Kenesset HaGedolah and Olat Tamid, cited in siman 673, seif katan 11, agree here that he relights with a blessing. There the Taz wrote about lighting in a place where wind is common, that perhaps it resembles oils not drawn after the wick; see there. There, too, it is not certain, only a concern that it will not draw well and will go out, or that it will not withstand the wind; but in any case the lighting itself was proper. Here, by contrast, at the very time of lighting it is known that it will not burn without this correction, and the blessing is in vain if he does not correct it. In such a case it is reasonable that he may bless after the lighting before the correction, for this correction certainly belongs to the lighting, and it is as though he had not lit. However, Halakhot Ketanot there wrote as follows: Question: If one lit the first lamp and did not bless, may he bless over the remaining lamps?
Answer: Perhaps as long as he is occupied with the enhancement of the mitzvah he may bless; end quote. This means as long as he is occupied with lighting the remaining lamps, which are the enhancement of the mitzvah, as we rule that the mehadrin add progressively, he may properly bless. But he is not speaking after completing the lighting of all the lamps, for then he may not bless even if he is still occupied with them, unless he is occupied with fixing the wicks and it is known that the lamp cannot burn without that correction, as I wrote above.
