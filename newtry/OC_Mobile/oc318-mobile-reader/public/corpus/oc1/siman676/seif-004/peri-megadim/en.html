And also. See Turei Zahav, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, who says that many authorities hold like Mordechai. Nevertheless, where there is doubt about blessings, we are lenient, and so wrote Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Regarding what he wrote about Beis Yosef, that Mordechai wrote this, from the arrangement of Beis Yosef, who brought the words of Rashi, yet in his house, and then the language of Ran, etc., and so wrote Mordechai; and Rosh brought the words of Mordechai after he had permitted Ran, and placed the student before the teacher, for Mordechai was a student of Rosh, as were Rabbeinu Yerucham and Mordechai. This shows that Beis Yosef's intent is that Rashi wrote it for one who had not yet kindled in his home, or one sitting on a ship, which one may say means that someone will kindle afterward that night; nevertheless one who sees blesses. About this he brought the language of Ran, of blessed memory: specifically when they did not kindle for him inside his home, meaning his wife, for in the Tur one may say that he hears the blessing, see Eliyah Rabbah 4, and he is not going to kindle that night. And so wrote Mordechai, meaning exactly like the words of Ran. Afterward, and so wrote Rosh, at least to agree that he is not going to kindle that night. The word yet is not precise, for he wrote one sitting on a ship. About this Turei Zahav properly wondered that Mordechai disagrees with Ran, etc. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 6, who asked against Turei Zahav; according to what I wrote, it is well understood. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham. See what I wrote in paragraph 2: if he kindled and finished kindling and did not bless, whether he may afterward bless the blessings of time and miracle. One may say that according to the view that when one's wife kindles for him he does not bless as one who sees, the same applies here, as stated.
