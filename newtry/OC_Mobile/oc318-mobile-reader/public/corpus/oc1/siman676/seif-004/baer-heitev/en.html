To the right. In Tur HaShulchan siman 106 he wrote that the people of the Rhine begin to kindle at the candle further left and turn toward the right because of "all corners," etc., and so did Maharam. The people of Austria begin on the right side and kindle as we — Bnei Brit — write; he wrote there to reconcile these views: where there is no mezuzah in the room, one must in any case kindle on the right of the entrance at the side of the tefach next to the door — and so the candle opposite his right is always next to the door and he must begin with it, for it is the essence of the mitzvah; by itself it suffices if he were not among the mehaderin. But the people of the Rhine speak where he has a mezuzah in his room — then he must kindle on the left of the entrance, and the candle next to the door is always opposite his left. If you ask: why then does Maharam need the reason of "all corners," etc.? — it could be resolved from the reason that one must begin with the tefach next to the door. One may say there is a difference if the candles were arranged from the side of the door toward the wall opposite the door — such as if the door is in the east and the candles are arranged from east to west, then he must turn his face south and begin with the candle next to the door and not turn north and begin with that candle because of "all corners," etc. — end. Beit Yosef wrote on this and it appears the custom of the people of the Rhine is correct, that one must kindle every night on the additional candle, as written here in Shulchan Aruch, and one should not distinguish between when the candles are on the right of the entrance and when on the left — and so we practice — end. {Rama: And here in Magen Avraham he wrote that Maharil and Mahariv speak when he eats in the room alone and it is not correct — see Eliyah Rabbah and it is explained in Bach and in Knesset HaGedolah that the participant must hear the blessings from the one who blesses, for otherwise he must bless on seeing.} And Rashal the meticulous wrote he should place them lengthwise on a spit so all are equal in the tefach next to the door, begin on the left and turn right — so too Bach. So too Taz: I practiced myself the most correct way to arrange the candles in length near the door as Rashal said so all candles are in the tefach next to the door; on the first night kindle the candle at the wall, on the second night add a second candle next to it toward the room's interior, begin with the additional candle and finish with the candle at the wall; on each subsequent night add one candle toward the room's interior, begin with it and finish with the one at the wall. If impossible to arrange thus, arrange them on the wall in a row from the right of the door — even if the others he adds are not all in the tefach next to the door there is no concern, since the first candle is in that tefach it suffices; each night begin with the additional candle which is the right available before the person and then kindle until the candle at the wall — as above, correct — end there. Machatzit HaShekel wrote in the name of Rashal to place them within the door's opening like the mezuzah — end there.
