Paragraph 4: With the lamp, etc. And there is no distinction whether he kindles on the right, etc. Meaning, the rightmost lamp means the one opposite the right of the one kindling. If so, not only when he kindles on the right side of the entrance, meaning where there is no mezuzah in his entrance, as written above in siman 671, section 7, should he kindle with the lamp that is opposite his right, which is adjacent to the entrance, for he must kindle within a handbreadth adjacent to the entrance. Rather, even if he has a mezuzah, so that he kindles on the left side of the entrance, where, on the contrary, the lamp opposite the left of the one kindling is adjacent to the entrance, nevertheless he kindles with the rightmost lamp, because every day one should bless over the added lamp, which indicates the miracle, since as the days increase the miracle increases. We hold that all turns that you turn should be to the right. Therefore, when on the first day he begins with the rightmost lamp, and on the second day he adds another lamp adjacent to it and begins with the added lamp and turns to the right, opposite the way we Jews write, this is called turning to the right. This is not so if he began on the first day with the left lamp, for either way he would not act properly: either he would not turn to the right, or he would not begin every day by kindling the added lamp.
He should place them lengthwise so that all of them, etc. This is how one should read it; the word like a spit is a scribal error and is not found in Responsa Maharshal. Meaning: if the entrance is in the eastern wall, and the wall extends widthwise from south to north, he should place the menorah along the length of the room within a handbreadth adjacent to the entrance, and the menorah will extend from east to west. If so, all the lamps will be within a handbreadth adjacent to the entrance.
And he should begin on the left, etc. Meaning, the one kindling should stand on the north side of the room with his face toward the south, and begin on the left, which is the lamp adjacent to the entrance, and turn to the right. But he should not do the opposite, standing on the south side with his face toward the north and beginning with the lamp adjacent to the entrance, which is opposite his right, for then he would not turn to the right but to the left. One must say that he does not hold like Maharik, that one must bless every day over the added lamp.
Within the open space of the entrance, like the mezuzah. So too Shnei Luchos HaBris explained. What Maharshal wrote, that he should place them lengthwise, etc., means that he should fix them in the doorpost itself, in the open space between the two doorposts.

And in Ran, etc. It does not imply this. For Ran disagrees there and says that for a mitzvah that can be performed through an agent one blesses over the mitzvah, while for mitzvos that cannot be performed through an agent one blesses with the lamed form. He asked from the baraisa in the Tosefta: one who separates terumos and maasros blesses to separate terumah and maaser. But terumah can be separated through an agent, and nevertheless he blesses with the lamed form, to separate. He answered that since one is not permitted to separate without the owner's knowledge, and since the owner's knowledge is required, it is as though he himself performed it, and therefore one blesses with the lamed form. Until here are his words. The same can be said regarding the Chanukah lamp: although it can be kindled by another, nevertheless the one kindling needs the owner's knowledge, for otherwise the owners have not fulfilled. If so, even when kindling through an agent, one should bless with the lamed form. Now Magen Avraham brought proof against Bach from what Ran wrote regarding terumah, and from it he learned to the Chanukah lamp. Why did he not bring proof from what Ran wrote regarding the Chanukah lamp itself, where he first asked from the Chanukah lamp, since it can be done through an agent and nevertheless we bless with the lamed form? He answered that since one must participate with coins, it is like something that cannot be done by another. Nevertheless, since afterward, because of the question from terumah, he needed another reasoning, as above, and that reasoning is sufficient to resolve the Chanukah lamp as well, there is no further need for the first reasoning that Ran wrote regarding the Chanukah lamp. We hold the reasoning that applies both to terumah and to the Chanukah lamp.
