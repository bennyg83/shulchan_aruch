Note: see Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Bach that if his wife kindles for him, he must go back and bless Shehecheyanu, because his wife exempts only his house, not his body, etc. According to Bach's words, the proof written by Turei Zahav and Magen Avraham above, that a woman can discharge a man, would be rejected. They proved this from what we say, a woman certainly kindles: since the statement that if a deaf-mute, minor, or mentally incompetent person kindled, he has done nothing, must mean that they do not discharge others, then what it says that a woman certainly kindles means that she discharges others. See there. According to the above, there is no proof, for one can say that there a woman never discharges a man, even if she kindles before him, but there it is speaking of exempting his house. Therefore it says that if a deaf-mute, minor, or mentally incompetent person kindled, he has done nothing, even to exempt his house; a woman certainly kindles. But to exempt his body, one may say that a woman does not discharge a man even if she kindles before him and as his agent. Regarding the actual dispute between Bach and Magen Avraham, it seems in my humble opinion that Magen Avraham's proof from Rabbi Zeira is no proof, for one may say that seeing is also a mitzvah, since one who sees blesses, etc. Yet in truth, generally we do not find a blessing on passive non-action, only on active performance, and not on passive non-action; for one does not bless on a negative prohibition, as Magen Avraham wrote in the laws of Sukkah in the name of Levush, that this is why one does not bless all seven days over matzah, because there is no mitzvah in eating matzah then, only the prohibition of chametz, and one does not bless over a negative prohibition. We also wrote similarly regarding removing tefillin on Shabbos and Yom Tov. If so, it is proven that one does not bless over passive non-action that is not active performance. Therefore here too, regarding seeing, where he performs no act but only sees, he blesses over it only the blessings of the miracle and Shehecheyanu. Therefore it is well understood: to exempt his body, he fulfills through seeing; but to exempt his house, he does not fulfill through seeing, only through kindling. The Sages obligated him in both, and placed the obligation both on his body and on his house. Therefore, as long as Rabbi Zeira was not married and had no one to exempt his house, seeing alone was not enough for him, and he therefore had to participate with coins. Then it was considered as though he himself kindled, and he was exempt from the mitzvah of seeing and the mitzvah of kindling. But when he married, his wife exempted him from the mitzvah of kindling and the obligation of his house. Nevertheless, his body was still obligated in seeing the Chanukah lamp, and for this he exempted himself through seeing. For this he did not need to participate with coins, since he himself was the one seeing and was fulfilling the mitzvah with his body. Granted, as long as his wife had not kindled for him, he was obligated in the mitzvah of kindling, and since he did not kindle himself but another did, he was not fulfilling the mitzvah himself; a mitzvah is greater when done by oneself than through one's agent. Therefore he had to participate with coins, so that he would be considered a partner, and a partner is preferable to an agent, as is proven in chapter 2 of Kiddushin; see there. But whenever he has a wife kindling for him, he is exempt from the mitzvah of kindling, only not from the mitzvah of seeing. That he fulfilled himself, for he saw it. Therefore he did not need to participate with coins. When he said, now I certainly do not need, he meant that he did not need to participate with coins; but seeing he still needed. Here in Shulchan Aruch, however, it is speaking where he also did not see. Otherwise he would already have blessed Shehecheyanu at the time of seeing. Perforce, he had not yet seen either, and in this case he must bless Shehecheyanu afterward because of seeing, for his body had not yet been exempted from seeing. The words of Bach are correct in my humble opinion; examine carefully.
