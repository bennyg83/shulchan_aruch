[3] That he should not extend. Some say that there is no concern if he extended the shevarim somewhat, provided that he does not extend too much, and so is the custom [Rema]. His reason is as written in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 140, that in tashat the tekiah is like three shevarim, which is nine forces, and in tashrat it is like three shevarim and a teruah. As for the Levush, who omitted this, it seems to me that he holds as the Beis Yosef wrote, folio 406b, that whenever one extends a shever as long as the tekiah of tarat, it leaves the category of a shever. Alternatively, he holds that one should be stringent like Rav Hai, that according to everyone a teruah is like three yevavos, but as a matter of custom we do all of them; if so, the same applies that the tekiah is always the measure of three yevavos. All this is forced; rather, the primary ruling is like the Rema, as I wrote in Eliyah Zuta. I also concluded there that one should not extend one shever more than nine forces, though in the order tashrat it is valid after the fact up to eighteen forces.
