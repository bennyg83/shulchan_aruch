And some say there is no concern, etc. This surprises me, for this "some say" is brought by the Shulchan Aruch itself, which wrote: some say that the measure of a yevavah is three tromitin, and therefore there is no concern if he lengthened, etc. If so, it is surprising that the Rama inserted this "some say" here unnecessarily. One may say that here it speaks even according to that opinion, that a yelalah is only one sound; nevertheless, now that he makes shevarim, namely groans, and each groan is longer than a yelalah, he may lengthen each shever somewhat more than the sound of a yelalah alone. This is the view of Hagahot Ashri. See afterward.
And according to this there is no concern, etc. Meaning, according to this, up to nine tromitin, which are merely sounds, but more than nine is the measure of a tekiah. In tashrat we would have had more room to be lenient and lengthen one shever up to eighteen sounds, since the teruah there totals that amount, so the measure of a tekiah would not be until eighteen, which is groans and wails. But since in tashat he may not lengthen that way, because there the teruah is only groans, and there is concern for confusion, lest once he make one shever longer than another and this bring him to error, therefore he should make them equal everywhere. But this is very difficult to me: what have we gained according to this opinion regarding lengthening one shever? Perforce three groans are not equal in measure to three wails according to any view, for Abaye said that the tanna of the Mishnah disagrees in this with the tanna of the baraita: the Mishnah teaches groans and the baraita teaches wails. One who is engaged in the tashat order assumes the groans are the teruah, not the wails. The groans do not change between the first and last opinions, for they dispute only regarding wails; groans are one matter. If so, regarding one shever we follow the measure of the groans, meaning he should not lengthen it to the measure of three ordinary groans. According to the first opinion, we gained that the length of the shever is not as short as three small sounds of three wails, but like three groans, like the "some say" of the Rama, which is Hagahot Ashri. But what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, gaining according to the latter opinion in lengthening one shever, and this is from the words of the Rosh, I do not understand: since he is making tashat at that moment, we do not follow a wail at all; it has no relevance to one shever. Rather, we follow three ordinary groans, that he not lengthen one shever to that measure. This is very reasonable. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 141, already agreed with this Hagahot Ashri, writing that although our other rabbis did not write so, the words are logical. Therefore in practice one should only ensure that one shever is not lengthened to the measure of three ordinary groans, even according to all opinions regarding the measure of wails. This applies even to the shevarim of tashrat, because we do not distinguish from the shevarim of tashat. In any case, it is not proper to shorten each shever very much, for it must at least be as the way a sick person groans, as Rashi explained. So too Hagahot Ashri wrote there that it appears to him that one does not fulfill the obligation if he shortens greatly. The Beit Yosef wrote that the world did not practice so, but rather shortens the shever as much as possible, because according to Rashi, with even slight lengthening it leaves the category of shever and becomes a tekiah, since according to him the measure of a tekiah is three minimal forces; end quote. According to what I wrote, this proof is removed, for now we do not follow wailing at all, as appears to my humble mind.
Like the measure of twelve tromitin, etc. In this he followed what the Rosh wrote according to this, that one must draw out the tekiah of tashrat like the measure of three shevarim and three forces, etc. This is an obvious scribal error, for we require the measure of three shevarim, which are nine forces, plus the measure of a teruah, namely nine forces, since now shevarim and teruah are all one unit. If so, the tekiah corresponding to them requires eighteen forces. Tosafot explicitly wrote: the measure of three shevarim and nine forces. Therefore certainly in the Ashri too it should say "and nine forces." The words of the Shulchan Aruch are imprecise, and in Shulchan Aruch it should say eighteen tromitin.
