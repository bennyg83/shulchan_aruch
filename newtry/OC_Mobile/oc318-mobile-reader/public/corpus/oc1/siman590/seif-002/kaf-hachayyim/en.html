4) [Seif 2] We are uncertain regarding the teruah stated in the Torah, etc. For it is written, "A day of teruah," and we translate teruah as yevava. Infer from this that it resembles the sound a person makes when crying and wailing, which is the meaning of yevava, as derived from the mother of Sisera, regarding whom it is written, "She wailed." We still do not know whether it resembles a person groaning from his heart, in the manner of a sick person who emits short sounds one after another and extends them somewhat, which is called groaning, or a person who wails and laments, emitting short sounds in rapid succession, which is called wailing, or whether it consists of both together. Therefore, because of the doubt, we perform all of them. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2. This is from the Gemara there.
5) It is stated in the Midrash that Yitzchak was bound upon the altar on Rosh Hashanah. On that day Sarah our mother heard, and she cried out, sobbed, and wailed. Therefore, Scripture says, "It shall be a day of teruah for you," which is translated as "a day of wailing," so that the Holy One, blessed is He, will remember for us the wailing of Sarah our mother and forgive us. The author of Sefer HaMinhagos wrote that a person must wail before his Creator over his sins; therefore, the Torah commanded us to sound the shofar in a manner resembling wailing and groaning. Abudraham, 100a; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. See above, siman 584, os 16.
6) Ibid. We are uncertain regarding the teruah stated in the Torah, etc. In the Zohar, Parashas Pinchas, 231b, it is written as follows: "For this reason, those Babylonians do not know the secret of sobbing and wailing, and they do not know that both are required. The wailing is severe judgment. The three broken sounds are softened judgment. Groaning is a softened groan. They do not know, and they perform both; we know, and we perform both, and all emerge upon the path of truth," see there. Likewise, Shaar HaKavanos gives a mystical reason for all 100 sounds that are sounded on Rosh Hashanah, and we cited his words above, siman 585, os 28, see there. What it says, that wailing is severe judgment and groaning is softened judgment, means that they serve to sweeten both the severe and softened judgments, for judgment can be sweetened only at its root, as is known to those who know the hidden wisdom.
7) Ibid. One must sound tashrat three times, tashat three times, etc. It is not sufficient to sound tashrat three times, because perhaps only shevarim or only teruah is correct, and the other sound would constitute an interruption. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. This is from the Gemara, Rosh Hashanah 34a. They did not also institute sounding tarshat, on the possibility that the teruah should precede the shevarim, because the Gemara there already answered this: Ordinarily, when misfortune befalls a person, he first groans and afterward wails, see there. Regarding the reason that tashat is performed before tarat, the Abudraham wrote, 99b, that just as in the tashrat series shevarim is performed before teruah, because ordinarily every person first groans and afterward wails, so too the tashat series, which represents groaning, must be performed before the tarat series, which represents wailing, see there. However, after the fact, this order is not indispensable, as stated at the end of siman 593. Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. Nevertheless, according to the mystical meaning of these matters, they must specifically be performed in the order tashrat, tashat, tarat, as written above, siman 585, os 28, see there.
8) If you ask, why are all these necessary? It should be sufficient to sound tashrat three times, followed by shevarim-tekiah, tashat, tashat, and then teruah-tekiah, tarat, tarat. For if the correct teruah is groaning followed by wailing, he has fulfilled the obligation with the tashrat series. If it is groaning alone, the final tekiah of tashrat could count as the straight blast preceding tashat, and the same could be said regarding tarat. The Ran already answered in the name of Tosafos that the reason is that people might come to err. If you say that in the first tashat he should not repeat and sound the initial tekiah, they might mistakenly say that only shevarim-tekiah is required. The Rosh answered that since he sounded the tekiah for the sake of the straight blast following a teruah, the Sages did not wish it to count as the straight blast preceding a teruah. The Re'em answered that they hold like R' Zeira, who says that mitzvos require intent. Therefore, the final tekiah of tashrat cannot be considered the first tekiah of tashat, nor can the final tekiah of tashat be considered the first tekiah of tarat. A stipulation is not applicable here, for if so there is no intent either for the final tekiah or for the first tekiah, and he does not fulfill his obligation through it for either purpose, end quote. His words are not satisfactory to me, because with a stipulation it is properly considered intent. Although he makes the stipulation because of a doubt, he nevertheless intends to fulfill the obligation of tekiah. Beit Yosef. The Rama, of blessed memory, cited his words below in the gloss to seif 6, see there. Nevertheless, we have already written above, siman 585, os 28, that according to the mystical meaning of these matters, all of them are required and none can be omitted, see there.
