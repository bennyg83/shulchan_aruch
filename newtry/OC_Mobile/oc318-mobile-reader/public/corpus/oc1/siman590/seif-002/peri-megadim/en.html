And some say. See Taz. This 'some say' is not the 'some say' of the Mechaber, for the 'some say' that the Rav brought in the gloss also holds that the measure of a teruah is only three small tromitin, three small wails being the measure of a teruah, like the tanna of the Mishnah in Rosh Hashanah 33b. Even so, in the order tashat, where we are uncertain that perhaps teruah means three shevarim, groans, like the tanna of the baraisa, then even if he makes three somewhat long shevarim, and each shever is as long as a teruah, meaning three small wails, nevertheless the shever is not a tekiah, for if teruah is three shevarim, then its tekiah is like three shevarim. All the more so in tashrat, if the teruah is the measure of shever and wailing, its tekiah is also as long as three wails and three shevarim together. This is the view of Hagahos Ashri; see Terumas HaDeshen 141 and Magen Avraham, letter 2. According to this, what the Rav wrote, 'provided that he does not extend too much,' means that in tashat he should not extend one shever as long as three shevarim; see Eliyah Rabbah and Eliyah Zuta. But in the first opinion, the Mechaber holds that since in the order tarat the tekiah is only like a teruah of three small wails, one may no longer extend one shever as long as three small sounds. Even according to Rabbeinu Hai, according to Torah law one fulfills with three small sounds; it is not a full uncertainty, only a custom. See Beis Yosef and you will understand.
