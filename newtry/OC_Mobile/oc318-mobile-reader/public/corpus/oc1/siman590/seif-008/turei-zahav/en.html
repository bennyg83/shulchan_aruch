If he interrupted with a teruah, etc. In all these cases he holds that it is an interruption. Even regarding two teruos one after another, the Tur brings an opinion that it is not an interruption requiring him to go back to the beginning, but rather he completes the order. However, the Ramban holds that since it is unnecessary, it is an interruption in all of these cases, and so he ruled here. See what I wrote above in siman 588, seif 2, that also one who hears the blasts has this law: if he heard in between something he did not need, it is an interruption and he goes back to the beginning, unlike the Levush's distinction above based on reasoning, which in my humble opinion is against reason.
Between the tekiah and the shevarim. Meaning, even in tashrat, where a teruah belongs there after the shevarim, only he advanced it and did not act properly, for we hold in the Gemara that a person groans and then wails, and not the reverse. What he wrote afterward, 'or where he interrupts with shevarim between the tekiah and the teruah,' does not refer to tashrat, for if so it would be the first case, where he reverses the teruah and shevarim. Rather, this refers to tarat, where he adds shevarim between the first tekiah and the proper tekiah there, as it seems to me.
