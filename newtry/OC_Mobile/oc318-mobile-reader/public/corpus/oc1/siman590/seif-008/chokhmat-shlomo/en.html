(There, seif 8) He blew after the teruah a tekiah as one merely occupied, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham, who brought the view of the Ran that one merely occupied is not an interruption; see there. It seems there is proof for his words from an explicit Mishnah in tractate Parah, chapter 4, Mishnah 2, as follows: if he sprinkled from the sixth as the seventh, and then went back and sprinkled a seventh, it is invalid; if he sprinkled from the seventh as the eighth, and then went back and sprinkled an eighth, it is valid. The Rav of Bartenura explains there that from the sixth as the seventh it is invalid because we require seven times in order. But from the seventh as the eighth it is valid, because there is no eighth sprinkling in the red heifer rite, and this person erred only in speech; 'and then went back and sprinkled an eighth' is not precise, for in every case it is valid, whether he went back and sprinkled or did not go back and sprinkle, etc. Anyone who sees this will see how forced it is to say that the Mishnah's phrase 'and then went back and sprinkled an eighth' is entirely unnecessary and imprecise. What he wrote as an alternative is also forced to one who examines it there. Rather, it seems that the Mishnah is precise, and the matter is as follows. Just as with the red heifer we require intention for its own sake, and if he did not intend for its own sake it is invalid according to the first tanna there, and so we hold, likewise we require that he intend in the sprinklings for which sprinkling he is sprinkling. If he did not intend for its own sake but for another sprinkling, then either way it does not help, in the manner explicit in the Mishnah: if he sprinkled from the sixth as the seventh, then either way, if it counts as the sixth, it was without intention, for he intended it as the seventh; and if it counts as the seventh and afterward he sprinkles the sixth, it is out of order and invalid. However, it is explicit in Pesachim regarding a sin-offering slaughtered as an olah that it is invalid, but slaughtered as chullin it is valid, because something of its own type ruins it, but something not of its own type does not ruin it. It seems that although it is valid, nevertheless it does not count for the obligation. It is no worse than all other offerings, which are valid when offered not for their own sake, but nevertheless do not count for the owners as fulfillment of the obligation; for fulfillment of the obligation requires specifically that it be for its own sake. If so, here too, although chullin is not of its type and does not invalidate, because something not of its type does not ruin it, nevertheless in any event it was not for its own sake and does not count for the owners as fulfillment of the obligation. Therefore here too, when he intends the seventh as the eighth, since there is no eighth sprinkling in the red heifer rite, it is like a sin-offering for the sake of chullin; it is not of its type and does not ruin it. However, in any event that sprinkling did not count, since he did not intend it as the seventh. Therefore he must sprinkle another eighth sprinkling intended as the seventh. If there are seven sprinklings here, the extra one is not an interruption in the middle, since it is like one merely occupied and not for the sake of a mitzvah, because there is no eighth sprinkling in the red heifer rite. If so, it is only like one merely occupied, and one merely occupied does not create an interruption in the middle. Therefore it specifically requires that he sprinkle an eighth, meaning with intent for the seventh. This is exactly like what the tanna first taught, that if he went back and sprinkled a seventh, meaning for the sake of the sixth, it did not count because it is out of order. One cannot say it is speaking of where he sprinkled the seventh for the sake of the seventh, for it is obvious that he did not fulfill, since he omitted the sixth. Necessarily it is speaking of where he went back and sprinkled the seventh for the sake of the sixth, and therefore it teaches that although there is a sixth and seventh here, nevertheless he has not fulfilled, because it is not in order. So too here, the meaning is as I wrote: 'and then went back and sprinkled an eighth' means for the sake of the seventh. Therefore in this case he fulfills, because of the either-way reasoning stated above. The language of the Mishnah works correctly. From this it is proven that even where order is required and out of order invalidates, that is only for something of its own type; but anything not of its own type does not create an interruption and is considered fully in order. The law of shofar blasts is exactly equal to the law of the sprinklings above. This is clear and correct in my humble opinion in the intent of the Mishnah, and it is a clear proof for the law above; analyze it well. That is, 'on the day,' even on Shabbos, meaning that even if Shabbos fell before the first day, the phrase 'even on Shabbos' refers to the preceding matter: even if Shabbos came earlier, nevertheless the fifteenth day is called the first for the counting of sins, and it is counted only from the fifteenth day. So it seems to explain by way of aggadah, and it works well; analyze it.
