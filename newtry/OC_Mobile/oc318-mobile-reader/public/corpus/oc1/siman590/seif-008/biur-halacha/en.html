Or he interrupted with shevarim between the teruah and the tekiah. See Mishnah Berurah, that this refers to tarat, and it is from the Taz. Other later authorities wrote that the law also applies to tashrat, for example where he made shevarim after the teruah, in which case he has also lost the first tekiah, because once he had initially made the shevarim-teruah properly, the shevarim afterward became an interruption.
As one merely occupied and not for the sake of a tekiah, etc. See Mishnah Berurah for what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, ab initio it is certainly good to be concerned for the view of the Mechaber. Therefore one must be careful in a place where synagogues are close to one another that they not blow in one synagogue until they have finished in the other, for otherwise they will not hear the blasts in order. Even though he does not intend to hear for the sake of the mitzvah and has no desire at all to hear, nevertheless according to the Mechaber, even if he heard as one merely occupied, he has lost the blasts that he heard [Chayei Adam].
He was silent and interrupted, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, that this means in two breaths. So it is proven in the Ran, from whom this law derives; see there. It is surprising that the Perishah explained it otherwise.
