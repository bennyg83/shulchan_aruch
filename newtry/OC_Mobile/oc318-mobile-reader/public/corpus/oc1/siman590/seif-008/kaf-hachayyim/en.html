8) If you ask, why are all these necessary? It should be sufficient to sound tashrat three times, followed by shevarim-tekiah, tashat, tashat, and then teruah-tekiah, tarat, tarat. For if the correct teruah is groaning followed by wailing, he has fulfilled the obligation with the tashrat series. If it is groaning alone, the final tekiah of tashrat could count as the straight blast preceding tashat, and the same could be said regarding tarat. The Ran already answered in the name of Tosafos that the reason is that people might come to err. If you say that in the first tashat he should not repeat and sound the initial tekiah, they might mistakenly say that only shevarim-tekiah is required. The Rosh answered that since he sounded the tekiah for the sake of the straight blast following a teruah, the Sages did not wish it to count as the straight blast preceding a teruah. The Re'em answered that they hold like R' Zeira, who says that mitzvos require intent. Therefore, the final tekiah of tashrat cannot be considered the first tekiah of tashat, nor can the final tekiah of tashat be considered the first tekiah of tarat. A stipulation is not applicable here, for if so there is no intent either for the final tekiah or for the first tekiah, and he does not fulfill his obligation through it for either purpose, end quote. His words are not satisfactory to me, because with a stipulation it is properly considered intent. Although he makes the stipulation because of a doubt, he nevertheless intends to fulfill the obligation of tekiah. Beit Yosef. The Rama, of blessed memory, cited his words below in the gloss to seif 6, see there. Nevertheless, we have already written above, siman 585, os 28, that according to the mystical meaning of these matters, all of them are required and none can be omitted, see there.
