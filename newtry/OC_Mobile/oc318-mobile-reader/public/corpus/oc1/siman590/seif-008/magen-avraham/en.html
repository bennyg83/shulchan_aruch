As one merely occupied, etc. So wrote the Tur. But in the Ran it appears that the Ramban disagreed only where one blew for the sake of a mitzvah in between; but where he was merely occupied, why should it invalidate? It is no worse than if he performed a labor in between, for we hold that if one heard nine blasts over nine hours of the day, he fulfilled his obligation. Is it speaking of a case where he sat all day and did not speak in between, or did not hear another sound from a person or animal in between? Here too, since we hold that one merely occupied does not fulfill, it is as though he heard another sound. So it also appears in Chullin at the beginning of folio 32, and even though he diverted his mind in between, he fulfilled. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham. Therefore it seems to me to be lenient in this, since there are great poskim who hold that in every case he fulfilled, as the Ran wrote.
And he interrupted and afterward blew. But if everything was done in one tekiah, even if he began to sound a teruah, it is valid, as written in seif 7. The same applies if he began to blow and his sound did not come out well, and he began to blow a second time; it is all considered one tekiah, as stated in the Tur. Know that all this, even in tashrat, is specifically when he sounds the teruah within the shevarim, for it is similar to the teruah that he must blow. This is unlike where he makes the teruah before the shevarim, or two teruos one after the other; he has not fulfilled his obligation.
The first tekiah. If he already blew the final tekiah as well, that tekiah counts for him in place of the first tekiah, and he completes from there onward in order. If he did not remember until he was standing in another set, for example he is standing in tashat and remembered that he erred in tashrat, it seems to me that he completes the entire set and afterward goes back and blows tashrat once. Although he interrupted in the middle of the tashrat set, it seems to me that this is nothing, for according to the law one must blow once for Malchiyos, once for Zichronos, and once for Shofaros, which implies that there is no concern if one interrupts between the orders. Although one might say that hearing another shofar sound is worse, it seems to me that it is nothing. It also seems to me that in all this, even if he erred in the sitting blasts, he must repeat; we do not say that we should rely on the standing blasts, because since he recited the blessing over these blasts he must perform them properly. Also in the standing blasts he must repeat, for they are primary [and see the end of siman 127]. But if he erred in the blasts after the prayer, it is nothing.
