(30) Between the tekiah and the shevarim. This means that in tashat he inserted a teruah before the shevarim. The same applies if he inserted it after the shevarim, even if he did not complete the teruah, as above. Likewise in tashrat, if he made the teruah before the shevarim, he has also thereby lost the first tekiah. Some later authorities are lenient in tashrat and hold that since teruah belongs in this order, only he advanced it, he need not go back to the first tekiah, but completes shevarim-teruah in his usual way, and that is sufficient.
(31) Between the teruah and the tekiah. This likewise means that in tarat he inserted shevarim after the teruah. The same applies if he inserted them before the teruah, and in all this even if he did not complete the shevarim, as above. There are places whose practice is to end the teruah with a long sound. See Pri Megadim in s.k. 2, who questions this, for perhaps it is considered shevarim. There are also some shofar blowers who are not expert in this craft, and when they make a teruah they begin it with a long sound, so that its beginning looks like shevarim; this too is not done properly.
(32) Likewise, if he sounded two teruos one after the other. For although both are relevant to the matter, nevertheless once he already completed one validly, the second becomes an interruption. This is specifically where he interrupted between them; if he made them in one breath, it is like a long teruah, as below regarding shevarim.
(33) As one merely occupied, etc. Magen Avraham inclines to be lenient in this, because one merely occupied is not considered a tekiah at all, and it is as though he heard another sound in between, which is not considered an interruption thereby.
(34) He was silent and interrupted, etc. This means that it was in two breaths; for if it was in one breath, it is considered one and is valid, since he may add as many shevarim as he wants, as above in seif 3. The same applies if he began to blow and the sound did not come out well, and he began to blow a second time: all of it is considered one tekiah [later authorities].
(35) The first tekiah. If he already blew the final tekiah as well, that tekiah counts for him in place of the first tekiah, and he completes from there onward in order. If he did not remember until he was standing in another set, for example he was standing in tashat and remembered that he erred in tashrat, he completes the entire set and afterward goes back and blows tashrat once. Although he interrupted in the middle of the tashrat set with other sounds, this is nothing, since in each unit by itself there was no interruption with other sounds. Know further that in all this, even if he blew in the sitting blasts, he must repeat; we do not say that we should rely on the standing blasts, for since he recited the blessing over these, he must perform them properly. Also in the standing blasts he must repeat, for they are primary. But if he erred in the blasts after the prayer, it is nothing [Magen Avraham and other authorities].
