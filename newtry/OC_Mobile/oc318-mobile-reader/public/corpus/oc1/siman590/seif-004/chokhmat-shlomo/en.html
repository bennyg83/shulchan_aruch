(Seif 4) But the three shevarim and the teruah of tashrat need not be made in one breath, etc. Note: This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam. The poskim wrote the reason in his name: people do not usually make groaning and wailing in one breath. At first glance, we do not know from where Rabbeinu Tam knew this by his own reasoning, to be lenient regarding a Torah concern. However, it seems that it works well. Rabbeinu Tam was troubled by the question of the Bach and the Taz: how could Rabbi Avahu be uncertain, perhaps teruah is groaning and wailing? We see that the tanna of the Mishnah and the baraisa disagree only whether it is this or that; from where in the Torah would we say that we need both? One can answer as follows. Certainly both are called teruah: groaning is called teruah, and wailing is called teruah. Since both are called teruah and the Torah wrote simply 'teruah,' which one would you exclude? Therefore necessarily the Torah's intent is both of them. But this reasoning, 'which one would you exclude,' applies to the law of making the body of the teruah, and therefore one must make both, as we say generally in such a case: which one would you exclude? But regarding the measure of a tekiah, where we hold it is like a teruah, certainly one teruah is enough, either this one or that one. If so, one can say that one master stated one thing and one master stated another, and they do not disagree: this one teaches that the measure of shevarim is enough, and this one teaches that the measure of teruah is enough. However, this is only if we say that each one by itself is also a teruah, and only because of 'which one would you exclude' do we require both, because both are included in teruah. Then one can say that regarding the measure of a tekiah, one of them is enough. But if we say that each one is not considered a teruah, only both together, and each one by itself is not a teruah, then certainly, since we need the measure of a tekiah to be like a teruah, we need it to be like both. If so, since in truth we see that each tanna mentioned only one, necessarily one of them is enough, and then there is no room for doubt that we require both. Therefore this is certain: if each one by itself is a teruah, and only because of 'which one would you exclude' we need both, then it is certainly enough to make them in two breaths; on the contrary, we must do so, since each one is an independent matter. But if only both together are the teruah, then they must be in one breath. This is clear to anyone who understands. This was Rabbi Avahu's uncertainty: if groaning and wailing are made only in one breath, then there is no room to be uncertain that we require both, because according to this the tannaim necessarily disagree, as stated above, and since both did not require both, there is no room to be uncertain that we require both. Or perhaps groaning and wailing are made in two breaths, and one can say that each one is a teruah by itself, only that nevertheless we require both because of 'which one would you exclude.' If so, one can again say that the tannaim do not disagree, but one master said one thing and one master said another, and they do not disagree, and the measure of a tekiah is enough with one of them. According to this, if one makes tashrat because of Rabbi Avahu's uncertainty, he must make them in two breaths and not one. For if it were in one breath, he would necessarily hold that one by itself is not a teruah, only together; if so, there is no room for this uncertainty, for according to this the words of the tannaim necessarily prove the opposite. Therefore, if we are uncertain about both, we must say that each one is like a teruah by itself, only that because of 'which one would you exclude' we require both; therefore one must make them specifically in two breaths. Because of the above question of the Bach and the Taz, Rabbeinu Tam was compelled to say this. This is clear and correct; analyze it well.
