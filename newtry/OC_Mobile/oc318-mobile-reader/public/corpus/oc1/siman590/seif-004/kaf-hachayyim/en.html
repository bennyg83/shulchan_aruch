4) [Seif 2] We are uncertain regarding the teruah stated in the Torah, etc. For it is written, "A day of teruah," and we translate teruah as yevava. Infer from this that it resembles the sound a person makes when crying and wailing, which is the meaning of yevava, as derived from the mother of Sisera, regarding whom it is written, "She wailed." We still do not know whether it resembles a person groaning from his heart, in the manner of a sick person who emits short sounds one after another and extends them somewhat, which is called groaning, or a person who wails and laments, emitting short sounds in rapid succession, which is called wailing, or whether it consists of both together. Therefore, because of the doubt, we perform all of them. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2. This is from the Gemara there.
