In the copy before my father-in-law, of blessed memory, it was written 'apart from the interruption,' and he labored to explain the words, but he labored for nothing. This is ab initio, but after the fact one fulfills even with two breaths, for he ruled in seif 7 that if one remembered immediately, he should blow one shever; and see what I wrote there.
Some say that one need not make them in one breath. This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam, in whose name they wrote so, because people do not groan and wail in one breath. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 142, he wrote that according to this view there is a prohibition if one makes them in one breath, and even after the fact he has not fulfilled. This refers to the blasts of the orders, where they blow tashrat each time, for then the teruah is considered as though it does not exist, since he began with shevarim. He brings proof from the end of the first chapter of Chullin, where such a case is considered blowing but not sounding a teruah when one sounds a teruah from within the tekiah. I do not recognize this as a proof, for this is what it says there: How do they blow, meaning on Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbos, where a change from other erev Shabbos days is needed? Rabbi Yehudah says: he blows a tekiah and sounds a teruah from within the tekiah, meaning before the tekiah is completed. Rav Asi says: he blows a tekiah and sounds a teruah in one breath. Rashi explains: nevertheless, he pauses in between. They challenged: they blow but do not sound a teruah. Rabbi Yehudah answers that this means: they do not sound a teruah by itself, but from within the tekiah. Rav Asi answers that this means: they do not sound a teruah in two breaths, but in one breath. If so, from where did Terumas HaDeshen learn that the teruah is considered as though it does not exist? We answered that the expression 'they do not sound a teruah' means in two breaths, but in one breath it is properly called blowing and sounding a teruah. Furthermore, granted that the name teruah is not upon it there, nevertheless here the essential force of the teruah is its mitzvah in this way, that it be together with the shevarim, and both as one are called teruah. Rather, certainly Rabbeinu Tam did not intend to prohibit one breath; he said only that it is not necessary, as Beis Yosef wrote, and after the fact he fulfilled. The 'some say' here is the Rosh, who wrote that one should make them in one breath. Although Rabbeinu Tam said that people do not groan and wail in one breath, nevertheless the teruah is groaning and wailing, and we require the teruah to be as one without interruption. Beis Yosef decided like Terumas HaDeshen, that in the sitting blasts one should practice with one breath, and in the orders with two breaths. We have only the custom that the Rema wrote, like the words of Rabbeinu Tam. Nevertheless, it seems to me that one should teach the blower not to make any delay between the shevarim and the teruah, but to make them as close together as possible, even though they are in another breath. It is proper that the caller should not call 'teruah' to the blower in the tashrat order, but should skip this call, and the blower should do so on his own. In a place where they do not want to change by skipping the caller's call of teruah in the tashrat order, nevertheless the blower should not wait for the call, but should hurry himself to make them as close together as possible. So it seems to me.
