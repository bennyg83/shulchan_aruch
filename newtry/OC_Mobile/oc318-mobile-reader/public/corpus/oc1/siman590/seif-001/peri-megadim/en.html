Or. See Taz, Rosh Hashanah 33b. There is a printing error, and this requires analysis: the Mishnah speaks of yevavos, and the baraisa of shevarim; groaning is longer than wailing. See Bach, who raised this difficulty and answered that according to Rabbeinu Hai it is satisfactory, but according to the Rambam of blessed memory, that it is a full uncertainty from the Torah, it is difficult; see there. See Pnei Yehoshua, who answered according to the one who derives it from the wilderness, that there, regarding war, one may say it includes both groaning and wailing; see there. The Taz answered, etc. See Tosafos, Rosh Hashanah 33b, s.v. 'the measure,' and the other commentators: why is it not enough to blow three times tashrat, then once shevarim-tekiah and twice tashat, and once teruah-tekiah and twice tarat, with the final tekiah of tashrat counting for one shevarim-tekiah, and the final tekiah of tashat counting for one teruah-tekiah? The Taz of blessed memory answered that in seif 3, the tekios of tashrat are longer than those of tashat, and the tekios of tashat are longer than those of tarat. Although if he extends greatly in all of them there is no concern, nevertheless they made a recognizable distinction not to shorten in tashrat, and likewise in tashat and tarat; therefore they divided each one separately. The implication of the Mechaber follows the Rambam of blessed memory, and his language in chapter 2 of Shofar is that all of them are a Torah uncertainty. Therefore, even if one heard tashrat three times and no more, and before him are two towns, in one he will hear tarat and tashat and in one he will hear the prayers, he should go to the shofar; see siman 595. However, this requires analysis, for one may say that it is a double uncertainty: perhaps he fulfilled with tashrat, for this is the Torah's intent, and perhaps an interruption with another sound is not an interruption. In seif 8, because of uncertainty he repeats; if so, it is certainly rabbinic, and with a Torah double uncertainty one may say that a certain rabbinic obligation is preferable. With Hashem's help, below in seif 8 and in siman 595 this will be explained further. Ab initio one should blow tashrat three times, tashat three times, and tarat three times, because one first groans and afterward wails; see Eliyah Zuta, Abudraham, and Eliyah Rabbah. After the fact, if he advanced tarat before tashat or even before tashrat, he fulfilled, as in siman 592, for their order is not indispensable; see there.
