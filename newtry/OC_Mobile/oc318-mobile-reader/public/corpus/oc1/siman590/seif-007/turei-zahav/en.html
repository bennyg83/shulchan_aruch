He should blow another shever. For he has not lost his order, since the teruah also now belongs to the shevarim, as both of them are the teruah in tashrat. This is the view of the Raavan, and the Rosh agrees with him, except that he disagrees where he completed the teruah; according to the Raavan he has lost his order and must blow tashrat anew, while the Rosh holds that even if he completed the teruah, he has not lost his order, but makes three shevarim anew and sounds a teruah, since he did not interrupt with another sound that is not relevant now in tashrat. He ruled here like the Rosh. In Mordechai he brings the words of Rabbeinu Yoel, who disagrees with the Raavan even in the first case, namely where he did not finish the teruah and then makes another shever. He wrote: I did not understand the words of my teacher, who distinguished between where he completed the teruah and where he did not complete the teruah, for anyone who begins a teruah can quickly make three yevavos, and that is the measure of a teruah. Even if you say that he made only two yevavos, from where do we know that it is not an interruption? What difference is there to me between two and three? End quote. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that his words are correct, and therefore it appears that even if he did not complete the teruah, he goes back and blows three shevarim, and it does not help to make another shever, even if he does everything in one breath, for in the end it is an interruption in the middle of the shevarim, etc., end quote. So it is proper to rule. What loss is there in making three new shevarim, since there is no trace of prohibition in this? He has already lost the first two shevarim even where there was only silence between them; for although we hold that silence is not an interruption, that means he has not lost his order. But if he is in one of the shofar sounds and was silent in the middle, certainly it does not help afterward to complete what remains of his obligation, because that sound was spoiled and he must make it anew. Nevertheless, if he did not remain silent during that sound but only interrupted with a breath between them, although he did not act properly, for he must complete all three shevarim in one breath, nevertheless he has not lost what he already made of it and should complete what remains, since the sound was not spoiled in between. So it seems to me correctly.
