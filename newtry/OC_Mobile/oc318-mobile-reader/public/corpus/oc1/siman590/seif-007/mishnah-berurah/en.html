(27) Another shever. This is sufficient, for he did not lose his order, since the teruah also now belongs to the shevarim, as both are the teruah in tashrat; therefore we consider it only like one who stumbled in the shevarim, which does not cause him to lose the shevarim thereby. See Biur HaGra, who concludes that this is specifically where it was in one breath; if it was not in one breath, he must go back and blow three shevarim and a teruah. There are poskim who hold that even if it was in one breath, he must also go back and blow three shevarim and complete the order, since in any event he interrupted in the middle of the shevarim. It is proper ab initio to be concerned and stringent like their words.
(28) He completed the teruah. Meaning, he made three forces, with which he fulfilled the teruah according to the first opinion cited above in seif 3. The reason for the distinction between completing and not completing is that as long as the teruah was not completed, it is a mere sound and it appears that he is still engaged in the shevarim, for he also did not finish the teruah, and he goes back to complete the shevarim. This is unlike where he completed the teruah, for it is a complete sound and it appears that afterward he is beginning other shevarim; therefore completing one shever does not help, but he must make three new shevarim. Nevertheless, he has not lost the first tekiah even in this case, for he did not interrupt with another sound that is not related to the matter, since both are part of teruah, only he did not make it properly.
(29) It happened in tashat. That is, he began with shevarim, one or two shevarim, and afterward made a teruah. We do not say that this is included in stumbling; all the more so if he made all the shevarim and afterward made a teruah, since it is not relevant to the matter, he spoiled the entire order. The same applies if he made the teruah before the shevarim; there is no distinction in all this whether he completed the teruah or did not complete it, for in every case he lost the first tekiah. Likewise, what he wrote, 'or tarat,' means that he began with a teruah and in the middle interrupted and began to make one shever; everything is as above regarding tashat, and the same applies regarding tarat, for since this does not belong at all to the teruah of this order, he also lost the first tekiah.
