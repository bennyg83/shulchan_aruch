One must sound tashrat three times, tashat three times, etc. It is not sufficient to sound tashrat three times, because perhaps only shevarim or only teruah is correct, and the other sound would constitute an interruption. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. This is from the Gemara, Rosh Hashanah 34a. They did not also institute sounding tarshat, on the possibility that the teruah should precede the shevarim, because the Gemara there already answered this: Ordinarily, when misfortune befalls a person, he first groans and afterward wails, see there. Regarding the reason that tashat is performed before tarat, the Abudraham wrote, 99b, that just as in the tashrat series shevarim is performed before teruah, because ordinarily every person first groans and afterward wails, so too the tashat series, which represents groaning, must be performed before the tarat series, which represents wailing, see there. However, after the fact, this order is not indispensable, as stated at the end of siman 593. Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. Nevertheless, according to the mystical meaning of these matters, they must specifically be performed in the order tashrat, tashat, tarat, as written above, siman 585, os 28, see there.
