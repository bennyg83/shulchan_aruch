(23) Of tashat. The Mechaber spoke according to the practice in his place, where they would blow tashrat for Malchiyos and tashat for Zichronos, as below in siman 592, and he intended it to count also as the first tekiah of Zichronos. The same applies according to the custom in our countries, where we blow tashrat each time, and he intended the last tekiah to serve as the first tekiah of Zichronos.
(24) Only for one tekiah. That is, for the last one, because every tekiah must have a beginning and an end. If this tekiah is divided into two tekios, as the blower intended, then the last tekiah has no beginning and the first tekiah has no end. Therefore we do not follow his intention in this at all; rather, we consider this tekiah one long tekiah, and it counts for him as the last one.
(25) And some say that even, etc. Their reason is that we follow his intention and divide the tekiah into two, and consequently he has nothing, as above. See the later authorities, who wrote that according to this view he has lost the whole unit, for he interrupted with another sound that was not according to the law, as written in seif 8, and he must go back and blow tashrat. However, as a matter of law there is no practical difference in all this, for the primary view is the first opinion [Eliyah Rabbah and Biur HaGra].
(26) One tekiah between two orders. That is to say, he blew tashrat, then shevarim-teruah, and stipulated regarding the final tekiah of tashrat: if this order is the correct one, let the tekiah count for it; and if the order of tashat is the correct one, let the tekiah count for it. For according to Torah law one order is sufficient, only because we do not know which order is correct we blow them all, as above at the beginning of the siman. Therefore after the fact he fulfills through his condition. Nevertheless, ab initio Chazal did not want to institute that he blow only tashrat, shevarim-teruah, teruah-tekiah, so that people not err and say that only shevarim-teruah or teruah-tekiah is needed.
