Some say. See Taz. One must contemplate his holy words of blessed memory. One who examines Terumas HaDeshen, siman 142, will see that he wrote that Rabbeinu Tam and the Rosh of blessed memory disagree: according to Rabbeinu Tam, shevarim and teruah together require an interruption specifically in two breaths, for people do not groan and wail at once; while according to the Rosh, it must be specifically in one breath, because this is the mitzvah of the Torah: we require one teruah, groaning and wailing, specifically at once. If so, in a place where they blow in the orders, meaning as in siman 592, once tashrat, once tashat, and once tarat, or once tashrat three times, once tashat three times, and once tarat three times, meaning thirty sounds as in the sitting blasts, as they do in Neustadt there, he can fulfill according to all views by doing tashrat, shevarim and teruah, in one breath in the sitting blasts and in two breaths in the orders, or the reverse, and either way he fulfills. This is unlike a place where in the orders they blow tashrat once in each section; there he must do the sitting blasts in one breath and the orders in two breaths, but not the reverse, with the orders in one breath and the sitting blasts in two breaths. For perhaps the primary teruah is wailing, meaning tarat; if so, when he blows tashrat each time in the orders, perhaps the shevarim are nothing and only the teruah is wailing, and one does not fulfill by sounding teruah from within shevarim, for that is nothing, as in the case in Chullin, etc. That is to say, one may say that even according to the Rosh he did not fulfill in the orders, because people do not do that at all. Granted, if the Torah commanded this, this is the teruah; but if the mitzvah is only the teruah alone, then when he makes shevarim with teruah in one breath it is nothing, like the case in Chullin, where the baraisa calls it, regarding Yom Tov on erev Shabbos, 'they blow but do not sound a teruah' in such a case. Although in seif 5, if he made tarat in one breath he fulfilled, and this is from the Yerushalmi, that is in the mitzvah of the Torah, where it is not nullified because of the others; unlike an optional case such as Chullin, and all the more so if the shevarim are not a mitzvah at all. Understand this. Now, regarding what the Mechaber wrote, 'and some say that it need not be in one breath,' that is Rabbeinu Tam, and 'some say it must,' that is the Rosh of blessed memory, and he holds that according to Rabbeinu Tam too one fulfills in one breath, only it is not necessary. If so, how did he write that a Heaven-fearing person fulfills both? One must say that this is the explanation: although the halacha is that if he did it in one breath he fulfilled according to everyone, nevertheless a Heaven-fearing person who is concerned for the reasoning of Terumas HaDeshen, that according to Rabbeinu Tam he did not fulfill in one breath, should do as Terumas HaDeshen wrote: in the sitting blasts in one breath and in the orders in two breaths. This includes every case, even for us below in siman 592; unlike according to the Mechaber there, where one could also do the reverse, as we said. What the Taz wrote, that nevertheless the name teruah is upon it, is from what the Rosh of blessed memory wrote; and Rabbeinu Tam too said only that it is not necessary, not that it is forbidden in one breath. See Terumas HaDeshen on this. The Rav of blessed memory wrote that the simple custom is in two breaths and one should not change; one may say that he holds like Terumas HaDeshen, that one did not fulfill in one breath, etc. Understand this. With Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, letter 4, regarding tarat in one breath, it will be explained further; understand this.
