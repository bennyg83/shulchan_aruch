(S.k. 6) And he stipulated, etc. But ab initio they did not want to institute it this way. That is to say, they could have instituted that in the final tashrat he should blow tashrat, and afterward, for the tashat set, he should not blow once, but only shevarim-teruah, with the final tekiah of tashrat counting either way, whether for tashrat or for tashat, and he would intend this. Likewise, for the first tarat set he would not blow a full set, but only teruah-tekiah. Rather, ab initio they instituted it in the best way, for what difference does it make if he blows two extra tekios or not? They did not want to rely on a condition, even where there is no concern for a mishap.
And furthermore, alternatively, so that people not err, etc. For in the second and third tashat as well they might blow only shevarim-tekiah, and people would err and think that in the tashat set one does not need the first tekiah at all. All this is the words of the Beis Yosef.
