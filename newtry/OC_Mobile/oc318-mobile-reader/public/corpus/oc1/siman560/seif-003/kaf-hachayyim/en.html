One may never build a plastered and decorated structure like the palaces of kings, but rather, etc. This is the Rambam’s view, that if one builds his entire house with plaster like the palaces of kings, it is forbidden even if he leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. Only when he coats his house with clay and then covers it with plaster is leaving an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished sufficient, as explained in the Beit Yosef. The Tur, however, holds that even if one builds the entire structure with plaster, it is permitted when he leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. Darkei Moshe, entry 1, wrote that the custom follows the Tur, for by leaving an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished, people customarily permit everything. End quote. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, cited his words. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, entry 1, that common custom follows the Tur, and whenever one leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished, it is permitted in every manner, even if the structure is plastered and decorated with plaster alone, and paintings upon it are also permitted when one leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, entry 1, that such is the custom.
