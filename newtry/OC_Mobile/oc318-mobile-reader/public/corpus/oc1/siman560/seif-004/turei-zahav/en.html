But one of braid is permitted for all. The Beis Yosef wrote: but in our text of the Rambam it is written, "if there was silver there," which implies according to this version that even though its primary material is braid, if silver or gold settings are fixed in it, it is forbidden. Accordingly it appears that covering the bride with a silk hinuma that has silver or gold threads involves a prohibition to cover the bride with this. And furthermore, it is stated at the end of Sotah: A Tanna taught that they also decreed concerning a chuppah of bridegrooms. What is a chuppah of bridegrooms? Gilded scarlet threads. Rashi explained: a garment dyed scarlet with gold threads fixed in it, until they set it up like a kind of dome; end quote. And this is precisely that hinuma which is on the bride's head like a kind of dome, with the remainder hanging behind. Accordingly one should be careful not to cover with a hinuma that has gold or silver threads. And in truth, nowadays people do not pay heed to this. And it appears somewhat [possible] to say that the Gemara forbade only if the hinuma was made in the first place for the sake of the bride, for they call it a chuppah of bridegrooms; and so we find that Ravina found Mar bar Rav Ashi braiding a wreath for his daughter. Which is not so regarding our custom, whereby they take from the sacred vessels in the synagogue and cover the bride with it, for it was not made in the first place for the sake of the bride; therefore they did not decree concerning this. And if one should say, how is it permitted to make use of sacred vessels for this? One can say that this too is a great mitzvah, namely escorting a bride, which is counted among the things that have no fixed measure, and there is no [mere] ordinary secular use here. And this requires further study.
