In order. See the Taz; see what I wrote above: the essence of remembrance is in the heart, not like those who depict in black the episode of Tzidkiyahu, for in any case it is fine looking, and we require that his heart be subdued. Regarding what the Rav wrote in the gloss, to break a vessel under the chuppah: see Eliyah Rabbah letter 7 in the name of Matteh Moshe: what is the reason for the custom to break a pot at the writing of the tenaim (see there), namely in order to startle and so that the joy not be complete, and to remember the destruction; and see there. And Even HaShoham, siman 51, that one should break specifically a glass cup; and in the name of the Maharit of blessed memory in the sefer Tzafnas Paneach, parashas Devarim: they break the cup of erusin, and it is a hint that like glass which was broken, there is a remedy, and they take a whole glass cup for birkas nissuin; if so, not earthenware, for what is broken has no remedy; see there. And from Darkei Moshe it implies that they take an earthenware cup for erusin, etc. And our custom is to break glass after all the seven berachos, as is apparent from the Kol Bo; see Darkei Moshe on this. And see Even HaEzer, siman 65, se'if 3 in the gloss; and from the exact wording of his language there, that they break the cup of erusin after the seven berachos, it implies that they take two cups: one for birkas erusin, and that one they break after the seven berachos, and a second cup for birkas nissuin; that is, after kiddushin and the reading of the kesubah he says Borei peri hagafen and begins "Who created everything for His glory," etc.; this is birkas nissuin. And a reason is required: why do they take another cup to recite the blessing, rather than [continue] upon the first cup, that is, the first vessel? Also, from his concluding words in Darkei Moshe here, "therefore they take an earthenware cup for birkas erusin," it implies specifically earthenware; and why, for earthenware is sometimes more expensive than glass? Rather it appears to explain that at the Giving of the Torah there were kiddushin, and it was fitting for nissuin as well, and through the sin of the calf and the breaking of the Tablets, and after the destruction of the First and Second Temples, those kiddushin were wholly nullified, Heaven forbid, and we do not have among us the resting of His Presence in unique closeness upon us in the bitter exile; rather the flow [of shefa] descends, etc. And in the future to come the Holy One, blessed be He, will again perform new kiddushin, as it is written, "And I will betroth you to Me in righteousness," etc.; hence there will be new kiddushin and nissuin, and as it is written, "Behold, I am doing a new thing," and Hashem will create for us a new and pure heart. And we hint at this by taking an earthenware cup for birkas erusin, and taking for birkas nissuin another cup of silver or glass, and after all the seven berachos they break the earthenware cup, for the first kiddushin were wholly nullified and have no remedy, as it is written, "The precious sons of Zion... How they are regarded as earthenware jugs"; but the second kiddushin will endure together with nissuin, and glass, whose interior is like its exterior, that the evil inclination not hold sway over us at all, as it is written, "I will remove [it] from the earth." And therefore they do not break the cup of birkas nissuin, for that will endure; only the earthenware cup do they break, as stated. So it appears to me to explain Darkei Moshe. But I have seen the custom that they take a glass cup for erusin; nevertheless it appears that they do not break that one after nissuin, but rather another glass that they break after the seven berachos. And it appears [proper] to break under the chuppah a whole cup, and there is no issue of "do not destroy," since they do it as a moral hint so that people take it to heart; which is not so regarding the tenaim, where they do it to startle and to diminish the joy, for which it is proper to take a broken earthenware pot of clay shards to break. And understand this. And in the Levush, Even HaEzer: "And we practice concerning it," meaning regarding what the Rav wrote, "and this is their custom," etc.; that is to say, we practice with an earthenware vessel, not a glass cup. And understand.
