And when a woman makes silver and gold ornaments, she leaves on her temple — meaning: treatment with lime that she treats on the place of the temples. Thus Rambam: they are accustomed there to lime in order to remove hair; and Ran wrote in the name of Ittur: it deals with when a woman braided all her hair and leaves from it a small amount between her ear and her forehead opposite her temple, and brings crushed lime, which is like a kind of clay, and smears it on the hair and does not braid it but casts it opposite its thickness — thus poor girls do; but rich girls perfume with spices and good oil so the hairs will adhere — end of his words. And in Spain they practice to place a wreath of olive on the groom's head, because olive is bitter, a reminder of the destruction; and in these countries they practice the two commemorations mentioned — namely to place ashes of a burnt wick on the groom's head, and also to break a cup after seven blessings; and in the community of Krakow I saw that they break the cup on which they recite the blessing of betrothal, as also the Gaon Maharam Tit wrote; therefore they practice to recite the blessing of betrothal on a glass cup.
