In the Shulchan Aruch: but he plasters his house, etc. And see Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that according to what he wrote in siman 1 in the name of Likkutei Maharil, that a house is called a room, it is possible that here one must leave [an unfinished spot] for every single room; see there. But in She'eilas Yaavetz, siman 169, he wrote that with an amah by an amah once is sufficient for all the rooms of the house, even though he has many rooms, and he has a supporting reason from [the case of] one who takes hold of a convert's property; see there. Nevertheless, when I added for myself a fine room for the sake of the mitzvah of sukkah, I left opposite the doorway some tefachim without white plaster of lime, in the manner that they do to walls upon the plaster by coating them with it. And it is possible that even when one builds a house anew, it is sufficient when one leaves an empty place without plastering, since its place is well recognizable; and it seems that the custom has become widespread to be lenient thus, and all the more so in this room. And I was further lenient in it not to be particular about the measure of an amah, for a stringency is enough, and as is written in the Shulchan Aruch that even a small amount suffices; see there. He also wrote that even if he builds only one room, only that it be fit for respectable dwelling, he must leave over; see there. And see Peri Megadim, that it is reasonable that "an amah by an amah" means square, not long and narrow; and see there. What he wrote "opposite the doorway" implies facing the doorway, and so it implies from the Levush, so that one see it upon entering, etc.; and some make it near the doorway, etc.; see there. And in Mishbetzos Zahav, who wrote that although the word "neged" usually means opposite and facing, nevertheless sometimes its meaning is beside, etc. And in my humble opinion, the language of Scripture is one thing and the language of the Sages is another, and everywhere in the language of the Talmud "neged" means facing; and all the more so since the reason is sensible, so that one who enters the doorway see it immediately. And there are those who leave empty spots above the doorway in height, and then it is not seen at all by one who enters; and it is possible [they do so] so that it always appear before the baal habayis who sits inside, for for the most part he sits by the wall that faces the doorway of the house; and this requires further study. But in these generations they went out to be lenient, and they were lenient and again lenient, until it is almost forgotten from the heart, and there is nothing to rely upon; and so several similar laws, because of our many sins, have gone as they have gone.
