1) [Seif 1] From the time the Beis HaMikdash was destroyed, the Sages ordained, etc. In every joyous matter there should be a remembrance of the destruction of the Beis HaMikdash. Tur and Beit Yosef. They supported this enactment with the verse, “If I forget you, Jerusalem, etc.” Levush. This is from the Gemara, Bava Batra 60b.
2) Ibid. One may never build a plastered structure, etc. However, regarding a synagogue, it is permitted in every manner, and such is the custom. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur. The same applies to a beis midrash, where it is permitted in every manner, even with plaster alone and without leaving an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. Eshel Avraham, entry 2; Mishna Berurah in Biur Halacha. It appears that the same applies to a sukkah used during the festival, and it is permitted in every manner. Tosafos Chaim on Chayei Adam, klal 137, entry 2. If one sometimes resides in it during the rest of the year, one should be stringent, as stated below in entry 5, see there.
3) Ibid. One may never build a plastered and decorated structure like the palaces of kings, but rather, etc. This is the Rambam’s view, that if one builds his entire house with plaster like the palaces of kings, it is forbidden even if he leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. Only when he coats his house with clay and then covers it with plaster is leaving an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished sufficient, as explained in the Beit Yosef. The Tur, however, holds that even if one builds the entire structure with plaster, it is permitted when he leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. Darkei Moshe, entry 1, wrote that the custom follows the Tur, for by leaving an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished, people customarily permit everything. End quote. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, cited his words. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, entry 1, that common custom follows the Tur, and whenever one leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished, it is permitted in every manner, even if the structure is plastered and decorated with plaster alone, and paintings upon it are also permitted when one leaves an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, entry 1, that such is the custom.
4) Ibid. Rather, one coats his house with clay, covers it with plaster, and leaves an area, etc. If one mixed sand or straw into the plaster, he need not leave an area of one cubit by one cubit unfinished. Beit Yosef; Shulchan Gavoah, entry 1. So too wrote Maamar Mordechai, entry 1, in the name of the Ran, see there.
5) Ibid. And he leaves an area of one cubit, etc. It is possible that one must leave such an area in every room. Eliyah Rabbah, entry 1, cited by Bigdei Yesha. However, Responsa She’eilas Yaavetz, siman 169, wrote that leaving one area of one cubit by one cubit is sufficient for all the rooms of the house, even if they are numerous, see there. Birkei Yosef, entry 3; Shaarei Teshuvah. Nevertheless, when I added for myself a pleasant room for the mitzvah of sukkah, I left opposite the entrance several handbreadths that were not covered with white plaster, of the sort applied over the plaster already coating the walls. It is possible that even when building a new house, it is sufficient to leave a space without plaster, because its place is clearly recognizable. It seems to me that the custom has become widespread to be lenient in this manner, and all the more so regarding this room. I was additionally lenient not to insist upon the measure of one cubit, for a stringency is sufficient, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 2, that even a small amount suffices. She’eilas Yaavetz there; Shaarei Teshuvah there.
