Since the Temple was destroyed etc. This seif is in the language of Rambam, and the Tur copied in this language; and Rambam wrote: one does not build a building of plaster like the buildings of kings, but one plasters his house with clay and coats with plaster and leaves in it a square cubit by a square cubit; and he wrote on this, and it does not imply so in the Gemara — for after it brings that braita, the rabbis taught: a person should not plaster his house with plaster; and if he mixed in it sand or straw it is permitted. R' Yosi says: sand — behold it is plastered plaster and forbidden; straw — permitted. And it concludes: thus the sages said: a person may coat his house with plaster and leave in it a square cubit by a square cubit — therefore by leaving a square cubit by a square cubit all types of plaster are permitted — end of his words. It appears to me to explain these words in this way: in the Gemara there is first a braita that one may not plaster and may not mark etc.; and afterward a braita: one should not plaster his house with plaster etc.; what the Tur wrote; and afterward a braita: thus the sages said etc.; and the Tur holds that there is no reason in the world to say the braitot contradict each other, but both are words of the living God; and if so, the first braita said one may not plaster — meaning he should not plaster his house with plaster, implying there is no remedy even if he wishes to leave a cubit etc.; and the braita said there is this remedy; and our rabbi the Tur holds that Rambam answers this difficulty: that the remedy in the latter braita is by plastering with clay first and afterward plaster, except that people did not know there is clay below since plaster covers it; therefore he must leave a cubit etc., and by this they will recognize and all will know there is clay plastering beneath; and leaving a cubit alone does not help, for the viewer thinks the building is not yet finished and he will still plaster there with plaster as well; therefore clay is needed beneath, and it will appear the building is finished, and nevertheless he left — and it turns out that the Tur too, who copied Rambam's words, does not make a building of plaster, and did not copy "plastered building" — all one: that "plastered" also implies the building is made covered with plaster; and if so, the Tur's copying is also in this way: one does not make a building of plaster — meaning to coat it with plaster, namely plaster alone, which has no remedy in the world; and the latter braita teaches there is a remedy that one plasters with clay first and afterward plaster and leaves as written. And the Tur was challenged on this: for in the latter braita they established it in the Talmud after the intermediate section; and in the intermediate section they said remedies — namely the first tanna holds mixing sand and straw, and R' Yosi holds specifically straw; and this is obvious that it deals with plaster alone without clay beneath, for they mentioned only the remedy of how the plaster should be; and furthermore, if so, this latter braita certainly also deals with plaster alone without clay beneath, and nevertheless it is permitted with the remedy of leaving a cubit; and we cannot say the braitot disagree — therefore our rabbi the Tur said: therefore by leaving a cubit all types of plaster are permitted — meaning even if there is no clay beneath, not like Rambam; rather it must be said that certainly leaving the cubit helps even without clay; and the first braita that forbade plastering means if he plasters it entirely; and that tanna did not descend to teach the remedy, but taught the intermediate section, and in the latter braita taught the remedy: here he taught the remedy of mixing sand or straw, and in the latter braita he taught the remedy of leaving; and both, without any plastering beneath, deal. And Beit Yosef wrote that there is a dispute between the braitot; and from this he challenged our rabbi the Tur why he ruled like the latter braita. And in Rambam's words he first explained that what the tanna taught "one may not plaster" means by means of a trowel — what the tanna said afterward — and the mind cannot bear this explanation; and afterward he wrote: and it is further possible to say that the way of the world is to plaster with clay first and then plaster, but kings do not plaster first with clay but all plaster — this explanation is certainly true in Rambam's words; and I say that our rabbi the Tur also explains Rambam's words thus, and what he wrote "but he disagrees" and holds one should not distinguish between plastering with clay first or not — for the reason we mentioned — and all is satisfactory in my humble opinion. And from Beit Yosef's words it appears he had the text in the Tur "plastered plaster," and from this wrote to distinguish whether the halacha is like the rabbis or like R' Yosi; and our text in the Tur is correct and emended; and I also saw Mahariach, may his memory be blessed, elaborate to explain the essence of the dispute between Rambam and the Tur — and as it appears to me I wrote; and one may say: why did our rabbi not write the law of the prohibition of trowel and lime that they mentioned in the Gemara — namely types of forms that our rabbi holds: if he left a cubit by a cubit it is permitted in all manners. Further, a braita states: one who bought a courtyard marked and distinguished — it remains in its presumption; if it fell he does not return and build it; and Rambam wrote in this language; and one who buys a plastered courtyard etc., as written here in Shulchan Aruch; and Beit Yosef wrote and was not concerned for his words that if it fell he does not return and build it — because it is an obvious matter; and I wondered greatly on this: is Rambam better than the tanna of the braita, who needed to write it? If so it is not obvious; and furthermore in the Gemara they challenge from this on one who holds regarding damage that if it fell he returns and builds, and needed to answer that the prohibition is different; and if so certainly Rambam should have written this to teach the distinction. It appears that since Rambam wrote "it remains in its presumption," we do not obligate him to scrape the walls — which would apparently permit his language — but it teaches that he has no permission except that he need not trouble and mar the wall since it is already built thus; if we know that if it crumbled by itself it is certainly forbidden to return and build thus — and the tanna of the braita did not use this language that we do not obligate him to scrape; therefore he needed to say if it fell he does not return and build it. And the Tur did not mention at all this matter of one who bought a plastered courtyard — it remains in its presumption — since it deals with a case where it may have been built before the destruction of the Temple, and this is its presumption that it was built permissibly; and this is an uncommon matter, and the Tur's way is not to write uncommon matters, unlike Rambam, whose way is to write even uncommon matters nowadays, for he is a treasury of laws — all this appears very correct to me, and Beit Yosef did not emerge from the investigation in this due to its brevity.
