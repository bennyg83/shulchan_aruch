And there are those who say, etc. The Rashba permits, since he is not performing an uprooting and placement, for if he himself did this he would be exempt but it would be forbidden. With his donkey, however, it is permitted initially. The Maggid, chapter 20, resolves the words of the 'there are those who say,' namely the Rambam, that since he leads it, it is considered as though he performs an act with his hands. The Magen Avraham wrote that Tosafot in Pesachim 66b implies like the Rashba, until here. In my humble opinion, there is no implication, for there it does not discuss one who leads it. So too the Kol Bo there and Sefer HaZikaron of the Gaon Rabbi Yehudah HaKohen ruled like the Rambam. I later found in Sefer Avodas HaKodesh, page 12, that the Rashba himself wrote that it is proper to be concerned for the words of the Rambam.
Levush. Because of fear, etc. As below, because of fear of bandits, etc., or even within, etc., for there is no prohibition at all when he goes on foot. It implies that outside the techum it is better to ride, and so it implies in Rivash, siman 100, Tashbetz, and Piskei Maharai, and so the Beit Yosef wrote. However, he wrote the reason as being that the laws of techum do not apply above ten handbreadths, and when riding he is above ten. The Magen Avraham challenged this, that the laws of techum apply to riding, because its use is comfortable; see there. This is difficult, for it seems to me that there is another reason, namely that a person does not violate anything in such a case, as Tosafot and the Rosh wrote in the chapter Mi Shehotziuhu, and as the Magen Avraham himself wrote in siman 246, seif katan 12. At first glance, there is a difficulty against all the great geonim mentioned above from the Gemara in Beitzah 37a, that one may not ride, as a decree because of techum, with respect to the person himself. However, there is the proof brought by Hagahos Mordechai from the incident of Acher, and the Beit Yosef elaborated in resolving it, and so did the Magen Avraham; see there. I am surprised that he did not see in Midrash Koheles on the verse, better is the end of a matter, etc., the following language: He said to him, with all this wisdom, will you not repent? Thus it refers to his sins, for since he was so wise that he could measure the techum by the hoofbeats of his horse, he should have repented.
Levush. And to place, etc. For the saddle is subordinate to him. It implies that if he will not ride, such as where there is no danger, he must throw the saddle off, in accordance with the words of Hagahos Mordechai, and he should have explained this explicitly. His teacher the Rema also wrote this without qualification and followed the Rivash cited in the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman. But it seems that the Rivash also agrees that one must throw off the saddle. Melbushei Yom Tov. In Hagahos Mordechai the wording is as follows: because of the small loss of a saddle, we do not permit him to ride. This implies that if the saddle is worth a great deal, it is permitted. It seems to me that his reason is like the case of a pouch of money, where they permitted a D'Rabbanan prohibition, such as giving it to a non-Jew, because a person is panicked over his money. So too they permitted riding, which is a D'Rabbanan prohibition. The Magen Avraham left this requiring study for no reason; see there. One should not ask against Hagahos Mordechai why they permitted riding and did not permit placing it on the donkey in a manner that it would not perform uprooting and placement, as with a pouch, or taking it from the animal less than four amos at a time. That is more severe and worse, for it is close to leading to a Torah prohibition, since he will not be careful each time to take it. Even nowadays, when we do not have a public domain according to the Torah, nevertheless it seems to me that it is more severe than other D'Rabbanan prohibitions, for its root is from the Torah. Furthermore, many disagree and hold that even now there is a public domain; see siman 345, seif 7. With a pouch too, they permitted this only because the permission of riding does not apply, for specifically a saddle is subordinate to the rider, but not a pouch. If he has a pouch and must ride because of danger, its law is not explained in the Beit Yosef. It seems that he should place it on the animal and ride less than four amos at a time. If the danger is so great that he cannot delay so much, he must throw it away. Nowadays, when we do not have a public domain, this requires study. Perhaps one may permit in this case so that he not come to a Torah prohibition, such as digging and hiding it, because a person is panicked over his money, as the Bach wrote in siman 334; see there. This requires study. The Magen Avraham wrote: If the wagon is ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, without the wheels, it is a full private domain. Therefore, if it grew dark for him while on the wagon, he must place his objects on the wagon before he descends. With a wagon there is no prohibition of resting one's animal, since it is not an uprooting, for it rolls, as written in seif 2. But if it is standing and afterward begins moving, it is a Torah-level labor. If he is on the ground, it is forbidden to place anything on the wagon; rather, it should be given to a non-Jew, as above. Under the wagon is a public domain if it is open below as well. But if it is covered below, he is exempt, for it is not comparable to the flags in the wilderness, until here. In my humble opinion, even in the first case he should give it to a non-Jew, meaning that he should command the non-Jew to take it from the wagon, so that he not come to a Torah prohibition if it is standing and afterward begins moving. However, nowadays, when we do not have a public domain according to the Torah, this requires study.
Levush. Equal, etc. From this the Shiltei Gibborim ruled that one should give it to a minor.
While it is still day, etc. So wrote the Rema. The Magen Avraham wondered: the Ramban and Rashba prohibit in chapter 10, and regarding the words of the Rambam, the Maggid wrote that they are ambiguous. In my humble opinion, this is not so, for we find this explicitly in the Ramban and Rashba. Rather, one can say that they speak specifically when he gives it after nightfall, and this seems to be the view of the Rema. But the Rambam seemed to the Maggid to be speaking even from nightfall, for he previously stated the law that one should give it to a non-Jew, and there it speaks even from nightfall, as the Beit Yosef wrote at the beginning of this siman. Therefore the Maggid returned and wrote that the words of the Rambam are ambiguous. So it seems to me. Examine this carefully.
Levush. By himself, etc. Giving it to his fellow within less than four amos at a time is more preferable than walking by himself. Tosafot Yom Tov, chapter 10 of Eiruvin. See below, siman 301, seif 42, and siman 349. It is obvious that whether he does it himself or gives it to his fellow, this does not help from a private domain to a karmelis, and certainly not to a public domain, for the prohibition of transferring from one domain to another is forbidden even by half a cubit.
