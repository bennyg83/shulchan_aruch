(29) He runs beneath it. As long as he has not stopped to rest, there has been no act of lifting on Shabbos. This applies specifically when there is no non-Jew, animal, minor, deaf-mute, or mentally incompetent person with him, and when he cannot carry it in increments of less than four amos, such as when he fears that robbers will seize it from him, or there is a body of water four amos wide. See what I write nearby, that according to our circumstances, where we do not have a full public domain, it is possible that this is permitted in every case.
(30) For sometimes he stops. If he stopped in order to adjust the load, he is exempt, because this is not considered placing it down.
(31) A reminder. He changes his manner of walking, and through this he will remember Shabbos and will not stop.
(32) In an unusual manner. This means that he turns his back toward the courtyard and throws it from his shoulders. So it is stated in the Rokeach. Carrying in an unusual manner is not forbidden according to the Torah, and because of the loss of his bundle they permitted him to do this.
