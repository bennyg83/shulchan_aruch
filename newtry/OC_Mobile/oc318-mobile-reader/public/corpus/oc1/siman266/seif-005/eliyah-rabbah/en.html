And if he does not have with him, etc. Or if he does not trust the non-Jew. Sefer HaTerumah and Semag. In this way it seems to me to explain the Rambam, chapter 6, and that matter over which the early and later authorities struggled, especially the Shiltei Gibborim at the beginning of the chapter Mi Shehechshich. In my humble opinion, it seems to me that the meaning of the Gemara's statement, but not a found object, is that with a found object there is certainly no trust in a non-Jew, for he will rule permissively for himself to keep it. Therefore they permitted him to walk less than four amos at a time when he has a non-Jew with him, since he has no animal with him. This is when it has not yet come into the Jew's possession. For if it already came into the Jew's possession, it is like his pouch, and the non-Jew does not hold it. I found support for myself in Kol Bo, page 30; see there. I have been brief, since we rule in practice like Rashi's explanation, as the Levush wrote in the previous seif. See above, seif 7.
