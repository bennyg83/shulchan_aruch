4) There: He gives his wallet to a non-Jew, etc. If it is a heavy load, he should give the donkey to the non-Jew or declare it ownerless, as stated in siman 246, seif 3, provided he does not drive it. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. This is because one is liable for driving even another person's animal, as Magen Avraham writes in paragraph 5. Levush Serad. See below, paragraph 13. It appears that in this case he need not follow the procedure in seif 2 of removing the load from the donkey and replacing it, since it is a heavy load and doing so entails great exertion. He may rely upon declaring the animal ownerless and upon the non-Jew driving it. He should distance himself from the donkey so that he does not drive it. You can know this is so, because otherwise there would be no reason to declare it ownerless, since seif 2 does not require doing so. It necessarily follows that here he need not remove and replace the load. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. Regarding the law of declaring one's animal ownerless or giving it to a non-Jew as a gift, see siman 246, seif 3, and our words there. The same applies here.
