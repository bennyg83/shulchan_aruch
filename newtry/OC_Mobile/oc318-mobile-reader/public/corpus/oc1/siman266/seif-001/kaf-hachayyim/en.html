1) [Seif 1] And he has his donkey with him. If the donkey belongs to a non-Jew, however, he may place the wallet upon it, because he is not commanded to ensure that it rests. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Although Magen Avraham writes in paragraph 5 in the name of Ran that one is liable for driving even an animal that is not his, Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, writes that all the halachic authorities appear to dispute Ran and maintain that one is liable for driving only his own animal; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, writes that since a non-Jew accompanies him, the Jewish person is certainly driving his animal, which recognizes his voice, and Eliyah Rabbah's question is therefore not difficult; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2, writes the same. The later authorities also maintain that one is liable for driving another person's animal, as stated below in paragraph 13; see there.
