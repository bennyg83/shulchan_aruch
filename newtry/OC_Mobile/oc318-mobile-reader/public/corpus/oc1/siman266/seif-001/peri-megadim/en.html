"He gives." See Ateret Zekenim and Taz. Shabbos 153a, and see siman 334, seif 2, and Magen Avraham, letter 3. Nowadays, when according to many authorities we do not have a Torah-level public domain, there is additional room for leniency. The same is written here in letter 3. Regarding carrying less than 4 amot at a time, which is a rabbinic prohibition, one may reason similarly. Telling a non-Jew nowadays is a double rabbinic prohibition, and in a case of substantial monetary loss the Mechaber rules in siman 307, seif 5, according to the first anonymous opinion, that it is permitted. What the Shulchan Aruch writes, that it is permitted even if he does not pay the non-Jew, appears to refer to a case after nightfall. Before nightfall it is certainly preferable to fix a fee for him. Then the arrangement is permitted in the same way that Beit Hillel permit contracting with a non-Jew before Shabbos. See Rosh at the beginning of Perek Mi SheHechshich. I later saw that Eliyah Rabbah, letters 3 and 4, noticed this point. See Magen Avraham, siman 261, letter 6, and Responsa Maharshal, siman 46, that during bein hashmashot rabbinic prohibitions are permitted in a case of monetary loss. On Shabbos itself the matter requires study. Magen Avraham, siman 334, letter 3, prohibits in an ordinary case; see there. See also Levush in his gloss, Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 334, Magen Avraham there letters 2 and 17, siman 349, seif 5, siman 362, seif 5 and 17, and the corresponding discussions that will, with God's help, be explained there.
