And there are those who say, etc. The Rashba permits, since he is not performing an uprooting and placement, for if he himself did this he would be exempt but it would be forbidden. With his donkey, however, it is permitted initially. The Maggid, chapter 20, resolves the words of the 'there are those who say,' namely the Rambam, that since he leads it, it is considered as though he performs an act with his hands. The Magen Avraham wrote that Tosafot in Pesachim 66b implies like the Rashba, until here. In my humble opinion, there is no implication, for there it does not discuss one who leads it. So too the Kol Bo there and Sefer HaZikaron of the Gaon Rabbi Yehudah HaKohen ruled like the Rambam. I later found in Sefer Avodas HaKodesh, page 12, that the Rashba himself wrote that it is proper to be concerned for the words of the Rambam.
