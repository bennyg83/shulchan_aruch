Levush. Four amos in the public domain, etc. See what I wrote in siman 334, seif 2, that there are those who say that nowadays, when there is no public domain according to the Torah, it is forbidden. Magen Avraham. And so he wrote there in seif katan 3, and he inferred this from the Tur, that it is forbidden to carry when one fears robbers. In my humble opinion, this is not comparable. There it is one act of carrying, which is D'Rabbanan, and carrying in our public domains is a D'Rabbanan prohibition. But here there is more reason to permit telling a non-Jew to carry in a D'Rabbanan manner, for it is a shevus of a shevus beyond an ordinary shevus. That is why here he ruled simply to permit.
And if he does not have with him, etc. Or if he does not trust the non-Jew. Sefer HaTerumah and Semag. In this way it seems to me to explain the Rambam, chapter 6, and that matter over which the early and later authorities struggled, especially the Shiltei Gibborim at the beginning of the chapter Mi Shehechshich. In my humble opinion, it seems to me that the meaning of the Gemara's statement, but not a found object, is that with a found object there is certainly no trust in a non-Jew, for he will rule permissively for himself to keep it. Therefore they permitted him to walk less than four amos at a time when he has a non-Jew with him, since he has no animal with him. This is when it has not yet come into the Jew's possession. For if it already came into the Jew's possession, it is like his pouch, and the non-Jew does not hold it. I found support for myself in Kol Bo, page 30; see there. I have been brief, since we rule in practice like Rashi's explanation, as the Levush wrote in the previous seif. See above, seif 7.
Levush. He moved his feet. In a manuscript of the Shulchan Aruch it is written, hand and foot, and afterward, when it returns, etc., it wrote foot, and so Rashi wrote there. But here Rashi did not specify, only stated generally, and wrote, after it moved. This author wrote feet, which implies two, and I do not know where he got this from. Melbushei Yom Tov. I found this in Bartenura in the chapter Mi Shehechshich, and so it seems to me to be primary. When Rashi wrote foot, it seems to me that his intent is also this, as it is written, those who send forth the foot of the ox.
