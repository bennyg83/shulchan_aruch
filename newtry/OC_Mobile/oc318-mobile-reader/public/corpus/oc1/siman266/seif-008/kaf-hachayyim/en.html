8) There: Although he gave it to him after nightfall, etc. This is because a person cannot restrain himself when his money is at stake. If this were not permitted, he might carry it four amos in the public domain. See what is written in siman 334, seif 2, that some say this is forbidden nowadays because there is no public domain. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. This means that moving muktzeh on Shabbos was permitted because of the concern that a person cannot restrain himself when his money is at stake and might carry it four amos in the public domain. Nowadays, according to the opinion that there is no public domain, moving muktzeh would be forbidden, because even if he fails to restrain himself and carries four amos, he commits no Torah prohibition, since the area is not a complete public domain according to Torah law. The dispute whether there is a public domain nowadays is recorded in siman 345, seif 7, and according to the Shulchan Aruch's unattributed opinion, there is one even nowadays; see there. Regarding Magen Avraham's principal ruling, however, Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, writes that it is unlike siman 334, seif 2. There, moving the item is rabbinically forbidden and carrying through our contemporary public domain is also rabbinically forbidden. Here, by contrast, there is greater reason to permit telling a non-Jew to carry, which is a rabbinic prohibition compounded with another rabbinic prohibition. This is why the ruling here permits it without qualification. These are his words.
