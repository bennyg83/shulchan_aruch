Levush. And to place, etc. For the saddle is subordinate to him. It implies that if he will not ride, such as where there is no danger, he must throw the saddle off, in accordance with the words of Hagahos Mordechai, and he should have explained this explicitly. His teacher the Rema also wrote this without qualification and followed the Rivash cited in the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman. But it seems that the Rivash also agrees that one must throw off the saddle. Melbushei Yom Tov. In Hagahos Mordechai the wording is as follows: because of the small loss of a saddle, we do not permit him to ride. This implies that if the saddle is worth a great deal, it is permitted. It seems to me that his reason is like the case of a pouch of money, where they permitted a D'Rabbanan prohibition, such as giving it to a non-Jew, because a person is panicked over his money. So too they permitted riding, which is a D'Rabbanan prohibition. The Magen Avraham left this requiring study for no reason; see there. One should not ask against Hagahos Mordechai why they permitted riding and did not permit placing it on the donkey in a manner that it would not perform uprooting and placement, as with a pouch, or taking it from the animal less than four amos at a time. That is more severe and worse, for it is close to leading to a Torah prohibition, since he will not be careful each time to take it. Even nowadays, when we do not have a public domain according to the Torah, nevertheless it seems to me that it is more severe than other D'Rabbanan prohibitions, for its root is from the Torah. Furthermore, many disagree and hold that even now there is a public domain; see siman 345, seif 7. With a pouch too, they permitted this only because the permission of riding does not apply, for specifically a saddle is subordinate to the rider, but not a pouch. If he has a pouch and must ride because of danger, its law is not explained in the Beit Yosef. It seems that he should place it on the animal and ride less than four amos at a time. If the danger is so great that he cannot delay so much, he must throw it away. Nowadays, when we do not have a public domain, this requires study. Perhaps one may permit in this case so that he not come to a Torah prohibition, such as digging and hiding it, because a person is panicked over his money, as the Bach wrote in siman 334; see there. This requires study. The Magen Avraham wrote: If the wagon is ten handbreadths high and four handbreadths wide, without the wheels, it is a full private domain. Therefore, if it grew dark for him while on the wagon, he must place his objects on the wagon before he descends. With a wagon there is no prohibition of resting one's animal, since it is not an uprooting, for it rolls, as written in seif 2. But if it is standing and afterward begins moving, it is a Torah-level labor. If he is on the ground, it is forbidden to place anything on the wagon; rather, it should be given to a non-Jew, as above. Under the wagon is a public domain if it is open below as well. But if it is covered below, he is exempt, for it is not comparable to the flags in the wilderness, until here. In my humble opinion, even in the first case he should give it to a non-Jew, meaning that he should command the non-Jew to take it from the wagon, so that he not come to a Torah prohibition if it is standing and afterward begins moving. However, nowadays, when we do not have a public domain according to the Torah, this requires study.
