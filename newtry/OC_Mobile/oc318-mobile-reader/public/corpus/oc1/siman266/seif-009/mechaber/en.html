When he reaches the outer, guarded courtyard, he removes from the donkey the articles that may be handled; and those that may not be handled, he unties the ropes and the sacks fall of themselves. And if it was laden with glassware that is forbidden to handle — such as the cups of bloodletters, which are fit for nothing on Shabbos because they are repulsive, and which would break if they fell to the ground — he places pillows and cushions beneath them. And this is specifically with small loads, which he can slip out from beneath them; but if they are large, such that he cannot slip the pillows out from beneath them, it is forbidden to place them beneath, for he thereby renders the vessel unusable {i.e., [removes it] from the use for which it was ready}. Rather, he unloads them gently so that they will not break; and he may not leave [the load] upon the animal, because of tzaar baalei chaim [the suffering of a living creature].
