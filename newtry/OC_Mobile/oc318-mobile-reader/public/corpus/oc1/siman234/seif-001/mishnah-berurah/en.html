(1) Mincha gedolah — it is from six and a half hours [after dawn], and Mincha ketanah is from nine and a half and above; and one who wants to pray both of them — one as an obligation and one as nedavah — and as we hold in siman 107 that a person may pray as much as he wishes as nedavah, provided that he renew something in his prayer that it is for nedavah — see there.

(2) It is not fit, etc. — for although always one prays the obligation first and afterward the optional, here before nine and a half the main time of Minchah has not yet arrived, for its main time l'chatchila is from nine and a half and above, for that was the time of offering the tamid each day, as above in siman 233.

(3) And if he prays, etc. — for b'dieved he fulfills [his obligation] with Shemoneh Esreh in Mincha gedolah, as above in siman 233; and in the plain case [that he did not explicitly intend for nedavah] it also appears that it counts as an obligation, and he may pray the second as nedavah if he wishes.

(4) Unless he recognizes, etc. — and at the beginning of siman 107 it implies that we also require that he renew something therein; and it is possible that where he recognizes in himself that he can intend well and it is a good prayer, we do not require renewal; but from the language of the Tur it does not imply so.

(5) One should not say Ashrei, etc. — and if they said it without a minyan and afterward a minyan came, they should say one psalm and then say Kaddish; and the same applies when learning in the beit hamidrash without a minyan and afterward people are called in the beit hamidrash to complete the minyan — they do not act properly, for the essence of the reason for Kaddish on study is because the Name was sanctified among the many; and if so we require that there be ten in the beit hamidrash at the time of study; nevertheless it is possible to learn one mishnah and the like after ten people have gathered and say Kaddish.

(6) Parshat hatamid — and it is also good to say Pitum HaKetoret; but all this is if the time of prayer will not pass; but if there is concern that the time of prayer will pass, even Ashrei he should skip; and unfortunately there are people who fail in this.

(7) Before Ashrei — the Taz and Magen Avraham wrote to say it after Ashrei, but the custom is not so. The Tur wrote the order of Minchah prayer: one says Ashrei and Kaddish, and they pray Shemoneh Esreh, and the shaliach tzibbur repeats the prayer as at Shacharis, and they fall on their faces and say "and we do not know," etc., and they say the full Kaddish, etc.; and the poskim wrote: if they prayed Minchah close to dark and continued in the recitation of Avinu Malkenu or the rest of the supplications after prayer until it became dark, one should not say Kaddish titkabal, for the prayer was on another day; but if it happened that the prayer itself continued into the night [such as Ne'ilah prayer] they say Kaddish titkabal.
