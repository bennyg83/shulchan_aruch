One should not say Ashrei, etc. — Taz and Bach, note 3, elaborated; see Levush here, and siman 55 — it appears at first glance that he holds like Magen Avraham here; and see there in Peri Megadim siman 25 on this.

The Levush wrote that one should say Ashrei between prayer and prayer so that there be a distinction; but tashlumin does not apply here, it appears. The view of the Rivash agrees with Maharai: Maharai said that there is no obligation to complete Ashrei three times, for it has no tashlumin; and the Rivash said that it is fit to say it so that there be a distinction. And in explanation: Maharai concedes to the Rivash that one says Ashrei for distinction, but one does not say Kaddish upon it; for when one says it as an obligation, Ashrei three times each day — therefore they say Kaddish upon it — see there. And one who examines Piskei Maharai there — it implies that Ashrei is not said at all; and the Mechaber in seif 2 holds like the Rivash and says Ashrei and Kaddish, for on verses of Tanach one says Kaddish — siman 55; and Be'er HaGolah, note 2, cited the writings of Maharai — perhaps it is a scribal error, and as listed in siman 108, seif 2 — see there.
