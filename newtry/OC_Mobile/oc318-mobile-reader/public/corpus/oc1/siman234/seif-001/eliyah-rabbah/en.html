[1] Parshat hatamid before, etc. — and in the responsum of Menachem Azariah, siman 14, and the later authorities wrote to say it after Ashrei. He wrote in Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah: we are accustomed to say Pitum HaKetoret after parshat hatamid, and this is correct — until here; and so Menachem Azariah wrote there, and see above siman 51, note 7. He wrote in Mateh Moshe, siman 374 — this is his language: the Rashba — when he came to Minchah at the synagogue close to dark, he would pray Shemoneh Esreh and afterward say Tehillah leDavid in order to pray with the congregation — until here his language. It is difficult to me: why specifically "in order to pray with the congregation"? Let it suffice for him that the time of prayer not pass, as he himself wrote afterward in name of Sefer Chassidim: one who delayed praying Minchah should pray before Tehillah leDavid so that the hour not pass. And it is further difficult: he should have said that so wrote Sefer Chassidim, and not write the words of Sefer Chassidim as an independent matter as though it were a separate subject. And were I not afraid, I would say there is a scribal error in what he wrote "close to dark"; rather he intended to say: if he came at the hour when the congregation begins to pray, or very close thereto, even though the hour is not passing, he should not begin Ashrei but should pray with them, and afterward say Ashrei; and so it appears to me for halachah. Magen Avraham wrote — this is his language: he wrote in Piskei Maharai siman 59: even if part of the congregation prayed in its time and it continued until night, one should not say Kaddish, since the day renewed after they stopped praying — until here his language. And if so, the same law applies if they continued in the recitation of Avinu Malkenu and the like, that one should not say Kaddish titkabal; and one must say Kaddish before tzet hakochavim — until here. And one might say that he cited the language of Maharai in error, for he did not write at all "and it continued until night" — see there. Also it appears to me plainly that Maharai's intent is the opposite: specifically since they stopped praying before the day renewed; but if it continued until night they say Kaddish. And furthermore, the closing of Yom Kippur proves [this], before Ashrei.
