The passage of the tamid before, etc. Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 14, and the later authorities write to say it after Ashrei. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that the practice is to recite Pitum HaKetoret after the tamid passage, and that this is proper. Menachem Azaryah writes likewise. See above, siman 51 seif katan 7. Mateh Moshe, siman 374, writes that Maharam, when he came to the synagogue for Minchah near dark, would pray Shemoneh Esrei and afterward recite Tehillah LeDavid in order to pray with the congregation. I find difficulty: why mention praying with the congregation? In any case he should pray first so as not to miss the time, as Mateh Moshe himself immediately cites from Sefer Chasidim that one who delayed Minchah should pray before Tehillah LeDavid lest the time pass. It is also difficult that he should have cited Sefer Chasidim as support rather than present it as a separate matter. Were I not hesitant, I would say there is a scribal error in "near dark." Rather, the meaning is that if he arrived when the congregation was beginning Shemoneh Esrei, or very close to it, even though the prayer time was not about to expire, he should not begin Ashrei but should pray with them and say Ashrei afterward. This appears correct in practical law. Magen Avraham cites Piskei Mahari, siman 59, as saying that even if part of the congregation prayed at the proper time but their prayer extended into the night, one should not say Kaddish because a new day has begun after they ceased praying. He concludes that similarly if Avinu Malkeinu or the like extended late one should say Kaddish Titkabel before tzeit hakochavim. This is surprising, because he copied Mahari inaccurately; Mahari does not say that the prayer extended into the night. Moreover, Mahari's intent appears clearly opposite: only because they stopped praying before the new day began would Kaddish not continue afterward, but if the prayer itself extended into the night they do say Kaddish. Ne'ilah on Yom Kippur also proves this, even before Ashrei.
These laws were explained in siman 55 and siman 108; see there.
