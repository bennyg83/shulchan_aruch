A fast for a dream. It appears to me that his intent is that he should fast until after Minchah and afterward eat. See what I wrote in siman 604 and siman 552, seif 11. In Sefer Tanya it is written that one who swore to fast on Purim should keep his oath and make the meal at night. Although the choicest way to perform the mitzvah is by day, he should do what he can. See the beginning of siman 553, for we do not rule this way.
And most of the meal, and so on. The reason the custom is not to make it in the morning is that people are occupied with mishloach manos, as we find at the end of chapter 5 of Chullin, that they would not increase the meal on the first day of Sukkos because they were occupied with sukkah and lulav, as Terumas HaDeshen writes.
Seeds on the first night. There are those who do not eat meat at night, so that they should not mistakenly say that this is the Purim meal, as the Kol Bo writes.
That if he caused damage. The Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that this applies specifically when he damaged the other's property, but if he damaged his body, he is liable. This is the end of his words. But the Agudah in Sukkah wrote that when the young men play on Shabbos and strike one another, they are exempt, provided that they did not intend to cause damage.
Because of the joy of Purim. This means that he did it because of joy. But if he intended to cause damage, he is liable; see Choshen Mishpat there.
