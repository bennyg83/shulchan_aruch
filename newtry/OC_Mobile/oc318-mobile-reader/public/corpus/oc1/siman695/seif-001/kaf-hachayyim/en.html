1) [Seif 1] Gloss: It is a mitzvah to increase the Purim feast. For the verse states, ‘feasting and joy.’ Levush.
2) There, in the gloss: It is a mitzvah to increase, etc. In the first chapter of Megillah, 7b, Rav Ashi was sitting before Rav Kahana. It grew late and the Rabbis had not arrived. He asked him, ‘Why have the Rabbis not arrived?’ He answered, ‘Perhaps they are occupied with the Purim feast.’ End quote. We learn from this that it is permitted to increase the Purim feast and not come to the beis midrash because of preoccupation with the meal; since one is engaged in the mitzvah of increasing the Purim feast, he is exempt from the mitzvah of Torah study in the beis midrash. Bach.
3) There, in the gloss: It is a mitzvah to increase, etc. If one held the Purim feast without bread, he fulfilled his obligation. Echad Kadosh, in a manuscript responsum; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah. See below, paragraph 32.
4) There, in the gloss: One fulfills the obligation with one meal. This excludes the view that one is also obligated to eat at night, analogous to the Megillah. The Bach wrote that nevertheless one should eat at night, even when Purim falls on Motzei Shabbos, as written below in the gloss to seif 1 and in Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. That is to say, even when the fourteenth falls on Motzei Shabbos and one held an important third Shabbos meal, he must nevertheless add somewhat to the evening meal in honor of Purim. It is not, however, a Purim feast like the meal on the following day, when one must increase more. Mishna Berurah, paragraph 3. Nevertheless, a wise person looks ahead and should not eat excessively at the third Shabbos meal, but should leave room for the evening meal. So also wrote Machatzis HaShekel.
