(א) Meat. Cooked fit for eating — Maharil; the same for drink — Shelah. One who sends money, clothing and vessels for portions — if he can sell them immediately to buy for the meal need — Leket Yosher ch. 2 siman 163. From words of Baal Turei Even siman 111 it implies somewhat he does not fulfill — see there Yad Aharon. I was in doubt one who sent his friend for portions a treif chicken and he did not know and they ate it, and later on Purim day itself it became known it was treif — whether he fulfilled mishloach manot obligation or not — see in Sefer Motzal MeEsh siman 61. I was further in doubt one who sent his friend abroad two portions, or two gifts to two poor people before Purim, and on Purim itself they certainly reach him — whether he fulfills or not: do we say since when he sent he was not yet obligated he does not fulfill, or perhaps since on Purim itself which is time of obligation they reached his hand he fulfills? See Yad Aharon whose view leans he fulfills properly in this — see there; one who sent his friend fish and egg on it or bread with face smeared with egg — doubt whether he fulfills mishloach manot — Edut B'Yaakov siman 92 see there; see Kanfei Nesharim.

(ג) A woman. I did not see women careful about this; perhaps specifically widows, but a woman who has a husband — her husband sends on her behalf to several people; nevertheless one should be stringent — Magen Avraham. It appears to me one who eats at his friend's table and prepared nothing for him is exempt from mishloach manot, and so somewhat in Rashi regarding they exchange their meals reciprocally; see Taz s.k. 3; nevertheless requires study and good to be stringent — Magen Avraham. He wrote in Sedei Chemed: one should gather all household members and friends, for if a person sits alone he cannot rejoice properly — so too Rashi on verse mishpacha u'mishpacha.
