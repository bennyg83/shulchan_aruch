(18) To send. In Responsa Binyan Tziyon, siman 44, he was uncertain whether, if one brought the portions himself and not through an agent, he fulfills the obligation. Since the verse says “sending,” perhaps we require specifically through agency; see there.
(19) Two gifts. Regarding the poor, they were particular to give to two poor people, for the mitzvah is to divide it. This is unlike a wealthy person, where it is enough when he gives to one person.
(20) Kinds of foods. But not garments or other things. The same applies to drink, which is acceptable, for drinking is included in eating. It is likewise sufficient to send one food and one drink. We require a type of food that is cooked, not raw meat, for mishloach manos implies portions fit to eat immediately. Some say that since it has been slaughtered and is ready to be cooked immediately, it is permitted.
(21) This one sends to that one, and so on. He fulfills his obligation even though afterward he goes to dine with him.
(22) To send portions by day. The same applies to gifts to the poor, unless he already fulfilled the mitzvah of sending portions to one person and gifts to two poor people by day; then it makes no difference if he wants to add more at night.
(23) Or he waives it for him. This means that he says, “I regard myself as though I had received it.”
(24) He has fulfilled. The Pri Chadash disagrees with this, and the Chasam Sofer in siman 196 likewise expresses wonder about it.
(25) And a woman is obligated, and so on. For they were all included in that miracle, and she must rejoice and gladden the hearts of the poor. It is written, “the Jews established and accepted,” and so on, and women are included as well. The Magen Avraham wrote: I have not seen people being careful about this, and perhaps it applies specifically to a widow. But a married woman, whose husband sends on her behalf to several people, may be different. Nevertheless, one should be stringent.
(26) A doubt of kiddushin. For people may say that these are gifts sent after kiddushin, and we are concerned lest he already betrothed her before witnesses who are not now before us.
(27) There is no concern. For this is with money, and there is no concern for gifts after kiddushin. Even if he gives the poor person food, it is in the manner of tzedakah and not in the manner of gifts after kiddushin.
