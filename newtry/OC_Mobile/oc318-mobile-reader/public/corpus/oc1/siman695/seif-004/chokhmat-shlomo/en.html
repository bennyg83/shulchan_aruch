Note: I was uncertain regarding mishloach manos if one sends to a sick person a prohibited food that is permitted for that sick person to eat, such as ordinary gentile wine for a sick person who is not in danger, or another Rabbinic prohibition, or a Torah prohibition for a dangerously ill person. Does he fulfill mishloach manos with this? Do we follow the recipient, for whom it is fit to eat, or do we follow the giver, for whom it is not fit? This can be resolved from the verse itself, from its wording “and sending portions, each man to his fellow.” The word “man” is extra, for it would have been enough to say, “and sending portions to his fellow,” as it says in Ezra, “and send portions to one who has nothing prepared for him.” Since it says “each man to his fellow,” the intent must be that they should be portions of a man, fit for him, which he sends to his fellow. But something not fit for him, even if fit for his fellow, does not fulfill this. Yet there is still room to be uncertain. If both of them are sick and permitted to eat prohibited foods, and the sender has one permitted portion and one prohibited portion, while his friend has nothing, and if he sends the permitted food to his friend then he himself will need to eat the prohibited food, what should he do? Generally we certainly say that your own life takes precedence, and he should eat the permitted food while giving the prohibited food to his friend. My question is regarding mishloach manos on Purim: since I concluded that one who sends portions must send something fit for himself, perhaps if he eats the permitted food, the prohibited food is considered unfit for him, since he has permitted food, and he will not fulfill mishloach manos. Since in the end the prohibited food will be eaten, perhaps it is better that he eat the prohibited food himself and send the permitted food to his friend, thereby fulfilling mishloach manos. Or perhaps we still say that in any event your own life takes precedence, and he should eat the permitted food even though he will not fulfill mishloach manos. A case like this requires great analysis; examine it carefully.
