There, seif 4. "One is obligated to send his fellow two portions, etc." NB: I was uncertain regarding mishloach manot: if one sends to a sick person an issur that is permitted for him to eat, such as ordinary non-Jewish wine for a sick person who is not in danger, or another rabbinic issur, or a Torah issur for a dangerously ill person, does he fulfill the obligation of mishloach manot with this? Do we follow the recipient, for whom it is fit to eat, or do we follow the sender, for whom it is not fit? This can be resolved from the verse itself, which says, "and sending portions, a man to his fellow." The word "man" is extra; it would have sufficed to say, "and sending portions to his fellow," as it says in Ezra, "and send portions to one for whom nothing is prepared." Since it says "a man to his fellow," the intent must be that they be portions of the man, fit for him, which he sends to his fellow. But something not fit for him does not discharge the obligation even for his fellow. Yet this may be questioned: if both are sick and permitted to eat forbidden foods, and the sender has one permitted portion and one forbidden portion, while his fellow has nothing, and if he sends the permitted item to his fellow he himself will need to eat the forbidden one, what should he do? Generally we certainly say that one's own life comes first; he should eat the permitted food and give the forbidden food to his fellow. My question is about mishloach manot on Purim. Since I concluded that mishloach manot must be something fit for the sender, perhaps if he eats the permitted food, then the forbidden food is not fit for him, because he has permitted food, and he will not fulfill mishloach manot. Since in the end the forbidden food will be eaten, perhaps it is better that he eat the forbidden food and send the permitted food to his fellow, thereby fulfilling mishloach manot. Or perhaps in any event one's own life comes first, and he should eat the permitted food even though he will not fulfill mishloach manot. A case like this requires Rabbah; it requires study. Examine very carefully.
