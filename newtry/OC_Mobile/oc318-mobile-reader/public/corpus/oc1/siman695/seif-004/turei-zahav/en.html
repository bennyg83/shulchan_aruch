He exchanges with his fellow. In the Gemara they said: Abaye bar Avin and Rabbi Chanina bar Avin would exchange their meals with one another. The Ran explained that neither one had enough to send and leave for himself; therefore each would send his meal to the other, so that they could eat the Purim meal and fulfill the mitzvah of mishloach manos. This is the approach written here. Rashi explained that they exchanged their meals: this one would eat with that one on Purim of this year, and in the second year his fellow would dine with him. The Beis Yosef challenged Rashi's explanation: if so, they were not fulfilling “mishloach manos, each man to his fellow.” And if you say that they also sent portions to one another, what is this teaching us? This is the end of his words. It appears to my humble opinion that according to Rashi, the novelty is that they said explicitly that this one would eat with that one, and afterward the reverse. One might have thought that this is like a loan, and there are no portions here, since it must be specifically a gift. Therefore it teaches that nevertheless they fulfilled the mitzvah. That is, in this year, the one who invited his fellow fulfilled the mitzvah, and his fellow sent to another person; and in the second year it was reversed. But one must still examine why Rashi did not truly explain it all in one year, with both of them fulfilling the mitzvah. Perhaps one can say that in that case it would indeed be an actual loan, since it would be evident to all that he immediately receives payment, and there was a condition between them. This is unlike when it is in two different years, for then it does not appear like payment, neither in this year nor in the other. We find a similar example in Yoreh De'ah, siman 170, seif 12 in the gloss: A person should not say to his fellow, “Come and eat with me what you fed me,” for it is like repaying his debt and appears as if he had lent him, and there is concern that he will feed him more, which involves ribbis. But it is permitted to say, “Come and eat with me, and I will eat with you another time,” and it is permitted to eat with him even a larger meal. This is the end of his words. We see that at the time of the second eating it is forbidden to mention that it is payment. So too here, for then it would be a loan. So it appears to my humble opinion, and it is correct.
