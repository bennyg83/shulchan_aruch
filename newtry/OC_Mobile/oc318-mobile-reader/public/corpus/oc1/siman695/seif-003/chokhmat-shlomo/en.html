(there seif 4) he must send his friend two portions etc. NB I was in doubt regarding mishloach manot: if he sends a sick person something forbidden that is permitted for him to eat, such as stam yayinam for a patient without danger, or another d'rabbanan prohibition, or a Torah prohibition for a dangerously ill patient — whether he fulfills mishloach manot if we follow the recipient and behold it is fit for eating, or we follow the giver and for him it is not fit. This can be resolved from the verse itself from what is written "and sending of portions ish lere'ehu" — the word ish is extra and it would suffice "and sending portions lere'ehu", and as Ezra wrote "and they sent portions to one not his friend"; since it says ish lere'ehu necessarily intent is portions ish fit for this one he sends to his friend, but what is not fit for him also for his friend he does not fulfill. Yet one could doubt: if both are sick and permitted to eat forbidden things and the sender has one permitted portion and one forbidden and his friend has nothing — if he sends the permitted to his friend he must eat the forbidden — what shall he do? In general certainly we say your life takes precedence and he eats the permitted and gives his friend the forbidden, for my question is in mishloach manot on Purim since I raised that mishloach manot sender must send what is fit for him — do we say if he eats the permitted then the forbidden is not fit for him since he has permitted, then he does not fulfill mishloach manot, and since in the end the forbidden will be eaten, better he eat the forbidden and send the permitted to his friend and fulfill mishloach manot; or do we say in any case your life takes precedence and he eats the permitted even though he does not fulfill mishloach manot — like this case Rava needs, requires study, examine well.
