He says Al HaNissim. If he forgot Al HaNissim in Birkas HaMazon, they make him repeat, for it is impossible that he not eat, as the Shelah and the Maharshal wrote in the name of Rabbi Yaakov Pollak and Mateh Moshe. Nevertheless, their words require analysis. Is it better than davening, which is obligatory, and even so they do not make him repeat? All the more so in Birkas HaMazon. Therefore it appears to me that if he already ate one meal during the day, they no longer make him repeat, for if he wished he would not eat any more, as the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 188 in the name of Tosafos; see there. Although the Maharshal did not write this, it appears to me that what I wrote is primary according to halachah. For by strict law it appears to me that they never make him repeat, since we have not found that one is obligated to eat bread on Purim, and he can discharge himself with other kinds of delicacies. Therefore in this case, where he already ate, it is obvious that one should not make him repeat and thereby enter a concern of a blessing in vain. I found written in the Mordechai on parchment: if one was eating the Purim meal on Erev Shabbos, he should daven, spread a cloth, make Kiddush, and in Birkas HaMazon say Al HaNissim. This is the end of his words. In Mateh Moshe and Minhagim they wrote that this is because he had not yet davened Maariv, which implies that if he already davened, he no longer says Al HaNissim. So too wrote the Shelah and the Maharil in responsum, siman 56. Therefore it appears to me that he should first recite Birkas HaMazon and afterward daven Maariv, in order to remove himself from dispute, and also so that he not become intoxicated and fail to recite Birkas HaMazon.
And the custom is, and so on. It appears to me that in this case, if he did not say it, they do not make him repeat.
