(1) They delay, etc. Although strictly speaking it is sufficient to refrain from melacha until the time explained in seif 2, ideally it is proper and correct, and this is the custom of all Israel, to delay the prayer so as to add more weekday time to the sacred. The custom is to chant before Maariv the psalm "May God favor us," Tehillim 67, and additional psalms listed in the siddurim. When Tishah B'Av falls on Motzei Shabbos, these psalms are not chanted, because that is not a time of supplication. Likewise, when Yom Tov falls on Motzei Shabbos, the custom is not to chant them. Peri Megadim writes that this may be because supplications are not recited on Shabbos or Yom Tov, whereas on an ordinary Shabbos one may recite the psalms after nightfall and before Maariv. See above, siman 49, in the name of Chavos Yair, that a person may recite by heart Tehillim with which he is familiar. How good and beautiful is this custom: first, it escorts Shabbos, which is called the bride and queen, with songs and praises, as stated in the Midrash; second, it delays Maariv and thereby adds from the weekday to the sacred; and third, it prevents people from afterward speaking idle matters. I later found in Knesses HaGedolah, siman 235, an idea similar to what I wrote.
