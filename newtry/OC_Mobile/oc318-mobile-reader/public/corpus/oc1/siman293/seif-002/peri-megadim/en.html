They delay. They delay Maariv beyond the ordinary measure, as Bach writes, so that Shabbos should not appear burdensome. Regarding whether one may ask a person who has already recited Havdalah to perform labor for him, see the discussion concerning the chapter “May God favor us,” Tehillim 67, and the relevant rulings in siman 293. One who has personally not yet ended Shabbos remains bound by its restrictions, but there is room to permit asking another Jew who has already ended Shabbos, since the prohibition now rests only upon the first person’s own acceptance. Nevertheless, one should not arrange the matter in a way that displays eagerness to be rid of Shabbos. The formulas “May the Merciful One grant us an inheritance that is entirely Shabbos” and similar expressions are prayers, not a continuation of the sanctity that prevents Havdalah. The practical ruling depends on whether the congregation has already recited Maariv and Havdalah and whether the individual has said the required declaration ending Shabbos. See siman 7.
