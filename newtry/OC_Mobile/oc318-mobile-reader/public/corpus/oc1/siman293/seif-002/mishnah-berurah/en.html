2) From performing labor. See below in siman 299, seif 10.
3) Until they see. Before then it is prohibited because the day may not yet have ended. Strictly speaking, three medium-sized stars suffice, but we are not expert in identifying them and may mistake the large stars visible by day for medium-sized stars. We therefore require small stars.
4) Three small stars. See Peri Chadash’s glosses on Yoreh Deah, who writes that if the sky still shines with a light resembling daylight, that time is counted only as bein hashmashos. Rabbi Akiva Eiger cites him in his novellae to Yoreh Deah, siman 262; see there. In his glosses there, the Vilna Gaon writes that if the reddish glow has disappeared from the entire section of sky on the side where the sun set, we are not concerned with the remaining brightness. Know further that Tiferes Yisrael, in the second chapter of Shabbos, requires not only three small stars but also three medium-sized stars. I do not know his source. Perhaps his reason is that we may err in assessing the stars and they may actually be large; we therefore require additional stars larger than those, showing that the first are at least not large. According to Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 235, in the name of Mateh Yehudah and Birkei Yosef, the words of Tiferes Yisrael are readily understood. Other Acharonim, however, do not mention this. If the sky in other places is cloudy and no additional stars can be seen, it is possible that all agree one may be lenient. It appears to me that according to the Vilna Gaon’s requirement that the reddish glow disappear from the entire section of sky where the sun set, after which three stars are effective, three small stars alone are then sufficient.
5) But adjacent to one another. This requirement is because of the mitzvah to add to Shabbos. The same applies if one sees three small stars that are not adjacent and then waits a little longer: it is permitted to perform labor, because the reason for requiring adjacent stars is to ensure the added time, and by waiting he has added that time. Degul Merevavah.
6) Until it ends. Shulchan Aruch does not mention waiting the time required to walk four mil from the beginning of sunset, in accordance with its ruling above in siman 261, seif 2. It is possible that this case concerns one who, because of cloud cover, also does not know the time of sunset.
7) From his heart. If he has an accurate clock and knows with certainty that at this time on the previous day it was halachic night (that is, three small stars were visible) it appears that he may rely on it. Birkei Yosef likewise writes that one may rely on a clock.
