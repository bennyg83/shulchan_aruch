1) [Seif 1] For Minchah, one says Ashrei, etc. In order to make an interruption between the Mussaf prayer and the Minchah prayer. Beit Yosef. Regarding the reason it is said only before Ne'ilah, see below, sec. 3.
2) Ibid. Ve'Ani Sefilasi is not said, etc. The reason is that on Shabbos it is said in praise of Israel, that even though they ate and drank, they pray, unlike the nations of the world. On Yom Kippur this is unnecessary. Tur; Taz, s.k. 1. According to the plain meaning of the words of the holy Zohar in Idra Rabbah, that it was instituted to be said at Shabbos Minchah because it is a time of favor, this applies even more strongly on Yom Kippur. So too is written in the work Imrei Noam, in the pamphlet Sefas Emes, 102c, see there. So it appears to me to be the primary view. Ruach Chaim, sec. 1. However, he concluded there in the pamphlet Sefas Emes, "One who chooses may choose," see there. The custom of the pious members of the Beit El congregation, may its Rock and Redeemer protect it, who follow the teachings of the Arizal, is not to say it, because the order of the kabbalistic intentions of the Yom Kippur prayers is not the same as on Shabbos, whether Yom Kippur falls on a weekday or on Shabbos, as is known. See above siman 292, sec. 7.
3) Ibid., gloss. Our custom is not to say Ashrei, etc. This is in order to hasten, so that the time for the Minchah prayer will not pass. It is sufficient that the Torah reading interrupts between the Mussaf prayer and the Minchah prayer, just as there is an interruption between Yotzer and Mussaf. Ashrei and U'Va L'Tzion are left until after Minchah, so that they will form an interruption between the Minchah prayer and the Ne'ilah prayer. Beit Yosef; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 2; Mishna Berurah, sec. 3.
4) The custom of Sephardic communities is to recite the passage of the Akeidah through "and I will remember the land." Afterward, they say the psalm, "For the conductor, upon the Gittis," etc., the passage of the daily offering, Pittum HaKetores, Ashrei, U'Va L'Tzion, and Kaddish. They then take out the Sefer Torah, say Kaddish, and pray Minchah. This is also the custom of the pious members of the Beit El congregation, may its Rock and Redeemer protect it.
