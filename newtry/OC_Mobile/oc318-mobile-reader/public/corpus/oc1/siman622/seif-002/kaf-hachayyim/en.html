2) Ibid. Ve'Ani Sefilasi is not said, etc. The reason is that on Shabbos it is said in praise of Israel, that even though they ate and drank, they pray, unlike the nations of the world. On Yom Kippur this is unnecessary. Tur; Taz, s.k. 1. According to the plain meaning of the words of the holy Zohar in Idra Rabbah, that it was instituted to be said at Shabbos Minchah because it is a time of favor, this applies even more strongly on Yom Kippur. So too is written in the work Imrei Noam, in the pamphlet Sefas Emes, 102c, see there. So it appears to me to be the primary view. Ruach Chaim, sec. 1. However, he concluded there in the pamphlet Sefas Emes, "One who chooses may choose," see there. The custom of the pious members of the Beit El congregation, may its Rock and Redeemer protect it, who follow the teachings of the Arizal, is not to say it, because the order of the kabbalistic intentions of the Yom Kippur prayers is not the same as on Shabbos, whether Yom Kippur falls on a weekday or on Shabbos, as is known.
