In the section of forbidden relations. Tosafos wrote the reason as a sign that Hashem should not uncover the nakedness of our sins. This does not seem to me; rather, since a person's soul desires forbidden relations more than all other transgressions, therefore we warn about this on Yom Kippur, which is an awesome day and is engraved in a person's heart more than the rest of the days of the year.
And he concludes with the blessing of Shabbos. This is not comparable to siman 284, regarding Rosh Chodesh on Shabbos, where we do not mention Rosh Chodesh in the haftarah because there is no reading from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh, and here too there is no haftarah at Minchah on Shabbos. For here, in any case, there is a haftarah now on this Shabbos, since the Sages instituted reading the haftarah at Minchah of Yom Kippur when it falls during the week. Therefore he should also mention Shabbos when it falls on Shabbos.
And one does not say 'Al HaTorah.' Meaning: our custom is not as the Shulchan Aruch wrote; rather, we do not say at all 'Al HaTorah' and 'Al HaAvodah,' because the service of the day has already passed. In the text of Avinu Malkeinu, 'Tear up the evil of the decree of our judgment,' it seems to me that one should say 'the evil of the decree' in one breath, because they belong together: that He, may He be blessed, should tear up the evil that is in the decree, and what remains in the decree should be for mercy. The decree of Hashem can be turned by those who receive it into mercy, as written in chapter HaNechenakin regarding the verse, 'Nineveh shall be overturned,' which can be interpreted for good. Likewise, 'Repentance, etc., remove the evil of the decree' is also in this manner. This is unlike the many people who pause in the tune between 'evil' and 'decree.' This is incorrect, because then the word 'decree' would be an expression of command or request, like 'zevach,' which means 'you shall slaughter,' or 'choose men for us,' which means 'you shall choose.' Here too it would be interpreted as 'decree our judgment,' meaning that we want You to decree our judgment, Heaven forbid. Therefore one must protest this, especially against the cantors, that they should not do so.
