Se'if 2 And three read in the section of forbidden relations until the end of the section, etc. Note: See the Taz, who wrote in the name of Tosafos the reason that the Holy One, blessed be He, should not uncover the nakedness of our fathers. Divine inspiration spoke through him, for I found this in Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 57, and this is its language: 'And all those are still Samael, the serpent, that we cause to make judgments, and they uncover it before Hashem; and things like this are called nakedness.' See there. This is explicit like their words, that uncovering nakedness refers to sins.
(There, in the same se'if) And the third reads the haftarah in Yonah, etc. Note: The reason for this haftarah is discussed by the later authorities. In my opinion there is another reason, according to what the authorities wrote: we read the section of Acharei on Yom Kippur in order to mention the death of the righteous, for the Holy One, blessed be He, says that they should mention before Him the death of the righteous and He will atone for their sins. If so, it seems that also in the haftarah it is proper to mention this. This is because it is written there: 'The men said to Yonah, Tell us, please, on whose account this evil is upon us.' One must understand: since they had already cast lots and the lot had fallen on Yonah, what further question is this, 'on whose account is this evil upon us'? It appears that the intent is as follows. The matter of the lot was only so that we would know who was designated by Heaven to be drowned, and whom it was necessary to cast into the sea; in this the lot fell on Yonah, for the decree was upon him to cast him into the sea. But they still did not know why Yonah had become liable to fall into the sea: whether it was for his own sin, or because of the sin of other people, in order to atone for them. Therefore they asked him, 'on whose account is this evil' - meaning, this evil, that they should cast him into the sea, on whose account is it? Is it because of his own sin, that he himself has a sin, or does he have no sin in his hand, but he bears their sins? They asked whether he sensed in himself any sin; if so, it was his own, and if not, it was because of their sins. If so, it is proven from this that the death of the righteous, and their suffering, atones for others. Therefore we read this on Yom Kippur. Similarly, in the first haftarah, that which is written, 'I will repay comforts to him and to his mourners,' refers to the mourners who mourn over the death of the righteous person, who will also receive reward. This too alludes to the death of the righteous. So it seems regarding the reason for these haftaros. Examine this well.
