Seif 2. "And three are called to the section of forbidden relations until the end of the parashah, etc." NB: See what the Taz wrote in the name of Tosafot, that the reason is that the Holy One, blessed be He, should not reveal the nakedness of our fathers. Ruach ha-kodesh spoke through him, for I found this in Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 57, which states: "And all those are still the Samekh-Mem, the serpent, who cause judgments to be made and reveal them before God; and things like this are called arayot." See there. It is explicit like their words, that the revelation of arayot refers to sins.
There, in the same seif. "And the third reads the haftarah in Yonah, etc." NB: See the later authorities for the reason for this haftarah. In my opinion there is another reason, according to what the poskim wrote, that we read Parashat Acharei on Yom Kippur in order to mention the death of the righteous, for the Holy One, blessed be He, says that they should mention before Him the death of the righteous and He will atone for their sins. If so, it appears that in the haftarah too it is fitting to mention this. It is because it says there, "The men said to Yonah: Tell us, because of whom has this evil come upon us?" One should understand: since they had already cast lots and the lot fell upon Yonah, what was this further question, "because of whom has this evil come upon us"? Rather, the intent appears to be that the lot only tells us who is fit from Heaven to be drowned and whom it is necessary to cast into the sea; in this the lot fell upon Yonah, that the decree was upon him to cast him into the sea. But they did not know why Yonah had become liable to be cast into the sea: whether because of his own sin, or because of other people, in order to atone for them. Therefore they asked him, "because of whom is this evil," meaning this evil, that they will cast him into the sea, because of whom is it? Is it because of his own sin, that he himself has a sin, or is he without sin and bearing their sins? They asked whether he sensed any sin in himself; if so, it was his own, and if not, it was because of their sins. This proves that the death and sufferings of the righteous atone for others, and therefore we read this on Yom Kippur. So too in the first haftarah, what is written, "I will complete consolations for him and for his mourners," means that those who mourn the death of the righteous also receive reward; the death of the righteous is also alluded to there. This appears to be the reason for these haftarot. Examine very carefully.
