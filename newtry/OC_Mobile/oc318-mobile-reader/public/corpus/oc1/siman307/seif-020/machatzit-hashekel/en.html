(s.k. 20) That he not touch, etc. — Agudah wrote: I practiced, etc., for we rule an object brought from outside techum is forbidden to one for whom it was brought; if so here with a letter sent to him forbidden to read — that is his benefit forbidden; unless bodily need — permitted; he was unsure to read himself lest no bodily need — therefore told another to read; another may read even without bodily need since not brought for him and he does not benefit from reading — not relevant to him; when the other saw bodily need he would tell the recipient what is written; if no need he would be silent — for this reason Rabbenu Yehudah wrote for forbidders reading is forbidden as above; nevertheless they did not forbid touching, for we rule food brought from outside techum permitted to move since permitted to eat for one for whom it was not brought; since fit for anyone, permitted to move; unlike a letter not fit for anyone — recipient forbidden to read and others have no benefit and ultimate purpose is reading — therefore moving also forbidden — see there.

And now I came, etc. — we fear lest, etc. — in transferring domain on Rosh HaShanah or carrying reshut ha-rabbim to reshut ha-yachid: if he does lifting and placing — Torah prohibition; lifting or placing alone — rabbinic half-melacha; standing to rest is placing; standing to adjust load on shoulder is not placing as 266 s.k. 8; when agent brought letter into reshut ha-rabbim forbidden for Israelite to take from agent before standing to rest — for then Israelite does placing; forbidders feared Israelite might take before agent rests; permitters did not fear; see below 347.

For there is no decree, etc. — and "for you and he brings," etc. — meaning Rashi's reason for gentile bringing gift to Israelite forbidden: lest Israelite say bring — all the more here where he does not know who will send him a letter — fits Rashi's reason; but per Rashi's view that what is brought from outside techum is forbidden until "so that they do" is lest he benefit from Shabbat or Yom Tov melacha as explained 315 — must hold essence like Rashi; and for us it appears 315 we hold essence like Rashi; therefore per Rashi nevertheless permitted here; and as Derishah wrote: reading a letter is not full benefit — not evident at time of reading since he does not yet know if there will be need — see there.

And also no muktzeh, etc. — for he did not designate in his mind.

And perhaps forbidders hold, etc. — meaning: for an object in the owner's mind it is not muktzeh for the owner — not muktzeh for anyone, as 308 below; but here from the hour the sender sent he designated it; recipient did not know to say it was in his mind — therefore forbidden; so Magen Avraham below 315: if letter is from gentile — gentile is owner and gentile's objects need not be so designated — perhaps they concede permitted; so below 315.

Even R' Shimon concedes, as 398 seif 3 — per Maharshal there.

If he is particular, etc. — meaning particular lest it get dirty, etc.; but if particular to form on it because secrets are written — permitted to move, for he can still form and leave vessel and letter in concealed place — see there.
