(s.k. 13) Because, etc. — requires study: in ch. 3 of Eruvin 39a they said explicitly the same within techum.

"Not evident," etc. — for they said there R' Yosi: when erev Pesach occurs on erev Shabbat, he joins on Yom Tov for Shabbat, etc. — until here; and Rashi's reason for prohibition: whatever forbidden to do on Shabbat forbidden to invite oneself; and Tosafot challenged: also here waiting and detaching fruit within techum should be forbidden for that reason; Tosafot answered: since not evident, etc., the sages were not strict; but to wait at end of techum even if not evident — forbidden, not because of suspicion but "pursuing your affairs" — forbidden to invite oneself; and although within techum when not evident they did not forbid inviting — nevertheless end of techum they were stricter; the distinction within techum between evident and not — for Rashi's reason must be: when not evident it is only like thought which is permitted; when evident it is like speech which is forbidden.

And the reason forbidden, etc. — it is a second challenge on the Maggid Mishneh who wrote the reason for prohibition when the matter is evident — because of suspicion; and Rashi wrote the reason from the law: whatever is forbidden to do, etc., included in "pursuing your affairs"; and Tosafot challenged: also here waiting and detaching fruit within the techum should be forbidden for that reason; Tosafot answered: since it is not evident, etc., the sages were not strict.

But to wait at dusk at the end of the techum — even though the matter is not evident, as above — nevertheless forbidden, not because of suspicion but "pursuing your affairs" — forbidden to invite oneself; and although within the techum when not evident they did not forbid inviting — nevertheless at the end of the techum they were stricter; the distinction within the techum between evident and not — for Rashi's reason must be: when not evident it is only like thought which is permitted; when evident it is like speech which is forbidden.
