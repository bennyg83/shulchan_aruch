His words are based upon Bach’s statement that the concern lest one erase applies only to the homeowner, who has authority to erase, and not to an attendant, who lacks such authority. This is the meaning of, “A person may count his guests from memory, but not from a written list.” The concern regarding ordinary business documents, however, applies even to an attendant according to Rashi, who derives the prohibition from “refraining from pursuing your affairs.” According to Rambam and Ramban, a letter of greeting is permitted. The same therefore applies to an attendant on Nittel night, and one should not rebuke him, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rashba: one who relies upon Ramban should not be rebuked. Nevertheless, the ruling regarding a letter of greeting is difficult. How does “refraining from pursuing your affairs” apply? Any matter whose performance involves no prohibition may be discussed on Shabbos, such as saying that one will go to a certain city; all the more so should reading a letter of greeting be permitted. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 31. In Machatzis HaShekel it is explained that according to Rambam one may say that even a letter of greeting is forbidden, whereas according to Ramban it is permitted; see there. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham. They also forbade examining it, because it is impossible that one will not articulate something aloud.
