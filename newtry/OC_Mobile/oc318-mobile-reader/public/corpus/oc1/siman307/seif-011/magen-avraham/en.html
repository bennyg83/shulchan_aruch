For a long period. An ordinary loan is for thirty days, but borrowing for use is due immediately. Therefore, in a place where the practice is that an ordinary loan may be demanded immediately, as is customary in our country, he may say ‘lend me.’ The Gemara implies that this applies specifically to wine, oil, and something needed for Shabbos, but for something not needed for Shabbos it is forbidden to leave collateral.
One may not repay. This is the wording of the Rivash: it is forbidden to redeem a firstborn son, for at the least he is repaying his debt, which is forbidden, as we say regarding levites and repayments on Yom Tov. Tosafot in the fifth chapter of Bava Metzia wrote that it is forbidden to use language of lending and repayment, but one may use language of borrowing and returning. See there.
