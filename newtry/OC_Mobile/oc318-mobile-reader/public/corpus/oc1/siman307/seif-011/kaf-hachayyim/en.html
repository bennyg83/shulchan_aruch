Scrupulous people take care not to speak even necessary matters except in the holy tongue. I received a tradition that when a person visits his friend on Shabbos morning, he should not say the weekday greeting tzafra tava, but rather Shabbos tava, thereby fulfilling, 'Remember the day of Shabbos to sanctify it.' Shelah; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, similarly writes that this permissive ruling was brought before the assembly of the pious, but the pious sage Shelah rejected it. He was followed by the pious author of Daas Chochmah, chapter 11, folio 12b. These holy sages said that according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation and the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that even weekday conversation may not be excessive, this too is forbidden. This is how the Shulchan Aruch should read, rather than the printed phrase, 'Even idle conversation may not be excessive,' which is a scribal error. The sacred Zohar explicitly states that such speech desecrates Shabbos, as Daas Chochmah explains with sound reasoning. All the more so, it will certainly lead to forbidden speech. A conscientious person should establish a boundary so that he is not included, God forbid, among those who desecrate Shabbos. Daas Chochmah strongly challenges the custom of groups visiting friends on Shabbos, etc.; see his holy, enduring words at length. These are the words of Machazik Berachah.
