(s.k. 11) To city P, etc. — otherwise why does he state the obvious? Explain: why did he need the reason of watchmen? Explain: there is no benefit in his speech and plainly permitted.

It implies without this it is permitted — for Rabbenu Yehudah wrote: tell him thus so he will prepare a horse, etc.; but otherwise permitted, for there is no benefit in his speech; and Rabbenu Yehudah infers from Rokeach's wording forbidding: he cannot rent on Shabbat — what relevance has gentile renting to why forbidden to say "I will ride tomorrow"? Or plain telling is permitted since no benefit in his telling, only prohibition in telling him to prepare a horse; if so it appears from Rokeach and Rabbenu Yehudah plain telling is permitted if he does not tell another his fellow should do on a weekday what is forbidden on Shabbat; and perhaps Maggid Mishneh also holds thus: his "why plain" is not that in such a case he told for watchmen' reason, but: even such a case needs watchmen' reason as Rosh holds; rather "plain" such a case watchmen help and he did not need to teach us; nevertheless Maggid Mishneh also holds forbidden to tell oneself "tomorrow I will do matter P" without asking a fellow to do something — forbidden if he has no recognition today such as through watchmen. See Tosafot — meaning from Tosafot also implied forbidden even "tomorrow I will do matter P": one challenge from what is written 103 s.v. "what is different," etc., they challenged the sugya's question on the mishnah "one may not hire workers on Shabbat and one may not tell one's fellow to hire workers for him" — the Gemara asks: what difference between him and his fellow? Rashi: the Israelite is like himself and cannot hire them; Tosafot challenged: and let us say "fellow" means he told him hire workers for me tomorrow — and one may answer: if so why mention fellow — he too cannot say "I will hire workers tomorrow," etc., permitted, etc., to city P, etc., only because if there are watchmen, etc. — until here; and so explicitly there 150b in Rosh HaShanah "but waiting at dusk," etc. — until here: I know a hundred places, etc., permitted to say about oneself and even to hire workers tomorrow, etc., his telling does not help, etc.; but impossible according to Shmuel, etc., all telling forbidden even about oneself except, etc., because if there are watchmen, etc. — until here.
