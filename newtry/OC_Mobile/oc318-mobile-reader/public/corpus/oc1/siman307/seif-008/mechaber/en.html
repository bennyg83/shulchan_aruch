One may say to another person on Shabbos, 'Tomorrow I am going to such-and-such a city,' and may likewise say, 'Come with me tomorrow to such-and-such a city,' since it would be possible to reach it today by means of huts extending the techum. The same applies to anything of this kind where there is some permitted way to do it today: one may tell another person to do it tomorrow, provided that he does not mention payment. But regarding something for which there is no permitted way to do it today, even if the prohibition is only rabbinic, such as fruit that is muktzeh and lies outside the techum, since it is impossible to bring it today, one may not tell another person to bring it for him tomorrow. He may likewise not wait at the end of the techum toward nightfall so that he can hurry there at night to bring it. {Rama: He should also not say, 'I will do such-and-such tomorrow.' [Rosh, chapter HaShoel; see above at the beginning of this siman]} He may, however, wait at the end of the techum toward nightfall in order to hurry there at night to guard the fruit, because even today he could guard it if it were within the techum. If his intention in waiting there was only to guard it, he may afterward also bring it.
