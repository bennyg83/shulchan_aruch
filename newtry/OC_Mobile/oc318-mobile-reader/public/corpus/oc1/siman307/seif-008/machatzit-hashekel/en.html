(s.k. 8) And some hold for milah permitted, as in Eruvin 68; and one must say we do not compare, etc.; and Sefer Acharonim challenged: behold "some" are Tosafot Gittin 8 and Bava Kama — behold he wrote: R' Yitzchak says not to permit for another mitzvah such as bringing a book via karmelit — it does not compare, only for milah, etc. — until here — behold they forbade even a book; and one may distinguish between learning in it and reading in it (i.e., in public); however in Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 6 he copied Tosafot to bring Torah scroll — and Torah scroll presumably to read; and so explicitly in Rashba's novellae Gittin to bring Torah scroll to read — requires study (and in Hagahot Maimoniot before me only "book" is mentioned, not Torah scroll); and to my view one may say: in Eruvin 91 they permitted only in time of danger when they could not read publicly and had to hide the reading in a concealed place; therefore they had to move Torah scroll from roof to upper story, etc.; if they did not permit shevut the public reading would be nullified until the decree ceased — similarly here; but Tosafot deal when there is no danger, such as when they cannot leave the Torah scroll all week in synagogue but elsewhere — as Tur wrote above 149, and by chance they forgot to bring before Shabbat — such a case once in a while they did not permit; further one may say: Tosafot Ketubot 7a s.v. "since it was permitted," etc. — until here: from an unjoined courtyard one may not learn to karmelit which is stricter; and with this one answers: Tosafot and Rashba explicitly deal with karmelit; since stricter, shevut was not permitted; but the sugya in Eruvin moving roof to upper story and enclosure has law of unjoined courtyard — therefore since lighter, shevut was permitted (and see Shabbat 64b in Tosafot s.v. "and R' Chanina," etc.).

And know that according to Shulchan Aruch and Rambam: an Israelite may tell a gentile on Shabbat to climb a tree to bring a shofar — we rule blowing shofar for us does not override Shabbat — perforce it must deal when Rosh HaShanah is Shabbat and day one after it, and he brings for day one's need which is the second day of Rosh HaShanah; and from this there is apparent contradiction to Taz 295 who wrote: even though telling gentile to bring lulav on Yom Tov was permitted — that is only to send after him on Yom Tov itself; but on Shabbat one may not send for the Yom Tov after it; but here regarding shofar he wrote permitted thus; and perhaps Taz concedes for shofar more than lulav, for Taz wrote the reason permitting telling gentile for lulav more than for metzora — lulav is called public positive command — see there; and shofar is more public positive command; and according to Magen Avraham 346: only shofar is public positive command because one extracts many; but not biur chametz since one does not extract many — see there; if so lulav resembles chametz; therefore Taz holds lulav is also public positive command; nevertheless he concedes shofar is more public, since one extracts many — therefore for shofar they were lenient even to bring on Shabbat for the Yom Tov after.
