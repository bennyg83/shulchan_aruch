(Seif katan 8) And some say that, in their view, it is permitted for a circumcision. This is as stated in Eruvin 68. It must be said that one does not compare one rabbinic prohibition to another, etc. Eliyah Rabbah asks that these “some say” are Tosafot in Gittin 8 and in Bava Kamma, yet they wrote: “Permission to instruct a non-Jew to bring a book on Shabbos through a karmelis cannot be derived from here, for the cases are not comparable; it is specifically for the sake of circumcision, etc.” Thus, they did not permit it even for a book. It is possible to distinguish between bringing it in order to study from it and bringing it in order to read from it, meaning publicly. However, Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 6, cites Tosafot as referring to bringing a Sefer Torah, and a Sefer Torah is presumably brought in order to read from it. The same appears in Chiddushei HaRashba on Gittin: to bring a Sefer Torah in order to read from it. This requires study. In truth, the text of Hagahos Maimoniyos before me does not mention a Sefer Torah, but only a book generally. In my humble opinion, one may answer as follows. First, in Eruvin 91 they permitted it only at a time of persecution, when they were not allowed to read publicly and had to conceal the reading, conducting it privately in a hidden place. They therefore had to carry the Sefer Torah from a roof to an upper story, etc. Had the rabbinic prohibition not been waived, the public Torah reading would have ceased entirely until the decree was annulled. In such a case they permitted it. Tosafot, however, discuss a time without persecution, such as where it is impossible to leave the Sefer Torah in the synagogue throughout the week, so it is kept elsewhere during the week and brought into the synagogue before Shabbos, as the Tur writes above in siman 149, but on one occasion they forgot to bring it before Shabbos. Since this is merely an occasional occurrence, they did not permit it. A further answer may be given from Tosafot in Kesubos 7a, s.v. “Since it was permitted,” who write: “One cannot derive the law of a karmelis from that of a courtyard without an eruv, because a karmelis is more stringent.” Accordingly, this is well resolved. Tosafot and the Rashba explicitly discuss a karmelis; since it is more stringent, the rabbinic prohibition was not waived. The Gemara in Eruvin, however, discusses carrying from a roof to an upper story or to a karpeif, which has the law of a courtyard without an eruv. Since that prohibition is more lenient, they waived it. See Tosafot to Shabbos 64b, s.v. “And Rabbi Anani,” etc.
Know that, according to what the Shulchan Aruch writes, and as the Rambam likewise writes, that a Jew may instruct a non-Jew on Shabbos to climb a tree and bring a shofar, although we rule that blowing the shofar does not override Shabbos nowadays, it must refer to a case in which Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbos and Sunday. He brings it for Sunday, the second day of Rosh Hashanah. At first glance, this contradicts the Taz in siman 655, who writes that although instructing a non-Jew to bring a lulav on Yom Tov was permitted, that applies only when sending for it on Yom Tov itself; one may not send for it on Shabbos for the sake of the following Yom Tov. Yet here, regarding a shofar, it is written that this is permitted. It is possible that the Taz concedes regarding a shofar, because it is superior to a lulav. The Taz explains that instructing a non-Jew was permitted for a lulav more readily than for a metzora because lulav is considered a positive mitzvah of the community; see there. Shofar is even more clearly a positive mitzvah of the community. According to what Magen Avraham writes in siman 446, only shofar is considered a positive mitzvah of the community, because one person can discharge the obligation of many, whereas destroying chametz is not, since one person cannot discharge the obligation of many; see there. Accordingly, lulav resembles chametz. Although the Taz maintains that lulav too is a positive mitzvah of the community, he nevertheless concedes that shofar has this status to a greater degree, since one person can discharge the obligation of many. Therefore, regarding shofar they were lenient even to have it brought on Shabbos for the sake of the following Yom Tov.
