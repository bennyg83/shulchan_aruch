He may say, etc. It is explained at the beginning of the siman that something forbidden to do on Shabbos may not be spoken of as something he will do tomorrow. Here it teaches that although on Shabbos it is forbidden to walk outside the techum, nevertheless one may say, ‘I am going tomorrow to such-and-such a city,’ because the walking itself is not inherently forbidden, it merely lacks guard huts. However, it is forbidden to say, ‘I will ride tomorrow,’ or that he will travel by wagon, since his speech concerns something inherently forbidden and there is no permitted device for it. He should say only, ‘I am going tomorrow.’
Guard huts. Meaning huts for guards, through which one can walk on Shabbos even many mil, if they are absorbed into one another within seventy amos and four handbreadths, as explained in siman 398.
And similarly anything like this, etc. For example, he may tell his fellow to bring him tomorrow fruit from outside the techum that had already been detached and has no muktzeh prohibition. Although it is impossible to bring them today because of the prohibition of techumin and domains, nevertheless it is permitted to say this, since there is a permitted possibility, for if there were partitions he could bring them.
That he should do it tomorrow. But to do it today is forbidden even to tell a non-Jew; and even after the fact, if he brought them from outside the techum, it is forbidden to use them, as below in siman 325, seif 8.
Fruits outside the techum that are muktzeh. The later authorities wrote that this is not specifically at the end of the techum; the same applies within the techum, since they cannot be brought because of muktzeh. It mentions this because of the latter clause, for waiting near the boundary is forbidden only at the end of the techum, as below in seif 9.
To go there to bring them. This refers to these fruits that are muktzeh. But regarding fruit that is not muktzeh, since it is permitted to tell his fellow to bring them after Shabbos, it is also permitted to wait near the boundary for them. Gemara.
And likewise he should not say, etc. This refers back to what was written at first, that it is forbidden to tell his fellow to bring them tomorrow. It teaches that one should not say this is forbidden only when he speaks with his fellow and consults with him, since he has a need for his speech, but that speaking to himself should be permitted. Rather, since it is something forbidden to do on Shabbos, even speaking to himself is called weekday speech and is forbidden, as above at the beginning of the siman.
For even today, etc. Meaning, the guarding itself is an activity permitted on Shabbos, and he is waiting near the boundary for something permitted. As for the fact that it is outside the techum, he could have gone there by means of guard huts if he had them, as above.
Even to bring them. This means even if they are attached and must be detached in the evening, since his main intent in initially waiting near the boundary was only for guarding.
