Good to be careful that he not touch it — but reading or at least examination is permitted as explained in Beit Yosef and only regarding touching one is stringent l'chatchila that it is good to be careful because of muktzeh and see in Biur haGra [and so other Acharonim] that they conclude regarding touching one need not be stringent for there is no concern of muktzeh here and as I wrote in Mishna Berurah but regarding the reading or examination itself Gra is very doubtful — behold one benefits from one brought for his sake from outside the techum and even though he settles finally that Shulchan Aruch holds like the poskim who hold the reason they forbade benefiting on Shabbat from one brought for his sake is not because of lest he benefit from Shabbat melacha but because of lest he tell a gentile to bring l'chatchila on Shabbat and this does not apply here and as stated in Mishna Berurah on erev Shabbat in Gra — nevertheless it turns out this law depends on great authorities. The conclusion of our words: regarding touching one need not be stringent at all but regarding reading and examination it is very correct to be stringent regarding one brought for his sake unless for great need then one may rely on the lenient:
