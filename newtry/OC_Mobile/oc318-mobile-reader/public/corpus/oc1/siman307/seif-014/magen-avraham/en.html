Permitted — lest something he needs is written — not called weekday documents (Beit Yosef, Rivan).

That he not touch — Levush: I have not seen practicing thus; Rabbenu Yehudah: plain custom to touch; thus Rambam siman 176: gentile bringing writing outside eruv — some forbid, Maharam and Rabbenu Yehudah permit; Agudah: I practiced to tell another to receive and read — perhaps bodily need; if sealed I tell gentile I cannot read while closed — until here (implies forbidden to tell to open); so Zichronot (and see 319); so world practiced Maharam and Rabbenu Yehudah who are later; and now I explain forbidders' reason: lest before the agent stands the Jew takes the writing from his hand; therefore place on ground or table so gentile makes hanachah; likewise gentile bringing beer outside eruv — do not give in hand or take from hand (see 325) — until here Rambam; and Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 2 letter 9 ruled Maharam to permit — no decree lest he increase for him; and "for you and he brings" — none; and no muktzeh; and perhaps forbidders hold muktzeh since not in his mind, as 315; and in muktzeh brought from outside techum even R' Shimon concedes, as 398 seif 3; see what is written 315; and in Gittin 88 Rosh: if particular about letter to form on its spout — forbidden to move; see 308.
