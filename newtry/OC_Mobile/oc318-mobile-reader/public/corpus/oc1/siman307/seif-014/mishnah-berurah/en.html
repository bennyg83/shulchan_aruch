(54) Permitted — lest something bodily need is written — unlike weekday documents which are only monetary need; and though some forbid even this way, as Beit Yosef — to be lenient one should only gaze without reading; and for gazing alone some permit even weekday documents.

(55) Better to be careful, etc. — because of muktzeh brought from outside techum [besides that there is no muktzeh on them though not a vessel — fit to form on its spout]; rather let the non-Jew open and he will examine; and though an object brought from outside techum — we hold forbidden to benefit one for whom it was brought, and here reading and examining is benefit to him — there the reason is decree lest he tell non-Jew to bring from outside techum; here with a letter not relevant, for he does not know who will send; and one may say it is not considered benefit since he does not benefit from the melacha itself.

(56) That he not touch it — Acharonim agreed there is no muktzeh at all, as we rule 316: object brought from outside techum has no muktzeh even for one for whom brought — forbidden to benefit since fit for another Israelite; if particular about the letter for merchant custom placing trade letters in a hidden place lest lost — they are muktzeh and forbidden to move. Acharonim wrote custom not to receive letter immediately from non-Jew bringing on Shabbat but tell him to place on ground or table, lest before the agent stands the Israelite take the letter from his hand — Israelite would complete transfer from reshut to reshut: non-Jew did lifting and Israelite placing. If letter is signed tell non-Jew "I cannot read it" while closed — he will understand and open; do not tell explicitly to open unless great need; see below end of siman 340 in Biur Halacha.
