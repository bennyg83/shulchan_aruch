(Seif katan 10) To his fellow, etc. “Because it is, etc.” This means that when one says it to a non-Jew, the non-Jew is more likely to understand that the speaker intends to hire him for the errand after Shabbos. Were the Jew to have no need of him, he would have no reason to involve himself with the non-Jew for no purpose.
Thought is permitted, as stated above in siman 306, seif 8. We expound from “and speaking a word” that one’s speech on Shabbos should not be like one’s speech during the week. The verse prohibited only speech, not thought. Therefore, one may say to a Jew, “What seems proper in your eyes, etc.,” because this constitutes thought alone. The same applies when he says this to a non-Jew: there is only thought, not an explicit instruction.
