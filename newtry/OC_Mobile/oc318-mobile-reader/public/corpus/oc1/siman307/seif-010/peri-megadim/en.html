Engraved. See Taz. This refers to a case containing two grounds for leniency: the writing is engraved, and it is on a wall, so it is not confused with ordinary business documents. If, however, it is written high upon a wall, it may be confused with ordinary business documents; and an engraved ledger may likewise be confused with ordinary business documents. Even according to the view that writing placed very high is forbidden lest one erase it, there is a practical distinction regarding an attendant. The attendant is also forbidden to read it, although the concern that he may erase does not apply because he lacks authority to do so, as Bach writes. For this reason the concern is stated as lest he read ordinary business documents. See the sugya in Shabbos 149a and Tosafos, s.v. “Le’olam.” The Levush writes that when the writing is very high there is no concern that one may erase it, but this is difficult in light of siman 275. Even two people may read it, perhaps because one will remind the other. Ordinary business documents, however, are forbidden even for ten people because of “refraining from pursuing your affairs,” as Levush writes, and as Bach writes in the name of Rashi in the chapter Mashilin. Rambam, however, in chapter 23 of Hilchos Shabbos, gives the reason that one may erase. I had thought to explain that the case concerns Mashita script or the like, whose writing is only D’Rabbanan, and whose erasure is likewise only D’Rabbanan, as in siman 306, seif 11, in the Rama concerning their script; see Magen Avraham there, and siman 340, seif katan 10. It is possible, however, that the text should not read “very high,” as in siman 275, because there is a Torah prohibition. Yet even erasing incurs liability only when done in order to write two letters, as Rambam writes in chapter 11, halachah 9. When it is not done in order to write, there is only a D’Rabbanan prohibition. On Shabbos 149a it is stated that according to the opinion concerning a height of two stories, the same rule applies here. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 17.
