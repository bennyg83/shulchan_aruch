(s.k. 7) Slight, etc. — even an Israelite does shevut for one opinion, and this is Rambam's view below; see Tur and Magen Avraham s.k. 12.

As written there seif 33: a sick person nursing milk from an animal — where there is distress from groaning they did not decree on shevut, for milking is a derivative of unloading; and likewise nursing from an animal is unloading indirectly; and although it is not illness comprising the whole body and no danger to a limb — nevertheless permitted since it is shevut not in its usual way, for it is not usual to nurse from an animal.

Until here nevertheless, etc. — meaning all he wrote in this s.k. from the start "for real illness," etc. — therefore all is nevertheless the Maggid Mishneh's words; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote his words to resolve Raavad's challenge on Rambam's view: "impossible if slight illness he himself does, for behold a groaning person nurses milk on Shabbat," etc., and "they said in loss such as a pipe on which scales grew from a press on his foot," etc. — and therefore the Maggid Mishneh wrote his words briefly; and Magen Avraham wrote his words in explanation — thus: the Maggid Mishneh's intent here is that there is no illness comprising the whole body nor danger to a limb, only slight distress; and this is what he wrote "slight illness," etc.; and further that what is done is in its usual way — until here. And it appears from his words he emended, etc.; but from Raavad it appears he emended, etc. — meaning Raavad therefore emended in Rambam "or great need"; if not he would emend only "slight illness or mitzvah" and "great need" would need "great need" in his girsa; if so what is Raavad's challenge? Behold in loss he himself is permitted shevut as we find regarding pipe — what relevance of loss here, for Rambam did not separate loss at all; or he emended as Shulchan Aruch "or great need"; and therefore Raavad challenged two points: on "slight illness," etc. — from groaning permitted for Israelite himself to transgress shevut; and on "great need," i.e., loss — challenged from pipe; but the Maggid Mishneh therefore did not emend "great need" but "distressed"; if so no need to resolve the challenge from pipe, only from groaning; therefore he explained Rambam's words first before resolving Raavad; and wrote Rambam was precise in wording "slight illness," etc.: for real illness comprising the whole body or even danger to a limb — even an Israelite does; afterward he resolved Raavad from groaning — end of the Maggid Mishneh's words; and further "what he does is in its usual way" — unlike groaning, though there too only slight distress, nevertheless permitted shevut for Israelite since there it is shevut not in its usual way, unlike here shevut in its usual way; since the Maggid Mishneh did not mention resolving pipe in his words, the Raavad challenge from pipe is also resolved from the Maggid Mishneh's words; and for Raavad's girsa "or great need" — even for pipe it is shevut not in its usual way; nevertheless impossible the Maggid Mishneh emended "or great need"; if so he must resolve the pipe challenge too.

And in Hagahot Maimoniot, etc. — he wrote regarding pipe, etc. — meaning in ch. Mah Tomin of Beitzah Rabbenu Baruch challenged: as they permitted shevut in loss such as pipe and more he brought there — likewise to permit in fire to tell gentile to extinguish; but we learned the opposite: gentile who comes to extinguish — do not tell him extinguish, etc.; and he answered: all are permitted in loss because if you do not permit a small prohibition he will come to a great prohibition — pipe: if you do not permit indirectly he will repair not indirectly; until here.

And in Rivash, etc. — for extinguishing is melacha not needed for itself — meaning for Rashi it is only shevut; nevertheless we rule and none dispute forbidden to command gentile to extinguish fire.

And Ran ch. Barrels also wrote on one version, etc., to resolve why shevut was permitted for pipe and not for fire — even telling gentile to extinguish forbidden (and he wrote pipe is different since shevut not in its usual way); further answer: extinguishing is different — if needed for itself, i.e., to make coals or to brighten wicks, he would be liable.

Therefore it appears to me, etc. — since for Hagahot Maimoniot, Rivash, and Ran shevut in its usual way was not permitted even via gentile — shevut di-shevut — even in loss; and so Rashba in responsum; therefore Rambam and Raavad who are lenient — Magen Avraham's view is not to rely except in great loss and specifically via gentile; and via Israelite specifically not in its usual way.
