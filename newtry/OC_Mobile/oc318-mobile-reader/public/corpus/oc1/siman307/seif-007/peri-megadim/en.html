Guard huts. See Taz, who refers to paragraph 4. Speech is permitted whenever a permitted manner of performing the contemplated act can be conceived, even though that manner is not presently available. This applies only to the speech itself and to performing the act after Shabbos. To instruct a non-Jew to perform it on Shabbos, however, is permitted only where a Jew presently has an available, fully permitted means of doing it himself, such as moving a lamp in the manner discussed in simanim 276 and 279, Magen Avraham seif katan 11 and seif katan 9, or constructing a human partition. Magen Avraham would concede this. By contrast, where the act would be permitted only if guard huts or another presently absent circumstance existed, it is forbidden to have a non-Jew perform it on Shabbos. Even after the fact, benefit from the act is forbidden, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 12. See there.
