It is forbidden to read it. In the Gemara two reasons are given. One is lest he erase from the guests or from the delicacies, meaning that sometimes he sees that he did not prepare enough for them and regrets inviting more guests than proper, and he erases them from the writing so that the attendant will not call them; and likewise with types of delicacies he sometimes regrets and reduces them. Another reason is lest he come to read weekday documents, which is forbidden as below in seif 13. Regarding the practice that the attendant reads from writing for a meal, some are stringent: granted, there is no concern lest he erase except when the host himself reads, for the reason we wrote, but there remains a decree lest he read weekday documents. However, where the meal is a mitzvah meal, one may be lenient for the attendant to read, for this is included in matters of Heaven, which are permitted, as written in siman 306, seif 6. Certainly it is permitted for the attendant to announce in the beis midrash from a writing. However, the Shaarei Teshuvah brought a justification for the custom to be lenient even for an optional meal, that the decree was never made regarding the attendant, so that he not err and cause ruin and mishap, as in the incident of Kamtza and Bar Kamtza.
Very high. Since he cannot reach there to erase.
Engraved into the wall. There is no concern lest he erase, because engraving is recessed and difficult to erase. Nor do we decree lest he read weekday documents, for this is not confused with a document. Although if it is written on a high wall it is forbidden lest he read, there writing can be confused with writing; but engraving in a wall will not be confused with writing in a document. However, a tablet and notebook, which are movable, can be confused with weekday documents.
Recessed engraving. But raised engraving is forbidden, for we decree lest he erase, even if it is very high and he cannot reach there to erase, because the Sages did not distinguish, as written in siman 275. The notes that buyers send, on which the amount of wine and beer they take was written before Shabbos, are improper, for it is forbidden to read them on Shabbos; so wrote the Eliyah Rabbah. It appears that if only a mere sign was written on them, it is permitted.
