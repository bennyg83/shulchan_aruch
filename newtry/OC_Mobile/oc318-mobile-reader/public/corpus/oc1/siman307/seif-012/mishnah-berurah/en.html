(47) Forbidden to read it — in Gemara two reasons: one lest he erase from guests and from delicacies — sometimes he sees he did not prepare enough for those he invited and regrets inviting too many and erases from the writing so the attendant will not call them; likewise sometimes regrets diminishing delicacies; second reason lest he come to read also weekday documents — forbidden as below seif 13. What they practice — attendant reads from writing for a meal — some are strict: though no erasing concern when the host himself reads, as we wrote, nevertheless decree lest he read weekday documents; but when the meal is mitzvah — lenient for attendant reading, as heavenly affairs permitted, 306 seif 6; all the more permitted for attendant to announce in synagogue from writing; however in Shaarei Teshuvah he justified custom to be lenient for attendant even for a mundane meal: originally they did not decree on the attendant lest he err and bring ruin — like the Kamtza and bar Kamtza incident.

(48) Very high — he cannot reach to erase.

(49) He carved in the wall — for erasing concern there is no decree, since engraving is sunken and hard to erase; and also for weekday documents they did not decree, for it does not interchange with a document [though if written high on wall forbidden lest he read from writing to writing it interchanges; but here wall engraving does not interchange with document writing]; but tablet and ledger, though movable, interchange with weekday documents.

(50) Sunken engraving — but protruding forbidden — decree lest he erase; even very high where he cannot reach to erase — because of "do not distinguish," as 275. And the notes merchants send on erev Shabbat with sum of wine and beer they buy improperly — forbidden to read on Shabbat, so Acharonim; and it appears if he wrote only a mere mark — permitted.
