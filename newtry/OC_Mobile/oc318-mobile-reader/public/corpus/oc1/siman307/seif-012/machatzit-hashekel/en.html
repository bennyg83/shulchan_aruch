(s.k. 12) Outside the techum — but within the techum, etc. — Beit Yosef challenged: why mention outside the techum — even within forbidden since impossible to move them? And he answered: within the techum, since permitted to wait at dusk for attached fruit as seif 9 (though he may not detach and bring) — likewise permitted to tell one's fellow — until here; meaning since Abba Shaul included the principle "all that I may tell I may wait at dusk for" — and likewise the reverse: all one may wait at dusk for, telling is permitted; since permitted to wait at dusk for muktzeh attached fruit within techum — likewise telling permitted that he detach fruit on motzei Shabbat. The included principle "all that I may tell," and likewise reverse, etc. — deals at end of techum: meaning in what has no prohibition except distance because of end of techum; but what is forbidden even within techum from muktzeh or attachment — though permitted to wait at dusk, nevertheless telling forbidden; therefore I say he mentioned outside techum not to infer within is permitted — for even within forbidden; rather he mentioned because of the mishnah's end "forbidden to wait at end of techum," etc. — there specifically outside techum; but within permitted as seif 9 — he mentioned also at the start outside techum.

And plain even a matter, etc. — forbidden to tell gentile to do on Shabbat — for such was not permitted except to tell gentile to do on motzei Shabbat; proof from 325 seif 8 and 10: if gentile brought from outside techum for Israelite need — forbidden for that Israelite though techum prohibition has permission for Israelite via watchmen and bringing possible via partitions — nevertheless even after the fact forbidden for the Israelite for whom it was brought, though the Israelite did not command the gentile to bring; all the more forbidden for Israelite to command gentile for this.

And Beit Yosef's words require study; also what he wrote via partition of people, etc. — meaning Rabbenu Yehudah brought the Gemara citing Rabbenu Yehudah: telling gentile even for d'oraisa not forbidden except where impossible to find remedy for Israelite to do, such as heating hot water; but if possible, such as Rosh HaShanah where he can bring via partitions of people (meaning each stands near another less than three handbreadths — full partition) — telling gentile permitted; and how if he told him perhaps it will be confused on Rosh HaShanah — forbidden telling gentile even rabbinic prohibitions, etc.; and on this Rabbenu Yehudah wrote the end contradicts the beginning: if possible as Rosh HaShanah via partitions permitted — requires study — until here Rabbenu Yehudah. Beit Yosef answered: in the beginning it deals telling gentile to bring something on Rosh HaShanah even a hundred amot — permitted since Israelite may carry on Rosh HaShanah if there were partitions — therefore gentile may bring even without partitions; he brought proof from seif 8: permitted to tell one's fellow "to city P I go tomorrow" since if there are watchmen, etc.; Tosafot wrote the same here; unlike the end dealing telling gentile to bring courtyard to unjoined courtyard — forbidden for Israelite because it may be confused on Rosh HaShanah and he will take from reshut ha-yachid to reshut ha-rabbim — no remedy via partitions, for what help partition when they did not join (even two courtyards adjacent with one wall between — if not joined, forbidden to take from one to the other by hand and via window) — likewise telling gentile forbidden — until now Beit Yosef in Tosafot's explanation; and Magen Avraham challenged all his words: what he wrote in the beginning — all Israelite may do via partitions permitted to tell gentile to do today on Shabbat — is not so: telling was permitted only that the gentile do after Shabbat, not on Shabbat; as clear from 325 as above.

Also what he wrote via partitions, etc. — meaning per Beit Yosef's view his explanation of end of Gemara words is incorrect: it deals there forbidden to tell gentile to bring courtyard to unjoined courtyard — forbidden for Israelite even via partitions; Beit Yosef disagrees with Magen Avraham: nevertheless telling gentile should be permitted, for the prohibition courtyard to unjoined is because it may be confused on Rosh HaShanah — telling gentile on Rosh HaShanah permitted per Rabbenu Yehudah; perforce Beit Yosef holds even via partitions of people forbidden for Israelite to take from reshut ha-yachid to reshut ha-rabbim — therefore also telling gentile forbidden; and on this Magen Avraham wrote: there is no such thing that partition of people is a full partition; and plain in Gemara, etc. — he brought this for tomorrow, etc. — such helps if there were partitions; but the gentile coming on Shabbat was not permitted, as above.

Also what he wrote in Tosafot's name, etc. — meaning Beit Yosef brought proof for law one: permitted to tell gentile to bring on Rosh HaShanah even on Shabbat — from Tosafot on seif 8 "permitted to tell one's fellow to city P," etc. — the same permitted to tell gentile, etc., as above; on this Magen Avraham wrote: Tosafot did not write their words on seif 8 but on seif 7 "permitted to tell one's fellow as you see fit," etc., as Magen Avraham wrote in Tosafot's name s.k. 10.
