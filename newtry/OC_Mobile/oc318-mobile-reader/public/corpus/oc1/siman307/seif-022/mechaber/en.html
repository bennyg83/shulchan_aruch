All rabbinic shevut is permitted during bein hashemashot for mitzvah need — such as to tell a gentile to light a candle for him during bein hashemashot; or if he was busy and needed to tithe during bein hashemashot.Every matter forbidden to tell a gentile to do on Shabbat is forbidden to hint to him to do it; but permitted to hint melacha to do after Shabbat (Or Zarua); gentiles who bring grain on Shabbat to Israelites who owe them, and the Israelite gives him the key to his storehouse and the gentile gives it there and they measure and count — some permit because the gentile is engaged in his own melacha and it is not the Israelite's until after the measuring, and he will calculate with him afterward; and likewise gentiles who make cheeses on Shabbat and the Israelite watches and will buy from them — nevertheless the gentile does it for himself, and even though the Israelite stands in the new pen or pens and intends to sell them to Israelites — permitted (Beit Yosef in the name of Agudah).
