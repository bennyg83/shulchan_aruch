(75) If he was busy — see below 342 in Mishna Berurah.

(76) Forbidden to hint, etc. — for this too is included in telling a non-Jew, since through his hint he does on Shabbat; and the same forbidden to tell him on Shabbat anything he will understand leads to melacha; therefore forbidden to tell non-Jew to wipe his nose so he understands to remove soot on the candle; but when the hint to the non-Jew is not in command language, such as "the candle does not shine well" or "I cannot read by this candle with soot" — non-Jew hears and fixes — permitted, for not included in telling; and do not forbid because he benefits from melacha the non-Jew did for him — not much benefit, for he could have read with effort before [Peri Megadim].

(77) In his own melacha, etc. — but if grain belongs to Israelite forbidden to tell non-Jew to clear it; and the same for all muktzeh — shevut di-shevut; permitted only for mitzvah or slight illness, as above s.k. 5 [Magen Avraham; Taz below 308 s.k. 7 disputes; Acharonim like Magen Avraham; see Peri Megadim and what we wrote 279 seif 4 in the gloss in Mishna Berurah].

(78) Until after measuring and he will calculate, etc. — meaning before measuring and accounting they are not his. Non-Jew who brought his objects on Shabbat to Israelite's house — even if forbidden to move and brought for the Israelite — since in non-Jew's hand permitted to tell him to clear them wherever he wishes [Magen Avraham in the name of Maggid Mishneh].

(79) Watches and will buy, etc. — meaning even though Israelite stands when cheeses are made and when milk is milked so he can buy afterward — permitted, for at the time of making the non-Jew acts for himself.

(80) And intending to sell them, etc. — and even if the non-Jew only makes cheeses on Shabbat — if Israelite accepts them as debt and if he does not accept he loses the debt — permitted, for at time of making they are still his.
