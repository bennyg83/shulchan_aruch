[22] To one's fellow, etc. — this is Magen Avraham's wording of Beit Yosef citing Tosafot that "to one's fellow" is not precise — until here; to me it is the same: Tosafot's reason there: since thought is permitted — the same for gentile and Israelite; and there is no weekday matter here; and in both laws he used "fellow"; if it were gentile he should teach Israelite here; so implied in waiting for bride 306 seif 7 — same for gentile; see Ran there; also proof from Tosafot 150a "to city P I go tomorrow" — even to fellow he could say "go to city P"; until here — since he used expansive language, specifically telling him to go is only to fellow permitted, not gentile; if we say gentile is truly permitted even without saying explicitly — all the more "fellow" is not precise; and below 325 seif 7 forbidden to gentile — to do on Shabbat itself; after Shabbat certainly permitted; Hagahot Mordechai I brought above also supports.
