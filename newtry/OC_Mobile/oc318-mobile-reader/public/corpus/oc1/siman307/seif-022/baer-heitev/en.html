To hint. And the same — forbidden to tell him a matter from which he will understand thereby to do melacha — Piskei Rekanati siman 99. And if so forbidden to tell a gentile to wipe his nose so he understands to remove the wick at the head of the lamp — specifically to tell him a matter he understands he will do after Shabbat permitted as stated seif 6 but when he does on Shabbat forbidden for even if the gentile did melacha by himself for Israelite need forbidden to benefit as stated siman 325 all the more to hint to him not like Bach. And see what is stated seif 14 in the name of R' Meir of Rothenburg that there he does not tell the gentile any command only tells him he cannot read — end quote Magen Avraham and Yad Aharon ruled like Bach and wrote removing the wick is only a d'rabbanan prohibition for the Israelite himself — if so permitted to hint to the gentile to remove the wick such as telling him to wipe his nose and thereby he understands and removes the wick and so Keneset HaGedolah that Mahari Abuhav often needed to see something in a book and the lamp did not shine well he would say "the lamp does not shine" and the maidservant understood and cut the wick head — on erev Shabbat:

A gentile who brought his objects on Shabbat to an Israelite's house even if forbidden to move permitted to tell him to remove them to whatever place he wishes and there is no shevut di-shevut since they are in the gentile's hand. And if the grain belongs to the Israelite forbidden to tell the gentile to remove and the same for every muktzeh matter for it is shevut di-shevut — see Magen Avraham. A gentile seized before the judge who owed a Jew — he may tell the judge to accept bail from the gentile and leave the seized. But he should not tell him to write in court records.
