Forbidden to tell him — thus Rambam ch. 6 halacha 21: non-Jew who brought his objects on Shabbos into Israelite's house — even if he told him "place them in this corner" — permitted — until here; Maggid Mishneh wrote: know telling non-Jew to do the non-Jew's own melacha is permitted; these objects the non-Jew deposits with the Israelite; even if he brings to the Israelite objects forbidden to move, since in non-Jew's hand permitted to tell him to clear them wherever he wishes — no shevut di-shevut since in non-Jew's hand — until here; and it deals with this and 308 seif 4; so Rakanati in his rulings siman 102 and Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 5: where Israelite does not benefit permitted to tell non-Jew to do; and that which is forbidden to tell him "muzzle my cow and thresh" even non-Jew's threshing — there he benefits by increased wage through muzzling; but "muzzle your cow and thresh" permitted (see Choshen Mishpat 338). One may ask from our mishnah: one may send a thigh to a non-Jew though the forbidden sinew is in it — apparently permitted to tell non-Jew to eat too; and daily practice feeding servants terefot — one may say in non-Jew's eating the matter is evident and it appears the non-Jew intends for himself — considered as "do your melacha"; until here. Plain difficulty: what does he challenge from sending to non-Jew — he already wrote where Israelite does not benefit it is permitted? Answer: since Israelite gives him to eat — considered benefit, for once he gives he wants him to eat — forbidden to give meat for him to cook for himself; permitted to say "cook your meat"; so Iggeret Avraham siman 5 and Yoreh Deah 297; and permitted to tell non-Jew "extinguish your fire."
