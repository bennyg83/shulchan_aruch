Forbidden to tell him — thus Rambam ch. 6 halacha 21: gentile who brought his objects on Shabbat into Israelite's house — even if he told him "place them in this corner" — permitted — until here; Maggid Mishneh wrote: know telling gentile to do the gentile's own melacha is permitted; these objects the gentile deposits with the Israelite; even if he brings to the Israelite objects forbidden to move, since in gentile's hand permitted to tell him to clear them wherever he wishes — no shevut di-shevut since in gentile's hand — until here; and it deals with this and 308 seif 4; so Rakanati in his rulings siman 102 and Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 5: where Israelite does not benefit permitted to tell gentile to do; and that which is forbidden to tell him "muzzle my cow and thresh" even gentile's threshing — there he benefits by increased wage through muzzling; but "muzzle your cow and thresh" permitted (see Choshen Mishpat 338). One may ask from our mishnah: one may send a thigh to a gentile though the forbidden sinew is in it — apparently permitted to tell gentile to eat too; and daily practice feeding servants terefot — one may say in gentile's eating the matter is evident and it appears the gentile intends for himself — considered as "do your melacha"; until here. Plain difficulty: what does he challenge from sending to gentile — he already wrote where Israelite does not benefit it is permitted? Answer: since Israelite gives him to eat — considered benefit, for once he gives he wants him to eat — forbidden to give meat for him to cook for himself; permitted to say "cook your meat"; so Iggeret Avraham siman 5 and Yoreh Deah 297; and permitted to tell gentile "extinguish your fire."
