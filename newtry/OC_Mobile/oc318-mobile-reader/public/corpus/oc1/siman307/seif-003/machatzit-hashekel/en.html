(s.k. 3) It is forbidden, etc. This is not comparable to what is stated in siman 252, seif 2, that one may give garments to a non-Jewish launderer, or hides to a non-Jewish tanner, close to nightfall on Friday if he fixed the payment; nor is it comparable to siman 246 concerning sending a letter through a messenger.
It is explicitly implied that this applies even if he did not tell him to purchase it, etc. The words in Hagahos Ashri, "that he should purchase it for him tomorrow," are not words spoken by the Jew to the non-Jew. Rather, they are the words of Hagahos Ashri itself. The Jew's words to the non-Jew were unstated. Nevertheless, we know on our own that the Jew intends him to purchase it tomorrow, because the discussion concerns villages and small towns where purchases are ordinarily available only on market day. The words, "for this is comparable to stipulating, etc.," are from Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 6, and complete its statement cited above. In the case of siman 252, the non-Jew can, if he wishes, refrain from doing the work until tomorrow. Concerning this it concludes that the case is comparable to stipulating that he rest. That is to say, at the beginning of siman 248 it is stated that one may set sail on a ship, provided that he stipulates with the non-Jew that he rest on Shabbos. If the non-Jew subsequently does not rest, that is of no consequence; see siman 248. The case in siman 252 is likewise comparable to that case.
This is contrary to Beit Yosef, who wrote that the case concerns specifically one who told the non-Jew to purchase it on Shabbos. The reference to market day is not precise; it merely describes the ordinary circumstance.
The ruling that it is forbidden to give him an item to sell likewise applies even when nothing is stated explicitly, if the circumstances are such that it can be sold only on market day, which is the following day, when many buyers gather.
He writes that on Wednesday and Thursday it is permitted. Eliyah Rabbah leaves this ruling requiring study, for certainly, if the Jew explicitly tells the non-Jew to purchase or sell on Shabbos, it is forbidden even on Wednesday or Thursday. The same should therefore apply when selling or purchasing is available only on Shabbos, which is market day. Even on Wednesday or Thursday it should be forbidden, because it is as though he expressly told him to purchase it on Shabbos. This is not comparable to siman 252, for there the non-Jew is not compelled to perform the work on Shabbos; see there. Eliyah Rabbah further comments on the Taz's statement that, even in a case that is permitted because the Jew did not tell the non-Jew to perform the work on Shabbos, the Jew may nevertheless not benefit from it, since the non-Jew in any event performed it for the Jew, which is comparable to a non-Jew who lit a lamp for a Jew, etc. Eliyah Rabbah writes that siman 252 explicitly states that one may wear a garment whose work a non-Jew completed on Shabbos, and that this is not comparable to siman 276 concerning the lighting of a lamp, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 252, s.k. 11. These are his words. However, Rama above in siman 252 writes that one should be stringent and not wear it. Yet if it is uncertain whether the non-Jew completed it on Shabbos or before Shabbos, it is explained there that according to all opinions it is permitted.
