(s.k. 3) Forbidden, etc. — and some say for what is written 292 seif 2: permitted to give garments to a launderer and hides to a gentile worker close to nightfall if he fixed wage; and likewise 246 regarding sending a letter.

It implies explicitly that even if he does not tell him to buy, etc. — and what is written in Hagahot Maimoniot "he should buy for him tomorrow" is not Israelite to gentile words but Hagahot Maimoniot's words; but Israelite to gentile is stam only, for we know the Israelite's intent is to buy tomorrow — it deals with villages and small towns where buying is only on market day. It resembles "one who waits at dusk," etc. — they are Gemara's words ch. 6 and his conclusion above: this 292 — if the gentile wishes he need not act until tomorrow; and he concluded it resembles one who waits at dusk. Meaning: what Rabbenu Yerucham 248 wrote: permitted to sail on a ship, etc., and a partner sits and waits — if afterward he does not wait, it does not matter — see 248; if so this 292 is also similar.

Not like Beit Yosef who wrote it deals only when he told him to buy on Shabbat; and "market day" he mentioned — some say; rather he mentioned the way of the matter.

Forbidden to give him something to sell — meaning also stam if it is such that impossible to sell except on market day which is tomorrow when many buyers gather.

And he wrote on fourth and fifth permitted — in Sefer Acharonim he left this law in requires study: certainly if he said explicitly to buy or sell on Shabbat day — even on fourth and fifth forbidden; and if so the same if impossible to sell or buy except on Shabbat market day — forbidden even on fourth and fifth, as telling him to buy on Shabbat; and some say for 292 that there he is not compelled on Shabbat — see erev Shabbat. Further in Sefer Acharonim on what Taz wrote: even what is permitted if he does not tell him to do on Shabbat — nevertheless the Israelite may not benefit from it, for in any case he did for the Israelite — resembles gentile who lit a candle for an Israelite, etc. — until here; he wrote this is explicit in 292: permitted to wear a garment the gentile finished on Shabbat; and some say for 276 regarding lighting a candle as Magen Avraham 292 s.k. 11 wrote — until now; however Rama above 292 wrote to be strict not to wear it; but presumably if he finished on Shabbat or before Shabbat it is clear there permitted for all.
