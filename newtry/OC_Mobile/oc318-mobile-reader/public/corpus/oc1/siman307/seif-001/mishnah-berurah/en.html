(1) "I will do tomorrow" — and specifically if it is something forbidden to do on Shabbat, even if only a rabbinic prohibition. Some wrote: even a mitzvah matter such as writing a Torah scroll and the like is forbidden to say "I will do tomorrow"; but Acharonim and Berkei Yosef disagree and hold that for any mitzvah matter it is permitted; nevertheless l'chatchila proper to be strict when there is no need in his speech today [see Turei Even]; and if he fears he will be lax in the matter — all agree permitted to urge himself, for speech to Heaven is like a vow [there in Turei Even]. Forbidden to detain a non-Jew on Shabbat because of a debt — all this is included in "pursuing your affairs"; but he may afterward on a weekday tell the judge why he did not detain the non-Jew on Shabbat so he will understand and detain him next Shabbat when opportunity arises; and in responsum of Bach siman 146 he permits one who has a debt with an distress and on Shabbat learned the distress will leave the city and perhaps not return — permitted to go on Shabbat to receive from the officer that he pay him, for in loss they did not decree. A non-Jew detained by the judge because of a Jew who owed him may tell the judge to accept collateral from a non-Jew and release the detainee, but not tell him to write in courts [Acharonim]. Acharonim siman 244: if a non-Jew owes him and he fears the non-Jew may travel on Shabbat — he may meet him so he will pay through another Jew. Forbidden to detain Reuven's property in Shimon's hand on Shabbat [Acharonim].
(2) Idle matters — meaning there is no mention of doing melacha or business at all, and also no disgraceful or frivolous words — otherwise even a little forbidden.
(3) Pleasure for them — and forbidden to tell on Shabbat any matter in which he is distressed.
(4) Permitted, etc. — nevertheless he should beware not to prolong much in this, for even in complete pleasure such as eating, drinking, and sleeping it is explained in siman 290 and Beit Yosef siman 288 that one should not prolong much, for to those not engaged in Torah on weekdays Shabbat was given chiefly for words of Torah; and even for Torah scholars weary in Torah all week — Acharonim also write not to increase too much because of bitul Torah.
(5) To tell them, etc. — not from Sefer Chassidim; and one who guards himself from weekday speech — "holy" will be said of him. Men of deed are very careful beyond this not to speak on Shabbat even necessary matters except in the holy tongue [and it appears their reason is to fence lest they come to idle talk] — so Acharonim. It is in Shelah: I received one who visits his fellow on Shabbat morning should not say as on a weekday "good morning" but "good Shabbat" [in holy tongue or vernacular as our custom today] to fulfill "remember the day of Shabbat."
(6) So that they may enjoy, etc. — he should not transgress "and speak a matter" for his fellow; and if he enjoys what they enjoy — perhaps there is room to be lenient.
