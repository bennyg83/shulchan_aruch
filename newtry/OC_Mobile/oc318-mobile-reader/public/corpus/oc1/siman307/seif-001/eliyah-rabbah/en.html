[1] "I will do tomorrow," etc. — even for a mitzvah matter (Sefer Chassidim 266, Magen Avraham); I followed Kenesset HaGedolah and Olat Tamid; and so I followed in my small book Eliyah Zuta; but I examined Sefer Chassidim — it deals when he says he will sleep in order to write a Torah scroll tomorrow; I brought it siman 290 s.k. 2 — then there is no pleasure of sleep for honor of Shabbos; but saying he will do a mitzvah tomorrow — from where? And on the contrary it implies from 306 seif 6 that it is permitted; and so from what Kolbo wrote. Further Sefer Chassidim siman 262: one should not dwell where non-Jews' market is on Shabbos — until here; and one must say specifically when the market is in Jewish neighborhoods, for then it is impossible not to sin in speech; and further he wrote: forbidden on Shabbos to tell one's friends who died or are in distress.
[2] Or merchandise, etc. — Rambam's wording ch. 24 and Semag: one may not speak with his partner what he will sell tomorrow, buy, how he will build this house, with what merchandise he will go to such-and-such a place — all similar forbidden; and it appears to me he mentioned "partner" as the way of the matter, and the same for another, as Rama wrote seif 8; and in Kolbo he omitted partner — and it appears even if he did not say to another at all but only to himself, as implied by the conclusion of Shulchan Aruch and Levush and Maharash and Kolbo — only presumptively he says to another: why speak to himself? Magen Avraham s.k. 11 brings poskim that even "I will do" to one's fellow is permitted — and if so Tosafot's questions Shabbos 150a s.v. "what," etc., are difficult — see there and investigate.
[3] Permitted to tell them, etc. — not from Sefer Chassidim; and one who guards weekday speech — "holy" will be said of him; men of deed are careful not to speak even necessities except holy tongue; and I received: one visiting on Shabbos morning should not say "good morning" as on a weekday but "good Shabbos" to fulfill "remember Shabbos." Shelah and Levush. And in essence of telling rumors it appears to me from Beit Yosef I brought siman 301 s.k. 5: initially one should not habituate in them, for Terumat HaDeshen siman 301 learned this from young men who delight in leaping.
[Levush] To distress, etc. It appears to me that even if he does not have distress from this, nevertheless, since there is no enjoyment, it is forbidden to violate the requirement of refined Shabbos speech. This is also implied by Terumas HaDeshen and the Rama. Perhaps he holds that when it does not distress him it is included in sinning so that your fellow may merit; see siman 306, seif katan 33, regarding a wart. This is not clear, and see the Taz, for he had an erroneous text in the Rama.
