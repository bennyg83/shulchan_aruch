(s.k. 1) I will do it tomorrow, even according to Darkei Moshe in siman 306. In Eliyah Rabbah he asks why this should be inferior to the case in siman 306, where one may speak on Shabbos about matters of a mitzvah. In Tevuos Shor he answers that the case in siman 306 is different, because there is a need for his words today, whereas here the discussion concerns a case in which there is no need for his words today; see there. However, Eliyah Rabbah writes that what is stated in Sefer Chasidim has no connection to what Magen Avraham wrote. He states: "I examined Sefer Chasidim, which discusses one who says that he is going to sleep in order to write a sefer Torah tomorrow. In that case the pleasure of sleeping is not for the honor of Shabbos. But from where do we know that one may say that he will perform a mitzvah tomorrow?" End quote. I examined Sefer Chasidim and saw that it states as follows: "On Shabbos a person should not say, 'Let us sleep so that we can do our work after Shabbos,' because it is forbidden to say, 'Tomorrow I will do such-and-such.' This applies even if he wishes to write Torah, because he thereby demonstrates that his sleeping and resting on Shabbos are for the sake of the weekday. Rather, he should say, 'Let us rest, for today is Shabbos.'" End quote. It appears that there is a textual error and that the passage should be written in the reverse order. According to the text before us, it is difficult to understand why, after stating that a person should not say, 'Let us sleep so that we can do our work after Shabbos,' it gives the reason that it is forbidden for him to say, 'Tomorrow I will do such-and-such.' It could instead have given the reason stated at the end, that he thereby demonstrates that he is doing this for the sake of the weekday. Furthermore, why was it necessary to add at the end the reason that he thereby demonstrates this, after it had already stated that it is forbidden to say such a thing? Rather, the passage must have been printed in error, and it should read as follows: "A person should not say, 'Let us sleep so that we can do our work after Shabbos,' because he thereby demonstrates that he is sleeping for the sake of the weekday. Rather, he should say, 'Let us rest, for today is Shabbos.' It is also forbidden to say, 'Tomorrow I will do such-and-such,' even if he wishes to write a sefer Torah." With this reading, everything is correct.
