(s.k. 1) "I will do tomorrow" — even a rabbinic matter (266). In Sefer Acharonim he challenged: why exclude from 306 that heavenly affairs speech is permitted on Shabbat? Sefer Turei Even answered: this case differs from 306 — there is need for speech today, here there is no need for speech today — see there; but in Sefer Acharonim he wrote there is no relevance to what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 8 — thus: I examined Sefer Chassidim — it deals when he says he will sleep in order to write a Torah scroll tomorrow — then there is no pleasure of sleep for Shabbat honor; but saying he will do a mitzvah tomorrow — from where? And on the contrary it implies from 306 seif 6 permitted; and also what he wrote at the end in the name of Kolbo. Further Sefer Chassidim siman 262: one should not dwell where gentiles' market is on Shabbat — until here; and it appears a printing error and should read the opposite: according to what we wrote before, how did he give a reason for "one should not say I will sleep," etc., that it is forbidden to say, etc. — explain: what he concluded at the end "because it appears," etc., that he acts for weekday; and why did he need another reason at the end "because it appears," etc. — he already said forbidden to say, etc.; therefore the words were printed in error and should read: one should not say, etc., "so our melacha will be done on a weekday" because it appears he sleeps, etc., for weekday — only "rest, for today is Shabbat." And forbidden to say "tomorrow I will do such-and-such," even if he wishes to write a Torah scroll — and with this everything is correct.
