1) [Seif 1] 'And speak a word,' so that your speech on Shabbos shall not be, etc. My teacher, of blessed memory, was exceedingly careful not to speak a foreign language on Shabbos, either at night or during the day, except when he delivered a teaching that had to be given in the vernacular so that the listeners would understand. He was also careful not to engage in idle conversation or weekday speech, even in the holy tongue. He followed the same practice on every Yom Tov as on Shabbos. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 74d; Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, end of chapter 21.
2) There: 'And speak a word,' etc. When Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's mother would speak excessively on Shabbos, he would tell her, 'It is Shabbos,' and she would become silent. Vayikra Rabbah, end of parashas Behar. The Yerushalmi says that permission to inquire after another's welfare on Shabbos was granted only reluctantly. Tosafos, Shabbos 113b.
3) There: I will do a particular thing tomorrow, etc. If it concerns a mitzvah, speaking is permitted, contrary to what the later authorities write, as Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, clearly proves, rejecting their evidence from Sefer Chassidim, siman 266; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1.
4) Similarly, one may not read a book on Shabbos for the purpose of identifying a passage that requires correction so that he can correct it the next day. If he intends to read and, should he find an error, will correct it the next day, it is permitted. Responsa Radbaz, siman 690; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2; Shaarei Teshuvah there.
5) It is forbidden to detain Reuven's property in Shimon's possession. Responsa Maharshadam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 105; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
6) It is forbidden to sit in a non-Jew's shop on Shabbos, even if one is not his partner. Responsa Zekan Aharon, siman 33; Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1.
7) It is forbidden on Shabbos to speak about one's loved ones who died or are suffering. Sefer Chassidim, siman 110; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1.
8) A person should not live in a place where a non-Jewish market operates on Shabbos, because it is impossible for him to avoid sinning through his speech or another matter. Sefer Chassidim, siman 262. This applies specifically when the market is in the Jewish neighborhood. Knesses HaGedolah there; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah there.
9) It is forbidden to detain a non-Jew on Shabbos because of a debt. During the week, however, one may ask a judge, 'Why did you not detain the non-Jew on Shabbos?' Agudah; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Responsa Bach, siman 146, permits the following case: a person has a debt owed by an oppressor, and on Shabbos learns that the oppressor will leave the city and might never return. He may go on Shabbos to complain to the government official so that the oppressor will pay him; see there. Eliyah Rabbah at the end of this siman and Chayei Adam, klal 61, paragraph 3, cite this. Nevertheless, if possible, it should be done through a non-Jew.
10) A person may not pursue his affairs on Shabbos or even speak about them, such as discussing with his partner what they will sell or purchase the next day, how they will build a house, or with which merchandise they will travel to a particular place. All this and similar matters are forbidden. Rambam, chapter 24, halachah 1. Sefer Mitzvos Gadol writes the same. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cites them and writes that Rambam mentions one's partner because this is the ordinary case, but the same law applies to anyone else, as Rema writes in seif 8. Kol Bo likewise omits 'his partner.' It also appears that this is forbidden even if he speaks to no one else but only to himself, as implied by the unqualified words of the Shulchan Aruch, Levush, Maharash, and Kol Bo. Ordinarily, however, he speaks to another person, because there is little reason to speak to himself; see there.
11) There, gloss: It is permitted to recount them on Shabbos, etc. This is not the teaching of the pious. One who guards himself from weekday speech will be called holy. Scrupulous people take care not to speak even necessary matters except in the holy tongue. I received a tradition that when a person visits his friend on Shabbos morning, he should not say the weekday greeting tzafra tava, but rather Shabbos tava, thereby fulfilling, 'Remember the day of Shabbos to sanctify it.' Shelah; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, similarly writes that this permissive ruling was brought before the assembly of the pious, but the pious sage Shelah rejected it. He was followed by the pious author of Daas Chochmah, chapter 11, folio 12b. These holy sages said that according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation and the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that even weekday conversation may not be excessive, this too is forbidden. This is how the Shulchan Aruch should read, rather than the printed phrase, 'Even idle conversation may not be excessive,' which is a scribal error. The sacred Zohar explicitly states that such speech desecrates Shabbos, as Daas Chochmah explains with sound reasoning. All the more so, it will certainly lead to forbidden speech. A conscientious person should establish a boundary so that he is not included, G-d forbid, among those who desecrate Shabbos. Daas Chochmah strongly challenges the custom of groups visiting friends on Shabbos, etc.; see his holy, enduring words at length. These are the words of Machazik Berachah. See above, paragraph 1, where we cited the same practice from the Arizal.
