(Seif katan 17) A sunken engraving, but a raised engraving, etc. The concern is that one may come to read ordinary documents, for the Sages decreed because they held that writing on a wall may be confused with a document. If we permit reading what is written on a wall, one may also read what is written on paper or in a document. When there is an additional distinction, namely that the writing is engraved below the surface, there are two differences: it is on a wall, and the writing is sunken. Such writing is unlike ordinary writing, and therefore will not be confused with documents. By contrast, with a tablet or ledger, even when the engraving is sunken, the Sages decreed lest one read ordinary documents, because there is only one difference, namely that the writing is sunken. A tablet or ledger resembles a document in that it is movable, so there is no distinction in that respect, as the Magen Avraham writes immediately afterward. Accordingly, if the writing is raised upon a wall, one might have thought that it should be permitted because there are two differences: it is on a wall, and the writing is raised, unlike a document in which the writing is merely inscribed. Thus, it should not be confused with a document. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because one may erase it, for raised writing can be erased, unlike sunken writing, which cannot be erased. And even if it is very high, etc. The Mechaber gives the reason for forbidding it even when it is very high as the concern that one may read, rather than the concern that one may erase combined with a rule that no distinction was made. This must be because the concern that one may read is the stronger rationale, since it is intrinsically forbidden and there is no need to invoke the rule that no distinction was made. The same is written in Tosefes Shabbos.
