(Seif katan 15) One may not, etc. This is because of borrowing and repaying, etc. For they say in Bava Metzia 75a, and likewise in Shabbos 148b, that members of a group who are exacting with one another, meaning that they are highly particular with one another and no one waives even a small amount for his fellow, although people do not ordinarily insist upon it, may come to violate a prohibition, etc., because of borrowing and repaying on Yom Tov. Tosafot explain that one may not use terminology of a loan or repayment. Rather, he must say, ‘Lend it to me,’ using the term for a temporary borrowing, and when returning it he must use the language of returning rather than repayment. They are afraid to use the language of borrowing because a borrowed object must be returned in its original form and is due immediately, and the borrower fears that the lender may demand it at once. He therefore uses the language of a loan, and consequently, when giving it back, he does not use the language of returning but of repayment. In this manner they violate a prohibition through their wording. This proves that the term ‘repayment’ is itself forbidden. The same therefore applies to the term ‘redemption,’ which is no better than ‘repayment.’ Moreover, with redemption it is impossible to alter the terminology. It is possible that even were it possible to alter the terminology of redemption, it would still be forbidden, since permission was granted only for a Shabbos need, as the Magen Avraham writes immediately afterward; and although redemption is a mitzvah, it may nevertheless be forbidden.
