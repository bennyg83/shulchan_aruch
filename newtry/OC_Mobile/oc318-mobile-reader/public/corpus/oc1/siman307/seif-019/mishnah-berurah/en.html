Merchandise that is being ruined, etc. He concludes regarding this that one may call a non-Jew, meaning even if the non-Jew will need to do a Torah-level melachah for this, such as carrying from a private domain to a public domain or the like. For mere moving of muktzeh, or from a private domain to a karmelis, it is permitted even to command him directly in a place of great loss.
It is permitted to call, etc. This too is permitted because a person is anxious over his property; if you do not permit him even in these ways, he may come to save it himself and perform a Torah-level melachah.
Whoever saves, etc. But to say to him directly, ‘If you save it, you will not lose,’ is forbidden. This is from Eliyah Rabbah, who brought it from Shiltei Gibborim, and so it is implied in Yoreh Deah 221:8.
And there is one who says, etc. One who is lenient has not lost. The Mechaber also rules simply this way below in siman 334, seif 26. To call a non-Jew to show him without saying anything to him is permitted according to everyone.
