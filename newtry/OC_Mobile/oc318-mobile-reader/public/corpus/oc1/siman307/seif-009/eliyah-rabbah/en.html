Provided that he does not, etc. The view of the Taz, Olas Tamid, and Magen Avraham is that if it is impossible to buy except on Shabbos, such as in villages where goods are available only on market day and the market is on Shabbos, then even if he gives the non-Jew money without specifying, it is forbidden, unlike what appears from the Beit Yosef. In Minchas Kohen he ruled like the Beit Yosef unless he says to buy on market day, which is like saying to buy on Shabbos; but this is not primary, and even without specification it is forbidden. The Magen Avraham wrote that the same applies to giving him an item to sell, and so wrote Masas Binyamin, adding that on Wednesday or Thursday it is permitted, as written in siman 253. This requires study, for it is certainly forbidden even from Sunday to tell a non-Jew to do something on Shabbos, as implied by the Levush and Masas Binyamin himself there, and as is explicit in the Rambam and Tur, who wrote simply that it is forbidden to say before Shabbos. This is also implied by the law of saying after Shabbos mentioned above, and by the responsum of Maharam there. Since it is impossible to sell or buy except on Shabbos, it is considered as if he explicitly said so, according to them, and it is not comparable to siman 252, seif 2, where he is not compelled to act on Shabbos, nor to a letter at the beginning of siman 247, where the Rama was nevertheless stringent. The Taz wrote that even regarding garments given to a launderer, where giving them to a non-Jew is permitted, there is a prohibition if he gives them close to Shabbos and says, ‘See, I need them on Motzaei Shabbos,’ for that is like explicitly saying that he should wash them on Shabbos. The same applies if he gives him money on erev Shabbos to buy and tells him that he will go on his way on Motzaei Shabbos. This is simple; see siman 252, seif 2. He further wrote that even where they permitted it when he did not mention buying on Shabbos, nevertheless the Jew may not benefit from it that Shabbos, even where he fixed a wage, like a lamp lit for a Jew. This requires study from siman 252, seif 4, where it is permitted to wear the garment; see seif katan 9 there. Furthermore, there the work is a Torah-level melachah, whereas here buying and selling is D'Rabbanan.
