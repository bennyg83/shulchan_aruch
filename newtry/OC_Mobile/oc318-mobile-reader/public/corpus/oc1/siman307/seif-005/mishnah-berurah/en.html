(19) Only because of shevut — but what is Torah prohibition forbidden via non-Jew even for mitzvah, as below 331 seif 6; therefore forbidden to tell a non-Jew to light a candle to learn and pray.
(20) And it is when there is, etc. — since the prohibition is only rabbinic and telling a non-Jew is also only rabbinic — it is shevut di-shevut; they did not decree in such cases.
(21) Slight illness — for real illness comprising the whole body or danger to a limb — even an Israelite does shevut for one opinion, as 328 s.k. 17; and know we do not compare matters in shevut — one may permit only what the sages said; for milah they did not permit telling a non-Jew shevut where there is Torah melacha, as 331 seif 6; and buying a house via non-Jew permitted, as 306 s.k. 11.
(22) Great need — see Magen Avraham who concludes only in great loss; but otherwise no leniency; and Acharonim question even this way; see Shulchan Aruch HaRav regarding merchandise lost by rain.
(23) And some forbid — they hold only for milah the Gemara permitted shevut di-shevut [such as bringing a knife via non-Jew through an unjoined courtyard to circumcise with it] because milah itself overrides Shabbos; but not for another mitzvah; see Levush and Acharonim: halacha follows the first view.
(24) Even Torah melacha — via non-Jew for mitzvah; however Rama already wrote above to be strict; and so is the essence, for this view is only an individual opinion — Rif, Rosh, Rambam, and many Rishonim dispute; only for milah Magen Avraham tends below 331 to rely on this view; see Acharonim there. And know what we rule telling a non-Jew is shevut — even if he tells a non-Jew to tell another non-Jew to do melacha for him on Shabbos [Gaon R' Gershon, author of Avodat HaGershoni]; and Chavot Yair siman 53 tends to leniency; all the more in what is only shevut; see Sefer HaChaim that in great loss one may rely on the lenient; see what I wrote s.k. 11 — applies here too.
