The citation to Rashi should read Shabbos 150a, s.v. “Of the king.” Rashi's words, “which the king does not need,” imply that whenever another person needs the calculation it is forbidden. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 18, who inclines toward permitting it. Nevertheless, it remains forbidden as idle conversation; see seif 1. Know that according to Rambam, chapter 23, halachah 18, calculations are forbidden lest one come to write. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 17. The Levush, however, derives the prohibition from “refraining from pursuing your affairs,” and Rashi on Kesubos 5a, s.v. “Because,” likewise cites “speaking of matters.” Magen Avraham in siman 306, seif katan 12, cites Maggid Mishneh as stating that no shevus was permitted for a mitzvah except the prohibition of “speaking of matters,” because the Torah itself permitted speech concerning affairs of Heaven. According to Rambam, then, who forbids calculations as a shevus lest one write, how were calculations for a mitzvah permitted? Instructing a non-Jew was permitted for a mitzvah only where it constitutes a double shevus. As for clearing space for guests in siman 333, seif 1, one may answer that excessive exertion was permitted for a mitzvah, but other forms of shevus were not permitted at all merely for a mitzvah. See Terumas HaDeshen, siman 72, which indicates that clearing the space is itself a shevus. With Hashem's help this will be explained there in siman 333.
