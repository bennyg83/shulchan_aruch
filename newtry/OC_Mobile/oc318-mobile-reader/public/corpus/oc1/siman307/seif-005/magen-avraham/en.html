Slight illness — for real illness comprising the whole body or danger to a limb, even an Israelite does shevut for one opinion, as 328 s.k. 17; and shevut not in its usual way even for one who is distressed permitted, as there s.k. 33. And know we do not compare one matter to another in shevut and one may permit only what the sages said; for milah they did not permit shevut, as 331 seif 6; and buying a house through non-Jew permitted, as 306 s.k. 11 — until here the Maggid Mishneh ch. 6; and it appears from his words he emended in Rambam "slight illness or distressed" [Maggid Mishneh]; and so Tur in his name; and some emend "or great need"; but from Raavad it appears he emended as written: "impossible if slight illness he himself does," etc., and "they said in loss such as a pipe," as 336 s.k. 9 — until here. And in Hagahot Maimoniot ch. Beitzah he wrote regarding pipe permitted lest he repair by hand; other shevut forbidden, as 334 s.k. 26; and Rivash siman 387 wrote even telling non-Jew to do shevut forbidden even in loss, for extinguishing is melacha not needed for itself; also non-Jew who brought a gift forbidden though only rabbinic prohibition — see there as 325; and Ran ch. Barrel wrote likewise one version: shevut in its usual way forbidden even via non-Jew in loss; and one answer: extinguishing is different since if needed for itself he would be Torah-liable, and not everyone is expert in this — therefore forbidden even via non-Jew. And in 334 seif 2 some say even shevut di-shevut forbidden, as Rashba responsa 484 and 482; therefore it appears in great loss one may permit shevut via non-Jew even in its usual way, and shevut by Israelite not in its usual way; but otherwise no leniency — it deals with 9.
And some forbid — they hold for milah shevut di-shevut is permitted, for milah itself overrides Shabbos; requires study: in Eruvin it should be said that in time of danger they brought Torah scroll from roof to courtyard to read — requires saying we do not compare shevut, as above.
