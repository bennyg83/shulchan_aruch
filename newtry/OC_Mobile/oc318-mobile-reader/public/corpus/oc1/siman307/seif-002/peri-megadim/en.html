As for hinting on Friday that the act should be done the next day, although speech is forbidden either by the words of the Sages or according to the Torah through “speaking of matters,” as explained in paragraph 1, the Torah permitted thought. See Shabbos 103a. Instructing a non-Jew, however, is a D'Rabbanan shevus and is more stringent in this respect. Even a hint intended to make him understand that he should perform the act on Shabbos is forbidden, as the Rama writes at the end of this siman, seif 22. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 31. The Rama's silence here may be because he relied upon what he wrote at the end of the siman, following the Tur. It is also possible that he concedes here to the Semag that hinting during the week that the act should be performed on the following Shabbos is more lenient than hinting on Shabbos itself, although at first glance I do not know the basis for distinguishing between them. This leniency applies only when the melachah will be performed outside the Jew's home and the Jew will derive no benefit from it on Shabbos, but only during the week. This is certainly true in the case discussed in siman 246. The Levush rules in seif 2 that it is permitted, but in seif 22 he rules in accordance with the Rama and adds that the act is to be performed on Shabbos. There he explains that when the instruction is given on Shabbos, the non-Jew appears to be the Jew's agent. This is so even though a non-Jew cannot halachically serve as an agent, for the prohibition was enacted lest the Jew come to perform the act himself, as Rambam writes in chapter 6, halachah 1. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 8.
