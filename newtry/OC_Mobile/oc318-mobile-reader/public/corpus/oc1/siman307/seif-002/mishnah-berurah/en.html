(7) Forbidden, etc. — this seif and the following seifim that are forbidden are because of "pursuing your affairs," for all are included in doing one's affairs.

(8) To hire workers for him — and the same for anything forbidden for an Israelite to do by law — forbidden to tell a non-Jew to do it; but in what is only custom and a mere stringency — permitted to tell a non-Jew to do it.

(9) On Shabbat — and even if he tells the non-Jew to hire workers after Shabbat — also forbidden [Chayei Adam].

(10) Even though he understands, etc. — meaning nevertheless permitted since he does not say explicitly, only by hint; and it is not similar to what Rama wrote in the gloss in seif 22 that even by hint to do on Shabbat forbidden — there when he hints on Shabbat itself about it they are stricter.

(11) That he wants him to do it, etc. — nevertheless the Israelite may not benefit from that melacha until after Shabbat, as law of a non-Jew who did melacha for an Israelite; or it deals with something the Israelite's body does not benefit from, such as there is already a candle and fire and the non-Jew adds — otherwise even if the non-Jew lit by himself forbidden for the Israelite to benefit [Acharonim].
