(25) And specifically while still, etc. — for he needs to know how much to give them — these are needed accounts forbidden because of "and speak a matter," as at the start of the siman.
(26) With him — and even if afterward it becomes clear from the account he owes them nothing — nevertheless since initially this was unknown and he needed the account for that — there is prohibition; but if from the start he knew he owes them nothing — idle accounts permitted; and if those accounts benefit his fellow and he calculates for his fellow's need — forbidden.
(27) Permitted — Rambam wrote: accounts without need permitted to calculate — how many se'im of grain we had in such a year, how many dinars we spent at our son's wedding, such-and-such king's levies, and similar idle talk without need — end of his words; Maggid Mishneh explained he hinted we should refrain because of idle talk unbecoming for G-d-fearing; and in Beurei Rashal also wrote even on a weekday not so proper for a Torah scholar — sitting of scoffers and neglecting study [Acharonim].
