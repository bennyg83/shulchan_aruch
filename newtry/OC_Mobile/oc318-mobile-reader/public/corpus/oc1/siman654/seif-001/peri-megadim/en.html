But. She should not replace. See the Taz and Rashi, Sukkah 42a in the Mishnah; see Beis Yosef and Magen Avraham. It should read 'tircha' without a vav, and so it is in Rashi; meaning, it appears as though he intends to repair a utensil, or perhaps it is excessive exertion. It implies that to place a lulav in water on Yom Tov, where it had not been in water before, is nevertheless permitted; only excessive exertion is prohibited. See siman 336:11 and Magen Avraham 13. In Kapos Temarim he wrote that from Rashi it implies that placing it in water where it had not been is prohibited, like flowers whose myrtle berries open, and even returning them is prohibited; see there. See the Levush, who says that it is excessive exertion, implying that to place it in water ab initio is permitted on Yom Tov. On Shabbos, if he erred and removed it from water, it is prohibited to return it, for it is muktzeh, as in siman 658; see Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim. This requires investigation: with muktzeh, as long as it is in his hand, he may return it to whatever place he wants. Perhaps returning it to water is prohibited in such a case; this requires investigation. With Hashem's help, in the Magen Avraham here, and in simanim 655 and 658, I will explain more regarding muktzeh in such a case.
