(s.k. 3) And he cancels them. Even though something that has a permitted form, etc.; here, since he does not benefit, etc. This is the difficulty of the Talmud and its answer.
And as he wrote in siman 677, the Tur wrote. (And it is a "there are those who say" there in the Shulchan Aruch:) Oil of a Chanukah lamp, even if mixed with permitted oil, it is forbidden to add upon it until sixty; and that is the view of Maharam, who holds that even regarding a rabbinic issur one may not add upon it in order to cancel it. And Maharam wrote: and this is not comparable to wood that fell from the palm into the oven on Yom Tov, upon which one adds, etc.; for that case is different, in that he does not benefit from them until after their burning; but here he benefits at the time the lamp is burning; end quote. And if so, from this Magen Avraham learns that even according to the view of Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah, siman 99, who holds that regarding every rabbinic issur it is permitted to add, as above: nevertheless here, since it is something that has a permitted form, it would have been proper to forbid; except that it is permitted to add because the issur is burned away. And since it is clarified from the words of Maharam that if he benefits from its light, the fact that the issur is burned away does not help, for then the issur is still present: if so, for us too, since here it is something that has a permitted form, it is forbidden to benefit from its light even though the issur is burned away, since then the issur is still present. And Magen Avraham now holds that likewise it is forbidden to warm oneself opposite it, for this is comparable to benefiting from its light; for the warming comes while the issur is still present.
And so it is implied in the Rosh, etc. who wrote in the name of Baal HaTerumah to forbid both wicks and torches that remained on the first day of Rosh Hashana or on Shabbos before Yom Tov, to light them on the second day of Rosh Hashana or on Yom Tov after Shabbos, because of preparation; as he wrote at the end of siman 501. And he concluded: unless he adds prepared wood upon the torches. And he did not also write a remedy for wicks to add upon them; for he included the prohibition of torches and wicks in one clause. He should have likewise included in the permission of the torches also the wicks; rather, necessarily, regarding wicks, since they were made to give light, they have no remedy by adding upon them, since he benefits from them at the time of their burning.
And so it is implied in Bava Kamma folio 101, where it is stated there that the prohibition of Shemittah does not fall upon wood, as it is written regarding Shemittah "for eating." And therefore Shemittah applies only to something similar to eating, whose benefit and destruction are equal; which is not so of wood, whose benefit comes after its destruction. And it objects: but there are oil-woods. And Rashi explained that they are oily woods that stand to give light, whose benefit and destruction are equal, so Shemittah should fall upon them. And it answers: most woods, even oily ones, stand for heating; and in heating, the benefit comes after its destruction; thus far the sense of the Gemara. From this it is proved that what was made to give light, its benefit is at the time of its destruction, and "burned away" does not apply.
And this requires further study, for Tosafos, etc. For it is stated there on 5a: what is written, "but on the first day you shall put away leaven," that is to say, on the eve of Yom Tov; for if on Yom Tov, Rabbi Akiva holds that the only destruction of chametz is burning, and it is written "you shall do no melacha," and we find burning to be a primary melacha. And Tosafos asked: but chametz is among the things that are burned, and we hold that of all things that are burned, their ash is permitted; and if so, he could destroy the chametz on Yom Tov, and when he makes coals then it is permitted to benefit from them; and if so, the burning is a need of Yom Tov, for by the coals of the chametz he will bake or roast. And they answered with a later answer, and this is their language: alternatively, since from the beginning of the burning he cannot benefit from it, even though he will have benefit from it afterward, it is forbidden, like vows and freewill offerings, etc.; end quote of Tosafos. And from this language it is implied as Magen Avraham wrote, that the view of Tosafos is that since he cannot benefit at the beginning of the burning, it is forbidden. (And the reason to forbid is not because his primary intent is to fulfill the mitzvah of destruction, and for that reason vows and freewill offerings too are forbidden; and so wrote Tosafos in Shabbos folio 24b, s.v. "because there is no," etc., that the reason is that the primary burning is not for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul; and not like the implication of Tosafos of Pesachim above. And see Beitzah folio 27b, Tosafos s.v. "and upon the challah," etc.)
It is forbidden to burn, etc. And if so, here regarding forbidden wood, even though he cancels them in a majority, nevertheless it is forbidden to benefit at the beginning of their burning by warming oneself or benefiting from their light. If so, even though in the end he will be permitted to benefit, nevertheless according to the words of Tosafos it would be forbidden to heat with them.
And one can say that it is permitted to warm oneself, etc. At first glance his intent in this is not to resolve the words of Tosafos of Pesachim. That is to say: even though Tosafos wrote that whatever one cannot benefit from at the beginning of the burning is forbidden to destroy, nevertheless here it is permitted. For even though it is forbidden to benefit from its light, as Maharam wrote as above, nevertheless it is permitted to warm oneself opposite it. And even though the reason of the permission of warming is that the heat comes from that little that has already been finished, nevertheless it is called that benefit comes to him from the beginning of the burning, and it is permitted to destroy it. If so, the difficulty of Tosafos returns to its place: for if so, also with chametz it is permitted to warm oneself opposite it immediately, for this very reason; and if so, he can still destroy the chametz on Yom Tov, since he can benefit from it immediately by warming himself; regarding muktzeh which is also forbidden for benefit. Rather, necessarily he did not come in this to resolve the words of Tosafos, but stated a law: that above it was obvious to him that from Maharam forbidding benefit from its light, likewise it is forbidden to warm oneself opposite it; and upon this he now wrote that it is not comparable: for even though benefiting from its light is forbidden, nevertheless warming oneself is permitted. However, his language implies that he came to resolve the words of Tosafos. For certainly the words of Tosafos are true, that whatever one cannot benefit from at the beginning of its destruction is forbidden to destroy. And even so, here it is permitted to heat with them, for it is possible to benefit from them from its beginning, namely to warm oneself; even though the reason of the permission is that the heat comes from that portion that has already burned, nevertheless it is called benefiting immediately, like one who heats with permitted wood to cook or bake, for that too is not fit to bake and cook immediately. And even so, that of chametz is fine, that it is forbidden to destroy it on Yom Tov; and why not say he can benefit immediately by warming himself opposite it? And one can say that Magen Avraham follows his own view, which he wrote in siman 501, s.k. 3, in the name of the Ran; and so he wrote in siman 511, that a fire to warm oneself was permitted only because of "since it was permitted for a need," etc.; and if so, Rabbi Akiva according to his own view, who does not hold "since," as is stated there in that sugya where Rava said, learn from Rabbi Akiva three things, one of them that we do not say "since"; see there.
And one can say: according to this the words of Tosafos there on the reverse side are difficult, who asked against the words of Rava who said, learn from this that Rabbi Akiva does not hold "since." And the simple meaning is that if we say that he holds that we say "since," it is permitted to destroy the chametz because of "since." And Tosafos wrote, and this is their language: and even though we require some need of the day, here what he destroys is a need; end quote. And according to what I wrote, without this it is proved that he does not hold "since"; for if not so, even if you say that the mitzvah of destroying chametz is not called a need, nevertheless since its ash is permitted, he is permitted to burn it and warm himself opposite it, as above. That is not so; for Tosafos wrote first another answer to their above difficulty, and this is their language: since chametz is among the things that are burned, etc.; and Rabbeinu Yitzchak said that it is reasonable that the verse "you shall put away" deals with every circumstance, even if he does not need its coal; alternatively, since from the beginning of the burning, etc., as above; end quote. And the two answers do not disagree with each other for the practical law. If so, it is not proved that Rabbi Akiva does not hold "since" from the fact that burning was not permitted to destroy the chametz and warm oneself opposite it; for one can say that Rabbi Akiva's proof is like Tosafos's first answer, that the verse deals with every circumstance even if he need not warm himself. Rather, necessarily Rava's proof is as its simple meaning: that destroying chametz itself should have been permitted because of "since"; and with this the words of Tosafos are fine, that we require some need of the day. And according to what I wrote in siman 325, that muktzeh is not forbidden for benefit, etc., there is no difficulty. That is to say: the words of Tosafos of Pesachim are fine; for here, since it is permitted for benefit, it is permitted to benefit immediately at the beginning of its burning by warming oneself or benefiting from its light; which is not so of chametz and likewise Chanukah oil, which are forbidden for benefit. And know that above in siman 501 in Magen Avraham s.k. 12 I brought in the name of the book Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote there that muktzeh is forbidden for benefit, and that is the opposite of what he wrote here. There is no difficulty; for certainly Magen Avraham for himself holds that muktzeh is forbidden for benefit. And even though in siman 325 he wrote in the name of Tosafos that it is permitted for benefit, in truth there too Magen Avraham asked against their words; see there. And here he wrote only according to the one who holds that muktzeh is permitted for benefit, namely Tosafos as he wrote in siman 325. The words of Tosafos of Pesachim are fine; and one can also say that Tosafos of Pesachim follow their own view, who hold that muktzeh is permitted for benefit; but Magen Avraham according to himself does not concede to Tosafos in this, and holds that muktzeh is forbidden for benefit; and see what I wrote below at the end of siman 509.
And so it is implied in siman 276, where he wrote: a non-Jew who heated on Shabbos in a Jew's house with a Jew's wood of his own accord: the Jew need not leave the room. And one might say: but it is forbidden to benefit from muktzeh, and the wood on Shabbos is muktzeh; so how is he permitted to benefit from the heat? Rather, necessarily muktzeh is permitted for benefit.
