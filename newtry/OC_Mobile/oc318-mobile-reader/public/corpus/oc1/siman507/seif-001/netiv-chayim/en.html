(Magen Avraham s.k. 4) He omits, for they are one reason. Marginal note: and it appears to me that this is the meaning: the Rif and the Rosh forbade them: the Rabbis forbade, that is to say, the Sages further forbade for a new reason, a decree lest one climb up and pick. And if you say, all is difficult: why are two reasons needed? And there is a further difficulty: for wood that fell is an uncommon matter, so why did the Rabbis decree regarding it lest one climb up and pick? And I am answered in this: that if he sits and waits, the reason of nolad does not apply, as Tosafos wrote in Chullin folio 14, s.v. "one may cut"; but if he sits and waits, it is common that they fall; if so, it is forbidden lest one climb up and pick. And if you say, why these reasons at all, since on Yom Tov after Shabbos it is forbidden because of preparation? The answer is that ordinary Yom Tov we decree because of Yom Tov after Shabbos, as we say above regarding an egg. Maharashif and Magen Avraham and Taz answered; and the words of Maharsha too can be aligned to their intent in this way, and that is correct: for it is unlike an egg; there we decree that if we permit an egg born on Yom Tov we would also permit an egg born on Yom Tov after Shabbos; but here there is no room to decree that if we permit on Yom Tov wood that fell, people will come to err if wood fell on Shabbos and permit it on Yom Tov; for between Yom Tov and Shabbos people do not err. And according to the view of the Ran, without this there is no difficulty, for Yom Tov after Shabbos itself is rabbinic; and if we decree Yom Tov on account of Yom Tov after Shabbos, it is a decree upon a decree. And now it is very difficult against the view of Maharshal that preparation is according to the Torah; and in chapter 1 of Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 16, in his critique of R' Yechiel, see there: he was not satisfied with that first Tosafos; if so, the difficulty stands in its place, and the ruling of R' Yechiel is firm and established.
