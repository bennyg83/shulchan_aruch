Darkei Moshe wrote, etc. And therefore all are forbidden. That is to say: we should have forbidden all of them.
And as the Tur wrote in siman 504, as it ought to be written; and so it is in the Shulchan Aruch there, se'if 3: to pound groats in a large mortar is forbidden, and in a small one it is permitted; and since we are not expert which is called small or large, all is forbidden.
That it is permitted to heat, etc. And there is in it no concern of hardening. And necessarily it deals with wanting to bake there; for if not so, for nothing it is certainly forbidden on Yom Tov; and so it is clarified there explicitly that it deals with wanting to bake there, as he wrote in se'if 5. And one might say it is forbidden lest it diminish. It implies that ordinary ovens of ours are called small.
In Yoreh De'ah, siman 97, as it ought to be written. Regarding bread that he baked with the roast in one oven, that there is concern of aroma being a matter; for there is a distinction between a large oven and a small oven; see there. Therefore one must say that we too are expert what a small oven is.
Of matzah. And Peri Chadash wrote in a later kuntres on Yoreh De'ah, siman 97, that this means that every loaf should not be a handbreadth thick, like the law of matzah; for if not so, you have given your words to measures: if he makes the bread very thick he will be able to bake a great deal even in a small oven, and all depends on the thickness of the loaf. Further Peri Chadash wrote there that it is obvious that the floor of the oven holds twelve tenths at once. And in Pleisi, siman 108, he wrote the opposite: that they gave the measure for their ovens, in which they stuck dough to the oven walls, and one must say that the walls hold twelve tenths; and according to this, the floor of the oven is not counted, for they did not bake at all on the oven floor, only to stick to the sides.
