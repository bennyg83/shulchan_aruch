From yesterday evening. Or that his mind was upon the clay in the street (Mordechai). And in Beis Yosef: I do not know what this teaches us. And it appears to me that it teaches us that even though he did not make a sign in it, it is permitted; for specifically clay in the river that is still attached to the ground needs to be detached from yesterday, but clay lying in the street is detached and does not need a sign. However, specifically clay that does not need kneading, similar to that on the riverbank which is presumably already kneaded.
To knead ash. And even according to the view in siman 324, se'if 3, that for something not subject to kneading, placing water is its kneading: nevertheless here it is permitted, since it is for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul, to preserve its heat; but with clay it is forbidden, for it appears like preparing for building. And in Yam Shel Shlomo he ruled like Rabbeinu Tam, that it is forbidden to knead the ash with water, only to place it upon it as is; see the end of siman 259.
