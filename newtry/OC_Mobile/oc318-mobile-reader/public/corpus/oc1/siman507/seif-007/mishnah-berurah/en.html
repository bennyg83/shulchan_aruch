{36} It is permitted to seal the mouth of the oven - For example, on Friday that falls on Yom Tov when he needs to insulate the cooked foods for tomorrow, or on Yom Tov itself so that the food shrink further.
{37} With clay and mire on the riverbank - For because of the abundance of water they are commonly soft and do not need kneading. And even though nevertheless he needs to smear them upon the oven at the time of sealing, and smearing too is a primary melacha: smearing is different, for it is impossible to do from before Yom Tov, and it is permitted for eating need.
{38} And that is when he softened it from yesterday evening - The language is murky, for we are dealing with what is already soft. And see Biur Halacha what we wrote on this.
{39} Or he made a sign in it - Because of preparation.
{40} And he detached it to another side - For if not so he makes a hole by taking it [Bach]. And regarding mire or softened clay in the street that are detached from the ground, the view of Magen Avraham is that one needs no sign or detaching at all, only that his mind be upon them from before Yom Tov to seal the mouth of the oven with them. And from the Levush it is implied that one should not distinguish in this; and see Biur Halacha where we brought a proof for the words of the Levush.
{41} But to knead, etc. - There are those who wrote the reason that this kneading to seal the oven is only preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul, which are permitted only when impossible to do from before Yom Tov; and here it was possible to knead it beforehand; and according to this reason there is in this a Torah prohibition. And there are those who wrote that sealing the oven so that it cook or so that the food shrink is called food-preparation needs of the soul itself, like burning; and even so the Sages forbade because they will say that he kneads for building.
{42} Clay - And likewise dust is something subject to kneading.
{43} And it is permitted to knead ash - Not only according to the view that on Shabbos one is not liable for kneading ash because it does not stick together by the kneading, is it certainly permitted; rather even according to the view in the Gemara that regarding something not subject to kneading, such as ash, from the moment he places water in it he is liable: nevertheless here it is permitted, for it is for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul. And the two reasons above do not apply regarding ash; for from before Yom Tov it was impossible for him to knead, for ash does not hold water and dries; and the second reason, that it appears like kneading for building, also does not apply to ash, for one does not knead ash for building, and everyone knows that he prepares it for the need of the oven. Nevertheless, if he has kneaded clay it is forbidden to knead ash on Yom Tov. And there are those who wrote to be stringent about kneading ash on Yom Tov. And even though that is not so according to the essential law, nevertheless one who is stringent, may blessing come upon him. And all this deals with ash that they heated from before Yom Tov, which is not muktzeh, as above in siman 498, se'if 15.
