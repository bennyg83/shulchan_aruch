And that is when he softened it from yesterday evening - See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah. And the words of the Mechaber: their beginning is the words of the Rambam and their end is the words of Rashi; and it is astonishing that he combined them together. And in truth they are separate matters and divided in explaining the words of the Talmud, as we will clarify. For it is stated in the Gemara: Ravina said to Rav Ashi: Rav Acha of Hutzal said to us that the master sealed an oven for him on a festival day (and Rashi explained that the difficulty is because of kneading on Yom Tov). He said to him: We rely on the bank of the Euphrates (and Rashi explained that one need not knead clay, for the bank of the Euphrates is kneaded clay). And this applies where he marked it from yesterday (Rashi explained: he made a sign in it and detached it to another side); see there. And according to this, clay on the riverbank is because of the aspect of kneading, and "he marked it from yesterday" is because of preparation. And the Rambam, as it appears, explained the opposite: that the question was because of muktzeh clay, and to this he answered him that we rely on the bank of the Euphrates and it is for us like prepared and standing ready; and upon this they conclude, "and that is where he marked it from yesterday," that is to say, that he kneaded it; for if not so, what does it help us that it is prepared, since he needs to knead? And if so, the words of the Mechaber who copied the two reasonings together and wrote "or he made," etc., are very difficult. And one can still force an explanation in the words of the Rambam that he explains well like Rashi's explanation, that on the riverbank it is because of concern of kneading; except that nevertheless one may be concerned that that clod of clay that he takes was softened today, and if so from yesterday it was muktzeh; therefore the Talmud concludes "and that is where he marked it from yesterday," and this "marked" is like "he surveyed from yesterday," that is to say, he supervised from yesterday that clod of clay that was softened; like "he surveyed his property" (Chullin 108). And so is the reading in the Maggid Mishneh and in Shibbolei HaLeket, laws of Yom Tov, siman 250. And according to this the intent is not that he softened it, but that it was soft already. And all this is in explaining the words of the Rambam; but the words of the Mechaber are still not fine even with this explanation; for even if we say so, nevertheless the Rambam disagrees with Rashi: according to Rashi one needs to designate with an act, and according to the Rambam one needs no designation at all, only to see that it was not kneaded on Yom Tov itself; and he combined the two reasonings; and this requires further study. And were I not afraid, I would say that one should read "and he made a sign in it," etc., namely that the Mechaber was concerned to satisfy also the view of Rashi. And I saw in Olas Tamid who explains "or he made a sign in it," etc., that is to say, and even if it was kneaded he needs to make a sign in it because of muktzeh; see there. And according to his words too it is difficult: what does "or" mean that he said, unless we read "and he made a sign in it," etc., and explain as our explanation above; and this requires further study.
And he detached it to another side - See Mishnah Berurah, what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham. And in truth, according to what we wrote above in Biur Halacha in explaining the words of the Rambam, so too is proved; for he explained "we rely on the bank of the Euphrates" because of muktzeh, thus showing that if he only relies in his mind, that is enough to remove it from muktzeh. However, it is difficult against this: for even if we say that thought removes from muktzeh and we do not need an act, as we hold above in siman 308, se'if 20 and 21, nevertheless this does not help regarding dust and clay in the street or on the riverbank, for they are certainly nullified upon the ground; for we hold above in siman 308, se'if 38, and siman 498, se'if 17, that a person may bring in a basketful of dust while still day and designate a corner for them, and he is permitted to use them for all his needs; thus showing that in any case he needs to designate a place for them. And in Shabbos folio 50 it is clarified explicitly that this law is according to the view that thought removes from muktzeh; for according to the view that an act removes, designating a corner does not help until there is a proving act; see there in Rashi. And from this it is proved that even according to the view that thought suffices, nevertheless he needs to bring in and designate a place; for if not so, it is not called thought at all. And if so, what we say "and that is where he marked it from yesterday" is not because it is attached to the ground, but because he needs to mark and designate the clay that he intends to use from tomorrow; and if so, even detached clay also needs to be designated at least in a place designated for this, and not by mere thought. And from this is a proof for the view of the Levush, who wrote that even for clay in the street one needs from before Yom Tov to make a sign in the place he wishes to take and to detach it to one side so that it be truly prepared; end quote.
But to knead clay on Yom Tov is forbidden - See the Rosh, from whom it is proved that even if he already placed water upon it while still day, even so it is forbidden on Yom Tov to knead it. And see above in siman 321 in Biur Halacha, s.v. "one does not knead," where we proved that both according to Rabbi and according to Rabbi Yehuda son of Rabbi Yehuda there is in this the prohibition of kneading.
And it is permitted to knead ash - See the Rosh, who wished to say that this permission of kneading ash follows only Rabbi [and according to Rabbi Yehuda son of Rabbi Yehuda it is forbidden in every circumstance], and that it deals with a case where he already placed water upon it while still day, for placing that is its kneading, and therefore it is permitted. But from the fact that the Mechaber stated it simply, it is implied that he holds like the other poskim that it is permitted in every circumstance; so wrote Olas Shabbos; and it is obvious.
