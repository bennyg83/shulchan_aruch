{5} Even though wood that fell, etc. - Because of muktzeh, for they were attached at twilight; and further because of lest one climb up and pick; as was clarified above in siman 501, se'if 5.
{6} On the Shabbos before it - Because Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov, as will be clarified below in siman 513, se'if 5.
{7} If they fell on Yom Tov - And the same applies if a non-Jew placed them in the oven without a Jew's knowledge. And it deals with a case where there was already wood in the oven, only that there was not a majority there opposite the wood that fell; for in such a case they add and cancel a rabbinic issur, as is clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 99, se'if 5; see there. And even according to the Rema there who is stringent not to cancel issur l'chatchilah even in such a case, he agrees here because the issur is burned, as below.
{8} He adds upon them prepared wood, etc. - And even though this wood has a permitted form tomorrow, and something that has a permitted form is not canceled even in a thousand, and even regarding rabbinic issurim: this issur is lighter still, for the primary benefit of the wood comes after heating, when he bakes with it [or warms his house], and then the issur is already not present; and in such a case the Sages were not stringent because it is something that has a permitted form. And also if after heating he wishes to set a pot to cook opposite the fire [or to warm his body, or even to benefit from the light of the fire] while the wood is burning, it is permitted; for muktzeh is not forbidden for benefit that comes of itself, only that it is forbidden to move muktzeh or to use it even without moving, as above in siman 501 in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 23 and 24. And here, since he uses it with permission for baking bread, automatically it is permitted to benefit also in other benefits, even though were it not for the benefits the wood would be muktzeh. And there are those who are lenient also to heat l'chatchilah in order to set a pot then, or to warm oneself opposite the fire; for even though the wood is still burning, nevertheless the primary benefit is the eating, and at the time he eats he benefits only from heat in the cooked food, and that heat is already separated from the fire in the wood that is present; and likewise one who warms himself opposite the fire: the primary benefit comes to him from the heat, and the issur has already been finished. However, to heat in order to benefit from its light is certainly forbidden, for he benefits from the fire that is present; as below in siman 677, se'if 4, regarding a Chanukah lamp; and see Biur Halacha.
{9} That the issur wood not be recognizable - For when they are recognizable, cancellation does not apply.
{10} Provided that he not touch them - And even though at the time he adds wood upon them and mixes them the prior wood is jostled, we are not concerned about this, for it is like moving from the side.
{11} Until they are canceled in a majority - And then when he turns them over at the time of lighting them from corner to corner, he turns with permission. And it implies that immediately from the moment they were canceled it is permitted to turn them over, even though he has not yet lit them. And know that there are poskim who are especially lenient in this law; and according to them, even if there was no wood at all in the oven, only what fell from the palm, it is also permitted to mix; and even if the issur wood will be recognizable after mixing in a majority, they are also not concerned, only that he not move the issur wood when he sees them. And all is for the reason above, that the issur is burned and benefit comes after the issur is not present; and see Biur Halacha what we wrote on this.
{12} It is forbidden to heat with them on the following Yom Tov - Because this picking prepares them for heating; and since on Shabbos they are not fit for heating, if so Shabbos prepares them for the need of tomorrow, which is Yom Tov; and Shabbos does not prepare for Yom Tov.
{13} Even by cancellation in a majority - For the prohibition of preparation is according to the Torah, and regarding a Torah matter one does not cancel issur.
