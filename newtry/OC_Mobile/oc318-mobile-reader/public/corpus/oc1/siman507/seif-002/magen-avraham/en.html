Darkei Moshe wrote: Beis Yosef wrote that in a small oven it is permitted; and even though we are not expert which is called small or large, and therefore all are forbidden, as the Tur wrote in siman 504; nevertheless, since the Tur wrote in this siman that it is permitted to heat a new oven, we learn from this that ordinary ovens of ours are not called large; and so it was clarified in Yoreh De'ah, siman 97; end quote. And further it appears to me to be lenient, for it is not the way of our ovens to diminish; and further, a large oven is one that holds twelve tenths of matzah; see what I wrote in siman 447. And it is implied from here and from the language of the Rambam that in any case there is no prohibition to inaugurate a new oven, for it is not finished with heating until he temper it with cold, as he wrote in se'if 5. However, one can say that this is specifically in their ovens, which were made like a pottery pot; but in our ovens they are certainly finished with the first heating, for the clay dries and is strengthened by heating, and it is worse than cooking in new pots, which is forbidden, as he wrote at the end of siman 502; and see what I wrote in se'if 5. Inside the oven. At first glance it implies that he stated "inside the oven" because other wood is commonly there, and therefore even though that wood is not more than this wood, it is permitted to add upon them; but if it fell in a place where there is no wood, it is forbidden to add upon them. And so he ruled in Yoreh De'ah, siman 99, se'if 6; and so ruled the Ran. And as for what he wrote in siman 501, se'if 6 in the gloss, that it is permitted to add upon vessels: that case is different, for the vessel is still permitted, only that after it begins to burn it will be issur; and that is not canceling issur.
That they not be recognizable, etc. And Yam Shel Shlomo siman 6 wrote that even if they are recognizable it is permitted, provided that he not move the issur wood when he sees them. And even though regarding other issur, when it is recognizable cancellation does not apply, here we are lenient because the issur is burned away. And the Bach wrote that in practice this requires further study; and see siman 626, se'if 1, what he wrote.
And he cancels them. Even though something that has a permitted form is not canceled, here, since one does not benefit from them until after they have been burned, baking bread with them is permitted; but it is forbidden to warm oneself with them at the time of their burning or to benefit from their light (as he wrote in siman 677; and the Eshel Avraham did not look carefully in the Tur). And so it is implied in the Rosh, who wrote regarding torches that one can cancel them in a majority, and did not write so regarding wicks; see the end of siman 501. And so it is implied in Bava Kamma folio 101b. And this requires further study, for Tosafos wrote in Pesachim folio 5 that anything from which one cannot benefit at the beginning of its burning is forbidden to burn on Yom Tov; see siman 446. And one can say that it is permitted to warm oneself with them at the time of their burning, for in any case the benefit comes to him from the heat, and the issur has already been finished; but to benefit from their light is forbidden, for he benefits from the light itself. And according to what I wrote in siman 325, se'if 4, there is no difficulty: for muktzeh is not forbidden for benefit, and the prohibition of heating is because of moving wood; and so it is implied in siman 276, se'if 1.
In an oven on Shabbos. For even though it is not actual preparation, nevertheless it is akin to Torah preparation, and one does not cancel a Torah issur. And in the Tur it is implied that it is according to the Torah. And in Yam Shel Shlomo he wrote that the picking is complete preparation. Nevertheless it is implied in the Ran that it is not Torah preparation, since if he had a sick person he would be permitted to cook with this wood; if so, Shabbos prepared it for itself; and see siman 513. And if they were detached on Yom Tov, according to everyone they are not forbidden on Shabbos except rabbinically; for according to the Torah it is permitted to heat with them on Yom Tov itself, only that the Rabbis forbade them as a decree lest one climb up and pick; and further, it is nolad, as Tosafos wrote on folio 2; and so wrote the Meor regarding a non-Jew who brought a gift; and in chapter 4 of Pesachim he requires both; see there. And the Rosh and the Rif wrote that it is nolad, and the Rabbis forbade them as a decree lest one climb up and pick; it implies that they are one reason; see there. [And if so,] wood that fell on Yom Tov is permitted to heat with on Shabbos through a non-Jew by cancellation in a majority; and see the end of siman 276, and what he wrote in siman 445.
