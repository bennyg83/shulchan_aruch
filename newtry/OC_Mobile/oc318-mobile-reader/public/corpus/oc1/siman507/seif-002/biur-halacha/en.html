He adds upon them prepared wood - See Mishnah Berurah, that to heat them in order to benefit from their light is forbidden according to everyone. So is the implication of the commentators; see Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and the other later authorities. And in my humble opinion this requires further study: for perhaps, since ordinary wood stands for heating, to bake bread with it or to cook, and for benefit after the burning the Sages permitted to mix and cancel, then automatically the wood is permitted like other prepared wood, and one may light them even to give light. And from the words of the Tur in siman 677 in the name of Maharam there is no proof; for there he speaks of the primary benefit of wood, which is to bake bread with it; which is not so of a wick, which stands only to give light. And at first glance there is a complete proof for this: for from the moment it was mixed he is permitted to move it, even though he has not yet heated with them and they have not yet been burned away; and necessarily one must say that for this reason it was already permitted from its root from the time of the mixture; and if so, perhaps the same applies to our case as well. However, according to the reasoning of Chemdas Moshe that we will bring below, that they permitted this primary law only under pressure, what we asked is not difficult; and this requires further study.
That the issur wood not be recognizable - See Mishnah Berurah that there are those who disagree with this law, and likewise with what the poskim brought, that specifically it fell into an oven that already had wood in it. And we already wrote in Shaar HaTziyun that the Rashba himself held the matter weakly in his hand. And in truth, from the Rosh regarding torches in chapter 1 of Beitzah, end of law 1, it is implied that he was not concerned about this at all; for the torches were not mixed beforehand, and even if one mixes them they will still afterward be recognizable; and the words of the Rosh in this derive from the words of Baal HaTerumah. And likewise from the sugya of Shabbos folio 29 it is proved at first glance that this rule is not so; for he said regarding vessels that one heats with, that it is forbidden to turn them over until he adds upon them and cancels them, even though it is reasonable that the vessel will still be recognizable afterward among the wood. And according to the reasoning that specifically it fell into an oven that had wood in it, that sugya is not difficult; for this case is different, in that it is still permitted, as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Magen Avraham. But regarding what we require to mix until it not be recognizable: what difference does it make if it was permitted at the outset? In the end, according to this reasoning it is not canceled; and if so, when he turns it over, he is turning over with issur. And see the Rashba there, who also wrote that it does not come under the law of cancellation, which does not apply when it is recognizable, but rather so that it not appear openly that he is moving muktzeh; see there. And in Beitzah the Rashba brought a second view; and this requires further study. And see Chemdas Moshe, who wrote a new reasoning: that perhaps this entire permission of adding upon it and canceling it is only where it fell into an oven; for if we do not cancel, he has no place to bake his bread, for the oven is full of muktzeh wood, and inevitably he must clear the muktzeh for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul; and if so, better that he cancel and bake; whereas in another circumstance this permission does not exist at all. And similarly wrote Nahar Shalom, that specifically in an oven; but if it was mixed in a field with permitted wood, we would not permit him to cancel. And I likewise found the Meiri like the words of Nahar Shalom. However, from the poskim it is not proved so; for they learned from this for all rabbinic issurim regarding canceling issur l'chatchilah; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 99; and so wrote the Alter Rebbe that one should not distinguish in this.
Even by cancellation in a majority - Know that this reasoning is not according to everyone; and from the Tur himself it is proved at the beginning of his words that in all of them cancellation in a majority works; except that afterward he brought the view of R' Yechiel. And see the Bach, who also wrote that from the other poskim it is implied that even this preparation is only rabbinic. And likewise Maamar Mordechai wondered that they brought no dispute in this. And likewise Chemdas Moshe tends to be lenient also in this by cancellation in a majority. And see Biur HaGra and Beis Meir. And see further Magen Avraham, who wrote that if they were detached on Yom Tov, according to everyone they are forbidden on the following Shabbos only rabbinically; for on Yom Tov itself too it is permitted according to the Torah to heat with them, only that the Rabbis forbade because of a decree lest one climb up and pick; and if so, according to the Torah Yom Tov prepared them for itself. And he concludes: and if so, if he needs to heat on Shabbos according to the ways clarified in siman 276, which are permitted through a non-Jew, it is permitted to cancel the wood in a majority of permitted wood; see there. And we did not copy them in Mishnah Berurah, for Bigdei Yesha and the Alter Rebbe rejected his words: for one needs no cancellation at all, since permitted wood too is muktzeh on Shabbos, and they permitted because of need; and if so, the same applies to this wood.
