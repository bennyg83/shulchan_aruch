It appears that the Taz holds that if the reason is that one is fixing a vessel, that is, that he does the entire act for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul, then this is the law of preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul, which according to the Sages is forbidden even if it was impossible to do from yesterday and even today it is impossible in any other way, and even so it is forbidden. Therefore the mishnah stated simply "one does not scrape," to say that even where it is impossible to bake it is forbidden. And even though in siman 514 the Taz brought in the name of the Rosh that where it is impossible to save them, even the Rabbis agree that it is permitted, one must say that there it is called extinguishing for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul and is permitted; which is not so here, where one is fixing a vessel and it is preparations. And extinguishing for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul is different, for it is permitted like cooking and baking, but to make a vessel is forbidden according to the Rabbis. Therefore he explained it as fixing a vessel. Whereas the beraisa cannot be saying that it is forbidden because of fixing a vessel, since it permits when it is impossible; and necessarily it holds that because of this they are in truth permitted, for it follows Rabbi Yehuda, who holds that preparations that were impossible to make from yesterday are permitted. And according to this, the beraisa should have permitted even where it is possible without scraping, as the Taz wrote in siman 514, that according to Rabbi Yehuda it is permitted even where it is possible to save it. And one cannot say that this is only regarding extinguishing for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul, as above according to the Rabbis; for that is not so, since according to Rabbi Yehuda preparations are permitted, so they are like food-preparation needs of the soul itself. And even where it would be possible without this, nevertheless if now it is a preparation, it is as fine as can be, just as there, even though it would have been possible to save the pot and do it another way, it is permitted; and likewise here, even though it would have been possible for him to bake without scraping, for example by baking somewhere to the side and guarding himself so as not to touch the plaster, nevertheless now that he is baking in this place and needs to scrape to prepare it, it is as fine as can be according to Rabbi Yehuda, even though he is fixing a vessel and it is possible another way afterward. Therefore he needed to say that if it is possible another way so that he not scrape, it is forbidden even according to Rabbi Yehuda because of moving. And therefore the Rosh wrote that according to this, even if he does not need this scraping except so that the bread be nice, etc., it is also permitted, since there is here only moving, and moving is permitted for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul, as is stated below in siman 509, se'if 7; and see Magen Avraham there. Which is not so if he does not need this scraping at all, even in order to beautify the bread; that is forbidden, for it is moving not for a need. Nevertheless this requires further study: why did the Taz need to say that the reason for the prohibition where it is possible without scraping is moving; let him derive it from this, that it is not within preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul at all, since he does not need scraping at all.
