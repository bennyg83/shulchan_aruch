{16} An oven into which there fell, etc. - It deals with the ovens of the days of the Sages of the Talmud, as he concludes below, which were like a pot, and they would smear the bread on the walls of the oven; and if so, the coals on the floor of the oven cannot damage the bread, only clay plaster or a brick that falls, and it sometimes reaches the place where the dough is smeared on the wall and scorches.
{17} Because he is moving it, etc. - And it is muktzeh, for it is not fit for anything.
{18} The fire and the ash - Because as long as it is hot, the name of muktzeh is not upon it, as above in siman 498, se'if 15. And at first glance according to this, if so even to remove the ash entirely from the oven should be permitted; so why did he state "they lay"? And so it is implied in Semag. And perhaps because there is fire in the ash, and by scraping the ash he will extinguish the fire; however by laying it is possible to be careful not to extinguish the fire, like that above in siman 502, se'if 1 in the gloss. [However afterward I found in the commentary of the mishnayos of the Rambam, and it is proved that he holds that even ash alone is forbidden to remove; and perhaps his view is that this too is considered fixing the oven, or because it is excess trouble since it is possible to bake otherwise.]
{19} Even though, etc., there was not enough in it to scorch it - For if it can scorch the bread, even scraping the clay is permitted, as below.
{20} It was possible to bake and roast - That is to say, that it would not burn.
{21} For it is considered moving for a need - And as will be clarified below at the end of siman 509 in the gloss, that it is permitted to move muktzeh for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul. And the poskim wrote that all this is where the plaster fell today, or even yesterday and he did not know of this, or he did not have time to fix it; but where he could have fixed it from yesterday and did not fix because of forgetfulness, it is forbidden to scrape, like all preparations that are possible to do while still day, which are forbidden to do on Yom Tov; and see Biur Halacha.
{22} And specifically in ovens, etc. - The language is somewhat murky. And in the Rosh, from whom these words derive, it is stated thus: that the Sages permitted to scrape only oven plaster, which is only moving muktzeh; but to scrape coals, by which one comes to extinguishing, they did not permit. And all this is in their ovens, where they stuck the bread to the walls of the oven; and if so, the coals at its bottom cannot burn the bread, only scorch a little so that the bread not be nice; and for this reason they did not permit scraping coals which comes to extinguishing. But in our ovens, where we bake on the bottom of the oven, even coals are permitted to scrape, because it is entirely impossible to bake. This is the content of the words of the Rosh, and so is the intent of the Mechaber.
{23} Since it is impossible, etc. - Namely, that it will burn. And if the oven is large and the coals are lying in one place in the oven and the rest of the oven is empty so that he can bake the bread without scraping, only that he comes to scrape the coals so that they not be near the bread lest part of it be scorched, it is forbidden. And likewise if he scraped the oven of the large coals and small coals and sparks remained in a manner that the bread will not burn, only that it is possible that it be scorched a little where they touch, it is forbidden to scrape them a second time because of extinguishing.
{24} It is permitted to scrape them of the ash and the coals - And even with a clearing tool dipped in water if he needs this, as below in se'if 5.
{25} And this is like placing meat upon coals - To roast it; for even though he extinguishes the coals at its beginning, it is permitted because it is for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul. And this is not like sweetening mustard with a wood-coal in it, which is forbidden (as below in siman 510, se'if 3), because the mustard is fit to eat without extinguishing a coal in it, only pampered people extinguish a coal in it; and further, it is possible to do this by extinguishing a metal coal, which is more fit and common [Avodas HaKodesh].
