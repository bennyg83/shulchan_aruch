And if so, ash that was heated on Yom Tov and cooled is also forbidden, as he wrote in siman 498, se'if 15. And it appears to me that if it cooled and then was reheated, it is permitted, as he wrote in siman 310, se'if 3; and see the end of siman 509.
It is permitted to scrape it. Even if it fell into it from before Yom Tov and he did not know of this, or he did not have time to fix it (Beis Yosef; Yam Shel Shlomo); see Rema, siman 509.
Our ovens. Nevertheless, if the oven is large and he scraped part of it, and it is possible to bake in that part, but he needed to clear it so that it not touch the bread and scorch part of it, it is forbidden. And regarding extinguishing we do not say "since it was permitted for a need, it was permitted not for a need" (Bach); and see Rema, siman 514; and see siman 502, se'if 1 in the gloss, where it is implied that even for the sake of eating, specifically at the time he is preparing the food is it permitted to extinguish, and not before then.
Upon coals. And even though it is possible to roast on a spit or to cook, it is permitted, since it is not a complete extinguishing, for its end is to kindle (the Ran and Milchamot; and in Beis Yosef, siman 511). And if so, what he wrote here requires further study; and this is like placing meat upon coals; for here it is a complete extinguishing, and it is permitted because it is impossible otherwise. Therefore it appears to me that he holds like Rashi's explanation, that it is a complete extinguishing, and it is permitted because it is considered impossible otherwise, for he wishes to eat it thus. And similarly is found in Yoreh De'ah, siman 69, se'if 14, regarding something that has a permitted form; see there.
