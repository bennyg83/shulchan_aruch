It is permitted to lay down the fire and the ash - See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah. And the commentators wrote that the plaster and dust that fell from the walls of the oven are forbidden to lay down, for they are muktzeh, and it is not for a need, for the bread will not be scorched. And in truth, even though so it is implied from the Rosh and Tur, nevertheless perhaps the other poskim do not hold so; for Rashi, Or Zarua, the Raah, the Ran, and Meiri explained that this "but one may press" refers to plaster and dust. However, in truth from Rashi there is no proof; for he established the mishnah like the Rabbis, that preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul do not override Yom Tov, and he explained "but one may press" so that it not touch the bread and burn it; and since the plaster can burn, he well permits, for muktzeh for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul is permitted to move; and according to the Rosh too it is permitted in such a case, as the Mechaber concludes. However, the Ran also explained that "but one may press" refers to dust, and in one reading he wrote to establish the mishnah even like Rabbi Yehuda, dealing with a case where it is possible to bake without scraping, in which even Rabbi Yehuda agrees; namely, that it also will not be scorched [for if it will not burn entirely, but will be scorched by the touch of clay plaster, certainly according to Rabbi Yehuda it is permitted; and also, if so, what the Ran wrote does not fit well, that the close of the beraisa, "and if it is impossible to bake unless he scrapes it, it is permitted," follows Rabbi Yehuda; for according to this, "impossible" would mean that it would burn entirely if he does not scrape, and in that manner certainly even according to the Rabbis it is permitted, according to what Beis Yosef copied in the name of the poskim]. And if so, according to this, what he concludes "but one may press," even where he does not need this, they also press the dust; and necessarily he does not hold the reason of muktzeh [and this appears to be the intent of Beis Yosef; see there in his words; except that his words are murky, for he wrote thus in the view of Rashi, and according to Rashi it is not so, but according to the Ran, as we explained]. And perhaps the reason of the Ran and those with him is that they were not concerned about muktzeh, for they hold that the Sages permitted in such a case where he does not move entirely, only presses; and also that it deals with pressing after he heated, and the clay plaster is hot, and they hold that this is no worse than hot ash fit for roasting an egg and the like; and likewise here similarly, unless the plaster was in the oven from yesterday at twilight when it was cold, and the name of muktzeh fell upon it as mere dust, and since it was set aside at twilight it is set aside for the whole day. And the words of Tosafos on folio 28b, s.v. "scraping," who also wrote to ask that it is forbidden because of muktzeh, are not relevant to our matter; for they asked this only according to what they innovated to explain the statement of Rabbi Chiya as about a new oven, dealing with wanting to clear the oven before heating; for in that case certainly the stones and clay found there are muktzeh; which is not so in our matter.
Since it would be scorched, it is permitted to scrape it - See Mishnah Berurah; and it is from Magen Avraham and Beis Yosef in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, who brought thus in the name of the Rashba; and so it is before us in his Avodas HaKodesh. And as for what we copied, for example where he did not know from before Yom Tov: even though the Ran is doubtful about this in the name of Baal HaTosafos, I saw in the novellae of the Rashba and the Meiri that they also copied Rashi's explanation, and it implies that they hold like him. And the Maggid Mishneh concludes there that the Rambam did not distinguish in this, implying that according to the Rambam it is permitted in every circumstance. And in truth at first glance the words of the Rashba are clarified in the sugya of folio 28b, where Rabbi Chiya said: regarding scraping an oven we have come to the dispute of Rabbi Yehuda and the Rabbis; and see there in Rashi's explanation, who explained as he explains in the mishnah of folio 32b; and also in the mishnah of folio 32b Rashi explains regarding the statement of Rabbi Chiya bar Yosef, "if it is impossible to bake," etc., that it follows Rabbi Yehuda; and if so, certainly specifically where it was not possible from before Yom Tov, like all preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul. And see Lechem Mishneh what he answered, and it is forced. And I saw that the Gaon R' Yonasan in his commentary on the laws of Yom Tov wrote that perhaps according to the Rambam this statement of Rabbi Chiya, that regarding scraping the oven we have come to dispute, is not like scraping an oven of the mishnah; for in the mishnah there is only the aspect of muktzeh, and in this there is no distinction at all between possible from before Yom Tov or not, for in every circumstance it is permitted; and regarding that of Rabbi Chiya he will explain like Tosafos, that it deals with a new oven from which he has not yet removed the bricks and clay, and with such scraping he completes the vessel and it is like the final hammer blow; and in this we come to permit only on the grounds of preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul, and that applies only when impossible from yesterday; see there. And so wrote Olas Shabbos, that the Mechaber who did not distinguish in this is because he copies the words of the Rosh and Tur, who deal with an old oven into which clay plaster fell, where there is here only a prohibition of moving muktzeh, and what is permitted for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul we do not distinguish in this between possible from the eve or not possible, for in every circumstance it is permitted [and he proved thus from the words of Tosafos on folio 28b, s.v. "scraping" there; however I examined there carefully and his words are not necessary]. Nevertheless, for the practical law one should not be lenient; for the view of Rashi, the Ran, and the Meiri is known, that the mishnah of folio 32 and likewise the beraisa, their reasons are all because it is fixing a vessel [and so is proved the view of the Ramban in Milchamot in the sugya of folio 22, that the episode of the wife of Rabbi Chiya where a brick fell for her is because of fixing a vessel, and the law follows Rabbi Yehuda; and not like the view of the Rosh who holds that there is in this only because of moving muktzeh, and the statement of Rabbi Chiya on folio 28b is only regarding a new oven, as Tosafos wrote there]; and it depends on the dispute of Rabbi Yehuda and the Rabbis; and automatically one should not be lenient even according to Rabbi Yehuda except where it was not possible to scrape from before Yom Tov, or he did not know of this while it was still day. And in truth perhaps the Rambam also holds so, that the reason is fixing a vessel; and with this it is fine that he did not copy the law of a new oven, for he holds that the statement of Rabbi Chiya on folio 28 and the mishnah and beraisa of folio 32 are all one matter; and that he did not mention that specifically where it was not possible to fix while still day, he relied in this on what he wrote in chapter 1, law 8; see there and in Hagahot Maimoniyot.
So it is permitted to extinguish for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul - See Biur HaGra, who wrote that this is according to the reading of Rashi and the other poskim [that is to say, they read in Shabbos 134, "there it is impossible," etc.; and the Rif reads there, "there (regarding meat on coals) there is no extinguishing," etc.]; but the Rif there, who reads "there there is no extinguishing," holds that extinguishing for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul is forbidden [and as for what is permitted regarding meat upon coals, see Magen Avraham]. And in truth like his words the Ran also wrote, and Beis Yosef brought him in siman 511; see there. But it is difficult: for the Rosh also copied this reading in chapter 2 of Beitzah, siman 23, and even so in chapter 4, siman 8, he copied that extinguishing for the sake of food-preparation needs of the soul is permitted even according to the Rabbis; and this requires further study.
