(s.k. 4) In an oven, etc. Even though it is not actual preparation. That is to say: in the Tur he wrote the reason for the prohibition specifically when they fell on Yom Tov, that its prohibition is only rabbinic, therefore it is permitted to add upon it and cancel it like the law of every rabbinic issur; which is not so if they fell on Shabbos before Yom Tov, for they are forbidden on Yom Tov because of preparation, for on Shabbos it is forbidden to heat with them. And if so, Shabbos did not prepare for itself; and if we permit heating with them on Yom Tov after Shabbos, Shabbos will have prepared for Yom Tov. And the prohibition of preparation is according to the Torah; and regarding a Torah issur it is forbidden to cancel or to add upon it. And upon this Magen Avraham wrote: even though it is not actual preparation. And it is not comparable to an egg born on Yom Tov after Shabbos, which was finished from yesterday: that is called actual preparation, for the finishing is complete preparation; which is not so here.
Nevertheless it is implied in the Ran, etc. Since if he had a sick person, etc. This reasoning the Ran wrote regarding a calf born on Shabbos before Yom Tov, that there are those who say it is permitted to slaughter it on Yom Tov, and we do not say that if so Shabbos prepared for Yom Tov; since if he had a sick person in his house he would be permitted to slaughter it and eat of it even if healthy of a piece of meat: thus Shabbos prepared for itself. It is brought in Magen Avraham, siman 513, s.k. 15. And if so, likewise one can say so regarding wood that fell; and if so, even if you say that it is complete preparation, nevertheless there is no Torah preparation, that is to say, for Shabbos prepared for itself.
Only that the Rabbis forbade them, etc. And if so, how can we say that on the following Yom Tov there is Torah preparation? For according to the Torah, Yom Tov prepared for itself. And so wrote Maharsha there regarding wood that fell, to resolve with this the view of the Rif; see there.
He requires both of them. That is to say: even though he holds regarding wood that fell that they are forbidden because of muktzeh even according to Rabbi Shimon, and it is forbidden, for regarding muktzeh of something attached even Rabbi Shimon agrees that it is like chased away by hands; see there: nevertheless he needed the reason of lest one climb up and pick; namely regarding fruits that fall, if he has ravens in his house they are prepared for the ravens, and what is prepared for ravens is prepared for a person. And in Tosafos of Pesachim they wrote further a circumstance that sometimes there is no concern of muktzeh, and sometimes "lest one climb up and pick" does not apply; namely in a place where one needs a spade and pickaxe, since there is trouble we are not concerned lest he climb up, etc.; for in the meantime he will recall and will not violate Shabbos; so wrote Tosafos at the beginning of Beitzah.
It implies that they are one reason. That is to say: he holds that it is not like other muktzeh, and according to Rabbi Shimon it is permitted, and there is only "lest one climb up and pick"; except that so that it not be difficult for you: how did they decree regarding wood that fell lest one climb up and pick; if so all wood, even what fell from before Yom Tov, should be forbidden to heat with, and we would decree lest one climb up and pick. Therefore he wrote that it is nolad, and therefore we decree lest one climb up and pick. Granted, what was detached from before Yom Tov, one will not err that it was detached on Yom Tov; but since wood that fell is nolad, that is to say, they fell today, a person will err that even by hands it is permitted to pick.
And wood that fell on Yom Tov, etc. That is to say: according to this, wood that fell on Yom Tov before Shabbos is forbidden on the following Shabbos only rabbinically. If so, according to everyone it is permitted to add upon them on Shabbos and to heat with them through a non-Jew. And see the end of siman 276, where it is clarified in which manner it is permitted to heat on Shabbos through a non-Jew.
