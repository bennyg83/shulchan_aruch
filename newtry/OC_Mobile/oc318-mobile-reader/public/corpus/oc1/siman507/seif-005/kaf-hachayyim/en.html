Provided that it is not new, etc. See Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman, who writes that one may be lenient with our ovens, which are not large, because they are not prone to collapse. Furthermore, a large oven means one that holds twelve isaron measures of matzah. He nevertheless writes that our ovens should be forbidden for another reason: they are completed by their first firing, through which the clay dries and hardens. This is worse than cooking in new pots, in which it is forbidden to cook initially on Yom Tov, as written above at the end of siman 502. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, and Chayei Adam, rule 93, subsection 7, cite him. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, however, writes that one may be lenient with our ovens, which are not extremely large and are never prone to collapse, even when new. These are his words. See his Kuntres Acharon, subsection 1, where he responds at length to the aforementioned ruling of Magen Avraham that forbids them. He also writes that since the Talmud says, 'lest it collapse,' it implies that no melachah is involved. See there. Mikraei Kodesh, siman 1, subsection 1, rules likewise. See below, se'if 5, and our words there, subsections 44 and 46. Nevertheless, even according to Magen Avraham, the prohibition applies specifically when the oven was newly made. If it was made many days earlier, it has already dried by itself and is permitted. Chayei Adam there. Likewise, if one was prevented from completing it and it was finished close to nightfall, he may bake on Yom Tov in a small oven or in one of our large ovens, which is not prone to collapse. Eshel Avraham, at the beginning of the siman. Eshel Avraham further writes there that the same applies to a new tile stove used to heat a house: in such a case it is forbidden to heat it for the first and second firings as long as the clay remains moist. These are his words. The later authorities cite him.
