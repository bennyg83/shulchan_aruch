It is permitted to heat them. For it is not finished with heating until he temper it with cold (the Rosh); and see what I wrote in se'if 1.
In order to bake in them, it is permitted. And it is unlike libun of stones, which is forbidden, as he wrote in siman 508; for there the fixing of the vessel is before its use with which he uses it, but here it is at the time of use that he fixes it for its use (the Ran). And Tosafos wrote: since he does it by way of liquids, it does not certainly harden them; end quote. And what he wrote, that it is forbidden to cook in new pots, as at the end of siman 502, requires further study. And it appears to me that pots are certainly hardened by liquids, as one must say according to the view of the Rosh. And the Mordechai wrote that it is permitted to temper an oven because it is preparations for food-preparation needs of the soul that were impossible to do from before Yom Tov; end quote. Meaning: he did not know yesterday that it would be heated so much that he would need to temper it with cold; which is not so of a pot, for he should have cooked in it from yesterday; and libun of stones is certain hardening.
It is permitted to soak. But if he already scraped and only wishes to scrape a second time the small coals in order to beautify, it is forbidden since he extinguishes (Yam Shel Shlomo; Bach). And so it is implied in the Rosh and Tur. Even though Beis Yosef wrote to infer from the words of the Maggid Mishneh that it is permitted to scrape in his weekday manner and he is not concerned, it does not appear so to me; for the Maggid Mishneh wrote "since it is impossible otherwise," implying that if it is possible to bake without scraping it is forbidden. Therefore one should be stringent. And the Agudah wrote that after they cleared the oven, it is forbidden to dip the clearing tool in water to extinguish it so that it not burn.
