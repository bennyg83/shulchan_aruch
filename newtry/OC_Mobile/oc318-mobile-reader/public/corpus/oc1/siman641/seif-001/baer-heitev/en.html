For himself. But when one makes it for others, it would have been proper for the householder to recite Shehecheyanu; see siman 585, se'if 2. This applies specifically to something made from year to year, but a shofar and Megillah, which are made for many years, one does not recite Shehecheyanu over their making. The Chanukah lamp is also different, because it is not recognizable that he makes it for the sake of the mitzvah. This is from Mordechai and Magen Avraham.
In the sukkah. Meaning, by day he must recite Shehecheyanu, etc. The Bayis Chadash in responsum siman 132 disagrees and writes: if on the first night rain fell in such a way that they did not eat in the sukkah at all, and they recited Kiddush and berachos in the house, one should not recite Shehecheyanu the next day when entering the sukkah to sit in it, in order not to enter a possible berachah in vain, unlike the Rema in siman 641. These are his words; see there. The Maharami in Sefer Masas Moshe, siman 5, rejected his words and wrote that one must recite it, like the Rema; see there. If he also did not eat in the sukkah on the first day, then when he eats in the sukkah on the second night, he must recite leishev ba-sukkah and afterward Shehecheyanu; see Magen Avraham in siman 661. See Magen Avraham in this siman, subparagraph 2, and what he wrote there. If he did not recite Shehecheyanu at all on the first night, see siman 473, subparagraph 1, and what I wrote there. The Be'er Heitev before me wrote here things that are not according to the law; see there and examine carefully.
