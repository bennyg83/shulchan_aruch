(1) When doing it - and precisely when he did it within thirty days of the holiday, but before the 30th, he made it kosher by entertaining it for the sake of the holiday, as explained in the sign of 1336 radar.

(2) For himself — meaning that when one makes it for others he cannot recite shehecheyanu, and only the homeowner himself recites shehecheyanu, as Magen Avraham wrote; see Peri Megadim. And that which Rama wrote anonymously above in siman 585 seif 2, that even if he already fulfilled his obligation and is reciting to discharge others, nevertheless he recites shehecheyanu — there it is not discussing one who has not yet fulfilled his obligation standing beside him to fulfill with the blessing of the one blowing; there we say that he can discharge him because one who hears is as one who recites and it is as if he recites for himself; not so here where it is not discussing in this manner. And similarly if the homeowner stands here and asks him to intend to discharge him — likewise it is permitted; thus I raised from the words of the Gra there. However the later authorities already raised there that if he can recite for himself it is not worthwhile for him to be discharged through his blessing. Know further that from the words of Tosafos on daf 46 s.v. ha'oseh sukkah le'atzmo it implies that for this reason he cannot recite shehecheyanu when doing for another, since this tanna holds one does not recite a blessing on doing (meaning: therefore the action is not the essence of the mitzvah and therefore one does not recite shehecheyanu on behalf of others). Therefore in a mitzvah where action is the essence of the mitzvah according to Tosafos, he can recite shehecheyanu even when performing the mitzvah for others; and per their view our question from siman 585 to here is also resolved — for there hearing the shofar is the essence of the mitzvah and therefore he can recite shehecheyanu for others as well; not so with sukkah where the essence of the mitzvah is dwelling in the sukkah and not the action.
