And the maftir is 'The hand of Hashem was upon me.' The Tur cites Rav Hai Gaon: 'I heard from the Sages that the resurrection of the dead is destined to occur in Nissan, and the victory over Gog and Magog in Tishrei.' Therefore, in Nissan the haftorah is the vision of the dry bones, and in Tishrei it is 'On the day Gog comes.'
And Pesach is not mentioned in the blessing over the haftorah, etc. For were it not Shabbos, there would be no haftorah on Chol HaMoed.
And the people have customarily not recited a blessing over them, etc. The Levush expressed astonishment at this and said, 'I do not know why they should not recite a blessing over them. Furthermore, who informed him of this custom? Perhaps they recite the blessing quietly, or perhaps they did not know what is written in the works on customs, which state that a blessing should be recited over these two Megillos.' In the newer printings of the Shulchan Aruch, the editor emended the text here to read, 'And the people have customarily recited a blessing.' All of this is empty talk. In Responsa Rama, siman 35, he discusses this at length and explains his words here, and his conclusion is that no blessing should be recited over any Megillah other than Megillas Esther, because we do not find in the Talmud that the Sages instituted reading the other Megillos in a manner that would permit one to say, 'and commanded us to read.' He discusses this at length. The principal reason is that even Megillas Esther, which one is fully obligated to read, requires a blessing only when it is read from a valid Megillah. If it is read from a Chumash, as stated in siman 691, no blessing is recited. All the more so regarding these Megillos, whose obligation is not clearly established and is merely a custom, and which are printed in Chumashim, no blessing should be recited over them. Had a blessing applied to them, people would have been careful to write them in Megillah form on parchment, as they do with Megillas Esther, but such a practice has never been heard of. The Beit Yosef likewise writes in siman 559: 'The people have not customarily recited a blessing over any Megillah other than Megillas Esther.' End quote. Therefore, anyone who recites a blessing over these Megillos recites a blessing in vain.
