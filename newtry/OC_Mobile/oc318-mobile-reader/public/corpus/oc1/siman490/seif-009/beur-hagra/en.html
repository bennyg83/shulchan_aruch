Seif 9: And they read the haftarah. Megillah there.
And they do not. As Tosafos write in Shabbos 24b, in the passage beginning “and is there not,” because we rule like Rav Gidal.
Not, etc. Even according to Rashi and Rif, who rule not like Rav Gidal, one nevertheless does not conclude with the Pesach theme, as Rosh writes there. This is the meaning of “not in the middle.” See Rosh there.
And the custom is. Shir HaShirim is read because it speaks of the redemption from Egypt. Darkei Moshe writes: Avudraham states that the universal custom is to read Shir HaShirim on the festival of matzah because it discusses the redemption. On Shavuos one reads Rus, because it contains the beginning of the harvest, and because our ancestors who received the Torah underwent conversion, as did Rus. On Sukkos one reads Koheles, because it states, “Give a portion to seven, and also to eight,” etc. Maseches Soferim states that one who reads any of the five Megillos recites the blessing “concerning the reading of a scroll,” even when it is written among Kesuvim. Maharil likewise requires this blessing even when the Megillah is not written upon a separate scroll. Hagahos Maimoniyos, Hilchos Tisha B’Av, rules similarly. This is the custom, unlike Mordechai, who writes in the first chapter of Megillah that one recites “concerning the reading of Kesuvim.” So writes Darkei Moshe.
Bach, Levush, Minhagim, and Mateh Moshe likewise require the blessing even when the Megillos are not written upon separate scrolls. Certainly the blessing is recited when they are written upon a scroll like a Sefer Torah. This was also the practice of all the early authorities, and Magen Avraham writes accordingly.
The statement that Koheles is excluded is incorrect. At the end of chapter 3 of Yadayim, the Mishnah concludes that although there was a dispute, the final ruling is that Shir HaShirim and Koheles are equal in rendering the hands impure. Rambam rules in chapter 9 of Hilchos Avos HaTumah, halachah 8, that all five Megillos render the hands impure like a Sefer Torah. Hagahos Mordechai, first chapter of Megillah, likewise writes that all are equal concerning this blessing. This is the primary ruling.
On Shabbos. Because it is the day on which people assemble. Even on Yom Tov they would be in the fields, as stated at the end of Rosh Hashanah 55a.
