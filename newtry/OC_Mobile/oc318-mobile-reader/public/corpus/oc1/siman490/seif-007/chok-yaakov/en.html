The season of our freedom. Maharil writes that some say, “the season of our rejoicing,” because of the joy over the drowning of the Egyptians. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 487, asks: if so, why do we not recite full Hallel, on the ground that Hashem’s handiwork was drowning in the sea? How, then, can we call it “the season of our rejoicing” because of that drowning? In truth, this is no difficulty. We refrain specifically from reciting Hallel as a song before the Holy One, blessed is He, as though there were joy before Him, because His handiwork was drowning in the sea. We may nevertheless say “the season of our rejoicing,” because there is rejoicing when the wicked perish. This distinction is stated explicitly at the end of the first chapter of Sanhedrin. Therefore, those who follow this practice should not be protested, although our custom is to say “the season of our freedom.” If the seventh day falls on Shabbos, how the yotzros are rearranged, and likewise how one conducts the piyyut “The Day the Sea Became Dry Land” when a bris occurs on the seventh or eighth day, is explained well in the Levush and the works on customs.
