8) There. If one omitted it, one does not repeat Birkas HaMazon. Prayer is unavoidable; if one did not pray properly, one must repeat it. On Chol HaMoed, however, a person need not eat food that requires Birkas HaMazon, because one may instead eat meat and fruit. Therefore, Birkas HaMazon is not repeated. Taz, subsection 1. 9) Regarding Mizmor LeSodah, see the gloss above at the end of siman 51 and our words there, with the help of Heaven. Regarding wearing tefillin on Chol HaMoed, see above, siman 31, se'if 2, and our words there. Regarding when those who wear tefillin on Chol HaMoed remove them, see the gloss above at the end of siman 25. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2, writes that in accordance with Rema, the established custom is to remove them only before Mussaf, and this should not be changed. See there. Chok Yosef, subsection 7, writes likewise. Although on Chol HaMoed Sukkos the custom is to remove tefillin before Hallel, on Pesach the custom follows Rema in siman 25 and they are removed before Mussaf. Sukkos differs because the esrog and lulav are taken, and the tefillin should not form an interruption. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, however, writes that people customarily remove tefillin before Hallel on both Pesach and Sukkos. See there. Yeshuos Yaakov, subsection 2, writes likewise, but states that his own custom on the first day of Chol HaMoed Pesach is to wait until after reading the passage of Kadesh, which contains the verse, 'You shall observe this statute,' and so forth. See there.
