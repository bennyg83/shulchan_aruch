We do not. See the Taz. In Sepharad they say on Yom Tov "the Festival of Matzos, this Festival day, a holy convocation," as in siman 487; and in Ashkenaz they do not say "Festival day, a holy convocation" at all on Yom Tov. And our custom is not to say "Festival day" at all, only "a holy convocation" on Yom Tov, and on Chol HaMoed also "a holy convocation"; and in Avodah, in Yaaleh VeYavo, we do not say "a holy convocation." And as for what the Rav [Rama] wrote, that we do not say "a holy convocation," neither on Yom Tov nor on Chol HaMoed, one may say he meant Yaaleh VeYavo in Avodah; but in Musaf they say "a holy convocation," as the Bach wrote: it is a mitzvah to gather in the synagogue for the sake of the musaf offering; see there. Nevertheless, why did he not write that we do not say "Festival day, a holy convocation"? And in the Levush, se'if 3, he wrote that we do not say "this Festival day, a holy convocation," neither on Yom Tov nor on Chol HaMoed; and in se'if 9 he wrote: why do we not say on the seventh and eighth of Pesach in Musaf after "And you shall offer," and "On the seventh day, a holy convocation," seeing that they practice to mention "a holy convocation"; see there; and that is in Musaf; see there. And our custom is to say "festival of such-and-such, the season," etc., "a holy convocation," and we skip "Festival day," both on Yom Tov in Shacharis and Musaf and on Chol HaMoed in Musaf; but in Yaaleh VeYavo that is in "You chose us" or in Avodah we say only "festival of such-and-such, remember us," etc., and we skip "a holy convocation" (Pri Chadash).
And behold. The reason for "a holy convocation": see Beis Yosef [on siman] 487 in the name of the Ramban, of blessed memory, on parashas Emor: that prayer on a weekday is rabbinic (unlike the Rambam, of blessed memory; see siman 106); and on Yom Tov one may say that prayer is according to the Torah, to gather in the house of Hashem to sanctify the day with prayer in public; see there. It implies that we specifically require ten adults of thirteen years; and Beis Shmuel [discusses] the matter of publicity in Even HaEzer regarding one who cohabits with a non-Jew; and likewise "And Boaz took ten men": every publicity implies ten (whereas "many" is three). And according to this, it is proper to be concerned for this and to seek adults who are known to have brought [two hairs]; see Magen Avraham, siman 55, note 7, and [siman] 199: regarding matters of Torah we require that it be known they brought two hairs. And if Hashem wills, in Magen Avraham note 2 it will be explained further.
