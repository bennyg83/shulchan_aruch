The last. Explanation in Taz. A word on the eighth [day] they say "a day of dry land" — which is not so on other Yamim Tovim, for they do not say "a day of dry land" unless they fall on Shabbat — Levush siman 684 and Minhagim. And if the seventh day falls on Shabbat they say in Yotzer the piyyutim of Shabbat chol hamoed, because they say Shir HaShirim on that day, and "a day of dry land" they say before "a new song." And if the last day falls on Shabbat they say Yotzer of Shabbat chol hamoed and reverse in the eighteen [blessings] to say the piyyut of the seventh day; and if there is a word [mila] they say "a day of dry land" before "a new song" — Magen Avraham:
