And Magen Avraham: "zman." Because it is not a festival in its own right. A word on the eighth [day] they say "a day of dry land" — which is not so on other Yamim Tovim, for they do not say "a day of dry land" unless they fall on Shabbat (Levush siman 684 and Minhagim); and it appears to me the reason is that on Shabbat there is not so much concern to interrupt between redemption and prayer, as written siman 111. And if the seventh day falls on Shabbat they say in Yotzer the piyyutim of Shabbat chol hamoed, because they say Shir HaShirim on that day, and "a day of dry land" they say before "a new song." And if the last day falls on Shabbat they say Yotzer of Shabbat chol hamoed and reverse in the eighteen to say the piyyut of the seventh day, which R' Shimon HaGadol also established; and if there is a word [mila] they say "a day of dry land" before "a new song":
