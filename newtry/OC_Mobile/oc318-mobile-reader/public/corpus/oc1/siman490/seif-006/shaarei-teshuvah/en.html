The final day. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 225, concerning the Ashkenazic custom not to recite an Ofan on the seventh day of Pesach, while employing the poetic expression, “He removed the wheels of their chariots.” He writes that one should not question the custom. There is support for it from the statement in Sanhedrin that on the night of the seventh day of Pesach the ministering angels wished to recite song, and the Holy One, blessed be He, said, “The works of My hands are drowning,” etc. See there also what he writes concerning Tosafos in Eruvin 10 regarding the phrase “and the chayos sing,” etc. One must be careful in the piyyut concerning the Song at the Sea on the final day of Pesach, in the stanza beginning “he fell asleep and perished,” etc. The common people are accustomed to pause at the word “Your pious one” and wait before saying “You led with Your kindness” until the chazzan completes the stanza, as they do with the other stanzas. This is an error. Here the entire stanza must be recited continuously, including the words “You led with Your kindness,” because those words are connected to the preceding phrase, “and the descendants of Your pious ones,” as is evident.
