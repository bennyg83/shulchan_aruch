And if it increased in the courtyard, he may remove it to the refuse heap. All of this is the language of the Tur. Below, in siman 535, he wrote: if it increased until the courtyard became like a cattle barn, one may remove it to the refuse heap. Here, however, he wrote simply, if it increased, implying that he holds that the fourteenth is more lenient than Chol HaMoed, like Rava's answer in the Gemara there, folio 25b, on the question: this itself is difficult; you said that manure is removed to the sides, and then it teaches that what is in the barn and in the courtyard may be removed to the refuse heap. Abaye said: this is not difficult; here it refers to the fourteenth, and here to Chol HaMoed. Rava said: both refer to Chol HaMoed, and this is what it means: if the courtyard became like a cattle barn, one may remove it to the refuse heap. Since Rava did not answer plainly that it also applies to the fourteenth, certainly his intent is that specifically on Chol HaMoed one may not remove it unless the courtyard became like a cattle barn, unlike on the fourteenth, where whenever it increased somewhat one may remove it. Nevertheless, it must have increased somewhat, by reasoning, for one should not remove it for no reason. So too the Tur explained the words of the Rosh, even though the Rosh's words are concise, as he wrote in the abridged rulings of the Rosh: Manure in the courtyard on the fourteenth is removed to the sides, and if there is much manure in the courtyard, it is removed to the refuse heap; on Chol HaMoed one does not remove it, but only moves it to the sides, end quote. It is explicitly clear as I have written. All this appears to me simple. But I saw in the Bach that he understood that according to the Rosh and the Tur, the fourteenth and Chol HaMoed are equal in this law, and that the phrase 'if it increased' that the Tur wrote here also means specifically that it increased until it became like a cattle barn. He strained greatly to settle the sugya of the Gemara according to them, and wrote that they disagree with the Rambam's view; see his words at length. Certainly the language of the abridged rulings of the Rosh that I cited escaped him, for it explicitly shows that there is a distinction between the fourteenth and Chol HaMoed, as implied by the language of the Tur here and below in siman 535. His words are correct according to the sugya, as I wrote. The Beis Yosef also seems not to have noticed all this. In truth, these matters are simple and clear to one who studies them, and there is no dispute among the poskim here at all.
