3) When the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos, Derashos Maharil, laws of Shabbos HaGadol, writes that one who wishes to be personally stringent and refrain from work after midday on Friday should do so because the Sages made no distinction. See there. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Pesach Me'ubin, subsection 93, and writes that it is merely a stringency. He concludes that he has not seen anyone observe it even as a stringency. See there. Zera Emes, siman 64, was likewise asked about this and concludes that since most authorities agree with the korban rationale, work is plainly permitted. See there. Nachpeh BaKesef, Orach Chaim, siman 7, likewise concludes that it is completely permitted by law, while one who is stringent will be blessed. See there. Admas Kodesh, volume 1, Orach Chaim, siman 10, however, concludes that it is prohibited. See there. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, writes that Admas Kodesh's proofs are not decisive and that the primary ruling follows Nachpeh BaKesef and Knesses HaGedolah. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, writes that in a place where the custom on the eve of Pesach is to work until midday, work on Friday after midday appears permitted even according to Rashi, because such a community does not accept his concern about preoccupation. See there. Most authorities thus agree that the prohibition is based upon the korban. Even in a place whose custom is not to work throughout the day, when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos one may work on Friday. In a place whose custom is to work until midday, it is permitted even according to Rashi. Nevertheless, in a place whose custom is not to work throughout the day, one who is stringent out of concern for Rashi's opinion will be blessed.
