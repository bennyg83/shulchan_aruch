(s.k. 3) Complete melacha. And everything that is permitted on Chol HaMoed, etc., is clear in the Gemara and the poskim. And this is the language of the Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 18: therefore the fourteenth day of Nisan is forbidden in doing melacha by rabbinic law like Chol HaMoed, and it is lighter than Chol HaMoed; and it is forbidden only from midday, etc.; but, etc.; end of his words. Behold he wrote that it is lighter than Chol HaMoed; and so it is clear in the Gemara that it is lighter, from the law that he wrote in Shulchan Aruch, se'if 7.
A hen, etc. And if she died they set another in her place, etc.; and on Chol HaMoed in such a case it is forbidden, as written below in siman 536, se'if 4; and those are the words of the Gemara, folio 55b; and in further matters we find that Chol HaMoed is more stringent than erev Pesach. And Chok Yaakov wrote in the name of Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah that if he has nothing to eat it is permitted to do [melacha] even after midday, for there is no Festival need greater than this; end of his words. But complete melacha implies from the words of Shulchan Aruch that even for the need of the Festival it is forbidden.
But even without that, if he has nothing to eat it is permitted, as on Chol HaMoed, as written in siman 542; and see there in Magen Avraham that if he has bread and water it is forbidden to do [melacha]; and see there further.
