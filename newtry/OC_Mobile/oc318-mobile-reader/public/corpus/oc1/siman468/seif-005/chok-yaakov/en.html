And some are lenient. According to this opinion, in a place where the custom is to perform labor it is permitted to begin and complete on the fourteenth until midday in every manner. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of the Ran, and so wrote the Kol Bo. This is simple according to the Rosh's view, since the mishnah's statement, 'he may not begin,' refers to a place where they do not perform labor, but in a place where they do perform labor it is permitted to begin in every manner until midday. The Rav omitted all this because according to this opinion he follows his approach, for he wrote above in seif 3 that for us it is a place where the custom is not to perform labor, and therefore there is no need for this. See what I wrote above in s.k. 7. See the Kol Bo in Hilchos Pesach, who wrote that cobblers are also included among the three trades. So too wrote the Rokeach, siman 269. This requires study, for how did he rule like Rabbi Yehudah and not like the Sages, when the halacha follows them? So too ruled all the poskim. Also in the work Tzeidah LaDerech, article 4, klal 3, chapter 10, he omitted the barbers and wrote cobblers, and this requires study.
And it is for the need of the festival. See the Shiltei Gibborim, who derived from the sugya of the Gemara in Moed Katan, chapter Mi Shehafach, that if he has nothing to eat, he may perform labor even after midday, for there is no greater festival need than this. See what I wrote above in s.k. 4.
