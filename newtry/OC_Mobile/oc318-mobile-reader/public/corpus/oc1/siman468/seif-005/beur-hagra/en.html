Se'if 5: Even, etc. The language of the Rambam; and he holds that the Mishnah deals with a place where they practiced to do [melacha]; and he explains that the Sages are more lenient than R' Meir and that they dispute to permit, not to forbid; and so wrote the Ra'avad there from what is difficult; and the Sages say they do only three crafts, etc.; and as written there in the Gemara: come and hear: R' Meir, etc., and they do, etc.; that is to say, they do, etc., finishing only in a place where they practiced to do; and that is what is written: a place where they practiced not, etc.; and specifically for the need of the Festival, as written in the Gemara there; and see Lechem Mishneh. And likewise the three crafts are specifically for the need of the Festival, as written in Moed Katan 13a. And he ruled like the Sages; and the Sages do not dispute R' Meir except regarding the three crafts.
And there are those who are lenient. Who explain the Mishnah as dealing with a place where they practiced not to do; and in the Gemara "and they do," etc., means even not for the need of the Festival; and that is what is written in the Gemara: a place where they practiced not, etc., for the need of the Festival, etc.: two proofs that [work] not for the need of the Festival [is discussed]; for according to the Rambam what is written "a place where they practiced not" does not fit the plain sense of the inquiry.
Three, etc. And specifically for the need of the Festival; and he need not have written "for the need of the Festival," as written in Moed Katan 13a; and so wrote the Ra'avad there in the hasagos, and he wrote that so it is proven from the Tosefta; and this is the language of the Tosefta: a place where they practiced to do melacha on erev Pesach, etc.; even in a place where they said not to do, three crafts they do: the tailors, etc.
And if he began, etc. That is to say, in a place where they practiced not to do; and he relied on the beginning; and also "until midday" needed to be said only to exclude what the Rosh wrote in the name of R' Yitzchak, that in this [case] the entire day is permitted.
