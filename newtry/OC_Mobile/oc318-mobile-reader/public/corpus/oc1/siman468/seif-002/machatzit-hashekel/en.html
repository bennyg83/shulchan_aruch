(s.k. 2) They place him under a ban, etc. And he also does not see a sign of blessing. And so it is implied in the Gemara, which challenges regarding that which was taught: a place where they practiced to do melacha on erev Pesach until midday, they do [it]; a place where they practiced, etc.; which implies that they come to teach that from midday onward, in every place, it is forbidden (see there in Maharsha). And it challenges: why specify erev Pesach? Even on erev Shabbos and erev Yom Tov as well, for it was taught: one who does melacha on erev Shabbos and on erev Yom Tov from Minchah onward does not see a sign of blessing. And it answers: there, specifically from Minchah it is forbidden, and here from midday; alternatively, there it is only that he does not see a sign of blessing, etc., while here they also place him under a ban. And the Tur ruled according to both resolutions, that both are true for practical law. And since the Gemara said here "they also place him under a ban," the word "also" indicates that even though he does not see a sign of blessing, nevertheless they also place him under a ban. And "sign of blessing" means from that melacha: even if he profits from it, he will lose correspondingly from elsewhere; so wrote the Taz.
