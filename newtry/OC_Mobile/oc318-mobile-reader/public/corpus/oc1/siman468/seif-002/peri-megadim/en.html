And so. See Taz and see Peri Chadash; and in Even HaEzer siman 5, se'if 14: for all prohibitions, telling a non-Jew is forbidden, even though it is not Shabbos, Yom Tov, or Chol HaMoed. And in Yoreh De'ah 297, and in the Shach letter 4, one must say it is specifically a prohibition of a negative commandment; but a prohibition of a positive commandment, telling a non-Jew is permitted among other prohibitions; which is not so regarding Yom Tov, Shabbos, and Chol HaMoed, where even in a rabbinic matter a shevus of a shevus through a non-Jew is forbidden, unless in a place of mitzvah; and see siman 307, se'if 5, and siman 543, se'if 1, and Magen Avraham there. And on erev Pesach, even according to the view that in Temple times it was perhaps forbidden: for an individual who brings a korban, his Yom Tov involves only a positive commandment, not a negative commandment. And see the responsa of Rema of Fano regarding Chol HaMoed, whether it has a negative commandment or a positive commandment concerning laying tefillin, siman 108; and see siman 530 in Magen Avraham, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. And it is difficult from siman 328 (1), which forbids there removing a tooth like one who was struck with makkos; and why then permit here to get a haircut through a non-Jew and to cut nails? And in Eliyah Rabbah (4) and Eliyahu Zuta he permitted, if he forgot to cut before midday, to cut after midday; and this requires further study. And it is possible that even nowadays after midday it is perhaps forbidden, for the reason of the korban Pesach as a matter [prohibited] by vote, etc.; like an egg, from Scripture it implies that it is perhaps so; and this requires further study.
I was asked. Someone had fowl called Capohner that walk in the garden, and he fears lest they fly outside: is it permitted to cut a small bone in the wings so that they not fly away, or not? And I answered: through himself it appears to forbid, for tzaar baalei chayim where there is no great need is forbidden; see Yoreh De'ah siman 23, se'if 6, and siman 24, letter 8 in the Shach there. And through a non-Jew: now, if tzaar baalei chayim is rabbinic, one may say that telling [a non-Jew] in a rabbinic matter, as a shevus of a shevus among other prohibitions, is perhaps permitted, except on Shabbos, Yom Tov, and Chol HaMoed, as written above and see Peri Chadash; and if tzaar baalei chayim is according to the Torah, it is forbidden. And see Choshen Mishpat siman 272, se'if 9, which implies that tzaar baalei chayim is rabbinic; and see Sema letter 15; and see Kesef Mishneh, chapter 13 of Hilchos Rotzeach uShemiras Nefesh; and if so, if it is according to the Torah it is forbidden. And one should not say that it is a double doubt: perhaps among other prohibitions telling a non-Jew is permitted, and perhaps tzaar baalei chayim is rabbinic; for Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 13 of Hilchos Sechirus, law 3, and in Sema, Choshen Mishpat, Be'er HaGolah letter 12, regarding our sugya of one who hires workers, it was not resolved; see there. Nevertheless one may say: even if tzaar baalei chayim is according to the Torah and has no negative commandment but only a positive commandment, like erev Pesach after midday, it is permitted through a non-Jew, as stated; and see Eliyah Rabbah (4) and Magen Avraham (3), and with Hashem's help it will be explained there further.
