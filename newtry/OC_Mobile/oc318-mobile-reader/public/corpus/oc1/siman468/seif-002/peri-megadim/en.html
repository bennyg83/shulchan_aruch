(א) And so Taz and Peri Chadash and Eshel Avraham siman 5:14 — all prohibitions forbid amira l'akum though not Shabbat, Yom Tov, and Chol HaMoed; Yoreh De'ah 297 and Shach letter 4 — must say specifically negative-commandment prohibition; positive-commandment prohibition permits amira l'akum in other prohibitions, unlike Yom Tov, Shabbat, and Chol HaMoed where even rabbinic matters forbid shevut through akum unless a mitzvah place — see Shulchan Gavoah 65 and 473:1 and Magen Avraham there and erev Pesach; even per those who say in Temple times perhaps forbidden because individual sacrifice is his Yom Tov — only positive commandment not negative. (ב) I was asked: one had fowl called capons walking in a garden and fears they will fly out — may he cut a small bone in the wing so they will not fly, or not? I answered: by himself it appears forbidden, for tza'ar ba'alei chayim without great need is forbidden — see siman 23:6 and siman 24 letter 8 in Shach there; through akum — if tza'ar ba'alei chayim is rabbinic, one may say amira in rabbinic matters in shevut of shevut in other prohibitions; perhaps permitted except Shabbat, Yom Tov, and Chol HaMoed as above and Peri Chadash; if tza'ar ba'alei chayim is d'oraisa forbidden — see Chayei Adam siman 272:9 implies tza'ar ba'alei chayim is rabbinic and see siman 75 letter 15 and therefore 13 from laws of murder and guarding life — if d'oraisa forbidden; if not, perhaps in other prohibitions amira l'akum is permitted and perhaps tza'ar ba'alei chayim is rabbinic — Maggid Mishneh laws of hiring 3 and Semag Choshen Mishpat 32 letter 12 they discuss regarding hiring workers — not resolved, see there; nevertheless one may say even if tza'ar ba'alei chayim is d'oraisa and has no negative commandment only positive, like erev Pesach after midday permitted through akum as stated; Ateret Zekenim 4 and Magen Avraham 3 — see there further.
