(6) Melacha to earn wages: and in this, even if it is not complete melacha it is forbidden, and even if it is for the need of Yom Tov. The later authorities wrote that a worker who has nothing to eat is permitted to do melacha to earn wages.
(7) And it is complete melacha: and in this it is forbidden even for the need of Yom Tov. And laundering his garments is included in complete melacha and is forbidden. And to a non-Jewish laundress it is permitted in every case to give [garments] after midday. The later authorities wrote that everything that is permitted to do on Chol HaMoed, such as a matter of impending loss, or where he does an amateur act and not a craftsman's act and there is Festival need in it, and similarly the other things permitted on Chol HaMoed, all the more so are permitted on erev Pesach after midday; and likewise all those who were permitted to cut hair and to launder on Chol HaMoed, such as one who comes from overseas and one who leaves prison, are also permitted on erev Pesach after midday.
(8) He repairs his vessels for Yom Tov: that is to say, an old vessel that became somewhat damaged, or his garments that tore somewhat: he may repair and sew them for the need of Yom Tov; and the same law applies that all this is permitted for the need of his fellow if it is for free; and the later authorities wrote that even a craftsman's act is permitted in this.
(9) For himself in the manner of his study: for since it is in the manner of his study and he writes for himself, it does not appear as complete melacha, for certainly he does not intend proper finished writing, and it is like mere repair that they permitted for Festival need. And for others, even for free, it is forbidden in this.
