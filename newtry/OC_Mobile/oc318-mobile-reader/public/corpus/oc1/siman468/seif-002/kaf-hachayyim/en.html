2) Tosafos write there that the Yerushalmi implies the prohibition is under Torah law. Responsa Rosh, rule 55, siman 10, writes likewise. Ran there, however, writes that it may be only rabbinic. See there. Tosafos, Chagigah 17b, beginning with the words 'Rather, is it not,' write that the Babylonian Talmud maintains that the prohibition is only rabbinic. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, chapter 6, likewise writes that Tosafos and Ran disagree only in their interpretation of the Yerushalmi; according to the Babylonian Talmud, even Tosafos agree that the prohibition is rabbinic. See there. Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov, chapter 8, halachah 18, likewise writes that performing work on the fourteenth of Nissan is rabbinically prohibited like Chol HaMoed, although it is more lenient than Chol HaMoed. It is prohibited only from midday onward, the time of slaughtering the korban, while from sunrise until midday the law depends upon local custom. See there. Sefer Mitzvos Gadol likewise writes that even nowadays, work is rabbinically prohibited on the day of the korban as on Chol HaMoed. See there. Peri Chadash therefore concludes, in accordance with Rambam, that the prohibition against work after midday on the eve of Pesach is only rabbinic. See there. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1, cites this and writes that it is also Rashi's opinion. See there. Magen HaElef, subsection 3, likewise writes that it is only rabbinic.
