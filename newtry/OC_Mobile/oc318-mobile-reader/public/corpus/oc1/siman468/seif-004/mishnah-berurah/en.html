(א) One who travels, etc. — the general rule of this seif per Magen Avraham and several commentators who decided halacha accordingly: when a person travels from his city to another where his city was lenient in something and the city he came to is stringent — if he does not intend to settle there permanently, the stringencies of that place do not apply to him by basic law and he may be lenient per his city's custom discreetly at home; but publicly he may not be lenient because of dispute. All this in matters doable discreetly without townspeople knowing; but matters impossible to do so discreetly, like melacha — he may not do even discreetly in the city but goes to the field outside the city boundary and may be lenient per his custom. If his intention is to settle, the place's custom applies immediately upon entering the city boundary; even if he later wants to go outside the city to be lenient per his custom he may not, since this place's custom already applied when he came to settle. Similarly if his city's custom is stringent and he went to a lenient place — also depends: if intending to settle there, stringencies of the place he left cease when he reaches the new place and he may be lenient per that place's custom; if not intending to settle, stringencies of the place he left have not ceased and he may not be lenient per this place's custom; nevertheless he should not appear before that place's people recognizing he does not practice like them because of dispute — be stringent discreetly. Now we explain in detail:

(ב) He should not do, etc. — deals when his intention is to return to his place; if he does not intend to return, even in the wilderness it is forbidden.

(ג) In the settlement — of Jews; anyone entering that city's boundary is considered coming to the settlement.

(ד) Because of the dispute — even discreetly, for melacha cannot be done discreetly without being known.

(ה) In the wilderness — meaning the city's field outside the boundary where settlement people are not found, since his intention is to return to the first place where they are lenient.

(ו) He should not do — this also deals with intention to return, therefore stringencies of the place he left have not ceased; but if intending to settle permanently, he need not be stringent per the place he left and may practice the leniencies of the place where he now dwells, as Mechaber proves below siman 474.

(ז) And stringencies of the place he went to — even though his intention is to return, because of the dispute, as at the beginning of the seif.

(ח) And nevertheless he should not appear, etc. — refers to where they impose stringencies of the place he left — he should not appear, etc.; and as long as they do not see he is idle because of prohibition there will be no dispute, for they will say he has melacha — Gemara.

(ט) And similarly, etc. — the language is very garbled, for the entire seif deals with intention to return as we wrote; Acharonim wrote there is abbreviated language here and the word "all" is missing — the intent is not specifically from a place that does melacha to one that does not, but similarly for any custom practiced differently in two places.

(י) And provided he does not appear, etc. — includes two cases: (a) where his place is lenient and he is lenient — must be careful not to appear before townspeople, as at the beginning of the seif, except here he need not go to the wilderness as at the beginning regarding melacha, since melacha cannot be hidden when done in the city unlike other customs hidden discreetly — therefore he was stringent only not to appear publicly; (b) where his place is stringent and he is stringent — must also not appear stringent before them, as regarding melacha. However when they cannot avoid noticing he changes from their custom, he may practice leniency like them even if intending to return to his stringent place, because of dispute. Only if the matter is not forbidden by basic law but only practiced as prohibition; but something forbidden by law, even rabbinically — heaven forbid to transgress because of fear of hatred and dispute; better to suffer quarrels than transgress rabbinic law, for one who transgresses rabbinic law is liable death by Heaven.

(כ) Because of the dispute — Poskim wrote: if his place is lenient and he practiced leniently discreetly and townspeople found him, he must stop his custom lest they recognize him because of dispute; but if Torah scholars found him he need not stop, for scholars know this depends on custom and there is no dispute; nevertheless initially he should not practice his custom even before scholars.
