(14) One who goes, etc.: the general rule of this se'if, according to the view of Magen Avraham and several commentators who decided like him for practical law, is as follows. When a person goes from his city to another city, and in his city they practiced to be lenient in some matter, and in the city to which he came they are stringent in this: if his intention is not to settle permanently in this city, the stringencies of this place do not take effect upon him according to the essence of the law, and he may practice to be lenient according to the custom of his place in private inside his house; but in public he may not be lenient because of controversy. And all this is regarding matters that can be done in private and will not become known to the people of the place; however, regarding matters that cannot be done so much in private, such as melacha, he may not do them even in private within the city; rather he should go out to the field outside the city's techum, and there he may be lenient according to the custom of his place. And if his intention is to settle, the custom of the place takes effect upon him immediately when he enters the city's techum; and even if afterward he wishes to go outside the city's techum and be lenient according to his custom, he is not permitted, for the custom of this place has already taken effect upon him since he came here to establish his place. And likewise, if the custom of his place is to be stringent and he went to a place where they are lenient, it also depends on this: if his intention is to settle there, the stringency of the place from which he left is removed from him once he came to the new place, and he is permitted to be lenient according to the custom of that place; and if his intention is not to settle in this place, the stringencies of the place from which he left have still not been removed from him, and it is forbidden for him to be lenient in that matter according to the custom of this place; and nevertheless he should not appear before the people of that place so that they recognize that he does not practice like them, because of controversy; rather he should be stringent in private. And now we will explain in detail:
(15) He should not do, etc.: and it deals with when his intention is to return to his place; for if he does not intend to return, even in the wilderness it is forbidden.
(16) In a settled place: of Jews; and anyone who entered within the techum of that place is called one who came to a settled place.
(17) Because of controversy: and even in private, for melacha cannot be done so much in private that it will not become known.
(18) In the wilderness: that is to say, in the field of the city outside the techum, where people of the settled place are not found there, since his intention is to return to the first place where they are lenient.
(19) He should not do: this also deals with when his intention is to return to his place, and therefore the stringencies of the place from which he left have not been removed from him; but if his intention is to settle here forever, he need not be stringent according to the custom of his place from which he left, and he may conduct himself according to the leniencies of the place among whom he now dwells, as is proven from the words of the Mechaber below in siman 574.
(20) And the stringencies of the place he went to: and even though his intention is to return, because of controversy, as above at the beginning of the se'if.
(21) And even so he should not appear, etc.: this refers to the case where they impose upon him the stringencies of the place from which he left: he should not appear, etc.; and so long as they do not see before them an indication that he is idle because of a prohibition, there will be no controversy, for they say: it is only that he has no melacha [to do] [Gemara].
(22) And likewise, etc.: the wording is very awkward, for the entire se'if deals with when his intention is to return to his place, as we wrote; and the later authorities wrote that there is abbreviated wording here and the word "every" is missing, and the intent is that it is not specifically from a place where they do melacha to a place where they do not do: rather the same law applies for every custom that in this place they practice thus and in another place they practice differently.
(23) And provided that he does not appear, etc.: this includes two manners. (a) Where the custom of his place is to be lenient and he is lenient: he must be careful at least not to appear before the people of this place, as above at the beginning of the se'if; only that in this he was not required to go out to the wilderness as in the first clause regarding a place where they do melacha, for melacha is different in that it is impossible that it will not become known to the people of the place when he does it in the city, which is not so regarding other customs, which will not become known when he does them in private; therefore they were stringent upon him only that he not appear before, etc., meaning that he not do it in public. (b) Where the custom of his place is to be stringent and he is stringent: he must also be careful not to appear before them that he is being stringent, as above regarding melacha. However, where it is impossible for him that they not notice that he is changing from their custom, he should practice leniency like them even when his intention is to return to his place where they are stringent, because of controversy. And this is specifically if this matter is not forbidden according to the essence of the law, but they only practiced a prohibition; but a matter that is forbidden according to the law, even a rabbinic prohibition: Heaven forbid to transgress because of concern of enmity and controversy; and it is better to suffer endless quarrels and not to transgress the words of the Sages, for one who transgresses the words of the Sages is liable to death at the hands of Heaven.
(24) Because of controversy: the poskim wrote that if the custom of the people of his place is to be lenient and he did according to his custom in private, and the people of the place where he is now found him, he must stop from his custom so that they not recognize it in him, because of controversy; but if Torah scholars found him, he need not stop, for a Torah scholar knows that this depends on custom and there is no controversy here; and nevertheless, l'chatchila he should not do according to his custom even before Torah scholars.
