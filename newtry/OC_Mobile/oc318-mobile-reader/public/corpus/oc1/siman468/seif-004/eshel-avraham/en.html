(א) To a place. Sons of chutz la'aretz coming to Eretz Yisrael — may sons of Eretz Yisrael do melacha for them discreetly? See siman 9 and siman 10; and similarly may a kohen from Eretz Yisrael ascend to the duchan for them on second day Yom Tov — see siman 11.

(ב) That he went there. A son of chutz la'aretz who comes to dwell in Eretz Yisrael and was not released, and before one Festival his intention is to return — must he observe second day Yom Tov? See siman 9 and siman 12; Maharival part 3 siman 93; and Yoreh De'ah siman 214. Rosh wondered: if they err thinking it forbidden we rule we tell them it is permitted; if prohibition is because of a fence, why mention erev Pesach even all year on erev Shabbat as a vow — see siman 214; it works out if they know it is permitted — only practical difference for those coming from other places who must practice stringency of the place they went to; Peri Chadash holds to strain — see there. Nevertheless Tosafot explain: a custom practiced as a fence, such as here — their children may not change their custom; but on other days of the year they may change — see Tosafot Pesachim daf 51. On this reasoning I compiled to answer Peri Chadash's difficulties: he wondered on Shulchan Aruch's contradictory words — Yoreh De'ah siman 228 wrote if it was with agreement as a fence for the public or safeguard, one may not permit, etc.; and further wrote: but a known permitted matter they were stringent as a safeguard — one does not permit automatically; he explains through regret and certainty one permits — see there; but truth guides otherwise after we find in the Gemara in Bishan they practiced not to go from Tyre to Sidon on Friday night — R' Yosi told them: your fathers could have done so; we cannot; you already accepted — hear, my son, your father's discipline, etc.; and Ran agreed that a matter ancestors practiced as prohibition and children thought it is forbidden by law — one reveals it is only a safeguard; per my view he wrote precisely: if with public fence agreement or safeguard — but when ancestors accepted, now children stand in their place for prohibition — do not abandon, etc. — one may not permit at all; unlike a known permitted matter one was stringent as safeguard alone — he alone was stringent, etc. — it is a vow and one permits through opening and regret; understand well.
