(א) One who travels, etc. — see Mishna Berurah; know this entire seif is Rambam's language and Acharonim disputed his explanation; Magen Avraham and several Acharonim also hold like Tosafot and Rosh as Magen Avraham explained the entire seif — as we copied in Mishna Berurah; Shach's view in Yoreh De'ah end of siman 214 and so Chayei Adam and Gra hold Rambam has another view: per him one traveling from a place where they do to where they do not, without intention to return — nevertheless in the wilderness permitted; per them forbidden unless in the wilderness outside the city boundary; and with intention to return — per him permitted even in settlement if discreet; per them forbidden unless wilderness outside the city boundary; and one traveling from where they do not to where they do without intention to return — per him forbidden, must practice stringencies of place he left; per them permitted; nevertheless for practical law Shach wrote there and Chayei Adam and I saw in other Acharonim we follow Tosafot and Rosh as the Mechaber ruled in siman 496 and siman 474 and Yoreh De'ah siman 214 — therefore I explained the seif per Magen Avraham since at least for halacha we follow him even Shach and Chayei Adam.

(ב) And stringencies of the place, etc. — see Acharonim who wrote stringencies of the place he left do not cease from him only if the place he came to has a fixed custom of leniency; but not in a city without fixed custom. They also wrote in a community that was destroyed and resettled by others who practiced leniency in places they came from — they are not obligated now to be stringent though that destroyed settlement's people practiced stringency, since its people were dispersed their custom ceased; in Peri Megadim he doubts even if some remained since most were dispersed — see there; in my humble opinion if some remain, even a minority, all who come there are obligated to practice this place's custom, for there is no minimum for a community as Peri Chadash below proves; even many coming from outside attach to the minority there unless the new community practices separately and does not mix with the old — as in large communities with several kehillot each practicing ancestral custom; explained in Sefer Peri Chadash: a community with a minyan is called a community and arrivals attach; plainly his intent is it has all community needs as usual — synagogue, daily public prayer, rabbi, mikveh, etc.; otherwise it attaches to neighboring cities; see many details in Peri Chadash and Chayei Adam general rule 127.
