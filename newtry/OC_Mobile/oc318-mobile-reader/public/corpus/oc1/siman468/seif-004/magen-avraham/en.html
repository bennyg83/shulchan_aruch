Because of controversy. For melacha cannot be done in private like other things that are permitted when his intention is to return (Tosafos and Ran).
He does so in the wilderness. When his intention is to return (Maggid Mishneh); and if he is still outside the city's techum, even if he does not intend to return, it is permitted (Ran).
That he left from there. When his intention is to return; but if he does not intend to return, he practices even the leniencies of the place he went to, as is stated in the first chapter of Chullin; and so wrote the Ran; and so it is at the end of siman 214 in Yoreh De'ah; and the words of the Shach there require further study.
And the stringencies of the place he went to. Even though his intention is to return, because of controversy.
And nevertheless he should not appear, etc. That is to say: when he practices the stringencies of the place from which he left, he should not appear, etc.
And provided that he does not appear, etc. It implies that this is a general rule for every matter of eating, and other things are permitted in private, except melacha, which cannot be done in private; therefore it is permitted only in the wilderness, as written above; and so it is clear in Maggid Mishneh. And it is implied in the Ran that if they came and found him eating, he must cover the food before them; but before Torah scholars who know that this depends on custom, he need not cover it; see what I wrote at the end of siman 496, and in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 83, and in the responsa of the Rashba, siman 337, and in the responsa of R' Levi ibn Chaviv. It is written in Maharil: the youths who eat in Mainz from lung examinations, in which they are lenient there, there is no concern, for it is written in the Mordechai that we are not expert in the stringencies of the place from which one left and of the place one went to. And again he said that it is in Tosafos, first chapter of Pesachim, folio 14: in Jerusalem it is reasonable to practice a prohibition of melacha on erev Pesach, since people gather there even from places where they practiced a prohibition; and from here he practiced not [to eat] pantzen of the people of the Rhine in his house, because youths gather there from other places; and this requires further study, what differs from the lung examinations above; and perhaps because the prohibition of cheilev is according to the Torah and involves kares, he was stringent; end of his words. And it appears to me: granted regarding examination it is called private, for from where would the youths know what happened in the examination of the animal? Only the examiner knows; but pantzen are recognizable and visible, that in other places they practice a prohibition regarding them; therefore we are not permitted to permit them before them, similar to Jerusalem, where they practiced a prohibition of melacha, which is a public matter. And what he wrote to permit the youths to eat from lung examinations, etc., implies that he holds that youths are considered as intending to return, even though their intention is to study there two or three years. And nevertheless this requires further study: why did he write that we are not expert, etc.? And further, if we are not expert, we should be stringent. And it is possible that he means one should not protest against them because we are not expert, and from doubt one should not protest; and there is a great difficulty in this, for there are many distinctions between country and country regarding lung examinations, and if so he should have practiced the stringencies of the place from which he left; and so it is clear in Yoreh De'ah siman 64, se'if 9 and se'if 14. And in the Mordechai at the beginning of Yevamos it is written: people of Mainz, when they come to Worms, the people of Worms must inform them of some matters in which they practice leniencies; and then, when they inform them, if their inclination follows after them, it is permitted to practice those leniencies even though they are forbidden in their place, as we say in chapter "Kirah": R' Abba, in the place of R' Yehoshua ben Levi, would carry a lamp; and Maharam there wrote; and in another Mordechai he concludes, and this is its language: and this requires further study in the chapter "A place where they practiced," that they impose upon him the stringencies of the place from which he left; and further, that R' Abba held to permit like R' Shimon, and in the places of R' Yehuda he was stringent; end of his words. And it appears to me that there is no difficulty: for the Mordechai too means this: if it appears to him according to the law that it is permitted, it is permitted to eat; but the first difficulty still stands in its place; and perhaps for this reason Maharil wrote that we are not expert.
