Se'if 4: One who goes, etc. All of it is the language of the Rambam; and see Tosafos there 51a, s.v. "Rabbah," etc. And the Rambam holds that the sugya of Chullin is pushed aside before the sugya of Pesachim, and the Mishnah deals with one who does not intend to return; and that which R' Zeira ate of a "mugremet" is because he disputes Rav Yosef, as written there: he said to him, who said it, etc.; and see Tosafos there, s.v. "Yosef," etc. But according to the conclusion of Pesachim one must say as written in the Yerushalmi there, and the Rosh brought it there: any stringency that is because of error, one need not practice like the stringencies of the place; and R' Zeira held that it was in error, as written there, as above. And that is what is written: "one who goes," etc., and likewise "one who," etc.; it is implied that at first it deals with one who does not intend to return; and see Rashba, siman 357, and his view on this.
He should not do. There, 52a; and he holds that one who does not intend to return is [the case], not like Tosafos there.
And one who goes, etc., and they impose, etc. The Mishnah there.
And even so, etc., forever. There, 51b; and according to Rava's explanation, which is not so when he appears before them; and he explained that the Mishnah means this: forever he should not change, etc., meaning that he should not appear before them, etc.; and that is what is written: "And even so," etc., "forever," etc.
And likewise one who. There, 51a.
And provided. There: since he saw them, he covered it; and even though Abaye objected, that is because since it is in private and they already found him eating, there is no concern of controversy among Torah scholars, for Torah scholars know that he was acting according to law; and see there in the Ran. And the view of Tosafos there and in Chullin, according to the first resolution, and the Rosh in chapter 4 of Pesachim, is that the Mishnah teaches for different sides, and everything follows the place where his intention is to settle, whether for stringency as in the Mishnah or for leniency as in these two incidents; and even so he should not change before them even to be stringent, as written in the Mishnah, specifically because they say "how many," etc.; otherwise he must act like them, since there is no Torah prohibition; and see Tosafos there 52a, s.v. "From a place," etc. And that case of a settled place being forbidden, etc., is where his intention is to return; for if he does not intend to return, once he reached a settled place, even in the wilderness it is forbidden, for everything follows the place where his intention is to settle, and even if he left from there, as above. This is well for the Rambam, that even if he does not intend to return the main factor is the place from which he left, only that they impose upon him the stringencies of the place he went to because of controversy; and see Tosafos there, the reason that melacha cannot be done in private. And that of Chullin in the incident of Rami bar Tamrei: one must say that he did not intend to return, for otherwise why say outside the techum? Even within the city, in private it is permitted, like the incident of Rabbah in the Bach; and one must say that even if he does not intend to return, it is not fixed like them until he reaches a settled place within the techum. And that is what is written in siman 496, se'if 3: people of Eretz Yisrael, etc., according to the view of Tosafos as above; and all this not from that of Chullin. But if: as above, that everything follows the place where he wishes to settle. And all, etc.: from that of Chullin as above.
