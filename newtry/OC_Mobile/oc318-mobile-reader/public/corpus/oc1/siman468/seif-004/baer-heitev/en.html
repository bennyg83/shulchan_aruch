(א) One who travels. See Kaf HaChayyim who expanded on this (and even discreetly it is forbidden); and it is explained there that if in some place they practiced prohibition regarding patzikan and that community was dispersed and other people settled there whose custom is to eat patzikan in their country — they are not obligated to practice the stringencies of the place they went to, since the community was nullified their custom ceased and the original act was removed — see there. And see Magen Avraham end of siman 493.

(ב) In the wilderness. When his intention is to return; and if he is still outside the city's boundary, even if he does not intend to return it is permitted — Ran, Magen Avraham.

(ג) That he left. When his intention is to return; but if he does not intend to return he practices even the leniencies of the place he went to — Shulchan Aruch, Eliyah Rabbah, and in the hands of Aharon.

(ד) That he went. Even though his intention is to return, because of the dispute.

(ה) Appear. Meaning: when he practices the stringencies of the place he left from there, he should not appear, etc.

(ו) Appear. It implies this is a general rule for everything edible; other things are permitted discreetly except melacha, which cannot be done discreetly — therefore it is permitted only in the wilderness. And it is implied in the Ran that if they came and found him eating he must cover the food before them, but before Torah scholars who know this depends on custom he need not cover it — see end of siman 496 (and Yoreh De'ah siman 83 and responsum of Rashba and Maharil Chaviv; and in Maharil it is written: since Tosafot Pesachim 14 wrote that in Jerusalem it is reasonable to practice melacha prohibition on erev Pesach since people gather there from stringent places, the practice arose not to eat Rhenish patzikan in his house. Magen Avraham rejected his proof; and what Mordechai wrote about sons of Germiza informing sons of Mainz of leniencies they practice — see there. And see in my composition Minhag Medinah that each person comes to his place). And see Magen Avraham at length.
