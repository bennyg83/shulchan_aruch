And even. As on Chol HaMoed; and see siman 542, se'if 1; and as I wrote below.
And there is one who forbids, etc. As on Chol HaMoed, as written in Moed Katan 12a; and it is implied in chapter 4 of Pesachim and in Moed Katan 13a that erev Pesach is equal to Chol HaMoed except in those matters in which the Sages distinguished. And even though erev Pesach is forbidden only by rabbinic law, as written at the beginning of Pesachim 2b, nevertheless even in rabbinic [matters] telling a non-Jew is forbidden, as written regarding a non-Jew who came to extinguish, etc.; and even if he did [it] of his own accord it is forbidden, as written regarding a non-Jew who brought flutes, etc., and they said: one who came from outside the techum, etc. And further, also regarding Chol HaMoed there are those who say it is rabbinic.
And there are, etc. As written in Bava Metzia 90a, "they asked them," etc. It implies that regarding a rabbinic prohibition it was obvious to them that it is permitted; and even though on Shabbos it is forbidden even in a rabbinic matter, as above: Shabbos is different, for its essence is according to the Torah, as Tosafos wrote in many places; and from Chol HaMoed there is no proof, as Tosafos wrote there, s.v. "But," etc. And according to the first reasoning one may say that the same applies on erev Pesach, for it too is included in Moed; and further one may say that regarding every prohibition of melacha they decreed because of Shabbos.
