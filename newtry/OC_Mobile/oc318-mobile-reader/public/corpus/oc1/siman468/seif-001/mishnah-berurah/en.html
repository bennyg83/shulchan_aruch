(1) From midday onward: the reason is because it is the time of slaughtering the Pesach, for every one of Yisrael is obligated in it, and a day on which they bring a korban is like a Yom Tov; therefore melacha is forbidden by rabbinic law; and even nowadays when there is no korban, the prohibition still stands in its place.
(2) They place him under a ban: and he also does not see a sign of blessing from that melacha.
(3) For free it is forbidden: if it is complete melacha, as below in se'if 2.
(4) Even through a non-Jew: as on Chol HaMoed.
(5) And there is one who permits: even if he does it in the house of a Jew, for they hold that they were not so stringent in this regarding telling a non-Jew as on Chol HaMoed and Yom Tov. And regarding getting a haircut through a non-Jew after midday, many later authorities agreed to be lenient even though he tilts his head toward the barber and assists somewhat; but through a Jew it is forbidden even for free, because it is complete melacha. And cutting nails: if he forgot to cut them before midday, one should be lenient even by himself or through another for free.
