1) [Se'if 1] One who performs work on the eve of Pesach from midday onward, etc. The Yerushalmi explains that the reason is that the time for bringing the korban Pesach begins at midday, and on a day when an individual brings a korban, that individual is forbidden to perform work. Work is prohibited on the eve of Pesach only from midday onward because the korban may not be offered before midday. Although there is no korban Pesach nowadays, once the work was prohibited, the prohibition remained even after its reason ceased to apply. Tosafos and Ran at the beginning of the chapter Makom Shenahagu. Rosh writes likewise there and brings a proof. See there. Beis Yosef cites his words. Rashi there, however, explains that the prohibition ensures that one will not become preoccupied with work and forget to eliminate chametz, slaughter the Pesach offering, and prepare matzah for the night. See there. Although Rashi gives this reason for communities whose custom is not to work the entire day, it creates a practical difference when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos. Even though the korban Pesach is not brought on Friday, according to Rashi those whose custom is not to work the entire day may not work that Friday. According to Tosafos, Ran, and Rosh, however, the prohibition is solely because of the korban, and since no korban is brought on Friday, work is permitted, as stated below in subsection 3. See there. Beis Yosef cites only Ran and Rosh, implying that he does not accept Rashi's rationale. Levush likewise gives only the rationale of Tosafos and Ran, as do Taz, subsection 2, and Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Peri Chadash writes that this is the primary rationale, contrary to Rashi. See there. Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 129, subsection 2, write likewise.
2) Tosafos write there that the Yerushalmi implies the prohibition is under Torah law. Responsa Rosh, rule 55, siman 10, writes likewise. Ran there, however, writes that it may be only rabbinic. See there. Tosafos, Chagigah 17b, beginning with the words 'Rather, is it not,' write that the Babylonian Talmud maintains that the prohibition is only rabbinic. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, chapter 6, likewise writes that Tosafos and Ran disagree only in their interpretation of the Yerushalmi; according to the Babylonian Talmud, even Tosafos agree that the prohibition is rabbinic. See there. Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov, chapter 8, halachah 18, likewise writes that performing work on the fourteenth of Nissan is rabbinically prohibited like Chol HaMoed, although it is more lenient than Chol HaMoed. It is prohibited only from midday onward, the time of slaughtering the korban, while from sunrise until midday the law depends upon local custom. See there. Sefer Mitzvos Gadol likewise writes that even nowadays, work is rabbinically prohibited on the day of the korban as on Chol HaMoed. See there. Peri Chadash therefore concludes, in accordance with Rambam, that the prohibition against work after midday on the eve of Pesach is only rabbinic. See there. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1, cites this and writes that it is also Rashi's opinion. See there. Magen HaElef, subsection 3, likewise writes that it is only rabbinic.
3) When the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos, Derashos Maharil, laws of Shabbos HaGadol, writes that one who wishes to be personally stringent and refrain from work after midday on Friday should do so because the Sages made no distinction. See there. Knesses HaGedolah cites this in Pesach Me'ubin, subsection 93, and writes that it is merely a stringency. He concludes that he has not seen anyone observe it even as a stringency. See there. Zera Emes, siman 64, was likewise asked about this and concludes that since most authorities agree with the korban rationale, work is plainly permitted. See there. Nachpeh BaKesef, Orach Chaim, siman 7, likewise concludes that it is completely permitted by law, while one who is stringent will be blessed. See there. Admas Kodesh, volume 1, Orach Chaim, siman 10, however, concludes that it is prohibited. See there. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1, writes that Admas Kodesh's proofs are not decisive and that the primary ruling follows Nachpeh BaKesef and Knesses HaGedolah. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, writes that in a place where the custom on the eve of Pesach is to work until midday, work on Friday after midday appears permitted even according to Rashi, because such a community does not accept his concern about preoccupation. See there. Most authorities thus agree that the prohibition is based upon the korban. Even in a place whose custom is not to work throughout the day, when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos one may work on Friday. In a place whose custom is to work until midday, it is permitted even according to Rashi. Nevertheless, in a place whose custom is not to work throughout the day, one who is stringent out of concern for Rashi's opinion will be blessed.
4) There. From midday onward, etc. See Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 696, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 5. He writes that although the custom on the eve of Pesach after midday is not to engage in commerce, that case differs because work after midday is prohibited due to offering the korban, unlike Purim. Nevertheless, we have neither seen nor heard of anyone penalizing a person who occasionally conducted business on the eve of Pesach after midday, especially in private. See there. Beis David, siman 243, however, writes that commerce is permitted on the eve of Pesach. See there. Zechor LeAvraham cites this in the present siman and writes that Beis David apparently did not see the cited words of Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, since he does not mention them. Zechor LeAvraham further writes that he instituted the closing of shops at midday in his city and heard that this is likewise the custom in Sofia. See below, subsection 30, and above, siman 251, subsection 2.
5) A worker who has nothing to eat on Pesach may perform work on the eve of Pesach after midday. The law cannot be more stringent than Chol HaMoed, which is called a sacred convocation. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 5; Olas Shabbos, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 5; Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 4; Chok Yaakov, subsection 11; Peri Chadash, se'if 5; Chok Yosef, subsection 8; Chayei Adam, rule 129, subsection 4; Ruach Chaim, subsection 3; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 6. See below, subsection 21; siman 542, se'if 2; and siman 545, se'if 3.
6) There. The court places the person under a ban. It is unnecessary to say that if the court did not impose a ban, it gives the person lashes for rebellious conduct. Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov, chapter 8, halachah 17. Maggid Mishneh explains there that a ban is more severe than lashes for rebellious conduct, as explained in chapter 1. See there. Tosafos, Kesubos 28a, beginning with the words 'Rav Huna,' write likewise, and this also appears in Rashi, Kiddushin 70b. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2, cites these sources. See there. A Torah scholar is not placed under a ban, but is given lashes privately. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1, based upon Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, siman 334, se'if 42. See there. See below, siman 496, se'if 1, and Responsa Sha'ar Ephraim, siman 64, which discusses at length that a Torah scholar is not placed under a ban. See there. See also below, siman 476, subsection 11.
7) There. The court places the person under a ban. In this respect as well, the Sages were more stringent regarding the eve of Pesach than other Fridays and eves of Yom Tov. In those cases, one who works from Minchah time onward will not see a sign of blessing from that work, but is not placed under a ban. On the eve of Pesach from midday onward, however, the court also places the person under a ban. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, based upon Pesachim 50b. See above, siman 251, subsection 5.
8) There. The court places the person under a ban. Tur cites the Gemara's statement that the person will never see a sign of blessing. Re'em and Maharshal explain that this refers specifically to that enterprise. 'Never' means that even if the person makes some profit from it, an equivalent loss will arise elsewhere. Bach and Taz, subsection 1, cite this explanation. Chok Yaakov, subsection 3. The later authorities write likewise. Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov, chapter 8, halachah 17, implies that the act is also prohibited. Chok Yaakov, there; Peri Chadash, siman 496, subsection 18; Mishbetzos Zahav, siman 251, subsection 1. See above, siman 251, subsection 7.
9) There. It is forbidden even to perform it without payment. This applies when it is full-fledged labor, as stated below in the gloss to se'if 2. See there. Bathing after midday, however, is permitted. Mateh Moshe, siman 565; Derashos Maharil, laws of Shabbos HaGadol, in the gloss.
10) There. Some prohibit even through a non-Jew. This is like labor on Chol HaMoed. Beis Yosef in the name of Mordechai. See below, siman 543.
