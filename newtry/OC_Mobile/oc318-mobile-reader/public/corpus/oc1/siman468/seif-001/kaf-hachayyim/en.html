1) [Se'if 1] One who performs work on the eve of Pesach from midday onward, etc. The Yerushalmi explains that the reason is that the time for bringing the korban Pesach begins at midday, and on a day when an individual brings a korban, that individual is forbidden to perform work. Work is prohibited on the eve of Pesach only from midday onward because the korban may not be offered before midday. Although there is no korban Pesach nowadays, once the work was prohibited, the prohibition remained even after its reason ceased to apply. Tosafos and Ran at the beginning of the chapter Makom Shenahagu. Rosh writes likewise there and brings a proof. See there. Beis Yosef cites his words. Rashi there, however, explains that the prohibition ensures that one will not become preoccupied with work and forget to eliminate chametz, slaughter the Pesach offering, and prepare matzah for the night. See there. Although Rashi gives this reason for communities whose custom is not to work the entire day, it creates a practical difference when the eve of Pesach falls on Shabbos. Even though the korban Pesach is not brought on Friday, according to Rashi those whose custom is not to work the entire day may not work that Friday. According to Tosafos, Ran, and Rosh, however, the prohibition is solely because of the korban, and since no korban is brought on Friday, work is permitted, as stated below in subsection 3. See there. Beis Yosef cites only Ran and Rosh, implying that he does not accept Rashi's rationale. Levush likewise gives only the rationale of Tosafos and Ran, as do Taz, subsection 2, and Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Peri Chadash writes that this is the primary rationale, contrary to Rashi. See there. Chok Yosef, subsection 1; Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 129, subsection 2, write likewise.
