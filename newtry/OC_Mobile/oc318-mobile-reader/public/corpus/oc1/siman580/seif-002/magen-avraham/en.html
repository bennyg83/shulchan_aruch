Sefer Tanya wrote: the Geonim were asked about these fasts and they replied, We do not know who enacted them, and therefore one should not fast on Rosh Chodesh; and so wrote the Shelah; and Beis Yosef wrote, I have never seen nor heard of anyone who practiced fasting on them, end quote. But I have seen that they are written in Sefer Megillas Taanis; if so, [the practice] was enacted in the days of the Sages of the Talmud (within the words of this sefer); and Halachos Gedolos also wrote them, for all of his words are words of received tradition; therefore every baal nefesh should be stringent upon himself if it is possible for him. It is written in the Shelah: these fasts are found in three sefarim, in the Tur and in Kol Bo and in Halachos Gedolos, and there are variant versions, and the custom is to follow the majority of the versions, end quote. And I have seen in Sefer Megillas Taanis some other versions, and it appears to me to follow them, for from there the root of the matter went forth. And even though in Sefer Megillas Taanis printed anew in Amsterdam there is written beside it "another version," that is a comment that the commentator added and took from the words of the Shulchan Aruch, but in the old sefarim "another version" is not found. It appears to me that one who is not accustomed to fast these fasts, and some trouble befalls him due to our many sins, and he wishes to fast on Rosh Chodesh Nisan for his trouble, it is forbidden, since he treats it as a day of fasting prohibition; see siman 468 and in Yoreh De'ah siman 214. When the moon is eclipsed one should fast (Sefer Chassidim, siman 230). On the 28th of it. In Megillas Taanis, on the 29th of Iyar Shmuel died. In Megillas Taanis and in Kol Bo and in Halachos Gedolos the reading is: on the 7th of Elul the bringers-forth [of the evil report] died, etc.; and so it is in the Responsa of the Rosh and in Beis Yosef; and therefore one must say that a scribe's error fell into the Shulchan Aruch.
