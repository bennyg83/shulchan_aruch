On every Monday and Thursday. Beis Yosef, siman 429, wrote that they fast on Monday and Thursday even in Nisan, and they have not practiced thus. Some abstain on Monday and Thursday from meat and wine, and also on the night beforehand; and it appears to me that in the summer days, when they eat before nightfall, one need not be stringent unless they accepted upon themselves to reckon it as night after they have prayed Maariv; and see siman 299, se'if 6 in the gloss. And some practice on the night after them not to eat meat and wine; and Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 7 of Bava Kamma, wrote the reason: because capital cases are judged by day and concluded at night, end quote. And it appears to me that it is a scribe's error, and one must say that monetary cases are judged, etc., for capital cases are not concluded at night; and he holds that the Holy One, blessed be He, judges even monetary cases, whether a person will lose or gain. And it appears from his words that he holds the reason they fast on Monday and Thursday is because they are days of judgment; and this is surprising, for where is this hinted, and on the contrary we hold that a person is judged every day; and what [reason] they fast on them is because they are days of favor, as is written in siman 134; and so wrote Tosafos and Rashi on Shabbos, and Tosafos on Kesubos folio 3; therefore there is no reason to fast on the night after them, for the day has already passed. And it appears to me that one who cannot fast should at least pray on Monday and Thursday concerning these matters. And it is stated in the writings that it is fitting for every Jew to weep over the burning of the Torah, for through this the Torah was delivered to the kelipos; may the Omnipresent restore it to us speedily in our days. Sefer Tanya wrote: on Friday of Parashas Chukas, individuals have the custom to fast, for on that day twenty wagonloads full of sefarim were burned in France; and they did not fix it by the days of the month, because through a dream-inquiry it became known to them that the day of the parashah causes [it]: the decree of the Torah, "This is the statute of the Torah," which is translated, "This is the decree of the Torah"; and also in the year 5408 two great communities were destroyed on that day, as is written in the Selichos that the author of the Sifsei Kohen composed. They also have the custom to fast on the 20th of Sivan throughout the kingdom of Poland; each river has its own course; heal its break, for it has declined.
