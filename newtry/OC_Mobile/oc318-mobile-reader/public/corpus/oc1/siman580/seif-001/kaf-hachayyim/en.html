1) [Seif 1] These are the days, etc. The Tanya wrote: The Geonim were asked concerning these fasts, and they replied, 'We do not know who instituted them, and therefore one should not fast on Rosh Chodesh.' So too wrote Shibbolei HaLeket. The Beit Yosef wrote: 'I have never seen or heard of anyone who practiced fasting on these days.' End quote. However, I saw them written in Sefer Megillas Taanis. If so, they were instituted in the days of the Sages of the Gemara. Behag also recorded them, and all his words are words of tradition. Therefore, every conscientious person should be stringent for himself if possible. Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman.
2) The Shelah wrote on 201a: These fasts are found in three works, the Tur, the Kol Bo, and Behag, and their textual versions differ. The practice is to follow the majority of the textual versions. End quote. I saw in Sefer Megillas Taanis several other textual versions, and it appears to me that one should follow them, for they are the source from which these matters emerged. Although in Sefer Megillas Taanis newly printed in Amsterdam the notation 'another version' appears alongside it, this was an addition made by the commentator, who took it from the words of the Shulchan Aruch. In the old editions, however, the notation 'another version' is not found. It appears that one who is not accustomed to observing these fasts, and, Heaven forbid, a calamity occurred to him and he wishes to fast on Rosh Chodesh Nisan because of his distress, is forbidden to do so, since his practice is to treat that day as one on which fasting is prohibited. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, os 1.
3) An eclipse of the moon is an unfavorable sign for the enemies of Israel, and therefore one must fast. Sefer Chassidim, siman 230; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, ibid.; Mishna Berurah, os 2. Nevertheless, Maamar Mordechai, os 5, raised the difficulty: What is the reason for this fast, since eclipses of the sun and moon necessarily occur according to the fixed order of the paths and movements of the celestial spheres, etc.? He answered that nevertheless, since it is a cause of distress for the world, it is proper to fast. See there. Likewise, the author of Iyun Yaakov, in his novellae to tractate Sukkah there, expressed wonder concerning this and wrote an answer. See there. Yafeh LaLev, part 2, os 12, cited his words; see there at length. See further what I, the lowly one, wrote in my novellae to tractate Sukkah, 29a. See there.
