In Shulchan Aruch: One says Al Hanissim, etc. See above, siman 676, that a convert may also say, that You performed for our fathers, etc. Regarding what he wrote, if he remembered in that blessing, etc.: if he mentioned the Name and completed the blessing, he is not permitted to go back as long as he is in the middle of his prayer, and if he goes back it is a blessing in vain. So wrote Siach Chachamim in the name of Mateh Yehudah. See there in the name of Radbaz, siman 561, that he may say Al Hanissim or Ya'aleh Veyavo between one blessing and another, for there is no blessing in vain here, and it is not an interruption either, since it is related to the blessing. He wrote that this is the proper practice. Knesses HaGedolah brought the words of Radbaz without objection, and this is also the view of Ma'adanei Hamelech. But in Birkei Yosef, siman 188, he questioned this; see there. See there in the name of Michtam LeDavid: one who forgot Al Hanissim and thought that he had to go back and begin praying again, and then remembered that he had erred in the law, for in truth he does not need to go back, should stop even in the middle of a blessing; see there. See there also regarding one who forgot Al Hanissim or the like, where he does not need to go back but wants to be stringent and go back and pray, that he must intend it as a voluntary prayer. He must also know of himself that he can concentrate well in his prayer from beginning to end; and if he knows how to add something new in it and will not be distracted, it is good to do so; see there. See Baer Heitev concerning the text of Al Hanissim, that one should say wicked with a hataf. In Pri Tevuah he wrote to read it with a chirik under the resh. Regarding what is written, to make them pass from the statutes of Your will, Pri Chadash wrote that it is more correct to say, from the statutes of Your will. So too wrote Abudarham, that this is the text in the order of Rav Saadiah, and so wrote Maharam of Rothenburg; see there.
