1) [Seif 1] During all 8 days of Chanukah one recites Al HaNissim, etc. It is written in Orchos Chaim that Yochanan was the Chashmonai, as written in Sefer Yosippon. Some say the word Chashmonai derives from the verse, "Nobles shall come," and denotes greatness. Beit Yosef. So too wrote Abudraham: Yochanan, the Kohen Gadol, who was called Chashmonai. It is likewise stated in Seder Olam that Yochanan the Kohen Gadol had 2 names, Yochanan and Chashmonai. However, Pri Chadash wrote that one must pronounce Chashmonai with a chirik under the alef, meaning that he came from the family of Chashmonai. Rav Pe'alim, vol. 4, siman 31, agreed with the words of Pri Chadash; see there. So too he wrote in his sefer Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Vayeishev, letter 25.
1) Olas Shabbos further wrote in the name of Orchos Chaim that some pronounce harish'ah with a chirik under the reish, but it is more correct to pronounce it with a chataf patach under the reish. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham. However, Pri Chadash wrote that harish'ah should be pronounced with a chirik under the reish, unlike Orchos Chaim cited by the Beit Yosef.
1) The customary wording of Al HaNissim in everyone's mouth is "in those days and at this time." Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur. Pri Chadash wrote that one must say "at this time," without the conjunction "and." So too is written above, siman 676, letter 5; see there. "Which You performed for our forefathers in those days" is the wording commonly used by everyone, and so it is in the siddur. However, Abudraham records the wording, "which You performed for us and for our forefathers." Knesset HaGedolah, ibid.
1) When the wicked Greek kingdom arose against them, such is the wording commonly used by everyone, and so it is in the siddurim. However, Abudraham records only "when it arose," without "against them," because afterward one says, "against Your people Israel," and so it is in the siddurim. It appears to me that there is no concern with this. Nevertheless, if the version is "when it arose against them," one must say, "and against Your people Israel," with a vav. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur. Pri Chadash wrote that one must omit the word "against them," and one who wishes to say it must say, "and against Your people Israel," with a vav.
1) The customary wording is "to make them forget Your Torah and to cause them to transgress the statutes of Your will." Orchos Chaim and Abudraham wrote that one should not say mitorasecha, "from Your Torah," with a mem, but rather "to make them forget Your Torah." Likewise, one should not say meichukei retzonecha, "from the statutes of Your will," with a mem, but rather "the statutes of Your will." The grammatical point concerning mitorasecha is correct, for it is better to say, "to make them forget Your Torah." However, concerning meichukei, it is more correct to say it with a mem, as the world says. Pri Chadash. So too wrote Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 323, column 4, that one must say, "to make them forget Your Torah and to cause them to transgress the statutes of Your will"; see there. So too wrote Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Vayeishev, letter 25. Meaning, they sought to abolish Rosh Chodesh, Shabbos, and circumcision from them, these being fundamentals of the Torah and some of its statutes.
1) And for Yourself You made a great Name, etc. Such is the wording commonly used by everyone. Abudraham records the version, "for Yourself You made," without a vav, and so it is written in the siddurim. Knesset HaGedolah, ibid.; Pri Chadash; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 324a.
1) A great deliverance and redemption like this very day. Such is the wording commonly used by everyone, and so it is in the siddur. However, Abudraham records only the wording, "a great deliverance and redemption." Knesset HaGedolah, ibid.; Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid.
1) They established these 8 days with Hallel and thanksgiving. Such is the wording commonly used by everyone, and so it is in the siddur. Abudraham records the version, "And they established these days of Chanukah to thank and praise You." Knesset HaGedolah, ibid. Magen Avraham wrote that one should say, "these days of Chanukah," just as we say, "and You gave us this day of such-and-such festival." So too wrote Eshel Avraham, letter 1, that one should say, "And they established these 8 days of Chanukah," and such is the custom, end quote. So too wrote Ben Ish Chai, ibid.
1) And You performed miracles and wonders with them. Some versions read, "And You performed a miracle and a wonder with them." In the siddurim, the wording of Al HaNissim for Chanukah is "And You performed miracles and wonders with them," while the wording for Purim is "And You performed a miracle and a wonder with them." It is possible that the reason for the difference in wording between Chanukah and Purim is that on Chanukah there was the miracle of deliverance and rescue, and the miracle of the flask of oil. One should therefore say, "And You performed miracles and wonders with them." On Purim, however, there was only the miracle of rescue, and one should therefore say, "a miracle and a wonder." Knesset HaGedolah, ibid.; Pri Chadash; Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid., column 2. Yet I have seen that in most siddurim both here and there, on Chanukah and Purim, it reads, "And You performed miracles and wonders with them." The wording is sound, for on Purim as well we heard of 2 matters, namely, the miracle of rescue and also that Mordechai was blessed with greatness, as the Taz wrote below, siman 695, s.k. 1; see there. Yafeh LaLev, vol. 2, letter 3.
