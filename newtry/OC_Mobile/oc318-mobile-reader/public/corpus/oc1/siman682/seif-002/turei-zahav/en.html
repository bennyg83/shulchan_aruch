Even though there is no mussaf, etc. The reason is that nevertheless it is a day on which one became obligated in the mussaf prayer. In Hagahos Mordechai he wrote about this that if he did not say it, they make him go back. Beis Yosef wrote that this follows the view of Ravya, who holds that nowadays it is obligatory like the evening prayer, but he was not precise in this. That view holds that although on a weekday we do not make him go back, nevertheless on Shabbos, which has a mussaf, we do make him go back, and this is included in what is written in chapter Bameh Madlikin: days that have a mussaf, they make him go back. In truth, according to this view, even in Shacharis, Maariv, and Minchah they make him go back. The reason it mentioned mussaf is as a novelty: even though there is no mussaf on Chanukah, it is nevertheless called days that have a mussaf. In any event, Beis Yosef ruled well that we do not make him go back, for it is called days that have a mussaf only when the day has a mussaf because of the sanctity of that day with regard to that prayer. This is not so on Shabbos Chanukah, where there is a mussaf because of Shabbos and not because of Chanukah. Therefore we do not make him go back at all because of Al Hanissim that he forgot at any time on Chanukah. See also siman 693, where I wrote concerning the words of Mordechai on this.
