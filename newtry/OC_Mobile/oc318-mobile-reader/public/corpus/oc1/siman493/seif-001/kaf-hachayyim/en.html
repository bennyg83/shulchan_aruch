1) [Se'if 1] The custom is not to marry a woman, etc. This applies in all places. Tur; Levush; Bach.
2) There. Not to marry a woman, etc. The unqualified wording of Shulchan Aruch implies that there is no distinction between a mitzvah marriage, such as that of a man who has neither a wife nor children, and a marriage that does not constitute a mitzvah. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, writes likewise. Zera Emes, Orach Chaim, siman 68, also writes that even in a place where most people are not careful to refrain from haircuts during these days, permission should not be granted during the omer for a man who has not fulfilled the mitzvah to be fruitful and multiply to marry. Marriage is treated more stringently than haircuts, for we have seen a permissive custom only regarding haircuts. See there. Machazik Berachah, subsection 1, cites this and writes that although Maharam Lonzano and Pri Chadash permit a man who has not fulfilled the mitzvah to be fruitful and multiply to marry, and their reasoning is sound, the widespread custom is nevertheless that no marriages at all are held until Lag BaOmer. These are his words. Knesses HaGedolah, in Hagahos HaTur, also writes concerning the ruling of Maharam mentioned above that this is not our custom. Olas Shabbos there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1, write likewise. See Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, siman 35, who writes that on one occasion a scholar ruled leniently regarding marriage during the days of the counting, contrary to the custom, and the match did not prosper. He therefore writes that one who guards his soul should distance himself from marriage during such days, even when there are many grounds for leniency, because danger is treated more stringently than a prohibition, and one should not publicly nullify the custom. These are his words. See below, subsection 12.
3) There. Not to marry a woman, etc. Even an elderly widower who wishes to marry an elderly widow is forbidden to do so, because no distinction was made. Rashbash, siman 652; Zechor LeAvraham, subsection 70. Remarrying one's divorcee after nisuin is permitted in every case, because there is no rejoicing here, as stated below, siman 546, se'if 2. Maharikash; Pri Chadash; Chok Yosef, subsection 2; Shulchan Gavoah, at the end of subsection 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1.
4) There. Not to marry a woman, etc. The reason is not to increase rejoicing, because the students of Rabbi Akiva died during this period. Tur. Nevertheless, one may recite the blessing of Shehecheyanu during these days from Pesach until Lag BaOmer. Although it is written below, siman 551, se'if 17, that it is proper to refrain from reciting Shehecheyanu during Bein HaMetzarim, etc., the destruction of the Beis HaMikdash is different because it is more severe for us. Maamar Mordechai, subsection 2. There is no distinction between a fruit and a garment. Ikarei HaDat, at the end of siman 21, writes likewise. See there. Yafeh LaLev, volume 3, subsection 3. This is also how the halachah is taught in the study hall, and no concern is given to the statement in Moed LeKol Chai, siman 6, subsection 12, that it is a proper custom not to begin wearing a garment, etc., between Pesach and Lag BaOmer. See there.
