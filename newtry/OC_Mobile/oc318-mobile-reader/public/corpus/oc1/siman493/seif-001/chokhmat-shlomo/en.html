Seif 2. {Rama: However if it falls on Sunday they practice to get a haircut on Friday for honor of Shabbos.} NB: behold if Rosh Chodesh Sivan falls on Sunday — whether it is permitted to get a haircut on erev Shabbos. I saw in Peri Megadim who wrote it is permitted, as on Lag BaOmer; and he wondered on Peri Chadash who forbids when Rosh Chodesh Iyar falls on Sunday to get a haircut on Friday, and wrote he did not know his reason, see there. It appears to me regarding Rosh Chodesh Sivan Peri Megadim's words are certainly not correct, for there is no proof from Lag BaOmer — there, since if he does not get a haircut on Lag BaOmer he cannot afterward even for the coming Shabbos, therefore we are concerned for disgrace for two Shabbosos; and on Lag BaOmer itself, since it is one day, we are concerned for oness and the like; especially since it is a disgrace that on Shabbos he does not shave and on Sunday after Shabbos he shaves — therefore permitted to shave on erev Shabbos; especially that not for this Shabbos they were concerned, since on it they forbade shaving and we are not concerned for his disgrace; but for the coming Shabbos we are concerned, since on it they permitted shaving before on Lag BaOmer and he will not be disgraced on Shabbos; and if he does not shave now, perhaps on Lag BaOmer an oness will be born for him and the like, and also disgrace for Shabbos as above — therefore they permitted shaving on erev Shabbos. But on Rosh Chodesh Sivan he can shave afterward for a long time — they were not concerned for oness; he can shave close to the coming Shabbos and will not be disgraced; and for that Shabbos alone there is no shaving, for on it they were not concerned for disgrace, since they forbade shaving until Rosh Chodesh whenever it falls. Further, on Lag BaOmer, which itself is permitted by law for most poskim — if it falls on Sunday, permitted adjacent to it. But on Rosh Chodesh Sivan it is explicit in Magen Avraham there is no leniency except on the three days of restriction; Peri Megadim himself wrote he did not know whence the custom to permit on Rosh Chodesh derives — if so, do not add to it: specifically on Rosh Chodesh, but not to permit even before it. This is Peri Chadash's reason: since on Rosh Chodesh Iyar most forbid and the Mechaber wrote it is an error in their hands — therefore even those who are lenient mean on Rosh Chodesh itself and not to be lenient even before it; it is not comparable to Lag BaOmer where most poskim permit, as above. Therefore both on Rosh Chodesh Iyar and Rosh Chodesh Sivan one should forbid shaving on erev Shabbos — so it appears correct for practical halachah:
