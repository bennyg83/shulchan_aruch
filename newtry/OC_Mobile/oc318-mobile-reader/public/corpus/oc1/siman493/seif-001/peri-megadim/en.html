There is. See Taz. Know that there are three views here. (1) That the students of R' Akiva died thirty-four days; and a proof from the Midrash that they died from Pesach until the propes of Atzeres, which is fifteen days before Shavuos; and when you remove fifteen days from forty-nine, thirty-four full days remain on which they died; only that part of the day is like the whole day; therefore they get a haircut on day 34 in the morning; and the same law applies that marrying a woman is permitted from then on; and this is the view of the Mechaber in se'if 1 and se'if 2. And what the Rav glossed, "and likewise marrying a woman is permitted from Lag BaOmer onward," is according to the view of the Mechaber; but our custom is not so, as Magen Avraham wrote in letter 2, and it will be explained below, God willing. Nevertheless, the language that the Taz wrote, "then they ceased dying," refers to day 34, for thirty-four full days they died, as stated. And according to this, Lag BaOmer day has no superiority at all; and it is possible that nevertheless on day 34 only a minority died and they ceased dying at midday, and this is strained. The second view is what they say in the name of Tosafos, that the students of R' Akiva died thirty-three full days, namely that on a day when Tachanun is not said they did not die; if so, when you remove the seven days of Pesach, namely from the second day of Pesach, and further six Shabbosos, and two days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar, and day one of Rosh Chodesh Sivan, on which they did not die, if so thirty-three full days remain on which they died; and according to this they died until Shavuos. And if so, according to this there are two customs. (A) That they do not get a haircut from Pesach until Lag BaOmer morning, for Lag BaOmer morning is not a sign that they ceased dying on that very day, for that is not so; rather it is a sign of the count of days on which they died, namely that they died thirty-three full days, and they mourn thirty-three days from Pesach until Lag BaOmer, and part of the day is like the whole day, and they get a haircut on Lag BaOmer itself, and from then on it is permitted to get a haircut and to marry a woman. And some say the reverse, namely that until Rosh Chodesh, and Rosh Chodesh Iyar included, they get haircuts, and it turns out that from the second day of Pesach until the second day of Rosh Chodesh Iyar there are sixteen days, and corresponding to that they mourn until Shavuos; and whether according to custom one or according to custom two they mourn thirty-three days; namely that on Lag BaOmer they get a haircut even according to the second custom, for part of the day is like the whole day. And perhaps there is some reason that they specifically chose Lag BaOmer day specifically to get a haircut on it and to say part of the day is like the whole day according to the second custom. The third view is as written in the name of Tosafos, that thirty-three days they died, and if so on a day when Tachanun is not said they did not die; and if so they get a haircut on the two days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar and on day one of Rosh Chodesh Sivan, for on those days they did not die; but on all other days they practice a prohibition. And according to this, those who are permitted to get a haircut on Chol HaMoed as in siman 531, one may say that the same applies on Chol HaMoed of Pesach during Sefirah that it is permitted; for if not so, one may say that two prohibitions rest upon it (however, during the days of Sefirah on an ordinary weekday one may say that it is not greater than Chol HaMoed). However, those who get a haircut from Lag BaOmer onward and also get a haircut on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, it is a mistake in their hands, for there are not thirty-three days; and that is what the Mechaber wrote in se'if 3, that it is a mistake in their hands, as stated. And the words of the Taz who wrote that for us, who practiced a prohibition only until Lag BaOmer, for then they ceased dying, are somewhat difficult, for on day 34 as well they died in the morning, as written according to the view of the Mechaber. Also what he wrote, "automatically for us every day of the thirty-two is forbidden and no more," is somewhat difficult, for for us it implies that from Pesach until Shavuos they practice a prohibition, only on Lag BaOmer day itself they are lenient; and see what I wrote in Magen Avraham letter 5, and God willing it will be explained further; see there.
