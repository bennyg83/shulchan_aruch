There is Taz — know there are three views here. (1) The students of R' Akiva died 34 days; proof from Midrash they died from Pesach until "kneading" of Shavuos, 15 days before Shavuos; remove 15 from 49 — 34 full days died, only part of the day is like the whole day; therefore they get a haircut on the 34th in the morning, and likewise marrying is permitted from then; this is Mechaber seifim 1–2. What Rama glossed — likewise marrying permitted from Lag BaOmer — is per Mechaber, but our custom is not so, as Magen Avraham letter 2, explained below letter 15. Nevertheless Taz's language "then they ceased dying from day 34" refers to 34 full days died, as stated. Per this Lag BaOmer day has no special status; possibly on day 34 only a minority died and they ceased at midday — strained. Second view: Tosafot — students died 33 full days, on days when Tachanun is not said they did not die; remove seven days of Pesach from second day, six Shabbosos, two Rosh Chodesh Iyar days, one Rosh Chodesh Sivan — 33 full remain; per this they died until Shavuos. Two customs: (A) no haircut Pesach until Lag BaOmer morning — Lag BaOmer morning marks not cessation that day but count of days died, 33 full; mourn 33 Pesach to Lag BaOmer, part of day like whole, haircut on Lag BaOmer, permitted after; (B) until Rosh Chodesh Iyar included — 16 days Pesach second to Rosh Chodesh Iyar day 2, mourn until Shavuos, haircut Lag BaOmer even per custom 2. Perhaps reason they chose Lag BaOmer for part-of-day for custom 2. Third view: Tosafot — 33 days died; on days without Tachanun they did not die; haircut on two Rosh Chodesh Iyar days and day 1 Rosh Chodesh Sivan; other days prohibition. Those permitted on chol hamoed (siman 531) — perhaps likewise Pesach sefirah; otherwise two prohibitions compound (but plain weekdays of sefirah perhaps not worse than chol hamoed). Those who haircut from Lag BaOmer and Rosh Chodesh Iyar err — no 33 days; Mechaber seif 3.
