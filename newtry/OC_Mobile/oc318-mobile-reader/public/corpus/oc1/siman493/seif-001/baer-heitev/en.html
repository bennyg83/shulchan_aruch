(א) To marry. Bach wrote, and it appears to me there is no distinction between a marriage of mitzvah — such as when he has no children — or when he has, for so they practiced not to distinguish, end of his words. But Maharam of Lonzano in his book Shnei Yadayim wrote that when there is a reason — such as he did not fulfill peru urvu, or he has no one to attend him, and the like — we are not concerned for the custom; Peri Chadash agreed. Rikash in Erech Lechem wrote that remarrying one's divorcee is permitted:

(ב) To betroth. Perhaps another will precede him. Magen Avraham wrote one may also make a betrothal feast. And now that we do not betroth except at the time of marriage, nevertheless one may make shidduchin and make a feast, for even an optional feast they practiced to permit; only to make excessive rejoicing with dancing and round dances they practiced prohibition. Magen Avraham and Kaf HaChayyim see there.

(ג) Permitted. That is, for those who practice haircutting from Lag BaOmer onward; but for us, who practice prohibition of haircutting, marriage is also forbidden. Bach, Levush, Minhagim, and Darkei Moshe: he wrote in responsa Chinuch Beit Yosef that one who has no wife and children may marry a woman after Lag BaOmer. In responsa Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 siman 35 he disagreed and wrote that danger is more stringent than prohibition — once a certain scholar was lenient regarding marriage during sefirah and their match did not succeed well, see there. However until Rosh Chodesh Iyar they may marry as they may get a haircut, as Magen Avraham wrote in seif 3. He wrote in Minhagim and Bach that later authorities agreed that if Rosh Chodesh Iyar falls on Shabbos, all may marry on that Shabbos, even per our custom that forbids everything before Rosh Chodesh; Kaf HaChayyim likewise, see there. Or Zarua wrote that his grandfather the Gaon instituted in Prague to forbid marriage and also not to get a haircut on Rosh Chodesh except on Lag BaOmer and erev Shavuos specifically, see there. See Taz small seif 2; Kaf HaChayyim wrote every place follows its custom and in doubt one does not be stringent, see there. Knesset HaGedolah wrote regarding what Shulchan Aruch states "they do not punish him" — that is specifically regarding marriage where he does a mitzvah; but if he got a haircut during the days of the Omer they practice to fine and punish, see there.
