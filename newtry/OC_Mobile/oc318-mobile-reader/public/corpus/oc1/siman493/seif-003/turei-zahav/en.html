Some practice to get a haircut on Rosh Chodesh Iyar. Beis Yosef wrote that their reason is according to what the later authorities wrote in the name of Tosafos, that Lag BaOmer has no superiority of its own that [the deaths] ceased then; rather they took Lag as a mere mnemonic, namely that on days when Tachanun is not said they did not die; and if so, when you remove from the forty-nine days the seven days of Pesach, the seven Shabbosos, and the two days of Rosh Chodesh, which are sixteen days in which mourning does not apply, there remain of the forty-nine days thirty-three days; and they saw there that on the two days of Rosh Chodesh mourning does not apply, so they practiced leniency on them. And this is a confusion: for they said regarding the two days of Rosh Chodesh that mourning does not apply on them only for those who practice a haircut prohibition until Shavuos, for without the two days one finds thirty-three days that are the mnemonic for the days of death; but for us, who practice prohibition only until Lag BaOmer, for we hold that then they ceased dying entirely, it follows that all thirty-three days it is forbidden to get a haircut, even on Rosh Chodesh; thus far the words of Beis Yosef. Automatically, for us, all thirty-two days are forbidden, and no more.
And those [others] should not get a haircut from Lag BaOmer onward. For they hold that even after Lag BaOmer they died until Shavuos, for Lag is not a sign that the dying ceased on that very day, but rather the count of thirty-three is a sign of how many days the dying lasted; which is not so for those who get a haircut after Lag BaOmer, who hold that the dying was even on a day when Tachanun is not said, only that on Lag BaOmer the dying ceased entirely; and if so, until Lag BaOmer it is forbidden on all the days, and after Lag BaOmer it is permitted on all of them. And certainly our custom is not like this latter view, since we get haircuts and increase some joy on Lag BaOmer; whereas according to the first view Lag BaOmer day has no superiority at all. And the custom in all places is that they do not make marriages except on Lag BaOmer, neither before it nor after it until Shavuos, and there is no reason for this; and if you say that one holds the stringencies of both, then on Lag BaOmer itself they should neither get haircuts nor make marriages, for the reason of the second [view]. And one may say that certainly the main [view] is that after Lag BaOmer they did not die, and even so they practice some mourning because of the decree of [the year] 4856 that occurred in Ashkenaz between Pesach and Shavuos, as is explicit in the yotzeros and piyyutim that we say on those Shabbosos, which were instituted like kinot; so it appears to me as the reason for our custom that we do not marry after Lag BaOmer; but as for getting a haircut, it is certainly permitted after Lag BaOmer, for because of this decree they were stringent only regarding the extra joy of marriage, which is not so before Lag BaOmer, when the students of R' Akiva died and it is incumbent on the whole world to mourn for them, [so] they were more stringent even regarding haircutting. In Hagahos Maimoniyos it is written that if Rosh Chodesh Iyar falls on Shabbos, they marry on it.
