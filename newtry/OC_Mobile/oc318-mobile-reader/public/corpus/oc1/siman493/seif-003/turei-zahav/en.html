(א) Some practice to get a haircut on Rosh Chodesh Iyar. Bach wrote their reason per Acharonim in name of Tosafot: Lag BaOmer has no inherent significance that they ceased dying that day, but as a general marker they fixed sign 33 — on days when Tachanun is not said they did not die; remove from 49 days seven Pesach days and seven Shabbosos and two Rosh Chodesh days — sixteen days on which mourning does not apply — 33 days remain; they saw on two Rosh Chodesh days mourning does not apply and practiced leniency there; this is an error — they did not say on two Rosh Chodesh days mourning does not apply except for those who practice haircut prohibition until Shavuos, for without two days you would lack 33 days that are sign for days of death; but for us, who practice prohibition only until Lag BaOmer — they hold then death ceased entirely — automatically all 33 days forbidden to haircut even on Rosh Chodesh; Bach's words automatically for us every 32nd day forbidden, etc.

(ב) And those should not get a haircut from Lag BaOmer onward. For they hold even after Lag BaOmer they died until Shavuos, for 33 is not a sign of cessation of death that day but count of 33 marks how many days death lasted; unlike those who haircut after Lag BaOmer who hold death was even on days without Tachanun but on Lag BaOmer death ceased entirely — forbidden all days until Lag BaOmer, permitted after. Certainly our custom is not this last view, since we haircut and increase some joy on Lag BaOmer; whereas per first view Lag BaOmer has no special status, and custom everywhere not to marry except on Lag BaOmer day — not before or after until Shavuos — without reason; and you asked one who holds both stringencies — then on Lag BaOmer he should not haircut or marry per second reason. One may say: certainly the main point is after Lag BaOmer they did not die; nevertheless some mourning from decree of 1096 in Ashkenaz between Pesach and Shavuos as explicit in yotzrot and piyyutim said on those Shabbosos established like kinot — so appears reason our custom not to marry after Lag BaOmer; but haircut certainly permitted after Lag BaOmer, for from this decree they were stringent only in excessive marriage joy, unlike before Lag BaOmer when R' Akiva's students died and all must mourn — they were more stringent even in haircutting.
