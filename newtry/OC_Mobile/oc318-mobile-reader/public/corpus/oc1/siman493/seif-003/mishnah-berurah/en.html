(יד) Some practice, etc., and it is an error in their hands — I will explain briefly. According to all they practice prohibition for 33 days, but there are different customs in this [and each has a reason for his custom as explained in Biur Halachah]. Some chose them from the second day of Pesach until Lag BaOmer, and from then until Shavuos they did not accept any prohibition upon themselves; however until Lag BaOmer they do not permit any day. Some deducted from the 49 days of the Omer the first 16 days — until the second day of Rosh Chodesh Iyar — and from then until Shavuos, which is 33 days, they do not permit any day (except Lag BaOmer itself they permitted partially because of part of the day like the whole day); and one is found holding the rope at both ends — lenient on Rosh Chodesh Iyar and also lenient from Lag BaOmer onward — an error is in his hand:

(טו) And those should not get a haircut from Lag BaOmer onward — meaning until Shavuos itself, as above. Behold in our country the custom is to marry and get a haircut on the three days of restriction; therefore Magen Avraham concludes one must be careful not to marry or get a haircut on the second days of Iyar and also on the first of the days of restriction — specifically in the morning — for we say part of the day is like the whole day; thus the 33 days of mourning we practice will be completed. Chayei Adam wrote the custom is likewise in Vilna — prohibition from day 1 of Rosh Chodesh Iyar until 3 Sivan in the morning, except on Lag BaOmer they practice leniency; and some places are lenient only on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, Lag BaOmer, and Rosh Chodesh Sivan until Shavuos. In these matters each follows his local custom:

(יז) Leniency in both — meaning he practices leniency until Rosh Chodesh Iyar per the latter view and also leniency from Lag BaOmer until Shavuos per the first view, since these two leniencies contradict each other, as above; but he may adopt the stringencies of the customs — practice prohibition from Pesach until erev Shavuos or until day 1 of restriction except Lag BaOmer; and even if he practices two contradictory stringencies he is not like a fool walking in darkness, since he does so only out of safeik not knowing which custom is primary; nevertheless he need not do so but may adopt whichever custom he wishes, and he need not fear perhaps that place's custom is not so, since it is only a general custom — one does not be stringent in safeik; but if he knows the place's custom he may not change, whether to be lenient or stringent [Acharonim]:
