However, in many, etc. The reason is that according to everyone they practice a prohibition of thirty-three days; and if so, when they get haircuts until Rosh Chodesh Iyar, and Rosh Chodesh is included, thirty-three days remain until Shavuos; and even though they get a haircut on Lag BaOmer day, and if so one day is lacking, nevertheless we hold that part of the day is like the whole day; and since they practiced a prohibition for part of it, that suffices; and if so, it is forbidden to get a haircut even on Rosh Chodesh Sivan until Shavuos (Bach). But in this country they practiced to marry and to get a haircut during the three days of restriction; therefore it appears to me that they should not marry or get a haircut on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, and on the first of the days of restriction we say part of the day is like the whole day; and so it is implied in Maharil; see there. And there is yet another view: when you remove the seven days of Pesach, six Shabbosos, two days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar, and one [day of] Rosh Chodesh Sivan, thirty-three days remain; and according to this there is permission only on Rosh Chodesh itself; and Rama omitted this view, implying that he does not hold thus; and in the gloss at the end of Minhagim it implies that it is permitted to marry on the Shabbos before Shavuos; however, he follows his view that it is forbidden only until Lag BaOmer; see there.
Some. Meaning that some people should not practice thus and some [should practice] thus, for that is "do not cut yourselves." And since this law was not explained so much in the poskim, I will present before you the sugya in Yevamos folio 13b, and this is its language: Megillah is read on the eleventh, on the twelfth, etc.; apply here "do not cut yourselves": do not make factions [upon] factions. He said to him: Thus far you have not taught [from] "a place where they practiced to do melacha on erev Pesach, they do [it]; a place where they practiced not to do [it], they do not do [it]." He said to him: I am telling you of a prohibition that the Rabbis forbade to the people of the fourteenth from reading on the fifteenth, and likewise the people of the fifteenth on the fourteenth, and it is like two Toros; but there it is a custom that for everyone is permitted; nevertheless, in a place where they were stringent, one should not change his way because of controversy. And we challenge: And there, is there no prohibition? Yet it teaches: On that night Beis Shammai forbid and Beis Hillel permit doing melacha; some act like Beis Hillel and some act like Beis Shammai, and it is factions here. He said to him: There it is not comparable to two Toros, for one who sees this one who is not doing melacha says [rather] that he has no melacha [to do]. And the conclusion is specifically [regarding] one beis din in one city: half instruct thus and half instruct thus, that is "do not cut yourselves"; but two battei din in one city, these do thus and these do thus, that is not "do not cut yourselves"; end quote. And if so, we learn from this that a matter that is only custom, we do not say regarding it "do not cut yourselves"; and regarding a matter that is forbidden, even if [only] by reason of custom, "do not cut yourselves" applies; and regarding a matter that can be attributed to [his] not intending a prohibition, such as melacha, there is no [violation of] "do not cut yourselves"; and if so, regarding haircutting as well one may say that an onlooker says he does not wish to cut his hair; however, one may say that according to the conclusion, in every matter "do not cut yourselves" applies regarding one beis din in one city; and so it is implied in Pesachim at the beginning of folio 14 in Tosafos, who wrote that in Jerusalem it is proper to practice a prohibition of melacha on erev Pesach, because people gather there from other places; end quote. And this is difficult: what of it? Rather their intent is that since the others practice a prohibition and they would practice permission, it is like "do not cut yourselves"; and so it is implied in the Rif and the Rosh, who wrote only the conclusion alone. And the Rosh wrote regarding Megillah: even though they read for the people of the city on the fourteenth and they advance [the reading] for the people of the villages, and it is like one beis din in one city, half instruct like Beis Shammai and half instruct like Beis Hillel, we do not apply to it "do not cut yourselves," since they do not do so because of dispute, but rather the place causes it; and if a native of this place would go to another place, he would act like them; it does not appear like two Toros. And there is further to say that Megillah is like two battei din in one city, for the people of the cities were not reading for the people of the villages; rather the people of the villages read for themselves in the cities; end quote. And in Remazim it is written, and this is its language: And a matter that depends on the custom of the places, that in this place they practiced thus and in that place they practiced thus, even if people of the two places gathered in one place and each did according to the custom of his place, there is no [issue] in it; end quote. It appears to me that he holds even if they are one beis din, like the first wording that the Rosh wrote. And it appears to me specifically regarding a custom similar to Megillah, for regarding Megillah the cities also hold that the villages are obligated to read earlier; and the same law applies, by way of example, that in the lands of Poland they practice to fast and say Selichos on the twentieth of Sivan, and if people of Poland and people of another land gathered in one place, each is permitted to act according to his custom, and there is in this no [violation of] "do not cut yourselves"; but regarding a matter in which they are divided in the substance, for example that these eat the fats of the Rhineland communities and those do not eat [them], if they gathered in one place they must practice a uniform custom because of "do not cut yourselves." And in Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah, it is written: if the place was destroyed of inhabitants and afterward they returned there from another place, this requires further study whether they are obligated to practice thus; end quote. See what I wrote in siman 468, and in Choshen Mishpat, siman 25; see what I wrote in siman 624. And likewise regarding an agreement that some enacted with a cherem, "do not cut yourselves" does not apply (Mahari Taitatzak); and in Tanya, siman 168, he wrote that there are who disagree. One who practiced a stringency because they were stringent in the place from which he left, "do not cut yourselves" does not apply (Rambam, chapter 12 of Hilchos Avodah Zarah) (and see what I wrote above). Where [regarding] people of one craft there is no division among them, even though the rest of the people of the city do not do so, it is permitted (Ra'em, part 1, siman 57); and see Responsa Radach, [section] 13, part 2, and R. Moshe Alshich, siman 29, and Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 1 of Yevamos; and if most of the congregation practice thus, the minority must practice like the majority (thus far in Kenesses HaGedolah). We say in Eruvin folio 7 that R' Akiva practiced two tithe [stringencies] like Beis Shammai and like Beis Hillel, and it challenges: yet that is two stringencies that contradict one another; and it answers that for R' Akiva the tradition was doubtful to him, for he did not know how Beis Hillel held; and so Tosafos wrote in Yevamos folio 15; and if so, because of doubt it is permitted to practice two stringencies that contradict one another; but where they entirely contradict one another, it is not applicable to practice both because of doubt, for whichever way you turn it is permitted, as is stated in chapter 2 of Chullin.
