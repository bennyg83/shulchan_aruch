(א) However in many places, etc. The reason is that according to all they practice prohibition for 33 days; if so, when they get a haircut until Rosh Chodesh Iyar and Rosh Chodesh is included, 33 days remain until Shavuos; and even though they get a haircut on the 33rd day and thus one day is lacking, nevertheless we hold part of the day is like the whole day; and since they practiced prohibition for part of it, it suffices; if so, it is forbidden to get a haircut even on Rosh Chodesh Sivan until Shavuos (Bach). But in this country they practiced to marry and get a haircut on the three days of restriction; therefore it appears they should not marry or get a haircut on Rosh Chodesh Iyar and on the first of the days of restriction — we say part of the day is like the whole day — as Maharil wrote, see there. There is another view: when you remove seven days of Pesach, six Shabbosos, two days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar, and one Rosh Chodesh Sivan, 33 days remain; per this there is leniency only on Rosh Chodesh itself; Rama omitted this view — it implies he does not hold thus; and in the gloss at the end of Minhagim it implies it is permitted to marry on the Shabbos before Shavuos; nevertheless he follows his view that it is forbidden only until Lag BaOmer, see there:

(ב) Partial. Meaning that some people will not conduct so and some conduct so — it is "do not cut yourselves"; and since this law was not explained much in the poskim, I will present before you the sugya in Yevamot daf 13b, and this is its language: Megillah is read on the 11th, 12th, etc.; it is called here "do not cut yourselves" — "do not make factions, factions"; he said to him: thus I did not teach — a place that practiced work on erev Pesach, they do work; a place that practiced not to work, they do not work; he said to him: I tell you an issur that the rabbis forbade to the 14-year-olds to read on the 15th, and likewise 15-year-olds on the 14th, and it is like two Torahs; but there it is custom permitted for all; nevertheless in a place that is stringent one should not change his way because of dispute. We challenge: there is no issur, for it teaches: on the night Beit Shammai forbid and Beit Hillel permit work — some act like Beit Hillel and some like Beit Shammai, and it is factions here; he said: there it is not like two Torahs, for one who sees this one not doing work says work is lacking for him; and the conclusion is specifically one court in one city — half instruct thus and half thus — it is "do not cut yourselves"; but two courts in one city, these do thus and these do thus — it is not "do not cut yourselves," end quote. We learn from this that for a matter that is only custom we do not say "do not cut yourselves"; but for something forbidden even from custom, "do not cut yourselves" applies; and for something that can be attributed to unintentional, such as work, there is no "do not cut yourselves"; so too regarding haircutting one could say one who sees may say he does not wish to cut himself; however for the conclusion, in every matter "do not cut yourselves" applies in one court in one city, as in Pesachim beginning 14 in Tosafot that in Jerusalem it is fit to practice work prohibition on erev Pesach because people gather from elsewhere — end quote. What of it? Their intent is: since others practice prohibition and they would practice permission, it is like "do not cut yourselves," as in Rif and Rosh who wrote only the conclusion; and Rosh in Megillah: even though they read for city children on the 14th and precede village children, and it is like one court in one city — half instruct like Beit Shammai and half like Beit Hillel — we do not call it "do not cut yourselves" since they do not do so because of dispute but place causes it; if a native of this place went elsewhere he would act like them — it does not appear as two Torahs; and explain that Megillah is like two courts in one city — village children did not read for city children but village children read for themselves in cities — end quote; and in Remazim it is written, and this is its language: matters that depend on local custom — even if people of two places gathered in one place and each acted per his place, "do not cut yourselves" does not apply — end quote. It appears even if they are one court as Rosh wrote first. And it appears specifically for custom like Megillah where cities also hold villages must read first; by analogy in Poland they fast and say selichot on the 20th of Sivan — if Polish and others gathered in one place, each may act per custom without "do not cut yourselves"; but for divided matters, if these eat cheese and those do not, if gathered they need uniform custom because of "do not cut yourselves"; and in ShSG ch. 1 of Avodah Zarah: if a place was destroyed of inhabitants and others returned — uncertain if they must practice thus; see siman 468 and Choshen Mishpat siman 25; see siman 494. And likewise for agreement enacted by some in cherem, "do not cut yourselves" does not apply (Mahari Taitatzak); and in Tanya siman 468 some disagree — one who practiced stringency because they were stringent where he left — "do not cut yourselves" does not apply (Rambam ch. 12 Maaser Ani) (and see above). Where all of one craft have no division, even if rest of city does not do so, it is permitted (Raavan ch. 1 siman 57); see responsa Radbaz 13:2 and Ralbag siman 29 and ShSG ch. 1 Yevamot; if majority of congregation practices thus, minority must follow majority (end quote Beit Yosef). We say in Eruvin 7 that R. Akiva practiced two stringencies like Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel; they challenge: behold two stringencies that contradict each other; answer: R. Akiva was uncertain in the Gemara how Beit Hillel holds; so Tosafot Yevamot 15; therefore from safeik one may practice two contradictory stringencies; but where they fully contradict, one cannot practice both because of safeik — as in ch. 2 Chullin.
