Some practice, etc., and they err — explanation of the matter: there are three views here, and we will explain two of them that the poskim ruled for practical halachah. First view: the students of R' Akiva died for 34 days; proof from the Midrash that they died from Pesach until "kneading" of Shavuos, which is 15 days before Shavuos; when you remove 15 days from 49, 34 full days remain that they died — only that part of the day is like the whole day; therefore they get a haircut on the 34th day in the morning, and likewise marrying a woman is permitted from then on; this is the Mechaber's view in seifim 1 and 2. However per Rama there in seif 2 it implies he holds they ceased dying on the 33rd day, as the Gra wrote there — part of the day is like the whole day; per this view the custom not to get a haircut from Pesach until the morning of the 33rd, and from then until Shavuos, is permitted. Second view: what is said in the name of Tosafot — the students of R' Akiva died 33 full days, meaning on a day when Tachanun is not said they did not die; if so, when you remove seven days of Pesach — from the second day of Pesach — and another six Shabbosos and two days of Rosh Chodesh Iyar and day 1 when they did not die, behold 33 full days remain that they died; per this they died until Shavuos. Israel accepted upon themselves partial mourning for 33 days corresponding to those 33 days (except that one day may be eased with part of the day like the whole day, and they chose Lag BaOmer — perhaps for some reason, Peri Megadim) regarding counting and marriage — meaning to deduct from the 49 days of the Omer the first 16 days, from the second day of Pesach until the second day of Rosh Chodesh Iyar; those days they are lenient, and 33 full days remain until Shavuos on which they mourn; per this, those who get a haircut from Lag BaOmer onward and also on Rosh Chodesh Iyar err, for there are not 33 days — this is the Mechaber's intent.
