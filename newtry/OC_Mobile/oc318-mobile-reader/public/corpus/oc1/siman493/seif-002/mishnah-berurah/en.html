(6) "Until Lag BaOmer." This is inclusive, meaning through 33 complete days, each with its night and day. On the 34th day they were stringent only during the night; once daylight appears after sunrise it is permitted because part of a day is considered like the entire day. (7) "For they say that then they ceased," etc. Although students also died on the 34th day, and for that reason they were stringent throughout the night of the 34th until sunrise, it is possible that the principal cessation was on the 33rd day and only a few died on the 34th. [Pri Megadim] (8) "On the 33rd day." This view holds that the deaths ceased entirely on Lag BaOmer. (9) "And one does not say Tachanun on it." This applies also on the preceding evening, the evening following the 32nd day. (10) "Until Lag BaOmer itself." Meaning after sunrise, because part of the day is considered like the entire day. (11) "And not from the evening." Some later authorities are lenient to permit haircuts from the preceding evening. Nevertheless, the later authorities conclude that regarding marriage no leniency was commonly practiced until Lag BaOmer by day, not on the preceding night. If Lag BaOmer falls on Friday and he is under pressure to make the necessary preparations by day, there may be room to be lenient from the preceding night. (12) "A baal brit." Meaning the sandak, the mohel, and the father of the infant receiving brit milah, even if the father is not himself the mohel. What Rama writes concerning "the brit milah of his son" means that his son is receiving brit milah. The attendants who bring the infant in and out are not included.
