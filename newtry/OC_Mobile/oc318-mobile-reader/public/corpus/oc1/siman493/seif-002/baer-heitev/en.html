Joy. And it is written in the Kavanos that a certain great person was accustomed to say Nachem every day, and they said it also on Lag BaOmer, and he was punished; see there.
Tachanun. Also on the eve of day 32. And in Ashkenaz the custom is that at Shacharis they do not say Tachanun, which is not so in the evening, when they do say Tachanun; and if so, what the Rav likewise wrote, that one may not get a haircut until Lag BaOmer itself, is according to the Ashkenazi custom; but for those who practice not to say Tachanun from the evening onward, the same law applies that it is permitted to get a haircut, for this depends on that; and so wrote Maginei Eretz. Chok Yaakov.
Shabbos. This is the view of Mahariv. And Maharil forbids, and so is the view of some later authorities; nevertheless, where the custom is to permit, that is the practice, and one should not be stringent in these laws, as is written in s.k. 3. Chok Yaakov.
Bris. See above in Beis Yosef, siman 559: it is explained that baalei bris are called the sandakim, the mohel, and the father of the son who is circumcised, even if he does not circumcise himself. And they are permitted to get a haircut on the eve before entering the synagogue; so it is written in the Minhagim; and so wrote Chok Yaakov and Peri Chadash, not like Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah; see there; and so is the practice.
