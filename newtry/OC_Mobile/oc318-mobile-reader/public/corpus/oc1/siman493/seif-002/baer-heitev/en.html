"Rejoicing." It is written in the Kavanos that a certain great person was accustomed to recite Nachem every day, and he recited it also on Lag BaOmer and was punished. See there. "Tachanun." This applies also on the evening of the 32nd day. In Ashkenaz the custom is that Tachanun is not said at Shacharit, whereas in the evening it is said. Accordingly, what Rama writes, that one may not take a haircut until Lag BaOmer itself, follows the Ashkenazic custom. Those whose custom is not to say Tachanun from the preceding evening may likewise take a haircut then, since the 2 matters depend upon one another. Mateh Yosef writes likewise. Chok Yaakov. "Shabbos." This is the view of Mahari Weil. Maharil prohibits, and so do some later authorities. Nevertheless, where the established custom is permissive, that custom may be followed and one should not be stringent in these laws, as stated in seif katan 3. Chok Yaakov. "Brit milah." See Beit Yosef, siman 559, where it is explained that the baalei brit are the sandakim, the mohel, and the father of the infant receiving brit milah, even if he does not perform the brit milah himself. They may take a haircut on the preceding evening before entering the synagogue. So state Minhagim, Chok Yaakov, and Peri Chadash, unlike Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. This is the custom.
