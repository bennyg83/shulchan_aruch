Se'if 2. In the gloss: however, if it falls on Sunday, they practice to get a haircut on Friday for the honor of Shabbos. Note: now, if Rosh Chodesh Sivan falls on Sunday, whether it is permitted to get a haircut on erev Shabbos. I saw in Peri Megadim that he wrote that it is permitted, for it is like on Lag BaOmer, and he wondered at Peri Chadash who forbids, when Rosh Chodesh Iyar falls on Sunday, to get a haircut on Friday, and he wrote that he does not know his reason; see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that regarding Rosh Chodesh Sivan, certainly the words of Peri Megadim are not correct, for there is no proof from Lag BaOmer; for there, since if he will not get a haircut on Lag BaOmer he will not be able to get a haircut afterward even for the coming Shabbos, therefore we are concerned for disfigurement for two Shabbosos; and on Lag BaOmer itself, since it is one day, we are concerned for an accidental impediment and the like; and especially that it is a disgrace that for Shabbos he will not shave and on Sunday after Shabbos he will shave; therefore it is permitted to shave on erev Shabbos; and especially that they were not concerned for this Shabbos, since on it they forbade shaving and we are not concerned for its disfigurement, but for the coming Shabbos we are concerned, since before it they permitted shaving on Lag BaOmer so that he not be disfigured on Shabbos, and if he will not shave now, perhaps on Lag BaOmer some impediment will arise for him and the like, and it is also a disgrace to Shabbos, as above; therefore they permitted to shave on erev Shabbos. But on Rosh Chodesh Sivan, when it is possible for him to shave afterward all the days for a long time, they were not concerned for an impediment, and he can shave close to the coming Shabbos and not be disfigured; and for the sake of that Shabbos one should not shave, for regarding it they were not concerned for disfigurement, for they forbade shaving until Rosh Chodesh whenever it falls. And further: on Lag BaOmer, which itself is permitted by the din according to most poskim, therefore if it falls on Sunday it is permitted in proximity to it. But on Rosh Chodesh Sivan, behold it is explicit in Magen Avraham that there is permission only during the three days of restriction; and Peri Megadim himself wrote that he does not know from where the custom spread to permit on Rosh Chodesh; if so, leave it and do not add to it: specifically on Rosh Chodesh, but not to permit even before it. And this is the reason of Peri Chadash: since regarding Rosh Chodesh Iyar most forbid, and the Mechaber wrote that it is a mistake in their hands, therefore even those who practice to be lenient, that is on Rosh Chodesh itself, and not to be lenient even before it; and it is not comparable to Lag BaOmer, for there it is permitted according to most poskim, as above. Therefore, whether on Rosh Chodesh Iyar or on Rosh Chodesh Sivan, one should forbid shaving on erev Shabbos; so it appears to me correct for practical law.
