"And one increases somewhat in rejoicing." Magen Avraham writes that a certain great person was accustomed to recite Nachem throughout the days of the Omer, recited it on Lag BaOmer, and was punished. "And one does not say Tachanun on it." This applies also on the evening of the 32nd day. Minhagim writes in the laws of Tachanun that this is the custom in Moravia and Bohemia. Maharil, however, writes that the custom in Ashkenaz is not to say Tachanun at Shacharit, while in the evening Tachanun is said. Accordingly, Rama's statement that one does not take a haircut until Lag BaOmer itself follows Maharil according to his custom. Those whose custom is not to say Tachanun from the preceding evening may likewise take a haircut from then, because the 2 matters depend upon one another. Mateh Yosef writes similarly. "They have the custom to take a haircut on Friday." Meaning for the honor of Shabbos. This is the view of Mahari Weil. Maharil prohibits, and some later authorities agree. Nevertheless, where the established custom is permissive, it may be followed, and one should not impose additional stringency in these laws. "And one who is a baal brit." The baalei brit are the sandak, the mohel, and the father of the infant receiving brit milah, even if the father is not the mohel. They may take a haircut on the preceding evening before entering the synagogue, as stated in Minhagim and by the later authorities.
