And they do not say Tachanun on it. Also on the eve of day 32; and so it is written in Minhagim regarding the law of Tachanun, that so they practice in Moravia and in Bohemia. And in Maharil it is written that the custom in Ashkenaz is that at Shacharis they do not say Tachanun, which is not so in the evening, when they do say Tachanun; and if so, what the Rav likewise wrote, that one may not get a haircut until Lag BaOmer itself, is Maharil according to his custom; but for those who practice not to say Tachanun from the evening onward, the same law applies that it is permitted to get a haircut, for this depends on that; and of this sort Maginei Eretz wrote.
They practice to get a haircut on Friday. And [this] for the honor of Shabbos: this is the view of Mahariv; and Maharil forbids, and so is the view of some later authorities; nevertheless, where the custom is to permit, that is the practice, and one should not be stringent in these laws, as is written in s.k. 2.
And one who is a baal bris. See above in Beis Yosef, siman 559: it is explained that baalei bris are called the sandakim, the mohel, and the father of the son who is circumcised, even if he does not circumcise himself; and it is permitted to get a haircut on the eve before entering the synagogue; and so wrote Minhagim.
