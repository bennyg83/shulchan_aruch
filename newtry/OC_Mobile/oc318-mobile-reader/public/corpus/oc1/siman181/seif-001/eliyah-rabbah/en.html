They are obligatory, etc. The first waters are optional in the sense that one may choose not to eat, but mayim acharonim become obligatory once he has eaten and must recite Birkat HaMazon. Therefore, if he has eaten and has only enough water for mayim acharonim but still needs to eat seudah shelishit, he forgoes the later meal and uses the water for Birkat HaMazon. If, however, he did not eat moist food and his hands are not dirty, he may recite Birkat HaMazon without mayim acharonim, then wash for the next meal, and that washing counts for both purposes. If he has fragrant oil or wine, he can clean his hands with that. So writes Kol Bo, folio 16. This makes clear that if one still wishes to eat and has water sufficient for only one washing, he uses it for the first washing and not for mayim acharonim, because at this point even regarding mayim acharonim we can say he may simply choose not to eat and thereby not become obligated. This is unlike Magen Avraham. Nowadays, perhaps one always uses the water for the meal rather than for mayim acharonim, since many authorities are lenient regarding the latter.

This is what Levush writes at the end of the siman, “and nowadays,” etc. This is readily understood.
