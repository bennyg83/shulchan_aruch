Beit Yosef writes at the beginning of siman 158 in the name of Orchos Chaim: Mayim rishonim is a mitzvah, while mayim acharonim is an obligation. What practical difference follows? To set aside one obligation in favor of the other. If one ate and has water for mayim acharonim, but must eat another meal that day, such as the third Shabbos meal, and will not have water then, he forgoes the later meal and washes his hands for Bircas HaMazon, because he is already obligated in the blessing. If he has fragrant oil or wine, he cleans his hands with it and recites the blessing. This implies that if one wishes to eat and has enough water to wash only once, he takes mayim rishonim and not mayim acharonim, since he may not eat until he washes. This requires study, for if so, in what respect is mayim acharonim more stringent than mayim rishonim? One must say that he may not eat unless he has enough water for both mayim rishonim and mayim acharonim for that meal. See siman 182. This requires study. It appears to me, however, that the original difficulty does not arise. The practical difference applies to soldiers in a military camp, who are exempt from mayim rishonim but obligated in mayim acharonim, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Eruvin. Tosafos writes likewise in Chullin 105. See siman 163, that if there is no water one need not forgo the meal. It appears to me that nowadays mayim rishonim takes precedence even for one careful about mayim acharonim, because mayim acharonim is not so strong an obligation, since Sodomite salt is no longer common. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that they pour the cup and afterward wash the hands. See siman 166. The order of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, however, implies that they wash first. Nevertheless, the Gemara appears to support Rabbeinu Yerucham, and the order of the Tur’s rulings is not conclusive. Only in a vessel. Nevertheless, one should not wash his hands inside the vessel. For the intermediate washing it is permitted. Yam Shel Shlomo.
