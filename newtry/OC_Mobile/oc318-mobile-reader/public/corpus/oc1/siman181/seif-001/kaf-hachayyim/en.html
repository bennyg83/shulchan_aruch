1) [Seif 1] The final waters are obligatory. Berachos 53b states: 'You shall sanctify yourselves' refers to the first waters, and 'you shall be holy' refers to the final waters, etc. Regarding the reason, our Sages, of blessed memory, said in Chullin 105b that Sodomite salt is present and blinds the eyes. Abaye said that it is found in the proportion of one grain in a kor. Rashi, of blessed memory, explains that the Sages said in Berachos 40a, 'With everything you eat, eat salt.' Since one touches the salt, if he afterward places his hand upon his eyes it blinds them, and therefore he must wash them. Rabbeinu Yonah writes in the chapter Eilu Devarim that the Rambam, of blessed memory, says that although we no longer have Sodomite salt, one must be concerned about another salt whose nature is like that of Sodomite salt. If one passes his hands over his eyes before washing, it will blind his eyes. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in the name of Rid that even if one did not eat salt, washing the hands is obligatory, because failure to wash the final waters caused the loss of life, as stated in Yoma 83b. Beis Yosef cites their words. These reasons follow the plain meaning. We have already written in several places that all the words of our Sages, of blessed memory, accord with the mystical dimension, but they clothe them in a plain explanation to make them understandable. The Zohar, parashas Terumah, 154b, reveals the secret of the final waters, stating: After a person has eaten and enjoyed himself, he must give the residue as a portion to that side. What is it? The final waters, the grime of the hands, which one must give to that side as the portion it requires. Therefore, they are certainly obligatory. They are an obligation and rest in the place of obligation, and a person is obligated to give it this portion. Therefore, no blessing at all is required over them, for blessing does not exist on that side. Sefer HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, likewise writes: Regarding the final waters, know that the Other Side stands at the table, as stated in the Zohar, parashas Terumah, and can then exercise greater control over a person than at other times. This is especially true when a person is alone and three men are not present to recite Zimun, because through Zimun one removes the Other Side from there, as stated in parashas Balak in the account of that child. One must be extremely careful about the intentions of the final waters so that it not prosecute him. Through this gift that one gives it, as is known, the Other Side withdraws and departs. Initially it was a guest, but afterward, if one does not properly direct his intent in Birkas HaMazon, it becomes the householder and prosecutes him, especially when he is alone without Zimun, as stated. We have already written the intention for the final waters above in siman 157, paragraph 21; see there. We thus find that the final waters carry a great obligation according to the mystical meaning, for they drive away the Other Side. Therefore, our Sages, of blessed memory, warned about them and said that they are obligatory. For this reason, it appears to me that one should not place his hand upon his mustache while his hands are still wet from the final waters, since these waters are the portion of the Other Side. See further below, paragraph 27, and what we wrote above in siman 158, paragraph 6; see there.
2) The same applies to one who measures salt: he requires washing, as stated there in Chullin. Measuring is not exclusive; rather, whenever one touches salt he requires washing, because we are concerned that he might place his hands upon his eyes.
3) Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: One pours the cup and afterward washes the hands. The ordering of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, however, implies that one washes first. Nevertheless, the Gemara implies as Rabbeinu Yerucham writes, and no inference can be drawn from the order of the Tur's teaching. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 45, paragraph 2. The expression 'one pours' is not literal, because the wine is mixed for the cup of Birkas HaMazon during Birkas HaAretz, as stated below in siman 183, seif 2; see there. Rather, it means that one rinses and fills the cup and places it before the person leading the blessing before washing the hands.
4) There: The final waters are obligatory. One need pour over his hand only once. Mordechai, chapter Eilu Devarim. No minimum quantity is required either. Shulchan Arba; Beis Yosef; Ateres Zekeinim, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4. This is also implied by what we wrote above in siman 157, paragraph 21; see there. See Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1, who writes that while washing one should say, 'This is the portion of a wicked man from God.' Some later authorities write the same. It nevertheless appears to me that not everyone may say this, for we are concerned that the external forces might be aroused against him. The words of the Arizal likewise do not mention saying it. Therefore, refraining from action is preferable.
5) Rabbeinu Yonah, of blessed memory, writes in the chapter Eilu Devarim that some say the final waters were required only when one eats something fit to be offered upon the altar, but this is nothing. Ran, of blessed memory, writes in the chapter Kol HaBasar that some say the final waters were required only for something unfit to be offered upon the altar. Bach writes that we rule that both this and that require the final waters. There is no distinction between the person leading the blessing and those listening; all must wash their hands before Birkas HaMazon if they are careful about the final waters. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 2; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. We have already written above in paragraph 1 that according to the Zohar and the Arizal, once one ate a kezayis of bread, the obligation of the final waters rests upon him in order to drive away the Other Side; see there. We also wrote above in siman 158, paragraph 53, that even if one ate only fruit, if his hands became soiled from the food, he must wash them in order to recite the blessing with cleanliness; see there.
6) One should take care that the final waters be a small quantity and should not use much, for they are the portion of the Other Side. If his hands are very soiled from the cooked food, and his mouth and mustache likewise require much water for washing, it is preferable that he wash them before taking the final waters, with the intent to eat more afterward, so that this washing will be like the middle waters. He should then actually eat a piece of bread from the table, so that the prior washing is not considered the final waters. After eating the piece of bread or a similar item, he should wash with a small quantity of final waters and recite Birkas HaMazon. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Shelach Lecha, paragraph 9; Kaf HaChaim, siman 25, paragraph 2, writes the same.
7) There: The final waters are obligatory. It appears that the obligation nevertheless does not exceed that of the first waters, namely, one must travel four mil ahead and one mil behind, as stated above at the beginning of siman 163; see there. If he is farther away than this measure, it appears to me that he should clean his hands with earth or a pebble, as stated above in siman 4, seif 22; see there. See below, paragraph 26.
