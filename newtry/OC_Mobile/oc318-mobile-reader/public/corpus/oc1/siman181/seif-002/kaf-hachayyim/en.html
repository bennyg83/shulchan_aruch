8) [Seif 2] But into a utensil, etc. If he has no utensil designated for the final waters, or if he is too indolent to rise and has no one to serve him, he may wash into an empty food bowl, as stated in Ikarei HaDinim, siman 9, paragraph 17. It is preferable, however, to remove it from before him during the blessing and place it to the side, off the table. Kaf HaChaim there, paragraph 3. We wrote the same above in siman 173, paragraph 15; see there.
9) There: But into a utensil, etc. Nevertheless, one should not wash his hands by immersing them in the utensil, although this is permitted for the middle waters. Yam Shel Shlomo; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Magen Avraham explains that the final waters serve to remove grime. Thus, although the water removes the grime from his hands, when he washes inside the utensil the water bearing the grime subsequently adheres to his hands again. This is unlike the middle waters, meaning washing between cheese and eating meat, which serve only to remove food adhering to the hands. Washing inside a utensil is effective, because in any event the food leaves his hands and does not subsequently adhere to them again. Machatzis HaShekel. Accordingly, it appears that if there is much water, such that even if the water returns to his hands it will not contain grime, washing by immersion is permitted even for the final waters. According to the mystical meaning, however, since they are the portion of the Other Side, it appears that they must specifically be taken by pouring.
10) There: But into a utensil, etc. One does not, however, need a utensil from which to pour, as is required for the first washing of the hands. Levush. This means that even a utensil invalid for the first washing, as stated above in siman 159, is valid for the final waters. Kol Bo, siman 23, writes in the name of Raavad the following differences between the first and final waters: The first waters require a reviis, while for the final waters one needs only enough to rinse the hands. The first waters may be taken only from a utensil, while the final waters may be taken from anything. Human force is unnecessary, and an interposition does not invalidate them. All water is valid for the final waters, even if work was performed with it, even if it is very turbid and a cow would not drink from it, and even the bitter hot springs of Tiberias. The first waters may not be taken in portions, while the final waters may be taken in portions. If, after the first waters, one rubbed his hands upon his body or a wall, he requires another washing; after the final waters, he does not. These are his words in brief. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, cites this and writes that all these are established halachos, and they are similarly explained in Toras HaBayis, contrary to Olas Tamid, who cites Shiltei Gibborim as ruling oppositely concerning some of these matters.
