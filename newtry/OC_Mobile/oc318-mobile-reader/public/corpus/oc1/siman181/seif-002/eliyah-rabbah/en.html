[3] Thin pieces of wood, etc. Kol Bo, folio 16, states: thin pieces of wood, stones, or a chip. Sefer HaChinuch writes that the same applies to anything that separates between the water and the ground. Magen Avraham writes that it is permitted even where there is a paved floor. Kol Bo writes in the name of Raavad: These are the distinctions between mayim rishonim and mayim acharonim. Mayim rishonim require a reviis, while for mayim acharonim one needs only enough to rinse the hands. Mayim rishonim require a vessel, whereas mayim acharonim may be taken from anything, do not require human force, and are not invalidated by an intervening substance. Any water is valid for mayim acharonim, even if work was performed with it, even if it is very murky, even if a cow drank from it, and even the hot springs of Tiberias, although they are bitter. Mayim rishonim may not be taken in installments, whereas mayim acharonim may. For mayim rishonim, rubbing the hands on one’s body or on a wall requires another washing; for mayim acharonim it does not. These are his words in brief, and all of them are accepted halachah. They are likewise explained in the long Toras HaBayis, unlike Olas Tamid, who cites Shiltei Giborim for several rulings to the contrary. Magen Avraham writes that one may wash beneath the table because people do not walk there. Although the table is sometimes removed, the water will dry in the interim.
[4] [Levush] But lukewarm, etc. Maharshal, chapter Kol HaBasar, siman 11, rules that specifically cold water must be used.
