[8] [Levush] And some disagree, etc. They require drying and only afterward does he recite the blessing. Malbushei Yom Tov. It is possible that he thereby meant to correct the ruling as follows: specifically the one who recites Bircas HaMazon requires drying because of “Prepare to meet your God,” but all the others do not require drying according to any opinion. Maharshal writes likewise in chapter Kol HaBasar, siman 10. It also appears to me as practical halachah that the first opinion is primary, for it is the view of most poskim: Kol Bo in the name of Raavad, Orchos Chaim, Sefer Shulchan Arba, and Rashba explicitly in Toras HaBayis, folio 159, and in the short version, folio 59. I am therefore astonished at Beit Yosef, who writes merely that this is implied by Rashba, when Rashba states it explicitly in two places.
[Levush] And nowadays, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that this is why people are not exacting about the law in seif 6 even when they do wash mayim acharonim, but begin with the greatest person and proceed according to seating order. See above seif katan 6. The Shelah, folio 81, and Maharshal strongly warn that mayim acharonim should still be observed. The Bach writes that one may not interrupt between mayim acharonim and Birkat HaMazon even with words of Torah. The Shulchan Aruch of Rabbi Yitzchak Luria likewise recounts an incident in which a sage read a mishnah after mayim acharonim and developed severe pain in his shoulder for violating the Sages' requirement of immediacy. One should not challenge this from my conclusion in siman 179 that one may eat or drink after mayim acharonim if he first recites a blessing, because in that case he apparently washes mayim acharonim again after the additional eating or drinking. The Perishah there implies this. Maharshal writes that his father in law and he had the custom to bring the cup for Birkat HaMazon already full. He would rinse the cup over his hand and thereby wash his hands for mayim acharonim, fulfilling two mitzvot at once, rinsing the cup and mayim acharonim. This somewhat conflicts with what I wrote earlier, that mayim acharonim should not be taken from water collected in a vessel because that water receives the grime and the hands may then receive it back. Regarding middle washing there is no concern because the point is simply to clean the hands. In truth, that reasoning is not conclusive. Since the water cleans the hands, perhaps it is permitted even for mayim acharonim. Moreover, the poskim write that middle washing and mayim acharonim are completely alike except where a distinction is explicitly stated, as explained in siman 173. The Shelah, folio 83, writes that the custom is to recite Tehillim 137 before Birkat HaMazon to recall the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash. On Shabbos and Yom Tov they instead recite Tehillim 126. The reason appears to be that one should not recall the destruction on Shabbos, as written at the end of siman 102. See below siman 183.
