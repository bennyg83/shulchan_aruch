[9] [Levush] And nowadays, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: therefore we are not exacting with the law in seif 6 even when washing; rather, we begin from the greatest and they wash in the order of their seating, end quote. See above seif katan 6, and Shelah p. 81 and Rashal there, who warned very strongly to practice mayim acharonim; see there. The Bach wrote that one may not interrupt between mayim acharonim and birkat hamazon, even with words of Torah. So too wrote the Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria, who brings an incident in which one sage read a Mishnah and a great pain came to his shoulder because he transgressed the words of Chazal, "immediately"; end quote. One should not ask from what I concluded in siman 179, that it is optional to eat and drink after mayim acharonim when he recites a berachah beforehand, for there it appears that he again washes mayim acharonim after the eating and drinking; so too it appears from the Perishah there. Rashal wrote there that his father-in-law and he practiced that they brought a full cup for birkat hamazon, and he would rinse it with his hand, and incidentally wash his hands with it for mayim acharonim; in this way two mitzvot were fulfilled, rinsing the cup and mayim acharonim, end quote. This somewhat contradicts what I wrote immediately before this, that one does not wash mayim acharonim from a vessel, for that water receives the filth and the hands then receive it back from the water. But with middle waters there is no concern, for in the end they clean the hands, end quote. In truth, his reasoning is not compelling, for since it cleans the hands perhaps it is permitted even for mayim acharonim. Furthermore, the poskim wrote that middle waters and mayim acharonim are entirely equal except for what is explicitly mentioned, as explained in siman 173. The Shelah, p. 83, wrote: the practice is to say the chapter of Tehillim "By the rivers of Babylon," etc., before birkat hamazon, to remember the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash; and on Shabbat and Yom Tov they say the chapter of Tehillim "When Hashem returns the captivity of Zion," etc., end quote. It appears that the reason is because on Shabbat one should not mention the destruction, as written at the end of siman 102. See below siman 183. See also siman 100.
