9) There: But into a utensil, etc. Nevertheless, one should not wash his hands by immersing them in the utensil, although this is permitted for the middle waters. Yam Shel Shlomo; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Magen Avraham explains that the final waters serve to remove grime. Thus, although the water removes the grime from his hands, when he washes inside the utensil the water bearing the grime subsequently adheres to his hands again. This is unlike the middle waters, meaning washing between cheese and eating meat, which serve only to remove food adhering to the hands. Washing inside a utensil is effective, because in any event the food leaves his hands and does not subsequently adhere to them again. Machatzis HaShekel. Accordingly, it appears that if there is much water, such that even if the water returns to his hands it will not contain grime, washing by immersion is permitted even for the final waters. According to the mystical meaning, however, since they are the portion of the Other Side, it appears that they must specifically be taken by pouring.
