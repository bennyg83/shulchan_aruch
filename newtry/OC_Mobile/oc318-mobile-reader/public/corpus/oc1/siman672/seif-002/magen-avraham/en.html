Therefore one must put, etc. It implies that if he lit some time after sunset, he need not put in so much oil, but only enough until there are no feet in the marketplace. In the Agudah he wrote that in Maimoni it implies that it should burn more than a mil. End quote.
This measure. Even now one must put in this measure, unlike those who are lenient about this; Darkei Moshe and Maharshal in a responsum. But there is no mitzvah in long lamps, for after they have burned this measure it is permitted to use them, and beautifying the mitzvah applies only at the time of the mitzvah. There is no proof from the Chanukah lamps of the synagogue, for there there is a mitzvah to light lamps every day, as appears to me. End quote from Darkei Moshe. It appears to me that certainly with an oil lamp there is no beautification of the mitzvah whether there is much or little, but with wax candles, when they are long they are more beautiful, as with Yom Kippur candles, lulav and esrog, and the like. It seems to me that the Kol Bo also wrote this. The Shiltei HaGibborim derived the measure of oil as half an hour from what we say regarding the half-log of oil for the Menorah for the long Teves nights; see there. In truth, everything depends on the thickness of the wick.
He may extinguish it. The Maharshal wrote that one may not use its light, but extinguishing it is permitted. The Bach wrote that since they have practiced a prohibition against extinguishing and using it, one should not be lenient. So too wrote Tzeidah LaDerech. End quote.
One need not be careful. He should light when it is night, and the members of his household are gathered together; Mitzvas Chanukah. It is good to light before eating; Minhagim. The Maharshal wrote that even learning is forbidden once its time has arrived, and even if he began, he stops, as written in siman 431, se'if 2. So too the Bach ruled. It appears to me that one who has not prayed Maariv should light first, for not so much time is needed, and there is no concern that perhaps he will forget Maariv. So too the Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that he practices this way.
All night. In the Beis Yosef it is written that this is a doubt in the law, and if so it implies that one should not recite a blessing. But since he wrote it without qualification in the Shulchan Aruch, it implies that his view is that one should recite a blessing. The Maharshal wrote: specifically until midnight; if so, after midnight one should not recite a blessing. In Hagahos Maimoniyos he wrote: as long as the members of the household are awake, even until dawn. End quote. So it appears to me to rule. But if they are sleeping, one should not recite a blessing over them.
