(5) "Therefore one must put in," etc. This implies that if he kindled some time after sunset, he need not put in enough oil for the full standard measure, but only enough to burn until pedestrian traffic has ceased from the marketplace. All this follows the Talmudic situation, where the publicizing of the miracle was directed toward passersby. Nowadays, when the recognition is primarily for the members of the household, he always requires the standard measure. [Pri Chadash] Even if he lights after pedestrian traffic has already ceased, he still needs this standard measure. [Pri Megadim] (6) "The measure." Even nowadays one requires this measure. If he puts in much more oil so that the lamp burns longer, there is no additional mitzvah in doing so. With wax candles, however, there is an enhancement of the mitzvah when the candles are long, although they should not be excessively large. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Orchot Chaim that if on the second night a person recited the blessing and lit only 1 lamp, he should afterward light another lamp without another blessing. Pri Megadim inclines that even if he initially intended to light only 1 and afterward another lamp became available, he should still not recite an additional blessing, because by strict law only 1 lamp is obligatory and the others are merely an enhancement. (7) "He may extinguish it," etc. He designates the oil for the mitzvah only for the amount required to burn for half an hour. Therefore afterward he may extinguish it and benefit from the remaining oil. Nevertheless, several later authorities agree that initially it is proper to stipulate that he designates only the amount needed for the required burning time, because some authorities hold that if he poured in the oil without stipulation, he designated all of it for the mitzvah. (8) "To use its light," etc. Some are stringent that while the lamps are burning one may never use their light, even after the required time, because an observer may not know the distinction between the required burning time and afterward. (9) "Since nowadays we light indoors." Meaning that the recognition is therefore only for the household members. (10) "He need not." Rather, he should light when the members of the household are gathered together. Once the time for lighting has arrived, even Torah study is forbidden, all the more so eating and drinking and other activities; even if he began earlier, he must stop. (11) "All night." Until dawn, with a blessing. This applies specifically when members of the household are awake. If they are sleeping, he should not recite a blessing for them, but should light without a blessing, because just as in the Talmudic period one recited the blessing only while publicizing the miracle to others, so too for us. If he returns home before dawn and finds the household asleep, it is proper to wake them so that he can light with a blessing. (12) "Like all other people." Meaning that on the second night he lights 2 lamps and on the third night 3. We do not say that because this person's second night is effectively his first, he should light only 1. [Darkei Moshe]
