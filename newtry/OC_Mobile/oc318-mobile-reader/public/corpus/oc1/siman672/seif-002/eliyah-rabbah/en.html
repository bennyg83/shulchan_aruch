[2] He may extinguish it, etc. The Bach is stringent that one should not extinguish it, and it is also forbidden to benefit from it even if it went out after half an hour, since it was set aside for the mitzvah. End quote. It is good for him to stipulate explicitly that he sets aside the oil only for the measure of the mitzvah, and then it is permitted to extinguish it and benefit after half an hour. However, while it is still burning, one should be concerned for the words of the Maharshal, because one who sees will think that it is permitted to use its light. The Taz discusses at length to permit even where he did not stipulate, and challenged the Beis Yosef from that which is collected for the needs of a deceased person, which is permitted to his heirs, for we say that what is not fit for him is not seized by the designation; the same applies here, that there is no prohibition except in what is needed for the Chanukah lamp. End quote. In my humble opinion, this is not difficult at all, for a deceased person is different from something set aside for a mitzvah. Furthermore, here, where an act was done with it, for he lit from it, it is different from collecting for the needs of a deceased person. It is also difficult against him that the Bach wrote this in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of the Geonim and Sefer Tzeidah LaDerech. Nevertheless, as a matter of law, when it became mixed, it is permitted to nullify it, for in this we rely on those who permit; see the end of siman 677. Likkutei Mahariv, siman 33, wrote: One should not move the Chanukah lamps until they have already burned for half an hour. End quote. According to what I wrote, one should not move them at all. The Rokeach wrote: We light thirty-six lamps on Chanukah, corresponding to the dedication of the world, 'and God set them in the firmament of the heavens to illuminate the earth'; and that light was used by Adam HaRishon for thirty-six hours: twelve hours of the sixth day, twelve hours of the night, and twelve hours of the seventh day.
