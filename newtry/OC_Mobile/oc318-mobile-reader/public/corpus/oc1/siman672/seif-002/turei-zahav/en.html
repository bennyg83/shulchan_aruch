One may extinguish it, etc. In siman 676 the Tur and Rosh ruled from She'iltot that excess Hanukkah oil one may make a bonfire, etc.; Beit Yosef questioned below from here that one may extinguish after the kindling measure, and answered that there it speaks of what remains of the oil needed for the kindling measure, which was designated for his mitzvah, therefore he makes a bonfire; here it speaks of one who put oil beyond the measure, and after the measure burned it is permitted to benefit, for only the required measure was designated for the mitzvah — such is the truth of this answer. There Beit Yosef brings another answer in the name of Mahari Abuhav: one should distinguish — if he put oil in the lamp without designating except for the kindling time measure, afterward he may use it if he wishes; but if he put oil in the lamp without specification, then it was designated for the Hanukkah mitzvah and he must make a bonfire — end. I find this answer difficult from what is stated in the chapter "the judgment was concluded" for Abbaye, who holds designation is a word — they challenged from what we learned: the dead person's property is permitted to his heirs, thus designation is nothing; they answered Abbaye holds this tanna holds designation is something — if it suits him he seizes it, and what does not suit him he need not take out with it; and Rava who argues there and holds designation is nothing — nevertheless they do not argue on this matter that the prohibition is not seized except for what suits him; for Rava, if one acts with something that is a thing, nevertheless the prohibition is not more stringent than Abbaye for designation alone — if so, we see the prohibition that comes through forbidden benefit to the dead is seized only in what suits him, not in what is permitted; if we were to say that what he designated much oil for Hanukkah became forbidden, etc., we should say the same for the dead's permitted property — but certainly the prohibition is only in what is needed for Hanukkah. Granted for Beit Yosef's answer there is no prohibition except in what remains of the required measure, that is, it went out and he need not relight — we still say it is forbidden because it was designated for Hanukkah in what he needs. It is also wondrous where to learn prohibition here in what is not needed for Hanukkah — not from the answer that answers the matter "from sunset until the market clears," meaning the measure Rif and Rosh explain one must put oil so it burns that measure; if it kept burning until that measure and he wants to extinguish or use its light, permission is in his hand — it implies explicitly there is no prohibition in excess beyond the measure; if you would say this speaks where he explains he designates only what is needed for the measure, obviously there is no other tanna — but certainly we say so anonymously, and even for the first answer that if he did not kindle he kindles — nevertheless it does not speak at all of the permitted case; from where do we forbid? On the contrary, it seems comparable to permitted property of the dead we mentioned; but if it went out before its time, we may say the remainder is forbidden because of designation for the mitzvah — from this She'iltot speaks, and explicitly Ran wrote: but some Geonim said if it went out and oil remained in the lamp on the first day, he kindles on the second day, etc. — that is the She'iltot we mentioned; it teaches specifically when it went out She'iltot and the Geonim speak, but Ran is difficult — for before that he wrote Rif does not hold thus, for it appears from his words that if it went out it is permitted to use the oil, and so too he, and the reason: because he did not designate it except for the mitzvah, that is, all while it burns within mitzvah time — if it went out it is permitted, similar to sukkah wood after the festival which is permitted — end. From making a dispute between Rif and the Geonim it implies he does not distinguish like Beit Yosef's distinction, and holds whoever permits extinguishing after the measure automatically permits benefiting from them; likewise if it went out by itself within the measure it is permitted to benefit, for that too is called after the measure since one need not relight — there is no designation for the mitzvah; but Rosh and Tur distinguish as Beit Yosef wrote, and they did not need to mention the distinction, for anonymously it implies thus — for here they wrote permission only if he put more than the measure, and in siman 677 they wrote the remainder of the oil, etc., implying the remainder of the measure that he presumably put there as measure — therefore Shulchan Aruch ruled well that one may extinguish after the measure; so too Maharshal in his summary of Hanukkah laws in his responsa, only he noted there it is forbidden to use its light if he did not extinguish because of the viewer — meaning the viewer thinks even within the measure it is permitted to use its light, but after extinguishing it is permitted to benefit from them if they already burned the measure; he too distinguished like Beit Yosef: "and that which we ruled above after he kindled his measure it is permitted to extinguish and do as he wishes — that is revealing the matter retroactively that he put more than the measure; but where he put only the measure and it went out, or the wick was bad and sputtering in such a case it is forbidden" — end; such is essential, and the stringency Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahari Abuhav does not appear to be halacha for the reason we said. Maharshal also wrote: immediately when his time begins he should not eat or study until he kindles; if he began he stops; if he delayed the time, even though he did the mitzvah improperly, nevertheless his time is until chatzot when there is still pirsumei nisa; but to advance is not possible at all on erev Shabbat; and the prayer leader who kindles in the synagogue on erev Shabbat kindles before Minchah — end.
