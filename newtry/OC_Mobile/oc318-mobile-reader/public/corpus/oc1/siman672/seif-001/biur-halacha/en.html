At the end of its setting. So wrote the Tur and the Rosh in the first chapter of Ta'anit. But the Rashba and Ran imply explicitly that it is the beginning of sunset, though apparently the second sunset, as the Gra notes. Meiri also writes like the Rashba and Ran, and adds that Mordekhai at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbat writes that the language "from when it sets" implies the beginning of sunset; this is primary. So too writes the Peri Chadash.
One does not delay. According to this one must light immediately after tzeit ha-kokhavim. If so, it requires study what one who always prays Ma'ariv at its proper time, after tzeit ha-kokhavim, should do, as Peri Megadim notes. To delay Ma'ariv for this is also not proper, because it contains the mitzvah of Keriat Shema, which is Torah law, and the frequent takes precedence, as the later authorities write. Therefore it appears that such a person should certainly light before Ma'ariv. Many Rishonim hold that this is the preferred lechatchilah practice, as the Gra cites. Moreover, even the Tur, who writes "the end of sunset," soon shows that he too holds one may light earlier, close to a half hour before. Especially if we understand the Rambam's statement that one lights "with its setting" to mean literally when the sun disappears from our sight, and since his view is that the mitzvah of lighting lasts only about half an hour or slightly more afterward, if one lights at tzeit ha-kokhavim he does not fulfill the mitzvah at all according to the Rambam and those who follow his time of sunset. Even those who allow lighting later do so only because of doubt according to several poskim. Therefore it appears good and correct lechatchilah to light before Ma'ariv. Sha'arei Teshuvah writes in the name of Machazik Berakhah that one who does so has not lost; according to what we have proven, this should be the lechatchilah practice.
And one does not advance. This is the Rambam's language; see the Ran and Rashba, who disagree, as the Gra notes. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam's source is Shabbat 23b, "provided that he neither advances nor delays," which he interprets as referring to Chanukkah. According to most poskim, who explain it as referring to the Shabbat lamp, we no longer have a source for the Rambam's law. The Tur in siman 263 also applies it to Shabbat. It is somewhat surprising that the Shulchan Aruch states it plainly in siman 263 regarding Shabbat and here regarding Chanukkah. Perhaps here the Tur, and possibly the Rambam, hold it from reasoning: if he advances too much, it is not recognizable as pirsumei nisa, for what does a lamp help in daylight? But this works if "from sunset" is understood simply. Since the Shulchan Aruch copied the Tur and Mordekhai's explanation, meaning the end of sunset, which is tzeit ha-kokhavim, certainly if he lights a little earlier, about a quarter hour in bein ha-shemashot or even somewhat more, it is recognizable as nighttime. For Shabbat too the Shulchan Aruch says not to advance because it is not recognizable that he lights for Shabbat, yet everyone agrees one must light before bein ha-shemashot. We must say that "do not advance" means lighting while it is still broad daylight, when it is not recognizable for Shabbat or Chanukkah. The Tur, when he copied "end of sunset," did not copy "provided he does not advance"; the Rambam, who copied "provided he does not advance," did not write "end of sunset," but "with sunset." His intent is actually some time before sunset, as in Shabbat, "three things a person must say on Erev Shabbat with darkness," including lighting the lamp, where "with" means some time before. Even if we explain that the Rambam follows Rabbenu Tam and means the second sunset, we must still say that bein ha-shemashot and a bit earlier are not called "advancing." All the more according to what we clarified above in siman 261 from several poskim, that the Rambam holds Shabbat candles must be lit before the sun is hidden from our eyes, so certainly lighting even before sunset is not included in "do not advance," only much earlier. The Mechaber's combination of the Tur's "end of sunset" with the Rambam's "do not advance" therefore requires great study.
Incidentally, while discussing this, I will write what is difficult to me in the Beit Yosef, who says that the Rambam's words are like Behag, that one does not light Chanukkah lamps before sunset but with sunset, neither delaying nor advancing. This is difficult, for the Ran challenges Behag from Shabbat, where after sunset one may not light, yet the same baraita says "from when it sets"; it must be that here too, if one wants to light earlier, he may. The Ran answers that Behag holds like Rav Yosef, and there too one may light after sunset. According to this, what will he answer for the Rambam, who rules like Rabbah, as the Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Shabbat? We must therefore say as above, that "with its setting" includes some time before, like the statement that three things must be said on Erev Shabbat with darkness, including "light the lamp." What he wrote, that one does not light before the sun sets, means a long time before.
Provided that he gives it oil until traffic ceases, etc. According to several poskim this is about a half hour after tzeit ha-kokhavim, which totals an hour and three quarters from the time of lighting; one may not use its light during that entire time, even if he lit while it was still broad day. If it went out on a weekday before tzeit ha-kokhavim, he must relight it, but without a blessing, because under pressing circumstances we say that from plag ha-minchah onward the mitzvah has already begun, as Peri Megadim writes.
