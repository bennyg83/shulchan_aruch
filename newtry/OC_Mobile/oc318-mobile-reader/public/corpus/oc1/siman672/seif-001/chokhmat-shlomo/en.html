Se'if 1. One does not light a Chanukah lamp before the sun sets, etc. Note: See the Taz, s.k. 1, regarding what he challenged against the answer of Mahari Abuhav from the Talmud in Sanhedrin. The words of the Taz are surprising, for regarding what is more than the measure of the Chanukah lamp and is not fit for him, why did he need to challenge from the Talmud in Sanhedrin and from the dispute of Abaye and Rava? He should have challenged from an explicit Mishnah in chapter 2 of Shekalim regarding leftover shekalim and leftover sin-offering money, where it is explicit that according to the Sages, and so we hold, leftover shekalim are non-sacred, even though he explicitly said 'for shekalim,' since more than that is not fit for him. But regarding a sin-offering, where it is fit for him because one may buy with this amount and one may buy with that amount, see there, all of it is consecrated. If so, the same should apply to a Chanukah lamp: since for him the leftover is not fit, it should be exactly like shekalim. Why should sanctity take effect on the leftover even if he said so explicitly? He should have challenged from the explicit Mishnah mentioned above, and this requires investigation. Analyze this carefully. See in my work on Yoreh Deah, fourth edition, in the laws of a Torah scroll, siman 276, in my responsum to the holy community of Darhaya, what I wrote to reject the proofs of the Taz from leftover money of a deceased person; see there and analyze carefully. See further there in the Taz what he wrote, that according to Rava it is like an act, just as according to Abaye with designation. In my humble opinion, with due respect to his honored and elevated Torah, he overlooked an arranged Mishnah in Menachos, chapter 10, Mishnah 4, where it says there: and it is obligated in challah and exempt from tithes; Rabbi Akiva obligates it in challah and tithes. See there in the Rav of Bartenura, who explains that Rabbi Akiva's reason is that he holds that consecration takes effect only on what is fit for it and no more. If so, this is the reasoning of the Talmud in Sanhedrin: that which is fit for him is seized by it, and that which is not fit for him is not seized by it. But the Sages disagree with this reasoning, and we hold there like the Sages. If so, how can the Talmud in Sanhedrin state this reasoning there plainly? It should have said that it is a dispute of tanna'im, and that this baraisa follows Rabbi Akiva, rather than stating it without qualification. Perforce, it holds that the Sages disagree only where there was an act, but with designation even the Sages agree that what is not fit for him is not seized by it. If so, we hold like the Sages, and the words of the Taz are refuted. Analyze and understand, for I have been brief. I have another difficulty with the above words of the Taz from the Talmud in Sukkah, folio 49b, where it says: and why not bring it in a sanctified vessel? He holds there is no measure for the water, and sacred vessels consecrate even without intent. See Rashi's explanation. This is difficult: even if there is a measure for the water, why should we not say that what is fit for it is seized by it, and what is not fit for it is not seized by it, and it consecrates at least the measure, especially if a sacred vessel consecrates without intent? Rather, it is certainly proven that if all of it is not fit for this, it cannot be consecrated by halves; rather either all of it is consecrated or none of it is consecrated at all. If so, why there do we say that even without specification, what is fit for him is seized by it and what is not fit for him is not seized by it? Perforce, prohibition is different from consecration. There, it speaks of a prohibition of benefit and not under the law of consecration, but only under the law of prohibition; about this we say that what is fit for it is seized by it and what is not fit for it is not seized by it. But with consecration, such as there regarding sanctification by a vessel, or regarding a Chanukah lamp, whose prohibition derives from the sanctity it has, this does not work by halves. Only if all of it is fit for consecration does consecration take effect on it; if all of it is not fit, it is not consecrated at all. The law of consecration is different from the law of prohibition. However, at first glance there would still be a difficulty: according to this answer, that Mishnah follows the opinion that there is bereirah, and therefore those logim that are later taken from them for libation are clarified retroactively to have been the ones initially fit and standing for this, and they became sanctified in the vessel, and it is as though there was no extra here. This is like one who says regarding the loaves of a thanksgiving-offering that forty out of eighty should be sanctified, where all agree that they become sanctified, as written in chapter 2 of Kiddushin and in Menachos. If so, one could answer that in truth perhaps there is a measure for the water, only it follows the opinion that there is bereirah. From this there appears to be a proof to the words of Tosafos regarding 'a perutah in this pouch is hekdesh,' that bereirah applies only when he specifies, 'the two logim that I will separate,' but when he says it without specification, it does not help even according to the one who says there is bereirah; see there. If so, it works well: here, since he specifies to sanctify forty out of eighty, they become sanctified; but with the water, since he gives it without specification and does not specify, it does not help even according to the one who says there is bereirah. Especially since one can find a case where he does not specify, it is difficult: why should he not put it in a sanctified vessel without specifying how much he separates, and the water would not become sanctified? Therefore one must say that it holds there is no measure for the water. There is no time now to elaborate, and it works well and correctly; analyze carefully. In my work Sefer HaChaim, in siman 677, I resolved this by saying that the Talmud says this only regarding thought and designation alone. Then, although it holds that designation is a legally effective matter, nevertheless what is not fit for him is not seized by it, and he agrees that designation in such a case is not a legally effective matter. But with an act, certainly all of it is seized; see there in my book. Now I have found an explicit proof to our words, which is an explicit Mishnah in Menachos, chapter 10, Mishnah 4, regarding the Omer. This is its language there: They placed some of it in a mill for groats and removed from it a tenth of an ephah, sifted through thirteen sieves, and the remainder is redeemed and eaten by anyone, and is obligated in challah and exempt from tithes; Rabbi Akiva obligates it in challah and tithes. See there in Rashi's explanation and the Rav of Bartenura; it is proven there explicitly that Rabbi Akiva holds this reasoning, that what is fit for him is seized by it and what is not fit for him is not seized by it. Therefore he holds that the leftover does not have the law of consecration. The Sages hold that even what is not fit for him is also seized by it. This is difficult against him from the above Talmud in Sanhedrin, where it explicitly says that what is not fit for him is not seized by it. Perforce, as I wrote, one must distinguish that there it speaks of designation alone, while the Mishnah speaks of an act, and then everything is consecrated. Since we hold like the Sages, this properly proves like our words, and with Hashem's help the view of Mahari Abuhav works well. This is a clear proof; analyze carefully. There, in that se'if. And if he put in more, he may extinguish it, etc. Note: See my work on Orach Chaim, year 5596, folio 88b, which pertains here. Also see there in my novellae to siman 681, where we wrote that there is room to distinguish: to extinguish it and then use it, he is permitted; but to use it while it is still burning, he is not permitted. See there for the reason, and analyze carefully.
