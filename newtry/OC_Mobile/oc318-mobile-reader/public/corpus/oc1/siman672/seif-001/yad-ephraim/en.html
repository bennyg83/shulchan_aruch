In Magen Avraham, s.k. 5. And even if he began, he stops. This requires investigation, for we hold in siman 652 that in a rabbinic matter, if he began he does not stop, even if he began in prohibition, where there is time; see there. Regarding what he wrote from siman 431, see there, for the Magen Avraham wrote that there it is like a Torah matter, since as long as he has not nullified the chametz, the checking is according to the Torah, and this is the reason of the opinion there that he must stop. As for what the Bach wrote to distinguish because it is frequent, the Magen Avraham rejected this there from the challenge from lulav. And so we indeed hold regarding lulav on the second day of Yom Tov, which is rabbinic, that he need not stop even though it is not frequent. See what I wrote in my responsa Beis Ephraim, siman 9.
