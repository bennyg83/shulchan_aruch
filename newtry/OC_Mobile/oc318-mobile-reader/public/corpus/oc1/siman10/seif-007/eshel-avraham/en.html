See Be'er Heitev, s.k. 10, in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov, siman 5: they asked one who had a silk surcoat for Shabbat—since according to Beit Yosef the first obligation of tzitzis is only rabbinic, and even according to poskim that it is by Torah law, is not the view of some poskim that it is not released except in its kind—whether he may put on and remove that same garment on Shabbat for one whose dwelling place is not enclosed by a partition, and there is concern for one who goes out with a tallit lacking tzitzis who is liable for a sin-offering? And see Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 12 regarding enough for a knot, etc. The aforementioned gaon brought to permit that our public domain is only a karmelit; and see in Shaarei Teshuvah siman 11 and in Eliyah Zuta. However, Magen Avraham wrote on them that one should not make a rabbinic doubt ab initio—see there. Nevertheless, in our case the garment was made for a mitzvah—for Shabbat honor and so his Shabbat clothes not be like weekday clothes, etc.; and if he must remove it always before leaving his house, it is burdensome—and this is like after the fact; see Pesachim page 11 and Shabbos 17. Therefore one should not be stringent since his obligation is only rabbinic—"they said" and "they said"—and see Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 295, who wrote in the name of Rashal. But after lengthy discussion the aforementioned gaon brought to the plain meaning that a garment obligated in tzitzis rabbinically is obligated to wrap him and he becomes properly tzitzit-attached—only according to Tosafos it is not so—see there—and one should not be so lenient; but nevertheless to answer the rabbinic [question] one should not doubt at all.
