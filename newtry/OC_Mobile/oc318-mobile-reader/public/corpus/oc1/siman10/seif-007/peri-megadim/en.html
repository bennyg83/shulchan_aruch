He explained according to Rashi that he gave the reason that tzitzis in the middle is not a corner, which implies that on the spread-out parts he does place them, while Rabbi Shimon exempts, for one need not put tzitzis on the fold as long as he has not sewn it; see Tosafot 37b, s.v. hai. This refers to a garment fit to be worn that way without folding. What Rashi wrote there, s.v. a folded tallis, that it is twice as long, means in the manner of a tallis, to cover most of his body, and it was twice as long relative to that; nevertheless, over his entire body, aside from covering the head, he can wear it without folding. The Nimukei Yosef exempts a folded tallis even when spread out, for he is speaking of one so long that it cannot be worn at all unless folded, and Rabbi Shimon exempts it entirely. He concluded in practice according to both explanations. Now you will see a special intention here, as I will state. Rashi there, Menachos 41a, s.v. ipshit, says that it was twice as wide as his height, so that it certainly could not be worn that way. He explained this intentionally, for there is a difficulty: why did he not tell him initially, as soon as he saw it, that it was a folded tallis with two tzitzis on the folded parts and three on the spread-out parts? According to Rabbi Shimon, with whom the halacha follows, he thereby transgressed a positive commandment, for he needs four on the spread-out parts, since he told him go and place them, meaning on the spread-out parts. This proves that it was folded to twice his height, as the Nimukei Yosef says, and is entirely exempt according to the Torah; only D'Rabbanan, because of maris ayin, is it obligated on the spread-out parts. Therefore he did not tell him at first, because of human dignity there; see siman 13. Only afterward did he tell him so: go and place them, D'Rabbanan, because of maris ayin. See letter 8 regarding this, and Eliyah Rabbah, letter 7. From there, the explanation that it was twice as long is in any event not difficult, as written above.
