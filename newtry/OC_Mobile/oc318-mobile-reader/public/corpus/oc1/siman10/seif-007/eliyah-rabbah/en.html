[9] Most of it is closed, etc. If one made tzitzit on it and afterward opened most of it, they are invalid, as stated in seif 5. It requires consideration whether the opening through which the hands are inserted, where the garment has no sleeves, also combines with the majority of the garment that is open. At first glance, since Beit Yosef derives this rule of the majority from seif 4 concerning the majority of a garment, and there it appears that even when leather lies between 1 section of cloth and another they combine, this opening too should combine. Likewise, Beit Yosef in siman 362, seif 8, regarding a breached area greater than the standing area, combines the breaches. [10] And one may not go out, etc. Levush explains that this means into a public domain. This implies that a karmelit is permitted because a doubt concerning a rabbinic prohibition is treated leniently, and Olat Tamid writes likewise. Magen Avraham disagrees and writes that one may not intentionally enter into a doubtful rabbinic prohibition. In my view, the Sages did not enact their decree in a case of doubt. Levush writes that no blessing is recited. Magen Avraham writes that if most of the tallis is cloth and the remainder leather it is obligated in tzitzit, and the reverse likewise follows the majority. If the tallis is leather and cloth is sewn beneath it, it is exempt, and conversely it is obligated, because that which is beneath is secondary to the principal garment.
