Magen Avraham, paragraph 9: The second is like the first. See Taz, paragraph 9. The Taz disqualifies cutting the second set, for he finds no reason to validate it. Magen Avraham holds that ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy applies only when the original act was performed invalidly; if it was initially valid and was later invalidated, its validity may be restored. Thus, whichever set he cuts, the remaining set is valid: if he cuts the first, the second was not considered an act at the time because of bal tosif; if he cuts the second, the first was initially made validly. The Taz appears to hold that even an act that was initially valid may later be subject to ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy. See siman 15.
