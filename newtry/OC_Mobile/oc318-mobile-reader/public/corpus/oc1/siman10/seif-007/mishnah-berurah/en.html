(א) Exempt — even if the minority that is open has the measure of a tallit. And if he made tzitzis on it and afterwards opened the majority of it, they are invalid because of "you shall make, and not from that which is already made," as above in seif 5. And he must untie them and tie them again.

(ב) If the majority is open, it is obligated — this is specifically the majority of the entire garment, and not like tailors make a tallit katan: they sew it from the sides and leave a hole through which one puts his hands, and they sew below the hole and leave the majority open, and they measure only from below the hands. This is a complete error, for then it is not called a four-cornered [garment], and it is close to the case where one who blesses blesses in vain. In any case it is no better than a garment half of which is open, which is obligated in tzitzis but forbidden to bless on and forbidden to go out with on Shabbat. Rather one must measure also from what is above the hands, and the hole is also considered as if closed. See Biur Halacha.

(ג) And it is obligated in tzitzis — but one does not bless on it.

(ד) On Shabbat — because perhaps it is exempt and the tzitzis are like a carried object, as below in siman 13. See Magen Avraham, who wrote that even into a karmelit it is forbidden, for one does not create a rabbinic doubt lechatchilah; and many later authorities agree with Olat Tamid, who is lenient in this.
