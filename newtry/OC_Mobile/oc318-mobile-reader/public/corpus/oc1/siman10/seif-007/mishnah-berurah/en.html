It is exempt. This is so even if the minority that is open has the minimum measure of a tallis. If he made tzitzis on it and afterward opened most of it, they are invalid because of "you shall make," and not from what is already made, as above in seif 5. He must untie them and then tie them again.
If most of it is open, it is obligated. This means specifically most of the entire garment. It is not like the practice of tailors who make a small tallis by sewing it at the sides, leaving an opening through which the hands are inserted, then sewing below the opening and leaving most open, while measuring only from below the hands. This is a complete error, for then it is not called a four-cornered garment, and it is close to the case of one who recites a blessing in vain. In any event, it is no better than a garment that is half open, which is obligated in tzitzis, yet one may not recite a blessing on it and may not go out with it on Shabbos. Rather, one must measure even the area above the hands, and the opening is also considered as though it were closed. See Biur Halacha.
