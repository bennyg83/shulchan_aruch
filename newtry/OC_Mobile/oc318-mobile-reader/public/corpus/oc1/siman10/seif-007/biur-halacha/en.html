"Most of it closed." It is simple that even if when the garment was made most of it was open and afterward most of it became closed on its own—the law is so; and if so, if he had buttons on his tallis katan below its midpoint but they were bent only slightly, its law is as open, as Magen Avraham wrote citing Tosafos haRosh; and afterward they were bent very much until an act was needed to open them—automatically the garment is exempt from tzitzis, and after he opens the buttons he must untie the tzitzis and tie them again:

"Most of it open—obligated." See in Mishna Berurah what he wrote that the hole is also considered as if closed, and this is from Chayei Adam, who although in Darkei HaChayyim cited in the name of Achronim that it joins when the garment has no sleeves, as explained in Achronim—Bach already drove a nail into this in Nekudas HaKesef; see there. Also the Baal Aruch HaShulchan is uncertain on this; therefore certainly one should not bless on such a tallis katan. Also Sefer HaChayyim to Gaon Maharash Kluger agrees plainly with Chayei Adam; see there:

"And if half of it," etc. If two-thirds were open and one-third, and more than that was garment, and a little less than one-third was leather—I saw one who was uncertain whether he is obligated in tzitzis, since without the leather most of it was closed; see there.
