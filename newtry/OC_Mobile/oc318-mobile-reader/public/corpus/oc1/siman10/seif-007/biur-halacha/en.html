Most of it is closed. Plainly, even if the garment was originally mostly open and later became mostly closed by itself, this law applies. Thus, if hooks below the midpoint of a tallis katan were only slightly bent and counted as open, as Magen Avraham writes from Terumas HaDeshen, but later bent so tightly that opening them required an act, the garment automatically became exempt. After opening them, one must untie the tzitzis and retie them.
If most is open, it is obligated. The Mishnah Berurah says the neck opening is counted as closed, following Chayei Adam. Although Derech HaChaim cites Eliyah Rabbah that it combines as open when the garment has no sleeves, Nishmas Adam seriously challenges this. Artzos HaChaim also remains doubtful, and Sefer HaChaim of Rabbi Shlomo Kluger plainly agrees with Chayei Adam. One should therefore certainly not bless upon such a tallis katan.
And if half, etc. Someone questions a garment two-thirds open, where slightly more than one-third is cloth and slightly less than one-third leather: perhaps it is obligated because without the leather most would be closed. This seemingly resembles the established rule that even if the garment itself is wholly round and only its corners are leather, it is obligated in tzitzis. The law of mostly closed and open is learned from that rule, as Beis Yosef states. Further consideration is warranted.
