Unless he sewed it completely, even from one side. This is Rambam's language. Beis Yosef explains that if it is later unfolded the tzitzis will no longer be in its middle, taking 'from one side' literally. Artzos HaChaim remains puzzled by Agur's citation of Rambam requiring sewing on all three sides. Agur likely noticed Rambam's apparent contradiction: first 'unless he sewed it completely,' then 'even from one side.' If only one side sufficed, Rambam should simply say so. Rather, it must be sewn completely, but the stitching need not be proper two-sided sewing, as required for tefillin in siman 32:51; stitching along one face is enough. Rambam derives this from Menachos 41a's answer that it was fastened with pins, meaning the sewing was incomplete, as Rambam similarly explains Beitzah's first chapter. 'Sewed it completely' reflects his reading of Rav Dimi, Menachos 37b: one who sewed his folded cloak accomplished nothing; Rashi's first explanation, that he folded all of it. The baraisa obligating a sewn doubled tallis then forces a distinction. Rav Dimi concerns one not fully sewn: if the fold were unwanted he would cut it away, and he will ultimately undo it. Sewing all three edges demonstrates intent to retain it, making sewing effective. This also explains Rambam's negative formulation, 'one may not fold,' which quotes Rav Dimi's 'he accomplished nothing.' If Rambam instead followed Rashi's second explanation, like other poskim, it is difficult why he omitted Rav Dimi's law.
