"Unless he sewed it entirely, even on one side." This is Rambam's language; see in Beit Yosef, who wrote the reason: because through this, if it is unfolded again the tzitzis will not be in its middle—it is implied from his words that he interprets "on one side" literally. See in Biur Halacha that doubt remains on the Agur, who wrote in the name of Rambam that sewing is required on all three of its sides. In my humble opinion it appears simple that the Agur found Rambam's language difficult from beginning to end, for he says "unless he sewed it entirely" and afterward concludes "even on one side"—he should have said "unless he sewed on one side"; therefore it is his view that we require that it be sewn entirely specifically, and that which he says "even on one side" means we do not require superior sewing from both sides, as we find regarding sewing of tefillin—there Rambam and Shulchan Aruch ruled below in siman 32 s.n. 51 that the sewing thread should wrap around two sides; but here we require only that it be sewn entirely, even on one side. Rambam derives this from what is answered in the Gemara: "it is not needed"—"it ends at the edges," meaning the sewing was not completed properly [and similarly Rambam explains in Beitza chapter 1 on the Mishnah regarding what is answered in the Gemara "it is not needed—it ends at the edges"; see there]. And that which he says "unless he sewed it entirely" is because he explains the statement of Rav Dimi there in Menachot 37b, who says in Menachot: "this one who sewed his cloak did nothing," etc., per Rashi's first explanation—that is, he folded it entirely; and he found difficult Rashi's and Tosafos' question on that passage from that baraita that obligates everyone in a folded sewn tallis; therefore Rambam distinguishes that there Rav Dimi refers to one who did not sew it entirely, and therefore we say: if it were so that he need not cut and spread it out, he would certainly end up undoing it in the end—unlike if he sewed it after folding from all three of its sides, for then we see his intent is that it remain so, and therefore his sewing helps. In our words it will be very clear why Rambam recorded this law in negative language—"one does not fold"—and did not state plainly the law of a folded tallis: because he cited here the statement of Rav Dimi, who says "he did nothing"; also, if we explain that Rambam explains that of Rav Dimi per Rashi's second explanation there, as other poskim explained, it would be difficult—if so, why did Rambam omit the law from his code?
