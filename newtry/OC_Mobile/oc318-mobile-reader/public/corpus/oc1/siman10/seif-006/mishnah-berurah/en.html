That it is folded — For if it is opened, the tzitzit are found in its middle, and we require tzitzit on the corner itself.

The unfolded corners — And if he folded it because the garment is very long, twice his height, so he cannot wear it unless he folds it, some rule to exempt it entirely from tzitzit since it is not a garment on him. Taz and Pri Megadim conclude he should affix tzitzit on the unfolded corners and not recite a blessing.

Even from one side — See Biur Halachah.

They are obligated — to affix on the folded corners, for as long as it remains folded and not opened, a tallit rests upon him as folded.

Not to bless — All the later authorities wondered: what is improved by not blessing? There still remains that perhaps the law is as Mechaber and one must affix on the unfolded, and how can he wear this garment—behold more than four tzitzit is forbidden to add according to everyone; see Pri Megadim. They conclude lechatchila one should be careful not to make a folded tallit at all unless stitched, even with wide stitches, for once stitched it is no longer made to open; then whether a square tallit or one originally too long, he affixes tzitzit on the folds and recites the blessing.

The first — Possibly he meant specifically that; but if he cut the second, it is invalid since he already nullified the first.

In any case — Whether he intended to nullify or to add, and whether he cut the first or the second.

And such is the main ruling — And so ruled the later authorities.

The first ones — He used "the first ones" because he refers to what Shulchan Aruch wrote initially, "he cuts the first ones"; therefore he wrote "and before he cut the first ones." But in truth, as the Rosh whom we rule like holds, certainly if he cut the second ones it is valid, as in Beit Yosef.
