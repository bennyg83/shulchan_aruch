And in my humble opinion — since it is explained in Tosafos in Sanhedrin that they were pressed in the sugya in Sukkah that rabbinically one does not add, it is explained that they hold one should not distinguish regarding "this stands alone" between the prohibition of bal tosif and detracting from the mitzvah — unlike what they hold in Sukkah. Rather they hold that mitzvah-detraction and bal tosif transgression are linked together, and therefore they were compelled to say it is rabbinic. If so, one can say the Rambam also holds thus. And he further holds that just as bal tosif rabbinically detracts, so too the rabbinic mitzvah — for every rabbinic enactment is enacted like Torah law. And this emerges for the Rambam from what we rule: tying onto [strings] already tied is included in te'aseh velo min ha'asui. Therefore ab initio, before he cuts them, they are invalid — and that is rabbinically. And likewise in the Ran, Sukkah ch. 3 p. 248, who wrote in the Rambam's view: since "this stands alone" there is no bal tosif except rabbinically, therefore for all that is fitting for him the Sages did not decree — see there. And behold the Rambam, according to our versions, wrote there: if one adds or subtracts it is invalid. Therefore rabbinically, even after the fact it is invalid. And all the more it appears almost compelled to say thus in Tosafos's words. For if we analyze: since rabbinically one transgresses, it does not detract from the mitzvah — if so, that which Menachos challenges from tying onto [strings] already tied, that it constitutes te'aseh velo min ha'asui — therefore it would be if we cut them off from the first ones. Since at the time of making the latter ones they were not needed for the second, he did not need them — therefore afterward it is "from what is already made." And he wrote that the Beit Yosef initially holds thus. If so, Rava's statement "now this man stands in bal tosif — there is no [valid] act" should be difficult — whether per Rashi, that because of bal tosif it is not called an act at all and their cutting is the act, or per the Beit Yosef's explanation following the Rambam, that because of bal tosif it is a valid act — it should be difficult: for by Torah law there is no bal tosif transgression of "this stands alone," and automatically the latter ones are a valid act; and when he cuts the first ones the latter are invalid because of te'aseh velo min ha'asui. And how can we say that because of rabbinic bal tosif the Torah invalidity of theirs goes away? Rather the principle is: wherever there is bal tosif it detracts from the mitzvah; and where bal tosif is rabbinic, it detracts from the rabbinic mitzvah. If so, one can say: by Torah law, in tying onto [strings] already tied, even if he cuts the first ones there is no [issue of] te'aseh velo min ha'asui — for nevertheless on both of them there is an act in valid form. And the Gemara's main challenge is only because rabbinically, before he cuts them, they are invalid; and through cutting them they become valid — it is rabbinic te'aseh velo min ha'asui. Therefore Rava properly said: "now he stands in bal tosif — there is no act" — from Menachos. Regarding Torah law, in any case there is no te'aseh velo min ha'asui; and regarding rabbinic law, because of bal tosif transgression there is no te'aseh velo min ha'asui.
