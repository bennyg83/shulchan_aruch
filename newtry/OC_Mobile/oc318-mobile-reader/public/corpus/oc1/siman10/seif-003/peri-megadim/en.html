One diagonally. See the Taz. Rashi, Menachos 37a, s.v. five-cornered, explains that he cut from the corner diagonally, and s.v. devatzir, that this is a notch; see there and in the Beis Yosef. If so, there is a distinction: if he cut a small piece from a three-cornered garment and made three into four, it is proper. But if he did not cut anything, only made a notch with a knife, tore at the corner, or cut diagonally, it does not become four-cornered until the majority is open, as below in seif 7. From here it appears that with our talleisim, where the warp threads extend outward, one cuts only near the corners and need not cut along the entire length, for cutting some piece from a three-cornered garment makes it a four-cornered garment properly. Even though the corner is more than three fingerbreadths relative to the rest, since there is no fabric there, it is well. Only where there is fabric do we say that the majority is closed and we follow it; this is not so here. See what I wrote further below, siman 11, in Magen Avraham, letter 7, regarding this.
