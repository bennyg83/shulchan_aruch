Seif katan 3: The first, and the same applies to the second, etc. See the Bach. This means that the Mordechai was uncertain whether the Shulchan Aruch specifically stated that a five-cornered garment is obligated, whereas a six-cornered garment, meaning one whose two corners were cut, is exempt, or whether there is no distinction. He asked his teacher, who answered that there is no distinction between a five-cornered and a six-cornered garment. Although the Beit Yosef wrote that this uncertainty is astonishing, since why should one have thought to distinguish, the Bach explains the basis of the doubt. One might have thought that when only one corner was cut, the garment remains obligated, because although the cut area somewhat resembles a rounded edge, the other three corners testify that the cut corner is also considered a corner. If two corners were cut, however, one might have thought that since those edges resemble rounded ones, they are not considered corners. Nor can the remaining two corners testify about them, for why should these two corners have greater force than the other two, so that one pair should testify concerning the other? This was therefore the subject of the doubt. In truth, the Bach's own view leans somewhat toward exempting a garment when two corners were cut. See there.
