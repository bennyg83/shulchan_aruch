(siman katan 3) One — the same law applies to two, etc. — Bach. He means to say that in Mordechai there was doubt whether the Shulchan Aruch cited specifically five corners as obligated, but for six corners — that is, cutting two corners exempts — or there is no distinction; and he asked his rabbi and they answered there is no distinction between five corners and six corners. And even though Rabbeinu Yonah wrote these are astounding words — how could one think to distinguish — nevertheless Bach explained the place of doubt: it could be thought that only if one cut one corner is one obligated, even though the place of the cut is somewhat like a circle; nevertheless his fellow [corners] will testify about him — the other three corners, for the cut corner is also a corner; but if one cuts two corners it could be thought that since they are similar to circles there is no corner and his fellow corners need not testify about them — the other two corners — for what advantage do these two corners have that those two corners should testify about these; and therefore he was in doubt. And in truth Bach's view somewhat inclines to exempt when cutting two corners — see there.
