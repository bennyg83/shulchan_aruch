There, in the continuation: 'See there, where it implies as I have written.' I do not know of any such implication. Mordechai there distinguishes only that even according to the view that leather corners are exempt, that applies where the entire corner is leather. It does not apply where the upper part of the corner is cloth and its lower part is leather. One can say that the text might have led us to think the reverse as well: when the upper part is leather and the lower part cloth, the garment is still obligated, because both are primary and the presence of any wool is sufficient to obligate it.
