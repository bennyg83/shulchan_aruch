Seif 1, "it has," etc. Not like Rabbi Simchah, who exempts it like that baraisa that exempts in Zevachim chapter 2. According to what is said in Menachos 37b, "a five-cornered garment is the difference between them," meaning that according to the Rabbis it is exempt and according to Rabbi Yehudah it is obligated, and the law is decided there like the Rabbis. There: one who reduced his cloak, etc., means that he did this to place tzitzis on it, but accomplished nothing, for it is exempt. Why? He made it five-cornered, etc. See Hagahos Maimoniyos chapter 3. Although the Sifrei teaches: "four corners" excludes garments of three, five, six, seven, or eight corners, and likewise in Zevachim 18b, the Rosh explained that this means one places tzitzis only on four of its corners; a three-cornered garment is exempt because it lacks four. So too the sugya at the end of Menachos chapter 3, 37b, says it is obligated, like Rashi's explanation there of "one who reduced," etc., and not like the above explanation; and the sugya there, 43b, where all hold unlike the baraisa that exempts.

"And he makes," etc. Like the first tanna there, 37b, as it says there that the halachah is not like him.

"The farthest," etc. Rambam. So it is also proven there, for it says: one who reduced it, etc., accomplished nothing. Since it says this language and does not say that he did not exempt it, it implies that it neither helps nor harms. If it were otherwise, he at least did something, since he could place tzitzis on these and not on the others.
