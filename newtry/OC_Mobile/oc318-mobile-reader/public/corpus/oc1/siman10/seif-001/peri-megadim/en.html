It is explained there that were it not for the inclusion from the word asher, a five-cornered garment would be exempt; and according to Rabbi Yishmael, the four corners are four separate mitzvos, so one places five tzitzis. At first glance one might say that the question is not, why do I need asher, but rather that he wishes to answer a second difficulty raised by the Beis Yosef and the Levush: why according to the Rabbis do we not include a five-cornered garment to require five tzitzis, and what difference does it make that the four are one mitzvah? To this he answered that the Rabbis hold that by logic four are included within five, so asher is extra and must necessarily exclude the fifth, so that he places only four tzitzis. Nevertheless, from HaKometz there remains a difficulty: if Rabbi Yishmael has the derashah of asher, it is difficult; and if he does not have it, then it does not depend on whether the four are separate mitzvos, etc. Also, from where do we know this? Perhaps according to everyone one places five tzitzis. At first glance one might say that in Zevachim the conclusion is that everyone agrees that anything extra is considered present, and the questioner, who held that it is considered absent, needs no inclusion. Therefore Shmuel validates oversized priestly garments, for extra is like deficient. We, however, rule in Chullin 58b and in Yoreh Deah 55 that anything extra is considered as if removed; if so, no verse is needed, except to teach that one does not place tzitzis on it. In HaKometz the explanation is as follows. So Tosafot wrote in Zevachim, s.v. ve'idach, that with regard to asher, if there is a clear derashah in it we expound it, and if not, we do not expound it; see there carefully. If so, according to the first Tanna, since the four are one mitzvah, we expound asher and include a five-cornered garment, but only for four tzitzis. By contrast, according to Rabbi Yishmael, who holds that the four are four separate mitzvos, if we include from asher, logic would be to include that one should place five tzitzis, since each is a mitzvah by itself. This is impossible, for if so the Torah should have omitted asher. Therefore he does not expound asher at all in such a manner, and in truth he places tzitzis on five corners; see Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 3 of Tzitzis, letter 2, and understand there. According to this, if one cut two notches in one corner, one might say that anything extra is considered as removed, and it is only a three-cornered garment. See Yoreh Deah, siman 25, that when they are distant it is only considered as removed, and when they are adjacent it is as if both are removed. None of this is satisfactory to me, for the Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, halacha 4, ruled that oversized garments are invalid, and nevertheless regarding treifos he ruled that an extra rib is considered as removed. This shows that they are not comparable, and it requires study.
