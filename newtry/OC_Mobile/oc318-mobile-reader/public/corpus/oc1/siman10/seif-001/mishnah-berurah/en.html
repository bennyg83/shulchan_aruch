(1) “That does not have, etc.” — Whether at the beginning of its making or whether afterward he cut away one corner and made it round.

(2) “It is exempt” — This is explained in the verse, as it is written: “On the four corners of your garment” (Deuteronomy 22:12). And we expound in the Sifri: “Four and not three.” And more than four we include from “that you cover yourself” (Deuteronomy 22:12). And the opposite we cannot say — that included in five is four, but in three four is not included — and it is not specifically [a garment] of five; the same applies to one of six and seven.

(3) “It is obligated” — And some exempt it; therefore one should not make [a garment] of five [corners] ab initio. And Peri Megadim wrote that according to those who exempt it, our small tallisos that have a neck opening nevertheless have a majority closed; however, if it happened that the majority was open, he should make in the collar that tailors make somewhat round.

On four — And if he made five, he transgresses bal tosif. And if he cut off one of them, it is valid, provided the four corners that are farthest from each other remain with tzitzit, as will be explained ahead.

That are farther from each other — For it is reasonable to affix them on those that are most recognizable among the others. And some say this is from the Torah, and one should be stringent if possible. But if it was already done on the nearer corner and it is impossible to fix, such as when the time for prayer has arrived, he may rely on those who are lenient and recite the blessing.
