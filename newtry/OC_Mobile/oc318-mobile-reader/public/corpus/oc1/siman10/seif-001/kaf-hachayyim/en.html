1) [Seif 1] A tallis that does not have four corners is exempt; if it has more than four, it is obligated. This is taught in a Baraita in the chapter HaTecheiles, and so ruled the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. R' Eliezer of Metz and Rabbeinu Simchah, however, ruled that a five-cornered garment is also exempt, in accordance with the Baraita of the Sifrei and the second chapter of Zevachim; Beit Yosef cites them; see there. Bach writes that a G-d-fearing person should therefore wear only a tallis with four corners. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1, who writes that Birkat HaZevach on the second chapter of Zevachim also inclines to this view; Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 1; Ateret Zekenim; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 5. Penei Yitzchak, may his light shine, writes likewise in the section for the letter tet, entry 126, in the name of Lev Chayyim, siman 78. Ben Ish Chai, may his light shine, writes likewise in parashat Noach, entry 6. Penei Yitzchak writes there that one should not recite a blessing over a five-cornered tallis because of the principle that in a case of doubt concerning blessings, one rules leniently. See below, entry 7.
2) There. “A tallis that does not have four corners is exempt.” If the threads at the corner's edge came loose until the corner became rounded, although the Sifrei states that it must be square, this is of no concern, because we consider its condition at the time the tzitzis are made, just as with the requirement that the hole be at least a thumb-joint from the edge; see siman 11, seif 10. So I found in a manuscript of R' Avraham Nachum, of blessed memory. Divrei Menachem, entry 1, in Hagahot HaTur.
3) There. “And he places four sets of tzitzis upon it,” etc. If he placed five, he violates bal tosif. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2, in the name of Rashi on parashat Vaetchanan. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 2; Nahar Shalom, entry 1; Ikarei HaDat, siman 2, entry 2, in the name of Sedei HaAretz, volume 3, siman 1; and Ben Ish Chai there rule likewise. Levush writes, “But he need not place them on five corners,” which implies that he may do so if he wishes. This is not correct. See Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, entry 2, and Yeshuot Yaakov, entry 2.
3) Further. Magen Avraham writes there, “See seif 6 regarding the case in which he cut them afterward.” Machatzit HaShekel explains that he means the following: if one placed five sets of tzitzis and afterward cut off one of the five, leaving only four, the ruling depends upon the dispute between Beit Yosef and Rama in seif 6 concerning one who placed tzitzis upon tzitzis and then cut one set off; this concludes his words. Maamar Mordechai, entry 4, likewise writes that according to Maran it is considered an addition and is invalid regardless of which set he cuts off, whether the first or the last. Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, however, writes concerning the foregoing words of Magen Avraham: “His comparison to seif 6, where one places tzitzis upon tzitzis, can be distinguished. There, even according to the opinion that invalidates it, the two sets were placed on one corner. Here, however, all agree that the first four sets, which were made validly, become valid when he afterward cuts off the tzitzis from one corner. Taz, se'if katan 6, also implies this”; this concludes his words. Yeshuot Yaakov there and Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 2, write likewise. Nevertheless, to avoid the dispute, one should untie all the tzitzis and then tie them again.
4) There. “Those that are farthest from one another.” Even after the fact, this requirement is indispensable, and the tzitzis are invalid if they were not placed on the corners farthest apart. See Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 1, who writes that this is also implied by Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 13. Yeshuot Yaakov there and Mishbetzot Zahav, se'if katan 2, rule likewise.
