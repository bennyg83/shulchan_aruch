1) [Seif 1] A tallis that does not have four corners is exempt; if it has more than four, it is obligated. This is taught in a Baraita in the chapter HaTecheiles, and so ruled the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh. R' Eliezer of Metz and Rabbeinu Simchah, however, ruled that a five-cornered garment is also exempt, in accordance with the Baraita of the Sifrei and the second chapter of Zevachim; Beit Yosef cites them; see there. Bach writes that a God-fearing person should therefore wear only a tallis with four corners. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1, who writes that Birkat HaZevach on the second chapter of Zevachim also inclines to this view; Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 1; Ateret Zekenim; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 5. Penei Yitzchak, may his light shine, writes likewise in the section for the letter tet, entry 126, in the name of Lev Chayyim, siman 78. Ben Ish Chai, may his light shine, writes likewise in parashat Noach, entry 6. Penei Yitzchak writes there that one should not recite a blessing over a five-cornered tallis because of the principle that in a case of doubt concerning blessings, one rules leniently. See below, entry 7.
