{Rama: Magen Avraham, note 5} That we follow the majority. And I was in doubt: if the garment with a minority of leather is only of the measure so that it can cover the head and most of a child — shall we say we follow the majority? And since this garment in general has the [required] measure and the majority of the species is an obligated species, all of it is an obligated species; or shall we say that since wool alone does not have the measure of obligation, it is exempt from tzitzis? And I asked in the presence of several sages, and one said thus and one said thus. And it seems one may learn this law from Magen Avraham's ruling above (siman 9, note 4) regarding a tallit whose warp is wool and woof silk: according to Maharam of Rothenburg it has no remedy, etc. — his intent is explained according to Maharam of Rothenburg, brought in Maggid Mishnah, that wool does not exempt in silk, only silk of silk, as Magen Avraham brought in note 3; therefore this tallit has no remedy, since if he makes silk it does not exempt the wool portion because of it. And it seems one may also say, like the view of the poskim that silk is biblically exempt in such a case, the tallit is biblically exempt since woof or warp alone is not obligated in tzitzis; and likewise here, if he makes tzitzis of silk he exempts the silk portion, and since the wool portion is not obligated in tzitzis it is exempt. What difference to me whether the silk is not an obligated species or it is exempted from tzitzis? In any case, since it is a complete garment we equate all of it to obligation; and likewise here. Nevertheless one may distinguish, as is clear. And examine.
