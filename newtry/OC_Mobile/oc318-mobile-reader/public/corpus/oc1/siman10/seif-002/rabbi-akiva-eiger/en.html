Magen Avraham, se'if katan 5: 'For we follow the majority.' I was uncertain about a garment containing a minority of leather when the entire garment is only the minimum size capable of covering the head and majority of a small child. Do we follow the majority, so that since the garment as a whole has the required size and most of its material is obligated, the whole is treated as obligated material? Or do we say that since the wool alone lacks the minimum obligated size, the garment is exempt from tzitzis? I asked this in the presence of several sages; some answered one way and some the other. At first glance this law can be learned from Magen Avraham above, siman 9, se'if katan 4, concerning a tallis whose warp is wool and whose woof is silk. According to Maharam of Rothenburg it has no remedy. His intent follows Maharam's view, cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot, that wool does not exempt silk; only silk threads do, as Magen Avraham cites in se'if katan 3. The tallis therefore has no remedy: silk tzitzis do not exempt its wool portion. At first glance, according to the poskim who hold that silk is exempt by Torah law, we should say that such a tallis is likewise exempt by Torah law because neither the woof nor the warp alone constitutes an obligated garment. If so, silk tzitzis should exempt the silk portion; and since the wool portion alone is not obligated in tzitzis, it too should be exempt. What difference is there whether the silk is not an obligated material or its obligation has been discharged? We must conclude that since it is a complete garment, the entire garment is treated as obligated. The same should apply here. Nevertheless, an obvious distinction can be made. Examine this carefully.
