One on a diagonal. The same law applies if he cut two corners on a diagonal, whereby it becomes a six-cornered garment.
A five-cornered garment. Similarly, if it originally had three corners and he cut one corner into two, it becomes a four-cornered garment and is obligated in tzitzis. The cut must be large and recognizable. See the Pri Megadim, who writes that this is specifically where he cut away part of the garment until that place thereby appears like two corners. But if he did not cut anything away, and merely made a slit with a knife, tearing the corner until that place was divided into two, it does not thereby become a four-cornered garment until most of it is open, as stated below in seif 7.
