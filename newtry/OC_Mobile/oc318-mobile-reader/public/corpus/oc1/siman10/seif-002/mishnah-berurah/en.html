One on a diagonal — The same law applies if he cut two corners on a diagonal and it became a six-cornered garment.

A five-cornered garment — And similarly, if it originally had three corners and he cut one corner into two, it becomes four-cornered and is obligated in tzitzit, and the cut must be large and recognizable. And see in Pri Megadim, who wrote that specifically if he cut away part of the garment until thereby that place appears as two corners; but if he cut away nothing and only pierced with a knife, tearing the corner until that place split in two, it does not thereby become four-cornered until the majority is open, as below in seif 7.
