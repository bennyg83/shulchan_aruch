5) [Seif 2] It has four corners, and he cut one diagonally, etc. The same applies if it originally had three corners and he divided one into two: it thereby becomes a four-cornered garment and is obligated. See Darkei Moshe, entry 1; Taz, se'if katan 3; Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 2; and Mishbetzot Zahav, se'if katan 3. This applies specifically if he actually cuts away a small piece, thereby turning a three-cornered garment into one with four corners. If he did not cut away anything, however, but merely made an indentation with a knife, tore the corner, or cut it diagonally, it does not become a four-cornered garment until most of the relevant section is separated, as stated below in seif 7. See Mishbetzot Zahav there. Mishbetzot Zahav further writes that it appears from here that regarding our talleisim, from which warp threads protrude, it is sufficient to cut them only near the corners; one need not cut along their entire length. See there.
6) There. “It has four corners, and he cut one diagonally,” etc. The same applies if he cut two corners, thereby making it a six-cornered garment. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 4. Levush explains that this states a novelty: one might otherwise think that because he cut two corners diagonally, it is considered to have a rounded corner and is exempt from tzitzis; this concludes his words. Bach's view, however, inclines toward exempting a garment when two corners were cut; see there. Machatzit HaShekel writes likewise.
7) If one's tallis tears, he should take care to sew it immediately, lest it become a six-cornered garment, which according to one opinion is exempt from tzitzis, as stated above in entry 1. This applies when most of the relevant section tore, and he must untie the tzitzis and then attach them again. See Chesed LaAlafim, entry 4. Ben Ish Chai, may his light shine, entry 8, writes likewise.
