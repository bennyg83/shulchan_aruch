Of cloth, etc. — For it is written "with which you cover yourself," and a person covers himself only with the main part of the garment; therefore we follow that. Even if the garment portion is entirely round and has no corner, only corners of leather attached to it, it is obligated, for we follow the main part of the garment.

And its corners are of leather — The same law applies if the majority of the tallit is cloth and the remainder leather: it is obligated.

And its corners are of cloth — The same law applies if only the majority of the tallit is leather, even if the minority of cloth has the measure of a tallit: it is exempt from tzitzit, since the main garment is not cloth, we do not apply "on the corners of their garments." Leather is exempt even rabbinically, for it is not in the category of cloth at all. The same law applies to a tallit whose underside is stitched with leather: it is obligated; but if the upper is leather, it is exempt, for the lower is secondary to the upper, which is the main garment.
