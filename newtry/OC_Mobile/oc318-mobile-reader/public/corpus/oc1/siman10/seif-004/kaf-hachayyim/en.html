10) [Seif 4] A tallis made of cloth, etc. This is Rava's view in the chapter HaTecheiles, Menachos 40b: we follow the material of the principal garment. The Rif, Rambam, and Rosh rule accordingly, and Maran follows their view. Rav Achai, however, disagrees and holds that we follow the material of the corner, and some of the Geonim were concerned for his view; see Beit Yosef. Although Maran, of blessed memory, writes in Beit Yosef that we need not be concerned for those who rule stringently, that applies as a matter of law. A G-d-fearing person should nevertheless be stringent initially if the corner tore or wore out and replace it only with the same material as the garment, thereby satisfying all opinions. A piece of leather or cloth placed on the corner merely to reinforce it, however, is permitted even according to the opinion that follows the corner's material; see Beit Yosef in this siman and at the end of siman 11. Although some authorities are stringent, the custom is already to be lenient in accordance with those who permit it, as Maran writes there in the Shulchan Aruch.
11) There. “A tallis made of cloth,” etc. If most of the tallis is cloth and the remainder is leather, it is obligated. The same applies in reverse, for we follow the majority. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 5; Ateret Zekenim; Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 8; and Chayei Adam, rule 11, entry 6.
12) There. “A tallis made of cloth,” etc. If the tallis is made of leather and cloth is sewn beneath it, it is exempt. The same applies in reverse: it is obligated, because the material beneath is subordinate to the garment's principal material. See Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 9; and Chayei Adam there. Mor U'Ketziah, however, is uncertain about this; see there. He writes: “If such a case came before me, I would attach tzitzis in either configuration (whether cloth was sewn beneath leather or the reverse) but I would not recite a blessing over them, even when both materials were present together”; this concludes his words. See Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, se'if katan 5.
