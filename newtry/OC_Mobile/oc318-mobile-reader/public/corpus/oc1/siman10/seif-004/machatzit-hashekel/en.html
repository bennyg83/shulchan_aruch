(siman katan 4) A fold, etc. — it appears in Tosefta, Parashas HaKometz, folio 34b, that if he folded, etc., so that the corner remained, etc. — regarding that which they say there: Rav Dimi said, one who sews his cloak does not accomplish anything, for it is impossible that he does not need the corners to cut and throw away. And Tosefta wrote, and this is its meaning: the reader in foreign tongue [explained] that he folded his tallis and sewed it so it would not return to its place — even so he does not accomplish anything, and it would not be explained thus, for below in chapter 85 it teaches, etc., and it is equal that if he folded and sewed he is obligated (as explained in seif 6) — hence that he is obligated in the place of the folds to tie tzitzis there, etc., and the tzitzis in its corners are effective — end quote. And they challenged thus the first explanation of Rashi, who would explain "sews his cloak" as sewing in a manner that after the sewing the corner still remains square but doubled, and he tied tzitzis on the fold — he does not accomplish anything; and on this Tosefta challenged and proved from seif 6 that in such a case it helps if one ties tzitzis on the fold; and they wrote no more, and without doubt their view is to agree from the necessity of this difficulty to the second explanation of Rashi, who explained "sews his cloak" as sewing and trimming the corners until they appear round, and he wished to exempt the cloak from tzitzis — and therefore he said he does not accomplish anything, for nevertheless he is obligated; and this explanation Rabbeinu Yonah brought here in Shulchan Aruch. In any case it appears from Tosefta's words that if he folded and sewed the corners and they still remain square, if he ties the tzitzis on the fold he fulfills [his obligation]. It is true that Rashi himself also rejected the first explanation above, as Tosefta rejected it from this [case] in seif 6; and therefore Tosefta did not have this in Rashi's version before them. Nevertheless Magen Avraham brought Tosefta's words, for in their language it is clearer that tying tzitzis on the fold helps. But in Rashi it is not so clear, even though Rashi also presumably intended thus; but in Mordechai, Laws of Ketanos, he wrote that here it is not similar to that [case] in seif 6 — and this is its meaning: that [case] in seif 6 deals with one who folded the whole [garment] for this purpose and is obligated, for such is the way of dress — end quote. And if so, the matter is in dispute as stated.
