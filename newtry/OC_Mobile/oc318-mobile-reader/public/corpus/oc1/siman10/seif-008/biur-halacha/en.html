See in Mishna Berurah what he wrote that he bends them very much, etc.—this is from Magen Avraham in the name of Tosafos haRosh; and although afterward Magen Avraham wrote that nevertheless it may be forbidden because of marit ayin that it appears closed, and Darkei HaChayyim cited his words; see in Achronim and Aruch HaShulchan, who agreed with Knesset HaGedolah and Sefer Zichronot to be lenient; also Gra in his chiddushim challenged this, and especially what we cited in Mishna Berurah in the name of chiddushei Re'ah: one who always goes in tallis katan under his garments, and even when he goes in the public domain it is concealed—one can say marit ayin does not apply here; certainly the law in this is that it is permitted according to our custom that we always go in tallis katan under the garments:

"Because of marit ayin." It is implied in Levush and Eliyah Rabbah that likewise garments that are mostly open must have most of what is visible to the eyes and not narrow, so that they do not think he blesses in vain. Aruch HaShulchan wrote: garments that have four corners and one wears them as a garment and girds them with his belt from half its length downward—even if he girds tightly, he is not thereby exempt from tzitzis; but if he fixes loops on both sides and ties them with a permanent knot, one may be uncertain whether they are thereby exempt from tzitzis—for perhaps one can compare it to kilayim, that through tying a connection is made; if so, in our matter does it nullify it from the status of four corners or not; see there in Machatzis HaShekel:
