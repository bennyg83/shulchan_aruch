To make it as closed. The Mishnah Berurah's requirement that the hooks be bent very tightly is from Magen Avraham in Terumas HaDeshen's name. Magen Avraham then suggests nevertheless forbidding it because it appears closed, and Derech HaChaim copies him. Eliyah Rabbah and Artzos HaChaim agree with Knesses HaGedolah and Sefer HaZichronos to permit it; Rabbi Akiva Eiger also questions the stringency. Especially under Rabbi Akiva Eiger's ruling cited in the Mishnah Berurah; that one who always wears a tallis katan beneath clothing remains concealed even in public; there is no appearance concern. It is certainly permitted under our custom of always wearing it beneath clothing.
Because of appearance. Levush and Olas Tamid imply that garments counted mostly open must also be visibly, not exactly, more than half open, lest people think the blessing is in vain. Artzos HaChaim writes that a four-cornered garment worn normally does not become exempt when one tightly belts it below the midpoint. If permanent loops are fixed on both sides and tied in a lasting knot, it is uncertain whether this exempts it: perhaps, as with kilayim, tying creates a connection and removes its four-cornered status, or perhaps not.
