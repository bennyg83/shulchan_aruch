(siman katan 8) The first. It is possible, etc., that Rabbeinu Yonah's view is like the Rambam's view, who holds that only if he intended to annul the first ones are the second valid — for there is no reason to disqualify because of ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy; and for this reason, behold he did a great act when he made the second, for his intent was to annul the first ones. The tallis is subject to obligation, for it has four corners and he annulled the first ones, and it is as if it has only four tzitzis; and therefore Magen Avraham wrote that if he cuts the second ones they are invalid, for the first ones were already annulled and invalidated, and it is now ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy; but if he did not intend to add to the first and did not annul them, the Rambam holds that both are invalid because of ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy. And he holds to explain thus the Gemara's words; but the Rosh, according to his explanation in the Gemara, validates in every case, and holds the reasoning is the reverse — and needless to say if when he made the second he intended to add and not annul, certainly there is no disqualification because of ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy, for when he made the second it is not called an act at all, since thereby he transgressed bal tosif — and how can it be called an act for validating tzitzis? Therefore when he afterward cut one of the tzitzis, then it is called an act; for until then it was not called an act — even if when he made the second he intended to annul the first, for he did not transgress bal tosif, nevertheless there is no act. And by cutting one of the tzitzis they become valid, for then it is called an act.

And the reason Rabbeinu Yonah wrote: since if he had intended to add it would not be called an act — for he does not intend to add — likewise there is no act. And he concluded himself that this is strained; and Bach wrote: since we rule that transgression at its time does not require intent, even without intent he transgressed bal tosif; and therefore even if he did not intend to add but only to annul the first, nevertheless he transgressed bal tosif and therefore there is no act until he cut one of the tzitzis, and then it is called an act (but according to what Magen Avraham wrote, siman 451, siman katan 27, in the name of Rivash, that for us we rule mitzvos require intent, as stated siman 60, seif 4 — to transgress a negative commandment also requires intent; and so too the beginning of Yoma in Eruvin — if so, Bach's words do not apply, and siman 34. Also in any case I did not understand his view, that lack of intent to transgress means from silence; but if he explicitly intends not to add, that also in such a case he transgresses a negative commandment — we have not heard this); and this is the view of the Maggid Mishneh in Shulchan Aruch, as Rama wrote — and this is the reason for their dispute. And thereby Magen Avraham's words in siman katan 9 will also be understood.
