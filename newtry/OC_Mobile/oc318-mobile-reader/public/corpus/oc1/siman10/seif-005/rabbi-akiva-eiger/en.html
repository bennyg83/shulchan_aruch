Seif 66 — He tied tzitzis on tzitzis. At first glance, Tosafos's difficulty at the beginning of [the chapter on] tekhelet from the sugya in Sanhedrin should apply — you find a zaken mamre in tying onto [strings] already tied. The Ra'avad's difficulty in his hasagah should also apply, for this reasoning of "this stands alone" ought to apply here. However, Tosafos's question is not so strong. One can say that tying onto [strings] already tied is not called "interpretation from scribal tradition," for interpretation explicitly stated in Scripture — "a tzitzis on the corners of their garments" — is as if explicitly stated for each corner one tzitzis. And only when adding threads to one tzitzis do we derive four threads from "twisted cord" (gadil), four twisted cords, and likewise four compartments of tefillin derived from "totafot," and likewise in the lulav "streambeds of water, two." That is called "interpretation from scribal tradition." But two tzitzis and two tefillin — one can say this is included in interpretation explicitly stated in Scripture. And at first glance it appears to me to resolve both difficulties above. One can say that what was difficult for the Rambam — Tosafos's question in Sanhedrin from the sugya in Sukkah that even outside the bound cluster they sit because of bal tosif — therefore the Rambam holds per the sugya in Zevachim 18b that they conclude not that everyone [holds] yeter ke-man she-eino dami. If so, one can say he relies on this: the reasoning "this stands alone" applies only if we say yeter ke-man she-eino; therefore the addition does not harm the body of the mitzvah. But if we say yeter ke-man she-yesho, it detracts from the mitzvah. If so, one can say R. Eliezer in Sanhedrin holds like the one who says in Zevachim, and holds like the one who says yeter ke-man she-eino. But per the conclusion in Zevachim, in truth in every matter it is detracting, and the sugya in Sukkah per the conclusion in Sanhedrin.
