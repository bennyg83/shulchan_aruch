Seif 6: He placed tzitzis upon existing tzitzis. At first glance, Tosafos' question at the beginning of the chapter HaTecheiles from the discussion in Sanhedrin is difficult: a rebellious elder can be found in a case where one added tzitzis to existing tzitzis. Ra'avad's objection in his gloss is also difficult, for the principle 'each stands independently' should apply here. Tosafos' question, however, is not so strong. One can say that adding tzitzis to existing tzitzis is not called a matter whose interpretation derives from the Sages, because its interpretation is explicit in Scripture: 'tzitzis upon the corners of their garments' explicitly means one set of tzitzis upon each corner. Adding threads to one set of tzitzis is different: the four threads are derived from 'a cord' (two) and 'cords'; four. The same applies to the four passages of tefillin derived from the word totafot, and to the two willow branches of the lulav derived from 'willows of the brook.' These are called matters interpreted by the Sages. Two sets of tzitzis and two pairs of tefillin, however, can be considered explicitly defined by Scripture. At first glance, both aforementioned questions can be resolved as follows. Rambam was troubled by Tosafos' question from Sanhedrin in light of Sukkah, which holds that bal tosif applies even when the added species is outside the binding. Rambam therefore follows Zevachim 18, whose conclusion is that all agree an extra item is considered present. The principle 'each stands independently' may apply only if an extra item is considered absent, so that the addition does not damage the mitzvah itself. If an extra item is considered present, it diminishes the mitzvah. R' Eliezer in Sanhedrin may follow the opinion in Zevachim that an extra item is considered absent; according to Zevachim's conclusion, however, every addition diminishes the mitzvah. The discussion in Sukkah would then follow Sanhedrin's conclusion. This is insufficient, however, because Rambam writes in Hilchot Mamrim that a rebellious elder is liable only regarding the mitzvah of tefillin. If Rambam held that Sanhedrin's discussion is rejected in the final halacha, then as a practical matter a rebellious elder could likewise be liable regarding tzitzis and lulav.
