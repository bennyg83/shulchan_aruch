"A sudar that people wear," etc.—exempt. See in Mishna Berurah; and the reason for this is written in Beit Yosef: the main bringing of those sudarim from the outset is to wrap them around the head from cold and to shade the head in heat, and also to cover the head with the garment's pieces when he goes up to read the Torah; therefore even though most of the day he wears it on the body, it is again considered a head-covering and exempt. One can also say that the main bringing of those sudarim is to use them for several things—to gird with them when doing any burdensome work, to wipe the hands, to bundle money, to carry fruit, to wipe sweat, etc.; even though one enjoys them in heat from the sun and in rain from rain, since their bringing is not for that, it is not considered and we do not read "with which you cover." For this last reason it is implied that Magen Avraham and Gra, who cited it in their explanations, agreed; and according to this it appears in our place, where it has spread anew also to raise like a large piece of an amah or more on the shoulders—there is none of these reasons, as is known to us, that obligates tzitzis; but for other reasons Beit Yosef brought—perhaps specifically when worn as clothing and not raised, or because it is only for honor and not for cold and heat—perhaps one can be lenient; but many disagree with these reasons, as explained in Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe; therefore one who is uncertain should make one corner round:
