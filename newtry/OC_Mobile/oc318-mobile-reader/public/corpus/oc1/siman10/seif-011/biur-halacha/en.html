The cloths that are worn, etc., are exempt. Beis Yosef explains that these cloths were originally brought to wrap around the head against cold, shade it from heat, and cover the head when called to the Torah. Even if worn on the body most of the day, they remain headwear and are exempt. Alternatively, their primary purpose is various utility: belting during carrying work, wiping hands or sweat, tying money, and carrying fruit. Although they incidentally protect from sun or rain, that is not why they are brought, so 'with which you cover yourself' does not apply. Magen Avraham and the Gra accept this latter rationale. In our locale a new practice has spread of placing a large cloth, an amah or more, over the shoulders. None of these rationales applies, so it is obligated in tzitzis. Other rationales in Beis Yosef may permit it; that Torah obligation requires dressing rather than draping, or that it is for honor rather than heat or cold; but many dispute these. A God-fearing person should round one corner.
