“They are exempt.” For the main purpose of putting on these garments is not for the benefit of covering but for use—to tie money in them (Beis Yosef). And he further wrote: one should examine our garments that have two corners near the neck and two near the ground—why do we not tie tzitzis on them, since we do not belt them with a sash? It appears to me: because only that which protects a person from heat and cold is called a garment, and a cloak one wears only for honor is exempt; alternatively, if they made tzitzis on it they would tear constantly from being trodden underfoot and would need constant repair every time, and one can say the Torah did not obligate thus—see Mishnah Berurah siman 21, end of quote. And Darkei Moshe wrote: on the first matter it is difficult—for then our tallisos that do not protect at all but only for going out with them should be exempt from tzitzis and we would bless a blessing in vain; and on the second matter it is difficult from the child with a tallis katan whose tzitzis dragged after him and they did not consider that he needed to repair them. It appears to me: since the mitzvah of tzitzis is that two [corners] be before him and two behind him, as it states in the Midrash, therefore people hold that the Torah obligated only in such a case; but in our garments where all four corners are before him and one cannot wear them properly alone, they are not obligated in tzitzis, and therefore they followed a lenient practice. And a garment called rak that has a square piece of cloth attached above, placed on his shoulder from behind, called kolner—plainly it is exempt, for on the upper side it has no corners—only the corners of the kolner that rest somewhat behind him—and these are not corners, since the two corners behind him are not aligned opposite the corners before him, as Beis Yosef wrote on this siman—end of Darkei Moshe. And because this halachah wavered in the hands of the geonim and they did not find a correct reason, one who fears Heaven should make one corner round, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the early elders—see siman 8.
