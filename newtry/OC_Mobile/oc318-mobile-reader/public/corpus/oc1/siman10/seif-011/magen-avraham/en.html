“They are exempt.” For the main wearing of these garments is not for covering benefit but for use—to tie money in them (Beis Yosef). He further wrote: our garments have two “wings” near the neck and two near the ground—why do we not tie tzitzis on them, since we do not belt them with a sash? It appears to me: they are not called a garment except what protects a person from heat and cold, and a cloak one wears only for honor is exempt; also, if we put tzitzis on them they would tear constantly from foot treading and need constant repair, and one can say the Torah did not obligate that—see Mishnah Berurah on this siman—end of quote. Darkei Moshe writes: on the first point it is hard—for then our tallises that do not protect at all but only for going out would be exempt from tzitzis and we would bless in vain; on the second point it is hard from the child with a tallis katan whose tzitzis dragged and they did not think he had to repair them. It appears to me: since the mitzvah of tzitzis is that two corners be before him and two behind, as the Midrash states, people therefore hold the Torah obligated only in such a layout; but in our garments all four corners are in front and one cannot wear them properly—they are not obligated in tzitzis, hence the lenient practice. A garment called “rak” with a square cloth attached above resting on the shoulder from behind, called “kolner,” is plainly exempt, for upward there are no corners—only the kolner corners resting somewhat behind him—and these are not “horns,” since the two rear corners are not aligned opposite the front corners, as Beis Yosef writes on this siman—end Darkei Moshe. Because this halachah wavered among the geonim and they found no solid reason, a God-fearing person should make one corner round, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the early elders—see siman 8.
