Study is also needed regarding what Levush himself wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 363: he wrote that wool sleeves that have knots or buttons called knepflach may be attached to linen buttonholes that have holes, and one inserts the knots or buttons into the holes; since they are loose, he can remove them with his finger and need not tear the holes, even though he leaves them attached always and does not remove them in dressing or undressing—end quote. And apparently the law of tzitzis is comparable to connection for kilayim; and so Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Sefer Zichronot that a belt and buttons are not considered closing. And what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahari"k that permanent lacing is considered closing—it appears this applies where one cannot remove them with a finger; and so it appears somewhat in Mahari"k, root 149, who compares it to sewing into a cloak; see there. And if Levush here refers to buttons called eyelets—in this it appears plainly there is no connection, since one can open them with a finger. Magen Avraham wrote that nevertheless it may be forbidden because of marit ayin. I say we should not innovate a prohibition that the ancients did not address. Also from Knesset HaGedolah and Sefer HaZichronot it is implied there is no prohibition in this at all.
