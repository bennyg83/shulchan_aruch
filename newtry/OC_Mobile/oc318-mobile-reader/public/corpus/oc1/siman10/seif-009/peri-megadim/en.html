He placed. See the Taz. He brought the Gemara in Menachos 42. The Rosh brought one wording of Rashi, and another wording: if he placed [tzitzis] on what already had tzitzis, it is valid with the later ones. Although when he made the later ones he had not nullified the first ones, and it turns out that those three are not tzitzis, for he does not need them, nevertheless when he made the fourth and nullified the first ones, we do not call those three later ones made and not from what is already made, as he explains, for with regard to do not add, it is not considered an act. The Rama and Beis Yosef were troubled as to what difference there is between the first wording and the second. The Taz explained that according to the first wording it is only when he cut the first ones, while according to the second, even if both remain, it is valid. One who examines the Beis Yosef will see that to add, certainly there is no logic to validate while both remain, only where he intended to nullify. Rashi explained so, for if not, there is no question at all, since placing tzitzis on what already had tzitzis is valid where both remain if he intended to nullify. On this he explained: and although he cut the first ones, and the same applies where he did not cut them at all. According to Rava, who was not concerned for Rav Pappa's challenge, this is so according to Rashi. Afterward the Taz explained the words of the alternative opinion in the gloss, which are the Tur and Ri; and one may also explain the words of the Rosh this way, that they ruled like Rava, and even to nullify it is not initially considered an act. He added seasoning to this, since one can picture a case where he transgresses do not add if he intends or does so without specification. But if he intends to nullify, he is not in violation of do not add, as Tosafot wrote in Menachos 40b, s.v. mimai, regarding that case in Eiruvin 8. It does not appear so from the Bach. The Taz holds that what the Rav wrote, when he cut the first ones, is specifically so, unlike the Magen Avraham, letter 9. The Magen Avraham already wondered about the Bach and the Taz in this, for it does not imply so in the Beis Yosef, as I wrote in Magen Avraham, letter 9, regarding this. The Taz explained the Rambam's words, which are the view of the Mechaber, as follows: The Gemara challenged Rava from Rabbi Zeira, who said that placing tzitzis on what already had tzitzis is valid, implying that made and not from what is already made does not invalidate. But Rava said that made and not from what is already made does invalidate. The case of Rabbi Zeira, which is a matter of the halacha of do not add, is not an act made in invalidity, in wonder. If so, the words of Rabbi Zeira are rejected. Rav Pappa strengthens the words of Rabbi Zeira, that Rabbi Zeira speaks where he intended to nullify the first ones; then the second ones were an act of validity. The Rambam ruled so, like Rav Pappa, and Rava in truth agrees to this. There is a slight omission in the wording, and it should read: and from this they challenged Rava from Rabbi Zeira, who permitted where [tzitzis] was placed on what already had tzitzis.
