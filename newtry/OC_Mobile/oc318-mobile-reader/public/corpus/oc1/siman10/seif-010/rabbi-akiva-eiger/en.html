There, in the continuation: 'Nevertheless it is forbidden because of marit ayin.' This is difficult for me. If so, it should likewise be forbidden because of marit ayin in Yoreh De'ah, siman 303. This is especially so because Levush writes that one should not place hooks below the belt even when the tallis is long and most of it remains open, because people may think most of it is closed merely because the hooks are below the belt. Thus we are concerned that a person might make two mistakes: he will think the hooks are bent closed and will think that most of the garment is closed by them. All the more so should hooks be forbidden regarding kilayim, where there is only one marit-ayin concern; that people will think the hooks are bent closed. This requires great further study.
