Taz, paragraph 9: The first set was nullified. This is contrary to Magen Avraham. The later set, having been attached while bal tosif applied, is treated as nonexistent until the other set is cut; it is then considered newly made. The first set, however, was initially valid, was invalidated by bal tosif, and afterward remains subject to ta'aseh velo min ha'asuy. Taz, paragraph 10, writes, "If he cut one," and derives this from the case in which a fifth fringe was attached. This is difficult: in the case of the fifth fringe, the original four were initially valid and then invalidated by the fifth. Cutting the fifth should therefore leave the original four invalid; alternatively, if he cuts one of the original four, the remaining three are invalid and the fifth is valid, since it was made while bal tosif applied. One might distinguish that only the fifth fringe was itself made invalidly, while the original four did not inherently violate bal tosif, unlike two complete sets at every corner, where the second set accompanies the first at every corner. Nevertheless, the Taz's inference would then validate only the fifth fringe if it remains, not the other fringes. A distinction is therefore necessary, and the inference is not conclusive. Magen Avraham in paragraph 2 follows his own view in paragraph 9, while the Taz remains difficult.
