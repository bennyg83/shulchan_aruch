{Rama: Taz, s.k. 9.} And the reason the first ones are annulled is not like Magen Avraham, s.k. 9—and the reason is: granted regarding the later ones we say that from when he made them there is bal tosif, and it is as though they were not made at all until the cutting, when they are made anew; not so the first ones, which were already made validly and afterward were invalidated on account of bal tosif—automatically they are afterward talui umechulal (suspended and void). And what Taz wrote in s.k. 10 to cut one—and likewise one could learn, etc.—requires examination, for there it is comparable to when the first ones remained, for they were initially made validly and invalidated by making the fifth; if so, when he cut the fifth, all are invalid—even if he cut one of four, at least three are invalid; only the fifth is valid because at the time of its making it was in bal tosif. And one must say that there is different, for only the fifth was made invalidly, but the others have no bal tosif invalidity at all; and here is different, for the second ones are with the first ones in every corner, unlike there, where the others were not made invalid, as Taz wrote there. Nevertheless, what Taz wrote in that passage "and likewise one could learn, etc." is not understood—for one cannot derive from this case except for the validity of the fifth if it remained, since initially it was made in bal tosif; unlike the rest of the tzitzis if they remained—if we sought to learn from this case, only the fifth would be invalid, but we are forced to distinguish as above, and in any case one should not learn from this case. And the words of Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, who also wrote like Taz—requires explanation—for he follows his approach in s.k. 9; but Taz's words require study, as we say—and see.
