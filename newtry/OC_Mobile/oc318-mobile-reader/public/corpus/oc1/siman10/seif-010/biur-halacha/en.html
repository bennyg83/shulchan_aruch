Since its primary purpose, etc. Artzos HaChaim conclusively obligates it when it actually covers the head and most of the body, based on several objections to Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch. They do not suffice. First, Sifrei excludes a head-cloth 'with which one does not cover his head and most of his body.' This plainly means it was not made to do so; its primary purpose is covering only the head. The Gra cites Sifrei without challenging Shulchan Aruch. Orchos Chaim in Behag's name similarly says only a garment encompassing most of a person's body is fit for tzitzis; meaning made for that purpose, unlike one made only for the head, as the Gra proves from clothing merchants. Orchos Chaim also says Sifrei excludes headwear from 'your garment,' for it is not clothing even if large enough for a minor's head and body. This does not imply actual use on an adult obligates it; throughout tzitzis law, a minor's head-and-body size is the garment measure, so only the ordinary case was stated. Artzos HaChaim also asks how Beis Yosef says Kol Bo exempts even actual head-and-body covering when Kol Bo obligates the western lands' head covering. Lechem Chamudos already asks this. Or Zarua correctly answers that Kol Bo means it is large enough to cover the body but is not actually used so; Darkei Moshe explicitly reads it so. Artzos HaChaim's objection that Shulchan Aruch expressly exempts even actual covering is no difficulty: that follows Beis Yosef's conclusion that Sephardim reject Kol Bo, and se'if 11 rules like them. One wishing to be stringent should round one corner.
