"Since its main purpose," etc. See in Mishna Berurah and Aruch HaShulchan: he decided that if one covers his head and most of his body with it, he is obligated in tzitzis, because of several difficulties raised against Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch; but in truth they are not sufficient to reject Shulchan Aruch's words, as I shall explain. (1) The difficulty is from what is stated in Sifrei [cited in Beit Yosef]: "with it" excludes a mafor with which one does not cover his head and most of his body—and it is simply the simple explanation that it is not made to cover head and most, for its main purpose is only to cover the head alone; and so it appears from Gra's explanation that he brought in his commentary on that Sifrei; nevertheless he did not challenge Shulchan Aruch on this. And what he further brought from Aruch HaShulchan, who wrote in the name of BaHaG that a mitznefet is not fit for tzitzis—for only a garment that covers most of a person's body is fit for tzitzis—here too the simple explanation is specifically if it was made from the outset to cover most of a person's body, unlike this, whose main purpose is to cover only the head, as Gra brought proof from clothing sellers; see there. And what he further brought from what Aruch HaShulchan wrote in Sifrei, that it excludes head-coverings from "your garment"—"this is not a garment," even though with it one can cover head and most of a child—it is exempt—end quote; and it is implied that if with it one covers the head and most of an adult, it is obligated—see there: this is no proof at all, for in all laws of tzitzis we do not find such a distinction; on the contrary, we find everywhere that this is the measure of a garment for tzitzis—and therefore it was unnecessary to teach us extra stringency; and that which it did not state plainly "head and most" is because it took the common case. Also what he challenged regarding what Beit Yosef wrote on the kufiya—that even if one covers head and most with it, it is exempt from the words of the kufiya itself cited in Beit Yosef, that it obligates the mitznefet of people of Western lands—and Levush Chadash already raised this difficulty; but in truth this too is not so, for the essence, as Eshel Avraham answered, is that it has enough to cover but he does not cover head and most—and so explicitly in Darkei Moshe's long comment per the words of the kufiya; see there. And Aruch HaShulchan rejected this in vain; and what Aruch HaShulchan challenged on this—that here in Shulchan Aruch he explained explicitly that even if one covers head and most it is exempt—it is simply that he wrote this per his conclusion in Beit Yosef, that Sephardim do not hold like the kufiya, and he rules like them, as he wrote in seif 1.
