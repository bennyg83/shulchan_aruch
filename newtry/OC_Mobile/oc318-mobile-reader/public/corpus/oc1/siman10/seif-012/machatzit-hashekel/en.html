(siman katan 12) Like sealed, etc. — and in Terumat HaDeshen, etc., 38 connections. His proof is from chalitzah, for they fasten the shoe by hooks and loops that are on one side of the shoe and on the other side there are eyelets and knots, and they insert the knots into the loops opposite them; and nevertheless they say in the Gemara that one must still wrap a strap around the leg to tie the strap. And the Rosh wrote that tying the strap is not sufficient, for we rule that a knot is not [considered] tying. And the a fortiori argument would be that he already inserted the knots into the loops — but necessarily this plugging does not help; therefore we require tying the strap, and the same is true regarding kilayim — nevertheless plugging. Rather, since we find in the Mishkan it is written "you shall bring the hooks into the loops and join the Tabernacle," etc. — behold, by hooks it is called a connection; and therefore one must distinguish: if they bend very much so that one cannot untie them except by an action without connection, as was in the Mishkan — but other than those that do not bend very much, nevertheless it is connection. And see there, siman 310, where Rama ruled like the above words of Terumat HaDeshen.

And it is possible, nevertheless, etc. — that is, it could be that one should compare regarding plugging and connection of tzitzis to kilayim; and if so, from the strict law it is permitted to make small hooks below the belt that do not bend greatly — nevertheless, it is possible that for tzitzis they were concerned for mar'is ayin and were stricter than for kilayim.

And so wrote Maharam's responsum, etc. — this has no relevance to our topic; it is a separate matter, and it seems one should note it — see above in Shulchan Aruch, seif 14.
