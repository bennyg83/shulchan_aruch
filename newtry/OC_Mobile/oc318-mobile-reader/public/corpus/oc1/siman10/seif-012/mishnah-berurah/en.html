Even though they have, etc. For the reason, see the Beit Yosef.
Since they are not, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote that one should examine our garments, which have two corners near the neck and two corners near the ground: why do we not place tzitzis on them? He offered several answers to this, and the Darchei Moshe rejected them and stated his own reason for the custom. Since the mitzvah of tzitzis is initially that he should turn two before him and two behind him, and our garments have all four corners before him and cannot be worn in another manner, they are not obligated in tzitzis. This is what the Rama wrote, "since their corners are not made, etc." Regarding a garment called a rok, to which a square piece of cloth is attached above and rests on his shoulders behind him, called a collar, it is obvious that it is exempt; on the upper side it has no corners except the corners of the collar that rest somewhat behind him, and these are not corners, since the two corners behind him are not aligned opposite the corners before him. End of the Darchei Moshe. The Magen Avraham wrote that since this law is uncertain in the hands of the Geonim and they did not find a proper reason, for there is also much to question regarding the reason of the Darchei Moshe, as the Pri Megadim wrote, therefore a G-d-fearing person should make one corner round, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the elders. This is also the opinion of the Pri Megadim and the other later authorities. This is especially so in our times, regarding those garments newly made open behind, the garment called a kapoteh, where it is common that much of it is open. According to the Darchei Moshe it is obligated in tzitzis, since the two corners behind it are aligned opposite the two corners before it. Therefore, one who wishes to exempt himself must at least make one of those four corners round, and it will be exempt from tzitzis, as above in seif 9; see there in Biur Halacha. It is better to round two more corners as well, so that only three remain, for at present it has six.
And two behind them. From this expression there arose an error among some people, who fold one part of the tallis very much until it becomes narrow and does not cover anything except the neck. Their intention is that two tzitzis should be literally behind them, on their shoulders. This is not the preferred manner, for if so there is no body covering here, only a head covering. It is better to follow the practice of the Togarmim, where the arm separates the middle of the tallis, in such a way that on each side one of the tzitzis is before him and part is behind him; the sides that are before and behind him are also called before him and behind him. Therefore, separation by the arm is sufficient.
