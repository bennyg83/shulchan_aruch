The weekly parashah. At least seven readers, and he says half Kaddish.
The portion of Chanukah. If it is the first day of Chanukah, he begins from And it was on the day that he finished. Levush. The maftir reads only one prince, that of that day alone. Likewise, on Rosh Chodesh that falls on Chanukah, the fourth reads only one prince of that day alone, namely the sixth day. And if Rosh Chodesh Teves is two days, then on the second day of Rosh Chodesh, which is the seventh of Chanukah, he reads only the seventh prince.
And he reads the haftarah of Rani Vesimchi. Because of the verse, I saw, and behold, an entire golden menorah.
They read the haftarah of Chanukah. This means that although generally the custom on a Shabbos with a groom is to set aside the haftarah of the week and read the haftarah of Sos Asis, which relates to a groom, as explained above at the end of siman 428, nevertheless on Shabbos Chanukah, whether on the first Shabbos or the second Shabbos, one does not set aside that of Chanukah, because publicizing the miracle is preferable. Now Magen Avraham brought that some say this applies only on the first Shabbos, but if a groom occurs on the second Shabbos, they read the haftarah of Sos Asis and do not read the Chanukah haftarah, since they already read the Chanukah haftarah on the first Shabbos. But Eliyah Rabbah wrote that from Rema it implies that both on the first Shabbos and on the second they read only that of Chanukah, and so is the practice.
