Paragraph 2: And on the second he reads, etc. In Tosafos, Sukkah 54b, beginning with the words and why, the frequent, etc., it is taught there that at the time of the offering of the daily offering they would say a song, and while saying the song they would sound nine blasts. If they offered a mussaf, they would also say the song of the mussaf, and they would add nine blasts for the song of the mussaf. Rav Acha taught: they sound for each and every mussaf. This means that if a Festival fell on Shabbos, so that it had the mussaf offerings of Shabbos and the mussaf offerings of the Festival, they would say the song of Shabbos for the mussaf offerings of Shabbos and sound, and the song of the Festival for the mussaf offerings of the Festival and sound. The Gemara says: an objection was raised, for we learned: when Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos, the song of Rosh Chodesh overrides the song of Shabbos. If it is so, let them say the song of Shabbos and of Rosh Chodesh. Rav Safra answers: what does overrides mean? It overrides by being first. That is, they say the song of Shabbos and of Rosh Chodesh, and what the teaching says, overrides, means that it overrides its priority, so the song of Rosh Chodesh precedes that of Shabbos. The Gemara asks: why? When there is something frequent and something not frequent, the frequent comes first. Rabbi Yochanan said: in order to know that Rosh Chodesh was established in its proper time. Rashi explains: we give it this importance as a recognition, so that they should recognize that it was clear to the court that they had sanctified it according to law, and no person's heart should hesitate about it, for most people did not see the renewal of the moon. The Gemara asks: but without this, does it not already have a recognition? For it was taught: the fats of the morning daily offering were placed, etc., and those of Rosh Chodesh were placed below the ledge of the altar and downward. Rabbi Yochanan said: this was in order to know that Rosh Chodesh was established in its proper time. Rashi explains: for this reason they arranged them higher than the other limbs, to show that they are important and to make known that Rosh Chodesh was established in its proper time. The Gemara answers: they make two recognitions, so that whoever did not see this one sees that one. Tosafos wrote on the Gemara's question, why, the frequent should come first, as follows: it could have asked this even without Rav Safra. Until here. On this, Magen Avraham finds difficulty, for we say that regarding overriding, we do not say that the frequent comes first. If so, without Rav Safra, who would have explained that what we learned, that the song of Rosh Chodesh overrides that of Shabbos, means that it overrides it entirely and that they do not say that of Shabbos, the question of the frequent comes first would not have been difficult, since the assumption would have been that they say only the song of one of them and the second is necessarily overridden entirely. In such a case we do not say that the frequent comes first. By contrast, after Rav Safra answers that overrides means it precedes, the question is proper: but the frequent comes first, for regarding precedence we follow frequency. Perforce one must say that Tosafos holds that even regarding overriding, the frequent comes first; and therefore it could properly have asked even without Rav Safra. I saw in Responsa Shev Yaakov, siman 22, that he wrote about this as follows: I do not see a refutation, etc., for if the non-frequent matter has no other superiority against it, certainly everyone agrees that the frequent comes first, etc. Why should we say that we leave the frequent? Only here, regarding Chanukah, one may say that there is a superiority of publicizing the miracle, and in that case one may properly say, etc. If so, since the questioner still did not know of any superiority of Rosh Chodesh over Shabbos until the answerer replied, in order to know that it was established, etc., Tosafos explained correctly that even without Rav Safra it could have asked: why should Rosh Chodesh override Shabbos, since Shabbos has the superiority of being frequent and Rosh Chodesh has no superiority over Shabbos? Certainly the frequent comes first even to override, etc. But regarding Chanukah, Chanukah also has a superiority, namely publicizing the miracle, and this is simple. Until here.
And so it is implied from the language of Tosafos, who wrote that without Rav Safra it could have said this. Meaning, it could have said: behold, the frequent comes first. For if the questioner did not know that Rosh Chodesh has a superiority, then even without the reasoning that Shabbos is frequent, he could have said: why should Shabbos be overridden more than Rosh Chodesh? What strength does this one have more than that one? Since even the first question of the questioner was only that two recognitions should not be necessary, even though regarding Chanukah we also make two recognitions to publicize the miracle, one through lighting the lamps and the second through Torah reading, nevertheless the assumption was that specifically regarding Chanukah, where through the two recognitions we do not override what is frequent, two recognitions are certainly preferable. By contrast, regarding Rosh Chodesh, where through the two recognitions they must override the rule that the frequent comes first, he did not conclude this until the answer was given to him that even in such a case we make two recognitions. According to this, without Rav Safra, when the assumption was that Shabbos is entirely overridden, one may say that the questioner also concluded that since there is no need to override, it is preferable to override that of Shabbos, even though it is frequent, because of Rosh Chodesh, which is to publicize that it was established, etc. For regarding overriding, the reasoning of publicity is stronger than the reasoning of frequency. And even though without this publicity and recognition are not needed because there is another recognition, nevertheless regarding overriding it is possible that even the questioner concluded that we make two recognitions. By contrast, after Rav Safra answered that it means precedence, and regarding precedence the reasoning of publicity is not so strong, the assumption was that at any rate we do not make two recognitions. Perforce, Tosafos holds that there is no distinction between overriding and preceding. They are both equal, and in both the frequent is preferable; and this is clear. Incidentally, I saw in the aforementioned responsum that he left the words of Tosafos and Rosh in need of examination according to what he wrote, that specifically regarding overriding publicity is preferable, but regarding preceding the frequent is preferable. If so, why does Rosh Chodesh precede Shabbos? Granted that regarding Rosh Chodesh there is publicity that it was established, etc.; nevertheless Shabbos, which is frequent, should have preceded. See there. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all. For regarding Chanukah, the reason that the frequent precedes is that in any event there is publicity of the miracle, only it is delayed until afterward, and we are not concerned. But there, regarding Rosh Chodesh, there is no publicity except through its preceding Shabbos. If they would put that of Shabbos first, it would not be known at all that Rosh Chodesh was established in its proper time. True, they would know that today is Rosh Chodesh, but they would still hesitate, as Rashi wrote. Through giving it the superiority of precedence it becomes publicized that it was clear to the court that they sanctified it according to law, as Rashi wrote. This is simple in my humble opinion.
