From the weekly parashah. The same applies if they want to read seven or more than seven readers in it; that too is permitted. The main point is that among all of them there should be no fewer than seven. When he finishes the parashah of the seventh, they place down the second Torah scroll, and they do not say Kaddish; rather, they lift the first scroll and roll it, open the second, and read from it. When they say Kaddish over the second, they place the third next to it.
The portion of Rosh Chodesh. Because the frequent comes first.
And he reads the haftarah of Rani Vesimchi. For the rule that the frequent comes first applies to precedence, but regarding overriding, publicizing the miracle is preferable. Also, since the final reading was from the portion of Chanukah, one should read the haftarah from the subject with which he concluded.
If Rosh Chodesh falls on a weekday. The order of Torah reading on Rosh Chodesh on a weekday is as follows: the kohen begins the parashah of the daily offering until one fourth of a hin; the levi reads until and its libation; the Yisrael reads until and its libations; and the fourth reads the portion of Chanukah. Pri Megadim.
And he read the fourth from the portion of Rosh Chodesh. The same applies if he erred and this occurred on Shabbos Chanukah.
He need not read more. For we do not take Chanukah into account, since Rosh Chodesh is primary. Even if they remembered at the beginning of the fourth reading, one should not say that the fourth reader himself should read from this Torah scroll the Chanukah portion before concluding the Rosh Chodesh portion, for that would be skipping, and one does not skip in the Torah between two subjects, as above in siman 144. Rather, once the fourth began reading the Rosh Chodesh portion, he should conclude and bless, and a fifth should come after him and read from the second Torah scroll, if they took it out, from the portion of Chanukah.
