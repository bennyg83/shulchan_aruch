(א) And if he erred and began, etc., he must stop, etc. — see Mishna Berurah in what we wrote in the name of Taz who disagrees and rules: since he began reading for Hanukkah the kohen must finish this Torah for Hanukkah; in Eliyah Rabbah he sides: if he has not yet read three verses for Hanukkah he stops in the middle and reads for Rosh Chodesh [if he read three verses he agrees with Taz the kohen blesses final blessing and others read for Rosh Chodesh and need not return to Hanukkah reading]; so he sides somewhat in Mor VeKetzia; nevertheless many Acharonim [Bigdei Yesha, Shulchan Shlomo, Penei Yitzchak siman 69, Dagul MeRevavah, Chayei Adam] — from them also Magen Avraham at end siman 140 — ruled like Taz: since he began for Hanukkah the kohen must finish this sefer Torah then read the three in another sefer for Rosh Chodesh (even though in this siman Magen Avraham explained Rama's reason — for halacha certainly his view as written there). Beginning is called from when he began to bless — i.e., began the blessing Baruch Atah; but if he did not begin to bless even though a scroll happened to be ready for Hanukkah — all agree not to be concerned; roll it and read in another scroll brought for Rosh Chodesh — no blemish to this scroll since they will read in it later [Acharonim]. On Rosh Chodesh Tevet falling on Shabbat requiring three scrolls — Hanukkah matters read in the third — as above; if they erred and read the second for Hanukkah and third for Rosh Chodesh — nevertheless read haftarah for Hanukkah because of publicizing the miracle [Chayei Adam]; similarly Peri Megadim sides at end of his words — see there; nevertheless b'dieved if they read Rosh Chodesh haftarah HaShamayim Kisai they fulfilled [there]. One who has Yom Tov Sheni of Hanukkah should not count per Hanukkah days but per month days — sometimes Rosh Chodesh Tevet is day one, sometimes two days.
