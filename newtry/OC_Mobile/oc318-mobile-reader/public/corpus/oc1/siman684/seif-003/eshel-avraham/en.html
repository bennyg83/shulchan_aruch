That of Chanukah. In Eretz Yisrael, where on the second day of Pesach, which is Chol Hamoed, they read three aliyos in the portion of An ox or a sheep, and the fourth in the mussaf of the day, and he erred and read the fourth also in the portion of An ox or a sheep. The people of the synagogue agreed that the fourth should also bless with intent to read the portion of the mussaf offerings in the second scroll, and so it was done. Afterward they raised an objection: do we not rule that one person may not read from two Torah scrolls because of the possible blemish to the first? Some of them say that the fourth should read only the portion of the mussaf offerings, and what remains in the portion of An ox or a sheep should not be read at all. Others say that they should add one more person to read the portion of the mussaf offerings. Now the author of Pri HaAdamah, part 2, section 6, initially inclined to say that they acted properly, since these are two subjects. See Maharikash to Orach Chayim, siman 114. However, he raised a difficulty with this: the portion of the mussaf offerings and the day are one subject. He brought proof for this from Kesef Mishneh, chapter 12 of the Laws of Prayer; see there. He likewise rejected the view of the aforementioned some say on the grounds that the obligation of the talmudic law is draw the ox, etc. The Talmud also asks, let us roll the Torah scroll, etc., and it needs the reason of possible blemish to the first scroll. It does not say that he should not read at all. See Rashdam, siman 14. However, there is room to challenge this ruling on the ground that reading the mussaf of the day is only the custom of Rav Amram; see the responsum in Yad, siman 269, see there. But in truth his words are difficult to sustain, for is it not talmudic law from that which is stated regarding And on the tenth of the month, that he reads it by heart? Perhaps specifically on Yom Kippur it is an obligation, though this is contrary to reasoning, for on the contrary, from the time that the Beis Hamikdash was destroyed, because of our many sins, there is certainly more reason to be obligated to read the portion of the offerings of the day; see there. In the end he concluded that it is better for the fourth reader to read and complete the obligation of the day in the first scroll, as long as he has not descended from the reader's platform, even though he already recited the concluding blessing; and he should not read from the second scroll. See there.
