(1) Due to Rach. Datdir before and because there is due to Hanukkah the publications of Nisa Liit Len in it because the day of the day will be read later as the day of the year:

(ב) If one erred and began [Chanukah reading before Rosh Chodesh]. So writes Abudraham: from "prevent" it implies that if he left it so he did not fulfill — he must place them in mitzvah order — his view is that for Chanukah and Rosh Chodesh too, if he preceded Chanukah he must return in the order Chazal said. Wondrous! First, this gemara he brings for tefillin is nowhere in the Talmud. Further, Beit Yosef siman 28: if one encountered head-tefillin first he passes over that mitzvah and puts arm-tefillin first; so Rosh and Rambam, unlike Raavad who wrote to put head first without passing over — all agree that if he already put head then arm he need not remove them and replace in order; Shulchan Aruch 25 likewise — not a great novelty that even after head then arm he need not remove; once they are on his head and arm b'dieved he fulfills tefillin — the reason in the gemara: "between your eyes" — while between your eyes let them be two; and so too Chanukah reading before Rosh Chodesh — b'dieved he fulfilled though he transgressed tadir kodem. This is proven in Kol HaTadir (Zevachim 91): if tadir and not-tadir and he preceded and slaughtered the not-tadir — do we say since he slaughtered he offers it, or perhaps he waits for its blood until he offers the tadir and then offers the not-tadir? If you thought that transgressing tadir kodem invalidates even b'dieved, why would the Talmud doubt what to do after this precedence — it would be totally invalid immediately upon transgressing tadir kodem? Rather certainly invalidation does not apply from this. Further proof from Zevachim: minchah prayer precedes musaf because minchah is more tadir; we hold siman 286 that if he preceded musaf prayer to minchah he fulfilled — only l'chatchila minchah comes first. From siman 108: one forgot minchah on Shabbat, prayed twice on Motzei Shabbat, made havdalah on the second not the first — the first did not count since he must pray the obligatory prayer first and must pray a third time — we do not say b'dieved suffices that he changed and prayed the makeup first; there it is different because the makeup prayer has no time at all that hour since he must pray the obligation of his hour — like praying a prayer not in its time; unlike musaf and minchah whose times are both present, only minchah has the advantage of tadir — I wrote this in siman 108. It appears to me that this Rama ruling is not solid reliance; since he opened b'dieved on the Chanukah portion we do not make him return to read Rosh Chodesh first; further there is a dispute whether he must return and bless on what he returns to read as noted siman 140 — how can we say he should stop and begin Rosh Chodesh reading and enter himself into a beracha doubt? Rather the main point is b'dieved the kohen reads the Chanukah portion and afterward others read Rosh Chodesh as above; so too Maharal R' Naphtali Katz in a responsum — only he came to analyze the gemara Abudraham brings; I told him it is not a general rule in the gemara as above and he regretted and said he relied on him thinking it was in the gemara but in truth it is not in the gemara. Written in Maharil responsum 280: once the chazan forgot and read the fourth [aliyah] on "and on your new months" and said Kaddish and read the fourth again the Chanukah portion — he ruled there one need not return and repeat Kaddish after Chanukah reading; what he wrote that the fourth read Chanukah again is already explained in Shulchan Aruch that one must read the fifth on the Chanukah portion.
