(6) [Seif 2] If one prays toward a direction among the other directions he should turn his face, etc. — That which is written "he should turn his face" and not that he should return his face entirely toward Eretz Yisrael — Magen Avraham wrote in se'if kaf-bet that it refers to a place where their custom is thus, or one sitting on a donkey; and similarly Razah ot bet. However, the Maamar ot bet explained that it refers to one who prays toward the north or south wind in order to fulfill their statement "one who wishes to become rich should face north, and one who wishes to become wise should face south" — therefore he warns that he should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael, etc., see there. But Eliyah Rabbah ot bet challenged this: if so, why did this rabbi write it in the gloss, when the author already wrote thus, see there. And similarly Nosei Keilim ot alef was not satisfied with the resolution of the Maamar cited, and wrote to resolve with another resolution: here it refers to one who has some reason that he cannot pray entirely toward Eretz Yisrael — for example, if the congregation already stood for prayer and he found no empty place where he could pray toward Eretz Yisrael, and the like; therefore Maran of blessed memory warns that at least he should turn toward Eretz Yisrael, for this thing he can easily do; but without a reason he must stand toward Eretz Yisrael; and if he wishes to become rich or wise he should turn his face to the north or south.
