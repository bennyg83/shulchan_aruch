(2) There: If he was standing in chutz la'aretz he should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael and also direct toward Jerusalem, etc. — It is implied that turning the face is only toward Eretz Yisrael, and Jerusalem etc. suffices with intent alone even though his face is not toward them; and so wrote the Prisha ot alef. But in Kol Bo it is implied that even one standing in chutz la'aretz must have his face toward the Mikdash, for he wrote: "And the one praying must direct his face opposite the Mikdash; how? If he was in chutz la'aretz — direct his face toward Jerusalem; if he was standing in Jerusalem — direct his face opposite the Mikdash and the Holy of Holies." End quote — it is implied that whoever prays in any place, his face must be toward the Mikdash; and what he wrote "if he was in chutz la'aretz, direct his face toward Jerusalem" means Jerusalem and the Beis HaMikdash; and what he did not explain he relied on the beginning of his words where he wrote "and the one praying must," etc.; and what the Gemara states there "if he was standing in chutz la'aretz he should direct his heart toward Eretz Yisrael," etc. — means his face and also his heart; and "Eretz Yisrael" is not precise but toward Eretz Yisrael and Jerusalem, etc., as stated above in the previous ot in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, see there. And similarly Eliyah Rabbah ot alef wrote that in Levush and Kol Bo it is implied that he holds there are two versions in the Gemara — direct the heart and stand with the face toward them, see there; and so is clear from the words of Chayei Adam klal 22 ot yud:
