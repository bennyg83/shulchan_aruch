Directions — meaning in a place where their custom is so, or one who sits on a donkey.

To the east. See Taz, who raised that if one forgot and prayed toward the north or south, he should turn his face toward the east; and if he prayed toward the west he should not turn his face to the side, for then his face would be toward the north or south. Nevertheless it seems he need not interrupt and uproot his feet in order to pray toward the east, for after the fact one may rely on the Divine Presence is everywhere — end quote. In responsa Yad Eliyahu siman 1 he disagrees and wrote that one must interrupt and go to pray toward the east, and one great authority agreed with him. But another great authority agreed with Taz — see there at length. In this synagogue they placed the aron kodesh toward the south of the world and all pray toward the aron kodesh, which is toward the south — one may pray on the east side even though the whole congregation prays toward the south, and there is no issue of yohara or hatred — Yad Eliyahu siman 1. And one great authority there disagrees with him — he should only pray toward the side where the congregation prays — see there.

The ark. See Levush, the diagram to establish an eastern wall opposite the southeast corner of the world. And Knesset HaGedolah wrote that even if the ark is fixed toward another direction, one must turn his face to the east — see in the seif katan before this.

To the east. And Rashi explains the opposite — that he turns his face to the north or south, but his primary standing is toward the east. And it seems more correct to do as Taz.
