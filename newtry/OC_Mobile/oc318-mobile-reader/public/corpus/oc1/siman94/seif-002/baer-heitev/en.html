Directions. Meaning, in a place where this is their custom, or when he is sitting upon a donkey.
Eastward. See Taz, who concludes that if one forgot and prayed toward the north or south, he should turn his face eastward. If he prayed toward the west, he should not turn his face sideways, for then his face would be toward the north or south. Nevertheless, it appears that he need not interrupt and move his feet in order to pray eastward, since after the fact one may rely upon the opinion that the Shechinah is everywhere. Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 1, disagrees and writes that he must interrupt and go pray toward the east, and one great authority agreed with him. Another great authority, however, agreed with the Taz; see there at length. In a synagogue where the Aron Kodesh was placed on the southern side of the world and everyone prays toward it, one may pray facing east even though the congregation faces south, and there is no concern of arrogance or enmity. Yad Eliyahu, siman 1. One great authority there disagrees and rules that he should pray in the direction in which the congregation prays; see there.
The Aron. See the Levush for the diagram showing that the eastern wall should face the southeastern corner of the world. Knesses HaGedolah writes that even if the Aron is fixed in another direction, one must turn his face eastward. See the preceding seif katan.
Eastward. Rashi explains the reverse, that one should angle his face northward or southward while his principal stance remains eastward. It appears more correct to do so. Taz.
