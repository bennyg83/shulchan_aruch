One should not make the place of the Aron, etc., but should align it toward midday. See Mishnah Berurah. Here is the wording of the Levush: Know that all these lands in which we dwell lie to the northwest of Eretz Yisrael and are not situated directly west of Eretz Yisrael. Therefore, it appears to me proper and correct that when constructing a synagogue, care should be taken that the eastern wall, where the Aron is placed and toward which they pray, should incline somewhat toward the southeast. We will then stand aligned toward Eretz Yisrael, Jerusalem, the Beis HaMikdash, and the Holy of Holies. Through this we will also not strengthen the claims of the sectarians, who say that we pray toward the sun as they do. How should it be positioned in these lands? It appears to me that on the day of the spring or autumn equinox, or within six or seven days thereof, when the sun rises in the morning and enters through the window at the center of the synagogue’s eastern wall, its ray should strike the western wall south of its midpoint, at such a distance that approximately half an hour or an hour after sunrise it reaches the exact center of the wall opposite the eastern window. A synagogue positioned this way certainly faces Jerusalem and the Beis HaMikdash from these lands. If, however, it is positioned so that on those days the sun’s ray strikes the center of the western wall directly opposite the window immediately at sunrise, it faces due east. This is their practice, and we would also be bowing beside Jerusalem and the Beis HaMikdash rather than toward them. A synagogue should therefore not be positioned this way. These are his words. See there further. Bach writes that all his words are included in the gloss of the Shulchan Aruch, which says to align it toward midday. This is also implied by the Derishah and Machatzis HaShekel.
