And we return and so on. Even the feeding on another side will pray to the east, the great Knesset:
[3] Opposite sunrise, etc. See the Levush’s diagram showing that the eastern wall should face the southeastern corner of the world, which is proper, and all the later authorities agree. Lechem Chamudos, folio 36, writes: I customarily angle myself southeastward unless I am praying while standing on the southern side of the Aron, for then I would appear to turn my back toward the Aron. In that case I turn my face only eastward, and it suffices that when bowing I incline slightly southward. This follows our custom, according to which the synagogue’s eastern side faces due east. According to the Levush, whose view we follow, that the eastern side should face southeastward, no further angling is needed. On this basis I answered one who asked where it is preferable to establish the rabbi’s seat in a new synagogue, south or north of the Aron Kodesh. I later saw in Sefer Chasidim, siman 761, that the righteous person should be seated to the right of the Aron, unless refraining from insistence will prevent dispute. Taz writes that one who forgot and is praying toward the north or south should turn his face eastward. If he is praying toward the west, he should not turn his face sideways, for then he will face north or south. Nevertheless, he need not interrupt and move his feet in order to pray eastward, since after the fact we rely upon the opinion that the Shechinah is everywhere. This is also evident from the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah. It appears to me preferable that he turn his face southward, for this too is toward Eretz Yisrael.
[4] He should angle his face, etc. It is likewise proper to do the reverse, standing toward the east and angling his face southward. Magen Avraham. I found, however, in the Derishah that this is preferable, because the verse says, “My eyes and My heart shall be there,” not that his feet must be positioned there.
