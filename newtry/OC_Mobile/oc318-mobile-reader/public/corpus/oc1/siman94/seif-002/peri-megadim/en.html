Opposite. See Taz, that a synagogue should not be positioned facing due east, but should incline somewhat southward. According to this, the rabbi’s place in the synagogue should be south of the Aron, and he need not turn southward because he already faces that direction. If the synagogue faces due east, it is preferable to place the rabbi north of the Aron so that he turns southward and becomes wise. If he sits south of it, he appears to turn his back upon the Aron Kodesh. Furthermore, one who constructs according to the Levush cannot turn northward, for he would appear to turn his face away from Eretz Yisrael, and certainly not directly north. See Eliyah Rabbah, Taz, Etz Chaim, and Sefer Chasidim, siman 768, that the righteous person’s place should be south of the Aron Kodesh, as cited by Eliyah Rabbah.
