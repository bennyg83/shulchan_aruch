Toward any other direction. Beit Yosef cites Rabbi Avraham ben Chaim as follows: It is difficult regarding our practice of praying toward the north or south, for we should pray only eastward. It appears that when praying toward the north or south we should turn our faces eastward, as stated in Bava Basra, chapter Lo Yachpor, that one should angle himself. Meaning, one who prays toward the north or south should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael. It appears to me that this correction of turning the face is effective only when praying northward or southward, because one can turn his face eastward. If one prays westward, however, turning his face eastward is ineffective, since he cannot turn his face completely behind him toward the east. The Gemara speaks only of angling sideways, not backward. Rabbi Yosef Abuhav therefore mentioned only one praying northward or southward, not westward. Although the Shulchan Aruch writes generally, “toward any other direction,” he does not speak specifically of those who dwell west of Eretz Yisrael and must pray eastward, as we do. Rather, he writes generally that one should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael from every side on which people dwell relative to it. He therefore also writes generally that one may angle his face toward Eretz Yisrael in whatever manner is possible. Reason itself requires this. The practical consequence is that one who forgot and is praying in another direction should, if facing north or south, turn his face eastward. If he forgot and prayed westward, he should not turn his face sideways, for then he would face north or south. Nevertheless, it appears that he need not interrupt and move his feet in order to pray eastward, since the Gemara in Bava Basra records an opinion that the Shechinah is everywhere, and after the fact he may rely upon it. Nor is there reason here to call him wicked, because others are not praying eastward at that time.
Opposite sunrise. The Levush writes that these lands lie northwest of Eretz Yisrael. Therefore, when constructing a synagogue, care must be taken that the eastern wall containing the Aron should incline southeastward. One then turns his face toward Eretz Yisrael, the Beis HaMikdash, and the Holy of Holies. If the eastern wall is positioned directly toward sunrise, there are two disadvantages: first, one does not face Eretz Yisrael at all; second, this is the practice of the sectarians, who bow to the sun. The eastern wall should therefore be positioned toward the southeast. He discusses this at length.
Nevertheless, he should angle, etc. One may also do the reverse, praying eastward while angling his face northward or southward. This is mentioned in Rashi’s explanation cited by Beit Yosef. It appears more correct to do so, for if he prays northward or southward he appears somewhat separate from the congregation, whereas he does not appear so when acting as described.
