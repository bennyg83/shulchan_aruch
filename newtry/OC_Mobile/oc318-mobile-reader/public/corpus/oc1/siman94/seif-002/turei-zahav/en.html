To the direction of the other directions. In Beit Yosef he brings in the name of Avudraham and this is his language: I wonder at the custom that we practice to face north and south, whereas we ought to pray only toward the east; and it seems we should turn our faces toward the east side when we pray toward north and south, as they say in the chapter Lo Yachpor: mitzadad etzladudei — meaning one who prays toward north and south should turn his face toward the side of Eretz Yisrael, end quote; and it seems to me this correction of turning the face is effective only for one praying toward north or south, for it is possible by turning the face toward the east; but one who prays toward the west, turning the face toward the east does not help him, for he cannot actually turn his face behind him toward the east; and they said in the Gemara only mitzadad etzladudei — he tilts to the side but not behind him; and therefore Mahari Abuhav mentioned only praying toward north and south but not toward the west; and although Shulchan Aruch here wrote generically toward the direction of the other directions, he is not dealing specifically with those who sit in the west of Eretz Yisrael who need to pray toward the east as we do, but rather wrote as a general rule that one must turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael for every direction whose inhabitants are opposite Eretz Yisrael; and therefore he also wrote here as a general rule for every direction where tilting his face toward the side of Eretz Yisrael is possible in the feasible way; and this is something reason requires; and we derive for one who forgot and prayed toward another direction: if he is in the north or south he should turn his face toward the east; but if he forgot and prayed toward the west, it seems he should not turn his face to the side, for then his face would be toward north or south; and nevertheless it seems he need not stop and uproot his feet to pray toward the east, for they say in the Gemara in chapter Lo Yachpor that some hold the Divine Presence is everywhere, and bedi'eved he may rely on this; and it is not applicable here to say as Bar Kappara etc., since others are not praying at that hour toward the east, as above.

Against the sunrise of the sun — it is written in the Levush that these lands are opposite the northwest of Eretz Yisrael; therefore one must be careful when building a synagogue that they place the eastern wall in which the ark tilts somewhat toward the southeast side, for now he turns his face toward Eretz Yisrael and the Temple and Holy of Holies; but if they place the eastern wall exactly toward the sunrise of the sun, there are two difficulties: one, that he does not turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael at all; the other, that this is the practice of heretics who bow to the sun; therefore they should place the eastern wall toward the southeast as such, and he elaborated on this.

Nevertheless he should turn sideways, etc. — and one can also do the reverse, namely that he prays toward the east and turns his face toward north or south; and this is mentioned in Rashi's explanation that Beit Yosef brings; and it seems more correct to do so, for if he prays toward north or south he will appear somewhat separate from the congregation, which is not so in the way I mentioned.
