He was riding on a donkey, etc., and they told him "sit in your place and pray" — See Mishna Berurah who wrote that nevertheless he should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael if possible. I brought out the words of Magen Avraham in se'if kaf-bet, who wrote regarding one riding on a donkey that he must turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael; and it is obvious that the same applies to one sitting on a ship — and all the more so here, where Shulchan Aruch is stringent lechatchilah that if he can stand he should not pray sitting. And here I saw in Tosefes Yom Tov chapter 4 of Berachos, mishnah 6, who wrote regarding "if he was sitting on a ship" — therefore the mishnah did not teach "he should turn his face," because he does not even need to stand but sits and prays, as Tur wrote in siman 94; and he meant that regarding one riding on a donkey, where the mishnah interrupted that lechatchilah he must dismount — therefore even if he cannot dismount, at least he should turn his face; whereas regarding one sitting on a ship, where it is permitted lechatchilah to pray sitting, therefore he need not turn his face. And his words are wondrous. One of them: how will he answer Rambam, who holds regarding one sitting on a ship that if he can stand he must stand? And further, according to his words regarding one riding on a donkey according to Rabbi, who holds he need not dismount but sits in his place — he also need not turn his face; yet Rokeach explicitly wrote the opposite and Beit Yosef brought it. And from this certainly flow the words of Magen Avraham in se'if kaf-bet. But certainly turning the face does not depend on sitting; and that the mishnah did not teach "he should turn his face" regarding a ship — perhaps it relied on the beginning [of the halacha], and so too Gra in both [cases] that he should turn his face. I reconsidered again: one can resolve the words of Tosefes Yom Tov, who holds that in what we are lenient regarding sitting we should not be stringent regarding turning the face [for prayer sitting compared to standing is as if it does not exist, as is proven from se'if 9; and without turning the face we find no opinion for invalidation in this]. And from Rokeach there is no proof, for perhaps he holds like Rabbeinu Yonah that even according to Rabbi one must also stop the animal so it stands in its place during prayer; therefore we are also stringent regarding turning the face. Whereas on a ship, which is moving — therefore according to us, who rule regarding one riding on a donkey that he need not delay during prayer, the same applies that he need not turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael, and not like Magen Avraham in se'if kaf-bet; and it requires study.
