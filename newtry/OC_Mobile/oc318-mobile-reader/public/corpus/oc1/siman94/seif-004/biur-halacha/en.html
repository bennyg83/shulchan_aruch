If he was riding upon a donkey, etc., and if not, he should remain seated in his place and pray. See Mishnah Berurah, which writes that nevertheless he should turn his face toward Eretz Yisrael if possible. I derived this from Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, who writes regarding one riding a donkey that he must angle his face toward Eretz Yisrael. Plainly the same applies to one seated in a ship, and all the more so, since there the Shulchan Aruch is stringent ab initio that if he can stand he should not pray while seated. I saw, however, that Tosafos Yom Tov on Berachos 4:6 writes that the Mishnah does not teach that one seated in a ship should turn his face because he need not even stand, but may sit and pray, as the Tur writes in siman 94. His meaning is that regarding one riding a donkey, since the Mishnah rules that ab initio he should descend, even when he cannot descend he should at least turn his face; but one seated in a ship may ab initio pray while seated, and therefore need not turn his face. His words are astonishing. First, what will he answer according to the Rambam, who holds that one seated in a ship must stand if he can? Further, according to his reasoning, one riding a donkey according to Rabbi, who holds that he need not descend but may remain seated, should likewise not need to turn his face, yet the Rokeach explicitly writes the opposite, as cited by Beit Yosef, and this is certainly the source of Magen Avraham’s words. Rather, turning one’s face is not dependent upon sitting. The Mishnah’s failure to state this regarding a ship may be because it relied upon the first clause. Shulchan Aruch HaRav likewise rules in both cases that he should turn his face. Upon further reflection, the Tosafos Yom Tov may be defended. He may hold that where we are lenient to permit sitting, we should not be stringent regarding turning the face, since prayer while seated in comparison with standing is as though it were absent, as is evident from seif 9, whereas failure to turn one’s face is not deemed indispensable according to any opinion. The Rokeach is no proof, for perhaps he agrees with Rabbeinu Yonah that even according to Rabbi the rider must at least stop the animal during prayer; we therefore also require him to turn his face. A ship, however, continues moving. According to this, since we rule that one riding a donkey need not stop during prayer, he likewise need not angle his face toward Eretz Yisrael, contrary to Magen Avraham in seif katan 2. This requires study.
