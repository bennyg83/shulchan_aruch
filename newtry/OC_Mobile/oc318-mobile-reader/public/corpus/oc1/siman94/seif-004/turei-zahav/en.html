Likewise, if he was in a ship, etc. Meaning, in a wagon or ship he can stand, because a wagon does not move so rapidly and a ship is broad, so there is no fear of the water while standing inside it. One might ask what standing in a wagon or ship accomplishes, since the wagon or ship moves and he should be considered as walking, just as one riding is considered walking. It cannot be answered that sitting in a wagon or ship differs from riding, for Bava Metzia, chapter 1, states regarding acquisition by a moving courtyard that a ship is not considered a moving courtyard because the ship rests while the water moves beneath it. Tosafos ask why this differs from riding, which is considered movement, and answer that for all other matters a ship is indeed considered moving; only regarding acquisition is it not called moving, because it is like his hand, which rests while the body carries it. It therefore remains difficult why standing in a wagon or ship helps. One must answer that regarding prayer the Sages required standing only so that awe of the Shechinah should be upon him. For this purpose, standing in a wagon or ship suffices, even if because of their movement it is not technically considered standing.
