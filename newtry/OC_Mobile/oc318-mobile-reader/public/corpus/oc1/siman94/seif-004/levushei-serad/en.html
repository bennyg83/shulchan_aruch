(Same) Rav Ashi prayed with the congregation. On Shabbat, his custom was to preach at dawn and he would not pray before the sermon but in the middle of the sermon; and when the time for Shema arrived he would whisper much to the translator, and while they were translating for the multitude he would read Shema and pray while seated — because he did not wish to leave so as not to trouble the congregation to rise before him; and because he prayed while seated, afterward he would stand and pray in his house. And the Rabbis said to Rav Ashi: Let the master act like the father of Shmuel and not pray in his house standing, and you will not need two prayers. He said to them: I did not see the elder rabbis from whom [I learned] act thus, to precede prayer to Shema; and since he waits for prayer until the time of Shema, it is a mitzvah to pray after Shema in order to adjoin redemption to prayer; and because he prayed while seated, he needed to return and pray a voluntary prayer:
