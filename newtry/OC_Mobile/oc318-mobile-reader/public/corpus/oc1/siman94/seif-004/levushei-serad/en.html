(Ibid.) Rav Ashi prayed together with the congregation. On the Shabbos of the public lecture he would expound at dawn and would not pray before the lecture, but during it. When the time for Krias Shema arrived, he would whisper at length to the interpreter, and while the interpreter conveyed his words to the public, Rav Ashi would recite Krias Shema and pray while seated. He did not wish to leave, so as not to burden the congregation to rise before him. Because he had prayed while seated, he would later pray again while standing at home. The Sages said to Rav Ashi: Let the master act like the father of Shmuel and Levi, and then he would not pray at home while standing and would not require two prayers. He answered: I have not seen the elder Sages among us act in that manner, by advancing prayer before Krias Shema. Since he waited to pray until the time of Krias Shema, it was a mitzvah to pray after Krias Shema in order to juxtapose redemption to prayer. Because he prayed while seated, he had to return and pray as a voluntary prayer.
