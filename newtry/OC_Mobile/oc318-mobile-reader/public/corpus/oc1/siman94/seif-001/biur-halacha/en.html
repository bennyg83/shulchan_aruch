If one was standing behind the Kapores, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, which explains that this means west of the Beis HaMikdash, as Magen Avraham explains. At first glance, why did he not explain like Rashi, that it means within the eleven cubits between the western wall and the rear of the Kapores? It appears to me that Magen Avraham found it difficult why the Shulchan Aruch did not state, “If he was standing in the Mikdash, he should direct his heart toward the Holy of Holies,” as the Gemara states. Necessarily, this is because nowadays it is forbidden to enter that place, since we are all impure through contact with the dead, as explained below in siman 561. The same applies here. He therefore explains that the Shulchan Aruch includes the entire side opposite the west of the Beis HaMikdash. Although this is seemingly already included in the earlier statement that one standing in Jerusalem should turn his face toward the Mikdash, which presumably includes all its directions, one might have thought that since one opinion in the Gemara holds that the Shechinah is in the west, it is improper to stand with one’s back to the west and bow toward the east, and that the instruction to turn his face applies only when he stands east, north, or south of it. The Shulchan Aruch therefore teaches that we do not rule so.
