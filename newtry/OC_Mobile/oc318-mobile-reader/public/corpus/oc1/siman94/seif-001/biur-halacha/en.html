He was standing behind the Kappores, etc. — See Mishna Berurah, that is, in the west of the Beis HaMikdash; thus explained Magen Avraham. And apparently, why did he not explain like Rashi, that is, in the eleven amos between the western wall and behind the Kappores? And it appears to my humble mind that it was difficult for Magen Avraham why Shulchan Aruch did not write "he was standing in the Temple, he should direct toward the Holy of Holies," as it states in the Gemara; and therefore, because now it is forbidden to enter that place, since we are all tamei mes, and as will be explained below in siman 561, so too here; and therefore he explained that Shulchan Aruch includes here the entire side that is west of the Beis HaMikdash. And although this would apparently be included in what he said at first, "he was standing in Jerusalem, he should turn his face toward the Mikdash" — which includes, in his words from the inference, toward all his directions — one could say that the reasoning is: since the Gemara says that the Shechinah is in the west, it is not fitting to stand with one's back to the west and prostrate toward the east; and therefore "he should turn his face" refers only if he is standing in the east, north, or south, teaching us that we do not rule this way.
