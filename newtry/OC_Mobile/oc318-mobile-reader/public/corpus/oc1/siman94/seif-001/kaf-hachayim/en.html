(1) [Seif 1] When he rises to pray, if he was standing outside the land, etc. — And so it is in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar page 30a: Our Rabbis taught: A blind person and one who cannot determine the directions should direct his heart toward his Father in heaven, as it is said "and they shall pray to the Omnipresent." If he was standing in chutz la'aretz he should direct his heart (Tosafos wrote that "his heart" is not in the text — see Bach) toward Eretz Yisrael, as it is said "and they shall pray to You toward their land." If he was standing in Eretz Yisrael he should direct his heart toward Jerusalem, as it is said "and they shall pray to the Omnipresent toward the city that You chose." If he was standing in Jerusalem he should direct his heart toward the Beis HaMikdash, as it is said "and they shall pray toward this house." If he was standing in the Beis HaMikdash he should direct his heart toward the Holy of Holies, as it is said "and they shall pray toward this place." If he was standing behind the Beis HaKappores he should see himself as if before the Kappores. It follows: standing in the east he turns his face to the west; in the west he turns his face to the east; in the south he turns his face to the north; in the north he turns his face to the south — thus all Israel direct their hearts to one place. R' Avin — and some say Ravina — what verse [supports this]? "Like the tower of David is your neck, built for turrets" — a mound to which all turn. End quote. And Tosafos wrote that this is difficult for me; and so we conclude in Bava Basra page 25b; and similarly Tur and Beit Yosef. And Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar: If he was standing in chutz la'aretz he should direct his heart toward Eretz Yisrael — do not say only toward Eretz Yisrael, but toward Eretz Yisrael and toward Jerusalem and toward the Mikdash and toward the Holy of Holies; and when standing in Jerusalem he need not direct toward Jerusalem, for he is already standing within it, but he should direct toward the Beis HaMikdash and toward the Holy of Holies. End of his words; and Maran of blessed memory brought it in Beit Yosef, and such are his words here in Shulchan Aruch. And the reason for all of them is because there is the gate of heaven through which all prayers ascend, as Chayei Adam wrote klal 22 ot yud; and see in Sifrei, book Devarim, on the parsha "You ascended the head of the peak," which they expounded from here — "they said those standing in chutz la'aretz," etc. — and this entire baraisa of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar, see there. And Rav Sifrei Devei Rav wrote that "from here they said" — meaning that apparently we did not know the reason what turning the face toward Eretz Yisrael adds when he stands outside it; but from "You ascended the head of the peak" we learned the reason: just as Moshe Rabbeinu, although he was in chutz la'aretz, his gaze from afar was effective to bring down influence and blessing to Eretz Yisrael — so too those standing in chutz la'aretz when they pray must turn their faces toward Eretz Yisrael, for influence does not descend from heaven except to Eretz Yisrael, and from there it can spread outside, as it is written "a land which the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent inquires after," etc., and our Rabbis expounded: through the inquiry He inquires after the other lands with it; and although this pray-er does not see Eretz Yisrael with his eyes, nevertheless through intent of his heart and turning his face toward it, it helps him somewhat, see there. And Rav Minchas Aharon of blessed memory, klal 15 ot alef, wrote: since thought disqualifies outside its time and outside its place, they enacted to turn his face so that it be for him a sign and he fulfill "I have set the Omnipresent before me always," see there.
