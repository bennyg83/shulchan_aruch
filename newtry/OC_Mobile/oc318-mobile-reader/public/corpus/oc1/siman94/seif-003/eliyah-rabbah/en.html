Toward sunrise, etc. — See Levush on the diagram to position the eastern wall toward the southeastern corner of the world, which is good; and so agreed all the Acharonim. And Lechem Chamudos page 36 wrote: I am accustomed to turn toward the southeast unless I am praying and standing on the south side of the Ark — for then it would appear as turning the back of the neck toward the Ark; then I turn my face only to the east, and it suffices then when prostrating that I incline slightly to the south — until here. And this is according to our custom that he wrote there, that the east of the synagogue is toward the east alone; but per the explanation of the Levush that we rule like him, that they should make east toward the southeast — then one need not turn at all. And based on this I answered those who asked me in a new synagogue how it is best to establish the rabbi's place in the east — whether on the south side of the Holy Ark or on the north side. I again saw in Sefer Chasidim siman 961 that it is fitting to seat the tzaddik to the right of the Ark; if not, to remove dispute, he should not be particular. Taz wrote: One who forgot and prayed toward the north or south should turn his face toward the east; but if he prayed toward the west he should not turn his face to the side, for then he would be toward the north or south; and in any case one need not say to interrupt and uproot his feet in order to pray toward the east, for we rely bedieved on the one who says the Shechinah is in every place — until here; and so is explained in Rambam at the beginning of chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah.
