According to what I wrote in letter 4 and in Magen Avraham, letter 14, the Mechaber’s ruling may distinguish between one who prayed while walking, who need not repeat, and one who prayed while seated, who must repeat. Rav Ashi followed the first Tanna. In practice, Magen Avraham, Taz, and Peri Chadash agree that even one who prayed while seated has fulfilled his obligation after the fact. The correct text should read “and not with a vav.” Indeed, the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 1, implies that none of the eight matters, including standing, prevents fulfillment after the fact; see there. Knesses HaGedolah writes likewise in the name of Rema MiPano, as cited by Peri Chadash. According to the Mechaber, “when he is able” means either before the prayer time has passed or by making it up in the adjoining prayer, since he is considered one prevented by circumstances beyond his control.
What the Rama writes. In the gloss to seif 6, that he should contemplate the prayer in his heart, it may be that after the fact he fulfills his obligation because he is prevented by circumstances beyond his control. See Peri Chadash. There is no need to elaborate.
