(7) There: He should turn his face, etc. — Taz wrote in se'if kuf-alef that this correction helps only for one praying toward north or south; but one who forgot and prayed toward the west — it appears he should not turn his face to the side, for then his face would be toward north or south; and in any case it appears he need not interrupt and uproot his feet in order to pray toward the east, for we say in the chapter Lo Yachpor that there is one who holds the Shechinah is in every place, and bedieved one may rely on this; and it is not applicable here to say "like a bar kiyama," etc., since others are not praying at that hour toward the east. End of his words; and Bach and Shulchan Aruch HaRav ot bet, Razah ot gimmel, Chayei Adam klal 22 ot yud. However, Eliyah Rabbah ot bet wrote that it is good to turn his face to the south, for that too is toward Eretz Yisrael; and Rav the author of Yad Eliyahu siman 1 wrote that he must interrupt and go to pray toward the east, and one great authority agreed with him — except that one great authority agreed with the words of Taz, see there and understand. And they brought this in Hagahos Beir Yitzchak, Shitah Mekubetzes there 109b; but Rav Maamar ot bet wrote that Taz's ruling is not decisive, for one may well say that whenever it is possible to correct we correct, and he must turn himself and uproot his feet somewhat; and he wrote: see below siman 104 regarding the law of walking while praying, which implies that whatever is for the need is permitted to uproot his feet — and all the more so in our case, where he almost does not move from his place. End of his words. And see in the Tikkunim, Tikkun 21, who wrote: "And because of this it is forbidden to pray toward the west, for it is the back; because there are other gods," etc., see there; and Rokeach brought it, see there. And that those in the east of Eretz Yisrael pray toward the west — because opposite them are the Mikdash and Holy of Holies where the Shechinah rests until now, as Zohar parshas Shemos page 5b wrote that the Shechinah never moved from the western wall of the Beis HaMikdash, see there; and similarly in Midrash Shemos Rabbah chapter 2, see there. But those sitting in the west of Eretz Yisrael are forbidden to pray toward the west, as written in the Tikkunim; and if it happened that he forgot and prayed toward the west, he must return his face to the east, and there is no interruption in this since it is for the need, as written in siman 104 — only if in the north or south there is a side toward Eretz Yisrael, he should return his face there and need not return specifically to the east, as Eliyah Rabbah of blessed memory wrote; and if he did not remember until he finished his prayer, it appears he need not return, as Rambam wrote in chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah:
