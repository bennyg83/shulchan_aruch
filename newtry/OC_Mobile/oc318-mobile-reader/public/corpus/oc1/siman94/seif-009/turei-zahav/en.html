He must repeat the prayer, etc. Beit Yosef derived this from Tosafos in Berachos, chapter Tefillas HaShachar, in the name of Maharam, regarding the dispute whether prayer while standing is preferable or juxtaposing redemption to prayer is preferable. Tosafos write that one may pray the Eighteen Blessings while walking on the road and need not stand. They ask why this differs from Havineinu, which must be recited while standing, and answer that Havineinu is short and therefore causes less delay to the journey. Maharam further explains that here, upon returning home, one prays again while standing, as stated below that Rav Ashi prayed with the congregation while seated and upon returning home prayed while standing. Rabbeinu Tam ruled like the father of Shmuel and Levi. Yet since Rav Ashi said that he had not seen the elder Sages act that way, it implies that the halachah does not follow them. I am greatly perplexed by Maharam’s explanation. According to him, Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, who holds that juxtaposing redemption to prayer is preferable, must also hold that one later repeats the prayer while standing, as Rav Ashi did. If so, why did the Gemara challenge Rav Ashi to act like the father of Shmuel and Levi? Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar supported his practice, and the Tannaim had already disputed the matter, each with his own reason. Rather, Rav Ashi did not act in accordance with Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar either, for according to Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar there is no need to pray again while standing. The Gemara challenged him that since he required two prayers out of concern for standing prayer, why did he not act like the father of Shmuel and Levi, who likewise valued standing prayer and therefore prayed earlier in the morning? Rashi explicitly explains the question this way, asking why Rav Ashi needed two prayers. It follows that Rav Ashi innovated the practice of repeating the prayer while standing, unlike Maharam’s explanation that Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar agreed. The words of Rabbeinu Chananel also plainly indicate that he did not accept Maharam’s explanation. Had he accepted it, how could he rule like the father of Shmuel and Levi, who followed the first Tanna, when Rav Ashi, the later authority, followed Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar? Even Tosafos, who challenge Rabbeinu Chananel, argue only from Rav Ashi’s statement that he had not seen the elder Sages act that way, implying that the halachah does not follow them. Why did they not argue from Rav Ashi’s actual conduct of praying twice, which according to Maharam would follow Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar? Necessarily, they did not accept Maharam’s explanation. Perhaps the answer cited in Maharam’s name was a later marginal gloss rather than the words of Tosafos themselves. The Rosh likewise clearly disagrees with Maharam, for he writes that the halachah follows the father of Shmuel and Levi, and that even Rav Ashi did not disagree with them but merely said that he had not seen the elder Sages act so. According to Maharam, however, Rav Ashi certainly disagreed with them, since he acted exactly like Rabbi Shimon ben Elazar, while they followed the first Tanna. Rather, Rav Ashi introduced a new practice of praying twice. We do not derive the halachah from his innovation, for he did so as a voluntary prayer, which was appropriate for him because he could concentrate during a voluntary prayer. This does not apply to us, as the Tur writes at the end of siman 107 and Beit Yosef also writes here. Beit Yosef later cites Maharam’s explanation and on that basis rules that anyone who prayed while seated must pray again while standing when able. It has already been shown, however, that Rashi, Tosafos, Rabbeinu Chananel, and the Rosh all disagree. Rambam and Tur also make no mention of this. Had they held that a second prayer was required, they would not have remained silent. We must therefore be concerned that repeating the prayer may constitute a blessing in vain. We have never seen great authorities who prayed while seated in a wagon on the road pray again upon reaching an inn or home. It therefore appears that here too one should not pray again unless he is certain that he can concentrate during the prayer, as written at the end of siman 107.
