(9) He must return and pray again, etc. — the rabbi in Beit Yosef learned this from Tosafos chapter Terumat HaDeshen in the name of Rambam page 30 in the dispute of the tannaim whether prayer standing is preferable or adjoining redemption to prayer is preferable, as they wrote, and this is their language: it implies that the eighteen blessings may be prayed on the road while walking and he need not stand; and you would ask from Havineinu that only standing; and one can say Havineinu is different for it is short and there is not so much cancellation of the road; and furthermore Rambam explained here that when he returns home he prays standing, as they say nearby Rav Ashi prays with the congregation individually seated, and when he returns home he prays standing; and Rabbenu Tam ruled like the father of Shmuel and Levi; but from Rav Ashi saying the elder rabbis do not look favorably on one who does this, it implies the halacha is not like them, end quote. And I wonder greatly at Rambam's explanation: one must say Rashba who holds there prayer adjoining redemption to prayer is preferable holds that afterward he must pray standing as Rav Ashi did; if so what does the Talmud challenge on Rav Ashi, and let the master act like the father of Shmuel and Levi, since Rashba holds like him and the tannaim already dispute this — and how can one challenge one who holds like Rashba why he should not act like the first Tanna of Rashba, for each tanna has his reason, some say standing is preferable and some say adjoining redemption to prayer is preferable; rather necessarily Rav Ashi acted not like Rashba too, for according to Rashba he need not pray afterward standing; therefore they challenge him: since you require two prayers in order to be concerned for prayer standing, why do you not act like the father of Shmuel and Levi who were also concerned for prayer standing and made an enactment for this by praying Shacharis early; and Rashi explained explicitly on this question why Rav Ashi needs two prayers — it implies Rav Ashi innovated this matter to return and pray standing, not like Rambam's explanation that Rashba also holds so; also from Rabeinu Chananel it seems plainly he does not hold like Rambam's explanation, for if he held so how did they rule like the father of Shmuel and Levi, they ruled like the first Tanna of Rashba, since Rav Ashi who is later holds like Rashba; and even Tosafos who challenge Peri Chadash only challenged from the language that he said we do not see elder rabbis doing this, which implies not like them — but why did they not challenge from what Rav Ashi did practically with two prayers, which is like Rashba according to Rambam? Rather certainly they do not hold like Rambam; and perhaps that comment in the name of Rambam is a gloss not from Tosafos themselves but added later in the margin in the name of Rambam; also from Rosh it is clear not like Rambam, for he wrote the halacha is like the father of Shmuel and Levi, that even Rav Ashi nearby does not argue on them but said the elder rabbis do not look favorably thus, end quote; and if according to Rambam Rashba holds exactly like Rav Ashi, certainly Rav Ashi argues on the father of Shmuel and Levi who hold like the first Tanna of Rashba; rather certainly Rav Ashi came to do something new, namely to pray two prayers, and one does not learn halacha from Rav Ashi's innovation, for R' Eliezer did so as a voluntary offering and it would have been fitting for him to do so because he had intention in voluntary prayer, unlike us, as Tur wrote siman 107 and as Beit Yosef wrote here too; but afterward Beit Yosef explained Rambam and on this basis ruled that everyone who prays seated must return and pray standing when he can; and it was already explained that Rashi and Tosafos and Rabeinu Chananel and Rosh all do not hold so; also Rambam and Tur did not mention this at all; and if they held one must pray again they would not have been silent about it; therefore we must be concerned for a doubt of blessing in vain if he prays again; and we never saw among the great ones who prayed on the road seated on the wagon that they prayed again when coming to an inn or their home; therefore it seems here too he should not pray again unless he is confident he can direct his attention in his prayer, as written in se'if katan 107.
