He must repeat. Shiltei Giborim writes likewise. Rema MiPano writes in siman 19 that Rav Ashi prayed a second time specifically as a voluntary prayer; see there. Rashi’s wording implies that because he could not concentrate the first time, since it was during the lecture, he prayed a second time. It is possible that this is why travelers customarily do not pray again.
So that they will not interrupt him. Meaning, it is very likely that he can pray while standing without interruption.
He should not pray at the inn. It is stated in Shabbos 127 that Rabbi Yehoshua did not wish to enter the house of a non-Jew while wearing tefillin; all the more so regarding prayer.
In some corner. So that the members of the household will not disturb him. There is no concern for their idols, since all their prayers were offered in towns filled with idols. All this is from Terumas HaDeshen. It requires study which is preferable when he cannot withdraw from the road and must pray while walking or seated, while at the inn he will not find a corner where he will be undisturbed. Is prayer while standing preferable, or is prayer on the road preferable because he can concentrate better? It appears to me that prayer on the road is preferable, since Rashi explains that standing prayer is preferable because one can concentrate better, and here we see that he can concentrate better on the road. Nevertheless, he should see whether he can pray at the inn in a field, courtyard, or garden, for then it is preferable and he fulfills both considerations.
