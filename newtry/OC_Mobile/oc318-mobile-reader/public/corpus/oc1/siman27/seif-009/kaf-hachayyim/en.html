(9) One born with the right hand severed only at the palm, while retaining the right upper and lower arm, who nevertheless performs all hand-dependent tasks with his left but says his right arm is stronger and uses it for tasks not requiring a palm, places tefillin on the left. The right does not become left merely because it was cut. This differs from a left-handed person, whose two hands demonstrate that ordinary human nature has reversed. With only one hand there is no proof that the left became right; the existing left remains left. Moreover, Bach considers the right arm a 'hand,' so we follow comparative strength, and here the right remains stronger [She'eiris Yehudah, Orach Chaim 2; Pischei Olam].
