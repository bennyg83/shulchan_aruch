Prone to flow. See Taz. This is the view of the Rashba and Rivash, as Be'er HaGolah notes, that the derivation is not from 'upon your arm' but from 'for you a sign,' and by shel rosh, on the contrary, it says 'and they shall see.' This is specifically a thin cap, for on a thick cap one cannot aim at the place where an infant's skull is soft. By shel yad it is certainly forbidden to place on bandages because of 'for you a sign' and not for others. According to those who permit shel yad on bandages, he should put on shel yad without a blessing and then put on shel rosh and recite two blessings; but one who puts shel rosh on a thin cap recites only one blessing, like by shel yad in siman 26.
