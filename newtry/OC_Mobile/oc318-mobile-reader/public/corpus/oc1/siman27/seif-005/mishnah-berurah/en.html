Prone to flow. Meaning he has pain or sensitivity in his head and the cold will harm him if he uncovers it. Since he is under constraint, he may rely on the Rashba, who holds there is no concern of interposition for the shel rosh, but he must cover it because of observers who do not know he is under constraint.
Tefillin shel rosh upon, etc. If he has a wound on the arm where the box is placed and the wound spreads over the entire kibores, he may place it on the bandage if there is no other way, but he should not bless, since most poskim hold that interposition invalidates. He should recite two blessings on the shel rosh as above, siman 26 in the gloss, and cover the shel yad with another garment, for by them it says 'it shall be for you a sign,' meaning for you and not for others.
A thin cap. But on a thick cap it is forbidden to place them, for he cannot aim and fit them in the place where an infant's skull is soft.
He should only bless, etc. He should intend to discharge the shel rosh, for in any event many poskim hold that one recites only one blessing for both. It appears from the Bach and Olas Tamid that it is proper to be careful that at least during Krias Shema and prayer there be no interposition at all.
