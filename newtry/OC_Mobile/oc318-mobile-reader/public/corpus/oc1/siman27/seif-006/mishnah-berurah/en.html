And a left-handed person, etc. See above, seif katan 12.
His right hand. Even if he became left-handed through later training and was not born that way, he places on the right, and certainly if Heaven made him so through illness or loss of strength in his right hand. If he recovered and became equally skilled in both hands, he is like every person.
All his labor. The same applies even to most of his labor.
He places on his left. If he placed it on his right, which is the left of every person, even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation; see Biur Halacha.
Controls both hands. This means he does all labor with both equally. If it is easier for him to use the left, even though he can also use the right, this is not called control of both hands.
Whose strength is weakened. Meaning that writing has no special superiority over any other single labor, and we follow the majority of actions; that hand is called the left.
Is considered the right. Since it is written 'you shall bind' and 'you shall write,' it implies that with the hand with which he writes he must bind the tefillin on the opposite arm. But if he was not born so and only later trained himself to write with the left while doing other actions with the right, he places on the left of every person. One who cannot write, according to all, follows his other labors; the hand with which he does them is called the right.
And so is the practice. See Biur Halacha.
