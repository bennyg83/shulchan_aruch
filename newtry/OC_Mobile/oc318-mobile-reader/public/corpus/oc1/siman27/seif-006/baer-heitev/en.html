His right. If two people traveled together, one left-handed and one not, and the left-handed one has only tefillin from one who is not left-handed, he should put the tefillin on his right arm, with the box toward the shoulder and the maavarta toward the hand. So too the reverse: one who is not left-handed and puts on tefillin of a left-handed person places them on his left arm, the box toward the shoulder and the maavarta toward the hand. Shevus Yaakov, part 1, siman 3; see Responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 41, and Chavos Yair, siman 167.
Every person. If he placed it on his right, which is the left of every other person, even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation. Chut HaShani, siman 28.
Right. If he writes with both hands, he places them on the left of every person. If he trained himself to write with his left but does all his labor with his right, he places them on the left of every person; see Magen Avraham. See Eliyah Rabbah, who disagreed with the compromise of Magen Avraham and ruled like the Shulchan Aruch. He concludes that a person who cannot write should place them on the right when he does all his actions with the left.
