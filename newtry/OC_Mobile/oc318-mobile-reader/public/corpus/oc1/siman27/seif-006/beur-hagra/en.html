Se'if 6: There are those who say, etc. Like Rav Ashi there.
And there are those who say, etc. Like Rabbi Nosson, who expounds 'you shall bind,' and Rabbi Yose HaChorem according to the first answer. 'Others' disagree with Rav Ashi. Although the first tanna agrees with Rav Ashi, in the second baraisa both views disagree with him according to Tosafos, who says they do not dispute one another; but the first understanding says they disagree, and the law follows the first tanna and Rav Ashi, as above in se'if 1. Mordechai in Rabbeinu Yitzchak's name also says they disagree, though he says both accept Rav Ashi and 'Others' derive both rules; this is difficult, for why call it dependent on tannaim? In any event, the first tanna plainly does not accept Rabbi Nosson's derivation, so he cannot distinguish writing from other acts and must follow the majority of all acts. Even Rabbi Nosson and Rabbi Yose HaChorem need not follow writing alone. Rashi's words are best understood as denying a distinction based on writing alone. His interpretation is primary, and independently the first opinion is primary, as above.
