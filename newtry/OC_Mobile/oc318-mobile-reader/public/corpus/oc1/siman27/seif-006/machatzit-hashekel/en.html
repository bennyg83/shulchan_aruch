(s"k 6) Head tefillin on hat, etc. As end of this siman — not like Bach; Magen Avraham: not that Bach permits arm — opposite, Bach stricter as explained; not like Bach in explaining Rashba source of this se'if — Bach challenged Rashba; Magen Avraham: not like Bach — Rosh: Torah concerned chatzitzah on arm from "sign on hand"; a fortiori head from totafot; Rashba: head no concern — totafot only placement; Bach: end Hakometz al yadekha height of arm — or only on hand — Torah said don hand and head; what head height, so arm height (Rashi: height of arm = cubit flesh; on hand = palm); we do not half-hekesh — head compared to arm for chatzitzah. Bach thought Rashba drashed "for you" for chatzitzah — not so Magen Avraham: Rashba no chatzitzah even arm bayit; forbid arm on garment not chatzitzah but "for you not others" — arm must be covered.

And as Magen Avraham end this siman. No need learn head from arm — maybe chatzitzah disqualifies — arm itself chatzitzah does not disqualify; not because head must be covered like arm. Why not head on garment? Opposite — head must be exposed — "all peoples see Hashem's name called on you" — R. Eliezer: these tefillin on head (this siman). Magen Avraham: permitted because Rashba no chatzitzah issur on arm, only covered; wound on hand — may on cloth — no chatzitzah; "not others" covered by other garment — shirt — still no berachah (Magen Avraham s"k 8); Rashba no chatzitzah issur; Rosh certainly issur; priests did not place arm tefillin on tunic — needed tunic on flesh — yilbash al besaro; per Magen Avraham Rashba no disqualifying chatzitzah on arm — could place on tunic; "for you" requires covered.

Could wrap another on arm tefillin unseen. Magen Avraham explains: priests could not wear garment over tunic — not that other garment on arm tefillin is issur; even arm tefillin forbidden on priestly tunic (Rabbi Yosef citing Ran on mitznefet) — challenged Rashba head no chatzitzah on garment — why Gemara hair between tzitz and mitznefet — could place on mitznefet? Ran: forbidden to put other mitzvah vessel on mitznefet; perhaps Magen Avraham garment means tefillin mitzvah vessel; voluntary garment covering — forbidden on priestly garments.
