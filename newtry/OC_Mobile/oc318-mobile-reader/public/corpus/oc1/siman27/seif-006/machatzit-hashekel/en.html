Seif katan 6: Head tefillin over a hat, etc. As Magen Avraham writes at the end of this siman, so it appears to me, unlike the Bach, and for this reason it appears, etc. His statement "unlike the Bach" does not mean that the Bach permits this regarding the arm tefillin. The opposite is true, for the Bach is more stringent, as will be explained. Rather, he disagrees with the Bach's interpretation of the Rashba, whose words are the source of this seif. Based on his interpretation, the Bach challenged and rejected the Rashba's ruling. Magen Avraham writes that the matter is unlike the Bach. The Rosh holds that just as the Torah was particular regarding an interposition for the arm tefillin, as it states, "it shall be a sign upon your arm," so too, by reasoning, even though the Gemara does not state it explicitly, the Torah was particular regarding the head tefillin, as it states, "they shall be as frontlets between your eyes." The Rashba holds that there is no such concern with the head tefillin, and the verse merely defines their place of placement. The Bach asks from the end of the chapter HaKometz: The Gemara expounds "upon your arm" as the raised part of the arm. Perhaps it means literally upon the hand? The Torah said to place tefillin on the arm and on the head. Just as there they are placed on the raised part of the head, so here on the raised part of the arm. Rashi explains that the raised part of the arm is the kibbores, the bulging flesh, while literally upon the hand would mean upon the palm. Since we rule that an analogy cannot be applied partially, the head tefillin should likewise be compared to the arm tefillin regarding interposition. These are the Bach's words. It appears that the Bach understood the Rashba to derive from "it shall be a sign for you" that there is no interposition. Magen Avraham does not understand him this way. He holds that according to the Rashba, even with the arm tefillin an interposition does not invalidate, even beneath the box. The prohibition against placing the arm tefillin over a garment is not because of interposition, but because of the Scriptural decree, "it shall be a sign for you," meaning for you and not as a sign for others. The arm tefillin must be covered.
As Magen Avraham writes at the end of this siman. There is therefore no difficulty that we should derive the law of the head tefillin from the arm tefillin. What should be derived? If that interposition invalidates, interposition does not invalidate even the arm tefillin according to the Rashba. If that the head tefillin too should be covered like the arm tefillin, and for that reason should not be placed over a garment, that is incorrect. On the contrary, the head tefillin must be exposed, as it states, "all the peoples of the earth shall see that the Name of Hashem is called upon you," which Rabbi Eliezer expounds as referring to the head tefillin, as Magen Avraham writes at the end of this siman. There is therefore no difficulty. This also explains Magen Avraham's statement, "For this reason it appears that it is permitted, etc." According to Magen Avraham's understanding of the Rashba, interposition is not prohibited even with the arm tefillin; they merely must be covered. If one has a wound on his arm, he may place the tefillin over the bandage, since there is no concern of interposition. As for the requirement that they be a sign for him and not for others, they are covered by another garment, such as a shirt or outer garment. Nevertheless, he should not recite a blessing, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 8. The reason is that although the Rashba sees no prohibition of interposition, according to the Rosh there certainly is such a prohibition. And the fact that the kohanim did not place them. The kohanim did not wear the arm tefillin because the tunic had to be worn directly upon the flesh with no interposition, as the verse states, "he shall wear it upon his flesh." According to Magen Avraham's understanding that the Rashba does not disqualify an interposition beneath the arm tefillin, why did they not place the arm tefillin over the tunic, so that there would be no interposition between the tunic and the flesh? If the problem is that the arm tefillin must be covered, it would have been possible to wrap another item over them so they would not be visible.
It would have been possible to wrap another item over the arm tefillin so they would not be visible. Magen Avraham answers that the kohanim were not permitted to wear another garment over the tunic. His intent is not merely that the particular covering placed over the tefillin would be prohibited. Even the arm tefillin themselves may not be placed over the tunic of the priestly garments. Beit Yosef writes similarly in the name of the Ran regarding the turban. The Ran asks that according to the Rashba, who holds that interposition does not apply to the head tefillin and that they may be placed over a garment, why did the Gemara need to state that the Kohen Gadol's hair was visible between the tzitz and the turban, where he placed the head tefillin? He could have placed them upon the turban. The Ran answers that perhaps it is prohibited to place another sacred article upon the turban. Perhaps Magen Avraham specifically mentions a garment because tefillin are sacred articles, concerning which the Ran writes only tentatively. But an ordinary garment placed over the arm tefillin to cover them would certainly be prohibited over the priestly garments.
