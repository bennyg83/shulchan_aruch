(1) [Se'if 1] The place for arm-tefillin is on the left arm. If he placed it on the right, he did not fulfill the obligation even after the fact [Chut HaShani 28; Yad Aharon; Beis Oved]. This also follows the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 5 on tefillin, who gives a mystical reason that it must specifically be on the left, unlike Beis HaRoeh's after-the-fact validation. One who blessed and placed it on the right must bless again when replacing it correctly on the left [Beis Oved]. This appears to apply when he also completed placement of the head-tefillin. If he remembered or was reminded before placing the head-tefillin, he need not bless again, since he had not completed the mitzvah and intended the blessing for both; see siman 25, paragraph 80. If he completed both, or possessed only arm-tefillin, he blesses again. The principle of doubt concerning blessings does not apply, since according to the Arizal the right-arm placement is no placement at all. With arm-tefillin alone his first blessing was in vain; with both, it took effect only for the head-tefillin, so he must bless when now fulfilling arm-tefillin. We do not invoke doubt against the Arizal, as above in siman 25, paragraph 75. See below, paragraph 7.
(2) There: Between the elbow and armpit. This is derived from, 'It shall be a sign for you'; for you and not for others, so it should not be visible outwardly. A person normally exposes the arm only to the elbow; beyond it is 'for you and not for others' [Levush; Taz; Shesilei Zeisim]. See Chukei Chaim, discourse 3 on putting on tefillin.
(3) There, gloss: It must be placed on the upper portion of the bone near the elbow, etc. The entire half of the upper-arm bone adjacent to the elbow is valid, as Beis Yosef writes from the gloss to Semak and Shimusha Rabba; so rule se'if 7, Levush, and later authorities. Although Bedek HaBayis says not to rely on this for the entire half but only the bulging flesh, Maran apparently retracted when later composing Shulchan Aruch. Rambam and Tur can likewise be read this way. 'From its midpoint to the elbow' in se'if 7 does not mean literally up to the elbow, where there is no bulging flesh, but excludes the half toward the armpit [Pri Megadim; Shulchan Aruch HaRav; Chayei Adam; Ben Ish Chai]. The Gra, however, holds that all bulging flesh along the entire upper arm between elbow and armpit is valid; Chayei Adam and Ben Ish Chai cite him. Ben Ish Chai says one wearing Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin together should initially make them small enough to fit on the bulging flesh of the lower half. When impossible, he may rely on the Gra that the whole kibores is valid. Although Chayei Adam says not to bless when placing on the upper half, for us there is no concern of a doubtful blessing, since one blessing covers both arm- and head-tefillin and he also wears Rabbeinu Tam arm-tefillin. See Kol Yaakov, siman 34, paragraph 10.
(4) There, gloss: An amputee who has no hand but retains the arm should put them on without a blessing. Opinions are numerous. Or Zarua exempts him; Tosafos, Menachos 37a, obligates him. Magen Avraham says Or Zarua had another text; Turei Even says he follows the first tanna's exemption. Bach distinguishes: if amputated above the kibores he is exempt entirely, but below it he puts them on with a blessing, unlike the gloss. According to the Gra, who validates the whole bone between elbow and armpit, even amputation above the usual kibores leaves a valid place on the remaining bone. Shevus Yaakov I 3 says an amputee retaining only an arm places tefillin on his right. To satisfy the doubts, whether the cut is below or above the kibores, he should first put arm-tefillin on the remaining left arm without a blessing. When placing head-tefillin, an Ashkenazi says two blessings and a Sephardi one, intending also to cover the arm-tefillin, and prays in them. After prayer he removes both, then puts the arm-tefillin on his right arm and the head-tefillin on his head without another blessing, having intended to put them on again. One whose left arm has withered and lost all sensation should do the same. The left is first because the Arizal gives a mystical reason for specifically left-arm placement; in every doubt it should therefore be used first, covered by the head-tefillin blessing, and worn during prayer.
(5) There, gloss: An amputee who has no hand but retains the arm, etc. If he has no arm either, Magen Avraham, the Gra, Panim Meiros III 11, and Beis Oved exempt him entirely and do not require placement on the right. Others require right-arm placement [Tiferes Shmuel in Maharshal's name]. He should therefore place it on the right without a blessing, then when blessing on the head-tefillin intend also to cover the arm-tefillin.
(6) One who has no arm and is exempt from arm-tefillin must still put on head-tefillin [Shaagas Aryeh 37], as is also proven by the Riaz cited by Shiltei HaGibborim. Machazik Berachah, Beis Oved, and Shaarei Teshuvah cite this, unlike Responsa Tzemach Tzedek 5, whose view opposes the implication of all the poskim.
(7) One born without a left arm may be obligated in arm-tefillin according to everyone, although they dispute one whose arm was amputated after birth. To avoid a doubtful blessing, he should put on arm-tefillin as well, bless twice over the head-tefillin, and intend to cover both [Shevus Yaakov I 3; Yad Aharon]. Under our custom of only one blessing for both, he should say only Al Mitzvas Tefillin over the head-tefillin and intend it also for the arm-tefillin [Beis Oved].
(8) If one's right hand withered or was amputated and he cannot tie the tefillin upon his left arm, he must have another person tie them for him. Even a minor may tie them, being no worse than a woman, who is exempt from tefillin yet may tie and place tefillin upon a man [Mishbetzos Zahav on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 9, paragraph 5, in Rabbi Binyamin's name].
(9) One born with the right hand severed only at the palm, while retaining the right upper and lower arm, who nevertheless performs all hand-dependent tasks with his left but says his right arm is stronger and uses it for tasks not requiring a palm, places tefillin on the left. The right does not become left merely because it was cut. This differs from a left-handed person, whose two hands demonstrate that ordinary human nature has reversed. With only one hand there is no proof that the left became right; the existing left remains left. Moreover, Bach considers the right arm a 'hand,' so we follow comparative strength, and here the right remains stronger [She'eiris Yehudah, Orach Chaim 2; Pischei Olam]. See the end of paragraph 4 above and paragraph 31 below.
(10) One born without the palm of his right hand who performs all his needs with the left must place tefillin on the left like everyone else [Responsa Divrei Nechemiah, Orach Chaim 11]. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek 5 says he should put them on his complete left arm during prayer and may bless according to all opinions under our custom of one blessing for both arm- and head-tefillin; afterward he should also place them on the right and recite Keri'as Shema. Pischei Olam cites this. According to siman 25, paragraph 78, that one who removes tefillin intending to replace them does not bless again, he should not bless when replacing them on the right: the head-tefillin need no blessing because he intended to replace them, and the arm-tefillin receive none because this placement is only due to doubt.
