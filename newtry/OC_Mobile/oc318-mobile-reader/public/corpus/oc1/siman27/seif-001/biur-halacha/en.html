On the bulging flesh. One must ensure no edge of the tefillin lies below the bulging flesh, just as no edge of head-tefillin may rest on the forehead. Menachos 37b derives the arm's elevated place from the head's elevated place through a gezeirah shavah.
At the upper portion of the bone. Magen Avraham and Machatzis HaShekel seemingly imply bulging flesh extends equally along the whole bone, while Rema permits tefillin only on the half toward the elbow. If so, how can only half the kibores qualify when Arachin 19b explicitly calls the entire kibores 'hand' in a tefillin discussion? The gloss to Semak cannot have lacked that text, which all Rishonim cite. Menachos 37a similarly says 'your hand' is the kibores and 'between your eyes' is the skull where an infant's brain is soft. Since it defines no smaller place within the kibores, the whole must qualify. Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Tur, and Rabbeinu Yerucham make no division. How then can Rema adopt Semak? It cannot be only a stringency, since se'if 7 implies a wound covering the entire lower half exempts him. Sefer HaTerumah, after following Rabbeinu Tam that tefillin rest on the upper arm between elbow and armpit, says to place them somewhat near the elbow, where the gathered flesh called kibores lies, opposite the heart. Thus kibores flesh actually spreads only near the elbow; no Rishon disputes this observable fact. Rema and Shulchan Aruch in se'if 7 therefore hold it extends only through the lower half. Bach likewise calls the lower half kibores. Magen Avraham's language can mean that although some bulging flesh appears above, the name kibores applies primarily only below the midpoint. The Gra disagrees with Shulchan Aruch and Rema, validating above the midpoint wherever kibores flesh still remains. He too may accept Sefer HaTerumah that its principal place is toward the elbow, but hold it extends beyond half the bone.
Who has no hand. See Mishnah Berurah. This is unlike Pri Megadim's claim that Or Zarua exempts every form of amputation; Or Zarua was unavailable in print in his time.
He should put them on, etc. Meaning on his left arm. Or Zarua itself shows he rejects the commentators who apply the inclusion to requiring right-arm placement; he applies it to the left, yet rules like the first tanna that one is exempt even if the kibores remains. He understands 'has no arm' as referring to the forearm. Rema's statement that Tosafos obligates means even if only the kibores remains, since Tosafos concludes that 'arm' begins beyond the elbow. Magen Avraham did not possess Or Zarua and was therefore greatly troubled by the ruling.
Without a blessing. Bach, Nachalas Tzvi, Eliyah Rabbah, and the Gra imply he should bless because the kibores remains. Derech HaChaim agrees with the basic obligation but inclines to omit the blessing. Even if only the portion above the midpoint between elbow and armpit remains, he may plainly rely on the Gra that any remaining kibores flesh qualifies, but should not bless. Better, under our Rema-based custom, he should say both blessings over head-tefillin (under Beis Yosef, one) and intend also for the arm-tefillin, even when the kibores remains, as Shaarei Teshuvah concludes.
