Left, etc. For it is written with an added letter, interpreted as the weak hand, namely the left whose strength is weakened. What he wrote, that he should tilt, etc., see Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 71.
Not on half the bone, etc. Bedek HaBayis to the Beit Yosef wrote that one should not rely on this to validate placing tefillin on the whole half of the arm, but only on the raised flesh. Malbushei Yom Tov questioned why the Levush omitted the words of his teacher, Rama, that an amputee who has no hand but only an arm places it without a blessing. In my humble opinion this is not difficult, for Levush holds like the Bach: only if it was cut above the kibores does the Or Zarua exempt him, but if below the kibores, since he still has an arm, all agree he is obligated and blesses. This is also the ruling of the Bach and Nachalas Tzvi. I found support in the small Mordechai, whose language implies that one without the hand but with the arm is obligated. Magen Avraham, however, understood from Sefer Yere'im and the old Mordechai that even if the kibores remains he is exempt, and this requires study; see siman 651 and Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 167.
