Se'if 1: On the arm. This is stated in several places in the Talmud.
Left. Menachos 36–37.
On the flesh, etc. There, and as Tosafos explains there, s.v. Kibores.
That is between, etc. Tosafos there, from Ohalos 1, where 'arm' means the section between the elbow and the shoulder; see there.
At the top. Meaning on the raised portion of the bone. Semak writes that our Rabbis required placement on the raised portion of the arm. Its gloss says that not the entire arm is valid, but only the half toward the elbow, based on Shimusha Rabba's phrase 'half the arm'; it means the raised part of that half, since the Gemara specifies the kibores and all poskim require the raised portion. This gloss requires examination: the Gemara says without qualification, 'your hand; this is the kibores.' Were it not for the gloss, I would explain 'half the arm' as referring to the entire kibores, which is the arm's relevant section; 'half' is not exact but means part of it, sufficient to place two pairs of tefillin, as stated at the beginning of Eruvin's final chapter. This appears primary: the entire kibores is valid for tefillin.
One whose hand is amputated, etc. Darkei Moshe cites Or Zarua that an amputee is exempt even if some upper arm remains, while Tosafos implies he is obligated. Magen Avraham says Or Zarua had Ra'em's reading, 'one who has only an arm,' and ruled like the first tanna. It appears that all agree an amputee does not place it on the left; the dispute is whether he places it on the right. Mechilta derives inclusion of an amputee whose tefillin are placed on the right. Our reading is preferable: why exempt an amputee when the whole arm and kibores are called 'hand'? Rather, the amputee in the sugya lacks even the kibores, as Bach writes; he is exempt according to the first tanna and Rav Ashi. If the kibores remains, all agree he is obligated and places it on the left. Thus Tosafos is primary according to our reading, while Or Zarua ruled like the first tanna according to Ra'em's reading. Tosafos was pressed to say the views do not disagree because it understood the case as amputation of the left hand with some arm remaining. According to the explanation above, the dispute concerns placement on the right, but there is no practical difference in the ruling.
