Left. From the verse written as 'your hand' with an added letter, meaning the weak hand, namely the left which is weak. They further expounded from 'you shall bind' and 'you shall write': just as writing is with the right, as is the way of people, so binding is with the right; since binding is with the right, placement is on the left. If he placed it on the right, even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation.
On the raised flesh. This is the place called kibores in the language of Chazal, and it is essential, as derived in the Gemara from the verse.
The elbow. Called elbow.
At the head of the bone. This does not mean the literal head of the bone, for there the flesh is still low and until it begins to be high it is not the place of tefillin. Rather, it means the height of the flesh on the bone. Rama comes only to exclude the half of the bone near the armpit. The Gra agrees in practice that the whole place of the kibores is valid; nevertheless below the place of the kibores is invalid according to all. Therefore it is proper to avoid very large tefillin, for often the end of the box rests below the kibores. If one has only large tefillin, it is better to tie them on the upper half where the raised flesh still is.
Who has no hand. Meaning that the left hand and forearm up to the elbow were removed. But if some of the forearm remains, even the Or Zarua agrees with Tosafot that he is obligated in tefillin and also blesses.
Without a blessing. See Biur Halacha. If he has no left hand at all, or even if a piece of the upper arm remains but he has no place of kibores at all, he is exempt from putting on the shel yad even on the right, though some are stringent. All this is when he became an amputee on the left, the place of tefillin; if he became an amputee on the right, even if the whole hand was cut off, he is obligated in tefillin and should ask others to put it on him.
