(1) Left — from yadekha (with aleph): weak hand, i.e. left lacking strength. Also drasha from bind and write — as writing is on right for most people, so tying on right; since tying right, donning is left. If placed on right — even bedi'eved not yotzei [acharonim].

(2) On swollen flesh — called kubitza in Chazal; me'akevah as learned in Gemara from verse.

(3) Kovedo (elbow) — Old French: alinebogin.

(4) On head of bone — not literal bone-head where flesh still low and no tefillin place (no kubitza there); means height of flesh on bone. Rama did not innovate — only concluded "not on half near armpit" — even if some swollen flesh remains; Mechaber too: only from half bone down (se'if 7); Mechaber's "swollen flesh on bone" also means half down. Gra agrees all kubitza area kosher (Perishah); below kubitza invalid for all — avoid oversized tefillin (patty often below kubitza); better upper half if only large pair — lechatchilah don't be lenient per Mechaber/Rama; if only large — tie upper half where swollen flesh remains — satisfies Gra somewhat vs. all invalid below kubitza; berachah le'vatalah if invalid placement.

(5) No hand — left hand-piece and forearm to elbow removed; if forearm stump remains Or Zaru'a agrees Tosafot — obligated and blesses (recent Or Zaru'a text).

(6) Without berachah — see Biur Halachah. No left hand or only arm above kubitza with no kubitza — exempt arm even on right (some stringent). Gidam on left (donning place) — different from gidam on right — even full right amputation — obligated; others must tie for him.
