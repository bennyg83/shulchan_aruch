In the place, etc. Meaning at the height of the flesh, lower than it toward the elbow, as the Levush wrote. The Gemara's statement that there is room on the head and arm for two tefillin means one above the other, not side by side, and so wrote the Beit Yosef. This appears from the Rosh, Mordechai, Tosafot, and Sefer HaTerumah. Rashi in another place seems to imply side by side, but this is difficult. In my humble opinion there is a textual error in Rashi, and it should read 'batim' rather than 'straps.' One who lays the head straps on top of his head without encircling and tightening around the head does not fulfill tefillin, for 'you shall bind' applies to the head as well, namely the encircling and tightening around the head. This is clear from the Tur and Shulchan Aruch and from siman 33; see also what I wrote below, siman 28.
