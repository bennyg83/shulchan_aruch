The proper custom is to arrange, etc. A left-handed person possessing only ordinary tefillin, or the reverse, may invert them so the ketzitzah faces the shoulder and the ma'abarta the hand [Shevus Yaakov; Artzos HaChaim]. This is certainly better than leaving the tefillin toward the heart with the yud-knot outside. The Gra says that although se'if 2 calls it a custom, according to the Geonim it is Talmudic law. Chayei Adam omits Shevus Yaakov's solution and says that if possible he should remove and reinsert the strap from the other side, keeping the yud near the heart even if its face cannot be turned toward the bayis; if impossible, he still puts them on rather than neglecting the mitzvah, though the yud-knot is outside. In my view Shevus Yaakov is certainly preferable, since inward knot placement is Talmudic according to several Rishonim while orientation is custom, as the Gra says. Even if the strap can be removed, perhaps a yud not facing the bayis lacks the form of a yud. At minimum, when it cannot be removed, follow Shevus Yaakov.
