The proper custom is that the yud of the knot, etc. Beis Yosef in Mahari ben Chaviv's name implies only the yud-knot itself must be below the bayis toward the heart, while the strap enters the knot's fold above the bayis; see there. Darkei Moshe implies it is preferable for both the strap's insertion into the knot and the fold to be on its right, toward the heart. Behag similarly implies the fastening itself must face the heart. Rosh's wording, properly read, says not to distance the knot from the ma'abarta. The knot's loop should be only wide enough for the strap, so the tightening point remains beside the bayis rather than below; certainly the yud-knot itself must not descend away from the bayis. All this is included in the Gemara's rule that the knot be above, not below. Shulchan Aruch's reference specifically to 'the yud of the knot' appears to follow Mahari ben Chaviv, leaving the strap insertion outward, but may only exclude Baal HaMaor's arrangement with the arm-tefillin toward the heart and knot outside; other details remain optional. Although we prefer Darkei Moshe's custom, one following Mahari ben Chaviv should not be protested, for he has authority and his method keeps the yud continuously against the bayis. Our Darkei Moshe method requires constant care, though another solution exists as stated in Mishnah Berurah.
One must be careful. The Gra cites the preceding source, meaning this too has a Talmudic basis: the knot must not be distanced from the tefillin, as he explained earlier.
