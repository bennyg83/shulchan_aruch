Seif katan 2: At the top of the bone, meaning on half of the bone. This means that since he concludes, "but not on the half of the bone adjacent to the armpit," it follows that the entire half of the bone adjacent to the elbow is valid. The Gemara uses the term kibbores. The Gemara expounds, "your arm" means the kibbores. Tosafos explain that the word denotes a gathering, as in the expression referring to a cluster of dates. Here too it means a gathering of flesh, namely the bulging muscle, but not the entire half of the upper arm.
It requires study why they wrote this in seif 7. This is the correct reading. The Beit Yosef, in Bedek HaBayit, rejected the Semak's ruling that permits the entire half of the bone, which is the source of this Rama, because it conflicts with the Gemara's term kibbores. Yet in seif 7 he wrote that the bone adjacent to the armpit, from its midpoint until the elbow, is the place for tefillin, which accords with the Semak. Magen Avraham left this as a difficulty. This statement is like an addition inserted into his words within parentheses, as is Magen Avraham's practice. Magen Avraham's intent, however, is to defend the Semak against the Beit Yosef's objection that his ruling contradicts the Gemara's use of kibbores. He therefore writes, "It therefore appears to me that even the Semak, etc.; rather, he comes to exclude, etc." The Rama's wording, taken from the Semak, "but not on the half of the bone, etc.," does not mean to exclude the half adjacent to the armpit. Even the half adjacent to the elbow is valid only on the bulging flesh, and all the more so the half adjacent to the armpit is not valid in its entirety. Rather, the phrase "but not on the half of the bone" excludes the half of the bulging flesh nearest the armpit. Tefillin may be placed specifically on the bulging flesh toward the elbow. The Semak may call the bulging flesh "half of the bone" because it extends along nearly the entire length of the half of the bone nearest the elbow.
