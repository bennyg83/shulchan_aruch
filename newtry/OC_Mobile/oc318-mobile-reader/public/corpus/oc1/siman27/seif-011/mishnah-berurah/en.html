And they should not turn over. If they turned over, it is pious conduct to fast or redeem it with charity. The concern applies only to what encircles the head and the kibores once. Later wraps and the hanging straps of the shel rosh are not essential to the mitzvah, though for beautification it is proper that the black side face outward even in the extra length.
Whether of the hand, etc. With the shel yad, since he sees it while putting it on, he can see that neither the knot nor the strap turns over. With the shel rosh, which he does not see while placing around the head, he should feel carefully with his hand from the box on every side to sense whether they are placed properly.
Until the navel. If he adds beyond this, we have no objection.
Or above it. See Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Artzos HaChaim, who conclude that the right strap should reach the navel and the left to the chest. The Tur also wrote another opinion: the right to the place of circumcision and the left to the navel.
Like the length of a barley grain. This means with its husk but without its awn, for it has very long awns. Some say the width of the length of a wheat grain, less than a barley, suffices, and in pressing circumstances one may rely on this. The later authorities wrote to be very careful at the fold of tightening, where the strap often creases or tears until the required measure is no longer present.
He puts them on, etc. Because some validate and some invalidate even after the fact, as explained in the Beit Yosef; therefore he ruled this way.
As they are. The later authorities conclude that if the shel yad lacks the required length mentioned above in seif 8, he should put them on without a blessing. If there is enough to extend to the middle finger, the wraps do not prevent fulfillment. Regarding the length of shel rosh straps, Artzos HaChaim wrote that if outside the head circumference there are not at least two tefachim, he should put them on without a blessing. He is also stringent regarding the width of the straps of both shel yad and shel rosh if they have only the length of a wheat grain; see there. Regarding whether repair helps for straps, see the end of siman 33 and Mishnah Berurah there.
