Seif katan 4: An intervening substance, etc. Regarding the priestly garments, the verse states, "he shall wear it upon his flesh," teaching that nothing may intervene between the garment and his flesh. The Gemara asks whether a live louse constitutes an interposition. Perhaps, because its natural mode is to move about, it is considered like part of his flesh, or perhaps not. The Gemara likewise asks what the law is if his hair came out beneath his garment. Rashi explains that hair from his head extended downward and entered between his tunic and his flesh. See Rambam, Hilchos Klei HaMikdash.
It appears to me that for this reason, etc., the hair on the neck, etc. Since the Gemara's question was not resolved, one should act stringently. Rambam likewise rules in chapter 10 of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash that initially these constitute an interposition. Perhaps with facial hair, which is short, we say that this is its normal growth. The hair of the armpit proves this, for the Gemara did not ask whether it interposes beneath the priestly garments. The Kohen Gadol also proves it, for he cut his hair every erev Shabbos, not with a razor but with scissors.
He did not cut it as close as a razor, but left some hair, as the verse in Yechezkel states, "they shall trim their heads," and as explained in Sanhedrin 22b. See Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, and the Raavad's gloss there. Nevertheless, the Kohen Gadol wore the head tefillin on the area of hair exposed between the tzitz and the turban. Necessarily, a small amount of hair does not interpose, because this is its normal growth. Hair toward the nape, however, when it is long and extends beyond its normal place, is an interposition, as Rashi explains concerning the Gemara's unresolved question above.
They did not follow this practice. One may say that because the Gemara's question was unresolved, they relied upon the Rashba's view cited in seif 5, and see what Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 5, that with respect to the head there is no concern of interposition, although the Rashba did not state this as a practical ruling.
However, etc. In this case the Gemara did not ask at all, but explicitly stated that it interposes. Since there is no doubt, and since the primary ruling follows the Rosh that interposition applies to the head as well, one should not be lenient.
Therefore one should not be lenient. For this reason I am distressed by the practice of those who grow long forelocks. Apart from this being a manner of arrogance and pride, and involving some prohibition as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 178, it also creates a prohibition regarding tefillin. Since the hair is very long, it cannot be called its normal growth and it interposes. One cannot call this normal merely because these people act this way. Even according to the Rashba, who stated as halacha but not for practical ruling that we are not concerned with interposition on the head, perhaps the Rashba would agree in such a case. According to him too, one may not place tefillin over a thick hat, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 7, because this is not considered placement upon the place where an infant's skull is soft. The same applies to this mass of hair, which is like a thick hat.
