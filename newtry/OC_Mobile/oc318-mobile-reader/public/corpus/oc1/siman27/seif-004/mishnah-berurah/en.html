There should not be, etc. For it is written regarding the shel yad 'upon your arm,' and regarding the shel rosh 'between your eyes.'
An interposing item. Even the slightest interposition. Initially it is proper to be careful even with a living louse, and at least with a dead louse and dirt one must be careful; therefore some wash the place where tefillin are placed. Ravid HaZahav wrote that when a strap sometimes interposes between the tefillin and the flesh, it is not called an interposition because its own kind does not interpose, though Levushei Serad implies that initially it is proper to be stringent.
There is no difference with the shel rosh. Machatzit HaShekel wrote strongly against those who grow long forelocks, for besides arrogance, there is a prohibition in placing tefillin, since when they are very large they cannot be called their normal growth and they interpose. Even apart from interposition, because of those many hairs it is impossible to position and tighten the tefillin in their proper place.
One need not be particular. The later authorities wrote that one may be lenient only in the place of the wraps, but what belongs to the tying should be treated stringently even with straps, both of the hand and head. They wrote open rebuke against those who place tefillin over a wig called peruke, even if only the strap lies over it. If one has a wound on the head only where the straps lie and not where the box lies, he may place the straps over bandages or a thin cap and bless. The same applies by the shel yad where the knot encircles the arm, but he should cover it above so that it remains a sign for him and not for others.
