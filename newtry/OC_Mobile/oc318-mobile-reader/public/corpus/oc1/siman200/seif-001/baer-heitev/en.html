Against his will. And if he did not interrupt, he does not fulfill zimmun. Magen Avraham.

Zimmun. Tur wrote: one who interrupted once may interrupt a second time; Bach wrote it refers to when at first three ate and one interrupted for two, and afterward two others came and ate with the one; and it is not fitting to say zimmun flew from him, for since two others joined him who did not invite, they may invite over him; and "a second time" — likewise a third time or more he may invite, end of his words. Magen Avraham wrote: if there were five in one group and one interrupted for two, he cannot again invite with the two remaining, for zimmun flew from all of them; but if there were seven or eight and he interrupted for two, he may afterward interrupt a second time. Tur wrote: if they are ten, three interrupt for seven, and they need not interrupt except until they said "Blessed are our God," and if the three returned and ate, they may invite together. Magen Avraham: and it appears they may join seven others to say "our God," for it is not fitting to say zimmun flew — for saying "our God," in every house of ten the Divine Presence dwells; and even if there are five who interrupted, they join with five others for the blessing "our God." Magen Avraham.
