(1) They ate as one, etc. — meaning they began eating together and two of them finished first and wish to bless Birkat Hamazon.
(2) He interrupts perforce — meaning it does not depend on his will whether to interrupt his meal to answer; rather by law he is obligated, since they are many against him they need not wait for him.
(3) Whether he answers or not, etc. — meaning nevertheless they fulfill zimmun since he stands there, hears, and can answer (unlike when he went outside, as above siman 194 seif 2); however he certainly does not fulfill unless he interrupted and answered with them.
(4) The obligation of zimmun does not apply, etc. — the wording is slightly garbled; the intent is they are not obligated now to invite with the one.
(5) He is not permitted — Bach disagrees and holds the obligation of zimmun does not apply until all three finish their meal; therefore before the two finish, the one may bless alone and leave — Magen Avraham brought this as halachah, but all later authorities disagree and hold like Shulchan Aruch: since they began eating together, the obligation of zimmun applies immediately, and none of them who finished before may bless alone and leave. However, if there was a very necessary matter involving monetary loss, etc., it is possible to be lenient for the one to bless alone before the two finish and leave; but in such a case it is better that the two act beyond the letter of the law and interrupt their meal and invite over him.
