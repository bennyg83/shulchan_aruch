They ate as one, etc. — see Mishna Berurah; and behold in Shulchan Aruch he did not mention at all whether it refers to when the two were compelled to leave and then the one must perforce interrupt his meal for them, or even when they are not compelled — whether by law he must interrupt for them when they wish to bless Birkat Hamazon. However, from Shulchan Aruch's conclusion at the end — "he too is not permitted to bless and go out to the market," etc. — this refers specifically when he is not compelled to go on his way, as explained in Beit Yosef to resolve Rav Hai Gaon's lenient view that the one may leave — it refers when compelled; and presumably the first clause too refers in this manner, and nevertheless one interrupts for two perforce (unless we say Beit Yosef wrote this only to resolve Rav Hai Gaon's words, but for Rashba perhaps even in such a case it is not a clear leniency to him — and as Bach truly doubts this leniency, and the author who copied Rashba's language — perhaps even in such a case it is forbidden; and per this view the author in the first clause does not refer at all to when not compelled — but all this is strained). However, Or Zarua implies that Rav Hai Gaon and R. Yitzchak (who is one of our teachers brought there, as one who examines will see) refer to when the two were compelled to leave, and only then must the one interrupt for them; and so Rabbenu Yonah wrote: "two do not interrupt for one" refers when the one has a necessary matter, and nevertheless two do not interrupt for one — and presumably likewise one interrupts for two refers in such a case; and so in Chiddushei Raah on Rif's laws it is explicit that it refers when the two were compelled to leave, see there. The upshot: where the two are not compelled to leave, this requires further study whether they can compel the one to interrupt his meal to answer; but from the attribute of "fear your teacher," it is possible that all agree he must interrupt in every case, as Or Zarua wrote for Rashi who holds one interrupts — from fear of one's teacher one interrupts in every case, see there; and it is possible other poskim do not argue on this.

Until the two finish — their meal; but once they finish their meal they have no permission to linger further to delay the third who wishes to invite and bless; and so is the precise language of the Gemara: "two do not interrupt for one" — meaning to interrupt from the meal specifically.
