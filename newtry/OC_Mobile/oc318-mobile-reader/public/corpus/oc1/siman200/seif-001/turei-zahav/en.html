He too is not permitted, etc. — Beit Yosef brought in the name of Rashba who wrote in the name of Rav Hai Gaon: if one of them asked to leave, the two do not interrupt their meal and invite with him; rather he blesses for himself without zimmun and fulfills, and they finish their meal, and this requires further study, end of his words. Beit Yosef wrote on this: it appears the Gaon refers only when compelled to leave and he cannot delay; but where he can delay, even per the Gaon's words he is not permitted to bless and go to the market until the two finish and invite with him, end of his words; and so Rabbenu Yonah. And per what is written in siman 193 in the name of Tosafot: if one began before his fellow, even though they ate together afterward, the later one need not wait for zimmun — we can also say it refers when one began with an olive's worth first; but otherwise it is obvious neither may leave and nullify zimmun, as above — obvious; and one who does so transgresses the sages' ordinance.
