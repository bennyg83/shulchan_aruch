(6) Until he says, etc. — for with this alone they fulfill the blessing of zimmun.
(7) Without a blessing at the start — meaning he need not again bless ha-motzi and ha-gefen, for when he interrupted for the zimmun invitation he did not divert his mind from eating more. And if he did not intend to return and eat, but returns and eats — he needs ha-motzi at the start and also ha-gefen automatically.
(8) And some say he must interrupt — he holds that although "Who feeds" is not entirely a zimmun blessing — for the individual also says it — nevertheless it relates to zimmun, for the invitation alone is not a blessing containing God's name and kingship, and refers to what the inviter blesses afterward — "Who feeds."
(9) Until he says "Who feeds" — and he returns and finishes his meal as the Mechaber wrote, and afterward blesses from the beginning of Birkat Hamazon as Rama concluded at the end; and the reason: although he heard "Who feeds" from the mouth of the inviter, nevertheless he must bless all of Birkat Hamazon for what he ate afterward. Rama also concludes: even if he did not eat afterward, if only in his mind [when he interrupted to hear the zimmun invitation] he intended to return and finish his meal — when he blesses afterward he begins from the start of "Who feeds," for since his mind was to eat more, presumably he did not intend to leave with "Who feeds" from the inviter's mouth; but if he did not intend to eat more and did not eat, when he blesses Birkat Hamazon he begins from "We thank You" as everywhere when hearing zimmun invitation; see above siman 183 seif 7 and Mishna Berurah there. Poskim wrote: one who interrupts for two may interrupt several times — e.g. at first three ate and one interrupted for two, and afterward two others came and ate with the one — this one may again interrupt for these two, for it is not fitting to say zimmun flew from him, since two others who did not invite joined him and may invite over him; and likewise a third time and more. But if there were five in one group and one interrupted for two, he cannot again invite with the two remaining, for zimmun flew from him [Magen Avraham and Shulchan Arukh HaRav]; and it implies even if they ate together afterward [Eben HaEzer disagrees, but many later authorities reconcile his words]. But if there were seven or eight and he interrupted for two of them, he may afterward interrupt again for the rest, for zimmun does not fly from him since there was among them enough for zimmun. Poskim also wrote: just as one interrupts for two to join zimmun, so three or four must interrupt their meal to complete to ten and bless with God's name; but in this all agree they need interrupt only until "Blessed are our God Who we have eaten from His bounty," etc., and no more; and if they ate together afterward after interrupting, they may bless with zimmun and zimmun did not fly from them, for they did not join originally except for mention of the Name; and likewise they may afterward join six others who ate with them to invite with God's name — for it is not fitting to say zimmun flew, for saying "our God" — in every house of ten the Divine Presence dwells; and likewise if there are even five who interrupted, they join afterward with five others for the blessing "our God" when they eat at least an olive's worth of bread together [later authorities].
