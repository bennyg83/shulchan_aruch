Until he says "Blessed are we Who have eaten," etc. — in the Gemara: until where is the zimmun blessing — Rav Nachman: until "let us bless" — meaning until saying "Blessed are we Who have eaten"; R. Shimon: until "Who feeds" — meaning until they finish "Who feeds"; and this statement means there are two types of zimmun. One: where specifically three are required and the individual does not say it at all — that is "let us bless." The second: when there is zimmun, one may not separate until he finishes that too — that is "Who feeds"; and if there is no zimmun, the individual says it anyway; and on this R. Nachman and R. Shimon disagree — R. Nachman holds it is called zimmun only the first way where the individual cannot say it, and this is Shulchan Aruch's ruling; and some say Rama brings Rav Sheshet's view that there is also the second zimmun — in "Who feeds" — and it is included here; and as they argue here regarding one who interrupts, so they argue if there are three and one wishes to go to the market — until where must he wait before leaving; and so Tur; and in what resolves what Beit Yosef wrote on Tur, see there.

All the more if he returned and ate, etc. — in Beit Yosef there is a scribal error in "and he wrote further, even if he reconsidered, he need not," etc. — this is not the error; in the printed text it reads "my brother R. Yechiel asked me per R. Yitzchak's explanation," etc., "and I wrote," etc., "nevertheless we learned," etc., end of his words. The matter is astonishing: since our master wrote thus that one must return to "Who feeds" — when he ate afterward; for if he did not eat, he need not return to "Who feeds," as above — and so R. Yechiel also refers to this; and it is difficult, for per R. Yitzchak's explanation and Tosafot — who are R. Yitzchak — wrote regarding eating afterward, certainly one must return to "Who feeds," and so Rosh as Beit Yosef brought. And it appears when this debate was between R. Yechiel and his father, Rosh held even regarding eating afterward one need not "Who feeds," and afterward held one needs "Who feeds" if he ate; and Tur himself possibly was uncertain of his brother's words; and on this he wrote "and I wrote" that one needs "Who feeds" if he ate; and therefore there is a difference between the brother's words and his father's — for even per our view that one must return to "Who feeds" if he ate, and if not, "on the land" suffices; nevertheless three who interrupt and return to eat must say "let us bless," etc., for they had permission after saying "our God" — with that they fulfilled the obligation of ten, and afterward returned to the obligation of three in what they ate afterward; and the same if they did not eat — here "on the land" does not suffice, only "let us bless" — so it appears simple per our master Tur. But in my humble opinion this seems very astonishing regarding three who interrupt for seven — for it appears they may bless separately in a group of three with zimmun, and they did not say the obligation is not to divide but that three who ate as one must invite; for even if one wished to finish his meal and leave, he may not except he waits for zimmun — unlike ten regarding mention of the Name: they did not say one must invite, but one may not divide — meaning certainly while they sit together at meal's end they may not divide and nullify mention of the Name; but not if they wish to finish their meal before their companions. Proof: chapter "three who ate," 50a — Rava said: when we ate at R. Gamliel's house we bless three-three; we challenged: and for ten-ten? R. Gamliel heard and was upset, etc. — and if dividing were absolutely forbidden, R. Gamliel's upset would not help here to nullify zimmun of three. And you could say likewise — the incident was with ten, and the same for three — for Tur siman 193 wrote for practical law: if the group was large, etc., they may not divide into groups of ten, etc., but may divide into groups of three-three; and he should have said a greater novelty: if there is this concern of the host's sensitivity regarding zimmun of three, they could divide one-by-one — rather certainly this is not permitted because of the host's sensitivity; and if so, specifically for ten this concern helps, since it is not in the category of obligated zimmun like three, but in the category they may not divide — i.e. if mealtime for Birkat Hamazon arrived and they are together; but not when three wish to advance themselves for some need — certainly they may bless in three; and the Rav did not bring these Tur words in Shulchan Aruch, implying he had no clarity in these matters, as it appears to me.

Without a blessing at the start — because they do not move from their place and there is no diversion of attention here, so Tur.
