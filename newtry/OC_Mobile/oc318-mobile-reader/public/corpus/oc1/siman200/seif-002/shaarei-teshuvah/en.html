Zimmun — and what is written in the name of Tur: see Taz and Sefer A"R and Beit Rabbi and Yad Ephraim; and see in Sefer A"R that four interrupt for ten since six is the majority, see there; and what is written in Hagahot Tashbetz in the name of Magen Avraham: if there were five in a group, etc. — see Eben HaEzer who disagrees and holds zimmun did not fly from him since the one who interrupted intended to eat more, see there — for from Tur's words it implies that even if the three blessed completely and left, and two remained and ate together, and a third came and ate an olive's worth of bread with them — they join for zimmun. And wrote in Beit Rabbi: where ten ate and five finished and need to bless, and five did not, and one of them agreed to interrupt — if we follow majority, the four must interrupt; one can say since it is not by law but this one agreed voluntarily it is not called majority, and his view is that because this one agreed the four are not obligated to interrupt, since truly five did not finish their meal — see there.
