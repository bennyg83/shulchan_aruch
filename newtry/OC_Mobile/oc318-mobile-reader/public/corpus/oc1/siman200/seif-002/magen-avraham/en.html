To return and eat bread. If he did not intend to eat bread, only parperet, he need not return except to the place he stopped (Mishna Medura, Agur, Bach); and if he ate parperet he must bless on them the final blessing, since he did not say "Who feeds," as above; nevertheless he should be careful l'chatchila not to talk in between (Shlah, Tanya), as in Tosafot; and see siman 183 seif 6; and this requires further study, for Rosh wrote regarding "flew" zimmun: even though these three ate together, they cannot invite, for zimmun flew from them even for what they ate afterward, since they already invited at the start of the meal — each returns to the place he stopped, end of his words; and why is Rosh and Tur brought here that one returns to the start? It appears Rosh refers to Rashi that he interrupts only until "let us bless," as above. Tur wrote: one who interrupted once may interrupt a second time; Bach wrote it refers when at first three ate and one interrupted for two, and afterward two others came and ate with the one; and it is not fitting to say zimmun flew from him, for since two others joined him who did not invite, they may invite over him; and not specifically a second time — likewise a third time or more he may invite, end of his words. But if there were five in one group and one interrupted for two, he cannot again invite with the two remaining, for zimmun flew from all of them — and see siman 194 in Hagahot; but if there were seven or eight and he interrupted for two, he may afterward interrupt a second time. Tur wrote: if they are ten and three interrupt for seven, they need not interrupt except until they said "Blessed are our God," and if the three returned and ate, they may invite together, end of his words; and it appears they may join seven others to say "our God," for it is not fitting to say zimmun flew — for saying "our God," in every house of ten the Divine Presence dwells; siman 193, note 23; and it appears even if there are five who interrupted, they join with five others for the blessing "our God."

When he wishes to bless — for at the start he did not intend to be discharged with this blessing.

Without a blessing — and if he did not intend to return and eat, but returns and eats, he needs a blessing at the start.
