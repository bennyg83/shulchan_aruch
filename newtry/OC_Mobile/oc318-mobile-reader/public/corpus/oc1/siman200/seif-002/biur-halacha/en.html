To return and eat bread — but if he did not intend to eat bread, only parperet or meat and fish and the like, he has fulfilled the obligation of "Who feeds" and need only begin Birkat Hamazon from "We thank You" onward; and he should be careful l'chatchila not to talk in between, and after Birkat Hamazon he blesses the equivalent of three [blessings] — the final blessing on the parperet, which is of the five species, for it was not discharged in Birkat Hamazon since he did not bless "Who feeds" there [Magen Avraham and Sefer A"R]; and see Peri Megadim who is uncertain regarding things whose blessing is "borei pri ha'eitz" whether he must bless again or perhaps he is discharged with "We thank You"; and also the first blessing requires further study whether one blesses "al ha'michyah." I copied the later authorities' words here, but my heart wavers — for from Magen Avraham it appears somewhat that even l'chatchila it is permitted to eat something that is not bread after joining zimmun and he is discharged with "Who feeds"; and in my view this is not clear — for although in Shulchan Arukh HaRav the examiner finds some support, nevertheless Raavad brought in Rosh siman 12 chapter 3 "three who ate" proves explicitly he does not hold thus; and Rashba agreed with his words, see there; and Rosh himself does not argue except because he is not blessing but interrupting his meal so they may invite over him — and per his view when he returns to his meal he presumably did not intend to leave at the start with "Who feeds"; but if he intended to leave with the zimmun invitation — which is like blessing himself — certainly he too concedes it is forbidden to eat anything in the middle of Birkat Hamazon before finishing it; and this requires further study.
