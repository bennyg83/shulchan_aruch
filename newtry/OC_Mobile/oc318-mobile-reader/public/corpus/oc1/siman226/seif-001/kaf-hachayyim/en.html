1) [Seif 1] One who goes out during the days of Nisan, etc. Since the Tur and Shulchan Aruch specify the days of Nisan, their words imply that this blessing is appropriately recited specifically during Nisan, neither earlier nor afterward. This is also implied by the Gemara's wording, 'One who goes out during the days of Nisan and sees trees blossoming.' Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 28, likewise writes that the phrase, 'one who goes out during the days of Nisan,' implies that one should not recite the blessing over almond blossoms that emerge much earlier than Nisan; see there. Some, however, say that the days of Nisan are not exclusive and that the same applies in another month. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, in the name of Tzedah LaDerech; Machatzis HaShekel and other later authorities write the same. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, however, writes that according to the true mystical teaching, this blessing belongs specifically to the days of Nisan. In Moreh BeEtzba, paragraph 198, he likewise writes that meticulous people insist that it be specifically in Nisan, that two trees be present, and that they go out to a field to recite it. Moed LeKol Chai, siman 1, paragraph 9, writes the same. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 2, similarly concludes this way and rejects the words of one who wrote in the name of Likkutei HaArizal that it may be recited after Nisan; see there. Therefore, since the matter is disputed, since the Gemara's wording also implies specifically Nisan, and since in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, the blessing should be recited specifically in Nisan. This is the common practice. One who delayed and did not recite it in Nisan, however, may apparently recite it afterward without the Name and kingship.
