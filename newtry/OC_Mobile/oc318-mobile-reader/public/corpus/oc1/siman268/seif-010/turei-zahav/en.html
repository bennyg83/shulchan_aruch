In a house of bridegrooms, etc. This means that they pray with a minyan in their home. It appears that this applies all the more to people who occasionally pray with a minyan in a private home and do not go to the synagogue. A house of bridegrooms has a more established practice of praying with a minyan, yet even it does not have the law of a permanent synagogue for this purpose; all the more so an occasional private minyan. The underlying principle is that in our times there is no intrinsic need to recite this blessing even in a permanent synagogue, because there is no danger, as the Tur writes. It is recited only because, once the Rabbis instituted it in places of danger, the practice continues. Any place lacking permanence is therefore not included. Nevertheless, when people establish a place of prayer for a number of days, as is commonly done at fairs, it appears comparable to a permanent synagogue, and this blessing is recited there.
