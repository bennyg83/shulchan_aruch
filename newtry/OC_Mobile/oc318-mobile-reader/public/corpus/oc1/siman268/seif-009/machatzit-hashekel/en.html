Paragraph 9: Musaf, etc.; this also challenges se'if 4. Why should Shacharis in place of Musaf, or the reverse, be worse than a weekday prayer, which se'if 4 validates? Magen Avraham later says it may be worse because one omitted the offerings, or in Shacharis falsely mentioned them before the Omnipresent. Yet his objection to Beis Yosef is sound, for Beis Yosef evidently does not accept these distinctions, otherwise Magen Avraham's later question would not arise.
I found no dispute concerning this ruling, and the authorities appear to state it without qualification. Why, then, does Beis Yosef phrase it as 'some say'?
It appears to me that Beis Yosef, etc.; Musaf is fit for eighteen blessings, so he fulfilled it. This view holds Musaf fundamentally fit for eighteen, as Rosh cites the Yerushalmi practice on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed of eighteen plus the offerings. Therefore, one who said eighteen for Shabbos Musaf fulfilled it. Shacharis in place of Musaf is then no worse than a weekday prayer.
Se'if 4 likewise implies this: Rema validates eighteen in place of Musaf specifically when the offerings were mentioned.
Therefore it appears that he wrote 'some say,' etc. The phrase belongs only to the first rule, that Musaf recited in place of Shacharis may fulfill it. No authority is found to validate Shacharis in place of Musaf. Tosefes Shabbos identifies the validating authority with se'if 4's rule that a weekday prayer mentioning Shabbos fulfills it; Musaf likewise mentions Shabbos and is no worse. This would mean the opposing view here also disputes se'if 4, awkwardly contradicting Beis Yosef's unqualified ruling there. Better to retain Magen Avraham's first explanation: the validator is the se'if 1 view that Musaf too is fundamentally fit for eighteen.
Se'if 4 does not prove Musaf prayer can replace Shacharis. A weekday prayer belongs to Shacharis, since it fundamentally requires eighteen; Musaf does not. Under the view that Musaf too is fit for eighteen, however, Musaf itself substitutes for eighteen and bears some likeness to it, so it may fulfill Shacharis. Under the view denying eighteen to Musaf, it has no relationship to Shacharis and is worse than a weekday prayer. This remains strained.
Therefore it appears that he repeats. 'Repeats' is not literal, for paragraph 8 says the prayer counts as Musaf and he now prays Shacharis. It means that if he wishes it counted as Shacharis, he must repeat; better to designate it Musaf.
