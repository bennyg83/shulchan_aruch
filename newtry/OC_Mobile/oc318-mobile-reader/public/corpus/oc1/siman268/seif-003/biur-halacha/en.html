And unintentionally, etc. See Mishna Berurah, where I wrote that his tongue slipped; this is from Terumas HaDeshen. The Acharonim write similarly. Terumas HaDeshen states: If he unintentionally began with the word ‘Atah’ (for he knew that it was Shabbos, but his tongue slipped because of habit) this is not considered an intentional beginning of an erroneous prayer. If so, even in Yismach Moshe, which does not begin with ‘Atah,’ we should nevertheless regard him as one who erred from one Shabbos prayer to another, because ‘Atah’ is also the beginning of a Shabbos prayer. Although weekday habit caused the error, this is not thereby considered the beginning of a weekday prayer, since he knew that it was Shabbos. This concludes his words. It remains difficult: since his tongue slipped, even if he said ‘Atah chonen,’ what significance does this have? Can this be called beginning a weekday prayer? See the Gemara, Shevuos 20a, regarding wording that slipped from his tongue; it implies that when one’s tongue slips, it is not considered speech at all. Perhaps that case differs because the mouth and heart must agree. There is also room to distinguish: there he did not wish to say that he would not eat at all; he intended to say that he would eat, but his tongue slipped and said that he would not eat, so this is not speech. Here, however, because of habit he did intend to say ‘Atah chonen’; only that act itself occurred without settled awareness, and he did not intend it as a weekday prayer. This case may therefore be stronger. Nevertheless, Terumas HaDeshen himself cites proof for the ruling from Mordechai in the first chapter of Berachos: when one knows that it is Yom Tov but his tongue slips and he concludes with the Shabbos formula, he need not return to the beginning of the blessing; rather, he corrects the conclusion within the time required for a short greeting and concludes with the Yom Tov formula. This ruling is cited below in siman 487. Thus, such a case is not considered an error. Yet the words ‘Who sanctifies Shabbos’ do not belong to Yom Tov at all, just as the word ‘Atah’ belongs to a Shabbos blessing, and nevertheless they are regarded as though they were not said. The same should apply here. This requires further consideration.
