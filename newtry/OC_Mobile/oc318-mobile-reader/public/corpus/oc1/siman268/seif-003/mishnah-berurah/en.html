(6) When he said the word Atah. Beis Yosef holds that even at Maariv and Minchah, although he could continue Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad, since he intended a weekday prayer he is considered to have begun a weekday blessing and must complete Atah Chonen. Magen Avraham disagrees: only at Shacharis, whose Shabbos blessing does not begin Atah, do his words clearly indicate a weekday prayer. At Maariv and Minchah his words do not reveal this; he should continue Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad. Pri Megadim and Derech HaChaim agree.
(7) Unintentionally. Meaning his tongue slipped from weekday habit, but he did not intend a weekday prayer, so it is not considered beginning one. This applies only if he said the single word Atah. If he said Atah Chonen, clearly indicating a weekday prayer, he completes that blessing even though it was only a habitual slip. See Biur Halachah.
(8) For we regard it, etc. The word Atah does not reveal an intention to say Atah Chonen, since he could continue Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad. This is Rema's meaning in concluding, 'for he could say, etc.' His law is therefore like one who mistakenly began Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad during Shacharis and remembered mid-blessing: he stops and immediately begins Yismach Moshe. Here too, saying Atah is treated as Atah Kidashta; he stops and begins Yismach Moshe rather than treating it as Atah Chonen and completing that blessing.
