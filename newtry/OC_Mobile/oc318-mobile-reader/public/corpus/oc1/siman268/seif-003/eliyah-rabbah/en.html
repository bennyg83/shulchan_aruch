And some say, etc. The Beit Yosef likewise agreed to this, and the Bach ruled so because of the doubt of a blessing in vain. So too ruled the Shibbolei HaLeket and the Tanya; and the Kol Bo concluded: and so too on Rosh Chodesh, end quote. Consequently, the same law applies to Yom Tov. In any case, even on the second day of Yom Tov, and in all the laws in this siman, Shabbos and Yom Tov are equal.
If he thought, etc. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 14, he brings proof from what the Raavyah wrote, that one who recited the blessing over the counting of the Omer and thought that the day was the fourth, and recited the blessing with intent for the fourth, while it was actually the fifth, has not fulfilled the obligation even if he concluded with the fifth, end quote. This requires study, for the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 489 in the name of the Mordechai that the reason of the Raavyah is because he holds that counting the Omer is according to the Torah; see there. If so, how does Terumas HaDeshen bring proof from there regarding prayer, which is D'Rabbanan? It seems to me that there a reason is needed that it is according to the Torah because the Gemara was in doubt, perhaps we follow the conclusion. But here we certainly follow the opening, for if he began with Atah Chonen he completes that blessing.
[Levush] Even if he is standing in Atah Kidashta, etc. The Magen Avraham objected, for we hold in siman 209 that a blessing is not ruined merely by lack of intent, and therefore the Rav Beit Yosef omitted what is cited in Terumas HaDeshen, even if he is standing in Atah Kidashta, etc., end quote. In my humble opinion, this is not difficult at all, for in siman 209 we do not approach the matter from that reason, but because perhaps we follow the conclusion, as I explained. So too it is explicitly proven in the Beit Yosef, siman 489, that a blessing is ruined through lack of intent. Also what he wrote, that the Rav Beit Yosef omitted it, etc., is astonishing, for he brought it explicitly in the Beit Yosef. And if his intent is that he omitted it in the Shulchan Aruch, that is no proof at all, for he did not need to write it, since it refers to what he wrote earlier in seif 2, whether in Maariv or in Shacharis, etc. Furthermore, this siman is primarily about the law of the Maariv prayer; on the contrary, if he did not hold this way, he should have explained that it refers to Shacharis. Moreover, since the Shulchan Aruch wrote at the end, even if he is in the Shacharis prayer, etc., necessarily the opening clause also discusses Maariv and Minchah; for if not, what is meant by 'even,' since the end clause would be the opposite of the opening clause, and the opening clause would be speaking only of Shacharis? So too in Sefer Mishpat Tzedek he wrote in the name of the Shulchan Aruch that even in Atah Kidashta the law is this way, and so is the primary ruling.
[Levush] The beginning of the Shabbos prayer, etc. It seems that nevertheless he does not complete either Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad, for which of them should he complete? Rather he says Yismach Moshe, and it is proper to say, 'You, Holy One, blessed be He, gladdened Moshe,' etc.; and similarly, Atah Tikanta, etc. (Lechem Chamudos, page 21). It follows from this that if in Shacharis he began Atah Kidashta, he completes it, and likewise at Maariv, if he began Yismach Moshe, he completes it. In my humble opinion this is not clear; rather, he must stop and begin the appropriate blessing. In seif 15 it says only that he does not go back, meaning if he already completed the blessing. But as long as he has not completed it, he stops. So too it is proven in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 14, in the question there; see there. This also appears from the Magen Avraham.
