12) [Seif 3] If he thought it was a weekday and began, etc. Even if he was at the point of saying Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad, it appears that he is considered to have begun the weekday prayer and must complete that blessing. Beis Yosef in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 14. Levush writes the same. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, however, writes that according to what is stated above in siman 209, even when one intended a different matter, the blessing is not invalidated. Therefore, if at Maariv or Minchah he said only the word Atah while thinking it was a weekday, he should continue Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad. This is not considered beginning the weekday prayer. Only in Shacharis did he also utter words that are inappropriate, as stated in siman 209; see there. Pri Toar in the addenda; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Chayei Adam, klal 28, paragraph 2; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 76, paragraph 17, rule this way. Although Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, challenge Magen Avraham's ruling and write that the Shulchan Aruch follows Terumas HaDeshen, so that even at Maariv and Minchah one who said the word Atah while thinking it was a weekday must complete that blessing, we already wrote above in siman 18, paragraph 7, that in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently, even contrary to Maran and even contrary to the majority; see there. Therefore, at Maariv and Minchah, if one said only the word Atah, although he thought it was a weekday, he need not complete the weekday blessing. Rather, he concludes Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad. If he said Atah Chonen, however, even at Maariv or Minchah he must complete that blessing and then return to say Atah Kidashta or Atah Echad.
