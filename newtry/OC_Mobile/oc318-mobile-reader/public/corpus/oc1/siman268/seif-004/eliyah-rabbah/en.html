If he thought, etc. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 14, he brings proof from what the Raavyah wrote, that one who recited the blessing over the counting of the Omer and thought that the day was the fourth, and recited the blessing with intent for the fourth, while it was actually the fifth, has not fulfilled the obligation even if he concluded with the fifth, end quote. This requires study, for the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 489 in the name of the Mordechai that the reason of the Raavyah is because he holds that counting the Omer is according to the Torah; see there. If so, how does Terumas HaDeshen bring proof from there regarding prayer, which is D'Rabbanan? It seems to me that there a reason is needed that it is according to the Torah because the Gemara was in doubt, perhaps we follow the conclusion. But here we certainly follow the opening, for if he began with Atah Chonen he completes that blessing.
