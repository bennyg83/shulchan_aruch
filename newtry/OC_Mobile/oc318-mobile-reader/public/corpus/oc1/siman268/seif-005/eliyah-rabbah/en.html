[Levush] Even if he is standing in Atah Kidashta, etc. The Magen Avraham objected, for we hold in siman 209 that a blessing is not ruined merely by lack of intent, and therefore the Rav Beit Yosef omitted what is cited in Terumas HaDeshen, even if he is standing in Atah Kidashta, etc., end quote. In my humble opinion, this is not difficult at all, for in siman 209 we do not approach the matter from that reason, but because perhaps we follow the conclusion, as I explained. So too it is explicitly proven in the Beit Yosef, siman 489, that a blessing is ruined through lack of intent. Also what he wrote, that the Rav Beit Yosef omitted it, etc., is astonishing, for he brought it explicitly in the Beit Yosef. And if his intent is that he omitted it in the Shulchan Aruch, that is no proof at all, for he did not need to write it, since it refers to what he wrote earlier in seif 2, whether in Maariv or in Shacharis, etc. Furthermore, this siman is primarily about the law of the Maariv prayer; on the contrary, if he did not hold this way, he should have explained that it refers to Shacharis. Moreover, since the Shulchan Aruch wrote at the end, even if he is in the Shacharis prayer, etc., necessarily the opening clause also discusses Maariv and Minchah; for if not, what is meant by 'even,' since the end clause would be the opposite of the opening clause, and the opening clause would be speaking only of Shacharis? So too in Sefer Mishpat Tzedek he wrote in the name of the Shulchan Aruch that even in Atah Kidashta the law is this way, and so is the primary ruling.
