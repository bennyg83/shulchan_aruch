Paragraph 5: For Shabbos, etc.; although he mentioned Yom Tov in the middle, etc. In siman 487 Magen Avraham retracts this and answers his present question by explaining that siman 487 concerns one who did not mention Yom Tov in the middle.
It must be that it is worse, etc.; with another day's conclusion. On Yom Tov, concluding for Shabbos has no relevance and does not fulfill the obligation. Weekday blessings on Shabbos do relate to Shabbos, since by fundamental law eighteen blessings could have been said, so that prayer can fulfill it.
In Kol Bo, etc. See siman 422: Darkei Moshe implies one doubtful whether he mentioned Shabbos need not repeat, while Kol Bo implies repetition. Magen Avraham says even on Rosh Chodesh one doubtful about Yaaleh VeYavo should repeat because experience shows habitual omission. Taz distinguishes whether immediately before the mention he knew it was Rosh Chodesh. The same distinction may apply here: did he know it was Shabbos immediately before beginning prayer?
Rosh implies specifically Musaf, etc. Rosh says only that one who prayed eighteen for Musaf and mentioned the Musaf offering fulfills the obligation; he does not state this for all prayers, implying a Musaf-specific rule. Perhaps because the Yerushalmi explicitly records eighteen blessings for Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed Musaf with the Musaf offering added, Shabbos too is fundamentally fit for that form and works after the fact. Shacharis on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed always used eighteen with Yaaleh VeYavo; there is no corresponding Shabbos form of eighteen plus a Shabbos mention. Yet perhaps Rosh would indeed validate Yom Tov Shacharis with eighteen and Yaaleh VeYavo.
Rambam also implies so. He states without distinction that one who prayed a weekday prayer on Shabbos did not fulfill it, without exempting one who mentioned Shabbos in the middle; apparently no form suffices.
