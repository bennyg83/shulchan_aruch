And while standing. Turei Zahav. The statement in Shabbos 119 that even an individual who recites it becomes a partner concerns its recitation within the prayer. After the prayer, however, an individual does not recite it, since Tur writes that it is recited together; the textual reading ‘individually’ is erroneous. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 14, writes that according to the first reason, because of the additional Yom Tov following Shabbos, an individual should recite it, but not while standing.
In the chapter Kol Kisvei, Shabbos 119b: Rabbi Chiya said in the name of Mar Ukva: Whoever recites Vayechulu, two ministering angels place their hands upon his head and say, ‘Your iniquity has departed and your sin shall be atoned.’ Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 12, writes in the name of his grandfather the Gaon that while saying ‘through His utterance all was created,’ one should reflect upon repentance; the same applies here. He also cites Rabbi David Abudraham that one who does not testify bears his iniquity. Since they recite testimony, on Shabbos night one should therefore seek a minyan for Vayechulu as well, because testimony before the Holy One, blessed is He, requires ten, analogous to ‘I shall be sanctified among the Children of Israel.’ Darkei Moshe cites Rabbi David Abudraham that one should pause between ‘the heavens’ and ‘and the earth,’ and between ‘God’ and ‘created.’ The same pause is made in Kiddush. According to this, the practice of standing during Kiddush when an individual recites Vayechulu has no reason, because an individual cannot give testimony. It may be appropriate only when a minyan is present in the home. See below in the laws of Kiddush, siman 271, Turei Zahav, paragraph 13.
