And standing See Taz. That which is stated in Shabbat 119, even an individual who says it becomes a partner, refers to within the prayer; but after the prayer, no, since Tur wrote that they say it "together"; this is a scribal error and should read "individually." Eliyah Rabbah, letter 14, wrote according to one reason that because of Yom Tov after Shabbat, an individual should say it, but not standing.
In the chapter Kol Kitvei, Shabbat 119b: Rabbi Chisda said in the name of Mar Ukva, whoever says Vayechulu, two ministering angels place their hands on his head and say, "your iniquity is removed and your sin atoned." Eliyah Rabbah 12 in the name of his grandfather the Gaon wrote that just as in Even HaEzer regarding testimony for gittin they should contemplate repentance, the same applies here. In the name of Radak he wrote that one bears sin if he does not testify, "and he shall bear his iniquity," for they are saying testimony. Therefore on Friday night one should seek a minyan also because of Vayechulu, which requires ten as testimony to the Holy One, blessed be He, like "I shall be sanctified among the children of Israel." Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Radak that one should pause between "for Your name" and "purpose," and between "God" and "et," as in Eliyah Rabbah 1. In Keriat Shema as well one pauses similarly, etc. According to this, I do not know the reason for the practice to stand during Kiddush when saying Vayechulu alone, for there is no testimony by an individual, unless there is a minyan in the house; then one can say so. God willing, below in the laws of Kiddush this will be explained in siman 271, Taz letter 13.
