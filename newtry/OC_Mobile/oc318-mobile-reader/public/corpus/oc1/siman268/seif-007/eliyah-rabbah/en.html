Some say, etc. This is surprising, for in the Beit Yosef he wrote this entire seif in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket without any dispute. Even the first opinion in seif 2 appears to agree here, since he mentioned nothing of the Mussaf prayer. I later saw that in Tanya, page 30, he cited the law of one who switched in general terms and did not distinguish between Mussaf and the others; perhaps for this reason the Beit Yosef wrote it in the name of 'some say.' Nevertheless, as practical law it seems to me that he must go back, and so wrote the Magen Avraham. The Magen Avraham wrote: It seems to me that if he fulfilled the obligation of Mussaf, he afterwards prays Shacharis, end quote. This requires study, for he wrote in seif katan 9 that if he said Mussaf in place of Shacharis, he must go back. It appears that if he already prayed Mussaf for Mussaf, he must go back and pray Shacharis. In the Magen Avraham one must read: if he did not fulfill, etc.; it seems to me to be a scribal error, and he meant that when he has not yet prayed Mussaf as Mussaf, he prays Shacharis. It is also possible that since he spoke falsely before the Omnipresent, he must go back; but it is strained to say that since he later prays Shacharis in place of Mussaf, this is called going back. Perhaps this is what he means: if he intends that the Shacharis prayer should serve as the Mussaf prayer, he has fulfilled Mussaf, and he later prays Shacharis, for since he initially intends it to be Mussaf, he fulfills the obligation of Mussaf. But this is difficult, for it is explicit at the beginning of siman 286.
[Levush] Testimony, etc. For this reason my elder, the Gaon of blessed memory, said that the teaching that whoever recites Vayechulu has the ministering angels who accompany him say, 'and your sin is removed,' etc., is because we say in Even HaEzer that witnesses to a get are told to think thoughts of teshuvah, since a wicked person is invalid for testimony; therefore they say to him, 'and your sin is removed.' Rabbi David Abudraham wrote that 'and your sin is removed' means that he is not included in 'if he does not tell, he shall bear his iniquity,' for they are giving testimony. In the Midrash it says that the word 'asher' appears three times in Vayechulu, as in the passage of the red heifer, to teach that one who says Vayechulu three times is atoned. In Tzror HaMor he gives another reason for the three times, corresponding to the three worlds, the upper, middle, and lower worlds, to say that the Holy One, blessed be He, created them all. Rabbeinu Yerucham, page 102, writes that because there is strength in three times, one says it three times: in the prayer, after the prayer, and over Kiddush. See seif katan 14 and siman 271, seif katan 19.
[Levush] To testify together, etc. This requires study, for in Choshen Mishpat, siman 28, he wrote that each witness says his testimony separately. One must say as the Sema wrote in seif katan 37, that after each one says it separately, they say it together. Nevertheless, according to the conclusion, this does not remain so (Magen Avraham). To me it seems that from the Shulchan Aruch in Choshen Mishpat, siman 30, seif 9, it is implied that specifically in monetary cases each one says his testimony separately. Moreover, this is comparable to testimony in capital cases, for through their testimony they render Shabbos desecrators liable to death. This requires study for me, whether in capital testimony we require that they testify together. See Rambam, chapter 2 of Kesubos, Mishnah 8.
