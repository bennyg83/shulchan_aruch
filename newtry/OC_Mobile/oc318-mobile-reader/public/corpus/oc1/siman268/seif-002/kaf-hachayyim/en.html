5) [Seif 2] He completes that blessing, etc. Strictly speaking, one should pray all eighteen weekday blessings even on Shabbos, including Mussaf. The Sages did not burden people with them because of the honor of Shabbos. Since he began one, however, he completes it. Some say that by strict law the eighteen blessings were not required in Mussaf. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
6) There: He completes that blessing, etc. One to whom this occurs should be concerned throughout the week, because it is an unfavorable sign. He should examine his deeds and contemplate repentance. Shelah, folio 193a; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 199a.
7) There: He completes that blessing, etc. See Chikrei Lev, siman 52, who raises the following question: if one began the weekday prayer, continued in the ordinary manner, and remembered during Shomea Tefillah, does he complete that blessing or stop? One might argue that eighteen blessings were instituted, but nineteen were not. He concludes as a matter of practical halachah that one completes it; see there. Shalmei Tzibbur cites this and writes that there is no basis for doubt, because the Gemara and halachic authorities make no distinction. Even if he remembers only during Shomea Tefillah, he completes that blessing and then begins the Shabbos blessing; see there.
8) Chikrei Lev further raises a question there in siman 53 regarding a prayer leader who erred and began a weekday blessing: does he complete that blessing, or should he stop because of the congregation's burden? He concludes that reason favors not completing it, but the matter requires further analysis; see there. Shalmei Tzibbur cites this and writes that regarding the prayer leader's repetition, which he does not require for himself because he already prayed silently, it is obvious that he need not complete that blessing; see there. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, writes that even if he needs that prayer, such as when he did not pray silently and intends to fulfill his obligation through the public repetition, as stated in siman 124, seif 2, if he erred and began a weekday prayer, he does not complete that blessing; see there.
9) There: Whether in Shacharis or Mussaf, etc. This is one of the laws regarding which Maran retracted in the Shulchan Aruch from what he wrote in Beis Yosef. Beis Yosef writes that we adopt the view of Rambam and those who follow him, that during Mussaf one stops in the middle of the blessing, whereas here the Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification like Raavad, Rosh, and those who follow them. As a matter of practical halachah, we adopt Maran's ruling in Beis Yosef because in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently. Bach rules this way. Eliyah Rabbah's words also incline this way, and Rema of Fano rules this way in the manuscript Alfasi Zuta. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2. Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 198d; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, also rule this way. The later authorities agree.
10) There: Some say that during Mussaf he stops, etc. The same applies on Rosh Chodesh. Kol Bo. It follows that the same applies on Yom Tov. Mateh Moshe. This includes the second day of Yom Tov. Similarly, throughout the laws in this siman, Shabbos and Yom Tov are equivalent. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1. See below, paragraph 18.
11) There: Even in the middle of a blessing. This applies even during Atah Chonen, contrary to Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah. Knesses HaGedolah, siman 267, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
