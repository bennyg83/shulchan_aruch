1) [Seif 1] He recites Vayechulu, etc. Rav Hamnuna said: Whoever prays and recites Vayechulu is regarded as though he became a partner with the Holy One, blessed is He, in the act of Creation. Do not read it as vayechulu, 'they were completed,' but vayechallu, 'they completed,' meaning the Holy One, blessed is He, and that person. Shabbos 119b; Tur; Taz, paragraph 1.
1) They further said there in the Gemara: Whoever prays on Friday night and recites Vayechulu, the two ministering angels who accompany him place their hands upon his head and say to him, 'Your iniquity has departed, and your sin will be atoned.' Therefore, one should contemplate repentance before reciting Vayechulu, because atonement comes through repentance, as is known.
2) There: He recites Vayechulu in the Maariv prayer. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes that the custom is to say vayanuchu vah in Maariv, vo in Shacharis, and vam in Minchah. The later authorities cite this. Mor U'Ketziah writes that the writings of the Arizal make no distinction by day, and even at Minchah one says vo. These are his words. The Sefer HaKavanos available to us does not mention this at all and does not discuss this detail. The disciples of the Arizal, however, upheld the custom and wrote an esoteric explanation, as stated in Mishnas Chassidim. This is specifically the custom of those versed in the mysteries who follow the Arizal, and it should not be changed. In particular, the words of Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah demonstrate that this was the widespread custom. I later found that Ramaz, in his glosses to Sefer HaKavanos, writes: You already know that the correct text is vah in Maariv, vo in Shacharis, and vam in Minchah. He explains its esoteric meaning; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 7; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 195b. Nevertheless, the pious members of Beis El in the holy city of Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt and established speedily in our days, who follow the Arizal, customarily say vah in Maariv and vo during the day, even at Minchah. Shemen Sasson writes the same in Pischei Einayim in the order of Shabbos Minchah; see there. Similarly, in Retzei VeHachalitzeinu in Birkas HaMazon, one says vah at night and vo during the day. Therefore, since this is not explicitly mentioned in the Arizal's own words, each community may follow its established practice. Those who say vayanuchu vam at Minchah should likewise say at Minchah, 'Grant us as an inheritance, Hashem our God, with love and favor, Your holy Shabbosos,' in the plural, corresponding to the plural expression vayanuchu vam, as Petach HaDevir, siman 292, paragraph 4, writes; see there.
3) There: They recite Vayechulu, etc. In some places it is not recited within the prayer, but only afterward. Every custom has support. Those who recite it have support in the Zohar and Tikkunim, and those who do not have support in Tosefta, Berachos, chapter 3. Maharash Shaar Aryeh in his manuscript glosses; Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 1d and folio 71c, however, gives an esoteric reason for the three recitations of Vayechulu instituted by the Sages: one within the Amidah, one after the Amidah, and one at the table as part of Kiddush; see there. One should therefore neither add to nor subtract from these recitations.
4) There: They recite Vayechulu, etc. Nevertheless, if one did not recite it in the prayer, he does not repeat the prayer, especially according to our practice of reciting Vayechulu in Kiddush. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. This applies specifically if he already concluded the blessing Mekadesh HaShabbos. Before concluding it, he returns to Vayechulu. Chayei Adam, klal 28, paragraph 1. It appears to me that if he remembers before beginning Retzei, he should recite it there. Since it is part of the established prayer text, it is not considered an interruption. If he already began Retzei, he should recite Vayechulu twice after the Amidah so that the three recitations are not lacking, because they have an esoteric reason, as stated in the preceding paragraph.
