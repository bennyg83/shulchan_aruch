He recites. Turei Zahav. See Shabbos 119b, Rashi’s explanation, and Iyun Yaakov regarding the textual reading, ‘The Holy One, blessed is He, and he completed it.’ This person thereby relates the praise of the Omnipresent, Who rested on Shabbos. The word Vayechulu is written without a yud, and is expounded as though it said, ‘they completed’; the Holy One, blessed is He, and the person who testifies that the Holy One, blessed is He, created His world. See Maharsha there. Rabbi Yitzchak Alfasi’s text reads: ‘The sixth day; the heavens were completed.’ The Holy One, blessed is He, created through speech, while the person accepts Shabbos through prayer while it is still day and thereby hastens to accept Shabbos; he is then prohibited from labor. The Gemara’s expression, ‘Whoever prays on Erev Shabbos and recites Vayechulu,’ does not refer to one who prays on Shabbos night. See siman 267 regarding praying early, and the statement of Rabbi Yose, ‘May my portion be,’ etc. One can question whether accepting Shabbos differs, and from where the Rabbis derive permission to pray Maariv early. Rav Hamnuna’s statement provides a proper source. Maharsha, of blessed memory, writes there that even an individual, whose testimony is not valid testimony, recites it; see there. This implies that it is obligatory. The prayer leader repeats Vayechulu to discharge one who is not proficient, as stated in seif 7. On a weekday the prayer leader does not repeat the prayer, since that repetition is optional; see Derishah. In any event, if he did not recite Vayechulu in the prayer, he is not sent back, all the more so according to our practice of reciting Vayechulu in Kiddush. See Eshel Avraham at the end of the siman.
