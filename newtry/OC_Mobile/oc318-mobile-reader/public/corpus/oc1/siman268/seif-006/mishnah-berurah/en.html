(14) He does not repeat. The principal element of the fourth blessing is 'favor our rest,' which appears in every Shabbos blessing. This applies only after he completed the blessing; if he remembers during it, he stops.
(15) And some say, etc. The later authorities wonder why this is introduced as 'some say,' since everyone agrees.
(16) Mussaf with another blessing. Even if the other blessing also concerns Shabbos, such as Yismach Moshe, since it does not mention the Shabbos Mussaf offering (and certainly if it is an entirely different prayer) the preceding rule applies: if he moved his feet, he repeats from the beginning; otherwise he returns to Tikanta Shabbos. Chayei Adam writes that if he remembers after completing the middle blessing but before beginning Retzeh, he should say, 'May we perform before You the Mussaf offering,' thereby fulfilling his obligation. See Biur Halachah for more.
(17) Another prayer during Mussaf. He recited Tikanta Shabbos intending Shacharis. Although the blessing mentions Shabbos, he must repeat because he said Mussaf within his prayer and spoke falsely before God. As above, if he moved his feet he returns to the beginning and properly recites a different prayer for Shacharis. He does not repeat Mussaf, since his first prayer fulfilled Mussaf despite his Shacharis intent. If he remembers before moving his feet, wherever he stands (even during one of the final three blessings) he stops and begins Yismach Moshe. See Biur Halachah.
