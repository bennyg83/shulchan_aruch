(א) On the mouth of the barrel, etc., because of ohel — and even though it is permitted to return the pot on the stove and we are not concerned because of ohel, since the partitions are already made [and in an ohel such as this where his intent is not for an ohel but only incidentally it becomes such, it is forbidden only if he also makes partitions, as stated in seif 3 in Mishna Berurah] — one can say that since the cover is very wide it becomes like an ohel [Magen Avraham in the name of Tosafos and the Ran]; and therefore what the Shulchan Aruch wrote "barrel" is also in great width; and Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 6 that the same applies to a wide chest that has a covering on it, if it is not fixed in hinges — forbidden to place on it on Shabbat; and a chest that is not so wide — permitted to cover it, and there is no ohel concern in this, since the partitions are already made. And where one moves it from this place to stand it in another place and covers it there — forbidden in all cases, for through standing it, it is as if he makes the partitions together with the covering; and therefore one must be careful at large meals where they place tables on barrels — one must be careful to turn the open side of the barrel toward the ground and on the closed side place the table [Chayei Adam]. He further wrote that a tablecloth that slips entirely from the table — if there is a tefach in the depth of its hollow, forbidden to return it, for he makes an ohel; and likewise a bed called shelf in English that has a cover and it is not attached with hinges — one may not return the cover on it, for there is making ohel in this; nevertheless one should not protest those who are lenient in all this, for they have upon whom to rely [they are the view of the Raavad and Rashba that I will bring below in s.k. 49, and also the view of the Bach, see there]; and see in Biur Halacha:
(ב) Lacking a tefach — meaning that it is not full; for otherwise there is no ohel here except when it lacks, that from the covering until what is inside the cover there is a tefach — therefore it is called an ohel. And the view of the Raavad and Rashba is that in every case there is no ohel prohibition in covering vessels; and that which they forbade in the Gemara to cover the entire cover [of a vat] — because it appears as straining; and it is fit to be stringent as the Shulchan Aruch, who is the view of many early authorities; and therefore a large barrel of water — one may not cover it entirely on Shabbat when it is not entirely full; and those who are lenient in practice — one should not protest them, for they have upon whom to rely, as stated at the end of s.k. 48:
