On the mouth of a barrel. In the Gemara it cites half a cover — permitted, for it is not a tent on the whole cover — forbidden; and nevertheless one requires it be lacking a handbreadth so there is a hollow of a handbreadth and tent language applies. Beit Yosef wrote — and Tosafos challenged all these are forbidden only where one fixes the tent in partitions; but if one made the tent without partitions — permitted; and therefore permitted to return a pot on the stove on Shabbat since the partitions were made from the start; but one could ask — for we say in Shabbos this cover on the whole cover is forbidden and there the partitions were already made; and one may say — since the cover is very wide it becomes like a tent — end of their words; and if so it is difficult on Tur who wrote the language of a barrel — it implies even if not very wide; and in truth Tosafos' words are astounding — what measure is there for the width of the utensil? And it appears to me to resolve their difficulty in this way: tent prohibition applies only if one first makes on Shabbat complete partitions so one can use there due to the partitions — that is, to place something dry there — and then if one makes a cover above due to tent he is liable specifically if he makes a cover above; but with this cover that has liquid inside as we find many times in Avodah Zarah that they cite this language of what they fill with wine — if so whenever there is no cover it is not fit to use at all since now one cannot place something dry there since there is liquid inside; and this use with liquid is not fit since something might fall into it from above — only one must cover it; if so both together — the cover and partitions — make it fit to use; and since he covers it, it is as making a partition too — for only now it is fit to use whereas initially it was as if there were no partitions — as appears to me very correct; and it is difficult — since there is no prohibition for a tent without partitions, why did they forbid the hat on Shabbat in siman 310 seif 40 due to tent when there are no partitions? And it appears everything made for shade from the outset — since they make a rim around it — is forbidden even without partitions; except when doing for another purpose and the tent comes about by itself — then partitions are also required; and this is also a reason to permit our britel as written in seif 8. And according to this it must be said that on a utensil tent matter applies when covering — and not like Ra'avad and Rashba that Beit Yosef brings here that there is no tent matter in covering utensils; and here on the cover they forbade because it appears as guarding; and one may say: covering utensils that one covers on Shabbat mentioned above in siman 314 seif 7 — there when opening the cover tied by rope it is still attached at the pegs behind it as mentioned above — and if so anything entirely removed is forbidden to return for it makes a tent; and if so one must be careful with small boxes called escutcheons that have a cover not tied there — only when needed one pulls the whole cover and then returns it — one must be careful not to extend all the cover when opening — for it is demolishing a tent; and likewise when returning it one makes a tent; and according to what Tosafos wrote — only on a cover forbidden because it is very wide — one need not forbid this; but according to what I wrote that all needing to cover it is forbidden — there is prohibition also in escutcheons if there is a hollow of a handbreadth — and I did not see people careful; but according to my humble opinion the essence is that the Sages did not forbid covering due to tent except if not designated — like that cover where one takes a garment from elsewhere and covers the barrel — unlike a cover designated for them and its manner is always thus — it does not appear as making a tent; therefore returning a pot on the stove is permitted since its manner is always thus; and likewise all utensil covering — so it should be read here — that one does not make partitions on Shabbat, only the cover alone; and this is a correct distinction:
