(s.k. 11) Making an ohel, etc. — know that in the first chapter they hang, daf 137b: R' Acha hangs the guard on Yom Tov, etc., and the Sages say one does not hang; and on Shabbat according to all one does not hang, for he makes a temporary ohel; and they asked them: what is "he hung"? R' Yosef said: he is liable for a chatas; Abaye said to him: rather from now he hung it like a peg — so too here he is liable; rather Abaye said: from rabbinic law, lest he do as he does on chol; and Rashi explained: from rabbinic law they forbade him, the Rabbis of the Mishnah do not let him hang, to do a weekday act, end of his words; and Tosafos wrote, and this is the language: even though regarding a peg there is not even a rabbinic prohibition in rabbinic matters one can well distinguish; but if the guard is by Torah law forbidden, one cannot distinguish, end of the words of Tosafos. It appears from their words that even according to Abaye's conclusion, the prohibition of hanging the guard is because of temporary ohel, and therefore they asked also per the conclusion like a peg; but the Rambam in his work chapter 21 law 17 wrote, and this is the language: and likewise it is forbidden to hang the guard as he does on chol, lest he come to guard, end of his words; and in a responsum he added to explain the matter, for they asked him whether it is permitted to hang a peg, and he answered that since the reason in the guard is only lest he come to guard — this does not apply to a peg, and the Sages did not forbid a weekday act on Shabbat except where there is concern that he will do a Torah melacha; and with a peg it is impossible through hanging to come to a Torah prohibition — behold in what he gave as the reason for the prohibition of a weekday act, lest he come to guard, the question of Tosafos cited above is resolved; and Magen Avraham did not bring the Rambam's responsum, even though in his work also he wrote the reason of the prohibition of a weekday act is lest he come to guard; but in the responsum he wrote explicitly that according to this reason it is permitted to hang a peg. However, what Magen Avraham concludes as he stated — the explanation of the Rambam — and in this the Beit Yosef expounded in vain — I do not know why; behold even so he explains the words of the Rambam and the responsum; and perhaps his intent is regarding what is written in the sugya, that he challenged R' Eliezer who permits hanging the guard: now according to R' Eliezer, to add to temporary ohel is forbidden, as we learned the window plug — R' Eliezer says if tied, etc., and if not, one may not plug with it — and how does he permit here to make temporary ohel l'chatchila? And he answered, see there; and per the Rambam's view, what is the difficulty? For per the conclusion, there is no ohel prohibition in hanging the guard, see there, etc.; and Magen Avraham's intent is to resolve this difficulty for the Rambam — for certainly the prohibition of making temporary ohel with partitions, the reason is that we decree lest he make a permanent ohel; and in hanging the guard, where there is no making of partitions, why should we decree lest permanent ohel? Nevertheless such ohel is forbidden because of lest he come to guard; if so, the prohibition of making ohel has two reasons: sometimes the reason is lest he make permanent ohel, and sometimes the reason is lest he come to guard; in any case the prohibition there is making ohel, only they differ in the reasons, as stated; and let us see which decree is closer and fitting to forbid — therefore the decree lest he come to guard, for the Sages permit adding temporary ohel and forbid hanging the guard; and therefore the Gemara challenged well: now according to R' Eliezer, temporary ohel addition is forbidden, and he decreed in it because of permanent ohel — all the more so one can say to forbid the guard for making ohel from concern lest he come to guard, which is a closer decree than ohel; but therefore Magen Avraham abbreviated more than necessary:
