[7] A bed that is, etc. Beit Yosef cites Ran as saying "our beds, etc.," and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 23, has the same wording. He explains that the space beneath a bed is used for placing sandals. Shulchan Aruch and Levush omit the word "our" because they follow their view that walls are specifically required. Sefer Tal Orot notes a question on Semag: concerning a bed he writes that the walls must reach the ground, implying that if they do not reach the ground it is not a tent, whereas concerning a bridal canopy he writes that even a tefach creates a tent. The answer is that a canopy is primarily made as a tent, whereas a bed is not recognizably made as a tent unless its walls reach the ground. Maharshal's commentary likewise implies this. See seif 11 of this siman.
[8] It is permitted, etc. Because he does nothing constructive, but merely sets it in place. Therefore it is permitted to erect and remove a wedding canopy. The same applies to a shelf fixed to the synagogue wall on which books are placed (Magen Avraham; Taz).
