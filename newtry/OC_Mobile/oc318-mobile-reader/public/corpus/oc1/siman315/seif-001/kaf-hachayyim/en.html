1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to make a covering on Shabbos and Yom Tov, etc. This is a decree because of building. Adding to a temporary covering is permitted, as stated in seif 2. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Rambam writes in chapter 22: One who makes a permanent covering is liable for building. Therefore, one may not initially make a temporary covering or dismantle one, by rabbinic decree lest one make or dismantle a permanent covering. If one built or dismantled a temporary covering, he is exempt. These are his words. Rashi in the chapter Kol HaKelim explains a temporary covering as one made for the moment and not intended to remain permanently. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. If one places a sheet upon four poles and ties it there, or even leaves it untied but intends it to remain for an extended time, it is a permanent covering and he is liable to bring a sin offering. This applies when it is at least one tefach by one tefach. One is also liable to bring a sin offering for adding to a permanent covering. It is possible that one is likewise liable for a permanent side partition measuring one tefach by one tefach. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 8; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. See below, paragraph 14.
2) There: It is forbidden to make a covering on Shabbos, etc. The mosquito net customarily used for sleeping in Egypt may not be erected, spread, or gathered on Shabbos, and it does not help for one tefach of it to have been spread before Shabbos. Ginas Veradim, klal 3, siman 26. Rabbi Perach Shoshan, klal 3, siman 4, permits it when one tefach was spread before Shabbos. Regrettably, the improper practice has spread in Egypt of having it spread and gathered on Shabbos through a non-Jew even when none of it was spread before Shabbos, and some even do so through a Jew. One whose heart has been touched by Heaven should ensure that at least one tefach in width is spread before Shabbos, and on Shabbos have a non-Jew finish spreading it. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. See above, siman 240, paragraph 47.
3) An umbrella made to protect from sun and rain may not be carried on Shabbos or Yom Tov, and certainly may not be opened. One may, however, tell a non-Jew to carry it to shelter him. Responsa Michtam LeDavid, siman 8; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2. Responsa Givas Pinchas, manuscript, volume 5, siman 19, however, wrote a complete response to all the preceding authority's arguments and concludes that if an umbrella was open before Yom Tov, it is plainly permitted to carry it. He regularly ruled that an umbrella opened before Yom Tov may be used on Yom Tov, and he saw that his teachers did not object to those who did so. On Shabbos, however, the prohibition of carrying creates grounds for a decree; see there. Birkei Yosef cites this in Shiyurei Berachah. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Orach Chaim, siman 30, who discusses this at length and concludes that when it was prepared before Shabbos, arguments can be made both to forbid and permit it. In any event, it must be forbidden because of appearances, for who knows whether it was prepared the preceding day, especially in this bereft generation; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 4, likewise discusses the matter at length and concludes that it is forbidden; see there. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 3, cites these authorities and concludes that the universal practice is to forbid it even when it was open the preceding day. This is the accepted stringent ruling, and one who is lenient may cause the public to stumble, Heaven forbid; see there. Accordingly, since the prohibition is because of making a covering and because of appearances, as stated above, there is no difference between Shabbos and Yom Tov. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Shemos, paragraph 8, rules the same and writes that since it may not be carried over one's head, it is also muktzeh and may not be moved on Shabbos or Yom Tov. This is the accepted practice.
4) There: Even a temporary covering. For example, spreading a mat over four poles as a shelter from the sun. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi; Levush.
5) There: This applies specifically to a roof, but partitions are permitted. A partition is not a covering and may be spread for privacy. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi. This applies only when it is temporary. If it is permanent, it is possible that one is liable, as stated above at the end of paragraph 1; see there. See below, paragraph 9.
6) There: Unless it is made to permit, etc. This is Tosafos' view, contrary to Rashi, who holds that even a partition that creates a halachic permission may be made on Shabbos when it is a side partition, as explained in Beis Yosef. This applies, however, only to a partition ten tefachim high that reaches the ground or comes within less than three tefachim of the ground, as stated in Beis Yosef, siman 502. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. See paragraph 32.
7) There: Unless it is made to permit, etc. This reasoning does not apply to covering excrement. Just as one may cover it directly, when one erects a partition that completely conceals it, this does not function as a partition but as a covering. Eshel Avraham from Hagahos Kodesh; Orach Chaim, paragraph 1. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 6, also appears to imply this. The matter is nevertheless questionable, because sacred books may likewise be covered, yet making a partition for them is forbidden, as stated below in the gloss. See paragraphs 16 and 17.
8) There: To render a sukkah valid, etc. For example, when it has only two walls and one wishes to make a temporary third partition to render it valid in the manner explained below in siman 630. To permit carrying means that one wishes to make a temporary partition in a place where carrying is forbidden so that the partition will permit carrying, as explained in siman 362; see there. Since it is regarded as a partition for purposes of permitting carrying or validating a sukkah, it is like building.
9) There, gloss: But a partition made for privacy, etc. For example, a partition erected during a discourse between men and women is permitted. Beis Yosef in the name of Mordechai, chapter Kirah; Taz, paragraph 1. See above, paragraph 5.
10) There, gloss: Although it is fixed there. Since the entire curtain moves in an ordinary wind and does not obstruct people walking and passing there, it is considered nonpermanent. If it is fixed above, below, and at the sides so that it does not move from its place, it is forbidden because of building. An ordinary curtain, however, is not fixed so thoroughly and is therefore permitted. Beis Yosef. It is forbidden, however, to add to a permanent covering. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. This implies that adding to a permanent covering at least one tefach wide is forbidden by the Torah. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. See above at the end of paragraph 1.
11) One whose sukkah collapsed on the first night of Yom Tov may not tell a non-Jew to place the sechach upon it. Since it was made to last eight or nine days, it is a permanent covering and is forbidden by the Torah. The Sages permitted having a non-Jew perform an act for a mitzvah only when it constitutes two layers of rabbinic prohibition. Eshel Avraham there.
12) There, gloss: Likewise, a curtain before the Aron Kodesh, etc. It is obvious that this is permitted. Since the Aron already has a door, the curtain is hung only for privacy, as stated in Beis Yosef. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Panim Meiros, siman 30, writes that it is permitted even if the Aron Kodesh has no doors. Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef. It appears obvious to me that decorative painted sheets may be hung on Shabbos even though they remain fixed there, because they are not made as a partition. Magen Avraham there; Yad Aharon there. This means that although they are fixed and do not sway because they are tied above, below, and at the sides, they are nevertheless permitted because they are not made as a partition. The wall of the house already forms a partition, so this resembles the curtain before the Aron. Machatzis HaShekel. See below, seif 12.
13) There, gloss: Before the sun, etc. This means to prevent sunlight from falling upon a person or food. Beis Yosef in the name of Mordechai. It is permitted because the partition is not permanent and does not create any halachic permission. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5.
