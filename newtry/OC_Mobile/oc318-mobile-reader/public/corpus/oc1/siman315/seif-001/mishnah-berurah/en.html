(א) Forbidden to make an ohel, etc. — for one who makes a permanent ohel, such as spreading a mat or sheets, etc., for an ohel and makes them so they will endure — even though this is not actual building, he is liable because of building, for making an ohel is a toldah of building; and one who demolishes it is liable because of demolishing; and they decreed upon temporary ohel because of permanent ohel, and likewise upon demolishing temporary ohel because of demolishing permanent ohel [Rambam]; and the details of the laws of permanent ohel will be explained below:
(ב) And specifically a roof — and even when there are no partitions beneath it, such as spreading a mat on four poles in a temporary manner to shade from sun and rain:
(ג) Permitted — for the essence of an ohel is the roof that shades over it, and therefore they decreed in it because of permanent ohel, unlike temporary partitions alone, which are not considered an ohel at all:
(ד) To release a sukkah, etc. — such as when it had only two walls and he made a temporary partition for the third wall, as is explained below in siman 630; for then this partition is considered as building through which the sukkah became fit; and the same applies when it releases that partition to move through it in a place where moving is forbidden — for since we consider it a partition for the matter of permitting moving, it is considered building:
(ה) For mere modesty — such as to separate between men and women when they hear the sermon:
(ו) That is fixed there — meaning that it is not made to remove from there but hangs there always; nevertheless it is considered a temporary partition, since it moves back and forth in a common wind and also does not impede passers on the way there; but to attach it above and below and from the sides in a manner that it is not moved from its place — forbidden because of building, for sealing it fixed is such:
(ז) And likewise the curtain — this is obvious that it is permitted, for since there is in any case a door to the Holy Ark, therefore the curtain is suspended only for mere modesty; and several later authorities are lenient even if there are no doors to the Holy Ark, such as a valance by the entrance — and as stated. Permitted to hang on Shabbat patterned sheets on the wall for decoration, and even to fix them so they will not be moved from their place — also permitted, since they are not made at all for a partition:
(ח) Provided he does not make, etc. — see seif 12:
(ט) And likewise permitted, etc. — it is obvious that all this is with a temporary partition; but to make a permanent partition that will endure thus — forbidden [Tosefta]:
(י) Before the light of the candle, etc. — for since it is forbidden to use before the light of the candle or books, it is called a releasing partition. And Magen Avraham wrote that this is specifically if he makes a partition ten tefachim high and the candle is high and visible above — for he darkens with his tallit and uses, as is implied in siman 301 seif 11 in the gloss [and in the Shelah it is implied that even in this manner one should be very careful, and see in Avodat HaKodesh there]; for since the candle is visible, therefore the permission here is only because there is on it a partition ten high, and we consider the candle thereby as standing in another room; and then he must also tie it so the wind will not move it — for otherwise there is no partition on it; and therefore it is forbidden to make it on Shabbat, for it is considered a releasing partition. But if the partition covers the entire candle until it is not seen — one need not that the partition be ten high specifically, since the candle is covered; therefore the permission here is not because of a partition but because of mere covering; and therefore even if the partition was ten high, it is permitted to make it, for it is not considered a releasing partition but only because of mere covering. And likewise regarding using and doing one's needs before books — if the partition is high until the books are not visible, it is permitted to make it, for we do not consider it a releasing partition but as mere covering; and therefore we do not require then also that it be tied below [and see in the later authorities who explained his words that nevertheless when he makes it on Shabbat, there must be additional covering on the books — then they will be considered as placed in a vessel within a vessel]. And behold, even though there are later authorities who question his distinction, and hold that since ultimately through the partition it is now permitted to use and do one's needs, it is considered a releasing partition and forbidden to make it — nevertheless it appears that in a time of need one may rely on his words. And in any case according to all it is permitted to cover the books with mere covering — namely a vessel within a vessel to permit use — for this is not a partition at all:
(כ) That was from erev Shabbat a tefach — and therefore a valance standing spread out a tefach from erev Shabbat is permitted to spread it entirely; but if it was folded, even though all of it is much more than a tefach, it does not help, since this was not done for a partition [Chayei Adam]:
