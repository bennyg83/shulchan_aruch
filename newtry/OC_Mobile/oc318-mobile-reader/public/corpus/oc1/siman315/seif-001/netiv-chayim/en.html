(In Magen Avraham s.k. 11) "lest he come to preserve." So it should read. And it appears to me: for what reason did he expound at length in vain, for in the Rambam making an ohel is not mentioned, only lest he come to preserve; and the Shulchan Aruch adopted the language of the Tur, which is according to Rashi and Tosafos; and as with a partition — nevertheless it is permitted, for one may distinguish in d'rabbanan prohibitions. And it must be said that it comes only to explain the Rambam — why he moved from the reason of ohel. For this he gave a reason: because there is no ohel without partitions — according to what is written in s.k. 21 see there; but the Tur holds that here they were stringent. But nevertheless it is difficult: they do not hang the sieve on Yom Tov — the Rambam wrote the reason that he not do an act of weekday. Although it is not relevant on Yom Tov "lest he come to preserve." But this is to answer with difficulty, since Abaye on daf 137 adopts the stringency of the mishnah regarding the sieve "he should not make"; and if he made — exempt but forbidden. And the Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Shabbat wrote: wherever they said exempt but forbidden — it is distancing from Torah labor; and therefore he needed to say regarding Shabbat "lest he come to preserve." And it is difficult; and further it is difficult per the Rambam's view: what does the Gemara on daf 137 challenge on R' Eliezer — "now adding onto a temporary ohel is forbidden l'chatchila" he should have required. For here it is not an ohel and the reason of the Rabbis is because of preserving; but R' Eliezer holds it is permitted to preserve. And upon all this I gave my heart to explain the Rambam's view: he deals where there was no utensil beneath the sieve and it is not similar to a stove; and nevertheless it is not an ohel according to what I found in Rashba in his novellae that an ohel is considered only when one needs to hollow space beneath it see there; and it must be said: since it was his intent to place a utensil there to preserve, it is considered an ohel. And according to my humble opinion on Shabbat where it is forbidden to preserve — he has no intent to place a utensil there; therefore it is not an ohel. And it is forbidden only because of an act of weekday — lest it be made for preserving; unlike on Yom Tov for the Rabbis and for R' Eliezer on Shabbat when they hang it — if so, his intent is to place a utensil beneath it, for it is permitted to preserve since it is already hung — for R' Eliezer on Shabbat and for the Rabbis on Yom Tov; if so, it is a temporary ohel; and now everything is settled — investigate:
