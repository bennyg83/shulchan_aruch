[1] In front of the candles, etc. Maharil, however, records Maharam as prohibiting. Perhaps in that case it was not certain that wind would enter through the crack and extinguish the candle, so the partition would have served no real purpose (Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah). This requires study. See siman 277, seif katan 2.
[2] [Levush] Permanent ones are prohibited, etc. Magen Avraham writes that decorative illustrated sheets may be hung even permanently because they are not intended as a partition. Therefore, a partition erected during a Shabbos sermon between men and women is permitted because it is for modesty (Mordechai). The same applies to protection from cold, etc., and rain (Rashi).
[3] So that he may have marital relations, etc. On Yom Tov it is permitted, because by strict law one could extinguish the candle, so the partition functions merely for modesty (Taz, siman 124). Even on Shabbos the prohibition applies specifically when the forbidden object is not fully covered and only a ten-tefach partition or an uncovered upper portion is involved. If it is fully covered all the way up, so that even less than ten tefachim suffices for either a candle or sacred books, it is permitted because it is merely a covering (Magen Avraham). There is room to question whether one can equate a covering with a partition. It appears that when ten tefachim high it should be prohibited because it is still a partition, but when lower it may be permitted. Taz writes that one may simply cover sacred books with two layers of clothing. See siman 240, seif katan 14. It is obvious that if a partition already protrudes one tefach from the side in front of the books, one may hang an extension before it, because this is adding to an existing tent, as stated at the beginning of siman 313. Magen Avraham writes that for a partition whose purpose is to create permission, strings do not help. See seif 10.
