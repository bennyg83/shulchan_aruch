(א) A folded tallit — that they place it on a pole placed between two walls and the two ends of the fold hang down and reach the ground, and he enters between the two ends and sleeps because of the sun; and it would be forbidden to do so on Shabbat, for it is called temporary ohel and exempt but forbidden, as is explained in seif 18; and he comes to say that if when the tallit was folded and placed on the pole from erev Shabbat there were threads hanging on it through which one spreads it to the ends — it is permitted to spread it on Shabbat along the entire length of the pole; and the reason is that since through the threads it is easy to pull it with them, it is as if the tallit were already spread a tefach from erev Shabbat, and this is only adding to temporary ohel and permitted, as stated in seif 2. And Magen Avraham wrote that it deals when after spreading it there will not be a tefach in width in its roof from above, and also not within three adjacent to its roof a tefach in width — for if there is a tefach in width in it, it is like permanent ohel and the threads do not help:
(ב) And permitted to remove it — for every ohel in which there is no building prohibition, there is no demolishing prohibition:
(ג) And likewise the curtain — included in this is also the valance before the entrance; and see in Magen Avraham that this "and likewise" is not precise, that it is permitted in this even though there were no threads on it, because there is no ohel except what is made like a roof. And behold according to what the author of Magdol Oz brought in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, and so it is in the commentary of R' Chananel that came out recently close to light — that regarding a curtain one also needs threads wrapped on it from erev Shabbat to hang on them — it is better to say that the Rambam, author of this law, also holds like R' Chananel, and it comes out well "and likewise" according to its simple meaning, and so it is proven from the explanation of the Gra; however for practical law there is no difference, for it is proven from the Tur and Shulchan Aruch that it is permitted to hang in all cases; and so too it is proven from the Rama above in seif 1 in the gloss, that since it was not made to release, there is no prohibition on it at all.
