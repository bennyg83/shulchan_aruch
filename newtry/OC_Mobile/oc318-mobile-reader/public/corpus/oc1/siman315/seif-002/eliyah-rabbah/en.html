[4] For shade, etc. I am surprised how this differs from the end of the preceding siman, where a partition against the sun is permitted. The law here is from the end of Eruvin, while the earlier law is Mordechai's inference in chapter Kirah. Malbushei Yom Tov suggests the Gemara in Kirah implies permission only with a change. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. Here the structure is a roof and is always prohibited, whereas above the case concerns a vertical partition, which is prohibited only when made to create a halachic permission. This distinction is evident from Tur and Beit Yosef at the beginning of seif 7. Mordechai learned the earlier permission from chapter Kirah, not from Eruvin, except that Eruvin provides the leniency when a tefach already existed. It is possible Malbushei Yom Tov's phrase "the Gemara there" refers to chapter Kirah. His inference that only a changed manner is permitted can also readily be rejected, and Mordechai clearly permits a sun partition even without a change, as Shulchan Aruch rules. Olat Shabbat erred here by claiming that the incident at the end of Eruvin concerns a partition, since otherwise why would Tosafot on Shabbos 125 cite it as proof that the tent prohibition applies to partitions. In fact Tosafot, Rosh, and Ran make clear that the Eruvin case concerns a roof and cite it to show a dispute regarding a roof tent. The proof that the law of tent can apply to a partition comes from the fourth wall of a sukkah, not from Eruvin. Magen Avraham writes that the practice of placing wooden hoops over an infant's cradle and spreading a sheet over them is prohibited unless prepared in the manner described here.
[5] Except for the rolled portion, etc. Meaning the material wrapped around the circular roll is not counted toward the required tefach. Taz writes that to be stringent one should follow Rashi and require one tefach of actual covering material apart from what is wrapped around the utensil. It may be possible to be lenient because Bach writes that Rashi himself agrees with the Tosefta.
