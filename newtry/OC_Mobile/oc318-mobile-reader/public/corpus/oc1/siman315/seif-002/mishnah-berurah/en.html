(א) And they bend their heads, etc. — namely that they make like hoops [called reifes] across the width of the ship at the top, and do so with many woods adjacent to one another along the entire length of the ship; and the same applies to a baby's cradle that has hoops on it and they spread a sheet over them to protect from flies — if the hoops were made from erev Shabbat in the manner explained, it is permitted to spread a sheet over them on Shabbat; and if not, forbidden; and the same applies that it is forbidden to spread a sheet on wagons, for it is also made to protect what is beneath it and is like an ohel, unless it was already spread from erev Shabbat a tefach in width, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote. And that all this is not considered permanent ohel is because they are made only for a time and not to endure:
(ב) A tefach in width — that the width of the round wood was a tefach, and even only in one of them; and likewise what he wrote "if there is not between this and this," etc. — this is also even in one place, and we say lavud and it is as if three tefachim were covered in one place:
(ג) They are considered an ohel and permitted, etc. — for specifically to make temporary ohel initially is forbidden; but if it already came about, it is permitted to add to it; and in this, since there was in one wood a tefach width that is the measure of an ohel, or there was less than three tefachim between this and that that is the measure of lavud and it is as if the woods are joined together in the measure of three tefachim — it is considered that an ohel already came about:
(ד) And for this reason, etc. — meaning that here too, since a tefach was spread from erev Shabbat, it is permitted afterward to spread more, for it is only adding to temporary ohel; but without this it is forbidden; and even though below in seif 3 we say that temporary ohel is forbidden only when he also makes partitions beneath it — here it is different, for he makes explicitly for an ohel for shade or to protect from cold and rain, as I wrote below in seif 3:
(ה) Except for the wrapping — namely that if the mat was spread for a roof and he wrapped it from erev Shabbat — he must leave a tefach spread so it will be permitted to spread everything; and as stated, the width of the circle of the wrapping does not count toward the measure of a tefach, for the wrapping is not considered spread on the roof for an ohel, but he must leave a tefach spread apart from the width of the circle of the wrapping [Beit Yosef]. There are later authorities who wrote that nevertheless to spread the mat when it is wrapped on some thing to which ohel applies — forbidden explicitly because of ohel; for specifically in this that it was spread initially and he wrapped it, it does not appear in this as leaving for an ohel; but to place it thus when it is wrapped on another thing — forbidden:
