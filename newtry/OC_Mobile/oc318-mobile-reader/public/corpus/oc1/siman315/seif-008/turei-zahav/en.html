Every tent etc. In Tur he wrote another condition: and we deal also when it does not descend below a handbreadth when upright without a slope after the slope ended; and so Rashi in the Gemara according to his version: but if it descends from the canopy a handbreadth — forbidden, for the handbreadth is a wall for the tent and the bed becomes a roof for it; and Ran wrote in the name of R' Yona and Rashba — and what we say in the Gemara that if it extends from the canopy a handbreadth it is forbidden — the reason is because the canopy is spread for shade; but our bed — even though sheets descend from the canopy a handbreadth — since in spreading sheets on the bed there is no tent matter — we have nothing in it — end of his words; and it is difficult on this from what is in chapter Tolim (Shabbos daf 138) — but now if a strip a handbreadth wide — likewise liable? Meaning: he placed a tallit on his head as one wraps and distanced it from his head a handbreadth — I brought it in siman 310 seif 40; and according to Rabbenu Tam's explanation that for the conclusion there also forbidden due to tent where it is tight — that is, it does not bend as stated above — why do we not also say there: since the tallit covering his head has no tent matter, likewise in the part extending beyond his head like here with the sheet that has no tent matter since above there is no tent matter? And in truth it appears to me that nevertheless there it is resolved for Rashi who holds per the conclusion that there is no tent matter at all even in what extends a handbreadth — only due to lest it fall from his head — that is, he holds as I wrote: since there is no tent matter in the essence of the hat, and for Rabbenu Tam it is difficult from here; and it appears the case there is different — for that part extending beyond his head is not in the manner of wrappers — therefore considered a tent independently and is not dragged after the part on his head; unlike here with the sheet whose manner of spreading is that part of it extends beneath the bed — therefore dragged after the part covering the bed; and from this is clear proof for what I wrote in siman 310 permitting our hat called a britel — the extending part is dragged after the part covering the head since that part is not designated and is not similar to the hat in the Gemara there where the extending part is an independent part like the brim of a hat as written there; and another reason to permit from the force of there being no tent without partitions as written in seif 13; and another proof for the britel from what is in chapter HaMeivi Kedei Yayin daf 33 — four things forbidden due to tent; and Rashi explained there — that is, like R' Yehuda who forbids even without intent; but for R' Shimon who permits without intent we rule leniently in all these since one does not intend a tent; and Tosafos and Rosh wrote there: it appears even R' Shimon agrees in them — for when R' Shimon said davar she'eino mitkaven mutar — that is when one does not intend to do that thing; but here behold he does what he intends to do and even though he does not intend building — nevertheless he intends to do something resembling building and the Sages forbade it due to a decree because of building — end of their words; and if so here with the britel he does not do with intent at all that part belonging to a tent — for he only intends to cover his head and the tent comes about by itself; unlike the hat in the Talmud where the tent is made with intent for a tent and that part becomes as designated for this — therefore forbidden even without intent, as appears to me:
