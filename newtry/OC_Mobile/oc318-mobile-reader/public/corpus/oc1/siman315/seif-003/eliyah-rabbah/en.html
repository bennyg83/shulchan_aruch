[6] And likewise the legs. Because it has no walls. Some say the reason is that the prohibition of a tent applies only when one needs the space beneath it, such as a bed or chair where one uses the space to place shoes, whereas one does not need the space beneath a table (Rabbenu Yerucham, netiv 13, part 4). It is clear that there is a dispute. According to the first view, everything depends on walls: with walls it is prohibited even if the space is not needed, and without walls it is permitted even if the space is needed. Rosh in chapter 5 of Beitzah likewise implies this, explaining that although a mat in seif 2 is prohibited without walls, that is because it is a significant structure and one's main intention is to form a tent, whereas a temporary structure of this type is not called a tent without walls. Taz at the end of this siman overlooked this. According to the second view everything depends on need for the space, and even a table with four walls is permitted when the space beneath is not needed. I write this to reject Taz's assumption that all agree four walls prohibit even without use of the space. Shulchan Aruch appears to follow Ran in the name of Tosafot that both factors are required: walls and need for the space. Therefore he permits our beds even though we use the space beneath, and in seif 7 nearby he permits arranging a book on each side with another above even though there are walls, because the space is not needed. Since the matter is rabbinic, he rules leniently in accordance with both views. In truth the second view appears primary to me, as Ran's conclusion and Maggid Mishneh imply, but I have no basis to be stringent beyond Shulchan Aruch. This is also contrary to Bach.
