3) An umbrella made to protect from sun and rain may not be carried on Shabbos or Yom Tov, and certainly may not be opened. One may, however, tell a non-Jew to carry it to shelter him. Responsa Michtam LeDavid, siman 8; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2. Responsa Givas Pinchas, manuscript, volume 5, siman 19, however, wrote a complete response to all the preceding authority's arguments and concludes that if an umbrella was open before Yom Tov, it is plainly permitted to carry it. He regularly ruled that an umbrella opened before Yom Tov may be used on Yom Tov, and he saw that his teachers did not object to those who did so. On Shabbos, however, the prohibition of carrying creates grounds for a decree; see there. Birkei Yosef cites this in Shiyurei Berachah. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Orach Chaim, siman 30, who discusses this at length and concludes that when it was prepared before Shabbos, arguments can be made both to forbid and permit it. In any event, it must be forbidden because of appearances, for who knows whether it was prepared the preceding day, especially in this bereft generation; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 4, likewise discusses the matter at length and concludes that it is forbidden; see there. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 3, cites these authorities and concludes that the universal practice is to forbid it even when it was open the preceding day. This is the accepted stringent ruling, and one who is lenient may cause the public to stumble, Heaven forbid; see there. Accordingly, since the prohibition is because of making a covering and because of appearances, as stated above, there is no difference between Shabbos and Yom Tov. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Shemos, paragraph 8, rules the same and writes that since it may not be carried over one's head, it is also muktzeh and may not be moved on Shabbos or Yom Tov. This is the accepted practice.
