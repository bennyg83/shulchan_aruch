(א) A bed, etc. — behold, in order that we understand well the words of this seif, I will precede with a brief introduction. And it is that even though regarding permanent building, even if he does some thing to add to the building, whether in partitions or in the roof, he is liable — and all the more so if he makes the entire roof — nevertheless regarding temporary ohel they did not forbid one who makes the roof alone unless he makes the roof in order to shade from sun and rain, etc., for then it is somewhat in the category of ohel; but otherwise they did not forbid except one who also makes temporary partitions beneath it on Shabbat; they also did not forbid except when he does in order: first the partitions and afterward the covering, for then it somewhat resembles permanent ohel; but if he did the reverse they did not forbid; and there are poskim who hold that we also require that he need the air beneath it — and according to them the Mechaber ruled below in seif 27:

(ב) Forbidden to place, etc. — in analysis he wrote this language, that specifically to place is forbidden, for this is as beginning to make partitions for an ohel and forbidden when he places afterward the boards above; but if they were already placed in this place, it is permitted to place the boards on them, and as stated in s.k. 17; however if the bed is very wide, forbidden according to Magen Avraham in s.k. 20 even in this manner — see there in seif 13 what we will write on this:

(ג) The legs first, etc. — for this is the manner of building, for the legs are the partition and the boards of the covering are like the roof, and it turns out as making an ohel on Shabbat; and therefore one must change — to grasp the boards of the covering in the air and afterward place the legs beneath them, for this is not the manner of building:

(ד) And these matters, etc. — the reason that in temporary ohel such as this, where he does not intend in making it for an ohel beneath it but for another use on top of it, and only incidentally it becomes like an ohel below — they forbade only when he also makes partitions beneath it. And where one spreads a sheet or mat for an ohel, such as to shade from sun and rain or so it will be fit to use something beneath it in some matter — forbidden even without partitions, as in that of seif 2 at the end. Magen Avraham wrote that to close a hole from which smoke exits [called chimney] with straw bedding and the like — it is possible that it is forbidden, for since that place has partitions by themselves, it is as making an ohel initially; and it is not comparable to what we permitted in siman 313 to close the skylight in the roof — there the skylight holds only part of the roof, but here the hole from which smoke exits holds the entire width of the partitions around it, and through closing with the bedding it is as a roof on the partitions; and therefore it is obvious that if the hole is in a wall from the side, it is permitted to close with bedding, for this is only adding a partition [Peri Megadim]. He further wrote that in our language what is made in a chimney and fixed there — permitted to close with it on Shabbat and Yom Tov, since it is fixed there it is like a door; and see in Tosefta, who wrote that what Magen Avraham questions is specifically when the hole from which smoke exits holds three tefachim — for otherwise we say lavud and it is as closed and permitted; and similar to what is written above in seif 2 [however Peri Megadim is not so explicit on this matter; nevertheless it appears that one who relies on Tosefta has not lost, for without this matter it is weak before Magen Avraham himself, for it is possible that the partitions beneath it are not considered partitions for this matter, since they are already made]. And it appears that all this is when the coals are glowing in the stove — otherwise in every case it is forbidden for a Jew to close the hole above, for through his closing he causes extinguishing of the coals; and even though he does not intend for this, it is psik reisha, as is written in siman 259 at the end and siman 277 seif 6:

(ה) Connected sides — in siman 502 Beit Yosef wrote that we require the partitions to reach the ground [but if adjacent to the ground less than three, there the side does not reach — we say lavud there, and see there in Magen Avraham and in other later authorities on this]; and regarding a chest that has rims also below [like a chest called shelf in English] — see in seif 13 what we will write on this:

(ו) The sides of the chest — the Taz wrote that even though in the Tur it states four connected sides, this is not precise; regarding a bed whose manner is to use the air beneath it for shoes, etc. — forbidden even when it has only two sides from two directions reaching the ground, for with two alone it is considered an ohel with partitions, as below in seif 6 regarding barrels; and so too Shulchan Aruch HaRav. But regarding a table that the Shulchan Aruch permits — even when it has few partitions it is forbidden only when it has four sides from all directions like a chest, for then it is considered an ohel when placing the board from above, because otherwise when closed it does not stand to use its air [and when he does not use its air it is not considered an ohel, as below in seif 7]; and therefore it must have four sides from all directions like a chest, for then it is fit to use its air; and there are later authorities who side to be lenient regarding a table to place the board from above even when it has four partitions from every side that presumably he does not need the air beneath it unless his custom is to use there — however the language of the Shulchan Aruch, who wrote "and likewise the legs of a table," implies that regarding a table also, if there were partitions, he would need to be stringent; and therefore it is certainly correct to be concerned for the words of the Taz — to grasp the board in the air and insert the legs beneath it; and it is implied from the words of Peri Megadim that all this is specifically when the legs are moved from place to place in order to place the board above on them — for then it is considered as if he began making the ohel; but if they stand in one place, even for the Taz it is permitted to place the board on them, even though the legs have partitions from every side, as we wrote above in s.k. 17 and s.k. 18 regarding a bed:

(ז) Permitted in all cases — for the legs of our beds and tables have no partitions.
