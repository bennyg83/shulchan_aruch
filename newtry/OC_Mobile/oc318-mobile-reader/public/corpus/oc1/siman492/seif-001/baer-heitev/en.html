And Monday. For we are concerned that perhaps amid feasting and joy they came to sin, as we find regarding Iyov. (And all the more so, a person easily commits sins through some melachah that on Yom Tov is permitted only for a slight need, and even on Chol HaMoed only something involving loss of principal is permitted, and when he does not specifically schedule his work for the moed; and there is no righteous man on earth, etc., in these days to transgress the religion somewhat; Chacham Tzvi.) But regarding Atzeres, which is only one day, we are not concerned.
And Tishrei. And even according to our custom that it is permitted to fast in Tishrei after Isru Chag, nevertheless one does not establish a fast in it initially; and they announce the blessing for it in the synagogue immediately on the first Shabbos after Rosh Chodesh, unlike the Hagahos Maimoniyos to wait until the 17th of Cheshvan. And they bless whoever fasts Bahab, and whoever answers Amen needs no [further] acceptance. And Magen Avraham wrote that when there is a milah or a wedding, the custom is to bless at Minchah, for the sake of "when it is possible to be good, do not call it bad"; see there.
Public fast. And if a bris milah occurs, it is a mitzvah to eat, and one needs no hatarah; see siman 566 and 568; and they may say Selichos even if there are not ten fasting, only that they should not say "Taanis Tzibbur kavu"; Responsa Avodas HaGershuni, siman 57.
