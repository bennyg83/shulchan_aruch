Of new earthenware. It implies that an old one one should not bake in even by means of libun, for he would spare it lest it crack; so wrote Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham, and after him Levush followed; however in truth it will be explained above in siman 451 se'if 1 regarding a kubya, which is the tpaka here, that if its way is to heat from inside, heating avails to kasher it; and see there s.k. 13, that so is the implication of all the poskim I brought there; and what he wrote here "new" is because here he does not discuss the law of kashering, as Beis Yosef also wrote at first to explain the words of the Tur, and that reason so dictates, not like Rabbeinu Yeruham after whom Levush followed.
Some forbid. And it is good to be careful after the fact: if he stuck and afterward heated beneath it, Semag and Kol Bo and Hagahos Maimoniyos brought in Beis Yosef wrote, and this is their language: and regarding after the fact it requires further study, lest the fire reach the matzah before the measure of a mil; and nevertheless it is proper to be stringent; end quote. (And see above in siman 459 s.k. 9 I wrote in the name of the poskim that in a place of heat it becomes chametz even before the measure of a mil; and this requires further study.) And for this reason it appeared to me to be lenient after the fact if they put matzah on paper inside the oven to bake it with the paper, for certainly the heat reaches through the paper quickly and it is not an interruption; and likewise Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 65 se'if 2 that it is permitted to roast the liver when it is wrapped in paper, and we do not say the paper is an interruption; if so the same applies here; and even though one can distinguish in several ways in our case, nevertheless from the implication of the poskim I wrote that one need not forbid after the fact with a tpaka when he stuck and afterward it was heated, if we knew that the fire reached before the measure of a mil: if so with paper the matter appears that the fire reaches it quickly and it does not become chametz; and all this after the fact, but ideally one should be careful about all this.
Not to make cakes in hot ash. And he wrote in Kenesses HaGedolah: and all the more so it is proper to protest against baking matzah in the sun; and so wrote Riaz that if he transgressed and kneaded, he does not fulfill his obligation on Pesach; end quote. It implies that the matzah is not forbidden after the fact because of leavening; and even according to what I wrote above in siman 459 s.k. 26, that if he transgressed and kneaded under the sun one should forbid the matzah after the fact, that is in a place where the sun's heat does not reach so much as to bake the bread; which is not so if the heat reaches so much: it will not become chametz at all, for it is baked by its heat like inside the oven, and it is permitted; and near the oven that is forbidden, since it was not baked properly, and this is easy; but it is forbidden to do so, and one does not fulfill the obligation of matzah.
