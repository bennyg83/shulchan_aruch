(21) If they baked, etc.: even during Pesach.
(22) Chametz with matzah: in one oven, even in a small and closed oven where they are stringent above in siman 447 at the end of se'if 1: here it is permitted, for the aroma of permitted bread with forbidden bread is considered nothing; and the later authorities agreed to permit in this even if they were kneaded with oil or fat; and nevertheless that place where the chametz stood in the oven needs heating to kasher it; but other places in the oven do not need kashering, for its lean absorption does not travel and spread through all of it without sauce.
(23) Touched chametz: namely in the oven when they are hot.
(24) The measure of removing the place: namely as the thickness of the width of a middle thumb; and some later authorities are stringent when it was done during Pesach to forbid all of it; and according to what the Gra wrote in s.k. 17 one may be lenient also in this.
(25) And stuck: but when it is not truly stuck one need not forbid. And nevertheless if its lower surface at the place of the fold did not crust, and even if he has a doubt about this, it is forbidden.
(26) Until the fire does not reach there: that is to say, since it stuck and the fire cannot reach there with power to bake at once, we are concerned that meanwhile it became chametz.
(27) They forbid it: all of it, for since the chametz is attached to the matzah in one piece we are concerned lest the taste of chametz spread through all the matzah; and even though there is in the matzah sixty opposite it, it is not nullified within it, for chametz on Pesach is by a morsel.
(28) During Pesach: that is to say, if the baking was during Pesach, for then its law is by a morsel (Gra).
(29) But other matzos in the oven, etc.: even if they touch and bite this one, for lean issur does not pass from piece to piece without sauce; and one must only be careful to leave a bit of the permitted with the issur at the place of contact as the measure of removing.
(30) Are permitted: even if they touched it at the place of the fold; and if one of them was smeared with fat, even if the permitted is fatty, if it touched at the place of the fold all of it is forbidden, for the permitted goes and fattens the issur and it is as if it too is fatty and the issur spreads through all of it; but not at the place of the fold, removing suffices for it; and if the folded matzah was fatty it forbids its fellow that touches it entirely.
(31) And before Pesach: namely even if found during Pesach, since it was baked before Pesach.
(32) Only the place of its adhesion: and around it the measure of removing, and the rest is permitted even though there is not in them sixty opposite the place of the fold. If a folded and puffed matzah is found in his house after Pesach, one should not be stringent. And it is good to remove a folded or puffed matzah with another oven peel if possible.
(33) Puffed: there are two interpretations in this: (1) that the thickness of the matzah split in the middle and the upper part rose upward; (2) that it did not split at all, but rather the body of the matzah puffed in its middle and rose as with chametz bread that leavens and rises upward in its middle; and the later authorities agreed in both to be stringent.
(34) Forbidden: the whole matzah even at a place that did not puff, and even if the puffing was only at one of the ends, since a cavity formed in its thickness we assume all of it became chametz; and even if there is sixty in the matzah opposite this place it does not avail; and there is no distinction between before Pesach or on Pesach; and see in the later authorities who agreed that one should not forbid unless the cavity is so large as a hazelnut (that they call Hazel-Nuss) or as a middle thumb; and specifically when the upper part of the matzah rose in height upward; but if it did not rise upward, only that within the thickness of the matzah there is a hollow hole of the measure explained, they forbid only the place of the hole, for that place resembles a folded matzah where the heat of the fire does not reach, and the rest is permitted even during Pesach; and they do not forbid even at the place of the hole except when there are blisters inside it (that they call belazlik), which is a sign of leavening; but if it is smooth it is permitted; and if there are in the matzah two holes, a hole within a hole, they are concerned to forbid all of it. And all this if the puffing was made in the oven the first time; but if after it was baked they smeared it with fat and returned it to the oven and it puffed, it is permitted according to all opinions.
(35) A crust: namely a thin crust from above, and not in the middle of the thickness of the matzah; some later authorities wrote that with our thin matzos one need not be concerned for the blisters and cavities recognizable from outside and inside in the manner of the great majority made because of the fire, that even if they are not thin crusts but somewhat thick one need not be concerned; and "puffed in its middle" means that half the upper thickness separated from the lower, which is common only with somewhat thick matzos. And know that even in a manner that it is puffed, according to all opinions the view of Chok Yaakov is that one need not forbid its mixture, only that matzah itself that puffed; and the same applies regarding a folded one; and in Eliyah Rabbah he is stringent to forbid the mixture with a puffed [matzah], but regarding benefit one may be lenient; see there; and it appears that at any rate with sixty one may be lenient; and especially with thin wafers like our matzos, even one who wishes to be stringent should at any rate not be stringent with mixtures, like the view of Chok Yaakov.
(36) If two matzos lie: namely even if only part of this matzah was lying in the oven upon part of this matzah; and the reason is that at the place of adhesion the fire does not reach there; therefore its law is like a matzah that folded over partially, and the whole upper matzah and the lower matzah are forbidden; and if this was before Pesach, he removes the place of adhesion with a thumb's width from this matzah, and likewise similarly from the matzah beneath it.
(37) Before their baking: that is to say, and he left them there until they were baked thus; but if the baker separated them at once, it appears to permit (Eliyah Rabbah).
(38) That they not touch one another: and after the fact, if they touched and bit one another, since both touch below in the heat of the oven, the heat of the fire reaches well below and it was baked properly and is permitted.
