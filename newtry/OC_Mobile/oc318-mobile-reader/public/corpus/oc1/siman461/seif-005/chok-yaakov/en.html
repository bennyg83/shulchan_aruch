The measure of removing the place. Maharshal and Bach are stringent to forbid all of it; however from the words of Taz and Magen Avraham it appears they agree with the view of the Shulchan Aruch, since there is no reason to decree because of something fatty, one need not forbid more than the measure of removing; and so appears primary; and see what I wrote above in siman 451 s.k. 54 in the name of Kol Bo; nevertheless if it was done during Pesach one should forbid all of it, as with a folded matzah nearby.
And stuck. But when it is not truly stuck one need not forbid; and nevertheless if its lower surface at the place of the fold did not crust, it is forbidden; so wrote the later authorities.
They forbid it. But other matzos one need not forbid because of a puffed or folded matzah, even if they scraped them with one oven peel, since that itself is a mere stringency, as I wrote above in siman 451 s.k. 54 in the name of Magen Avraham; and see there that the oven peel itself is permitted, and also the oven does not need kashering a second time; and see there the matter with its reason.
And before Pesach one need forbid only the place of adhesion. Even if found during Pesach, since it was baked before Pesach: even if there is not sixty opposite the place of the fold it is permitted; so is the agreement of most later authorities, and so is primary; and so wrote my brother-in-law the Rav in Seif Alef Zayin in the name of my maternal grandfather the Gaon as practical law; and he further concluded there regarding the law if a folded and puffed matzah is found after Pesach, that one should also discuss and forbid; see there. And to my humble opinion, according to what is written in Responsa Maharam of Lublin and the agreement of Taz and Magen Avraham, that a folded and puffed matzah, since it was not mentioned in the Gemara and early poskim, only the law of a folded one was mentioned by Maharai and a puffed one in the words of Maharil: (and see what I wrote nearby in s.k. 19) therefore one should not be so stringent with it, since that itself is a mere stringency; if so, do not add upon it; and one may be lenient at least when found after Pesach, since chametz that Pesach passed over is forbidden only rabbinically; and especially according to what I wrote above in siman 449 that doubtful chametz that Pesach passed over one may permit; and if so also a puffed and folded matzah is only a mere doubt, as Magen Avraham wrote later in siman 471; and so it implies in Terumas HaDeshen siman 126 and in his rulings siman 145; and one should permit them when found after Pesach, as aforesaid.
A matzah puffed in its middle. Behold there are two interpretations in this: one interpretation, that the thickness of the matzah split, that the upper part rose upward; and another interpretation: puffing in its middle, that it did not split at all, but rather the body of the matzah puffed as it is in chametz bread; so wrote Bach; and Maharam of Lublin wrote according to the first interpretation. And Taz lengthened to permit according to the first interpretation and concluded that there is no prohibition except according to the second; and he toiled in this in order to align the view of Maharash who permitted a puffed [matzah] with the view of Maharil who forbids; see his words at length; but in truth one who examines Maharil will see clearly that they disagree with one another. And Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Taz that he permitted a puffed [matzah] entirely, and that he wrote, and this is its language: I testify upon heaven and earth that I never once saw from my father-in-law the Bach that he forbade any matzah that puffed, etc.; only that in his composition he ruled interpretations to forbid, and they are not reliable, for all his days he never forbade this matter, and on the contrary he laughed at those who forbid; therefore one should not cause loss of Jewish money for this; end quote of Taz; and he exaggerated his measures to take lightly a matter that our fathers and fathers' fathers practiced as forbidden; end quote of Magen Avraham. Behold, what he wrote in the name of Taz is not found thus in the Taz book before us; rather he makes a compromise between the opinions as I copied in his name; however I saw in Responsa Taz from his own manuscript that he wrote the language exactly as Magen Avraham copied in his name; and so I heard from the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Leib son of the author of Shaar Ephraim that the sages of Venice and the Latins ridicule the law of a puffed [matzah] that we practice to forbid; and one sage brought some proof for his words from what is stated at the beginning of chapter "Two loaves" regarding the bread of the Presence that was made in three molds, and the Gemara asks: and let him return to the first mold; and the Gemara answers: once he baked it, it puffed; and the bread of the Presence was of matzah, and even so it does not harm if it puffs in baking; from this we learn that it did not become chametz for this during its baking; end of his words. And behold certainly one should practice as Magen Avraham wrote, and one should not be lenient in a matter that our fathers practiced as forbidden; I also found some support for this prohibition from what is stated in the Aruch, entry "Latash," and this is its language: what we say "if it puffed, scrape it in cold": another interpretation: if it puffed inside the oven, strike it with one's hand while it is soaked upon the cake, because when the cake puffs it rises upward from the matzah toward chametz that has not yet come; therefore they obligated to scrape it in cold in order to diminish the power of puffing; end quote. And it is possible that upon this interpretation Maharil relied to forbid a matzah that puffed in its baking, which was not mentioned in the words of the early ones; and Maharash who permitted holds like Rashi and other commentators regarding "if it puffed, scrape it in cold"; and in this they disagree. Nevertheless it is enough to forbid that matzah itself, not as I have seen some who rule who wrap everything in a great wrap and forbid even mixtures even in a morsel as if it were complete chametz, and likewise forbid the Riv Eisen and other vessels and what was used with them. And so it is by majority that they notice the puffing after the matzah was already mixed into dishes and the like; and through this they destroy and cause loss of Jewish money and withhold Yom Tov joy. And to my humble opinion it is an excessive stringency without reason; for those who are lenient are fit to rely upon in every such case; and especially since Maharash was great in his generation and the teacher of Maharil, and had power to disagree with the Tur, as Taz wrote; and further, it is possible that even those who forbid never intended to forbid the whole mixture so much, for the law of mixtures was not mentioned in their words to forbid, and this stringency suffices regarding the matzah itself.
The whole matzah is forbidden, and there is no distinction between baked before Pesach or after Pesach; so wrote in Responsa Maharam of Lublin siman 105; and so is the agreement of the later authorities; and so wrote my brother-in-law the Rav in Seif Alef Zayin in the name of my maternal grandfather the Gaon of blessed memory; not as newly printed in Kitzur Shenei Luchos HaBris folio 54 column 4 in the name of Or Zarua several distinctions of law regarding this matter, and that is a complete error that was not mentioned at all in his words. And he wrote there in the name of my maternal grandfather that one should not forbid unless it is recognizable that the hole rose upward, and the hole must have in it a cavity that holds like a hazelnut that they call a large Hazel-Nuss, or a middle thumb; and so wrote in Magen Avraham; Magen Avraham further wrote: and even with a hole that did not rise upward I have seen the geonim of our generation rule to forbid only the place of the hole, and they permit the rest of the matzah even during Pesach; and so it is written in the name of the Gaon Mahari Horowitz; and if there are two holes, a hole within a hole, they are concerned to forbid all of it; and I found written: if the hole is smooth it is permitted, and if there is belazlik inside it, it is forbidden; end quote.
That they not reach one another. And after the fact Rashal ruled in a responsum, siman 57, to permit; and so is the agreement of the later authorities.
