It was not forbidden. For aroma like this, which has no greasiness at all, according to all opinions does not forbid; see siman 447 at the end of se'if 1.
Removing the place. Namely as the thickness of a finger's width.
That the fire does not reach there. Namely that the fold is stuck; but when they are near each other without adhesion, even though the fold is almost stuck to each other, it is permitted (Darkei Moshe from Maharil); and Bach wrote that even though it is not truly folded, if its surface did not crust it is forbidden (as written in se'if 3); and so wrote in She'eris Yosef that even if it is doubtful whether its surface crusted it is forbidden; and it appears to me that specifically with a folded matzah one should be stringent, for there are two disadvantages; but if it has no disadvantage except that its surface did not crust, one should not forbid it, as Beis Yosef wrote according to the view of the Tur; see there; and so it appears in the Shulchan Aruch and in the gloss; and even though the view of R' Shmuel in Mordechai is not so, it appears to me that one should not be stringent.
The place of adhesion. And one discards it (writings of Maharai): it is possible he holds that since it is not complete chametz it does not require burning; but Rashal wrote that it requires burning; and from Rama's silence it implies he holds that even though there is not sixty opposite the place of the fold it is permitted; and so wrote Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 that so is Rama's view; and so wrote Moreinu HaRav of Lublin siman 105, and this is its language: since the place of adhesion is not complete chametz, and it is also not something that dissolves and mixes, only a little taste and aroma spread from it into the rest of the matzah, as the Tur wrote that it resembles hot bread and an open barrel; if so it is impossible that there is not in the matzah sixty opposite the little taste that spread; and so practiced my teacher and father-in-law Maharik, and so I practice; end quote; and so it appears in Maharil; but Rashal and Bach and Maharai require sixty opposite the adhesion; and so it appears in Darkei Moshe; and nevertheless it appears to me that we do not require sixty except opposite the inner side where they are stuck to each other, for on the outer side the fire reaches well. Rashal wrote: a matzah that has a fold, and that matzah touched its fellow even with a bite, does not forbid its fellow, for a bite is only like contact, and absorbed issur does not pass from piece to piece without sauce (and it appears to me that if the matzah is smeared with fat one should forbid, for even though he wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 se'if 7 that if the issur is not fatty, even if the permitted is fatty it does not pass from piece to piece without sauce, nevertheless our practice is to be stringent even with roasting and salting, as written in siman 447 at the end of se'if 1); only when one separates them from one another one must be careful to leave a bit of the permitted with the issur as the measure of a peel; and specifically when it bit its fellow at a place that was not folded; but if it bit at the place of the fold, then it forbids when hot even without sauce, as with other issurim; end quote. And nevertheless it appears to me that according to us it does not forbid more than the measure of removing, as Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 105; and further, it is no better than complete chametz that touched matzah; for Moreinu HaRav of Lublin wrote, and this is its language: a folded and puffed matzah is not found in the poskim; only Maharai wrote the law of a folded one, and Maharil wrote the law of a puffed one; nevertheless we must align our view with their view; end quote; if so one should not be so stringent with it; and see siman 451 se'if 19 regarding the oven peel; and see Radbaz part 2, siman 160.
Puffed in its middle. Meaning that the thickness of the matzah split and the upper part rose upward; and if after it was baked they smeared it with fat and returned it to the oven and it became puffed, it is permitted (see what I wrote in se'if 3); and another interpretation: puffed in its middle, that they did not split at all, but rather the body of the matzah puffed in its middle as it is in chametz bread (Bach); and Moreinu HaRav of Lublin wrote according to the first interpretation. And Taz lengthened for his purpose not to forbid a puffed matzah, and concluded, and this is its language: I testify upon heaven and earth that I never once saw from my father-in-law that he forbade any matzah because it puffed in its middle, and he said every time that this comes from piercing the matzos or because the dough was not rolled properly; only that in his composition he ruled that there are explanations to forbid, and they are not reliable, for all his days he never forbade this matter, and on the contrary he laughed at those who forbid; therefore one should not cause loss of Jewish money for this; and the rabbi Maharash is fit for this, who is lenient in every matter of baking, as written in Maharil; end quote of Taz. And he exaggerated his measures to take lightly a matter that our fathers and fathers' fathers practiced as forbidden; and also Maharash permitted only when a crust rose on its underside as rises on bread, even though it was like a fist, as Rama wrote; but puffed in its middle he did not permit. (However in Hagahos Maharil of the Nevau printing he wrote that Maharash permitted a puffed [matzah] that rose because of baking.) And as for what he wrote that Bach laughed at those who forbid: it appears to me that he heard and erred; namely there are those who forbid if a hole is found in the matzah and it is as large as a nut or a little finger, even though the upper part did not rise upward; about this he laughed and said it comes from the piercing and the rolling; but when the upper part rose upward, that is certainly forbidden, as he wrote in his composition; and even sixty does not avail, even before Pesach, for all of it became chametz; and so wrote Moreinu HaRav of Lublin in a responsum; and even regarding a hole I have seen the geonim of our generation rule to forbid only the place of the hole, and they permit the rest of the matzah even during Pesach; and see what I wrote in siman 439 at the end of se'if 2 in the name of Maharil, which implies that even if it came from the rolling or the piercing it is like a folded matzah; nevertheless they are not stringent to forbid all of it; and so it is written in the name of the Gaon Moreinu HaRav Yeshayah Horowitz; and if there are two holes within a hole they are concerned to forbid all of it; and it is written: if the hole is smooth it is permitted; if there is belazlik inside it, it is forbidden.
That they not touch one another. And after the fact, if they bit one another and both touch the oven below, then the heat of the fire reaches well below and it was baked properly and is permitted (Rashal in a responsum; and see in Mahariv).
