A person fulfills his obligation, etc. This is after the fact. An elderly or ill person who finds dry matzah difficult to eat may even initially soak it in water, provided it is not soaked for twenty-four hours, since soaking for that period is comparable to cooking.
Soaked. This means soaked in water. The poskim dispute whether it may be soaked in other beverages, fruit juice, or soup. Some prohibit this because those liquids weaken the taste of matzah by imparting their own flavor, while others maintain that only cooking removes the taste of matzah. Therefore, an elderly or ill person who cannot eat matzah soaked in water may soak it in wine or another beverage. An ordinary person who ate matzah soaked in a liquid other than water has not fulfilled his obligation and must eat another matzah, both for the kezayis of the matzah blessing and for the afikoman. This applies to soaking, but merely dipping the matzah is permitted because its taste is not nullified. The leniency of soaking in water applies when a whole kezayis is soaked together. Pieces smaller than a kezayis do not fulfill the obligation if they were soaked until the water whitened, because they thereby lost the status of bread. If they were soaked only briefly and the water did not whiten, even pieces smaller than a kezayis retain the status of bread.

If he cooked it, etc. Even if each piece remains a kezayis and retains the appearance of bread, cooking nullifies the taste of matzah. The Acharonim write that the same applies to soaking in boiling liquid. One should be stringent even in a secondary vessel unless needed, whenever the liquid is hot enough that the hand recoils. This applies to water; in soup one should be stringent in all circumstances. See reference 168.
