(17) "A person fulfills his obligation," etc. This is after the fact. For an elderly or ill person who finds it difficult to eat dry matzah, it is permitted even initially to soak the matzah in water. One must, however, take care that it not remain soaked for 24 hours, because something soaked for 24 hours is treated as cooked. (18) "Soaked." Meaning soaked in water. Whether it may be soaked in other beverages, fruit juice, or soup is disputed by the halachic authorities. Some prohibit it because those liquids weaken the taste of the matzah by imparting their own flavor. Others hold that only cooking removes the taste of matzah, but soaking does not. Therefore, an elderly or ill person who cannot eat matzah soaked in water may soak it in wine or another beverage. An ordinary person, however, who ate matzah soaked in a liquid other than water has not fulfilled his obligation and must eat another matzah, both for the kezayit over which the matzah blessing is recited and for the kezayit of afikoman. (19) "Provided it did not dissolve." If the matzah dissolved to the point that it lost the form of bread, one does not fulfill the obligation with it. If it merely softened but its substance and appearance remain recognizable, it is valid. (20) "As long as the flavor of matzah remains." Even if the water is warm, it is valid provided it is not so hot as to constitute actual cooking. If the water reached a temperature at which ordinary contact cannot be comfortably maintained, there is concern that the matzah has been cooked and one does not fulfill the obligation with it. The same applies all the more when it was placed in a kli rishon.
