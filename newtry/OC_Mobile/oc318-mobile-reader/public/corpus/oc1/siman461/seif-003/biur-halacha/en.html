And one should be careful, etc.: see what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, that if he does not return it at all it will certainly become chametz at least in the measure of a mil; and see in Magen Avraham s.k. 11 and Eliyah Rabbah and Chok Yaakov and Shaarei Teshuvah, who weighed and deliberated on this; and Magen Avraham tends to say that after the fact one need not forbid at all when its surface did not crust; see there; and if so, the same applies when threads trail from it, for it is one measure, as explained in Pesachim folio 37 in Rashi s.v. "And likewise," and in Or Zarua in the name of Ritzva, and likewise in Tosafos Menachos 78 and in Shittah Mekubetzes there; and we ruled simply that it certainly becomes chametz, in accordance with the view of R' Shmuel of Falaise brought in Or Zarua, Hilchos Pesachim folio 59, and in Mordechai, for we have not found explicitly anyone who disagrees with him; on the contrary, from the commentary of Rabbeinu Chananel who wrote that if threads trail it is dough and forbidden, and so too in the commentary of R' Manoach on the Rambam, it is proven from this that it can come to leavening like other dough at least; and so Rashi explained in Menachos folio 78 that if threads do not trail it is baked at least somewhat, and if so before that it is not baked at all; and since that is so, it certainly becomes chametz in the measure of a mil at least; and the author of Noda BiYehuda already noted this in siman 80; and so it is explained in Tosafos Menachos 57b that once its surface has crusted it no longer becomes chametz, and there is a practical difference regarding matzah; see there and in Piskei Tosafos; and so wrote in Piskei Tosafos Menachos 78, and this is its language: if he broke it and threads no longer trail, it no longer becomes chametz, and that is crusting of its surface in the oven; end quote. Behold it is proven that before that it properly becomes chametz; and so I found that Ritva on Pesachim there wrote in the name of his teacher the Ra'ah, and this is its language: whatever one breaks and there are no threads, etc., and since that is so, from that time onward it no longer comes to leavening; end quote; and so he wrote later on the page regarding something baked that he cooked, and this is its language: that after it was baked to the measure of matzah it no longer becomes chametz, etc.; see there. Behold it is proven that before that it comes to leavening. (And against this we find only an implication of the Tur who wrote "and if not, one does not fulfill," from which the lenient ones wished to prove that regarding chametz there is nothing here, only that one does not fulfill with it the mitzvah of matzah; but the later authorities already rejected this, for the Tur's main law comes to teach us that it is not called bread, namely even when he wants to eat at once, for certainly it has not yet become chametz, for even according to what we are stringent above in siman 459 when the dough was hot and even in less than the measure of a mil, nevertheless it is simple that some measure is required at least, and in one moment it will not become chametz; and we already wrote there on this in the name of Maamar Mordechai along these lines; but if it waited the measure of a mil, and possibly even less than this, the Tur agrees; and in the gloss that warned to take, etc., and to return it, which implies even if he wants to return it at once, that is only ideally, and for fear that he will delay and it will come to leavening.) And since that is so, it appears that even if it is doubtful whether threads trail, one should not be lenient after the fact even with great loss, for it is a doubt of Torah law; and so ruled She'eris Yosef and Chok Yaakov and Noda BiYehuda siman 80.
