Trail. Even though they crumble when one moves them, even so one fulfills with them, so long as threads do not trail from them; Menachos folio 78. Magen Avraham. And when threads trail from them it is forbidden and one should burn them at once; and so the world practiced to forbid the matzah when threads trail from it; and if one cannot determine whether threads trail from it or not, he wrote in Responsa She'eris Yosef siman 48 that one should forbid out of doubt; see there; and crusting of the surface is the measure that threads no longer trail; Mordechai; Taz; and see above siman 294 se'if 5 in the gloss. And if one inserts a finger into the matzah and dough does not stick to it, we are no longer stringent, for in such a case there is certainly no room to doubt threads trailing from it. Chok Yaakov.

And if he took it before then, Taz wrote that one should forbid even after the fact, for it became chametz in that time that it was outside the oven; and the oven peel also became chametz; see there; and in Responsa Nachalas Shiva siman 11 he permits if he returned it at once to the oven; and Chok Yaakov disagrees with him and agreed with Taz; however, if there is reason to doubt whether threads trail from it, for example its surface crusted somewhat, then it is possible to tend to permit if he returned it at once (and Peri Chadash concluded: when it was baked somewhat so that it reached the food of a dog), and not to establish a prohibition that threads were trailing from it before he returned it; see there.
