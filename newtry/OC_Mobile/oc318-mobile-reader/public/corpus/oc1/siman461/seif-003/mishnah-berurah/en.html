(13) No threads trail: and even if it crumbles when one moves it, one fulfills with it. Chok Yaakov wrote that if there is reason to doubt whether threads trail from it, and one inserts a finger into the matzah and dough does not stick to it, one should permit it, for this is a sign that threads do not trail from it. And all this if the matzah is hot; but if it cooled, it is possible this is no proof (Eliyah Rabbah).
(14) From it: and this sign is only while the matzah is still hot, and not when it has cooled.
(15) One fulfills with it: the obligation of matzah. And even if he eats it after a long time, for once it was baked to this measure, according to all opinions it no longer comes to leavening; and before this measure it is still not in the category of bread, only dough; and there is another sign for bread that has reached the status of bread, namely when we see that its surface crusted, and it is one measure; therefore if the matzah cooled and he broke it and saw a flaw inside, that it is recognizable it was not baked well, and he can no longer recognize it by the aforementioned sign, he should look at the surface of the matzah, and if he sees that its surface crusted with a proper crusting about which there is no doubt, one should permit it; but if even in the crusting itself there is a doubt, it is a Torah-level doubt according to several poskim and forbidden even with great loss and withholding of Yom Tov joy.
(16) Before then and to return it: that is to say, not only to take it without returning it is certainly forbidden, for since threads trail from it it is still in the category of dough and will certainly become chametz at least in the measure of waiting a mil; rather even to take it in order to return it at once one should also be careful, for it can come to chametz even with a little waiting, since it was warmed by the heat of the oven, as explained above in 459 in se'if 1 and se'if 2. And nevertheless if he transgressed and removed it and returned it at once, one need not forbid.
