(1) When they heat it: and even though our ovens are made of bricks burned in a kiln and have the law of an earthenware vessel that does not leave the status of its walls, nevertheless heating avails since they heated them from inside and one does not spare them lest they crack, as explained above in siman 451 se'if 1. And there is no distinction between ovens whose mouth is on the side like ours and small ovens whose mouth is above.
(2) Over its entire surface: and this heating itself suffices to kasher it to bake matzah in it. And if it is doubtful for him with this heating whether the coals went over its entire surface, one should forbid after the fact the matzos they baked in it; and specifically if it is still within a day-cycle of when chametz was used in it, for if not it is permitted after the fact if it is before Pesach (Peri Megadim; and see there further); but if they heated it several times, even though he did not intend to permit it for Pesach need, it is permitted after the fact, for with several times we assume that presumably the whole oven was heated.
(3) A new floor: finger-thick (Levush); and the chametz absorbed in its roof and walls is expelled through purging by the flame when they heat it to bake the matzos in it; and likewise the stove needs heating so that the coals go over its entire surface, or that he plaster with clay its floor and also its sides to the height of the place where the pots stand. And kuchlin made inside the ovens need that he purge them with coals, or that he plaster with clay their floor and around them and also above; and Beis Meir is lenient that one need not plaster above at all, and on the sides he requires only a tefach's height; see there.
(4) It is good and upright, etc.: that is to say, even though by law one heating suffices for kashering and for baking, nevertheless it is good and upright, etc.; and the reason is perhaps the first time one place remained without coals and it was not on his mind; and further, with one heating there is reason to be concerned that he will not purge well, because he fears he will burn the bread; and according to this reason he must wait from heating a second time until it cools a bit; and so they practiced. And the later authorities wrote that if he forgot to heat the oven and kasher it before Yom Tov and needs to kasher it on Yom Tov, it is permitted to do everything in one heating and not to make a fire not needed for Yom Tov, since by law it is permitted, only that he be careful that the coals go over its entire surface.
