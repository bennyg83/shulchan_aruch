Seif 1. “An oven in which chametz is baked,” etc. Note: In certain places the practice is to plaster the oven on Pesach with cattle dung. I was asked whether one must fear that barley may be present in it and become chametz. I answered that there is no basis for concern. The oven is used only after it is first heated. What concern is there? If the concern is that the body of the oven became prohibited because the dung contained fermented barley, what follows? They do not subsequently bake in it; they merely place the pots in it for Shabbos. Even if chametz had affected the oven, the food would be permitted after the fact under the law of two pots, etc. If the concern is that the heat itself was generated by the barley and thus came from chametz, the oven is also heated by wood, for it is not used merely by holding a flame opposite it. This is therefore a case of two contributing causes, one permitted and one prohibited, which many authorities permit even regarding chametz on Pesach. Accordingly, when even definite chametz would not prohibit the food after the fact, there is no reason to prohibit the doubtful case initially. There are many doubts: perhaps no barley is present; even if barley is present, perhaps the Rokeach is correct that barley found in cattle dung does not become chametz; even if it can become chametz, perhaps it was already digested, for those who prohibit it prohibit only where it was not digested, while they agree where it was digested, as is evident from the ruling concerning wheat found in a chicken; even if it was not digested, perhaps it became putrid and unfit for a dog before the prohibition began. In light of these many doubts, together with the basis for leniency after the fact even without them, there is no concern initially. Leave Israel to its custom, and do not invent new stringencies in such a case. This appears correct and clear in my humble opinion.
