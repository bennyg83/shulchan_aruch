1) [Se'if 1] An oven in which chametz is baked, etc. There is no distinction between ovens whose openings are on the side, like ours, and small ovens whose openings are on top. Bach; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1. Although our ovens are made from bricks fired in a kiln and thus have the law of earthenware, which never loses its defect, internal heating nevertheless kashered them. Since they are heated from within, one is not protective of them out of concern that they might crack. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 1.
2) There. The coals must pass over its entire surface, etc. Tur writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. Bach explains that Tur appears to understand Rabbeinu Yonah as disagreeing with Rav Natrunai. According to Rav Natrunai, heating an oven is effective once coals or flame are present upon it. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, writes that flame is insufficient and the coals must pass over its entire surface. See there. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1, likewise writes that according to Rav Natrunai and Rav Amram, heating an oven is effective even if the coals did not pass over its entire surface. He therefore concludes that initially one should take Rabbeinu Yonah's opinion into account, but should not prohibit after the fact. He writes that this is the opinion of the Shulchan Aruch, which takes Rabbeinu Yonah's position into account only initially. See there. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1, however, writes that there is no room for leniency at all and that Magen Avraham plainly rules stringently. See there. This is the ruling cited below in subsection 4.
3) There. Just as it absorbs through coals, etc. This means that chametz is placed beside the coals during baking, and it therefore absorbs through coals.
4) There. Therefore, if it was heated several times, etc. After the fact, it appears that one may be lenient. If, however, it was heated only once without intent to kasher it, and it is uncertain whether the coals passed over its entire surface, the food should be prohibited after the fact. An oven has the law of an earthenware utensil and requires thorough heating, as stated at the beginning of siman 451. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Chok Yaakov, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 125, subsection 2.
5) If the oven was heated and the coals passed over half of it, while it is uncertain whether they passed over the remainder, and matzos were baked on the side certainly covered by coals, one may say they are permitted because of a compound doubt. Perhaps the coals passed over the entire oven, and perhaps cooking and baking do not spread absorption through the entire oven. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Pis'chei Teshuvah.
