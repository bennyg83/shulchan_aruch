It appears to me that a distinction should be made. Chametz is used throughout the year, and actual chametz is absorbed throughout every part of the oven. On Shavuos, however, the item was baked there only incidentally. Accordingly, even though the coals do not pass over the oven's entire surface, perhaps that particular place did not absorb anything initially. Even if it did absorb, perhaps at the time the oven was fired there had likewise not originally been coals in that place, and the principle is that it expels in the same manner that it absorbed. This differs from chametz, whose use is frequent, for the oven was heated regularly throughout the year and absorbed actual substance through repeated heating in every place.
