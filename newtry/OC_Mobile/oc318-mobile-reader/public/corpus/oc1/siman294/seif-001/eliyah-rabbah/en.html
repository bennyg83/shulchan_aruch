[1] [Levush] Therefore they established, etc. Another reason is that one should not request his personal needs before reciting Havdalah. They therefore established Havdalah in the blessing of knowledge, which is the first of the requests (Abudraham; Rashba, siman 739).
[2] [Levush] And afterward he begins, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov, Piskei Tosafot, Bach, and all the later authorities agree that one should begin Atah Chonantanu before "Grant us knowledge, etc.," and such is the custom. This also appears from the Abudraham cited above, for if one begins it after "Grant us knowledge, etc.," he would be requesting his needs before reciting Havdalah.
[3] [Levush] They are not relevant, etc. See Bach and Lechem Chamudot for explanations of this. One who did not pray on Motzaei Shabbos should pray Shacharit twice the next morning and should not say Atah Chonantanu, even if he did not recite Havdalah (Olat Tamid in the name of Radbaz). This requires study, because originally, when Havdalah was recited in the prayer, he certainly had to recite it the next day, and the same should apply now. The Gemara likewise implies that even if he recited Havdalah over a cup before praying, he must still recite Havdalah in the prayer. I do not have the responsum itself available for examination (Magen Avraham). One may answer that forgetting the entire prayer is comparable to having prayed but forgotten the insertion, in which case he need not repeat the prayer because we rely on Havdalah over the cup. This still requires study.
