1) [Seif 1] Havdalah is recited in Chonen HaDaas. This is because one may not request his needs before Havdalah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Another reason is that without understanding, one cannot distinguish. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
2) There: In Chonen HaDaas. The custom is to begin, 'You graciously grant knowledge to man and teach understanding to a mortal.' One then recites Atah Chonantanu, and afterward concludes, 'Graciously grant us from Yourself,' etc. Bach. See there for his challenge to Levush. This is also the view of Taz, paragraph 1; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1.
3) There: If he erred and did not recite Havdalah, etc. If circumstances beyond his control prevented him from praying after Shabbos, he recites two prayers during the day, the first for his present obligation and the second as compensation. He should not recite Atah Chonantanu, although he did not recite Havdalah. Radbaz, volume 1, siman 176, and in the new edition, siman 361; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. Although Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah question Radbaz's ruling and maintain that he must recite Atah Chonantanu in the compensatory prayer, and Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2, agrees, nevertheless, since the matter is disputed, passivity is preferable so that he will not interrupt the prayer. According to the opinion that he need not recite it, doing so would constitute an interruption. This applies when he can rectify the omission over a cup, such as when he recited Havdalah over a cup after Shabbos or has a cup over which to recite Havdalah on Sunday. If he has no cup, as in seif 2, however, he must recite Havdalah in the compensatory prayer even according to Radbaz. Mateh Yehudah there. See above, siman 108, paragraph 6.
4) A prayer leader who did not pray after Shabbos should pray Shacharis silently for his Shacharis obligation and recite the second prayer aloud as compensation and to fulfill the obligation of one who is not proficient. Radbaz in the aforementioned responsum. Magen Avraham questions this because of Atah Chonantanu, but Radbaz already explained his words in that responsum; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2.
5) An onen after Shabbos, who was not obligated to pray, should not recite Atah Chonantanu when he prays Shacharis on Sunday after the burial. Rabbi Moshe Amarillo in Responsa Devar Moshe, volume 3, Yoreh De'ah, siman 15; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1.
