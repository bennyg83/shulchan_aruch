Seif 3, and likewise regarding borei peri haAdamah, etc. There:

Which of them, etc. And Tosafot there, Magen Avraham s.k. 1 s.v. aval, etc., and it may be explained, etc., and as is implied in the Gemara there that it says one blesses on this, etc., one may bless whichever he wants first; and per Tosafot's explanation, the cherished one is first — as above.

And some say, etc. Beit HaGedolot — and Tosafot, Tosafot Rid, Rosh, and Shiltei Gibborim brought it — wrote that Beit HaGedolot explains there is no dispute except that one does not exempt the other through shehakol; and Tosafot wrote that it follows the view that even when their blessings are not equal — regarding shehakol, Tosafot s.v. aval, etc., and s.v. mitovi, etc. — therefore one needs, etc.; and see Rashba who was very puzzled by this. The essence appears to be a textual error in Tosafot s.v. mitovi, etc., and what they wrote "therefore," etc., belongs to the previous s.v. aval, etc.; they mean that Beit HaGedolot's ruling is not resolved except per the one who says when their blessings are not equal, etc., they dispute precedence, and he explains like R' Yehuda — if so, borei peri haEtz is first, as Beit HaGedolot ruled. Unlike this version of "when their blessings are not equal," etc., s.v. one blesses on, etc. — and per Tosafot's and Rosh's explanation in the name of Ba'al HaMa'or and Rif, that R' Yehuda concedes to the Sages — Beit HaGedolot's ruling is not resolved in radish and olive, for borei peri haEtz precedes, as above; and for this they wrote "therefore one needs," etc., in apples and nuts — meaning that in apples and nuts Beit HaGedolot's ruling resolves well even per the conclusion that when their blessings are not equal, s.v. one blesses on, etc., borei peri haEtz precedes even though it is not of the seven species, as above.
