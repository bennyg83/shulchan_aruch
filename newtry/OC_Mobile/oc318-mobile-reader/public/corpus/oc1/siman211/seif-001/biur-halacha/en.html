Radish and olive — and this is not like the view of "some say" in seif 3, which is Beit HaGedolot's view that borei peri haEtz precedes borei peri haAdamah — and even though the second is cherished it precedes; and Avudraham brought that many Rishonim hold like Beit HaGedolot that borei peri haEtz is first, and that is his halachic view — but Magen Avraham and Taz agreed that we follow the cherished one; see what is written below.

And some say that in this too one must precede the cherished — what the Mechaber wrote this view in the name of "some say" is not precise; on the contrary, the first view is the view of certain individuals [Rosh and Rabeinu Yitzchak's chiddushim on Berachot, and possibly Rashi's view too — see on this in Tosafot s.v. aval and in Rabeinu Yitzchak's words, who disagree about Rashi's view here]. The second view is the view of many Rishonim — behold it is the view of R' Hai Gaon brought in Rashba's chiddushim, and also the view of R' Chananel and Rif as is proven there in Rabeinu Yitzchak's words — that per their explanation Ula's view is that when their blessings are not equal, all agree the cherished is better; and so too is the view of Tosafot, Rabbenu Yonah, and Rashba. We can also add to this the view of Rambam and those who stand in his method, who also hold the cherished is better — only they hold that even when their blessings are equal, the cherished is also better than the seven-species type; and at any rate when their blessings are not equal it is certainly reasonable that we can add their view. See Bach, who also wrote that the essential practice follows this view, which is the view of most poskim; and so it appears from the Gra there. Know that Avudraham brought a different method — that even when their blessings are not equal the seven-species type precedes — and he leans thus in practice because many poskim exist in this method [and also because borei peri haEtz is considered from borei peri haAdamah per Beit HaGedolot's view]. In my humble opinion, in practice it is better to act like the Acharonim in this that the cherished is better — for although Avudraham brought many Rishonim who hold like R' Yehuda that the seven-species type is better even when their blessings are not equal, against this there are also many Rishonim who hold that even when their blessings are equal the halacha is like the Sages that we follow the cherished — behold they are Rav Hai Gaon and Rabeinu Yitzchak [as brought in Rashba and Hagahot Maimoniot], Rambam, Eshkol, Or Zaru'a [and see there that he brought Rif also holds thus], Ra'avad, and Ramban in Sefer HaChinuch and in his chiddushim on Rif; and at any rate we can add their view when their blessings are not equal to still more many poskim who hold we follow the cherished. Indeed, if the olive was also always cherished to him, it is certainly better to bless on it even though now the radish is more cherished to him per the Shulchan Aruch's ruling that this is called cherished; and even if the radish was always cherished to him but now the olive is cherished to him, it appears better to bless on the olive alone — from the side of many poskim Avudraham brought who hold the seven-species type is always better than the superiority of the cherished. And further, Rambam's view that cherished is called what is now cherished to him; and so appears Or Zaru'a's view, who wrote cherished is what one desires to eat — see there [and although the Shulchan Aruch ruled like Tosafot Rid and Rosh, we can still add their view to this]. We can also add the view of Rosh and Rabeinu Yitzchak [brought above] who hold that when their blessings are not equal one can bless on whichever he wants and we do not follow the cherished at all — and this is the first view in the Shulchan Aruch. From all these reasons it appears better that he bless on the olive if now it is cherished to him. One can also say that even in an unspecified case of borei peri haEtz and borei peri haAdamah — if borei peri haEtz is now cherished to him he should bless on it even though borei peri haAdamah is always cherished to him — because we combine Beit HaGedolot's view brought in seif 3, and also Rambam who holds this is also cherished, and also the aforementioned Rosh; and so Chayei Adam. However, if borei peri haAdamah is always more cherished to him and also now — it appears better in practice to act like the Acharonim that he bless on it first, like the first view in seif 3, and not like Beit HaGedolot which is the "some say" view there — and not like the aforementioned Avudraham who contradicts the Shulchan Aruch's ruling.
