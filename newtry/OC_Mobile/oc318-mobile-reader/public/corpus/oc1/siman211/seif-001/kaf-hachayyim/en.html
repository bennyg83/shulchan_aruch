1) [Seif 1] If their blessings are identical, etc. For example, olives and apples both require Borei Pri HaEtz, and one intends to cover both with a single blessing. Rashi on Berachos 41a; Beis Yosef.
2) There: He gives precedence to a species of the seven, etc. Since their blessings are identical and one is covered by the blessing over the other, a species of the seven is preferable. Tur. The reason is that since Eretz Yisrael was praised through these species, it is fitting to give them precedence for the blessing. Levush; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. The requirement to give precedence to a species of the seven applies specifically if he wishes to eat from both. If he does not wish to eat both, he recites the blessing over the one he wishes to eat, even if a species of the seven is before him, as stated below in the gloss to seif 5; see there.
3) There: He gives precedence to a species of the seven, etc. This applies even if it is half a fruit and the other is a whole fruit. If both are species of the seven, or neither is a species of the seven, he gives precedence to the whole one. If one is whole and the other is favored, the whole one takes precedence. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 57, paragraph 5. The mitzvah to recite the blessing over a whole item applies specifically at the time of the blessing. Afterward, if he does not wish to eat a kezayis, he should cut off a small piece so that he not eat an entire created item and enter into uncertainty regarding the concluding blessing, as stated above in siman 210, seif 1; see there.
4) There: If neither is a species of the seven, etc. For example, if he has an esrog and an apple, he gives precedence to the favored one. All agree with this, both R' Yehudah and the Sages. Beis Yosef.
5) There: He gives precedence to the favored one. The favored item or the item mentioned earlier in the verse takes precedence when both are before him and he has no reason to eat one before the other. If, however, the order of eating follows medical guidance or common practice, such as eating fruit and afterward drinking, the laws of precedence do not apply. Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 155.
6) There: He may give precedence to whichever he wishes, etc. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, however, writes that even if their blessings are not identical, he gives precedence to a species of the seven. If neither is a species of the seven, Borei Pri HaEtz precedes Borei Pri HaAdamah, and we do not follow which is favored; see there. This is also implied below at the end of seif 3, which says that some hold Borei Pri HaEtz takes precedence, apparently even when the item requiring Borei Pri HaAdamah is favored; see there. The universal practice is likewise to give precedence to Borei Pri HaEtz over Borei Pri HaAdamah even when the ground produce is favored. It appears to me that the reason is that according to Maran's unqualified ruling here, when the blessings differ, meaning Borei Pri HaEtz and Borei Pri HaAdamah, one may give precedence to whichever he wishes and we do not follow the favored one. Since there is another opinion that Borei Pri HaEtz takes precedence, the practice developed to give Borei Pri HaEtz precedence in order to satisfy the 'some say' opinion in seif 3 as well; see there.
