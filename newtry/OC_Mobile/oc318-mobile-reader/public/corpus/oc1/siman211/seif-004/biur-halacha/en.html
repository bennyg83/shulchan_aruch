Precedes for the blessing. The later authorities found this difficult. Above, in seif 1, the Mechaber cites anonymously the first opinion that where the blessings are different, one may bless over whichever item he wishes, and there is no mitzvah of precedence even if one of them is among the Seven Species. Yet here he rules anonymously that whichever species appears earlier in the verse takes precedence for the blessing. This implies that wheat precedes other fruits requiring Borei peri ha’eitz even though their blessings are different. The words of the Rosh and Tur themselves are also very difficult, for they are the source of the first opinion in seif 1, yet they also cite this rule that whichever appears earlier in the verse takes precedence. See Bach and Magen Avraham for their explanations. The Gra, in seif katan 15, cites Rabbeinu Yonah, who limits the rule that wheat takes precedence to a case where it was made into a cooked dish, which is especially significant, and therefore properly precedes all the other species. Eliyah Rabbah appears to understand it similarly, and Beit Meir writes likewise. Although the words of the Rosh and Tur remain strained according to all the explanations, the words of the Shulchan Aruch may be understood in this manner, as he himself explains in seif 5.
