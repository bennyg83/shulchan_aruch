Therefore dates precede, etc. In Tur it is written earlier "therefore olive precedes barley," etc.; and it appears this deals with a dish made from barley — for if you interpret specifically chewing barley whose blessing is borei peri haAdamah, then olive precedes it. But in a cooked dish of barley whose blessing is borei minei mezonot — more specific than borei peri haEtz of olive — this is not so; if so, why did Tur write afterward regarding wine? However a cooked dish of wheat and barley precede wine because its blessing is borei minei mezonot, which is also important and more specific to it, and also earlier in the verse — end of his words. And he should have written a great novelty at the beginning regarding olive — that even where barley is later in the verse, such as relative to olive — even so borei minei mezonot is better; and why did he wait to divide this, for it is not such a novelty but clear that here it deals even with a cooked dish. And you might say — in truth in a cooked dish the blessing is more specific — why not precede olive? It appears to me Tur did not consider this "more specific" blessing against borei peri haEtz; and against wine Tur did not write it is more specific than it. For he wrote only that it is also important and also earlier in the verse, and did not say more specific — only that he equated wine and borei minei mezonot regarding specificity. And further — if it were more specific than wine, it would turn out borei minei mezonot has two advantages — difficult why I need two advantages; behold the rule in our hands that all that is earlier precedes even without any advantage, only from precedence alone, as Tur wrote afterward regarding wheat preceding olive. And for this reason too barley precedes honey — our teacher did not write two reasons but this reason alone, meaning the reason of precedence in the verse alone as Tur wrote explicitly; but it is clear that thus he speaks — ostensibly wine has advantage from its blessing that it is more specific. And it turns out if the barley dish has advantage from precedence — they are equal together; therefore he preceded to say that also in borei minei mezonot it is more specific like wine, and also extra from its precedence in the verse; and also found in Rosh's words who wrote: certainly the blessing of HaMotzi or borei minei mezonot is considered from borei peri haEtz — it appears Tur does not hold so from the proof I wrote; and the examiner in Rosh will see he wrote this as a dismissal to say one should not bring proof to Rashi from this; and therefore it is also reconciled in what he wrote "therefore olive precedes barley" — meaning even in a cooked dish of barley, for barley is equally more specific with olive and there is precedence for olive in the verse, as it appears to me in explaining Tur's words correctly. And Rama wrote: but in a cooked dish of the five grains it is more important than the blessing on wine — not from the blessing itself that it is more specific, as he wrote, but from precedence in the verse, as he wrote. And in Levush I saw regarding this that he wrote this "therefore" that I mentioned in Tur's name, and wrote that the blessing of borei minei mezonot is more specific than wine; and he elaborated in analysis that one challenged him to say certainly this "therefore olive precedes barley" is specifically in chewing barley whose blessing is borei peri haAdamah, and olive means olive not fully grown, whose blessing is also borei peri haAdamah — see there if you wish; and all this is without necessity, for certainly here it deals with all barley, even in a cooked dish as he wrote, and it is clear after examination.
