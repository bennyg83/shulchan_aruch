There, seif 6. If one had before him a cooked dish made from spelt flour, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham, seif katan 13, who writes that if one had wine, a cooked barley dish, and an olive before him, what should he do? Wine precedes the olive, barley precedes wine, and the olive precedes barley. A similar problem appears in Zevachim 90b: the Gemara asks which takes precedence among a bird sin-offering, an animal burnt-offering, and tithe cattle. In Babylonia they answered that a sacrifice of the slaughtered type has precedence; in Eretz Yisrael they said that the animal burnt-offering enters together with the bird sin-offering. See Rashi there, who explains at length that the animal burnt-offering lends its superior status to the bird sin-offering that accompanies it, because the animal burnt-offering is superior to tithe cattle, which is of lesser sanctity. Accordingly, the same applies here. Although barley has the advantage of preceding grapes in the verse, the Torah nevertheless decreed that an olive precedes barley. Barley also possesses the same advantage that wine has, namely a more significant blessing, for the superiority of Borei peri hagefen over an olive comes from the fact that wine has its own distinct blessing whereas the olive requires Borei peri ha’eitz. Barley likewise has its own distinct blessing, Borei minei mezonos. Nevertheless, the Torah decreed that an olive precedes barley. Therefore, even now, the olive precedes barley, then barley precedes wine, and then wine follows. This is precisely analogous to the case there. Rambam rules likewise in chapter 9 of Hilchos Temidin UMusafin. The matter is even clearer according to Rambam’s wording there: since the bird sin-offering precedes the burnt-offering and the burnt-offering precedes tithe cattle, and so forth, the same applies here. Since an olive precedes barley and barley precedes wine, one first blesses over the olive, then the barley, and then the wine. One cannot reverse the order and argue that wine precedes the olive, the olive precedes barley, and therefore wine should come first. In the sacrificial case one could similarly argue that the burnt-offering precedes tithe cattle and tithe cattle precedes the bird sin-offering. Necessarily, the reason is that the burnt-offering’s superiority comes from its being an animal sacrifice, but this same advantage is overcome by the Torah’s decree that the bird sin-offering precedes it. Likewise here, wine’s precedence over the olive comes from its uniquely significant blessing. Barley possesses that same advantage through Borei minei mezonos, yet the Torah decreed that the olive precedes it. Therefore, the order remains olive, then barley, then wine. This appears clear and correct to me, and in my view it is a decisive proof. Examine it carefully, with Hashem’s help.
