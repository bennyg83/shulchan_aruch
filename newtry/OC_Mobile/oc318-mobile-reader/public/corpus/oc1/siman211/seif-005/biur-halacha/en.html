Provided that he intends, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that even if both items are before him, he must explicitly intend that the blessing exempt the second item. It is unreasonable that the less significant item should automatically exempt the more significant one. This applies whether the item not among the Seven Species is intended to exempt one of the Seven Species, or whether a less preferred item is intended to exempt the preferred one. This is evident from Beit Yosef at the end of the siman, citing Rashba, and Peri Megadim rules accordingly. If one is eating as a guest, however, since he relies upon the host’s table, it is as though he explicitly intended to exempt whatever the host may wish to bring him. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, citing Responsa Ginas Veradim.
