And he explained Bach: that is what is meant "whichever he wants," etc. meaning what Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote that when their blessings are not equal one blesses on whichever of them he wants — the intent is not to bless without consideration, but on that which is cherished to him now and therefore he wants it. Bach wrote the reason: since their blessings are not equal and one does not discharge his fellow, there is only precedence in a light consideration — we set aside the superiority of the seven-species type and the superiority of the cherished and follow what is cherished to him now; and when now both are equal in dearness, then we follow what is cherished habitually — for since the superiority of the seven-species type is set aside on account of what is now cherished, likewise it is set aside on account of what is cherished to him habitually. That which they said "everything earlier in the verse is earlier for blessing" — why did Tur write that wheat precedes olive? It deals when both were equal to him in dearness both now and always; then we follow the seven-species type — if so, what is earlier in the verse precedes:

And his words do not hold up, etc. when there is not, etc. — meaning Rosh gave a reason for what he said that they argue specifically when their blessings are equal, but when their blessings are not equal — according to his view. And he explained, and these are his words: one can say that certainly when their blessings are equal, that with one blessing one discharges his fellow, then it is reasonable to bless on the seven species and discharge the second; but when one must bless on each separately, this and that are not relevant at all — since one must bless on the second after eating the first, therefore one blesses on whichever he wants, end of his words — behold he wrote that when their blessings are not equal there is no relevance between them at all. And similarly Shelah wrote. meaning Shelah also wrote the opposite of Bach's reasoning: specifically when both were mentioned in Scripture we follow what is earlier, but what is mentioned in Scripture is not preceded to what was not mentioned in the verse; but he gave another reason than Magen Avraham: since Rosh wrote that when their blessings are not equal one blesses on whichever he wants because one is not discharged by his fellow's blessing, they have no relevance to each other and are separate from each other — if so, when both were mentioned in the verse they still have attachment and are not called separate, even though one is not discharged by his fellow's blessing — namely that both are of the seven species through which the Land of Israel was praised; if so, since they have one importance thereby they are called attached and have relevance to each other at present:

And likewise in the hints afterward he resolves this, and these are his words: on borei peri haEtz and borei peri haAdamah one blesses first on whichever he wants, end of his words; and afterward he wrote everything earlier in the verse is earlier for blessing, and wrote why wheat precedes olive, and barley precedes dates, and concluded: wheat and barley precede specifically when one made from them a cooked dish or bread, but chewing wheat whose blessing is borei peri haAdamah — borei peri haEtz precedes, end of his words — meaning in chewing wheat or olive which is borei peri haEtz it precedes; and his reason is unnecessary, for since borei peri haEtz precedes borei peri haAdamah it is already proven there is no precedence of borei peri haEtz over borei peri haAdamah; if so, because he holds Scripture does not deal with chewing wheat but only when one made from it a cooked dish or bread — if so, since olive is mentioned in Scripture and wheat is not mentioned in Scripture, therefore olive precedes it. If so, for our view it should be difficult: behold he wrote there that radish and olive — one blesses on whichever he wants; he should have preceded olive which is mentioned in Scripture and radish was not mentioned in Scripture. And in Rosh certainly Bach's explanation is unnecessary, as above — if so, perforce as Magen Avraham wrote.
