(2) And if their blessings are not equal, etc. This reasoning is the view of Rosh and Rabbenu Yonah; and the reasoning of "some say" that the cherished precedes — Tur and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semak. I wondered in Eliyah Rabbah, for in Semak siman 151 it implies the seven-species type precedes even when their blessings are not equal; and now I found in Piskei Rekanati siman 78 who wrote explicitly thus in the name of Semak. It appears to my humble opinion after investigation that our rabbi the Tur, may his memory be blessed, did not analyze Semak properly — for what he wrote whether their blessings are equal or not equal refers specifically to if among them there is of the seven species he precedes it even though it is not cherished; for even when their blessings are not equal the law is thus, as written in Hagahot Semak there. Our rabbi the Tur erred in his explanation and understood it refers to the cherished preceding; and I wonder more at Bach, Magen Avraham, and all the Acharonim who wrote that Ba'al Halachot Gedolot — who is "some say" — that seif 3 "borei peri haEtz precedes the blessing of peri haAdamah" argues with Semak; and therefore they ruled borei peri haEtz does not precede, and wrote Ba'al Halachot Gedolot is a lone individual. A great wonder — for Semak there ruled explicitly like Ba'al Halachot Gedolot that borei peri haEtz precedes; and so wrote Rekanati in his name. And further wonder — for in Semag siman 27, Mordechai, Agudah chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, Rokeach siman 329, Shibolei HaLeket, Tanya, Sefer Yere'im, Tashbetz siman 322, Piskei Rekanati, and Kaftor VaFerach ruled like Ba'al Halachot Gedolot — behold on the contrary Rosh and Rabbenu Yonah are lone individuals relative to all those great Rishonim and Acharonim. Also the reason Rosh rejected Ba'al Halachot Gedolot's words is because he explained the words "everyone blesses on this and returns and blesses on this" did not come to teach us a law of precedence at all, but that one does not exempt the other — and he challenged him that the language does not imply so; see there. I wonder — for in Tosafot Berachot daf 41 they explain that Ba'al Halachot Gedolot also explained regarding precedence, only he ruled like the one who says they argue also when their blessings are not equal; and so is explicit in Agudah; and so appears to me the explanation of Rambam's words — and not like Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh. Behold Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mordechai and Rabbenu Peretz that even when their blessing is not equal one blesses on the seven species; and he strained the Talmud's explanation to their words — and in my humble opinion it is clear the Talmud's explanation is as I explained in Ba'al Halachot Gedolot. Also what he wrote that it is against all commentators — I wonder, for in all the poskim I brought it is proven and clear thus, and so it implies in Agudah; and so it appears to me for halacha — for per the first reasoning one precedes whichever he wants. If so, he certainly does not commit a prohibition when blessing on the seven species; and likewise if it is not of the seven species, borei peri haEtz precedes borei peri haAdamah and we do not follow the cherished; see below s.k. 7.
