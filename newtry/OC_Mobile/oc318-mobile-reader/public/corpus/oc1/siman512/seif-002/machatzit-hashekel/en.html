(sk 2) Through a gentile it is permitted. Even food items via public domain — there are two reasons of stringency: taking out via public domain for gentile need; and also food items — lest he increase for him, it teaches us it is permitted: because of lest he increase there is no concern since he does not invite him; and also because of carrying out there is no concern since it is through a gentile — permitted; thus Rabbi Yitzchak. However Rabbi Yitzchak is likely to hold the Hagahot Maimoniyot who is the source of this law permitted only via public domain the prohibition of carrying out; but food items are forbidden, for even through agency — lest he increase. But Darkei Moshe disputes Rabbi Yitzchak — Hagahot Maimoniyot permits even food items as above.

(ב) And Yeshuot Shalom forbids etc. in the first chapter of Beitzah siman 37. He brought proof from Tosafot in the first chapter of Kesubos daf 7a that a Jew is forbidden to take out for a gentile on Yom Tov. Tosafot wrote: even though our public domain is only karmelit, for our public domain is not sixteen amot wide and its prohibition is only rabbinic — nevertheless on Yom Tov they decreed. Afterward he brought Yeshuot Shalom these words of Hagahot Maimoniyot that it is permitted to send to a gentile through a gentile; he wrote on it Yeshuot Shalom: I do not know why. Per what we rule: whatever is forbidden for a Jew to do is forbidden to tell a gentile to do, even rabbinic prohibitions — all the more so Torah prohibitions per what I explained — end quote. (Meaning Maharshal follows his view who wrote in chapter Ein Tzadin siman 8: even if sixty myriad do not traverse it, nevertheless it is called public domain if sixteen amot wide and open from gate to gate; and this is why he wrote all the more Torah prohibitions — meaning when it is not sixteen amot wide he concedes it is only karmelit; nevertheless telling a gentile is forbidden — all the more when sixteen amot wide there is Torah public domain per Yeshuot Shalom — evidently forbidden to tell a gentile. Nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote it appears to me to be lenient in our time, for we do not have a complete public domain; and this is as written above siman 345 sk 7 that we rule like the poskim that it is not called public domain unless sixty myriad traverse it daily — this is not in our time, unlike Maharshal — see there. If so, since public domain is not found at all in our time, one may be lenient in telling a gentile; and see above siman 508 seif 2.

(ג) After he prepared etc. — it is permitted to invite him. It appears to me specifically etc. that the gentile came to him near the meal. It should be said: per this what did Magen Avraham add by bringing Tanya's words — for this permission was brought in Rabbi Yitzchak who wrote: likewise a gentile who comes on his own etc.; and he said it comes to exclude the view of Taz who wrote in sk 4 that Rabbi Yitzchak permitted only when he comes on his own — then permitted to feed him without invitation and without urging; but if he urges and invites him, even if he comes on his own it is forbidden — what difference whether he invited him through agency to his house or invites him after he came to him — see there. And from Tanya's words who wrote after etc. permitted to invite him — it implies he permits even inviting and urging him since he came on his own.
