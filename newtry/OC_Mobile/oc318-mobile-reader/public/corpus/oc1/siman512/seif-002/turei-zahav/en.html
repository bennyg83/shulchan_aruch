Sons of the soldiers etc. In the second chapter of Beitzah they say R' Hillel ruled thus, but they say there and it disputes R' Yehudah ben Bava who said one invites a gentile on Shabbat and does not invite a gentile on Yom Tov lest he increase for him; if so it is difficult on Rambam who rules both. Tur wrote: truly forbidden in that of the soldiers even if they are not particular etc.; he wrote Rambam permits, and it is not so per our Gemara text — end quote. It implies he thinks Rambam's text lacks and it disputes; nevertheless one may say they certainly dispute — Rav Huna was not concerned lest he increase, for he truly increases here in all the bread he bakes; and one may say this case of Rav Huna is different — Israel has no share in the flour except through the bread he will give the child, as long as the gentile is not particular; therefore permitted, since the Israelite cannot take flour for one loaf for the child unless he bakes everything; unlike R' Yehudah ben Bava's case of inviting the gentile — Israel's portion would not be diminished if he did not invite the gentile; and likewise the Maggid wrote this. Question: if so the Tosafot's difficulty in ch. 2 daf 21 on dough of partnership with a gentile forbidden to bake on Yom Tov because possible to divide in kneading — here there is permission that Israel benefits that bread bakes well when the oven is full; and Tosafot answered this helps only if all the bread is Israel's that the choice is in his hand to eat each or this or that, and also lest guests come; but here half is a gentile's — forbidden to bake on Yom Tov — end quote. If so it is evident Israel's benefit that depends on what belongs to the gentile does not help — why does it help here that he benefits giving one child one of them? One may say it is different there — the body is Israel's and he can divide the dough, but because of benefit of good baking he wants to trouble for the gentile's — this is forbidden; unlike here where he has no bread at all without the gentile's share — here permitted to bake everything. Maggid wrote: possibly Rambam's text also has and it disputes in the Gemara, but he explains it on something else — namely they say there before these pots, meaning date pits — how throw to animals on Yom Tov per R' Yosi haGlili who said for you and not for dogs; and two via bread one carries for them — learn: where one does not do melacha such as for an animal, carrying is also forbidden except via bread; and on this R' Yehudah ben Bava disputes and says one invites a gentile on Shabbat — learn permitted to carry even though forbidden in melacha for him on Yom Tov. In truth this explanation does not fit well at all — carrying on Shabbat is different, permitted since it is fit at least for Israel; and Maharshal already rejected there ch. 2 siman 22 the Maggid's explanation for this. He explains the dispute is between R' Yehudah ben Bava and Rav Huna whether the flour is Israel's — there Rav Huna permitted and R' Yehudah ben Bava forbade, for Israel should not knead for a little bread to give the child and add for the gentile when he can take a little in kneading, except the gentile does not allow him. But Rambam who wrote the flour was the soldiers' — permitted to bake — end. This explanation is more astounding: if so whence for the Talmud that they dispute — perhaps Rav Huna deals with soldiers' flour like Rambam; and explicitly from the Gemara's words: those sons of the valley threw upon them flour of the soldiers etc. — it is clear the flour is the soldiers'; why make a dispute in the Gemara in vain? And further — certainly it should not enter the mind that Rav Huna would permit Israel's flour to bake from it for a gentile for baking a small loaf for a child when he could bake otherwise; rather Rambam's text must lack and it disputes in the Gemara; and likewise Behag whom Tur cites holds like Rambam. But if it is difficult for me — it is difficult: Tur wrote in the name of Baal HaItur siman 503 that what one can increase in cooking on Yom Tov is specifically before eating, but after eating she cannot cook and feed him an olive's worth from it — you have no greater evasion than this — end quote; and here we permit baking gentile bread for the evasion of bread for the child; and one should not strain to interpret here as before eating — for then Rav Huna should have disputed thus, as he distinguishes whether they are particular about bread for the child or not; and further the Gemara challenges Rav Huna from Shimon's act who did not come last night to the house of study because he needed to slaughter a calf for the soldiers; R' Yehoshua ben Bava told him he did not act well, for Torah said for you and not for a gentile — why, behold he may eat from it? Per what you would establish R' Hillel's words specifically before eating — it does not challenge from that calf, for there it was after eating, for it was last night; and this matter requires much study, for it cannot be reconciled per my humble view. My humble view: certainly from the law's side the evasion is forbidden like Baal HaItur we mentioned and Talmud Yerushalmi is there — only that one should not evade except here where obligation was upon them from royal law and difficult to be exempt; therefore they sought a pretext to find permission even any whatever is not otherwise permitted; therefore he did not find a question there except regarding something that was also necessity from the soldiers who needed them to slaughter a calf — and nevertheless R' Yehoshua ben Bava said: their gain came through their loss etc. I find slight support: Shimon haTimni slaughtered the calf on Yom Tov — how could he think? For all say for you and not for a gentile — rather certainly he did so from duress, for the soldiers sought to seize all they had as they say there, and it was loss of Yom Tov joy for they had nothing to eat; and per the second view in siman 514 Rama brings permitted to increase for this if he feared they would destroy everything — likewise here. R' Yehoshua ben Bava holds like the first view forbidding to increase even for this; it appears in this the statement of R' Yehoshua ben Bava who said their gain came through their loss — Rashi explained: what you profited in money you lost in being punished for desecrating Yom Tov — implies there is neither loss nor gain in truth, for one offsets the other. At first glance this is astounding — certainly punishment for Yom Tov desecration is very great in the World to Come — how is money gain weighed against it? Rather certainly there is no punishment in the World to Come since it was from duress; but since this is not a clear permission for him, he exaggerated that they would have punishment, God forbid. It appears in this way they say in the Gemara R' Yehudah ben Bava disputes from what he said one invites a gentile on Shabbat and does not invite on Yom Tov — this certainly deals from some necessity, for otherwise certainly not proper to eat with a gentile, especially Shabbat as stated in Pirkei Avot: whoever eats with a gentile is as if he eats repugnant things — rather certainly it deals from necessity; and nevertheless forbidden on Yom Tov; therefore Rav Huna disputes and permits since there is some necessity. We hold there is truly a distinction between duress or not; therefore Behag and Rambam properly ruled like R' Hillel who holds where there is duress, even money may be permitted through this evasion that each loaf is fit for the child — but not another way. And that which one does not invite on Yom Tov that poskim mentioned is where there is no duress — for it was not mentioned here that one invites on Shabbat; and that he said the reason lest he increase for him and did not forbid because of eating with him — one matter; and further he said on Yom Tov; and similar to this we find in chapter Bava Metzia. This cannot be interpreted in R' Yehudah ben Bava's words — for he said one invites a gentile on Shabbat, implying it deals from duress; and with this the complaint against Rambam rests from what they challenged him; and likewise proper to rule practically that one should not rely on this evasion fit for the child except specifically combined with a place where there is duress that will come through it cancellation of Yom Tov joy as above — correct and clear, with God's help.
