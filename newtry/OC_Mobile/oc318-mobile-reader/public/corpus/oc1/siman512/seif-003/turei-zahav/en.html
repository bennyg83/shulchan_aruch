(א) Forbidden to cook etc. — for we rule like R' Yosi haGlili who diminishes from you gentiles and dogs, and not like R' Akiva who diminishes only gentiles.

(ב) But forbidden to take out etc. — for he permitted only ordinary carrying, but Torah prohibition not; and it is difficult — for we challenge in the Gemara these pots how do we throw to animals, and this is from muktzeh reason as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rosh here nearby — since they are not fit for eating except through coercion; and the Gemara needed to answer via bread — why not answer since here there is only muktzeh prohibition which is rabbinic, permitted as they permitted here carrying which is also rabbinic; and who troubled to divide rabbinic prohibitions? This requires further study.

(ג) If he has something else, etc. For otherwise it is forbidden, since it is possible to separate in the dough what belongs to dogs; and it requires further study why he ruled that he must have something else — behold Ran's conclusion that even if he does not have, it is permitted "since it is possible" that something else will be available to him, as Beit Yosef wrote; and Beit Yosef challenged — even if he has a carcass, why is it permitted to bake all the dough, for since dogs eat from it, it turns out he does not need all of it for a person on Yom Tov; and one can answer in the name of Yerushalmi that it follows Rabbah Shimon ben Gamliel who said a woman fills an oven with bread because bread bakes nicely when that oven is full; and therefore one must say why Tur did not bring this law — for since Tur wrote in siman 507 that for us this reasoning does not apply in our ovens, therefore it is forbidden in this; but it appears to me there is no question at all, for since we say this matter of being able to appease with something else is as though it is impossible to separate in the dough — if so, certainly it is permitted, for everything is for the person's need and it is like an animal — half belonging to a gentile that it is permitted to slaughter on Yom Tov, as is evident in the Gemara; and it appears Yerushalmi disagrees with our Talmud in what it permits on account of being fit to appease with a carcass:
