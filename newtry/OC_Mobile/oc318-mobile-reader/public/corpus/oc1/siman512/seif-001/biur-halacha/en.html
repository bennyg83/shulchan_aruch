(א) One may not cook for a gentile, etc. — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote because it is written "it shall be made for you," etc. And behold in siman 518 we wrote that even regarding roasting and cooking we likewise say: since it was permitted for the need of food consumption it was also permitted not for need; nevertheless we require that there be some need of the day, and for a gentile's need it is not called a need of the day at all, as is evident from Tosafot Ketubot daf 7; and in this there is a Torah prohibition; and even per the view of those who hold there in Beit Yosef that by Torah law it is permitted even not for need at all, nevertheless by rabbinic law it is forbidden as is evident from Beit Yosef's conclusion there; and furthermore we can say that for a gentile's need, since the Torah revealed "for you" and not for a gentile, it is worse; and according to all views there is in this a d'oraisa prohibition; and likewise it is evident from Rambam in halacha 13 who holds there is a Torah prohibition in this; and apparently it is evident there in halacha 15 who holds there is also a full negative commandment in this, for we say we return it to its original prohibition, since it is written there one is not lashed for the reason of "since"; and it implies when this reason does not apply, such as if he cooked close to nightfall or a treifah, he is also lashed; nevertheless from the sugya of Beitzah daf 12 regarding slaughtering a voluntary burnt offering, for it says there one is not lashed for the reason of "since" although there too it expounds "for you" and not for Heaven — and therefore as Tosafot wrote there, the reason of "since" diminishes lashes and through the diminution of "for you" there is at least a positive commandment prohibition; and likewise in our matter according to Rambam who permitted entirely not for need there is only a positive commandment prohibition [and see in Mishnah Berurah halacha 15 who also proved that for Rambam there is a d'oraisa prohibition and the end of his words there apparently contradict these words of Tosafot]; and why Rambam needed for cooking for a gentile the reason of "since" although without it there is only a positive commandment prohibition — one can say because of "the other" he cited thus, for he included there cooking for profane need, see there; and Rambam copied in this the language of the Gemara according to Rabbah (Pesachim daf 46); and apparently likewise for carrying out for a gentile Rambam concedes to the other poskim that there is at least a positive commandment prohibition from the verse "for you," and it is worse than carrying out entirely not for need, and as for roasting and cooking as above; and likewise I found afterwards in Shulchan Aruch HaRav who wrote that even according to Rashi [and likewise according to Rambam who stands in that view] who holds regarding carrying out that we say "since" and it was entirely permitted even not for need at all, nevertheless for a gentile's need the Torah revealed to us the verse "for you" that it is worse and forbidden by Torah law, see there; nevertheless from the language of Maggid Mishneh it implies carrying out is different from roasting and cooking; and for Rambam who holds carrying out entirely not for need is also permitted, likewise it is permitted for an animal's need; and likewise that therefore it is also permitted for a gentile's need even though it is not a need of the day at all, for from one verse of "for you" we expound for both; and Gra copied and wrote that because of this the Mechaber omitted this law of Rama; but it is difficult — behold below in siman 518 seif 2 the Mechaber ruled anonymously to forbid carrying out for a gentile; and it is possible that regarding an animal, since early authorities were found who ruled like R' Akiva that melacha is permitted for an animal's need, therefore the Mechaber did not wish to be stringent regarding carrying out and relied on Maggid Mishneh who is lenient in this according to Rambam; but Rama who ruled anonymously in siman 518 even regarding carrying out — specifically when he needs it somewhat — and like the stringent view there, ruled anonymously in our matter also to be stringent:

(ב) It is forbidden to believe, and so on, from what has been brought in the name of the psalmist, that it is not possible for him to eat what he has prepared for himself:

(ג) And it is permitted to increase for his slave etc. — see Mishna Berurah that the reason is because it is one trouble, as above in siman 503; and for other gentiles it is forbidden lest he increase in another pot — thus many later authorities explained, as is evident from Darkei Moshe that his view is Mordechai disputes Raavyah and holds in one pot even if it is impossible to appease them with something else it is permitted. However in Beit Me'ir and likewise in Eliyah Rabbah they side with Mordechai not disputing Raavyah but explaining his words that even if it is possible to appease them with something else it is not permitted except in that pot (see there at length in Beit Me'ir); and in Avodat HaKodesh to Rashba it also implies this approach — see there. However it is clear from their words that with slaves and maidservents presumably it is possible to appease them with something else; if so for practical law there is not so much difference. However regarding an animal — Eliyah Rabbah at the end of the siman holds one may be lenient in one pot when he cooks for himself to increase also for it even when he has nothing with which to appease it with something else; for otherwise many early authorities hold the halachah follows R' Akiva and they are not diminished from you except idolaters — see there.
