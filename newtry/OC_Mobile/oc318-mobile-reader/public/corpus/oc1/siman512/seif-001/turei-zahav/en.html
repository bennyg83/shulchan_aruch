(א) For the sake of the UN. It will be done to you and not to the dogs

(ב) Lest he increase for him. Meaning lest he cook a pot by itself that he would not cook if not for the gentile — for if he did not increase for the gentile but only in the pot in which he cooks, there is no concern of prohibition; thus the Rosh; and for this reason there is permission regarding his slave, for there is no concern lest he cook another pot for him; and see what I wrote nearby in this siman that contradicts this.

(ג) Send him to his house by Rach. It seems that there is no ban on the house of Israel, but it is not necessary for the people of Israel, but for the purpose of the people of Israel, and if the people of Israel are not allowed to be shaken by the messenger of Israel, that they are not here:

(ד) And likewise a gentile who comes on his own. Rambam likewise wrote and concluded the reason that they already prepared for him; Tur wrote on this and it does not appear — even though they already prepared, still there is concern lest he increase for him if he is important and fit for this; rather he must tell him: if what we prepared for ourselves suffices for you, come and eat — end quote. It appears to me to decide for halachah: if he comes on his own and the Israelite gives him to eat without invitation and urging that he eat with him — no prohibition lest he increase; but if he urges him to eat with him, even if he came on his own — nevertheless since he is dear to him that he eat with him, we are concerned lest he cook another pot for him; and what difference whether he invited him through agency to his house or invites him after he came to him; and it is implied from Rambam's words that where there is concern lest he increase, what they already prepared does not help — we must fear lest he cook another pot, as it appears to me. And what Tur wrote he must tell him if it suffices etc. — thus in the Gemara: Marimar and Mar Zutra when a gentile happened upon them on Yom Tov told him: if you are pleased with what we troubled for you, good; and if not, extra trouble on your account we did not trouble ourselves; and therefore Rashba and Tur wrote he must say thus. Question on Rambam who did not mention this statement — he holds it is not decisive, as Maggid wrote. Why did Marimar and Mar Zutra say this? It appears on the contrary one should infer on this statement what benefit is there, since the matter does not depend on the gentile but on the Israelite who should be careful not to increase for the gentile — and why this statement? Therefore it appears Marimar and Mar Zutra said thus as appeasement to the gentile that he not be offended that he does not cook for him, since per our Torah law we have no obligation to trouble for him; if so this statement has no benefit for law — therefore Rambam did not mention it, and this is correct.

(ה) But for other gentiles in every matter it is forbidden. This matter is astounding — for Beit Yosef wrote thus in the name of Achronim, and this is his language: one should not send a gift on Yom Tov to a gentile, neither bread nor cooked food, if he intended to send to him, for it is impossible not to increase on his account; and what he wrote "a woman fills a pot," etc. — that is specifically for a Jew, end quote; and as Beit Yosef on this — the poskim's view is not so; and that is because Beit Yosef wrote before this in the name of Maggid Mishneh in the name of Raz who permits to send to a gentile through a gentile, etc.; and Rama himself brought this at the beginning of the siman and did not distinguish there whether he intended to send to him — for if the permit were without intending to send to him, one should permit even if he intended, for even without intending initially there is concern lest he cook another pot on his account, as written nearby in the name of Rambam; rather certainly here through sending to his house there is no concern lest he increase; and likewise Achronim wrote they are concerned lest he increase, which implies even in one pot this prohibition of increasing applies — this is not as Beit Yosef wrote in this siman in the name of Rosh and Ran that they are concerned specifically for another pot, as written at the beginning of the siman: Again I saw in Rashal chapter 2 of Beitzah siman 23 who wrote on these words of Achronim, and this is his language: it appears to me what he wrote to forbid even in one pot for a gentile's need — from that which it is permitted to increase in one pot for one's slave and maidservant, since the reason is one effort, likewise regarding a gentile; but specifically regarding bread they distinguish between Jew and gentile because each loaf requires effort separately and they permitted only because the bread bakes nicely in this — one can say they permitted specifically for a Jew since each loaf is fit for him and there is also the reason that the bread bakes nicely; but not for a gentile's need; but regarding a pot where the reason is not because it cooked nicely but because of one effort and there is no prohibition at all, likewise regarding a gentile, provided he does not say explicitly that he does it for a gentile's sake; but nevertheless the law is true that there is concern lest he cook another pot, end quote; and if Rama also intended this, he should not have permitted initially to send to his house for this reason:
