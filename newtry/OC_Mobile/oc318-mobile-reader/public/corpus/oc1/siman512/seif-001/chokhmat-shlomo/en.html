Seif 1 — one does not cook for a gentile on Yom Tov etc. I will note here one comment: in the Gemara of Makkot daf 22 it challenges there — if so, because of kindling he should not be liable, for he is already liable for cooking etc. This is very difficult for me: regarding lashes the case here is certainly intentional. Now regarding intentional — why does the Gemara challenge? Granted in what it challenges — let him also be lashed for sowing on Yom Tov — in that case sowing and plowing are one act, therefore it properly said there is no division of melachot on Yom Tov, and whoever does many prohibitions in one lapse is lashed only once. But with kindling and cooking they are not done together — first kindling and then cooking; if so what of it that there is no division of melachot — if he cooked twice and it is one prohibition he would be liable for each one, and changing names does not reduce if it were one name. One cannot say kindling and cooking are together — this is not found together, for regarding cooking he is liable only when it is cooked at least as food of the Temple court, as is evident in the first chapter of Shabbat regarding one who stuck bread in the oven — they permitted him to lower it before it comes to a sin-offering liability; it is evident he is not liable until it is baked; if so necessarily there is kindling first and then cooking; if so if he does cooking and cooking in two times he would be liable for two warnings; therefore also with kindling and cooking it is so. But if it is literally like one act of sowing and plowing — in that if he sows together in two places he is exempt — therefore also division of melachot such as plowing and sowing is exempt; this requires further study — examine it.
