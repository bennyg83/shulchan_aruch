And he does not wish to have relations. Beit Yosef proves this from the baraisa: “A house containing a Sefer Torah or tefillin is forbidden for marital relations.” It does not say that it is forbidden merely for one's wife to be with him in bed. Beit Yosef concludes that if he does not intend relations, it is as though his wife were not with him. I am astonished that such a leniency was derived from so weak a proof. The tanna states the principal prohibition, marital relations. It follows automatically that when one's wife is with him, one must ensure the situation is not one in which relations are forbidden, lest he forget and have relations. There is a clear proof from the end of the chapter HaYashen. The baraisa states that one who enters to sleep during the day, etc. Rabbi Yose says that young men always remove their tefillin because they are accustomed to impurity. Abaye explains that this refers to young married men whose wives are with them, lest they come to intimacy. Thus the Sages were concerned that one sleeping while wearing tefillin might come to relations. This is an a fortiori argument here. There, the tefillin are upon his body and the fear of Heaven rests upon him, as in the incident with Rabbi Zeira, who was very cheerful because he wore tefillin and therefore did not fear frivolity. Even so, we fear he may forget that he is wearing tefillin and have relations because marital relations are habitual. All the more so here, where the tefillin are beneath the pillow, we must fear that he may forget and have relations. One cannot argue that tefillin upon the body are more severe and therefore the Sages were concerned only there. The opposite is true: the lighter a prohibition appears, the greater the concern that one may violate it, as in the beginning of Kesubos regarding mourning, which is lighter for a bridegroom than the laws of niddah and therefore requires greater supervision. There is another proof from Pesachim concerning Yom Kippur. Whether communities light lamps or do not light them, both practices are intended to prevent marital relations, lest desire arise. We are clearly not concerned with deliberate transgression, for otherwise we should have the same concern with every menstruant wife. Rather, we fear that desire will arise and he will forget that it is Yom Kippur. The same applies here: we fear he will forget that tefillin are in his bed. Semag also writes that if they are placed in one vessel inside another, it is permitted to place them between the pillow and cushion even when his wife is with him in bed. This implies that in a forbidden placement, it is forbidden whenever his wife is with him, even if he does not currently intend relations. Below, Shulchan Aruch in siman 44 likewise speaks of a case where no woman was with him. Therefore, although I am unworthy to disagree with the Mechaber, it appears clear that one should prohibit this even when he does not intend relations, for the reasons stated.
