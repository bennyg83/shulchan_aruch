(Seif katan 4) A container, etc. “Since he altered the wording, etc.” Rambam writes in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillin: “If he placed them inside one container within another, he may place them beneath his pillow between the pillow and cushion.”
Beit Yosef writes that this implies that the pillow is not considered a container.
This is also implied by Tosafos, page 24, at the end of s.v. “But was it not taught,” etc. After Tosafos gives the answer to its question, that perhaps Rabbi Chiya refers to tefillin not directly opposite the head and that in such a case the pouch would be unnecessary, Tosafos writes: “Nevertheless, it is difficult. Let the Gemara answer that Rabbi Chiya refers to a case where they are not directly opposite the head and his wife is with him, so that one container within another is required, and that is why the pouch is needed. This requires study.” If the pillow were not considered a container, what would the pouch accomplish? There would still be only one container. It is therefore clear that Tosafos holds that the pillow counts as a container. Magen Avraham could also have brought proof from the beginning of Tosafos's words. Tosafos says that if the tefillin are not directly opposite the head, why is the pouch necessary? Yet at least one container is required. If the pillow were not considered a container, the pouch would still be necessary. This is not conclusive, however. A vessel designated for the tefillin counts as a vessel when only one vessel is required, but when one container within another is required, two designated containers do not suffice. Magen Avraham therefore preferred the proof from the end of Tosafos, which shows that the pillow counts as a container even toward the requirement of one container within another.
