Shulchan Aruch seif 3 And if his wife is with him — per what the author wrote in seif 4 that his wife with him means specifically when he wishes to have relations — if so, what is he teaching here when placing tefillin under his head on the bed? Even if he places them in that room he needs vessel within vessel as explained in seif 2. And per Beit Yosef that halacha is like Rambam: what is placed between pillow and bolster in their pouch is not like vessel within vessel but requires vessel within vessel before placing between pillow and bolster — if so, certainly if tefillin are not on the bed where he sleeps but only in that room, it suffices to place them in their pouch between pillow and bolster even if he wishes relations — kal vachomer from Magen Avraham s"k 2 regarding spreading tallit on the pouch even if not wrapped below it is vessel within vessel — all the more between pillow and bolster covered above and below. Therefore he teaches that when placing them on the bed under his head, even not opposite his head and wishing relations, he needs vessel within vessel before placing between pillow and bolster. But per Magen Avraham s"k 4 who retracted and holds halacha is not like Rambam — why did the author write this law here? It is kal vachomer from seif 2, as we say; and one may say the author wrote here to inform us that even here, where tefillin are on the bed under his head, specifically when wishing relations he needs vessel within vessel; but if he does not wish relations it is as if his wife is not with him and he does not need vessel within vessel — this is not proven from seif 2, for one could say there specifically when tefillin are only in the room and not on the bed the prohibition is only when wishing relations — requires investigation. Similarly Bach wrote per his approach brought in Taz and Magen Avraham s"k 3: what the author wrote "his wife not with him — permitted" means through vessel within vessel; "and if his wife with him needs vessel within vessel" means even if tefillin are not on the bed but in the room as in seif 2; and what he repeated here is to inform us that only when wishing relations is it called his wife with him — see there; but this is astonishing, for in seif 2 he wrote forbidden to use his bed — it is clear there the prohibition is only when having relations — what more is he teaching here? But per Taz and Magen Avraham, when his wife is not with him in seif 2 we do not require vessel within vessel — he teaches that even if his wife is with him and he does not wish relations he need not vessel within vessel — not proven from seif 2 — requires investigation.
