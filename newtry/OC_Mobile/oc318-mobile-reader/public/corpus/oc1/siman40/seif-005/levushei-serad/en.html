Shulchan Aruch, seif 3. “If his wife is with him.” According to what the Mechaber writes in seif 4, that “his wife is with him” applies only when he intends marital relations, what is added here by the rule concerning tefillin placed beneath his pillow in the bed? Even if the tefillin were merely in the same room, one container within another would be required, as explained in seif 2. According to Beit Yosef, who rules like Rambam that tefillin placed between the pillow and cushion while inside their pouch are not considered one container within another, the matter is clear. If the tefillin are not in the bed in which he sleeps but merely in the room, it is sufficient to place them in their pouch between the pillow and cushion even when he intends relations. This is an a fortiori inference from Magen Avraham, seif katan 2: when a tallis is spread over the tefillin pouch, even without wrapping beneath it, this is considered one container within another. All the more so when the pouch is between the pillow and cushion and covered above and below. The Mechaber therefore teaches that when the tefillin are in the bed beneath his pillow, even though not directly opposite his head, and he intends relations, they must first be placed inside one container within another before being placed between the pillow and cushion. According to Magen Avraham, seif katan 4, however, the Mechaber retracted and did not rule like Rambam. It is then difficult why the Mechaber repeated this law, since it follows a fortiori from seif 2. One may answer that he wrote it here to teach that even when the tefillin are in the bed beneath his pillow, one container within another is required only when he intends relations. If he does not intend relations, it is regarded as though his wife were not with him and no double covering is required. This cannot be derived from seif 2, where the tefillin are only in the room and not in the bed. Bach similarly explains according to his view, cited by Taz and Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, that when the Mechaber says it is permitted if his wife is not with him, he means with one container within another. When he says that if his wife is with him one container within another is required, he means even if the tefillin are not in the bed but only in the room, as in seif 2. According to Bach, the repetition teaches that one is considered to have his wife with him only when he intends relations. See there. This explanation is surprising, because seif 2 already says that it is forbidden to have relations in such a room, clearly showing that the prohibition applies only when relations occur. What then is added here? According to Taz and Magen Avraham, however, when his wife is not with him no double covering is required in seif 2. The present seif therefore adds that even when his wife is present but he does not intend relations, no double covering is required. This cannot be derived from seif 2, as explained.
