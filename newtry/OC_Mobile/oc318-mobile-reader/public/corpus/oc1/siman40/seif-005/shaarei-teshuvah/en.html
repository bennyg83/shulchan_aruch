Permitted. See Be'er Heitev and Ohel Yaakov, siman 1, concerning synagogue benches. Some people make a small box beneath the bench at their designated seat in which to store their tallit and tefillin, and then sit on the bench. In some cases there is no empty space one handbreadth high between them and the tefillin stored there. He initially writes that this is forbidden. In the following responsum, however, he presents a basis for permission. One may say that the prohibition against placing them beneath one's feet (and likewise sitting upon them) applies where there is no height or empty space at all between the tefillin and the person sitting or his feet, because he presses upon them with his feet or with the weight of his body, which is degrading, etc. He concludes: “Were I not hesitant, I would say that the fact that he thereby protects them is preferable,” etc. All the more so when this keeps them away from his house and safeguards them in the synagogue, a holy place; if care for their sanctity is maintained through some elevation, there appears to be no basis for objection. See there. Nevertheless, it appears that one should be stringent to provide a height of one handbreadth, in accordance with the dispute in the Yerushalmi that he cites there regarding a Torah scroll. A conscientious person should be stringent here as well, because one must be exceedingly careful with the sanctity of tefillin, as explained above.
