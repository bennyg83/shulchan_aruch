It is permitted. See Taz and Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, on this matter. You can see that the Taz added a qualification here. The questioner inferred from the ruling concerning a cap that placing tefillin opposite one’s head is also permitted. Yet Rabbi Yirmeyah required only that they not be directly beneath his head when they are in a single covering. According to Rabbeinu Yonah’s understanding of the Rambam, however, even when they are not directly beneath his head, two coverings are required. The question is therefore valid: since the only explicit permission concerns a place not opposite his head, it follows that opposite his head even two coverings are forbidden. Otherwise the Gemara could simply have taught that two coverings are permitted opposite his head. Rav Chiya’s reference to a cap necessarily permits even a position opposite his head when there are two coverings, for if it were not opposite his head no cap would be needed. The Gemara answers that the cap itself proves the opposite. The permission applies only when the tefillin are not opposite his head. A long cap can project beyond the pillow, so that the tefillin are not opposite his head. Opposite his head they are always forbidden, even with two coverings. When they are not opposite his head, even the cap alone is permitted, since its projecting portion constitutes one covering, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 3. See also siman 80.
