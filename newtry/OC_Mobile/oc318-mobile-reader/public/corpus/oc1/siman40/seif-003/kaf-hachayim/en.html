(3) Permitted to write verses on paper and hang at the synagogue entrance — Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tur in Mahari'at part 2 Orach Chayyim siman 3; Magen Avraham s"k 1; Beit David note 1; Eliyah Rabbah note 1; Shitilei Zetim note 2. But for another reason one should forbid, for one may not write a megillah for a child to learn from, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 283 — see Beit Hillel. Even per Rif who is lenient, it is because of "a time to act for Hashem, they have voided Your Torah," for not everyone can write the whole Torah, unlike these verses; and so Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 283 s"k 40 protests against those who write verses on the wall for a good sign — see there. Nevertheless there is a difference from Mahari'at's responsum: if he writes only three words in one line it is permitted due to the prohibition of not writing a megillah, as in Yoreh De'ah there seif 3; and so Machatzit HaShekel s"k 1 and Beit David Petchei Olam note 1. Machatzit HaShekel further wrote: if one writes on the synagogue wall things written in prayer books, such as thirteen attributes of mercy or the verse "and it was when the ark traveled," we have no concern, for as permitted in prayer books due to "a time to act" relying on Rif, so permitted on the wall — what difference wall or prayer book? Both are megillah; and such is custom — end quote. See Beit Hillel on Yoreh De'ah there and Petach Tot note 2 — see there.
