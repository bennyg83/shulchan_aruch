(Seif katan 3) It is permitted, etc. This citation was mistakenly printed upon seif 2. It refers to the words in seif 3: “If his wife is not with him, it is permitted,” etc.
And Bach means, etc. Meaning, with one container inside another. The Mechaber first says that directly opposite one's head it is forbidden even with one container inside another. He then says that when not directly opposite the head it is permitted, which Bach interprets as permitted with one container inside another. Bach then addresses the Mechaber's later phrase, “If his wife is with him, one container inside another is required.” Since, according to Bach, even when his wife is not with him a double covering is required, he explains that the Mechaber means: if his wife is with him, one container inside another is required even where the tefillin are not in the bed in which he sleeps, but only in the room. Bach writes that this also appears from the Gemara in Berachos 24a. The Gemara asks how tefillin should be placed at one's head. Rabbi Yirmiyah says: between the pillow and cushion, not directly opposite the head. Yet Rabbi Chiya taught that they may be placed in their pouch beneath one's pillow. The Gemara answers that the projecting end of the pouch extends outward. The “pouch” is the tefillin bag, and the protrusion is the end containing the tefillin. Tosafos and Rabbeinu Yonah ask why Rabbi Chiya's teaching cannot simply mean beneath the pillow but not directly opposite the head, like Rabbi Yirmiyah. They answer that if the tefillin were not directly opposite the head, why did Rabbi Chiya require them to be in their pouch? It must therefore be that they lie beneath the pillow itself. Bach understands the Gemara's answer, that the pouch protrudes outward, as meaning that it still lies between the pillow and cushion but the tefillin are positioned toward the outside. He then asks: why is the pouch still required? Since the tefillin protrude outward, they are no longer directly beneath the head. He concludes that even when they are not directly opposite the head, because they are at the head of the bed, one container inside another is required.
On the contrary, the opposite is more reasonable, etc. Namely, the case may simply be one where they are not directly opposite the head. There was no need to say that the pouch protruded outward.
This is the explanation: since the pillow, etc. This differs from Bach's understanding. “Outward” means entirely beyond the pillow and cushion. The pillow therefore does not cover the tefillin, and the pouch is required so that there is at least one container. That is sufficient. One container inside another is unnecessary because the tefillin are not directly beneath the head.
