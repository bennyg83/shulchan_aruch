(s"k 3) Permitted, etc. Printed in error on seif 2; it refers to what is stated in seif 3: if his wife is not with him — permitted, etc.

And Bach — meaning, etc. In vessel within vessel; it refers to what is written opposite his head, even vessel within vessel forbidden — therefore "but not opposite," etc. — permitted, meaning without vessel within vessel; it refers to what was first written in Shulchan Aruch and afterward "and if his wife with him needs vessel within vessel" — behold even without his wife he needs vessel within vessel. Bach explains: if his wife is with him he needs vessel within vessel even if tefillin are not on the bed where he sleeps but in the room — since his wife is with him he needs vessel within vessel. And what Bach wrote "as in the Gemara Berachot 24a: how does he place them? R' Yirmiyah: between pillow and bolster not opposite his head; and R' Chiyya taught: he places them in a hat under his head; and he answers: he takes out the brim outside" — Tosafot and R' Yitzchak challenged: why ask? Perhaps under his head means not opposite his head; and they answered: if so why need a hat? Even without a hat; rather we learn it deals even opposite his head. And Bach understood the answer "he takes brim outside" that tefillin protrude outside and are still under pillow and bolster; the challenge remains: why need a hat? He concludes: even not opposite his head requires vessel within vessel.

On the contrary it is more reasonable, etc. E.g. not opposite his head, and he need not say he takes brim outside, etc.

Meaning: since the pillow, etc. Not as Bach understood; rather outside — meaning outside pillow and bolster, and the pillow does not cover the tefillin; therefore he needs at least one vessel, the hat, and that suffices; we do not need vessel within vessel since they are not under his head.
