Seif 3: Even to place, etc. This is inferred from the statement, “Even if his wife is with him,” rather than saying, “Even beneath his feet,” since where his wife is with him it must necessarily be one vessel within another.
Even within a vessel, etc.; and even. See Tosafos there, 24a, s.v. “but was it not taught, etc.,” where the teacher says, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah writes likewise. This must refer to a case where his wife is not with him, as Tosafos asks. Nevertheless, etc. From here he also infers that when his wife is not with him and the tefillin are not directly beneath his head, one vessel within another is unnecessary. This is the meaning of “but, etc.; and if, etc.” The pillow is the second vessel, as Tosafos writes there. This is unlike Rambam.
