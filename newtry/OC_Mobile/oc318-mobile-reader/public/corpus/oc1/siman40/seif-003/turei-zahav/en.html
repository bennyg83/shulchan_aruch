It is permitted. Meaning, even without one vessel inside another. Beit Yosef writes likewise. At first glance this seems difficult from the Gemara. Rabbi Yirmiyah says to place the tefillin between the pillow and cushion, not directly beneath one's head. Rabbi Chiya teaches that one may place them in their cap, meaning their pouch, beneath one's head. The Gemara answers that the protruding portion of the pouch extends outward. Tosafos asks why the Gemara does not simply answer that “beneath one's head” means not directly beneath the head. Tosafos answers that if so, why specify that they are in their pouch? Even without the pouch it would be permitted. This implies that even when not directly beneath the head, one vessel within another is required, contradicting Shulchan Aruch's ruling based on Rabbeinu Yonah. It appears to me that the Gemara actually supports Shulchan Aruch. Rabbi Yirmiyah says to place them between the pillow and cushion, not directly beneath the head. He does not mention their pouch. This implies that when one's wife is not with him, there is no difference between one or two vessels so long as the tefillin are not directly beneath his head. If they are directly beneath his head, it is forbidden in every case. The Gemara therefore challenges him: since he found permission only when they are not directly beneath the head, it appears that directly beneath the head is always forbidden. Yet Rabbi Chiya permits it in one vessel within another, namely inside their pouch. The answer is that Rabbi Chiya did not mention the pouch to teach that one vessel within another is required and thereby permit even directly beneath the head. Rather, the pouch clarifies that the case was not directly beneath the head: the portion of the pouch containing the tefillin protruded beyond the head. This appears correct. My father-in-law Bach explains that even according to the Gemara's answer, one vessel within another is required even when they are not directly beneath the head. This is difficult from Rabbeinu Yonah's wording, which mentions only between the pillow and cushion without a pouch. Tosafos's question itself arose because Rabbi Chiya specifically mentioned the pouch, implying that the ordinary case is without one.
