Permitted. It seems plain his intent: with one vessel permitted — so Terumat HaDeshen and Rabbenu Yerucham, that this is Rambam's view; Bach wanted to explain Shulchan Aruch's "permitted" means vessel within vessel — every examiner will judge justly that it is not so. And what Bach wrote "as in the Gemara, on the contrary," etc. — if you hold not opposite his head requires vessel within vessel, Tosafot and R' Yitzchak's challenge returns; rather answer e.g. they were placed not opposite his head, and therefore do not require vessel within vessel, as R' Yitzchak said; and what he answers "he takes brim outside" — meaning since pillow is not on the hat there was only one vessel, the hat containing tefillin.

Vessel within vessel. From Rabbenu Yerucham's change of Rambam's wording it implies he holds only one vessel is needed and pillow is a second vessel; and so Tosafot 24 end s.v. "and R' Chiyya taught," and Agudah: it implies by law forbidden to place them at his head except the Sages permitted to guard from thieves and mice; if guarded elsewhere he should not place on bed — as siman 43 seif 7; however if above his head a place high three handbreadths or low three — permitted — see there in Gemara.
