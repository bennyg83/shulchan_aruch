It is permitted. It is obvious to me that the Mechaber means it is permitted with one vessel alone. Rabbeinu Yonah writes likewise, and Beit Yosef states that this is Rambam's view. Bach wished to interpret Shulchan Aruch's word “permitted” as meaning permitted only with one vessel inside another. Any careful reader will judge that this is incorrect. Bach claims the Gemara implies his interpretation, but the opposite is true. If even when the tefillin are not directly beneath one's head one vessel within another were required, Tosafos and Rabbeinu Yonah's question would return: the Gemara should answer that the tefillin were placed away from directly beneath the head. Rather, one vessel within another is unnecessary, as Rabbeinu Yonah writes. When the Gemara answers that the protrusion of the pouch extends outward, it means that because the pillow does not rest upon the portion of the pouch containing the tefillin, there is only one vessel, namely the pouch itself.
One vessel inside another. Since Beit Yosef altered Rambam's wording, it appears he holds that only one actual vessel is needed, with the pillow counting as the second. Tosafos on page 24, at the end of s.v. “but Rabbi Chiya taught,” implies this, and Agudah writes likewise. The Gemara implies that by strict law it is forbidden to place the tefillin near one's head. The Sages permitted it only to protect them from thieves and mice. Therefore, if they can be protected elsewhere, one should not place them in the bed, as stated in siman 43, at the end of seif 7. If, however, there is a place above one's head higher than three tefachim, or below it lower by three tefachim, it is permitted. See the Gemara there.
