(1) It is forbidden to hang them. On a peg, because this is a disrespectful manner of treating them.
(2) Whether by the boxes. This means whether the boxes hang downward or the straps hang downward. Nevertheless, if one merely happens to be holding the tefillin in his hand while the straps hang downward, there is no need for concern. If the tefillin themselves hang, however, it is forbidden in every circumstance. This does not include the head-tefillin hanging in the air while one holds it by its straps and places it upon his head, because this is necessary for putting it on. Some people place the head-tefillin on a shelf or the like while putting it on.
(3) In their pouch. It is forbidden to suspend a Sefer Torah in any manner, even when it is inside the aron kodesh and one suspends the aron kodesh. See Chayei Adam, rule 31, who writes that even if the aron kodesh is fastened to the wall with pegs, it is forbidden as long as nothing stands beneath it. Sacred books, such as volumes of the Talmud and the like, have the same law as tefillin. Nevertheless, sacred books or siddurim affixed within covers that have a silver chain may not be suspended by that chain. The covers are not comparable to a pouch, because they are attached to the siddur and are therefore considered part of the siddur itself. It is permitted to write verses on paper and hang them at the entrance of a beit haknesset. The common practice is to fast if one's tefillin fall from his hand to the ground without their case. The same applies if a Sefer Torah falls, even inside its case. See Eliyah Rabbah, who writes that if tefillin fall even inside their case, one should give a perutah to tzedakah.
