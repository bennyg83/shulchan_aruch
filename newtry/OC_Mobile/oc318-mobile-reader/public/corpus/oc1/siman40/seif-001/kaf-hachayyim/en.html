1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to suspend tefillin, etc. If one merely happens to hold the tefillin in his hand while the straps hang down, there is no concern; but if the tefillin boxes hang down, it is forbidden. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1. This means that it is permitted only when he holds the boxes in his hand and the straps happen to hang down. If he holds the straps while the boxes hang down, it is forbidden even temporarily. Taz, however, writes in se'if katan 1 that this does not include holding the straps of the head tefillin while the box hangs in the air as one places it upon his head, because this is necessary for putting it on and is not degrading. It appears that they do not disagree: Magen Avraham forbids holding the straps while the tefillin hang only when one is not putting them on, but when putting them on he concedes to Taz that it is not degrading. So write Machatzit HaShekel, ot 1; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1; and Shemen HaMaor, ot 1. Mattei Yehuda, ot 2, however, writes that in his opinion one should hold the box and allow the straps to hang, since this is possible. He reports seeing meticulous people observe this precaution, and understands Magen Avraham's prohibition against suspending tefillin even temporarily as applying even while putting them on. Only the straps may hang, because that is necessary for putting them on; see there. Chesed LaAlafim, ot 1, likewise writes that even while putting them on one should be stringent not to hold the knot while the box hangs until it is placed on his head. Instead, he should hold the knot and box together, place the box in position, and then place the knot in position. Shitilei Zeitim, ot 1, also writes that one should be stringent even while putting them on when it is possible to hold the box; Taz discusses a case in which that is impossible. Therefore, initially one should act as Mattei Yehuda, Chesed LaAlafim, and Shitilei Zeitim write. If this cannot easily be done, he may rely upon Taz, who permits it for the purpose of putting them on. Soferim U'Sefarim, ot 1, and Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, cite Taz and appear to agree with him. This is especially so because Peri Megadim, Machatzit HaShekel, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, and Shemen HaMaor write that Magen Avraham also concedes to Taz and permits it while putting them on. Yafeh LaLev, ot 1, likewise writes that when it cannot easily be done one may rely upon those who permit it; see there.
2) There. It is permitted, however, to suspend tefillin in their pouch. A Sefer Torah may not be suspended even in its covering. Berachos 24a at the end of the page and Chiddushei HaRashba; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1; Soferim U'Sefarim, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Peri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, ot 1; Erekh HaShulchan, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; Petach HaDevir, ot 1; and Shitilei Zeitim, ot 2. Even if the Sefer Torah stands inside the Aron Kodesh, it is forbidden to suspend the Aron Kodesh. Chayei Adam, rule 31, ot 43, writes that it is forbidden even when the Aron Kodesh is attached to the wall with pegs; see there and Nishmat Adam, ot 5. Pischei Olam, ot 1, cites this and writes there in the name of Mishna Berurah that volumes of Talmud and the like have the same status as tefillin.
3) It is permitted to write verses on paper and hang them at the entrance to a synagogue. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, in the name of Maharit, part 2, Orach Chayyim, siman 3; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1; Soferim U'Sefarim, ot 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; and Shitilei Zeitim, ot 2. For another reason, however, it should be forbidden, because one may not write a scroll of isolated passages for a child to study, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 283, seif 2. Even according to the Rif, who is lenient, this is only because, 'It is a time to act for Hashem; they have voided Your Torah,' since not everyone can write the entire Torah. That reason does not apply to the aforementioned verses. Taz, Yoreh De'ah 283, se'if katan 1, likewise protests against those who write verses on a wall as a good omen; see there. Nevertheless, Maharit's responsum is relevant if he writes only three words on a single line, which is permitted with respect to the prohibition against writing a scroll of isolated passages, as stated there in Yoreh De'ah, seif 3. So writes Machatzit HaShekel, se'if katan 1, cited by Pischei Olam, ot 1. Machatzit HaShekel further writes that if one writes upon a synagogue wall passages found in prayer books, such as the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy or the verse 'When the Ark would journey,' there is no concern. Just as reliance upon the Rif permits writing them in prayer books because 'It is a time to act,' so it permits writing them upon a wall. What difference is there between a wall and a prayer book? Both are scrolls of isolated passages. Such is the custom. See also Beit Hillel on Yoreh De'ah there and Pitchei Teshuvah, ot 2.
4) Prayer books that are permanently attached to boards having a silver chain may not be suspended by that chain. This is unlike tefillin, which may be suspended in their pouch, because there the pouch is an independent article. Here, however, the boards are attached to the prayer book and are considered part of the prayer book itself. Magen Avraham there; Soferim U'Sefarim there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Yad Aharon, Hagahot HaTur; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 2; and Shitilei Zeitim there.
5) Knesset HaGedolah writes in Hagahot HaTur: 'The custom is that one whose tefillin fall fasts. In Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 12, you will find support for this custom.' Magen Avraham cites this in siman 44, se'if katan 5; see also Yad Aharon, Hagahot HaTur; Machazik Berachah, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, siman 44, ot 2; and Shitilei Zeitim there, ot 5. Mishpetei Shmuel likewise writes that one from whose hand a Sefer Torah falls should fast. Knesset HaGedolah cites this in Yoreh De'ah 282, as does Birkei Yosef there in Shiyurei Berachah, ot 1, and Magen Avraham in the aforementioned passage. Magen Avraham writes there: 'It appears to me that regarding tefillin this refers to their falling without their case, but regarding a Sefer Torah the rule applies even in its covering.' This means that one must fast when tefillin fall only if they fell without their case; if they fell in their case, he need not fast. If a Sefer Torah fell, he must fast even if it was in its covering. So write Shulchan Aruch HaRav and Shitilei Zeitim there. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, siman 566, ot 3, cites the following from a manuscript of Mahari Bruna: 'My teachers were stringent and required a fast even if the tefillin fell to the ground together with their pouch. In such a case I am lenient and require giving a perutah to charity. A tefillin box must actually fall to the ground, even if the straps remain in his hand; there is no concern if only the straps fall.' Eliyah Rabbah here, ot 5, similarly writes in his name that if they fell in their case one should give a perutah to charity, while there is no concern if the straps fall to the ground; see there. So write Baer Heitev, siman 571, se'if katan 1; Machazik Berachah here, ot 2; Machatzit HaShekel, siman 44, se'if katan 5; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 5; Yafeh LaLev, siman 44, ot 2; and Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Chayei Sarah, ot 18. Chida writes in Chayyim Sha'al, part 1, siman 12, that even one from whose hand the uncovered tefillin themselves fell may give charity to atone for the sin of their fall if he is weak and fasting is difficult for him. This is certainly so if he is a Torah scholar and fasting would reduce his Torah study. Charity is powerful enough to atone even for an intentional sin, as our Sages of blessed memory said, and all the more so for an unintentional act or one beyond his control; see there. So write Chesed LaAlafim, Yafeh LaLev, and Ben Ish Chai there. R' Chayyim Palachi likewise writes in Kaf HaChayyim, siman 10, ot 38, in the name of Orchot Yosher, chapter 15. If, however, a Sefer Torah fell from his hand, Heaven forbid, one may not be lenient and he must fast. Chayyim Sha'al there, cited by Rav Pe'alim, part 1, siman 33.
6) One who fasts because his tefillin fell should fast that same day, even though he did not accept the fast on the preceding day, just as with a fast for a dream; and he recites Aneinu as on a fast for a dream. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah there, ot 7, in the name of the aforementioned authority; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 5; Baer Heitev there; Chesed LaAlafim there; and Ben Ish Chai there. R' Chayyim Palachi, in Kaf HaChayyim, siman 10, ot 38, writes that he should fast on the following day and accept the fast the preceding evening. If his tefillin fell on Shabbos, however, he should not fast on Shabbos but wait until Motzei Shabbos. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah there, ot 5, in the name of the aforementioned authority; Eliyah Rabbah there; and Baer Heitev there.
7) If a person's tefillin fell and he must fast, or if they were inside their pouch and he must give a perutah to charity, the rule is not as people say; that it applies only if they fell from more than ten tefachim. The concern is degradation of the tefillin, and degradation exists in every case, even if they fell less than ten tefachim. So writes Ben Ish Chai, of blessed memory, Parashat Chayei Sarah, ot 18. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if they fell from a height of less than three tefachim he need not fast. Shabbos 97a and many other passages state that anything less than three tefachim is treated as joined. Shabbos 5a similarly discusses one who lowered his hand to within three tefachim, and Rashi explains that it is considered as the ground. Tosafos, Ketubos 31b, s.v. 'Rav Ashi,' likewise write that below three tefachim is treated as joined and as though the object rested upon the ground; see there. This principle appears in many places. Accordingly, since anything within three tefachim is regarded as resting upon the ground, there is no degradation when tefillin fall from a height of less than three tefachim. Nevertheless, one should give a perutah to charity, as when they fall inside their pouch.
8) One who sees tefillin fall is not obligated to fast. Chayyim Sha'al, part 1, siman 12, and Machazik Berachah, ot 3. He writes that this is evident from Mishpetei Shmuel, siman 12, and Mahari Bruna, who wrote only that the person whose tefillin fall fasts, and this is the ancient custom; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1, and Petach HaDevir, ot 2, cite this. It is unlike Sedei HaAretz, part 3, siman 31, who writes that even one who witnesses the fall must fast, as cited by Zechor LeAvraham, part 1, ot 400, and Petach HaDevir there. See Birkei Yosef, Yoreh De'ah 282, Shiyurei Berachah, ot 4, who writes that even one who sees a Sefer Torah fall is not strictly obligated to fast; see there. He writes the same in Chayyim Sha'al there and Ledavid Emet, siman 3, ot 11; see there.
