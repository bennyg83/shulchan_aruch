1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to suspend tefillin, etc. If one merely happens to hold the tefillin in his hand while the straps hang down, there is no concern; but if the tefillin boxes hang down, it is forbidden. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1. This means that it is permitted only when he holds the boxes in his hand and the straps happen to hang down. If he holds the straps while the boxes hang down, it is forbidden even temporarily. Taz, however, writes in se'if katan 1 that this does not include holding the straps of the head tefillin while the box hangs in the air as one places it upon his head, because this is necessary for putting it on and is not degrading. It appears that they do not disagree: Magen Avraham forbids holding the straps while the tefillin hang only when one is not putting them on, but when putting them on he concedes to Taz that it is not degrading. So write Machatzit HaShekel, ot 1; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1; and Shemen HaMaor, ot 1. Mattei Yehuda, ot 2, however, writes that in his opinion one should hold the box and allow the straps to hang, since this is possible. He reports seeing meticulous people observe this precaution, and understands Magen Avraham's prohibition against suspending tefillin even temporarily as applying even while putting them on. Only the straps may hang, because that is necessary for putting them on; see there. Chesed LaAlafim, ot 1, likewise writes that even while putting them on one should be stringent not to hold the knot while the box hangs until it is placed on his head. Instead, he should hold the knot and box together, place the box in position, and then place the knot in position. Shitilei Zeitim, ot 1, also writes that one should be stringent even while putting them on when it is possible to hold the box; Taz discusses a case in which that is impossible. Therefore, initially one should act as Mattei Yehuda, Chesed LaAlafim, and Shitilei Zeitim write. If this cannot easily be done, he may rely upon Taz, who permits it for the purpose of putting them on. Soferim U'Sefarim, ot 1, and Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, cite Taz and appear to agree with him. This is especially so because Peri Megadim, Machatzit HaShekel, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, and Shemen HaMaor write that Magen Avraham also concedes to Taz and permits it while putting them on. Yafeh LaLev, ot 1, likewise writes that when it cannot easily be done one may rely upon those who permit it; see there.
