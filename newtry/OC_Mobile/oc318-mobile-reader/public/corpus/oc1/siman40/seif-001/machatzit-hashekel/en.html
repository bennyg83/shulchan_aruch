(s"k 1) Forbidden, etc. But incidentally, etc.; but if tefillin hang — forbidden; meaning even incidentally. Taz s"k 1 wrote that when donning head tefillin, holding by straps with bayit hanging until placing on head — in my humble opinion there is no degradation, for this is need of placement; perhaps Magen Avraham agrees with Taz. R' Yom Tov wrote it is permitted to write verses on paper and hang at the synagogue entrance. I summarized in part 2 siman 3 where they asked about the custom in synagogue to write verses on paper to arouse people to repent, such as "for on this day atonement," or Shema, and hang above the ark or doorways — he answered: no concern of hanging verses; unlike hanging tefillin which has degradation, unlike those verses written for hanging so viewers look upward and direct their hearts — no degradation in hanging. But for another reason they came to forbid, for one may not write a megillah for a child to learn — Yoreh De'ah siman 283; even per Rif who is lenient due to "a time to act," etc.; and so Taz Yoreh De'ah siman 283 s"k 40 protests those who write verses on walls for a good sign — see there. Nevertheless there is a difference from Mahari'k's responsum: if he writes only three words in one line it is permitted — Yoreh De'ah there; also permitted to hang even on synagogue wall things in prayer books, such as thirteen attributes or "and it was when the ark traveled" — we have no concern; as permitted in prayer books relying on Rif, so on the wall; and such is custom.
