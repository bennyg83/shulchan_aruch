In Taz, se'if katan 3: The Gemara answers that he projects the bulge outward. Taz understands 'projects the bulge outward' to mean that the tefillin's protrusion lies away from the area opposite his head; otherwise it would be forbidden. The entire case with the tefillin, however, remains beneath the pillow. The questioner thought that opposite one's head they are permitted even without a case. The answer teaches that opposite the head they are permitted in every manner, while away from the head they require a separate case whose bulge projects away from the head; nevertheless, the case remains beneath the pillow, so there are two coverings. Taz rejects this. On the contrary, the passage proves that away from the head two coverings are unnecessary. The questioner necessarily held so. It is unreasonable that the questioner inferred from the omission of a case that none is needed, while the answerer silently disagreed and still required one. Rather, the answerer agrees that Rav Yosef discusses tefillin without a case. When Rav Chiya mentions a case, he does not mean that both case and tefillin remain beneath the pillow away from the head. He means that the bulge projects completely outside the pillow. As Magen Avraham likewise writes, only a small portion of the case remains under the pillow. Taz's statement that 'the tefillin lie in a case beneath his head area' means that the tefillin themselves are not placed beneath the pillow; they lie independently in a case, and that case is positioned by his head area. 'Beneath' does not mean below the point where his head rests, toward the ground, which would indeed place them under the pillow. It means outside his head area: The edge of the case is under the pillow, while the tefillin bulge hangs outside it. A position outside the head area is also called 'beneath his head area.' Thus Rav Chiya's phrase means that the bulge projects outward. Perhaps he holds that only this is considered away from the head. If the tefillin are beneath the pillow but not presently opposite his head, he may turn over involuntarily in his sleep, as often explained, and place his head directly upon them. When the tefillin protrude beyond the pillow, there is no such concern.
