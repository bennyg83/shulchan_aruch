Tefillin. And if one spread a tallit over the tefillin pouch, even if it is not wrapped from below it is permitted, for the tallit is not called kelim. However, those who place the tefillin pouch and the tallit into a large pouch — the large pouch too is called kelim and requires another covering on top of it. However, if the tefillin pouch is attached above to the tallit pouch, the other side of the large pouch is not called kelim — Magen Avraham. And in responsa of Maharitatz siman 131 he wrote: the pouch in which we place tallit and tefillin is called a vessel that is not kelim, and it is permitted to use his bed — and this is specifically when he did not prepare the pouch except for the tallit and afterward placed tefillin in it; then it is permitted. But if from the outset he prepared the pouch for tallit and tefillin, it is forbidden, for it is called kelim — see there. If he wraps the pouch in a handkerchief in a time of need, the handkerchief too is called another vessel — Maharitatz there. If the mezuzah is inside in the room, he also needs a vessel within a vessel; and if there is glass over the Name, it suffices to spread a cloth over it — Magen Avraham and Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 286 wrote that a glass covering does not avail; see there — it seems to deal even if one spread a cloth on top, for with one vessel it is obvious that it is forbidden; and Magen Avraham disagrees on this.

For them. Meaning: for vessel within vessel we do not require that both be vessels that are not designated — only one of them; and the vessel that is designated joins with the vessel that is not designated. And Rabbi Rama did not come except to explain the author's words — Beit Yosef: do not think that if both are not designated for them, or the inner is not designated and the outer is designated, it would be forbidden, since the author used the wording "and this is where the second is not designated for them" — he teaches that it is permitted; but one vessel, even if not designated, does not avail and is forbidden.
