And if both, etc. Beit Yosef writes that Rabbeinu Yonah's words imply this arrangement is ineffective. Rabbeinu Yonah's text reads: “If he placed them in a vessel not designated for them, even ten such vessels are regarded as one vessel.” One might understand this as saying not only that ten designated vessels count as one vessel, but even ten non-designated vessels count as one vessel. According to this reading, two non-designated vessels would not help, which is impossible. Maharshal therefore emends the text. It appears to me that it should be emended as follows: “Do not say that two vessels designated for them resemble one vessel; rather, even ten designated vessels are regarded as one vessel.” The point is to prevent an error in understanding Abaye's initial statement. One might think that because he says the tefillin were placed in a vessel not designated for them, two non-designated vessels are required, while a designated vessel together with a non-designated one does not help. Therefore he says: if they were placed in their designated vessel, even ten such vessels, etc. Meaning, not only does one designated vessel not help when combined with a non-designated vessel, but even ten designated vessels count as one. He then rejects that understanding. The correct reading is that not only do two designated vessels fail, but even ten designated vessels fail. His conclusion therefore works well. The result is that all approaches agree that a designated vessel combines effectively with a non-designated one.
