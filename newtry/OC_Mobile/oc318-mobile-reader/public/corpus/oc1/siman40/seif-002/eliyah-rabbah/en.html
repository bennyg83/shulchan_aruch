(2) Both are not designated, etc. Meaning, one should not think that because both containers are non-designated this is somehow worse and regarded as only one vessel. Rama teaches that it is permitted. In truth, it is likewise permitted when the outer container is non-designated even though the inner one is designated. Beit Yosef and Lechem Chamudos write this. See below, siman 240, seif 6, where sacred books, even volumes of Gemara, are treated like tefillin regarding sanctity. Tur indeed writes so. Yet Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos and Raavad appears to imply that a simple covering is sufficient and one vessel within another is unnecessary. Kol Bo, page 204, likewise implies this. The matter requires study. Responsa Masas Binyamin, siman 99, writes that printed books possess sanctity like handwritten books. Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, likewise writes that whoever is lenient concerning the sanctity of printed books will ultimately be held accountable. Still, one might question this where the printers do not sanctify the books or the Divine Names. Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 187, inclines toward leniency in pressing circumstances. See above, siman 32, seif 42, and siman 43, seif katan 5. Masas Binyamin, siman 100, further writes that even books not written in full Ashuris script, such as ordinary cursive script, possess a lesser sanctity and must be treated with honor and holiness. See there also regarding blank margins of books. Magen Avraham writes that a mezuzah fixed inside the room requires one vessel within another. If glass covers the Divine Name, spreading a cloth over it is sufficient. Ri'az writes that if one places the tefillin inside his tallis and places the tallis inside a chest of books, this is regarded as one vessel within another because the tallis is not their designated container. Piskei Rekanti explains that spreading a tallis over a tefillin pouch is sufficient even if it is not wrapped beneath it. This appears to conflict with Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon, siman 131, who is uncertain whether wrapping the pouch well in a cloth counts as one vessel or whether an actual vessel is required. Since merely spreading a tallis over it counts as a vessel, wrapping it well in a cloth should certainly count. Magen Avraham writes that those who place the tefillin pouch and tallis inside one large bag should know that the large bag is also considered their designated container. If, however, the tefillin pouch is attached above the tallis compartment, the other side of the large bag is not considered their designated container. I saw in Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon that he discusses the first case at length and rules leniently in pressing circumstances where no other vessel is available, since the large bag is not designated for tefillin alone.
