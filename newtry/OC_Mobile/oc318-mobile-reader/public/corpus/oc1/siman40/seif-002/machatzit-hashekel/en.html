(Seif katan 2) A room, etc. “Although it is not wrapped beneath, it is permitted.” The Gemara on Berachos 25a likewise implies this. Rava states that a cloak spread over a chest is considered one container within another. Since he says “over a chest,” it implies that the cloak is spread only above the chest and not beneath it. Nevertheless, it is permitted.
Shiltei HaGibborim writes, etc. “The large bag is also considered their designated container.” If the tallis is inside the large bag and placed above the tefillin, it is already permitted, because the tallis is certainly not their designated container. As explained above, it makes no difference that the tallis lies only above and not beneath the tefillin.
As written in siman 75: although one may recite Krias Shema opposite excrement enclosed in glass, meaning a transparent vessel, one may not recite opposite nakedness enclosed in glass. Regarding nakedness, the Torah made the prohibition dependent upon sight, as the verse states, “No naked matter shall be seen among you,” and it remains visible through the glass. Know that Rama writes in the gloss to seif 2 that it is permitted when the inner container is not designated for the tefillin while the outer one is designated. Eliyah Rabbah writes in the name of Beit Yosef and Lechem Chamudos that the reverse is certainly also permitted: when the inner container is designated and the outer one is not.
