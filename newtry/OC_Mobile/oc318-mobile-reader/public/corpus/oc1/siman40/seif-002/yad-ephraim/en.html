Se'if katan 4: The same may be learned from Rambam and Semag. This is the correct text. In my humble opinion, however, his proof requires study. If he infers from their phrase 'his wife is with him in bed' that he does not intend marital relations, yet Rambam and Semag still require two coverings, this is no proof. According to Rabbeinu Yonah's understanding of Rambam, Rambam makes no distinction based on whether one's wife is present; even without her, two coverings are required when the tefillin are placed opposite his head. Beit Yosef cites him this way, leaving no proof. Even according to Beit Yosef's explanation that Rambam mentions two coverings because he is discussing one's wife, there is no difficulty. Rambam and Semag, who prohibit when one's wife is with him in bed, discuss an ordinary case that may include an intention for marital relations. Beit Yosef infers from the wording 'it is forbidden to have relations, etc.' that if two coverings were required even without such intent, the law would not depend upon relations and the source should simply have said, 'his wife is with him.' Rambam's phrase 'even when his wife is with him,' however, certainly can include intent for relations. Proof is that he does not state the greater novelty explicitly; 'even to have relations.' In truth, Rambam is not presently discussing the law of relations, which he wrote earlier, but placement beneath the head area. He teaches that with two coverings it is permitted in every case: Even if one's wife is ordinarily with him in bed and he may intend relations, it is permitted. This must be Rambam's meaning, for otherwise what source establishes a need for two coverings? The Gemara there does not mention placing the tefillin between mattress and cushion. Rambam must reason that otherwise a mattress and cushion would be superior to two coverings, as Kesef Mishnah explains. This follows because the case includes intent for relations, which requires two coverings. Rambam holds that the tefillin should be placed in one vessel within another between mattress and cushion, as the incident involving Rava implies. Alternatively, perhaps the phrase 'even when his wife is with him in bed' means even when he does not intend relations, and in that case two coverings truly are unnecessary. Since the principal ruling concerns intent for relations, however, Rambam should have stated the greater novelty; that even when he intends relations, two coverings permit it. It must therefore be that whenever his wife is with him, the situation is treated as an intention for relations.
