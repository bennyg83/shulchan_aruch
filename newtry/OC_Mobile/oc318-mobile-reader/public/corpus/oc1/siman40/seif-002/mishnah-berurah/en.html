(4) Tefillin. The same applies to Chumashim, siddurim, and other sacred books, whether handwritten or printed. All possess sanctity and require one vessel within another. This applies even when they are written in ordinary Ashuris script rather than the special script used for sifrei Torah, tefillin, and mezuzos. A book's binding is not considered a covering, because it is part of the book itself. Two additional coverings are therefore required. See Taz in Yoreh Deah, siman 271, seif katan 8, who writes that anyone who treats printed books leniently regarding sanctity will ultimately be held accountable, because printing is considered writing. Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 187, nevertheless inclines toward leniency in pressing circumstances when no covering is available.
(5) His bed. The same applies to relieving oneself in the room.
(6) Until he removes them. He may remove them to another room, or erect before them a partition ten tefachim high. See below, siman 240, Magen Avraham, seif katan 15.
(7) One vessel within another. This need not mean actual vessels. Two coverings are equally effective. If one spreads a tallis or another covering over the tefillin pouch, it is permitted even though the covering reaches only above and along the sides, but not underneath. See Biur Halachah. Those who place the tefillin pouch and tallis inside a single large bag should know that the large bag is also considered their designated container. Another covering is therefore required over it. If, however, he covers the tefillin with the tallis inside the bag, it is permitted, because the tallis is not their designated container. Likewise, if the tefillin pouch is attached above the large bag, the second side of the large bag is not considered their designated container and is effective if he turns the bag over. See Biur Halachah concerning the ruling cited from Eliyah Rabbah. Magen Avraham writes that a mezuzah fixed inside a room likewise requires one vessel within another. It must therefore be covered with two coverings, at least one of which is not designated for it, as explained below. If the mezuzah has a glass covering, then even though the mezuzah is visible through the glass, that glass counts as one covering. It is sufficient to spread a cloth over the mezuzah, because it is then no longer visible. Derech HaChayim rules likewise. Everyone agrees, however, that adding a second glass covering above it is ineffective, even though there are now two coverings, because the mezuzah remains visible through both. Chochmas Adam writes that for a mezuzah or other sacred books, if one initially made two coverings with the intention that they count as one vessel within another, such as wrapping the mezuzah in paper and then placing it inside its case, it is permitted because that was the original intent. The remaining laws will be explained below in siman 240, with Hashem's help.
(8) That the second one is not. This wording is not exclusive. The same applies if the inner vessel is not designated while the outer vessel is designated, or if neither is designated, as Rama writes. One vessel within another is forbidden only when both are designated. If one is not designated, it combines with the designated vessel and it is permitted. Rama comes only to explain the Mechaber's wording so that it not be misunderstood. A single vessel, however, does not suffice even when it is not designated.
