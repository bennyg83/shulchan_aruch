(4) Tefillin — and the same for chumashim, prayer books, or other books, written or printed — all have holiness and require vessel within vessel; even if written in non-STaM script, book binding is not considered covering, for it is part of the book, and two coverings are required. See Yoreh De'ah siman 271, Taz s"k 8: whoever is lenient about printed books will give judgment, for printing is like writing; but Chut HaMeshulash siman 287 leans to leniency in a time of need when he lacks covering.

(5) His bed — or to do his needs.

(6) Until he removes them — to another room, or separate with a partition ten handbreadths high. See below siman 140 in Magen Avraham s"k 15.

(7) In vessel within vessel — meaning two coverings; and if one spread tallit or other covering on tefillin pouch, even if covering only above and sides and not below — permitted; see Biur Halacha. Those who place tefillin pouch and tallit in one large pouch — the large pouch is kelim designated for them and requires covering unless tallit covers tefillin inside — then permitted, tallit not kelim; likewise if tefillin pouch attached above to large pouch, the other side is not kelim and helps if inverted. See Biur Halacha in Eliyah Rabbah's name. Magen Avraham wrote: fixed mezuzah inside room needs vessel within vessel — two coverings, one at least not designated; if glass over Name it counts as one covering and cloth spread suffices — so Darchei Halacha; but all agree extra glass on top does not help though two coverings, for mezuzah visible. Chochmat Adam: if from outset he made two vessels for mezuzah or books, e.g. wrapped in paper then placed in case — permitted since intended so. More laws below siman 140 with God's help.

(8) That the second — not precise; likewise if inner not designated and outer designated, or both not designated — as in the gloss; vessel within vessel is forbidden only if both designated; if one not designated it joins with the designated and permitted. The gloss came only to explain the author lest one err in his wording; one vessel, even not designated, does not avail — forbidden.
