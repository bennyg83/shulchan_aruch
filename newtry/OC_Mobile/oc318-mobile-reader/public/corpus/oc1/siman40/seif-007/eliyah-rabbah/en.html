(5) And he experienced a seminal emission, etc. Sefer Chasidim, siman 157, writes that it is improper to place books upon a bed containing a drop of semen, or upon a bed where a person passes wind. It is likewise improper to suspend words of Torah between one's legs or behind oneself. Likkutei Maharil records that when Maharil saw a book upside down, he turned it upright and kissed it. If, Heaven forbid, several books fell, he delayed kissing them until he had raised all of them. When he walked while carrying a book, he did not enter or leave a doorway ahead of the book. Below, siman 564, I cited Knesses HaGedolah and Rabbi Yisrael Bruna that one whose tefillin fall should fast that day. If they fall on Shabbos, he waits until the following day. This applies specifically when the batim fall, not merely the straps. If the tefillin fall while in their pouch, he should give a perutah to charity. If an empty pouch or case falls without the tefillin, there is no concern. Magen Avraham at the end of siman 44 overlooked this. If a Sefer Torah falls, one should fast even if it was in its case.
