One who enters for a meal, etc., until the time of the blessing. Rambam writes that he puts them on after washing with mayim acharonim. Presumably his reason is that he should not put them on with soiled hands. The later authorities did not cite this. Perhaps their reason is that, according to our ruling at the beginning of siman 179, even ordinary speech may not interrupt between mayim acharonim and Bircas HaMazon. All the more so, putting on tefillin would be forbidden then. See Beit Yosef there and Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 179. See what we shall write immediately below concerning the blessing.
And he puts them on again. See the Yerushalmi in the chapter Hayah Korei. According to every version, as may be seen in Penei Moshe and Sefer Chareidim, one who removes his tefillin for a fixed meal must recite a new blessing when putting them on again. According to the Mechaber's ruling above in siman 25, seif 12, he certainly must recite a new blessing. Even according to Rama there, who holds that when one removes them intending to put them back on immediately he does not recite another blessing, here too he removed them and placed them beside him on the table intending to put them on again for Bircas HaMazon. Nevertheless, the authorities there write that when one removes them to enter a lavatory, everyone agrees that he must recite a new blessing. Since he is not permitted to enter the lavatory wearing them, that period interrupts the mitzvah. The same reasoning may apply here. Since the Sages decreed that one should not wear tefillin during the meal, that period interrupts the mitzvah. This requires study. No proof can be brought from the Yerushalmi. According to Penei Moshe's version, and Gra's emendation of the Yerushalmi, the case is one where he removed the tefillin because the baraisa requires one to relieve himself before entering a fixed meal, as stated in Berachos 23b. In that case he certainly must recite a new blessing. Our uncertainty concerns one who did not relieve himself beforehand.
