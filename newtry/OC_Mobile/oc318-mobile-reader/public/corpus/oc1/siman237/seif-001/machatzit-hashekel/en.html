(Note 1) "And there is no [repetition]," etc. — and so is correct, etc. — see above in Magen Avraham siman 131, note 9, and what Shulchan Aruch wrote that the shaliach tzibbur does not repeat the prayer. They wrote the reason: since the essence of the enactment to repeat the prayer is to discharge one who is not expert, and since Maariv prayer is optional — behold there is no one obligated in the matter and the shaliach tzibbur need not discharge him; and even though if he prayed once at Maariv he already made it obligatory upon himself, nevertheless since he prayed once he is in any case expert and does not need the shaliach tzibbur; and if he is not expert, in any case he never prayed at all; and even though now that everyone accepted it upon themselves as obligatory, nevertheless they did not accept upon themselves to trouble the congregation to repeat the prayer with the shaliach tzibbur — so wrote Levush. And Eliyah Rabbah wrote in name of Shibolei haLeket that in some places they are accustomed to say before Maariv the psalm Shir haMa'alot "Behold, bless the the Master," etc., according to what is written in Berachot page 5 — unless he was accustomed to read [something else], he reads; and if he was accustomed to recite differently, he recites differently and afterward reads Shema and prays; and here the reason is in order to stand in prayer amidst words of Torah; and they chose this psalm because it is written in it "those who stand in the house of the the Master at night." And he wrote: for this reason, if they pray Minchah and Maariv adjacently, they do not say the psalm — since he already prayed before; and before Minchah prayer he said Ashrei, which is like words of Torah, and they are adjacent one to another — therefore there is no need.
