[Levush] Because there is not always, etc. According to this reason, VeHu Rachum ought also to be said on Friday nights and Yom Tov nights. According to the alternative reason, however, it is understandable because lashes are not administered on Shabbos. Rabbi David Abudarham writes likewise. See below, siman 267. Mateh Moshe explains according to sod that three officers rule over Gehinnom, named destruction, anger, and wrath, beginning from Maariv, and the wicked are judged at night for having darkened their deeds. Therefore we say VeHu Rachum: "He will not destroy" corresponds to destruction; "He frequently turns back His anger" to anger; "and does not arouse all His wrath" to wrath. It is not said on Shabbos because the wicked have rest in Gehinnom and these officers do not rule. Rabbi David Abudarham explains "and does not arouse all His wrath" as not arousing anything at all of His wrath, with "all" meaning any, like "the poor man has nothing." Kol Bo, page 33, likewise explains this. Yalkut Tehillim on siman 78, however, expounds that He does not arouse all His wrath but does arouse part of it, and Rashi there explains similarly.
