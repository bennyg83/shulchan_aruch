(1) There is no shaliach tzibbur [repetition], etc. — for Maariv prayer is not obligatory but only a mitzvah, and no person became obligated in it such that the shaliach tzibbur would need to discharge his obligation [Rambam]; and even nowadays that they established it as obligatory, nevertheless custom is not strong enough to make it obligatory so as to trouble the congregation to repeat the prayer with the shaliach tzibbur [Levush and Eliyah Rabbah].

(2) And one does not fall [on one's face], etc. — and so too Bach and Magen Avraham. And even if they prayed Maariv voluntarily, they do not fall — for night has already begun. And even prayer that he prays as compensation for Minchah prayer — likewise one does not fall [Peri Megadim]. Shibolei haLeket wrote at the beginning of Berachot: in some places they are accustomed to say Shir haMa'alot "Behold, bless," etc., before Maariv — because we say "if accustomed to read, he reads" — for one must read Shema amidst words of Torah; and that which we say this psalm more than others — because it is written in it "those who stand in the house of the the Master at night," or because it is written "lift your hands in holiness and bless the the Master" [Eliyah Rabbah — see there further].
