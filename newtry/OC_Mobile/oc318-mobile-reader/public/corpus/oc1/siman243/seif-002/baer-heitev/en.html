(א) From a non-Jew. In Beit Yosef he wrote that even if he acquired it from a non-Jew it is permitted to hire it out since he still has not lived in it and his name is not called upon it — therefore it appears to permit even upon acquisition. Magen Avraham.

(ב) In his house. This requires further study, for it is written in siman 244 that even one who lives among non-Jews — we should be concerned for guests coming there; here too we should be concerned for this. And further, we hold that whatever the Sages forbade due to marit ayin, even in innermost chambers it is forbidden — Taz see there. And Magen Avraham expanded and concluded that we require both: that he stands in another's domain by itself, and that none bathe in it except his neighbors — see there (and in Eliyah Rabbah he resolves Magen Avraham's difficulty and concludes like Rama's view — see there):
