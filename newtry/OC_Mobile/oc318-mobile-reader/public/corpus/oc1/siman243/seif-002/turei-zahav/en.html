(א) Permitted to hire out to a non-Jew. In Tur it is written: therefore permitted in contracting, for the non-Jew's melacha is not called on the Jew's name; and Beit Yosef wrote it must read "through absorption" in a place of contracting, as so in Piskei Rosh and Rabbeinu Yeruham; and he wrote further that if we read "through absorption" — then "permitted to hire out the bathhouse or oven for a month" — by law permitted since he does not take payment for Shabbat except through absorption, and for marit ayin there is none here since it is customary to hire out; and if we read "in contracting" — then "permitted to hire a non-Jew for a month" — the melacha of bathhouse or oven becomes etc. But the word "he hires out" is difficult on this, for he should have written "but if he was hiring him" — and this is proof for the reading "through absorption," for then the word "he hires out" fits well, end of his words. And in the name of Mahar"a he wrote one cannot read "in contracting," for even according to those who permit hiring a non-Jew for a known amount to do land melacha — this applies in a field, for even if he does not do melacha on Shabbat he will complete on another day; but in bathhouse or oven — if he does not do melacha on Shabbat the Jew loses profit that day, and the Jew benefits from melacha of Shabbat and it is forbidden; and he concludes in Beit Yosef: even though it is possible to reject this reason, nevertheless one should read "through absorption" not "in contracting," so as not to contradict Rosh who disagrees with the poskim who forbid in this etc., end of his words; and this is what is written from siman 244; and in my humble opinion it appears there is no distinction between the readings "absorption" and "contracting" here, and there is no explanation of "contracting" here that the non-Jew accepts the melacha upon himself and the Jew takes the profit — for this is certainly forbidden as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahar"a; and preferably from it, for he wrote for those who permit hiring a non-Jew etc. — he agrees in this; and in truth we hold also in a field for prohibition as Beit Yosef wrote so in siman 244 in the name of agreement of poskim; and so here siman 244 seif 1 — and this is Rambam's view; and what Beit Yosef wrote that it is possible to reject this reason — his intent is that even in this we say a non-Jew since he toils, he toils for his own benefit — there is no real rejection, for I already recalled before the words of Beit Yosef and agreement of poskim that whenever the Jew takes all the profit — it is forbidden; only that "contracting" which Tur mentioned means that the non-Jew accepts upon himself to take all the profit whatever it may be and work on whatever day he wishes — and this is literally absorption; and I wonder at Beit Yosef who himself brought thus in siman 244 on another matter, and thus he wrote: and it is more reasonable that Rav Natronai thus said — water mills are vessels, permitted to hire them out to a non-Jew; and he explained "in contracting" — meaning the non-Jew accepts upon himself whatever their produce yields, much or little, for a known amount he gives the Jew each month or year, end of his words; and if so, why did he not explain Tur's words here thus? And there is no need for any emendation; but contracting where the non-Jew does melacha for the Jew but the permission is because he accepts for a month or year alone — this is certainly absolute prohibition as Mahari Weil wrote, and Mahari Chayyim wrote on Mahari Weil's words that they are an error — for if so, mills where they practiced permission for sharecropping — behold if the non-Jew did not do melacha of the mill on Shabbat the Jew loses profit that day; but certainly the non-Jew works for his own benefit and permitted to all by law etc.; and in vain he wrote thus, for we hold in all cases forbidden as written in siman 244 even in contracting — and this is Rambam's view and agreement of poskim as Beit Yosef wrote: the general principle emerging from our words according to Beit Yosef's rulings — in contracting where the non-Jew accepts to do melacha for a month or year for the Jew and the Jew receives the profits and the non-Jew takes a fixed amount — absolute prohibition in profit of Shabbat even when publicized that he hires out to a non-Jew — for this is no better than a field where anonymously it stands for sharecropping and nevertheless forbidden in siman 244; and here Tur and Shulchan Aruch permitted only in contracting where the non-Jew accepts all the profit and gives the Jew a fixed amount — and this is literally absorption; and there is no need to emend Tur at all, for such contracting and absorption are one matter here; and for law our master author of Shulchan Aruch properly intended that he hire out to a non-Jew to accept all profits in a field even anonymously, and bathhouse where it is publicized that it stands to hire out — and the same if he gives to a non-Jew in sharecropping — permitted from the reason that since the non-Jew takes a share in profit of the body of the field and bathhouse — namely the money received from bathers — considered produce of the bathhouse as Beit Yosef wrote. And this "the matter became publicized" means that on weekdays he hired this non-Jew for many days. And all permission of hiring here means that he hires him through absorption with weekdays; but for Shabbat days alone forbidden in every case; and this "he rented them from a non-Jew" that Rama wrote — meaning the Jew never lived in it and his name was never called upon it — as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of responsum of the Geonim:

(ב) Only those in his house. It appears to me that it discusses where others do not know at all when this bathhouse was heated except the neighbors — therefore there is no awareness even on Shabbat; but if they sense at the time of heating — certainly there is suspicion there; however this requires further study, for this law per the apparent view is wondrous: one, for it is written in siman 244 — Beit Yosef in the name of Ashkenazic responsum and Rama brings it — even one who lives among non-Jews, we should be concerned for guests coming there; here too we should be concerned; and further we say in chapter HaChavit and they ruled it in siman 301 — one whose clothes fell should go etc. and not spread them before the people due to marit ayin, and even in innermost chambers; and we say on this in the Gemara that Rav holds thus from the reason that whatever the Sages forbade — even in innermost chambers it is forbidden; and the same here the prohibition is due to marit ayin that they will say he hired him — why permitted where people are not commonly present? And this can be resolved: that even if the non-Jew is the Jew's hired worker and works for him — there is no d'oraisa prohibition here but shvus — telling a non-Jew is shvus; here they were not stringent wherever there is no marit ayin, for so Rosh wrote there in chapter HaChavit that in a d'rabbanan prohibition we do not say even in innermost chambers it is forbidden; only this requires further study, for Beit Yosef wrote in siman 244 in the name of Semag that melacha through a non-Jew and for a Jew is d'oraisa — and see siman 244 seif 6 regarding tax:
