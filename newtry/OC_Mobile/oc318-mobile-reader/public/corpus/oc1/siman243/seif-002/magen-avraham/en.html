(א) Only he rented them from a non-Jew. I do not know why he wrote "if he rented them," for in Beit Yosef he wrote that even if he acquired them from a non-Jew it is permitted to hire it out since he still has not lived in it and his name is not called upon it; and it is possible that in Or Zaru'a he wrote "he rented them" etc.; nevertheless it appears to me to permit even upon acquisition:

(ב) Those in his house etc. Bach wrote, and this is literally the opposite of what Mordechai wrote in chapter 22 in the name of R' Chaim: specifically when the bathhouse is in a Jew's possession it is forbidden because it is called by his name; but when the bathhouse is in another's domain by itself and his neighbors recognize that it is hired out to a non-Jew — there is no concern, end of his words; and it appears to me that both apply: when bathing Bava Batra alone, even standing in his domain it is permitted provided it is in his house; but if he stands in reshut harabbim in a visible place it is forbidden; and when standing in another's domain by itself, even if others bathe in it — permitted as Mordechai wrote; nevertheless what Rama wrote here requires further study, for concern is only in a Jew's place as written in siman 244, and a Jew is forbidden to bathe on Shabbat — if so, whence would he know that he heats it on Shabbat? Perforce it must be that perhaps one of them will transgress and see smoke emerging; if so, even if only those in his house bathe — what of it? Therefore it appears to me that Mahari Weil's intent is also like Mordechai, for both wrote it in the name of R' Chaim, and thus Rabbeinu Yehudah in his name: if a Jew has a dwelling (meaning: in another courtyard where he does not live inside it) and inside it a bathhouse where none bathe except neighbors of the dwelling — permitted, for since none bathe in it except neighbors of the dwelling, it is already known to them that he hired a non-Jew, end of his words — meaning: if an ordinary person bathed in it on a weekday, since he enters and exits and knows the bathhouse belongs to a Jew, when he passes on Shabbat and sees smoke emerging they will suspect him that he hired the non-Jew; not so when none bathe in it except neighbors — they know the truth that he hired a non-Jew; and if an ordinary person passes he will not know at all the bathhouse belongs to a Jew — as stated, clearly both are required: standing in another's domain and that none bathe in it except his neighbors:

(ג) He transgressed and hired it out etc. Bach wrote that they do not disagree where it is permitted by law, such as when he hired it out to a non-Jew for a year — it is not forbidden except due to marit ayin; b'dieved the rental payment is permitted; but if he brought Shabbat payment alone it is forbidden as written end of siman 245; and if the melacha is imposed on the Jew — end of siman 245 and seif 246:
