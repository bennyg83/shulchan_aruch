(א) (Note 2) Permitted, and specifically etc., and this is the view of Rambam in siman 244; and know that there are three disputes in the matter, and in presenting them the words of Magen Avraham will be understood: Rabbeinu Tam's view — since we find at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah that they permitted giving his field to a non-Jew in sharecropping, for a non-Jew works for his own benefit even though the Jew's share increases through the non-Jew's melacha on Shabbat — all the more so it is permitted to hire a non-Jew to build a house for a Jew in contracting even though the non-Jew builds on Shabbat, for a non-Jew works for his own benefit, for in such a case the Jew does not benefit, for he hired him in contracting — meaning for a fixed amount for all the building; if he does not build on Shabbat he must build in its stead on a weekday. Rosh disagrees and thus he wrote at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah: from what we heard that we say in a field "his sharecropping he does" — there is no proof for contracting houses, for in a field the only regular way is sharecropping and leasing, but most house-building is by day-laborers — therefore the observer says they are day-laborers, etc.; therefore all contracting in attached property like building a house is forbidden, end of his words — behold contracting in houses is forbidden. But in a field he agrees it is permitted even in contracting, since an ordinary field stands for sharecropping; and even if observers will not attribute it to contracting, nevertheless they will attribute it to sharecropping which is also permitted. But Rambam was stringent that even in contracting it is forbidden in a field and in all attached property, and it was permitted only in sharecropping — for that is what they stand for; not so contracting, since they do not stand for that it is forbidden due to marit ayin, for even so they could attribute it to sharecropping; nevertheless after investigation that it is not in sharecropping they will not attribute it to contracting, since it is not its way — they will attribute that he is a day-laborer and agent of the Jew; and so the anonymous ruling like Rambam in siman 244, and as Magen Avraham wrote there in note 5. And therefore Magen Avraham wrote here "and specifically when he gives the non-Jew a share in the produce"; but when the Jew takes all the produce and gives fixed hiring payment to the non-Jew — by law it is permitted; nevertheless forbidden to Rambam, for the anonymous ruling follows him in siman 244 due to marit ayin:

(ב) And in a bathhouse forbidden by law. Meaning: even in a place where there is no marit ayin, such as where their way is also in a bathhouse in sharecropping, and as written in seif 2 (and for Rosh) — for Rambam nevertheless it is forbidden due to marit ayin, for after investigation it will become known there is no sharecropping — they will attribute that the non-Jew is a day-laborer; but even for Rosh who is not concerned for this in a field as stated — nevertheless in a bathhouse he agrees they are forbidden by law:

(ג) And it appears to me the same in a mill etc., not like Bach. Meaning: Bach challenged Mahari Weil in this law and brought proof from mills that stand for sharecropping and its law is like a field. For Mahari Weil it would be forbidden in contracting; and so wrote Magen Avraham — the law follows Mahari Weil; and in truth a mill too is forbidden in contracting:

(ד) And it appears to me that even he did not say etc. — meaning: also Bach who challenged did not say etc.:

(ה) For even Rosh — meaning: for Rambam certainly forbidden due to marit ayin as stated, and even in a field; except Rosh who is not concerned for marit ayin that they will attribute to sharecropping:

(ו) Except in house and field. Meaning: Rosh did not discuss permitting in contracting except in house and field, where in both it is permitted by law; and Rosh weighed whether it is forbidden due to marit ayin and concluded that in a house it is forbidden — not like Rabbeinu Tam — and a field is permitted as stated; but in bathhouse and mill he agrees they are forbidden by law even in a place where there is no marit ayin as stated:
