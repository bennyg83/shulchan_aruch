(א) Because it is called. Explanation: that by law it is permitted to hire it out to a non-Jew that he give him such-and-such whether the non-Jew heats it or does not heat it; or that he give the non-Jew a share in the bathhouse profit — and if so the non-Jew works for his own benefit as written regarding a field, and this is sharecropping; only it is forbidden because it is called etc. But to hire it out to a non-Jew for days that the non-Jew gives him each day such-and-such — forbidden by law, for it is like hiring for Shabbat — Tur and Rosh siman 306 seif 4 and end of siman 244 and siman 246:

(ב) A field is permitted. And specifically when he gives the non-Jew a share in the produce — see siman 244; and in a bathhouse forbidden by law to hire a non-Jew to work in it all year and the Jew takes all the profit from all the Shabbatot, for the Jew profits from what the non-Jew does on Shabbat, and we cannot say the non-Jew works for his own benefit, for even if the non-Jew does not work on Shabbat he must pay him; and see siman 244 seif 5 (Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Weil); and it appears to me the same in a mill — forbidden, and it is permitted only to hire out to a non-Jew as written end of siman 244 — not like Bach see there; and it appears to me that even he did not say for halachah, for even Rosh spoke only regarding house and field — then fixed payment is permitted by law, for if he does not work on Shabbat he will work on a weekday and then the Jew does not profit from his melacha; not so in bathhouse and mill; and this is clear that he wrote on Mahari Weil contrary to Torah law — end of siman 244 and siman 245:
