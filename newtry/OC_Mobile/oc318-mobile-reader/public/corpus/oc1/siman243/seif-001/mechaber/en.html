The law of one who hires out a field and bathhouse to a non-Jew. And in it are 2 seifim:
A person may not hire out his bathhouse to a non-Jew because it is called by his name, and this non-Jew does melacha in it on Shabbat — for ordinarily a bathhouse is not hired out for sharecropping [meaning: an aris is one who works to take a portion of what he improves for the owner], and they say that all the profit is the Jew's and he hired the non-Jew for such-and-such per day, and it turns out that the non-Jew does melacha as the agent of the Jew. But a field is permitted, for it is customary to lease a field on shares, and even though they know it belongs to the Jew they say the non-Jew took it in sharecropping and works for himself. And an oven — its law is like a bathhouse; and a mill — its law is like a field. Rama: Even though the non-Jew took only a third or a quarter, and the Jew has benefit from what the non-Jew works on Shabbat, it is permitted, for the non-Jew works for his own benefit. [Beit Yosef in the name of Meiri chapter 7 and Beit Yosef siman 245 in the name of Sefer HaTerumah]:
