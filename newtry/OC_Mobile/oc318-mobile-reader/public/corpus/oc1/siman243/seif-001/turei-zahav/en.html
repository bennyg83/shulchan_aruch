Namely the non-Jew takes a share in the body of the melacha — in this we say the non-Jew toils for his own benefit; but if the Jew takes all the profit of the melacha even though the non-Jew accepted upon himself for the whole year and only gives the non-Jew a fixed amount — forbidden, lest they say he is a day-laborer with him; and this is explained in Beit Yosef siman 244, who wrote in the name of the Rav Maggid and Ran, and thus he wrote: the Sages did not permit because of the reason they attribute to sharecropping except when the truth is so — if they investigate the matter they will find it is so, that the non-Jew divides in the produce; but in such a case where the owners take all the produce — it appears to people like a day-laborer; and so Tosafos wrote; and from here we learn for oven, mill, and bathhouse that it is forbidden to hire a non-Jew for a known amount for a year or month so that he accept upon himself all melacha needed for them, end of his words; and he wrote that so agreed the poskim:
