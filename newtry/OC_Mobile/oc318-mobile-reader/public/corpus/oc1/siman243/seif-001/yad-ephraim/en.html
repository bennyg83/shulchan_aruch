In Magen Avraham note 2 "and we cannot say a non-Jew works for his own benefit" etc. — many were perplexed by this; and in my humble opinion he came to resolve here Bach's difficulty who challenged Mahari Weil's words, who held that in a bathhouse since the Jew profits through the non-Jew working on Shabbat — it is forbidden. And on this it was challenged: but in seif 1 mill's law is like a field and permitted to give in sharecropping — apparently even though the Jew benefits from melacha of Shabbat through the non-Jew doing melacha in the mill we are not concerned, since he takes a share in sharecropping it is permitted by law, for whoever works for his own benefit — there is no concern in what the Jew has benefit from what the non-Jew works, as explained in seif 1 in the gloss; and due to marit ayin there is none, for the mill works for sharecropping like in a field. And if so, also in a bathhouse where it was publicized that he hires in contracting — such is Bach's difficulty; and on this Magen Avraham disagrees and holds that also in a mill there is no permission by law in contracting, only in sharecropping specifically; and the same in a bathhouse — specifically in sharecropping, that if he does not heat there will be no profit at all for the non-Jew, and he does so for himself; and even though benefit reaches the Jew — it is incidental benefit and permitted; not so in contracting — perforce if he accepted upon himself to heat specifically on Shabbat also, and if not they will reduce his payment — obviously he is a real hired worker and forbidden, and it is not applicable that he works for his own benefit, for he is literally an agent to work for him on Shabbat; and if it is in ordinary contracting that if he does not heat at some time he is still obligated to give him his payment — he pays; if so this is not literally his agent, for he is not insistent that he heat also on Shabbat day, and this is complete contracting — nevertheless forbidden, for it is not applicable "works for his own benefit" except where they root benefit for the non-Jew through this heating on Shabbat; not so here where the Jew must pay him even if he does not heat on Shabbat — if so this heating of the non-Jew on Shabbat is for the Jew and it is not applicable that he works for his own benefit; not so in house and field — by law permitted in contracting, for in any case the non-Jew is obligated to complete the melacha and the Jew does not care if he works on Shabbat or weekday — if so what the non-Jew does on Shabbat there is no profit for the Jew and he does not work for the Jew's intent on Shabbat. And it is close that one should emend this "and we cannot say" etc. until what is written in Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Weil — it applies after the words "and in bathhouse and mill"; and so it should read "and in bathhouse forbidden by law" etc. in what the non-Jew does on Shabbat — Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Weil; and it appears to me the same etc.; and then the Jew does not profit from his melacha (meaning: and if so the non-Jew who does melacha on Shabbat does not do it to profit the Jew but works for his own benefit on Shabbat so he will have leisure to rest on weekdays); not so in bathhouse and mill where we cannot say the non-Jew works for his own benefit (meaning: and then we are not concerned for what profit the Jew has from what the non-Jew works on Shabbat, like in a mill and as Bach challenged) — for even if the non-Jew does not work on Shabbat he must pay him; and see siman 244 seif 5 — and this is clear etc., so it should read. And according to what is before us it requires further study, for Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahari Weil this matter that even if he does not work etc. he must pay him — and this was not mentioned in Mahari Weil's words at all; also what he wrote that even Rosh spoke only regarding house and field etc. — these words are not understood, for they are Mahari Weil's very words; except Bach's difficulty is that even in bathhouse and mill we should permit in contracting from what he wrote regarding mill in sharecropping that we are not concerned for profit and there is no marit ayin here since it was publicized — according to what Eshel Avraham wrote. And what Machatzit HaShekel wrote to see siman 244 seif 5 — it appears he means that there he speaks of hiring in contracting to write or weave for him — permitted according to Rambam and Ra'avad forbids because the Jew profits from what the non-Jew does on Shabbat, lest tomorrow too he will need to write; and even Rambam permits only because he holds there is no known profit for the Jew; not so in an oven where the Jew has known profit from heating it on Shabbat — also Rambam agrees forbidden, for it is not applicable that he works for his own benefit, since there is no profit at all for the non-Jew in what he writes or weaves or heats that day, for in any case he is obligated to pay him; and an oven is similar to hiring him for all melachot — also Rambam agrees in this since certainly there is profit for the Jew and the non-Jew does not profit thereby. And see Taz siman 244 note 5 who wrote if the Jew is insistent on his cancellation — also Rambam agrees forbidden; and if so here perforce it discusses where he is not insistent whether he heats or not he gives him full payment — and it is not applicable that he works for his own benefit. And in Tosafos Shabbos Bach explains that Mahari Weil means that even in contracting such as where he stipulated with him each day "if you heat I will give you such-and-such" — Mahari Weil also holds forbidden since the Jew has profit; therefore Bach's challenge is properly on what he wrote regarding mill in sharecropping; and in my humble opinion the term contracting cannot bear explaining thus, for in such a case obviously it is not in the category of contracting; and further in my humble opinion in such a case too one should forbid, for even though he does not fix his melacha on Shabbat — nevertheless since the non-Jew is a Shabbat hired worker with him — it appears like his agent and is not comparable to hides to work them which is permitted for a fixed sum — for here the melacha is known to belong to the Jew; and if so what is publicized that it is in contracting in this manner that if he heats he will give such-and-such — nevertheless it is like the agent of the Jew to heat for him on his behalf and forbidden and not permitted by law except where the non-Jew has a share in the profit itself which is sharecropping for half etc., for then agency from the Jew is not applicable — the non-Jew works to take his share; and likewise if the non-Jew does not take his share in profit but only payment — permitted where the Jew does not profit from what the non-Jew does on Shabbat, such as hiring a scribe according to Rambam or hides to work them — if he does not work on Shabbat he will work on a weekday; not so if the Jew profits from what the non-Jew does on Shabbat and the melacha is known to be the Jew's — obviously it appears like his agent even if he fixed to give such-and-such for each heating — unless he takes his share in the payment of the melacha itself. And as a general rule in my humble opinion in explaining Magen Avraham's words — there are two matters to permit through one: where no benefit reaches the Jew from what the non-Jew does on that day even though also for the non-Jew there is no benefit in what he does that day specifically — nevertheless since the Jew was not insistent he work that day specifically — this is the matter of house and field in contracting where he gives the non-Jew a fixed amount to complete his melacha, and the non-Jew works for his own benefit on Shabbat even though he does not receive benefit from this work he does today — nevertheless the Jew also does not benefit from this, for what does he care if he works today or on a weekday? And if so what the non-Jew does today to hurry his melacha — he works for his own benefit; and there is also matter to permit where benefit reaches the Jew from the non-Jew's work on Shabbat but the non-Jew also has benefit from this that he works today because he takes a share in profit of this melacha he works today — we also say he works for his own benefit and it is permitted; and this is the matter of mill in sharecropping and likewise oven and bathhouse in sharecropping — by law there is no place to forbid because the Jew benefits since the non-Jew also benefits; not so mill and oven and bathhouse in contracting where the Jew takes the profit and gives the non-Jew a fixed amount whether he does melacha today or not — forbidden by law, for the Jew benefits from what this one does in melacha which is profit by itself, and he has no compensation for what he will do tomorrow which is profit by itself, and the non-Jew does not profit from doing his melacha today — for the Jew is obligated to give him what is fixed even if he does not work today; and since no profit reaches him — why should he work for his own benefit? And since benefit reaches the Jew — forbidden; and Bach wants to compare mill in contracting to mill in sharecropping where we say he works for his own benefit. And on this he said thus: that here there is no need that he work for his own benefit etc. — meaning as written that here there is no profit for the non-Jew. And afterward Magen Avraham wrote "for even Rosh did not say" etc. — meaning one should not compare to house and field in contracting which is permitted by law even though there is no profit for the non-Jew who takes his fixed amount — meaning that there, in the end he is obligated to complete his melacha, and if he does not work today he will work on a weekday — if so there is no profit for the Jew; in such a case we are not concerned that there is no profit for the non-Jew from doing that day specifically; not so in bathhouse and mill where there is profit for the Jew from doing that day — we need to insist there be profit for the non-Jew also in the act of that day, such as in sharecropping; not so in contracting where the non-Jew does not care — as stated above. And from now the connection of Magen Avraham's words is thus: first he wrote "and we cannot say a non-Jew" etc. — meaning and if so it is not comparable to mill in sharecropping and according to Bach's view because here there is no profit for the non-Jew; but apparently Bach still needed from what Rosh wrote that by law a field is permitted with fixed payment — apparently even though the non-Jew has no profit from melacha of that day specifically it is permitted since in any case he takes a fixed amount — "works for his own benefit" applies; and if so the same in mill with fixed payment; and if because in mill there is profit for the Jew — behold we must from mill in sharecropping that we are not concerned for profit for the Jew as long as there is profit for the non-Jew; and since it is proven from a field that fixed payment is called profit for the non-Jew — automatically there is no place to distinguish between sharecropping and such fixed payment — he said that even Rosh did not say except regarding house and field etc. — meaning that certainly fixed payment is not called profit for the non-Jew except there there is no profit for the Jew — both are required specifically; and according to this there is no need to emend Magen Avraham's words as stated; nevertheless if I were not afraid I would say to emend as stated, since the first distinction is not mentioned in Mahari Weil but only the second distinction:
