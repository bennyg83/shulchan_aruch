1) [Seif 1] A person should not lease a bathhouse, etc. According to the strict law, it is permitted to lease it to a non-Jew who pays a fixed amount whether or not he heats it, or to give the non-Jew a share of the bathhouse's profits. The non-Jew then acts for his own benefit, as stated regarding a field, and this is sharecropping. It is nevertheless forbidden because the bathhouse is known by the Jewish owner's name. Leasing it to a non-Jew by the day, with the non-Jew paying a fixed amount each day, is forbidden by strict law because it constitutes Shabbos wages. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. It is obvious that if he leases it to a non-Jew on condition that the non-Jew perform no labor on Shabbos, and the non-Jew obeys and performs no labor on Shabbos, it is permitted. Beis David, siman 97; Zechor LeAvraham, Hilchos Shabbos, under shin.
2) There: Because it is known by his name, etc. Accordingly, outside the techum it appears permitted according to all opinions, as stated below in siman 244, seif 1. Eliyah Rabbah there. Olas Shabbos writes that according to Raavad one should be stringent, but Eliyah Rabbah there writes that this is incorrect and that even Raavad permits it here; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, agrees and writes that this is also the Shulchan Aruch's view, but it relies upon what is stated in siman 244; see there. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, writes the same.
