[Levush] A d'oraisa prohibition, etc. Beit Yosef at the end of siman 244 cites this from Semag. See Chizkuni on Parashat Bo, on the verse "no work shall be done," where he challenges Rashi but apparently overlooked these words of Semag and Levush. I also found in Sefer Yereim, siman 113, that the rule that instructing a non-Jew is a shvut applies when it is not the Jew's own labor, such as telling the non-Jew to make his own fire for the Jew's benefit. But to remove the Jew's pots from the coals, where the Jew himself is d'oraisa forbidden, is different. This may answer what I asked in seif katan 8 regarding Semag. Nevertheless, the poskim generally imply that instructing a non-Jew is always d'rabbanan.
Because it is called, etc. By strict law one may rent the bathhouse to a non-Jew for a fixed annual payment or give the non-Jew a share of its profits as an aris, in which case the non-Jew works for his own benefit. The prohibition is only because of marit ayin, since the bathhouse is publicly associated with the Jew. Accordingly, outside the techum it appears permitted according to all opinions, as below in siman 244 regarding a field. Olat Shabbat writes that according to Raavad, who prohibits where a Jew profits from the non-Jew's labor, one should be stringent even with an aris in a bathhouse. This is incorrect. Raavad said so only where all the profit belongs to the Jew and the non-Jew receives a fixed annual wage for his labor, because then the non-Jew does not work for himself; he receives his wage even if he does no work. With an aris in a bathhouse, however, the non-Jew owns a share of the profits, so even though the Jew also profits it is permitted because the non-Jew acts for his own benefit.
