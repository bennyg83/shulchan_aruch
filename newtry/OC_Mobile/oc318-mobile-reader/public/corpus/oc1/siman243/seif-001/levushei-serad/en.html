Shulchan Aruch seif 1 — "A person should not hire out." Know that there are three types in these matters. First type: hiring a non-Jew his field or bathhouse, oven, and mill for a hundred gold coins for a year whether the non-Jew engages in them or engages in nothing at all — and this is the best of all for permission, for there are two advantages in it: one, since all the profit is for the non-Jew — when he does melacha on Shabbat he works for his own benefit, not for the Jew's intent; second, no enhancement or benefit reaches the Jew from melacha of Shabbat, for he took his fixed hiring payment even if the non-Jew did nothing at all — therefore this is not permitted by law in all of them (and specifically through absorption, but if he hires out the Shabbatot alone it becomes hiring for Shabbat not through absorption — forbidden, brought in Magen Avraham note 1); only in bathhouse and oven it is forbidden due to marit ayin that observers will think the non-Jew is a day-laborer, and when publicized it is permitted as explained in seif 2; and likewise outside the boundary where there is no marit ayin it is also permitted as explained in siman 244. Second type: the Jew hires the non-Jew to do all year the melacha needed for his field or bathhouse, oven, and mill, and all melacha is imposed on the non-Jew and he is not a day-laborer but is called contracting — that all needed melacha is imposed on the non-Jew and the non-Jew's hiring is for half, third, or quarter; and this type is called sharecropping. This type is inferior to the first, for the Jew's share is enhanced through melacha done on Shabbat; nevertheless in a nearby field this is close to the first type, for even though the Jew's share is enhanced through melacha of Shabbat, nevertheless there is no profit for the Jew from this, for if he does not work on Shabbat he will work on a weekday since the melacha in contracting is on the non-Jew — and if so there are also two advantages here: one, the non-Jew works for his own benefit since he has a share and the Jew's share is automatically enhanced; two, the Jew has no profit from Shabbat — therefore it is permitted; but in a bathhouse and the like there is only one advantage — the non-Jew works for his own benefit — and one disadvantage: the Jew profits from melacha of Shabbat, for if the non-Jew does not work on Shabbat the Jew will not take that profit. And nevertheless the Sages permitted from that reason that he works for his own benefit alone; only in bathhouse and oven it is forbidden due to marit ayin — outside the boundary or when publicized it is permitted. And since the first type is lighter than this type, for it has two advantages, therefore Shulchan Aruch began "A person should not hire out" for breadth and concluded "but a field is permitted" — meaning permitted to hire out, for its way is in sharecropping and there is no marit ayin (and even though in truth it is not in sharecropping, and in such a case he forbids in siman 244 — see there in Taz note 2 — it is not comparable, for there in truth it is worse than sharecropping and here it is preferable, and understand clearly); and automatically it implies that sharecropping too is permitted; only since permission of sharecropping is not stated explicitly in Shulchan Aruch, even though the implication tends to permission, nevertheless Magen Avraham toiled and wrote it for her explicitly. Third type: hiring the non-Jew in contracting without sharecropping but paying him money. And this type in a field has two advantages, for the non-Jew works for his own benefit since all melacha is imposed on him to finish — he wants to hurry to finish — and the Jew does not profit at all, for if he does not work on Shabbat he will work on a weekday — therefore by law this type in a field is permitted; only some forbid due to marit ayin in siman 244 — see there in Taz note 2.
