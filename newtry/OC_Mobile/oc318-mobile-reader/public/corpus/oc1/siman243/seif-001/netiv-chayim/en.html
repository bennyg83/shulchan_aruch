(In Magen Avraham note 2) And specifically when he gives the non-Jew a share in the produce; and in a bathhouse forbidden by law. Editor's note: The continuation — specifically when he gives the non-Jew a share in the produce for half, third, or quarter; but when he gives in money for half or third — forbidden due to marit ayin also if he hired the non-Jew to work in it all year and the Jew takes all the produce from a field — also forbidden due to marit ayin, but not by law, for a non-Jew works for his own benefit as he concludes — even if he does not fertilize, plow, and harvest the field on Shabbat he can do on a weekday, and he comes to say: but in a bathhouse in such a case forbidden even by law to work in it etc.; and the practical difference is whether if he hired b'dieved it is permitted or forbidden; and in Ba'er Heitev he was not precise, for he wrote on mill and bathhouse — and the same applies, it implies they are forbidden only due to marit ayin — and understand clearly:
