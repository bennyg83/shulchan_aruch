His name. Know that there are three divisions in the law of hiring. One: hiring a non-Jew to do melacha in a field or bathhouse and the profits or produce they divide. Two: the non-Jew takes all the profits or produce and gives the Jew a fixed amount each year. And these two divisions are permitted in a field and bathhouse by law, for a non-Jew works for his own benefit — only the Sages forbade these two divisions in a bathhouse due to marit ayin because it is called by his name, etc. But division three — namely that all the profit is for the Jew and only the Jew gives the non-Jew a fixed amount each year — this is certainly forbidden by law even in a field, all the more so in a bathhouse, for the non-Jew is the agent of the Jew and the Jew benefits from melacha on Shabbat; for even if the non-Jew did not do melacha on Shabbat he takes his fixed payment each year, and we cannot say that a non-Jew works for his own benefit. Automatically, if the custom of most people of the place is to hire them out or give them in sharecropping as explained in seif 2, the bathhouse returns to being like a field — the first two divisions are permitted and division three is forbidden.
