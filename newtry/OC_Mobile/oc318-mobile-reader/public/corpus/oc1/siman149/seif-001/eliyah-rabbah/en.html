[1] And likewise the one who rolls it, etc. The writings concerning the kavanos state that the same applies when the Sefer Torah is taken out: he should accompany it until the bimah. Rama writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 242, seif 18, that when a Sefer Torah is on the bimah, the congregation in the synagogue need not stand, because the Sefer Torah is in a separate domain. Taz writes that the same applies when it is on a table ten tefachim high and four tefachim wide, and also while the Sefer Torah is resting in the aron, even if the aron is open. Nevertheless, people always stand as an act of honor while it is open, though there is no obligation to do so. In Eliyahu Zuta I proved that when the Sefer Torah is resting on the table, sitting is permitted in any event, because the prohibition applies specifically while it is standing upright. This is also implied by Magen Avraham, siman 146, which states that the attendants or the one who rolls the Torah sit on the bimah itself while the Sefer Torah is upon it. This will be explained at length in Eliyah Rabbah. It is also possible that since the chazzan is seated while holding the Sefer Torah, the congregation may sit. During hagbahah, however, the entire congregation must stand out of respect. This appears correct to me, unlike Magen Avraham. Rama in Yoreh Deah there discusses a case where one is standing while holding the Sefer Torah.
