To its place. Eliyah Rabbah writes as follows: Rama writes in Yoreh Deah that when a Sefer Torah is on the bimah, the congregation need not stand, because the Sefer Torah is in a separate domain. He further writes that the same applies when it is on a table ten tefachim high and four tefachim wide, and also when the Sefer Torah rests in the aron even though it is open, etc. It is also possible that since the chazzan sits while holding the Sefer Torah, the congregation may sit. During hagbahah, however, they must stand, unlike Magen Avraham. See there.
To kiss the Torah. The widespread custom is to recite Tehillim 24 when returning the Torah, and on Shabbos morning the psalm beginning, “Render to Hashem, sons of the mighty,” etc. Regarding the phrase “He has not borne My soul in vain,” some pronounce the word nafshi with a chirik, as though both the written and read forms were so. In an accurate Masorah, however, the vav is not called a small vav but a severed vav. The phrase is also not counted among the seventy-one words written with a vav but read with a yud, and “his soul in vain” is not included among them. See the Midrash to VeZos HaBerachah, and see my responsum in Yoreh Deah, siman 562.
