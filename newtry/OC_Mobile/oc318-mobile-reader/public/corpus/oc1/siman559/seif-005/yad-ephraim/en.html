(ח) It is permitted to go to the synagogue and now everything fits; and so in the rulings of Tosafot; and see in Tosafot there daf 21 and as the Rosh wrote — his words require study; in my humble opinion one can explain what Tosafot wrote and on this they rely that those who come to synagogue on Tisha B'Av does not refer to mourners but to all people, for even though in Mishna Berurah they are mourners they may come to synagogue — and now everything fits. However from the rulings of Tosafot and Rosh it appears it refers to mourners, and in Tosafot one cannot explain as Magen Avraham wrote below since they brought proof from this that one may lay tefillin on Tisha B'Av — all this I wrote in the margin; in my novellae I wrote to explain the words of the Rosh that in the simple sense it is astounding and very forced; in my opinion the simple sense does not refer to mourners entering synagogue but to every person — that if we said on Tisha B'Av it is like within three [days of mourning] they would not be permitted to enter synagogue on Tisha B'Av to synagogue, for a mourner within three may not go to another mourner even if he too is within three as Magen Avraham wrote — but Tisha B'Av where all are mourners is different; one learns this from what is stated there in Moed Katan beginning daf 23 regarding a nasi whom they call seven in Scripture and they leave — Rashi explained they do not pray in synagogue but each one prays in his house — end of his words; it is clear that even though all are mourners each prays for himself, and so in the commentary of Rif; but in the words of Rosh it states they pray in the house of mourning, and Beit Yosef Yoreh Deah siman 374 wrote in the name of Rashi and likewise one could say in our Rashi it should read in the mourner's house; but the wording "each one and one" does not imply so; in any case one could say Tosafot hold that in such a case of mourning that is stringent, such as a nasi — even though all are mourners they do not go to synagogue, and from this they proved Tisha B'Av is like mourning after three days, and the reason is that it is mourning of a different kind as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Biur HaGra — but the words of Rosh in Taanit cannot be explained thus, and what Magen Avraham wrote is very forced; therefore it appears to me that certainly the law that a mourner goes to synagogue on Tisha B'Av since Tisha B'Av is like a mourner after three is obvious, since the baraita explicitly states that after three one goes to the house of mourning — therefore mourning of Tisha B'Av differs from ordinary mourning, and a sign for this is tefillin: if its law were like ordinary mourning it would be like a mourner on the first day who is forbidden tefillin — if so one should not learn anything of Tisha B'Av from a mourner; one could say specifically to another mourner one goes after three, unlike Tisha B'Av; therefore it comes to say Tisha B'Av is not different from a mourner — even though one cannot say it is like a mourner on the first day because it is mourning of a different kind, nevertheless it is like a mourner from the third day onward, and there is no proof from tefillin — for although it is like a mourner after three, nevertheless tefillin are permitted, and there is no proof from tefillin to say Tisha B'Av is lighter than ordinary mourning, for a mourner after three is also permitted tefillin; and accordingly the words of Rosh are well explained — his intent is not to say Tisha B'Av is like after three in order to give a reason for going from synagogue, for that is obvious to him; rather he writes by way of premise that Tisha B'Av is like a mourner, but one should not grasp it as an ordinary mourner implying the first day when tefillin would be forbidden; therefore he explained his words that it is properly like a mourner except it is like a mourner after three, therefore tefillin are permitted; nevertheless going is permitted as going to another mourner which is not lighter than ordinary mourning after three. The reader will see this is clear in the intent of Rosh as I wrote; also in the intent of the Rosh it appears that even though they said what Tosafot wrote and from here they relied etc. on mourners after three — as also implied from Rosh's words, since mostly he copies from Tosafot — nevertheless what they wrote "and from his words we learn" etc. does not mean only in the wording "from here they relied" but hints at what Tosafot HaRav wrote at greater length from which this is proven; and it seems to me that there Tosafot HaRav wrote in the language of Rosh in Taanit, therefore they wrote "and from his words" etc.; and there is a difference regarding tefillin. Perhaps his intent in what he wrote that he came to answer his words — even for this law he did not need the permission because he is like after three, for if there were concern of within three all the more he would be permitted to go to synagogue — only therefore he used the words after three, which matters for tefillin: if we say it is like before three the law would be not to lay tefillin on the first day; and in truth Tosafot HaRav agrees tefillin are permitted on Tisha B'Av, therefore he used this premise that it is like after three even though for this law of going to synagogue there is no difference; and one could also say per what the Rav wrote — and I do not know if it is a good custom — and what is his doubt? It is obvious one may go to the house of mourning as taught explicitly in the baraita, implying for every mourner even after three, and all the more on Tisha B'Av where he holds it is like before three; and it must be that he holds Tisha B'Av is only like after three; and one might have thought that even though it is also after three he may not go, for a mourner after three may only go to a mourner more stringent than himself — but from the baraita's wording that states generally "he goes to a mourner" it implies even when he is after three, namely that they wrote "on this they relied" meaning on the implication of the baraita's language that implies one goes to a mourner like himself who is also after three; therefore he was uncertain and wrote he does not know if it is good custom — one could explain their intent thus; but what he wrote first seems more correct in my opinion, and in the intent of Maharsha I also explained, and here I abbreviated.
