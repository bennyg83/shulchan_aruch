1) [Seif 1] Tzidkascha is not recited, etc. This is like Rosh Chodesh that falls on Sunday, when Tzidkasecha Tzedek is not recited on Shabbos, for Tisha B’Av is also called a festival. Tur and Beit Yosef. It is called a festival because it is written, “You have called as on a festival day.” The verse, “He proclaimed a festival against me,” was expounded by our Sages regarding Rosh Chodesh, as stated in Shevuos 10a, Pesachim 77a, and Taanis 29a, see there. Kerem Shlomo. However, see Mateh Yehudah at the end of siman 552 and Chayei Adam, klal 135, entry 21, who brought a proof from the verse, “He proclaimed a festival against me,” see there. This is from the words of the Tashbetz cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, see there.
2) Ibid. Tzidkascha is not recited, etc. However, Av HaRachamim is recited even when Tisha B’Av falls on Shabbos. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 554, entry 18; Yad Aharon, this siman; Mishbetzos Zahav, entry 1. Those whose custom is to mention departed souls every Shabbos do so on this Shabbos as well. Mishbetzos Zahav there; Levushei Serad at the end of siman 552; Mishna Berurah there, entry 30.
3) Ibid. Tzidkascha is not recited, etc., and all the more so Tzidduk HaDin is not recited if a death occurred on Tisha B’Av. So too wrote the Rama at the end of this siman in the gloss. Taz, s.k. 1. Maharshal wrote in responsum 32 that Tzidduk HaDin is recited on Tisha B’Av even though Tachanun is not recited, see there. The Taz cited his words there. So too, the Rokeach, siman 317, wrote that there are places where Tzidduk HaDin is recited on Tisha B’Av, see there. However, Mateh Moshe, siman 734, wrote regarding the aforementioned words of the Maharshal that the custom not to recite it has already become widespread, see there. Eliyah Rabbah, entry 14, cited his words. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah, siman 554, entry 18, that the custom is not to recite it, and so too wrote Chayei Adam there and Derech HaChaim, entry 9. See below entry 82.
4) Ibid., in the gloss. If one forgot to recite Havdalah, see above siman 294, etc. There it is written that if one erred and did not recite Havdalah in the Amidah, he need not repeat the Amidah, since he will recite Havdalah over a cup on Motzei Tisha B’Av. End quote. See above siman 536, entries 1 and 5, and onward from there, see there.
5) Ibid., in the gloss. The same is done during the reading of Eichah. One should pause briefly between every verse, and somewhat longer between each chapter of Eichah. The final verse of each chapter is recited aloud. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B’Av, Order of Prayer; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Before beginning Eichah, one recites the blessing Al Mikra Megillah. Derashos Maharil there; Levush; Magen Avraham there; Ateres Zekeinim; Chayei Adam, klal 131, entry 23. Even when it is printed and written on paper, the blessing is recited. Levush; Machatzis HaShekel. Chayei Adam there wrote that the blessing is recited only when it is written on parchment and rolled in accordance with the law. Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman wrote that even if it is written on parchment, one should not recite the blessing, see there. Nevertheless, see above siman 490, entry 81, where we cited the dispute concerning this. Although we rule that where there is a dispute regarding blessings one should not recite a blessing because of the principle of uncertainty regarding blessings, as stated above in siman 18, entry 7, nevertheless, where the custom to recite the blessing has already been established, we do not take this principle into account, as stated above in siman 17, entry 2, see there. Therefore, each place should follow its custom. Where there is no established custom, one should not recite the blessing because of the principle of uncertainty regarding blessings.
6) The primary kinah is Megillas Eichah, which was said with Divine inspiration in accordance with the prophecy of Yirmiyahu. Therefore, it is a proper custom for each individual to read Megillas Eichah again the next day privately. Shelah, 199b; Magen Avraham there.
7) Ibid., in the gloss. The congregation recites Hashiveinu aloud, and the prayer leader repeats it aloud. He concludes quietly, “For even if You have utterly rejected us, etc.” The congregation then repeats, “Hashiveinu, etc.,” and the prayer leader repeats it after them. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B’Av, Order of Prayer; Eliyah Rabbah, entry 6.
8) Ibid., in the gloss. And likewise the chazzan. He then begins again, “Remember, Hashem, what has happened to us, woe! Look and see our disgrace, woe, what has happened to us!” He similarly divides all the verses, inserting “woe” in the middle and “woe, what has happened to us” at their ends. At the conclusion of the kinnot, he recites several verses of consolation. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 5 of the Laws of Fasts; Darkei Moshe, entry 2; Ateres Zekeinim; Mishna Berurah, entry 3. This custom is also mentioned in Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B’Av, Order of Prayer. Knesset HaGedolah cited his words in Hagahos HaTur, entry 2.
