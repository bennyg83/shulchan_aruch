It is difficult for me — his words contradict what he himself wrote in siman 549 s.k. 6 to the contrary, that a bridegroom on Taanis Esther should not drink the cup of blessing while still day and they give to children on erev — implying he must complete; one should not say there his intent is if the chuppah is before plag hamincha, for from his language "while still day" implies whenever chuppah is by day he may not drink even near evening. One must explain Magen Avraham here means Taanis Esther postponed — he does not complete his fast; no need to explain otherwise, for it is learned from context — the whole seif deals with this; even without postpone, a bridegroom is no better than a baal brit who must fast — necessarily when postponed. Therefore Magen Avraham that on other feast days he completes means even postponed Taanis Esther he must complete — so it is, correct; likewise Ateret Zekenim: all fasts he must observe, only 17 Tammuz he does not fast during the seven feast days. This too appears to require annulment, since it is like a vow; unlike if 17 Tammuz falls on Shabbat or other 17 Tammuz days when not fasting — no annulment needed, for the matter depends on fixation and they certainly apply as if he stipulated not to fast those days and his intent did not apply from the start to those days; but on his wedding days, did he know from the start his wedding would fall specifically on his 17 Tammuz? His wedding day could be another day — then his obligation begins on this day too, as if he accepted this day to fast; now when his wedding day occurred he requires annulment as appears to me — examine carefully. (Ibid. same seif) Baal brit prays minchah while day is still great, etc. N.b.: I was asked by the renowned Rabbi Yosef, av beit din of Zbariz, now av beit din of Senitin [the gaon av beit din of Tarnopol, author of Minchat Chinuch] on the sugya in Eruvin 41 that asks: the reason of their Yom Tov is — behold on Yom Tov they complete — what is the question? Granted Shabbat and Yom Tov that begin at night — therefore Rabbenu Gershom held they do not complete even on Yom Tov; but there, since their Yom Tov is for the sacrifice and sacrifice does not apply at night — Yom Tov does not apply at night, only melacha forbidden after chatzot since earlier was time of sacrifice — all the more at night since not sacrifice time it is not Yom Tov; Maharshal wrote their Yom Tov is because of the sacrifice itself, not wood — so night too is not Yom Tov — why ask? Thus the content of the question of Rabbi Yosef av beit din. I answered: in my opinion it fits well — the Gemara proves they certainly made night Yom Tov too; else if night not Yom Tov, why not complete? We hold in all these: if one began, he stops; but if began in permission, all agree he does not stop — thus on every Yom Tov Rabbenu Gershom does not complete since whole day and night are Yom Tov and all began in prohibition and stops; but if we say here night is not Yom Tov and on their Yom Tov they could fast — since they began fasting on Tisha B'Av night not yet Yom Tov, they began in permission, and though morning their Yom Tov came, it should be as one who began in permission and does not stop. Also compare Taanit 12: if they vowed before our decree, vow cancels our decree — if night too Yom Tov and in Second Temple they did not fast Tisha B'Av as midrash brought in Magen Avraham after drinking, etc. — our decree for Yom Tov preceded Tisha B'Av, so even after Temple destroyed their Yom Tov pushes off Tisha B'Av prohibition — they did not complete; but if night not Yom Tov, they began fasting per law and when Tisha B'Av severity applied as if vow preceded decree — obligated to complete even by day; necessarily night too is Yom Tov and began in prohibition — therefore asks; one could derive it is Yom Tov and asks from both sides as appears now. Thus I answered the rabbi — examine carefully.
