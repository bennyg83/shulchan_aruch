They sit on the ground. So wrote Beis Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos, that it appears to Maharam that the custom of the French Jews is that they do not sit on benches until the Minchah prayer, analogous to a mourner who sits on the ground all seven days of his mourning, for it is stated in the Gemara in a beraisa: all the mitzvos that apply to a mourner apply on Tisha B'Av; end quote. And my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, asked: for the Tur wrote at the beginning of siman 555 that that beraisa is taught only regarding negative commandments; but positive commandments of mourning do not [apply]. And this is not difficult, for this too is a negative commandment, that it is forbidden to sit on a bench; and indeed if one wishes to stand, there is certainly no obligation upon him to sit specifically, nor to sit on the ground. And that which is permitted here at Minchah prayer, and according to our custom after midday, is because the beraisa teaches that all mitzvos that apply to a mourner apply on Tisha B'Av, and afterward it explains, for example washing and anointing, etc., and it does not list sitting on the ground; and in the first clause it implies a general rule, meaning also sitting on the ground; rather, necessarily in this there is a distinction, that part is forbidden as on Tisha B'Av and part is permitted; therefore they divided the time within the day itself. But washing and anointing, etc., which are listed in the latter clause, are certainly forbidden until nightfall, like eating and drinking, as it appears to me regarding this. And the common folk err and walk after midday in shoes, and they do so from lack of knowledge; and some, even though the prohibition is revealed to them, do not abandon their custom; and one may almost say about this, better that they be unintentional transgressors, etc.
One candle. In the Minhagim it is written: one candle for the chazzan; and this is puzzling to me, for the entire congregation must say Kinot, and certainly it is not sufficient otherwise, and of what use is the chazzan's saying alone? Therefore it appears that in each neighborhood there should be one candle, so that even under cramped conditions they can say Kinot by its light. And that which is written in the Minhagim "for the chazzan" means in a small synagogue, where the entire congregation can say Kinot by that light even under cramped conditions, as it appears to me.
