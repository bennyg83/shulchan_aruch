Seif 4 in the gloss: and they read from the Torah "ki tolid." I wondered at what I saw practiced — that they say kaddish after the third reader finished the Torah reading before they say the haftarah; behold it is explained that the maftir must read in the Torah first so the honor of the haftarah should not be like the honor of the Torah — to ascend only for the haftarah alone; rather by law the seventh is the maftir who reads the haftarah and ascends for the count of readers. However since they add on Shabbat (and likewise on Yom Tov there are views) they act for everyone to read from the count of readers aside from the maftir; but in any case the maftir must read in the Torah first. And if so, if they interrupt with kaddish when afterward they say the haftarah it is as ascending for the haftarah alone and it would be necessary for another to ascend to read in the Torah and read the haftarah; and therefore Magen Avraham wrote siman 282 s.k. 96 regarding that case that the seventh should read the haftarah and not say kaddish after him. And if so what difference whether the seventh is the maftir that they do not interrupt with kaddish for his Torah reading until the haftarah, or Tisha B'Av where the third is the maftir that they say kaddish? And I found in Minhagim written thus to say kaddish before saying the haftarah; and so I saw in Levush, and he wrote the reason: not to say kaddish before the third's reading since the count of readers is not yet finished — see there. But nevertheless it is not a long ascent on saying kaddish between the reading and saying the haftarah, and my heart was not filled to abolish the custom; and it appears to me today in responsum of R' Yosef Minz he was asked why they do not practice saying kaddish on Tisha B'Av and likewise on Yom Kippur at minchah after the third's reading before the haftarah — and he answered like the words stated, that one may not say kaddish for it is an interruption and it would be necessary to ascend again to read in the Torah and read the haftarah, and this is impossible — see there. It appears the custom was not to say kaddish until the questioner stood; why not say kaddish? And in my humble opinion thus is from the essence of the law: on Tisha B'Av one says kaddish after the haftarah, for kaddish refers to Torah reading; and the haftarah is not an interruption as Magen Avraham wrote siman 282 s.k. 96. And at minchah on Yom Kippur the kaddish they say immediately after the reading before the ark precedes minchah tefillah and ascends upon the reading; and therefore I was accustomed when I was in the community of Friedland that the custom at minchah — some go to the small synagogue to read Torah there to say kaddish after the haftarah, since they do not pray minchah there and go back to the large synagogue — it is an interruption. And kaddish of minchah tefillah does not refer to the reading; and the same on Tisha B'Av when they say kinot after the reading it is a great interruption, and it is fitting to say kaddish after reading the haftarah:
