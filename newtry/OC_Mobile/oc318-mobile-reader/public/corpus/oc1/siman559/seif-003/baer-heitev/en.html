One. And the Maharil wrote to light yet another candle, so that if the chazzan's candle goes out he can light from it. This implies that the congregation does not say Eicha and Kinot, but only hears them from the chazzan (Magen Avraham). And the Taz wrote that the entire congregation must say the Kinot, for of what use is the chazzan's saying alone? Therefore it appears that in each neighborhood there should be one candle, so that even under cramped conditions they can say Kinot by its light; see there. And Baer Heitev wrote that what was said about one candle each is specifically at nightfall, but at the time of reading Eicha and Kinot each one may light a candle; see there. The Maharil would leave a short pause between each verse, and a slightly longer pause between each chapter of Eicha. And the last verse of each chapter of Eicha he says in a loud voice. And it is good that an individual also read Eicha by day (Shelah).
