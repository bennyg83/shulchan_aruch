[3] [Levush] Nevertheless they light, etc. This implies that the congregation did not say Eicha and Kinot, but only heard them from the chazzan (Magen Avraham); but the Taz wondered of what use is the chazzan's saying alone, therefore he explained that in each neighborhood there should be only one candle, so that under cramped conditions they can say Kinot by its light; end quote. And in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote that [the one-candle practice] was said regarding nightfall, but at the time of reading Eicha and Kinot the custom is that the gabbai of the congregation goes around and distributes, and gives a candle to each one to read Eicha and Kinot; end quote. And this is puzzling, for he wrote "and to say Eicha and Kinot by its light," and he was forced to explain that one lights from this light to say by its light. I also wonder about all the earlier great authorities mentioned above, who did not see in the Kol Bo that he wrote: indeed there are places where only the chazzan alone reads and the entire congregation is silent, and there are places where the entire congregation reads.
