(Ibid., se'if 9) Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos and is postponed to Sunday, etc. A side note: see in the Magen Avraham what he wrote, that a chosson on a communal fast day should not complete his fast. And it is difficult to me, for his words contradict what he himself wrote in siman 549, s.k. 6, to the opposite effect: that a chosson on a communal fast day should not drink from the cup of blessing while it is still day, and they give it to young children; see there. This implies that he is obligated to complete. And one cannot say that there the intent is if the chuppah was before plag haMinchah, for from his wording "while it is still day" it implies that so long as the chuppah is by day it is forbidden for him to drink even when it is close to evening. And one must explain that the intent of the Magen Avraham here is regarding a communal fast that was postponed: in that case he should not complete his fast; and he need not spell this out, for it is something learned from its context, for the entire se'if deals with this. Also, without postponement a chosson is no better than a baal bris who is obligated to fast; necessarily he is dealing with a postponed case. And accordingly, that which the Magen Avraham wrote that on the other days of feasting he completes means that even on a postponed communal fast he is obligated to complete; and this is clear and correct. And so is the implication of the author of Ateret Zekenim: that for all the fasts he should fast, and he excluded only a yahrtzeit, that he should not fast during the seven days of feasting. And even regarding this it appears that he needs hatarah, since it is like a vow; and it is unlike when a yahrtzeit falls on Shabbos or other yahrtzeit days on days when they do not fast, where he needs no hatarah, for there the matter depends on the fixed calendar and they will certainly fall, and it is as if he stipulated from the outset that on them he would not fast, and upon those days his intent never took effect from the beginning; but regarding the days of his wedding, did he know from the outset that his wedding would fall specifically on his yahrtzeit day? It is possible that his wedding day would be on another day; if so, his initial obligation applied even to this day, and it is as if he accepted upon himself also this day to fast; and now that his marriage day falls, he needs hatarah, as it appears to my humble opinion; and examine carefully. (Ibid., in that same se'if) A baal bris prays Minchah while the day is still great, etc. A side note: I was asked by the famous outstanding rabbi, Moreinu HaRav Yosef Babad, formerly av beis din of the community of Zbarazh and presently av beis din of the community of Snitin [he is the Gaon, av beis din of the community of Tarnopol, author of Minchas Chinuch], regarding the sugya of Eruvin folio 41, where it asks: the reason is that it is their festival day; but on erev Yom Tov they complete. And it is difficult: what does it ask? Granted regarding Shabbos and Yom Tov, which begin immediately at night, therefore Rabban Gamliel holds that they do not complete even on erev Yom Tov; but there, since their festival day is because of the korban, and the korban does not apply at night, if so festival day does not apply at night on erev Pesach, for melacha is forbidden only after midday, since beforehand is the time of the korban; if so, all the more so at night, since it is not the time of the korban, it is not a festival day. And the Mishneh LaMelech wrote that the essence of their festival day was because of the korban itself, not because of the wood; if so, also at night it is not a festival day; so what does it ask? This is the substance of the question of the aforementioned outstanding gaon, Moreinu HaRav Yosef Babad. And I answered him that in my opinion it fits well: the Gemara proves that necessarily they made the night also into a festival day; for if not, if the night was not a festival day, then why did they not complete? For we hold regarding all these matters that if one began he stops; nevertheless if he began in permissibility, according to all he does not stop. If so, here too: granted regarding every erev Yom Tov, Rabban Gamliel holds that he does not complete, since the whole of it, both day and night, is all included in festival day, and all of it began in prohibition, and since he began in prohibition he stops; but if we say here that the night is not a festival day for them, and on their erev Yom Tov they were permitted to fast, if so, since they began fasting on the night of Tisha B'Av which was not yet a festival day, and he began in permissibility, even though afterward in the morning their festival day took effect, it ought to be like the law of one who began in permissibility, that he does not stop. And one may also compare this to what is written in Masseches Taanis folio 12: if they vowed before our decree, their vow nullifies our decree. If so, granted if the night also was a festival day, and in the Second Temple they did not fast on Tisha B'Av, as is written in the Midrash brought also in the Magen Avraham: after they would drink and become intoxicated, etc.; if so, our decree making it a festival day preceded Tisha B'Av, therefore even after the Temple was later destroyed, their festival day overrides the prohibition of Tisha B'Av, so they did not complete. But if we say that the night is not a festival day, then he began fasting according to law, and since the stringency of Tisha B'Av already took effect upon them now, it is as if their vow preceded our decree, and they would be obligated to complete even by day. Necessarily it is proven that the night also was a festival day, and he began in its prohibition; therefore it asks: if so, you could derive it from the fact that it is erev Yom Tov; and it asks from whichever side you take; as it appears to my humble opinion at present. This ends what I answered the aforementioned rav; and examine carefully well.
There, in that same seif. "A baal berit prays Minchah while the day is still great, etc." NB: I was asked by the famous great rabbi R. Yosef Babad, formerly av beit din of Zbarazh and now av beit din of Sniatyn, the gaon av beit din of Tarnopol and author of Minchat Chinuch, about the sugya in Eruvin 41. The Gemara asks: the reason is that it is their Yom Tov, but on the eve of their Yom Tov they complete the fast. He asked: what is the question? Granted, Shabbat and Yom Tov begin immediately at night, so Rabban Gamliel holds that one does not complete even on their eve. But there, since it was their Yom Tov because of the korban, and the korban is not relevant at night, the Yom Tov should not apply at night on erev Pesach; work is forbidden only after noon, since before then is not the time of the korban. If so, all the more at night, since it is not the time of the korban, it is not Yom Tov. The Mishneh LaMelech wrote that their main Yom Tov was because of the korban itself, not because of the wood; if so, night is not Yom Tov. This was the substance of that rabbi's question. I answered him that in my opinion the Gemara is well resolved. The Gemara proves that perforce they also made the night into Yom Tov. If not, if the night were not Yom Tov, why did they not complete the fast? We hold in all such matters that if one began, he stops; nevertheless, if he began permissibly, all agree he does not stop. If so, regarding every eve of Yom Tov Rabban Gamliel holds that one does not complete, because the whole period, both day and night, is included in Yom Tov and it is as though he began while it was forbidden; since he began in prohibition, he stops. But if here the night is not their Yom Tov and on the eve of their Yom Tov they were permitted to fast, then since they began fasting on the night of Tisha B'Av when it was not yet Yom Tov, they began permissibly. Even though their Yom Tov later arrived in the morning, it should be like a case where he began permissibly and does not stop. One may also compare this to Ta'anit 12, where if their vow preceded our decree, their vow overrides our decree. Thus, if the night is also Yom Tov, and in the Second Temple they did not fast on Tisha B'Av, as stated in the Midrash brought also by the Magen Avraham, after they would drink and become intoxicated, etc., then our decree of Yom Tov preceded Tisha B'Av, and even though the Temple was later destroyed, their Yom Tov overrides the issur of Tisha B'Av, so they did not complete. But if the night is not Yom Tov, then he began fasting lawfully; since the stringency of Tisha B'Av had already taken effect upon them, it is as though their vow preceded our decree, and they would be obligated to complete even by day. Perforce it is proven that the night too is Yom Tov, and therefore it is considered that he began in its prohibition. The Gemara therefore asks: if so, let it derive from the fact that it is the eve of Yom Tov, and it asks from either side. This appears to me for now. This is what I answered that rabbi. Examine very carefully.
