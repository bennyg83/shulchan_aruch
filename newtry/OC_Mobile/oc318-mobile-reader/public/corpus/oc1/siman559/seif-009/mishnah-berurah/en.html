(35) And it was postponed, etc. But if a milah falls on Tisha B'Av that was not postponed, one must complete the fast; and it is not specifically on Tisha B'Av, for the same applies to an ordinary communal fast that the community decreed, or to any of the four fasts, if they were not postponed, one must complete; and likewise is the law regarding a chosson on the day of his wedding for an ordinary communal fast or any of the four fasts: if they were not postponed he must complete the fast, and they give the cup to a child to drink; but if they were postponed he need not complete, and he is permitted to drink the cup and likewise to eat after his wedding, for it is his festival.
(36) A baal bris. Included in this are the father of the son and his mother, the mohel, and the sandak, but not the one who brings in and takes out [the infant], and all the more so other invited guests. Nevertheless, even though they are permitted to eat, they should not make a large meal by day as they do on these other days when it is not Tisha B'Av.
(37) He prays Minchah. And the later authorities agreed that this means Minchah Gedolah after midday, and afterward he is permitted to eat; but he must make Havdalah over the cup before he eats.
(38) For it is his festival day. Some say that the same applies if a pidyon haben falls on a postponed Tisha B'Av: it is also called a festival for him, and the father and the kohen need not complete the fast; nevertheless it is not permitted to make the meal, for the mitzvah of the meal does not override the fast, as above regarding a baal bris. And this is specifically when the pidyon haben falls in its time, when his thirty-first day falls on that very day; but if not in its time, and even if the thirty-first day was on Shabbos, for even so it is not in its time, they must complete.
