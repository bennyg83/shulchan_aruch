And it was postponed, etc. If a bris occurs on Tishah B'Av itself, those involved complete the fast. [Beit Yosef] This is also implied by what is written in siman 558 in the name of Maharil. An objection was raised before Maharam Lublin from Eruvin, at the end of chapter 3. Rabbi Elazar bar Tzadok said: Once Tishah B'Av fell on Shabbos and was postponed until Sunday. We fasted but did not complete the fast because it was our festival, meaning that the wood offering of his family was brought on the tenth of Av. The Gemara asks: The reason they did not complete it was that it was their festival, but on the eve of their festival they would complete it. This proves that one may complete a fast on Friday. The objection is as follows: This festival, had it coincided with Tishah B'Av, would not have displaced the fast, so certainly one should complete the fast on its eve. Shabbos, however, does displace Tishah B'Av when they coincide, so perhaps one should likewise not complete a fast on Friday. It must therefore be that even on their own festival they would not complete Tishah B'Av itself. How, then, could Beit Yosef rule that one completes the fast when a bris occurs on Tishah B'Av? He answered that the questioner indeed assumed that they would not complete the fast even on Tishah B'Av itself. The answer of the Gemara, however, was that a privately observed festival instituted by the Sages is different: since they fast for part of the day, they may complete the fast on its eve, but Tishah B'Av itself is not displaced by it. It appears to me that the Gemara's question contains an additional difficulty. According to the questioner's assumption that Rabbi Elazar bar Tzadok did not follow Rabban Gamliel, why did they not complete the fast? Even on Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, and Purim one completes a fast, as stated in siman 472:2, all the more so on their private festival. The necessary conclusion is that because Tishah B'Av had been postponed from its proper date, it was treated more leniently. If, however, he followed Rabban Gamliel, who holds that one never completes a fast on the eve of a festival, then they should not have completed any ordinary Tishah B'Av either. The Gemara answers that a festival instituted by the Sages is different, etc. Accordingly, since we do not follow Rabban Gamliel and rule that even on Rosh Chodesh one fasts and completes the fast, all the more so on a private festival. Only a postponed Tishah B'Av is treated leniently. Similarly, when a communal fast is decreed, everyone must complete it. See siman 568:2 and Taanis 12. Knesses HaGedolah writes that one should nevertheless not make a festive meal. The present custom is to complete the fast even when one of the four fasts has been postponed. Radbaz likewise rules this way in part 2, siman 35. When the bris takes place after midday, one may not be lenient even on one of the four fasts, unless the fast was postponed. Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi writes that a bridegroom on a communal fast does not complete his fast, even though he could have postponed the wedding, unlike the case of a bris. During the seven days of celebration, however, he does complete the fast. This applies in a place where the custom is not to fast on the wedding day. Knesses HaGedolah records this ruling. Nowadays the widespread custom is that most wedding meals are held at night. Accordingly, there is no basis for leniency during the daytime fast.
