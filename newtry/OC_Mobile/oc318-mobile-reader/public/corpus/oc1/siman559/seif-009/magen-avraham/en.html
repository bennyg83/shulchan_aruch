(א) And it was deferred etc. But if circumcision falls on Tisha B'Av they complete [Beit Yosef], and so from what is written in siman 558 in the name of Maharil; and the questioner questioned Moreinu of Lublin in responsa — for in chapter 3 of Eruvin they say from this [proof] of R. Eliezer b. Tzadok who said once Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbat and they deferred it until after Shabbat and fasted and did not complete because our festival day was then (meaning the wood offering on the tenth of Av) — and they challenged: what did he challenge? Granted on that festival if it fell on Tisha B'Av they would complete, so all the more one should complete on the eve of the festival; but Shabbat one does not fast on it if Tisha B'Av falls on it, so likewise on the eve one should not complete — rather it must be that even on Tisha B'Av itself they do not complete on their festival; if so why did Beit Yosef rule they complete on Tisha B'Av itself? And answer: in truth the questioner holds they do not complete even on Tisha B'Av itself, but they answered that a festival day is different since they fast on them — therefore they complete on the eve of their festival; if so there is still a question on what the questioner holds that he does not act like R. Gamliel — if so on every Tisha B'Av they should not have completed, and even on Rosh Chodesh and Chanukah and Purim they complete as written siman 572 seif 2 — a fortiori on their festival. Rather it must be that since Tisha B'Av was deferred from its place it is not so stringent; but if he holds like R. Gamliel that one never completes, then on every Tisha B'Av they should not have completed, and they answer that a festival day is different since it is their matter — if so we do not defer Tisha B'Av itself because of them. And for us who do not hold like R. Gamliel — we rule that even on Rosh Chodesh one fasts and completes — a fortiori on our festival; except on Tisha B'Av when deferred we are lenient; and also in the congregation who decreed a public fast they must all complete — see siman 568 seif 2 and Taanit daf 12; and see in Knesset HaGedolah. Nevertheless one should not make a meal (Tosafot Yom Tov Moed Katan from a king Magen Avraham) and the custom now is to complete even on one of the four fasts that were deferred; and likewise Radbaz volume 2 siman 35; and where they circumcised after chatzot it is forbidden to be lenient even on the four fasts if they were not deferred — Maharash Halevi siman 2 wrote a groom on the tenth of Av should not complete his fast even if he could defer the wedding, unlike circumcision; but within seven days of feasting he completes, and it refers where they did not practice to fast on the day of the wedding — end of his words; and now the custom spread that most feasts are at night, so on the fast day too one should not be lenient.
