9) [Seif 2] On the night of Tisha B’Av, one prays Maariv and recites Kaddish with Tiskabel after the Maariv Amidah. Tur and Beit Yosef. So too wrote the Avudraham, 95a; Levush. Some say that only half-Kaddish should be recited, meaning until “da’amiran b’alma.” Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B’Av, Order of Prayer. However, the view of the later authorities follows the Tur, that Tiskabel is recited after completing the eighteen blessings of the Maariv Amidah. Taz, s.k. 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Mateh Yehudah; Eshel Avraham, entry 1; Chayei Adam, klal 135, entry 19; Derech HaChaim, entry 2; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 124, entry 1; Mishna Berurah, entry 4. But after Eichah, until Minchah the next day, Kaddish is recited without Tiskabel. Levush, seif 2; Taz there; Mateh Yehudah; Eshel Avraham there. So too is stated below in seif 4 in the gloss, see there. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that the reason is that Eichah already states, “You have shut out my prayer.” So too wrote the Levush there. There are places that omit Tiskabel only at night after the Amidah, but recite it at Shacharis, since they have already finished and concluded the kinnot and recite several verses of consolation at their end. Beit Yosef in the name of the aforementioned authority; Levush there. So too, the Rokeach, siman 311, wrote that some require Kaddish with Tiskabel to be recited during the day after Kedushah D’Sidra. He wrote that their reason is that the primary mourning applies only at night and no longer, and on the next day, part of the day is considered like the whole day, see there. So too is stated in the Avudraham, 95b. This is also the custom of Beit El, may it be rebuilt speedily in our days, in our holy city Jerusalem, may it be rebuilt and established, whose members follow the words of the Arizal, to recite Tiskabel at Shacharis on Tisha B’Av as on other days.
