Not actual white garments. Likewise, on Shabbos Chazon one does not change his garments except for the outer shirt. A gloss explains that the word "shirt" means a cloak, not an undershirt. Maharam Tiktin writes that the reason is that an undershirt is a private matter, whereas a cloak is worn publicly, and public mourning does not apply on Shabbos. He himself, however, explains that the accepted ruling is the reverse: the cloak is forbidden because a conspicuous garment is treated more stringently on Tishah B'Av than during ordinary mourning, as stated in siman 554, while the undershirt is permitted. So states Mordechai. It therefore appears that the participants in a bris may wear a white shirt, as stated in siman 551 in the name of Mateh Moshe. This is also implied by Minhagim, which states that the participants in a bris wear white, though not completely white garments, and not on Shabbos Chazon, etc. This implies that Shabbos Chazon is not treated more stringently. Shabbos garments that are still new and whose pressing is noticeable may not be worn, as stated in siman 551:6. Maharil writes that after the bris they remove these garments.
