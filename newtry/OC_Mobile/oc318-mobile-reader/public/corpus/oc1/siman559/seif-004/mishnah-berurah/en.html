(16) They do not say Tachanun, etc. In the morning they rise somewhat early to the synagogue; and if by this they will finish saying Kinot a long time before midday, it is good not to rise so early.
(17) Because it is called a festival. As Scripture says, "He has proclaimed a set time against me," etc.; nevertheless it is permitted to deliver a eulogy for a sage who died and to sit on the ground, and even after midday, for that entire day is fixed for weeping and eulogy; and regarding this we pay no heed to its being called a festival.
(18) Assof asifem. With the melody of Eicha.
(19) And not Lamnatzeach. For there it is written "May Hashem answer you on the day of trouble; may the Name of the God of Yaakov set you on high," etc., and it is not fitting to say this.
(20) And not Pitum HaKetores. For it is not called the order of the day, since not every person says it. But Mizmor LeTodah they do say, for it is included in the order of the day.
(21) And one need not change his place. For it is enough that we show signs of mourning in that we descend from the benches and sit on the ground.
