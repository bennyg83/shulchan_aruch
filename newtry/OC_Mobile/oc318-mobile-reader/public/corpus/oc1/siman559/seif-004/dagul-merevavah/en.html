His words are puzzling — what relevance do eleven hours have, since minchah ketanah begins from nine and a half hours? If Magen Avraham is stringent that one wait until the end of minchah time, then necessarily: if like the Rabbis, he waits until near evening; if like R' Yehuda, minchah time has already passed, for R' Yehuda's time is only until eleven hours less a quarter. But certainly hours before minchah gedolah — and so in Hagahot Ashiri chapter "ve'elu megalechim," and an incident with Rabbeinu Yaavetz, etc., who commanded to pray minchah gedolah after ten hours, etc.; and certainly it must be after six hours, since he wrote minchah gedolah — and in print vav was exchanged for yod; therefore the main point is that one prays minchah gedolah. I also saw Eliyah Rabbah noted this.
