Se'if 4 in the gloss And they read in the Torah "Ki solid." However, I wondered at what I have seen practiced: that they say Kaddish after the third has read in the Torah, before they say the haftarah. For it is explained that the maftir must first read in the Torah so that the honor of the haftarah not be like the honor of the Torah, ascending for the haftarah alone; rather, midina the seventh is the maftir, for the maftir counts among the called ones. But since they add on Shabbos (and likewise on Yom Tov there are opinions), they act according to all views to read the [regular] number of called ones aside from the maftir; but in any case the maftir must first read in the Torah. If so, if they interrupt with Kaddish, when he afterward says the haftarah it is as if he ascends for the haftarah alone, and it would be necessary for another to ascend to read in the Torah and to recite the haftarah. And for this reason the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 282, se'if 16, regarding that case there: the one who ascends as the seventh should recite the haftarah, and one should not say Kaddish after him. If so, what difference is there if the seventh is the maftir, that they do not interrupt with Kaddish between his reading and the saying of the haftarah, and what difference is there on Tisha B'Av, where the third is the maftir, that they should say Kaddish? And I found in the Minhagim that he wrote thus, to say Kaddish before saying the haftarah, and so I saw in the Levush; and he wrote the reason not to say Kaddish before the reading of the third, since the number of called ones is not yet completed; see there. But nevertheless this does not remedy the saying of Kaddish between the reading and the saying of the haftarah, and my heart did not fill me to abolish the custom. And thank God, today I found in the Responsa of Mahari Mintz that he was asked why they do not customarily say Kaddish on Tisha B'Av, and likewise on Yom Kippur at Minchah, after the reading of the third before saying the haftarah; and he answered in the aforementioned words, that one cannot say Kaddish, for it is an interruption, and it would be necessary for another to ascend to read in the Torah and to recite the haftarah, and that is impossible; see there. It appears that the custom was not to say Kaddish until the questioner arose asking why they do not say Kaddish. And in my humble opinion, according to the essential law one should say Kaddish on Tisha B'Av after saying the haftarah, for the Kaddish applies to the Torah reading, and the haftarah is not an interruption, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 282, s.k. 15; and at Minchah on Yom Kippur, the Kaddish they say immediately after the reading before the amud before the Minchah prayer applies to the reading. And for this reason, when I was in the community of Friedland, where the custom at Minchah is that some go to the small synagogue to read the Torah there, I practiced saying Kaddish after saying the haftarah, since they do not pray Minchah there and go back to the main synagogue, which is an interruption, and the Kaddish of the Minchah prayer does not apply to the reading. And likewise on Tisha B'Av, since they say Kinot after the reading, that is a great interruption, and it is proper to say Kaddish after the reading of the haftarah.
Magen Avraham, s.k. 5 In the morning they rise early. See Eliyah Rabbah.
