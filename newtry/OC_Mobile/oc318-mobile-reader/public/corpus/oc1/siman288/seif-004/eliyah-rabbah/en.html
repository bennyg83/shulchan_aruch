[7] [Levush] Nevertheless he must, etc. If Sunday is a communal fast, Shiltei Gibborim is lenient in an inadvertent case and holds that it counts. Responsa Mas'at Binyamin, siman 93, rules that it does not count and that he must fast another day. He proves this from Terumat HaDeshen, siman 278, which states that one who observes a dream fast on Rosh Hashanah need not fast an additional fast for having fasted on the festival because some hold that it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah. This implies that otherwise an additional fast would be required and Tzom Gedaliah would not suffice. Olat Tamid and Magen Avraham rule likewise. Taz objects and gives two answers. First, the case of Rosh Hashanah may concern one who did not fast on Tzom Gedaliah because of circumstances beyond his control. Second, the purpose of the compensatory fast is that he intend it as atonement and be moved to repentance, so the requirement means that on Tzom Gedaliah he should intend that the fast serve as atonement. In my humble opinion, Taz did not see Terumat HaDeshen itself, for it states that he saw many people who fasted on Rosh Hashanah and none observed an additional compensatory fast, apparently because fasting on Rosh Hashanah is itself a mitzvah. The first answer is therefore difficult, for it is implausible that all of them failed to fast on Tzom Gedaliah because of unavoidable circumstances. The second is also difficult, since perhaps they did intend Tzom Gedaliah for atonement. The clear proof Taz cites from Yoreh Deah, end of siman 217, also appears below in siman 568, seif 11, but in truth it is no proof, as Magen Avraham writes, because here atonement is required, and we do not find atonement obtained without cost. I also examined Shiltei Gibborim itself and found that it remains in doubt for this very reason. I heard an objection to Mas'at Binyamin: perhaps Terumat HaDeshen needed the reason that fasting on Rosh Hashanah is a mitzvah because if one fasted both days of Rosh Hashanah, how could Tzom Gedaliah compensate for both? It appears to me that if a communal fast counts, then one fast can likewise compensate for fasting both days. Furthermore, Terumat HaDeshen itself asks whether a fast during the Ten Days of Repentance counts, since he fasts then in any event. This implies a case in which he fasted throughout the Ten Days of Repentance, yet it still invokes the reason that fasting on Rosh Hashanah is a mitzvah. We therefore learn that none of the Ten Days of Repentance count for one whose practice is to fast on them, contrary to Olat Tamid, who writes that days a person voluntarily accepts as fasts, such as the Ten Days of Repentance, do count. Terumat HaDeshen does not imply this. This also supports Magen Avraham's ruling that one whose custom is to fast on the first day of Selichot cannot count that day. If one has a bad dream on a communal fast, Olat Tamid rules that the communal fast counts for him.
[8] [Levush] And it is proper to fast, etc. So that he not delay seeking mercy and forgiveness (Rashba). Piskei Recanati, siman 178, states that the preferred fulfillment is on Sunday, for then it is evident that he fasts because of Shabbos. Although Shulchan Aruch uses the expression "must," Levush writes "it is proper," because Rashba himself in the chapter Ein Omdin writes that it is not an absolute obligation. Therefore, when Levush says "if his strength is weakened," it means even if his strength is only somewhat weakened. So it appears to me.
[9] Chanukah, etc. This implies specifically these days, but during Nisan, Sivan, and Isru Chag one may fast, as Rabbi Shlomo HaLevi writes. The same applies between Yom Kippur and Sukkot. Magen Avraham adds that even if Sunday is Rosh Chodesh Nisan, Rosh Chodesh Av, or Erev Pesach, he fasts. Perishah, however, quotes Rama as saying that one does not fast on a day when Tachanun is omitted. It is proper to rely on this, since Chiddushei Rashba cites an opinion that a dream fast does not require a compensatory fast at all, because the Gemara refers to one who voluntarily accepted a fast on Shabbos. Responsa Binyamin Ze'ev writes similarly. Although Rashba there and Rivash siman 513 write that most authorities disagree, nevertheless many authorities also hold that he need not fast specifically on Sunday, and even according to Rashba it is not an absolute obligation. Therefore, one should not fast on a day when Tachanun is omitted. See the end of siman 420 regarding my objection to Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, if one actually observes a dream fast on such days, even on Rosh Chodesh Nisan or Av, he need not observe a compensatory fast for having fasted then. The law of Erev Yom Kippur will be explained in siman 568, seif 5. The fifteenth of Av and the fifteenth of Shevat require study below.
[10] Until midnight, etc. The sermons of Maharam of Rothenburg state only until nightfall, and write that he must fast on Sunday because there is an opinion that one who completes a fast on Erev Shabbos until nightfall requires a compensatory fast (Malbushei Yom Tov). It appears to me that one may rely on this on the first day of Yom Tov, or when Yom Tov follows Shabbos on Sunday, or on the second day of Yom Tov when it falls on Friday, and then he need fast only until nightfall. It also appears so regarding one whose practice is to fast even on Shabbos and who has such a dream in the afternoon. Although it is possible that a person who was already fasting does not obtain the benefit of a dream fast, it appears to me that he is not obligated to observe a compensatory fast, because he did not thereby cancel Shabbos delight, since his practice is to fast. Shelah, p. 200, writes that if one dreams a troubling dream even in the middle of the night, he should taste nothing thereafter. It is not sufficient to begin fasting at dawn as on other fasts. He proves this from one who sleeps in the afternoon, who begins fasting immediately. It also appears to him that the man should separate from his wife, even on Shabbos when it is the prescribed time for marital relations, for if fasting and canceling Shabbos delight were permitted, this is all the more so. Magen Avraham in siman 564 writes likewise. In my humble opinion, however, one can distinguish between afternoon sleep during the day and nighttime sleep. There is some proof from those who observe a dream fast on both days of Rosh Hashanah but do not fast at night. Magen Avraham in siman 596 also writes that in a dream the matter was shown to him by day and not by night. It likewise appears possible to distinguish between a fast, which concerns him personally, and the mitzvah of marital relations, which he owes to his wife.
