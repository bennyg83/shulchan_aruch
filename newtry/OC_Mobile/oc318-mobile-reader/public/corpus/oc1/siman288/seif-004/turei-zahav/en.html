(4) Permitted to fast on it a fast. Tur wrote the reason: this is pleasure for him that he thinks the fast will tear his decree — his soul is aggrieved over what he saw and he thinks it will be nullified through the reward of the fast — and such is the truth. And I say from what Tur wrote siman 568 in the name of Ra'avan and R' Chaim that one does not fast on Shabbat a fast in our time — because we are not expert in interpreting dreams; here it refers to Talmudic words when they were expert; and further it appears and this is primary that here it deals even in our time; and that which is forbidden below is from the side of law that a person thinks he is obligated to fast since Heaven showed him evil, Heaven forbid — therefore it is not so because we are not expert; therefore there is no obligation from the side of law; but here it deals from the side that he worries and is distressed even though he is uncertain in interpretation — nevertheless he worries about the uncertainty and fasts to nullify the uncertainty — this is pleasure for him and permitted. And accordingly it appears to me to distinguish in this ruling: if he dreamed one of the dreams mentioned here and afterward fasts — then even though he does not worry so much and his soul is not aggrieved over it, he fasts; and for other dreams where he is uncertain in their interpretation — we do not instruct him to fast unless his soul is aggrieved over it — then he has pleasure from this; and therefore Tur mentioned here "his soul is aggrieved"; and likewise siman 128; and further it appears that in every case where he thought during the day and it came to him in a dream afterward — there is no relevance to "Heaven showed him" — rather the thought causes it; and like the incident in the chapter "The Seer" — Shapur the king said to Shmuel: you say much wisdom — tell me what I saw in a dream — meaning the coming night; and he said: you saw Romans come and enslave you and grind you with golden millstones — he thought all day and saw — behold the matter depends on thought and there is no ruling from Heaven; therefore he should not fast on Shabbat for this, as is my primary view for practice: it is written in Mishna Berurah siman 293 in Taz Taz on Shabbat and the next day 17 Tammuz that one must fast after 17 Tammuz for what he fasted on Shabbat and 17 Tammuz does not help for this; and proof from Tosefta siman 248 who wrote: one who fasts a fast on Rosh Hashanah need not sit a fast for his fast — for some say it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah; it implies if not so a fast is required and the Fast of Gedaliah does not suffice; and likewise in Beit Yosef Orach Chayyim siman 568: one who fasts on Yom Kippur needs a fast for his fast — behold even Yom Kippur does not help — a fortiori 17 Tammuz; but in my humble opinion thus he learned this from the implication of the words of Tosefta and Beit Yosef; and if they intended for this they should have brought proof that it is so; and on the contrary it appears clear proof to the contrary from what Maharil wrote in responsum siman 125: one who vows to fast a number of consecutive days and a obligatory fast occurs among them — it is simple he is released from it — for he accepted it to afflict himself; and they brought it in Shulchan Aruch in the gloss Yoreh De'ah siman 217 at its end; and he brings further proof there in responsum from Yisrael who fulfills eighteen days of festival joy with vows and donations — evidently even though he must bring peace offerings of vows nevertheless he fulfills festival joy and we do not say since he vowed he must pay peace offerings of festival joy; and if so likewise this fast he is obligated because of Shabbat — he is released through an obligatory fast — for there is no difference between obligation of fast and obligation of festival joy; and this is clear proof for what he learned from Tosefta and Beit Yosef — two responsa on the matter: one — they did not want to say only that there is prohibition in fasting on Rosh Hashanah and therefore one needs a fast afterward; and practical difference if it happened he did not fast on Yom Kippur or on the Fast of Gedaliah such as he had coercion — he is obligated to fast because of the Shabbat fast afterward; second — the essence of the fast is that he intend it be atonement for that matter for which he fasted and think repentance on it — and on this he says one needs a fast for his fast — meaning he should intend on the obligatory fast day that it be atonement for what he fasted on Shabbat; but certainly not for that obligatory fast does he ascend to atone for everything — in particular Yom Kippur; and according to reasoning it appears after Yom Kippur one need not a fast for his Shabbat fast even if he did not fast on Yom Kippur truly from coercion — for Yom Kippur atones for positive and negative commandments he transgressed; and certainly he is obligated for this like his fasts; and Yom Kippur atones all the more in this, as above — clear:
