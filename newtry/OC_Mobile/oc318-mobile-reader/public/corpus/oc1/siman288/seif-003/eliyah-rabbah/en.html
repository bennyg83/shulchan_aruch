[4] And eating, etc. If he has no distress, then even if he fasts for his sins and as an act of repentance, it is prohibited (Beit Yosef; Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah). All the more so, a penitent should not weep on Shabbos because of his repentance (Shelah).
[5] [Levush] The dream that he saw, etc. This implies that if his fellow dreamed something about him, he does not fast, and Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah writes likewise. On a weekday, however, he does fast. The same applies when one dreams of monetary loss concerning himself. Nevertheless, if the dream concerns something for which Shabbos may be desecrated, Sefer Chasidim 868 writes that one fasts even on Shabbos. Magen Avraham in siman 220 cites Sefer Chasidim that one who dreams a troubling dream about his fellow should fast. It is obvious that on Shabbos he need not do so.
[6] [Levush] Seventy years, etc. Meaning, even if the decree was issued against him in his youth (Rashi, Berachot 31b). Seventy is mentioned because "the days of our years are seventy years." See Bach. Zohar, parashat Emor, explains that these are the seventy royal crowns that agreed upon evil against him, God forbid. Rashi there implies that this tearing of the decree applies specifically to Shabbos, when fasting is particularly difficult because everyone else delights while he fasts. Raavan siman 179 writes likewise. Levush, however, appears to imply that this applies even on a weekday, which requires study. He should also occupy himself with Torah throughout the day and thereby obtain atonement, and he may confess his sins while fasting just as on a weekday (Seder HaYom). Shelah, p. 131, implies that he may even cry.
