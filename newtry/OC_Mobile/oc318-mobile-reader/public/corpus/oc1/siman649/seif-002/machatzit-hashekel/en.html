(Subparagraph 2) He should not recite a blessing, etc. And perhaps there, with change of name and despair, it is better. According to the simple understanding, those who examine the Magen Avraham understand that he distinguishes between change of name and physical change. So too the Chok Yaakov understood in siman 454, subparagraph 6, and so too the Eliyah Rabbah understood; and the Eliyah Rabbah asked that the difficulty still remains: what did the Talmud ask initially, that he should acquire it by the physical change of the binding? In the Chok Yaakov he added a difficulty, that it is explained in all the authorities that change of name and physical change are equal regarding acquisition. But my heart does not see it this way. Rather, the Magen Avraham agrees that there is no distinction between change of name and physical change. The Magen Avraham's intent is to distinguish as follows: in the Shulchan Aruch, which is speaking before despair, as he wrote in the next subparagraph, there is only the acquisition of change, and therefore he should not recite a blessing. But in the Gemara, which is speaking where there are two factors, despair and change of name, it is better. The same applies to despair and physical change. The Maharsha wrote similarly in explaining Rashi, and I will bring it with Hashem's help in subparagraph 3. This is simple in my humble opinion. The reason the Magen Avraham asked his difficulty on the Talmud's question, "let him acquire it through change of name," and did not place his difficulty on the earlier Talmudic question, "let him acquire it through physical change," is that he was drawn after the words of Tosafos mentioned above. This is explained at greater length in tractate Bava Kamma 67b, s.v. "Ulla said," where they also proved that specifically where he acquires only through the mitzvah is it called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; but where he acquires without the assistance of the mitzvah, it is no longer a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. According to this, they asked from what we say: if one robbed a se'ah of wheat, ground it, kneaded it, baked it, and separated challah from it, he does not recite a blessing but blasphemes, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, even though he acquired it before separating challah. In one answer they answered that regarding a blessing, where one mentions the name of Heaven, it is proper to be stringent. There Tosafos first brought proof that where he acquired it without the assistance of the mitzvah, it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, from the Talmud's question regarding those gentile merchants: let him acquire it through change of name. Necessarily, in such a case it would not be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Since Tosafos placed their difficulty on the Talmud's question, "let him acquire it through change of name," the Magen Avraham also placed his words on this difficulty. There is no difficulty on Tosafos themselves as to why they did not bring proof from the Talmud's question, "let him acquire it through physical change," because at the beginning of their words they wrote this regarding one who robbed and consecrated as an offering. Although he acquired through despair and change of name, for initially it was called unconsecrated and now it is consecrated, nevertheless if despair does not acquire and the acquisition is completed by means of the mitzvah, where there is change of name, it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; see there. If so, since Tosafos at the beginning of their words regarding consecration are speaking of change of name, they wanted to bring proof from the gentile merchants likewise from change of name. Although there is no distinction between change of name and physical change, nevertheless it was more convenient for them to bring proof from the very matter they were discussing. Therefore the Magen Avraham was drawn after their words, since from their words the law regarding the blessing emerges. See the Maharsha in tractate Bava Kamma, who also asked the difficulty of the Magen Avraham. See there what he wrote.
