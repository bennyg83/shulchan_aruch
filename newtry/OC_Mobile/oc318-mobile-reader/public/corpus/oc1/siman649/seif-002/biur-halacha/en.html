And one who is prohibited by vow from benefit from his fellow's lulav, etc. See the Peri Megadim, who questions the language of the Rema, for he should have said: one who is prohibited by vow from benefit from his fellow's property does not fulfill his obligation with his lulav, for that is how it appears there in the responsum of the Rashba. It seems to me that according to what the Peri Megadim wrote, that regarding an esrog, even if he prohibited upon himself only eating it, he also does not fulfill the obligation, it is somewhat understandable that he mentioned lulav to exclude esrog.
Or from his own lulav. If he prohibited himself from benefit from an esrog, even if he prohibited upon himself only eating it, he also does not fulfill the obligation, because we require it to be permitted for eating; see there in the Peri Megadim. In the book Bikkurei Yaakov he disagrees with him, for according to Rashi and the Rashba, if a Jew took an esrog of terumah he fulfills the obligation with it because it is fit to be fed to others, namely to a kohen. Here too, he can feed it to others. But if he prohibited the esrog upon himself for benefit, he cannot give benefit to others with it, for that which is prohibited to him for benefit is prohibited for him to give as a gift to another, as is proven from that which Pesachim 22 infers from the case that one may send a thigh to a non-Jew, that the sciatic nerve is permitted for benefit. Therefore, we read regarding it that it has no monetary law; see there.
He does not fulfill the obligation with it on the first day, etc. According to what the Mechaber copied below in se'if 5, that an esrog prohibited for eating is invalid all seven days, and this is the language of the Rambam, and the Gra wrote there that his reason is that something that has no permitted eating or has no monetary law, meaning that it is prohibited for benefit, is considered as though its required measure is crushed. Accordingly, if he prohibited the esrog upon himself for eating, seemingly he does not fulfill the obligation with it all seven days, for we require permitted eating all seven. However, one can reject this and say that perhaps only something that has no permitted eating for anyone is considered as though its required measure is crushed; unlike this case.
