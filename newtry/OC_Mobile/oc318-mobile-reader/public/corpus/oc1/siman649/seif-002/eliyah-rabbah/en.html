[2] However, he should not recite a blessing, etc. This requires analysis. If so, what does the Gemara ask on page 30 regarding the sellers of hoshanos: let him acquire it through a change of name? In any event he would not be permitted to recite a blessing. But when a gentile cut it, he may recite a blessing, since he did not steal it. Perhaps there, a change of name together with despair is better. Magen Avraham. This is astonishing, for the Talmud there also asks: let him acquire it through a physical change. The main difficulty, in my opinion, should be answered as follows: the two questioners do not ask about the sellers themselves, but ask about Rav Huna, who gave the reason that there is acquisition and change of domain in our hands. Let him instead give the reason of physical change or change of name. And if he wanted to say that they should not be robbers, what does that depend on change of domain? He should have said the reason: so that you should not be robbers. Now my brother-in-law, the Rav Maharil, in Chok Yaakov, siman 454, answered that the rule that one should not recite a blessing applies to actual robbery, but Rav Huna is speaking of a distant concern, that perhaps gentiles stole it, and in such a case one may recite a blessing. He concluded as follows: know this, for otherwise why did Rav Huna instruct specifically regarding hoshanah of mitzvah and not regarding all other items that one takes from them while detached? Furthermore, what does it ask, "let the sellers themselves cut it"? Rav Huna instructed them to do this initially; how could he instruct them to perform robbery? Rather, certainly there is no robbery here at all, only because of the stringency of the mitzvah we are stringent. In all this, it appears that the Turei Zahav erred at the end of siman 664, where he asks from the final case of Rav Ashi, end quote. I wonder why he directed his complaint there against the Turei Zahav, for the Beis Yosef also, in the name of "I found written," asked this; and so too it is in Responsa Rashba, siman 652, and in Yam Shel Shlomo there. And from the sugya and Tosafos it implies that it has the full law of actual robbery. As for what he asked, why Rav Huna did not instruct so regarding other items, one can say that in any case gentiles would cut them, but regarding hoshanah for mitzvah they wanted to acquire merit in the mitzvah and cut it themselves. And as for what he asked, what does it ask, "let the sellers themselves cut it," etc., I already answered in my novellae to Sukkah that it asks about what he said, "and change of domain is in our hands," and did not say plainly, "and change of domain"; see there and examine it.
