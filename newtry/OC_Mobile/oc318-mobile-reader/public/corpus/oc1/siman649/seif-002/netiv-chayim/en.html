(Subparagraph 17) And so it implies in siman 645 se'if 6. Note: see there, where he brings proof for this from the Yerushalmi. It appears to me that there is clear proof from the words of Tosafos at the beginning of chapter Lulav HaGazul, that the disqualification of "its pitom was removed" is because it is deficient. For in Tosafos, s.v. "it states and teaches," they wrote: this is difficult, for we say below that if it was pierced and is lacking any amount, it is invalid, even though on the second day of Yom Tov it is valid. And if it is because it teaches stolen in a way comparable to dried, here too regarding pierced it teaches that if a chazazis rose over most of it, it is invalid on the second day of Yom Tov because of beauty. However, in the Yerushalmi it implies somewhat that because of chazazis it is invalid only on the first day of Yom Tov, etc.; see there in Tosafos. This is difficult: regarding pierced it teaches "its pitom was removed," which is invalid on the second day of Yom Tov because of beauty; and according to the reasoning of the Magen Avraham, it implies from the Yerushalmi that it is invalid on the second day of Yom Tov. If so, what did Tosafos answer regarding chazazis based on the Yerushalmi? In any event there remains difficulty from "its pitom was removed." Rather, learn from this that Tosafos holds that "its pitom was removed" is invalid only because it is deficient. Now at first glance one could say that there is no difficulty at all, for according to the answer of Tosafos we do not say that it is comparable. But the main proof is that Tosafos should have contradicted it from "its pitom was removed." This can be answered: therefore Tosafos contradicted the Yerushalmi's explanation from chazazis and not from "its pitom was removed," because according to one explanation below, "its pitom was removed" means the stem; and with the stem it is certainly because of deficiency that we approach it. Certainly, according to that explanation, "its pitom was removed," meaning the dad at the top of the esrog, one can say it is because of beauty, since it is at the top of the esrog, on the nose. Therefore Tosafos contradicted the Yerushalmi's explanation even from chazazis, which is fine according to all explanations. Still, I remain in my place, for on page 37 Tosafos brought in the name of the Yerushalmi: "its pitom was removed" means there they said its shoshanta was removed, implying that it is toward its top. If so, Tosafos should have challenged the Yerushalmi from the Yerushalmi itself.
