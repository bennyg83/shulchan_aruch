And likewise borrowed, etc. See the Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim. The substance of their words is that certainly ab initio it is good that on the first day he specify that he gives it to him as a gift on condition that it be returned. But after the fact, if he asked him without specification to give him his lulav in order to fulfill the obligation with it, and he gave it to him, he fulfilled the obligation, for presumably he gave it to him in a way that permits him to fulfill the obligation with it, namely as a gift on condition that it be returned. The Shulchan Aruch is speaking where he explicitly said to him that he gives it only as a loan and not as a gift, or where the giver does not know the law that one does not fulfill the obligation with a borrowed lulav. In that case he certainly does not fulfill the obligation, as Bikkurei Yaakov wrote.
From his fellow's lulav, etc. Meaning, his fellow wishes to give him the lulav as a gift on condition that it be returned. It teaches us that even though, since we rule that mitzvot were not given for benefit, this is not called benefit, nevertheless it is at least not called 'for yourselves,' because they are objects prohibited for benefit.
On the first day. But on the second day, when we do not require 'for yourselves,' it is permitted, for it is not called benefit, as above.
