If stolen or robbed. For it is written: 'and you shall take for yourselves on the first day,' etc., and this 'for yourselves' means from your own.
After despair. For despair by itself does not acquire. Now on the first day, certainly according to all opinions he has not fulfilled the Torah obligation; and regarding the other days this will be explained below.
Without the assistance of the mitzvah. This means that the acquisition by which he acquired it was made before the performance of the mitzvah, excluding where it comes through the mitzvah of taking the lulav, such as if he sold it to him in a way that he acquired it by pulling, and he lifted it to fulfill the obligation with it. If so, the acquisition was made through the mitzvah, and he has not fulfilled it, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Rather, he must lift it afterward a second time in order to fulfill the obligation with it. [Levushei Serad]
If he stole a lulav and smoothed it, etc. This means that at the time he stole it, it was not yet properly prepared such that the name lulav could be applied to it, and he prepared it by smoothing it, through which its name became lulav.
It is valid, for he acquired it through a physical change. This is effective even without despair, and even on the first day it is valid for fulfilling the obligation, for we read 'for yourselves' with respect to it, since he acquired it and he owes him only money. The same applies where there is despair and change of domain, such as where the robber sold it or gave it to another: that other person may fulfill the obligation with it even on the first day, for he acquired it through change of domain since despair had already occurred beforehand, and we read 'for yourselves' with respect to it.
Nevertheless, he should not bless over it. Since it originally came into his hand through robbery, we are stringent regarding a blessing, which contains mention of the Name of Heaven. See the Magen Avraham, whose view is that this applies specifically without despair; but if there was despair together with physical change, change of name, or change of domain, it is also permitted to bless. However, the view of the Taz above in siman 25 and the Gra there is that in every case he may not bless. See above in siman 11:6 in Biur Halacha, s.v. ve-l'inyan berachah.
Only for the thief and robber himself. It is proven from this that the Mechaber's view is that the robber does not fulfill the obligation even on the other days, when we do not require 'for yourselves.' The reason is that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, and there is no practical difference in this between before despair and after despair.
Except for the first day. That which we are stringent regarding others must necessarily be speaking before despair, and the Merciful One said 'for yourselves,' while this is not theirs. If despair had already occurred, then for them it would be despair and change of domain, a full acquisition for them, and we would read 'for yourselves' with respect to it. Even so, on the other days it is permitted for others, because the 'there is one who says' holds that a mitzvah that comes through a transgression does not apply to others, since they did not steal it. See the Magen Avraham, who brought in the name of Milchamot that it disagrees with this and holds that even regarding others who did not steal it, nevertheless, since the object was not acquired by them, for it is before despair and the stolen item is in their hands, the concern of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression applies to them too, and they do not fulfill the obligation with it. Therefore the view of the Magen Avraham is not to be lenient in this. But the Eliyah Rabbah wrote that one may rely leniently on the 'there is one who says,' for even regarding the thief and robber himself, several poskim hold that on the other days he fulfills the obligation with them; at least one may rely on them regarding others. The Gra in his commentary also implies that he inclines in practice toward the 'there is one who says.' However, all this is only regarding fulfilling the obligation with it; but regarding blessing over it, even according to the Gra it is forbidden to bless over it, for even where there is despair and change of domain it is prohibited to bless, as written earlier, and all the more so in this case.
That he should not cut, etc. The explanation of the matter is that he should not cut it himself from the land of a non-Jew, even when the non-Jew gives him permission, for perhaps he stole the land from a Jew, since ordinary non-Jews are robbers of land. We rule that land cannot be stolen, meaning that it is not acquired by the robber through any despair, for it always remains in the possession of its original owners, and fruit attached to the ground is like the ground. But once the fruit is detached, it becomes stolen property, and the one who detaches it is stealing it. Therefore the Jew should not cut it himself, for he would be the robber. Rather, the non-Jew should cut it, and then despair from the original owners will take effect upon the detached item, and he will hand it to the Jew; the Jew then acquires it through despair in the hand of the non-Jew and change of domain into his hand.
Robbers, etc. All this is ab initio, but after the fact it is valid if it is in the possession of the non-Jew, and one blesses over them, for out of doubt we are not concerned that it is stolen. And one who stole by conquest in war acquired it, as stated in Gittin daf 38. [Magen Avraham]
A non-Jew should cut it. The same applies when a Jew cuts it and gives it to his fellow: it is permitted, since it is after despair.
And buy from them. The same applies if the non-Jew gives it to him as a gift; it is permitted, for it is despair and change of domain. He may also bless according to all opinions, for even though we wrote above the view of the Taz and the Gra that through despair and change of domain it is prohibited to bless, that is specifically with definite theft. But here, where it is only a mere concern that perhaps the non-Jew stole the land, we are not concerned about this where he has already acquired it according to law.
Or outside Eretz Yisrael. Meaning, even though there most lands have always belonged to non-Jews, and if they stole them they stole them from non-Jews, nevertheless it is prohibited, for theft from a non-Jew is also prohibited.
That a non-Jew bound it, etc. Meaning, even though ab initio there is a mitzvah to bind it because of the adornment of the mitzvah, nevertheless it is valid if done by a non-Jew. See the Magen Avraham, whose view is that ab initio a non-Jew should not bind it. The reason is that anyone who is not obligated in the matter may not prepare it, and for this reason a woman also should not bind the lulav ab initio. [Magen Avraham in siman 14] See there in Biur Halacha, where we clarified that according to the law there is no concern in the matter; nevertheless, it is good to be careful about this. This is specifically with the lower binding, where he binds all the species together, for there is the primary mitzvah of binding; but regarding that which they bind above, there is no concern. [Bikkurei Yaakov]
