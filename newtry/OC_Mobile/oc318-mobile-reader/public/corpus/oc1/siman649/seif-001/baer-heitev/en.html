Valid. Even on the first day. Magen Avraham; see there. And see Yad Aharon.
On the other. If it is after despair, see Magen Avraham.
That he should not cut. Even with the permission of a gentile. And regarding an esrog in Eretz Yisrael, if he himself cut it, it is considered to have had its processing completed by the hand of a Jew, and it is tevel. Responsa of Rabbi Betzalel, siman 2. Magen Avraham.
Robbers. It appears to me that after the fact it is valid and one recites a blessing over them. Magen Avraham.
Gentile. The same applies when a Jew cuts it and gives it to his fellow: it is permitted, since it is after despair. Magen Avraham. See siman 637 se'if 3, what is written there in the name of the Turei Zahav. The Sefer Mitzvos Gadol wrote: it is forbidden to cheapen an esrog and other mitzvah items that gentiles sell, end quote. That is, one must give them more than their value. See siman 39 se'if 7. Magen Avraham.
