1) [Seif 1] All four species, etc. The Semag wrote: It is forbidden to belittle the value of an esrog and the other mitzvah items that non-Jews sell, end quote. This means that one must pay them more than their actual value. Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman. This means a little more, but not a great deal, so that they do not raise the market price. Eshel Avraham, beginning of the siman. The reason one must pay them more appears to be so that they will become accustomed to bringing them, and mitzvah items will thus be readily available:

They are invalid if stolen, etc. If one takes a stolen lulav, it goes and cries out before the Holy One, blessed is He, and says, ‘I am stolen.’ The ministering angels say: Woe to this person, whose defender has become his accuser. Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1, in the name of the Rokeach. 5) Ibid. They are invalid if stolen, etc. If the theft is only rabbinic, such as an object found by a deaf-mute, an imbecile, or a minor, concerning which we rule that taking it constitutes theft because of the ways of peace, or a similar case, the author of Shaar HaMelech, chapter 8 of Hilchos Lulav, was uncertain whether one fulfills his obligation with it. Since according to the Torah it is regarded as ‘yours,’ he discussed the matter, and his view tends toward the conclusion that one does not fulfill his obligation, because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; see there. Machazik Berachah, sec. 1. Shaarei Teshuvah, sec. 1. However, the view of Erech HaShulchan, sec. 3, is to permit it. So too is the view of Beis HaShoevah, sec. 2. 6) Ibid. They are invalid if stolen, etc. It appears to me that even if one stole or robbed it from a non-Jew, and the non-Jew did not despair of recovering it, since he can recover it through the courts, we do not call it ‘yours,’ and it is invalid. Riaz, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Lulav HaGazul. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. This implies that if the non-Jew despaired of recovering it, it is valid. However, Olas Shabbos, sec. 1, holds that no distinction should be made between a Jew and a non-Jew, and that even when stolen from a non-Jew, it remains invalid after despair. So too ruled Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1. Therefore, one who stole one of the species from a non-Jew has an invalid species and does not fulfill his obligation with it until he pays its owner. Mishna Berurah, Biur Halachah. Although he can acquire it through a physical change, nevertheless he cannot recite a blessing over it, as the Shulchan Aruch writes below.
