1. But if one stole, etc. For there Rabbi Yochanan said: only after despair, etc. It appears that he holds that despair effects acquisition. And in Bava Kamma 68b Rabbi Yochanan challenges Reish Lakish: and if you would think that despair effects acquisition, etc.; and likewise there, 114a, he holds that despair effects acquisition. Yet there, 67a, Ulla said: from where do we know that despair does not effect acquisition? And likewise in Gittin 54a Ulla holds that despair does not effect acquisition. One should not answer that everywhere Ulla holds that it does acquire, only with respect to consecration it is not fit for offering because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, for in Gittin there Rava inquired according to Ulla regarding shearings and offspring. Moreover, it is difficult, for there they challenge Ulla from the baraita, if one stole and consecrated, while in Merubah Rabbi Yochanan challenges Reish Lakish from that baraita in the opposite direction. And here one cannot answer as he answers there, that the owners consecrated it in the thief's house, for if so, what is the challenge to Ulla? Rather, everywhere Ulla and Rabbi Yochanan hold that despair by itself does not acquire; only with consecration they hold that it acquires, because it is despair together with a change of name: at first it was unconsecrated, and now it is consecrated, as stated in Bava Kamma 67a. And that case in Bava Kamma 114 is also with a change of name, for at first it was hides and now it is coverlets, as stated there 66b. And that which Abaye challenges Rabbah there, from 'his offering' and not stolen property, etc., and derives from it that despair does not acquire, is because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as written in Sukkah. That is, through the consecration he acquires it and fulfills the mitzvah with it; but if he had acquired it without the consecration, it would not be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Now it proves well that despair without consecration necessarily does not acquire. Ulla and Rava likewise prove this there, 67b, in this manner, and this is Ulla's reason in Gittin. Another proof is from that which they ask in Sukkah: let him acquire them by a physical change, and let him acquire them by a change of name; meaning that he bound them in a bundle and it is called a hosha'ana. This shows that if he had acquired it, it would not be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. But now that he acquires it only because of the change of name that made it into a lulav for the mitzvah, it is different. And they further said there: but if he stole wood, etc., he acquired it by a change of name; and likewise a beam of a sukkah, the Sages made an enactment, etc., that he acquires it and it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, because he acquired it without the mitzvah of sukkah. So too the Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, etc. And what is different from a stolen sukkah, which the Rabbis validate, etc.? If he stole wood and roofed with it, he acquired it by a physical change; if so, by lulav also, he holds that a lulav does not require binding, and if you would say, etc., as stated in the Gemara there. Furthermore, we invalidate one of an asherah and of an idolatrous city because its required measure is considered crushed, and we do not invalidate because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. And in the Yerushalmi, Sukkah there: Rabbi Chiyya taught, 'and you shall take for yourselves,' from your own, and not stolen. Rabbi Levi said: one who takes a stolen lulav, to what may this be compared? To one who honored the ruler with a tray, and it was found not to be his. They said: woe to this one, whose advocate has become his accuser. They disagree, for according to Rabbi Chiyya this applies specifically on the first day, and according to Rabbi Levi even on the other days, because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. In what case do they disagree? When he stole it smoothed; but if he stole it and smoothed it, he owes him only money, meaning that we read 'for yourselves' with respect to it, and it does not involve a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. This is what he wrote: such as if he stole, etc. See Tosafot 9a, s.v. hahu, and 30a, s.v. mishum, and s.v. ha.
Nevertheless, he should not, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma 94a. And Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov said so there only regarding a blessing, etc., even though he acquired it by change, as stated there.
And there is one who says, etc. He is speaking before despair, for after despair it is not prohibited even on the first day except because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as stated there: granted, etc., but this is not his; rather, etc. And there is no distinction between the first day and the other days, as stated there. Rather, it is before despair, where on the first day it is prohibited because it is not his, and the same applies to others, for how can he transfer to him property belonging to another? But on the other days, because of the concern of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, that does not apply to others. So the Ba'al Halachot Gedolot wrote: for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; but if he assigned it to another person, he acquires it and fulfills the obligation with it, whether on the first day of Yom Tov or on the second day of Yom Tov. However, there he is speaking after despair, as stated in the Gemara regarding despair and change of domain, in the case of those merchants. The Orchot Chayim holds that the same applies before despair, that there is no issue of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, for the Yerushalmi said that his advocate becomes his accuser, as above, which does not apply when he did not steal it. And regarding the difficulty he raised, why the Gemara needed to say despair and change of domain, there it is because of the first day, when we require 'for yourselves,' as written here: except for the first day. And see Rashi there, s.v. vekarka, who writes: and he holds that despair alone, etc.; and if so he acquired it, etc. This implies according to the first view, that despair does not acquire, there is no need for the reason of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, but rather because of 'for yourselves.' With this it is understandable why a stolen sukkah is excluded from 'for yourself.' Why is that needed? Let it be because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. For necessarily it is in a case where he did not acquire it, since otherwise it would be yours and valid, as with one who stole wood. Rather, because of the issue of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression it would be valid for others. This resolves Tosafot's question on 30a, s.v. mishum, where they ask from that which is taught below, etc.; their answer is forced, for the Gemara says explicitly: to exclude stolen property, and it is prohibited only on the first day, like borrowed property, whereas because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression it would be prohibited all the days. Moreover, 'for yourself' by sukkah cannot be interpreted that way, for a borrowed sukkah is valid there.
And because of this, etc. This is the Gemara there.
And there is no distinction, etc. That is to say, that which we say in chapter HaMakdish Sadehu of Arakhin, that land outside Eretz Yisrael is treated like movable property, applies specifically regarding dedications to sacred property, which is derived from an ancestral field, which applies only when the Jubilee applies and specifically in Eretz Yisrael, like an ancestral field. But regarding other matters there is no distinction, as written in Sefer HaTerumot, gate 7, section 2, and in Tur and Shulchan Aruch Choshen Mishpat 95:1. The same applies here, that it is not like movable property. See Bava Batra 44a.
A lulav, etc. Meaning, even though ideally there is a mitzvah to bind it, as written in the first chapter, it is valid if done by a gentile.
