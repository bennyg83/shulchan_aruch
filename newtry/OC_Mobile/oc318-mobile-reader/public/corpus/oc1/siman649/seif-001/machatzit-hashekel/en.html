The Magen Avraham wrote. The Sefer Mitzvos Gadol wrote that it is forbidden to cheapen, etc., meaning that one must give them more than their value. See siman 39 se'if 7. That is to say, there it is explained in the Shulchan Aruch that one does not buy tefillin, etc., for more than their value, so as not to accustom them to steal and rob them. The Magen Avraham was precise there in subparagraph 10 and wrote as follows: but one is obligated to buy them from them for a little more than their worth, so that they should not treat them contemptuously, end quote. So too in Yoreh De'ah siman 281 regarding buying a Torah scroll from a gentile; see there. The Rav Beis Yosef wrote there in Yoreh De'ah as follows: it implies there in the Gemara, Gittin 45, that one should not tell a gentile to give them to him too cheaply, lest he become angry and throw them to a place of destruction, as in the incident of that gentile woman merchant who brought a bag of tefillin to Abaye, etc.; see there. It is clear from this that regarding tefillin, mezuzah, and a Torah scroll there are two laws. First, one should not tell the gentile to give them too cheaply. Even if the Jew's intent is that if he will not give them cheaply he will add to the purchase price, nevertheless there is concern that perhaps immediately when the gentile hears the Jew's first words, where he asks for such a cheap price, he will become angry at his words and throw them away. This law does not apply to an esrog. First, this is specifically with tefillin, mezuzah, and a Torah scroll, which are fit for a gentile only to sell to a Jew; and also a Torah scroll, for the most part, a gentile has no need for it. There is concern that when he hears the Jew asking for such a cheap price, he will become angry and say: since the Jews do not want to give for them anything but a small amount, it is better for me to take revenge on the Jews and throw them away before him than to take a small amount, since they are not fit for himself, meaning for the gentile. This is unlike an esrog, which is fit for the gentile himself, to smell it or eat it or preserve it. Why should he throw it away in his anger and cause himself a loss? Furthermore, even if he throws the esrog away, what is it to us? Does it have holiness? Specifically tefillin, mezuzos, and a Torah scroll, which are holy items, one should not cause the gentile to disgrace them by throwing them away. This is unlike an esrog, even if they already recited a blessing over it; see the end of siman 21, siman 42, and the end of siman 154. All the more so where they have not yet recited a blessing over it and it has not been used for the mitzvah. But the second law, that one is obligated to give a little more than their worth so that they not treat them contemptuously, applies also to an esrog. For the intent of what was written, that they should not treat them contemptuously, is necessarily not that in his anger he will throw it away. For it is not human nature, if the buyer does not want to give more than the value for his merchandise, to become angry about this and throw it away. Rather, the intent is necessarily that it should not appear to the gentile that mitzvos are degraded and contemptible in the eyes of Jews. For the way of the world is that something a person greatly needs for his use, if he needs to buy it and can obtain it only by giving a little more than its value, he will not refrain from buying it, but will give a little more than its value because he needs it. If the gentile sees that regarding a mitzvah item a Jew does not conduct himself so, but is particular not to give even a little more than its value, it will appear that bodily needs are dearer to Jews than mitzvah needs. This is contempt and disgrace for the mitzvah. If so, this reason applies also to an esrog.
