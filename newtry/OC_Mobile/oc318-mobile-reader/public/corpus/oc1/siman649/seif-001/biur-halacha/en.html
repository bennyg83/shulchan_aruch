All four species are invalid, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, and see above in siman 11, Mishnah Berurah se'if katan 27; it is relevant to our matter as well.
If stolen or robbed. Whether from a Jew or from a non-Jew. Therefore, if someone stole one of the species from a non-Jew, it is invalid and he does not fulfill the obligation with it until he pays the money to its owner. And if he stole or robbed and eventually paid and appeased the owner of the garden, whether he fulfilled retroactively, see siman 658 in Sha'arei Teshuvah se'if katan 3 and Mishnah Berurah se'if katan 15.
Such as if he stole a lulav and smoothed it, etc. This law is according to all opinions, that is, even according to the Mechaber, who rules plainly at the end of the se'if that for the thief or robber himself the lulav is invalid even on the other days, when we do not require 'for yourselves,' and its invalidity is because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Here it is different. There, since the object now, at the time he fulfills the obligation with it, is not his but belongs to the robbed party, it is called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, meaning that at the time of performing the mitzvah he has a stolen object in his hand; for if they would sue him in court, he would be obligated to return that very object. This is not so here, where he acquired it through a physical change and is not obligated to return the object but only ordinary money. It follows that at present the object does not belong to the robbed party but to him, and what remains against him is only regarding the earlier time when he stole it. Therefore it is not called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Only regarding a blessing, which contains mention of the Divine Name, are we more stringent, since originally the object came into his hand through theft.
Nevertheless, he should not bless. If he transgressed and took it, he does not need to take another lulav again, and certainly he should not bless over it, for since according to the law he fulfilled the obligation with the first taking, the later blessing would be a blessing in vain. [Bikkurei Yaakov]
But for others it is valid. An heir is not included in this, for inheritance is not called a change of domain, as explained in Choshen Mishpat. [Peri Megadim]
That he should not cut, etc. See below in Biur Halacha what we wrote in the name of the poskim regarding our times. Now regarding aravot, the custom in all places is that the attendants themselves go before Sukkot and cut many aravot for the needs of the community. It is common that they do not even take permission for this from the owner of the field. I am not judging here regarding the attendants themselves, but regarding the community: how do they fulfill the obligation with this on the first day, when we require 'for yourselves'? One should not be lenient on the ground that there was despair in the hands of the attendants and change of domain in the hands of the buyer, for that applies only to the case of the merchants brought in the Gemara. There the land had been stolen long before, and certainly the owners had already despaired; only because land is not subject to theft, the despair did not take effect until now, when they cut it and it became detached. But in our matter, where the owner of the field did not know at all at the time of the cutting, despair does not apply. Even if he will despair later when he finds out, that is despair without knowledge, and it is not despair. Only change of domain remains, and that alone does not acquire. Therefore, in my humble opinion, one should be lenient only when he takes permission from the owner of the field, or from the watchman there, or when it is known that this place is ownerless for all.
Himself. According to this, a Jew should not cut it himself even from his own field if this field was purchased from a non-Jew; rather, he should leave it for a non-Jew to cut and receive it from him. However, the Birkei Yosef already brought in the name of Sefer HaTerumot, the Semag, and the Radbaz that in our times we do not say that non-Jews are robbers of land. The Taz in siman 664 also concluded this way; see there. Therefore, among us, where non-Jews are not presumed to steal land from Jews, and all lands are held by their owners under the law of the kingdom, it is possible to be lenient in this, that a Jew may cut even from the field of a non-Jew with his permission. [Bikkurei Yaakov] Now all this was written by the Bikkurei Yaakov in his time, but in our time, according to the common disturbances, one must consider this very carefully, and it depends on the place.
Rather, a non-Jew should cut it, etc. The Peri Megadim wrote: those who buy from the sharecropper without the knowledge of the owner of the garden, one can say that the owner knows and waives it, and there is no theft in them. The Bikkurei Yaakov strongly questions this, for it is known that ordinarily he does not waive it, since lulavim and hadasim are significant items in our countries and are found only in the gardens of nobles. Since so, even though he did not know that it was stolen from him, nevertheless this is despair without knowledge, and the halachah follows Abaye; and change of domain alone does not acquire. Therefore one must be careful not to buy from sharecroppers without the knowledge of the owners of the gardens, for it is not called 'for yourselves' on the first day, and on the other days as well it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, for theft from a non-Jew is prohibited. End quote.
Whether Eretz Yisrael, etc. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of the responsum of Rabbi Betzalel that with an esrog in Eretz Yisrael, if he cut it himself, aside from the prohibition stated here, there is another prohibition, for it became finalized in the hand of a Jew and is tevel. Regarding tevel there is a dispute among the poskim whether one fulfills the obligation with it; see the Ran. See the Peri Megadim, from whose words it appears that regarding theft, even if the Jew has not yet acquired it from the non-Jew, nevertheless the Jew should not cut it in order to fulfill the obligation with it, for he is the one stealing. But regarding finalization in the hand of a Jew, the prohibition applies only if the Jew had already acquired it from the non-Jew with money at the time he cuts it.
