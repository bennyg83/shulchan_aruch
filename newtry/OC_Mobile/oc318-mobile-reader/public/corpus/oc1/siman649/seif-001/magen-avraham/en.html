The Semag wrote: it is prohibited to cheapen an esrog and other mitzvah items that non-Jews sell. End quote. That is, one must give them more than their value. See siman 39:7. And he smoothed it, it is valid. Even on the first day. So it appears from Tosafot, s.v. ha kanya, that by despair, 'for yourselves' is read as meaning from your own. This is stated explicitly in Milchamot. See Choshen Mishpat siman 353 regarding when it is acquired, and so too above, siman 11:6.
He should not bless over it. This requires investigation, for if so, what does the Gemara ask regarding the merchants: let him acquire it by a change of name? In any case, he should not be permitted to bless over it. But when the non-Jew cuts it, he is permitted to bless, since he did not steal it. Perhaps there, change of name together with despair is stronger. This requires investigation. See the Rashba.
Except for the first day. One must say that he is speaking before despair, for after despair it is acquired by despair and change of domain, and we read 'yours' with respect to it. So wrote Milchamot in the name of Ba'al Halachot Gedolot and Ba'al Halachot Pesukot, and so it appears from Tosafot. Rather, he is speaking before despair. Even so, for others it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. So it appears from Rashi on daf 30, who wrote that even if despair acquires, it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and even so, for others it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. But from Milchamot it implies that before despair, even for others it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since it has not yet been acquired. So it also appears from the Gemara regarding the merchants, where it says that there should be despair and change of domain; see there. Therefore one should not be lenient.
That he should not cut. Even with the permission of a non-Jew. And with an esrog in Eretz Yisrael, if he cut it himself, it has been finalized in the hand of a Jew and is tevel. [Rabbi Betzalel, 2]
And ordinary non-Jews. It appears to me that after the fact it is valid and one blesses over them, for out of doubt we are not concerned that it was stolen. And one who stole through conquest in war acquires it, as stated in Gittin daf 38.
A non-Jew should cut it. The same applies when a Jew cuts it and gives it to his fellow: it is permitted, since it is after despair.
Or outside Eretz Yisrael. Meaning, even though there the lands were stolen from non-Jews, nevertheless it is prohibited, as written in siman 637:3.
A non-Jew made it, it is valid. This implies that ab initio a non-Jew should not bind it, as written in siman 14 regarding women, and so wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 1 of Hilchot Tzitzit, in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. This requires investigation, for we rule that a lulav does not require binding, and if so the binding is not part of the mitzvah. See Sukkah 11b. One must say that since it is an adornment of the mitzvah, it is considered like the mitzvah itself. See siman 39:2.
