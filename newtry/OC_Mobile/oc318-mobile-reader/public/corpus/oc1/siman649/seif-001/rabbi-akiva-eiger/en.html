Magen Avraham subparagraph 3 And nevertheless, for others it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. This is astonishing to me. Since there, if despair acquires, it came into his hand permissibly, and therefore it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Although Rashi holds that where one made an acquisition before the mitzvah, it is also called a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, that is for the robber himself. But regarding another person, one may say there is a distinction: since it came to him permissibly, it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. I thought to say that the intent of the Magen Avraham is as follows: he holds that what Rashi wrote, "alternatively, if despair acquires, it would be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression," does not mean that one may say Rav Huna holds despair acquires, and nevertheless he required that the gentile cut it because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. For if so, why did he mention "and change of domain is in your hands"? The acquisition was completed in the gentile's hand by the law of despair. Rather, perforce, Rav Huna holds that despair does not acquire. But Rashi explained that even if the law were that despair acquires, nevertheless it would be necessary for the gentile to cut it because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. According to this, it is difficult: Rav Huna necessarily does not hold of mitzvah that comes through a transgression, for even where a gentile cut it, since he holds despair does not acquire and the gentile acquires through despair and change of domain, if so it came to their hands through prohibition, and it should be a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Rather, perforce, for another person, even though it came into his hand through prohibition, it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. If so, the same would apply before despair.
