(There, se'if 3) Likewise, one from an idolatrous city and one from an asherah of a Jew is invalid. Note: we read in the Mishnah in tractate Parah, chapter 11, Mishnah 7: any hyssop that has an adjectival name, etc., and one of impure terumah is invalid, and one of pure terumah should not be taken, etc. The Rash and the Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura explained that one of impure terumah is invalid because the hyssop itself is impure. The Rash added that the same applies to unconsecrated hyssop, only it mentioned terumah because of the latter clause. This is very astonishing to me. Why did they not explain the reason that hyssop requires a measure, as is written later in that chapter, and if so one of impure terumah is invalid because its measure is considered pulverized? This would be fine, for it specifically mentioned terumah because unconsecrated hyssop does not require burning, and therefore the rule of being considered pulverized would not apply and it would be valid. This requires analysis: why did they move away from this reason? If we say that where it is less than the measure it does not require burning by Torah law, this would be fine, since it is speaking there of less than the measure, as the Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura wrote. But if so, we cannot sustain the sugya of Sukkah with this, and it requires analysis. Examine it.
