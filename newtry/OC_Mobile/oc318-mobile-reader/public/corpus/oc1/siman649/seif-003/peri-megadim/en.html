If so, a gentile's asherah should also be invalid on the first day of Yom Tov because it is not his, etc. Incidentally I will mention what was written in Kapos Temarim, 31b, on Rashi's explanation, of blessed memory, that a lulav of a gentile's offering has no nullification, as the Rambam, of blessed memory, wrote in chapter 8, halachah 9, of Hilchos Avodah Zarah, and Yoreh De'ah 139; only idolatry itself of a gentile has nullification; see there. See Yoreh De'ah, in the Turei Zahav and Shach, paragraph 34, even though it is not like an inside offering. And in se'if 13 regarding Chanukah lights it is invalid; one can say that it is considered pulverized, and Chanukah lights require a measure. An offering resembling an inside offering may also perhaps be considered with lamps, like the menorah. See siman 154 se'if 11, where the Rav ruled that when their attendant extinguished them, they are permitted for ordinary use; see there. It is evident that he does not consider it an offering except specifically an animal, sacrifices of the dead, etc. Know further: "for yourselves" means from your own. If one found a hefker lulav and lifted it but did not intend to acquire it, he does not fulfill with it. So wrote the book Kapos Temarim there, and this is forced, for a gentile's is specifically from the second day onward. A borrowed one, with which one does not fulfill, is incidental; because one is liable for accidents. The same applies to stolen. It is not comparable to chametz on Pesach, where if one is liable for responsibility we call it yours. The exclusion of "for yourselves" can be established as referring to borrowed without responsibility, or hefker, and the like. And according to the one who says a cause is not like money, regarding chametz on Pesach it is written "it shall not be found," which is fine; see Pesachim 6a. See Magen Avraham 586, paragraph 5, in the name of the Bach and Magen Avraham, that it is comparable to chametz on Pesach. This requires analysis, for there it is written "it shall not be found," since when it comes, it returns in its original form; all the more so according to the Sages who disagree with Rabbi Shimon in Bava Kamma 71; see there. From where do we know with other matters that because of responsibility it is considered his? See siman 586 se'if 4: an idolatrous offering has no nullification; and the Levush here regarding a gentile's lulav that was used for idolatry; see there. Regarding what is written about a grafted esrog, see Magen Avraham 648, paragraph 23; see there. According to the Turei Zahav, all seven days one takes it without a blessing if there is no valid esrog. According to the Levush, one might say that a grafted hadas is valid, because no transgression was performed, for it appears that the myrtle is a barren tree, even though grape-like growths grow on it. See the laws of blessings on this, and Yoreh De'ah 295 in the Shach, paragraph 3, and in se'if 6 in the gloss: a barren tree with a barren tree may be grafted. According to the Magen Avraham it is forbidden, for it is not a hadas. What the Levush wrote, that a Noachide may be warned regarding grafting trees, see Yoreh De'ah 296 in the Shach, paragraph 3, that we maintain like the Sages, that it is permitted for him. It is not comparable to a gentile cooking meat with milk, Chullin 115, for there it is nevertheless an abomination, but it is called a case where no transgression was performed. If so, the reason must be that it is not an esrog, and the same applies to a hadas. One can say that an esrog is like rejected, since it does not have the form of an esrog, unlike a hadas if it truly resembles it. However, in Responsa Chacham Tzvi, of blessed memory, siman 141, and also Beis Yaakov, everyone agrees that a grafted hadas is invalid; see there. If so, hadasim in these lands, if it is known that they are grafted, are invalid, and in a doubt the matter requires analysis; see Yoreh De'ah regarding a fixed doubt in this matter. Those who take from a tenant farmer without the knowledge of the owner of the garden, one can say that he knows and waives it, and there is no concern of robbery.
