Of an idolatrous city. Because it stands to be burned, as it is written: 'and all its spoil you shall gather,' etc. A lulav requires a measure of four handbreadths, as below in siman 650, and since this stands to be burned, its measure is not intact, for it is considered as though burned. Therefore it is invalid even for the second day.
And of an asherah. This is a tree that is worshiped. If he planted it and afterward worshiped it, then the fruits that grew before the worship are permitted for ordinary use but prohibited ab initio for use before Heaven, because they are repulsive; and the fruits that grew after it was worshiped are prohibited even to an ordinary person for benefit, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 145, and it is invalid even on the second day. See below at the end of se'if 4.
One belonging to an Israelite is invalid. Since nullification by a non-Jew is not effective to permit it for benefit, it stands to be burned; and anything that stands to be burned is considered as though burned, and its measure is not intact, as above.
Ab initio one should not take. Even if the non-Jew nullified it, for although it is permitted for an ordinary person, it is prohibited for use before Heaven because it is repulsive.
And if he took it, he fulfilled the obligation. He does not need to return and take another, even if the non-Jew did not nullify it and it is still prohibited for eating and benefit. Nevertheless, since nullification can be effective to permit it for benefit, it does not stand to be burned, for it is possible that the non-Jew will nullify it. And taking it in order to fulfill the obligation is not considered benefit, for mitzvot were not given for benefit, as above.
For we do not require 'for yourselves.' But on the first day, when we require 'for yourselves,' he does not fulfill the obligation with it even after the fact so long as the non-Jew did not nullify it, for it is prohibited for benefit and is not called 'for yourselves.'
And see above, siman 586. Meaning, there the Rema brought that some are stringent even with one belonging to a non-Jew, not to fulfill the obligation even after the fact before it was nullified. Regarding the practical law, see above in siman 586, Mishnah Berurah se'if katan 18.
