Section 3. Belonging to an Israelite. As stated there regarding the asherah of Moshe, and the same applies to one belonging to an Israelite, for it has no nullification, as stated there in Avodah Zarah 53a: Rabbi Shimon ben Menasya, etc.
But one belonging to, etc. As stated there in Sukkah, and likewise regarding shofar in Rosh Hashanah and a sandal in Yevamot. But in Chullin they said that he did not fulfill the obligation, and there the case is one belonging to an Israelite.
From the first day, etc. As he writes below: and specifically, etc.
And specifically, etc.; but, etc. For then it has no nullification, as stated in Avodah Zarah 42a, as a decree lest, etc. This is what he wrote: and specifically, etc.
He fulfills after the fact. But not ab initio, for they inquired in Avodah Zarah 47a: one who bows to a palm tree, etc.; and see Tosafot there, s.v. mi ma'is, etc.: but here, etc. Meaning, there it is like after nullification, for according to the Rabbis it is permitted even without nullification. So too wrote the Rashba, and so it is in Rabbeinu Yerucham, who learned it from the above. And the inquiry there regarding an asherah that he nullified must also be explained this way, that the inquiry is whether ab initio it is permitted; for after the fact, even if he did not nullify it, it is permitted in a place where nullification is effective. Tosafot in Sukkah answered differently.
And see above, siman 586. For there he wrote: and some are stringent, etc. This is the opinion of Rabbeinu Tam in Tosafot in Sukkah there, that the case where we say he did not fulfill the obligation is before nullification, even if it belongs to a gentile; and the case where we say he fulfilled is after nullification, and specifically after the fact. And the inquiry in Avodah Zarah there regarding an asherah that he nullified concerns after the fact, and where he nullified it on Yom Tov, for then rejection applies, as stated there regarding a date that grew on it on Yom Tov: what is the law? And in doubt we rule stringently. But if he nullified it before Yom Tov, he fulfilled after the fact.
