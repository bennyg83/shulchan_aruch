They are invalid if stolen, etc. It appears to me that even if one stole or robbed it from a non-Jew, and the non-Jew did not despair of recovering it, since he can recover it through the courts, we do not call it ‘yours,’ and it is invalid. Riaz, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Lulav HaGazul. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. This implies that if the non-Jew despaired of recovering it, it is valid. However, Olas Shabbos, sec. 1, holds that no distinction should be made between a Jew and a non-Jew, and that even when stolen from a non-Jew, it remains invalid after despair. So too ruled Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1. Therefore, one who stole one of the species from a non-Jew has an invalid species and does not fulfill his obligation with it until he pays its owner. Mishna Berurah, Biur Halachah. Although he can acquire it through a physical change, nevertheless he cannot recite a blessing over it, as the Shulchan Aruch writes below.
