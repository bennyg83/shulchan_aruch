All invalid ones. Due to a blemish; but those that are not of their species, and a stolen one, are invalid even in pressing circumstances, like a quince and a pomegranate, for we decree lest people come to err with them. [Darkei Moshe; Minhagim; Bei Bach] See what I wrote at the end of this siman.
And we do not bless. Radbaz, part 1, 111, wrote that there are places that are accustomed to bless.
A dry lulav. But not a dry esrog, and so it appears from the language of Mordechai. [Darkei Moshe] But in the Mishnah Berurah, siman 17, he wrote that the Tur, the Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Kol Bo hold that all four dry species are valid in pressing circumstances, and the same applies to other invalidations. The fact that the Mordechai did not write the law regarding a dry esrog is because throughout the year new esrogim are common. And the reason one blesses over lulavim and hadasim even when there are other moist ones is because the moist ones are not fully moist, only having a small amount of moisture; if so, the moist and dry ones are almost similar to one another. But if there are fully moist ones, even if the dry ones are not completely dry, one should not bless. After the fact, if one blessed over a dry one, there is doubt whether he fulfilled the obligation; therefore he should return and take a moist one without a blessing. But if he blessed over completely dry ones while fully moist ones were available, it appears there that even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation. End quote. His words are built on the later poskim; therefore one should not be lenient.
For a deficient one is valid. If enough of it remains for the required measure with which to fulfill the obligation. [Tur] The Ran wrote: specifically when most of it remains and its name is still upon it, etc. But in the rulings of Mahari, siman 92, it implies that even if a minority remains it is sufficient. End quote of Darkei Moshe. And this is the language of Mahari: even though a deficient one is valid in pressing circumstances, that is when it became deficient on its own, but it is not permitted to cut the esrog and bless over that piece. Furthermore, that which a deficient one is valid means that he cuts it for eating and blesses over the remainder, which is the remnant of a mitzvah, as we find regarding tzitzit. But to cut from the esrog and bless over the piece is prohibited, for it is not called an esrog but a piece of esrog. End quote. This implies that even if most of it does not remain it is valid, provided only that an egg-bulk remains. This is the view of the Tur and Rema.
