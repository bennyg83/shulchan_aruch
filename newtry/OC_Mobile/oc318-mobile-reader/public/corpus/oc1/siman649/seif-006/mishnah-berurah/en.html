(52) "In a pressing situation," etc. It is not considered a pressing situation unless there are no 4 valid species anywhere in the city. But if one's fellow has valid species, even though he himself cannot otherwise obtain them, this is not considered a pressing situation. He must make the effort to recite the blessing over his fellow's set if it is lent to him, and if he cannot perform the naanuim with his fellow's set, he should perform the naanuim with his own. [Later authorities] (53) "All invalid species," etc. Meaning species invalid because of a defect. But items invalid because they are not of the required species, such as hadas shoteh or tzafzefah, or a grafted species, are invalid even in a pressing situation, because people may come to err and use them regularly. The same applies to stolen species; they are invalid even in a pressing situation. If there are no valid 4 species anywhere in the city, he should take whatever 2 or 3 species are available and perform all the naanuim, and the shaliach tzibbur should do likewise, but without a blessing. One may not take a different species in place of a missing one, lest people err in future years. With only 3 of the proper species, however, there is no such concern, because everyone knows that 4 species are required. (54) "They are taken and no blessing is recited." They are taken even on the first day, so that the mitzvah of lulav not be forgotten from Israel. No blessing is recited because the act is only commemorative. (55) "A dry lulav." Rambam's view is that even in a pressing situation leniency applies only to a dry lulav, but not to the other species when they are dry, as explained below. He also holds that one should be lenient only regarding dryness, not regarding other invalidating defects, even in a pressing situation. (56) "Even when others have moist ones." The reason is that this concerns countries where fully fresh lulavim are not available, and the dry lulavim are not completely dry either, meaning they have not reached the point where they crumble under the fingernail. The same applies to hadasim that have not reached the stage where their appearance turns white. Thus the "fresh" and "dry" examples are nearly alike, and therefore the custom is to recite the blessing over one's own. (57) "Even a dry hadas." Because fully fresh hadasim are likewise not readily available. Aravot, however, are commonly available fresh. Regarding an etrog, according to this opinion they did not wish to be lenient, because Scripture explicitly calls it "the fruit of a beautiful tree." (58) "And one may rely upon them in a pressing situation." The later authorities agree in practice that in a pressing situation we may rely upon those authorities who hold that one may recite a blessing over all 4 species when they are dry. There are 4 distinctions. If no fresh species are available in the city and only dry ones are available, all 4 species are taken with a blessing. If he has completely dry species while others have completely fresh species, the blessing is recited only over the fresh ones, and even after the fact he did not fulfill the obligation by blessing over the fully dry ones. In countries where fully fresh lulavim and hadasim are unavailable, and the dry ones are not completely dry as explained above, one may recite the blessing over the dry ones he has even initially. Where others possess fully fresh species, however, initially he should not recite the blessing over his own dry species even if they are not fully dry. If he already did so, he should take the fresh species belonging to others again without another blessing. (59) "He may take," etc. See Magen Avraham, who asks what Rama adds to the Mechaber's ruling above in seif 6. Bikurei Yaakov explains that the Mechaber wrote that in a pressing situation, where no valid set is currently available, one should take the invalid set without a blessing. We still would not know the law if a valid set will become available later, but at present is not in his possession, for example where another person is presently reciting Hallel with his valid lulav and has promised to lend it to him afterward. Could he meanwhile take his own invalid lulav during Hallel, or might that create a doubtful blessing later when he takes the valid set? Rama therefore teaches that he may initially take the invalid set without a blessing, because it is certainly invalid, and afterward he may recite the blessing over the valid set when it becomes available.
