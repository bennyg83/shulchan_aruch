An esrog grafted from a lemon and an esrog is invalid. There are three signs: first, the grafted one is smooth, while an esrog has small bumps over its whole body and they protrude. Second, on the grafted one the stem protrudes, while the stem of an esrog is sunken. Third, the inside of the grafted one, meaning the pulp, is wide and its juice is abundant, and the middle peel is narrow; with an esrog it is the reverse, for the peel is wide and the inside is narrow and is almost dry. These signs are clear. Responsa Rema, siman 126. In Olas Shabbos he wrote another sign: in an esrog the seed stands upright along the length of the esrog, while in a grafted one the seed lies across the width of the esrog; see there.
