The youths. This refers to the attendants of the idolaters.
It is permitted to take from there, etc. According to what is explained in Yoreh De'ah 143:3 and in the Shach and Atzei Zahav there, it is permitted to take from them only when it is free and not for payment, even if the priests take the money for themselves and not for idolatry. Perhaps there it is speaking where at the outset they donated the garden for idolatry and the fruits belong to its attendants; therefore it is prohibited to benefit its attendants. Here, however, it speaks where the garden was never donated for idolatry. And according to the Rema there in the gloss, in any case one may be lenient if the garden does not stand in the courtyard of the idolatry and the money goes only to the priests and not for the needs of idolatry; see there.

So long as they do not worship the tree. But once they began to worship it, the lulavim and fruits that were then on the tree are prohibited ab initio for use before Heaven, because they are repulsive; and the growths that emerged afterward are prohibited even to an ordinary person, as explained in Yoreh De'ah 145:1. Some say that if the non-Jew afterward cut it and gave it to a Jew, it is permitted because of nullification, and even for use before Heaven it is permitted and is not called repulsive, because it underwent a great change, for at the time of worship the esrog and lulavim did not exist and grew only afterward.
