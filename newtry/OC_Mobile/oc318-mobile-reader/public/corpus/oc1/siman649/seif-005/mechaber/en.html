All those species that we said are invalid because of the defects explained above, or because they were robbed or stolen, are invalid only on the first day of Yom Tov; on the other days, all are valid. {Rama: Some authorities invalidate a stolen lulav for all 7 days, and this is the custom. A borrowed lulav, however, fulfills the obligation. [Tur; Maggid Mishneh in the name of the authorities] On the other days it is permitted to take another person's lulav without his knowledge, because a person is pleased that a mitzvah be performed with his property, and it is regarded as borrowed. [Terumat HaDeshen, siman 100, and his rulings, siman 159] One should nevertheless return it to the place from which he took it.} Regarding defects arising from the species itself, the rules on the remaining days follow the distinctions stated by the authorities. In a place where two days of Yom Tov are observed, one may use on the second day species that are invalid only because of the first-day requirement, but should not recite a blessing over them when a valid set is available. If another person has a valid lulav and etrog, he should take the valid set with permission and recite the blessing over it.
