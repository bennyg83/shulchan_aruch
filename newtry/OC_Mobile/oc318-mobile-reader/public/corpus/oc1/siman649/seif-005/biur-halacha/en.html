Because of robbery and theft, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote: and it is difficult, for in se'if 1 he said, etc.; and the Magen Avraham answered, etc. The Mateh Yehudah wrote: this is not correct in my humble opinion, for in se'if 1 he mentioned the language of invalidity, and the implication of invalidity is even after the fact. It seems more to me that this mention of robbery and theft was taken by the Beit Yosef merely as a common expression, and he stated it incidentally, adopting the wording of the Rambam of blessed memory as it stands. Its main point is the distinction he makes between invalidations because of blemishes and invalidations for other matters, namely because of idolatry or because they are not of their species. According to this, what the Rema added, that some invalidate a stolen one, etc., is only with respect to the Rambam's view and not with respect to the Beit Yosef, for he too holds this way in practice, as he ruled in se'if 1. This is also implied by the Gra: the Mechaber copied only the Rambam's language to teach regarding all invalidations, whether because of beauty or because of deficiency, that they apply only on the first day. But regarding theft, he ruled plainly at the beginning of the siman stringently, for so the Gra wrote at the end of s.v. ve-chazazit; see there.
But if it was dry or spotted, etc., even if he cut, etc. According to what is explained above in siman 648:12 in the Mishnah Berurah there, that according to all opinions dryness in one place does not invalidate when it is not in the majority, it must be that here he is speaking where most of it was dry, or where it was in two or three places, or on its nose, and he holds like the 'some say' there; otherwise it would be valid even without cutting, and even on the first day.
Invalidations of the first day are taken on the second, but as for blessing, etc. The same applies to a borrowed one on the second day: one should not bless over it.
