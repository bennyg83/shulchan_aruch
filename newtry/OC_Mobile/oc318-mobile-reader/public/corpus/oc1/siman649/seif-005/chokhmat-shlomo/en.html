(There, se'if 5 in the gloss) But if it was dry or spotted, it is invalid all seven days, etc. Note: so too the Magen Avraham brought in siman 648. See a support for this in the Talmud and authorities regarding one who betroths on condition that she has no blemishes: even if she went to a sage and he healed her, she is not betrothed, for she is repulsive in his eyes because he remembers what she was like initially. So too here. In this, the view of the Turei Zahav appears reasonable regarding mice eating from it; see there here in subparagraph 8. The difficulty of the Turei Zahav appears possible to answer. Granted, dry and spotted are repulsive in the body itself, and since it is recognizable from its body, even after removing it he remembers what it was originally. But mouse-eating from its body is not recognizable; perhaps it was done by people. Only the person knows it was done by mice. In this case it is repulsive only while it is still so, but if it was removed, it is permitted, since the repulsiveness is not from its body. This is fine with Hashem's help. I saw in the Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos subparagraph 8, in the name of the Eliyah Rabbah, that he asked from oil in which a mouse was found, which is repulsive for a mitzvah. There is no difficulty at all, for two reasons. First, there its body did not change, whereas here it changed, for a piece from which the mice ate was cut off. Furthermore, there the intent is that before Him, may He be blessed, it is repulsive, and before Heaven it is revealed that mice drank from it. But with lulav, where the intent of beauty is that it be beautiful in people's eyes, and in the eyes of a person it is like a finger pointing to the Creator to praise Him, anything whose repulsiveness is not from its body is no longer recognizable after removal, so it is not repulsive. We are not speaking of the disqualification of beauty that actually invalidates and forbids reciting a blessing over it; there we say that in this case it is valid. Rather, the concern that it is repulsive for the mitzvah is only an initial caution where another is possible, or where it is possible not to light it at all. But where he must fulfill the mitzvah, which is an obligation upon him, and there is no other, certainly one should not be particular about what is revealed before Heaven as repulsive. Examine it, and this is fine with Hashem's help.
