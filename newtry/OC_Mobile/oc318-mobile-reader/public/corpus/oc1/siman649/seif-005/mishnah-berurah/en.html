All these, etc. This is the language of the Rambam, and the Mechaber rules plainly in practice like him. The Rema's view in this will be explained below.
That are invalid because of blemishes, etc. Know that some of them are invalid because they are not beautiful, and some because they are included in deficiency. The Rambam's view is to validate all of them from the first day onward, and the view of the other poskim will be explained below. As for what he wrote, because of robbery and theft on the first day of Yom Tov, etc., his reason is that since the second day of Yom Tov is rabbinic, a stolen item is permitted even though it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. It is difficult, for in se'if 1 he said that stolen and robbed items are invalid only for the thief and robber himself, and it is proven there that this is even on the second day of Yom Tov. The Magen Avraham answered that there it refers only to ab initio, whereas here he is speaking of the essential law, that after the fact one fulfills on the second day of Yom Tov even with a stolen item. But on the first day of Yom Tov, according to all opinions one does not, because we require 'for yourselves.' See Biur Halacha.
Everything is valid. Nevertheless, regarding a blessing on a stolen or robbed item, even according to the Rambam one should not bless, for 'the robber who blesses has blasphemed Hashem.'
And some invalidate, etc. Their reason is that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, and he does not fulfill the obligation with it.
On the other days. For on the first day, the fact that he is pleased, etc., does not help, since we require 'for yourselves,' that it actually be his, and it is no better than borrowed. See below, siman 658, how to conduct oneself on the first day.
For he is pleased, etc. Since there is no concern of damage when he takes it and waves it, this is therefore not comparable to one who studies from his fellow's book without his knowledge, which is prohibited because he worries that he may damage it. The Peri Megadim wrote that specifically one time it is permitted to take it and wave it according to halachah, but for all seven days perhaps he is particular and it is prohibited. For this reason it appears to me that the same applies that he should not himself give it to his fellow, for perhaps the owner is particular about this. See above in siman 14, where he wrote that in every case it is proper to be careful when the owner is present to ask him, because we do not rely on a presumption in a place where it can be easily clarified; and certainly if he knows that the owner is particular, it is prohibited by law to take it without his knowledge. And even when the owner is not present, this is specifically to take it in that place; but to remove it from his house to the synagogue, or the reverse, is prohibited. [Acharonim]
And a deficient one is valid on the other days. According to all opinions, for the first day is biblical in the provinces and the other days are rabbinic as a remembrance of the Temple. Therefore the essence of taking, such as the four species in the lulav and taking each one separately, and not that one take one for all of them, was enacted on the other days as well in a biblical-like manner. But regarding deficiency they did not enact so, and likewise regarding borrowed. Matters whose reason is because it is not beautiful are disputed among the poskim: the view of the Rambam and those who support him is to validate them like deficiency, and the view of the Rosh and those who support him is to be stringent in this, for we require beauty all seven days because of beautification of the mitzvah. The Rema below rules plainly like them in this.
And its pitom was removed. See above, siman 648:7. See the Magen Avraham, who brought that some poskim hold that removal of the pitom is invalid because of beauty, and he also inclines to their view. Therefore one should not be lenient with a removed pitom all seven days. Nevertheless, if it is impossible to find another, one may rely on the lenient opinions, for in any event most poskim hold like the Rambam, that even those which are invalid because of beauty are valid on the other days. [Eliyah Rabbah] It is implied from his words that one may also bless; some question this regarding the blessing. However, if another doubt joins this, whether its pitom was removed, see above in siman 648:7 in the Mishnah Berurah, he certainly may bless on the other days.
Until he removes, etc. Therefore we are lenient here, for by law it is included in deficiency, and a deficient one is valid on the other days; only ab initio are we stringent here because it is repulsive. Therefore, when he removes the repulsive place it is valid. But with a spotted or dry one, which by law is invalid because of beauty, repair is not effective, because it came through invalidity.
Since it came through invalidity. See the Gra's commentary, where he inclines to say that according to what the Mechaber ruled above in siman 648:14, that if a boil-like discoloration arose on it or it was spotted and he peeled it, if it returns to the appearance of an esrog it is valid, it is proven that he disagrees with this. The Ma'amar Mordechai also inclines this way in his conclusion; see there. Therefore the Ma'amar Mordechai inclines that one may discuss being lenient if he cut off the dried or spotted area on the other days, since the Mechaber ruled plainly like the Rambam and those who support him, that all invalidations because of blemishes, which includes whether the invalidation is because of deficiency or because of beauty, are valid on the second day of Yom Tov. This includes even dry or spotted, whose reason is because of beauty. Even without cutting, in any case we should not be stringent when he cut it. In pressing circumstances one may certainly rely on this. See what we wrote below regarding boil-like discoloration.
To make a condition regarding the esrog. That is, on the eve of Yom Tov, before twilight.
That all of it should be for the first day. For he cannot make a condition that he is not separating himself from it and will be able to benefit from it, since on the first day all of it was set aside, because a deficient esrog is invalid on it. Therefore he must make a condition that he is not separating himself from it from twilight of the second night onward.
And then it is permitted ab initio. Meaning, if he did not make a condition, he is not permitted to eat from it even during all the days of the festival, since it was set aside at twilight of the first day. If he designated it initially for the second day, the view of the Magen Avraham in the name of the Rosh is that it is permitted to eat from it even without a condition, provided that he leaves the measure fit for an esrog, for we say it was set aside only as to the measure fit for an esrog and no more. Some say that even if he designated it for the second day, we say the entire esrog was set aside and it is prohibited to eat from it unless he made a condition at the outset. The Gra agreed with this in his commentary.
To eat from it, etc. And to fulfill with it, etc. Meaning, to eat on the second day and fulfill the obligation with it on the third day, for it is prohibited to eat before taking, as below in siman 652.
The remainder. The Ran wrote: specifically where most of it remains, so that its name is upon it; otherwise it is not called an esrog, and the Merciful One said esrog, not half an esrog. But from the rulings of Mahari it implies that even if only a minority of it remains, it is sufficient on the second day. [Darkei Moshe] Provided that an egg-bulk remains, as explained above at the end of siman 648. See the Bach.
Which is because of idolatry. Such as an asherah belonging to an Israelite, as above in se'if 3; see the reason there in the Mishnah Berurah.
That that esrog is prohibited for eating. This implies that even though it is permitted for benefit, such as tevel, since it is prohibited for eating it is invalid all seven days. All the more so an esrog of orlah or impure terumah, which stand to be burned, are invalid all seven days. Orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is valid even on the first day. An esrog of demai is valid. And an esrog that became prohibited because it absorbed a prohibited substance, such as where part of it fell into boiling milk, one should be stringent not to fulfill the obligation with it on the first day; but on the other days one may be lenient, since its prohibition does not come from itself.
That are not of their species. Such as a wild hadas in siman 646, a tzaftzafah in siman 647, and a grafted esrog.
They lack the required measure. For even though it was explained above that a deficient one is valid on the other days, nevertheless in each one its required measure must remain.
And boil-like discoloration invalidates, etc. This is only according to those who are stringent about beauty all seven days. But according to the Mechaber, who copied the language of the Rambam, even regarding beauty it invalidates only on the first day, and he should have written it in the language of 'some say,' except that this is his manner in several places. Likewise, what he wrote in the gloss above, that a dry or spotted one is invalid all seven days, also follows his own approach, which is stringent about beauty all seven days. But according to the Mechaber, who copied the Rambam's language, even dry and spotted are invalid only on the first day.
All seven days. In pressing circumstances one may rely on the lenient view regarding boil-like discoloration on the other days, since on the other days the obligation of taking is only rabbinic.
We do not bless. For there are opinions among the poskim that since we do this because of the doubt of the day, perhaps its law is like the first day; or, since we are expert in the fixing of the month, its law is like the other days. Therefore one takes when there is no other, but does not bless. And even though when there is no other on the first day as well, one takes and does not bless, as explained below in se'if 6, there it speaks where there is no other at all, while here it speaks where one can be found with effort, and it teaches that on the second day one does not need to exert himself.
He should bless over his fellow's, with his knowledge. Meaning, his fellow should give it to him as a gift in a way that is effective even on the first day. But without his knowledge, even though he wrote above that it is permitted because a person is pleased that a mitzvah be performed with his property, nevertheless it is no better than borrowed and is invalid on the first day of Yom Tov, and on the second day as well because of doubt. Therefore he should give it to him as a gift and not as a loan. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Darkei Moshe that afterward he should take this one. That is, he should bless over his fellow's lulav that he received as a gift, and afterward take his own for Hallel and hosha'ana.
