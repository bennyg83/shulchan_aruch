Because of blemishes, etc. As stated there: and according to Rabbi Chanina the mishnah is difficult, etc. Likewise all blemishes are because of deficiency or beauty. The Rambam holds that beauty invalidates only on the first day of Yom Tov, as stated there, that on the second day of Yom Tov we do not require beauty, except for an esrog eaten by mice, because it is especially repulsive according to one version; and even in that case the Rif ruled leniently. And that which is written at the beginning of the chapter, 'granted, a dry one lacks beauty,' etc., that sugya disagrees, as the Ra'avad wrote. It is stated only for a broader explanation, that a dry one is not so difficult, for one can say we require beauty all day, but, etc. Yet we rule like Rabbi Chanina. So too the Yerushalmi says on the mishnah of one upon which a boil-like discoloration arose, etc.: Rabbi Yitzchak bar Nachman said in the name of Shmuel: all invalidations invalidate only on the first day of Yom Tov. He means from boil-like discoloration onward, which invalidate because of beauty or deficiency. Tosafot wrote that the Yerushalmi disagrees with our sugya and holds that beauty invalidates only on the first day of Yom Tov. But according to what I wrote, they do not disagree.
Or because of, etc. This follows Shmuel there, and Rava, who is later, supports him, and not the initial assumption that said: it teaches categorically, etc. Rather, both stolen and dry are invalid only on the first day of Yom Tov. Here he did not enter into distinguishing the law of beauty, and he relies on what he wrote there regarding the law of one punctured by mice.
And some invalidate, etc. This follows Rabbi Yochanan, for between Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan the halachah follows Rabbi Yochanan, as stated in the fourth chapter of Eruvin. Likewise, all invalidations because of beauty are as he writes below: but if it was dry or, etc.; and as Rav Ami said, a dry one is invalid, etc., a stolen one is invalid, etc. It is specifically regarding deficiency that we say there that on the other days it is valid, and likewise all invalidations because of deficiency. But invalidations because of beauty, we rule as stated at the beginning of the chapter: granted, a dry one, etc.; and so too Rabbi Ami, as above. Rather, one must say that specifically by deficiency do we validate on the other days. This is the view of Tosafot and the Rosh.
And it is permitted to take, etc. As stated in the first chapter of Pesachim and in the last chapter of Bekhorot. And even though in the second chapter of Bava Metzia we do not say so, there it is because he causes him a loss. See above, siman 14 and siman 437.
However, etc. This follows the first version. But Rabbeinu Chananel, the Rif, the Rosh, and all the Geonim rule like the latter version.
Until he removes, etc. Since it is not an intrinsic invalidation, for a deficient esrog is valid; rather, it is because it is repulsive. When it is not repulsive it is permitted. This is unlike an intrinsic invalidation, such as invalidations of beauty. This is what he wrote: but if, etc. The Hagahot Ashri wrote: this is not comparable to berries that outnumber its leaves, for there the reduction is not recognizable. It appears that he learned this from the mishnah saying repair regarding its berries, etc., more than other invalidations. See above, siman 648:14, where the gloss is careful about this and writes: and nothing is missing. This is unlike deficiency, even on the other days. But it appears that the Rosh there disagrees.
And it is permitted, etc. So wrote the Rashba, for the case of Rabbi Chanina there is speaking in this manner. But otherwise the whole of it was set aside, like sukkah wood, which is prohibited even beyond the amount needed for its validity; all the more so here, where it is one body. This is unlike the Rosh there.
And the invalidation, etc. For in the Yerushalmi he said this only regarding boil-like discoloration and the like; but the first clause of the mishnah is prohibited forever.
Because of idolatry. Since he had to establish the case there, 31b, as the asherah of Moshe, necessarily we are dealing with the second day, as Tosafot wrote there, s.v. be-asherah, etc., and as above in section 2. See Tosafot 35a, s.v. lefi, etc.: and one can say that he mentioned, etc.
Or, etc. This implies even impure terumah and orlah, not as Rashi and Tosafot explain, because of 'for yourselves,' but rather because, since it has no permitted eating or monetary law, its required measure is considered crushed, for these are among the items that must be burned at the end of Temurah, as the Rashba and Ran wrote there; see there. The Magen Avraham did not see this. With this Tosafot's question is resolved: why did it need to say that it is considered crushed, etc.; it should have said because it has permitted eating. According to this, it is understandable that all of them are because of the rule that their measure is considered crushed, etc.
Or because they are not of their species. From that which it says: a wild hadas is valid for roofing, and it does not raise a difficulty from the other days.
Or because they are deficient, etc. For the reason of asherah, etc., is that it is considered crushed, etc., and therefore lacks the required measure.
And boil-like discoloration, etc. He wrote this according to his own approach; see Tosafot, s.v. ka-pasik, etc., and the Rosh. But according to the Rambam, it applies specifically on the first day, as stated in the Yerushalmi. The Ramban has another approach: certainly dry, boil-like discoloration, and everything due to beauty and deficiency do not apply nowadays, when the seven days are only rabbinic. This is Rabbi Chanina's law. But the mishnah speaks in the Temple, where all seven days are equal, and there is no distinction between the first day and the other days except for borrowed and stolen according to one opinion; but regarding other matters there is no distinction. This is what it means when it says that the mishnah teaches categorically, etc., for the mishnah speaks in the Temple, since afterward it teaches: at first the lulav was taken, etc., and until there it had not discussed Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai's enactment. And according to the one who says that a mitzvah that comes through a transgression is the issue, there is no distinction except by borrowed, as written explicitly in the mishnah: because they said a person does not, etc. And according to the one who says even stolen, it also refers to stolen, as written in the baraita there: 'for yourselves,' to exclude, etc. But Rabbi Chanina's law, and likewise the Yerushalmi, refer to nowadays, and also to stolen according to Shmuel's own view, for he holds that a stolen one is valid even in the Temple, as stated in the Gemara. But according to the one who invalidates, it is invalid even nowadays because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Other invalidations, however, are invalid even nowadays, as the Rambam wrote. He holds nowadays like the Rambam, except that regarding stolen he rules like Rabbi Yochanan. So the Shulchan Aruch ruled plainly at the beginning of this siman: all, etc.; and there is one who says, etc.; and likewise borrowed, etc.
And those who do, etc. As stated on 43b: we are not expert in the fixing of the month. The Rashba there explained that this means it is as if we are not expert, because in the first chapter of Beitzah they said: they sent from there, be careful, etc.; behold, we follow the custom of our fathers, etc., who did not know the fixing of the month. A proof is that for us also, the first day of Yom Tov does not override Shabbat, just as for them, because we are not expert in the fixing of the month, as stated at the beginning of the fourth chapter. Because of this, even for them it does not override, since for us, etc., as stated there. And just as they, because of doubt, would take it and not bless, so too we do the same. Even according to those who explain that we act like them only to be stringent and not to be lenient, this is not leniency, since we do take it. The Rif wrote in the first chapter of Megillah that this is the reason shofar is deferred because of Rabbah's reason: also because we do not know the fixing of the month. The Rashba in Sukkah there wrote likewise, and wrote that therefore circumcision is not deferred because of Rabbah's reason, since its time is clear; see there, and see the Ran in Megillah there. Also, from the fact that we bless on the second day of Yom Tov even though we do not bless over a doubt, we bless so that people not treat it lightly; all the more so he should not bless here, so that people not treat it lightly. This is not comparable to counting the Omer, for there it is biblical, unlike here. See the Ran, chapter 5.
And if there is, etc. For taking without his knowledge is also among the invalidations of the first day. He means specifically as a gift. See Magen Avraham.
