Because of robbery, etc. This is the view of the Rambam, who ruled like Shmuel, that on the second day of Yom Tov, since it is rabbinic, a stolen one is permitted even though it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. This requires investigation, for if so, why did he write in se'if 1 that for others it is valid except on the first day? According to this view, even for the robber himself it is valid. So too the Kol Bo wrote that these two opinions disagree with each other. Perhaps the Beit Yosef holds that ab initio the robber himself should not take it, but for others it is permitted.
And it is permitted to take. But it is prohibited to take it from his house to the synagogue or the reverse, as written in siman 14:4. See what I wrote there.
Its pitom or its stem was removed. Rabbi Eliezer of Prague wrote that it invalidates all seven days, and in Darkei Moshe he disagrees with him and wrote: since the reason is deficiency, it is valid on the other days. End quote. Even though deficiency invalidates only when it is the size of an issar, here it is different, since it is at its top, and therefore it is invalid. [Hagahot Maimoniyot] But from Maggid Mishneh it appears that the invalidity of the pitom is because of beauty, for regarding a removed stem he asks: this is obvious, since a deficient one is invalid; see there. So too it appears in siman 645:7 and in the poskim. Therefore one should not be lenient regarding the pitom.
Until he removes. It appears to me that the Rema holds that puncturing by mice is valid by law, according to the view of most poskim; only ab initio he should not take it until he removes the gnawing of the mice. But if it is dry or spotted, which is invalid by law, removal is not effective.
All of it for the first day, etc. For on the first day all of it was set aside, since a deficient esrog is invalid. Therefore he must make a condition that he is not separating himself from it from the night of the second day onward. But if he designated an esrog for the second day, it is permitted to eat from it, provided that the required measure remains, as the Rosh and Tur wrote. It also appears so, for at the beginning it did not write that he made a condition; see there. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham, unlike what the Beit Yosef wrote. And in Beit Yosef siman 653 he wrote in the name of the Rashba that a condition helps with a hadas to smell it on Motza'ei Shabbat, for why should it differ from an esrog, with which one fulfills the obligation and eats them, and it is not comparable to sukkah wood, for there it is written 'the festival of Sukkot to Hashem.' End quote. See his responsa, siman 169, which implies that he holds that a condition is effective even for what is needed for the required measure of an esrog, and therefore he wrote that it is not comparable to sukkah wood, etc. See the Ran at the beginning of the first chapter of Beitzah and in the fourth chapter, and this is his language: a condition is effective for a sukkah, and for him it is like a sukkah that is not a mitzvah, like one who has an esrog and made a condition about it to eat it if he wishes, and if he changes his mind he fulfills the obligation with it. End quote. So too it is written in Milchamot. See Shabbat daf 22 and Tosafot there: it says to eat from it, etc., and to fulfill with it, etc.; this means to eat on the second day and to fulfill with it on the third day, for it is prohibited to eat before taking, as written in siman 652.
Prohibited for eating. This implies that he holds that even though it is permitted for benefit, since it is prohibited for eating it is invalid all seven days. As for Tosafot's question, why is a reason needed by a lulav of idolatry because its required measure is considered crushed; let him derive it from the fact that it has no monetary law? The Mechaber answered this by writing that that esrog, etc., is prohibited for eating; meaning that specifically by esrog, since it is fit for eating, we expound 'for yourselves' to mean it must be yours to eat. But by lulav, which is not fit to eat, such an exposition is not relevant, and since it is his, even though it is prohibited for benefit, we read 'for yourselves' with respect to it. An esrog of orlah is invalid. [Gemara] Orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is valid, for he can ab initio feed it to his fellow who does not recognize it. [Shiltei Gibborim] So too appears to be the view of the poskim, who omitted this case. But according to what the Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 294, that it is prohibited to feed it to his fellow, if so it is invalid. Nevertheless, it appears to me that one should validate it, since the plain meaning of the Talmud implies that he may feed it to his fellow, and one may rely on that regarding esrog. An esrog that became prohibited because it absorbed a prohibition is invalid. [Shiltei Gibborim] This is not compelling, for one can say that only when its prohibition comes from itself is it invalid. Therefore it appears to me that one should validate it on the other days, for Tosafot, Rashi, and Kol Bo validate even one of orlah on the other days, and therefore one may rely on them in such a case. An esrog of demai is valid; regarding tevel, see the Ran.
