[4] "In the rainy season," etc. — for we are concerned he will err with "grant dew and rain" on Motzei Shabbos, for that is the beginning and an error does not apply; nevertheless we are concerned he will think it is a blessing in its own right, Talmud. And Levush added: one might come to err and bless in the Shemoneh Esrei "You grant understanding" as a blessing in its own right, and "You have granted us" as a blessing in its own right — up to here. And it is difficult: if so, on the request for rain we should also say thus — and why did the Talmud give the reason that perhaps he will err? And it is strained to say that "You have granted us" is different, for there is a tanna who holds one says it as a blessing in its own right, and one might come to err — which is not so regarding the request for rain, for no one says so. And Perishah answered: specifically regarding Havdalah, which is not related to the blessing "You grant understanding," it would be considered a blessing in its own right — which is not so regarding the request for rain, which is of the same kind as "and satisfy us from Your bounty," up to here; and see below at the beginning of siman 294. And Beit Yosef answered: there is no concern at all that one will bless in the Shemoneh Esrei "You have granted us" as a blessing in its own right, since all year we are accustomed that it is included in "grant understanding"; rather the concern is that he will err to say it is like other blessings, that if one forgets they return him — and therefore regarding the request for rain there is no concern, for in truth they return him, and this is correct. But I found in Sefer Zikaron to the gaon R' Yehuda ha-Kohen, parashat 255, who wrote thus in his wording: "and if one says the request for rain and Havdalah, it will appear that they are two blessings in their own right" — up to here his wording. And behold in truth this reason exists regarding the request for rain; and if so, all the resolutions mentioned above fall away. Therefore it seems to me that the Talmud, when it gave another reason regarding the request for rain, was to strengthen Mar Zutra who says Havdalah, and he does not accept the reason that one might err that it is a blessing in its own right; but as we hold like Shmuel that we do not say Havdalah — from the reason that it is a blessing in its own right, the same applies to the request for rain. And that which the decisors wrote this reason that perhaps they will err regarding the request for rain — is for amplification of the matter, that even for one who says we say Havdalah, he admits regarding the request for rain that we do not say. And now it works out that which is implied in Shulchan Aruch, etc., that even if he is certain he will not err, he does not mention even the request for rain; and similarly wrote Kesef Mishnah chapter 2 of the Laws of Prayer — and this contradicts apparently below siman 128 seif regarding the law of raising the hands; but as I explained, it fits somewhat, for there is another reason of a blessing in its own right.
