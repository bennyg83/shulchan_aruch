(16) One who sets out on a journey. This seif applies even after he has already recited Shemoneh Esrei.
(17) Toward peace. One who takes leave of another should not say, “Go in peace,” but rather, “Go toward peace.” When taking leave of the deceased, one should say, “in peace,” and not “toward peace.” For the reason, see the end of Berachos, in Ein Yaakov, HaKosev, and Maharsha.
(18) And he must. After the fact, he has fulfilled his obligation even if he recited the entire text in the singular.
(19) To recite it, etc. Meaning, the entire text of Tefillas HaDerech should be in the plural, because this makes his prayer more readily accepted. In the name of Sefer HaKanah they write that the word “and grant me favor” should be said in the singular.
(20) In the plural. This applies specifically to a prayer established for the public. When a person wishes to make a particular request for himself, he need not formulate it in the plural. He should engage in Torah study while traveling, for Chazal have said that one who travels and turns his mind to idleness endangers his life. He should not, however, study a halachic matter in depth, lest he become distracted, as stated in Taanis 10b. Magen Avraham writes that perhaps one who sits in a wagon while another person drives the horses may even study in depth. The Gemara further implies that one traveling should eat somewhat sparingly, because satiation is then difficult for the intestines due to the exertion of travel. Every person should take bread with him, even when traveling nearby and even where bread is readily available, because unforeseen events may occur on the road. It is proper for every God-fearing person, when setting out, to take his tallis and tefillin even when traveling nearby and intending to return that day, lest some occurrence prevent his return and he miss the mitzvah. Regrettably, some travelers do not even take tefillin, relying on borrowing from others along the way. Their sin is great, because they often thereby miss the proper time for Krias Shema and prayer while waiting until tefillin become available. Moreover, they take whatever tefillin come to hand without ensuring that the knot of the head-tefillin fits their measure.
(21) If possible. For example, when his traveling companions are willing to wait.
(22) He should stop walking. If stopping will distract him, however, he may recite it while walking or sitting.
(23) He need not dismount. Because dismounting and remounting would distract him. Rather, if possible, he should stop the animal, since riding is equivalent to walking. The same applies to one sitting in a wagon: he need not descend, but should stop the horses if possible.
