One who sets out on a journey. The custom of seeking permission from Torah sages when setting out on a journey, and of receiving their blessing, has support from the statement of our Sages in the first chapter of Berachos, that they would consult the Sanhedrin. Rashi explains that they sought permission so that the sages would pray on their behalf. (Ateres Zekeinim, citing Rabbi Shmuel in the name of Maharal of Lublin.)
And, if possible, he should stand. This applies specifically when it is feasible for him, such as when his companions will wait for him. Strictly speaking, however, it is sufficient to recite it while walking. (Beit Yosef, Rosh, and Rif; Rabbi Yonah and Rashba write likewise.)
If he is riding, he need not dismount, for this prayer is no more stringent than the Amidah itself. (Rabbi Yonah, chapter Tefillas HaShachar.) Some say that he must dismount, since this is a short prayer and does not require such extensive concentration, so there is no concern that delay on his journey will distract him. (Rabbi Yonah.)
