One who sets out on a journey. And what people are accustomed to do—taking leave from the elders when they go on roads and receiving blessings from the elders—has support from what our Sages said in the first chapter of Berachos: "They take counsel in the Sanhedrin." Rashi explained: they took leave so that they would pray for them (Ammudai Zion in the name of Maharash, i.e. Maharal of Lublin).

And if possible, he should stand. And this is specifically where it is possible for him, such as when the party waits for him; but by law it suffices while walking (Beit Yosef, Rosh, Rif, and likewise Rabbeinu Yonah and Rashba).

And if he was riding, he need not dismount, for it is no better than the prayer of eighteen blessings itself (Rabbeinu Yonah in the chapter on the Morning Prayer). And some say he must dismount, since the prayer is short and does not require so much concentration, and there is no concern that he may be interrupted on account of mixing up his route (Mahari).
