(א) "During the days [of rain]," etc. — "and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land." For Havinenu in brief words includes all the middle blessings of the Eighteen blessings. And the three words "and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land" are in place of the blessing of the years. And it challenges: just as in the Eighteen one says "and grant dew and rain" in the blessing of the years, it should have said in Havinenu when saying "and satisfy us [with the goodness of Your land]," etc., "and grant dew and rain" in the blessing of the years. That is, lest one err, etc. And this is the language of the Beit Yosef; and it seems that the reason they are more concerned one will err in Havinenu than in the prayer of the Eighteen is because a person is apt to err more in brief matters, for easily one can skip two or three words or add them — until here is his language.

(ב) And the Bach wrote, and this is his language: "And you might say that if so," etc. — even without the [rain] request one may come to skip two or three words and Havinenu will lack one of the middle [blessings] — end quote. And therefore Magen Avraham [explains] that the intention of the Beit Yosef is: by his adding during the rainy season the request "and grant dew and rain," when he concludes "and grant dew and rain" he will not know to return to the place where he stands in Havinenu, since they are brief words — which is not so when he does not add the request, for there is no need to skip.

(ג) As written in siman 128 regarding a kohen who is the prayer leader — he should not raise his hands, lest he become troubled. And even so, if he is confident he knows how to return to his prayer, he raises his hands; and the same applies here.

(ד) And see there in siman 128, that even for a kohen and prayer leader, even if assured, it is not permitted for him to raise his hands unless there is no other kohen but he.
