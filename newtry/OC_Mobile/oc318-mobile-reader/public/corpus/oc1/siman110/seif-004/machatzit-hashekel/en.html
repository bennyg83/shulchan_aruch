(Seif katan 4) During the rainy season, etc. “And satisfy us with the goodness of Your land.” Havineinu, in abbreviated wording, includes all the intermediate blessings of the eighteen blessings. The three words, “and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land,” correspond to Bircas HaShanim. The Gemara asks that just as in the full Shemoneh Esrei one says “grant dew and rain” in Bircas HaShanim, so too in Havineinu, when saying “and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land,” he should add “grant dew and rain” in Bircas HaShanim. The answer is that he may become confused, etc. The Beit Yosef writes: “It appears to me that the reason we are more concerned that one will err in Havineinu than in the full Shemoneh Esrei is that a person is more prone to err in short formulations, for he can easily omit two or three words or add them.”
The Bach wrote as follows: It is astonishing, for if so, etc. Even without the seasonal request he might omit two or three words, and Havineinu would then lack one of the intermediate blessings. In response, the Magen Avraham explains that the Beit Yosef means that by adding the request “grant dew and rain” during the rainy season, after he finishes saying “grant dew and rain” he may not know where to resume in Havineinu, because its wording is brief. Without adding the request, however, there is no reason to fear such an omission.
As stated in siman 128 regarding a kohen who is also the chazzan, he should not recite Bircas Kohanim because he may become confused. Nevertheless, if he is certain that he will know how to return to his prayer, he may recite Bircas Kohanim. The same applies here.
See what I wrote there in siman 128, that even a kohen who is the chazzan and is certain that he can return to his prayer was permitted to recite Bircas Kohanim only when there is no other kohen present.
