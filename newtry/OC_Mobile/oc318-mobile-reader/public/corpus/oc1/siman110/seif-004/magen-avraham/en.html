For peace. A person should not say to another, 'Go in peace,' but rather, 'Go toward peace.'
In the plural form. This applies specifically to a prayer established for the public, as stated in siman 565 at the end of seif 1. However, the phrase, 'Grant me favor,' should be said in the singular. Sefer HaKanah writes this according to Kabbalah. One should engage in Torah study while traveling, but should not delve deeply into a halachic matter, lest he become distracted, as stated in the Gemara in Taanis. It is possible that when he is seated in a wagon and another person is driving the horses, even in-depth study is permitted. He should not eat more than in years of famine, because of intestinal illness. He should enter while it is still light and depart while it is light. Tosafos at the beginning of Pesachim wrote that within one's own city it is permitted to travel at night when one is not alone, since there is no concern either for harmful forces or for pits.
Riding. Nevertheless, if possible, he should stop the animal, for we rule that riding is considered like walking.
