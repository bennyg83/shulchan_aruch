(29) "That he took hold of the road" — meaning that he should not say it when he is still within the city where he dwells, even if he prepares himself to set out on the road. And the city's outskirts — that is, seventy cubits and a bit more adjacent to the city — after all the houses have already ended, he is considered as within the city. And the Taz permits even within the city from the time he resolved in his heart and prepared himself to leave. But the Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim and other later authorities disagree on this, and they agreed that lekhatchilah one should be careful not to do so; but bedieved one may rely on his words. And all this is at the beginning of his leaving his house; but when he lodged on the road he may say Tefilat HaDerech in the morning when he prepares himself to set out, even when he is still in the city, for he has already taken hold of the road. See below in siman 231 regarding what one prays when leaving the city, and in Mishnah Berurah there.

(30) "And one may not say it," etc. — for less than a parsah near the city is not a place of danger ordinarily, unless it is established that one lodges in that place and it is a place of danger — then one recites Tefilat HaDerech in all circumstances. And there is no difference between traveling by ship and traveling on land; {Rama: and therefore also one who travels on a railway should recite Tefilat HaDerech} even if he travels only a parsah. And therefore one should be careful lekhatchilah to recite Tefilat HaDerech immediately when he begins to travel, as Rama wrote nearby that lekhatchilah one should return within the first parsah, and bedieved one may bless until the end, as long as he still has one parsah to travel on the track.

(31) A parsah — it is 8,000 cubits; for a mil is 2,000 cubits and a parsah is four milin.

(32) "But less," etc. — and because of this, when someone comes from the road within a parsah, the custom of the world is not to greet him with Shalom, for it is not considered that he comes from the road, for he does not say Tefilat HaDerech.

(33) Within a parsah — and even if he intends afterward to travel several parsaot from this city, it is a journey on its own; and now in any case it is less than a parsah, and there is no danger.
