(29) He has begun the journey. Meaning, he should not recite it while still within the city where he resides, even though he is preparing to depart. The city’s extension, namely seventy cubits and a little more beyond the last houses, is considered part of the city. Taz permits reciting it even within the city once he has firmly decided and prepared to depart. Eliyah Rabbah, Peri Megadim, and the other later authorities disagree and conclude that initially one should be careful not to do so, though after the fact one may rely on his view. All this applies when initially leaving home. If he lodged while traveling, he may recite Tefillas HaDerech in the morning while preparing to depart even while still in the city, since he has already begun the journey. See below, siman 230, regarding the prayer recited when leaving a city, and the Mishnah Berurah there.
(30) And it should not be recited, etc. Because less than a parsah near a city is ordinarily not considered dangerous, unless that particular place is known to be dangerous, in which case Tefillas HaDerech should be recited in all circumstances. There is no distinction between traveling by ship and traveling on land, as Eliyah Rabbah writes. Accordingly, one traveling by railway should also recite Tefillas HaDerech even if traveling only one parsah. Therefore, initially he should be careful to recite it immediately upon beginning the journey, as Rama writes nearby that initially one should seek to recite it within the first parsah. After the fact, he may recite it until the end, provided that at least one parsah of railway travel still remains.
(31) A parsah. This is eight thousand cubits, because a mil is two thousand cubits and a parsah is four mil.
(32) But less than, etc. For this reason, when a person returns from a journey of less than a parsah, the common custom is not to greet him as one returning from travel, since this is not considered returning from a journey, as Tefillas HaDerech is not recited for it.
(33) Within a parsah. Even if he intends afterward to travel several additional parsaos from that city, that later journey is a separate trip. At present, in any event, he is traveling less than a parsah and there is no presumed danger.
