"And one does not say it," etc. — see in Mishna Berurah the reason, and it is from the Rosh; and apparently therefore even if he has to travel a parsah together, but he travels the parsah via some city, or he knows that some city is near the side of his route within a parsah — he likewise should not say tefillat ha-derekh with a concluding blessing, for there is no place of danger; and it is possible they only exempted if his entire route of travel was less than a parsah. And what he said afterward, "provided that he has not arrived," etc. — that is different there, for what he already traveled cannot combine to obligate him thereby; and what he travels tomorrow is another journey — unlike in our matter. And it requires study.
