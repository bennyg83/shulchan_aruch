And ideally, etc. Meaning, he should not delay reciting it beyond the first parsah. He may recite it even while still in the city before setting out. The statement that he must have "begun the journey" means that he is definitively committed to traveling. I have seen some who are particular not to recite it until they are actually outside the city in the open field, but this has no basis or foundation in any of the interpretations. Maharam would recite it in the morning on the day he intended to travel. Nevertheless, there is a reason for the custom of reciting it outside the city, because there he can recite it together with others, and the merit of the community is greater. In any event, he should be careful to recite in his home the prayer for one who leaves a city, mentioned in siman 230.
