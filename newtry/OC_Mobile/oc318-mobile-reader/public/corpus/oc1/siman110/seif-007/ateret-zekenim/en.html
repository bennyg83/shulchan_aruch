He says it after he has set out on the road. And some explain this to exclude saying it immediately upon leaving the city (Emek HaBrachah and his son Rabbi Isaiah in his book Shlah). See Beer Sheva, page 109. And in my humble opinion, even if he prepared himself to go on the road but has not yet gone, he may say the Traveler's Prayer; and likewise Rashi explained in Horayos in the first chapter: "he has set out on the road" applies. And likewise it is proper to do: if he does not have to go even one full parsa, then he should say the Traveler's Prayer in his house when he leaves, after blessing [God for having made] a full parsa. And likewise he should do when he is on the road: pray in the morning and include the Traveler's Prayer within Shema Koleinu or after "Who bestows good kindnesses," as Maharam did; and through this he fulfills the views of all. And in a responsum I expanded on this as above.
