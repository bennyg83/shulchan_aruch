[7] "And if he can stand," etc. — the reason is written in Nachalat Tzvi: the Rambam's textual reading in the Talmud [Berachos] 29 is "a short prayer even while walking" — implying that ab initio it is better standing — up to here. And he did not examine closely, for he already wrote in Kesef Mishnah chapter 4 from Mahari that the Rambam learned from what we say: Rav Chisda and Rav Sheshet were going on the road; Rav Chisda stood and prayed, and Rav Sheshet also stood and said "from being good," etc. — and similarly Rashba explained explicitly in his novelae. But it is difficult regarding what he wrote in this wording: "this shade is a short prayer" — whereas Havineinu does not derive from "from being good," for by law one must stand — up to here his wording. And behold the plain meaning of the Talmud implies that even tefillat ha-derekh is referred to, that Rav Sheshet permits while walking — and similarly is explained in Beit Yosef. And it appears Kesef Mishnah did not mean specifically a short prayer, but similar to a short prayer that implies one law applies to them — and similarly is implied in Abudraham 131. But it is difficult, for the Talmud says 29b "and the halacha: Havineinu standing," and we hold halacha from a general principle that they disagree — and perhaps he did not have the word "and the halacha" in his reading; and similarly in the Rif and Rosh it is absent.
