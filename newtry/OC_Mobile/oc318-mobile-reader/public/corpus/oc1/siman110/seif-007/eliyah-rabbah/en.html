[7] And if he is able to stand, etc. Nachalas Tzvi explains the reason as follows: the Rambam's text of the Gemara, Berachos 29, reads, "A short prayer may be recited even while walking," implying that ideally it is preferable to stand. This is imprecise, for the Kesef Mishneh in chapter 4 already wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that the Rambam derived this from the incident in which Rav Chisda and Rav Sheshes were traveling. Rav Chisda stopped and prayed, and Rav Sheshes also stopped, applying the principle, "Since it can be done in the better manner, do not be called bad." The Rashba explains this explicitly in his chiddushim. There is, however, difficulty with the Rashba's statement: "This prayer was a short prayer, for if it had been Havineinu, standing would not derive merely from the principle of doing the better thing, because by law Havineinu must be recited standing." The plain meaning of the Gemara indicates that the discussion also refers to Tefillas HaDerech, since Rav Sheshes permits its recitation while walking, as is also clear from the Beit Yosef. It appears that the Kesef Mishneh did not mean a short prayer specifically, but rather any prayer analogous to a short prayer, because they share the same law. This is also implied by Abudraham, folio 131. Yet there remains a difficulty, for the Gemara on 29 says, "The halachah is that Havineinu is recited standing," and the expression "the halachah" implies that there is a dispute. Perhaps his text did not contain the word "the halachah," as it is also absent from the Rif and the Rosh.
