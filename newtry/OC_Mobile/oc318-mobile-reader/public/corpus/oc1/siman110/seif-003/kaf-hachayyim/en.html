3) There: In pressing circumstances, etc. If one recited Habineinu when there were no pressing circumstances, Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes that after the fact he fulfilled his obligation and need not pray again; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, disputes his ruling and writes that he did not fulfill his obligation. Rabbi Lechem Yehudah, on chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 2, likewise writes that he did not fulfill his obligation. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, however, writes that he did fulfill it, because in most matters an after-the-fact situation and pressing circumstances are treated alike. He writes that Eliyah Rabbah's position is not conclusive; see there. Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim, siman 54, page 92a, likewise writes that he fulfilled his obligation after the fact. Chikrei Lev writes there that he found this novel ruling in no authority, but he overlooked the aforementioned words of Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah, who disagree on this very point. Chayei Olam on Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, likewise writes that he fulfilled his obligation after the fact and that the words of Eliyah Rabbah are not compelling; see there.
