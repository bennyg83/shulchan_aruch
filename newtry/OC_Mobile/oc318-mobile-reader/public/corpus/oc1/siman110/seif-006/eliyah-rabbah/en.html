[6] "The needs of Your people," etc. — in Levush this is its wording: "Master of the worlds, the needs of Your people Israel are many and their understanding short; may it be Your will before You, the Master my Heaven, that You give each and every one his livelihood, and every body what it lacks; and the good in Your eyes do. Blessed are You, the Master, Who hears prayer" — up to here. And Malbush Yom Tov wrote: that it opens with "Master of the worlds" is correct, but I have not seen so in the Talmud and decisors; and perhaps they relied on the rule "a person should always arrange his praise," etc. — up to here; and similarly wrote Lechem Chamudos 26. And in my humble opinion one may say: since he is in a place of danger, the Holy One blessed be He does not desire his praise — an example: "why do you cry to Me?" And furthermore, in the Mishnah they said "save, the Master," etc. — it is impossible to say one would say "Master of the worlds, save, the Master." Also one may say that the essence of the prayer begins from "may it be Your will," and they begin with it as with other matters explained below in siman 230; but I found in Abudraham 147 who wrote like the formula of Levush "Master of the worlds." "And the good in Your eyes," etc. — Rabbeinu Menachem explained: do not give us the good in our eyes, lest perhaps its end not be good; rather the good in Your eyes, which is the complete good; and Kesef Mishnah brought this from chapter 4 of the Laws of Prayer. And see (in Shach) [what] the Rambam wrote in his commentary on the Mishnah, chapter Tefillat ha-Shachar: one may pray it walking or riding or as it happens to one, and it has no bowing — up to here; and see below at the end of this siman.
