(27) "For the road in the morning" — meaning when it was sometimes necessary to go on the road in the morning before prayer and he would say the blessings on the road, he would adjoin Tefilat HaDerech after Yehi Ratzon. But when he travels after prayer he cannot say Tefilat HaDerech before prayer, for in any case it is forbidden to say it until he has taken hold of the road, as stated in siman 97.

(28) "Adjoined to its fellow" — that it does not open with Baruch. And if he travels in the middle of the day he adjoins it to another blessing, such as that he eats or drinks something and recites a concluding blessing, or he relieves himself and says Asher Yatzar. And in a place where he cannot do any of this — such as that he has nothing on which to recite a blessing and also he sits in a wagon among people and they do not wish to wait for him until he descends and ascends — nevertheless it is permitted to say Tefilat HaDerech, for many early authorities hold that Tefilat HaDerech need not be adjoined to its fellow; see Tur and Beit Yosef. The custom is to take permission from the great ones and they bless when they travel on the road. And there is support from what Chazal said — they take counsel in the Sanhedrin — and Rashi explained: they took permission so that they would pray for them. And one should set out at a favorable hour and arrive at a favorable hour — that is, in the morning at sunrise and in the evening at the beginning of sunset, lest one fall into one of the pits; and therefore when he is near his city, where he knows to be careful there of the pits, he may go there at night if he does not go alone, for there is no concern for harmful beings.
