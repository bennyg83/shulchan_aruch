(27) On a journey in the morning. Meaning, when he sometimes had to set out early before prayer and recited the blessings while traveling, he would place Tefillas HaDerech immediately after the Yehi Ratzon. When traveling after prayer, however, he cannot recite Tefillas HaDerech before praying, because in any event it may not be recited until he has actually begun the journey, as stated in seif 7.
(28) Adjacent to another blessing. Because it does not begin with “Blessed are You.” If he sets out in the middle of the day, he should place it after another blessing. For example, he may eat or drink something and recite the concluding blessing, or relieve himself and recite Asher Yatzar. Where none of this is possible, such as when he has nothing over which to recite a blessing and is sitting in a wagon among people who will not wait for him to descend and reenter, he may nevertheless recite Tefillas HaDerech, since many Rishonim hold that it need not be adjacent to another blessing. See Tur and Beit Yosef. The custom is to request permission and a blessing from Torah leaders before traveling. This has support from Chazal’s statement that they would consult the Sanhedrin, which Rashi explains to mean that they sought permission so the Sanhedrin would pray for them. One should enter while it is still good daylight and depart while it is still good daylight, meaning at sunrise in the morning and at the beginning of sunset in the evening, lest he fall into one of the pits. Therefore, when near his city, where he knows how to avoid the pits, he may travel there at night provided that he is not alone, so there is no concern for harmful forces.
