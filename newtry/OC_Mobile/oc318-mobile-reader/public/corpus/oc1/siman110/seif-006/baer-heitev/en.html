In the morning, etc. Some understood that the phrase "in the morning" refers to what follows, meaning that when he would set out on a journey, even after prayer, he would recite it in the morning so as to connect it to the blessing "Who bestows acts of kindness." This is also the view of the Taz. This is incorrect. Rather, the phrase "in the morning" refers to what precedes it: when he would set out in the morning before prayer and recite the blessings while traveling, etc. For it is forbidden to recite Tefillas HaDerech until he has actually begun the journey, as stated in seif 7. Be'er Sheva, folio 109; Magen Avraham; Olas Tamid; and Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah. According to the Taz, the statement (in seif 7) "after he has begun the journey" means that he has definitively resolved to travel. See there. This explanation is strained. I am uncertain whether one fulfills the obligation after the fact if he recited it in his home. As for the prayer recited by one who leaves a city, mentioned in siman 230, all agree that it is recited in his home. See there.
To another blessing. Since it does not begin with "Blessed are You," if he travels in the middle of the day he should connect it to another blessing. For example, he may eat or drink something and recite the concluding blessing, or relieve himself and recite Asher Yatzar. Shelah; Nezer HaKodesh; and Agudah. The custom is to seek permission from Torah sages and receive their blessing when setting out on a journey. This has support from the statement of our Sages that they would consult the Sanhedrin. Rashi explains that they sought permission so that the sages would pray on their behalf. Ateres Zekeinim.
