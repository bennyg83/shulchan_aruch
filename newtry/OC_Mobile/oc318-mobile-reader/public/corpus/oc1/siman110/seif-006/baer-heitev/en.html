In the morning, etc. Some understood that this "in the morning" refers below—that is, when he set out on the road, even after the prayer he would say "in the morning" in order to juxtapose it to the blessing "Who bestows good kindnesses"—and such is the view of Taz; but this is not so. Rather, the phrase "in the morning" refers above: when he set out in the morning before the prayer and said the blessings on the road, etc.; for one is forbidden to say it until he has set out on the road, as written in seif 7. Beer Sheva page 109, Magen Avraham, Elyah Rabbah, and Shaarei Knesset HaGedolah. And according to Taz, what [the Mechaber] said (in seif 7) "after he has set out on the road"—its meaning is that he must be firmly resolved to travel on [that] road. And this is strained; and I was uncertain whether he fulfills [his obligation] after the fact if he said it in his house. And the prayer of one leaving the city mentioned in siman 210—in the view of all he says it in his house on [that] road.

Adjacent to it. That it does not open with "Baruch." And if he travels in the middle of the day, he should juxtapose it to another blessing, such as if he eats or drinks something and recites a concluding blessing, or relieves himself and says "Who formed [the human body]." Shlah, Nefesh Tzvi, and Aggudah. They are accustomed to take leave from the elders and receive blessings when they travel on the road; and there is support from what our Sages said: "They take counsel in the Sanhedrin"—Rashi: they took leave so that they would pray for them. Atzei Chaim.
