(א) "And not," etc. — since it is the beginning of a blessing; and Rashi explained "at the beginning of the prayer" — that is, at the beginning of the middle blessings — one can focus one's mind more than in the middle.

(ב) "And if you ask" regarding the [rain] request, etc. — as above, not like the Bach.

(ג) In order to establish his intention, I will copy the words of the Gemara (Berachos 29a): R' Nachman said in the name of Shmuel: all year one may pray Havinenu except at Motzei Shabbat and the departure of Yom Tov, because one must say havdalah in "Who grants knowledge." Mar Zutri challenged him: and include it in Havinenu — "Heaven our Heaven, Who separates between holy and profane" — difficult. R' Bibi bar Abaye [said]: all year a person may pray Havinenu except during the rainy season, because one must say the [rain] request in the blessing of the years. Mar Zutri challenged him: and include it in the general [formula] — "and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land, and grant dew and rain" — one will become distracted. If so, havdalah in "Who grants knowledge" will also cause distraction. They said there: since it comes at the beginning of prayer, one is not troubled, etc. And the Rashba wrote that R' Hai Gaon, the Rif, the Rambam, and the Rosh all ruled like R' Nachman who said in the name of Shmuel — even though they raised the difficulty. Since no refutation was stated, it is not rejected from practice; and so is the sugya throughout the Talmud.

(ד) And the Mahari wrote to resolve Mar Zutri's difficulty: if one included havdalah, it would appear that havdalah is a blessing on its own, since they instituted a type of havdalah as they instituted a type of the other blessings — until here are his words. And from now we can understand what the difficulty was for Magen Avraham, who wrote "and if you ask" regarding the [rain] request too — say this reason. That is, here in any case this reason must be said regarding havdalah; it should have been said also regarding the [rain] request itself, and there would be no need to answer "one will become distracted" — this is not a difficulty. First, for we see that in the Gemara regarding havdalah too they did not answer Mar Zutri on his difficulty — this reason that it should not appear to be a blessing on its own — and they left the matter in difficulty; therefore they did not adopt his view, or for whatever reason; and how can one question that he should have answered thus on Mar Zutri's second difficulty regarding "and grant dew and rain"? Moreover, it must be that which it challenges — "if so, regarding havdalah too one will become distracted." What is the difficulty? Perhaps in truth it is so; on the contrary, it would be resolved by this statement of R' Nachman who said in the name of Shmuel, that at Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov one does not say Havinenu because of havdalah — and there is a difficulty and inclusion, and they raised the difficulty. And now he answers because of distraction — and it is strained to say that his difficulty is why he left the statement of R' Nachman who said in the name of Shmuel in difficulty; he should also have answered this reason because of distraction. However, it appears more that the Gemara's difficulty is because R' Bibi bar Abaye said "all year," etc., except during the rainy season, etc., and he did not exclude Motzei Shabbat and the departure of Yom Tov from the general rule; therefore he disagrees with R' Nachman who said in the name of Shmuel, and holds that also at Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov one says Havinenu and includes havdalah. And on this it challenges well: "if so, havdalah too will cause distraction," and answers him: "since it comes at the beginning of prayer, one is not troubled." And if so, there is no longer a difficulty that he should answer lest it appear the [rain] request is a blessing on its own — for then the Gemara's difficulty returns to its place: "if so, regarding havdalah too." And therefore R' Bibi did not need this concern, and therefore it must be that Magen Avraham's difficulty is on the codifiers who wrote regarding the [rain] request the reason that one will become troubled — why this reason? Since we rule like Shmuel that at Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov one does not say Havinenu. And so wrote the Mahari — lest it appear to be a blessing on its own; if so, he should have stated this reason also regarding the [rain] request, and there would be one reason for both havdalah and the request. And if we say Magen Avraham's intention is to question the words of the Gemara, it must be that he questions R' Nachman who said in the name of Shmuel, who said "all year," etc., except Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov because of havdalah — and since he did not exclude the rainy season from the rule, he holds that also during the rainy season one prays Havinenu and includes the [rain] request, and was not concerned about distraction. Nevertheless, is it not impossible to include the [rain] request without it appearing to be a blessing on its own, as Shmuel was concerned regarding havdalah, as the Mahari wrote? And accordingly it must be that Shmuel was not concerned about distraction but was concerned lest it appear as a blessing on its own; therefore at Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov one does not pray Havinenu, and during the rainy season one prays Havinenu. And R' Bibi bar Abaye holds the opposite — he was not concerned lest it appear as a blessing on its own, but we are concerned about distraction; therefore the law is the opposite, that during the rainy season one does not pray Havinenu and at Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov one prays Havinenu. And we adopt both stringencies of Shmuel and R' Bibi, and the codifiers who do not pray Havinenu either at Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov or during the rainy season — so it appears to explain Magen Avraham's words. But the Bach wrote, and this is his language: after he wrote his method in explanation of the Mahari's words above, he wrote: "and with this it is satisfactory that the Gemara strained regarding the [rain] request and answers that we do not include it in Havinenu because one will become troubled — and it did not state this reason, that it would appear to be a blessing on its own" — end quote. According to him, what I questioned above is difficult — and requires study.
