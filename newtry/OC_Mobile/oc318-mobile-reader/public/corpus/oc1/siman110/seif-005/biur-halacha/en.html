According to this, it appears that certainly if he lodged at an inn and rose before morning to continue his journey, he must recite Tefilas HaDerech even though daylight has not yet begun, for this constitutes a complete interruption of intent. This is also somewhat implied by Biur HaGra, who writes that it is comparable to Bircas HaTorah, etc. Regarding Bircas HaTorah, we rule above in siman 47 that one must recite it immediately upon rising. However, the wording of Peri Megadim, who writes that he should recite it every morning, somewhat implies otherwise. Perhaps he used this expression because ideally it is proper to depart in daylight. Even were one to say that it is preferable to delay Tefilas HaDerech until daylight, if a circumstance may arise in which he will then be unable to recite it, such as when he will already be within a parsah of his home, it is preferable that he recite it before daylight. So it appears to me at first glance.
