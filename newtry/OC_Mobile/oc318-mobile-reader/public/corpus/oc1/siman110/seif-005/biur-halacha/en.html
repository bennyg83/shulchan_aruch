"He must return and pray," etc. — see in Mishna Berurah the reason; and therefore it appears that all the more so if he lodged at an inn and rose before morning to travel on his way, he must bless tefillat ha-derekh even though day has not yet dawned, for this constitutes complete distraction of the mind; and similarly it is implied a little in Biur HaGra from what he writes that it is like the blessing on Torah, etc. And in Beit Yosef we rule above in siman 47 that he must bless birchot ha-Torah immediately when he wakes; however the wording of Peri Megadim, who wrote that every morning he should say it, implies somewhat not as we say. Perhaps he used this language because ab initio it is proper to set out with a good heart; and even if one would say it is better to wait to say tefillat ha-derekh until daylight — nevertheless if it happens to him that then he cannot say it, such as if then he will be within a parsah of his home, it is better that he say it before day has dawned, as appears to me.
