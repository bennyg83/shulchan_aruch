[8] "Lead us," etc. — "all who see us," and "return us," etc. "to my house in peace, for You," etc. — thus is the wording of the Rosh; and Ma'adanei Melech wrote: although in the Talmud and Rif it is absent, nevertheless the matter is correct — up to here; and similarly I saw in the novelae of Rashba, R' Yehuda, and Abudraham. He wrote in Sefer Tzidah la-Derekh 9: some are accustomed after tefillat ha-derekh to say "yoshev ba-seter Elyon," and likewise Shir la-ma'alot "Esa einai," etc.; and similarly men of deeds say when leaving the city gate: "Beit Ya'akov lekhu venelkha ba-or Heaven," for all the nations walk in the name of their Heaven, and we shall walk in the name of our Heaven forever. Shelah wrote 115: if tefillat ha-derekh is fluent in his mouth, then he will go securely on his way; and if not, he will not guard himself from danger, Heaven forbid — and on this every person of understanding should reflect; and this is the explanation of "the king in your encampment and go." Emek Berachah wrote: when one goes on the road it is good to give charity, as it says "righteousness shall go before him," etc.; and it is good that he also study before his departure. Shelah wrote 185: there is a great obligation on one who travels on the road to occupy himself with Torah; therefore he should not cease reflecting on words of Torah, to fulfill what is said "in your walking it shall guide you"; and he should learn by heart what he can, or occupy himself with some psalms or statements to explain them — up to here. And in the Talmud Taanis 5 it says looking is forbidden; and it is possible that sitting in a wagon is permitted. A person should not eat more than two meals' worth because of intestinal illness.
