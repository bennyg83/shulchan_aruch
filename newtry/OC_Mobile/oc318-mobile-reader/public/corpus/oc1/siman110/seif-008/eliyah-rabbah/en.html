[8] That You lead us, etc. The complete text is: "and return us, etc., to my home in peace, for You are, etc." This is the wording of the Rosh. Maadanei Melech writes that although this phrase is absent from the Gemara and the Rif, it is nevertheless an appropriate addition. I also found it in the chiddushim of the Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah, and in Abudraham. Sefer Tzeidah LaDerech, folio 9, writes that some are accustomed to recite after Tefillas HaDerech "He who dwells in the shelter of the Most High" and "A song of ascents, I lift my eyes, etc." People of piety also recite upon leaving the city gate: "House of Yaakov, go and let us walk in the light of Hashem. For all the nations will walk each in the name of its god, but we will walk in the name of Hashem our God forever and ever." The Shelah, folio 115, writes that if Tefillas HaDerech is fluent in a person's mouth, he may travel securely. If not, he should guard himself from danger, Heaven forbid. Every discerning person should take this to heart. This is the meaning of the teaching, "Consult your Creator and then depart." Emek Berachah writes that when setting out on a journey it is proper to give tzedakah, as it is stated, "Righteousness will go before him, etc." It is also proper to study Torah before departure. The Shelah, folio 185, writes that a traveler has a great obligation to engage in Torah study. He should therefore never cease reflecting on Torah matters, thereby fulfilling the verse, "When you walk, it will guide you." He should study orally whatever he can, or occupy himself with psalms or teachings and explain them. The Gemara in Taanis 5 says that analytical study is forbidden while traveling, though perhaps it is permitted when one is seated in a wagon. A person should not eat more than in years of famine, because of intestinal illness.
