That You should lead us, etc. The wording continues: “In the eyes of all who see us, and return us, etc., to my home in peace, for You are, etc.” This is the text of Rosh. Maadanei Melech writes that although these words do not appear in the Gemara or Rif, the thought is proper. I likewise found this formulation in the novellae of Rashba, Rabbeinu Yonah, and Avudraham. Tzeidah LaDerech, page 9, writes that some are accustomed to recite after Tefillas HaDerech the psalm Yoshev BeSeiser Elyon and also Shir LaMaalos Esa Einai. Men of action recite, upon leaving the city gate, “House of Jacob, go and let us walk in the light of Hashem. For all the nations walk in the name of their god, but we shall walk in the name of Hashem our God forever and ever.” Shelah, page 115, writes that if Tefillas HaDerech is fluent in a person’s mouth, he may proceed confidently on his way. If it is not fluent, he should guard himself from danger, Heaven forbid. Every discerning person should take this to heart. This is the meaning of, “Consult your Creator and then depart.” Emek Berachah writes that when setting out on a journey it is proper to give charity, as the verse states, “Righteousness shall go before him.” It is also proper to study Torah before leaving. Shelah, page 185, writes that there is a great obligation upon one traveling on the road to occupy himself with Torah. He should therefore not cease reflecting upon words of Torah, thereby fulfilling, “When you walk, it shall guide you.” He should study orally whatever he can, or occupy himself with psalms or sayings and explain them. The Gemara in Taanis 5 states that analytical study is forbidden while traveling. Perhaps one who sits in a wagon is permitted. A person should not eat more than two portions during a famine, because of intestinal illness.
