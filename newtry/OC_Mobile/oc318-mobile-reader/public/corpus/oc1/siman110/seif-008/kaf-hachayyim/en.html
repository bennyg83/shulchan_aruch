8) There: One does not recite Habineinu during the rainy season, etc. Rabbeinu Manoach writes that the Gemara asks both concerning the rainy season and concerning Motzaei Shabbos and Motzaei Yom Tov why the necessary addition cannot be incorporated, and answers that the person may become confused. This implies that if he is certain that he will not become confused, he is permitted to recite Habineinu, as the Gemara says concerning the priestly blessing. End quote. Maran cites this in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 4, and writes concerning it: "I sought a colleague who agrees with him but did not find one." End quote. Magen Avraham cites this in the present siman, seif katan 4. Pri Chadash, paragraph 1, likewise rules that even if one is certain that he will not become confused, he must recite the complete Shemoneh Esrei; see there. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, nevertheless writes that if the text is written before him so that he will not become confused, and time is extremely pressing such that otherwise he will not pray at all, he may recite Habineinu even during the rainy season and mention rain; see there. However, in light of what we wrote above; that Maran states that he found no colleague for Rabbeinu Manoach, and Pri Chadash rules accordingly; it appears difficult to permit this even in pressing circumstances, etc. Rather, he should hasten to recite the prayer properly within its time. If no time remains, as where he erred or circumstances beyond his control prevented him, he should recite tashlumin with the following prayer, as stated in siman 108; see there. In any event, we already wrote above in paragraph 5 that nowadays the practice is not to recite Habineinu, even while traveling or in pressing circumstances. Rather, if people are occupied, they hasten to pray within the proper time and with composure; if circumstances beyond their control prevent them and no time remains, they make up the prayer with the following prayer.
