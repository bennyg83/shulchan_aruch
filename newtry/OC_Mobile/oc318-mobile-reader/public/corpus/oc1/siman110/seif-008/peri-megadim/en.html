One who enters. See Taz. It is possible that 'prohibition and permission' refers to matters of forbidden and permitted foods and to monetary law. 'Halachic ruling' applies to Shabbos and similar matters, and in Even HaEzer to gittin and the like. 'Study' applies even when the matter has no practical legal consequence. See the explanation in Beit Yosef, Levush, Eliyah Rabbah, and Taz. Regarding the statement, 'I toil and they toil; I receive reward and they do not receive reward. I run and they run; I run toward life in the World to Come, and they run toward the pit of destruction,' in my humble opinion, based on Rabbeinu Yonah, the repetition of 'I' teaches that when I see days passing I take care to run and prepare provisions for the journey. This is the meaning of 'I run toward the World to Come,' analogous to 'it hastens toward its place, where it rises.' They, however, do not prepare provisions, because they believe there is no reward and punishment and that the grave alone is their permanent home. Why, then, should they perform mitzvot? This is the meaning of 'their inward thought is that their houses will endure forever.' They believe so, but it is not so, for the soul is taken out and judged. See Levush.
