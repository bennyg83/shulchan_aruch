One who enters. See Taz. It is possible that 'prohibition and permission' refers to matters of forbidden foods and monetary law. 'Halachic ruling' applies to Shabbos and similar matters, and in Even HaEzer to gittin and the like. 'Study' applies even where the matter has no practical legal consequence. See the explanation in Beit Yosef, Levush, Eliyah Rabbah, and Taz. Regarding the statement, 'I toil and they toil; I receive reward and they do not receive reward. I run and they run; I run to the life of the World to Come, and they run to the pit of destruction,' see the commentary of Rabbeinu Yonah. The repetition teaches that when I see that days have passed, I take care to run and prepare provisions for the road. This is the meaning of 'I run to the World to Come,' similar to 'it hastens toward its place, where it rises.' They, however, do not prepare provisions, for they believe that there is no reward and punishment, but only the pit of the grave, which they regard as their eternal home. Why, then, should they perform mitzvos? This is the meaning of 'their inward thought is that their houses will endure forever.' They think so, but it is not true, for the soul is taken out and judged. See Levush.
