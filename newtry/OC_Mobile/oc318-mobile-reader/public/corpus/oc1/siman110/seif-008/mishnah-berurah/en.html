(34) One who enters the study hall — the Ari, may his memory be for a blessing, would say it every morning, and afterward would say: "for the the Master gives wisdom; from His mouth come knowledge and understanding; open my eyes and let me behold wonders from Your Torah."

(35) He should pray "Yehi ratzon" — and it has no closing blessing. Taz wrote that the same applies to one who sits to study alone, and especially if he has attained [the level of] issuing rulings — he must pray that he not err in study and in ruling, declaring impure pure and forbidden permitted; and it is good to say one short formula that includes much, and this is it: "Yehi ratzon — the Master our Heaven, may the light of Your Torah illuminate my eyes and save us from every stumbling block and error, whether in laws of prohibition and permission, whether in monetary laws, whether in issuing rulings, whether in study; open my eyes and let me behold wonders from Your Torah; and what I have already erred in — establish me on the truth; and do not remove from my mouth a word of truth for very long, for the the Master gives wisdom; from His mouth come knowledge and understanding." And when he studies in a group, he must also ask that he not rejoice in their stumbling block and they not rejoice in his stumbling block, as it is stated in the Gemara.

(36) And upon his departure, etc. — Rambam wrote in his commentary on the Mishnah that these are two obligatory prayers. And he may pray sitting or standing as happens to him, and he should not turn his face to the east or to the west, and he should not bow, etc.; see there, and Eliyah Rabbah and other Acharonim brought this.

(37) He should say "Modah," etc. — and the same applies to one who engages in Torah alone all day; he must say thus every evening after finishing his study [Magen Avraham].
