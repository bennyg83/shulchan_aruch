(8) There — one does not pray Havinenu during the rainy season, etc. R' Menuch wrote: in the Gemara it challenges [the distinction] between the rainy season and Motzei Shabbat and Yom Tov and includes it from the general principle, and answers "perhaps one will become distracted" — implying that if one is assured one will not become distracted, one is permitted, as we say regarding raising the hands [for the Priestly Blessing] — end quote. And our teacher of blessed memory brought it in Kesef Mishnah ch. 2 of Hilchot Tefillah law 4, and wrote on it: "I sought a companion for it and did not find one" — end quote; and Magen Avraham in this siman sec. 4, and likewise Peri Chadash sec. 1 — that even if one is assured he will not be troubled, he should pray the full Eighteen — see there. However, Chesed LeAvraham sec. 1 wrote that if it is written before him that he will not be troubled and the time is very pressing so that if he does not say this he will not pray at all, he may say it even during the rainy season and mention rain — see there. But according to what our teacher of blessed memory wrote on the words of Rambam that he did not find a companion for it, and likewise Peri Toar, it appears the matter is difficult to permit even at a time of pressing need, etc.; rather one should urge oneself to pray the prayer at its proper time, and if time remains — or such as if one erred or was compelled — one prays make-up [prayer] in the prayer after it, as written in siman 108 — see there. And in any case, we already wrote above in note 5 that the custom is not to pray Havinenu nowadays, even on the road and at a time of pressing need; rather they urge themselves to pray at its time with a settled mind. If they are troubled and if they were compelled and no time remains, they complete [the prayer] in the prayer after it.
