1) [Seif 1] In pressing circumstances, etc. The same applies if the time for prayer is about to pass. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Olas Tamid, paragraph 3, who writes that this means during the summer; Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 24, paragraph 31; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, explains that the reason is that one must complete the entire prayer within its proper time. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 3, likewise writes that one must complete the entire prayer within its proper time. He writes that this is contrary to the work Batei Kenesiyos, siman 89, which remains uncertain on this point; see there.
2) There: In pressing circumstances, etc. The same applies to one whose speech is too limited to recite the full prayer. Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 2; Magen Avraham, seif katan 2; Solet Belulah there; Shitilei Zeitim, paragraph 3.
3) There: In pressing circumstances, etc. If one recited Habineinu when there were no pressing circumstances, Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, writes that after the fact he fulfilled his obligation and need not pray again; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, disputes his ruling and writes that he did not fulfill his obligation. Rabbi Lechem Yehudah, on chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 2, likewise writes that he did not fulfill his obligation. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, however, writes that he did fulfill it, because in most matters an after-the-fact situation and pressing circumstances are treated alike. He writes that Eliyah Rabbah's position is not conclusive; see there. Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim, siman 54, page 92a, likewise writes that he fulfilled his obligation after the fact. Chikrei Lev writes there that he found this novel ruling in no authority, but he overlooked the aforementioned words of Olas Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah, who disagree on this very point. Chayei Olam on Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, likewise writes that he fulfilled his obligation after the fact and that the words of Eliyah Rabbah are not compelling; see there.
4) There: And he fears that they will interrupt him. This also refers to the preceding case: he is traveling and fears that they will interrupt him. Without that concern, he should pray while walking or while seated, as stated in siman 94, seifim 4 and 6. Bach likewise writes this in the name of Tosafos; Magen Avraham, seif katan 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
5) There: After the first three blessings, he recites Habineinu, etc. It is one blessing that encompasses all the middle blessings. There are several versions of the text of this blessing, as may be seen in the Gemara, Berachos 29a; Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 3; Tur in the present siman; Levush; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; and Shiurei Berachah, paragraph 1. It appears that this is why the practice nowadays is not to recite Habineinu. See below, paragraph 8.
6) There: One must recite it while standing. Since it is brief, standing will not distract him, unlike during the other prayers. Magen Avraham, seif katan 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 3. Maamar Mordechai writes that this is contrary to Pri Chadash, who writes that while traveling one may recite it even while walking; see there.
7) There: One does not recite Habineinu during the rainy season, etc. This is because one must say "Grant dew and rain," while on Motzaei Shabbos and Motzaei Yom Tov one must recite Havdalah. Everything stated in Habineinu in abbreviated form corresponds to the blessings of Shemoneh Esrei, whereas Havdalah is not an independent blessing; it is therefore not mentioned in Habineinu. Tur, Beis Yosef, and Levush; Taz, seif katan 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. If one nevertheless recited Habineinu during the rainy season or on Motzaei Shabbos or Motzaei Yom Tov, he fulfilled his obligation after the fact. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
8) There: One does not recite Habineinu during the rainy season, etc. Rabbeinu Manoach writes that the Gemara asks both concerning the rainy season and concerning Motzaei Shabbos and Motzaei Yom Tov why the necessary addition cannot be incorporated, and answers that the person may become confused. This implies that if he is certain that he will not become confused, he is permitted to recite Habineinu, as the Gemara says concerning the priestly blessing. End quote. Maran cites this in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 4, and writes concerning it: "I sought a colleague who agrees with him but did not find one." End quote. Magen Avraham cites this in the present siman, seif katan 4. Pri Chadash, paragraph 1, likewise rules that even if one is certain that he will not become confused, he must recite the complete Shemoneh Esrei; see there. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, nevertheless writes that if the text is written before him so that he will not become confused, and time is extremely pressing such that otherwise he will not pray at all, he may recite Habineinu even during the rainy season and mention rain; see there. However, in light of what we wrote above; that Maran states that he found no colleague for Rabbeinu Manoach, and Pri Chadash rules accordingly; it appears difficult to permit this even in pressing circumstances, etc. Rather, he should hasten to recite the prayer properly within its time. If no time remains, as where he erred or circumstances beyond his control prevented him, he should recite tashlumin with the following prayer, as stated in siman 108; see there. In any event, we already wrote above in paragraph 5 that nowadays the practice is not to recite Habineinu, even while traveling or in pressing circumstances. Rather, if people are occupied, they hasten to pray within the proper time and with composure; if circumstances beyond their control prevent them and no time remains, they make up the prayer with the following prayer.
