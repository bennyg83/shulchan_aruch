Regarding what he wrote, "They should be judged, and not according to their constellation, etc.," the Beit Yosef wrote similarly. This is puzzling, for what connection is there between "those who err in judgment" and "not according to their constellation"? I saw in Malbushei Yom Tov that he writes that there is a scribal error in the Beit Yosef, and the text should read: "According to Your will they should be judged," meaning that those who err should be judged according to Your will and not according to their constellation, etc. This is the correct reading. [4] During the rainy season, etc. We are concerned that he may err regarding "Vesein tal umatar." On Motzaei Shabbos, however, Havdalah appears at the beginning of the blessing, where error would seemingly not apply. Nevertheless, we are concerned that people may think it is a separate blessing. Gemara. The Levush adds that one might therefore err in the regular Amidah and recite "Atah chonein" as one blessing and "Atah chonantanu" as a separate blessing. This is difficult, for if so, we should say the same regarding the request for rain. Why, then, does the Gemara give the reason that he may err? It is strained to answer that Havdalah is different because one Tanna maintains that it is recited as a separate blessing, and therefore one may err, whereas no authority says this regarding the request for rain. The Perishah answers that only Havdalah, which is unrelated to the blessing of Atah Chonein, could be considered a separate blessing. The request for rain, however, is germane to the phrase "and satisfy us from Your land's goodness." See below, at the beginning of siman 294. The Beit Yosef answers that the concern is not that one will recite Atah Chonantanu as an independent blessing in the regular Amidah, since throughout the year the custom is to include it in Chonein HaDaas. Rather, the concern is that one may mistakenly think it is like other blessings for which omission requires repetition. Regarding the request for rain, no such concern exists, because its omission truly does require repetition. This explanation is correct. However, I found in Sefer Zikaron by the gaon Rabbi Yosef HaKohen, paragraph 255: "If he recites both the request for rain and Havdalah, they will appear to be two independent blessings." Thus, this reason is in fact stated even regarding the request for rain, and all the answers above fall away. It therefore appears to me that when the Gemara gives another reason for the request for rain, it is responding to Mar Zutra's challenge that Havdalah should be recited, and it does not accept the reason that it may be mistaken for a separate blessing. According to our ruling, however, which follows Shmuel that Havdalah is not recited because it would appear to be a separate blessing, the same applies to the request for rain. The poskim nevertheless gave the reason that one may err regarding the request for rain as an additional consideration, meaning that even one who permits Havdalah agrees that the request for rain is not recited. This also explains the implication of the Shulchan Aruch that even one who is certain not to err does not mention the request for rain. The Kesef Mishneh, chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, rules similarly. At first glance, this contradicts the ruling below in siman 128 concerning Bircas Kohanim, but according to my explanation it is somewhat resolved, because here there is the additional concern that it will appear to be a separate blessing.
