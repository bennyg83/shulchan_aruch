[1] "At a time of extenuating circumstance," etc. And if he prayed after the fact not at a time of extenuating circumstance — Olat Tamid wrote it appears he has fulfilled, and he need not return and pray; for if not, why would Abbaye rebuke him? He would need to return and pray — up to here. And one can reject that he rebuked so that one not accustom oneself to pray Havineinu at home when one cannot direct one's heart with long intention, even though it is permitted by law at a time of extenuating circumstance; rather it is better for him to restrain himself and see that he can direct his heart — and similarly is implied by the words of Semag. Also study is required: for from what the Talmud says [Berachos] 30b, "when he arrives at his house he need not return to pray" — it is implied specifically when he prayed at a time of extenuating circumstance he need not return to pray, as the Rif and Rosh learned from this, that specifically at a time of extenuating circumstance one may say Havineinu; but when he prayed the blessing of Havineinu he must return to pray — for if not, why did the Talmud teach a distinction in law between a time of extenuating circumstance after the fact and not ab initio? And examine closely.
