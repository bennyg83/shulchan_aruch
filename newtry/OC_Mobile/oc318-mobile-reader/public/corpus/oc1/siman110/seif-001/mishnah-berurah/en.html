(1) In a pressing circumstance, etc. Outside a pressing circumstance, the Gemara states that Abaye cursed one who recites Havineinu. Nevertheless, after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation and need not pray again. Eliyah Rabbah is stringent even after the fact. The later authorities write that if he sees that the time for prayer will pass if he recites the full prayer, he may initially recite Havineinu. This applies during the summer.
(2) And he fears they will interrupt him. This also refers back to the preceding case, where he is traveling and fears that passersby will interrupt him. Otherwise, even while traveling it is preferable to recite all eighteen blessings while walking or sitting, as explained above in siman 94, rather than recite Havineinu while standing. See Peri Megadim.
(3) Havineinu. It incorporates all the intermediate blessings. See the Tur and Beit Yosef for its text and explanation.
(4) Standing. Since in a pressing circumstance it substitutes for the full eighteen blessings, as it incorporates all of them. In this respect they gave this prayer greater force than the full Shemoneh Esrei, which one may recite while walking on a journey, because if he were required to stand he would be distracted and unable to concentrate. Havineinu, however, is short, so standing will not distract him. If he transgressed and recited it while seated, Peri Megadim remains uncertain whether he must repeat it while standing upon reaching home.
(5) And he does not recite, etc. Because during the rainy season he must say “grant dew and rain” in Bircas HaShanim, and on Motzaei Shabbos and Yom Tov he must recite Havdalah. Even if he wishes to incorporate these into Havineinu, he may not do so. See Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham for the reason. Peri Megadim is uncertain whether, even after the fact, if he recited Havineinu on these days and incorporated them, he may not have fulfilled his obligation because he violated the enactment of Chazal. See Biur Halachah regarding what it cites in the name of Rabbi Akiva Eiger.
(6) Havineinu. See Chayei Adam, who writes that only Havineinu is prohibited. It is permitted, however, for one who is ill or in an extremely pressing circumstance to abbreviate each individual blessing while including the request for rain and Havdalah. See there, klal 24, for the text.
(7) During the rainy season. Rather, he recites the full eighteen blessings. If he was interrupted, see above, siman 104, seif 5, and the Mishnah Berurah there.
