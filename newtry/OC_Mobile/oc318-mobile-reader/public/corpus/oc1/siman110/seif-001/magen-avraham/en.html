(א) At a time of pressing need. Or the hour is passing [thus].

(ב) "And he fears they will interrupt him." This also refers to the above, when he is on the road and fears they will interrupt him; but in any case he prays while walking or seated, as written in siman 94 secs. 4 and 6; and likewise the Bach in the name of Tosafos — the language of the Rambam — or if his tongue is short from praying, he prays Havinenu.

(ג) "Standing." Since it is short, one will not be troubled by standing, unlike other prayers.

(ד) "During the rainy season." Because one must say the [rain] request in the blessing of the years, and it challenges and includes it that he should say "and satisfy us with the goodness of Your land, and grant dew and rain," and answers "one will become distracted" — that is, lest he err and not know to return to his prayer and skip or add (Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishnah, not like the Bach), since the words are brief. And Rambam wrote that if one is assured one will not become distracted, he is permitted, as written in siman 128 sec. 2; and I sought a companion for it and did not find one (Kesef Mishnah). And see there.

(ה) "During the rainy season." Rather he prays the Eighteen; and if they interrupted him — see siman 104 sec. 5.

(ו) "And not at Motzei [Shabbat]." Because one must say havdalah in "Who grants knowledge," and it challenges and includes it; and here there is no concern he will err, since it is the beginning of the blessing. And the Mahari answered so that it should not appear havdalah is a blessing on its own. "And if you ask" regarding the [rain] request too — say this reason. One can say: regarding havdalah, since there is R' Akiva who holds it is a blessing on its own, they will come to say the law is like R' Akiva; not so regarding the [rain] request — there is no one who holds thus, and they will not come to say so — as explained above, not like the Bach.
