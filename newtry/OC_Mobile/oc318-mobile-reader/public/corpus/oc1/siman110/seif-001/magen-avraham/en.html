In a pressing circumstance. Or when the time for prayer is about to pass.
And he fears that they will interrupt him. This also refers back to the previous case, when he is traveling and fears that they will interrupt him. Otherwise, he should pray while walking or sitting, as stated in siman 94, seifim 4 and 6. The Bach likewise wrote this in the name of Tosafos. The Rambam's wording, 'or if his time is limited from praying,' means that he should recite Havineinu.
Standing. Since it is a short prayer, standing will not distract him, unlike the other prayers.
During the rainy season. This is because he must request rain in Bircas HaShanim. The Gemara asks that he should incorporate it by saying, 'Satisfy us with the goodness of Your land, and grant dew and rain.' It answers that he may become confused, meaning that he may err and not know how to return to the prayer, and may omit or add words. This is the view of the Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh, unlike the Bach, since the added words are brief. The Rambam wrote that one who is certain that he will not become confused may do so, as stated in siman 128, seif 2. I sought support for this ruling but did not find it. See what I wrote there.
During the rainy season. Rather, he must recite the full eighteen blessings. If he was interrupted, see siman 104, seif 5.
Nor on Motzaei Shabbos. This is because he must recite Havdalah in the blessing of Chonen HaDaas. The Gemara asks that he should incorporate it, and here there is no concern that he will err, since it is at the beginning of the blessing. Rabbeinu Yonah answers that this is so it should not appear that Havdalah is a blessing of its own. One may ask that the same reasoning should apply to the request for rain. The answer is that regarding Havdalah, since Rabbi Akiva holds that it is a separate blessing, people might conclude that the halachah follows Rabbi Akiva. There is no such opinion regarding the request for rain, so no one would reach that conclusion. This appears correct, unlike the Bach.
