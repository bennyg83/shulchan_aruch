Or if he will be unable, etc. This means because of the extent of his preoccupations, or because he is ill. Nowadays the practice is not to recite Havineinu because of preoccupation. It appears to me that the reason is as follows. At first glance, the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch here is difficult in light of what appears above in siman 101, seif 1, where it states that if one cannot direct his heart throughout all the blessings, he should at least direct his heart during Avos. This implies that he may nevertheless recite the entire prayer, since he is compelled and unable to maintain concentration, and concentration during Avos suffices. However, one may distinguish that here the case is one in which he can concentrate during the seven abridged blessings. They therefore permitted him to recite Havineinu, and no more, so that he can encompass the entire Shemoneh Esrei in abbreviated form and with concentration. There, however, the case is one in which he cannot concentrate even during these seven blessings, and they therefore did not permit him to abbreviate. Accordingly, it is understandable why the practice nowadays is not to recite Havineinu, for we are concerned that one will not concentrate even during the seven blessings, similar to what the Rama writes there in seif 1 in the gloss. Alternatively, if we were to abbreviate nowadays because of preoccupation, we would never recite a complete prayer because of our many preoccupations, due to our sins.
And he does not recite Havineinu, etc. Nor does he recite it on Motzaei Shabbos or Motzaei Yom Tov. See the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who is uncertain regarding Motzaei Shabbos and Motzaei Yom Tov when the time for prayer is about to pass. It may be that in such a case it is preferable to recite Havineinu without mentioning Havdalah, since after the fact one generally fulfills his obligation even without Havdalah, rather than not pray at all.
