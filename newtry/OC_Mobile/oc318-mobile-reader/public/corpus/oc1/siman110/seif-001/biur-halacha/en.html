"Or if he cannot," etc. — that is, because of his many distractions, or he is sick; and nowadays people are not accustomed to pray Havineinu due to distraction. It seems to me the reason for this is that apparently it is difficult against the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch above in siman 101 seif 1, where it states that if one cannot direct one's heart in all of them, one should direct one's heart at least in the Avot; and it implies that by this one is automatically permitted to pray the entire prayer, since he is under duress (anus) and cannot direct his heart. But one could say that here it deals with one who can direct his heart in the seven blessings; therefore they permitted him to pray Havineinu and no more, so that he includes all of Shemoneh Esrei in brief form with intention. But there it deals with one who cannot direct his heart even in those seven blessings; therefore they did not permit him to shorten. Thus it works out that today people are not accustomed to Havineinu, since we are concerned that even the seven he will not direct his heart — and as Rama wrote there in seif 1 in his gloss. Alternatively, because if we were to shorten as today due to distraction, we would never pray a complete prayer due to most distraction, God forbid.

"And one does not pray Havineinu," etc. — and not on Motzei Shabbos and Yom Tov. See in the novelae of R' Akiva Eiger, who is uncertain regarding Motzei Shabbos and Yom Tov if the time passes — one could say that in this case on Motzei Shabbos and Yom Tov it is better that he pray Havineinu and not mention Havdalah, for at least after the fact he has fulfilled in any case without Havdalah, than that he not pray at all.
