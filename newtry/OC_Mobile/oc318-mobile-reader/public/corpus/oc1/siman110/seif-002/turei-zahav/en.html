The Beit Yosef writes that the Tur's wording implies that he understood this distinction to be the Rambam's own ruling, but this is incorrect, because it is an explicit Gemara in the chapter Hayah Korei. There the Gemara asks: they pray the full Amidah, but was it not taught that they pray an abbreviated Amidah? Rav Sheshes answers that there is no contradiction. One teaching follows Rabban Gamliel and the other follows Rabbi Yehoshua, as taught in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar: Rabban Gamliel says that every day a person recites the full Amidah, while Rabbi Yehoshua says an abbreviated Amidah. The Gemara challenges that if the ruling follows Rabbi Yehoshua, why mention laborers, since every person may recite the abbreviated prayer? It therefore concludes that both teachings follow Rabban Gamliel. There is still no contradiction: one refers to laborers working for wages, while the other refers to laborers working only for their meals, in which case they recite the full Amidah. The Gemara supports this from a baraisa that makes the same distinction regarding Bircas HaMazon. Yet the Rif and the Rosh do not cite this distinction at all regarding Havineinu, but only regarding Bircas HaMazon. How, then, could the Beit Yosef say that the distinction concerning Havineinu is explicit in the Gemara? Rather, the correct approach is as follows. The primary law of Havineinu appears in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar. The conclusion there indicates that Havineinu is recited only in pressing circumstances, from the statement that Abaye cursed one who recited Havineinu in the city, according to one version in Tosafos, as cited by the Rif and the Rosh. We must therefore say that the final ruling does not follow either extreme view, that according to Rabban Gamliel one must always recite the full Amidah, while according to Rabbi Yehoshua one always recites Havineinu. Rather, the Gemara rules that in pressing circumstances one may rely on Rabbi Yehoshua, while outside pressing circumstances we follow Rabban Gamliel. In the chapter Hayah Korei, the Gemara merely analyzes which Tanna authored the baraisa requiring laborers to recite Havineinu. That discussion does not distinguish between pressing and ordinary circumstances, but only identifies the underlying tannaitic view. It therefore asks that if the baraisa follows Rabbi Yehoshua, why specify laborers? The Gemara there does not reach the later principle distinguishing between pressing and ordinary circumstances. Once the Gemara's final ruling adopts that distinction, no further distinction remains. This is how the poskim rule. Consequently, the Rambam's distinction between laborers working for wages and those not working for wages is difficult, since he himself already distinguishes between pressing and ordinary circumstances. Once that distinction is accepted, there should be no second distinction, because the Gemara's ruling turns specifically on pressing circumstances. The Tur therefore writes that perhaps the Rambam regards hired laborers as being in pressing circumstances. Laborers working only for their meal are not regarded as being in pressing circumstances. It follows that the Tur was correct to present this ruling specifically in the Rambam's name. This appears correct, with Hashem's help.
