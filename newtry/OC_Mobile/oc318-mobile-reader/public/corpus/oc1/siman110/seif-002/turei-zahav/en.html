If he does not give, etc. In the Tur likewise in the name of the Rambam, and he wrote on it: and it is possible that he considers workers as a time of pressing need; and in Beit Yosef from the words of our teacher it appears that he holds these are the words of the Rambam — and it is not so, but rather an arranged Gemara in the chapter "He was reading," and so it is stated, etc. — end quote. And in truth Beit Yosef did not look into this as much as needed, for in the chapter "He was reading" (Berachos folio 17) it is stated thus: and they pray the eighteen[-blessing prayer], and it was taught: a short eighteen — R. Sheishes: there is no difficulty: here is Rabbenu Gershom, here is Rabbenu Yonah, as it was taught in the chapter "These are said": Rabbenu Gershom — every day a person prays eighteen; Rabbenu Yonah — a short eighteen. And he challenges: if Rabbenu Yonah, why specify workers? Even every person as well. Rather both are Rabbenu Gershom, and there is no difficulty: here regarding those working for their wages, here regarding those working at their meal — for then they pray eighteen. And he brings proof from a baraisa that likewise distinguishes between those working at their meal or not regarding Birkas HaMazon; and the Rif and the Rosh did not bring this distinction at all regarding Havineinu, only regarding "Baruch." If so, how did he write that the Talmud is arranged to distinguish regarding Havineinu? Rather it appears that is not the way — for in the morning prayer is the main law of Havineinu, and there it is proven in the conclusion that one does not pray Havineinu except at a time of pressing need, from that of Abaye who cursed one who prays Havineinu in the bathhouse, per one version of Tosafos, and as the Rif and Rosh wrote there. If so, one must say that the Talmud ruled there not like one who says that for Rabbenu Gershom one must always [pray] eighteen, and for Rabbenu Yonah always Havineinu; and the Talmud decides for halachah that at a time of pressing need one may rely on Rabbenu Yonah, and when not at a time of pressing need we act like Rabbenu Gershom. And in the chapter "He was reading" the Gemara discusses which of the tannaim does that baraisa that they say Havineinu come from — there it did not descend at all to distinguish between pressing need or not, but only in the words of the tannaim themselves does the Gemara discuss which tanna it comes from; and on this we challenge: if Rabbenu Yonah, why specify workers? And they did not adopt there this view that there is a distinction between pressing need or not. But in the Talmud's conclusion, where they descend to this to decide between pressing need or not, there is no further distinction afterward — so we hold, and likewise the poskim distinguish thus. And it is found that what the Rambam wrote to distinguish between those working for their wages or not — and he already distinguished himself between pressing need or not — is difficult for him: since there is a distinction between pressing need or not, there is no further distinction, for these rulings of the Talmud divide only between a time of pressing need. And on this our teacher the Tur wrote: and it is possible that he considers workers as a time of pressing need — meaning this distinction of those working for their wages is like a time of pressing need, but for the wage of their meal it is like not a time of pressing need. And it is found that our teacher the Tur properly wrote this law in the name of the Rambam specifically, as explained — correct, with Heaven's help.
