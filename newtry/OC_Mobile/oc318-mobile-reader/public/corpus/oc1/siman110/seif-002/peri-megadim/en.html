If — Taz — he generalized his words, certainly there are two baraitot; therefore this opinion is according to Rabbeinu Gershom, who holds that even in extenuating circumstances one does not pray Havineinu, and the other baraita of Havineinu is also according to Rabbeinu Gershom — only regarding their reward they were lenient because of the loss to the proprietor; but if according to Rabbi Yehudah, every person even without extenuating circumstance likewise. But for halacha in siman 29 seif 1 we rule that extenuating circumstance [applies to] every person for Havineinu; if so, certainly laborers at their meal are also considered extenuating circumstance (and all the more so he [the employer]), and they are permitted to pray Havineinu. And why did the Rambam, of blessed memory, write specifically regarding their reward — because this is Rabbeinu Gershom, and this is what Tur wrote. And it is possible that the Rambam considers laborers specifically regarding their reward at a time of extenuating circumstance; but at their meal, no — and this is difficult. Nevertheless, two baraitot — therefore according to Rabbeinu Gershom, etc., that this resolution is in the Talmud for the Amoraim. Nevertheless, what did Taz write — Rif and Rosh did not bring this distinction only regarding the study hall and not regarding Havineinu — it is difficult, for Rif one could say thus; but Rosh copied and they pray eighteen — and how can this be conceived? And see Perishah, and one need not extend. And there is uncertainty in Rambam chapter 2 Laws of Prayer 2 he rules like Rabbi Akiva — if so he contradicts the baraitot thus.
