If. See Taz. The essence of his words is that the two baraisos must necessarily be understood as follows. The teaching concerning the full Shemoneh Esrei follows Rabban Gamliel, who maintains that even in a pressing situation one does not recite Havineinu. The other teaching, which permits Havineinu, also follows Rabban Gamliel, but the Sages were lenient for hired workers because of the employer's loss. It cannot follow Rabbi Yehoshua, for according to him every person may recite Havineinu even without pressing circumstances. As a matter of practical halachah, however, in siman 89, seif 1, we rule that every person in pressing circumstances may recite Havineinu. Certainly, then, workers who receive their meals from the employer are also considered to be in pressing circumstances, and all the more so, and may recite Havineinu. Why, then, did the Rambam specify only hired workers, a formulation that fits Rabban Gamliel? This is what the Tur means when he writes that perhaps the Rambam considers only hired workers to be in pressing circumstances, but not workers who receive their meals, and this is difficult. In any event, the two baraisos must necessarily follow Rabban Gamliel, for this resolution is given by the Amoraim in the Gemara. Still, the Taz's statement that the Rif and Rosh cited this distinction only regarding Bircas HaMazon and not regarding Havineinu is difficult. This can be said regarding the Rif, but the Rosh explicitly copied that they recite the full Shemoneh Esrei, and how is that case to be understood? See Peri Chadash. There is no need to elaborate. The Rambam's ruling in chapter 2 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 2, also requires study, since he rules like Rabbi Akiva. If so, the baraisos should be established accordingly.
