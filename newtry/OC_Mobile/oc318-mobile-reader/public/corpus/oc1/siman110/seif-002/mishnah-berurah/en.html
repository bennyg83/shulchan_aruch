(8) They recite the full eighteen blessings. Three times each day, like everyone else, because the employer does not object to their delay since he does not pay them wages.
(9) But they do not, etc. Because this entails a substantial delay and the employer objects.
(10) And if he gives, etc. For then the employer objects if they delay to recite all eighteen blessings, so the workers are considered to be in a pressing circumstance, as explained above.
(11) Havineinu. This applies only during the summer, as explained above in seif 1.
(12) That they recite the full eighteen blessings. The same applies to the entire prayer service, which they recite like everyone else. Lechem Chamudos writes that they are likewise permitted to go to the synagogue to pray with a minyan. See Magen Avraham, who limits this to a place where employers are not ordinarily particular about this. Nevertheless, they should not serve as chazzan, as Peri Megadim writes. Peri Chadash states that nowadays they may also serve as chazzan. It appears to me that one need not be stringent where this will not cause any additional delay.
