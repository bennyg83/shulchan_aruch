And a bandage. See Ateres Zekeinim and Magen Avraham, siman 162, seif katan 18, in the name of Bach. Lechem Chamudos answers that below the case is one who does not mind the bandage, whereas here the case is one who does. There is a significant practical difference where the bandage covers most of the hand: according to Lechem Chamudos it constitutes an interposition even when one does not mind it. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. According to Bach, it does not constitute an interposition, because it is as though the hand were severed at the place of the bandage: one washes what remains, and this is not considered washing only part of the hand. See Tur, siman 162. We are not concerned that he may remove it, because it causes him pain. Taz offers another answer: here the case is one who is not careful to prevent the water on the bandage from flowing back onto the hand, as explained there. Therefore, the bandage constitutes an interposition, because otherwise the returning water would render the hand impure when he did not pour a reviis at one time, as in siman 162 there. Accordingly, if the bandage covers only a minority of the hand and one does not mind it, even the water upon the bandage is purified by the second pouring, since the bandage is subordinate to the hand and considered as though it were part of the hand because it adheres to it. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. With Hashem’s help, I will explain further in siman 162, seif 9.
