(11) Anything, etc. This applies specifically when it covers a minority. Although the Mechaber already stated this at the preceding se'if's end, he repeats it because this entire se'if reproduces Rashba's language cited by Beis Yosef.
(12) And his hands were stained. Meaning part of the washing area; if most were stained, even something he does not mind interposes. In the latter clause concerning women who customarily color their hands decoratively, even if their entire hands are colored it does not interpose, because they intend and desire it, so it is like part of their bodies.
(13) The coloring does not, etc. A butcher with blood on his hands likewise has no interposition, nor does a fat seller with fat on his hands. But if these substances have tangible mass, they interpose [later authorities]. Magen Avraham inclines to leniency even where a person is both a butcher and fat seller and has both blood and fat on his hands.
(14) For the ink, etc.; and wet ink does not interpose. Just as wet ink dissolves in water and the washing water reaches the body, so too with paint resembling ink: only tangible paint can interpose; a mere visible stain cannot. Black discoloration without substance likewise does not interpose [later authorities]. For one who writes constantly, even tangible dry ink on his fingers does not interpose, since he does not ordinarily object [later authorities].
(15) Scabs of a wound. The discharge that emerges, dries, and forms a crust [Rambam]. Others explain it as a healed boil over which skin and scabs have formed.
(16) If he does not object. Meaning he does not remove it from his hand because removing it causes pain [Maamar Mordechai's conclusion; see Eliyah Rabbah and Shaarei Efraim].
