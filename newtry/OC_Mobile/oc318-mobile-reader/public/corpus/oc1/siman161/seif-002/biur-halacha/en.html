And his hands were stained, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, which explains that this means only part of the area requiring washing, and so writes the Shulchan Aruch HaRav. This also appears somewhat from Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 198, where he cites the Rashba concerning women who dye the hair of their heads for beauty. The Rashba writes that although this dye covers all the hair, and thus most of it, since they do not object to it, it would nevertheless constitute an interposition were it not for the fact that they intentionally desire and want it. See there. Such a woman is no less than other artisans who are accustomed to such stains, yet even so, without the Rashba’s reasoning it would constitute an interposition. I later found that the Shulchan Aruch HaRav explicitly writes that in our case too, if it covered most of the hand, it would constitute an interposition. Chayei Adam, however, writes that regarding a dyer and similar artisans, even if the substance covers the entire hand it does not constitute an interposition. I do not know his source. Perhaps his reasoning is that here there is no basis to decree regarding a majority that one does not mind because of a majority that one does mind, which is the reason that such a substance ordinarily constitutes an interposition, as stated in the first chapter of Eruvin, since among these artisans objection is uncommon because all are accustomed to it. Alternatively, since they constantly go about this way, the stain is considered like part of their body and the concept of an interposition does not apply. All this, however, is a novel ruling, and I have found no indication of it in the poskim. What I wrote further in Mishnah Berurah, that regarding women who customarily dye their hands for beauty, etc., follows from the Rashba’s reasoning cited above, and Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly in Yoreh Deah, siman 198, seif 17. See there and Shach, seif katan 21. According to this, the wording of Shulchan Aruch here is somewhat imprecise when it says, “And likewise women,” since regarding women the law is stronger: even if the entire hand is dyed, the concept of an interposition does not apply.
