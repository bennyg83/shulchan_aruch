4) There: Not opposite the flesh, etc. What is considered not opposite the flesh? If a fingernail has grown long and extends beyond the flesh, and grime is found beneath the nail under the portion extending beyond the flesh, this is called not opposite the flesh. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba; Levush; Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, seif 18, which states that because women cannot determine precisely what is called opposite the flesh and what is called not opposite it, they customarily cut their nails at the time of immersion; see there. Sefer Chassidim, siman 58, similarly states that a person should not grow his nails, lest dirt enter beneath them and form an intervening substance. Furthermore, forbidden fat or another prohibited substance may enter beneath them and cause him to transgress; see there. Etz Chaim, shaar 31, chapter 2, implies that even the nail itself intervenes when it extends beyond the flesh. It states: 'The portion of the nail that extends beyond the flesh of the finger must be cut, for the external forces attach themselves there and draw nourishment to the utmost degree. This is what the Zohar states in parashas Vayakhel, folio 208b: Therefore, a person should not grow those nails, etc. This is the measure that intervenes regarding washing the hands, as mentioned by our Sages, namely the portion that extends beyond the flesh. Therefore, the punishment of one who grows his nails is extremely severe.' Shach in Yoreh De'ah there, paragraph 25, similarly writes that according to one opinion, if a nail is long and stands ready to be cut, it intervenes even when there is no dirt beneath it; see there. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, cites him. Therefore, one must take great care not to allow the nail to grow beyond the flesh. If one is compelled to do so, such as a shochet or mohel who must retain a nail to inspect the knife or perform periah at a bris milah, it may be considered valid. Nevertheless, he must take great care before washing and inspect whether anything is attached to the nail. See below, paragraph 13.
