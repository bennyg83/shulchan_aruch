Seif 4 — measure, etc., and one says, etc. — see Rashi there and the gemara's conclusion there like Shmuel — and this is the "one says" here; and Tosafot there on "he said" — but in the name of R. Avraham explained the joint of Chullin until the end of the knuckles of his fingers — if so, when we hold like Shmuel it is until the palm, per the first version; but Tosafot there rejected it from gemara Arakhin; and Tosafot in Menachot 37a on "occiput." But Rif's text in Chullin is until the joint, which is the end of the knuckles of his fingers; and in kiddush and terumah until the joint, which is until the palm; and when we hold like Shmuel, Chullin also needs until the palm. And they disagree: Rav's text in Chullin until the joint, R. Shimon also in terumah, and Shmuel's text in all until the joint; and per Tur — that is the first version; and Tur there that one cannot explain until the first joint in the knuckles (1) because it is a small matter, and also the sizes do not match — whereas per Rashi he takes from each two joints and from the size of one joint — see there. Another approach for Ramban: in the baraita it means terumah until the second joint in the fingers and Chullin until the end of the knuckles of his fingers; and as Yerushalmi in Rav's name — netilat yadayim for terumah until the joint and for Chullin until the knuckles of his fingers; and so in the gemara they said "they permitted from here," etc.; and so Ra'ah and Ritva; nevertheless for halacha he holds like Rashi and Tosafot and the text of the books, as Peri Chadash wrote; and Rif supports him, who wrote kiddush, then Chullin, then terumah — so Ra'ah; and one can say Rif's words likewise, and now "lamed" for Rif is like the last version; but Rashba challenged him and said it is established, etc., and there is no proof from the cloth there — the reason is that priests are diligent; and he explains that Yerushalmi means for terumah until the palm and for Chullin until the knuckles of his fingers; and in our gemara Rav said ostensibly, etc., likewise like Yerushalmi, and he is more stringent in each joint — more in Chullin like terumah, in terumah like kiddush, and Shmuel is more stringent in both like kiddush — and this is what is written "between," etc., for leniency. For Rashi's explanation there is no stringency in terumah; R. Shimon says both for leniency — until the end of the knuckles of his fingers, and leniency from what Shmuel said; and all were more stringent than the baraita; and with this Tosafot's difficulty is resolved; but for R. Shimon it is difficult, and there is no need to strain Rav in explaining the baraita as Tosafot wrote; and one can say R. Shimon, etc. — strained like Tur's difficulty as above; and we hold like Shmuel, which is the first reasoning.
