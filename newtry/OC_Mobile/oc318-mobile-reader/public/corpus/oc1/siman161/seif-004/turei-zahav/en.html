The joint of the forearm. Meaning, immediately after the hand ends begins what is called the forearm. One must wash up to the beginning of that forearm, namely through the end of the hand. This is the view of the Rif.
It is proper to follow, etc. Beit Yosef writes that it is proper for one to stipulate explicitly that he does not accept this practice as a binding obligation, lest on some occasion he not have so much water available and then be unable to be lenient contrary to his established practice. Likewise, one should make such a stipulation regarding every stringency he wishes to adopt. It appears, however, that this stringency creates concern because observers may think that this is the essential law. At times they will see others washing only as the law requires and say that those people eat without proper netilas yadayim. It will appear to them like two Torahs, Heaven forbid, and they may cast aspersions upon those people, as stated in the first chapter of Gittin regarding refraining from an unnecessary stringency in a get. The Rosh likewise writes in the first chapter of Pesachim regarding the blessing al biur chametz that one may use that wording, although saying levaer is preferable, because it is necessary to publicize that the other wording is also valid, lest people mistakenly say that one who blessed that way did not fulfill his obligation. I cited this below in siman 167, seif 2. It therefore appears to me that even the Shulchan Aruch wrote this only where one is by himself, not before the public. In my humble opinion, one who is lenient in this respect everywhere has lost nothing, since we rule that the halachah follows the lenient opinion regarding netilas yadayim, as stated in siman 160, seif 11. This is not included in the statement, “I fill my hands with water,” etc., because there the subject is the area that actually requires washing, unlike an area outside the required place. So it appears to me.
