The flesh. This means the place where the nail extends beyond the flesh.
His flesh. Meaning he has no wound, only a mere ache, and he can remove the bandage whenever he wants; this depends on whether he is particular about it. But if he has a wound and cannot remove it, then he does not need to wash the place of the bandage at all, as Taz writes at the end of siman 162. See Magen Avraham 162 seif katan 18. See also Halachot Ketanot part 2 siman 133, who wrote that if there was a wound on the back of his hand and he wrapped a cloth over it and washed properly, and during the meal the cloth fell off, he does not need another washing.
Is particular. If most of the hand is covered, even though he is not particular, it is a chatzitzah. Beit Yosef; Magen Avraham.
To be concerned. And if you ask: in siman 162 seif 9 it is written that if something is found on the hand, it is impure. One must say that here it refers to when he washed with a revi'it.
For washing. Magen Avraham wrote: but where it is not opposite the flesh, certainly most people are particular, and it is a chatzitzah even though he himself is not particular. On Shabbat it is forbidden to scrape the mud beneath the nail with his nails; rather, he should wash it with water. Tashbetz; Magen Avraham. See above siman 302.
