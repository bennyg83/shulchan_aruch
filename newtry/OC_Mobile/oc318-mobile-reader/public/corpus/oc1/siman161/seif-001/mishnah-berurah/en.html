(א) To be careful — I precede this with a short introduction: regarding immersion of the body we hold one must be careful there be no interposition, meaning nothing adherent on the body at immersion, for thereby that place is not fit for water to enter; if there was interposition the immersion did not avail. And although from the Torah interposition applies only if the interposition was on most of the body's flesh, rabbinically it is forbidden even if on a minority of the body — a decree lest one come to [neglect] most; but the Sages forbade only when people's custom is to be fastidious that it remain so, even if now at immersion he is not fastidious. But something people are not accustomed to be fastidious about does not interpose unless most of the body was covered, then it is rabbinically forbidden even when something people are not accustomed to be fastidious about adheres. For netilat yadayim too — although rabbinic, whatever the Sages enacted they enacted like Torah law; therefore if interposition is on most of the hand, i.e. most of the place of netilah, it is forbidden even if people are not accustomed to be fastidious; if only on a minority of the hand it is not forbidden unless people are accustomed to be fastidious. Now we explain the Shulchan Aruch's words. When he wrote to beware of interposition — that one remove from the hands before netilah anything people are accustomed to be fastidious to remove; if he washed his hands and afterward something interposing was found, whether on a minority or majority of the hand, we are lenient that it arose afterward; but l'chatchila one should inspect before netilah that nothing interposing is on him.

(ב) Excrement under the nail, etc. — meaning filth of the nails that customarily gathers there; but actual excrement even opposite the flesh — it seems netilah is not considered if it remained there.

(ג) Not opposite the flesh — such as if the nail is large and extends beyond the finger flesh and excrement is there under that excess; most people are accustomed to clean it because of disgust, therefore it interposes and one must remove that filth before netilah. With dough people are accustomed to be fastidious even opposite the flesh; see Yoreh De'ah 198 where several more interposing things are listed — here he listed common things. Acharonim wrote in the name of Semak it is proper for this reason that a person not grow his nails long.

(ד) Moisture — what they put of milk or wax or other ointment on a cloth to place on pain [Arukh].

(ה) On his flesh — and although explained below siman 162 seif 10 that one need not wash at all on the place of moisture, because since he cannot wash that place it is as if his finger were cut off and he need only wash the rest of the hand — there it deals with one who has a wound, therefore we are not concerned he will remove the moisture and touch his flesh to the food because it pains him; but here it deals with one who has no wound, only ordinary discomfort at the place of netilah, and he has no pain in netilah and can remove the moisture whenever he wishes; therefore we are concerned he will remove the moisture during the meal and touch his flesh to the food; therefore he must remove the moisture and wash his hands, for if he washes while moisture is on his hand it is interposition.

(ו) And Greek mud, etc. — meaning mortar of pits, like potters' clay that adheres greatly and prevents water from entering underneath even when moist; and likewise other types of mud when dry and adhering to the flesh interpose — all this whether on the hand or under the nail, even opposite the flesh [unless it was so minute that people are not fastidious, then they do not interpose wherever found; and likewise feces and dough similarly as I wrote in Biur Halacha]. However where one's occupation is in mud such as making bricks or pottery, certainly it does not interpose wherever found since it is his craft and he is not fastidious — and as below seif 2, unless most of the place of netilah was covered thereby.

(ז) But, etc. — this law applies to the beginning of the seif regarding the law of interposition itself: if any interposing thing is found on a minority of the hand and he is not fastidious, one need not be concerned. Tur holds that even in those the Mechaber mentioned earlier — things most people are accustomed to be fastidious about — nevertheless if he is not fastidious it does not interpose. Magen Avraham and other Acharonim hold that in all those the Mechaber mentioned we do not follow him but follow most people.

(ח) In its small amount when he is not fastidious — and if most of the hand is covered, i.e. most of the place of netilah, even if he is not fastidious it interposes.

(ט) The mud — Magen Avraham explained it applies only opposite the flesh; even Greek mud and potters' mud, because in those lands people are not fastidious and it is like a small amount one is not fastidious about. But not opposite the flesh — certainly most people are fastidious even regarding excrement, and it interposes even if he is not fastidious, per his words; Chayei Adam and Da'at Kedoshim cited them. Some are lenient regarding excrement and ordinary mud.

(י) He is fastidious — meaning even though most are not fastidious, since he is fastidious we follow him and it interposes. Nevertheless it seems this is specifically potters' mud or dry mud; but moist mud or dirt under the nail opposite the flesh that does not adhere much — one need not be stringent, especially if all day he is not accustomed to be fastidious, only when he reaches netilah.
