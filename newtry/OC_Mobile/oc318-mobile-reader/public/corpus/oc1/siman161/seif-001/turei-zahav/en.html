Not opposite the flesh. Meaning, at the portion where the fingernail extends beyond the flesh.
And a bandage on the flesh. Meaning, he has no wound, only a minor ailment, and can remove the bandage whenever he wishes. The law therefore depends on whether he is particular about it. If he has an actual wound and cannot remove the bandage, however, he need not wash the place of the bandage at all, as written at the end of siman 162. Alternatively, here too the case may involve an actual wound, but he is not careful to prevent the water upon the bandage from flowing back onto the hand, as explained there. It therefore constitutes an interposition. This is why the Shulchan Aruch writes here that if he does not mind it, it does not constitute an interposition.
For this reason they did not customarily clean beneath, etc. In Yoreh Deah, siman 198, seif katan 19, I wrote another reason why the custom is to be lenient regarding netilas yadayim.
