That one should not. See Ateres Zekeinim. It is proper to cut one’s fingernails every Erev Shabbos; see Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 1, and Levush. An interposition invalidates netilas yadayim because, since washing purifies the hands, it must resemble immersion. See Levush. Even according to Rashi in Shabbos 14a, who explains that hands were decreed second-degree impurity because they are active and touch unclean places, so that where a bandage covers part of the hand that area was not touched, nevertheless, once the Sages rendered the hands second-degree impure, purification without an interposition is required. The washing of ordinary food was instituted because of its relationship to terumah.
If. If one washed his hands and afterward found an interposing substance upon them, although regarding immersion she requires another immersion, as stated in Yoreh Deah 199:10, here, regarding netilas yadayim, a doubt is treated leniently. Nevertheless, initially one should certainly inspect his hands before washing to ensure that no interposing substance is present. Even if afterward it is found over most of the hand, regarding ordinary food one may be lenient.
