Seif 3: One must, etc. The Tosefta, cited there by the Rash, states: tight rings constitute an interposition, while loose ones do not. This is the basis of the statement, “The custom is,” etc. The statement, “And even,” etc., is as written below, “But there are,” etc. Beit Yosef writes likewise.
And even, etc., since, etc. These are the words of Terumas HaDeshen: One might ask why a ring constitutes an interposition, since it is only a minority that one does not mind. The Raavad already explained that since she is particular about removing it while kneading and the like, it constitutes an interposition. The same applies to woolen and linen threads and straps: although women are not particular about them at the time of immersion, they are particular about them when washing their hair. The Rosh likewise cites this in the name of the Raavad in tractate Niddah.
