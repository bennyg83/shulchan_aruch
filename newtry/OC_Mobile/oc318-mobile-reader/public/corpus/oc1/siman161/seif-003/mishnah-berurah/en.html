(17) To remove the ring: because it constitutes an interposition.
(18) And even, etc.: Meaning, even though the water reaches the finger, the ring should nevertheless be removed because we are not proficient in determining what is considered loose, as explained below. After the fact, the washing is valid if the ring was loose, provided that he poured a reviis over it at one time, as written below in siman 162, seif 9.
(19) At the time, etc.: The later authorities conclude that this applies specifically to a woman who is ordinarily particular to remove her ring while working, meaning while kneading. A man, however, who is not ordinarily particular to remove it while working, since he does not generally knead, need not remove it when washing even if it is not loose. If the ring contains a precious stone, however, even a man is ordinarily particular to remove it when washing so that it not become soiled by the water, and he should therefore remove it because of an interposition.
