(S.k. 1) One need not return etc. — not like Levush who wrote that it is customary to return. And the reason Levush wrote is that "ohev tzedakah u'mishpat" is somewhat similar to the title HaMelekh HaMishpat; except that "ohev tzedakah u'mishpat" implies righteousness is primary, and HaMelekh HaMishpat implies judgment is primary — as Taz wrote, or according to Taz's second explanation; and therefore (plainly) he was not obligated to return. Rather one can say "ohev tzedakah u'mishpat" — not that He, may He be blessed, always does judgment, but He loves if we, His children, do righteousness and justice. So he wrote one need not return. And if he said during the rest of the days of the year HaMelekh HaMishpat, Peri Chadash wrote in siman 582 one need not return — not only according to Rama here who holds if between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur he said Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat he need not return; if so according to him "ohev tzedakah u'mishpat" and HaMelekh HaMishpat are certainly one and equal in their explanations, and certainly one need not return if he said during the rest of the year HaMelekh HaMishpat. Rather even according to those who disagree with Rama — nevertheless if he said during the rest of the year HaMelekh HaMishpat he need not return, for we say on Rosh Hashana: "As whom do we pray nowadays?" All days of the year — "shorten and evil" (that is, for the sick, for the decree already went out on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur) — like R' Yosi who said a person is judged every day. If so, every day is fit to say HaMelekh HaMishpat, for a person is judged every day. And the distinction between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur and the rest of the days of the year — see in Magen Avraham end of siman 591.
