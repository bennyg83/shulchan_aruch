To return — Avnei Chefeitz, and in the name of Ramah in Alfasi Zuta wrote in Machaneh Binyamin that if during the rest of the days of the year he concluded HaMelekh HaKadosh HaMelekh HaMishpat, he returns; but in Tiferet Shmuel in Hagahot Rosh wrote one need not; and in Mateh Yehuda wrote that most of the later authorities agreed. And what Baer Heitev wrote — that according to Taz's reason one should also return and pray as a voluntary prayer — is not necessary; one can say that during the Ten Days of Repentance one must be more precise to satisfy all opinions, not so during the rest of the days of the year, since according to many poskim after the fact he fulfilled, it suffices so. And Peri Chadash siman 582 wrote, and these are his words: and if he wants to return, let him return, and one need not add anything in it, for there is innovation in it — end quote; and for this Shulchan Aruch Tzemach Tzedek agreed. However if he is in doubt, it is obvious one need not pray — even as a voluntary prayer one need not, for even Ramah concedes this, as written in his name in Alfasi Zuta that from doubt one need not, even the day after Yom Kippur, for we do not say what one is accustomed to he mentions in something not fixed except for the ten days — end quote. And see in Shulchan Aruch Tzemach Tzedek in the blessing Tekah — see in Nagid uMitzvah that he skips the word "meharah," and it seems this is because of the name that comes out from "yachad" of the four corners. And some are precise that one does not skip words "meharah" and say "and gather us quickly together" etc., and this is correct. And in the blessing VeLamalshinim one should conclude only "Who breaks enemies and subdues the wicked," as the ancient Kabbalists wrote — for it is an acronym of the name Shaddai, and that name is the subduer — not like Peri Chadash. And in the blessing VeLiYerushalayim one must say "and the throne of David Your servant," as written in the name of the Ari; and the matter of saying Nachem on the ninth of Av will be explained elsewhere in laws of the ninth of Av. And when he says "to Your salvation we hoped," he should intend what they ask him: "Have You hoped for salvation?" — and therefore he should intend that You are from the place of his salvation. And Mahari Tzemach wrote: and I too intend to hope for Heaven's salvation that He does with us to save us from several bad afflictions every day and every moment, and I found great benefit many times in times of distress through this — end quote.
