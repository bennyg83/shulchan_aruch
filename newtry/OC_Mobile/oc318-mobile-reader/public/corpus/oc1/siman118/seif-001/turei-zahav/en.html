However, if he said etc. — Tur wrote here in the name of his brother R' Chiel that it was difficult for him: what is the difference between Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat and HaMelekh HaMishpat regarding needing to return to the beginning if he forgot. And he wrote afterward in the name of Machzor Vitri that the gemara's version is: all year one says HaKel HaKadosh, HaKel HaMishpat — and therefore there is properly a change in HaMelekh HaMishpat that mentions kingship in it, for now He sits on judgment to judge the whole world and shows Himself king over judgment. And some say all year Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat, for regarding judgment it is fit to say kingship more than other blessings etc. And therefore Rama wrote here one need not return if he said Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat — and so too Ri and Hagahot Maimoni. But Tur itself wrote in section 5 of Rosh Hashana that one must return, and there is the main law; and so too Tosafos and Rosh at end of first chapter of Berachos. And it seems there is a difference even in the text Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat — for all year judgment is not primary but righteousness is primary, and therefore they put righteousness before judgment; but from Rosh Hashana until Yom Kippur judgment is primary and therefore they do not mention righteousness. And further it seems there is a difference between them — namely that there is a difference between times, comparable to an earthly court that has a limited day for the court's sitting when they sit on their own, but on other days they do not sit unless people come and request they sit and judge between them. So too the heavenly court — all year they do not sit for judgment unless they request of Him, may He be blessed, from the merit that there is prosecution of the prosecutor; and on this he said all year Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat — namely that He loves judgment when stirred by whoever rouses Him to fulfill judgment per that one's will. Not so on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur — He, may He be blessed, Himself is the judgment from His own side, as written in this section in the name of Beit Yosef in the name of R' Menucha that HaMelekh HaMishpat means He is the judgment, and this explanation is possible; therefore he wrote. And it seems for law: since we rule that in a matter of doubt one prays as a voluntary prayer, as stated in siman 117 section 4 regarding a matter where there is doubt in a blessing, and one need not add anything new in it — all the more here where the majority of opinions hold so, as Beit Yosef wrote in section 5 of Rosh Hashana — one should do so to satisfy all opinions, and it is proper to do so to pray again as a voluntary prayer if he did not say HaMelekh HaMishpat, as I understand it. And Tur wrote in siman 119: one who erred and concluded Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur returns to the blessing Hashivah — and that is before he lifted his feet; but after he lifted his feet he returns to the beginning — so too siman 582.
