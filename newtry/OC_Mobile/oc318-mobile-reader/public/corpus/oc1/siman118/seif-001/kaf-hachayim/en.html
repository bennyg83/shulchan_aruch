(1) [Seif 1] From Rosh Hashana until Yom Kippur one concludes HaMelekh HaMishpat; Rama: however if one said Melekh ohev tzedakah he need not return etc. Behold in this law opinions multiplied; therefore we bring first the gemara's language in Berachos page 12 side b: Rabbah bar Chinena the Elder said in the name of Rav — all year long a person prays HaKel HaKadosh, Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat, except the ten days between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur when one prays HaMelekh HaKadosh and HaMelekh HaMishpat. R' Elazar said: even if he said HaMelekh HaKadosh he fulfilled etc. What was the conclusion? R' Yosef: HaKel HaKadosh and Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat; Rabbah: HaMelekh HaKadosh and HaMelekh HaMishpat — and the halacha is like Rabbah. End quote. And Tosafos there wrote one must conclude HaMelekh HaKadosh and HaMelekh HaMishpat, and if he did not say it they return him. So too Tur in siman 582 in the name of Rif, Rambam, and Rosh — only in the name of Ravyah wrote that after the fact he does not return. Beit Yosef wrote there for law: since Rif, Rambam, and Rosh agree that after the fact also he did not fulfill, so we rule; and Kaf HaChayim there in Shulchan Aruch seif 1, see there. Except that R' Chiel, brother of Rabbeinu Yaakov author of Tur, wrote he does not know what difference there is between Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat and HaMelekh HaMishpat regarding needing to return to the beginning if he forgot — and Tur brought it in this siman. And Terumat HaDeshen at end of first chapter of Berachos — if he said Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat he need not return, since he mentions kingship in it, see there. So too Mordechai in Darkei Moshe siman 582 note 3 in the name of Hagahot Maimoni and Rashba — and this is why he ruled here one need not return if he said Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat. Bach in this siman, and Magen Avraham in s.k. 1 in the name of Mateh Moshe and Shelah — and wrote not like Levush who wrote it is customary to return, for Hagahot Maimoni wrote one need not and he was expert in customs, see there. So too Eliyah Rabbah siman 150 in the name of Rambam's responsum, see there. However Peri Chadash siman 582 note 1 ruled that even if he said Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat he must return, see there. So too Mateh Yehuda there. Kaf HaChayim in this siman note 2 and in siman 582 note 1 from Aderet Eliyahu note 2 and Shulchan Aruch page 312 side 3. However Taz in this siman s.k. 2 wrote he returns and prays as a voluntary prayer, see there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav note 1; and Raz in siman 582 note 2; and Shakhnehaga siman 582 in Beiur HaGra note 2 wrote that the majority of poskim hold he does not return, and wrote also in Maran's opinion one can say so — if he said Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat he does not return; and he concluded: since there is dispute of poskim on this, from battle line to battle line doubt in blessings to be lenient and he does not return, see there. And so too Aderet Eliyahu siman 582 note 5 and Eliyah Rabbah there in Beiur HaGra. Chida, klal 24 note 16. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 129 note 3, Ben Orach Chayyim, parshat Nitzavim note 19 — wrote that even though Maran holds one returns, one may rely leniently even against Maran's reasoning, see there; how is it Mishnah Berurah note 3. And as stated, the primary matter is since one may rely leniently even against Maran, as Rav Ben Orach Chayyim wrote, and so we in our poverty earlier siman 4 note 10 and siman 80 note 7 and many places, see there. And one should not say he should return and pray as a voluntary prayer, because voluntary prayer needs greater intent, as written in siman 107; and therefore in such a case where the majority of poskim hold one need not return, one may rely on them.
