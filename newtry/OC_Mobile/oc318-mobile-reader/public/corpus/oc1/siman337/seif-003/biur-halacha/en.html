And one may not rinse it even if it is paved — Know that in Tosefta and in Rambam it is further explained, and Magen Avraham brought that one does not blow on the ground on Shabbat — I omitted this because it is not common; also I do not know its correct explanation; if its explanation is to blow with his mouth on the dust and earth from atop the ground — why forbidden, and what difference from sweeping that Rambam permits at least on paved; and on the contrary, for one who forbids sweeping there on account of moving dust and it is like digging — it should be permitted here, for with his mouth he does not move dust from its place; also for one who forbids there on account of muktzeh — it should be permitted here as is explained in siman 308 s.k. 63 [and one could say the braita holds moving from the side is considered moving, and there is a dispute of tannaim on this — if so, why did Rambam bring it as law]. And even for one who forbids there on account of leveling bumps — it is likewise difficult, for it should be permitted at least on paved for one who permits sweeping; and I found in Peri Megadim in his short words a hint of an answer — that since sweeping is a Shabbat need, Rambam was lenient on paved, unlike here; and as Maggid Mishneh explained Rambam's reason that he is stringent regarding rinsing; and one can further say that all these matters were for a bathhouse need and the like that requires beautifying the ground, and therefore we are concerned lest he come to level bumps intentionally on unpaved, and they also decreed on paved because ground to ground changes as is in the Gemara, and likewise regarding blowing it also deals with such a case [and even though Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote this law of not oiling and not rinsing regarding a house, perhaps he too deals with bathing and the like that requires beautifying the ground, as is customary in our time that rinsing is for washing the ground]; but in any event, if one needs to sweep the house [for one who permits] and he has no broom, it is permitted for him to blow with his mouth on the dust and earth, and on the contrary it appears that this is no worse than sprinkling that the Gemara permits in the conclusion even on unpaved, because it is not psik reisha to level bumps, and likewise here; and it is possible further that even to blow with a vessel in the air it is also permitted, and even for one who questions the permission of a goose wing admits here that it is permitted:
