1) [Seif 1] An unintended result is permitted. The law follows Rabbi Shimon, who states this principle. Beis Yosef. The Gemara concludes at the end of the chapter Kirah that Rabbi Shimon permits an unintended act even when performing it intentionally would violate a Torah prohibition. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. This is contrary to Levush.
2) There: Provided that it is not an inevitable consequence. This means that although one does not intend to produce the result, it cannot fail to occur. Tur; Taz, paragraph 1. Regarding the distinction between an inevitable consequence and labor not needed for its own purpose, see above, siman 278, paragraph 3.
3) There: Therefore, a person may drag a bed, etc. Even when one intends to make a furrow, dragging a chair or bench does not involve a Torah prohibition. When the chapter Klal Gadol, Shabbos 73b, states that digging is a derivative of threshing, it appears this is a scribal error and should read that it is a derivative of plowing, as stated there in the Gemara and Rashi on page 46b, and that one is liable, this applies when using a hoe or similar implement, but not a bed, chair, or bench. Beis Yosef.
4) There: Whether large or small, etc. An extremely large object may not be dragged, because making a furrow is then inevitable. This is forbidden even upon a marble floor. Beis Yosef in the name of Rokeach. Although digging in this manner is rabbinically prohibited, since Torah liability requires digging with a hoe or similar implement, as stated in the preceding paragraph, an inevitable consequence is nevertheless forbidden. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 40, paragraph 6. A heavy barrel likewise may not be dragged even across a marble upper story. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. Even a very large child's wagon, however, may be pulled over earth, because a wagon presses the earth down and does not form a furrow. Metzudos Yissachar, siman 14; Pischei Teshuvah. See Beitzah 23b.
