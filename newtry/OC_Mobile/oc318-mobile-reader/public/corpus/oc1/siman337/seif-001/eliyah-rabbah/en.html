[1] [Levush] And also R' Shimon, etc. Olat Shabbat wondered on Rokeach who wrote in the conclusion that the reason R' Shimon permits dragging is because even when he intends there is no d'oraisa prohibition, etc. — behold explicitly we conclude at the end of chapter Kirah that even for a d'oraisa prohibition we say thus, until here his words; and I examined Rokeach and did not find thus — see siman 58 there; therefore it appears to me Olat Shabbat erred in understanding Beit Yosef's words, who opened in the name of Rokeach that on very large ones it is forbidden even on marble ground, and afterward wrote at the end of chapter Kirah etc. "these matters are in a marah" etc., and he thought these are Rokeach's conclusion, but he did not examine — they are Beit Yosef's own words. And now I will explain Beit Yosef's words: he did not write except to learn from them that there is no d'oraisa prohibition in dragging even when he intends, and much derives from this between d'oraisa and rabbinic prohibition; but in truth regarding davar she'eino mitkaven that it is permitted, he certainly agrees that even for a d'oraisa prohibition it is permitted, as is explained in siman 276, 277, and 336, and many places — and this is clear.
