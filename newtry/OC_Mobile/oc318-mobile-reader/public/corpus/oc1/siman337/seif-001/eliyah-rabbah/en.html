[1] [Levush] And Rabbi Shimon also, etc. Olat Shabbos writes in astonishment at the Rokeach, saying that the Rokeach concludes that Rabbi Shimon permits dragging because even when one intends to make the furrow there is no Torah prohibition, whereas at the end of Perek Kirah the Gemara clearly concludes that the rule of unintended action applies even where the possible result is a Torah prohibition. I examined the Rokeach and did not find such a statement; see siman 58 there. It therefore appears that Olat Shabbos misunderstood the Beit Yosef. The Beit Yosef begins by citing the Rokeach that very large objects may not be dragged even over a marble floor, and then later writes regarding the end of Perek Kirah that this is only with a hoe, etc. Olat Shabbos thought this later discussion was the Rokeach's conclusion, but it is the Beit Yosef's own statement. I will now explain the Beit Yosef. His point is only to derive that dragging does not involve a Torah prohibition even when one intends to make a furrow, which has many practical consequences in distinguishing Torah from rabbinic law. But concerning an unintended action, the Beit Yosef certainly agrees that it is permitted even where the possible prohibition is d'oraisa, as is clear in simanim 276, 277, 336, and many other places. This is clear. It appears that the Levush likewise stumbled throughout this seif by understanding that the Beit Yosef gave this as the reason dragging is permitted. That is incorrect, as explained.
