[2] [Levush] And it is not a Torah prohibition, etc. This is incorrect. Tosafot in Shabbos 95, s.v. hamechabed, implies that everyone agrees one is liable if he intends the prohibited result. The reason some permit sweeping is that it is unintended and they hold it is not an inevitable consequence.
[3] Through non-Jews, etc. This is the primary ruling, unlike the Maharshal. Nevertheless, regarding sweeping with a goose wing one should be stringent in accordance with Maharshal, Perek 2 of Beitzah, siman 33, who writes that it actually levels depressions just like a broom. It is therefore a utensil whose primary use is prohibited and may not even be moved from sun to shade, except when needed for its permitted physical use or for its place. See siman 308, seif 49, which permits moving even a broom. The Magen Avraham writes there that according to the opinion prohibiting sweeping the house, it is a utensil whose primary use is prohibited. I say that sweeping a table is permitted according to everyone. Many authorities permit even a floor paved with boards. It appears prohibited there because one might confuse it with a stone floor, but regarding a table there is no basis for such a decree. See siman 338, seif 5. Although the Levush at the beginning of siman 302 writes that there is concern one may break splinters, this does not apply to a goose wing. It is therefore a utensil whose primary use is permitted. See siman 308, seif katan 61. So too it appears in siman 316, seif katan 37 regarding saliva.
