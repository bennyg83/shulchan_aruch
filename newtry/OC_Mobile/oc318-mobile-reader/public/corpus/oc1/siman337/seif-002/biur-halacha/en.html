(ב) And some are stringent even on paved — See in Mishna Berurah that the reason is because they decreed paved on account of unpaved; it is from Sefer HaTerumah and they brought it in Taz. And therefore per this, if all the houses of the city or at least most of them [Rivash] are paved with stones or with bricks [and all the more if they are covered with boards], it is possible that sweeping is permitted, for they did not decree on the majority on account of the minority, nor on this city on account of another city, as they wrote in Sefer HaTerumah; and likewise Tosafos on daf 29b "gzeira" and also Rashi holds thus and hinted to this on daf 95a "zilyakha," and the Gra brought this law too in his Beur in the name of Sefer HaTerumah. However, there is another reasoning in Sefer HaTerumah there that between the pavers [that is, between the rows between stone and stone] there is concern for leveling bumps even on paved floors, and this reasoning is also mentioned for one answer in Tosafos 95a "she'ra zilyakha" regarding rinsing [but it is not mentioned in their words regarding sweeping], and I do not know what they answer in the sugya on daf 151b where they answer there "ground to ground changes" — it is evident from there that on paved there is no relevance at all to leveling bumps, only from the aspect of change; and perhaps one can say that there the ceiling of the bathhouse was made of boards that are well fitted one to another, unlike stones [and from Rashi there and Rambam in chapter 23 it is evident that also with stones on paved there is no relevance to leveling bumps], and therefore when all the houses of the city are paved entirely or mostly with boards, one should not be stringent on paved; and especially if it was honored before Shabbat, one can combine this too with Raavad's view brought in Beit Yosef that he holds that even on unpaved it is not psik reisha; nevertheless this matter is not yet clear, for it is possible that the practice Rama brought that they practice to be stringent in the prohibition of sweeping was established from ancient days because they were concerned for muktzeh prohibition that is common to be on the ground — bones and shells not fit for animal food — and likewise it appears somewhat in Rabbeinu Yerucham part 13 and in Bach — see there; and therefore in every case forbidden; however, from the fact that Rama concludes the reason to be lenient with a goose wing because it is a light thing and does not level bumps, it is implied from this that the reason of the practice that they practiced to be stringent is on account of leveling bumps; and therefore one should not be stringent when all the houses of the city are paved, and especially if the house was honored before Shabbat as well, as above; and also for muktzeh prohibition there is no concern, one reason being what Rashba wrote that it is permitted to clear them because it is like a refuse pit, and further that it is moving from the side via something else for Shabbat need, as the later authorities wrote. And all this applies only regarding the prohibition of leveling bumps or muktzeh that is not in this; but there is another concern on account of what Sefer HaGedolim wrote and Rama brought that the palm fibers break, and likewise tree branches in our time [that they call in German], and one may not permit except with a broom of soft date-palm branches that bend and do not break, as Sefer HaGedolim wrote, and likewise what they call [in Yiddish] which is also a soft thing; but with tree branches there is concern for breaking; however, per what several acharonim questioned the words of Sefer HaGedolim that Rama brought, also with tree branches one should not be stringent when all the houses of the city are paved. The conclusion from all this: if all the houses of the city or at least most are paved with boards, and especially if the house was also honored before Shabbat, and one now wishes to sweep the house with soft date-palm branches or with what we call in Yiddish — certainly one should not be stringent [and from Kalkalas Shabbat it appears that with the Yiddish broom it is permitted even on unpaved, since sweeping with the Yiddish broom is like a goose wing — see what we wrote below]; and with tree branches of our time the permission is not so clear on account of concern for breaking the palm fibers as Rama wrote; nevertheless one who is lenient in this — one should not protest against him, for several acharonim questioned this, as above:

(ג) And likewise via clothing, etc., or a goose wing, etc. — Magen Avraham brought in the name of Rashal that he is stringent on a goose wing, and his reasoning is that through this he can also come to level bumps like via other brooms; and likewise the Acharonim lean to be stringent on a goose wing like him; and in Beur HaGra it further appears that he questions all this permission even via clothing, for he wrote that from Rashi's words [that is, on daf 124b "of wool"] it appears forbidden, and that is what is implied from Rashi there that with a wool broom it is forbidden to sweep on the ground; and nevertheless it is reasonable that on paved one may be lenient in all this, for otherwise many poskim permit on paved even with an actual broom, and even on unpaved one who practices to be lenient like Rama — certainly one should not protest against him, for we can combine the view of the rishonim who hold that sweeping is not psik reisha:

(ד) Light, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah what we wrote in the name of Kalkalas Shabbat that likewise via a soft Yiddish broom; and in truth the main permission of clothing or goose wing is also not a clear permission, for several poskim questioned this, as above; and therefore in my humble opinion one may not permit except that which Rama specified, and because it is a great change — also that it is not his way to sweep with this on a weekday, unlike with a Yiddish broom whose way is to sweep with it on weekdays too, and it is a degradation of Shabbat; and likewise the language of Levush who wrote: and likewise via clothing or a cloth or a soft goose wing, and he does not move dust from its place and does not level bumps, and it is a great change — permitted, end of his words; and it is implied from this that if there were not a change they would not permit this; however, if the house was paved one may rely on this, for many rishonim hold that even with an actual broom it is permitted to sweep on paved — see what we wrote above at the end of the words "forbidden to sweep":
