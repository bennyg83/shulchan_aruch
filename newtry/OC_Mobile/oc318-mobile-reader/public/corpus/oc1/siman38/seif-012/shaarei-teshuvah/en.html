In Torah—Av Beit Din; these are the words of the Magen Avraham. What he wrote, "see Beit Yosef"—meaning in Beit Yosef it is explained that although he wrote to reconcile the words of Baal HaItur, who distinguishes only in written Torah because of remembrance of the Exodus, etc., so it should not contradict what the Tur wrote, nevertheless he concludes there that Baal HaItur's words do not seem correct; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch here it seems he agrees with Rabbenu Yona—see there; and likewise from Yerushalmi, beginning of Shabbat, regarding interrupting for Keriat Shema, etc. And see in responsum Beit Efrayim, part 1, letter 8; and in Mishnah Berurah, note 1, in the name of Or Tzaddikim, who wrote in the name of the Ramban and Maharachav that every person is obligated to say the four parshiyot of tefillin while they are on him; the two parshiyot one says in Keriat Shema—Kadesh and V'hayah ki yevi'acha must be said before Baruch She'amar, since they precede Shema, and V'hayah im shamo'a, etc.—end of quote; and it seems if he cannot say them before Baruch She'amar, he should say them after Aleinu before removing the tefillin—end of quote; and people are accustomed to say Kadesh and V'hayah ki yevi'acha and Keriat Shema in the head tefillin worn after prayer; and some are meticulous to wrap also the arm tefillin—Kadesh and V'hayah ki yevi'acha before Baruch She'amar; and the custom is that if he did not say them before Baruch She'amar, one should not rely on what he will say afterward in the head tefillin, but should read Kadesh and V'hayah ki yevi'acha before removing the arm tefillin.
