In Torah. See Be'er Heitev. These are the words of Magen Avraham. His reference, “see Beit Yosef,” means that Beit Yosef explains the following. Although Beit Yosef writes to reconcile the words of Baal HaIttur, who distinguishes that specifically Torah study is different because of the remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt, etc., so that Tur's words do not present a difficulty, he nevertheless concludes that Baal HaIttur's words do not appear correct. Here in Shulchan Aruch as well, he appears to agree with Rabbeinu Yonah; see there. The same appears from the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Shabbat regarding interrupting for Keriat Shema, etc. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Orach Chayim. Machazik Berachah writes in the later addenda, in the name of Or Tzadikim, that he found it written in the name of Ramban and Rabbi Chayim Vital that every person is obligated to recite all four passages of the tefillin while wearing them. Two of the passages are recited in Keriat Shema. Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha should be recited before Baruch She'amar, because they precede Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa. These are his words. It appears that if one cannot recite them before Baruch She'amar, he should recite them after Aleinu, before removing the tefillin. These are his words. The common practice is to recite Kadesh, Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, and Keriat Shema while wearing Rabbeinu Tam's tefillin, which are put on after prayer. Some are meticulous also to recite Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before Baruch She'amar while wearing Rashi's tefillin. One who follows this practice but did not recite them before Baruch She'amar should not rely on what he will later recite while wearing Rabbeinu Tam's tefillin. Rather, he should recite Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha before removing Rashi's tefillin.
