(Note 13) "One who reads," etc.—perhaps there is no dispute—that Tur brought in the name of Mechilta that one reading in Torah is exempt from tefillin; and Rashba wrote we do not rule thus, for elder rabbis whose Torah was their occupation put on tefillin, etc.; and Baal ha-Itur distinguished: specifically reading Torah is exempt but not Talmud—and they deal with Gemara in the Gemara. Tur wrote it does not seem to distinguish, for regarding the blessing on Torah, Scripture and Gemara are one law—he said; and Beit Yosef wrote to reconcile: in written Torah, in which the Exodus is mentioned several times, likewise one remembers through the Exodus—he said; unlike when engaged in Talmud; nevertheless Baal ha-Tur's words do not seem correct—end quote Beit Yosef; and he brought in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote Mechilta exempts one reading Torah only from wearing them all day, but at Keriat Shema and prayer he concedes one is obligated; if so the Rashba's difficulty no longer applies; and so ruled in Shulchan Aruch; therefore Magen Avraham wrote one may possibly distinguish also in wearing all day between written Torah and Gemara, as Beit Yosef distinguished for Baal ha-Itur regarding wearing at Keriat Shema and prayer.

Whoever is exempt from Keriat Shema is exempt from prayer—thus it should read in my humble opinion. For we learned there: one whose dead lies before him is exempt from Keriat Shema, prayer, and tefillin; and Tosafot do not have "from prayer," and the latter clause teaches: bearers of the bier, etc.—those below need them are exempt, and those above who do not need them are obligated; both are exempt from prayer. And Tosafot, s.v. anu, etc., wrote: in the first clause, when his dead lies before him, all the more he is exempt from prayer—as Tosafot explain.

(Seif 14) Before, etc.—and some say in Tur, etc.—thus it should read.

Their time to remove (and Beit Yosef wrote: therefore it is forbidden only when he removed them close to sunset, for then is the time of removal, as above, siman 40. In my humble opinion this is specifically for those who wear all day; but for those who wear only at Keriat Shema and prayer, the prohibition is when he removed them after "Kedushah" and "Barekhu"—as above, siman 25, seif 13); but in Shulchan Aruch he rules like Rashi's reason, that it appears he treats lightly the honor of his teacher by baring his head before his teacher; and likewise even if his teacher already removed them it is forbidden.

Also his teacher has no, etc.—meaning: not that they will say he is a slave, but where there are two factors for ill repute—that he does degrading slave-work, and also he has no tefillin on his head, for slaves do not customarily put on tefillin.
