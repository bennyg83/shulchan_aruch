Seif katan 13, one who reads, etc. Perhaps this is not difficult. The Tur brought in the name of the Mechilta that one who reads the Torah is exempt from tefillin. The Rashba wrote that we do not rule this way, for the elder Sages, whose Torah was their occupation, put on tefillin, etc. The Baal HaItur distinguished that only one who reads Scripture is exempt, but not one engaged in Talmud, and they were engaged in Gemara. The Tur wrote that it does not appear correct to distinguish this way, for regarding the blessing over Torah, Scripture and Gemara have one law. Rabbi Beis Yosef wrote to explain that in the Written Torah, in which yetzias Mitzrayim is mentioned several times, he thereby remembers yetzias Mitzrayim and needs no other remembrance, unlike when one is engaged in Talmud. Nevertheless, the words of the Baal HaItur do not appear correct, end quote of Rabbi Beis Yosef. He brought in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that the Mechilta exempted one who reads Torah only from wearing them all day, but at the time of Krias Shema and prayer it concedes that he is obligated. If so, the aforementioned question of the Rashba no longer applies. So he ruled in Shulchan Aruch. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps one may also distinguish, regarding wearing them all day, between Written Torah and Gemara, just as Rabbi Beis Yosef distinguishes according to the Baal HaItur regarding wearing them at the time of Krias Shema and prayer.
Anyone who is exempt from Krias Shema is exempt from prayer, this is how it should read in my humble opinion. For we learned there: one whose deceased lies before him is exempt from Krias Shema, from prayer, and from tefillin. Tosafot do not have the text 'from prayer.' The latter clause teaches: those who carry the bier, etc., those whom the bier needs are exempt, and those whom the bier does not need are obligated; both these and those are exempt from prayer. Tosafot, s.v. we, wrote that in the first clause, where his deceased lies before him, all the more so he is exempt from prayer; see there.
Seif katan 14, before, etc.; and some say in the Tur, etc., this is how it should read.
Their time to be removed. Rabbi Beis Yosef wrote that according to this, it is forbidden only when he removes them close to sunset, for that is the time to remove them, as above in siman 30. In my humble opinion, this is specifically for those who wear them all day. But for those who wear them only at the time of Krias Shema and prayer, the prohibition applies when he removes them after Kedushah of Uva LeTziyon, as above in siman 25, seif 13. But in Shulchan Aruch he rules like Rashi's reason, that it appears as though he is belittling the honor of his rebbe by uncovering his head before his rebbe. Therefore, even if his rebbe has already removed his tefillin, it is forbidden.
His rebbe too does not have, etc. That is to say, we are not concerned that people will say he is a slave except where there are two negative factors: he performs lowly labor, the work of a slave, and he also has no tefillin on his head, for slaves are not accustomed to put on tefillin.
