Seif katan 8, and their merchants, etc. This is like one who returns a lost object, etc., even though he receives payment; see there. For we learned: one who is forbidden by vow to derive benefit from his fellow, etc., may return his lost object to him, and in a place where people take payment for returning a lost object, the benefit falls to hekdesh. On that which was taught, 'he may return his lost object,' Rav Ami and Rav Asi dispute. One says this is specifically when the property of the returner is forbidden to the owner of the lost object, since he returns to him something that is his own and this is not called benefit. But when the property of the owner of the lost object is forbidden to the returner, it is forbidden to return it to him, because the owner of the lost object benefits him with the perutah of Rav Yosef, for he does not need to give a perutah to a poor person, since one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from the mitzvah of tzedakah. The other says that even if the property of the owner of the lost object is forbidden to the returner, it is permitted, because the perutos of Rav Yosef are uncommon. It is explained there in the Rosh and Ran that according to the one who says it speaks specifically when the property of the returner is forbidden to the owner of the lost object, the latter clause, which teaches 'in a place where people take payment,' speaks when the returner is an important person and does not want to take the payment from the owner of the lost object; he may not keep it for himself, and therefore it falls to hekdesh. In any event, it is explained there in the sugya that the latter clause, 'and in a place where people take payment,' refers back to the first clause, and it implies that there is no difference between the first and latter clauses except that the payment falls to hekdesh. If so, it should have distinguished in the latter clause and taught a novelty: that in a place where people take payment, even when the property of the owner of the lost object is forbidden to the returner, nevertheless he may return it, because since he receives payment there is no issue of the perutah of Rav Yosef, for he is not considered engaged in a mitzvah. The payment is permitted to take, as explained there in the Ran, because he takes only compensation for lost labor; if so, he gains nothing, for corresponding to that payment he is idle from his work and loses that amount; see there. Rather, it must be that even when he takes payment, he is still called engaged in a mitzvah.
See siman 433, seif 8. That is to say, it is explained there that one need not search in a heap because of the danger of a scorpion. Although agents of a mitzvah are not harmed, nevertheless we are concerned that perhaps he lost a needle before the search and will look for it after completing the search, for then he is no longer engaged in a mitzvah. But for this, we need not say that he will search during the search, for then he is engaged in a mitzvah, and although he is also searching for the needle, nevertheless, since the primary intent of the search is for the mitzvah of searching for chametz, he is called engaged in a mitzvah. See there in Magen Avraham, who brought proof from tzedakah, and the same applies there regarding returning a lost object.
