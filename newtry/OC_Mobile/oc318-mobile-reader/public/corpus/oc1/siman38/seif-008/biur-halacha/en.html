Scribes of tefillin and mezuzot, etc.—thus it is in the Gemara and all the Rishonim.
They and their agents, etc.—see Mishnah Berurah what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham; and in my humble opinion there is difficulty from Bava Metzia 82b: one master holds he does a mitzvah when he lends him; and one master holds he does not do a mitzvah—he intends for his benefit; and see Rashi there, s.v. be-melaveh tzarich, etc.—nevertheless there is a mitzvah; end quote—evidently Rabbi Akiva holds the halachah follows him, that even where he intends for his benefit he is still called engaged in a mitzvah and exempt from giving bread to a poor person, for thereby he fulfills "and you shall lend him," etc.; and perhaps the Gemara's intent in what the one master said "he does a mitzvah when he lends him" is that presumably he intends thereby to fulfill his Creator's commandment and also intends for his benefit; and Rabbi Eliezer holds he intends for his benefit alone, like what Rashi explained in Pesachim 9b, s.v. harei zeh, etc., and intends also, etc.; and there on 12a, s.v. ve-khahiga, etc.—see there. But if we know his intent is only to profit, he is not called engaged in a mitzvah; nevertheless it seems to me that if his intent is for both equally he is called engaged in a mitzvah, like what the Avnei Nezer ruled, siman 489, note 8: if he blew the shofar to learn and also to fulfill the mitzvah thereby, he fulfilled his obligation. Know further that regarding writing sacred texts itself—as is common that the scribe intends to earn—it is sufficient to say he is nevertheless included among those engaged in a mitzvah, though apparently this is the Magen Avraham brought from Rashi; in my humble opinion one may distinguish, though thereby the Magen Avraham's difficulty falls away anyway: regarding writing itself, which is certainly the essence of a mitzvah as written "and you shall write them," and only it is not completion of the mitzvah before binding, as shown in Menachot 41b—he is always engaged in a mitzvah, even if we know his main reason for beginning to write was only wages and without that he would not have begun; nevertheless we say that now that he writes he intends only to write for the sake of the mitzvah of tefillin as required. And what Rashi explained, "those who buy in order to supply them," etc.—is it not only on "and their agents" that he commented, and his intent is that commerce in tefillin matters is not mentioned anywhere as a mitzvah; and even if we say that what he sells to one who needs tefillin is engaged in a mitzvah—at the time he buys the tefillin from the scribe in order to trade them there is not yet the essence of the mitzvah act in the deed itself; and the Gemara exempts them in all cases—therefore Rashi explained "those who buy in order to supply them to sell to whoever needs them," meaning that then by his thought that it is for the sake of a mitzvah he considers his act engaged in a mitzvah—unlike returning a lost object and writing sacred texts, where the act itself is engaged in a mitzvah.
And if they needed, etc.—see Arukh ha-Teshuvah and Magen Avraham and the Gra's Beur and other Acharonim that Rama's view is to rule like our Talmud and not like Yerushalmi; therefore he exempts them even from Keriat Shema and prayer itself, unlike the Mechaber—for according to him in all cases he is obligated in Keriat Shema and prayer; and it seems from Arukh ha-Teshuvah and Magen Avraham that the halachah follows Rama.
They need to do their work, etc.—see Mishnah Berurah, note 26, that I copied from Levush to the law; and so it seems from the language "need" that Rama wrote. But it is difficult: since Rama's view is like the Ran, as he concludes himself—and for the Ran one is exempt in all cases if he already began to write, as proven in Beit Yosef—perhaps one could say in such a case that one wishes to travel by sea or caravan where he will not obtain tefillin for several days, he is permitted even ab initio to begin writing even after the time of Keriat Shema arrived, even if thereby the time will pass; nevertheless it is not clear why he should obligate himself to begin writing or selling in order to be engaged in a mitzvah and remove from himself the positive time-bound commandments of Keriat Shema and prayer, etc.—and they say to a person, sin, etc.—uncertain. Also from Magen Avraham at the end of note 10, who wrote that all this deals, etc., it does not seem so; but one may say he did not deal with such a case as Levush; and were it not for Levush one could say Rama's intent in "they needed" is that the scribe estimates buyers will come that day, therefore he advances and hurries—for otherwise presumably a scribe would not write day and night so that he has no leisure for any mitzvah, even receiving Shema and tefillin; and so it seems in Avodat ha-Yom.
Then they are exempt—see Mishnah Berurah what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham; and post factum, even if he began in violation, nevertheless he need not interrupt, for he is engaged in a mitzvah; and as is there in siman 72: if they began to take him out they do not interrupt, even after the time of Keriat Shema arrived—as Peri Chadash wrote there.
For whoever is engaged, etc.—see Sukkah 26: agents of a mitzvah, such as one who goes to greet his teacher or to redeem captives, are also included among those engaged in a mitzvah and exempt from all mitzvot.
If he must trouble himself—see Mishnah Berurah; and so wrote the Ran; and although Tosafot, Rosh, and Ri'az are strict in this, nevertheless it seems from Beit Yosef and Rama that they agree to the Ran's halachah; and Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Or Zaru'a, because of the enormous proofs they brought for this; and Magen Avraham brought in the name of Hagahot Maimoni that Rambam and Geonim also hold one is exempt even if he could fulfill both; and the Ran wrote the reason: while one is engaged in his Master's work the Torah did not obligate him to trouble himself and fulfill other mitzvot, though he could then fulfill several mitzvot; so too one who digs a grave for the dead is exempt from all, though he rests a little—even while resting he is still called engaged in a mitzvah, for thereby he strengthens himself to return and dig; therefore he is exempt then even though he could then give a perutah to a poor person standing by him. But one wearing tefillin, though he fulfills a mitzvah, is not called engaged in a mitzvah to be exempt from all mitzvot except while wearing them, not afterward; and likewise regarding guarding a lost object, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, note 27—it is explained from the Ran and Or Zaru'a; and what Rama wrote, "but if," etc., "without trouble"—meaning he does not add any trouble at all for the second mitzvah, but one trouble for both; and as in the first mitzvah he can fulfill both, then certainly he should fulfill both—for "from good let not evil be named," as the Ran wrote there; and from the proof he brought there from the Gemara—the law is otherwise the Gemara would not challenge immediately—and see.
