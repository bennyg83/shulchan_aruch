"And his challenge." Rashi: those who buy in order to supply them to sell to whoever needs them — end quote; it implies that if he does so in order to profit he is not called engaged in a mitzvah. And it requires study: in Nedarim, folio 53, it implies that returning a lost object is engaged in a mitzvah even though he takes wages for it; and one could answer that there he takes only wages for lost time, or there his main intent is to return the lost object, but here his main intent is to profit — see siman 433, section 8.

"Except at the time of Keriat Shema." He raised a difficulty in Darchei Moshe: for in the Gemara they say they are exempt also from Keriat Shema; therefore he distinguished "if they needed," etc., as in the hagahah. And what Beit Yosef strained over — why Rambam omitted it — one could say he relied on what is written at the beginning of chapter 5 of Keriat Shema, that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah; and likewise in chapter 5 of Sukkah. And Magen Avraham on Mishneh Torah wrote that in his view and in the geonim's view, even if he need not exert himself, after both he is exempt; and Beit Yosef followed the Yerushalmi. And in my humble opinion the Yerushalmi deals when it is possible to fulfill both, but when he cannot fulfill both he is exempt — as is clear, for Rabbi Chanina ben Akiva himself said in the Gemara he is exempt, and in the Yerushalmi he said he is obligated; rather it must be said as written.

"Must exert himself" — such as agents of a mitzvah who must exert themselves to sleep in a sukkah; and likewise one guarding a corpse is exempt from all mitzvot even though he could fulfill all mitzvot; and one digging a grave, even if he rests a little, is exempt. But a guardian of a lost object while it rests in his box — even though he performs a mitzvah — is not called engaged in a mitzvah. And likewise regarding a groom who challenged and to obligate him in sukkah: since those making merry do not rejoice only in the chuppah but also in their rooms and upper chambers, and since when his guests arrive it is a mitzvah they should do both — and they answered: joy is only in the chuppah [thus Ran] — and it must be said that all this deals when he already began writing before the time of Keriat Shema arrived; but once the time of Keriat Shema arrived it is forbidden to begin writing, as written, siman 72, seif 2 — end of doubt, 287.
