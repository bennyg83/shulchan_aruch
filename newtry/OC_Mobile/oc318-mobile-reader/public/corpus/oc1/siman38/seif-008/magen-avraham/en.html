And their merchants. Rashi explains: those who buy them in order to make them available for sale to whoever needs them. This implies that if one does it to earn profit it is not called occupying himself with a mitzvah. This requires study from Nedarim, where one who returns a lost object is considered occupied with a mitzvah although he takes payment. One may answer that there he takes only payment for lost time, or that there his main intent is to return the lost object, whereas here his main intent is profit; see siman 433, seif 8.
Except during Krias Shema. The Darkei Moshe asked that the Gemara says they are exempt even from Krias Shema. Therefore he distinguished whether they are needed, as written in the gloss. The Beit Yosef's pressure over why the Rambam omitted this can be answered: he relied on what he wrote at the beginning of the laws of Krias Shema, that one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah. The Yerushalmi speaks where both can be fulfilled, but where both cannot be fulfilled he is exempt.
He must trouble himself. Such as agents of a mitzvah troubling themselves to sleep in a sukkah. One guarding the dead is exempt from all mitzvos even though he could perform them, and one digging a grave is exempt even when he rests a little. But one guarding a lost object while it is lying in his chest is not called occupied with a mitzvah. This follows the Ran, and it must be said that this speaks where he began writing before the time for Krias Shema arrived; once the time for Krias Shema arrives, it is forbidden to begin writing.
