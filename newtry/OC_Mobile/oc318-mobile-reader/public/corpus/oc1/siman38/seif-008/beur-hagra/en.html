Seif 8—other than, etc. Beit Yosef wrote that the Rambam omitted this law, and wrote that he relied on Yerushalmi, first chapter of Berakhot: R. Yochanan in the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai—such as we who are engaged in Torah, even for Keriat Shema we do not interrupt. R. Yochanan said about himself: such as we who cannot be engaged in Torah, even for prayer, etc., we do interrupt. R. Yosi: arise, R. Yirmiyah—the teaching of R. Yochanan is like R. Chanina ben Akiva, who taught: scribes of tefillin and mezuzot interrupt for Keriat Shema and do not interrupt for prayer. R. Chanina ben Akiva said: just as they interrupt for Keriat Shema, so they interrupt for prayer, tefillin, and all other mitzvot of the Torah. And Rabbi Shimon does not concede that one interrupts to make a sukkah or to make a lulav, etc.; Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's reason: this is study and this is study, and one does not cancel study because of study, etc.; and he wrote, and it is possible the baraita in the Gemara exempts only all day, other than, etc.; and already the Beit Yosef raised a difficulty—for in the Gemara it exempts even Keriat Shema and tefillin; but in Shulchan Aruch he relied on Yerushalmi, and the rabbi ruled like our Gemara; and this is what "and if," etc.

For all, etc.—if, etc. Mishnah, Sukkah 25a: agents of a mitzvah, etc.; and Rashi and Tosafot—even at the time of their encampment, as written, 26a: those going for a matter of mitzvah, etc.; and there, 12a: we are agents, etc.; and they raised a difficulty from what is in Nedarim 33b, a perutah of Rabbi Yosei is not common—evidently one is exempt only while engaged in it, such as to spread it, and the like; otherwise it is common and common. And one could answer that in Sukkah there and there it deals with such a case that if they return after the sukkah they will cancel another mitzvah; and so the Rosh and Ri'az; but the Ran there challenged them, for it does not seem so—for this reason they would cancel; and further they say: a groom is exempt from Keriat Shema until, etc.; and if so he should settle his mind, as in mourning there in Sukkah; and one could say it is impossible for him to settle his mind, for we learned: a groom, if he wishes to recite, etc.; and further, the gravedigger, etc., and it is impossible he does not rest a little; and further, one who guards the dead, etc., as in the beginning of chapter 3 of Berakhot—and he could fulfill several mitzvot; but certainly he is exempt even though he could fulfill both—specifically one engaged who is still going to fulfill; unlike that case in Nedarim where he is not engaged; and so in Hagahot Maimoniyot there; and the Ran wrote, and nevertheless I concede that whenever one must trouble at all, if as usual he can do both he should do both. And this is what it challenges there: "Let them recline in the sukkah," "Let them be alone in the sukkah"—for there is no way to rejoice only in the chuppah but also in rooms and upper chambers and sukkah.
