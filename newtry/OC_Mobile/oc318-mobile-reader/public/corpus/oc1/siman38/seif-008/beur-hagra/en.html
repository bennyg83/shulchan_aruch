Seif 8, except, etc. The Beis Yosef wrote that the Rambam omitted this law, and wrote that he relied on the Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 1: Rabbi Yochanan said in the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, people such as we, who are engaged in Torah study, do not interrupt even for Krias Shema. Rabbi Yochanan said about himself, people such as we, who are not engaged in Torah study, do interrupt even for prayer, etc. Rabbi Yose said before Rabbi Yirmiyah: the view of Rabbi Yochanan follows Rabbi Chanina ben Akiva, for it was taught: scribes of scrolls, tefillin, and mezuzos interrupt for Krias Shema but do not interrupt for prayer. Rabbi Chanina ben Akiva says: just as they interrupt for Krias Shema, so they interrupt for prayer, for tefillin, and for all other mitzvos of the Torah. Does Rabbi Shimon not concede that one interrupts to make a sukkah, to make a lulav, etc.? The reason of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai is that this is study and that is study, and one does not suspend study because of study, etc. He wrote that perhaps the baraisa in our Gemara exempted only all day except, etc. The Darchei Moshe already challenged this, for the Gemara exempted even from Krias Shema and tefillin. Rather, in Shulchan Aruch he relied on the Yerushalmi, while the Rav ruled like our Gemara, and this is why he wrote: and if, etc.
For anyone, etc., if, etc. The Mishnah in Sukkah 25a states: agents of a mitzvah, etc. Rashi and Tosafot explain that this applies even while they are encamped, as stated there 26a: those going for a mitzvah, etc., and there: we are agents, etc. They challenged this from Nedarim 33 to 34, where the perutah of Rav Yosef is uncommon, implying that he is exempt only while engaged in it, such as spreading it out and the like; otherwise it would be common indeed. They answered that in Sukkah there and there, it speaks in such a case that if they were to seek a sukkah, they would lose another mitzvah. So wrote the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham. But the Ran there challenged them, for it does not imply this, and because of this they would be neglecting it. Further, we say that a chasan is exempt from Krias Shema until Motzaei Shabbos, etc.; if so, let him settle his mind, as stated regarding an avel there in Sukkah. One cannot say that he cannot settle his mind, for the Mishnah teaches that if a chasan wishes to recite, etc. Further, one who digs a grave-niche, etc., surely rests a little. Further, one who guards the dead, etc., as written at the beginning of the third chapter of Berachos, can certainly fulfill several mitzvos. Rather, he is certainly exempt even though it is possible to fulfill both, but only when he is occupied in going to perform the mitzvah. This is unlike the case in Nedarim, where he is not occupied. So wrote the Hagahos Ashri there. The Ran wrote: nevertheless, I concede that whenever no effort is needed at all, but he can do both in his usual manner, he should do both. This is what it asks there: let them eat in a sukkah and rejoice in a sukkah, for the way to rejoice is not only in the chuppah, but also in rooms, upper stories, and a sukkah.
