Seif "One who reads," etc. Mechilta, parasha Bo: "So that the Torah of the the Omnipresent should be in your mouth"—from here they said: whoever puts on tefillin is as though he read in the Torah, and whoever reads in the Torah is exempt from tefillin; and they brought this in Tosafot, Rosh Hashanah 17a, s.v. karkapta.

Other than R. Yona, as written in chapter 2 of Berakhot: "Whoever reads," etc.—and the Rosh raised a difficulty in the name of R. Shmuel ben Hafni: we see elder rabbis who would put on tefillin and recite a blessing on them. So too we find in the Gemara in several places, as written above, siman 37, seif 2, and in chapter 4 of Sukkah—we see Rava, etc.; we see R. Pappa, that whenever, etc.; and in the third chapter of Menachot, Rav Chisda and Rabbah bar Rav Huna would pray wearing them in the evening, etc.; and in the third chapter of Berakhot, when they would walk behind R. Yehudah, etc., when he would hold tefillin, etc.; and we say at the end of chapter 6, and the people of the West, etc.; and Tosafot explain specifically that he removes them at night. And in Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Berakhot, R. Yehudah be-Rabbi Zeira did not have tefillin taken from him neither in summer nor in winter, etc.; R. Yochanan in summer, etc., as above—as when he would go to bathe, when he would reach the bathhouse he would remove them. R. Yitzchak: until Yaakov of Tarmodera he would wear them, and when he would leave the bath they would give them to him, etc.; and in many places in Shas and Yerushalmi. But the Mordekhai wrote specifically when he is engaged in Torah he is exempt from putting them on—for one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah; and even if he wishes to interrupt and put them on he may. But before he engages, or at the time of Keriat Shema and prayer, he is obligated to put them on and to wear them all day, even while engaged—as written in the book that preceded in the Hagahah; and specifically one whose Torah is his occupation, like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, as in the aforementioned Yerushalmi. And Rabbi Shimon does not concede that one interrupts to make a sukkah or to make a lulav; and Rabbi Shimon did not need "who learned in order to do" and "who did not learn in order to do"—for one who did not learn in order to do, it were better he had not been created, etc.; Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's reason: this is study and this is study, etc., as above; therefore he is also exempt from tefillin, which is also study; and this is what "so that it shall be," etc.—from here they said, etc. But in other mitzvot he is obligated, as in the first chapter of Kiddushin: every mitzvah whose obligation is upon him, he interrupts words of Torah for it—see there, 32a. Therefore they said he is exempt only from tefillin, and specifically such as Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai; but they said in the first chapter of Shabbat, 11a: except such as Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his colleagues, etc.—evidently such as we nullify study because of study, and so on.
