Seif 10, one who reads, etc. The Mechilta, Parshas Bo, on 'so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth': from here they said that anyone who wears tefillin is considered as though he read the Torah, and anyone who reads the Torah is exempt from tefillin. Tosafot cite this in Rosh Hashanah 17a, s.v. karkafata.
Except, etc. This is from Rabbeinu Yonah, as written in the second chapter of Berachos: anyone who reads, etc. The Rosh challenged in the name of Rav Shmuel ben Chofni, for we see the elder Sages who would wear tefillin and recite a blessing over them. We likewise find in the Gemara in several places, as I wrote above in siman 37, seif 2, and in the fourth chapter of Sukkah: I saw Rava, etc.; I saw Rav Pappa, that whenever, etc.; and in the third chapter of Menachos, Rav Chisda and Rabbah bar Rav Huna would pray with them in the evening, etc.; and in the third chapter of Berachos, when we would go after Rabbi Yochanan, etc., when he was holding tefillin, etc.; and we say at the end of chapter 6: and for the people of the West, etc. Tosafot explains that this was specifically when he removes them at night. In the Yerushalmi, Berachos chapter 2, Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai's tefillin did not move from him, neither in summer nor winter, etc.; Rabbi Yochanan in summer, etc., as above. When he would go to bathe, once he reached the bath attendants he would remove them. Rabbi Yitzchak said: until Yaakov of Tarmusra he would wear them, and when he came out from bathing they would give him, etc. So in many places in Shas and the Yerushalmi. But the Mordechai wrote that only while engaged in Torah is he exempt from putting them on, because one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah; and if he wants to stop and put them on, he may. But before he is engaged, or at the time of Krias Shema and prayer, he is obligated to put them on, and they should be upon him all day, even while he is engaged in Torah, as written in the preceding siman in the gloss. This is specifically one whose Torah is his occupation, like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, as written in the aforementioned Yerushalmi: does Rabbi Shimon not concede that one interrupts to make a sukkah, to make a lulav; and according to Rabbi Shimon, why do I need 'one who studies in order to do' and not 'one who studies not in order to do,' for one who studies not in order to do would have been better off not having been created, etc.? The reason of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai is that this is study and that is study, etc., as above. Therefore he is exempt also from tefillin, for they too are study. This is why it says 'so that it shall be, etc.,' from here they said, etc. But regarding other mitzvos he is obligated, as stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin: every mitzvah whose obligation rests upon him interrupts Torah study; see there 32a. Therefore they said that it exempted only from tefillin, and only someone like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai. But they said in the first chapter of Shabbos: this was taught only regarding someone like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his colleagues, etc. Thus, people such as we do suspend study because of study. See Beis Yosef.
