(32) In Torah—perhaps specifically in written Torah, but not one engaged in Gemara, for Torah may be said to be itself a sign commemorating the Exodus.

(33) Exempt—meaning he need not interrupt his study to put them on; but beforehand he is obligated. Also in the middle, if he wishes to interrupt and put them on, he may, and he may recite a blessing on them—for although he is exempt from interrupting for them, nevertheless since he wishes to interrupt his study, he is obligated in tefillin immediately upon interrupting.

(34) From putting on tefillin—and although he is obligated to interrupt Torah study to fulfill all mitzvot incumbent upon him, as in Yoreh De'ah, siman 200, the mitzvah of tefillin is different since its main benefit is for Torah, as it is written, "and for a reminder between your eyes so that the Torah of the the Omnipresent be in your mouth"; therefore, since he is already engaged in Torah, he need not interrupt Torah for this except at the time of Keriat Shema and prayer, to accept the yoke of the kingdom of heaven, and also because then he is not yet engaged in Torah and is obligated to wear tefillin. The Gra ruled in his Beur that this applies only to one whose Torah is his occupation, like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his colleagues; but as for us, we must interrupt even for tefillin.
