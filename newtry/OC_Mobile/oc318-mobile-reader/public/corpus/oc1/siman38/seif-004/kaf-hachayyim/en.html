11) If it is impossible for him to avoid improper thoughts, it is preferable that he not put on tefillin. This applies only when he is certain, not where he merely has a doubt. Magen Avraham 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 4.
12) It is better that he wait until his thoughts clear and he draws his heart toward fear of Heaven, dismissing the vanities that damage body and soul, and accepts the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven in fear and holiness. Levush; Shesilei Zeitim 7.
13) Nevertheless, he is held accountable for failing to do what he should: settle his mind and forcefully repel improper thoughts at all times, especially while wearing tefillin and praying. He bears a double obligation not to mix holy with mundane or impure with pure, like placing excrement in a king's food, which arouses great wrath. God is merciful and does not judge compulsion like willing conduct. Thoughts arise knowingly or unknowingly, and Chazal say no person escapes sinful thoughts every day. No claim is made against one who guards himself, battles to reject them, and exchanges evil thoughts for good ones. The author found that deeply and repeatedly reciting the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy is very helpful. But one who willingly prolongs such thoughts should fear punishment unless he repents. Chesed LaAlafim 2. Compare Kol Yaakov on Shoftim, regarding 'You shall not plant an asherah.'
14) Knesses HaGedolah 2 calls this opinion astonishing: should the mitzvah of tefillin be canceled because of thoughts? The Yerushalmi's definition of a clean body as clean thoughts may concern wearing tefillin all day like Elisha Baal Kenafayim, not exemption from tefillin during Krias Shema. Shaliach Tzibbur, page 40d; Yafeh LaLev 3. Yafeh LaLev questions why Beis Oved omitted this dissent. There is no difficulty: the dissent contradicts Maran and Rema and therefore was omitted. Beis Yosef's practical counsel is to persuade such people, draw their hearts toward fear of Heaven, remove vanity from them, and have them accept the heavenly yoke. See paragraphs 12-13.
