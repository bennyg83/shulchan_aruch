Thought. Beit Yosef raised a difficulty on this from Sukkah: Rabbi Yehuda said children always remove tefillin because they are accustomed to impurity, and Rashi explained that impure thought is common among them; and he challenged: let us say Rabbi Yehuda holds a baal keri is forbidden to put on tefillin; and they answered: here we deal with children and their wives with them — lest they come to habituation to the matter. In Beit Yosef what he answered and his resolution do not fit well, for nevertheless it is difficult why they answered "children and their wives with them — lest they come to habituation," for in any case habituation cannot occur without thought, and it should have said "lest they come to thought." And it must be said that "lest" they did not forbid putting them on except when he knows for certain that he cannot manage without thought — then it is forbidden to put them on.
