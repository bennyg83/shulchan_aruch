Rather. See Turei Zahav. In chapter Chelek, page 101, Rashi explains that uncovering his head showed disrespect, etc. Shimusha Rabba cites the statement: “What is the remedy? His teacher should wrap them first, and afterward his students should wrap them.” This explains both the rule that one removes tefillin close to sunset and the prohibition against ruling halakhah in one's teacher's presence; both are true. Throughout the day, only in his teacher's presence is the student forbidden to remove the head tefillin; it is possible that removing the arm tefillin is permitted. Toward evening, even turning his face away is ineffective, and removing the arm tefillin is also forbidden. But when his teacher removes his tefillin first, the student's act does not appear to be a ruling, and he may turn his face away and remove them. The apparently superfluous phrase “and afterward his students wrapped them” means that the student must remove, wrap, and place them in their case. If he did not wrap them properly but simply left them there, while his teacher had already wrapped and cased his own, the student would still appear to contradict his teacher's view: the teacher's conduct indicates that the time for removal has not yet arrived and that he merely wishes to enter the privy, etc. The same law applies if the teacher did not wrap them but merely removed them before the student; that is permitted. The teaching describes the usual case, because one ordinarily wraps the tefillin after removing them, and the student must act likewise. Bach, of blessed memory, however, explains the phrase as “to wrap his teacher's tefillin first”: the student must first wrap his teacher's tefillin, because they have necessarily already been handed over to him, so that the teacher will not divert his attention from them; afterward the student has permission to wrap his own. From here one may learn not to perform labor while wearing tefillin. See siman 42, where this is described as a mitzvah; see there.
