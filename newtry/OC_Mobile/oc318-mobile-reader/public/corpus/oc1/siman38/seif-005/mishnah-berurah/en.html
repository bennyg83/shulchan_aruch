(16) On the first day—meaning even on the day he is buried when it is not the day of death, since it is the first day of mourning and of comforters; therefore even if burial was at night he does not put on tefillin by day. But if death or burial was on chol ha-moed, he puts on tefillin whether on chol ha-moed [and the Peri Megadim questions in siman 71 whether it is still a bitter day for him; in any case he must be careful not to bless on them, and otherwise the custom is not to bless on tefillin on chol ha-moed] or after the festival—even though it is the first day of mourning, comforters already comforted him on the festival; and likewise if buried on Yom Tov, on the second day of Yom Tov he puts on tefillin, for the second day of Yom Tov counts toward the seven days and the day after the festival counts as the second. Likewise if he heard a close report [within thirty days, even on day thirty itself] the law is like the day of burial; therefore even if he heard at night he does not put on tefillin by day; and even if a close report came when he already wore tefillin and began to pray, he removes them. If he heard a distant report—after thirty days, when mourning is only one hour—he may wear tefillin and certainly need not remove them; therefore if the report came in the middle of p'sukei de-zimra and the like, he does not remove the tefillin but removes his shoes for mourning; and if due to the report he came to weeping, he must remove them, as shown in Yoreh De'ah, siman 388, seif 2, and siman 402, Shakh, note 2; and see below, seif 9.

(17) Forbidden—for tefillin are called "glory" in Scripture, and a mourner is "diminished in ashes," and it is unseemly to place glory beneath ashes.

(18) From then on—for it is written, "and its end like a bitter day," from which we learn that the main bitterness is the first day.

(19) Obligated—it implies he is obligated immediately; and some poskim hold that on the second day he does not put them on before sunrise; therefore it is proper to wait to put them on until after sunrise.

(20) New faces—to comfort him; and it implies he may put them on initially; but the Acharonim conclude one should not put on tefillin in their presence until they leave, but if he put them on before new faces arrived, he does not remove them.
