Seif katan 5, obligated, implies that he is obligated immediately. But in Yoreh Deah siman 388 it says after sunrise; this is how it should read. His intent is to challenge the words of Rabbi Beis Yosef against each other. This is a dispute among the Rishonim. In chapter Eilu Megalchin we rule like Rabbi Yehoshua, who says that an avel is forbidden to put on tefillin during the first two days. It answers that the second day is included as permitted, and Rav ruled that if he put them on the second day, even if new visitors came, he does not remove them. The Rif holds that when we say that the second day is included as permitted, although it first said that two days are forbidden, this is because on the second day we say that part of the day is like the whole day. Some say that since night is not a time for tefillin, the night does not count, and part of the second day is required, meaning after sunrise.
And some say that nevertheless the night counts. Rabbi Beis Yosef wrote as follows: although night is not a time for tefillin because of the concern that one may sleep in them, he is also forbidden then because of mourning, end quote. Therefore he is not forbidden at all on the second day, even before sunrise.
And it implies that he puts them on initially, etc. This is a separate matter and does not follow from what was written before. It means that it implies that he puts them on initially even if new visitors are with him at the time he puts them on.
