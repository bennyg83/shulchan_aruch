Seif 5—from here, etc.—the language of the Tur here, and it is per the Rambam in chapter 4 there, who wrote anonymously that on the second day he puts on and did not distinguish at all, and relied on the passage in chapter 2 of Sukkah; but the passage in Moed Katan there is specifically when he does not remove them before new comforters; and so in the Rif, chapter 3 of Moed Katan; and although in the passage there it seems specifically after part of the second day, on account of "part of the day is like the whole day," as in siman 388, seif 1—the Rif relied on what is written in chapter 2 of Sukkah, and this is the meaning, etc.; and what is in Moed Katan "two days and three" is not on account of mourning but because otherwise night is forbidden for tefillin—he said two and three, etc.; and wrote that thus in Arukh and Yerushalmi there: it is taught, a mourner on the first day does not put on tefillin; on the second day he puts on; and if new comforters came he removes them all seven—R. Yehudah, R. Elazar: on days one and two he does not put on, and on the third he puts on; and if new comforters came he does not remove them. With this is answered what was difficult for the Ramban, who holds that on the second day it is not on account of mourning—why does he hold he removes them? But according to Yerushalmi it is satisfactory: all seven days the law is thus, and not on account of the stringency of the second day; and so Rashba; and so in Tur, Yoreh De'ah, siman 388; but in Shulchan Aruch there they omitted it, and the Rosh and Tur there wrote specifically after sunrise; and as in the Gemara there, which says two days, etc.; and Tosafot there, s.v. mi-shelishi, etc.
