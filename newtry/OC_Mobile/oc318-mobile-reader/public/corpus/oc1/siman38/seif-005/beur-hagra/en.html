Seif 5, from here onward, etc. This is the language of the Tur here, and it follows the view of the Rambam at the end of chapter 4 there, who wrote plainly that on the second day he puts them on and did not distinguish at all, relying on the sugya in the second chapter of Sukkah. But the sugya in Moed Katan there refers specifically to one who does not remove them in the presence of new visitors. So wrote the Rif in the third chapter of Moed Katan. Although the sugya there implies specifically after part of the second day, because part of the day is like the whole day, as written in siman 388, seif 1, the Rif relied in this on what is stated in the second chapter of Sukkah: this applies only, etc. And that which is stated in Moed Katan, two days and three, is not because of mourning, but because in any event night is forbidden for tefillin, therefore it said two and three. This is difficult. He wrote that so it also appears in Eliyah Rabbah and the Yerushalmi there, where it is taught: an avel does not put on tefillin on the first day; on the second day he puts them on, and if new visitors come, he removes them all seven days. These are the words of Rabbi Yehoshua. Rabbi Eliezer says: on the first and second days he does not put them on, and on the third day he puts them on, and if new visitors come he does not remove them. With this is resolved what is difficult according to the Ramban, who maintains that the second day is not because of mourning, for why then does he hold that he removes them? According to the Yerushalmi it is understandable, for this is the law all seven days and not because of the severity of the second day. So wrote other poskim, and so too in Tur Yoreh Deah siman 388, but in Shulchan Aruch there they omitted it. The Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch there wrote that this is specifically after sunrise, and so it appears in the Gemara there, which says two days, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. from the third, etc.
