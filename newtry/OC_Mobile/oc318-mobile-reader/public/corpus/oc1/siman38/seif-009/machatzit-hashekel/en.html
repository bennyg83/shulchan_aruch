Seif katan 9, except, etc. He asked, etc., that they are exempt even from Krias Shema, for they are exempt from all mitzvos. If so, how can we find the statement that they must put them on at the time of Krias Shema and prayer?
And therefore he disagrees, etc. As written in the gloss, etc. The intent of this distinction is not that the Rama wanted thereby to answer his question on Rabbi Beis Yosef, for that is not so, since in Darchei Moshe he did not answer anything. Nor is this answer sufficient to explain the words of Rabbi Beis Yosef, for either way: if effort is required, he is exempt even from Krias Shema; and if no effort is required aside from Krias Shema, he is obligated in tefillin and in all mitzvos all day. Rather, the intent of Magen Avraham in writing 'and therefore he distinguished, etc.' is that because he challenged Rabbi Beis Yosef, he therefore disagrees with Rabbi Beis Yosef's ruling and writes what appears to the Rama to emerge from the view of the poskim, even though the Rama did not write it in the language of 'some say.' This is his way, as Magen Avraham also wrote above in siman 33, seif katan 5; see there.
Why did he omit it, etc. That is to say, this baraisa that scribes of tefillin, etc., are exempt, etc. He wrote that the Rambam relied on the Yerushalmi, which teaches there that the first Tanna obligates scribes of scrolls in Krias Shema but not in prayer, while Rabbi Chanina ben Akiva obligates them even in prayer. Therefore he ruled like the Yerushalmi, for they are many; see there.
And Rabbi Beis Yosef wrote that he followed, etc. It should read: that it is possible to fulfill both, etc. But the Maggid Mishneh whom Magen Avraham cited, who wrote that the Rambam holds that even if he does not need to exert himself at all he is exempt, necessarily does not hold this.
