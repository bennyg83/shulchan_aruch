(9) There—Hagahah: if women wish to be strict on themselves we object to them; and so agreed Birkei Yosef, letter 1—see there; and see the late Rabbi Beit Hillel in Yoreh De'ah, siman 282, note 2, who brought from Targum Yonatan, parashat Ki Teitzei, on "there shall not be a man's vessel on a woman," who translates "there shall not be exile"—tzitzit and tefillin, which are men's adornments, on a woman—therefore it implies not only that she is exempt from tzitzit and tefillin but also transgresses a prohibition; and they brought Petach Devir, letter 2—see there. And further see in Sefer Shomer Emunim, parashat Ki Teitzei, on "there shall not be a man's vessel on a woman," who wrote: a man's vessel—in alef-beit, bet-shin-gimmel—this is tefillin, which are a secret of the masculine world, etc.; and there is no proof from Michal bat Shaul who put on tefillin—end quote. They brought well in Yafeh Lelev, letter 2, who wrote: what he said "and there is no proof from Michal bat Shaul," etc.—one could answer Michal bat Shaul was a wise woman and knew herself she had a soul from the masculine world, therefore Michal bat Shaul had no children—end quote. And see Shaar ha-Gilgulim, introduction 9, who wrote that a woman in whom a male soul is incarnated cannot conceive unless by great merit some other female soul incarnates in her—in secret of pregnancy, etc.—see there; and see above, siman 172, letter 5.
