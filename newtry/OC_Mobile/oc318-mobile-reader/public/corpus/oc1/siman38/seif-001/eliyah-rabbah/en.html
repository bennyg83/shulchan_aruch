[1] If one is distressed, etc. But from Levush's language, who wrote "sick," it implies he states two things: sick or distressed even though not sick—and that is why he concluded "they are exempt," etc., "because their minds," etc., in plural language; for if he spoke only of sickness he should have said "he is exempt, for his mind," singular. Now it is satisfactory that he omitted in this siman an entire seif from Shulchan Aruch—his language: distressed, and one whose mind is not settled upon him properly, is exempt, because it is forbidden to divert his attention from them—end of quote. Rather it seems to me he explained in this seif as he wrote "and distressed," etc.; and in any case it seems he also concedes a sick person without distress is obligated, since he wrote "intestinal illness even if he is not distressed," etc.—it implies other illnesses specifically require distress; but he holds that unspecified illness has distress, and the unspecified follows his explanation. Magen Avraham wrote distressed even from cold is exempt; and "whose mind is not settled" means when he cannot settle his mind. Intestinal illness—even walking in streets and markets is exempt, and forbidden to be strict on himself except at Keriat Shema and prayer time—end quote. But in responsum of Menachem Azariah, siman 60, it seems even not at Keriat Shema and prayer he may put them on when he has a time of fitness; and at his time of distress he removes them; and if he was compelled and did not remove the hand-tefillin he did not transgress. Acharonim wrote the same for one who cannot guard himself from passing gas except by removing head-tefillin—he puts on hand-tefillin too. Sefer Chasidim, siman 249: one who wears trousers and is bare from the trousers upward shall not put on tefillin, because his heart is bare and he should not bless; and it is not fitting to be bare and wear tefillin. One who hears and does not speak puts on tefillin; and likewise one who speaks and does not hear—end quote. What he wrote "and it is not fitting," etc., seems extra; he meant even where no blessing is needed, it is not fitting to be bare and wear tefillin. Kenesset ha-Gedolah wrote: a deaf-mute who does not hear and does not speak is exempt from tefillin, being exempt from all mitzvot; nevertheless if he wishes to put them on we do not object. Beit Yosef wrote: the Rosh wrote in Sefer Itim that a blind person is exempt from tefillin, as written "and you shall see them"—and he disagrees, writing "and you shall see them" regarding tzitzit is written—end quote; therefore Shulchan Aruch omitted it; but I saw Rabbeinu Yerucham ruled like Sefer Itim, and so in Barukh She'amar in the name of the Rosh. On the Rosh's difficulties one may say Sefer Itim learned from "and all the peoples of the earth shall see," written regarding tefillin, as Madanei Melekh wrote, page 96—and it needs study.
