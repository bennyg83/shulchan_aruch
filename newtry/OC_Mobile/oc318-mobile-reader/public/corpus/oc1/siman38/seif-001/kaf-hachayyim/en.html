1) [Se'if 1] An intestinally ill person is exempt from tefillin, etc. He is exempt even if he presently feels no pain and walks in the marketplaces and streets. It is forbidden for him to be stringent, except that he may don them during Krias Shema and prayer if he can keep his body clean, as stated in se'if 2. Magen Avraham 1; Soles Belulah 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1; Siddur Beis Oved, laws of those obligated in tefillin 1; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 10:23. Responsa Rema MiPano 60 implies that even outside Krias Shema and prayer he may wear them at a suitable time and remove them when distress begins. If he was overtaken by circumstances and did not remove the arm-tefillin, he committed no prohibition. Eliyah Rabbah 1. See below, paragraph 5.
2) A person who wears trousers but is bare from the trousers upward should not don tefillin, because his heart is exposed. Sefer Chasidim 774, cited by Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur, siman 37; Magen Avraham 1; Eliyah Rabbah 1; Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1; Machatzis HaShekel 1; Shesilei Zeitim 2; Pischei Olam 1. An open neckline is not included in this prohibition. Pri Megadim and Pischei Olam there.
3) A person who hears but cannot speak should don tefillin, and so should one who speaks but cannot hear. Sefer Chasidim there, cited by Knesses HaGedolah. Knesses HaGedolah writes that Sefer Chasidim means these people are obligated, whereas the deaf-mute discussed by Chazal (who neither hears nor speaks) is exempt from tefillin because he is exempt from all mitzvos. Nevertheless, if he wishes to don them, he is not protested; Knesses HaGedolah saw several such people in Constantinople wearing tefillin without rabbinic protest. So also write Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah 4; Olas Tamid; Eliyah Rabbah 8; Shaliach Tzibbur, page 40d; and Shesilei Zeitim 2.
4) A blind person is obligated in tefillin. Pri Megadim and Pischei Olam there. See what we wrote above in siman 17, paragraph 2, and what Beis Yosef and Maamar Mordechai 1 write in this siman.
