That the time caused it—see also the Rosh brought in Beit Yosef, may his memory be blessed, that night is a time for tefillin for us only rabbinically (not like Rambam, siman 64, seif 2). And see from Magen Avraham that a rabbinic time-bound commandment is the same; if Shabbat were a time for tefillin, night would not be a rabbinic time. Perhaps one can say Tosafot, Sukkah 43a, at the end of the discussion that whatever the Sages instituted and decreed—if one performed the mitzvah nevertheless and transgressed their decree, he did not fulfill the mitzvah nevertheless; also "do not turn aside" is a Torah commandment. See Rabbenu Yona at the beginning of Berakhot whether one who read after midnight—it is a rabbinic enactment so as to distance; if he transgressed, he fulfills afterward, unlike other cases; if so, at night too, rabbinically, there is no mitzvah whatsoever nevertheless. And see Magen Avraham, siman 58, note 7; and according to this it is resolved that rabbinically after the fourth hour one has no reward for Keriat Shema but only like one reading Torah—it is a positive time-bound commandment even though the time is only rabbinic; if so, it will be explained further, and in siman 106, Magen Avraham, note 12, from this.
