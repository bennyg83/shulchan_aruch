Rosh writes likewise, as cited by Beit Yosef, of blessed memory: according to our view, night is a time for tefillin by Torah law and is excluded only rabbinically. This is unlike Rabbi Meir; see siman 30, se'if 2. One must examine the status of a time-bound positive commandment where the time restriction is only rabbinic. For example, suppose Shabbat were a time for tefillin and only night were excluded rabbinically. Tosafos in Sukkah, page 3a, s.v. de'amar, writes at the end of the passage that whenever the Sages prohibited and decreed something, one who performs the Torah mitzvah while violating their decree does not fulfill the Torah mitzvah either; the Torah command “you shall not depart” gives their enactment Torah force. See Rabbeinu Yonah at the beginning of Berachos regarding one who recited after midnight. There, the Sages instituted the deadline only as a protective measure; if one violated it, he nevertheless fulfills his obligation afterward. That differs from ordinary rabbinic exclusions. If so, at night too, according to the Sages, there is no Torah mitzvah at all. See Magen Avraham, siman 58, ot 7. According to what I wrote, it is also understandable that rabbinically, after the fourth hour one no longer receives the reward for the positive mitzvah of Keriat Shema, but only the reward of one who reads Torah. It is therefore a time-bound positive commandment even though its time limitation is only rabbinic.
