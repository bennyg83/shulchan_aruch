(22) And all members of the wedding party, etc.—specifically in the place of the chuppah where intoxication and frivolity are common.

(23) Exempt—in responsum Rama, siman 132, he wrote that nowadays, since even the groom is obligated in Keriat Shema and prayer [as in siman 70], the groom and all members of the wedding party are likewise obligated in tefillin—see there; and see in the Magen Avraham who questions his ruling and leans to rule like Shulchan Aruch that they are exempt from tefillin during the feast; and regarding prayer too, one who is lenient like Rashi to exempt during the feast has not lost if the groom reclines among them, for then it is a mitzvah according to Rashi; but from the Gra's words in his Beur it seems one should not be lenient regarding prayer, as in Beit Yosef, siman 432; and see in Olat Tamid and Birkei Yosef that the custom spread like Rama—that the groom and all his company read, wear tefillin, and pray from the first day through the seventh.
