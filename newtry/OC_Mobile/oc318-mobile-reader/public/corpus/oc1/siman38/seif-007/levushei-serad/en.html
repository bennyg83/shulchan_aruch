In the second chapter of Sukkah. The bridegroom, his attendants, and the wedding party are exempt from prayer and tefillin but obligated in Krias Shema. Rabbi Shila says the bridegroom is exempt from all of them, while the others are obligated. Rashi explains that the first Tanna exempts them from prayer because it requires concentration, and from tefillin because intoxication is a concern, but obligates them in Krias Shema because intention is needed only for the first verse. Rabbi Shila exempts the bridegroom because he is preoccupied, but obligates the others. According to Rashi, neither opinion bases the law on exemption while engaged in a mitzvah: the first Tanna obligates even the groom in Krias Shema, and Rabbi Shila obligates the wedding party. The Rosh, however, explains that both do rely on this exemption. The first Tanna holds that the groom can readily concentrate for the first verse, while Rabbi Shila holds that he cannot; Rabbi Shila also holds that the rest of the wedding party are not considered engaged in the mitzvah. The halachah follows Rabbi Shila. According to Rashi all are engaged in the mitzvah; therefore, since we rule that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah, all are exempt even nowadays. This explains Magen Avraham.
