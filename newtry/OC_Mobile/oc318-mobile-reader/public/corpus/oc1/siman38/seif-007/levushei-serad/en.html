Note 7: as Rabbi Shila taught—Sukkah, chapter 2: groom, attendants, and all wedding party exempt from prayer and tefillin, obligated in Keriat Shema; Rabbi Shila: groom exempt, others obligated. Rashi: first Tanna—prayer needs concentration, tefillin because of intoxication; Keriat Shema needs only first verse concentration briefly. Rabbi Shila: groom exempt from Keriat Shema because preoccupied; others obligated. These tannaim do not hold "engaged in mitzvah exempt from mitzvah"—for first Tanna obligates groom in Keriat Shema; Rabbi Shila obligates attendants. Rosh: tannaim hold engaged exempt; unlike Rashi—first Tanna obligates Keriat Shema because concentration is mainly first verse, easy briefly; Rabbi Shila: groom cannot concentrate at all. Rabbi Shila does not say attendants obligated in prayer and tefillin—only Keriat Shema; they are not "engaged"—if so, exempt from Keriat Shema too. Exemption from prayer: cannot concentrate due to wedding joy and frivolity—end of Rosh. Peri Megadim explains Rosh's "not because engaged" difficulty for Rabbi Shila re Keriat Shema. For us, who hold engaged exempt, all are exempt without concentration reason—only engaged-in-mitzvah—and this applies nowadays; through this understand all Magen Avraham's words here.
