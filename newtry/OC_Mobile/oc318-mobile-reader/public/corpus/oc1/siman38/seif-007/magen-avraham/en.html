"A groom," etc. It is written in Rama's responsum, siman 132, that nowadays, since they are obligated in Keriat Shema and prayer [as written, siman 70, seif 3], they are also obligated in tefillin, for it is as though he bears false testimony against himself — end quote. And it requires study: for per the Rosh, what Rabbi Shila taught — groomsmen are obligated — deals with Keriat Shema, but for tefillin they are exempt, and we do not say he bears false testimony against himself since he is exempt at times like Shabbatot, Yom Tov, and nights; and also for the first tanna they are obligated in Keriat Shema and exempt from tefillin. And it is strained to say that what teaches "exempt" means all day but at the time of Keriat Shema they are obligated — this is strained; and it should read that in the Gemara it does not say they are obligated in Keriat Shema but only in the first verse; therefore it is not like false testimony — unlike for us, who are obligated in the full Keriat Shema, who are also obligated in tefillin so he not be like one bearing false testimony. But from Rashi's language it implies they are obligated in the full Keriat Shema, only that intent is not required except in the first verse. And in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 7, he wrote that they were reclining at a wedding feast on Shabbat and the time for Minchah arrived, and many geonim exempted them from praying Minchah, for they held like Rashi that the reason they are exempt is because one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from a mitzvah — if so there is no difference between their time and ours. But for the Rosh, who holds they are not engaged in mitzvot but the reason is they cannot intend — if so we, who in any case do not intend well, are obligated. And in Levush he also wrote they are exempt in the wedding banquet hall of the chuppah; and in Beit Yosef, siman 232, it implies he holds to forbid; and in my humble opinion one who is lenient has not lost; and likewise in summer days when sometimes the wedding feast continues until morning they are exempt from all, provided the groom reclines with them — for then it is a mitzvah according to Rashi. End of doubt, 70 and 232; and see responsum of Ra'ah, siman 48; and thus he wrote that in his country they are accustomed that the groom does not put on tefillin on the first day; but Rabbi Shimon Halevi wrote he must put them on; and in my humble opinion, as written, at the time of the feast he is exempt — as is clear from the words of Shulchan Aruch.
