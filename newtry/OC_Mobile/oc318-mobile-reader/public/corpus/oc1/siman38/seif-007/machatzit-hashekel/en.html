Seif katan 7, a chasan, etc. And nowadays, when they are obligated, etc., although in the time of the Shas they were exempt because concentration was required and they could not concentrate, this is unlike our time, when at all times, because of our sins, we do not concentrate. A chasan is therefore obligated to recite it, and the same applies to prayer.
That which Rabbi Shimon taught, etc. In Sukkah 25b it is taught: a chasan, his attendants, and all members of the wedding party are exempt from prayer and from tefillin. Rashi explains: from prayer, because it requires concentration; from tefillin, because drunkenness and levity are common. They are obligated in Krias Shema. Rashi explains: because its mitzvah of concentration is only for the first verse, and they can settle their mind briefly to recite the first verse. In the name of Rabbi Sheila they said that the chasan is exempt, but his attendants and all members of the wedding party are obligated. Rashi explains that the chasan is exempt from Krias Shema because he is preoccupied, while the others are obligated, and these Tannaim do not hold that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah. The Rosh wrote as follows: these Tannaim do not hold that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah, unlike the aforementioned Rashi. Rather, the first Tanna obligates them in Krias Shema because its primary concentration is for the first verse and one can easily concentrate and settle his mind for one verse. Rabbi Shimon holds that a chasan cannot concentrate his mind at all. Rabbi Shimon says only that attendants and members of the wedding party are obligated in Krias Shema, but he concedes that they are exempt from prayer and tefillin, not because they are engaged in a mitzvah, for if so they should be exempt even from Krias Shema. Rather, they are not called engaged in a mitzvah, and they are exempt from prayer because due to their joy they cannot concentrate for prayer, since joy and levity are common at a wedding, end quote. The Bach explained his words: when he wrote that it is not because they are engaged, etc., for if so they should be exempt even from Krias Shema, although he wrote above that since the main concentration is for the first verse they can easily concentrate, nevertheless there is a difficulty according to Rabbi Shimon, who exempts a chasan from Krias Shema. If so, if they were called engaged in a mitzvah, they too should be exempt, for when they are reciting Krias Shema and the chasan is not reciting at that time, they cannot rejoice with the chasan. Rather, because of this reason they are not called engaged in a mitzvah, end of his words. I copied all of this, because through it all the words of the Magen Avraham in this seif katan will be understood.
Since he is exempt, that is to say, for this reason it is not false testimony, because at this time the mitzvah does not rest upon him. Just as on Shabbos, Yom Tov, and at night, when Krias Shema is recited without tefillin, since one is exempt at those times, it is not false testimony.
And it is difficult to say in this, etc. For this is difficult; see there. It must read that the Gemara did not say, etc. The Magen Avraham's intent in writing that it must read that the Gemara, etc., is not to explain that these are the words of the Rama, for the Rama necessarily does not hold this, since in that responsum the Rama himself wrote to explain the sugya of the Shas that the Gemara exempted them only all day, but at the time of Krias Shema they are obligated, like the difficult answer to say that the Magen Avraham wrote. Rather, the Magen Avraham comes to explain the common practice that he wrote there in the responsum, that the practice is for the chasan and attendants to put on tefillin. Therefore one must say that it is because of appearing like false testimony; see there. Therefore one must distinguish between this case and Shabbos, Yom Tov, and night. But to explain the sugya of the Shas, one must say that the Gemara speaks only of the first verse; therefore it is not like testifying false testimony in the first verse, for the mitzvah of tefillin is not mentioned there. But from Rashi's language, etc., where he wrote that mitzvos require intent, etc., and that they can settle their minds, etc., as above, he holds like Rashi's view that the reason, etc. The language is difficult, for certainly even according to Rashi the reason of those Tannaim is that they cannot concentrate. According to this, for us, who in any case do not concentrate, they would be obligated. Rather, he means that for us there is another reason to exempt according to Rashi, since Rashi holds that they are called engaged in a mitzvah, only those Tannaim hold that people engaged in mitzvos are obligated in mitzvos. Therefore he needed the reason that they cannot concentrate. If so, for us, who rule like the opinion that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah, without the reason that they cannot concentrate they are exempt, even nowadays.
