(Note 7) groom—nowadays obligated in Keriat Shema and prayer—therefore groom and party obligated in tefillin too.

What Rabbi Shimon taught—Sukkah 25b: groom, attendants, party exempt from prayer and tefillin (Rashi: prayer needs kavanah, tefillin—intoxication), obligated in Keriat Shema (Rashi: kavanah mainly first verse, brief). Rabbi Shimon: groom exempt. Rosh: tannaim hold engaged exempt—not Rashi; first Tanna obligates Keriat Shema—easy first verse; Rabbi Shimon: groom cannot concentrate at all; Rabbi Shimon exempts attendants only from Keriat Shema, agrees exempt from prayer and tefillin—not because engaged (would exempt from Keriat Shema too) but cannot concentrate due to joy. Peri Megadim on Rosh's difficulty re Rabbi Shimon and Keriat Shema. For us, engaged exempt—all exempt without concentration reason—applies now—understand Magen Avraham here.

Since exempt—meaning then not false testimony, for mitzvah not incumbent that time; likewise Shabbat, Yom Tov, night when reading Keriat Shema without tefillin—not false testimony when exempt then.

Rama strained—Gemara did not say obligated in Keriat Shema except first verse—so not false testimony; Rama in responsum reconciled Gemara: exempt all day, obligated at Keriat Shema; strained wording reconciles custom that groom and attendants wear tefillin; distinguish from Shabbat and Yom Tov; to reconcile Gemara say only first verse—Rashi: mitzvot need kavanah, can settle briefly—tannaim hold engaged exempt yet obligate Keriat Shema. For us, no kavanah anyway—were obligated; Rashi holds engaged—tannaim hold engaged obligated in mitzvot; need cannot-concentrate reason—so nowadays exempt even at feast—Peri Megadim on Rosh vs Rashi.

Beit Yosef, siman 432: that view not accepted—exempt from all; Rama in responsum reconciled Gemara and custom.
