3) “On the first day, etc.” The same law applies on the day one receives a recent report of a death, as the Taz and Shach write in Yoreh De’ah, siman 388. If he heard the report after praying Maariv (when, as we rule at the end of siman 375, that day does not count) there is room to question whether he puts on tefillin the following day, which is called his first day of mourning. The Taz in Yoreh De’ah, siman 402, se’if 11, implies to me that he does put on tefillin. It appears that on the day of burial one does not put on tefillin even if the person died the previous day, just as with a recent report, whose status is rabbinic and is comparable to a burial day when the death occurred the day before. R’ Yom Tov Tzahalon writes that tefillin are omitted specifically when the day of death is also the day of burial, for then the obligation is by Torah law. In my humble opinion this is the primary view. Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 82, and the Taz, siman 398, likewise imply this; examine carefully. The Taz here also somewhat implies it. It appears that if a person’s relative died and was buried on Yom Tov, he puts on tefillin on the first weekday after Yom Tov, since he has already observed the private aspects of mourning. Consolation is also offered on Yom Tov, as stated below in siman 548. Regarding one who becomes a mourner on Chol HaMoed, the Magen Avraham in siman 548 writes that he puts on tefillin, because mourning does not take effect on Chol HaMoed.
