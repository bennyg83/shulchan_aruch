But—Magen Avraham; the wording is unclear. And in Orach Chaim, note 3, he explained his words: on the day of burial even if it is not the day of death; and the same for a close report—he does not wear on the day of the report; see siman 388, Taz, and Shakh, and siman 402, seif 2: for a distant report he does not remove—see there. One who died and was buried on Yom Tov wears tefillin on chol ha-moed, and on the first day of mourning after Yom Tov—for comforters already comforted on Yom Tov; see Orach Chaim 548 in Magen Avraham and Elyah Rabbah here. If he heard a close report after evening prayer of that day—which does not count—Elyah Rabbah wrote that the next day he wears tefillin, as in Yoreh De'ah 402, seif 11—Magen Avraham. His proof is from what is written there in note 7; but there it deals with day thirty, where the day certainly begins and the next day is the second day of mourning; but the day after, one could say day one is a bitter day and one should not place glory beneath ashes or divert attention—and it is forbidden; perhaps on day two, which is his first day, there is not such bitterness that the Torah forbade a mourner on the second day—see Berakhot 11a, Tosafot, s.v. shene'emar, that day one is bitter and glory is not fitting on a bitter day, but on day two his bitterness is not so great; apparently the Torah did not forbid a mourner on the second day—see Moed Katan 15. The Levush wrote the reason that on day one it is bitter and he diverts attention from them—this is not said in Moed Katan or Berakhot; according to his words, on day one of chol ha-moed one does not wear tefillin because he is distressed and diverts attention, unlike Rashi in Berakhot 11—see Magen Avraham, note 6, on this, with God's help. Per the Levush he is exempt but permitted.
