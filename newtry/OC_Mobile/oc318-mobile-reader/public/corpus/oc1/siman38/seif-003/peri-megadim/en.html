But. See Turei Zahav; the wording is obscure. See Eliyahu Rabbah, ot 3, who explains that the law applies on the burial day even when it is not the day of death. The same applies to a recent report of a death: one does not put on tefillin on the day the report is heard. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 388, Turei Zahav and Shach, and Tevuot Shor, se'if 2. Upon a distant report one does not remove them; see there. If someone died and was buried on Yom Tov, the mourner puts on tefillin on Chol HaMoed and on the first day of mourning after Yom Tov, because the comforters have already comforted him. See Orach Chayim, siman 548, Magen Avraham, and Eliyahu Rabbah here. If one heard a recent report after Ma'ariv, so that that calendar day does not count toward mourning, Eliyahu Rabbah writes that he puts on tefillin the next day. This appears from the cited responsum in Yoreh De'ah, part 2, siman 11; see Turei Zahav. His proof is from what is written there in ot 7. Yet that passage concerns the thirtieth day, where the current day certainly begins the mourning and the next day is the second day. If the report was heard on another day, one may say that the following day is his first day, a bitter day, when “his horn is rolled in the dust,” or he may divert his attention from the tefillin, and it is forbidden. It is possible that although it is the second calendar day, it is his first day of mourning. In such a case we do not apply the rule that an unmentioned distinction is used only to rule stringently, as explained above. Regarding the stated reason, see Berachos, page 11a, Tosafos, s.v. shene'emar: the first day is bitter, and pe'er is unsuitable on a bitter day; on the second day his bitterness is presumably less intense, and the Torah did not forbid tefillin to a mourner then. See Moed Katan, page 15. Levush writes that the reason is that on the first day he is bitter and will divert his attention from the tefillin. I do not know the source for this either in Moed Katan or in Berachos. According to his reasoning, on the first day of Chol HaMoed the mourner would not put on tefillin because he is distressed and will divert his attention, unlike Rashi in Berachos, page 11; see there.
