7) [Se'if 3] Women and slaves are exempt from tefillin because it is a time-bound positive commandment. Although we rule like Rabbi Akiva that nighttime is a time for tefillin, Shabbos and Yom Tov are not times for tefillin, so it remains time-bound. Rosh, Halachos Ketanos, Hilchos Tefillin 29; Beis Yosef; Levush; Taz 1. See Rosh there for additional reasons.
9) The same applies according to the Arizal. See Etz Chaim, gate 34, chapter 1; Shaar HaKavanos, fifth discourse on tefillin; Pri Etz Chaim, gate of tefillin, chapter 7; and Olas Tamid, new edition, page 31a.
10) Rema writes that women who wish to be stringent should be protested. Birkei Yosef agrees. Beis Hillel on Yoreh De'ah 182:2 cites Targum Yonasan on 'A man's garment shall not be upon a woman': tzitzis and tefillin are male ornaments. This implies not merely exemption but prohibition. See Pischei HaDvir II. Shomer Emunim on Ki Seitzei writes that the numerical value of 'kli gever' through Atbash alludes to tefillin, which pertain to the male world; Michal's conduct is no proof. Yafeh LaLev explains that Michal was wise and knew that her soul derived from the male aspect, which is why she had no child. Shaar HaGilgulim, introduction 9, teaches that a woman whose soul is a reincarnated male soul cannot conceive unless, through great merit, an additional female soul enters her by ibbur. See siman 17, paragraph 5.
