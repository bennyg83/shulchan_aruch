(s.k. 5) And the same applies to untying — that is according to Rambam. Meaning the prohibition of tying; and likewise untying with two knots one upon the other when not permanent — specifically according to Rif and Rambam and those who rule like them, Mechaber ruled that a artisan's act alone, even if not permanent, is nevertheless forbidden l'chatchila; therefore two knots that are tight and called a artisan's act, as Shulchan Gavoah wrote in the previous s.k., are forbidden whether tying or untying. But according to Rosh whom Rama brought — a artisan's act does not raise or lower; it depends only on the nature of the knot if permanent; therefore even two knots one upon the other when not a permanent knot — per the view that it is made to be untied that day, and per the lenient view within seven days — both tying and untying are permitted l'chatchila, not like Eliyah Rabbah who wrote that when the Gemara resolves the two baraitot — "liable" deals with a artisan's act and "exempt" with a layman's act — Rosh too holds one is liable for a pure artisan's act even if not permanent; rather Rosh holds that on a permanent knot even a layman's act is liable; therefore for Rosh two knots one upon the other that are a artisan's act — liable for tying and untying. But Magen Avraham's view is not so: for Rosh a artisan's act does not raise or lower at all; everything depends on the nature of the knot as stated. And when the Gemara resolves "liable" in a artisan's act — it means that it is permanent, i.e., forever or a long time; and it mentions "artisan's act" because it is the manner of speech that a artisan ties a permanent knot.
