A bow is permitted, etc. — Meaning even if he tightens it well and his intent is that it endure so always; see at the beginning of the chapter VeEilu Kesharim in the Gemara. And incidentally I will explain another law I saw in Rambam and copied in Mishna Berurah regarding making a knot from animal food; and thus Rambam there: Whatever is fit for animal food is permitted to tie on Shabbat; therefore if his sandal strap broke in a karmelit he takes a moist reed fit for animal food, wraps it on the strap, and ties. And one may challenge: it appears he contradicts himself, for later in law 16 he wrote permitted to tie a non-permanent knot for a mitzvah matter; and the source of the law Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote is from Shabbat 157, where it states "and from their words we learned we plug, measure, and tie on Shabbat" — yet there it states they tied the measuring reed with a reed; and see Rashi there — the intent is moist reed; and if so per the aforementioned Rambam there is no knot at all, as stated. And if so, what proof does he bring that permitted to make a non-permanent knot d'oraisa [for Rambam even if a artisan's act] for a mitzvah? Therefore Rambam must hold not like Rashi, but the act was with dry reed not fit again for animal food; and he holds that with dry reed there is at least a rabbinic knot concern where one makes a artisan's act; and it is also possible there is d'oraisa prohibition where one also makes a permanent knot. And for Rashi who wrote the act was with moist reed fit for animal food — possible he truly holds there was no knot at all by law, not even a rabbinic knot, as Rashi explained he does not nullify it; and what he said afterward "from their words we learned we tie on Shabbat" — that is the law itself that a non-permanent knot is permitted l'chatchila, and it does not deal with a knot somewhat permanent that is forbidden rabbinically and permitted here due to mitzvah need; and Beit Meir sided thus for Rashi's view, as I wrote in his name in Mishna Berurah. And according to what we explained and resolved Rambam's words, Chayei Adam's words are very difficult — he begins in seif 8 with Rambam's words and concludes for Rashi's view; and see siman 324 seif 4 in the gloss, where it is proven that also for animal food there is tying and untying; and so appears from Rashi's language Shabbat 157b in the passage "with a reed"; and so appears Rav HaMeirich brought in Kesef Mishneh chapter 10 of Hilchot Shabbat, see there.
