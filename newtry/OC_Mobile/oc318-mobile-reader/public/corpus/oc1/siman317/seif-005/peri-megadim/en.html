(א) One may untie. In Taz: for Rambam in seif 1 it deals when otherwise forbidden even if not permanent; and whatever is not a artisan's knot, even if not untied that day — whatever is not permanent forever, Mechaber may say permitted, as is written in sign 1. And through a non-Jew permitted — shevut of shevut where there is great need, as in siman 307 seif 5.

(ב) If. Permitted to tell a non-Jew to make a knot or untie a permanent knot without artisan's act where there is great need — if one may say there is doubt of dispute between Rambam and Rosh, and Rama did not decide in seif 1, as appears from seif 2 and Taz 4 as written there; if we say the doubt is rabbinic — it is shevut of shevut; nevertheless one may say wherever there is d'oraisa concern it is not called shevut of shevut in such a case; and behold with gentile cheese we are not lenient in their doubt — the same here; nevertheless for tying there is no "great need," and he will think to tie for another day; and for untying one may say yes. Again I saw it is not so: for whom do we say Rosh permits untying with intent to tie — liable; otherwise exempt — and it would be shevut of shevut; and for Rambam it appears liable; for him without artisan — exempt but forbidden. See there; and there's help in Magen Avraham it will be explained further.
