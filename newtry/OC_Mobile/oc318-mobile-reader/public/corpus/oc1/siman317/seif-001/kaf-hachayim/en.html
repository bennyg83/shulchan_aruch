(a) [Seif 1] One who ties a permanent knot, etc. The principle here is that there are three legal divisions: for Rif and Rambam (and Shulchan Aruch, who rules like them) the law is thus: if it is a permanent knot — such as his intent that it remain tied so forever — and it is also a craftsman's act — liable to a chatas, whether for tying or untying; if permanent and not a craftsman's act — exempt but forbidden; if neither craftsman's act nor permanent — permitted l'chatchila. Rosh and Tur's view (and this is Rama's words in the gloss) is that it does not depend at all on a craftsman's act; rather the main division is: if it is a knot made to stand so forever — liable to a chatas; if he does not intend to tie forever — it depends on the dispute: some say if he does not intend to untie until seven days — exempt but forbidden; if his intent to untie within seven days — permitted even l'chatchila. Kol Bo's view is that only if he intends to untie that very day is he permitted l'chatchila; and more than that — exempt but forbidden. With this the words of Mechaber and Rama are explained; see section 1, and each matter will be explained further in its place.
