1) [Seif 1] One who ties a permanent knot, etc. The general rule contains three levels of law. According to Rif and Rambam, whose view the Shulchan Aruch follows without qualification, if it is a permanent knot, meaning one intends it to remain tied forever, and it is also a craftsman's knot, one is liable to bring a sin offering both for tying and untying it. If it is a permanent knot but not a craftsman's knot, one is exempt but the act is forbidden. If it is neither a craftsman's knot nor permanent, it is permitted initially. Rosh and Tur, whose view is stated by Rema in the gloss, hold that the law does not depend upon professional craftsmanship at all. The essential distinction is that one is liable to bring a sin offering for a knot made to remain forever. If he does not intend it to remain forever, the matter depends upon a dispute. Some say that if he does not intend to untie it for seven days, he is exempt but the act is forbidden, whereas if he intends to untie it within seven days, it is permitted initially. Kol Bo holds that it is permitted initially only when he intends to untie it that same day; beyond that, one is exempt but the act is forbidden. The words of the author and Rema are explained accordingly. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Each matter will be further explained below in its place.
