(א) One who ties, etc. — Know that a knot that is not permanent, for which one is not liable, is called such when the knot is made not to endure constantly [meaning the way of this knot is to untie it, and it does not endure constantly, as the Gemara illustrates: a knot they tie for a time]. And even if made in a strong manner that he cannot untie it with one hand — it is called, both according to Rif and Rambam and according to Rosh, a knot that is not permanent, since the way of this knot is not to maintain it constantly. Proof: what is taught in Tosefta [the beginning is according to R' Meir, as Darkei Moshe wrote]: any permanent knot that he can untie with one hand, or a non-permanent knot that he cannot untie with one hand — one is not liable. Know further that what Shulchan Aruch rules — a knot that is a artisan's act is liable — applies even if that knot is not tightened well and he can untie it with one hand, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah; and so Darkei Moshe ruled, for the Rabbis dispute R' Meir who said if he can untie with one hand he is exempt [as Yereim wrote, and as is proven from the Mishnayot commentaries; and Rambam and Ra'avad disagree] and hold one is liable, and the halacha is like them, as all these poskim ruled; and it is proven in the Gemara that such a knot is not tightened well; and thus when it is a artisan's act — otherwise Rif and Rambam would not obligate — behold a artisan's act is called even if not tightened well. Therefore it appears there is a difficulty on Taz who wrote a artisan's act is when the knot is very strong; and it is possible his intent is very strong meaning it will not come undone by itself in any manner, like a artisan who knows how to fix his work; and a layman's act is when the knot is not made well enough that sometimes over time it may come undone by itself. And one may ask: why does Rosh dispute Rif on this reasoning? One may say Rosh holds that since he makes it to endure forever, it is likely he makes it not to come undone by itself; and what happens sometimes that it comes undone does not void the permanent knot from it. But this is astonishing on Taz who wrote that according to Rosh and Tur everything depends on the mind of the person tying alone: if his intent is forever — permanent knot; if for a time — exempt and forbidden; if his intent to untie that day — permitted l'chatchila, as is written in this siman. And accordingly, even the camel-drivers' and sailors' knots themselves, or knots of captains, if at the time of tying his intent is to untie that day — there is no knot upon him at all, even if he made it like a captain himself; and in my humble opinion I am astonished at this, for it appears that a knot whose way in the world is to make it fixed — we do not follow the tier's thought to void the knot from it. And what the Gemara illustrates on a shoe with the Sages' knot and on a sandal with a knot tied for a time — those are people whose way is not to make their knot fixed, only for the hour; and this is like the liability discussion on a sandal where two amoraim prove there it is not called a knot — even if he made it strong like a captain's knot, as is proven there, in the Gemara, see Rashi there; and the reason is that for such people the way of this knot is not to endure — unlike in our matter regarding captains, where the way of the whole world is to make such a knot fixed, and he also made it strong properly — we do not follow his intent that he thinks to untie that day and thereby void the knot from it. And from rabbinic law I need not say it is forbidden; and as Magen Avraham brought from Kol Bo, and so in Yereim: a knot even if not permanent, if a person's way is sometimes to reconsider and leave it so — forbidden; and it appears in our matter, where the way of the whole world is to make it fixed, we do not follow his intent; and d'oraisa it is forbidden. And afterward I found Beit Meir wrote thus clearly; and with this Peri Megadim's difficulty is resolved — why we do not make tzitzit on Shabbat: one might think that what he said "they will untie immediately" — see there, which is very forced; and according to our words it is reconciled. And also Gra's difficulty on the sugya in Eruvin 103 regarding "let us say in the Temple" that for a mitzvah matter he will think to untie at Motzei Shabbat — according to our words it is a d'oraisa prohibition and reconciled; and when you examine Rashi's words well in several places in that sugya daf 112 you will find like our words. But regarding prohibition it is clear and simple that it is forbidden; and even with a weaver's rope it is in the Gemara that Chazal decreed lest he come to untie with an ordinary rope; and all the more so here.

(ב) And a knot, etc. — See Rambam chapter 10 of the laws of Shabbat, who copied several illustrations for this law; and it stems from the mishnah at the beginning of the chapter VeEilu Kesharim: a woman ties from the openings of her cloak even though it has two openings [meaning she can spread and wear with difficulty even if she will not untie afterward except one knot, and one might say one knot voids it and she will not untie it — Gemara]; and the threads of her head-covering even though loose [head-covering is what she wears above on the hair to cover; the amplification is: one might say since loose she pulls the covering from above and will not untie the knot, and automatically it becomes permanent — it teaches that a woman is careful for her hair not to tear and unties it]; and straps of shoe and sandal that they tie on the foot at the time of dressing; and wine and oil pouches even though they have two ears [meaning ears folded in and tied, and through one he can remove wine — one might say one of them voids it and he will not untie — there]; and a pot of meat even though he can remove the meat and not untie the knot, end of his words. All these are mentioned in the mishnah to permit l'chatchila; and for Rambam the reason is that all these are not made as a artisan's knot, only a layman's knot, and also made to be untied; but if one does these with a artisan's knot [a firm knot that will not come undone by itself in any manner], even though their way is to untie every day — likewise forbidden l'chatchila; and for Rosh and Shulchan Aruch there is no distinction here — such a knot has no name of knot at all.

(ג) That is not permanent and is not a artisan's act, etc. — All this is Rambam's language; meaning even if made for some time; and according to Rosh — specifically when it is not permanent at all, as in the gloss.

(ד) Its law is like, etc. — See Beit Yosef who wrote: Rosh wrote it appears to him one is not obligated for untying except untying with intent to tie a permanent knot, similar to trapping the chilazon that was in the Mishkan; and Beit Yosef wrote on this that rabbinically even for Rosh it is forbidden in all cases. Know that many dispute Rosh on this — Rashi in the chapter Klal Gadol (Shabbos daf 74b) in the passage "one is permitted," as Tosafos brought in his name on daf 73 in the passage "one who ties"; and so Rambam in Hilchot Shabbat law 8: One who separates the twist, etc., see there; and as Sefer Ma'aseh Rokeach wrote; and so in Sefer Tiferet Shmuel on Rosh — Rambam and Semag disagree, see there well, his words are very correct; and according to his words there, R' Chananel too can hold like Rashi; and the proof of the aforementioned Tosafos who wrote that for R' Chananel we require untying with intent to tie; and also from Yerushalmi there is no proof. And in the essential law there is a difficulty for me on Rosh's words: for even if we say we require here similarity to the Mishkan — behold also in tearing, as the mishnah states explicitly, we require with intent to sew, because in the Mishkan it was so, as in the Gemara; and nevertheless we rule the same applies to tearing with intent to fix, as is proven at the end of siman 340, see there; and therefore the reason is that since there is repair through tearing, like tearing with intent to sew — so too here where there is repair through untying the knot, like untying with intent to tie. And also according to his words, what does he challenge on daf 112 with the baraitot against each other regarding permitting shoe straps — let us say the one that teaches liable is untying with intent to tie, and the one that teaches forbidden is untying not with intent to tie; rather certainly untying is a matter in itself, and one is liable even without intent to tie.

(ה) Or if he tied an animal's rein — And what is stated above that the camel-drivers' knot is liable deals when the camel owner made the knot, which is a artisan's act; and here it is an ordinary person, which is a layman's act.

(ו) On one end of a thread — And the same applies to a rope — Semag there; and see in its explanation from Rosh that on the head of the thread of sewing, one knot also suffices [and in Mordechai chapter VeEilu Kesharim it is stated explicitly thus]; and what is in the Gemara "and he ties them and does not consider" — the mishnah speaks only of the melacha of sewing; Rosh explains it deals when he ties both ends of the thread together.

(ז) Its law is like two knots — See in Mishna Berurah what he wrote that this is a artisan's act; and even though in Semag there it does not discuss a artisan's act at all, because he did not copy in the melacha of tying Rambam's view that a artisan's act — nevertheless since we see Semag holds one knot on the head of the thread tightened well is called a knot d'oraisa like two ordinary knots, Rama holds that regarding a artisan's act it is equivalent to two ordinary knots.
