In the Gemara they say there are three divisions: one liable to a chatas; one exempt but forbidden; one permitted l'chatchila. According to Rif, Rambam, and Mechaber — the anonymous ruling is thus: if it is a permanent knot, such as its way is to remain so forever [meaning he does not set a time in his mind to untie it, and it is a knot made to endure always], and it is also a artisan's act — liable to a chatas; and it will be explained below at the end of the seif in the gloss what a artisan's act is. If it is a permanent knot and not a artisan's act, or a artisan's act and not a permanent knot — exempt but forbidden. If it is neither a artisan's act nor a permanent knot — permitted l'chatchila. Rashi and Rosh and Shulchan Aruch hold that it does not depend at all on whether the knot itself is a artisan's act; rather the main division is: if it is a permanent knot — meaning its way is to remain so forever, as stated — liable to a chatas, even if a layman's act; if its way is only to tie for a time — exempt but forbidden, for it somewhat resembles a permanent knot, even if a layman's act; if its way is to untie it that same day — permitted l'chatchila, even if a artisan's act, for there is no permanent knot upon it.
