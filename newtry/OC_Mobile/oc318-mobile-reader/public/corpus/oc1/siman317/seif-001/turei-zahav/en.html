(א) One who ties a permanent knot. In the Gemara they say there are three divisions: one liable to a chatas; one exempt but forbidden l'chatchila; one permitted l'chatchila. It is obvious that what is permitted l'chatchila is a knot that is not permanent; only regarding a permanent knot is there a division — sometimes liable, sometimes forbidden l'chatchila — and there are two views on the division. How is the division? For Rif and Rambam — the first view: if not permanent — meaning made to be untied within seven days; and regarding permanent, the division is in the essence of the tying — how it is: if a artisan's act, automatically it is very strong, then liable; and even if not permanent, nevertheless since it is strong — forbidden l'chatchila; if a layman's act, it is not so strong; even though strong enough for permanence, nevertheless not liable, only forbidden; and there is prohibition, since made for permanence. Thus with two adverse factors — permanence and strength — liable; permanence alone or strength alone — forbidden; with two favorable factors — not for permanence and not strong — permitted l'chatchila. Rashi and Rosh do not divide by whether the knot is strong because it is a artisan's act; everything depends on the mind of the person tying alone: if his intent is that it remain so forever — meaning as long as it can endure and he need not untie it, and he does not set a time in his mind when he will untie it — liable; if he sets in his mind some time when he will certainly untie it — exempt whatever the time, for there is no liability since he has time in any case; and there is prohibition, for during that time it is called a knot of permanence; but if he thinks in his mind to untie it that day — that is not called any time, and permitted l'chatchila. This is Tur's view, who wrote first: any knot standing to endure forever — meaning he does so with intent and sets no time at all in his mind — liable; standing to endure seven days — meaning he resolves in his mind thus — exempt and forbidden; and not specifically seven days, but anything more than one day is in this category, whatever the time; and he did not mention seven days except since Rashi wrote "because sometimes one leaves it a week or two weeks" — to teach that a week's time is not like fixed forever; from this we know the same for two weeks, for since neither has "forever" upon them. But if he has no time in mind except to untie today — permitted l'chatchila. Beit Yosef wrote that Tur's view is that anything less than seven days is in the category of permitting that day; and he learned this from Rashi who wrote: forbidden l'chatchila to tie there because sometimes one leaves it a week; and also wrote that sometimes it endures through Shabbat — evidently less than that has not even prohibition; and regarding liability it appears from Rashi's words that when he wrote "and sometimes it endures Shabbat or a month" — whatever stands to endure more than a month is liable, end of Beit Yosef's words — and they are very astonishing and not reasonable at all, in my humble opinion; for certainly Rashi himself should wonder at Beit Yosef's understanding — as if a week or month were written in the Torah that less than a week is permitted and more than a month liable! And further, when Rashi mentioned a week he also mentioned two weeks; and just as with two weeks you certainly have no precision that less than two weeks is permitted, so too do not derive precision from a week; rather the clear principle is not a month and not Shabbat the day, but as stated. And Rashi also intended to teach that there is no division between liability and prohibition except in fixing time in his mind: liability when there is no time at all; prohibition when there is time, whatever it be — week or month; and the same for more than a month — for once we come to measuring time, there is no distinction between two days and half a year — all forbidden and not liable; and the week Rashi mentioned is per the context, for the Gemara deals with it. And Morenu HaRav challenged Tur's refinements against each other; and according to what I said everything is reconciled, there's help. And even though Beit Yosef brings the Gaon's view Mahari Abulafia that standing seven days is called a permanent knot — we have only what appears clear in their words, and not to divide between time and time, for this would be called a decree of the scribes, for there is no reason for this division; and so Beit Yosef in the name of Kol Bo in the name of Ram, and so in Mordechai — after one day forbidden l'chatchila. And this matter we mentioned — that the matter depends on fixing time — Tur also intended this in what he brought from Sefer HaTerumot: straps they tie on a belt and do not untie except when changing trousers — forbidden to tie them on Shabbat, end of his words; the reason is that it is like fixing time for a week, since its way is to leave it thus — we say his intent was certainly thus, and we do not say to be lenient that his intent was to untie today. And what Tur wrote afterward — straps on a garment on the shoulder that they tighten so the garment not slip out — forbidden to return them, lest he tie — it appears to me those straps they tie to be there without fixed time, and automatically there is liability to a chatas; therefore they must decree even on returning; for otherwise in the tying there would only be a rabbinic prohibition, and we would not forbid returning for that, as appears correct to me in all the above.

(ב) And there are those who disagree. This is Tur's view, which we explained nearby; except what Rama wrote afterward "and some are lenient," etc. — that is Beit Yosef's explanation of Tur's words, and it was already explained one should not explain it so. In this — that they say in the division of prohibition and not liability it appears in the Gemara — namely the sandals of the Sages: Sefer HaTerumot in the name of R' Yehuda — meaning from the time they tie them on their feet on Shabbat morning they do not remove their shoes even at night until Shabbat night, because they study all weekday nights in their garments and lie down a little, etc. There is a difficulty: if so it should be in the category of exempt but forbidden — how do they tie it Shabbat morning? And one may say: nevertheless it is not counted in the category of fixing time we mentioned, for many times they also lie down on weekdays and remove their shoes; and the Gemara mentioned it only as a sign in general, since their way is thus most of the time — automatically when a person resolves in his mind they will remain, certainly there was prohibition for some time; and an example is in Rashi's explanation on the Sages' sandals: he explained there that sometimes they remove them and return and wear without untying the knot; and nevertheless it was not a permanent knot, since at muddy time they untie and tie well.
