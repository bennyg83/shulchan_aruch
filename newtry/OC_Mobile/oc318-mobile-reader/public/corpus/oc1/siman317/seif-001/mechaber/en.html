The law of tying and making bows on Shabbos. It contains 7 seifim: One who ties a permanent knot that is a craftsman's knot is liable, such as camel drivers' knots, sailors' knots, and the knots in shoe and sandal straps that shoemakers tie when making them, and all similar knots. One who ties a permanent knot that is not a craftsman's knot is exempt from liability. {Rama: Some disagree and hold that one is liable for every permanent knot, even a layman's knot. [Rashi, Rosh, Rabbeinu Yeruham, and Tur] Some say that any knot that one does not intend to open that same day is called permanent. [Kol Bo and others] Others are lenient and hold that a knot intended for less than 7 days is not called permanent. [Tur and Mordechai]} It is permitted initially to tie a knot that is neither permanent nor a craftsman's knot. {Rama: The law of untying is the same as the law of tying. [Tur]} Thus, if one's strap tore and he tied it, a rope snapped and he tied it, he tied a rope to a bucket, or he tied an animal's rein, he is exempt; the same applies to all simple knots intended to remain. A craftsman's knot tied temporarily is forbidden, though one is not liable. It is permitted to tie a temporary knot for a mitzvah purpose, such as in order to measure a required halachic measure. See above, siman 314. {Rama: Some say that one should not untie any double knot, because we are not certain what constitutes a craftsman's knot. Even when intended only temporarily, such a knot may not be tied or untied, and this is the custom. [Hagahot Alfasi] Nevertheless, where it causes distress it may be untied, since the prohibition is rabbinic and the Sages did not impose it in a case of distress. This law of a double knot concerns tying 2 ends together; but making a single knot in the same cord or thread is treated like a double knot. [Semag]}
