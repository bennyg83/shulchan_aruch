(s.k. 1) It is called, etc. Know that in the Gemara there are three types of knots: one for which one is liable to a chatas for tying or untying; one exempt but forbidden; one permitted l'chatchila. The view of Rif, Rambam, and Mechaber is that where there are two factors — namely a craftsman's act and also permanence — one is liable; with one factor — a craftsman's act without permanence, or permanence without a craftsman's act — exempt but forbidden; with neither — a layman's act and not permanent — permitted l'chatchila. Rashi and Rosh hold that a craftsman's act does not raise or lower; the three divisions depend on the essence of the knot: if made forever or for a long time, that is true permanence and one is liable; if for an intermediate time — exempt but forbidden (though poskim dispute what "intermediate" is: the first view Rama brought — more than one day; the lenient view — up to seven days); for a short time — permitted l'chatchila. This is Rama's view in the gloss.
