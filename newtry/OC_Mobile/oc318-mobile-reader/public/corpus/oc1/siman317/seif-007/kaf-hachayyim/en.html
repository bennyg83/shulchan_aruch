7) There: But one who ties a permanent knot that is not a craftsman's knot is exempt. The same applies conversely, because both elements are required. The author's words, and likewise Rif and Rambam, imply that even if a knot can be untied with one hand, one is liable when it is a craftsman's knot and permanent. Shibolei HaLeket, however, writes in the name of the Tosefta: One is not liable for any permanent knot that can be untied with one hand, nor for a nonpermanent knot that cannot be untied with one hand. These are its words. It appears that this follows Rabbi Meir, who states this view in the Gemara, but the Sages disagree, and we rule like the Sages. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 3. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1, writes the same.
