One who ties a permanent knot, etc. The reason is that a permanent knot is one of the avot melachot, similar to tying the broken threads of the curtains in the Mishkan — Rashi beginning of VeEilu Kesharim. And it appears to me that what Mechaber wrote — what is liable to chatas and what is exempt but forbidden — even though nowadays we do not offer a chatas, it matters for disqualification of witnesses: if one transgressed what requires a chatas intentionally he is disqualified for testimony d'oraisa; if he betrothed a woman before him, no get is needed; but if he transgressed exempt but forbidden — disqualified only rabbinically; if he betrothed a woman before him, she needs a get, as explained in Magen Avraham on rabbinically disqualified witnesses. And further difference regarding announcement: we are in doubt in rabbinically disqualified witnesses whether they are disqualified only after announcement, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 34, see there; and further that it matters greatly in matters of doubt, see there section 1; and further one may say it matters for repentance: if one transgressed and repented and comes to ask how to repair — there is a difference between unwitting and intentional and between d'oraisa and d'rabbanan, as will be explained below in siman 334, there's help.
