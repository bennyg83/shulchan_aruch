Rashi at the beginning of the chapter Eilu Kesharim. It appears to me that although today we do not bring a sin offering, the author's distinction between an act for which one is liable to bring a sin offering and one for which he is exempt but that remains forbidden has practical relevance to disqualification as a witness. If one deliberately transgressed an act for which he is liable to bring a sin offering, he is disqualified as a witness by Torah law. If a woman was betrothed in his presence, she does not require a get. If he transgressed an act for which he is exempt but that remains forbidden, he is disqualified only rabbinically, and a woman betrothed in his presence requires a get, as Magen Avraham explains concerning witnesses disqualified rabbinically. Another practical difference concerns public announcement, because those disqualified as witnesses rabbinically are not disqualified until an announcement is made, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 34; see there. There are also many practical differences in cases of uncertainty. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. One may further say that it matters regarding repentance. If one transgressed, repented, and asks how to rectify his act, distinctions exist between inadvertent and deliberate transgression and between Torah and rabbinic prohibitions, as will be explained below at the end of siman 334, with the help of Heaven.
