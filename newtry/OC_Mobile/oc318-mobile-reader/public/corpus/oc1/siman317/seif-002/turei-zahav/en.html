(א) Or if most of the foot slipped out. I did not know the explanation regarding tying — what tying applies to straps in what slipped from the shoe off the foot; and in Yerushalmi, the source of this law, this "or if most of the foot slipped out" does not appear at all; also in Mordechai who copied the Yerushalmi it is not found — only Rambam chapter 10 wrote thus: and straps of shoe and sandal that they tie on the foot at the time of dressing, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote that Rambam added "on the foot" to explain that otherwise it would certainly not be a craftsman's act; but in any case the text here has no explanation. And we find this language regarding chalitzah in the chapter of mitzvot of chalitzah (Yevamos daf 102); and it seems to me that through error it was copied here. And even though the dress [levush] copied it as written before us, there is no proof he always follows what he found before him; and truth guides its way that here too it must be Rambam's language that I mentioned.

(ב) And specifically that he can return it, etc. I did not understand this gloss in what it depends — the decree of tying if there is effort. One view: even if they depend on each other, thus Rosh wrote in chapter Bameh Madlikin, brought by Beit Yosef: loops of garments on the shoulder — if they came out, some permit returning them on Shabbat if there is a wide hole and he can return them easily, since they came apart; and it appears forbidden to return them, for they are accustomed to tie them and it is a permanent knot, as they say in the chapter Kol HaKelim — a decree lest he drive in, etc., end of his words. Behold even with a wide hole they decreed lest he tie. And further, Tosafos chapter Bameh Madlikin wrote: R' Yehuda — if the cloak's threads came apart, if the hole is wide and he can insert them without effort — permitted, since they are worn out, provided he places them in a manner that there is no concern lest he drive in, end of their words. Behold before you: even without effort we are concerned lest he drive in. And that Tosafos require no effort — not due to concern lest he drive in, but because if there is effort it is like metaken mana, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mordechai chapter Eizehu Mekoman; and there is a difficulty.
