Both. One who follows this practice and puts on both pairs together should place Rashi tefillin lower and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin above. Taz. The Arizal likewise writes that he should first insert the Rashi arm tefillin and position them toward the shoulder, and afterward the Rabbeinu Tam tefillin toward the hand, with both resting upon the biceps. On the head, he should place the Rashi tefillin lower and the Rabbeinu Tam tefillin somewhat higher, both within the proper place for tefillin. The Rabbeinu Tam straps should lie beneath the Rashi straps and be covered by them, so that only the Rashi straps are visible. The Rabbeinu Tam tefillin should be smaller than the Rashi tefillin. See Sefer HaKavanos, Hilchos Tefillin, chapters 10 and 12. See Eliyah Rabbah, who rejects Taz's words and proof. If a person puts on arm tefillin according to Rabbeinu Tam and head tefillin according to Rashi and Rambam, does he fulfill his obligation? Shevus Yaakov, part 1, siman 2, rules that according to all opinions he does not fulfill the obligation. In pressing circumstances, however, he may wear them this way, because in pressing circumstances one fulfills the obligation by wearing only one of the two tefillin. Nevertheless, he should recite a blessing only over the tefillin written according to our custom, which follows Rashi and Rambam. If the head tefillin were written according to Rashi, he should put on the head tefillin first, and when removing them he should remove the arm tefillin first. See there.
Merely. For in any event one of the pairs is invalid. If, however, one puts on two valid pairs of tefillin, he violates bal tosif. Magen Avraham. See Taz, who discusses this at length, and Yad Aharon, who challenges him.
The place, etc. The statement “some say that if he cannot, etc.” requires study, because it appears to repeat the middle case. Furthermore, even in the first case, where he knows how to position them properly, he recites a blessing over only one pair. It appears to me that the meaning is as follows. Initially, Shulchan Aruch speaks of placing both pairs together upon the arm, reciting the blessing, and tightening both at once. The same applies to the head. He then says that if he does not know how to position them properly and put them on together, meaning that if he tightens both simultaneously he will be unable to align them properly and must instead bind them one after the other, he should put on the arm and head tefillin according to one opinion, then put on the other pair relying upon the first blessing. The words “and he should immediately remove them” should not be included in the text. Afterward he writes, “Some say that if he cannot, etc.” This means that even one after the other he cannot put on both pairs at the same sitting, such as where he has an injury or is embarrassed before others. Magen Avraham. See Darkei Noam, Orach Chayim, siman 2, and Bnei Chayei.
