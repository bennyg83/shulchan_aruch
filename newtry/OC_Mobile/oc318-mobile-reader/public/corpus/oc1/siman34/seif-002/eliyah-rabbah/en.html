[2] He should put on according to one opinion, etc. He should then first put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, afterward Rashi tefillin, and pray wearing the Rashi tefillin. Levush. It appears that Levush saw the repetitive wording in Shulchan Aruch and therefore understood it as establishing two separate cases. He writes first that one should put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin and afterward Rashi tefillin, and then adds that it is preferable to put on Rashi tefillin first, in accordance with Shulchan Aruch's conclusion. Lechem Chamudos, page 91, however, writes: “It appears to me that Shulchan Aruch first says that if he does not know how to position them, he should put them on one after the other and recite a blessing over only one pair. It then explains which pair should be put on first. There are not two separate cases.” Nachalas Tzvi likewise disagrees and writes that according to all opinions one should not first recite a blessing over Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. Nevertheless, Sefer HaTerumah, siman 250, appears to me to permit reciting the blessing first over Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. Taz and Rabbi Moshe Rivkash emend Shulchan Aruch to read, “Some say that if he cannot put them on, etc.” The edition of Shulchan Aruch printed with Olas Tamid likewise implies Levush's interpretation. Magen Avraham explains that the first clause instructs him to put on both pairs and recite the blessing while tightening them simultaneously. The next clause, “if he does not know how to position them,” means that when tightening both simultaneously he cannot position them correctly and therefore must bind them one after the other. He should then follow one opinion, etc. This is difficult. If so, why does Shulchan Aruch say that he should immediately remove them from his arm?
[3] Shema and Vehayah, etc. But not Vayomer, because tefillin are not mentioned in that passage, and omitting it is therefore not comparable to giving false testimony.
