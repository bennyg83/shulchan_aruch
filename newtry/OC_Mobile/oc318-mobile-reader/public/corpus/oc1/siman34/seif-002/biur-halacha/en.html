**And he should have intention in placing them in those that they are, etc.** — See Magen Avraham, who wrote that specifically in this; but with two pairs of valid tefillin one violates bal tosif. And what they said in Sanhedrin ch. 9 — if one made another bayit and placed it with the four batim, "this one stands alone" — meaning when he wrote another parasha that is not relevant to tefillin at all. See Peri Megadim, who explained: not a different parasha; likewise if he wrote one parasha of the four parshiyot and placed it in that bayit — we also say "this one stands alone" and "that one stands alone." {Rama: Now, even though apparently the law is certainly with him, for the four parshiyot invalidate each other; if so, as long as he has not written all four, the tefillin are still invalid and are not relevant at all to these valid ones. And so it seems in Gra's explanation. Nevertheless, it is very difficult for me regarding Peri Megadim's words, who explained Magen Avraham's words thus, for Magen Avraham brought support for his words from Beit Yosef in the name of Mishnah L'Melekh, which is below in siman 651, and it is proven there explicitly from Maggid Mishneh — if one took another species alone besides the four species, we say "this one stands alone" and "that one stands alone"; and if he took alone the four species and another lulav or another etrog, he violated bal tosif. And he compared this there to two pairs of tefillin that one violates bal tosif — see there. And behold there as well we maintain that the four species in the lulav invalidate each other, as with the four parshiyot in tefillin; nevertheless Mishnah L'Melekh did not require for bal tosif that he take all four species doubled — since he holds that since that species is among the species fit to fulfill the mitzvah thereby, there is in this bal tosif. If so, the same in our matter; and if so, it seems that what Magen Avraham said "another parasha" is specifically that case. And what Peri Megadim challenged on this — see Machatzit HaShekel, who settles this. However, Machatzit HaShekel's words are also very difficult to reconcile with his view — for according to him that one parasha of the four batim violates bal tosif, it will be difficult regarding these invalid tefillin from one parasha in a fifth bayit in which two parshiyot are also written, which are valid according to all views. And perhaps one may distinguish — for on this compartment already rests something invalid because its parshiyot were exchanged within it, unlike there where its parshiyot are not yet finished — on this account.} And see there in Mishnah and Gemara that it is proven that even if he intends to add to the Torah in this, nevertheless he did not violate bal tosif; if so, apparently it is difficult why Shulchan Arukh required intention in placing them that the two tefillin be like a strap merely — since they are invalid tefillin, bal tosif does not apply in this (later I found in Machatzit HaShekel that he posed this difficulty). And what seems to me in this: this counsel is not so that thereby he not have the prohibition of bal tosif; rather, it is not known that Rosh holds mitzvot require intention, as Tur brought in his name in siman 60; and here Tur required in his name that he place both and fasten them in one bayit, as Magen Avraham explained in s.k. 4; if so, how will he fulfill the intention required for the mitzvah if he has no intention at all — certainly he will not fulfill the mitzvah; and if he intends generically in this fastening that he wishes thereby to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin, he will likewise not fulfill thereby according to many poskim, since one of them is not tefillin at all [and behold this is like one who betroths two women as one and one was a maidservant or gentile who is unfit for kiddushin — Rambam and Ba'al HaHalakhot hold that even the fit one is not betrothed; see Even HaEzer siman 41 in Beit Yosef and Shulchan Arukh s.d. and Choshen Mishpat there s.k. 4 and 5; and Rosh as well is uncertain in this, as explained in Beit Yosef there; and here as well are we not dealing with one who wishes to satisfy all views?] And if he wishes to specify one pair and say that with these he wishes to fulfill and with those he does not wish — he does not know which are the valid ones to specify fulfilling the mitzvah with them; therefore it says he should intend generically in those that are made according to their correction, etc. And behold this is like saying there "the fit one should be betrothed to me" — see there; and one who examines Tur's language will see it proven as we say — what he wrote afterward "and there is no bal tosif in this" is a separate matter; for otherwise what he concludes afterward that it is not called bal tosif of an extra matter is as the commentators wrote; and what he wrote afterward "and the rest are like straps merely" — he explains that otherwise there would be a break between placing of hand and head of the valid pair; and Rosh himself wrote so, and so I found in Azrieli in Rabbeinu Yeruham explicitly, and in Chayei Adam klal 68 law 23 — apparently meaning that when he places Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin in one bayit he must intend because of doubt, lest otherwise he violate bal tosif; but from Machatzit HaShekel's words it seems as we wrote, only rabbinically because it appears as adding; and also from Gra's words, as seems from his explanation, that he agrees with the views of the commentators that what they said in Sanhedrin "this one stands alone," etc. — they meant not to violate bal tosif as well; and there it does not deal even with one who intends to add to the mitzvah, as likewise from the Mishnah. And in truth all his words there require study — in what he wrote there at the end that if one takes the lulav several times in a day and says that from the Torah the tradition came on this that he violates bal tosif — in Tosafot Rosh HaShanah 27b it is proven explicitly that even if he intends to fulfill thereby each time the mitzvah of lulav he likewise does not violate; and Chinuch also explained explicitly. And perhaps Chayei Adam's view in all this is to distinguish between if he intends generically to fulfill the mitzvah each time and between if he says the tradition came thus — and it is strained that one who intends to fulfill the mitzvah must also say thus — and it requires study. And know further that according to other poskim who hold we do not say at all "this one stands alone" regarding bal tosif — see Tosafot Sukkah 31 — Shulchan Arukh's words "and he should intend in placing them," etc. fit well in the plain sense, lest otherwise he violate bal tosif. And know further that from what the author advised us to make specifically two pairs of tefillin if we wish to satisfy all views, it implies it is forbidden to place Shema and V'hayah im shamoa in the third bayit and likewise in the fourth bayit to fulfill thereby in any case [see Tosafot Rosh HaShanah 28b d.h. u'mena timra] because of the prohibition of bal tosif, and he disqualifies the tefillin entirely thereby; and even if he says explicitly he does not wish to fulfill only with the parshiyot that are according to law and the rest shall be like parchment merely — likewise it does not avail and is not comparable to what was permitted here in Shulchan Arukh with two pairs of tefillin, for each one stands alone, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah in s.k. 8 — and see nearby.

**Merely** — See in Mishna Berurah that if he placed two pairs, etc. — this is from Levush, Beit Chadash, Magen Avraham, and Gra's explanation, not like Taz. And in Sefer Tanya he brings proof for Taz from Yerushalmi on one who finds tefillin. However, I found in Sefer HaTerumah that he brings this ruling and explained it with a different explanation [and Gra copied it in his explanation in siman 27 s.e. 9], and it is proven there that he holds the opposite. And see in Eliyahu Rabbah, who proved from the Gemara in Eruvin that even if he intends not to fulfill except with one, nevertheless he violated bal tosif in this, and he challenged Beit Chadash on this. However, according to what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 651 — for us, who maintain mitzvot require intention — even in his time he does not violate except when he intends to add; Beit Chadash's words fit well in this, for he refers to Rosh, and Rosh holds mitzvot require intention, as Tur wrote in siman 60; but Eliyahu Rabbah follows his method, that he challenged Magen Avraham there on this from Rosh's own words. However, what seems from Beit Chadash's words — that even with five batim he does not violate bal tosif except when he intends to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin with five batim, and it seems that if he intends the fifth bayit shall be only like a strap merely he does not violate bal tosif — many did not agree with him on this; see Sefer Nahar Shalom and Sefer Damesek Eliezer, who burdened this on Gra's intentions — for Beit Chadash's view does not fit Tur's language (and we may truly say Gra's intention plainly: Beit Chadash's difficulty applies — behold, even with two pairs there is bal tosif; but according to this, the intention in what he wrote that it is not called bal tosif is when one fixes the invalid next to the valid — and examine). Also according to Beit Chadash's words it will be difficult why he did not advise placing two parshiyot in each and placing them, as we wrote at the beginning — after he is lenient even with a bayit of five batim, which is like five parshiyot plainly [and perhaps because there is a separation between the parshiyot and the batim; see Mordechai, beginning of Laws of Tefillin]; and perhaps in truth Beit Chadash would also concede a condition does not avail in a bayit of five batim, but what remains at the end is strained on Tur, and his language will not settle so well. And all this regarding bal tosif; but regarding invalidity, certainly Beit Chadash also concedes they are invalid with five batim in all manners, even with a condition — per the reason explained there in Sanhedrin ch. 9 p. 1, that any outer bayit that does not see the air is invalid. And know further that it is straightforward among poskim that even if he did not fix any parasha in that fifth bayit, nevertheless it is invalid because it says in the Gemara there that the outer bayit does not see the air. And what Mishna Berurah wrote in the name of Gra that even through tying merely it becomes invalid — see Sefer Damesek Eliezer, what he wrote on this. However, what he wrote in our rabbi's words "but if it is tied," etc. — Damesek Eliezer's words are not clear at all in this, for what mitzvah applies to tying the fifth bayit; and it is also proven in Gra's words in siman 651 s.e. 14 that for the rabbis who do not require binding by Torah law, one does not violate bal tosif. And in my humble opinion, Gra's intention plainly is: as there for Rabbi Yehuda — since the four species require binding, automatically the binding avails that he violate through it itself the prohibition of bal tosif — so too here, since through tying the fifth bayit the four batim become invalid because the outer bayit does not see the air, automatically he violated through it itself the prohibition of bal tosif; and this is the plain meaning of the sugya there for one who examines it; and in what Gra explained the statement of Rabbi Zeira, which deals even with tying and not actual joining — it fits very well why it says "any outer bayit that does not see" and does not say "any fourth bayit," but it is still outer.

**He should place Rabbeinu Tam's** — It seems to me that here as well one must be careful to intend that he is not doing so except because of doubt; and thereby, according to all views, he does not violate bal tosif and bal tigra; for if he intends generically in this to fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin — although there is no bal tosif in this, for "this one stands alone," etc. — nevertheless perhaps in truth the law is like Rashi and these tefillin are invalid because he exchanged their parshiyot, as Rosh and Tur wrote; and it turns out he violates bal tigra in this, for it is explained in Semag negative commandment 265 that one who makes three totafot in tefillin violates bal tigra; and the same applies to his counterpart — for he has only two parshiyot fixed in their place, and one may not distinguish that in any event three are valid there and there is only bal tigra, unlike here — by fixing some parshiyot in the compartment not in their place, we say the whole compartment is called an invalid compartment and there is no bal tigra on it, meaning there certainly there is no name of tefillin on the compartment at all, for we maintain four totafot invalidate each other, and nevertheless this is included in the prohibition of bal tigra. And conversely one may not distinguish that there one detracts from the essence of tefillin, unlike here — for one bayit, since he did not place it in its place, is likewise as if he omitted; and further, this detracting is compared to those given in one giving in place of those requiring two givings that are four, as we say in Eruvin p. 100a and in Rosh HaShanah 27 — one violates bal tigra; although we maintain in Zevachim in the chapter of Beit Shamai that b'dieved it atones, nevertheless he detracts l'chatchila in the command of God [Rama: and this is an open rebuke to a person who wishes to be lax from the mitzvah at times in matters that are only l'chatchila] — how much more so here, where it is me'akev even b'dieved. And further, Rambam wrote in his Sefer HaMitzvot that this prohibition applies whether one detracts from what is written in the Torah or whether one detracts from what is received orally; and the same here, since it is received orally that the order of placing them is thus, and it is certainly a change — he detracts in this if it is not said as Eshel Tzvi wrote in the name of the kabbalists that both are valid; but we see Shulchan Arukh and all poskim did not accept this for halachah, and it is known that wherever poskim disagree with kabbalists, the law follows the poskim; and so Gaon R. Baruch Frankel proved in his glosses from the Gemara. And further, a God-fearing person who places Rabbeinu Tam tefillin wishes to satisfy all views and not that he fall thereby into doubt of Torah prohibition; therefore the correct thing is as we wrote — that he intend explicitly he is not placing them except because of doubt; and even generic intention does not avail in this, for we maintain in Rosh HaShanah 27b that to violate in his time does not require intention; and although there the matter is discussed regarding bal tosif, it is straightforward the same applies regarding bal tigra; see Taz at the end of siman 651. And one may not say that here it is called not in its time because he already fulfilled with Rashi tefillin — and it is comparable to lulav, where Taz ruled there he does not violate bal tigra afterward if he takes three species, unless he intends in them explicitly for the sake of the mitzvah — for there it is different, since from its height he fulfills; unlike tefillin, whose time itself is the whole day, and he blesses on them each time he places them, and he does not detract in any event from bechor, where Rosh HaShanah concludes its time is the whole day, or in priestly blessing where another congregation presents itself — although with strain one may distinguish, nevertheless the more correct path is as I wrote; and in particular, since there are views among poskim who hold the obligation of tefillin from the Torah is that they be on him the whole day — see Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov — certainly it is called its time the whole day. And I wrote this to exclude what I saw from someone who wrote the opposite of this and did not examine it as needed. And there are many other matters I found later in the introduction to Peri Megadim on the matter of bal tosif, and so forth to copy them.
