And he should intend when putting them on that those which are, etc. See Magen Avraham, who writes that this applies specifically here, but that one who wears two valid pairs of tefillin violates bal tosif. What Sanhedrin says, that if one added another compartment beside the four compartments each stands independently, refers to a case where a different passage, unrelated to tefillin, was placed in it. Peri Megadim explains that the same applies if one of the four tefillin passages was placed in that compartment: each stands independently. Although this appears logical, since all four passages are indispensable and until all four are written the tefillin are invalid, it is very difficult to attribute this to Magen Avraham. Magen Avraham cites support from Beit Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh, below in siman 651, where it is explicit that taking an additional species beyond the four species is treated independently, whereas taking an additional lulav or esrog violates bal tosif. Maggid Mishneh compares this to wearing two pairs of tefillin. The four species likewise prevent one another, yet Maggid Mishneh did not require that all four species be duplicated. Evidently, since the added item is itself of a species fit for the mitzvah, bal tosif applies. Accordingly, Magen Avraham's phrase “a different passage” appears deliberate. Peri Megadim's question is resolved by Machatzis HaShekel. Still, his own explanation is difficult, for if a single passage from the four in an additional bayis involves bal tosif, why should this differ from a fifth compartment that contains two valid passages? Perhaps the distinction is that where the passages were exchanged in the extra bayis, that compartment already carries a status of invalidity, whereas in the fifth compartment the passages were not yet completed. The Mishnah and Gemara there show that even if one intends to add to the Torah, he does not violate bal tosif in that case. Why, then, did Tur and Shulchan Aruch require him to intend that the second pair be like mere straps? Since one pair is invalid, bal tosif should not apply. It appears that this intention is not a device to avoid bal tosif. Rosh maintains that mitzvos require intent, as Tur cites in siman 60. Here Tur requires both pairs to be put on and tightened at once. If he has no intent, he certainly does not fulfill the mitzvah. If he intends generally to fulfill the mitzvah through the tightening, many poskim hold he likewise does not fulfill it because one pair is not tefillin at all. This resembles one who betroths two women at once when one is ineligible for betrothal, where Rambam and Baal Halachos hold that even the eligible woman is not betrothed. He also cannot choose one pair explicitly, since he does not know which is valid. Therefore, he should intend that whichever pair is properly made should fulfill the mitzvah. This resembles saying, “Let the woman fit for betrothal be betrothed to me.” Tur's wording proves this. His later statement that there is no bal tosif is a separate point. His concluding phrase, that the others are like mere straps, means that otherwise there would be an interruption between the arm and head tefillin of the valid pair. Rosh writes this explicitly, and I found the same in Rabbeinu Yerucham. Chayei Adam appears to imply that when wearing Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin together, one must make this conditional intent to avoid bal tosif. Machatzis HaShekel, however, implies as we have written, that the concern is only D'Rabbanan because it appears like adding. Gra likewise seems to agree with the explanations of Sanhedrin that “each stands independently” also prevents bal tosif, even where one intends to add. Gra's concluding statement elsewhere, that one who takes the lulav several times a day and says that the Torah requires this violates bal tosif, requires study. Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah 27b explicitly imply that even if he intends each time to fulfill the positive commandment of lulav, he does not violate bal tosif. Sefer HaChinuch says the same. Perhaps Chayei Adam distinguishes between ordinary intent to fulfill the mitzvah each time and explicitly claiming that such was the received Torah requirement. This is strained. According to the poskim who maintain that “each stands independently” does not apply at all to bal tosif, the wording of Tur and Shulchan Aruch is straightforward: without the conditional intent, he violates bal tosif. Know further that the Mechaber's advice to use specifically two pairs, if one wishes to satisfy all opinions, implies that one may not place Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa in both the third and fourth compartments and rely upon whichever order is correct. Doing so would involve bal tosif and invalidate the tefillin entirely. Even if he explicitly says that he wishes to fulfill the mitzvah only through the passages positioned according to law and that the others should be like blank parchment, this does not help. That differs from two separate pairs, where each stands independently.
Merely. See Mishnah Berurah. The ruling that wearing two valid pairs involves bal tosif is from Lechem Chamudos, Bach, Magen Avraham, and Gra, unlike Taz. Sefer Tosefes Yom HaKippurim brings proof for Taz from the Yerushalmi concerning one who finds tefillin. I found, however, that Sefer HaTerumah cites that Yerushalmi and explains it differently, and Gra copies his explanation in siman 27, seif 9. It proves the opposite. Eliyah Rabbah infers from Eruvin that even if one intends to fulfill the mitzvah through only one pair, he nevertheless violates bal tosif, and on this basis disputes Bach. According to Magen Avraham in siman 651, however, since we rule that mitzvos require intent, even during the proper time bal tosif applies only when one intends to add. Bach's words are therefore understandable, for he follows Rosh, who holds that mitzvos require intent. Eliyah Rabbah follows his own view, where he disputes Magen Avraham from Rosh himself. Bach also implies that even with five compartments, one violates bal tosif only if he intends to fulfill the mitzvah through all five. If he intends that the fifth compartment be merely like a strap, there is no bal tosif. Many authorities do not agree with him. See Nehar Shalom and Damesek Eliezer, who explain Gra in a manner that leaves Bach's reading of Tur difficult. In truth, Gra may be understood more simply. He was troubled by Bach's question that two pairs should also involve bal tosif. Gra answers that Tur's statement that this is not called bal tosif refers to attaching the invalid pair beside the valid one. According to Bach, one might ask why the authorities did not advise placing two passages in each disputed location and making a condition. Since Bach is lenient even with five compartments, which resembles five passages, this should work. Perhaps the interruption between the passages and compartments is different. See Mordechai at the beginning of Hilchos Tefillin. Possibly Bach himself would agree that a condition does not help with five compartments, but his final reading of Tur is then forced and the wording remains difficult. All this concerns bal tosif. Regarding validity, Bach certainly agrees that five compartments are invalid in every case, even with a condition, because Sanhedrin explains that an exterior compartment that does not face the air is invalid. It is also clear from the poskim that even if no passage at all was placed in the fifth compartment, the tefillin are invalid because the exterior compartment does not face the air. Mishnah Berurah cites Gra that even merely tying the fifth compartment to the others invalidates the tefillin. Damesek Eliezer questions this, but his explanation that a mitzvah is created merely by tying the fifth compartment is not convincing. Gra's intent is straightforward: just as, according to Rabbi Yehudah, the required binding of the four species causes the prohibition of bal tosif to take effect through the binding itself, so here, since tying the fifth compartment makes the four compartments invalid because the exterior bayis no longer faces the air, the prohibition of bal tosif takes effect through that act itself. This fits the plain meaning of the sugya. Gra's explanation of Rabbi Zeira as referring even to tying rather than full attachment also explains why the Gemara says “every exterior compartment that does not face the air,” rather than “the fourth compartment.” It still remains an exterior compartment.
He should put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. It appears to me that here too he must intend explicitly that he does so only because of the doubt. In this way, according to all opinions, he violates neither bal tosif nor bal tigra. If he intends generally to fulfill the positive commandment of tefillin, then even if there is no bal tosif because each pair stands independently, perhaps the halachah follows Rashi and these tefillin are invalid because their passages are exchanged. He would then violate bal tigra, for Semag writes that one who makes only three compartments in tefillin violates bal tigra. Here too only two passages are in their proper places. One cannot distinguish that in Semag's case three compartments are valid in themselves and only one is missing, whereas here the entire bayis is invalid because some passages are misplaced. In Semag's case as well, the object does not have the legal status of tefillin, since all four compartments are indispensable, yet it is still included in bal tigra. Nor can one say that there the deficiency is in the basic structure while here it is merely in placement. Misplacing a passage is equivalent to omitting it. This is no less than the law of sacrificial blood requiring four applications, where giving fewer applications can involve bal tigra even though after the fact the offering effects atonement. Here the omission invalidates even after the fact. Rambam writes in Sefer HaMitzvos that bal tigra applies whether one diminishes something written in the Torah or something transmitted orally. Since the received law fixes the order of the passages, changing it is a diminution. This would be avoided only if one accepted the kabbalistic view cited by Ateres Zekeinim that both arrangements are valid. Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and all the poskim did not accept that view as practical halachah. It is known that where the poskim disagree with kabbalists, the halachah follows the poskim. Rabbi Baruch Frankel likewise proves this from the Gemara. Moreover, a God-fearing person who wears Rabbeinu Tam tefillin wishes to satisfy every view, not to enter a possible Torah prohibition. Therefore, the correct practice is to state explicitly that he wears them only because of the doubt. Even unexpressed ordinary intent does not suffice, because Rosh Hashanah 27b rules that during the proper time intent is not required to violate bal tosif. The same should apply to bal tigra. See Taz at the end of siman 651. One cannot say that this is outside the proper time because he already fulfilled the mitzvah with Rashi tefillin, as Taz rules regarding one who afterward takes only three species of the lulav. Tefillin differ because their time extends throughout the entire day, and a blessing is recited whenever they are put on. This is at least comparable to a firstborn offering, whose entire day is considered its time, or to Birkat Kohanim when another congregation becomes available. There is some room to distinguish, but the safest practice is as stated, especially because some poskim maintain that the Torah obligation of tefillin is to wear them all day. I write this to reject one who wrote the opposite without sufficient examination.
