Merely like straps. For whichever way one looks at it, one pair is invalid. If, however, he puts on two valid pairs of tefillin, he violates bal tosif, as stated at the beginning of chapter 10 of Eruvin. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Maggid Mishneh. Levush and Lechem Chamudos express uncertainty about this, but they evidently overlooked the words of Maggid Mishneh and the Gemara. The passage in HaNechenakin is no proof to the contrary, because there the fifth compartment contains a different passage and therefore does not belong to tefillin at all. With two valid pairs, however, he violates bal tosif. See siman 10, seif 6.
And if he does not know, etc.; and if he cannot, etc. This requires study, for the latter appears to repeat the middle case. Furthermore, even in the first case, where he knows how to position them, he recites a blessing over only one pair. Lechem Chamudos offers a strained explanation. It appears to me that the meaning is as follows. The first clause says that he should place both pairs together upon the arm, recite the blessing, and tighten them simultaneously. The same applies to the head. The next clause, “if he does not know how to position them,” means that if he tightens both simultaneously he will not be able to position them correctly and must tie them one after the other. He should then put on the arm and head tefillin according to one opinion, etc. Mordechai writes that although the blessing over the head tefillin interrupts between the first blessing over the arm tefillin and the second arm tefillin, this is permitted.
If he cannot, etc. Meaning, he cannot put on both pairs even one after another in the same sitting, such as where he has an injury, as stated in siman 27, seif 7, or where he is embarrassed before people who mock him.
