(s.k. 2) And for Rabbeinu Tam, etc. — as written siman 32, meaning in the order they are written in the Torah; and this is parashat Shema before parashat VeHayah Im Shamo'a, as in Rashi's tefillin.

Therefore he must leave a blank space — meaning in the arm tefillin, where the four parshiyot are written on one klaf, as above in siman 32, seif 47.

And Vehayah im shamoa is in the middle; but some scribes err regarding open and closed parshiyot and write as in Rashi tefillin — they end parashat Vehayah im shamoa at the end of a line, meaning at "ha'aretz," the end of the line, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 32, seif katan 48 — and they begin parashat Shema at the start of a line. Then there is no space of nine letters between parashat Vehayah im shamoa and parashat Shema; thus Shema has neither the law of an open parasha nor a closed one, and there is no parasha name on Shema. Therefore they must leave a blank space at the end of Vehayah im shamoa, the measure of nine letters, and finish at "ha'aretz" in the middle of the line, and begin Shema at the head of the line; and between parashat Vehayah ki yeviacha and parashat Vehayah im shamoa likewise Vehayah im shamoa. For those who practice as the Rama wrote in siman 32, seif 36, who make all four parshiyot open — so too in Rabbenu Tam tefillin one does as in Rashi tefillin and leaves a blank space at the end of Vehayah ki yeviacha, the measure of nine letters, and begins Vehayah im shamoa at the start of the line. But those who practice as the Taz wrote there, seif katan 26, to make parashat Vehayah im shamoa closed — according to everyone in Rabbenu Tam tefillin one must leave a blank space at the end of parashat Vehayah im shamoa, less than nine letters, and also at the beginning of parashat Vehayah im shamoa leave a space less than nine letters, and the two spaces together are the measure of nine letters.
