(Seif katan 2) And according to Rabbeinu Tam, etc. As stated in siman 32, meaning in the order in which they are written in the Torah: the passage of Shema before Vehayah Im Shamoa, just as in Rashi tefillin.
Therefore, he must leave blank, etc. This refers to the arm tefillin, in which all four passages are written on one parchment, as explained above in siman 32, seif 47.
Vehayah Im Shamoa is written in the middle. Some scribes err regarding the open and closed passages by writing as they do for Rashi tefillin. They end Vehayah Im Shamoa at the end of a line, with the words “upon the earth” at the line's end, and begin Shema at the beginning of the next line. There is then no blank space of nine letters between Vehayah Im Shamoa and Shema, so Shema is neither an open nor a closed passage and lacks the legal status of a distinct passage. They must therefore leave a blank space of nine letters at the end of Vehayah Im Shamoa, ending “upon the earth” in the middle of the line, and begin Shema at the beginning of a line. Between Vehayah Ki Yeviacha and Vehayah Im Shamoa, those who follow Rama in siman 32, seif 36, making all four passages open, should do in Rabbeinu Tam tefillin as in Rashi tefillin: leave nine letters blank at the end of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha and begin Vehayah Im Shamoa at the start of a line. Those who follow Taz there, seif katan 26, to make Vehayah Im Shamoa valid according to all opinions, should leave fewer than nine letters blank at the end of Vehayah Ki Yeviacha and fewer than nine letters at the beginning of Vehayah Im Shamoa, with the two spaces together equaling nine letters.
