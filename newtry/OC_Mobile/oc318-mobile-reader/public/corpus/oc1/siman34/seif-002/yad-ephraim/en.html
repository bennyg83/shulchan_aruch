(In Magen Avraham note 4) He shall place per the words of one: shel yad and shel rosh — and he should not place first two tefillin on the hand and afterward two on the head, for he would interrupt with placing the second shel yad between the shel rosh belonging to the first shel yad, and likewise interrupt with the first shel rosh between the second shel yad and shel rosh; therefore he places per the words of one: shel yad and shel rosh. And interruption of blessing is not here; and the author, per his view, did not mention one blessing — Mordechai that Magen Avraham brings: even per the view that one does not mention, also two — nevertheless it is better than to interrupt in their placement; and it requires study: apparently one should be strict about interruption of blessing more than interruption of placement; and it is possible this is not called interruption, since the blessings on the mitzvah apply also to shel yad — if so the blessing "to place" and on the mitzvah of both apply also to placing the two tefillin, and the first shel rosh blessing also helps for the shel yad that follows, and this is not interruption.
