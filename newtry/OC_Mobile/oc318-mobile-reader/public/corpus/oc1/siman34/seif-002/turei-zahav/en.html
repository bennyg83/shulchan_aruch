And he shall place both and intend, etc. Tur wrote in the name of Rosh: there is no bal tosif here except in one who makes five batim — end quote. Apparently it implies he comes to exclude if one placed two pairs of tefillin on his head that he does not transgress bal tosif; and in Levush I saw he is uncertain and wrote arguments pro and con; and a complete sugya escaped me at the beginning of "one who finds tefillin," where it says that on Shabbat he brings in one pair but not two at once on his head, since he transgressed bal tosif it is like a burden on Shabbat. But I saw in Maadanei Melech who wrote on these words of Rosh: "except in one who makes five batim — for if he made four batim and brought another and placed it with them, this stands alone and this stands alone," as stated in chapter HaNichnakin — meaning it is not joined with the other batim — end quote. And afterward he wrote that the sugyot should be challenged against each other — the sugya of one who finds tefillin implies one who places two pairs transgresses bal tosif, and in HaNichnakin it implies there is no Torah bal tosif, for this stands alone and this stands alone; and to settle the sugya he wrote that in HaNichnakin one need not say only that he does not become zaken mamre from this, for this stands alone, but there is a Torah prohibition like that in Eruvin — end quote. And I do not perceive a reason for this distinction that this prohibition of rebelling against the court is from Torah prohibition. And it seems for this matter Tosafot already asked a similar difficulty on lulav in chapter HaNichnakin: "but it is difficult, for it implies he concludes for Rabbi Yehudah and all the more for Rabbanan we do not say this stands alone," etc., and here it implies for Rabbanan we say this stands alone; and one may say there it is rabbinically forbidden — end quote. Explanation regarding lulav: one who added one species and did not bind it with the four species — by Torah law there is no bal tosif, and rabbinically there is bal tosif; if so, likewise in tefillin in HaNichnakin, which deals by Torah law regarding death penalty of zaken mamre — we must say this stands alone and there is no bal tosif; but in one who finds tefillin, which deals how the finder should act — rabbinically bal tosif is forbidden even if this stands alone; and all they mentioned there in one who finds tefillin because of bal tosif is rabbinic, for they made bal tosif rabbinic there; and with this the words of Rosh are settled from what is still astonishing: since he came only to permit placing two tefillin, namely of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, that there should not be bal tosif — why did he distinguish between two pairs of tefillin and five batim? He should have distinguished only in this, that here he intends the rest should be mere straps; and according to what he wrote it is settled that he could not answer thus, for we conclude at the end of chapter Ra'uhu beit din that to transgress bal tosif one is liable even without intention; therefore he wrote there is bal tosif only with five batim by Torah law, for in placing another pair it is permitted by Torah law, for this stands alone, etc.; but rabbinically it is forbidden even with two pairs; and in this certainly the rabbis did not decree except when intending that both be for the mitzvah together; but one who intends to fulfill with one of them and the second will be like straps — permitted. And according to what he wrote that rabbinically it deals in one who finds tefillin — for we say in the Gemara there which answers the first tanna and Rabbi Shimon ben Gamaliel disagree whether one is liable for bal tosif without intention, and we say rabbinically it does not require intention — it is not difficult from him, for there all answers do not stand, only the answer that they disagree regarding Shabbat being time for tefillin, even rabbinically; and according to this, what reason did they not permit also in one who finds tefillin to bring in two pairs, since there is no Torah bal tosif, for this stands alone, but rabbinically; and since he intended only for rescue and not for the mitzvah of tefillin — permit him, as in placing tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam. For here the prohibition was only because of Torah bal tosif — rabbinically we say it is permitted without intention; but in one who finds tefillin, without Torah bal tosif there is prohibition because there is no mitzvah except in one pair — it follows the second is a burden on Shabbat; therefore it is fit to forbid even rabbinically even without intention, as seems correct to me. And Maharach Or Zarua challenged Rosh: what he wrote five batim from two pairs — and the Gemara of HaNichnakin also escaped him, that with two pairs it is lighter because of "this stands alone." I was asked: one who places two tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam — which should he place lower toward the face, or is there no concern in this at all? Answer: it seems he must place specifically Rashi's below and Rabbeinu Tam's above them; for according to what appears from this matter the poskim brought, that there is room on the head to place two tefillin is not clear halacha and proof, for in the last chapter of Eruvin the first tanna and Rashbag disagree in one who finds tefillin on Shabbat: the first tanna holds only one pair he brings in, and Rashbag holds he brings in two pairs at once; and they interpret their dispute there first: the first tanna holds we do not say there is room on the head to place two tefillin, and Rashbag holds we say so; and afterward they interpret their dispute: the first tanna holds Shabbat is time for tefillin, and Rashbag holds Shabbat is not time for tefillin. And apparently the latter wording is primary, for what we say there "rather it is clear as we taught initially" refers to this wording, and Rashi explained there explicitly; and therefore halacha contradicts halacha, for all poskim ruled there like the first tanna, and Tur siman 301 below; and Tur already wrote siman 31 that Shabbat is not time for tefillin; therefore it must be said the poskim hold there is no need to say they disagree specifically on Shabbat being time for tefillin as the latter wording, but as the first wording — there is room on the head, etc.; and so is implied from Tosafot there folio 96 s.v. "it is not reasonable": Rambam's words are not settled that he said one pair, no more, unless he holds Shabbat is time for tefillin as we concluded above, unless we say he does not hold Rabbi Shimon bar Yitzchak who said there is room on the head to place two tefillin — end quote; if so we too hold this doubt; and therefore although we hold like the first tanna who said one pair, no more, nevertheless it is possible the reason is that there are two places to place two tefillin there; but on Shabbat, where it is not time for tefillin as is clear from the conclusion of Kometz folio 37 that all amoraim hold we do not say Shabbat is time for tefillin — if so the reason is as he wrote and means: thus the poskim wrote regarding those tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam that there is room on the head to place two tefillin — for they said so as advice: in any case there would not be bal tosif; if above the tefillin of Rashi there is still room to place tefillin, we say he intends explicitly they should be like straps; and if there is no more room there, all the more it is settled above the tefillin of Rashi the place of tefillin is finished, and plainly it is like straps, and there is no prohibition in what he adds there; and this is similar to what is written at the beginning of chapter Ra'uhu beit din: to transgress bal tosif in its time one transgresses even without intention; not in its time one transgresses only with intention; and likewise here — not in the place of tefillin there is prohibition only when intending both together. And according to this it is better to place them in one bay afterward than to place in one bay at once, lest there be no room for the second tefillin; one may say nevertheless it is better in this that both be adjacent to the blessing than to place them afterward without a blessing; therefore it is better to place them in one bay. From all this it seems whether shel rosh or shel yad he must place tefillin of Rashi in the place that is the primary place of the tefillin, and on the added place place Rabbeinu Tam's, for one must fear perhaps we do not hold there are two places to place tefillin; and if so there is practical difference from our words that everyone who places tefillin must place them specifically toward the face and not above that — i.e. the place where a baby's soft spot sinks — lest there be no room for two tefillin.

And if he does not know how to intend — this is Semag's view as he wrote initially: one for whom the two pairs of tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam are equal in his eyes and he does not know how to intend, etc. — he places one of them first; and even though Rabbeinu Tam's happened to him first and there is a slight interruption, nevertheless he is compelled to do so. But what the author wrote afterward "and if he cannot place them," etc. — is the view of the Ashkenazi responsum that Beit Yosef brings: tefillin of Rashi are primary; therefore he must bless specifically on them and afterward place Rabbeinu Tam's without a blessing; and so is Semag's own view in the conclusion that he wrote that in the land of Edom and Ishmael they practiced like Rashi. And it requires study why the author wrote the first way first, which contradicts what he wrote afterward; and I saw one who wrote they are one explanation of the other and it is a scribal error. And the main thing seems it must read "and some say: if he cannot place them," etc., for we see the explainers on this in Beit Yosef disagree with each other.
