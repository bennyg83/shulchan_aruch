And he should put on both and intend, etc. Tur cites Rosh that there is no bal tosif here unless one makes five compartments. This initially suggests that wearing two pairs does not involve bal tosif. The chapter HaMotzei Tefillin, however, states that on Shabbos one may bring in only one pair at a time, not two, because bal tosif would make the second pair a burden. Me'adanei Melech reconciles this with Sanhedrin: a separate fifth compartment stands independently and does not create Torah-level bal tosif, whereas there may still be a D'Rabbanan prohibition. Taz accepts this distinction. Thus, with two pairs there is no Torah-level bal tosif because each stands independently, but there is a D'Rabbanan prohibition when both are intended as mitzvah tefillin. Rosh therefore requires intent that one pair fulfill the mitzvah and the other be like mere straps. Taz was asked which pair should be placed lower toward the face. He rules that Rashi tefillin must occupy the primary lower position and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin the higher position. The statement that there is room on the head for two pairs is not entirely unquestioned. The dispute in Eruvin can be explained either as concerning whether there is room for two pairs or whether Shabbos is a time for tefillin. Since the halachic rulings appear to follow different sides of these explanations, one should act safely: Rashi tefillin should be in the primary valid place, and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin above them. The same applies to the arm: Rashi tefillin should occupy the primary place and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin the additional area. This also teaches that one who wears only one pair should place it toward the face, not high above, because perhaps there is only one valid tefillin position.
And if he does not know how to position them. This is Semag's view concerning one who regards Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin as equally doubtful. He puts on one pair first, even if Rabbeinu Tam tefillin happen to come first, despite the resulting slight interruption. The later clause, “and if he cannot put them on,” comes from the German responsum cited by Beit Yosef, which regards Rashi tefillin as primary. According to it, he must recite the blessing specifically over Rashi tefillin and afterward put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin without a blessing. Semag himself likewise concludes that the lands of Edom and Ishmael follow Rashi. It is difficult why Shulchan Aruch first cites the earlier procedure, which conflicts with the later one. The text should read: “Some say that if he cannot put them on, etc.” The two approaches are disputed opinions, not one explanation of the other.
