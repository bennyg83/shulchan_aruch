(5) And he should put on, etc. One who wears both pairs should place the Rashi tefillin lower, toward the face, and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin above them. The Arizal writes that on the arm one should first insert the Rashi tefillin, positioning them toward the shoulder, and afterward the Rabbeinu Tam tefillin toward the hand, both upon the biceps. On the head, Rashi tefillin should be lower and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin somewhat higher, both within the valid area. The Rabbeinu Tam straps should be beneath and covered by the Rashi straps, so that only the Rashi straps are visible. The Rabbeinu Tam tefillin should be smaller. One who has arm tefillin according to Rabbeinu Tam and head tefillin according to Rashi and Rambam does not fulfill the complete obligation according to any view. In pressing circumstances he may wear them, since one may then fulfill the mitzvah with only one tefillin. He should recite a blessing only over the tefillin written according to our accepted Rashi and Rambam practice. If the head tefillin are according to Rashi, he should put them on first, and when removing them remove the arm tefillin first.
(6) Both. He should place both arm tefillin together, recite the blessing, and tighten them simultaneously. The same applies to the head tefillin, so that the placement of both pairs is close to the blessing.
(7) Those that are, etc. He cannot intend, while tightening both together, that both are tefillin, because one pair is not valid tefillin. Some authorities also maintain that this would involve bal tosif at least D'Rabbanan.
(8) Merely. For whichever way one looks at it, one pair is invalid. Here there are two grounds for leniency: one pair is invalid, and each pair stands independently. If he wears two valid pairs, or makes head tefillin with five compartments, whether from the outset or by attaching a fifth compartment afterward, he violates bal tosif. Gra implies that even tying the fifth compartment to the other four without fully attaching it invalidates the tefillin and results in bal tosif.
(9) Both together. Meaning, if tightening both at once prevents him from positioning each correctly, he must bind them immediately one after the other without interruption. The original blessing then covers the second pair as well. This is Shulchan Aruch's meaning when it says to place the others relying upon the first blessing: he places the second pair beside the first, but not in the same act of placement.
(10) He should put on. The text should read: he should put on the arm and head tefillin according to one opinion and place the other pair relying upon the first blessing; and if he cannot, etc. The words “and he should immediately remove them” should be omitted.
(11) According to one opinion. Shulchan Aruch does not specify which pair. The later authorities conclude that it is preferable to connect the blessing to the Rashi tefillin.
(12) The arm and head tefillin. According to the Mechaber, one blessing is recited; according to our custom, two blessings, as explained in siman 25. He then places the second pair beside them. Although putting on the first head tefillin, and according to our custom its blessing, intervenes between the first arm tefillin and the second arm tefillin, this is permitted because the blessings apply to both pairs. He may not, however, put on both arm tefillin first and afterward both head tefillin, because the second arm tefillin would interrupt between the first arm and head tefillin.
(13) He cannot. Meaning, he cannot wear both pairs even one after another at the same sitting, such as where he has an injury and insufficient room, or is embarrassed by people who mock him. He should then put on Rashi tefillin with a blessing, since they are the principal halachah, and put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin after prayer to satisfy all opinions. Biur Halachah proves that he must explicitly intend to wear Rabbeinu Tam tefillin only because of the doubt, and not simply for the mitzvah.
(14) After prayer. This means in accordance with siman 25, seif 13, not like those who remove Rashi tefillin and put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin immediately after Kedushah in the repetition of the Amidah. Besides acting contrary to that law, one must listen attentively to the chazzan's repetition and not occupy himself with another matter. Peri Megadim.
(15) Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa. He need not recite the passage of tzitzis, because the concern that he may have testified falsely by reciting Krias Shema without tefillin is already remedied by the first two passages. Peri Megadim implies that this is the proper practice: put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin only after removing Rashi tefillin. One who wears tefillin all day should plainly wear Rashi tefillin.
