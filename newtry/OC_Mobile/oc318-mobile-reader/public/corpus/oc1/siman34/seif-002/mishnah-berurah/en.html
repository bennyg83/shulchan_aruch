(5) And he shall place, etc. — and the custom of those who place two pairs of tefillin is to place Rashi's below toward the face and Rabbenu Tam's above. And so the Ari wrote that one should insert Rashi's first and stand them toward the shoulder, and after them Rabbenu Tam's toward the arm, and both on the kiboret; and on the head he places Rashi's lower and Rabbenu Tam's higher, and both in the proper place for tefillin; and the straps of Rabbenu Tam should be covered under the straps of Rashi so that only the straps of Rashi are visible, and the tefillin of Rabbenu Tam should be smaller than those of Rashi; see Sefer HaKavanot, Tefillin, chapter 10, section 12. One who places arm tefillin according to Rabbenu Tam and head tefillin according to Rashi and the Rambam — whether he fulfills his obligation: Shvut Yaakov, part 1, siman 2, ruled that he does not fulfill according to anyone; but in a time of need it is permitted to place them thus, for in a time of need one fulfills if he places one alone; nevertheless he should bless only on that written according to our custom, per Rashi and the Rambam. And if the head tefillin were written per Rashi's view, one may place the head tefillin first, and when removing, remove the arm tefillin first; see there.

(6) Both of them — together on the arm, and he blesses and fastens them in one compartment, and the same for the head, so that the placement of both is adjacent to the blessing.

(7) With those that are, etc. — for he cannot intend on both at the time of fastening, since he fastens them together for the sake of tefillin — for one of them is not tefillin; and some say further that there is bal tosif in this, at least rabbinically.

(8) In the world — for from necessity one of them is invalid, as above in seif katan 1; and specifically in this one does not transgress bal tosif, for here there are two elevations: one, that one of them is invalid; two, that each stands independently. But if he placed two pairs of kosher tefillin, or the head tefillin he made of five batim — whether he made it of five from the outset or of four and afterward attached another bayit — he transgressed the prohibition of bal tosif. And see the Gra's commentary, for it is implied from his words that even if the fifth bayit was not attached completely with the four batim but only by an ordinary knot, nevertheless he invalidates the tefillin thereby, and thereby he transgressed the prohibition of bal tosif.

(9) Both together — meaning: when he fastens both in one compartment he cannot direct the place so that each rests in its proper place; therefore he must tie them immediately one after the other without interruption, and the blessing he recited at the beginning is effective also for the second tefillin; and this is what the Mechaber wrote nearby, "and he shall place the others upon reliance," etc. — meaning he places the second pair next to the first pair, as explained, but not in one placement.

(10) He shall place — according to one view, hand and head, and place the others upon reliance on the first blessing; and if he cannot, etc., so is the reading, and we do not read "and he shall remove them immediately."

(11) Per the words of the one — the author left his words unspecified, and per the ruling of the later authorities it is better that the blessing follow the tefillin of Rashi.

(12) Of the hand and of the head — per the author with one blessing, but per our custom with two blessings, as explained above in siman 25; and afterward he places with them a second pair. Even though he interrupts between them with placing the shel rosh of the first pair and also in the blessing he recites over it per our custom, nevertheless it is plausible that the second blessings refer to both of them; but he may not place first both shel yad from the two pairs and afterward both shel rosh, for he would thereby interrupt between the shel yad and the shel rosh in placing the second pair.

(13) He may not — meaning: even one after the other he may not place, in a case where both are equal (e.g. he has a wound and has no room for both, or he is ashamed before people who mock him) — he places those of Rashi and blesses on them, for they are the primary view for practical halacha, and those of Rabbeinu Tam he places after prayer in order to fulfill the doubt; and see in Biur Halacha where we proved that one must be very careful not to have intention when placing them only out of doubt and not in general for the sake of the mitzvah.

(14) And after prayer — meaning as the law explained above in siman 25, paragraph 13; and not like those who remove tefillin of Rashi and put on those of Rabbeinu Tam immediately after the Kedushah of the Shemoneh Esreh; and in truth, besides doing contrary to the law explained above in siman 25, paragraph 13, one must also have intention during the Shemoneh Esreh prayer toward the Shatz not to engage in another matter [Peri Megadim].

(15) Shema and "and it shall be" if you hearken — but he need not the portion of tzitzit, because the concern lest he testify false testimony was already remedied with two portions; and see in Peri Megadim that it is implied from it that so should one act who places tefillin of Rabbeinu Tam — he places them only after removing those of Rashi; and it is obvious that one who wears tefillin all day should preferably wear tefillin of Rashi.
