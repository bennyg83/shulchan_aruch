In Shulchan Aruch the words “and he should remove them immediately” are now inserted, as are the words “and some say that if.” This, however, is the gloss of Be'er HaGolah, letter 5. The intermediate view is that of Semag: if one does not know how to direct his intent properly while wearing two pairs simultaneously, he should put on one pair, remove it immediately, and put on the second pair, relying on the first blessing, even though he interrupts by reciting the first paragraph of Keriat Shema before putting on the second pair; this is permitted because the two blessings apply to both pairs. The third view is that of the Ashkenazic responsum, namely Maharil, and this is also the view of Turei Zahav. Turei Zahav explains that Semag speaks of one who regards the two pairs as equally authoritative. If Rabbeinu Tam tefillin came into his possession first, he should not pass them over; he recites the blessings over the arm and head tefillin, removes them, and then puts on Rashi tefillin, or the reverse. The third view (“if he cannot”) means that if he cannot direct his intent, he should recite the blessings specifically over Rashi tefillin, because they are primary, and put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin later, after prayer, without a blessing. He need not remove Rashi tefillin immediately and put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin relying on the same blessing, because Rashi's view is primary, like the law of maror: initially one recites the blessing over lettuce and eats horseradish afterward without a blessing. According to the old text, which did not contain “and he should remove them immediately,” nor “some say that if,” the passage seemed self-contradictory: it first cited Semag and afterward the Ashkenazic responsum. The reading “some say,” etc., was therefore inserted. The words “and he should remove them immediately” need not be inserted, because reliance on the same blessing itself implies that there may be no interruption. He removes the first pair and puts on the second; this applies to one who regards both as equally authoritative. This appears to be the Levush's understanding. What he writes concerning the intermediate view (that Rabbeinu Tam tefillin are put on first) is the Levush's addition: since we are discussing one who regards both pairs equally, he recites the blessing, puts on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, removes them, and then puts on Rashi tefillin and recites Keriat Shema with them so as not to separate himself from the congregation. According to the third view, the Ashkenazic responsum, Rashi tefillin are primary. Nechalat Tzvi understood the latter two views as one, but this is difficult. Turei Zahav explicitly writes that Semag's ruling concerns one who regards the two pairs as equal. See what I wrote in Mishbetzot Zahav, letter 4, on this matter. If one found an arm tefillin and strap without passages and is uncertain whether they were made for Rabbeinu Tam, it is proper to insert Rashi passages, because there are multiple grounds for leniency, we are expert in their manufacture, and Rashi's view is primary. The required processing, including whether straps may be transferred from a lesser to a greater sanctity, still requires study.
