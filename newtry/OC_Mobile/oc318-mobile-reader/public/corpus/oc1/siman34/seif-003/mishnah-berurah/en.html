(16) So. This applies even if he wishes to put them on only after prayer. Baer Heitev cites a case of one who publicly put on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin after prayer before the congregation. Responsa Maharash HaLevi ruled that this appeared arrogant and that he must discontinue the practice. Shevus Yaakov likewise writes that even if some people do so, there can still be arrogance. If he puts them on before a great scholar who wears them only privately at home, it certainly appears arrogant.
(17) In acts of piety. Since the world follows Rashi, one who is stringent for himself in this matter appears arrogant unless he is known to be similarly stringent in other matters.
