(Seif katan 3) Like mere straps, etc. For whichever way one looks at it, one pair is invalid. Rosh writes that the other pair is like mere straps and there is no bal tosif unless one makes five compartments. Levush was therefore uncertain whether Rosh meant specifically five compartments, while wearing two pairs of the same order, both Rashi or both Rabbeinu Tam, would not involve bal tosif, comparable to wearing several four-cornered garments with tzitzis; or whether two pairs of the same order also involve bal tosif, since every four-cornered garment independently requires tzitzis. Magen Avraham regards it as obvious that two pairs of the same order involve bal tosif. His proof is from the beginning of the tenth chapter of Eruvin: one who finds tefillin in the public domain on Shabbos brings them in one pair at a time, while Rabban Gamliel permits two pairs. One explanation is that everyone agrees Shabbos is not a time for tefillin, and they dispute whether there is room on the head and arm for two pairs. Another explanation is that everyone agrees there is room for two pairs, but the first Tanna maintains Shabbos is a time for tefillin and therefore more than one pair involves bal tosif. Other explanations there likewise show that two pairs of the same order can involve bal tosif. Lechem Chamudos cites the same proof. Beit Yosef, in the name of Maggid Mishneh in siman 651, likewise compares this to taking two lulavim, which involves bal tosif, whereas adding a species not among the four species does not, because each stands independently, as in the chapter HaNechenakin. The discussion in Sanhedrin says that if one made four compartments and placed a fifth separate compartment beside them, each stands independently and there is no bal tosif. If he made five attached compartments from the outset, the tefillin were defective from their beginning. Rav Zeira explains the case as one in which he first made four valid compartments and afterward attached a fifth, thereby causing the outer compartment no longer to face the air. This appears to support Levush's doubt, for if a separate fifth compartment does not involve bal tosif, all the more so two complete pairs might not. Magen Avraham answers this below.
For there he wrote another passage, etc. This is Maggid Mishneh's distinction. Why did the Gemara not answer that the fifth compartment contained one of the four tefillin passages? Rashba writes that in such a case, although he violates bal tosif, he still fulfills the mitzvah. The case of a rebellious elder requires that the addition invalidate the original mitzvah, and therefore that answer would not suffice. The question from Sanhedrin was based upon the challenger's wording that each stands independently, implying no bal tosif. Magen Avraham therefore answers that the separate fifth compartment contained a passage entirely unrelated to tefillin. The challenger naturally chose the more common error of adding a fifth, different passage. Rosh nevertheless had to say that the invalid pair is like mere straps because Maggid Mishneh writes that although adding a different species to the lulav does not involve Torah-level bal tosif, it remains prohibited D'Rabbanan. Since Sanhedrin concerns the Torah law of a rebellious elder, that was irrelevant there. To avoid even a D'Rabbanan prohibition, Rosh requires intent that the invalid pair be like mere straps. See Magen Avraham, siman 651, seif katan 27.
