[4] One who is established, etc. A letter was sent from Eretz Yisrael reporting that the platform above the grave of the prophet Yechezkel collapsed and old tefillin were found there arranged according to Rashi. Some reject this proof and say that the tefillin were invalid and therefore buried there. This is unconvincing, because burial would not have been necessary; one could merely have transferred the passages from one compartment to another. Bach writes this. Sefer Baruch She'amar writes that all who fear Hashem should carefully preserve and bury every component of tefillin, including straps, ma'abarot, titurot, batim, patches, and certainly the passages themselves. How should they be buried? They should be placed in an earthenware vessel near the grave of a Torah scholar. Otherwise, they will ultimately be discarded, even if placed in an Aron HaKodesh, for that is not a secure long-term genizah. Know that according to Rabbeinu Tam as well, the passages must be written in their scriptural order, as stated in siman 32, seif 1. Therefore, one must leave blank the place of Vehayah Im Shamoa, write Shema at the end of the parchment, and afterward write Vehayah Im Shamoa in the middle.
