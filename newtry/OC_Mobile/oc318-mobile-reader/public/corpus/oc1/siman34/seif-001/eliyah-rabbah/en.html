[1] Two pairs of tefillin, etc. This is difficult. If so, why do we rule in siman 301, concerning one who finds tefillin on Shabbos, that he may bring in only one pair? If we say, as the Gemara concludes at the beginning of HaMotzei Tefillin, that Shabbos is a time for tefillin, and therefore the prohibition of bal tosif renders the additional pair a burden, this is difficult, for we rule in siman 31 that Shabbos is not a time for tefillin. I further found that Tosafos Yom Tov, in the chapter Bameh Ishah, questions Tur's words in siman 31 from siman 301, where Tur writes that one should not go out wearing tefillin lest he come to carry them in the public domain if he removes them when he needs to relieve himself. This implies that he holds Shabbos is a time for tefillin. After considering these many difficulties, it appears to me that there are three views in the matter. The statement that Shabbos is a time for tefillin means that there is even a mitzvah to put them on on Shabbos. This is the view of the Tanna in the baraisa who obligates women in tefillin because it is a positive commandment not dependent upon time, and the Gemara assumes that the Tanna of the Mishnah agrees with that Tanna. Examine this carefully. According to the authority who expounds “You shall observe this statute” as excluding Shabbos and Yom Tov, there is a prohibition against putting on tefillin on Shabbos, as explained in siman 29. According to Rabbi Akiva, who derives from “It shall be for you as a sign” that Shabbos and Yom Tov are excluded because they themselves are signs, there is no prohibition against putting on tefillin on Shabbos, but there is also no obligation or blessing. We rule like Rabbi Akiva, as explained in siman 31. Therefore, we also rule that one may bring in only one pair. Since there is no prohibition against putting on tefillin, even though it is merely optional, adding another pair is forbidden and renders it a burden. For this reason Tur in siman 301 needed to give the concern that he may come to carry them in the public domain, rather than saying that they are a burden because wearing tefillin on Shabbos is forbidden. This resolves everything, with Hashem's help. Accordingly, the lengthy responsum printed in Taz, concerning one who wears Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin together and instructing that the Rashi tefillin be placed lower toward the face and the Rabbeinu Tam tefillin above them, is entirely rejected by the explanation I have given. Even without this, his words are strained.
