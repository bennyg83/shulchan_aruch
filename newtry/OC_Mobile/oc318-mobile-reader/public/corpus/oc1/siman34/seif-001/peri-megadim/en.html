For Rashi and the Rambam — Taz: see Menachot 34b. There are three views; two the poskim mentioned: (1) the view of Rashi and Rambam, (2) Rabbeinu Tam — and both hold Kadesh and Vehayah ki from the left of the one who places, only Shema and Vehayah im shamo'a. For Rashi in order, and for Rabbeinu Tam Shema from outside, from the right of the one who places and the reader in order; for Rashi plainly, and for Rabbeinu Tam in the order they are placed in the batim. And Netziv in the name of Nekudot Yisrael: in order as they are written — Kadesh and Vehayah ki — and afterward the reader looks to the left and reads Shema and Vehayah im. However there is a third view, Raavad chapter 3 of Hilchot Tefillin halacha 5 from a braita: How are they arranged? Kadesh and Vehayah ki on the right — meaning the one who places — Shema and Vehayah im on the left of the one who places, and the reader reads in order, meaning Shema and Vehayah im shamo'a. And per his words it is unnecessary for Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, but for Raavad he teaches us that Rabbeinu Tam is not like Rashi — Kadesh, Vehayah ki, Shema, Vehayah im — and Kadesh on the right of the one who places; it turns out the reader does not read at all in order; therefore he teaches us "in order" that Shema is on the left of the one who places in the outer bay, that the reader reads in order Shema and Vehayah im as they are written in the Torah; and he linked it to "for there the name of Hashem is called upon you" and "they shall see from you" Shema and Vehayah im, where acceptance of the yoke of heaven is mentioned. Nevertheless we have only the explanation of Rabbeinu Tam as Tosafot write, and one should not make three pairs of tefillin but only two as the author wrote. And it is a novelty in my eyes that Beit Yosef did not mention anything from the words of Raavad — perhaps because this view is rejected in his eyes he did not mention it.
