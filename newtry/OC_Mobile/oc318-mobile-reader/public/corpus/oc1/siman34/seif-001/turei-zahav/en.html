According to Rashi and Rambam, etc. Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam dispute the baraisa: “How are they arranged? Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha on the right; Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa on the left.” The Gemara explains that “right” means the reader's right, as he stands facing the wearer, which is the wearer's left. Rashi understands that just as Kadesh is followed by Vehayah Ki Yeviacha, so Vehayah Ki Yeviacha is followed by Shema, and Shema by Vehayah Im Shamoa. Thus Vehayah Im Shamoa is on the wearer's right. Rabbeinu Tam understands that Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa refer to the opposite side: Kadesh and Vehayah Ki Yeviacha are on the wearer's left, while on the wearer's right the outer compartment contains Shema and beside it Vehayah Im Shamoa. A mnemonic is that the two passages beginning Vehayah are adjacent, and Kadesh and Shema, whose initial letters are shin, are beside the shins on the outer sides of the batim.
