For Rashi and the Rambam, etc. Here Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam disagree, for the Tanna taught: How are they arranged? Kadesh and Vehayah ki yevi'acha on the right — Shema and Vehayah im shamo'a on the left — and we interpret afterward that this right is the right of the reader who stands opposite the one who places, and it turns out to be the left of the one who places. And Rashi holds that just as Kadesh and Vehayah ki, etc. is the order of their placement one after another — i.e. first Kadesh on the left of the one who places, and after it Vehayah ki, so too Shema after Vehayah ki and the portion Vehayah im shamo'a after Shema; it turns out on the right of the one who places is Vehayah im shamo'a. But Rabbeinu Tam explains that Shema and Vehayah im shamo'a refer to the second side — meaning on the left of the one who places is Kadesh and Vehayah ki, and on the right is Shema and Vehayah im shamo'a — meaning on the outer second side, which is the right of the one who places, is Shema, and adjacent to it Vehayah im shamo'a; a sign for mutual existences and "shins" — meaning Kadesh and Shema beside the "shins" on the sides of the batim.
