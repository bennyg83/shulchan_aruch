He should not place them. See Mishnah Berurah; this is from Magen Avraham. All the laws below in siman 42, seif 3, apply here. It appears that even if the pouch was originally made for one pair, before that pair was ever placed in it, one may explicitly revoke the original designation. This is not a case where speech cannot nullify an action, because making the pouch is only designation, not an actual act of sanctification. See Sanhedrin 48a.
The two pairs. This refers to a case where the pair for which the pouch was originally designated is placed in it without its own case. Even if it is sometimes in a case and sometimes temporarily placed directly in the pouch, the pouch becomes sanctified through that direct use, as stated in Megillah 26b. If it is always kept inside its own case, however, the outer pouch becomes only an accessory to an accessory and has no sanctity. It appears that this is true only if the inner case also covers the ma'abarta and titura, for they too are part of the sacred body of the tefillin. Megillah lists only the pouch and straps as accessories of sanctity, and Shabbos 28b likewise discusses the shin of the tefillin. I later found a similar point in Mordechai, Hilchos Tefillin.
It is non-sacred. See Mishnah Berurah. I copied the entire ruling from Baer Heitev in the name of Responsa Darkei Noam. Its practical conclusion requires some study. He writes that if the tefillin were never worn, their components may be exchanged, evidently relying on siman 42, where new sacred articles may be changed. Yet the later authorities there permit only lowering from a more severe sanctity to a lighter one, not to ordinary use, because designation for the body of sanctity has halachic force. Here this author himself regards the exchange as a reduction to ordinary status. He further writes that even an initial condition does not permit transferring from sacred to ordinary use. Shaarei Teshuvah concludes that regarding straps a condition does help. This is straightforward, because straps are accessories of sanctity, and siman 42, seif 3, permits a condition to reduce them to ordinary use. Even regarding batim and passages, the matter requires study. Concerning parchment processed for tefillin passages, which is designation for the body of sanctity and may not be reduced to ordinary use, I prove below in siman 42 that one may write other Torah teachings upon it because it thereby retains some sanctity. Here too, even after the change, the passages still retain sanctity, and the batim remain accessories to sanctity. Perhaps a condition should therefore help. I do not have the responsum before me for examination. It may be possible to be lenient through an initial condition in changing Rabbeinu Tam head-tefillin batim for Rashi use when no other passages are available. This requires study. I also cited Peri Megadim concerning one who finds a strap, and omitted Responsa Yad Eliyahu, cited by Baer Heitev, which says that nowadays the majority are Rashi straps. See Pischei Teshuvah. Peri Megadim writes: If one finds an arm or head bayis and strap without passages, and it is uncertain whether it belonged to Rabbeinu Tam tefillin, he may insert Rashi passages because of a double doubt, because the majority follow Rashi, because this raises rather than lowers sanctity, because we are expert in the law, and because Rashi is the principal ruling. The double doubt is simple: perhaps it was made for Rashi, and perhaps Rashi is the principal halachah. Yet this reasoning alone would seemingly allow using it for Rabbeinu Tam as well, or perhaps forbid both, since the doubts conflict. Therefore, Peri Megadim adds the majority practice and the fact that Rashi is the principal ruling. His concluding remarks about processing are difficult. There is no distinct processing for Rashi or Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. It appears that his intent is based upon siman 39, seif 9, where one who buys tefillin from a nonexpert seller need check only three units for defective and excessive letters, and we do not suspect they were processed not for the sake of the mitzvah because everyone is expert in that requirement. Peri Megadim is uncertain here because, even if everyone knows that the passages require processing for the sake of the mitzvah, we cannot say everyone knows that the batim and straps require such processing, since this is disputed among the poskim, as explained in siman 33. Perhaps the person who lost them made the batim or straps without processing for the sake of the mitzvah. The finder would then be elevating an ordinary object to sanctity without adequate prior designation. The Gemara's permission to buy tefillin, apparently with straps, applied in their time when the law was well known. Nowadays, when buying from a nonexpert, one must ask whether the batim and straps were processed for the sake of the mitzvah. In a found object this cannot be determined. This concern is especially serious today regarding a found strap. Perhaps it was merely blackened for the sake of the mitzvah but not processed for that purpose, for many people incorrectly rely upon the lenient view, contrary to law, as Shaarei Teshuvah explains above in siman 33. Peri Megadim is the source for the final ruling.
