(18) He should not place — and it deals with a case where the pouch was designated at first only for the view of one of them; but if it was made from the outset to place both in it, it is permitted.

(19) In one pouch — of tefillin, as will be concluded; but in the tallit bag it is permitted to place both, even if they are without a pouch, for the bag is designated also for a mundane matter, namely the tallit.

(20) It is mundane — and from this it follows that it is forbidden to switch the pouches, and all the more the batim, straps, and parshiyot from those of Rashi to Rabbeinu Tam or vice versa. If a person never placed them on him for the sake of the mitzvah of tefillin he may switch them; but if a person placed them on him even once for the sake of the mitzvah of tefillin and they were arranged per the view of one of them, Rabbeinu Tam or Rashi, it is forbidden to switch them afterward, and even if he stipulated from the outset to switch when he wishes it is ineffective, for from holy to mundane a condition does not avail (later authorities). Pri Megadim wrote that if he found straps and does not know whether they are of Rashi or Rabbeinu Tam he may place them for parshiyot of Rashi, for the majority place those of Rashi and our view is also that Rashi is primary, and we elevate in holiness. And see in Baer Heitev and Biur Halacha. And some of the later authorities are more lenient, for according to us who hold Rashi is primary, as above, he may switch and take a strap from Rabbeinu Tam for Rashi when he has no other; but from Rashi to Rabbeinu Tam it is forbidden to take in any manner. And if it happened that he took out a pouch of Rabbeinu Tam first he should transfer them, for according to us Rashi is primary and this is not "do not pass over mitzvot"; nevertheless ab initio he should be careful not to come to this. On chol ha-moed one should not place those of Rabbeinu Tam. And on Tisha BeAv for Minchah — whoever wishes may place them.
