Seif katan 6: If he fixed a price, etc. See the Beit Yosef in the name of the Hagahot Mordechai. Concerning the authorities' requirement that the price be fixed, the Beit Yosef writes that this is unlike the Hagahot Mordechai, who cite Tosafot that giving garments to a launderer or hides to a tanner without an express agreement is itself treated as a fixed price, since the worker does not act gratuitously. According to what was explained, this is readily understood. The prohibition applies when the non-Jew is uncertain whether the Jew will pay him, and likewise when he knows he will be paid but does not know how much and whether the Jew will give the proper amount. In the case of a launderer or tanner, however, the non-Jew knows that the Jew will pay and the customary rate is known. The Hagahot Mordechai therefore regard this as a fully fixed price.
