Seif 6: In his hand. This is stated there according to Rava and Rabbi Meir, since the course of the sugya follows him, as stated above in siman 301, seif 12. This does not appear correct as practical halachah, however; rather, the ruling should follow Rabbi Yehudah, because in a dispute between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah, and the anonymous Mishnah also follows his position.
It is permitted to go out wearing tefillin. This is stated in the Gemara there, 12a: “The school of Rabbi Yishmael taught...” Beit Yosef and Taz ask that even without Rabbah bar Rav Huna it should be permitted to go out wearing them, since the Sages decreed only where going out with the item on Shabbos would incur liability, whereas one would not incur liability for going out with tefillin. See the strained answers they offer. It appears to me that the Gemara specifically relies on the fact that one does not forget tefillin. Were that not so, it would be forbidden even according to Rava, for Rava concedes that there are cases in which the Sages enact a decree upon a decree, namely where one is likely to come to negligence. This is as stated: anything with which one may not go out into the public domain may not be worn even in a courtyard, including items for which one is exempt but whose carriage is forbidden. The reason is that a woman is likely to remove her ornaments and a man to set down his articles. The primary explanation appears to be that the Sages did not decree shortly before nightfall, but they did decree at nightfall itself. Therefore it states there in a baraita: “Chananya says: A person is obligated... to feel his garments... at nightfall.” Although one who went out with the item on Shabbos would not incur liability, he must nevertheless inspect himself, because at nightfall the decree applies.
