With his needle in his hand. With his garment, however, it is permitted, because even on Shabbos there is no liability to bring a sin-offering, as stated in siman 301, seif 12. See siman 301, seif 8, and siman 303, seif 9, where at times there is liability to bring a sin-offering. Nowadays, however, there is no public domain of Torah status, and it is permitted close to nightfall. See the Gemara.
With tefillin on his head. We do not decree lest he need to relieve himself and remove them, as stated in siman 301, seif 7, because he does not forget them on his head, since it is prohibited to divert one's attention from them. In his hand, however, it is prohibited, because he does divert his attention from them, and we are concerned that he may forget to put them away until dark. Ultimately, when he remembers that he has an object in his hand, he may forget that it is Shabbos and carry it out. One should not explain that he may forget the object in his hand and carry it out, because there is no liability to bring a sin-offering unless he intends to perform the act but forgot that it was Shabbos, or did not know that this labor was prohibited (Tosafot; see Bach, contrary to Beit Yosef).
Because he does not forget them. Although diversion of attention means only frivolous conduct, as stated in siman 44, nevertheless, since it is prohibited to conduct oneself frivolously while wearing them, he remembers them and removes them before Shabbos (Darkei Moshe).
