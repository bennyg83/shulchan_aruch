Close to nightfall. The Peri Megadim inclines to define this as half an hour before twilight, like “close to Minchah,” and the Nehar Shalom writes likewise. The Jerusalem Talmud states that here “close to nightfall” means close to Minchah, namely Minchah Ketanah. Although the other authorities do not cite this Jerusalem Talmud, it is proper at least to be stringent like the Peri Megadim and Nehar Shalom. The Gra likewise shows that “close” does not mean the moment of sunset itself.
In his hand. The Mishnah Berurah explains that this excludes an object inserted into his garment. Even for a craftsman, carrying it that way is considered an unusual manner of carrying, in accordance with Rabbi Meir and the ruling in siman 301:12. The Gra, however, rules like Rabbi Yehudah, that a craftsman is liable on Shabbos even when the object is inserted into his garment; accordingly, he may not go out close to nightfall even in that manner. The Meiri and Bartenura also rule like Rabbi Yehudah. The Gra further writes that the Gemara’s entire discussion whether this is a decree upon a decree applies only close to nightfall. At nightfall itself, meaning immediately before sunset, even an act whose Shabbos prohibition is rabbinic is prohibited. Tefillin are an exception and may remain upon him even at sunset itself.
