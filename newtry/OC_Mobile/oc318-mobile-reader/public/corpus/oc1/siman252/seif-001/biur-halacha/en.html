To sell to a non-Jew and load it for him. This means loading it onto his donkey, as Rashi explains there, even if he sold it to him beforehand. The same applies to lifting the burden onto his shoulder, as stated in the Mishnah there, or to giving him a gift or a loan; see the Gemara there.
While it is still day. If the non-Jew left the object with the Jew and came at night to take it, it appears that in pressing circumstances one may give it to him even if he is not a violent person. Since the prohibition is only because of appearances, one need not be stringent regarding a suspicion of a rabbinic prohibition where no one sees, as we wrote at the end of siman 301, seif katan 165 in the Mishnah Berurah.
