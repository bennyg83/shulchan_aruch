Lest he stoke the coals. I was asked concerning distillers of liquor from grain where, close to Shabbos, the distillation was not yet complete: must one remove the cover from the boiler and thereby suffer a monetary loss, or, since the liquor continues to flow on its own and he performs no action, is there no concern of prohibition? Responsa Panim Meirot, siman 80, concludes that he is not required to remove the cover from the boiler and incur a loss, and that there is no trace of prohibition in this. See there.
One should be stringent. See siman 246, seif 2, where one may be lenient with a loan, but with a sale one should be stringent even in a walled city where the non-Jew resides in the city. Since people know that he sold it to him, they may say that he sold it to him on Shabbos. This does not apply to a loan, and especially with merchandise that is never ordinarily lent, it is certainly prohibited. It is possible that one may be lenient with a coercive non-Jew if he designated a place for him. Magen Avraham. Taz writes: I have seen authorities rule stringently even regarding an article belonging to the non-Jew, and this is reasonable, since the prohibition is based on the observer thinking that he gave it to him in order to take it out. There is no distinction, for the observer does not know to whom the article belongs. Therefore, one should permit it only in a case of great need and loss; and where the non-Jew is coercive, it may be permitted even without designating a place, for the Sages did not decree in such a case. See there. See also Responsa Be’er Esek, siman 30, which permitted returning collateral to a non-Jew on Shabbos through another non-Jew who would take the money lent upon it, because the creditor was coercive and the matter involved a possible danger to life. See there. Olat Tamid writes that if one rented the room to the non-Jew for a perutah for the duration of Shabbos, there is no reason at all to be stringent. See there. Magen Avraham, however, writes that even by renting the room one should not rely on this leniency. See there.
