1) [Seif 1] It is permitted to begin labor on Friday, etc. All this follows Beis Hillel's view, which permits it. The principal reason is that Beis Hillel does not require one's utensils to rest, nor does it impose a decree shortly before nightfall on Friday lest one act after nightfall. Bach.
2) There: Such as soaking ink, etc. On Shabbos this is forbidden because of kneading. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
3) There: And placing bundles of flax, etc. Three explanations have been given. Rashi explains that these are bundles of flax placed in a pot inside the oven so that they become warm and whiten. Hagahos Mordechai writes in the name of Riva that it means flax removed from its soaking pit and placed by the fire to dry, which involves cooking. Aruch explains that it means spun fibers. Bach; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1.
4) There: And placing bundles of flax into the oven, etc. We do not decree lest he stoke the coals, because exposure to the wind is harmful to them and therefore he will not uncover the oven. Gemara; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
5) There: And it is sealed with clay, etc. He will not exert himself to this extent, and he will remember. Bach. Rabbeinu Yerucham, section 64, however, writes that it must be sealed in a manner that leaves no concern that he might stir it. This is also implied by Tosafos, Shabbos 18. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
6) There: Lest he stir it, etc. Even if the dye ingredients have been cooked but the wool has not yet absorbed the color, it is nevertheless forbidden to stir them because of dyeing. When the Shulchan Aruch attributes the prohibition to cooking, it discusses a case in which the dye ingredients have not yet been cooked. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
7) There: Even if it is not on the fire, he is liable, etc. A kli rishon cooks even after it has been removed from the fire, as long as yad soledes bo, as stated below in siman 318, seif 9; see there.
