Into the oven. We do not decree lest he stir the coals, because the wind is harmful to them and he will therefore not uncover the oven (Gemara). See siman 254.
Because of cooking. This implies that if it is fully cooked, it is permitted, as stated in siman 318, seif 18. Tosafot and the Rosh, however, wrote that here it is nevertheless prohibited because of dyeing, since when one stirs it the color is absorbed.
The non-Jew designated a place for it. For then it is regarded as the property of the non-Jew.
One should be stringent. For an observer will nevertheless assume that the Jew instructed him to carry it out. See what I wrote in siman 246, seif 2: regarding lending, one may be lenient in a city surrounded by a wall, but regarding a sale one should be stringent even in a walled city when the non-Jew lives in the city, because one who knows that the Jew is selling it to him will say that he sold it to him on Shabbos. This is unlike lending. A similar distinction is written in siman 244 regarding a field. Particularly with merchandise, such as a linen garment and the like, which is not ordinarily lent at all, it is obvious that this is prohibited. It is possible, however, that in the case of a coercive non-Jew one may be lenient if the Jew designated a place for him.
To be stringent. This is so that the non-Jew not become accustomed to doing so. It appears there in the Hagahot Mordechai that the reason is that the non-Jew has not yet acquired it until he removes it; consequently, it is as though the Jew sells it to him on Shabbos. If, however, he designated a place for him, the non-Jew fully acquires it, and there is no concern regarding carrying out, since we rule that when the poor person extended his hand inside, the householder is exempt and the act is permitted. The Hagahot Mordechai concludes that many disagree and maintain that even rental does not effect acquisition, except with regard to chametz; see there. See also what I wrote in siman 246, seif 3, where we rule accordingly. Even regarding chametz, we rule in siman 440 that designating a place does not suffice, and in siman 347 we likewise rule stringently. One should therefore not rely on this leniency.
