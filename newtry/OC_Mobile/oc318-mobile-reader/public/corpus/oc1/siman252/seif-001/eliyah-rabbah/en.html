Bundles of flax. Meaning combed flax tied in bundles and placed in a pot inside an oven to heat and whiten. We do not decree lest one stir the coals, because wind harms them and he will not uncover the oven.
Sealed with clay, etc. Because he would not trouble himself to break the seal merely to stir, and he will remember Shabbos, as Bach explains. Rabbenu Yerucham, however, writes that it is sealed in a manner that removes the concern that he may stir. Tosafot, Shabbos 18, also imply this.

[4] [Levush:] To take it out, etc. Magen Avraham, however, explains that when people know that he sold it to the non-Jew, they will say that he sold it to him on Shabbos. According to this, it is prohibited even in a walled city. The reason a loan is permitted in siman 246 is that the loan itself entails no prohibition. He concludes as follows: “Especially with merchandise, such as a linen garment and the like, which is never ordinarily lent, it is certainly prohibited. It is possible that one may be lenient with a coercive non-Jew if he designated a place for him.” Taz, however, writes that in the case of a coercive non-Jew it may be permitted even without designating a place, and in a case of great need and loss one may be lenient even where he is not coercive. It is obvious that renting the room has the same status as designating a place. This also appears from Bach and the later authorities, contrary to Olat Tamid.
