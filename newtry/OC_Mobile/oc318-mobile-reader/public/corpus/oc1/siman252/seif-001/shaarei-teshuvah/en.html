Lest he stir the coals. See Baer Heitev and the responsum Panim Meiros cited there. The reason is that the cover of the still is sealed with dough, and the mouth of the oven is closed and thoroughly plastered with clay. If one opens the oven’s seal, the spirits are spoiled. This is the type of case permitted below in siman 254. Therefore, according to all opinions, one may leave the cover in place here rather than suffer a financial loss. No general leniency should be derived from this case where the vessel is not sealed or where exposure to the wind does not cause loss. See Yad Ephraim regarding Panim Meiros’s additional basis for leniency, namely that the droplets produced by condensation constitute a newly created substance; see there.
