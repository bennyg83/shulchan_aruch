Magen Avraham will be discussed, with God’s help, in seif katan 2. The same applies to other materials whose processing requires heat. Stirring a pot hastens and improves the cooking, and therefore constitutes cooking even when the food was already placed on the fire. Where the contents are fully cooked and no further cooking is possible, the prohibition depends on whether stirring improves the mixture or causes another prohibited effect. The Rishonim disagree concerning a pot removed from the fire and concerning food that has not yet reached the degree of maachal ben Derusai. One must therefore distinguish between a vessel on the fire, a first vessel removed from the fire, and a second vessel; between raw, partially cooked, and fully cooked contents; and between merely moving the food and actively mixing it so that the heat is distributed. The Mechaber’s language indicates that actual stirring is forbidden while the food still requires cooking. Even removing a portion with a spoon can resemble stirring when the contents are thick, because the remaining material is displaced and mixed. Where there is no cooking concern, one must still consider dyeing, kneading, or selecting, according to the substance involved. The practical rule is to avoid stirring a pot on the fire and to remove it before serving whenever the food is not unquestionably fully cooked.
