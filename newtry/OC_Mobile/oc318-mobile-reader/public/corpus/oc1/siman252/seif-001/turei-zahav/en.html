Provided that he leaves the entrance of his house. The Rambam, chapter 6, explains that when the non-Jew leaves the Jew's house on Shabbat carrying the Jew's article, it appears as though the Jew lent it to him, gave it as collateral, fixed terms with him, or sold it to him on Shabbat.
If the non-Jew designated a place for it, etc. The Mordechai, in the first chapter of Shabbat, cites Raavyah, who derives the leniency from the statement in the first chapter of Pesachim that if one designated a place for the non-Jew's chametz, he need not remove it. He offers another proof from the Mishnah: if the poor person extended his hand inside and took an object from the householder's hand, the householder is exempt and permitted. He nevertheless says one should be stringent so as not to accustom people to this. Rav Mordechai adds that careful study of the first chapter of Pesachim shows that even rental does not ordinarily effect acquisition; it does so only for chametz by a special scriptural decree. The Rama therefore rules stringently. In my opinion, Raavyah's proofs are difficult. Designating a place in Pesachim proves nothing here, because the present prohibition is based on appearance, as the Rambam explains below. What benefit, then, is gained by designating a place for the non-Jew? His second proof is also inconclusive, for the Rosh writes in the first chapter of Shabbat that the householder certainly violates 'do not place a stumbling block before the blind'; the Mishnah addresses only the laws of carrying on Shabbat. The Mordechai further writes that in Germany he saw authorities prohibit even when the object belonged to the non-Jew, and this is reasonable, because the prohibition rests on the observer's assumption that the Jew gave it to him in order to carry it out, and an observer does not know to whom the object belongs. Therefore one should permit this only in a case of great need or loss. If the non-Jew is violent, however, it is certainly permitted even without designating a place, for the Sages did not impose their decree in such a case.
