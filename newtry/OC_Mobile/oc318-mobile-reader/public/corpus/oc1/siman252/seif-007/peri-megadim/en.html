And not - See Taz, Shabbos 18a, and Tosafot there, s.v. 'and now.' In a case of loss one may rely on the authority who permits it. See Magen Avraham seif katan 21.
Setting up a clock on Shabbos appears from here and from Darkei Moshe to be forbidden on Shabbos. Nevertheless, allowing it to sound is permitted because everyone knows that it was set up the day before. A mill is different, because people may say that the wheat was placed in it today. See siman 338, seif 3; the Rama writes this even according to the authority stringent regarding mills. The clock may be moved. Likewise, a glass timepiece containing sand, called an hourglass, may be turned over on Shabbos so that the sand empties from one side to the other, because he performs no prohibited act. It is not muktzeh, since it was designated for this use and is a utensil permitted for use of its function or place; see siman 308. Whether one may instruct a non-Jew to set it up on Shabbos requires study. It may involve Torah-prohibited labor, and a double rabbinic restriction was permitted for a mitzvah, such as knowing the time for Shema and prayer, but not Torah-prohibited labor. If a non-Jew repaired it on Shabbos for a Jew, it may be forbidden to benefit by listening and learning the time, like a lamp lit for a Jew, even though hearing a sound has no tangible substance. See Responsa Panim Meirot on this matter. With God's help I will explain it in siman 338; there is no room to elaborate here. It will also be addressed, with God's help, in siman 308, seif 11, and in Pri Chadash 123 and siman 338.
