And not - See Taz, Shabbos 18a, and Tosafot there, s.v. 'and now.' In a case of loss one may rely on the authority who permits it. See Magen Avraham seif katan 21.
Setting up a clock on Shabbos appears here and in Darkei Moshe to be forbidden. Nevertheless, allowing it to sound is permitted because everyone knows it was set the previous day. A mill is different, since people may say the wheat was put in today. See siman 338:3; Rema writes this even according to the stringent view regarding mills. The clock may be moved. Likewise, a glass timepiece containing sand, called an hourglass, may be inverted on Shabbos so the sand empties from one side to the other, because no prohibited act is performed. It is not muktzeh, since it was designated for this use and is a utensil permitted for use of its body or place; see siman 308. Whether one may instruct a gentile to set it on Shabbos requires examination. It may involve Torah-prohibited labor; a double rabbinic restriction was permitted for a mitzvah, such as knowing the times for Krias Shema and prayer, but not Torah labor. If a gentile repaired it on Shabbos for a Jew, benefit from listening and learning the time may be forbidden like a lamp lit for a Jew, although sound has no tangible substance. See Responsa Panim Meiros on this matter and, with God's help, the discussion in siman 338. There is no room to elaborate here. It will also be explained in siman 308:11 and Responsa Panim Meiros 123.
