Some prohibit wearing it, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah. Their reasoning applies to every form of labor performed by a non-Jew, as in the Mishnah concerning a ramp made for descending from a ship. According to the prohibiting opinion, this applies even where a price was fixed, as is evident from the authorities and from the Gra at the beginning of siman 276.
Which the non-Jew completed on Shabbos. This applies specifically where the work is prohibited by Torah law. If it is only rabbinically prohibited, one need not be stringent.
Which he needs for Shabbos. This means that he has no other garment for Shabbos. It is therefore unlike the ordinary “need of the festival” discussed regarding Chol HaMoed.
For then one may be lenient. Eliyah Rabbah questions this ruling, for here the Rama appears to rely on the Mechaber’s adoption of the Ran’s view that, where a price was fixed, we do not prohibit benefit from work done on Shabbos, whereas in siman 276:1 the Rama records the same ruling without dissent. The Magen Avraham distinguishes between the two cases, as explained in the Mishnah Berurah. Yet it remains difficult why the Rama there omitted the rationale of the Shiltei Giborim and instead cited the rationale of benefiting from the work, regarding which he is lenient in pressing circumstances where a price was fixed. It is also difficult, according to the Magen Avraham’s distinction, why the Mishnah prohibits use of a ramp or water drawn by a non-Jew. The latter clause appears to address even a fixed-price arrangement, like the earlier case of a lamp, yet those cases resemble wearing a garment, because the non-Jew intends only to complete his own undertaking. Perhaps the Mishnah concerns a case in which it is evident that the work is being done for the Jew, which the Peri Megadim states is prohibited regarding wearing the garment according to all opinions. The Meiri similarly explains the position of the Geonim, who permit wearing garments given to a launderer or hides given to a tanner where a price was fixed, yet prohibit use of a ramp or lamp.
From the craftsman’s house. See the Mishnah Berurah that this is prohibited even from a Jewish craftsman. Where the article is needed for Shabbos, the matter requires study. According to the Nimukei Yosef and the Ran, the concern is appearances, lest people say that he gave it to be repaired during the festival. Perhaps on Shabbos and Yom Tov we do not entertain this suspicion regarding a Jewish craftsman. According to Rashi’s reason, namely exertion, the Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah write that there is no concern where it is needed for Shabbos. But according to the Tosefet Shabbos and Peri Megadim, who explain that bringing it even through a non-Jew is prohibited as weekday conduct, and their case concerns an article needed for Shabbos, it would appear prohibited here as well. The matter requires study.
On Shabbos and Yom Tov. If the Jew does not trust the craftsman to leave the article with him until after Shabbos, the law regarding Chol HaMoed is explained at the end of siman 534. Regarding Shabbos it is not explicit in the authorities. It appears that if the Jew also needs the garment for Shabbos, one may be lenient and have it brought discreetly into his house.
But a non-Jew who works, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah that the Magen Avraham and many other later authorities disagree and maintain that this provides no advantage over any other craftsman, since the Jew goes to his premises. Accordingly, even if the Jew knows with certainty that the work was completed before Friday evening, or even if the craftsman is Jewish, it remains prohibited.
A Jew is permitted, etc. The Beit Yosef cites two opinions from the Hagahot Ashri as to whether an article completed on Yom Tov is muktzeh. The Rama evidently rules like the lenient opinion. See the Biur Halachah to siman 325:10, where we explain why that ruling does not contradict this one.
