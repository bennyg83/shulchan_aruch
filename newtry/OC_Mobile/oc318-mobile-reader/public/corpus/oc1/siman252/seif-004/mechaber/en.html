Whenever one has fixed [a set price with a non-Jew for a task], even though the non-Jew will perform the work on Shabbos, it is permitted for a Jew to wear the article on Shabbos itself, for whoever fixes [a price] acts on his own behalf. {Rama: And some forbid wearing it whenever it is known that the non-Jew finished the work on Shabbos (Hagahos Ashiri, first chapter of Shabbos; Maharil; Beis Yosef in the name of the Rokeach); and one must wait after Shabbos the time it would take to make it (Or Zarua). And so is the practice lechatchilah, unless he needs it on Shabbos, in which case there is room to be lenient; and if there is reason to assume it was finished on Erev Shabbos, it is permitted in any case [Hagahos Alfasi]. And this is specifically where the non-Jew sent it to his house; but it is forbidden to take utensils from the craftsman's house on Shabbos and Yom Tov [Mordechai; Or Zarua; Hagahos Maimoni; Hagahos Ashiri]. And all of this is with articles that he made for a Jew (Beis Yosef); but a non-Jew who makes shoes for sale — it is permitted for a Jew who is his acquaintance to go and take them from him on Shabbos and wear them, provided that he does not fix the purchase price with him [Hagahos Ashiri].}
