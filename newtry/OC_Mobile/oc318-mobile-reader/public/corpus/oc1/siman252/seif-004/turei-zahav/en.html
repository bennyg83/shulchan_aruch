Whenever it is known that the non-Jew completed it, etc. This implies that in an ordinary case of doubt, where it may have been completed before Shabbat, it is permitted, as the Mechaber subsequently writes, 'if it can be attributed, etc.' This is unlike the Rokeach, who prohibits an unspecified case.
All this concerns utensils made for a Jew, but a non-Jew who makes shoes, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that shoes are different, because people do not ordinarily give him the work to perform; rather, he makes them from his own materials and sells them. Therefore, even if he made them on Yom Tov, we are not concerned, because he labored for himself.
