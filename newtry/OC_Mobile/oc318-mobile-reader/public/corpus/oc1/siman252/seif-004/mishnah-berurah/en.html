(26) Whenever he fixed a price, etc. - Provided there was no prohibition in giving him the work, and all the other conditions required for fixed-price work, explained above in seif 2, were also met.
(27) Even though, etc. - This differs from siman 276, seif 1 in the gloss, where a lamp lit by a non-Jew for a Jew may not be used even when the lighting was contracted for a fixed price. Here, although the Jew benefits on Shabbos from the non-Jew's labor by wearing the garment, the non-Jew did not work on Shabbos to provide the Jew with immediate benefit; he acted for his own benefit, to finish his job sooner. There, by contrast, he lit the lamp on Shabbos so that the Jew could use it immediately. Accordingly, if shortly before nightfall the Jew asked the non-Jew, 'Why have you not finished my work?' and the non-Jew replied, 'I will do it for you on Shabbos,' then even according to the Mechaber the Jew may not wear it on Shabbos, just as in the case of the lamp [Peri Megadim].
(28) Some prohibit, etc. - Their reason is that a Jew may not benefit on Shabbos from labor performed for him, under any circumstances.
(29) The time needed for it to be done - So that he should not benefit from Shabbos labor. Accordingly, on the two festival days observed in the Diaspora, if a non-Jew performed the work on the first day, it becomes permitted on the second day after the amount of time needed to do it, whichever day is holy: if the first is holy, the second is weekday; if the first is weekday, the work was certainly permitted. In all this there is no distinction between the person for whom the work was done and others. Eliyah Rabbah is lenient for others when a price was fixed.
(30) If it can be attributed, etc. - For some authorities permit it in every case. If the shoe was already completed before Shabbos and the non-Jew merely repaired or polished it on Shabbos, it is permitted, because it could have been worn without that work, as stated in siman 253, seif 1, gloss 4. The same applies to linen garments that a non-Jew placed under a press on Shabbos.
(31) To take articles - Even when he certainly knows that they were completed before Shabbos or Yom Tov, and even when they are needed for Shabbos. The reason is marit ayin, for people may say that the craftsman made them on Shabbos at the Jew's direction. The Levush states that even wearing them in the non-Jew's house, without carrying them away by hand, is forbidden for this reason. The Magen Avraham writes that perhaps even having a non-Jew bring them is forbidden, because it is a weekday activity.
(32) From the craftsman's house - There is no distinction between a Jewish and a non-Jewish craftsman.
(33) But a non-Jew who makes, etc. - He works on Shabbos for his own commercial benefit. The Rama maintains that the prohibition against bringing an item from a craftsman's house does not apply here, even when the Jew goes to him, because everyone knows that the non-Jew regularly makes such items for sale, and no one will think the Jew instructed him to make them. The Magen Avraham and many other later authorities disagree and maintain that even here one may not go and take the item from him, just as from any other craftsman. Once the non-Jew brings it to the Jew's house, however, it may be worn, for in a city whose majority is non-Jewish he presumably did not make it specifically for the Jew, as explained in siman 515. If the non-Jew is a shopkeeper rather than a craftsman, perhaps there is room for leniency, though the Tosefet Shabbos and Peri Megadim question this as at least a weekday activity.
(34) He should not fix a price with him - Meaning, he should not mention a sum of money, for otherwise it falls under buying and selling even though he does not pay immediately.
