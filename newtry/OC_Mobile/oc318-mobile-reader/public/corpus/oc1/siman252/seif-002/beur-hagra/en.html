Or when he performs it; and some disagree if... Their dispute will be explained below.
This applies. As stated above in siman 247, seif 1.
And also. This is from the Jerusalem Talmud, as stated above in siman 244.
It is forbidden on Friday. This follows his position in siman 247, seif 1. According to the Shulchan Aruch it is forbidden in every case, and therefore it states without qualification that it is permitted specifically when he fixed a price.
If one saw him... if. This is from the Jerusalem Talmud. From here Beit Yosef proved that work performed in exchange for mere goodwill is permitted only if the Jew sees the non-Jew on Shabbos, as stated there; otherwise, how could he leave the item in the non-Jew’s possession on Shabbos? This applies specifically according to Beit Yosef’s position that, when no price was fixed, it is always forbidden. According to Rama’s position, however, that it is permitted when given on Wednesday or Thursday, one may say that he gave it to him on Wednesday or Thursday. This is why Rama writes, “Even if he gave it to him...” He writes this lest one infer from his earlier statement, “Some disagree if he performs...”
