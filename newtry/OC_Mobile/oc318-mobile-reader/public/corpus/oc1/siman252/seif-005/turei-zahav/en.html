And the oil and wine that flow from them are permitted. This includes liquid that emerges on Shabbat. We do not decree lest one squeeze them, as we do concerning liquids that flow from produce, because there squeezing can incur a sin-offering and the Sages therefore prohibited even liquid that flows by itself. Here, however, the produce already releases liquid naturally because it was crushed by the beam; manual squeezing merely causes additional liquid to emerge, and that added act is only rabbinically prohibited. Liquid that flows by itself on Shabbat is therefore entirely permitted. The Tur, however, permits the emerging wine and oil only where the produce was first crushed and then placed under the beam before Shabbat until it was thoroughly pounded. The Beit Yosef understands the Tur to permit even completing the process by hand in such a case, following Tosafot. His proof is the Tur's ruling at the end of siman 321: garlic and unripe grapes crushed before Shabbat may not have their pounding completed on Shabbat if they still require pounding, but if they require only grinding, one may complete it on Shabbat. This is difficult. If manual completion were permitted, why does the Tur say here only that the liquids which flow are permitted, implying that direct action is not? Likewise, in siman 320 he writes that if they were thoroughly crushed before Shabbat, the liquids that flow from them are permitted, again implying that one may not act by hand. It therefore appears that the Tur does not follow Tosafot on this point, since the Rosh did not record the leniency of manual completion. The Tur's phrase 'he may complete it' at the end of siman 321 does not mean by hand; he uses the Talmudic expression. Rashi explains that after crushing and piling them under a weight, he may place them in a vessel and allow the liquid to emerge by itself. 'He may not complete it' means that one may not place them under their weight before Shabbat in order for the process to finish after nightfall. This is also the correct interpretation of the Tur.
And we are not concerned about producing sound. This follows Rav Yosef. Those who prohibit rule like Rabbah, who says that even Beit Hillel prohibit it. Although Beit Hillel do not require one's utensils to rest on Shabbat, the sound produced is nevertheless prohibited because it demeans Shabbat.
