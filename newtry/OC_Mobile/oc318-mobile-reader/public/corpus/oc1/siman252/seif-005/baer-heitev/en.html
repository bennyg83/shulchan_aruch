Noise. This also entails a degradation of Shabbos. If the mill belongs to a non-Jew, it is plainly permitted to place grain into it before nightfall. If a Jew gives wheat to a non-Jew before Shabbos, it is permitted even if the non-Jew grinds it on Shabbos, as stated in seif 2, provided that the Jew does not stand beside him, for then it appears that the non-Jew is acting as his agent. Where circumstances require it, however, such as shortly before Pesach, the Jew may watch the mill to ensure that the grain does not become chametz. This is a shvut of a shvut, for even if the Jew placed grain into a mill on Shabbos, he would not incur a sin-offering unless he ground it with a hand mill. This also appears from Tosafot, Shabbos 18a, s.v. “Let the master say,” who explain that Beit Shammai’s reason is that we decree, etc. One might ask: even regarding a mill, why not state that the reason is a decree? It must therefore be that even placing grain into it on Shabbos does not incur a sin-offering. These are the words of Magen Avraham; see there. He was not precise in this, for the Gemara could not have explained it as a decree, since the first clause of the baraita, concerning incense, would then be difficult: why is that permitted? Maharsha writes likewise. See there and examine carefully. Eliyah Rabbah likewise questions Magen Avraham on this point and concludes: nevertheless, as a matter of law it appears to me that one may watch it even when no mitzvah is involved, as stated above at the end of siman 244.
