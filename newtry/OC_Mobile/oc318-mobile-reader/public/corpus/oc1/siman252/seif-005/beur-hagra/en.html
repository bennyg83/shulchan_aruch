And the oil... This is stated there regarding the oil of the press workers, following Tosafot’s explanation there, s.v. “Shemen.”
Likewise, unripe grapes. Meaning, even when they still require pounding, for the Gemara compares them to the Mishnah and states, “These too...” This rejects Tosafot’s first answer there, s.v. “Rabbi Yishmael,” and follows their second answer, as stated above in siman 321, seif 19, and in the baraita there concerning olives and grapes. The same applies to garlic that was crushed, and to the Mishnah permitting one to load a press, since the Gemara compares all of these cases with the Mishnah concerning loading. See below, siman 321, seif 19. Sefer HaTerumah, Semag, and Semak, however, rule according to Tosafot’s first answer there, and when pounding is still required the liquid is forbidden.
It is permitted... This follows Rav Yosef, since Rabbi Oshaya supports his position. See Tosafot there, s.v. “VeLeima” and s.v. “VeHashta.” See also Hagahot HaGra, seif katan 60, which cites Rabbah; that citation is an error.
Some prohibit... This follows Rabbah. See Tosafot there, s.v. “VeHashta...” Their further statement that “the course of the sugya...” means what they wrote in s.v. “VeLeima...”: “It appears...” So too the Tosefta states: Beit Shammai said to Beit Hillel, “Do you not concede...” Rather, Beit Shammai derive from “Six days shall you labor, and you shall perform all your work” that all one’s work must be completed before Shabbos, whereas Beit Hillel derive from “Six days shall work be performed” that one may cause it to be performed throughout all six days. It follows that in the Mishnah they do not dispute the resting of utensils, but rather the issue explained by Rabbah and by the course of the sugya.
