It is permitted to open water into a garden so that it continues to flow throughout Shabbos; to place a thick collyrium [a medicinal salve] upon the eye, even though it is forbidden to place it [there] on Shabbos [itself]; to place incense beneath garments so that they become perfumed of themselves throughout Shabbos — and even where the incense is placed in a vessel, for a person is not commanded concerning the resting of his vessels; to place barley in a tub to soak; and one may load the beams of the olive press and the winepress upon olives and grapes while it is still day, and the oil and wine that flow from them are permitted [and see below, siman 320, se'if 2]. Likewise, unripe grapes and ears of grain that one crushed while it was still day — the liquids that flow from them are permitted. And it is permitted to place wheat into a water-mill close to nightfall. {Rama: And we are not concerned about the producing of sound — that people will say "so-and-so's mill is grinding on Shabbos." And some forbid regarding mills and in any place where there is concern about producing sound (Tur; Tosafos; Rosh, third chapter of Shabbos; Semag; Sefer HaTerumah; Hagahos Maimoni; Responsa of Mahari Weil, siman 130; Agur), and so is the practice lechatchilah; however, in a case of loss there is room to be lenient, as explained above at the end of siman 244. And it is permitted to set a weight-driven clock, called a zeiger, on Erev Shabbos, even though it produces sound to announce the hours on Shabbos, for everyone knows that it is customary to set it from the day before (Tur; Agur); and see below, siman 338.}
