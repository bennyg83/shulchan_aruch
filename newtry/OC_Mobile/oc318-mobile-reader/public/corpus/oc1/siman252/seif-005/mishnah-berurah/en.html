(35) To open, etc. - He opens the water near nightfall from the edge of the spring so that it will flow into the garden and irrigate it.
(36) And to place - Even though it remains there on Shabbos and thereby continues healing. Likewise, one may then place a bandage on a wound [Gemara].
(37) A thick eye-salve - A thin, liquid eye-salve may be applied even on Shabbos itself if it was soaked before nightfall, as explained below in siman 328, seif 21; see there.
(38) Which is forbidden, etc. - Lest he smooth it.
(39) To place incense - Frankincense and other spices are placed on the fire, and garments are fumigated so that they emit a fragrance [Rashi].
(40) Even incense, etc. - Certainly when the incense is resting on the ground it is permitted.
(41) To soak them - Doing so on Shabbos is forbidden, as explained below in siman 336, seif 11; see there.
(42) And they load the press, etc. - Even if the produce was not previously crushed, one may load the press because the process begins before sunset. Regarding the oil and wine that emerge, however, the Magen Avraham writes that they are permitted only if the press was loaded sufficiently early for the produce to be crushed before Shabbos by the beam's pressure. In that case, even squeezing it by hand afterward on Shabbos would not incur a sin-offering, and the spontaneously emerging liquid is permitted because there is no basis for a decree lest one squeeze. If he loads it immediately before sunset, so that the produce will not be crushed before Shabbos by the beams, the emerging liquid is forbidden lest he squeeze it.
(43) The oil and wine that emerge, etc. - Meaning, even what emerges on Shabbos. We do not decree lest he squeeze, as we do concerning liquids that flow from olives and grapes not previously crushed, below in siman 320, seif 1. There, squeezing would incur a sin-offering, so the Sages prohibited even liquid that flowed by itself. Here, the fruit has already been crushed by the pressure, and the liquid would flow by itself; manual squeezing merely causes more to emerge and is only rabbinically forbidden. Therefore liquid that flows by itself on Shabbos is entirely permitted. The same reasoning applies to unripe grapes and partially ripened ears that were crushed. Biur Halachah explains that permission applies only when they were also pounded before Shabbos, pounding being more extensive than crushing.
(44) Likewise unripe grapes - At their early stage, when they are small, liquid is extracted from them for dipping meat because it is sharp and close to fermentation.
(45) And partially ripened ears - Ears of grain not fully ripe are crushed and weighted with stones; liquid flows from them and is used for dipping.
(46) To place wheat, etc. - Likewise, he may himself open the water gate before Shabbos, thereby causing the mill to grind.
(47) Water-powered - Not animal-powered, because a person is commanded that his animal rest.
(48) In every place - This includes every similar device that makes a conspicuous noise, creating publicity and degradation of Shabbos, as Darkei Moshe writes. If the mill belongs to a non-Jew, it is certainly permitted to place grain in it before nightfall, and likewise if it was rented to a non-Jew. If the Jew gave the grain to a non-Jew before nightfall, then when a price was fixed the non-Jew may grind it on Shabbos, because he acts for his own benefit. The Jew should not stand beside him, however, because he then appears to be his agent, as stated at the end of siman 307. For Pesach needs, the Jew may stand there to ensure that it does not become chametz. He may also appoint a guard to prevent theft, provided he does not discuss any business with the non-Jew, as written above at the end of siman 244 [later authorities].
(49) In a case of loss, etc. - Then one may rely on the first opinion, which permits placing it there before nightfall. On Shabbos itself, however, one may not put it in even through a non-Jew, even if the non-Jew is his employee. See above, siman 243, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 10, that he should execute a bill of sale to the non-Jew, selling it to him for Shabbos.
(50) For everyone knows, etc. - They will not suspect that he set it up on Shabbos. This proves that setting it up on Shabbos is forbidden. Even instructing a non-Jew to set it up on Shabbos for a mitzvah purpose, to know the time for the recitation of Shema and prayer, requires study, for it may involve Torah-prohibited labor, which may not be performed through a non-Jew even for a mitzvah [Peri Megadim, who discusses this at length]. See below in siman 338, Mishnah Berurah, where this will be addressed.
