It is permitted, and likewise, etc. The Mechaber’s unqualified language implies that he follows the authorities who permit the liquid that emerges even where the grapes were not crushed beforehand, and likewise unripe grapes and ears of grain that were merely crushed but still require pounding. There are three stages: crushing, pounding with a utensil, and then fine grinding. The stringency in siman 321:19, where the item still requires pounding, concerns actively performing the operation by hand on Shabbos. Here, by contrast, the issue is only liquid that emerges on its own. Eliyah Rabbah, however, rules in accordance with the other authorities that the liquid is permitted only if the produce was pounded before Shabbos. The Gra also appears inclined to this view. One should therefore not permit liquid emerging from unripe grapes or ears of grain unless they were pounded before Shabbos. Likewise, where a beam is placed upon them, they must first have been crushed, so that the pressure of the beam may be regarded as the pounding stage.
Unripe grapes and ears of grain. The Magen Avraham asks why the Tur omitted garlic, which is mentioned in the Gemara. He answers that squeezing garlic does not incur a sin-offering even if it was not crushed before Shabbos, because Torah liability for squeezing applies only to olives and grapes; therefore the Sages did not prohibit liquid that emerges from garlic. The garlic mentioned in siman 321:19 concerns completing its grinding by hand. This is difficult according to Tosafot’s first explanation, which understands the dispute of Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Akiva as concerning whether one may allow the squeezing to finish and then eat it on Shabbos, for there garlic that had been crushed is explicitly mentioned. Machatzis HaShekel already raises this objection. The Gra expressly writes that the same rule applies to crushed garlic. The reason appears to be that although squeezing garlic is not prohibited by Torah law, liquid that emerges remains prohibited if the garlic was not crushed beforehand. Tosafot’s statement that wherever there is no liability to a sin-offering the liquid is permitted refers to a case where, by Torah law, the squeezing process was already completed before Shabbos; there is then no decree lest one squeeze. But where squeezing did not begin at all before Shabbos, the Sages did not distinguish between produce whose squeezing is biblically prohibited and produce whose squeezing is only rabbinically prohibited. Strawberries and pomegranates are likewise only rabbinically prohibited to squeeze, yet their liquid is prohibited when they were designated for juice. When the Sages prohibited liquid emerging from olives and grapes, they included anything somewhat intended for squeezing. Garlic in this context is comparable to strawberries and pomegranates, and the same applies to ears of grain.
Because of the production of sound. The Magen Avraham suggests that placing wheat into a watermill on Shabbos may not incur a sin-offering, because the grinding then occurs automatically, and he cites Tosafot. All the later authorities reject his proof, and no support for it appears in the Semag, Semak, or Rambam. They agree with the Magen Avraham’s own proof from trapping: one who spreads a net and the animal enters and is caught at that moment is liable although the result follows automatically. The same is true of baking, where the bread is brought to the fire and the fire thereafter completes the process. So too, although the grinding is completed automatically, the person is liable. This discussion concerns a case where the water-gate is already open and he places wheat into the hopper, so that grinding begins immediately. If other grain is already beneath it, whether adding more creates Torah liability requires study. If, however, he opens the water-gate on Shabbos, it appears that even the Magen Avraham would agree that he is liable, for grinding begins immediately and his act directly causes the waterwheel and millstones to move. This is comparable to turning the millstones with his own force, for the first water released through his act certainly grinds at least the measure of a dried fig. The Tiferet Yisrael writes that with a windmill there is no sin-offering according to all opinions because the warning is uncertain, since the wind may stop. This is unconvincing, for even if the wind stops immediately, the millstones’ existing momentum will certainly grind at least the requisite measure.
And this is the accepted practice. The Gra cites proof for the stringent opinion from the ruling that we follow Rabbah, who is concerned about the production of sound. This proof also appears in the Rashba. Nevertheless, the Darchei Moshe implies that where the practice is lenient, one should not protest.
