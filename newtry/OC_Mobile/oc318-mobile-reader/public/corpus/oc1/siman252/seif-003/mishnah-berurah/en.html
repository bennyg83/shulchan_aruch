(24) If it was, etc. - Hides given for tanning and garments given for laundering are ordinarily not recognizable as belonging to a Jew. Therefore, when a price was fixed, the Jew need not protest even if the non-Jew tans or launders them publicly. If, however, the work is known to belong to a Jew and is performed openly before everyone, he must protest against its being done on Shabbos, because an observer does not know that a price was fixed and may suspect that the non-Jew is a day laborer, as explained above in siman 244, seif 1. See there in seif 4, where the Shulchan Aruch maintains that this is forbidden by law, and Biur Halachah there, s.v. 'its law.'
(25) And it is known that it belongs, etc. - Accordingly, shirts recognizable as belonging to a Jew, when laundered by a non-Jew at a river, a public and conspicuous place, require the Jew to protest if he sees him on Shabbos [Magen Avraham]. The Tosefet Shabbos writes that suspicion does not apply because it is not known whose garments they are. The Chayei Adam is even more lenient for another reason: since everyone knows that the townspeople customarily give garments to a launderer as contracted work, even public laundering is permitted, because no one will suspect employment by the day, similar to siman 243, seif 2. The same applies to all articles ordinarily given as contracted work. It is forbidden only when the work is done in the Jew's house, where no other permission is effective, as stated above in seif katan 17.
