There In seif katan 20, Magen Avraham writes that there is no liability to bring a sin-offering. See Even HaOzer below, siman 328, who rejects all of his proofs and concludes that there is liability to bring a sin-offering; the practical ruling therefore requires study. Accordingly, in a pressing situation close to Pesach, I customarily instruct that the wheat and all the utensils be sold to the non-Jewish owner of the mill. They tell him that if he protects them from leavening, they may buy the flour back from him after Shabbos. See below, siman 307:3. A Jew may remain present to supervise; see Shach, Yoreh Deah, siman 120, and this is readily understood. As for the objection to Magen Avraham’s proof from Tosafos, I have already answered it well in Tosafos Shabbos. Tosafos means that Rabbah should have said that with labor performed automatically, such as a mill, Beis Hillel agrees with Beis Shammai, because there is greater room for error and therefore greater reason to decree on Friday because of Shabbos, lest people say that no sin-offering liability exists. Since Rabbah did not say this, it follows that there is indeed no sin-offering liability. Still more persuasive is what I wrote in the margin of the small Shulchan Aruch, in Shaarei Teshuvah, which is close to the explanation of Machatzis HaShekel. Opening a water channel into a garden also entails no sin-offering liability merely because the water fills the garden all day. Liability applies only when one actually irrigates seeds. See Rashi’s wording in Moed Katan 2b. Merely filling the garden channels with water involves neither plowing nor sowing. Magen Avraham’s position is also supported by the Yerushalmi, according to Rashba’s explanation in his novellae, which indicates that the reason follows Rabbah, namely a decree lest one come to perform the act on Shabbos. The Yerushalmi then concludes that according to Beis Shammai one may open water into a garden, etc., but may not place wheat in a mill because of the sound it produces. This shows that there was no reason to decree against placing wheat in a mill on account of Torah-level labor on Shabbos, because even if done on Shabbos itself it would not involve a Torah prohibition. One must distinguish baking in an oven, for this was the manner of the labor in the Mishkan: all cooking and baking continued automatically. Grinding in the Mishkan, by contrast, was performed with a hand mill; likewise, trapping was performed directly, not through setting a trap. Therefore, whenever the labor occurs automatically one is exempt, as Korban Nesanel also wrote. This removes Even HaOzer’s proof in siman 328 from winnowing with the assistance of the wind, because that was precisely how winnowing was performed in the Mishkan, whereas water mills differ from the hand mills used there. His proof from trapping a deer is likewise no difficulty. Since the deer runs because a dog is chasing it, the person who stands before it and blocks its path is himself trapping it directly, like a deer inside a house when a person sits at the doorway, for which one is liable on account of trapping.
