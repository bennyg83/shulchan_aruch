It is proper to be stringent and prohibit it. This means that if the Jew sees him doing it on Shabbos, he must protest even where a price was fixed. Initially, however, he may give it to him, and we do not suspect that he will perform it on Shabbos. Accordingly, garments that are recognizably Jewish-owned and that non-Jews wash at the river require protest when the Jew sees the work being done on Shabbos. This applies specifically to an object upon which the Jew's name is not attached, but a report has spread that it was made for a Jew, as stated at the end of siman 664. Where the Jew's name is attached to the object, it is prohibited by law, as stated in siman 244, seif 4. The truth, however, points to the conclusion that here and in siman 244 the authorities intended the same rule. The Beit Yosef's formulation here, that one should be stringent, therefore requires study.
