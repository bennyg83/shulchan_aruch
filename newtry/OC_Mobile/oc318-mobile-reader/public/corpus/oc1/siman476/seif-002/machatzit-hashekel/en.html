(א) (Siman kaf bet) Coarse eating, etc. Bach siman 477 seif 5 — in truth these are Tosafot's words in Pesachim 107b s.v. dilema, etc.; only Tosafot wrote their words regarding Pesach. And Bach added that also for afikoman, which is a commemoration of Pesach, the law is the same.

(ב) Beit Yosef wrote, etc. 'and transgressors will stumble in them,' and 'if he finished,' etc. — apparently this 'if he finished' is an explanation of Rokeach's words; for in brief Rokeach's words oppose the Gemara in Nazir 23a.

(ג) For Rava said in Bach in the name of R' Yehuda: 'when the righteous walk in them they succeed and transgressors stumble in them' — a parable of two people who roasted their Pesach offerings: one ate for the sake of mitzvah and one for coarse eating — this, etc., and this one who ate for coarse eating — 'transgressors will stumble in them.' R' Levi said to him: you call this one wicked? Even though he did not perform the mitzvah optimally, he nevertheless performed Pesach. Rather the parable is of two people: this one's wife and sister with him, etc. — if so Rokeach holds afikoman has the law of Pesach; nevertheless for Pesach too we say if they ate for coarse eating he is not called a transgressor, only he did not perform the mitzvah optimally; and one must say he holds as Tosafot wrote there and in Pesachim 107, who challenged the above Gemara difficulty — we say in Yoma one who eats coarse eating in the Temple courtyard is exempt, thus it is not called eating. And in Nazir it implies in any case he fulfilled Pesach; and one can answer there are two types of coarse eating — one disgusting in eating, and that is Yoma's case where it is not called eating; and Nazir deals with one not desirous at all, but nevertheless has taste and did not finish — therefore he fulfilled; only he did not perform the mitzvah optimally. And one must say Rokeach's intent in 'let it not be,' etc., that we say in Nazir, etc., 'transgressors,' etc. — behold for coarse eating they are called transgressors; and R' Levi rejected and said nevertheless he performed Pesach — specifically such a case where he did not finish; but where he finished, as proven in Yoma that it is not called eating, 'transgressors,' etc., as the Gemara holds there. And one must say he holds the same in Nazir at the end, that coarse eating there too is when he finished, and properly called transgressor, as Maharsha wrote in Pesachim, see there. However one can say Rokeach holds like Tosafot's second version in Pesachim — that which we say in Nazir he is not a transgressor and nevertheless performed Pesach, because eating of Pesach offerings is not me'akev; if so for matza whose eating is me'akev and whose performance has no mitzvah except eating — he is certainly a transgressor; see Chok Yaakov.
