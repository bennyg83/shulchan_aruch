Even veal and poultry, etc. The Tur concludes in the name of Avi Ezri that it is certainly a proper custom to eat cooked meat in a place where the custom is not to eat roasted meat. The Beit Yosef gives a strained explanation of what makes this custom proper. It appears to me that Avi Ezri intends to exclude the practice of certain stringent individuals who refrain from eating any meat at all on Pesach night, lest others mistakenly say that they are eating roasted meat. He teaches that this is not a proper stringency, because it would diminish the joy of Yom Tov. It is therefore preferable to eat cooked meat, and we are not concerned that people will err on this account. In siman 486 the Tur mentions the foreleg of a lamb.
Eggs, as a remembrance of mourning. In siman 473, the Beit Yosef cites the Kol Bo that the egg taken as one of the two cooked foods is also a remembrance of mourning. I already wrote there that if foods are roasted, eggs too would seem to be forbidden, as stated by the Mordechai in the chapter Kol Sha'ah in the name of the Yerushalmi: Rava asked before R' Ami whether the prohibition includes even veal or poultry. They initially thought to say that it includes even an egg and even kolyas ha'ispanin, a type of fish. R' Yudan bar Chanan said that it applies only to a species requiring shechita. End quote. This implies that according to the conclusion, eggs and fish are permitted, and only a species requiring shechita is forbidden. The initial view considered forbidding even roasted eggs and fish, but the halacha does not follow that view. This is also implied by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, which prohibit only a species requiring shechita. I therefore wondered at the Maharshal, who writes that even a roasted egg is forbidden, as stated in the Mordechai. How can he bring proof from an initial suggestion that was not retained in the conclusion? It therefore appears that there is neither a prohibition nor a custom against eating a roasted egg.
