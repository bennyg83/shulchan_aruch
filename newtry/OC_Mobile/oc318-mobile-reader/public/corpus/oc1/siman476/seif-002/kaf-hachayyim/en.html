2) There. On the nights of Pesach. The plural 'nights' includes the second night, which is observed like the first. Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, subsection 1. Regarding the daytime, Responsa Beis Yehudah, siman 51, writes that in a place with the prohibitive custom, it applies both at night and by day; an exact parallel to the time of eating the Pesach offering is unnecessary. He concludes that he received this tradition from his teacher: for us, roasted meat is prohibited throughout the first two days, both at night and by day, and this is how people should act and be instructed. See there. He writes likewise on page 108c, siman 15. Chasdei David, in his commentary to the Tosefta, chapter 2 of Beitzah, agrees. See there. Birkei Yosef, subsection 1; Machazik Berachah, subsection 1; Chaim Sha'al, volume 2, siman 38, subsection 89; and Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1, cite this. Nevertheless, we already wrote above in siman 473, subsection 63, that several later authorities agree with Magen Avraham, and this is also Rema's opinion in Darkei Moshe, that roasted meat may be eaten during the day. See there. The Mishnah's use of 'nights of Pesach' likewise implies that even where the custom is not to eat it, the prohibition applies only at night. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 3, likewise writes that it is permitted by day everywhere, because the Pesach offering is eaten only at night. We do not issue a daytime decree because of the night when the entire prohibition is merely customary. He concludes that this is the custom. Nehar Shalom, subsection 2, likewise writes that the unqualified language of all the authorities proves that it is permitted by day. He writes that Beis Yehudah's proofs are not decisive. Even if a place adopted a prohibitive custom, he concludes, one might not rule leniently there; however, one certainly should not institute a prohibition in a new city, and all the more so where the custom is permissive, because the law depends upon local custom. See there. The custom in Izmir is not to eat roasted meat on the first two nights of Pesach and on the first day only; it is eaten on the second day, as Batei Knessiyos writes in this siman. See there. Ruach Chaim, subsection 1.
