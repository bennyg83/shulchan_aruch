(1) Not to eat etc.: And in these lands they do not practice eating roast on both nights, and even pot-roast [meaning that which was roasted in a pot without water or any liquid, but cooks in the juices that leave it], even though it is not similar to the roasting of the Pesach offering, for a Pesach that was roasted in a pot is invalid; even so, one should forbid it because of appearances, lest people err and permit fire-roast as well. And even if one initially cooked it in water and afterward made it into pot-roast, one should forbid it for this reason. However, for the need of a mildly ill person, one may be lenient in this. And if it was roasted and afterward cooked, it is permitted for everyone.
(2) A sheep: Whether a lamb of sheep or a kid of goats.
(3) Entirely as one, as one: That is, its head upon its legs and upon its innards, as with the Pesach offering.
(4) And if it was cut up, or something was missing, etc.: That is to say, that he roasted it when it was cut up, or even when it was still joined, only that one limb had been cut from it before the roasting, and at the time of roasting he placed it beside it and roasted them together.
(5) Or he boiled etc., and it is joined: That is to say, and afterward he roasted all of it together; since it is not similar to the roasting of the Pesach offering, this is permitted in all such cases.
(6) Gross eating: That is, when he has no desire at all to eat; then he is not performing the mitzvah in the choicest manner. For even though the afikoman is a remembrance of the Pesach offering, and the Pesach is eaten when satiated, that is, when he is already full, and therefore they eat the afikoman after finishing the meal, as will be explained in siman 477; nevertheless, he needs to have some desire to eat, and if not, this is not the choicest manner. And if he is so full that his soul is disgusted by eating from excessive satiety, even if he forces himself to eat, he does not fulfill his obligation at all in this mitzvah, for such gross eating is not called eating at all. The poskim wrote: let the eating of the afikoman not be a burden upon him, for through that the mitzvah is not fulfilled in the choicest manner.
(7) And he goes to sleep immediately: And it is taught in the Tosefta: a person is obligated to occupy himself with the laws of Pesach all night.
