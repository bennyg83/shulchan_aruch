On the nights of Pesach. See in Kenesses HaGedolah and in Olas Shabbos what they copied in the name of a responsum of Mahariv; and their words are puzzling, and I also did not find thus in the words of Mahariv. Perhaps it is a printing error, and his intent is upon what was written above in siman 473, s.k. 17, in his name: that the roasted meat that they bring to the table as a remembrance of the Pesach offering, that roast they do not eat even in a place where they practiced to eat roast.
They do not eat. And the Magen Avraham wrote: even pot-roast (and Nachalas Tzvi permits pot-roast), and even if he cooked and afterward roasted it as pot-roast, one should forbid it because of appearances; but if he roasted and afterward cooked, it is permitted. And he wrote the Levush: And in these lands they do not practice eating roast.
A sheep roasted whole. And it is explained in the Gemara, chapter Makom SheNahagu, that one who transgressed and accustomed his generation to eat roasted kids prepared like the Pesach on Pesach nights, they put him in niddui; as they sent to Todos: Were you not Todos, I would decree niddui upon you; as is explained there. And it is proven in this sugya that sometimes even one who is fit to be put in niddui, it is permitted to show him favor so as not to put him in niddui if he is a great Torah scholar, as with Todos, or if he supplies merchandise for the sake of Torah scholars, or even a powerful man, as is stated there in the Gemara (and see Yoreh De'ah, siman 157). And all this is when he has repented from his sin; but when they sent to him and warned him and he still stands in his rebellion, they put him in niddui without showing favor. And sometimes for a great transgression they put him in niddui in secret, as is stated in Moed Katan, folio 17, regarding that powerful man; see there. And I elaborated on all this in my responsum to the land of Moravia.
That one should not eat the afikoman on gross eating. For he does not perform the mitzvah in the choicest manner; but nevertheless after the fact he has fulfilled, as is stated in Nazir, folio 53, regarding a Pesach offering that he ate by way of gross eating: granted that he did not perform the mitzvah in the choicest manner, nevertheless he did perform the Pesach. And Tosafos wrote: and it is difficult, for in the final chapter of Yoma it is implied that gross eating is not called eating; and Rabbeinu Tam says that there are two kinds of gross eating: when his soul is disgusted by eating from his excessive satiety, it is not called eating at all; and here, where his soul is not disgusted, only that he is not hungry to eat with appetite; end quote. And the Rokeach, siman 283, wrote, and these are his words: When he eats the afikoman, it should not be a burden upon him, as we say in Nazir: if he ate the matzah by way of gross eating, concerning him it is said, And the sinners will stumble over them (what it says matzah should read Pesach; also, what it says concerning him it is said, And the sinners will stumble over them, does not stand in the conclusion, but rather that he did not perform the mitzvah in the choicest manner; and examine carefully). And if he ate actual gross eating, he has not fulfilled; end quote. It appears that his view is to distinguish in a different manner between one who ate for the sake of gross eating versus actual gross eating. And see above, siman 612, it is implied that the view of the poskim is like the view of Tosafos. And see above, siman 471, se'if 2, it is explained that also on the second night one must take care to eat with appetite.
