(Siman kaf alef) To eat, etc. See in the Gemara Pesachim 41a: regarding roasted in a pot there is a dispute between R' Yehuda and the Rabbis on the Pesach offering whether if they made it roasted in a pot one is lashed for eating it; but for the positive commandment all agree one violates, as it is written 'roasted in fire' and not by heat of something else. And if they roasted and afterward cooked, or cooked and afterward roasted — all agree one is lashed. And Rambam ruled whether roasted and afterward cooked, or cooked and afterward roasted, or roasted in a pot — one is lashed; see in Kesef Mishneh. If so, one could think: if cooked and afterward roasted, or roasted in a pot, one may permit eating on Pesach, for there is no concern lest they say it is Pesach, for such in Pesach is forbidden; therefore Magen Avraham wrote there is no such thing — even if there are two preparations, cooked and afterward roasted and that roasting was roasted in a pot, nevertheless it is forbidden, for there is marit ayin lest onlookers not know it is roasted in a pot, and all the more if they do not know it was cooked first; but roasted and afterward cooked, since now it appears cooked, there is no marit ayin — therefore permitted. However for a sick person or elder one may permit this — not specifically where there are two preparations; one suffices: cooked and afterward roasted, even spit-roast, or roasted in a pot even if not cooked first. But Peri Chadash disagrees and holds the Rabbis did not distinguish, and anything under the name 'roasted' is forbidden; if so roasted and afterward cooked is also forbidden, for it too is called roasted — specifically by the offering where a verse limits what is not called roasted, but generally 'roasted' is called and forbidden.
