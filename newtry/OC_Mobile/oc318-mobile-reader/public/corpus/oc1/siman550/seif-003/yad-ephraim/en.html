(In Magen Avraham, s.k. 6) One does not say viduy at Minchah, etc. The language of Abudraham: and if Asarah B'Teves falls on Friday, they pray Shacharis like the other fasts, and likewise at Minchah, but they do not say viduy and nefilat apayim, because it is erev Shabbos, end quote. This implies that the sheliach tzibbur says Aneinu between Go'el and Rofei; and so it is implied in siman 470 in Magen Avraham, that he does not disagree with Kenesses HaGedolah except because in any case there is no communal fast; and greater than this is the view of Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, siman 208, that if there are ten fasting Torah scholars on Shabbos they say Aneinu; and even though Baer Heitev brought Magen Avraham there, who wrote that it is not proper to do so, that is because there is no communal fast and also because it is on Shabbos itself; which is not so here. And one must say that even though he concludes "Who answers in time of distress," and Shabbos is not a time of distress, as is stated in siman 111; and according to this there is somewhat of a distinction between the permission of siman 111 and that of siman 470, which implies that on erev Pesach they would properly say it if it were a communal fast, because on Yom Tov the conclusion "Who answers in time of distress" properly applies, as is stated in Magen Avraham there in the name of Darkei Moshe, because on another day it is a day of distress, or all the more so in the gloss there because they are days of judgment; see there. Nevertheless, from Abudraham it is properly implied that they say Aneinu even on erev Shabbos, because we properly say "Who answers in time of distress" regarding this day, which is a communal fast, and Shabbos has not yet entered. And what Magen Avraham wrote as a proof regarding reading at Minchah from the Mishnah, chapter 4 of Taanis: see Shibbolei HaLeket, who wrote this proof in the name of Moreinu VeRabbeinu Avigdor, and wrote that it is a correct proof.
