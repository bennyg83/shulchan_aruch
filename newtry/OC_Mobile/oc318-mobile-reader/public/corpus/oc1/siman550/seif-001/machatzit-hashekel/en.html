(s.k. 1) And it is forbidden, etc. — by law, etc. In Rosh Hashanah 18b the Gemara juxtaposed the verses of the fasts — calling them in one verse with language of joy and with language of fasting (see Maharsha on the question) — and answered: when there is peace, i.e. when the Temple stands, they are for joy; when there is no peace, when the Temple does not stand and there is a decree, God forbid, then it is a fast and we must fast. When there is no peace, i.e. the Temple does not stand and there is no decree — if they wish they fast, if they wish they do not fast, except Tisha B'Av in which troubles were multiplied, i.e. the First and Second Temple were destroyed on that bitter day; even if there is no decree, if the Temple does not stand one must fast. Nevertheless the early authorities wrote that all Israel accepted upon themselves to practice fasting even on the three fasts; and he concluded, end quote: behold they are an obligation on all Israel until the Temple is rebuilt, end quote.
