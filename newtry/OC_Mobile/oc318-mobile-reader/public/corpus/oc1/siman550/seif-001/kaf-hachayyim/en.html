1) [Se'if 1] Everyone is obligated to fast, etc. A girl twelve years and one day old and a boy thirteen years and one day old are obligated to fast, but not before then. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 549, subsection 7. They are obligated even if they have not developed two pubic hairs. Eshel Avraham, subsection 2; Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah. See below, siman 554, subsection 23.
2) There. Everyone is obligated to fast, etc. One who cannot observe one of the three fasts other than Tishah BeAv because of an illness does not require annulment. One who ruled that annulment is required did not properly consider the matter and made a clear error. Responsa Zera Emes, Orach Chaim, siman 75; Machazik Berachah, subsection 1; Zechor LeAvraham, subsection 9; Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 2. Beis David, siman 347, likewise writes regarding the Fast of the Firstborn on erev Pesach and Taanis Esther that no annulment is required when participating in a seudas mitzvah. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 2. Zera Emes's phrase 'other than Tishah BeAv' does not mean that the Tishah BeAv fast requires annulment. The question concerned only the other three fasts because they result from custom, as stated in the following subsection. Tishah BeAv, however, is obligatory, so it is obvious that annulment is unnecessary and there was no need to ask, as is evident from the cited question. See there. See above, siman 470, subsection 10.
3) There. It is forbidden to breach the boundary. Although under the strict law, when no danger exists, fasting is obligatory only on Tishah BeAv, since the custom is to observe all the fasts, one may not breach the boundary. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Mateh Yehudah; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 1.
4) There, in the gloss. Nevertheless, pregnant and nursing women, etc. When the community wished and accepted the fasts, they were lenient in these respects: eating was permitted during the preceding nights, and pregnant and nursing women were exempt. Chayei Adam, rule 133, subsection 6; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, writes in the name of Maadanei Yom Tov that they should make up the fast on another day, as written below, siman 686, se'if 2, in the gloss, but this is not the custom.
