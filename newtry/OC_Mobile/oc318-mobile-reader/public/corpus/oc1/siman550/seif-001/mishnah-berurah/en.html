(1) And it is forbidden to breach the fence - Meaning, although we conclude in the Gemara that at a time when decrees of the gentiles against Israel are not common, the matter depends on Israel's will, namely that if most of Israel wished and agreed not to fast on the three fasts, they have permission, the poskim wrote that now all Israel has already wished and accepted them upon themselves from generation to generation, and it is forbidden to breach the fence.
(2) However, etc. - For at the time when they wished and accepted upon themselves to fast, they were lenient with them at the outset.
(3) Pregnant and nursing women, etc. - It is reasonable that she is called pregnant from the time the fetus is recognizable, as we hold regarding vesatot. Nevertheless, it is possible that even after forty days from formation of the fetus she is included as pregnant for this matter if she feels pain; but less than this, it appears that she is like all women for every matter, unless she suffers greatly. If she feels weakness, one should not be stringent with her at all [Mor Uketzi'ah, see there].
(4) Should not fast - All the more so if he is sick without danger, he is certainly exempt from fasting and it is forbidden for him to be stringent upon himself.
(5) Except that they practiced stringency - If they are weak, it appears that they should not be stringent. Nevertheless, even one who needs to eat should not delight himself with meat and wine, but only according to what he needs. Likewise minors who have understanding to mourn, although one is not obligated to train them, even at age twelve and even for hours of fasting, nevertheless it is proper to train them not to eat except what is needed to sustain the body, bread and water or another simple food according to the child, so that they mourn with the community.
