[Levush] A person should not be accustomed, etc. This is the wording of the Rama in Even HaEzer siman 25: He may do with his wife as he wishes, have relations whenever he wishes, kiss any limb, and have relations with her in the usual manner or not in the usual manner, provided that he does not emit seed in vain. Some are lenient and say that not in the usual manner is permitted even if he emits seed, if he does so incidentally and is not accustomed to it; Tur in the name of Tosafot, end quote. The first view is the opinion of the Rambam in chapter 21 of Hilchos Issurei Bi'ah. The Beit Yosef wrote in this siman in the name of the Rosh that the answer of Tosafot is primary, for since the Gemara said simply, 'the Torah permitted her to him,' and did not investigate whether there was emission of seed, it follows that it is permitted in every manner; and that incident involved an occasional act, end quote. So too it is clear in the Levush here that the words of Tosafot are primary. It is surprising that R. Moshe Rivkash wrote in the gloss there that the Rosh, Beit Yosef, and Tur rejected the second view; perhaps there is a scribal error in his words. The words of the Rama imply that if he does not emit seed, it is permitted even if he is accustomed, and only where he emits seed is it forbidden when done regularly. So it is clear in Tosafot at the end of the chapter Arba'ah Achin and in the Tur in this siman. The Bach in Even HaEzer there inferred from the Tur's conclusion, 'but it is forbidden to be accustomed to this,' that even without emitting seed it is forbidden when done regularly. This is not convincing; rather, it discusses where he emits seed, and teaches that there is a prohibition in the matter. Examine this carefully. Now, in Sefer Charedim he greatly lengthened in wondering at the words of Tosafot and the Rosh, and concluded that one is liable to death even one time; the Shelah brings him on page 20. In my humble opinion, with forgiveness for the honor of his Torah, there are several mistakes and errors in these words. First, what he wrote, that the Rosh permitted specifically one time in one's entire life, does not appear from the words of Tosafot, the Rosh, the Tur, and the Rama, who wrote that it is forbidden specifically when done regularly; this implies that if it is not regular, even if one desires it many times, it is permitted. So is also proven in the Agudah, chapter Arba'ah Achin, who wrote as follows: it is not like the act of Er and Onan except when he regularly does so in order that she not become pregnant; but incidentally, etc. I also found this explicit in Tosafot, Sanhedrin 58b: it is forbidden only when he does so constantly so that she not become pregnant and her beauty not weaken; but if the majority of his relations are in the usual manner, and only incidentally he desires her not in the usual manner, it is permitted, end quote. This implies that even many times are permitted if most of his relations are in the usual manner. Now what he wondered about Tosafot and the Rosh is automatically rejected: if it is considered vain emission once, who permitted it; and if it is not vain emission, who forbade it many times? These are words that reason cannot tolerate, end quote. According to what I explained, it is resolved, for in truth it is not vain emission, and the reason it is forbidden when done regularly is that he does so in order that she not become pregnant. He further objected from what the Gemara stated immediately before, that Rabbi Yochanan disagrees and says that overturning his table causes children lame from birth; this implies that it speaks of relations fit for conception, and not because of another reason, end quote. This is not a difficulty, as I will write in the name of the Zohar regarding one who has relations by candlelight, and in the next seif katan I will write more about this. He also wrote as follows: the Rosh objected to the Rambam's answer that if Rabbi and Rav permitted relations not in the usual manner quickly and without emission of seed, they should have strongly warned those men about the matter, since one can easily come to liability of death. If so, the same difficulty applies to the Rosh's answer, for they should have warned the man not to make it habitual, end quote. These words are astonishing, for the Rosh never raised this objection against the Rambam. Rather, he objected that why did Rabbi and Rav immediately answer the woman, 'the Torah permitted her,' and not ask whether he emitted seed, for then there is a prohibition in the matter. Rather, incidentally it is permitted even if he emitted seed.
