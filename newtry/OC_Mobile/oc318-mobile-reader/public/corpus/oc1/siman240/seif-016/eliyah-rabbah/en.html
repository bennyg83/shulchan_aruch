[Levush] With male children, etc. So wrote the Tur in this siman, and in Even HaEzer siman 25 in the name of the Raavad. But I examined Sefer Baalei HaNefesh itself, and it did not write 'with male children,' but rather 'with beautiful children.' This is also clear from the matter from which it is learned, for the Gemara at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim brings regarding it: why are your children especially beautiful? He said to him: because he reveals a handbreadth and covers a handbreadth. Furthermore, if the reward is male children, it is seemingly contrary to nature, for since he does not linger on the abdomen, she does not emit seed first. R. David Abudraham, page 15, wrote that the first thing that emerges is compared to the earth, and the second thing is the seed; what emits seed is what emerges. Therefore, when the man emits first, he is the earth, and when the woman's seed comes afterward, it is the seed, and therefore she gives birth to a female, and the reverse as well. He further wrote in the name of Sefer HaToldos that a woman has seven openings, three on the right, three on the left, and one in the middle. When she lies on her right side, the seed enters those on the right and she gives birth to a male. When she lies on her left, she gives birth to a female. If the seed enters the middle, she gives birth to a tumtum or androgynus, end quote. The Shelah, page 103, wrote that when he wishes to unite with his wife, he and she should remember the holy forefathers and foremothers and the tribes, and pray in their hearts that this seed be drawn after his tribe. If he is from the tribe of Levi, it is good that he recite from 'And these are the names of the sons of Levi' at the beginning of Parshas Va'eira, the whole passage until 'These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites according to their families.' He must learn it by heart, for then he has no candle. There should always be a vessel of water near the bed, and they should wash their hands before relations and after relations, end quote. He further wrote that before union one should engage in Torah, and how good it is that he engage in this siman, end quote. What the Mechaber wrote, that she wears an apron, etc., even though in Sefer Chasidim it says that he had relations unclothed, as is stated in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 76, it appears to me that an apron is different, for she is not so covered. Alternatively, one may say that when both of them consent, this is modesty, Magen Avraham.
