(1) He should not be overly accustomed. See Ramban at the beginning of Parashas Kedoshim in his commentary on the Torah.
(2) Rather, according to the prescribed frequency, etc. He must fulfill the obligation of onah even when she is pregnant or nursing. He may not withhold her onah unless she consents and waives it, and he has already fulfilled the mitzvah of procreation.
(3) Stated in the Torah. All this applies to one whose body is healthy. One who is not healthy is obligated only according to what he is assessed as capable of fulfilling. Even HaEzer, siman 76, seif 3.
(4) Men of leisure. These are healthy and pampered people. Rambam. This applies even if they are Torah scholars, as long as one is not a talmid chacham. Eliyah Rabbah. Beis Shmuel in Even HaEzer, siman 76, likewise writes that for a talmid chacham, whose strength is weakened by Torah study, the obligation is in all circumstances from one Friday night to the next. This also appears from Responsa Mishnas Chachamim, siman 51. See below, seif katan 6.
(5) Camel drivers. These are those who transport loads on camels from distant places.
(6) A talmid chacham. During the weekdays he must engage in Torah study at night, because Torah is his occupation. A person should not say, “I will make myself like a talmid chacham,” for Scripture states, “He shall not diminish her onah.” Magen Avraham in the name of Sefer HaKanah. This means that he may not remove her onah without her permission. With her permission, however, a blessing will come upon him. Peri Megadim. See there, where he is uncertain whether in our times one is considered a talmid chacham for this purpose. Nevertheless, he inclines to say that since relative to the generation he is a talmid chacham and she married him with that understanding, his obligation of onah is only on Friday night. See Biur Halachah.
(7) From Friday night, etc. Some write that the same applies on Rosh Chodesh and Yom Tov. Seemingly, according to Magen Avraham's reason above in seif katan 6, that he must engage in Torah study at night, there is no basis to obligate him specifically on Rosh Chodesh. See Biur Halachah. One should not have relations on the first night of Pesach, the night of Shavuos, the two nights of Rosh Hashanah, or the night of Shemini Atzeres, unless it is the night of her immersion. All this applies only to a person who is filled with fear of Heaven and will not sin, God forbid. Those whose inclination overcomes them, and who mistakenly think this is comparable to a Torah prohibition and thereby come, God forbid, to various stumbling blocks, have a mitzvah to have relations even on Rosh Hashanah and immerse the following day. Strictly speaking, relations are forbidden only on Yom Kippur, Tisha B'Av, and during his or her period of mourning. Chochmas Adam.
(8) On the night of her immersion. Meaning, even when it is not the time of her regular onah.
(9) On a journey. See below, seif 15.
(10) Nursing. Meaning, even if she is nursing or pregnant, he is nevertheless obligated to attend to her. Beit Yosef.
(11) To attend to her. Meaning, even when it is not the time of her regular onah. The same applies when he is departing on a journey, although there her waiver is effective. Peri Megadim.
(12) Like a person paying his debt, etc. He should have this intention even during pregnancy and nursing.
