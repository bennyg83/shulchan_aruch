In the Torah. The obligation of marital relations must be fulfilled even during pregnancy and nursing. Regarding winter, the Arizal was asked whether a husband is exempt because of the cold, since he would need to immerse after a seminal emission, and likewise whether he is exempt during pregnancy and nursing. He answered that if the wife waives her right, he is exempt, although it is nevertheless proper to fulfill the obligation. They testified that he himself did not fulfill the mitzvah of marital relations during those periods. This is from Sefer HaKavanos. See Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer, siman 1, seif katan 1.
Men of leisure. Healthy people who live comfortably. Rambam. The Gemara implies that this applies even if they are Torah scholars.
For Shabbos night. The same applies on Rosh Chodesh and Yom Tov. A person should not say, “I will regard myself as a Torah scholar,” because the verse states, “He shall not diminish her marital rights.” This is from Sefer HaKanah.
Her immersion. Even when it is not the scheduled time for marital relations. It is proper to recite before relations the chapter of Tehillim “Hashem is my Shepherd, I shall not lack,” etc. All the preceding cautions restricting relations to once from Shabbos to Shabbos apply when relations are intended for conception. This is from the Arizal. One should not engage in marital relations on either night of Rosh Hashanah. One who is careful throughout the Ten Days of Repentance is praiseworthy. This is from Knesses HaGedolah. The Arizal writes that one should not engage in marital relations on Shemini Atzeres night, Shavuos night, or the first night of Pesach, unless it is the night of immersion. Relations are permitted on the seventh night of Pesach. This is from Sefer HaKavanos.
