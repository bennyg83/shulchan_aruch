1) [Seif 1] If he was married, etc. Regarding marital relations during pregnancy and nursing, a person is certainly obligated to fulfill the mitzvah of onah even during these periods. One should not say that the seed then appears, Heaven forbid, to be wasted. During these periods, however, if the husband wishes to abstain, he may do so, provided his wife does not object and forgives him. If she objects, he must fulfill her onah. Regarding relations intended to produce souls, however, he cannot exempt himself at all, even if his wife forgives him. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Bereishis. It implies there that a man whose wife is infertile or has ceased bearing children has the same law as during pregnancy and nursing; see there.
2) One who is not a Torah scholar may engage in marital relations on weekday nights, but not during the day, and even at night only after midnight. Torah scholars are forbidden to engage in marital relations on weekdays and are permitted only on Shabbos night, also specifically after midnight. Before midnight on Shabbos night has the same status as after midnight on weekday nights, except that it is now more fragrant on Shabbos night than during the week; nevertheless, it was permitted only for an am haaretz. Yom Tov and Rosh Chodesh nights have the same law as Shabbos nights, and even Torah scholars are permitted after midnight, although they do not possess as much holiness as Shabbos night. The same applies to the night of a mitzvah immersion even if it is a weekday. Shaar HaMitzvos there; see there for the mystical reason. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 73a; Pri Etz Chaim, gate 16, chapter 11, and gate 18, chapter 18. It is obvious that all this applies to one who secludes himself with his Creator and does not come to improper thoughts if he waits for the stated times. If his inclination overpowers him and he comes to improper thoughts, Heaven forbid, he may act when necessary, especially during pregnancy and nursing, when no soul is drawn to a child, as stated in the following paragraph. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 4, writes the same.
3) Some write that all the aforementioned care applies specifically when one engages in marital relations for conception. He must therefore wait until Shabbos night and after midnight in order to draw a holy soul to the child. During pregnancy and nursing, however, since no soul is drawn to a child, he need not be careful. Others say no distinction was made: even during pregnancy and nursing, one must be careful, and a Torah scholar may engage in marital relations only on Shabbos nights after midnight, as explained in the gloss to Shaar HaMitzvos there and in Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; see there. We have already written in the preceding paragraph that when necessary one may be lenient; see there.
4) Shaar HaMitzvos writes there: I heard that my teacher, of blessed memory, would remain awake every night of a mitzvah immersion until midnight and engage in Torah. Afterward, he would engage in marital relations, then recite Krias Shema and sleep. His reason was to avoid entirely coming to improper thoughts in bed and perhaps to a seminal emission, Heaven forbid. It is obvious that the statement that he afterward recited Krias Shema and slept means that he wished to fall asleep amid words of Torah. He certainly recited Krias Shema al HaMitah at its proper time before midnight, in order to direct its intentions and effect at midnight the rectification produced by Krias Shema al HaMitah. The glosses to Shaar HaMitzvos there write the same. It further appears to me that after midnight he also recited Birkas HaShachar and Tikkun Chatzos, studied a little, afterward engaged in marital relations, and then recited Krias Shema and slept, so that he would not taste the taste of death, as explained in our words above in siman 1, paragraphs 7 and 10.
5) On the first night of Pesach, marital relations are forbidden. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 81, side 3. The same applies on Shavuos night, there, folio 89a; on the nights of Rosh Hashanah, there, folio 90, side 4; and on Simchas Torah night, there, folio 103, side 4. On the seventh night of Pesach, however, marital relations are permitted, there, folio 86, side 3. Know that outside Eretz Yisrael, where two days are observed, marital relations are forbidden on both nights of Pesach, Shavuos, and Simchas Torah. This appears from the words of the Kabbalists. We have already written above in paragraph 2 that this applies specifically to one who will not come to improper thoughts. If his inclination overpowers him, it is preferable to engage in marital relations than to come to improper thoughts. Likewise, if a mitzvah immersion occurs on one of these nights, it is permitted, for it is no worse than a weekday, when it is permitted as stated above in paragraph 2. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3. See Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1, who adds other days for separation from one's wife. Since they are not mentioned in the Arizal's words, however, it appears there is no need to be concerned about them. Olas Tamid, paragraph 4, writes the same.
6) Know that among all Torah transgressions, even the severe ones, none actually produces harmful beings as does emitting seed in vain. See how extensively the Zohar discusses this in many places, especially parashas Vayechi, 219b, where it says that every transgression depends upon repentance, except emitting seed in vain, for which repentance is unavailable except through great and exceptional exertion, and one does not see the King's face forever, etc.; see there. One who emits seed in vain causes the drops of souls destined to emerge from him as children to intermingle with the Other Side. There they become clothed in bodies made from the side of the serpent, the licentious woman. He thereby reverses holiness into impurity and good into evil. Another transgression resembles this: one who engages in marital relations and does not remain until all the drops have entirely emerged. Although the remnant drops emitted in vain are unfit to produce children, aspects of harmful and destructive beings are nevertheless created from them in the aforementioned manner, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 56b; see there. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 16, chapter 5, and Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Noach, write the same. Regarding rectification, Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, rectification 27, writes that one should fast eighty-four consecutive days. The days of Shovavim are more suited than the rest of the year for this transgression because they mention the Egyptian exile with mortar and bricks, which served to rectify Adam HaRishon's seminal emission; see there. See further Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, folio 6, which writes that fasting three consecutive days and nights counts as forty days, while two consecutive days and nights count as twenty-seven days; see there.
6) Therefore, many pious men and people of deeds fast the equivalent of eighty-four days each year during Shovavim to atone for the aforementioned transgression. On Sunday of parashas Shemos, they cease eating before nightfall and fast Monday night and day, and Tuesday night and day, two consecutive days and two nights, counting as twenty-seven fasts. They eat on Wednesday night after nightfall. On Wednesday they fast from dawn until Minchah Ketanah so that the days remain consecutive. At Minchah Ketanah they begin eating until shortly before sunset, stopping before nightfall. They then fast Thursday night and day and Friday night and day until nightfall, another twenty-seven fasts, totaling fifty-four. They resume fasting on Sunday of parashas VaEira from dawn until Minchah Ketanah. They then begin eating until before sunset, stop before nightfall, and fast Monday night and day and Tuesday night and day until nightfall. This is another twenty-seven, totaling eighty-one. They then fast separately on Wednesday from dawn until nightfall, and similarly on Thursday and Friday. These three separate days added to the first eighty-one total eighty-four.
6) One who cannot perform this fasting rectification because he is weak should fast Monday and Thursday during parashas Shemos, because the principal enslavement is mentioned there, and redeem the eighty-four fasts with money. Some say that redeeming a fast helps one who cannot fast, as stated below in the gloss to siman 568, seif 2; see there. During Minchah on the fast day, he should give poor people the value of eighty-four meals, one meal for each of the eighty-four days. When separating the money, he should say aloud: I hereby give this amount of money in place of this number of days that I am obligated to fast for the particular transgression. In Your abundant mercy and kindness, consider it as though I fasted that number of complete days, atone for everything I sinned before You through that transgression, and accept me favorably in Your abundant mercy. May the words of my mouth find favor, etc. If he cannot do even this, because he is too weak to fast the aforementioned eighty-four days and is also poor and cannot redeem his fast, he should at least fast every Monday and Thursday during Shovavim, or Thursday alone if he cannot fast two days each week.
6) Know that one who fasts to atone for a transgression must say when accepting the fast after Minchah, before Oseh Shalom: Master of the universe, I hereby undertake a fast before You tomorrow so that in Your abundant mercy and kindness You atone for me for the particular transgression and accept me favorably in Your abundant mercy. May the words of my mouth find favor, etc. On the fast day, after silently completing Minchah and before Oseh Shalom, he should say the following text.
6) Master of the worlds and Master of masters, King full of mercy: It is revealed and known before You that we are weak and lack strength to fast eighty-four days to atone for having sinned before You through emitting seed in vain and damaging the sign of the holy covenant. Therefore, may it be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our ancestors, that through the fast I observed today and the fat and blood I offered before You through my fast, which I heated through the warmth of my body's weakness, and, if he redeemed the fast, through the redemption I performed for eighty-four fast days, in Your abundant mercy and kindness, may it rise before You as though I fasted eighty-four complete days. Atone for everything I sinned before You through emitting seed in vain and for the damage I caused in the supernal attributes, whether through thought, whose damage reached Chochmah and Binah, or through action, through which I damaged Tiferes and Malchus. May it be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our ancestors, Father of mercy and forgiveness, that this damage be rectified, whether I damaged the masculine and feminine waters of Chochmah and Binah, which are five kindnesses and five severities, the palate and throat, or the two combined Names E-heyeh, which contain eighty-four letters. May everything rise to its supernal root. May the four letters alef, chet, heh, ayin, which also have the numerical value eighty-four, rise to their supernal root. May the three aspects of the expanded spelling of the expansions of the Names whose values are seventy-two, sixty-three, and forty-five be drawn, namely: yud-vav-dalet, heh-yud, vav-yud-vav, heh-yud; yud-vav-dalet, heh-yud, vav-alef-vav, heh-yud; yud-vav-dalet, heh-alef, vav-alef-vav, heh-alef. Each contains twenty-eight letters, and together they total eighty-four letters. Draw them to the aspect of Havayah whose value is fifty-two, spelled yud, heh-heh, vav-vav, heh-heh, within Malchus.
6) Please, Hashem, our God and God of our ancestors, act for the sake of Your great Name and Your abundant mercy and kindness. Pardon, forgive, and atone for everything through which I sinned, acted iniquitously, and rebelled before You. Rectify what I distorted and gather all the sparks of holiness that I scattered among the husks. 'He swallowed wealth and shall vomit it out.' Let everything return to its original strength, and let none be banished from Him. Illuminate all the holy Names I damaged, through the Name formed by the initials of the phrase, 'Tear Satan,' and through the Name formed by the initials of, 'Please, Mighty One, guard those who seek Your unity like the pupil of the eye.' 'Light is sown for the righteous and joy for the upright of heart.' May the words of my mouth find favor, etc. See Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, rectification 27, our words above in siman 119, paragraph 15, and what Birkei Yosef writes in siman 685, paragraph 1, and understand this. There is a tradition that one who experiences a seminal emission, immerses specifically that day, and confesses properly, causes the harmful being produced by the emission to be erased. Tziporen Shamir, paragraph 104. See our words above in siman 76, paragraph 21, and siman 88, paragraph 5, and understand this.
7) There: He should not be accustomed to more, etc. As stated in Berachos 22a, Torah scholars should not be frequently with their wives like roosters. This does not apply only to Torah scholars, but to every person. The Tur mentions Torah scholars because restraint is common among them.
8) There: Men of leisure, etc. These are healthy people who live comfortably. Rambam. The Gemara implies this applies even if they are people of Torah. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, they are not Torah scholars. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. The Gemara's example of Rav Shmuel bar Shilas is merely illustrative. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 3. Beis Shmuel on Even HaEzer, siman 76, paragraph 1, writes the same.
9) If he teaches children Scripture and Mishnah but is not a Torah scholar, it appears that his law is like that of a laborer. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 4.
10) There: The onah of Torah scholars is from Shabbos night, etc. An allusion is found in the initial letters of the Hebrew phrase, 'The children of Yisrael shall observe Shabbos,' which spell 'marital relations.' Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 245; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 18, chapter 18, writes the same.
10) Onah on Shabbos night is praiseworthy for a Torah scholar. The mystical reason is known: there is a distinction concerning souls, for new souls descend only on Friday night. Therefore, one should engage in marital relations only after midnight. It appears to me, Chaim, that these are the very souls that descend on Shabbos night. If a person engages in marital relations that night and his wife becomes pregnant through that union, that soul will certainly rest upon that drop. Since it is a new soul that has not been punished, the Sages said that such children will be pious, supernally holy people. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 16, chapter 11.
11) There: The onah of Torah scholars is from Shabbos night, etc. This refers to healthy people. Those of weak strength are obligated only according to their ability. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. The same applies to all laws of onah: if one is not healthy, he is obligated only according to an assessment of what he can fulfill. Eliyah Rabbah there. A person should not say, 'I shall make myself like a Torah scholar,' because it is written, 'He shall not diminish her onah.' Sefer HaKanah; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. This means without her permission. With her permission, one who acts stringently shall be blessed. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3.
12) There: From Shabbos night, etc. If for some reason Shabbos night passed and he did not fulfill onah, he need fulfill it only when the next Shabbos arrives. Olas Tamid there derives this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that this is not a proof and that perhaps he must fulfill it earlier; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, writes the same. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 7, however, agrees with Olas Tamid, but concludes that everything depends upon the individual; see there.
13) There: On the night of her immersion. A person should not refrain from relations with his wife on the night she immersed. Testament of R' Yehudah HaChasid, siman 41. If circumstances beyond his control prevent him from approaching her, women practice spreading a garment belonging to the husband over themselves. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1.
14) A woman should not immerse when her husband is not in the city, because of concern for attachment by a spirit. If she immersed after the fact, she should keep a child beside her or place a knife beneath the pillow. Shevus Yaakov, volume 3, Yoreh Deah section, siman 78. Alternatively, she should spread her husband's garment over herself, as stated in the preceding paragraph.
15) It is dangerous for a woman returning from immersion to walk alone at night, and all the more so she should not go alone to draw water. Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh Deah, siman 116, paragraph 125, in the name of Neve Shalom, maamar 3, chapter 10. If a woman immersed and encountered a dog, and then engaged in relations with her husband, her children will be ugly. If she encountered a donkey, they will be foolish. If she encountered an am haaretz, her children will be amei haaretz. If she encountered a horse, it is beneficial for the child. Rokeach, siman 317.
16) A woman who delays her immersion to distress her husband bears a great transgression and causes many evils in the world, unless he is indifferent and pays no attention to it. Zohar, parashas Mishpatim, 111a; see there. Olas Tamid, paragraph 8, cites this.
17) There: And when he departs on a journey. Seif 15 says not to engage in relations on the day of departure. It appears that seif 15 concerns one traveling on foot, who requires greater strength, whereas here the case concerns travel by wagon. Levush writes that here the case is the preceding night, but the phrase 'at the time' does not imply this. Furthermore, seif 15 says, 'and not before them.' Taz, paragraph 1. Magen Avraham, paragraph 29, answers that the Rambam speaks from a medical perspective, while the Gemara speaks as a matter of law. He therefore writes that if the husband can appease her until she forgives him, it is permitted; see there. It therefore appears that if he travels by ship, wagon, or horseback, he should fulfill the mitzvah on the night of his departure. If he travels on foot and requires strength, he should appease her with words. Likewise, if his wife is a niddah, he should appease her with words.
