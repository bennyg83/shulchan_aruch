That he should not be accustomed, etc. In the Shelah, page 102, he warned that one should study this siman, and Even HaEzer siman 25, and chapter 14 of Reishis Chochmah in Shaar HaKedushah, many times, so that they be fluent in his mouth by heart. The Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote: we have practiced abstinence ourselves on the two nights of Rosh Hashanah, for perhaps the King is sitting on the throne of judgment, and a person will come to have relations with his wife and perhaps intend for his own pleasure, and it will become a snare for him. For this reason, one who is careful about this during the Ten Days of Repentance, may blessing come upon him; and on the night of Hoshana Rabbah, and the three days before Shavuos, and certainly the nights of Shavuos themselves; and from Rosh Chodesh Av until the night of the eleventh of Av. Even if Tishah B'Av was postponed until the tenth, it is proper to refrain on the night of the eleventh, as with the law of meat and wine, end quote. The Olas Tamid wrote about this that his words do not appear correct, to add onto the words of the Sages, except that on the seventh night of Pesach the masters of hidden wisdom wrote to be careful. The Magen Avraham wrote that one should not have relations on the first night of Pesach, the night of Shavuos, or the night of Shemini Atzeres, unless it is the night of immersion, from the writings. From the Baal HaTurim in Parshas Ki Seitzei it appears that everything is permitted, for this is his wording: 'and he shall gladden his wife', the numerical value of the days of 'and he shall gladden' indicates that he is obligated in marital relations all the days of the year except Yom Kippur. They further wrote that when one is aroused with an erection in a dream, he should be careful not to have relations then, Heaven forbid, for the children born through that erection will be blemished; and even if she is pregnant, he brings into being harmful spirits. A person should also be careful that when he leaves the bathroom, he should not have relations for that entire hour. In Seder HaYom he wrote: it appears to me that this is specifically in a fixed bathroom, which has a set place and seat, but not in another place used incidentally. Know that the conjugal obligation must be fulfilled even during pregnancy and nursing. But one who is accustomed to immerse for seminal impurity, and it is a cold season, if she waives it he is exempt from the conjugal obligation, since she is pregnant or nursing.
And the times of Torah scholars, etc. This refers to those who are healthy. But those whose strength is weak are obligated only according to their strength, Olas Tamid. So it also appears from the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Dei'os. In the Gemara, chapter Chelek, page 107, Rav said that even during David's illness he fulfilled eighteen conjugal duties. See Even HaEzer siman 76, seif 3, that regarding all laws of conjugal duties, if he is not healthy he is obligated only according to what they assess he is able to fulfill. The sefer Derech Chochmah wrote in the name of the Tikkunim that all the warnings of Chazal from Shabbos eve to Shabbos eve apply when one has relations for the sake of conception, for children are needed in order to draw to that drop a holy upper soul. If his wife is already pregnant or nursing, this reason no longer applies, end quote. But in the Shelah, page 102, he wrote in the name of the kabbalists that even when his wife cannot become pregnant, nevertheless he arouses above and brings down a soul, and therefore there is permission to have relations with his wife even if she is already pregnant, nursing, barren, or elderly, for a holy soul is formed from this, end quote. According to this, it seems somewhat that one should always be careful specifically from Shabbos eve to Shabbos eve, and so it appears from the plain implication of the Gemara and all the poskim. Also, it implies that the conjugal time of a Torah scholar is comparable to the times of men of leisure, laborers, and the others. In Teshuvas Binyamin Ze'ev he wrote that the initial letters of 'And the children of Israel shall keep the Shabbos' form the word for relations, end quote. The Arizal of blessed memory wrote that the night of Yom Tov and the night of Rosh Chodesh have the same law as Shabbos night. But the Magen Avraham wrote that on weekdays they must engage in Torah, and the same applies to Rosh Chodesh and Yom Tov, and a person should not say, 'I will make myself like a Torah scholar,' for it is written, 'and her conjugal right he shall not diminish,' end quote. In the Levush in Even HaEzer there, a Torah scholar means one whose Torah is his occupation and weakens his strength. The Magen Avraham wrote that men of leisure, etc., means even if they are bnei Torah, end quote; that is, nevertheless they are not Torah scholars. What the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote, that they do not pay tax, appears to contradict what is written in Even HaEzer siman 76. One may answer that what he wrote, 'and they lodge in their homes,' must mean that the Shulchan Aruch stated an even greater novelty, and the same applies if they do not lodge there. But in the Levush one must say that he stated it specifically; if they do not lodge there, their conjugal time is once in eight days, as stated in Even HaEzer. With this the questions of the Magen Avraham are resolved, and examine this carefully.
And on the night of her immersion, etc. The Olas Tamid proved from this that if, due to some circumstance, Shabbos night passed and he did not fulfill the conjugal obligation, he need not fulfill that obligation until the next Shabbos arrives. For if not, why was it necessary to teach this; let it follow that the time had already passed, end quote. This is no proof, for while she is a niddah he is not obligated in the conjugal duty. But when she is tahor and he did not fulfill the duty, it is possible that he must fulfill it.
For the purpose of a mitzvah, etc. In Yevamos 62b the Gemara states the reason: because they are preoccupied. Rashi explains: he is preoccupied with the mitzvah; alternatively, he may become preoccupied with relations and neglect the mitzvah, end quote. See siman 248, the beginning of siman 415, and the beginning of siman 536. The Bach wrote that for commerce it is called optional activity, and the Olas Tamid inferred this from his words; but he did not examine properly, for the Bach wrote this explicitly.
One who satisfies hunger, etc. This means that he increases his desire, and so Rashi explains in Chelek, page 107. But in Sukkah, page 52, Rashi explains that 'hungry' means his body lacks strength, which is harmful in old age.
He shall be under nidui, etc. Tosafot in the chapter Kol HaYad explain that he is not automatically excommunicated, but is fit to be excommunicated. So wrote the Rashba, Ramban, and Ri. In Even HaEzer, where the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote that he sits in nidui, implying that he is automatically excommunicated, one can say that there it discusses where he emitted seed. But here, where he deliberately caused an erection, as long as he does not emit seed he is not automatically under nidui, but is fit to be excommunicated. See Lechem Chamudos on the chapter Kol HaYad, and above siman 3, seif katan 16, and see the Shelah, page 99, from this responsum. He further warned that two male children should not lie in one bed, nor next to a strange young man; and so ruled the Chelkas Mechokek in siman 24. The Shelah further warned to marry off sons in their youth before they come to sinful thoughts, and afterward they should learn in purity. He further warned that the groom should not sit next to the bride. He further wrote that the groom should not lie next to the bride until the night when he intends to consummate the marriage. He further wrote: I saw a proper person who was accustomed to immerse on the day that his wife immersed, in order to draw sanctity above at the time of union.
[Levush] According to the Torah, etc. This is not precise, for even D'Rabbanan he is not a mamzer, as stated in Even HaEzer siman 4. Rather, it means that even though the Torah validates him and he is not a mamzer, nevertheless the child is blemished. So wrote the Maharshal.
His two wives, etc. In Sefer Baalei HaNefesh he wrote: some ask, if so, there are tribes who were children of substitution, for Yaakov intended Rachel and Leah came to him. One may answer that the Torah had not yet been given, and they were not yet forbidden in the fine points of the mitzvos. Alternatively, it is possible that she did not conceive that night, end quote.
[Levush] That she did so, etc. Although she said, 'come to me,' nevertheless this is modest language that can bear the interpretation, 'come to me, to my room.' For she should have said, 'lie with me,' as it is stated, 'therefore he shall lie with you tonight.' But an immodest woman is one who explicitly speaks of relations in her words, Perishah. In the Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilchos Ishus, this is his wording: she should not demand relations with her mouth, and she should not speak about this matter, end quote. If she desires him, he should first speak with her about relations, for if she demands it the children will not be good, Sefer Chasidim 516. Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Niddah wrote that it is forbidden to have relations with one who is sleeping; the Beit Yosef brings this in Yoreh De'ah siman 184. So wrote the Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchos Dei'os.
[Levush] A person should not be accustomed, etc. This is the wording of the Rama in Even HaEzer siman 25: He may do with his wife as he wishes, have relations whenever he wishes, kiss any limb, and have relations with her in the usual manner or not in the usual manner, provided that he does not emit seed in vain. Some are lenient and say that not in the usual manner is permitted even if he emits seed, if he does so incidentally and is not accustomed to it; Tur in the name of Tosafot, end quote. The first view is the opinion of the Rambam in chapter 21 of Hilchos Issurei Bi'ah. The Beit Yosef wrote in this siman in the name of the Rosh that the answer of Tosafot is primary, for since the Gemara said simply, 'the Torah permitted her to him,' and did not investigate whether there was emission of seed, it follows that it is permitted in every manner; and that incident involved an occasional act, end quote. So too it is clear in the Levush here that the words of Tosafot are primary. It is surprising that R. Moshe Rivkash wrote in the gloss there that the Rosh, Beit Yosef, and Tur rejected the second view; perhaps there is a scribal error in his words. The words of the Rama imply that if he does not emit seed, it is permitted even if he is accustomed, and only where he emits seed is it forbidden when done regularly. So it is clear in Tosafot at the end of the chapter Arba'ah Achin and in the Tur in this siman. The Bach in Even HaEzer there inferred from the Tur's conclusion, 'but it is forbidden to be accustomed to this,' that even without emitting seed it is forbidden when done regularly. This is not convincing; rather, it discusses where he emits seed, and teaches that there is a prohibition in the matter. Examine this carefully. Now, in Sefer Charedim he greatly lengthened in wondering at the words of Tosafot and the Rosh, and concluded that one is liable to death even one time; the Shelah brings him on page 20. In my humble opinion, with forgiveness for the honor of his Torah, there are several mistakes and errors in these words. First, what he wrote, that the Rosh permitted specifically one time in one's entire life, does not appear from the words of Tosafot, the Rosh, the Tur, and the Rama, who wrote that it is forbidden specifically when done regularly; this implies that if it is not regular, even if one desires it many times, it is permitted. So is also proven in the Agudah, chapter Arba'ah Achin, who wrote as follows: it is not like the act of Er and Onan except when he regularly does so in order that she not become pregnant; but incidentally, etc. I also found this explicit in Tosafot, Sanhedrin 58b: it is forbidden only when he does so constantly so that she not become pregnant and her beauty not weaken; but if the majority of his relations are in the usual manner, and only incidentally he desires her not in the usual manner, it is permitted, end quote. This implies that even many times are permitted if most of his relations are in the usual manner. Now what he wondered about Tosafot and the Rosh is automatically rejected: if it is considered vain emission once, who permitted it; and if it is not vain emission, who forbade it many times? These are words that reason cannot tolerate, end quote. According to what I explained, it is resolved, for in truth it is not vain emission, and the reason it is forbidden when done regularly is that he does so in order that she not become pregnant. He further objected from what the Gemara stated immediately before, that Rabbi Yochanan disagrees and says that overturning his table causes children lame from birth; this implies that it speaks of relations fit for conception, and not because of another reason, end quote. This is not a difficulty, as I will write in the name of the Zohar regarding one who has relations by candlelight, and in the next seif katan I will write more about this. He also wrote as follows: the Rosh objected to the Rambam's answer that if Rabbi and Rav permitted relations not in the usual manner quickly and without emission of seed, they should have strongly warned those men about the matter, since one can easily come to liability of death. If so, the same difficulty applies to the Rosh's answer, for they should have warned the man not to make it habitual, end quote. These words are astonishing, for the Rosh never raised this objection against the Rambam. Rather, he objected that why did Rabbi and Rav immediately answer the woman, 'the Torah permitted her,' and not ask whether he emitted seed, for then there is a prohibition in the matter. Rather, incidentally it is permitted even if he emitted seed.
