It may mean that the phrase explains the day of departure: “before them” means before he sets out, and “after them” means on the day he returns from the journey. In Shulchan Aruch, seif 15, the wording “not before them” appears to imply that even the preceding night is included. Perhaps it means only the hour immediately adjacent to departure. See Eliyah Rabbah there, seif katan 23. See also Levush here concerning a time close to her expected menstruation, and Yoreh Deah, siman 184, seif 10, with Shach, seif katan 27. Levush there writes “some say,” and perhaps his view accords with the first opinion cited anonymously. See Even HaEzer, simanim 25 and 76, concerning these laws. If one has not yet fulfilled the mitzvah of procreation, he fulfills two positive mitzvos, procreation and populating the world. If he has already fulfilled procreation, he still fulfills the mitzvah of populating the world. If she waives her conjugal rights after he has fulfilled procreation, the waiver is effective; before he has fulfilled it, the waiver is ineffective. See Even HaEzer, siman 76, seif 6, and Beis Shmuel, siman 1, seifim katanim 1 and 15.

We have one doubt, which I will record here: Are persons disqualified from marrying into the congregation, such as an Egyptian, Edomite, Ammonite, or mamzer, commanded in the mitzvah of procreation? See Tosafos, Gittin 41b, s.v. lo, who cite Rabbeinu Yitzchak ben Rabbi Mordechai that procreation does not apply to the slave portion of a half-slave, only to his free portion, whereas the mitzvah of populating the world applies even to a slave. What Tosafos write that this is also implied by the Yerushalmi requires some study. See Gittin, chapter HaSholeach, halachah 4, and Moed Katan, chapter 1, halachah 7. The commentary Pnei Moshe explains it in accordance with Tosafos. At first glance, one might say that the Yerushalmi asks whether he performs a mitzvah of procreation and concludes that he does. A half-slave is disqualified from the congregation, yet procreation applies to his free side. One may distinguish that he has a remedy through emancipation, unlike a mamzer and other disqualified persons. See siman 546, where the law that one may not marry during Chol HaMoed does not exclude persons disqualified from the congregation. Three reasons are given there, including that one may not combine one joy with another and because of the burden. See Taz and Magen Avraham there.
