Although the light is visible. The same applies if the light is visible above the partition: it is permitted. Nevertheless, one must darken with his garment, because since the room is full of light it is no better than daytime. Yam Shel Shlomo, Beitzah, chapter 2, siman 30. The kabbalistic intentions likewise state that no light should be visible, for this can cause epilepsy in one’s children, even if she is pregnant. Seder HaYom writes: One who sleeps in a canopy may have relations if the curtains are thick and darken the area, even if they do not make it completely dark. If there is no canopy, it is forbidden even if the mouth of the lamp is turned in another direction and does not illuminate much, or if it is enclosed in glass. It appears to me that the same applies when a wax candle burns inside a tube. If moonlight shines directly upon them, it is forbidden; if it illuminates the room but not them, there is less concern, though Knesses HaGedolah forbids it. If they are under the open sky, it is forbidden even in the moon’s shadow. Starlight is not considered light for this purpose, though if the stars shine brightly it may be proper to refrain.
