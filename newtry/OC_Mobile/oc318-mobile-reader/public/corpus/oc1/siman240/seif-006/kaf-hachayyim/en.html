6) Know that among all Torah transgressions, even the severe ones, none actually produces harmful beings as does emitting seed in vain. See how extensively the Zohar discusses this in many places, especially parashas Vayechi, 219b, where it says that every transgression depends upon repentance, except emitting seed in vain, for which repentance is unavailable except through great and exceptional exertion, and one does not see the King's face forever, etc.; see there. One who emits seed in vain causes the drops of souls destined to emerge from him as children to intermingle with the Other Side. There they become clothed in bodies made from the side of the serpent, the licentious woman. He thereby reverses holiness into impurity and good into evil. Another transgression resembles this: one who engages in marital relations and does not remain until all the drops have entirely emerged. Although the remnant drops emitted in vain are unfit to produce children, aspects of harmful and destructive beings are nevertheless created from them in the aforementioned manner, as explained in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 56b; see there. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 16, chapter 5, and Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Noach, write the same. Regarding rectification, Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, rectification 27, writes that one should fast eighty-four consecutive days. The days of Shovavim are more suited than the rest of the year for this transgression because they mention the Egyptian exile with mortar and bricks, which served to rectify Adam HaRishon's seminal emission; see there. See further Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, folio 6, which writes that fasting three consecutive days and nights counts as forty days, while two consecutive days and nights count as twenty-seven days; see there.
6) Therefore, many pious men and people of deeds fast the equivalent of eighty-four days each year during Shovavim to atone for the aforementioned transgression. On Sunday of parashas Shemos, they cease eating before nightfall and fast Monday night and day, and Tuesday night and day, two consecutive days and two nights, counting as twenty-seven fasts. They eat on Wednesday night after nightfall. On Wednesday they fast from dawn until Minchah Ketanah so that the days remain consecutive. At Minchah Ketanah they begin eating until shortly before sunset, stopping before nightfall. They then fast Thursday night and day and Friday night and day until nightfall, another twenty-seven fasts, totaling fifty-four. They resume fasting on Sunday of parashas VaEira from dawn until Minchah Ketanah. They then begin eating until before sunset, stop before nightfall, and fast Monday night and day and Tuesday night and day until nightfall. This is another twenty-seven, totaling eighty-one. They then fast separately on Wednesday from dawn until nightfall, and similarly on Thursday and Friday. These three separate days added to the first eighty-one total eighty-four.
6) One who cannot perform this fasting rectification because he is weak should fast Monday and Thursday during parashas Shemos, because the principal enslavement is mentioned there, and redeem the eighty-four fasts with money. Some say that redeeming a fast helps one who cannot fast, as stated below in the gloss to siman 568, seif 2; see there. During Minchah on the fast day, he should give poor people the value of eighty-four meals, one meal for each of the eighty-four days. When separating the money, he should say aloud: I hereby give this amount of money in place of this number of days that I am obligated to fast for the particular transgression. In Your abundant mercy and kindness, consider it as though I fasted that number of complete days, atone for everything I sinned before You through that transgression, and accept me favorably in Your abundant mercy. May the words of my mouth find favor, etc. If he cannot do even this, because he is too weak to fast the aforementioned eighty-four days and is also poor and cannot redeem his fast, he should at least fast every Monday and Thursday during Shovavim, or Thursday alone if he cannot fast two days each week.
6) Know that one who fasts to atone for a transgression must say when accepting the fast after Minchah, before Oseh Shalom: Master of the universe, I hereby undertake a fast before You tomorrow so that in Your abundant mercy and kindness You atone for me for the particular transgression and accept me favorably in Your abundant mercy. May the words of my mouth find favor, etc. On the fast day, after silently completing Minchah and before Oseh Shalom, he should say the following text.
6) Master of the worlds and Master of masters, King full of mercy: It is revealed and known before You that we are weak and lack strength to fast eighty-four days to atone for having sinned before You through emitting seed in vain and damaging the sign of the holy covenant. Therefore, may it be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our ancestors, that through the fast I observed today and the fat and blood I offered before You through my fast, which I heated through the warmth of my body's weakness, and, if he redeemed the fast, through the redemption I performed for eighty-four fast days, in Your abundant mercy and kindness, may it rise before You as though I fasted eighty-four complete days. Atone for everything I sinned before You through emitting seed in vain and for the damage I caused in the supernal attributes, whether through thought, whose damage reached Chochmah and Binah, or through action, through which I damaged Tiferes and Malchus. May it be Your will, Hashem, our God and God of our ancestors, Father of mercy and forgiveness, that this damage be rectified, whether I damaged the masculine and feminine waters of Chochmah and Binah, which are five kindnesses and five severities, the palate and throat, or the two combined Names E-heyeh, which contain eighty-four letters. May everything rise to its supernal root. May the four letters alef, chet, heh, ayin, which also have the numerical value eighty-four, rise to their supernal root. May the three aspects of the expanded spelling of the expansions of the Names whose values are seventy-two, sixty-three, and forty-five be drawn, namely: yud-vav-dalet, heh-yud, vav-yud-vav, heh-yud; yud-vav-dalet, heh-yud, vav-alef-vav, heh-yud; yud-vav-dalet, heh-alef, vav-alef-vav, heh-alef. Each contains twenty-eight letters, and together they total eighty-four letters. Draw them to the aspect of Havayah whose value is fifty-two, spelled yud, heh-heh, vav-vav, heh-heh, within Malchus.
6) Please, Hashem, our God and God of our ancestors, act for the sake of Your great Name and Your abundant mercy and kindness. Pardon, forgive, and atone for everything through which I sinned, acted iniquitously, and rebelled before You. Rectify what I distorted and gather all the sparks of holiness that I scattered among the husks. 'He swallowed wealth and shall vomit it out.' Let everything return to its original strength, and let none be banished from Him. Illuminate all the holy Names I damaged, through the Name formed by the initials of the phrase, 'Tear Satan,' and through the Name formed by the initials of, 'Please, Mighty One, guard those who seek Your unity like the pupil of the eye.' 'Light is sown for the righteous and joy for the upright of heart.' May the words of my mouth find favor, etc. See Shaar Ruach HaKodesh, rectification 27, our words above in siman 119, paragraph 15, and what Birkei Yosef writes in siman 685, paragraph 1, and understand this.
