In a brazen manner, etc. It is difficult, for it should be forbidden because of emitting seed in vain, since we hold in chapter Arba'ah Achin that when she overturns herself she does not become pregnant. One can answer that since it is through the place of relations, it is not included in destruction of seed; and similar to this the Levush wrote in Even HaEzer siman 23, seif 5, regarding a woman who has abdominal pain. So wrote the Lechem Chamudos in the chapter Kol HaYad. He further wrote that one may say that the manner of relations face to face is not included in this category of one who does not conceive, end quote. In my humble opinion, there is proof to his words from Sefer Charedim and the Shelah cited above, who challenged the explanation of Tosafot of the Gemara at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim: why are children lame? Because they overturn their table. Tosafot explained that this means they have relations not in the usual manner. They objected that it is impossible to father children through relations not in the usual manner, and therefore wrote that Rashi's explanation is primary, that he has relations in the usual manner, but she is above and he below, or according to the Raavad's explanation, his face is opposite the back of her neck, like the lying of an animal, in order to conceive, end quote. If in these two manners it is not fit to conceive, then the difficulty still remains: how do the children become lame if she cannot become pregnant? Rather, certainly they can conceive. But I am astonished from this at the Levush there, who wrote simply that with the lying of an animal she certainly does not conceive, and therefore greatly strained himself with forced reasons to permit lying like an animal if the woman has abdominal illness; the Lechem Chamudos brings him there, see there. Yet to Sefer Charedim and the Shelah it is obvious that they can conceive.
Awake, etc. It implies that even if the person is not paying attention and does not know that this one is having relations, it is forbidden, even if he is mute. The Olas Tamid wrote that one should be uncertain whether a deaf person is comparable to a minor who does not know how to speak, who is like an animal. All this is according to the law, but as a trait of piety one should be careful even in front of an animal, as we say that Abaye would drive the flies away from the bed. Many sages were also careful even before mice, end quote. It is obvious that all this discusses at night or in a dark place, and so Tosafot wrote in Niddah 17b.
