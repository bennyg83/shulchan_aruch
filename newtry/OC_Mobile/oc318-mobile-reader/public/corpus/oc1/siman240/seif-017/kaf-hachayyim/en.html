17) There: And when he departs on a journey. Seif 15 says not to engage in relations on the day of departure. It appears that seif 15 concerns one traveling on foot, who requires greater strength, whereas here the case concerns travel by wagon. Levush writes that here the case is the preceding night, but the phrase 'at the time' does not imply this. Furthermore, seif 15 says, 'and not before them.' Taz, paragraph 1. Magen Avraham, paragraph 29, answers that the Rambam speaks from a medical perspective, while the Gemara speaks as a matter of law. He therefore writes that if the husband can appease her until she forgives him, it is permitted; see there. It therefore appears that if he travels by ship, wagon, or horseback, he should fulfill the mitzvah on the night of his departure. If he travels on foot and requires strength, he should appease her with words. Likewise, if his wife is a niddah, he should appease her with words.
