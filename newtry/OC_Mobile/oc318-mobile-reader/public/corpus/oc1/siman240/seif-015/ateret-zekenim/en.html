One should not have relations on the day he departs on a journey, etc. This is difficult, for they said that a man is obligated to attend to his wife when he departs on a journey, even close to the expected time of her menstruation, as stated above in siman 1. I heard an explanation that when he travels on foot he should not have relations, whereas the obligation to attend to his wife refers to one who travels seated in a carriage or riding. Maharshal. Another explanation is that it is forbidden on the day of departure, but permitted the night before or after it, similar to the statement that one should not have relations on the day he enters the bathhouse. Only that actual day is forbidden, but the preceding or following night is permitted, for the conjugal interval of Torah scholars is Friday night and it is a mitzvah to bathe on Friday, as stated below in siman 260. However, this is difficult from the wording, “not before them and not after them,” which implies that the day before departure is forbidden. How, then, can the preceding night be permitted while the preceding day is forbidden? It therefore appears that “not before them and not after them” refers to the day of bloodletting and the day of bathing. It means before and after bloodletting, and before and after bathing. It cannot refer to departure or arrival from a journey, for what does “after them” mean regarding departure, or “before them” regarding arrival? The phrase applies to each item. Examine carefully. Rav Nosson Shapira answered that when one’s wife accompanies him on the journey, relations are forbidden, for then there is no need to attend to her before departure.
