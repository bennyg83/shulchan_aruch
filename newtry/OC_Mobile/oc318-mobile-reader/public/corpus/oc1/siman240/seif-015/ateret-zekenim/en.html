And he should not have relations on the day he departs on a journey etc. One may ask: they said a man is obligated to visit his wife when he departs on a journey, even close to her veset, as above siman 1. I heard an explanation that when he walks on foot on the road he should not have relations; and what is written 'obligated to visit his wife' refers when he sits in a carriage or rides (Maharshal). Furthermore, on the day of departure it is forbidden, but on the night before or after it is permitted — similar to what is written 'not on the day he enters the bath,' where relations are forbidden only that day itself, but on the night before or after it is permitted, for the onah of Torah scholars is on Shabbat night and one is commanded to go to the bath on erev Shabbat as below siman 260. However it is difficult: 'not before them nor after them' implies the day before departure is forbidden to have relations — if so, how can one say the night before departure is permitted while the day before is forbidden? Therefore it seems to explain that 'not before them nor after them' refers to the day of bloodletting and the day of the bath; thus he properly says not before them nor after them — meaning before bloodletting and after bloodletting, and likewise before the bath and after the bath. How can this be said about departure on a journey and 'not after them,' and about return from a journey 'not before them' — what does 'before' mean on return and what does 'after' mean on departure? For 'not before them nor after them' applies to each separately; understand. And R' Nissim answered: when the woman goes out with him on the journey, then it is forbidden to have relations on erev Shabbat, for then he need not visit her.
