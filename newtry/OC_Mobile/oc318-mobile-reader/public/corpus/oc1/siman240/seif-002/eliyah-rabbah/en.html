And the times of Torah scholars, etc. This refers to those who are healthy. But those whose strength is weak are obligated only according to their strength, Olas Tamid. So it also appears from the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Dei'os. In the Gemara, chapter Chelek, page 107, Rav said that even during David's illness he fulfilled eighteen conjugal duties. See Even HaEzer siman 76, seif 3, that regarding all laws of conjugal duties, if he is not healthy he is obligated only according to what they assess he is able to fulfill. The sefer Derech Chochmah wrote in the name of the Tikkunim that all the warnings of Chazal from Shabbos eve to Shabbos eve apply when one has relations for the sake of conception, for children are needed in order to draw to that drop a holy upper soul. If his wife is already pregnant or nursing, this reason no longer applies, end quote. But in the Shelah, page 102, he wrote in the name of the kabbalists that even when his wife cannot become pregnant, nevertheless he arouses above and brings down a soul, and therefore there is permission to have relations with his wife even if she is already pregnant, nursing, barren, or elderly, for a holy soul is formed from this, end quote. According to this, it seems somewhat that one should always be careful specifically from Shabbos eve to Shabbos eve, and so it appears from the plain implication of the Gemara and all the poskim. Also, it implies that the conjugal time of a Torah scholar is comparable to the times of men of leisure, laborers, and the others. In Teshuvas Binyamin Ze'ev he wrote that the initial letters of 'And the children of Israel shall keep the Shabbos' form the word for relations, end quote. The Arizal of blessed memory wrote that the night of Yom Tov and the night of Rosh Chodesh have the same law as Shabbos night. But the Magen Avraham wrote that on weekdays they must engage in Torah, and the same applies to Rosh Chodesh and Yom Tov, and a person should not say, 'I will make myself like a Torah scholar,' for it is written, 'and her conjugal right he shall not diminish,' end quote. In the Levush in Even HaEzer there, a Torah scholar means one whose Torah is his occupation and weakens his strength. The Magen Avraham wrote that men of leisure, etc., means even if they are bnei Torah, end quote; that is, nevertheless they are not Torah scholars. What the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote, that they do not pay tax, appears to contradict what is written in Even HaEzer siman 76. One may answer that what he wrote, 'and they lodge in their homes,' must mean that the Shulchan Aruch stated an even greater novelty, and the same applies if they do not lodge there. But in the Levush one must say that he stated it specifically; if they do not lodge there, their conjugal time is once in eight days, as stated in Even HaEzer. With this the questions of the Magen Avraham are resolved, and examine this carefully.
