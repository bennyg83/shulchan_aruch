(א) In "Who builds Jerusalem" — nechamenu. And if he forgot he should say it at "and upon all." (Magen Avraham in the name of Beit Yosef and Abudraham.) But Taz wondered about what Abudraham wrote that one should say this in hoda'ah — this is his language; it is difficult, for does he not bring Beit Yosef's Yerushalmi: whatever is for the future one says in avodah, and whatever is for the past one says in hoda'ah — and this prayer of nechamenu is for the future; it appears there is a scribal error in Abudraham and one must say in avodah — up to here his language, Taz. And I wonder on him that he did not examine Abudraham itself daf 41 that brought the Yerushalmi and also wrote that in the Yerushalmi one must say it in hoda'ah, and he repeated and tripled there — certainly there is no scribal error in all the places, especially since Beit Yosef and Magen Avraham ruled thus. I again found (at the beginning) [in Rosh] end of Taanit that brought the aforementioned Yerushalmi and Rashi explained there, and this is his language: for the past — when one prays about what passed, such as nechamenu on the destruction and on miracles — up to here his language; behold nechamenu is for the past, and Taz overlooked it. Taz wrote that if he remembered earlier he should mention it in Shomea Tefillah; after its subject one asks one's needs in Shomea Tefillah — up to here. And see siman 119; also it appears to me, Heaven forbid to act against the Yerushalmi, that also in "Who builds Jerusalem" we would not say it were it not that custom is so, as Rif and R' David Abudraham wrote.
