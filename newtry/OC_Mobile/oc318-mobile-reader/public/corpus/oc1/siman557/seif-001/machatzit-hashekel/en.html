s.v. If he forgot etc.: he should say it beside "VeAl Kulam."
For in the Yerushalmi they asked where one says it. Said R' Yirmiyah: Whatever relates to the future (that is, a prayer and request regarding what is to come) one says in Avodah (in the blessing of Retzeh); and whatever relates to the past (that is, praise to the Omnipresent, may He be blessed, for what He has already done well with us) one says in Hoda'ah (in the blessing of Modim, before "VeAl Kulam"). And Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rif and the Rosh that even so, they customarily say Nachem in Boneh Yerushalayim, relying on that which we hold in siman 119: if one wishes to add at the end of each of the middle blessings something of the nature of that blessing, he may add; and Nachem is of the nature of the blessing of Boneh Yerushalayim. And for this reason Abudraham wrote that if they forgot to say it in Boneh Yerushalayim, they say it in the place specially assigned to it by law, namely in Hoda'ah. But the Taz objected: since Nachem is a prayer regarding the future, he should have said that they say it in Avodah; therefore he emended that in Abudraham it should read that they say it in Avodah. But in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah etc. he found one who explains that which the Yerushalmi said, "and whatever relates to the past," as meaning that one prays regarding what is past, such as Nachem over the destruction, and Al HaNissim, end quote. And one must say that in any case it has a connection to "VeAl Kulam," along the lines of what they said: we bless over the bad just as we bless over the good. And the Taz wrote that if one forgot, he may say it in Shomei'a Tefillah, for in Shomei'a Tefillah one may include whatever he wishes, as above in siman 119, and he concludes with Shomei'a Tefillah.
