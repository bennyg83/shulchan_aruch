(א) In "Who builds Jerusalem" — nechamenu. Because it belongs to its subject, and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Abudraham: he erred and did not mention it in its customary place that one should say it in hoda'ah, which is its designated place by law etc., end of his words. And it is astonishing, for does Beit Yosef not bring the Yerushalmi: wherever it is for the future one says it in avodah, and wherever for the past in hoda'ah? And Rif and Rosh wrote that the world is accustomed to say in "Who builds Jerusalem," and they relied on that which they said one asks one's needs in Shomea Tefillah, and if one comes at the end of any blessing with something akin to that blessing one says it — end of his words; and this prayer of nechamenu is for the future — why did Abudraham write its place is in hoda'ah? It appears there is a scribal error here and one must say in avodah. And in my humble opinion, since we rely on that which one asks one's needs etc., if so, one who forgot to say it in its place — since the reason is because it is akin to the blessing, one may say it in Shomea Tefillah, for this blessing is inclusive and there is place there for all requests; Shomea Tefillah is for all blessings akin to each blessing; and in any case one should not conclude with "Blessed are You Who comforts Zion" but in Shomea Tefillah, like "and upon" and "upon" before nechamenu, for it is frequent.
