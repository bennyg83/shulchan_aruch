In Boneh Yerushalayim, Nachem. Because it is of the nature of that blessing. And Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Abudraham: If one erred and did not mention it in the customary place, he should say it in Hoda'ah, which is its special place according to the law, etc., end quote. And this is difficult for me, for Beis Yosef himself brings the Yerushalmi: Where does one say it? Said R' Yirmiyah: Whatever relates to the future, one says in Avodah; and whatever relates to the past, one says in Hoda'ah. And the Rif and the Rosh wrote that the world has adopted the custom of saying it in Boneh Yerushalayim, relying on that which they said: A person may request his needs in Shomei'a Tefillah; and if one wishes to add at the end of each blessing something of the nature of that blessing, he may say it, end quote. Now this prayer of Nachem is regarding the future; so why did Abudraham write that its place is in Hoda'ah? And it appears there is a scribal error here, and it should read "in Avodah." And in my humble opinion, since we rely on that teaching of requesting one's needs, etc., therefore if one forgot to say it in its place, since the reason is that it is of the nature of the blessing, he should say it in Shomei'a Tefillah, for that is a comprehensive blessing, and all requests have a place there, for Shomei'a Tefillah serves for all the blessings as being of the nature of each blessing. And in any case he should not conclude with "Baruch Menachem Tziyon," but only with Shomei'a Tefillah, as with Aneinu; and Aneinu comes before Nachem, because it is the more frequent.
And the widespread custom is that we do not say Nachem, etc. This implies that the custom is for the chazzan to say Aneinu also at Shacharis; for the Shulchan Aruch wrote "and Aneinu in Shomei'a Tefillah," and regarding this the Rama did not write that the custom is not to say it; and in the Minhagim it is likewise written to say it, except that he wrote at the end: but R' Yitzchak instituted not to say Aneinu at Shacharis, because it is called a festival, and for this reason we do not fall on our faces; and if Tisha B'Av falls on Thursday, we do not say "Eil Erech Apayim" or "Lamnatzeiach," and a fortiori not "VeHu Rachum," and so is the practice, end quote. And in the glosses it is written on the words of R' Yitzchak: and this does not seem correct to me; and it is from the words of the Mordechai. And it appears that one should not rely on this, for the Mordechai holds that they also customarily say "Eil Erech Apayim," whereas we simply customarily do not say "Eil Erech Apayim" nor "Lamnatzeiach"; also Tzidkasecha we do not say, as is written in siman 559. Therefore one should not say Aneinu either; and so it appears that passive restraint is preferable.
