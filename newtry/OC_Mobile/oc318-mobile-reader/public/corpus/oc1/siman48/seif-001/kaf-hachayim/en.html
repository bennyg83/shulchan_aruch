A) And know one preface: from the beginning of prayer until Baruch she'amar is the world of Asiyah. From Baruch she'amar until Yotzer Or is the world of Yetzirah. From Yotzer Or until the prayer of eighteen blessings is the world of Beriah. The prayer of eighteen blessings is in the world of Atzilut. From "Happy are those who dwell in Your house" until "A prayer of David" returns to be the world of Beriah. From there until "There is none holy like the the Omnipresent" returns to be the world of Yetzirah. From there until "Upon us to praise" is the world of Asiyah. The intention is that one must, in prayer, raise the worlds from below to above and connect the lower world to the upper world from it until all the worlds are found together bound in the world of Atzilut at the time one prays the prayer of eighteen blessings standing—and then the supernal union is made there. And afterward one must receive the supernal abundance drawn from that union and bring it down in the lower worlds from above to below. Therefore when we say "Happy are those who dwell in Your house" until "A prayer of David" we bring the abundance down from Atzilut to Beriah—not meaning to bring down the world of Beriah itself. And afterward from "A prayer of David" until "There is none holy like the the Omnipresent" etc. we bring abundance from Beriah to Yetzirah; and afterward from "There is none holy like the the Omnipresent, for there is none besides You" until "Upon us to praise" we bring abundance from Yetzirah to Asiyah. And afterward at "Upon us to praise" the aforementioned worlds descend each to its place. Therefore they instituted Kaddish after every change of world. Only between redemption and prayer—even though they are two worlds, Beriah and Atzilut—they did not institute saying Kaddish in them, as the reason is explained in Shaar HaKavanot in the discourse on Kaddish end of section 14 side 4. And all this is explained in Shaar HaKavanot discourse 1 of Shacharit section 11 side 4 and section 12 side 1 and in the discourses on Kaddish; and in Pardes Rimonim Shaar HaTefillah chapter 6 and Shaar HaKaddish chapter 1 and in many places. And see also above siman 25 note 27. And there are more words and there is no place to explain them here—only an opening of words, to know how great is the virtue of prayer, which are matters standing at the height of the world, as our sages said in tractate Berachot page 6 side 2. And even if we do not know what He will do with what our hand touches, at least one should intend the meaning of the words and especially in the mention of the divine names, and not omit any word or letter—and the the Omnipresent who knows secrets and examines hearts will join thought to deed in His abundant mercy and kindness.
