The passages of the offerings should be recited while standing, corresponding to the offerings, which were brought while standing. Emek HaBerachah. And he should intend the first time, etc. This is merely an added precaution, so that it not appear as though he is offering two daily offerings.
The verses of the Musaf offering. Although Shacharis has not yet been prayed, and one may not bring the Musaf offering before the daily offering, nevertheless the passage of the daily offering has already been recited. If reciting the verses is regarded like offering the sacrifice, then reciting the verses of the daily offering is likewise regarded as its offering.
In order to publicize. A support for this appears in the Mishnah at the end of Shekalim, where the limbs of the Rosh Chodesh offering were placed above on the altar in order to publicize that it was Rosh Chodesh. See the end of Sukkah.
To sway. This refers to Pesukei DeZimrah. During the Amidah one should not sway. Responsa Menahem Azariah, siman 113. The work Yismach Moshe, cited in Knesses HaGedolah, part 1, siman 33, writes that some pious individuals would move gently only at the conclusions of blessings, in fulfillment of, “Before My Name he was humbled.” Maharil writes that he would sway during prayer from front to back and from back to front. Darkei Moshe writes that Recanati, in parashas Yisro, gives a kabbalistic reason for swaying during prayer, and Zohar says likewise. One who acts according to one view may do so, and one who acts according to the other may do so, provided he concentrates.
