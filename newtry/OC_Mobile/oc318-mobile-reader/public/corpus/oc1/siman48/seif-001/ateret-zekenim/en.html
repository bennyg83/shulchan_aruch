The passage of the daily offering is recited. Before the passage of the daily offering, one should read from Bereishis each day the portion corresponding to that day and the following day, as they did in the Beis HaMikdash. Afterward one recites the passage of the laver, then the passage of the removal of the ashes, and then the passage beginning, “This is what you shall offer upon the altar,” in parashas Tetzaveh, through “where I shall speak with you,” because it resembles the passage of the daily offering. Afterward one recites the passage of the daily offering as printed in our siddurim, followed by, “He shall slaughter it on the northern side of the altar,” etc., “This is the law,” etc., and then the verse, “Then the offering of Yehudah and Yerushalayim will be pleasing to Hashem.” Afterward one says, “Abaye would arrange the order,” etc. Tur. Then one recites the passage beginning, “You shall make an altar for burning incense,” through “throughout your generations,” followed by the identifying signs and preparation of the incense, because the incense was burned after the daily offering. Beit Yosef, siman 1. Nevertheless, on Shabbos and Yom Tov one should also recite Pittum HaKetores after completing the prayer, as is our custom. The reason is that it drives away the external forces so that they not attach themselves to the prayer and to the spiritual abundance drawn down through prayer. Lechem Min HaShamayim. Afterward, before Baruch SheAmar, one should recite the passages of the burnt offering, meal offering, peace offering, sin offering, and guilt offering, if time permits. Some also recite the passage of the pure olive oil in parashas Emor and the passage of the lamps in parashas Beha'aloscha. Sichas HaSadeh. It appears, however, that this is not the proper place for those passages. Their appropriate time is before Minchah, toward evening, for the verse states, “In the morning, when he cleans the lamps,” and, “When Aharon kindles the lamps toward evening.” No authority, however, states that the lamps were kindled in the morning, except Rambam in chapter 4 of Hilchos Temidin UMusafin, who holds that their kindling is their cleaning. All the later authorities disagree and hold that the lighting occurred only toward evening, while the morning service involved merely preparing the wicks. This is also the view of Rashi and Onkelos at the end of parashas Tetzaveh, Raavad at the end of Hilchos Avodas Yom HaKippurim and Hilchos Temidin UMusafin, and Responsa Rashba, siman 79. Afterward one recites the order of the Temple service, “And this was the order of the daily offering,” etc. See there.
