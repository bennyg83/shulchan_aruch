(Note 1) And one should intend, etc.—for the relief of the matter; that is, from the perspective of law one need not intend explicitly that it be like reading in the Torah, and it suffices if he does not intend that his recitation be considered as a sacrifice-offering—and by default it is like reading in the Torah. Only for the relief of the matter should one intend that it be like reading in the Torah.

So that it should not be, etc.—this is the reason it requires that one not intend, lest the second time also be like a sacrifice-offering.
