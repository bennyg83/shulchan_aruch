(1) The daily offering. Its recitation stands in place of bringing the daily offering. Our Sages taught that when the Beis HaMikdash does not exist and offerings cannot be brought, one who engages in studying the offerings and their passages is regarded by Scripture as though he brought them. For this reason some also recite the order of the altar service, and this is the basis of the widespread custom today to say each day, “Abaye would arrange,” etc. It appears obvious that one who can study from a sefer should learn in the Gemara the explanation of this teaching. Likewise, when we recite each day the details of preparing the incense, one should understand what he is saying. In this manner, the recitation is regarded in place of the incense service. The sefarim likewise write that when the Gemara says, “Anyone who engages in the passage of the burnt offering,” etc., it means that he engages in understanding its subject, not merely reciting the words. Magen Avraham writes that the passages of the offerings should be said while standing, corresponding to the offerings, which were brought while standing. Shaarei Teshuvah cites several later authorities who disagree. Peri Megadim writes that one should nevertheless stand for the passage of the daily offering when it is read aloud by the congregation. Baer Heitev cites Derech Chochmah that one should take care to recite the passage of the daily offering with the congregation together with the chazzan. Shaarei Teshuvah challenges this. Peri Megadim writes that the wording printed in siddurim, “as though we had offered the daily offering,” is preferable to the version “as though I had offered.” The first-person singular wording is appropriate in the passage of a sin offering, where the individual is the owner, but for the daily offering no individual is called its owner. Tur's version reads, “as though the daily offering had been brought at its proper time,” and this is correct. Shaarei Teshuvah also defends our customary wording.
(2) With the congregation. This refers to a place where the custom is for the congregation to recite only the passage of the daily offering aloud.
(3) And repeat it. Their earlier custom was to recite everything together aloud. Accordingly, an individual could not remain silent when the congregation recited the passage, because his silence would be conspicuous.
(4) And he should intend. See Tur. This is merely an added precaution, so that it not appear as though he is offering two daily offerings.
(5) While they pray. Some poskim disagree and hold that one should not sway during the Amidah. The custom is to sway during Pesukei DeZimrah, the blessings of Krias Shema, and Torah study, even Oral Torah. Magen Avraham writes that one who acts according to one view may do so, and one who acts according to the other may do so. Everything depends upon the individual. If movement helps him concentrate, he should sway. If not, he should stand still, provided that he directs his heart properly. Some people move in a distorted manner, keeping the body still while turning the head arrogantly from side to side. One should not act in this way.
(6) In order to publicize. Yom Tov does not require such publicizing because it was already publicized the preceding day. Shabbos, however, although it is well known, is mentioned at Shacharis because its Musaf passage cannot be read from the Torah, since it contains only two verses, as will be explained in siman 283.
