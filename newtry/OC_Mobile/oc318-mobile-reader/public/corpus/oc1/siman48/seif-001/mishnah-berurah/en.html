(1) The tamid—and it is in place of offering the tamid sacrifice, for the Sages accepted that when the Temple is not standing and one cannot offer sacrifices, whoever occupies himself with them and with their sections, Scripture considers it as if he offered them. Therefore some also say the order of the arrangement for this reason; and this is what spread as custom in our days to say every day "Abbaye was arranging," etc. And it seems to me straightforward that one who knows Sefer Mitzvah should learn in the Gemara the explanation of this statement, and likewise what we say every day regarding making the incense, so that he understand what he is saying; and thereby his recitation counts in place of burning incense. And so the books wrote regarding what the Gemara said "whoever occupies himself with the section of olah," etc.—the intent is that he occupies himself to understand its matters, not merely reciting the words alone. Magen Avraham wrote: the section of the offerings one says standing, like the offerings that were standing; and see in Sha'arei Teshuvah in the name of several later authorities who disagree on this; and in Peri Megadim he wrote that nevertheless in the section of the tamid it is fitting to stand, since they recite it in the congregation in a loud voice. Also what Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of Derech Chochma that one should be careful to say the section of the tamid in the congregation with the prayer leader—see in Sha'arei Teshuvah who challenged this. Pri Megadim wrote: what is printed in siddurim in the nusach of the Holy One, blessed be He, "as if we offered the tamid sacrifice," and there are texts that read "I offered"—this is not correct, for "I offered" in the section of sin-offering refers to owners, whereas in the tamid an individual is not called an owner; and in the Tur the text is "as if the tamid was offered in its time," etc., and this is correct; and in Sha'arei Teshuvah he reconciles our text as well.

(2) With the congregation—meaning that in that place their custom was to say only the section of the tamid alone with the congregation.

(3) And to return—because their custom was to say everything together in a loud voice; and if so, he could not remain silent when they said it in the congregation, for it would be conspicuous.

(4) And one should intend—see in the Tur that this is only for the relief of the matter, so it should not appear as if offering two daily offerings.

(5) At the time of praying—and there are decisors who disagree on this and say that in prayer one should not sway, only in pesukei dezimra, the blessing of Shema, and Torah study—even by heart the custom is to sway. Magen Avraham wrote: "one who acts like this master acts, and one who acts like that master acts." And everything depends on the person: if he concentrates well through movement, he may sway; and if not, he should stand still—provided he directs his heart. And some sway with a distorted movement: the body stands in place while only the head turns once right and once left in a manner of arrogance—one should not do so.

(6) In order to publicize—but on Yom Tov there is no need to publicize, since it is already known from yesterday; but on Shabbat, even though it is known, they say it at Shacharit, since it cannot be read in the Torah because it has only two verses, as will be explained in siman 283.
