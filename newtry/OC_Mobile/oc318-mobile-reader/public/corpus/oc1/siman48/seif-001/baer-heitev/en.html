The Tamid—standing, similar to korbanot which were offered standing; and above siman 1 seif kaf yud-beit. And one should be careful to say it with the congregation together with the shaliach tzibbur, for the Korban Tamid was offered by the congregation (Derech Chochmah).

To move—this means in pesukei dezimra, but in prayer one should not move, as in Orach Chayyim siman kuf yud-gimmel; and Knesset HaGedolah brought that some commentators are accustomed to move gently only at the conclusions of blessings to fulfill "because of those who are at ease" etc.; and in Maharil it is written that he would move in prayer from in front to behind and from behind to in front, with God's help; and in Darkei Moshe it is written that the Rekanati gave a kabbalistic reason for moving in prayer; and Magen Avraham wrote: one who acts like the master acts, and one who acts unlike the master acts—provided that he have proper intention.
