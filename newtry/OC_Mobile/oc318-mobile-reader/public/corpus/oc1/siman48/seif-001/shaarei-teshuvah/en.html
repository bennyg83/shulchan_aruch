The tamid—see Be'er Heitev. And see Magen Avraham who wrote that the section of offerings one says standing, etc.; and in Bekhor Shor he wrote one need not stand even for the section of the tamid that Magen Avraham brought from Orach Chayyim—and I have never seen anyone concerned for this; and so in Eliyah Rabbah; and in Machatzit Hashekel he wrote that many Torah scholars were not concerned for this; and Mor u-Ketzia likewise ruled, and wrote that he saw his father the gaon say it seated. And in Sha'arei Teshuvah he wrote to prove this from the Ari on the verse "the Lord is King" on Shemini Atzeret in Bechor Shor—that what one says "as if I offered" refers to owners who gave to the priest, and therefore one need not stand; and in Peri Megadim he wrote that nevertheless in the section of the tamid it is fitting to stand, since they recite it in the congregation in a loud voice. And in Bechor Shor he wrote there what Or Zarua said—that one should read in the synagogue like the service—also one need not be concerned for this; but since it issued from Rabbi Kruspedai's mouth the language "like synagogues," etc., it is good to do so. See below what Derech Chochma wrote on this; and see there what is in siddurim "as if I offered" or "as if we offered"—this is not correct, for "I offered" in the section of sin-offering refers to owners, whereas in the tamid an individual is not called an owner; and in the Tur he wrote "as if the tamid was offered," etc., and this is correct; see there. And for this reason it does not apply, as we say in Taanit that therefore they instituted ma'amadot—because a person's sacrifice is not offered while he does not stand; therefore they instituted, etc. And this is because every person in Israel has a share in the communal tamidim taken from the chamber contribution, and it is as if they gave the priest the tamid sacrifice on their behalf; and therefore when saying "may it be desired" in the section of the tamid it is also fitting to say "as if we offered" or "as if I offered"—that is, through the priest who is our agent and God's mercy. And what Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of Derech Chochma that one should be careful to say it in the congregation with the prayer leader because the tamid sacrifice was offered in the congregation—is difficult, for is the whole congregation standing at the time of offering? Only the ma'amad people; and since each says to himself "may it be desired, as if offered," etc., the congregation has no relevance here at all. And what Rama wrote "and to return to read the section of the tamid alone with the congregation and intend," etc.—this is not an obligation; only the intent of the words is explained in the Tur that it is proper for each to say "Master of the worlds," etc., and if he cannot say it because he is with the congregation, it is good that he read the section of the tamid alone and say those words, and one need not be concerned if he reads it again with the congregation; and for the relief of the matter he should intend that it is like reading in the Torah, etc. And the meaning is: since their custom was that the whole congregation said everything together in a loud voice, when he says it alone he would have to be silent when they reach the section of the tamid with the congregation—and this is not proper; therefore he wrote one need not be concerned if he reads it again, etc. But it is obvious that if he prays alone, not with the congregation, he need not say it in the congregation specifically—for he wrote that even if he says it again in public he says it like reading in the Torah, and therefore the public recitation does not count as a sacrifice-offering example at all.

Siman 1—in Shulchan Aruch they say musaf verses; and the Arizal's view is not to say musaf verses after the section of the tamid, and certainly not Rosh Chodesh verses. See in Sha'arei Teshuvah several textual refinements in the order of Pitum Haketoret: one should say "You are Hashem our God" as the Ari taught; and "the tzori" must be with hataf-kamatz; and see Knesset HaGedolah in the name of Rav Rav Bezalel. "Returning them to the mortar"—not "and returning them." Even "keft of the Jordan"—and one says "mikeft" with mem, for its letters are like "shoulder," etc. One must say "whatever it is," not "whoever it is"—so wrote the Ari. "In order to smooth with it the nail"—so it should be, not "to wrap," as some books have. One must say "this is the rule: if according to its measure it is fit for halves, and if one is missing," etc.—so too Mahari Saruk. "Abbaye was arranging," etc.—the Rav wrote that "preceded" must be in each one only "and incense to the limbs" until "in the pans," when one must say "and in the pans precede," etc. "Please, by strength"—one says both words together "please, by strength" and pauses, then "greatness of Your right hand" and pauses, "release the bundle" and pauses; and similarly the rest, according to "with two he covers his face," etc. And in Shiray Knesset HaGedolah, siman 51, he wrote that some stand when saying "please, by strength." And what is not said after the incense "as if we burned incense"—Beit David wrote the reason: because we said "if one is missing," etc., and one should fear lest we were missing, etc.; see below siman 132 and in Magen Avraham note 5. And in Birkhei Yosef he wrote in the name of Maharam of Lanzano regarding what we say "speedily may the Temple be built"—one may refine and say "may the Temple be built," for the House will be the work of the hands of the Fashioner; "the Temple of God, His hands established," etc. And so one may say before "which is their place" and after the eighteen blessings on Shemini Atzeret—and it is not required. See in Tosafot, second chapter of Ma'aseh, in the name of the Yerushalmi; and also in the word "may it be built" one may explain that it depends on Heaven. See there what he wrote regarding "give thanks to God," etc., that it is in Divrei Hayamim; and in Tehillim a mistake fell into books, as with "the land of Canaan," and the two where it is written "God, God of Israel"—it is not so in Divrei Hayamim; and what we say is from Divrei Hayamim. One must pause slightly between "in the water" and "the mighty," for the reason is a tefach under "in the water" and a break at the word "the mighty," for "the mighty" does not refer to "in the water" but to the Egyptians, as written "this mighty one, the Almighty God," etc., "from the mighty ones, these Egyptians," as written "they sank like lead in the water"; see there. Although "the mighty" can also refer to the water, nevertheless since it also refers to the Egyptians one should pause slightly, not saying it in one breath.
