1) [Seif 1], gloss: "And they say the passage of the Tamid, and some say the order of the arrangement," etc. The following is the text of prayer that Rabbi Chaim Vital received from the Arizal. It is recorded in Shaar HaKavanos, page 50d, and in Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaKorbanot. "Elokeinu v'Elokei avoteinu, remember us with a favorable remembrance before You and recall us with a remembrance of salvation," etc. The dalet of ufekudat is pronounced with a dagesh. Afterward one recites the passage of the Akeidah, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos, page 1b. In the prayer "Master of the universe, just as Avraham Avinu suppressed his compassion," etc., one should say "with the attribute of kindness and the attribute of mercy, enter for us beyond the strict line of judgment, and through Your great goodness let Your anger turn back," etc. One continues with the verses "Hashem your God will bring you," "He will benefit you and increase you more than your fathers," "Hashem, be gracious to us, we have hoped in You," "at a time of distress," "It is a time of distress for Yaakov," and "In all their distress He was distressed." In the siddur of the Rashash are also printed the verses from "Who is a God like You" through "which You swore to our fathers from days of old," and the verse "I will bring them to My holy mountain," etc. In "These are the things whose fruits a person eats in this world," the correct form is miperihem. In "A person should always be God-fearing in private as in public," the word kivgalui is with a kaf. One should say: "Sanctify Your Name in Your realm upon the people who sanctify Your Name, and through Your salvation, our King, raise and exalt our strength and save us soon for the sake of Your Name. Blessed is He Who sanctifies His Name among the multitude. Our Father in heaven, living and enduring, deal kindly with us," etc. After the passage of the Tamid, before the verse "Then the offering of Yehudah and Yerushalayim will be pleasant to Hashem," one should say Pittum HaKetoret, and afterward the verse "Then it will be pleasant." The text of Pittum HaKetoret is the same in the korbanot section, at the end of prayer, and at Minchah. The text begins: "You are Hashem our God before Whom our fathers burned the incense," etc.; then, "The Rabbis taught: the compounding of the incense," etc. One should not add the verse "The incense that you make according to its composition," as some people do. "He takes from it a double handful," etc. Shibolet nerd is 1 substance, not 2. "Kilufah, 3." In "yayin Kafrisin," the first yod is pronounced with tzerei, not patach, because it is construct to Kafrisin. "Sodomite salt, a quarter," and there is no need to add "of a kav," because an unspecified quarter in Mishnah and baraita means a quarter-kav. "Kipat HaYarden, any amount," is read with a chirik. Mishnat Chassidim writes that the proper reading is kipat, whose letters correspond to katef. Shaliach Tzibbur and the siddur of the Rashash agree. "Why is borit karshina brought? To polish the tziporen so that it be attractive. Why is yayin Kafrisin brought? To soak the tziporen so that it be strong. Would not urine be better for it?" etc. "It was taught, R' Natan says," etc. "Bar Kappara taught," etc. "Because the voice is beneficial for the spices." The bet of labesamim has a sheva, not a kamatz, because the Gemara in Keritot 6b explicitly teaches that the breath of the voice emitted while pounding the spices helps their fragrance spread. One concludes: "For no leaven and no honey may you burn as a fire-offering to Hashem." At this point one says the 3 verses: "Hashem Tzevakot is with us," "Hashem Tzevakot, fortunate is the person who trusts in You," and "Hashem, save; may the King answer us on the day we call." Every time Pittum HaKetoret is recited these 3 verses should follow it. Afterward one says "Then the offering of Yehudah," etc. No other verse and no additional prayer should be added after it, including the prayer printed in some siddurim beginning "Master of the universe, it is revealed and known before You that the attribute of judgment is extended," even during an epidemic, and one should likewise not add other verses customarily recited after Pittum HaKetoret. The Arizal did not recite on Shabbos at this point "And on the Shabbos day, 2 lambs," as is the custom of some Sephardim, because this belongs to the Shabbos Mussaf offering and its time does not arrive until after completion of Shacharit. "Abaye would arrange," etc. In Yoma 33 and in Tur the word hava is absent, and it is proper to say simply "Abaye mesader," lest the final yod of Abaye join the next word and produce an inappropriate pronunciation of the Divine Name. Some move the word hava after mesader for this reason, saying "Abaye mesader hava," and this too is proper. When reaching "the incense precedes the limbs," one need not repeat the word "precedes" thereafter; rather, say "the incense, the limbs; the limbs, the minchah," and so on, until the bowls, which are last. Then say, "the bowls precede the afternoon Tamid," etc., and "upon it complete all the offerings," not "he completed." In Ana Bechoach, the words should be joined in pairs: "Ana bechoach," pause; "gedulat yemincha," pause; "tatir tzerurah," pause. It appears that the reason is that each of the 7 Names of 42 contains 6 letters divided into 3 pairs, corresponding to "with 2 he covers his face, with 2 he covers his feet, and with 2 he flies." Each pair should therefore be joined. The Arizal would recite this praise with melody and song. At its conclusion one says Baruch Shem Kevod Malchuto Le'olam Va'ed. "Master of the universe, You commanded us to offer," etc. One should say, "May this utterance of our lips be considered, accepted, and pleasing before You," etc. Whenever the Arizal read a passage or verse from Torah, Prophets, or Writings, he would chant it according to its proper cantillation. He did not recite before Eizehu Mekoman the verses "Awaken, north wind, and come, south wind," or "I have come to My garden," as some people do. "R' Yishmael says: through 13 principles," etc. In the phrase "and here are 2 verses that contradict one another," the kaf is pronounced with patach, not tzerei. Then one says Kaddish Al Yisrael. "Hodu laHashem kiru vishmo," etc. One should say "mofetav umishpetei fihu," not piv. "Hashem Melech, Hashem Malach, Hashem Yimloch," etc., is said twice while standing, and afterward the verse "Hashem shall be King over all the earth," also standing. Afterward one may sit and say "Save us" through "Hallelujah." On weekdays one then says "For the conductor, with instrumental music, a psalm, a song." On Shabbos one says "The heavens declare the glory of God," and afterward the regular sequence of psalms from "Sing joyously, righteous ones, in Hashem" through completion of the Great Hallel. For Baruch She'amar one must stand and remain standing until the conclusion "King praised with praises," and afterward one may sit and say Mizmor LeTodah. Upon reaching Baruch She'amar one should touch the 2 front tzitzit and hold them until the end of the blessing. The 2 tzitzit and Baruch She'amar both correspond to the realm of Yetzirah. Baruch She'amar contains 87 words and 13 occurrences of Baruch. The text is: "Blessed is He Who spoke and the world came into being; blessed is He. Blessed is He Who says and does. Blessed is He Who decrees and fulfills. Blessed is He Who performs creation. Blessed is He Who has mercy upon the earth. Blessed is He Who has mercy upon the creatures. Blessed is He Who gives a good reward to those who fear Him. Blessed is He Who lives forever and endures eternally. Blessed is He Who redeems and saves. Blessed is His Name. Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, God, merciful Father, praised by the mouth of His people, lauded and glorified by the tongue of His pious ones and servants. With the songs of David Your servant we shall praise You, Hashem our God, with praises and songs; we shall magnify You, praise You, glorify You, enthrone You, and mention Your Name, our King, our God, Unique One, Life of the worlds, King praised and glorified forever and ever, His great Name. Blessed are You, Hashem, King praised with praises." The word hamerachem is pronounced with sheva beneath the bet and chirik in the resh; mehulal befi amo is said with peh, not befi with a yod, because befeh has the numerical value 87, corresponding to the 87 words in Baruch She'amar. In betishbachot the shin has a chirik, not a shuruk. Mizmor LeTodah is said seated. In Yehi Kevod, the verse "He is merciful" is said even on Shabbos. Vayevarech David is said standing until the 4 words Atah Hu Hashem HaElokim, whose initials form Ehyeh. Afterward one may sit at Asher Bacharta. In Az Yashir, the verse "Hashem will reign forever and ever" is said twice in the Holy Tongue and a third time in the Aramaic translation, "Hashem malchutei ka'im," etc. One then continues "For Pharaoh's horse came," "and the children of Israel walked," "For kingship belongs to Hashem," "Saviors shall ascend," "Hashem shall be King," and Yishtabach. One should not say the verse Shema Yisrael before Yishtabach, as appears in some printed siddurim. Nor should one say "uvechen Yishtabach" except on Shabbos, when Nishmat Kol Chai is recited before it. In Yishtabach, "greatness, might, praise, and splendor," greatness precedes might, because that is the order of the 10 sefirot, and so appears in the Zohar, Parashat Terumah. "Blessed are You, Hashem, great King and praised with praises"; the tav in tishbachot has a chirik for the reason stated above concerning Baruch She'amar. One concludes, "Who chooses songs of praise, King, God, Life of the worlds. Amen." I heard from a sage that this praise was instituted by Avraham Avinu and that this is hinted in the initials after the blessing, but I did not receive this tradition from my teacher. Another tradition states that the beginning of Yishtabach was instituted by Shlomo HaMelech and the concluding blessing by Avraham Avinu. One then says Kaddish through da'amiran be'alma. The Kaddish text is the standard siddur text. The words di vara are 2 separate words, not devara as 1 word. From veyishtabach through veyithalal, each of the 7 words begins with vav. In tushbechata the first tav has a chirik. This form applies in all Kaddish recitations, even the mourner's Kaddish recited for a deceased father. The shaliach tzibbur says "Bless Hashem, the blessed One," and the congregation answers, "Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One, forever and ever." While the shaliach tzibbur says Barchu, the congregation quietly recites the praise "May the Name of the supreme King of kings be praised and glorified," as printed in the siddur and as Rama writes in siman 57. The remaining order of the blessings of Shema, Shema itself, Emet VeYatziv, the Amidah, and the sections following prayer should be recited according to the established text received from the Arizal, with exact care for the order of words and the Divine Names. One should avoid additions not recorded in Shaar HaKavanos and Pri Etz Chaim, because each section corresponds to a specific spiritual world and rectification. The arrangement is not merely stylistic; changing the placement of Kaddish, Kedushah, Barchu, or the principal Scriptural passages can alter the intended sequence of ascent from Asiyah to Yetzirah, Beriah, and Atzilut. The general principle throughout this order is that every Scriptural verse should be read accurately, every blessing should retain its received wording, and the transition prayers should be said only in their proper place. Where the Arizal omitted a customary additional verse or prayer, one should likewise omit it. Where he stood, sat, held the tzitzit, joined words, paused between pairs, or repeated a verse, one should follow that practice. This is the prayer-order transmitted by Rabbi Chaim Vital from the Arizal and recorded in Shaar HaKavanos and Pri Etz Chaim.
