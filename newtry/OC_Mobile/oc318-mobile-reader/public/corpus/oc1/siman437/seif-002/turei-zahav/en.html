(א) The tenant rents a house on the 14th and does not know if it was checked, etc. — this is also per Tosafot's explanation: it discusses where the kinyan — chazaka — was not at the beginning of the night of the 14th but after part of the checking time had passed the tenant entered part of the checking time; and before that the key was in the landlord's hand — therefore the obligation was on the landlord; and now that he entered part of the time, the tenant must check the part he did not check.

(ב) If he is in town, etc. In the Gemara Bava Metzia: Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak — one who rents on the 14th — is it presumed checked or not presumed checked? He challenges per the one who says we ask him; and answers there is no case of asking. Ran wrote on this challenge of asking: if he is in town, even if it would be presumed checked we ask him; and from the fact the Gemara challenged asking, it implies that even when presumed checked we ask — unlike the view that when presumed checked one need not ask.
