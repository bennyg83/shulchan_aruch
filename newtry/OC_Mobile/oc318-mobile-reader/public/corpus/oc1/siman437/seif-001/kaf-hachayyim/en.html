1) [Se'if 1] One who rents a house to another for the fourteenth, etc. This means that one rented it to him on the thirteenth for use from the fourteenth onward. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
2) There. And acquired it through one of the modes of acquisition, etc. This means by money, by a document, by taking possession, or by an act of symbolic acquisition. In other words, by money or by a document, and so forth, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, simanim 190, 191, and 192. See there. If the tenant has not yet acquired it, however, then even if the key is in the tenant's possession, the tenant need not search. This is like one who entrusted a key to another person, where the custodian need not search. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Peri Chadash; Chok Yaakov, subsection 1. All this follows the Shulchan Aruch, who rules according to the explanations of Ran and Maggid Mishneh. Tosafos, however, explain that once the key is in the tenant's possession, the tenant must search even if the tenant has not yet acquired the house. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 2, halachah 18, rules likewise. Levush also cites this position of Tosafos in the name of some authorities. Chok Yaakov writes there that it is proper to take into account the stringencies of both explanations. Chok Yosef, at the end of subsection 1, similarly writes that it is reasonable to be stringent in accordance with Tosafos. Peri Chadash, however, agrees with the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. Maharam Galanti rules likewise, as cited by Yad Aharon in Hagahos Beis Yosef. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, and Chayei Adam, rule 119, subsection 18, also rule likewise. This also appears to be the opinion of the Vilna Gaon.
3) Nevertheless, if a person entrusted the key to the house to someone else, even according to Tosafos the custodian is not obligated to search it. Their ruling applies only when the other person wishes to take possession of and acquire the house, as explained in Tosafos there and cited by Beis Yosef and the later authorities. It appears that if one entrusted the entire house to another person, that person must search, since both the key and complete responsibility for guarding the house were transferred to that person. Chukas HaPesach, subsection 2.
4) If the tenant acquired the house on the thirteenth through one of the modes by which a rental of land is acquired, but the key is still in the landlord's possession, Bach writes that according to Tosafos the tenant is obligated to search. Chok Yosef writes likewise there. Peri Chadash, however, writes that even according to Tosafos the landlord is obligated, since the key remains in the landlord's possession. See there. Chok Yaakov writes likewise there.
