For the purpose of the fourteenth. This is Ran’s wording. Although the Gemara initially says, “One who rents a house to another on the fourteenth,” Ran explains it to mean that it was rented on the thirteenth for use on the fourteenth. This interpretation is correct according to the Gemara’s conclusion, which says, “If the fourteenth arrived after he delivered the key,” implying that the rental had already taken effect. Rambam, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Agudah, and the other poskim follow this. Tosafos at the beginning of Pesachim initially understands the question as limited to a rental occurring on the fourteenth, when the inspection obligation had already begun before the rental. If rented on the thirteenth, Tosafos initially assumes the tenant must inspect. It is possible, however, that after the Gemara concludes that everything depends upon delivery of the key, Tosafos also accepts that there is no distinction between renting on the thirteenth or fourteenth. Rif, Rosh, and Tur copy the Gemara’s language and therefore provide no decisive indication. Their words may also be explained like Ran. Tur’s intent is explicit in Kitzur Piskei HaRosh, which says, “One who rents a house on the thirteenth or fourteenth.” Maggid Mishneh also holds this view, and it is primary, unlike Bach, who insists that the law concerns only a rental on the fourteenth and attributes this to Rif, Rosh, and Tur without proof.
And he acquired it through one of the methods by which a real property rental is acquired. This implies that the tenant must have made a valid acquisition. If he has not yet acquired the rental, he has no inspection obligation even if the key is in his possession. Ran writes explicitly that where the tenant acquired the rental properly, but the landlord retained the key and the tenant still cannot enter, the landlord must inspect because the house and chametz remain his. If the landlord delivered the key before the fourteenth, then because the tenant acquired the rental properly and can enter, the tenant must inspect. Thus, according to Ran, valid acquisition is essential. Mere possession of the key without acquisition is only a deposit of the key and imposes no obligation. Maggid Mishneh holds similarly. Their words further imply that this distinction concerns rental, because ownership of the house remains with the landlord. In a sale, once the purchaser acquires the property, the obligation rests upon him even if the seller still holds the key, because the seller retains no ownership. Tosafos, however, appears to hold that even without formal acquisition, if the person intends to acquire the house and possesses the key, he must inspect. The same would apply to a sale. If he lacks the key, the landlord or seller must inspect even after acquisition. This is the clear implication of Tosafos in Bava Kamma 22. Bach misunderstood Tosafos as referring only to a case without acquisition, while holding that after acquisition the tenant must inspect even without the key. This is not correct. The Gemara’s question plainly concerns a case where the tenant acquired the property, as shown by the phrase that the prohibition is in his domain. Without acquisition, it would not be in his domain. According to Tosafos, the concluding baraisa, which makes the law depend upon the key, applies equally whether or not formal acquisition occurred. For practical halachah, Bach favors Tosafos and notes that Kesef Mishneh also appears to do so. The Mechaber, however, rules plainly like Ran. Levush cites both opinions. It appears proper to act stringently in accordance with both interpretations.
If he did not deliver the key. Even if the key had been delivered to the tenant and the tenant then redeposited it with the landlord, the landlord must inspect. This is ruled in Responsa Maharam Schick, siman 1, and follows Ran because the chametz is his and the key is in his possession. If the key was deposited with a third person, however, the landlord is not obligated according to all opinions, as Tosafos writes and as the Yerushalmi explicitly states.
The key. In a house that is not locked, if the tenant has already taken possession, the tenant must remove the chametz, as implied by Ran and Tosafos.
The landlord must inspect. According to the opinion in seif 3 that in places where inspection is performed for payment the tenant must pay, it is possible that the tenant must reimburse the landlord for the inspection, since the tenant acquired and took possession of the house.
The tenant must inspect. He must also nullify. Although the chametz belongs to the landlord, the landlord is presumed to have abandoned it, and the tenant who may acquire it should nullify it. Magen Avraham concludes that the landlord should also nullify and says this is implied by Tosafos. I see no such implication in Tosafos.
