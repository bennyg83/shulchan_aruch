"And some say that he must return to him the wage for the bedikah, etc." NB: See my work on Yoreh De'ah, mahadura 7, Hilchot Bekhor, siman 320, in my work siman 43, where I wrote a proof to this from the Gemara in Bekhorot; see there, with God's help. Now it appears to me that there is proof to the contrary from Bekhorot 48, where it says that even though we learned that before the threshing season arrives it is permitted to slaughter and sell, nevertheless a person is pleased to fulfill a mitzvah with his property. See there. In that case, if one animal becomes sacred, he loses money; although maaser belongs to the owner, the sacrificial portions go to Heaven, and perhaps it will be found to be a treifah and he will need another firstborn. And you should know that one cannot say there is no monetary loss, for if so why do we say in Bekhorot 35: Rav Ketina said that a doubtful firstborn born in a Jew's herd requires two witnesses from the marketplace to testify about its blemish, while Rav Nachman said that the owners may testify; for if you do not say so, regarding maaser according to Rabbi Meir, who testifies about it? A definite maaser is believed, for if he wished he could place a blemish on the whole herd. Why say he could make them all blemished? It could have said simply that a definite firstborn involves monetary loss because he must give it to a kohen, so he is not believed, whereas maaser belongs to the owners and involves no loss, so he is believed. Perforce this proves that maaser too involves monetary loss, as above, and nevertheless we say that a person is pleased to fulfill a mitzvah with his property even where he has loss. Yet from Rashi's wording there, "a person is pleased to fulfill the mitzvah with his property, that it be tithed and he tithe," it seems otherwise. His intent cannot be merely that there are two mitzvot, that his whole herd be subject to tithe and that he tithe the tenth, for why would he need such length? One cannot say that his difficulty was that he could sell everything first and consecrate one animal as shelamim, for what difficulty is that? In shelamim he would have to give the breast and thigh to the kohanim, whereas in maaser he would not have to give anything at all. Since he could fulfill the mitzvah without monetary loss, he would not be pleased to lose money. What forced Rashi to write that he is pleased "that it be tithed and he tithe"? Therefore it appears that his intent is based on the Sages' teaching, "You shall surely tithe," tithe so that you become wealthy. This is Rashi's intent: he is pleased to fulfill the mitzvah with his property and to tithe in order to become wealthy, for this will make him rich. Why was this needed? Perforce Rashi holds like those who say that where there is monetary loss we do not say that a person is pleased to fulfill a mitzvah with his property. Therefore it was difficult to him why here he is pleased, and he explained that maaser is different because he becomes wealthy through it, so it is not considered monetary loss. Rashi thus supports the view that he must return the wage for the bedikah. Examine carefully.
