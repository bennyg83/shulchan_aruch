This rationale is insufficient to void a completed transaction, because the sale has already taken effect and money cannot be extracted on that basis. But one may not initially use another person’s deposit for a mitzvah by saying that the owner is pleased for a mitzvah to be performed with his property. In truth, this landlord also acted improperly and deceitfully.
And some say that he must reimburse him for the cost of the inspection. Beit Hillel, Yoreh Deah, siman 291, seif 1, wonders why Rama did not add the same ruling there regarding one who rents a house on the assumption that it has a mezuzah and discovers that it does not, where the landlord should reimburse him for the money spent. He leaves the matter requiring study. See Magen Avraham, who distinguishes that there the mezuzah itself retains the value of the money paid for it even after the rental period ends. Bach rules like the first opinion, that money cannot be extracted from one who is already in possession. Olas Shabbos likewise questions this ruling, because the statement that a person is pleased to perform a mitzvah with his money implies that the landlord need reimburse nothing. Although Magen Avraham strains to distinguish the cases, his explanation is difficult.
