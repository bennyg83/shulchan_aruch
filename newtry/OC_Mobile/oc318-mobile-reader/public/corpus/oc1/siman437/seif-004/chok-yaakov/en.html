Because it had an established presumption that it was not inspected. This implies that the ruling applies specifically where the house is presumed not to have been inspected. If it is known with certainty to contain chametz and they say, “We inspected and removed it,” perhaps they are not believed. This appears to be Rama’s view at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 127. He writes that one who testifies leniently concerning a Rabbinic prohibition is believed when no prohibition has been established, such as bedikas chametz, because the Sages trusted him in a Rabbinic matter. Here, however, Rama rules without qualification that they are believed even where the house was presumed not inspected. His intent must therefore be as explained above. This resolves Magen Avraham’s question against Rama.
