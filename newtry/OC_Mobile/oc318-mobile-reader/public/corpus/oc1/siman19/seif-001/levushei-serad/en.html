Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. “Your garment” implies specifically. Meaning, a nighttime garment is excluded by the verse, “and you shall see it.” Rambam maintains that the verse does not indicate that the law depends upon the garment's designation, but only upon the time when it is worn. This garment, however, is excluded by the phrase “your garment.” Rambam agrees that this wording indicates that the law depends upon designation. Magen Avraham asks from the Gemara's statement, “And Shmuel concedes.” This implies that according to Rav, who maintains that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person, there is no practical distinction in this case. Yet even according to Rav there should be a distinction if a living person wears it, according to Rambam, who does not make the law depend upon designation. The Gemara should therefore have cited “your garment” to teach that designation is determinative, creating a practical distinction even according to Rav. We must therefore conclude that even the phrase “your garment” does not prove that the law depends upon designation. Magen Avraham concludes that according to Rosh it is nevertheless reasonable that burial garments are exempt even if worn by a living person. If one asks, according to Rosh why does the Gemara say “Shmuel concedes,” the answer is that according to Rav this is no novelty at all. Since Rav maintains that even the law of a nighttime garment depends upon designation, the same plainly applies here.
