{Rama: Magen Avraham, seif katan 2:} “Your garment” implies specifically—that is, a night garment is excluded, since its status as a garment is diminished. The Rambam holds the word “garment” when hanging alone has no implication, only when worn; but this is diminished from “your garment,” and the Rambam concedes this wording implies hanging alone. On this Magen Avraham challenged from “and Shmuel concedes”—it implies that for Rav, who holds it is an obligation on the person, there is no difference here; yet even according to him there is a difference if he wears it, for per the Rambam, who does not require hanging alone, the Gemara should have brought “your garment” to teach hanging alone, with a difference even for Rav, since even the wording “your garment” does not imply exclusivity for hanging alone. He concludes: nevertheless, per the Rosh it is reasonable that shrouds are exempt even when worn; and if one challenges the Rosh—why does it say Shmuel concedes?—one can say that for Rav it is no special distinction at all, since he holds that even a night garment requires hanging alone. Clear.
