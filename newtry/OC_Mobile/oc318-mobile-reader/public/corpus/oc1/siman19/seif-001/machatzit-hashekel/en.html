(Seif katan 1) Over, etc. See the Gemara in the fourth chapter of Menachos, which teaches that this depends upon whether tzitzis is an obligation upon the person or upon the garment. We rule that one recites a blessing over an act that completes a mitzvah, but not over an act that does not complete it. According to the view that tzitzis is an obligation upon the garment, the obligation begins immediately when the garment is completed. Attaching tzitzis therefore completes the mitzvah, and a blessing is recited. According to our ruling that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person, the garment is not obligated until it is worn. Attaching the tzitzis therefore does not complete the mitzvah, and no blessing is recited at that time.
Unlike mezuzah, etc. For mezuzah one must recite: “Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to affix a mezuzah.”

See Shabbos 118a. He wishes to bring some indication that once a tzitzis string is severed, one must remove the garment immediately. The prohibition against walking in a tallis whose tzitzis are severed is obvious. The indication concerns even one who remains standing in one place while wearing such a tallis. The Gemara relates that Rav Yosef asked Rav Yosef, son of Rabbah, in what mitzvah his father had been especially careful. He answered: tzitzis. One day he was climbing a ladder, a tzitzis string broke, and he did not descend until he had repaired it. If the law requires everyone to remove the tallis immediately when a string breaks, then Rabbah certainly removed it at once. Once he was no longer wearing a four-cornered garment, the strict law permitted him to descend the ladder. Since we rule that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person, one who is not wearing a four-cornered garment is exempt, and is not obligated to purchase one merely to bring himself into a state of obligation, though doing so is proper, as stated at the beginning of siman 24. For the short period until the tzitzis could be repaired, there was certainly no concern. Rabbah's special stringency was therefore that he did not descend. If, however, the strict law did not require immediate removal, then either Rabbah did not remove the tallis, in which case there was no novelty in his not descending, because everyone agrees that walking while wearing a tallis with severed tzitzis is forbidden, or he did remove it and nevertheless did not descend, in which case the essential novelty is missing from his son's account. He should have said that Rabbah removed it and nevertheless did not descend. We must therefore conclude that everyone is required to remove the tallis, and there was no need to mention this because it follows the strict law.
In every case, to live in the house, etc. He should not use the formula “to affix a mezuzah,” because when he affixed it, the mitzvah was not yet complete, since he was not yet living there. The completion occurs only now, when he begins to reside there. He should therefore recite a blessing over dwelling in the house.
