(Seif katan 1) On this, etc. See in the Gemara, chapter 4 of Menachos, where they say there that this depends on whether one holds tzitzis is an obligation on the person or on the garment. As we rule: whenever one completes the mitzvah, one blesses; but when the mitzvah is not completed, one does not bless. Therefore, per the view that it is an obligation on the garment—if so, as soon as the garment is finished he is obligated in tzitzis, and when he makes tzitzis on it he completes the mitzvah; therefore he blesses. But according to us, who rule it is an obligation on the person—he is not obligated until he wears it, and it is not completion of the mitzvah; therefore he does not bless.

What he wrote regarding mezuzah, etc.: that one must bless “Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to affix a mezuzah.”

He affixes it when he dwells in it. If so, it would be completion of the mitzvah; however, one could say that as soon as [the tzitzis] snap he is obligated to remove [the tallis], even though Magen Avraham brought above, siman 13, seif katan 1, in the name of Mordechai: since it is not written “you shall not wear a garment without tzitzis,” it implies one may wear it without tzitzis—only afterward is there a positive commandment to attach tzitzis to it—see there. Nevertheless, Magen Avraham wrote there that this is from the Torah, but rabbinically it is forbidden to wear it without tzitzis.

And see Shabbos, page 118. He wanted to bring some implication that as soon as [tzitzis] snap one must remove [the tallis]—certainly one may not go out wearing a tallis whose tzitzis snapped, which is obvious to him as forbidden; rather, he wanted to bring some implication that even if one stands in one place and a tallis whose tzitzis snapped is upon him, he must remove it. For they say there that Rav Yosef said to Rabbi Yosef, son of Rabbah: “In what was your father especially careful?” He answered him: “In tzitzis.” And he related: one day he was going up a ladder; a tzitzis string snapped for him, and he did not descend until he attached tzitzis to it. If so—if the law for everyone is that one must remove the tallis immediately when [tzitzis] snap—then certainly Rabbah too would have removed it immediately; and since he was not wearing a four-cornered tallis, it would have been permitted by law to descend the ladder, for by law, as we rule an obligation on the person, when one is not wearing tzitzis one is exempt, and he also need not buy a tallis to bring himself to obligation—only that it is good and proper, as Rabbenu Shimshon wrote—and for a short time until he repairs the tzitzis there is certainly no concern; if so, the distinction of Rabbah is that he was stringent on himself and did not descend. But if from the law’s perspective one need not remove it immediately—if so, necessarily: if Rabbah did not remove it, what is the distinction that he did not descend the ladder? After all, going out wearing a tallis whose tzitzis snapped is certainly forbidden for everyone. And if in truth Rabbah removed it and nevertheless did not descend—that would be the distinction; if so, the main distinction is missing from his son’s testimony about him, and it should have said “and he removed it, yet nevertheless did not descend.” Rather, the conclusion is that everyone must remove it; therefore he did not need to mention the removal, for that is by law.

“To dwell in a house,” etc. And not with the wording of affixing a mezuzah, since at the time of affixing the mezuzah the mitzvah was not completed, as he did not yet dwell in it; the completion is now, at the time he dwells—therefore one blesses on dwelling.
