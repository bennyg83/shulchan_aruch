“Exempt.” Since its main making is not to cover a living person, it is like a night garment that is exempt even by day (Nachalas Yisrael). Even for the Rambam, who obligates a night garment by day, here is different: since it is written “upon your garments,” it implies specifically a garment designated for a living person, but this is not called a garment at all and is exempt (Beis Yosef). Question: the Gemara says Shmuel holds tzitzis is the tallis’s obligation, and Shmuel concedes regarding an elder who made [a garment] for his honor—meaning for his shrouds—that he is exempt, because “that you may cover yourself with it,” the Merciful One said, and this is not made for covering; Rabbeinu Yerucham should have said “your garments,” the Merciful One said, and it is exempt even per the view that it is the person’s obligation. Therefore it appears to me that specifically per the view that a folded vessel is obligated in tzitzis even if he does not wear it—for that the Gemara says: if it is made for shrouds and he does not wear it in his lifetime he is exempt because of “that you may cover yourself with it,” etc.; but per the view that it is the person’s obligation, meaning when he wears it—then even if made for shrouds, if he wears it in his lifetime he is obligated, for in any case he wears it—and so the Rambam, who omitted that we say regarding shrouds that they are exempt. Still, it is possible that per the Rosh, who holds a night garment is exempt even by day, then shrouds are exempt even if he wears them in his lifetime—see siman 18. Tosafos Nedarim, page 61, wrote: clothing sellers who wear the garments intending to show them to buyers are exempt; and if he wears them to pass customs duty, possibly he is obligated in tzitzis, as with kilayim—and Yoreh Deah siman 381.
