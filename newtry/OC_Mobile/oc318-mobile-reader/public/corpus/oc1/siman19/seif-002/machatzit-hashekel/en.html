(Seif katan 2) “Exempt,” etc. It is considered a night garment—that is, it has the status of a night garment, which is exempt even if worn by day, since it is designated for a time when one is exempt, namely night; and the same applies here, for it is designated for a dead person, who is exempt—even if a living person wears it he is exempt, per the Rosh regarding a night garment in siman 18.

“It is considered ‘your garment,’” etc. That is: why did the Gemara cite the exposition of “that you may cover yourself with it,” from which we do not learn exemption except when lying in a box—even for Shmuel, who generally obligates [tzitzis] when lying in a box? It should rather have cited the verse that Rabbeinu Yerucham brings, from which we learn exemption even when wearing it.

Therefore it appears to me that specifically [it is exempt], etc., and we do not expound “your garment,” as Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote.

And likewise the Rambam, who omitted this, etc.: for necessarily: if it is not worn—for us, who rule it is an obligation on the person—he is in any case exempt, even if made for a living person; and if he wears it, even if made for a dead person, he is obligated.

Tosafos in Niddah wrote, etc., regarding passing customs duty, etc.: in Kilayim we learned: garment sellers sell in their usual way, even wearing and showing to buyers; and the Gemara explains that it follows Rabbi Shimon, who holds that an unintended act is permitted. Afterward we learned: it is forbidden to wear kilayim even to pass customs duty with it; and Rash and Rosh explain that these two mishnayos disagree—for necessarily the mishnah that forbids passing customs duty holds like Rabbi Yehudah, that an unintended act is forbidden; but the Rambam ruled both mishnayos. And Rabbeinu Yerucham and Shach wrote there that this reason—that Rabbi Shimon holds that for passing customs duty he too concedes it is forbidden—for garment sellers, who do not intend wearing at all, is permitted according to Rabbi Shimon; unlike passing customs duty, where his intent is in any case for wearing, for the purpose of passing customs duty is: from garments one need not pay duty if one will not wear them—if so, he intends wearing; even if he does not intend warming, nevertheless he intends wearing. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote: since Tosafos in Niddah compare tzitzis here to kilayim regarding garment sellers for exemption—the same applies to comparing wearing them to pass customs duty regarding obligation.
