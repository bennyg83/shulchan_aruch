(Seif katan 2) Exempt, etc. It is treated like a nighttime garment, etc. Meaning, it has the law of a nighttime garment, which is exempt even if worn during the day because it is designated for a time of exemption, namely night. The same applies here: it is designated for a deceased person, who is exempt. This follows Rosh's view concerning a nighttime garment in siman 18.
It should be “your garment,” etc. Meaning, why does the Gemara cite the exposition of “with which you cover yourself”? From that verse we learn only that it is exempt when lying in a box, even according to Shmuel, who ordinarily obligates a garment lying in a box. It would have been preferable to cite the verse brought by Beit Yosef, from which we learn that it is exempt even when worn.
Therefore it appears to me that this applies specifically, etc., and we do not expound “your garment” as Beit Yosef writes.
This is also Rambam's view, for he omitted, etc. In any event, if it is not worn, then according to our ruling that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person, it is exempt even if made for a living person. If it is worn, then even if it was made for a deceased person, it is obligated.
Tosafos on Niddah write, etc. Regarding avoiding customs duties, etc. The Mishnah concerning kilayim teaches that clothing merchants may sell garments in their ordinary manner, even wearing them to display them to customers. The Gemara explains that this accords with Rabbi Shimon, who permits an unintended act. The next Mishnah teaches that it is forbidden to wear kilayim even in order to avoid paying customs duties. Rashi and Rosh explain that these two Mishnayos disagree. The Mishnah prohibiting wearing it to avoid customs follows Rabbi Yehudah, who forbids an unintended act. Rambam, however, rules in accordance with both Mishnayos. Beit Yosef and Shach explain there that Rambam maintains that even Rabbi Shimon agrees that wearing it to avoid customs is forbidden. Clothing merchants have no intention to wear the garment at all; they wear it only to display it. One who wears it to avoid customs, however, intends at least to wear it, because the entire purpose is to avoid customs duties. Garments worn as clothing are exempt from customs. Although he does not intend to obtain warmth, he does intend to wear the garment. Magen Avraham therefore writes that since Tosafos on Niddah compare tzitzis to kilayim regarding clothing merchants, exempting them, the same comparison should apply to one who wears garments to avoid customs duties, obligating him in tzitzis.
