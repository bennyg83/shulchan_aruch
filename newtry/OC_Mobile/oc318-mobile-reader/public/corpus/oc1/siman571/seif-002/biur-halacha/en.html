The Acharonim copied this. And similar to this Chayei Adam wrote, thus: even for known sins that are explained in Rokeach that one must fast many days for atonement — if he is a Torah scholar and his Torah is his occupation, even in this he should not afflict himself so much, only return before the Omnipresent with a whole heart and weep from the walls of the heart and learn more than he was accustomed, for Torah is a mikveh of purity and is compared to fire — whoever enters fire is entered in fire and purified; and he should reduce all pleasures and eat only for sustaining the body lest he weaken so he have strength for service of the Omnipresent, and not go to any feast, and all the more a feast of companionship; and he should observe Shabbat in every detail and fine point; and if possible he should fast at least one day each week, or at least each Monday-Thursday day until noon. And the essence is weeping and confession and fences that he never do this again, and the Omnipresent sees to the heart — end of his words. I copied this at length. And similar wrote Chayei Adam, thus: even for known sins explained in Rokeach that require fasting for atonement many days — if he is a Torah scholar and his Torah is his occupation, even in this he should not afflict himself so much, only return before the Omnipresent with a whole heart, etc. — as above. And the essence of Biur Halacha is the Acharonim and Chayei Adam who permit on known sins in Rokeach for a Torah scholar to increase learning rather than fast many days; if so, for sins not known in Rokeach all the more so he should not fast many days, only one day a week or Monday-Thursday until noon as they wrote; and Gra and Acharonim that one should not do this for the community — requires study.
