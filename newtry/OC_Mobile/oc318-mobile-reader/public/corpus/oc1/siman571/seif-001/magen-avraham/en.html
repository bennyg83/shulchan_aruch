And it appears to me that [the ruling] is not speaking of sins known to him, for even a Torah scholar is obligated to fast for them, as the Bach wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel; and even if he cannot afflict his soul. And see the Shelah, Parashas Nasso. However, in the Laws of Yom Kippur he wrote that a Torah scholar whose Torah is his occupation need not afflict himself so much for [his] sins, and should learn more than he was accustomed. And the Tur, siman 240, wrote in the name of the Raavad: a person who can fast one day without great effort, and even so eats a little in the manner of ordinary people, is not held to account for that eating, and receives reward for it, for he shows his soul a little pleasure and does not wish to afflict it, in the manner of that which is said, "He who is kind to his own soul is a man of kindness" (end quote). And the mussar works wrote that in the middle of his meal, while he still desires to eat, he should withdraw his hand from it, and this is accounted for him as a great [form of] affliction.
