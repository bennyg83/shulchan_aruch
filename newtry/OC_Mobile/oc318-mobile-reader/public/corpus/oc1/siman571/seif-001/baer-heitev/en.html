A sinner. This means if he wishes to fast as an act of piety; but if it is known that he sinned, he is obligated to fast even if he is a Torah scholar — Bach in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. However in Laws of Yom Kippur he wrote that a Torah scholar whose Torah is his occupation need not afflict himself so much over sins and should learn more than he was accustomed. And the books of musar wrote that in the middle of his eating, while he still desires to eat, he should withdraw his hand from it — and this is considered for him a great affliction. And know that all actions a person does and Torah he learns while he is wicked add strength to the kelipot until he repents and extracts from the kelipah. One whose tefillin fell — he should fast that day, and if on Shabbat — the next day. And for the strap — not required. And if the tefillin fell with the pouch, he gives a perutah to charity [Knesset HaGedolah]. Sefer Chassidim siman 283 wrote in fasting one should not eat at dawn a root called ekratz, and similarly on erev Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av, for when one swallows saliva he swallows sweetness. And in siman 336, one who strikes — needs to fast.
