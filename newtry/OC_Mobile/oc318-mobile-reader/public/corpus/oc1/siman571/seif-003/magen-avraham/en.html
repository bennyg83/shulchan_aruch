And teachers of children. For there are two matters here: they diminish work of Heaven, and they rob the creatures; for even other workers are forbidden to fast, for they diminish work in vain; and so in mishnah chapter 7 of Demai. And see Choshen Mishpat siman 333 seif 5; and in Sefer Chassidim siman 587 and 617 he wrote that all occupied with many communal needs, such as charity collectors — their law is like teachers; and see Choshen Mishpat siman 337 seif 9. And there is difficulty on what Rambam wrote chapter 2 of Laws of Nezirut: a Hebrew slave vows vows that involve affliction of the soul or impede work — his master need not compel him (meaning the vow is void by itself) — unlike the law to forbid other fruits upon him — end of his words; and in the Gemara Nazir 62 they explain the reason: it is like one who swore to harm others, for there is loss to the master who contradicts him and he is not strong for work — so Rashi and Tosafos (Avodah Zarah siman 267 seif 2). If so, there is a question: even a hired worker and day-laborer too the vow should not take effect, as explained that it is forbidden to fast. And it may be said: for a hired worker, since he can return at midday, he may vow; and if the master's mind is not at ease with him, he says go from my presence; and also a hired worker can leave within his time — and so Rambam end of Laws of Shevuot: only one whose soul is owned by another does the vow take effect. A minor boy of twelve who accepted a fast — his vow stands; and the father cannot annul his daughter's vows except until she is a girl of twelve years and six months, as Yoreh Deah siman 234; and in any case he must speak the language of annulment — see there regarding a husband in his wife's vows. And it appears to me even if the year she is pregnant, once six months after twelve years have arrived she is a bogeret; as in Torah scholar — specifically where they say "year or half-year," even the month of pregnancy is included; but here they say there is no difference between naarus and bogeret except six months alone — if so the month of pregnancy is not included. And it is in Kiddushin 64b that the father alone is believed regarding their years, and afterward he is not believed unless there are two witnesses; and so Rambam chapter 2 of Ishut; and see Even HaEzer siman 155 seif 15 and 169 seif 11; and it is implied somewhat in the Gemara that if their years are not known, his vow is not a vow — requires study; for behold it is a safek d'oraisa; and it may be said thus: when the father says he is a boy of twelve years, it is as certain even if he is very small.
