And teachers of children. For there are two [reasons]: they diminish the work of Heaven, and they rob people; for even other laborers are forbidden to fast, because they diminish the work of the employer. And so it is in the mishnah, chapter 7 of Demai. And see Choshen Mishpat, siman 333, se'if 5; and in Sefer Chassidim, simanim 507 and 617, he wrote that all who are occupied with the needs of the community, such as shtadlanim, their law is like that of teachers; and see Choshen Mishpat, siman 337, se'if 19. And there is a difficulty with what the Rambam wrote in chapter 2 of the Laws of Nezirut: regarding the vow of a slave, vows that involve affliction of the soul or that impede the work, his master need not compel him (meaning the vow is void of itself), unlike the law of one who forbids other produce upon them (end quote). And in the Gemara, Nazir folio 62, they explain the reason: it is like one who swore to harm others, for there is a loss to his master, in that he wastes away and is not as strong for work; so Rashi and Tosafos (see Yoreh Deah, siman 267, se'if 2). If so, there is a difficulty: even a Hebrew slave and a day-laborer too, the vow should not take effect, as explained above, that it is forbidden to fast. And one may say: a Hebrew slave, since he can withdraw at midday, may vow; and if his master's mind is not at ease with him, he says to him, "Go from before me"; and also a Hebrew slave can leave within his term. And so it appears from the Rambam at the end of the Laws of Shevuot, that specifically [this special rule applies to] one whose person is acquired by him. A minor boy of twelve years who accepted a fast: his vow stands; and the father cannot annul his daughter's vows except until she is a girl of twelve years and six months, as is written in Yoreh Deah, siman 234. And in any case he must speak in the language of annulment; and see there regarding a husband with his wife's vows. And it appears to me that even if the year is a leap year, once six months after twelve years have arrived she is a bogeret, as is written in Toras Chaim: specifically where they say "a year" or "half a year," even the intercalary month is included; but here they say there is nothing between naarus and bogeret except six months alone; if so, the intercalary month is not included. And it is [stated] in Kiddushin folio 64 that the father alone is believed regarding their years, and another is not believed unless there are two witnesses; and so wrote the Rambam, chapter 2 of Ishut; and see Even HaEzer, siman 155, se'if 15, and 169, se'if 11. And it is somewhat implied in the Gemara that if his years are not known, his vow is not a vow; and this requires further study, for behold it is a doubt of Torah law. And it is possible that this is what it means: when the father says he is twelve years old, it is treated as certain, even if he is very small [in stature].
