And likewise one who smells vessels etc. Tur disagrees on this, and this is his language — I did not understand his words, since he considers it made for smell as his words imply, for he did not exempt from blessing on it except because it has no substance; and it is true it was made for smell so vessels be perfumed — if so why not bless on it etc. Bach challenged: why did Tur not challenge in siman 216, i.e. what Shulchan Aruch seif 6 wrote that if they filtered perfumes from oil, some say one does not bless at all since it has no substance — he should have challenged thus as here; and he answered what Tur wrote above and Magen Avraham wrote plainly on all in Bavli he truly disagrees from this aspect that Tur mentioned here — this is not so, for Rosh does not deal at all with filtering, he only refers to what is mentioned in the Gemara. It appears there the perfumes produced smell in the oil, and whatever they filtered — the oil remained empty and oil itself is not made for smell but to eat or anoint; and we already ruled seif 2 that whatever one takes for food and not smell one does not bless even if it has good smell; therefore that oil's blessing is on what it is made for, i.e. eating, unless the perfumes remain visible — then he blesses on smell of perfumes, not on smell in the oil. But here in vessels he makes that they have smell and the benefit stands for them to smell good fragrance and they have no other blessing — therefore one should bless on smell in them per Tur's view. Afterward I found Rashal also explains similarly. And apparently it is difficult why the author wrote above it is proper to be careful not to smell due to doubt — he should have written here too, for one reason: what difference if smell entered in oil or in vessel? In my humble opinion David certainly holds for practical law one does not bless on what has no substance, meaning in a thing where smell is prominent, as Rambam — doubt in blessings is lenient; but above one can be careful not to smell that oil intentionally, certainly proper; not so here in perfumed vessels where it is customary — one cannot say to cancel worldly need people are accustomed to; therefore do not cancel and rely on Rambam's view and not bless, as appears to me. I saw: some householders who do not have perfumes for havdalah but only the vessel in which perfumes are ground has smell — if of wood they bless on it and make blessing in vain for all views, for even per Tur blessing applies only in vessels perfumed intentionally that vessels have smell; not so here he did not intend except to do his work in the vessel, not to produce smell there, but smell happens of itself — it is made not for smell; therefore blessing does not apply at all — simple in my humble opinion.
