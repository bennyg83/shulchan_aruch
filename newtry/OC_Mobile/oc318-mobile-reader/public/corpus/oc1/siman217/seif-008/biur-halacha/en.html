Mixed smell — this is Rambam's language; and Sefer HaRokeach wrote it refers only to oil and perfumes of the dead and of the Temple and the like that are not for smell; and on this he wrote if smell of these mixed with good smell we follow the majority; but it does not refer to what arose in the law of majority idolaters and majority Israel — for in that certainly even if a minority smell of idolaters mixed in one does not bless, for idolatry is not nullified in the majority, see there; and do not challenge him from that majority Israel bless even though there is minority idolatry — for that case is different, since we follow majority Israel and we attribute the smell entirely to them, and from idolaters not at all since they did not make mugmar at all; not so when all made and they mixed — he holds that following the majority does not apply here, for idolatry is not nullified — thus appears his view; and from Gra's words in the last seif kaf it appears he does not hold so, for he wrote we derive this law from siman 67 — thus Gra does not distinguish between siman 67 and 68 [and it appears further that Rambam's words flow from R' Yosi's statement in the Gemara who said the majority is not made for smell, and Rambam explains even though smells mixed with each other; but Gra holds from the beginning of the baraita we can also infer Rambam's law] — and see in Machatzit HaShekel what he wrote in the name of Chemed Moshe and he concluded on his words; requires further study; and in truth one who examines siman 296 regarding mixed wood of perfumes with herbs of perfumes — poskim wrote he blesses minei besamim, see in Kolbo in the name of Raavad and Eliyah Rabbah, and they did not write at all to look at the majority of which kind; therefore as Chemed Moshe — we do not pay attention to this at all, for in any case one smells also from the minority.
