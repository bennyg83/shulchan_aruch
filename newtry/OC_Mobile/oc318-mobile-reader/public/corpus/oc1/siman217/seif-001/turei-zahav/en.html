He enters and leaves, etc. See siman 639, seif 8, and what I wrote there regarding this. The Gemara asks: It was not made for fragrance, so why should he recite a blessing? It answers that it was indeed also made for fragrance, so that people would smell it, come in, and purchase spices. It appears to me from this that one is obligated to recite this blessing only when he enters and intends to smell. If he had no such intention and merely happened to smell it, he is not obligated. One who does not intend to smell is no different from something not made for fragrance. This also explains why the perfume merchant is not said to be obligated to recite a blessing every time he handles the spices. He is certainly exempt because he does not intend to smell. This is likewise implied by the Gemara's wording, “one entered and smelled a fragrance.”
