[1] To his shop etc. Which they call in Ashkenaz apothecary — Orchot Chayim. Olat Tamid wrote it is difficult — for above siman 296 seif 2, one who takes fruit to eat and did not intend to smell — even though it gives off smell he does not bless; yet here too one who enters does not intend to smell. One may say it deals with when he enters and intends to smell; but when he enters for other needs, even though he smells he is not obligated to bless since his intention is not to smell — requires study of the law, end of his words. And I wonder how he did not see Bach's words in this siman who raises this difficulty and resolves that this case is different, since the perfume was intended that entrants smell in it — it is made for smell because of people in the world, i.e. made for smell, end of his words; and if so it implies even one who enters for his needs is obligated to bless. Also without this there is no difficulty from taking fruit to eat per what Levush wrote there — the reason of the blessing of eating which is primary exempts the secondary; however above I wondered on Levush. Again Taz was printed and wrote plainly that even if he did not intend to smell (he does) not bless, and so he wrote in this siman and did not mention Bach's words cited above.
