1) [Seif 1] One who enters a perfumer's shop, etc. The spices are also placed there for fragrance, so that people will smell them, enter, and purchase from him. Berachos 53a. Therefore, when spices are placed in a room rather than a shop, one does not recite a blessing even if he intends to smell, because the spices were not placed there for smelling. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. According to this, the same applies to one who enters an orchard from which a fragrant aroma emerges: he does not recite a blessing even if he intends to smell, since the plants were not placed there for smelling. Nevertheless, it appears to me preferable that upon initially entering the room or orchard, he take some of the spices in his hand, recite the blessing over them, and intend to cover the fragrance that rises by itself, so that he not derive enjoyment from this world without a blessing.
2) There: He recites Borei Minei, etc. He is obligated in this blessing only if he enters intending to smell. If the fragrance reaches him incidentally, he is not obligated, for one who does not intend is equivalent to a case involving something not made for smelling. This explains why the rule does not say that a perfume seller must recite a blessing every time he works with the spices. He is certainly exempt because he did not intend to smell. Taz, paragraph 1. His words imply that both conditions are required: the item must be made for smelling, and he must intend to smell. Bach, however, implies that once it was made for smelling, he must recite a blessing even if he does not intend to smell; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1, and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, write the same. Therefore, since the matter is disputed, he should consciously intend that he wishes to smell and then recite the blessing.
3) There: He recites Borei Minei, etc. One should not challenge this from siman 216, seif 10, where one recites the blessing appropriate to each species. There, three species are brought before him and he takes each species in his hand to recite a blessing over it. Here, however, he takes no spice in his hand but smells several types of spices simultaneously. Perishah there, paragraph 24; Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, paragraph 2.
4) There: If, however, he intended to return, etc. This applies specifically if he intended to return immediately. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. See, however, Taz, siman 639, paragraph 20, who writes that whenever he intends to return and has not diverted his attention, even if he remained away a long time, he need not recite another blessing; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes in the name of Tashbetz and Birchas Avraham, folio 78, like Magen Avraham, that he must return immediately and must also not have diverted his attention; see there. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, holds the same view.
5) There: He should not recite a blessing. In siman 178, all agree that regarding foods that do not require a concluding blessing in the place where they were eaten, if one changes location he must recite another blessing even if he intended to return. One must say that this case is different because when he returns he ultimately smells the same spices he smelled previously, and therefore he need not recite another blessing. It resembles one who leaves his dining group intending to return. Eating is different because he eats another item and must therefore recite another blessing. Accordingly, if upon returning he smells different spices, he must recite a blessing. Alternatively, here no action is lacking: when he enters, he smells automatically, and therefore his intention is effective. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, answers that the case concerns one who went only to the shop entrance and can see his original place, which is effective as explained above in siman 178 and below in siman 273. He challenges Derishah, who writes that the case concerns one who left some members of the group behind, because that is ineffective here; see there.
6) There: He should not recite a blessing. If one left this perfumer's shop and entered another perfumer's shop, he must recite another blessing even if he initially intended to go from place to place. Birchas Avraham, volume 4, siman 114; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3.
