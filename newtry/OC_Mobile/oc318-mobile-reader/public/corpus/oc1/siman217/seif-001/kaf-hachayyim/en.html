1) [Seif 1] One who enters a perfumer's shop, etc. The spices are also placed there for fragrance, so that people will smell them, enter, and purchase from him. Berachos 53a. Therefore, when spices are placed in a room rather than a shop, one does not recite a blessing even if he intends to smell, because the spices were not placed there for smelling. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. According to this, the same applies to one who enters an orchard from which a fragrant aroma emerges: he does not recite a blessing even if he intends to smell, since the plants were not placed there for smelling. Nevertheless, it appears to me preferable that upon initially entering the room or orchard, he take some of the spices in his hand, recite the blessing over them, and intend to cover the fragrance that rises by itself, so that he not derive enjoyment from this world without a blessing.
