2) There: He recites Borei Minei, etc. He is obligated in this blessing only if he enters intending to smell. If the fragrance reaches him incidentally, he is not obligated, for one who does not intend is equivalent to a case involving something not made for smelling. This explains why the rule does not say that a perfume seller must recite a blessing every time he works with the spices. He is certainly exempt because he did not intend to smell. Taz, paragraph 1. His words imply that both conditions are required: the item must be made for smelling, and he must intend to smell. Bach, however, implies that once it was made for smelling, he must recite a blessing even if he does not intend to smell; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1, and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1, write the same. Therefore, since the matter is disputed, he should consciously intend that he wishes to smell and then recite the blessing.
