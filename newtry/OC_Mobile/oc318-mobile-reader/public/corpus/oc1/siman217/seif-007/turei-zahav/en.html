He was walking outside the city, etc. It appears to me that the blessing is certainly recited over the fragrance he will continue to smell willingly and enjoy. One cannot recite a blessing over what he has already smelled, for smelling is like eating, as Tur writes at the beginning of the siman, and a blessing over food must be recited before eating. Accordingly, if he merely passes that city and does not intend to enjoy the fragrance, he need not recite a blessing, as I wrote at the beginning of the siman regarding a perfume shop. Further proof is found in Pesachim 25a, concerning forbidden benefit that reaches a person unintentionally, where there is no prohibition. All the more so here, where a blessing is recited only over something made for fragrance.
