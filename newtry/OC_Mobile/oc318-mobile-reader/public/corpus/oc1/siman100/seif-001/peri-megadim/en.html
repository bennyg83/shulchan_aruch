An additional reason is given there, namely that these prayers are beloved and people therefore concentrate upon them. See the Beit Yosef. The Eliyah Rabbah writes that the purpose of review is that the prayer should be fluent in one’s mouth. One may ask how a siddur helps. Regarding Rosh Chodesh there is a compound doubt, for perhaps the halachah follows the Tur, who holds that review is unnecessary, unlike the festivals. Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan, however, is comparable to the festivals; see the Bach. Know that Yaaleh VeYavo likewise requires review, or else it must be recited from a siddur, and the same applies to Al HaNissim on Chanukah and Purim. See the Peri Chodosh, who writes that the custom is to recite the first prayer from a siddur, after which that suffices; see there. You should also know that the Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 4:19, mentions the Mussaf prayer of Rosh Chodesh, implying that this does not apply to Yaaleh VeYavo or Al HaNissim. The concern was with an entire blessing, lest one err in it, not merely that the words be fluent in his mouth. Although one who changes it somewhat has fulfilled his obligation, this is still improper initially. This differs from Yaaleh VeYavo and similar insertions. The Bach and Peri Chodosh, however, indicate that the same rule applies to Chanukah and Purim; see there. In some editions of the Shulchan Aruch the abbreviation is printed as “Rosh Hashanah,” and so it appears in the edition of the Peri Chodosh. The wording there proves that this was his reading, since he separately notes, “and the Rosh Chodesh prayer as well.” Thus, in the Shulchan Aruch the reference is to Rosh Hashanah, following the Rif and the Rosh; see the Beit Yosef. The Tur’s attribution of this view to the Rosh is not exact; see the Bach. This may be the understanding of the Olas Tamid; see Eliyah Rabbah. Nevertheless, the Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef imply that, in their view, those authorities agree with the Rambam. The Levush likewise rules that it applies to Rosh Chodesh, and see Nezirus Shimshon on this matter. In any event, the Taz also writes that difficult words whose meaning is not readily understood must be reviewed.
