[1] When they pray from a siddur, etc. The Rama writes this, and the Bach challenges them, writing as follows: “The fact that people are not accustomed to review the prayer at all, even for Chanukah, Purim, Tisha B’Av, the festivals, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur, and rely upon the siddur in their hands, is entirely improper.” The Taz brings proof from the following siman that one may pray aloud from a siddur. The cases are not comparable, however, because here the reason is that the prayer should be fluent in his mouth, which is easy to understand. It appears that the Bach refers specifically to those occasions mentioned above. Regarding Rosh Chodesh, however, he would concede that our custom is correct, because one may rely upon the Tur, who is lenient regarding Rosh Chodesh even when one is not praying from a siddur. This enables me to defend the Olas Tamid, who comments on the Shulchan Aruch’s ruling that one must review the Rosh Chodesh prayer: “It appears that the same applies to one who has not prayed it for thirty days, or on Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan, when he did not recite the Rosh Chodesh prayer on Rosh Hashanah, and he must review it.” At first glance this is puzzling, since the Shulchan Aruch rules regarding every Rosh Chodesh that one must review the prayer, as he rules in the Beit Yosef and the Levush. Many scholars say that the Olas Tamid erred and confused Rosh Hashanah with Rosh Chodesh in the text of the Shulchan Aruch, assuming that the Shulchan Aruch referred to Rosh Hashanah rather than Rosh Chodesh. According to my explanation, however, his words can be resolved. He means that even when one prays from a siddur, which ordinarily permits one to omit advance review on Rosh Chodesh, on Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan one must nevertheless review the prayer. In that case we cannot rely upon the Tur, because even the Tur concedes that review is required. Examine this carefully. The Taz writes that a prayer whose meaning one must understand requires prior review according to all opinions.
