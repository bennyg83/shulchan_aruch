[1] When they pray from the siddur, etc. Thus wrote Rama, and Bach challenged them and wrote these words: that what the world is not accustomed to prepare at all, even that of Chanukah and Purim and Tisha BeAv and of festivals and Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and they rely on the siddur in their hand — this is not correct at all; until here his words. And Taz brought proof from the next siman that one may pray aloud from the siddur; and it is not comparable, for here the reason is that it should be fluent on his tongue and easy to understand. And it seems that specifically those mentioned does Bach speak; but for Rosh Chodesh he agrees that our practice is correct, for one may rely on the Tur who is lenient on Rosh Chodesh even when not praying from the siddur. And with this I may teach merit on Olat Tamid, who wrote on the words of Shulchan Aruch who wrote that the prayer of Rosh Chodesh must be prepared — he wrote: and it seems the same law applies to one who did not pray for thirty days, or on Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan who did not pray on the Rosh Hashanah of Rosh Chodesh, that he must prepare; until here. And seemingly it is astounding, for in every Rosh Chodesh Shulchan Aruch ruled must prepare, as ruled in Beit Yosef and Levush. And many learners say he erred and was confused in Shulchan Aruch between Rosh Hashanah and Rosh Chodesh, for he thought Shulchan Aruch speaks of Rosh Hashanah and not Rosh Chodesh; but according to what I explained, his words may be resolved: his intent is even when praying from the siddur, for then it is permitted on Rosh Chodesh; nevertheless on Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan one must prepare, for on this we may not rely on the Tur, for the Tur admits one must prepare, and finis. Taz wrote: the prayer whose meaning one must understand must be prepared according to all.
