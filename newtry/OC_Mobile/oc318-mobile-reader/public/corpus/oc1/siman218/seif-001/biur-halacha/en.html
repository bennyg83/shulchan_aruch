Such as the crossings. Kaphtor VaFerach, chapter 7, writes as follows: “The crossings, etc. It appears from his explanation that one does not recite the blessing until he sees the particular place where the miracle occurred. Thus, one who sees the Yam Suf does not bless unless he sees the place where they crossed, and likewise one who sees the Jordan must see the place where they crossed, as the verse states, ‘The people crossed opposite Jericho’; the same applies to the Arnon Valley. Nevertheless, whenever one sees any part of the sea or the Jordan, it is proper to recall Hashem’s kindnesses and providence toward us, and to thank and praise Him according to one’s ability,” meaning ordinary praise without Hashem’s Name and kingship. “The same applies upon seeing a place where Hashem performed a wonder or miracle for certain prophets, such as seeing on Mount Carmel the place where Eliyahu made the altar, or seeing in Shunem the place of Elisha’s upper chamber, and the like. There is no doubt that the episode of Eliyahu was a wonder and miracle for him, since the king was pursuing him, whereas the episode of Elisha was a wonder for the Shunammite’s son through the prophet’s prayer.” These are his words. Regarding his principal ruling that one must see the precise site of the miracle, this is also implied by the other poskim, and Rashi writes likewise in his explanation of “the crossings.” Although Rambam’s wording, “One who sees a place where miracles were performed, such as the Yam Suf,” might superficially imply that merely seeing the Yam Suf suffices, this is not compelling, for he concludes, “or the crossings of the Jordan,” rather than simply, “or the Jordan,” showing that his earlier formulation was not exact. Regarding the second ruling, concerning seeing Mount Carmel, Kaphtor VaFerach appears to mean only that one should offer ordinary praise. This follows what he wrote earlier, that even upon seeing another part of the Jordan one should praise Hashem, and he then concludes that the same applies upon seeing Mount Carmel. The blessing was not instituted for every wonder of the Creator, for otherwise one should bless upon seeing Mount Sinai, where the Torah was given. Rather, it was instituted only for a person’s miraculous rescue from death. The stone upon which Moshe sat was likewise connected to a miraculous rescue through Moshe’s prayer, as it is written, “Hashem is my banner.” In Eliyahu’s case, although there was a miracle and wonder, the event on Mount Carmel was not performed in order to save Eliyahu from death, for Eliyahu had been hidden and later went to Achav at Hashem’s command, “Go, appear to Achav, and I will give rain upon the earth.” The miracle on Mount Carmel was performed to strengthen Israel’s faith in Hashem. Therefore, Kaphtor VaFerach must mean only ordinary praise, as is clear from his continuation concerning Shunem, which certainly refers only to ordinary praise, and as is also evident from the conclusion of his own words. Accordingly, his statement that the king was pursuing Eliyahu serves only to magnify the praise for the kindness Hashem performed for the prophet by preventing Achav from killing him. Perhaps one may further explain that Kaphtor VaFerach holds that the miracle on Mount Carmel also served to save Eliyahu from death. Although Hashem commanded him to appear before Achav, Achav still possessed free choice regarding Eliyahu’s life, just as Uriyah of Kiryas Yearim was a prophet and Yehoyakim killed him because of his prophecy, as stated in Yirmiyahu 26. Since the king’s wrath burned against Eliyahu for having stopped the rain, to the point that he said, “Are you the one who troubles Israel?” and the prophet answered, “I have not troubled Israel, but you and your father’s house, because you have abandoned Hashem’s commandments and followed the Baalim,” Eliyahu’s life was certainly in great danger until he could prove that truth was with him. He therefore said, “Now send and gather all Israel to me,” and through the event on Mount Carmel it became clear that he was correct: the rain had been withheld because they followed the Baalim, and immediately after they repented and declared, “Hashem, He is God,” rain came upon the earth. Everyone thereby saw that Eliyahu was a faithful servant of Hashem, as the verse states, “Today let it be known that You are God in Israel, and I am Your servant, and that I have done all these things at Your word.” One may say that he thereby indicated that his initial withholding of rain had likewise been through the spirit of Hashem that rested upon him. The king’s wrath was then removed, and he even consented to the killing of all the prophets of Baal. Although this explains Kaphtor VaFerach’s words, in practice it still requires study whether one should bless upon seeing Mount Carmel. Regarding the Shunammite’s son, there is likewise no reason to bless. One cannot bless on account of Elisha’s being a distinguished person, for no danger befell Elisha himself; rather, he performed the miracle for another person, and that other person was not distinguished. Nor is it clear that one blesses because Hashem’s Name was sanctified through this miracle. First, it did not occur publicly like Daniel’s miracle. Second, it is uncertain whether resurrection of the dead is included among those events for which the Sages instituted this blessing, since the blessing was established for someone who was in danger and was saved, not for someone who had already died and was revived by a prophet. It would be preferable in such a case to recite the blessing “Who revives the dead.” See Kaphtor VaFerach there for the formula of thanksgiving customarily recited upon seeing the Jordan.
And the stone that Og sought, etc. See Chiddushei Aggados for what is written concerning this. According to the plain meaning, there is not so much cause for wonder regarding such a man, since he was among the giants, and his father was among those concerning whom Scripture states, “The sons of God came to the daughters of man, and they bore children to them.” As stated in Niddah 61a, Sichon and Og were sons of Achiyah son of Shamchazai. See Rashi there, who explains that Shamchazai and Azael were the angels who descended from heaven in the generation of Enosh. Their physical strength was therefore of a kind resembling the strength of angels.
He blesses, “Who performed miracles.” The poskim explain that one need not detail or mention what the miracle was. It is sufficient to say, “Who performed a miracle for me in this place.” See Tosafos Rabbeinu Yehudah and Tosafos HaRosh. Taz agrees.
In this place. It appears that one may bless only while he can still see the place. If he has already passed it and can no longer see it, he has lost the opportunity to recite the blessing, just as with hearing thunder below in siman 227, seif 3. The same applies to seeing a person for whom a miracle was performed, as discussed in seif 6 in the gloss. If that person has already passed and moved so far away that he is no longer visible, one no longer blesses. It is also possible that even if one sees the person or the place again several days later, he does not bless so long as thirty days have not elapsed since the first sighting, even if he did not bless the first time. Regarding a new fruit, several poskim hold that once one failed to bless the first time he saw it, he may no longer bless upon seeing it again, as Magen Avraham writes in siman 225, seif katan 9. Although our practice does not follow that view, that is because we hold that the matter depends upon eating, as is evident there from Biur HaGra. Here, however, the blessing was certainly instituted solely upon seeing the site of the miracle. Once he has already seen the place and the sight is no longer new to him, he need not bless until thirty days have passed, at which point it is considered a new sighting.
