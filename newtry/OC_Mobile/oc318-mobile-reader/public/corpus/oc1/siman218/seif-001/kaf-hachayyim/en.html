1) [Seif 1] One who sees a place where miracles were performed, etc. This implies that the blessing is recited specifically in cases like those listed, where the miracle is evident from the place. Otherwise, such as the area surrounding Yerushalayim where Sancheriv's camp fell, no blessing is recited, as Tosafos and Mordechai write. Over one's own miracle, however, one recites the blessing even if it is not evident from the place. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. See Padeh Es Avraham, under mem, paragraph 20, who writes in the name of Kometz Minchah that one who passes through the locations mentioned in the journeys of the Jewish people must recite, 'Blessed is He Who performed miracles for our ancestors in this place'; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1.
2) There: Such as the crossings of the sea, etc. This refers to the place where the Jewish people crossed the Yam Suf and the crossings of the Yarden in the days of Yehoshua. Rashi on Berachos 54a.
3) There: And the crossings of the Arnon streams, etc. The Gemara there explains that this was a known deep passage between two mountains through which the Jewish people had to pass. The Emorim hid there and wished to kill them. A miracle occurred: the mountains joined together and killed them all. The miracle of the hailstones was that large stones were cast from heaven. Regarding the miracle of the stone that Og sought to cast, he said: How large is the camp of the Jewish people? Three parasangs. I will go uproot a mountain three parasangs wide, cast it upon them, and kill them. He went, uprooted a mountain three parasangs wide, and carried it upon his head. The Holy One, blessed is He, brought ants upon it, which bored through the mountain, and it fell around his neck. He wished to remove it, but his teeth extended to this side and that side, and he could not remove it. This is the meaning of the verse, 'You broke the teeth of the wicked,' etc. The wall of Yericho sank into its place; see there. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1.
4) One does not recite the blessing until he sees the precise place of the miracle. Thus, one who sees the Yam Suf does not recite the blessing unless he sees the place where they crossed. Likewise, one who sees the Yarden must see the place where they crossed, for the verse states, 'The people crossed opposite Yericho.' The same applies to the Arnon stream. Nevertheless, wherever one sees part of the sea, the Yarden, or the stream, it is proper to mention Hashem's kindnesses and His providence over us, and to thank and praise Him according to one's ability. The same applies when seeing on Mount Carmel the place where Eliyahu, of blessed memory, made the altar, and when seeing in Shunem the place of Elisha's upper chamber, peace be upon him, and similar locations. We are accustomed to say upon seeing the Yarden: Beside the Yarden I shall bless, praise, extol, and thank Your Name. For Your people You split the Yam Suf. You made it the boundary of Your land, and in the end You divided it for Eliyahu, and Elisha passed through it. Bare Your holy arm, Holy One of Yisrael, to save an impoverished people. In Your favor, do good to Tziyon. Kaftor VaFerach, chapter 7. It appears that if one does not know the precise location, he should say, 'Blessed is He Who performed miracles for our ancestors in this place,' without mentioning the Name and kingship.
5) There: All of them include mention of the Name and kingship. This excludes Raavad's view that although other blessings require the Name and kingship, blessings recited upon seeing something, since they are merely praise and thanksgiving, do not require them. Tur and Beis Yosef. See Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, who brings support for Raavad's words; see there. Tosafos, Rabbeinu Yonah, Rosh, Rashba, and Mordechai in the chapter HaRoeh, however, all agree with the Rambam that blessings recited upon seeing also require the Name and kingship. They write that the Yerushalmi says so explicitly. Rosh likewise writes in a responsum that all our teachers ruled this way and that the practice is to mention the Name and kingship. One should not be concerned about Raavad's words in these matters. Beis Yosef cites all this; see there. The later authorities likewise hold that one recites the blessing with the Name and kingship. See above, siman 17, paragraph 2, and understand this.
