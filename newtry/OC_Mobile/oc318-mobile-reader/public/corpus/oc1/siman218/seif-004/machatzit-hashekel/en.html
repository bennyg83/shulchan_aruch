Seif katan 4: From thirty, etc. This requires study, etc. For example, he saw it on the first day of one month and then saw it on the second day of the next month. In that case there are only twenty-eight days between the first sighting and the second.
And from what is written in siman 422 in the name of Abudraham, etc. Abudraham holds that one recites Shehecheyanu over something that recurs from thirty days to thirty days. He therefore asks why Shehecheyanu is not recited over Hallel on Rosh Chodesh. Certainly, when the preceding month is deficient and Rosh Chodesh occurs on day thirty, it is understandable that Shehecheyanu is not recited. But when the month is full and Rosh Chodesh is observed on both day thirty and day thirty-one, since we are expert in establishing the calendar and know that the second day is the principal day from which the festivals are counted, the Hallel recited on day thirty is regarded as though it had not been recited, or as though ordinary chapters of Tehillim had been said, since its recitation is only due to ancestral custom. Accordingly, one should recite Shehecheyanu over the Hallel said on the second day of Rosh Chodesh, day thirty-one. The reason one nevertheless does not is that the Sages did not distinguish between a full month and a deficient month. This shows that, regarding a deficient month, Abudraham accepts that no Shehecheyanu is recited because only twenty-eight days separate the Hallel of the previous Rosh Chodesh from the Hallel of the present Rosh Chodesh, and this is not considered an event recurring from time to time for which Shehecheyanu is recited. The same applies here.
It appears to me that this depends upon the dispute, etc. At the end of tractate Rosh Hashanah it is stated that one must review a prayer recited only at intervals before praying it, so that it will be fluent in his mouth. Rosh holds that this applies only to the prayers of the festivals, whereas the Rosh Chodesh prayer is not considered a prayer recited only at intervals and need not be reviewed. Rambam holds that even the Rosh Chodesh prayer is considered one recited only at intervals and must be reviewed. Beit Yosef ruled above in siman 100 in accordance with Rambam. The same applies here, for Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter HaRo’eh write, s.v. one who sees: “It appears to Rabbeinu that this applies specifically to one who sees it once in thirty days, like one who sees the Great Sea at intervals, for the Yerushalmi states that ‘at intervals’ means thirty days.” Thus, however the term “at intervals” is defined regarding prayer in siman 100, the same definition applies here.
Abudraham implies that one blesses in such a case, for he wished to say that when Rosh Chodesh lasts two days, Shehecheyanu should be recited on the second day because it is considered an event recurring from time to time, even though only twenty-nine days separate the previous Rosh Chodesh from the second day of the present Rosh Chodesh.
