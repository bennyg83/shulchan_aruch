(29) That departs from custom of the world — such as incident mentioned in Talmud of one who went in desert and was in danger of death from thirst and a miracle occurred and a spring of water came forth for him and the like.

(30) And was saved — also in way of custom of the world — meaning he cried for help or people happened to be there and robbers fled.

(31) Not obligated to bless — when he reaches there another time blessing Who made miracles for me; nevertheless at time salvation occurred he must bless Birkat HaGomel — for in HaGomel we are not so strict that it be a complete miracle; as below end siman 219 in first opinion many Achronim agreed.

(32) And there is one who disagrees — that even if salvation was not above way of nature — nevertheless since he was in danger — if salvation had not occurred it would be called miracle — he must thank and praise the Creator Who prepared salvation at this moment. If stone fell on him etc. or laden wagon passed in way of nature he would die thereby and he was saved from death — when he reaches that place from thirty to thirty he must bless. If stone fell near his head or iron rod near his eye and miracle was done that stone did not fall on head and iron did not pierce eye — per first opinion one does not bless; per second opinion possible they bless — for any person close to matter with danger who was saved blesses; and therefore he should bless without God's name and kingship; see Biur Halacha. Achronim wrote one for whom miracle was done should set aside charity according to his means and distribute to Torah scholars and say I give this to charity — may it be considered in place of thanks I would have owed in Temple times and fit to say thanks portion. Good and fit for him to fix some public needs in city; and each year on this day seclude to thank God and rejoice and tell of His kindness.
