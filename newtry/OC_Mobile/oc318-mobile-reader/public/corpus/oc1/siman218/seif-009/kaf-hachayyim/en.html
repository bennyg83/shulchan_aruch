9) The custom has spread, and it is likewise stated in the Shelah, that many great Jewish people who experienced distress and were saved seclude themselves each year on that date for prayers and praises to God, may He be exalted, for the salvation they experienced through the miracle. Afterward, they celebrate somewhat with their friends and companions according to their means, in order to publicize the miracle. This is a proper custom, for who knows whether they will have an opportunity to be at the place where the miracle occurred and recite the blessing there. Shulchan Gavoah there. At the end of a handwritten commentary of the Rambam on Rosh Hashanah, I saw that the Rambam accepted upon himself to seclude himself each year on the day of his distress and to rejoice on the following day. Sefer Chareidim cites this wording; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 4; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 5. Some practice not reciting Tachanun on the day when their miracle occurred. This is likewise the custom of the great city Baghdad, may God protect it, where miracles occurred for them during the siege by Tamasp, king of Persia, on the eleventh of Av and the sixteenth of Teves. They do not recite Tachanun on those days. See further below, paragraph 39.
