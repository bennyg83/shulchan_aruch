[Levush] Even if he is not his teacher, etc. Magen Avraham argues at length that we find no authority who rules that one blesses over a miracle done for a particularly distinguished person. I am surprised, because Tosafot, Berachot 54b, Mordechai, Rokeach, and Chiddushei Rashba in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin explicitly rule this way. He undoubtedly overlooked them and therefore, with respect, did not interpret the Yerushalmi correctly. Rashba makes clear that the earlier question about father and teacher remains unresolved. The Yerushalmi's concluding phrase "if he is a distinguished person" begins a new matter and does not refer back to that question. This is clearly Ramban's intent as cited above, and so appears from the Rosh. Bach, Beit Yosef, and Maadanei Melech also struggled with the Yerushalmi, leaving room for this explanation. Nevertheless, it appears that the person must be exceptionally distinguished.
