There is one opinion, etc. Bach and Lechem Chamudos, page 61, question this. Horayos 5 records a dispute between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah, and the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah that one tribe is called a congregation. Rambam rules this way in chapter 5 of Hilchos Shegagos. In truth, however, in chapter 7 of Hilchos Korban Pesach Rambam does not rule this way. Magen Avraham answers that only regarding an erroneous ruling by a beit din do we rule like them, because the verse explicitly uses the word “congregation.” This appears supported by the Jerusalem Talmud in the chapter HaRoeh, which does not decide the halachah here. See there carefully, and as Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes. Nevertheless, the proof brought by Lechem Chamudos and Maadanei Melech from Tosafos requires study. Tosafos writes that one does not recite a blessing at the place where Sancheriv’s army fell, because the miracle is not recognizable at the place. At the crossings of the sea, only the person for whom the miracle occurred recites a blessing. Maadanei Melech infers that Tosafos holds that a tribe is called a congregation. Otherwise, it would suffice to say that the miracle did not occur to all Israel. Upon careful examination, however, it appears that Tosafos precisely writes that Chizkiyahu is no less than a distinguished individual. Their intent is that regarding a distinguished individual, a blessing may be warranted even though the miracle did not happen to most of Israel. This is easy to understand. Accordingly, Tosafos actually provides support for the view that a tribe is not called a congregation.
