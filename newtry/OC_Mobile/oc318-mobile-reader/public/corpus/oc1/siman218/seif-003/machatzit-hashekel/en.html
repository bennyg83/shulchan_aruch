(Seif 3) For part etc. — and dispute of R' Meir etc. in tractate Horayot daf 5 — we taught: the great court erred and instructed to permit something whose intentional violation carries karet and the congregation acted on their word (and this is all Israel or most of them; and regarding one tribe it depends on dispute of R' Meir and R' Yehudah and R' Shimon) — the congregation brings a bull; and if the court erred and instructed regarding idolatry and the congregation acted on their word they bring a bull and a goat; and R' Meir holds that the entire congregation brings in every matter whose intentional violation carries karet if they acted on court instruction — one bull; and for idolatry one bull and one goat; and R' Yehudah holds in a matter whose intentional violation carries karet they bring twelve bulls — a bull for each tribe; and for idolatry twelve bulls and twelve goats; and R' Shimon holds in a matter whose intentional violation carries karet thirteen bulls — his reason: twelve bulls for twelve tribes and the thirteenth bull for the court; and for idolatry R' Shimon holds similarly thirteen bulls and thirteen goats; and concludes that their dispute — R' Meir holds from Scripture and the congregation brought — one tribe is not called congregation — therefore all Israel suffice with one offering; and R' Yehudah and R' Shimon both hold every tribe is called congregation — therefore they need an offering for each tribe; and they bring proof from what the Holy One said to Yaakov I will break and multiply you and make you a congregation of peoples — and no more was born to him except Binyamin — and nevertheless they called congregation of peoples.

And since we hold like R' Yehudah and R' Shimon against R' Meir as stated in Eruvin 46.

For this general principle does not exist — and so in responsum Maharik.

In Tosafot Zevachim daf 6 s.v. one — they challenged that it implies for R' Yehudah one inherits his father's vow in sacrifice and this is dispute of R' Meir and R' Yehudah; and R' Yehudah holds he does not inherit; and R' Yehudah ruled R' Meir and R' Yehudah — halachah follows R' Yehudah; and see responsum Chavot Yair siman 94 who also raised this — see there.

And requires study — nevertheless many etc. The question from Tosafot Zevachim is like the gloss of Magen Avraham; and now he brought again conclusion of Levush's words who challenged thus — nevertheless they are many and this general principle certainly applies even in matters not practiced — for it is written follow the majority; see there.

And questioned in Pesachim etc. — it is Magen Avraham's question.

If one tribe became impure etc. — we hold if a majority of the congregation are impure all act in impurity; and if a minority are impure the pure act on the first and impure are deferred to second Passover; and if pure and impure were half and half — there is a tanna who holds pure act for themselves in purity and impure act for themselves on the first in impurity; and if only one tribe was impure and the rest of tribes pure — R' Yehudah and R' Shimon hold it is like half and half and we say their reason because they hold one tribe is called congregation.

If so his words contradict each other — for in chapter 12 of Shegagot he ruled like them; therefore it seems to me specifically regarding court instruction we hold like them — meaning certainly R' Yehudah and R' Shimon everywhere hold congregation is called one tribe even where congregation is not written — and it would have been fit to rule like them as above; but in Passover the anonymous mishnah is not like them and in order to diminish the general principle that individual and many — halachah follows the many; and also R' Meir and R' Yehudah and R' Shimon — we hold like R' Yehudah and R' Shimon — we say from the reason of the mishnah it argues only in Passover and its like where congregation is not written; but where congregation is written the mishnah agrees we hold like them that one tribe is called congregation. And here for Passover we compare since congregation is not written as in Passover — since it is not written explicitly that impure congregation act in impurity but from inference of what is written if a man is impure etc. and we say a man is deferred to second Passover and impure congregation are not deferred to second Passover — if so it implies Torah did not exclude except all or most of Israel; but one tribe even though called congregation — nevertheless sometimes it is also included in man and was not excluded from if a man is impure; and the same here.
