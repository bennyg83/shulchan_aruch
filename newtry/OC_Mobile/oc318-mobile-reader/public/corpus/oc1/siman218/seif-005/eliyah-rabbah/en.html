He must mention, etc. Magen Avraham writes that other descendants need not mention the other places of miracles, as with a miracle of the public. It appears to me that a son and grandson should mention all of them, and likewise a student regarding his teacher; so appears from the remazim. Olat Tamid writes that if on the same day he goes to another place where a miracle occurred to him, the law requires study, so because of doubt he should not bless. According to him, for the entire thirty-day period he would never bless at any of the places. In my humble opinion he should bless even on the same day, because he has not seen that particular place within thirty days. Moreover, Kesef Mishneh, chapter 9 of Hilchot Berachot, writes that according to Rif and Rambam one need not mention all the places, and Mar son of Ravina acted beyond the strict law when he did so. This makes me wonder at Taz, who at length challenges the Rosh for deriving the law from Mar's incident and says this is simply the Gemara's law. He overlooked Kesef Mishneh, who holds that Rif and Rambam did not adopt that Gemara as obligatory, and thus the Tur needed to cite the Rosh as understanding it to impose an obligation. Taz's strained answer that the Rosh teaches one must name the places is also difficult, because that novelty is not stated in the Rosh. It would have been better to say that the Rosh teaches the rule even where there were many places, whereas the Gemara mentions only two.
