He is surprised that Maharshal simply follows Rambam's apparent wording that one blesses at any lions' den, asking what basis Rambam would have for such a distinction. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah asks the same. I am surprised, because Rashi on Berachot 57 explains "the fiery furnace" as the pit heated for Chananyah, Mishael, and Azaryah, yet does not similarly qualify "a lions' den" as Daniel's. This implies that one blesses at any lions' den. The reason is that a lions' den is exceedingly uncommon, so wherever one sees one the heart is aroused by Daniel's miracle. As for Bach's and Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah's question regarding the source for distinguishing them, it appears that Rambam was precise from the Gemara's wording: it says "a lions' den" without the definite article, but "the fiery furnace" with the definite article. The Rosh, Tur, and poskim use the same wording. Although Rashi on Kiryat HaArba notes a Hebrew usage where the definite article is placed on the second noun, here the Gemara changes its formulation and does not write the article with lions' den, implying deliberateness. Orchot Chaim cites Seder Berachot that one blesses even at every fiery furnace, and has a different text of the blessing; according to my explanation this fits no opinion. Moreover, in Chiddushei Rashba and Abudarham I found like Shulchan Aruch and Levush that even for a lions' den one blesses only at Daniel's. The Gemara itself supports this, saying that one who sees wicked Babylonia recites five blessings and listing among them the lions' den and fiery furnace, implying specifically Daniel's and Chananyah, Mishael, and Azaryah's sites in Babylonia. It is also possible that Rambam and Rashi agree, but omitted the qualification because it was obvious. They explained the fiery furnace only lest one mistakenly think it refers to the furnace into which Avraham Avinu was cast. The Gemara did not need to teach a blessing there, because we are all Avraham's descendants. Rather, it speaks of the furnace of Chananyah, Mishael, and Azaryah, like Daniel's lions' den, where one blesses because of the kiddush Hashem that occurred there even though one is not their descendant. At other lions' dens one could perhaps bless without Hashem's Name and kingship.
