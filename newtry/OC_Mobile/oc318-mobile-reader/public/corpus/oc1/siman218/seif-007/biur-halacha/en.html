For a miracle performed for a distinguished person. See Magen Avraham, who questions this ruling because it is an unresolved inquiry in the Yerushalmi. The later authorities challenge him; see Eliyah Rabbah and the other later authorities. Nevertheless, I am surprised that they challenged him only because they found some poskim who cite this Yerushalmi statement as a definite ruling rather than as an unresolved inquiry. Magen Avraham’s underlying point remains correct: perhaps the person must be comparable specifically to Yoav, whose fame had spread throughout the entire world. This is also the straightforward implication of the Yerushalmi itself, since it mentions “a distinguished person, such as Yoav son of Tzeruyah,” and “a person through whom Hashem’s Name was sanctified, such as Daniel and his companions.” This implies that even Daniel and his companions would not warrant a blessing merely because of their personal stature. Rather, the blessing is recited because Hashem’s Name was sanctified through the miracle that occurred to them. Had they merely faced some danger and been saved miraculously without a sanctification of Hashem’s Name, all Israel would not bless for them. Thus, even people of their stature are not automatically considered “distinguished” for this purpose. The same inference emerges from the Mechaber’s own wording, “Therefore, one who sees,” which implies that had Hashem’s Name not been sanctified through the miracle that occurred to them, no one else would need to bless for them, even though they were famous and important Jews and officers in the palace of the king of Babylonia. Rather, unless the person’s fame has spread throughout all Israel, like Yoav, concerning whom it is stated, “David administered justice and righteousness, and Yoav was over the army,” one does not bless for him. Magen Avraham was therefore correct in writing that there is no comparable person nowadays. Eliyah Rabbah’s statement that the individual must be “especially distinguished” is insufficient. If an ordinarily important person is considered distinguished, then it is indeed astonishing that the case of a miracle performed for a tribe remains doubtful and no blessing is recited, even though a full tribe certainly contains many great and honored individuals. Necessarily, the person must be honored throughout all Israel. Olas Tamid likewise challenges the fundamental ruling concerning a distinguished person from the law that one does not bless for a miracle performed for a tribe, nor for any group smaller than the majority of Israel, even though several tribes presumably include many great and honored individuals. He suggests that Yoav was different because he acted as an emissary of all Israel, and it was therefore as though the miracle had occurred to all Israel. His position thus agrees with Magen Avraham and is even more restrictive: the person must specifically be an emissary of all Israel. If such an emissary experiences a miracle, even if the miracle concerns only his own bodily welfare and not the needs of all Israel, it is nevertheless considered as though it affected all Israel, since he is their representative who leads them out and brings them in. Such a person is not found in our generations, when we are dispersed throughout the world. The correct practical ruling is therefore certainly in accordance with Magen Avraham, that this blessing is not recited nowadays. This is especially so because Raah likewise understands all these inquiries in the Yerushalmi as unresolved.
Therefore, one who sees a lions’ den, etc. This is the Tur’s wording. It implies that if, after Hashem’s Name had previously been sanctified through a certain person, that person later faced some unrelated danger and was miraculously saved, another person does not bless for that later miracle, since no sanctification of Hashem’s Name occurred through it. Were it not for the Tur’s wording, I would have thought that the Yerushalmi means that once Hashem’s Name was initially sanctified through a person, as in the case of Daniel, who endangered himself for the mitzvah of prayer and was miraculously saved by the Holy One, blessed is He, that person is thereafter regarded by everyone like a teacher. One must honor him and thank Hashem for any miracle He brings about for him.
