For a miracle. Shabbos 33 implies that when a miracle occurs for a person, he should establish some public benefit in the city.
For some of the tribes, etc. Bach asks that the Jerusalem Talmud says that according to the opinion that each tribe is called a congregation, one must recite a blessing for a miracle that occurred to one tribe. This is a dispute between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon. Since we rule like Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon, and Rambam also rules this way in chapter 12 of Hilchos Shegagos, why does the ruling here state that no blessing is recited? Lechem Chamudos answers that this general rule applies only to matters whose laws are operative in the present case, and therefore we may hold like Rabbi Meir here. This requires study, because Tosafos, Zevachim 6a, implies that this principle applies everywhere. Moreover, Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon are the majority, and Rambam rules like them. There is an additional difficulty. Pesachim 80a states that according to Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon, if one tribe became impure, that tribe offers the Korban Pesach in impurity because it is called a congregation. Rambam rules unlike them because an anonymous Mishnah follows the opposing view. His rulings would therefore appear contradictory. It seems to me that only regarding an erroneous ruling by a beit din do we rule like Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Shimon, because the Torah explicitly uses the word “congregation” there. Regarding Pesach, however, the Torah does not explicitly use that term. The present law is compared to Pesach.
