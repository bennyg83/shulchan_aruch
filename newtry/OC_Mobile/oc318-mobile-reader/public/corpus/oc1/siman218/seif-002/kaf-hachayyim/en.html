6) [Seif 2] Over a miracle performed for some Jewish people, etc. Shabbos 33b implies that when a miracle occurs for a person, he should provide some public need in the city. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. The reason appears to follow Sefer Chassidim: when a supernatural miracle sometimes occurs for a person, it is because it is revealed before Him, may He be exalted, that the public will obtain some merit through that person. Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 2. Ruach Chaim writes in the name of Emes LeYaakov that the same applies to composing and printing a work needed by the public to enlighten them. See there, where he writes of the many virtues accruing both to the author and to the benefactor who sponsors its publication; see there. See further about this in our sacred work Chukei Chaim, discourse 13, concerning the acquisition of a Sefer Torah.
7) There: For some Jewish people, etc. The Yerushalmi records a dispute. According to the view that one tribe is called a congregation, one recites the blessing over a miracle performed for one tribe. According to the view that only all the tribes together are called a congregation, one recites it only over a miracle performed for all the tribes. The Tur cites this, and Beis Yosef rules that because of the doubt one should not recite the blessing; see there. Such are his words here in the Shulchan Aruch. Therefore, it appears that over a miracle performed for one tribe, one should recite the blessing without the Name and kingship. See below, paragraph 15.
8) Know that when the Holy One, blessed is He, saves a person from distress, the salvation comes through the souls of the righteous. The Zohar, parashas Metzora, 55b, in the Tosefta, writes concerning Yaakov, 'Angels of God encountered him,' that these were Avraham and Yitzchak, who encountered Yaakov when he was saved from Lavan. This does not apply only to him. Regarding every righteous person, the souls of the righteous come before him to save him, and they are exalted holy angels; see there. Shulchan Gavoah, paragraph 2.
9) The custom has spread, and it is likewise stated in the Shelah, that many great Jewish people who experienced distress and were saved seclude themselves each year on that date for prayers and praises to God, may He be exalted, for the salvation they experienced through the miracle. Afterward, they celebrate somewhat with their friends and companions according to their means, in order to publicize the miracle. This is a proper custom, for who knows whether they will have an opportunity to be at the place where the miracle occurred and recite the blessing there. Shulchan Gavoah there. At the end of a handwritten commentary of the Rambam on Rosh Hashanah, I saw that the Rambam accepted upon himself to seclude himself each year on the day of his distress and to rejoice on the following day. Sefer Chareidim cites this wording; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 4; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 5. Some practice not reciting Tachanun on the day when their miracle occurred. This is likewise the custom of the great city Baghdad, may God protect it, where miracles occurred for them during the siege by Tamasp, king of Persia, on the eleventh of Av and the sixteenth of Teves. They do not recite Tachanun on those days. See further below, paragraph 39.
