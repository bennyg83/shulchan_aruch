Seif 2. For a miracle performed for only part of Israel, so long as it was not performed for all Israel or the majority of Israel, no blessing is recited. Even if it was performed for part of the tribes, no blessing is recited. Note: See Magen Avraham through the end of his discussion, where he writes that regarding Pesach the term “congregation” is not stated, and so forth; see there. At first glance, from where did Rambam derive this distinction? It appears that he learned it from Yoma 50a. The Gemara there asks what practical difference results from whether kohanim are considered a congregation, and answers that kohanim would not bring a bull for an erroneous communal ruling. One may ask why the Gemara did not say that the practical difference concerns a case in which most kohanim became impure: would they offer the Korban Pesach in impurity or not? For example, there might be a minority of pure kohanim, allowing the general public to offer in purity while the impure kohanim would offer separately. This would be especially relevant if the family assigned to serve that week were pure, enabling the public offering to be brought in purity, whereas the kohanim themselves, if they are considered a congregation, would offer in impurity, and if not, would be deferred to Pesach Sheni. Why did the Gemara not mention this practical difference? It could also have said that the practical difference concerns reciting a blessing for a miracle performed for the kohanim: if they are considered a congregation, one blesses, and if not, one does not bless. Rambam inferred from this that we follow Rabbi Shimon’s view only regarding erroneous communal rulings, but not regarding Pesach or blessings. Therefore, the Gemara mentioned only the practical difference that affects the accepted halachah. The question there is specifically why Rava challenged Rabbi Akiva’s explanation if it ultimately resolves the difficulty. The Gemara could not answer that there is a practical difference regarding Pesach or a miracle, since we do not follow Rabbi Shimon in those matters. Because no practical halachic difference would result, there would have been no basis for challenging Rabbi Akiva. It therefore states that the practical difference concerns whether kohanim bring a bull for an erroneous communal ruling, where we do follow Rabbi Shimon. This is clear and correct, and it provides a proof for Rambam. Examine it carefully.
