[2] [Levush] Unless he interrupted etc. It implies we require thirty days apart from the day he saw it and the day he stands on it — such as if he saw on day one we require that he see on day four. In my humble opinion from the language of Yerushalmi chapter HaRo'eh — one for thirty days — and so wrote Baal HaHalachot Gedolot and Agudah implies that even if he sees on day thirty he must bless; and from this is also explained that it is not like Magen Avraham's view that even if he sees on day two which is day thirty they bless as above siman 20 — for here and there the Talmud uses the language of chapters that do not exist, for this would be two times within thirty. And this seems to me the intent of Tosafot in chapter HaRo'eh who bring proof from Yerushalmi; and Maharsha wondered — for it is stated so in our Talmud. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty — for in the Talmud it says until thirty days and we can interpret until inclusively; see there. And so in Ra'ash and Semag and Semak and other poskim and Lechem Chamudot it is explained that on day thirty he does not bless. Nevertheless on day thirty-one it seems he blesses as I explained; and one may interpret so also the Levush's words — since he sees on day thirty-one it is like an interruption of thirty days; see there.
