[Levush] He need not, etc. This requires study, because Beit Yosef makes clear that he is not permitted to bless. Perhaps once there is no obligation, a blessing would automatically be in vain. See above, siman 210 seif katan 2. Alternatively, the Levush means one may say it without Hashem's Name and kingship, in which case there is no prohibition. Bach rules likewise.
[Levush] Unless thirty days have elapsed, etc. This implies that thirty full days are required apart from both the day on which he saw it and the day on which he sees it again, so that if he saw it on Sunday he would not bless until Wednesday four weeks later. In my humble opinion, the Yerushalmi in chapter HaRoeh says "once in thirty days," and Baal Halachot Gedolot and Agudah use the same wording, implying that if he sees it on the thirtieth day he must bless. This also shows that Magen Avraham's view is incorrect that if he sees it on Monday, which is the thirtieth day, he blesses, based on the Gemara's use of the term "periods" as above in siman 20. Here and there, however, two sightings within thirty days would result. This appears to be Tosafot's intent in chapter HaRoeh in bringing proof from the Yerushalmi. Maharshal wonders why they need the Yerushalmi when the same rule is in our Gemara. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty, for our Gemara says "until thirty days," which could be read inclusively. The Rosh, Semag, Semak, other poskim, and Lechem Chamudot make clear that on day thirty he does not bless. Nevertheless, on day thirty-one it appears that he does bless. The Levush too can be understood this way, because seeing on day thirty-one follows an interruption of thirty days.
[Levush] For there is an opinion, etc. Bach and Lechem Chamudot, page 61, wonder that in Horayot 5 this is a dispute between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah, and the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah that one tribe is called a congregation. Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchot Shegagot, rules so. Yet in chapter 7 of Hilchot Pesach he does not rule this way. Magen Avraham answers that only regarding a ruling of beit din do we follow that view, because the verse explicitly uses the word congregation there. It appears to me that there is proof from the Yerushalmi, chapter HaRoeh, where no halachic conclusion is given. See Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. Still, the proof of Lechem Chamudot and Maadanei Melech from Tosafot requires study. Tosafot write that one does not bless over the place where Sancheriv's camp fell because the miracle is not recognizable at the site, just as at the crossings of the sea only the person for whom the miracle occurred blesses. Maadanei Melech infers that Tosafot hold one tribe is called a congregation, for otherwise the simpler reason would be that it did not involve all Israel. But upon examination, Tosafot's precise wording is that Chizkiyahu is no worse than a distinguished individual, meaning that for a distinguished individual a blessing may be warranted even without a majority of Israel. Thus, if anything, Tosafot prove that one tribe is not called a congregation here.
