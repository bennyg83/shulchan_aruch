(1) In the Prophets. Since, as is known, the law does not prescribe specific verses for the haftarah, but only requires that it relate to the parashah, as stated below at the end of siman 428 in the gloss, this teaches that the maftir may skip in the Prophets from one passage to another. This is indeed the custom in several haftaros, where verses from other passages are added.
(2) But one may not skip in the Torah. This means that on Monday and Thursday, when the law requires only ten verses for three readers, or on Yom Tov, when five readers are required, one should not initially read them by skipping, but in sequence.
(3) Lest the listener become confused. Words of Torah contain warnings, punishments, and mitzvos, and they must enter the hearts of the listeners. When a person moves from one passage to another, it is difficult to contemplate them properly. This is not so with the Prophets.
(4) Such as “After the death” and “But on the tenth.” Although there is a considerable gap between them, since the first is in Parashas Acharei Mos and the second in Parashas Emor, nevertheless, because both concern Yom Kippur, one may skip and read them together. The same applies to the practice on a communal fast, when “Vayechal” is read from Parashas Ki Sisa and one skips to “Pesol lecha.” This too is because both passages concern one subject.
(5) Provided that he does not read, etc. Meaning, they permitted skipping and rolling the Sefer Torah to the second passage, although the matter is noticeable, so that he should not come to read the second passage by heart, which is forbidden.
(6) That he not delay, etc. In their times it was customary to translate between each verse and the next. This teaches that the passages must be close enough that the skipping does not take longer than the meturgeman needs to complete his translation, because it is disrespectful to the congregation for them to stand silently and wait. The poskim write that nowadays, when translation is not customary, it is nevertheless permitted, since all the haftaros are written in separate booklets or in Chumashim. One can mark and quickly locate all the verses belonging to that haftarah, so the congregation will not need to stand silently. For this reason, nowadays it is permitted even when the verses come from two different prophets and are very far apart.
(7) One may not skip. Because this causes excessive confusion. This applies specifically when the passages concern two subjects, but if they concern one subject, one may skip. Peri Chadash.
(8) And within Trei Asar. They are considered one prophet for this purpose because they are short.
(9) Toward its beginning. Meaning, toward the beginning, namely backward, even when the passages are adjacent. There is a dispute among the poskim. Some say that only from one prophet to another may one not skip backward, even within Trei Asar, but within a single prophet one may skip backward if the passages are close and the congregation will not need to stand silently. Magen Avraham inclines to prohibit skipping backward even within one prophet, and Eliyah Rabbah rules accordingly. Peri Megadim, however, inclines to permit reading backward within one prophet when the passages concern one subject.
