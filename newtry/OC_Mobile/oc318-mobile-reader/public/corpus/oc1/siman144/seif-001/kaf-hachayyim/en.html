1) [Seif 1] One skips in the Prophets, but one does not skip in the Torah, etc. Rashi explained: because the words of the Torah are words of warnings and punishments and mitzvos, and they must enter the hearts of the listeners; and when a person goes from one subject to another, it is not easy to contemplate. Perishah, ot 2.
2) There: And one does not skip in the Torah, etc. The Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the Hagahos HaTur in the name of the Maharam Mintz, siman 85, that the same applies that one should not read on one Shabbos in two scrolls, even though there is no skipping there, such as parashas Pekudei and parashas Vayikra; see there. But the Pri Chadash, ot 1, wrote that there is no reasoning in his words to rely upon him, and therefore one who reads in two scrolls has not lost [i.e., it is valid]. End of his language. And the Yad Aharon cited him in the Hagahos HaTur. And so wrote [the author] above in siman 135, ot 5, in the name of several poskim, that if it happened that they omitted and did not read parashas Pekudei, that it is permitted to read it with parashas Vayikra; see there.
3) There: Such as 'Acharei Mos' and 'Ach BeAsor,' etc. That is to say, even though they are distant from each other, this one in parashas Acharei and that one in parashas Emor, it is permitted, since both are of the matter of Yom Kippur. Taz and Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. And this is also the reason that on a public fast they have the custom to read 'Vayechal' in parashas Ki Sisa and skip to 'Pesol lecha,' because they are of one matter. And see the Eshel Avraham, ot 1, who wrote another reason in this, because they read them [with] two men. End of his words. And the practical difference between the two reasons: for the first reason it is permitted to begin 'Pesol' even [for] one who read 'Vayechal,' but for the second reason one should not read 'Vayechal' except [for] the second [reader]; and although the Kohen Gadol would read both, that was because it was not possible, for the entire reading was incumbent upon him, but where it is possible we do the skipping [with] two men. And the world has the custom to be stringent like the second reason, as the Emes LeYaakov wrote in the law of ordinary and leap years, ot 9; Ledovid Emes, siman 10, ot 9; Yafeh LaLev, ot 1. And see the Mishnah Berurah in the Shaar HaTziyun, ot 3, who wrote that [with] two men it is permitted to skip in two matters, and this is the opposite of what Maran, of blessed memory, wrote in the Bedek HaBayis, that in two matters one does not read from the Torah in one scroll; and what he permitted in seif 3 is specifically in pressing circumstances, so as not to nullify the enactment of the Sages, as stated in the Beis Yosef.
4) There: And [only] provided that he does not read by heart, etc. That is to say, although they permitted skipping in one matter, that is specifically to read in the Sefer Torah, but by heart it is forbidden. And that which the Kohen Gadol would read 'and on the tenth of the month,' etc., by heart -- there it is different, for it was not possible, since one does not roll a Sefer Torah before the congregation, and also one does not bring another [scroll], because one does not read for one man in two Sifrei Torah, as stated in the Gemara there in Yoma, folio 70a; and the Kesef Mishneh cited it, chapter 12 of Hilchos Tefillah, law 8. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2.
