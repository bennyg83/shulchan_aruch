[3] They roll, and the honor of the congregation is set aside, etc. We do not follow the responsum of the Rashba, who wrote that they should read from a Chumash. This is difficult to me, because the Rashba appears to contradict himself. Rabbi David Abudraham, page 10, writes in the name of the Rashba that even if one already recited the blessing in the morning, he must recite another blessing for the mitzvah of reading instituted by Ezra, and that enactment was established only for reading from a Sefer Torah. If so, the enactment certainly does not apply to a Chumash. The Rashba’s proof from the Kohen Gadol, who read “But on the tenth” by heart, is likewise no proof according to this, because there the reading was not part of the enactment. Magen Avraham also questions the Shulchan Aruch from the case of the Kohen Gadol and gives a forced answer, but in my humble opinion the matter is resolved. He also overlooked what Bach writes at the end of this siman, that in their time the Sefer Torah stood outside the building, and only then did they not roll it. According to what I explained in siman 143, seif katan 3, that the discussion concerns a Chumash made as a scroll in the manner of a Sefer Torah, the matter is understandable. However, I wrote there that the wording implies that it concerns our ordinary Chumashim. See Taz, Yoreh Deah 279, who discusses this at length but overlooked the words of the great authorities cited above. It also appears from their words that Bircas HaTorah is only D’Rabbanan. This follows from the responsum of the Rashba cited by Beit Yosef in siman 47, beginning “He wrote,” and so too Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 59. Be’er Sheva, page 58, disputes this at length and holds that even one who already recited the blessing in the morning is obligated by Torah law, but he overlooked the poskim cited above. His inference here from the Tur in siman 47, that the Tur wrote “that blessing was instituted” rather than “they instituted,” is also astonishing, for in siman 139 the Tur writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yechiel using the expression “they instituted.” The poskim cited above likewise use the term “was instituted” even regarding Ezra’s enactment. His proof from Berachos 21a, “From where do we know that Bircas HaTorah before study is from the Torah? As it is stated, ‘When I call the name of Hashem,’” is also no proof, for it appears to me that this is an asmachta. I likewise found in Megillas Esther, page 79, that he explains Rambam’s view in this way. It further appears to me that since Rabbi Abahu at the beginning of the chapter Sheloshah She’Achlu says, “From where is the blessing of zimun derived? As it is written, ‘When I call the name of Hashem,’” he does not accept that verse as the source for Bircas HaTorah.
