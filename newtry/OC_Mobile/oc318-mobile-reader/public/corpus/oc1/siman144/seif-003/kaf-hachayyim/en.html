3) There: Such as 'Acharei Mos' and 'Ach BeAsor,' etc. That is to say, even though they are distant from each other, this one in parashas Acharei and that one in parashas Emor, it is permitted, since both are of the matter of Yom Kippur. Taz and Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. And this is also the reason that on a public fast they have the custom to read 'Vayechal' in parashas Ki Sisa and skip to 'Pesol lecha,' because they are of one matter. And see the Eshel Avraham, ot 1, who wrote another reason in this, because they read them [with] two men. End of his words. And the practical difference between the two reasons: for the first reason it is permitted to begin 'Pesol' even [for] one who read 'Vayechal,' but for the second reason one should not read 'Vayechal' except [for] the second [reader]; and although the Kohen Gadol would read both, that was because it was not possible, for the entire reading was incumbent upon him, but where it is possible we do the skipping [with] two men. And the world has the custom to be stringent like the second reason, as the Emes LeYaakov wrote in the law of ordinary and leap years, ot 9; Ledovid Emes, siman 10, ot 9; Yafeh LaLev, ot 1. And see the Mishnah Berurah in the Shaar HaTziyun, ot 3, who wrote that [with] two men it is permitted to skip in two matters, and this is the opposite of what Maran, of blessed memory, wrote in the Bedek HaBayis, that in two matters one does not read from the Torah in one scroll; and what he permitted in seif 3 is specifically in pressing circumstances, so as not to nullify the enactment of the Sages, as stated in the Beis Yosef.
