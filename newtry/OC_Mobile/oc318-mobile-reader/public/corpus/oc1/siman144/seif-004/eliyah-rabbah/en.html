[4] One person with two Sifrei Torah, etc. With one Sefer Torah, however, such as if he was called for the fourth aliyah and someone honored him with the sixth or final aliyah, Perishah writes that it appears permissible. This is also implied by the Rivash cited by Beit Yosef in siman 282 regarding maftir, where he considers whether one who already read from the Torah may be called again. He inclines to prohibit it because all the required aliyos have already been completed and it would be a blessing in vain, implying that before their completion it is permitted, even outside a pressing circumstance. Bach, however, writes plainly that it is permitted even for maftir, and Maharil likewise records that he was called for maftir although he had already received the levi aliyah. This requires study. I later examined the words of the Rivash and found that he agrees that one may be called again for maftir even after the required aliyos have been completed. His uncertainty concerns only whether the final reader, who will serve as maftir, must be called again to recite another blessing, since there is no concern for latecomers in such a short interval. See there. This is astonishing in relation to Perishah’s reading. Practically, however, one should not be lenient and call a person twice even among the seven required readers except in a pressing circumstance. I explained the reason in siman 282, seif katan 10, in the name of Or Zarua and Darchei Moshe: “In a multitude of people is the King’s glory.” Perishah overlooked all this regarding one Sefer Torah, where there is no concern of casting aspersion upon the scroll. On Shabbos, however, when two or three Sifrei Torah are taken out, it is prohibited. At first glance, one who was called to the first Sefer Torah should be permitted to be called to the third, because there is no concern of casting aspersion upon the first, since he did not read from the second. Yet the Gemara in Yoma and Sotah says, “There, three men read from three Sifrei Torah, so there is no aspersion,” implying that with two men there is an aspersion. This is also implied by the unqualified rulings of the poskim. The reason is that people may say that a defect in the first scroll has now become known. This is somewhat difficult in light of what I wrote in siman 135 in the name of Beit Yosef regarding a kohen, that we do not say that his disqualification became known only now. See there; the cases may be distinguished. It further appears at first glance that the prohibition applies only when the final reader is called to the second Sefer Torah, similar to the Kohen Gadol, who read all the passages himself. If the final reader is not called to the second scroll, there is no aspersion, since the final reader did not read from it. This resolves Bach’s difficulty with the custom of calling for maftir from the second Sefer Torah even one who had already been called to the first. See there. It appears that this custom applied only to the other readers, not to the final one, as stated above. Practically, however, the unqualified rulings of the poskim imply that there is no distinction. Nevertheless, this may be joined to the reasons I gave in siman 669 for leniency on Simchas Torah, when one who was called to the first Sefer Torah is also called as Chassan Bereishis from the second. See there. Magen Avraham was later printed, and I saw that he is lenient about this even on other days of the year. In my humble opinion, one should not be so lenient for the reasons I explained.
