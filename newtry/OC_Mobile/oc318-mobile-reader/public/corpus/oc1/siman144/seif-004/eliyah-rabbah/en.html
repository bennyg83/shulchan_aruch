F(4) For one person in two Torah scrolls, etc. — but one Torah scroll, such as he went up for the fourth and someone honored him for the sixth or last — Perishah wrote: it appears to me it is permitted; also from what Ribash wrote in Beit Yosef siman 482 regarding maftir, whether one who read in the Torah can go up — he leaned to forbid because the obligations were already completed and it is like a blessing in vain; this implies that before that it is permitted, and even not in a time of pressure — end of his words. But Bach wrote simply that even for maftir it is permitted; likewise Maharil that he went up for maftir though he had gone up for Levi — needs study. I again examined Ribash's words: he admits one goes up also for maftir even if they already went up, and he is not satisfied only regarding the last one who goes up for maftir, whether he must return to read him to bless — because there is no reason for concern of those entering in such a short time — see there; and this is questionable on Perishah. However for the law one should not be lenient even among the seven readers themselves to read twice, except in a time of pressure; the reason I wrote in siman 482 note 10 in the name of Or Zaruah and Darkei Moshe — due to multitude of people is glory of the king; and Perishah omitted all this regarding one Torah scroll where there is no defect; but on Shabbat when they take out two or three Torah scrolls it is forbidden. And it appears that one who went up in the first Torah scroll is permitted to go up on the third, for there is no concern of defect of the first here since he did not read in the second; but from what the gemara says in Yoma and Sotah, three men in three Torah scrolls — there is no defect — implies that with two men there is also defect; and likewise from the conclusion of the poskim; and the reason we are concerned is lest the defect of the first become known now. It is somewhat difficult from what I wrote in siman 135 in the name of Beit Yosef regarding a kohen that we do not say its defect became known now — see there, and one can distinguish. And further it appears that only when the last one goes up to the second Torah scroll it is forbidden, similar to the High Priest who read alone for everyone; but when the last one does not read, there is no defect, since the last one did not read in the second. With this the Bach's wonder on the custom to read maftir in the second Torah scroll even for one who went up first is resolved — see there; it appears the custom was only for the other readers and not the last, as mentioned above. However for the law it appears from the conclusion of the poskim there is no distinction; and in any case there is a branch for the reasons I wrote in siman 669 that one may be lenient on Simchat Torah that one who went up in the first Torah scroll also goes up for Bereishit in the second Torah scroll — see there. Again the book Magen Avraham was printed and I saw he is lenient in this also on other days of the year; in my humble opinion one should not be so lenient for the reasons I explained.
