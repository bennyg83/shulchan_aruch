(17) With two Torah scrolls — whether regarding one subject or two subjects, such as on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh or on Yom Tov, where the law is that they read the haftarah maftir from another scroll already rolled and prepared for that place, so they need not roll the first scroll in the presence of the congregation, as likewise in siman 63. One should not call for maftir one who was called up last, that he too ascend for maftir on the second scroll, even if there is no other who knows how to read the haftarah — for it appears to cast a defect on the first scroll as though it is missing [a portion] or otherwise pasul; rather they roll the first Torah scroll until they reach that place. And if they wish to call for maftir from among those already called up, before the last one — Magen Avraham permits, for a defect applies only when one reads in both one after the other, for they will say: why did he not read in the first, except that the portion was missing in it; but when he interrupted in between with another person, it is now like reading anew. Bach forbids; and Eshel Avraham sides with the law per Bach's reason, lest they now say the defect of the first scroll is known, and therefore he read from another; but on Simchat Torah Eshel Avraham sides to be lenient in this, like Magen Avraham, that it is permitted to call for Bereishit one who already ascended in the first Torah scroll, since he has further branches to permit there.
(18) Three men with three scrolls — and the same applies to two men with two scrolls. And if the first of the three wishes to read from the third Torah scroll for maftir — it depends on the dispute of Bach and Magen Avraham that I wrote at the beginning.
(19) When it falls on Shabbat — that one reads the subject of the day, and the second for Rosh Chodesh, and the third for Chanukah as maftir.
(20) There is no concern of defect — even regarding one subject [Peri Chadash]; and see in Peri Megadim.
