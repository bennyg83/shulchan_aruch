With two Sifrei Torah. Even when the two readings concern different subjects. Kesef Mishneh.
Three men. This implies that the first reader may not read from the third Sefer Torah. This is surprising, because we see that the custom is for one who already read from the Torah to be called for maftir from the second Sefer Torah. Bach writes that this is an improper custom and should be protested. I have seen no proof for his words. It may be that if no one else knows how to read the haftarah, the first reader may indeed be called for maftir. See siman 282, seif 5. The Gemara is discussing a case where others are capable, and its purpose is to teach the number of readers, as it enumerates many such cases in Megillah 29. There is also another difficulty: according to Bach, he should have said in the earlier challenge why the Tur specified three men, namely to teach that the first reader may not read from the third Sefer Torah. See Bach. It therefore appears to me that it is permitted. There is concern of aspersion only when one person reads from both scrolls consecutively, for people may say that he did not read the first passage from the first scroll because that passage was missing from it. When there is an intervening reader, however, it is like a new reading. Nevertheless, the seventh reader should not be called for maftir from another Sefer Torah. Rather, they should roll the first Sefer Torah until they reach the required passage. So it appears to me.
