The custom on a Shabbos where a groom is present is to recite after the haftarah of that week, two or three verses from the haftarah of "Sos Asis" ["I greatly rejoice" - Isaiah 61:10]. And when Rosh Chodesh falls out on Shabbat and Sunday, after we recite the haftarah for that Shabbat, we recite the first verse and the last verse of the haftarah of "Va-yomer Lo Yehonatan, machar chodesh" ["And Yehonatan said to him 'tomorrow will be the new moon'" - Samuel 20:18]. And one should not protest [this custom].[(The word "haftarah": some say it is [related to] the phrase ain maftirin achar ha-pesach ["We do not eat anything after the [korban] pesach"], meaning, remove [it] [i.e. which signals the end]; that is to say, it is the removal/ending of the morning prayers) [And see below in siman 425, se'if 3, and the end of siman 428 for how we [Ashkenazim] practice.]] השבוע שנים או שלשה פסוקים מהפטרת שוש אשיש וכשחל ר"ח בשבת וביום א' אחר שמפטירין ההפטרה בשבת אומרים פסוק ראשון ופסוק אחרון מהפטרת ויאמר לו יהונתן מחר חודש ואין למחות בידם.
