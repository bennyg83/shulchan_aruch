From the end. See Taz and Rashi, Megillah 24a, s.v. “from the end,” meaning backward. It does not necessarily mean all the way to the beginning, but merely toward the beginning. Skipping all the way to the beginning would be forbidden even from the beginning toward the end, because it would take longer than the meturgeman’s translation. See Peri Chadash and Tosafos in the chapter Ba Lo. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 12 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 13, holds that within one prophet one may skip even backward. Only when skipping from one prophet to another within Trei Asar may one not skip backward, because in such a case there is also confusion. Peri Chadash writes that even when the passages concern one subject, one may not skip backward from one prophet to another. Now, one may not skip from one prophet to another because this causes excessive confusion, and so writes Terumas HaDeshen, siman 20. This is difficult, for if so, why may one skip within Trei Asar? Levush adds that one may not skip from one prophet to another because it causes confusion, since this entails some additional delay. He thereby gives a reason: there is somewhat greater delay, even though the meturgeman has not yet finished, and the combination of two prophets and some delay causes confusion. Trei Asar, however, are short, so there is not as much delay. Peri Chadash writes that one may not skip from one prophet to another when the passages concern two subjects, implying that for one subject it is permitted. Knesses HaGedolah, in the name of Maharam Mintz, writes that one may not read Pekudei and Vayikra from two Chumashim, and Peri Chadash cites this. If so, why does the Gemara ask its question? Perhaps one may skip in the Prophets from one prophet to another when the passages concern one subject, but in the Torah one may not move between two Chumashim even for one subject. Apparently, then, one may not skip from one prophet to another even for one subject. This is not a proof, however, for if so, why mention skipping? The same would apply even without skipping. See there. Moreover, the unqualified wording implies even within one Chumash. In any event, I do not know a compelling proof for this except by logic.
