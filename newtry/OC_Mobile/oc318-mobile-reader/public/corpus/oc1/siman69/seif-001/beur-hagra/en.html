Seif 1, if there are, etc. This is the wording of the Ran in his explanation of Rashi.
The wording, etc. This is like the expressions 'within half of Pesach,' 'the time required to eat a peras,' and similar usages. So too it is evident there, folio 24, that they would recite the first blessing, as stated in seif 2.
And nowadays, etc. The reason they would recite the first blessing is, as stated in siman 59, because of the Kedushah contained in it, according to the second opinion there in seif 3. According to the first opinion there, however, which the gloss there rules in accordance with, the Beit Yosef was pressed here and agreed in Darkei Moshe with Rabbeinu Yonah's explanation, that it was to discharge the obligation of one who was not proficient, as stated in seif 4. Nowadays, however, we are all proficient, and it is needed only in order to answer Kaddish and Barechu. This is certainly so when they have already recited the blessings of Krias Shema. See there. The reference to one who is not proficient is not exact. Rather, it means one who wishes to discharge his obligation through another, whereas nowadays people cannot concentrate properly while listening, as stated there in seif 4.
And they did not follow this practice, etc. This accords more closely with what was stated above, that the reason is the Kedushah found there, which does not apply to the evening service. Although the Ran wrote that no distinction was made, that was in accordance with his local custom.
After they have finished, etc. This is stated there in the Mishnah, 'and they do not pass before the ark,' etc., according to the Ran's explanation above, after each individual has prayed separately. He wrote that the Mishnah divided them into two clauses because of the rule stated above at the beginning of siman 55, seif 2, that if they began, etc., as stated in the Yerushalmi.
And You are holy. The Tur writes this. Meaning, he concludes there that one passes before the ark only because of Kedushah, but he nevertheless completes the blessing, just as with leading the responsive recitation of Shema. Sefer HaManhig, however, writes that he should complete the entire prayer, and the gloss below likewise states this.
Because it is considered, etc. This is stated in the Gemara there.
And it is necessary, etc. As stated at the end of chapter 10 of Maseches Soferim: 'Kaddish and Barechu may not be recited with fewer than ten. Our rabbis in the West say with seven,' and they give as support for their view, 'When breaches are repaired in Israel, when the people volunteer, bless Hashem.' Some say even with six, through Barechu. Tosafos explain that they do not disagree with the first Tanna or with our Mishnah. Rather, the discussion here concerns how many of those present have not yet heard these prayers.
And if they are not, etc., and even. Tosafos and the other poskim write this in the name of Rashi's disciples, who wrote it in Rashi's name. Maseches Soferim likewise concludes there: 'In a place where there are nine or ten who have heard both Barechu and Kaddish, after the prayer one of them stands and recites Barechu or Kaddish, and they answer him, and he has fulfilled the requirement.' The meaning of 'nine' is that this may be done even for the sake of one person. It then says 'ten,' meaning that if there is one additional person who did not hear, one of those ten may lead the responsive recitation. They brought proof from the shaliach tzibbur who passes before the ark, since at the end of Rosh Hashanah we say that Rabban Gamliel's enactment was to discharge even one who is proficient, although the shaliach tzibbur has already prayed. The earlier statement in Maseches Soferim, 'Our rabbis in the West,' etc., means that this may be done even when there are only seven people altogether, and it disputes the first Tanna. This is also implied by the wording there, which first says, 'Kaddish may not be recited,' etc., and then, 'Our rabbis,' etc., implying a dispute. The Rosh likewise agrees. Rabbeinu Tam, however, did not wish to rely on this according to the above explanation of Maseches Soferim. The Shulchan Aruch therefore writes, 'And it is necessary,' etc., followed by, 'And if they are not,' etc., because Rashi's view is primary, and the poskim agreed with it.
Nevertheless, if, etc. This is because Tosafos also dispute this view and write that no proof can be brought from the Amidah. See the Rosh there, though the matter there requires study.
And one who passes, etc. This is the ruling of Sefer HaManhig, as above. This is also implied by the plain meaning of 'they do not pass before the ark,' since the term 'lead the responsive recitation' is not used there.
But the others, etc. They need only listen to Kedushah. Mahari Abuhav.
And certainly, etc. He expresses doubt about this there. Although the Mishnah appears to indicate it, as the Yerushalmi asks why the cases were divided into two clauses and answers, 'If they began,' etc., that passage concerns one who had already prayed. See Magen Avraham.
And it is forbidden, etc. Maseches Soferim states there: 'The Sages already enacted for the chazzanim to say after the blessing of redemption, “May the name of Hashem be blessed from now and forever,” followed by “Bless Hashem, Who is blessed,” in order to discharge those who did not hear,' etc. 'The people of the West and the people of the East practiced saying it after Oseh Shalom in each of the three Amidah prayers,' etc. The passage there requires study.
And he began, etc. If he did not begin, he may recite Barechu a second time. Sefer HaManhig. See Taz, and see Sukkah 53b.
Nevertheless, a man, etc., even, etc. This is evident from the passage in Maseches Soferim cited above. We also say in Berachos 3a, 'When we eat bread,' etc., 'and let them recite Barechu,' etc.
Only, etc. As stated regarding one person reading from two Torah scrolls in Yoma 70a: 'Three,' etc.
