Section 61, "if there are," etc. The language of the Ran in his explanation of Rashi.

The language, etc. And as in "poreis hapesach" — eating a portion, and the like; and so too there (Megillah 24) they would say the first blessing, and as written in section 2.

And now, etc. For the reason they say the first blessing as written in siman 59 is on account of sanctity, for the second version there has 63, but for the first version there and the chapter in the gloss there accords with his view — he was pressed here in Beit Yosef, and Darkei Moshe agreed with Rashba's explanation of Rabbeinu Tam to fulfill the obligation of one who is not expert, as written in section 4; and now we are all expert and there is no need except in order to answer Kaddish and Barchu, and all the more so when they already blessed the blessing of Keriat Shema "al shem." And some explain "not expert" means one who wishes to leave, unlike now when it is impossible — they can intend with hearing, as written in section 4.

And they did not practice so, etc. This accords more with what he wrote that on account of sanctity of the Name, unlike that of Arvit; even though Ran wrote they do not distinguish so much per his custom.

After they finished, etc. In the Mishnah "one does not pass," etc., and per the Ran cited above — after they prayed each one independently; and he split them into two on account of the above in beginning of siman 55 seif 2 "if he began," etc., and as written in the Jerusalem Talmud.

And You are holy. So too the Tur; and he means that he ends there that one does not pass before the Ark except on account of sanctity — rather he ends the blessing, and as in repeating Shema; but Sefer HaMinhag writes that he should complete the entire prayer, and so in the gloss below.

Because it is, etc. Gemara there.

And one needs, etc. As written at the end of the first chapter of Megillah; and it is impossible with Kaddish and Barchu with fewer than ten — our rabbis in the West say with seven, and siman 59 per their words in "bifroa peraot" in Israel when they volunteer with Barchu Hashem; and some say even with six until Barchu; and Tosafos explained: not per the Tosafot and our Mishnah text, but the case here is how many will be those who did not hear.

And if they are not, etc., "and even." So too Tosafos and Rif in the name of students of Rashi who wrote in the name of Rashi, and so concludes there in Megillah; and in a place where there are nine or ten who heard between Barchu and Kaddish, after prayer one of them stands and says Barchu or Kaddish and they answer after him and fulfill their obligation; and he means nine even for one; and he said ten when there is another one who did not hear — one of these ten may repeat; and they brought proof from the shaliach tzibur who went down before the Ark — we say at the end of Rosh Hashanah for Rabban Gamliel to fulfill even the expert, even though the shaliach tzibur already prayed. And what is written in Megillah initially "our rabbis in the West," etc., means even if there are only seven in all and disagrees with Tosafot — the language there "and it is impossible our rabbis," etc., forty who disagree; and Rosh agreed except Rabbeinu Tam did not wish to rely on this per explanation in Megillah as above; therefore wrote in Shulchan Aruch "and one needs," "and if they are not," etc., for Rashi's words are primary and so agreed the poskim.

Nevertheless if, etc. Because Tosafos also disagree on this book and wrote that there is no proof from the Amidah — see Rosh there, and it requires study there.

And one who passes, etc. Sefer HaMinhag as above; and so 40 — the plain meaning is one does not pass, for here there is no repeating section.

But the others, etc. — they need only to hear kedushah, so Mahari.

And all the more so, etc. There he is uncertain about this, even though in the Mishnah text it reads so; and as the Jerusalem Talmud asked why he split them into two and answered that if he began, etc. — there the case is where he already prayed, per Magen Avraham.

And it is forbidden, etc. In Megillah there; and the Sages already enacted for prayer leaders to say after redemption "Y'hi shem Hashem m'vorakh me'atah va'ed olam," and after it "Barchu et Hashem hamvorakh" in order to fulfill those who did not hear, etc.; and the people of the West and the people of the East practiced to say them after "Oseh shalom" in the three Amidah prayers, etc. — and it requires study there (tzarich iyun).

And he began, etc. But if he did not begin — he says Barchu independently (Sefer HaMinhag), and Tur, and per the language in Sukkah 53b.

Nevertheless one, etc., "even," etc. So in Megillah cited above; and we say in Berachos 3a: "When we ate fodder," etc., and for Barchu, etc.

Only, etc. As written regarding one man in two books in chapter 7 of Yoma 71a, "three," etc.
