Barukh Hashem, etc. And if he did not hear Barchu but only heard the congregation answering, he answers Amen — see siman 57 sk 1.

Shema. And if he heard from the Torah reader, it suffices and they need not go back on his account (Magen Avraham). And likewise they pray the entire prayer on behalf of one who has not prayed (Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe). And Magen Avraham wrote; however, nireh li that if there are not ten who have not prayed, the shaliach tzibur should not pray silently but should begin immediately aloud, for the main intent is on account of sanctity. (And Magen Avraham wrote in siman 54 that if they repeat after the final Kaddish, he should say some verses before repeating Shema, for Kaddish is never said except after sections or after the Amidah.)

Vayaavor. And now the custom is that mourners repeat Shema even if they heard, and there is one who did not hear — and it is not correct (Magen Avraham).

First. That he should complete his prayer — meaning all the more so that the one who repeats and the one passing before the Ark should not interrupt if he did not pray first, even though he will need to read Keriat Shema afterward and will not adjoin redemption to prayer; and Magen Avraham wrote that lekhatchilah he should not do so, but rather pray in order and adjoin redemption to prayer, and afterward repeat Shema.

Or. If he did not begin, he returns and says Barchu to those who came to the synagogue (Beit Yosef, Levush).

To interrupt. Between redemption and prayer — for without need they interrupt with verses, but in the middle of blessings he should not interrupt (Magen Avraham).

The first. Even the first prayer leader (Levush). In Mishpetei Shmuel he wrote that if the first ones took out a Torah scroll and read from it, the second ones may not take out a Torah scroll a second time and read from it.
