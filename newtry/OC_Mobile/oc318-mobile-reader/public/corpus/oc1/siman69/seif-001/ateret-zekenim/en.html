And now the custom is not to say the entire blessing of Yotzer Or, but rather they say Kaddish and Barchu, and they answer after him "Barukh the Omnipresent..." This follows the view that the kedushah of Yotzer Or — one alone says it — and they do not appear like heretics, as Maharil wrote. Even per the view that one alone says it, nevertheless they must bless the first blessing when alone, for otherwise they would appear like heretics, the Omnipresent forbid — because he tells them to bless and they are not willing. And I do not know the reason for his words why they would be like heretics, for behold they already answered on Barchu "Barukh the Omnipresent hamvorakh leolam vaed," for behold they did what he told them, as in the blessing of the Torah: when one goes up to the Torah scroll he says Barchu and the congregation answers what they answer after Barchu, and that suffices (Darkei Moshe).
