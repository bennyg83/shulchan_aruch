The Rema of Fano wrote, etc. The elder Rabbis instituted nothing, etc. Meaning, they did not protest against people who acted in this matter differently from the interpretation that appeared correct to those Rabbis.
Between the pillars, etc. Meaning, in their beis midrash, where there was no concern for dispute.
He needs to recite Yotzer, etc. Meaning, the chazzan does not recite Barechu on his behalf, which would constitute being Pores al Shema for him, since he at least already heard Barechu, although he had not yet reached the blessing of Yotzer.
He is permitted to begin, etc. Meaning, he himself is permitted to recite Barechu. However, he did not act properly, meaning by prolonging the passage of the daily offering and Pesukei DeZimrah.
Nevertheless, since he places Barechu immediately before Yotzer Or, meaning that he is now standing at the beginning of the blessing of Yotzer Or, it is proper. Meaning, although their custom was that the chazzan would not be Pores al Shema for him in such a case, the Rema of Fano nevertheless wrote that since he places Barechu immediately before Yotzer Or, the chazzan may recite Barechu for him. This is also implied by his wording below, and Eliyah Rabbah wrote likewise in his name; see there. Except where he has not recited the blessing, etc. Meaning, he has not yet begun the blessing of Yotzer Or. In that case, even if he already heard Barechu, the chazzan or another person may recite Barechu for him, since he now places Barechu immediately before Yotzer Or, as explained above.
In every case. Meaning, even if he heard Barechu and already recited the blessing of Yotzer, so that the chazzan may not recite Barechu for him, he himself may nevertheless recite Barechu when nine others are present who will respond to him, even though those nine have already fulfilled their obligation of Barechu.
This requires study, for if so, etc. For none of the listeners will recite, etc. Meaning, the difficulty is understandable if the person who already heard Barechu is Pores al Shema, because then the one who did not hear it will recite the blessing of Yotzer. If, however, the person who did not hear Barechu is Pores al Shema, while the listeners have already recited the blessings of Yotzer, none of the listeners will now recite the blessing of Yotzer. In truth, however, the authorities wrote the opposite, that it is preferable for one who did not hear Barechu to be Pores al Shema.
This is what Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein wrote there: there is no one among them, etc. Meaning, how can such a case occur? Even if the listeners have already recited the blessings of Krias Shema, they nevertheless recite the blessing of Yotzer now. He therefore explains that the case occurs when they wish to leave and go about their affairs.
Seif katan 1: And now, etc. This refers to Bircas HaTorah. The person called to the Torah also recites a blessing, while the listeners say only, “Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One, forever and ever.” Therefore, even in this case he does not appear to deny Hashem.
