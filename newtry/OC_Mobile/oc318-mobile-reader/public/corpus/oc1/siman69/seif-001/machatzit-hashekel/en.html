So wrote the Rama, etc.: they established nothing, etc. — meaning they did not protest against people who acted in this matter contrary to the explanation that seemed correct to these rabbis.

Between the pillars, etc. — meaning in their beit midrash where there is no dispute.

He must say Yotzer Or, etc. — meaning the sheliach tzibbur does not say Barechu for him, namely to recite Kerias Shema for him, since in any case he too heard Barechu, except that he had not then reached the blessing Yotzer Or.

It is permitted for him to begin, etc. — meaning he himself is permitted to say Barechu. It is true that he does not act properly — meaning in that he lengthened in Parashat HaTamid and psukei dezimra.

However, since he adjoins Barechu to Yotzer Or (namely, since now he stands at the beginning of the blessing Yotzer Or), it is fine — meaning it was their custom that the sheliach tzibbur does not recite Kerias Shema for him in such a case. Nevertheless he writes that since he adjoins Barechu to Yotzer Or, the sheliach tzibbur is permitted to say Barechu for him. And so it is implied from his language afterward. And so too in Eliyah Rabbah in his name — see there — except where he has not blessed on Shema — meaning that he has not yet begun the blessing Yotzer Or. For then even if he heard Barechu, the sheliach tzibbur or another may say Barechu for him, since he now adjoins Barechu to Yotzer Or, as explained above.

And in all cases — meaning even if he heard Barechu and has also already said the blessing Yotzer Or, even though the sheliach tzibbur is not permitted to say Barechu for him — but he himself is permitted to say Barechu when he has another nine who obey him, even though those nine have already fulfilled Barechu.

Requires further study — for if so, etc. — since there is not one of the listeners, etc. — meaning: it is fine if the one who recites Kerias Shema for him heard Barechu — then the one who did not hear may say the blessing Yotzer. Whereas if the one who recites is someone who did not hear, and the listeners have already said the blessings of Yotzer — then now none of the listeners may say the blessing Yotzer. And in truth the poskim wrote the opposite — that it is preferable that someone who did not hear should recite.

And this is what Mahari Weil wrote there — “there is not among them,” etc. — meaning, how does this occur? For even if the listeners said the blessing of Kerias Shema, nevertheless they now say the blessing Yotzer; and therefore he writes that this occurs when they wish to leave on their way.
