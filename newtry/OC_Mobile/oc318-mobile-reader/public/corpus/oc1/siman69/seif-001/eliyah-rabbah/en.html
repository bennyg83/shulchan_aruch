And now they did not practice, etc. For the reason of Yotzer Or was for one who says we do not say this kedushah individually (Levush), and you can challenge him — if so, we who hold in siman 59 that an individual may say kedushah, how do we resolve the complete Mishnah in chapter 3 of Megillah that teaches "they repeat Shema" and they explained it refers to Yotzer Or. Therefore it seems to me the main point is as Beit Yosef wrote — even for one who says they say it individually, nevertheless since the shaliach tzibur says Barchu in any case they need to bless some blessing, for if not they would appear as if they deny, Heaven forbid, the Supreme the Omnipresent who tells them to bless and they do not wish — therefore they say the first blessing of Keriat Shema, until here. And that they do not say the first blessing — Tosafos Yom Tov already wrote in Megillah that the reason is because they practice to repeat before the shaliach tzibur begins the prayer aloud, and it turns out the shaliach tzibur immediately begins with the eighteen blessings and it is not as denial, until here. And from this reason Darkei Chachmah wrote what they practice in some communities that after prayer they repeat Shema — they must be careful that in any case there will be one who can immediately say the blessings of Yotzer, for if not it would be as deniers, until here. And it appears not precisely — for the same applies when one can say the Amidah or Ahavah Rabah and similar; and it appears therefore I have not seen practiced to be careful to inquire after this, for presumably there is one who can say some blessing. But in truth requires study — for sometimes there is no one who can say a blessing at all and even so they repeat. And further it seems to me that according to this one must say the blessing aloud so all hear, for if not it would be for the remaining as if they do not wish to bless. And what appears to my humble opinion is as our reason — we do not hold like Beit Yosef that they appear as deniers when they do not say the blessing of Yotzer, for behold in what they respond "Baruch the Omnipresent hamvorakh" they show they wish for His blessing; and so Nachalas Tzvi wrote in the name of Darkei Moshe and brought proof from the blessing of the Torah that they respond only "Baruch the Omnipresent hamvorakh," and Derishah agreed except he challenged Darkei Moshe's proofs that the main praise is what the chazzan goes up and blesses "asher bachar banu" and "asher nasan" enacted with ten and the listener is as one who responds, until here. And to my humble opinion to resolve — Rama appears to hold that specifically Barchu must be said aloud so they respond, but not asher bachar, as will be explained in siman 339. And from this I wonder at Tosafos Yom Tov there who wrote that Baruch the Omnipresent hamvorakh is only a response to the mention of the Name as stated in chapter 3 of Yoma — and they respond after the High Priest who mentions the Name "baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed," and still what the shaliach tzibur told them to bless was not fulfilled until here his words, and he omitted the words of Darkei Moshe. Also it seems to my humble opinion it is not proof from the High Priest — for one can say that in this response too is a blessing, and further that the High Priest who mentioned the explicit Name as written required a response, which is not so after the shaliach tzibur, for a response is not called a blessing. And further it seems to me since here they respond and mention the Name "baruch the Omnipresent hamvorakh" it is certainly also a blessing, which is not so after the High Priest — they do not mention the Name in the response; and proof for this seems to me from what Beit Yosef wrote at the end of the siman [re] birchat hazimun — if three stop for seven they need not stop more than birchat hazimun since they mention the Name it is considered a proper blessing — see there and examine. But still need to resolve the Mishnah mentioned above why in the time of the Gemara they said the blessing of Yotzer Or. And I saw in Kesef Mishnah there who wrote the reason as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote — even though the shaliach tzibur fulfills for the non-expert, nevertheless from Keriat Shema they are not released except through saying and responding Amen; and therefore they do not repeat Shema to release the listeners with responding Amen except with ten, for if not responding Amen is nothing, until here. And if so it appears the reason was to fulfill the non-expert; and if so in our time when we are all expert in blessings and they do not repeat except in order to answer Kaddish and Barchu, there is no need to say the first blessing. And one should not challenge — if so they should have blessed the rest of the blessings to fulfill them — for perhaps Rabbeinu Yonah appears to hold they do not respond Amen after habocher b'amo and after geulah on account of the interruption, and if so the repetition did not help them for this, until here the language of Rama in Darkei Moshe. And it is difficult for me — behold Rama himself ruled siman 59 and siman 66 that they respond Amen after habocher and after Ga'al Yisrael. And further behold we hold below siman 215 and many places that responding Amen is not precisely that whoever hears fulfills. And we must say Keriat Shema is different, and see above siman 62 seif 3 and requires study. And further — because of the interruption they will not fulfill them at all? Certainly it is preferable they interrupt so they fulfill the blessing. Magen Avraham wrote siman 74 that if they repeat after the final Kaddish they should say some verses before repetition of Shema — for Kaddish is never said except after verses or after the Amidah.
