1) [Se'if 1.] If there are people each of whom prayed individually, etc. Ran and Rabbi Yitzchak Abuhav, of blessed memory, write likewise. The widespread custom among us is that Kaddish, Barechu, and Kedushah are recited only by one who has not yet prayed and arrived late at the synagogue. When he reaches Yishtabach, he recites Yishtabach aloud, followed by Kaddish and Barechu, and stops reciting aloud there; he does not recite the blessing Yotzer Or aloud. When he reaches the Amidah, he recites its first three blessings and Kedushah aloud, and completes his prayer silently. But whenever everyone has already prayed individually, Kaddish and Kedushah are not recited at all. Apparently, the words of Rama in Darkei Moshe indicate that this is also their custom, although his gloss below does not entirely imply this; see there. In any event, our custom is correct in my eyes. Although the words of the halakhic authorities indicate that it is permitted even when they have already prayed, that permission was not adopted as customary practice. Ma'amar Mordechai, ot 1. Petach HaDevir, ot 1, and Yafeh LaLev, ot 1, write likewise. However, if six or seven people are present, the practice is for them to pray the entire Amidah silently and afterward bring three people from outside. Even though those three have already heard the prayers and fulfilled their obligation, they join the others so that they may fulfill the mitzvah of the repetition of the Amidah and Kedushah, answer Amen to the blessings of the Amidah, and recite Modim DeRabbanan; at Shacharit, this also enables them to fulfill the mitzvah of Birkat Kohanim. But if six people who have not prayed are not present, they should not initially pray silently in order to conduct the repetition afterward. As Magen Avraham writes in se'if katan 4, if six who have not prayed are not present, the shaliach tzibbur should not first pray silently but should begin immediately aloud; see there. See also Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 5.
