He says Avot, etc. — See Mishna Berurah seif kaf (8) in what he wrote in the name of the gloss, and see in Biur HaGra that it implies from him that the Tur's view is not to say only the three blessings; but nevertheless for practical law it seems from him that he leans toward the gloss nearby — see there where he brings proof from the Mishnah. And see further in Mishna Berurah in what we wrote in the name of Magen Avraham; and be-di'eved, if there was not a majority minyan and they prayed the Amidah silently — whether afterward the shaliach tzibur can go before the Ark requires study (tzarich iyun), for according to Shulchan Aruch who rules at the beginning of the seif that even if they prayed initially each one individually, afterward one can go before the Ark — it is simple all the more so in this case, for I am asked per the view of Radbaz that we practice according to him, as I wrote in Mishna Berurah seif alef — if he also disagrees on this whether this would be considered only individual prayer, or perhaps in this case it is preferable since at the time of prayer there was a minyan in the synagogue, and also since when they gathered to ten there was upon them an obligation of sanctity before they began to pray, and as I wrote in Mishna Berurah seif 1 at its end — see there that in any case there was the obligation; and you should know that according to Radbaz who holds that the obligation of sanctity departed from them after they prayed — behold we always do the repetition of the shaliach tzibur even though we are expert, and per the words of Tosafos in Megillah (p. 23) the reason is on account of sanctity — therefore it is because at the time the ten gathered together there was upon them an obligation of sanctity, and if so, so too in our case. And I found in Shulchan Aruch of Gra haZvi that he is lenient in this be-di'eved; but nevertheless there is to reject — for perhaps he goes only per the view of Shulchan Aruch because he did not mention the view of Radbaz; but for our practice that we practice according to Radbaz, as Chatam Sofer wrote — requires study (tzarich iyun).

He should complete the entire prayer — It seems to me that he means silently, for the others can go on their way, as the Levush wrote.

He should complete his prayer — And see Mishna Berurah seif 23 at its end; and it is simple that for the sake of Keriat Shema repetition — if they do not want the rest of the people to wait for him, he can skip the verses of Pesukei Dezimra and say only Baruch She'amar and Ashrei and Yishtabach and repeat Shema; but if they do not want to wait for him at all, he should say a few verses and then Kaddish and Barchu; but requires study somewhat what he should do afterward — whether it is better to skip entirely and begin immediately after Barchu from Yotzer Or, for that is the superior Keriat Shema repetition when he says immediately Yotzer Or after it as is known, or he should begin afterward from Baruch She'amar; and see above in siman 54 what we wrote in the name of the Acharonim that Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach is a very old enactment — therefore seemingly it is better that he begin afterward from Baruch She'amar.

He may interrupt, etc. — And even though above he wrote that they did not practice in Arvit to repeat Shema, nevertheless there is a difference for a place where they practiced [Peri Megadim].

Nevertheless another man, etc. — That is, even though we prevented the shaliach tzibur from interrupting to fulfill them for the Amidah, but another man, etc.; and regarding Keriat Shema repetition he did not write with a yud, and regarding prayer he wrote with a yud — see Darkei Moshe who does not agree on this with Mahar"i Mintz, for practical law who holds that for prayer we require specifically ten who did not pray, but prayer is equivalent to Keriat Shema repetition; and so too Magen Avraham in seif 4.
