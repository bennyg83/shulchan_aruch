He recites Avos, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 8, regarding what is written there in the name of the gloss. See also Biur HaGra, from whom it appears that the Tur's view is that he should recite only the first three blessings. Nevertheless, as a practical ruling, it appears that Biur HaGra inclines toward the gloss cited nearby; see there, where he brings proof from the Mishnah. See further in Mishnah Berurah what we wrote in the name of Magen Avraham. After the fact, where there was not a majority of a minyan who had not yet prayed, and they prayed the Amidah silently, whether the chazzan may afterward go before the amud requires study. According to the Shulchan Aruch, which rules at the beginning of the seif that even when each person originally prayed individually, one of them may afterward go before the amud, it is obvious that this case is certainly permitted. My question is according to the Radbaz, whose view we follow, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 1. Does he also disagree in this case, because this is considered merely individual prayer, or is this case preferable, since there was a minyan in the synagogue at the time of prayer? Moreover, once ten had assembled, they were obligated in Kedushah before they began praying, as I wrote at the end of Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 1; see there, for the obligation applied to all of them. This may be demonstrated as follows. According to the Radbaz, who holds that their obligation of Kedushah lapses after they have prayed, why do we always repeat the Amidah, even though we are proficient? According to Tosafos in Megillah 23b, the reason for the repetition is Kedushah. Necessarily, it is because when the ten assembled together, the obligation of Kedushah rested upon them. The same should apply in our case. I found that the Shulchan Aruch HaRav rules leniently after the fact. Nevertheless, this proof may be rejected, for perhaps he follows only the view of the Shulchan Aruch, since he does not mention the view of the Radbaz. According to our custom, however, whereby we follow the Radbaz, as the Chasam Sofer wrote, this requires study.
He should complete the entire prayer. It appears to me that this means silently, since the others may leave and go about their affairs, as the Levush wrote.
So that he may complete his prayer. See the end of Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 13. It is obvious that for Pores al Shema, when the other people do not wish to wait for him, he may omit most of Pesukei DeZimrah and recite only Baruch She'amar, Ashrei, and Yishtabach, and then be Pores al Shema. If they are unwilling to wait for him at all, he should recite a few verses, followed by Kaddish and Barechu. It requires some study what he should do afterward. Is it preferable to omit Pesukei DeZimrah entirely and begin immediately after Barechu with Yotzer Or, since the finest form of Pores al Shema is to recite Yotzer Or immediately afterward, as is known? Or should he afterward begin from Baruch She'amar? See above, siman 54, where we wrote in the name of the later authorities that Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach are a very ancient enactment. It therefore appears preferable that he afterward begin with Baruch She'amar.
He may interrupt, etc. Although the Mechaber wrote above that the custom at Maariv is not to be Pores al Shema, this ruling is nevertheless relevant in a place where they do follow that practice. Peri Megadim.
Nevertheless, another person, etc. Meaning, although we prevented the chazzan from interrupting in order to discharge their obligation, another person may do so. Regarding the fact that in connection with Pores al Shema the text does not specify ten, whereas in connection with prayer it does specify ten, see Darkei Moshe. He does not agree as a matter of practical halachah with Rabbi Yehudah Mintz, who holds that for prayer there must specifically be ten who have not yet prayed. Rather, prayer has the same law as Pores al Shema. Magen Avraham likewise wrote this in seif katan 4.
