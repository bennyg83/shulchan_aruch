The matter of poreis al Shema: customs differ from place to place. Some places are accustomed that they poreis al Shema — “poreis al Shema” means saying the Bar'khu that we say — for those who arrive late to the synagogue after the congregation has finished the silent Amidah. And some places are accustomed that the shaliach tzibbur poreis al Shema for the latecomers after the final Kaddish — and see above siman 54 in Magen Avraham, who requires those who follow [the practice of Kaddish] after the final Kaddish to say from the beginning some verses if they wish to say Kaddish and Bar'khu. And see below siman 133 in Beit Yosef, where it implies their custom is that even if it happened that no one came to the synagogue after Bar'khu, nevertheless they are not strict about this except on Shabbos and Yom Tov as stated there; however one should not abolish their synagogue custom because of dispute. But where there is no concern for dispute, the practice should be that the shaliach tzibbur not say Bar'khu unless someone came after Bar'khu who had not heard [it] at all that day; and even if he already said Yotzer Or, it is also permitted to say [Bar'khu] for him even without adjoining Yotzer Or to it. And similarly one who already heard Bar'khu before prayer and now prays alone and reaches Bar'khu, and ten men happen to him who already heard Bar'khu — nevertheless it is permitted to poreis al Shema and say Bar'khu, he or another for him, since he will say immediately afterward the blessing of Yotzer Or. But if both [factors] lean to the stricter side — that is, he already heard Bar'khu, or heard from those ascending to the Torah who said Bar'khu, and moreover he is not now standing before Yotzer Or — he may no longer poreis al Shema nor say Bar'khu, he or another for him.
