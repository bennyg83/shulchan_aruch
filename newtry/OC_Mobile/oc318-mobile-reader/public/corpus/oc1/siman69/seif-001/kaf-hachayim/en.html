(1) [Seif 1] If there are people who each prayed individually on their own, etc. — So too Ran and Mahari, may his memory be blessed; and the common practice among us is not to say Kaddish and Barchu and kedushah except for one who has not yet prayed and was late to come to the synagogue — then when he reaches Yishtabach he says Yishtabach aloud and Kaddish and Barchu and stops there, and he does not say the blessing of Yotzer Or aloud; and when he reaches the Amidah he says the first three blessings and kedushah and completes his prayer silently. But all who prayed individually — they do not say Kaddish or kedushah at all; and apparently from the words of our teacher Rama, may his memory be blessed, in Darkei Moshe, such is their practice, even though below in the gloss it does not entirely imply so — see there. Nevertheless our custom is correct in my view, and even though from the words of the poskim it is clear there is permission even if they prayed — they did not practice it. Magen Avraham, letter alef; and so Pri Chadash, letter alef; Yafeh Lelev, letter alef. However, if they are six or seven they practice to pray the entire Amidah silently and afterward bring three from the marketplace even though those three already heard them and fulfilled the Amidah — in order to join with them and fulfill the mitzvah of repetition of the Amidah and kedushah and responding Amen to the Amidah blessings and Modim derabbanan; and if in Shacharit, also to fulfill the mitzvah of Birkat Kohanim. But if there are not six who did not pray, they should not pray initially silently in order to make repetition afterward, as Magen Avraham wrote in seif 4 — that if there are not six who did not pray, the shaliach tzibur should pray silently, only he should begin immediately aloud — see there. And see Gra haZvi, letter heh.
