And now. See Taz and Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2. Bach explains that for this reason they are Pores al Shema after the chazzan has finished, so that they should not pass in front of those praying. If so, the wording of Taz is not precise. This also caused an error in the reference marker, which does not belong here at all. Were I not afraid to differ from my colleagues, and certainly from my teachers, I would say that the author intended a particular explanation. The expression Pores al Shema used in Megillah 23b has two possible meanings. First, in the Holy Tongue, it can mean a portion or a piece, as in, “Is it not to share your bread with the hungry,” and “a split hoof.” Second, it can be an Aramaic expression, as Targum Yonasan translates “he shall bless the offering” as “he shall pronounce a blessing over the offering,” denoting blessing and commencement. Each of these linguistic explanations can itself be understood in two ways. According to the meaning of a portion, the first explanation is that according to the authority who holds that an individual omits the Kedushah in Yotzer, the Sages ordained that the entire blessing now be recited with Hashem's Name and kingship, although each person had already prayed and recited Krias Shema and its blessings individually. Otherwise, why is it called a portion, since the entire first blessing must be recited? Nevertheless, they enacted this in a manner comparable to the ruling in siman 67; see there. The second explanation is that this was enacted so that they should not appear to deny Hashem, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein; see Magen Avraham and paragraph 1. There is a practical difference between these two explanations. According to the first, an individual may not recite the Kedushah of Yotzer, and the leniency is that the Sages permitted the blessing to be recited a second time. There is no appearance of denying Hashem, because they respond, “Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One.” According to the second explanation, an individual may recite Kedushah, and the enactment is only so that they should not appear to deny Hashem. Since we rule in siman 59, seif 3, that an individual may recite it, the reason must be to avoid the appearance of denying Hashem. How then do we satisfy this requirement? It must be that after the chazzan has finished, he immediately begins the Amidah aloud, as Tosafos Yom Tov wrote in Megillah, chapter 4, mishnah 3; see there. Yet this corrective practice resulted in a problem, because people then pass between those who are still praying.
Now, according to the second explanation. Pores is Aramaic and means commencement. On this explanation, they had not yet recited Krias Shema, but only now came, and the Gemara states that there must be no fewer than ten. This itself has two explanations. First, ten are needed because of the Kedushah in Yotzer, as according to the first explanation of the word as a portion. This understanding is more satisfactory, because the blessing is not recited twice. Second, these blessings differ from others in that one person can discharge another's obligation only when they are actually recited aloud. By responding Amen, the listener is regarded as having recited more effectively than the person who pronounced the blessing, and this is possible only with ten, as the Rama wrote in siman 59, seif 4. According to the first linguistic meaning, however, whereby Pores means a portion, this is not necessary, and one could say that even Barechu does not require ten. For this reason, the Mechaber omitted this requirement in siman 59. The Rama follows his own position here that Pores means commencement, and therefore follows Rabbeinu Yonah that one cannot discharge another's obligation with fewer than ten. According to this, the Mechaber and Rama disagree both in their interpretation of Pores and in the practical halachah. The Rama did not explicitly mention this because the opposite is not stated expressly. God willing, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham. One cannot say that the Rama also understands Pores as a portion, while Amen is not answered after Yotzer Or, as Darkei Moshe wrote according to Rabbeinu Yonah. That cannot be the Rama's explanation of the Mishnah, since in siman 61, seif 3, the Rama does not hold this way and rules that Amen is answered after “Who chooses His people Israel with love.” Understand this.
