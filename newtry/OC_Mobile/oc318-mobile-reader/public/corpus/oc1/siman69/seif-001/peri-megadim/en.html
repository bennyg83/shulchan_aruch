**And now:** Taz in Orach Chayyim gives two explanations in Bach: therefore they poreis after the shaliach tzibbur finished — so people not pass in front of those praying — if so the Taz’s wording is not correct, and from this follows harm; the marginal note here is not relevant at all. Were it not that I fear slighting the author — and certainly my teachers — I would say the Master of blessed memory had one intent here: the word “poreis” stated (Megillah 23b) has two roots. One in Hebrew: “prusa,” a piece, like “prus lera’av,” “lifros resah,” or “poreis” in Aramaic as Beit Yosef writes — “the one who blesses the sacrifice spreads (lifros) upon the sacrifice” — language of blessing and beginning; and Magen Avraham explains both roots with two meanings. For the sense “prusa” (a cut portion) there are two readings: following the view that Kedushah in Yotzer a lone worshipper skips, they enacted that he now say the whole blessing with Shem uMalchus even though they already prayed and each recited Kriat Shema and its blessings individually — for if not, what “partial” [recital]? One would need all of Ahavat Rabbah as well; nevertheless they enacted by analogy to siman 67, see there. The second [reason] is so it not appear like denial, as Beit Yosef cites in the name of Mahari Abuhav — and on note (1) there is a distinction for each of the two explanations: for explanation (1), Kedushah in Yotzer a lone worshipper does not say, and the leniency they enacted to bless again does not look like denial since they answer “Blessed be the Lord, the blessed One”; for explanation (2) a lone worshipper says Kedushah only that it not look like denial. If so for us who rule in siman 59 se'if 3 that a lone worshipper says it — so it not look like denial — how do we resolve that after the shaliach tzibbur finished he immediately begins the Shemoneh Esreh aloud, as Tur and Magen Avraham write regarding Megillah, see there — except that from this enactment follows harm: interrupting between worshippers.

**And behold** — on the second reading they poreis the Aramaic opening and “spread”; the meaning is they had not read Shema until now with proper intent, and they said there must be no fewer than ten. There are two explanations in this: one, because of Kedushah in Yotzer like explanation (1) of “prusa” — and this is more plausible because they do not say the blessing twice; the second, that these blessings one does not discharge another except through actual speech and through answering Amen louder than the blesser — and this specifically requires ten; as Rabbeinu Bechaye writes in siman 59 se'if 4 in the gloss — unlike the first sense of “prusa” this is not forced; and one could say even Bar'khu does not require ten, and therefore the Mechaber omitted this in 59, and Rabbeinu Bechaye here according to his view is that they poreis the opening — and thus like Rabbeinu Yonah that one does not discharge with fewer than ten — therefore the Mechaber and Rabbeinu Bechaye disagree on the meaning of “poreis” and on the halacha as well; and why Rabbeinu Bechaye did not mention this, since the opposite is not spelled out explicitly — with God's help Magen Avraham will clarify more from this. One cannot say to Rabbeinu Bechaye likewise [that for] “prusa” and Ahavat Rabbah one does not answer Amen, as Darkei Moshe there [explains] to Rabbeinu Yonah; but for Rabbeinu Bechaye in his Mishnah work one cannot explain thus, for Rabbeinu Bechaye does not say so in siman 61 se'if 3 that they answer Amen after Ahavah Rabbah — understand this.
