The Rema of Fano wrote in responsum 84 that the elder Rabbis instituted nothing in the synagogue because of the danger of dispute. Rather, the proper law was practiced between the pillars where they sat and studied: for the first ten, and anyone present with them at the time of the first blessing of Yotzer Or, Barechu may no longer be recited, even if one of them did not pray with the congregation but prolonged the order of the daily offering and Pesukei DeZimrah, and now needs to recite Yotzer Or by himself. If ten people happen to be present with him, he may begin with Barechu even though he heard and responded at the outset. Admittedly, he did not act properly, since it is preferable to pray with the congregation. Nevertheless, since he places Barechu immediately before Yotzer Or, it is proper. Likewise, for an individual who did not hear Barechu at all, they permitted Barechu to be recited for him even without placing it immediately before Yotzer Or. If, however, he heard Barechu from one of those called to the Torah, that suffices, and Barechu is not repeated for him, except where he has not yet recited the blessing over Krias Shema, as stated above. In every case, he himself may begin Barechu on his own behalf, provided that a congregation is present, or another person may recite it on his behalf. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein that care must be taken not to recite Barechu unless at least one individual present wishes to recite the blessing over Krias Shema. Otherwise, Heaven forbid, it would appear that the chazzan tells them to bless Hashem while there is not even one among them who will recite a blessing. Darkei Moshe wrote concerning this that it requires study, for if so, one who did not hear Barechu would be forbidden to be Pores al Shema, since none of the listeners will recite the blessings of Krias Shema. I do not understand this objection, for according to their view, although each person recites the blessings of Krias Shema independently, they repeat the first blessing so that they should not appear to deny Hashem. This is what Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein meant: if there is no one among them who wishes to recite the blessing over Krias Shema, such as when they wish to leave, they should not be Pores al Shema at all. And now the custom is not, etc. Since they respond, “Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One,” it no longer appears as though they deny Him, just as with Bircas HaTorah. Darkei Moshe.

For there is no Kaddish. We are not so particular merely for the sake of Barechu alone.
And Kedushah. The same applies that they recite the entire prayer for one person who has not yet prayed. This is the view of Darkei Moshe, Beit Yosef, Piskei Rekanati in the name of the She'iltos, Agudah, Hagahos Maimoniyos, Rambam, and Semak, unlike Rabbi Yehudah Mintz. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if there are not six people who have not yet prayed, the chazzan should not first pray silently. Rather, he should begin immediately aloud, since the principal purpose is Kedushah. Knesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Radbaz, part 1, responsum 241, that if all of them prayed individually and did not hear Kaddish or Kedushah, they do not repeat the prayer merely to fulfill Kaddish and Kedushah. Lechem Chamudos wrote that the same applies to Torah study: even if only two or three study, they may recite Kaddish when ten are present. This means that ten must have been present during the study. If ten were not present during the study, even though they later came to the synagogue, Kaddish may not be recited, as written in siman 234. Bach wrote that the custom is for one who did not hear Barechu to go to the entrance of the synagogue, where they are Pores al Shema, provided that he takes care not to pass in front of those praying, as stated in siman 102, seif 4.
