The Rama wrote in siman 84 that the elderly rabbis established nothing in the synagogue because of the dispute, but rather ruled so “between the pillars” — how they sit and study — for the sake of the first ten and everyone present with them at the time of the first blessing of the blessing Yotzer Or: again it is impossible to say Barechu even if one of them had not prayed with the congregation but rather lengthened in the order of Parashat HaTamid and psukei dezimra and needs to say Yotzer Or alone; and if there are ten who happen to be with him, it is permitted for him to begin Barechu even though at first he heard and responded. It is true that he does not act properly — for it is preferable that he pray with the congregation. However, since he adjoins Barechu to Yotzer Or, it is fine. And for an individual who did not hear Barechu at all, likewise they permitted saying Barechu for him even without adjoining Yotzer Or. But if he heard from one of those ascending to read from the Torah it suffices and they do not repeat on his account except where he did not bless on Shema as we said; and in all cases it is permitted for him to begin Barechu himself, provided it is with a congregation or another for his sake — end of his words. And Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in the name of Mahari Weil that one should be careful not to say Barechu unless even one individual is present who wishes to bless on Shema, for if not it would appear, Heaven forbid, as if the sheliach tzibbur says Barechu to them and among them there is not even one who will bless — end of his words. And it is written in Darkei Moshe on this; and requires further study — for if so, it is forbidden for that one who did not hear Barechu to recite Kerias Shema, since there is not one of the listeners who will say the blessings of Kerias Shema — end of his words. And I did not understand, for according to their reasoning, even though each says the blessings of Kerias Shema independently, they return and say the first blessing so that it should not appear like denying Hashem. And this is what Mahari Weil said: if there is not among them one who wishes to bless on Shema, such as when they wish to go on their way, they should not recite Kerias Shema at all. And now they are not accustomed thus: since they say “Blessed is Hashem, the Blessed One, forever,” it no longer appears like denying at all, as was the case regarding birchas HaTorah (Darkei Moshe).

Like Shacharis. And for Rabbeinu Yerucham one must say the first blessing as well.

For there is no Kaddish. And on account of Barechu alone we are not so stringent.

And Kedushah — the same law applies: they pray the entire prayer for one who did not pray (Darkei Moshe in Beit Yosef, Piskei Rokeach in the name of She'eltos and Darkei Moshe and Aggur and Hagahos Maimoniyos ch. 8, and so too Rambam and Semag, unlike R' Yisrael Mintz). However it appears to me that if there are not six who have not prayed, the sheliach tzibbur should not pray in a whisper but should begin immediately in a loud voice, for the primary kavanah is because of Kedushah. And he wrote thus in the name of Radbaz, chelek 1, siman 241, that if everyone prayed individually and did not hear Kaddish and Kedushah, they do not repeat and pray to fulfill Kaddish and Kedushah. It is written in Levush Chayyim the same law applies to study — even two or three who study say Kaddish when there are ten present — end of his words — that if there were not ten at the time of study, even though they afterward came to the synagogue, Kaddish is impossible as written in siman 234. Bach wrote the practice: one who did not hear Barechu goes to the entrance of the synagogue where they recite Kerias Shema, provided he is careful not to pass in front of those praying, as written in siman 102 end of seif 4.

Preferable that he recite Kerias Shema, etc. — see siman 113 end of seif 4. And now the practice is that aveilim always recite Kerias Shema even if they heard, and there is another who did not hear — and this is not correct.

And he should not adjoin, etc. — nevertheless l'chatchila he should not do so, but rather pray in order and adjoin redemption to prayer, and afterward recite Kerias Shema if there will be ten there. And Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of Mahari Weil is uncertain about this; therefore it appears to me to be stringent — see siman 89.

**And he began, etc.** But if he did not begin [the blessing of Yotzer Or], he goes back and says Bar'khu for those who came to the synagogue (Beit Yosef, Levush).

**He may interrupt between redemption and prayer.** For in any case one interrupts with verses, but in the middle of blessings one may not interrupt.

**The chazzan may stand [there] —** even the first chazzan (Levush). And he wrote in Mishpetei Shmuel that if the first group took out a Sefer Torah and read from it, the second group may not take out a Sefer Torah again and read from it.
