[2] Because there is no Kaddish, etc. In the responsa of Menachem Azariah I saw another reason. An Aggadah states that every morning the person 'who is esteemed as nothing, whose breath is in his nostrils' does not regain composure until he answers Barechu. At the evening service, where this reason does not apply, the practice was not instituted. Regarding the custom to conduct the responsive recitation after the congregation has finished praying, the Bach explains that all those who did not hear Kaddish and Barechu gather at the entrance. Since it is forbidden to pass in front of those still praying, they wait until the congregation has finished.
