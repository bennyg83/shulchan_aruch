A blind person, although, etc. It was necessary to record this law here even though it was already stated in siman 53 that a blind person may pass before the ark. A new point is taught here, namely that he may recite Yotzer HaMe'oros. One might have thought that when the Mishnah teaches that a blind person may lead the responsive recitation of Shema, it means that he must nevertheless omit Yotzer HaMe'oros. It therefore teaches that he need not omit it. This also resolves the Tur's wording here: 'My father the Rosh likewise wrote that he may pray and discharge others, provided that he does not read, because it is forbidden to read from the Torah by heart.' This is difficult. Why did the Tur cite this in the Rosh's name when it is explicit in the Mishnah according to the Sages, whose view is the halachah? Moreover, why did the Tur need to add, 'provided that he does not read from the Torah,' when he had already written this in siman 53? According to what I have explained, the matter is clear. The Rosh's words also prove that he need not omit Yotzer HaMe'oros, since the Rosh concludes only, 'provided that he does not read,' and does not conclude, 'provided that he does not recite Yotzer HaMe'oros.' He therefore certainly does not omit it. I saw someone force an explanation of this point unnecessarily. See the end of siman 298, where the Beit Yosef distinguishes between the rule there, that a blind person may not recite the blessing over the flame on Motzaei Shabbos, and this rule concerning Yotzer HaMe'oros.
