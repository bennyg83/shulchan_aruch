And if there are not ten present — even for one who did not hear — they say as well: the Agur, for in Ashkenaz they practiced everywhere like Rashi and the Rokeach, who practiced to pray even on behalf of one alone; but Tosafos and the Rosh wrote that six who did not hear are required, meaning a majority of the minyan, and so is implied from the words of Rambam chapter 8 of Tefillah, and so too in Machzor Vitry and in Sha'alotos — Rashi's custom was to pray even on behalf of one alone, and so too Mahari Abuhav. But one should be careful not to say Barchu unless there is there even one alone who wishes to bless on Shema (Beit Yosef in the name of Maharil), for otherwise they would appear, God forbid, as though the shaliach tzibur tells them Barchu and there is not among them even one who will bless (Beit Yosef). And it requires study: for if so, this one who has not yet heard would be forbidden to repeat Shema, for there is not one of those remaining obligated to bless on Shema who could say to him Barchu; and Beit Yosef wrote himself to the contrary, that it is better to repeat Shema for that one who did not hear (Darkei Moshe). And I already wrote that they do not appear like heretics, for behold they answered on Barchu "Barukh Hashem hamvorakh leolam vaed"; so I hold, and there is no distinction between repeating Shema and prayer, for in prayer too they may pray even on behalf of one who has not prayed, for it is all a matter of sanctity and one should not distinguish between them — so is implied from the words of Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe; all the more so that one may pray for one who has not yet prayed. And so I have seen practiced by the great gaon Moreinu Harav R' Yehoshua, av beit din of the holy community of Krakow. And one must be careful not to repeat Shema until after the congregation has finished the Amidah, because all those who did not hear Kaddish and Barchu gather at the door where they repeat Shema, and pass before those standing in prayer who have not yet finished the Amidah, and he commits a prohibition, for it is forbidden to pass in front of one praying within four cubits (Bach). And so I have seen practiced in the holy community of Krakow, that those who did not hear Kaddish and Barchu gather at the door, and it is correct as will be written below in siman 139 seif 6 in the gloss. And if they did not hear "Barchu et Hashem hamvorakh," they should not answer "Barukh Hashem hamvorakh leolam vaed."
