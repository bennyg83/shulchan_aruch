When ten who have not heard are unavailable, they may recite it even for one person who has not heard. The Agur writes that throughout Germany the practice follows Rashi and the Rokeach, who permit the service to be recited even for a single person. Tosafos and the Rosh, however, write that six who have not heard are required, constituting the majority of a minyan. This also appears from the words of the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, and it is likewise written in Machzor Vitry. In the She’iltos, Rashi explains that the practice is to recite the service even for one person, and Mahari Abuhav writes likewise. One must nevertheless be careful not to say Barechu unless at least one person present wishes to recite the blessings of Krias Shema. (Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav.) Otherwise, Heaven forbid, it appears that the chazzan tells them to bless while not even one of them will recite a blessing. (Beit Yosef.) This requires study, for according to this, the person who has not yet heard would be forbidden to lead the abbreviated service, since none of the others is obligated to recite the blessings of Krias Shema to whom he could say Barechu. Yet the Beit Yosef himself writes the opposite, that it is preferable for the person who has not heard to lead the abbreviated service. (Darchei Moshe.) I have already written that they do not appear as deniers, since they responded to Barechu, ‘Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One, forever and ever.’ This appears correct to me. There is no distinction between the abbreviated Krias Shema service and the Amidah. Even the Amidah may be repeated for one person who has not prayed, since both are matters of sanctity and there is no basis to distinguish between them. This is implied by the Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe. Certainly, the person who has not yet prayed may himself lead the Amidah, and I saw my grandfather, the great gaon Rabbi Yehoshua, rav and rosh yeshivah of the holy community of Krakow, conduct himself this way. One must take care not to lead the abbreviated Krias Shema service until after the congregation has finished the Amidah, because all those who did not hear Kaddish and Barechu gather at the doorway where the abbreviated service is conducted. They then pass before those who are still praying and have not yet completed the Amidah, thereby committing a prohibition, since one may not pass within four amos in front of a person praying. (Bach.) I likewise saw that in the holy community of Krakow, those who had not heard Kaddish and Barechu would gather at the doorway. This is proper, as I wrote below in siman 139, seif 6, in the gloss. One who did not hear ‘Bless Hashem, the blessed One’ should not respond, ‘Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One, forever and ever.’
