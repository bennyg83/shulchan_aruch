2) There: One who sees his friend, etc. There is no distinction between men and women. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashba. It is obvious that a man recites the blessing upon seeing a woman and a woman upon seeing a man. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, writes the same. It appears that this applies specifically if she is his relative and he is familiar with her. If she is not his relative, he should not recite the blessing, because there is concern that he may come to improper thoughts. I later saw that Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 213, writes the same; see there. See Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, in the later edition, paragraph 1, who writes that a distinguished man should not recite the blessing over his wife, and that the practice is not to recite it even over his daughter; see there.
