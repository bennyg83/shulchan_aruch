Seif 2 in the hagahah. Some say: when one's son becomes bar mitzvah he blesses Blessed are You the Omnipresent our God King of the universe Who relieved me from his punishment. NB: see in Magen Avraham two explanations; and see in midrash parashat Toldot perek 66, and this is its language: R' Eliezer said — a person must involve himself with his son until thirteen years; from then on he must say Blessed is the Omnipresent Who relieved me from his punishment — end. Behold from this language it implies per the first explanation that the obligation is upon him to involve himself with him and guide him on the straight path, and if not the father is punished on his account; but from thirteen and onward he says Who relieved me from his punishment — that he is no longer punished for the son's sin of not guiding him on the straight path; and this is explained per the first explanation; examine. And behold in what Magen Avraham wrote, and he brought in the name of Levush that the explanation is that until now the son was punished for the father's sin — end; and his words are astounding, for it implies a grown son is not punished for his father's sin; but we say in the verse "and with Aaron the Omnipresent became very angry to destroy him" — destruction is only loss of children, except that Moshe Rabbeinu's prayer made half, etc.; and behold then Nadav and Avihu were more than thirteen years, for the Omnipresent commanded them to be priests and make garments for them, and a minor priest does not serve — perforce they were adults; and also from what the Omnipresent said to Elazar, and it is stated "your heads do not grow long lest you die" — if they were minors that day they are not subject to mitzvot and punishment at all — perforce they were adults; and all the more Nadav and Avihu who were older were subject to punishment, and nevertheless they were punished for their father's sin — it is proven a grown son is punished for his father's sin. And perforce what he wrote "from his punishment" means per Magen Avraham's explanation that the father will not be punished for the son's sin; and Levush's words require study; examine. And behold a scholar answered me that one may say: since we say at the end of tractate Makkot daf 24 — four things Moshe decreed on Israel and prophets came and annulled them — Moshe Rabbeinu said visiting the sins of fathers on sons, and Yechezkel said the soul that sins shall die — end; and therefore one may say Aaron's matter was before Yechezkel annulled it. And I answered him that this is impossible to say, for behold chazal expounded in their language "four things Moshe decreed on Israel" — it implies this was Moshe's statement, not His blessed statement; and the verse "the Omnipresent etc. visiting sins of fathers" was Moshe Rabbeinu's statement that he said thus. And accordingly behold the statement visiting sins of fathers was in the last forty as explained in parashat Ki Tisa, and the statement "also with Aaron the Omnipresent became very angry to destroy" is explained in parashat Ekev that it was in the middle forty — therefore the Omnipresent's decree to destroy Aaron's sons was before Moshe decreed visiting sins of fathers; and how does the Talmud attribute this to Moshe Rabbeinu who decreed thus on Israel — perforce these were two separate matters: what Moshe Rabbeinu said visiting sins of fathers on sons was when the father already died first — then what the sons are punished for in this world is not considered punishment to the father since he is no longer in the world, and the punishment was not to the father only to the son punished himself — this is the punishment the son bears for the father's sin, and this Moshe decreed and Yechezkel annulled; but if the father lives, Heaven forbid he is punished by his son's death — this is punishment to the father himself who suffers this pain, as written "with Aaron the Omnipresent became very angry to destroy" — it implies the obligation of this punishment is on Aaron's account — this is the Omnipresent's decree from the beginning and Yechezkel did not come to annul it, for one cannot say "the soul that sins shall die" applies here too, for here too the main punishment is to the father himself and a prophet has no power to annul His blessed will — and therefore I asked well that it is proven even adults are punished for the father's sin; examine. And with this is resolved what the scholar further asked: why does the Talmud not answer in Makkot and Berakhot where it challenges "it is written visiting sins of fathers on sons" and "it is written fathers shall not die for sons" — why not answer here for minors here for adults, etc.; and per what I said it works well: for regarding punishment of the sons themselves there is no reason to distinguish minor from adult, and Tosafot and Magen Avraham did not distinguish minor from adult only when the father lives and the punishment is called on the father's account — then one may distinguish whether the son is minor in the father's domain or adult not in his domain; but if the punishment is on the son himself there is no reason to distinguish minor from adult, and since there it is written visiting sins of fathers on sons implying punishment falls on the son — there is no reason to distinguish minor from adult. And since I speak of this I will mention what is astounding in Tosafot chapter 2 of Shabbat who wrote that below it is proven children are not punished for fathers' sins only when they are minors — in my opinion this is astounding, whence do we know this; and if from what chazal said that for the sin of vows children die when they are minors — whence that they are not punished when adults; perhaps the Talmud's intent is if he sins in vows he does not merit to raise children and does not extend his life until they grow — only while still minors they die; but always if he began transgressing vows when his sons already grew, or any other sin, one may say his grown sons too are punished — requires study. And there was room to expand on this but time does not allow; and see in Yalkut Ruth that until thirteen years the son is punished for the father's sin — explicit per Magen Avraham there; examine in my work Ein Yaakov on Berakhot 45 in the incident of R' Yehudah's son who was ill — there I proved why R' Yehudah did not answer that a prisoner does not free himself from prison as he answered above — from this I proved a grown son is not punished for his father's sin and therefore it does not apply here that a prisoner does not free himself from prison; examine.
