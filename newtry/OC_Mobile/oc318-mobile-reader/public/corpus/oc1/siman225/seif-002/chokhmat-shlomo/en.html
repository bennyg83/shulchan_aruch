In seif 2, in the gloss. Some say that one whose son becomes bar mitzvah should recite: Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, Who has exempted me from the punishment of this one. Note: See the two explanations in the Magen Avraham, and see Midrash, Parashas Toldos, section 66: R' Elazar said, A person must attend to his son until thirteen years. From then onward he must say, Blessed is Hashem Who has exempted me from the punishment of this one. This wording supports the first explanation, that the father is obligated to attend to him and guide him on the proper path, and if he fails to do so the father is punished because of him. From age thirteen onward he says that he has been exempted from the punishment of this one, because he is no longer punished for the son's sin on account of failing to guide him properly. This clearly follows the first explanation. Consider this carefully. The Magen Avraham cites the Levush as explaining that until then the son is punished for the father's sin. This is astonishing, for it implies that an adult son is not punished for his father's sin. Yet the Gemara expounds the verse, Hashem was very angry with Aharon to destroy him, that destruction means the destruction of children, except that Moshe Rabbeinu's prayer accomplished half, etc. Nadav and Avihu were then older than thirteen, since Hashem commanded that they be kohanim and that garments be made for them, and a minor kohen does not perform the service. They necessarily were adults. Furthermore, Hashem said to Elazar and Isamar, Do not let your hair grow wild, lest you die. Had they been minors that day, they would not have been subject to mitzvos or punishment. They therefore necessarily were adults, and all the more so Nadav and Avihu, who were older than they, were subject to punishment. Nevertheless, they were punished for their father's sin. This proves that an adult son may be punished for his father's sin. The phrase from the punishment of this one must therefore mean, as the Magen Avraham's first explanation states, that the father will no longer be punished for the son's sin. The Levush's words require further study. Consider this carefully. A learned person answered me that one could say that the Gemara at the end of Makos, folio 24, states that Moshe decreed four matters upon Israel and the prophets came and annulled them. Moshe Rabbeinu said, He visits the sin of fathers upon children, while Yechezkel said, The soul that sins shall die. Accordingly, the incident involving Aharon occurred before Yechezkel annulled that decree. I answered that this cannot be said. The Sages were precise in saying that Moshe decreed four matters upon Israel, implying that this was Moshe's statement rather than Hashem's. Thus the verse, Hashem, Hashem, etc., visiting the sin of fathers, was a statement of Moshe Rabbeinu. That statement was made during the final forty days, as stated in Parashas Ki Sisa, whereas the verse, Hashem was very angry with Aharon to destroy him, refers, as stated in Parashas Eikev, to the middle forty days. Hashem's decree to destroy Aharon's children therefore preceded Moshe's decree that the sin of fathers is visited upon children. How then could the Gemara attribute this principle to Moshe's decree upon Israel? It must be that these are two separate matters. Moshe's statement that the sin of fathers is visited upon children applies where the father has already died. The children's punishment in this world then cannot be considered a punishment of the father, since he is no longer in the world. It is punishment of the child himself, who bears the father's sin. This is what Moshe decreed and Yechezkel annulled. If the father is alive and, God forbid, is punished through the death of his child, this is a punishment of the father himself, who suffers that grief. Thus the verse says, Hashem was very angry with Aharon to destroy him, indicating that the punishment is attributed to Aharon. Hashem decreed this initially, and Yechezkel did not annul it, for the verse, The soul that sins shall die, is inapplicable. The principal punishment is upon the father himself, and a prophet has no power to annul Hashem's will. My challenge therefore stands, for it is proven that even adult children may be punished because of their father. Consider this carefully. This also resolves another question raised by that learned person. Why did the Gemara in Makos and Berachos, when asking that one verse says, He visits the sin of fathers upon children, while another says, Fathers shall not be put to death because of children, not answer that one refers to minors and the other to adults? According to what I have written, the answer is clear. Regarding punishment of the children themselves, there is no basis to distinguish between minors and adults. Tosafos and the Magen Avraham distinguish between minors and adults only when the father is alive and the punishment is attributed to him. There one may distinguish between a minor son who remains under the father's authority and an adult son who is no longer under his authority. Where the punishment rests upon the son himself, however, there is no reason to distinguish between a minor and an adult. Since the verse says, He visits the sin of fathers upon children, implying that the punishment rests upon the child, there is no reason to distinguish between a minor and an adult. While discussing this, I will mention a difficulty in Tosafos in the second chapter of Shabbos. They write that the later passage proves that children are punished for their fathers' sins only while they are minors. In my opinion, this is astonishing. From where is this known? If it is inferred from the Sages' statement that because of vows children die while young, perhaps the Gemara means that one who sins regarding vows does not merit to raise children, and Hashem does not prolong their lives until adulthood, but they die while still minors. It may nevertheless be that if he began violating vows after his children had already reached adulthood, or committed some other sin, even his adult children could be punished. This requires further study. Much more could be written, but time does not permit. See Yalkut on Rus, where it states that until thirteen years the son is punished for the father's sin. This explicitly follows the Magen Avraham's cited view. See there. See also my work on Ein Yaakov, Berachos 45, regarding the incident in which R' Yehoshua ben Levi's son became ill. There I proved from the fact that R' Yochanan ben Zakkai did not answer that a prisoner cannot release himself from prison, as he answered earlier, that an adult son is not punished for his father's sin. Therefore, the principle that a prisoner cannot release himself from prison did not apply. Consider this carefully.
