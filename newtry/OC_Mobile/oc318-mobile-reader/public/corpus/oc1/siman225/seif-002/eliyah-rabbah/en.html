And it is good to bless, etc. The reason in Lechem Chamudos is that this blessing is not mentioned in the Gemara. So I also saw in Darchei Moshe. He should bless when he reads from the Torah, for then it becomes known publicly that he is an adult, since beforehand he could not read from the Torah. Therefore, if before then he prayed with the congregation, he should bless at that time. It is clear that it is forbidden to read before thirteen complete years, even lacking one day. This is also clear in siman 55 and in siman 282, that although a minor counts toward the number of seven aliyos, nevertheless it is forbidden for him to read to discharge the public. Even regarding Birkas HaMazon where he ate a D'Rabbanan measure, we say in Perek Sheloshah SheAchlu: A curse should come upon one whose wife and children bless for him, although a minor is also obligated in Birkas HaMazon D'Rabbanan, as above in siman 186. Furthermore, even nowadays the primary reader who discharges others is the chazzan, and for this reason we call up a blind person and an ignoramus, as above in siman 39 and as below in siman 669. So wrote Lechem Chamudos. The formula in Mahari Weil is 'Blessed is He Who has exempted me,' and Orchos Chaim wrote that this is correct, but it is not proper to say as we mistakenly say, 'Who has exempted me.' I say there is no concern, for this is the wording in Midrash Rabbah, and it is also similar to siman 216 regarding the blessing, 'Who gave a pleasant fragrance'; see there.
