And he sent him letters. Meaning that although they exchanged letters and thereby became friends, since they never connected face to face, their love is not so strong that he derives pleasure and joy from seeing him. The Peri Megadim writes that if a man was informed that his wife gave birth while he was overseas, and he now sees the child, he recites Shehecheyanu, or Mechayeh HaMeisim if more than twelve months have passed. He certainly rejoices over his child even though he had never seen him.
Bar mitzvah. This means when he becomes thirteen years and one day old. The present custom is not to recite the blessing until the youth leads the congregational prayer as shaliach tzibbur or reads from the Torah on his first Shabbos, for then it becomes publicly known that he is bar mitzvah. It is a mitzvah for a person to make a meal on the day his son becomes bar mitzvah, like the day he enters the wedding canopy, namely on the day he enters his fourteenth year. The reason for the meal is that he has now become an adult Jew commanded in the mitzvos of the Torah. If the youth delivers a Torah discourse, it is a seudas mitzvah even if it is not held on that day.
Who has exempted me from his punishment, etc. Until now, when the son sinned, the father was punished because he had failed to educate him in the mitzvos of the Torah. Now that the son has become an adult, he himself is obligated to strengthen himself in the mitzvos of Hashem. Know that although the obligation of chinuch no longer applies, the father still has a mitzvah of rebuke when he sees that his son is not behaving properly. If he does not protest, he is punished because of him, for the son is no less than any other Jew. It is well known that the Sages said: Whoever is able to protest the conduct of the members of his household and does not protest is held accountable for the sin of the members of his household. Whoever is able to protest the conduct of the people of his city and does not protest, etc.
It is proper to recite the blessing without, etc. This is because this blessing is not mentioned in the Gemara. Derech HaChaim likewise records it this way. The Gra, however, maintains in his commentary that since the blessing is mentioned in the Midrash and Maharil recited it in practice, it should be recited with Hashem's Name and Kingship. See Chayei Adam, who likewise writes that one who recites it has not lost.
