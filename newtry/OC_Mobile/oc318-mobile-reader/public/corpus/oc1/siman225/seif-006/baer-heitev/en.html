From year to year. This means that over something that does not grow from the ground, such as birds and fish, he does not recite the blessing. Although he himself has not eaten it for many days, others have eaten it. Alternatively, it means a fruit that remains on the tree for more than a year. Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 35, likewise writes that one does not recite Shehecheyanu over an esrog that remains on its tree from year to year. See Halachos Ketanos, part 2, simanim 233 and 257. The Radbaz writes: I recite Shehecheyanu over a fragrance that renews annually. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 2, siman 38, likewise rules. Above, siman 216, s.k. 2, I wrote in the name of Lechem Chamudos that one does not recite Shehecheyanu over a fragrance. I found written in the Tashbetz, on parchment, that one recites Shehecheyanu over kids and lambs. This requires further study. Magen Avraham.
Twice a year. Birchas HaZevach, folio 28, Gan HaMelech, siman 24, and the Magen Avraham bring proof that Shehecheyanu is recited from the statement in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin and in the chapter Eilu Menachos that one who eats menachos recites Shehecheyanu, although he ate them only twice a year. See there. Yad Aharon challenges them, for the Shehecheyanu they recited was not over eating the menachos, but over the mitzvah, because Hashem had enabled them to reach and endure until that time to stand and serve in the House of Hashem. See there. If two kinds of fruit were before him, each requiring Shehecheyanu, one blessing suffices for both. Yad Aharon likewise writes this. See there.
New vegetables. This includes every vegetable. One recites the blessing over radishes and varieties of turnips. Prishah. One does not recite it over new bread, because new bread is not distinguishable from old bread. Shelah. Nevertheless, one recites it over porridge made from new grain, because it is clearly recognizable as new. Magen Avraham. See Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Temidin UMusafin, who writes that one who offers a minchah from the new grain recites Shehecheyanu. Rashi likewise writes this in the chapter Eilu Menachos. See there in the Kesef Mishneh, who appears not to have seen Rashi's words in that chapter. Thus, even when the new cannot be distinguished from the old, Shehecheyanu is recited. Yad Aharon. One may distinguish that the Omer offering is recognizable. A person will in the future be required to give an accounting for having seen delicacies and not eaten them. R' Elazar would save small coins in order to eat every new species. Yerushalmi; Mateh Moshe; Magen Avraham.
