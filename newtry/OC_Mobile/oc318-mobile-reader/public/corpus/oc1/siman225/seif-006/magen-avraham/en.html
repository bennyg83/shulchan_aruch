That does not renew. This means that over something that does not grow from the ground, such as birds and fish, one does not recite the blessing. Although he himself has not eaten it for many days, others have eaten it. Regarding a fragrance that renews annually, such as Indian rosemary and roses and the like, I recite Shehecheyanu. Radbaz, part 2, siman 74. See what I wrote in siman 216, where some disagree. One recites Shehecheyanu over almonds that grow in Egypt. Radbaz, part 1, siman 43. I found written in the Tashbetz, on parchment, that one recites Shehecheyanu over kids and lambs. This requires further study.
Twice a year. This is also implied in Berachos, folio 37, in Tosafos: one who eats menachos recites Shehecheyanu because the priestly watches rotate, and he would eat them only twice a year. See there.
That has no fixed season. The Beit Yosef writes in the name of Maharam: Over an herb that renews, such as basil and the like, one does not recite the blessing, because it can sometimes endure throughout the year, unlike gourds and other tree fruits. End quote. Mahariv writes: The reason Shehecheyanu is not recited over a new vegetable is that it remains in the ground throughout the year, and one cannot distinguish between new produce grown this year and old produce grown last year. End quote. In this country I have not seen it grow throughout the year. Perhaps for this reason the Rama changed his wording and wrote that it remains in the ground throughout the year, meaning that it is buried in pits in the ground and endures throughout the year. This is unlike fruit. Although some people bury fruit, that is an exceedingly small minority and is disregarded. Shelah writes that one does not recite the blessing over any vegetable, and the same applies to new bread, because new produce is not distinguishable from old. End quote. It nevertheless appears to me that one recites the blessing over porridge made from new grain, because it is clearly recognizable as new. In the future, a person will be required to give an accounting for everything delightful that his eye saw and he did not eat. R' Elazar was concerned for this teaching and would eat from every new species each year. Yerushalmi, chapter 10 of Kiddushin; Mateh Moshe.
