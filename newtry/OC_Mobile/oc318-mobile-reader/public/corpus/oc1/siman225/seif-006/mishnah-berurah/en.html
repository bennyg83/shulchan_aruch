That does not renew, etc. This means that even during winter it does not change in appearance, such as an esrog and the like that remains on its tree from year to year. Since it remains green throughout the year, no perceptible pleasure is produced by its renewal. This also excludes something with no fixed growing season, as stated below in the gloss, and something that does not grow from the ground, such as birds and fish that become available to him. Even if he has never tasted that species, he does not recite Shehecheyanu, because the species itself is not new. Although he has not eaten it, others have. One does not recite Shehecheyanu over truffles and mushrooms, because they also have no fixed season and grow from the air rather than from the ground. Peri Megadim. Regarding a fragrance that renews annually, such as roses and other items intended for fragrance, the poskim disagree whether Shehecheyanu is recited. The custom is not to recite it. Shaarei Teshuvah in siman 216. Some say that one recites Shehecheyanu over kids and lambs, which commonly renew twice a year. This requires further study. Magen Avraham. The universal custom is not to recite it.

Because it remains in the ground throughout the year. This means that some vegetables ordinarily remain in the ground throughout the year, while Shehecheyanu was instituted only for something that appears periodically. In truth, this is found only in certain vegetables and not all of them. Perhaps the reason is that no distinction was made between one vegetable and another. Prishah. Alternatively, the Rama's statement that it remains in the ground throughout the year may mean that people bury it in pits in the ground and it lasts throughout the year. One therefore cannot distinguish between a new vegetable grown this year and an old one grown last year. This is unlike fruit. Although some people bury fruit, that is an exceedingly small minority and is disregarded. Magen Avraham. According to this, it is clear that one should not recite Shehecheyanu over the ground fruit called earth apple, since people commonly bury them in pits during winter and they last a long time. In the name of Shelah, the later authorities write that one should not recite Shehecheyanu over any new vegetable, nor over new bread, because new produce is not distinguishable from old, and people may err and come to recite a blessing in vain. The Magen Avraham writes that one nevertheless recites the blessing over porridge made from new grain, because it is clearly recognizable as new. Mor UKetziah disagrees, reasoning that since no difference is perceptible in its taste, it has no advantage. See there. One should minimize doubtful blessings. Regarding new legumes that are recognizable as new, such as when they are still partially green, it appears that one may recite Shehecheyanu. Eliyah Rabbah also implies this. One recites Shehecheyanu over varieties of turnips called carrots, beets, and cucumbers. Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChaim.
