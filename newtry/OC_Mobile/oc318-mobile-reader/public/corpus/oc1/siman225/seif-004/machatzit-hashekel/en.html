S.k. 4: Bar mitzvah. This means when he becomes thirteen years and one day old, namely at the beginning of his fourteenth year, on the anniversary of his birth, as the Magen Avraham wrote above, siman 53, s.k. 12.
It is a mitzvah for a person to make a meal, etc. A proof is found in Bava Kamma, folio 87a, and Kiddushin, folio 31. Rav Yosef, who was blind, said: Now that I have heard R' Chanina's teaching that one who is commanded and performs is greater than one who is not commanded and performs, whoever tells me that the halacha does not follow R' Yehuda, who holds that a blind person is exempt from all mitzvos stated in the Torah, I will make a festive day for the Sages. Why? Because when I am commanded, I receive greater reward. Accordingly, when a son becomes an adult and is now commanded and performs, a meal should be made, as Rav Yosef intended. After examining the matter, I found that Maharshal writes this in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 4, siman 37, concluding: Although Rav Yosef was already obligated, and only upon hearing news of an obligation that had been unknown to him until then did he wish to make a festive day, all the more so when the actual time arrives at which one becomes obligated, a festive day should be made. End quote.
