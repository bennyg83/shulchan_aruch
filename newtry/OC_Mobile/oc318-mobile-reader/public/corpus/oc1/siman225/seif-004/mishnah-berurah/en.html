When he eats gindash, etc. Although all the varieties fall under one general species, they are considered two species regarding Shehecheyanu, because they produce two distinct joys. See Magen Avraham and the other later authorities, who write that even if they do not differ in name, if they differ in taste, such as white figs and black figs, they are two species for this purpose, and Shehecheyanu must be recited over each one when they are not before him at the same time. The same applies to two kinds of pears, apples, or nuts. In his commentary, the Gra questions all this and rejects their proofs. His view appears to be that since it is essentially one species, the first blessing suffices. Mor UKetziah likewise cites his father, Chacham Tzvi, as holding nearly the same view. Ultimately, however, he inclines to say that although for other matters they are called one species, regarding Shehecheyanu, which was instituted for the heart's joy, it is reasonable that each distinct item produces a renewed joy. The Shulchan Aruch's ruling is therefore understandable. See there.
