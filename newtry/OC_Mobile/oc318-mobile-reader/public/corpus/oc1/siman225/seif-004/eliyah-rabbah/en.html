[4] [Levush] In their hands, no. Meaning, he holds that the explanation is not that the father is exempted from the son's punishment, for the son is still a minor and not subject to punishment. Rather, the father blesses that the son has been exempted from him, so that he is not punished on account of him; and toward this the wording "from his punishment" appears directed, as Tosafot wrote in Bava Batra 22, that each one was distressed lest he be punished through him; see there. On this the Levush asked: but he has still not been exempted. However, Lechem Chamudot wrote that the meaning of the berachah is that until now he was obligated to educate him in mitzvot and raise him in Torah, and now the father is exempt. So too it is found explicitly in the Midrash, Parashat Toldot: Rabbi Akiva said, a person must attend to his son until thirteen years; from then onward he must say, "Blessed is He who exempted me," etc. It is written in the Yalkut: some say it is a mitzvah to make a bar mitzvah meal for his son like the day he enters the chuppah; and it implies that this means on the day he enters his fourteenth year. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kama chapter 7 siman 37, wrote that if the youth gives a derashah it is a seudat mitzvah even if it is not on that day. He wrote that the main reason for the meal is because one who is commanded and performs is greater. Now Rashal wondered how we rely on him for birkat hamazon, which is Torah law, perhaps he did not bring two hairs; and he answers that by Torah law one fulfills the obligation with the berachot of a minor, only the Rabbis said that he does not discharge an adult, and therefore we establish him on the presumption that he brought two hairs. In siman 199, Magen Avraham challenged him: if so, let the Talmud on page 20 answer that since he does not know how to recite the berachah, the Rabbis did not decree, so that the Torah obligation of birkat hamazon not be nullified, end quote, as stated at the beginning of siman 193, end quote. This is not difficult at all, for we hold that the Sages upheld their words by requiring passive non-action. The beginning of siman 193 is different, for even when both know how to recite, there is only the choicest mitzvah to divide; see there. As for what he further asked, why they ruled that unresolved question stringently at the beginning of siman 186, one may say that there it is at the outset; and in any event many rabbinic doubts are treated stringently, as explained in the poskim. It also appears to me that since regarding a minor, necessarily it speaks where he ate an amount obligated rabbinically, as the Talmud says there, then presumably regarding a woman, whom they mention in the same clause, it also speaks in that case. What he asked, that a minor does not belong to arvut, so how can the adult fulfill the obligation, is not difficult at all, for he holds that the reason of arvut is rabbinic.
