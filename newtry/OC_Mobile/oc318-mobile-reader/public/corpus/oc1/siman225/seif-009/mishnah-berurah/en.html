(22) The amputee - Whose hands were cut off.
(23) The blind person - In both eyes.
(24) Small spots - That is, he has spots like slightly red lentils, but between one lentil-like spot and another there is a clear, bright white; because of that whiteness he is called bahakani. Machatzit HaShekel. See Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim. However, if many such people are common there, it stands to reason that this is not a disgrace or an unusual change over which one would bless "the true Judge" or "Who changes creatures." All the more if this happened because of the heat of the sun and is destined to pass, it appears that according to everyone one should not bless over it.
(25) From their mother's womb - This refers to all of them except the amputee.
(26) He blesses the true Judge - For this came upon him through punishment.
(27) For specifically, etc. - This refers to the blessing "the true Judge," which applies only to one who is distressed and accepts the justice of the decree. But the blessing "Who changes creatures" does not apply specifically to one who is distressed, just as with an elephant or monkey.
(28) But over an idolater - He stated a clear-cut case; the same applies to anyone over whom he is not distressed.
(29) And he does not bless, etc. - This also refers to the blessing "Who changes creatures," as Taz writes. Meaning, even if he saw another dark-skinned person, or similarly the other things mentioned above, after thirty days, he still does not bless. The reason is that for an unusual change the blessing applies only the first time he sees the thing and the change is great in his eyes. It appears from Taz that certainly the Mechaber also refers to the blessing "the true Judge." However, in the book Nahar Shalom he wrote that regarding "the true Judge," which applies specifically to one's relative or beloved over whom he is distressed, it stands to reason that if he sees this again upon another relative, his distress is certainly increased further, and it is relevant to bless "the true Judge" over this.
(30) And some say, etc. - In practice one should rule that after thirty days he blesses without the Divine Name and kingship, as Eliyah Rabbah writes; see there.
(31) From thirty days - Meaning that he did not see another like him within thirty days, as Magen Avraham writes.
