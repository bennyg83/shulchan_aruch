He blesses Shehecheyanu, etc. The Rokeach, siman 351, wrote: Why three expressions, Shehecheyanu, vekiyemanu, vehigianu? They correspond to, 'Let my soul praise Hashem,' 'I will praise Hashem with my life,' and 'I will sing to my God while I yet exist.' We learned at the end of Perek BeChol Me'arvin: They asked, what is the law regarding saying zman on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur? Rav Yehudah said, I say zman even over a new gourd. He said to him, I am not asking about permission; I am asking about obligation, what is the law? The Kenesses HaGedolah wrote as follows: The Shiltei Giborim wrote that it appears from this language that there is no obligation of Shehecheyanu over new fruits, for since it is optional there is no punishment for one who did not bless. It appears that he interprets 'optional' as referring to the blessing. To me it seems that 'optional' refers to the new gourd, meaning something that one is not compelled to do or eat. If he wants to eat a new gourd, this is not our question. Our question concerns an obligation, meaning something that he cannot evade doing. I am astonished why he went far afield, for the Beit Yosef already wrote in this siman in the name of a responsum of the Rashba, and also in siman 223 in the words of the Shiltei Giborim, that Shehecheyanu is optional. So too wrote the Kesef Mishneh in chapter 10 of Hilchos Berachos, and so ruled the Bach and Olas Tamid. I later found that the Magen Avraham challenged him on this. However, I found in Hagahos Semak, siman 151: 'over a new gourd,' meaning upon seeing it; nevertheless, there it implies that it is optional and not obligatory, whereas inside it implies that it is obligatory, like over eating it. Thus, there is an old dispute regarding this. In the Semak itself it implies that it is obligatory. It further seems to me from the language of the Hagahos Semak cited above that regarding eating them, meaning the blessing of Shehecheyanu over eating them, everyone agrees that it is obligatory. Even according to the opinion that it is optional, that is only over seeing them. Therefore I say that one should be careful not to neglect it. Furthermore, see what is stated in the Yerushalmi in Perek Asarah Yuchasin: 'The humble shall hear and rejoice.' Rabbi Bun said: A person is destined to give judgment and accounting for not having eaten varieties of delicacies. Rabbi Elazar was concerned for this teaching and would save coins to eat from every new species during the year. The Tashbetz, siman 320, concluded that the reason is in order to increase blessings. This implies that it refers to the blessings of Shehecheyanu. The Semak and Kol Bo wrote that if he does not derive pleasure except from eating them, he blesses only upon eating them. According to this it appears that even if he already blessed upon seeing it, he blesses upon eating it, since he does not derive pleasure from seeing it.
Levush: to bless the second time, etc. The Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah disagrees with this, saying that one should be stringent in disputes, as where one ate grapes and afterward drank new wine, where we are stringent because of doubt. In my humble opinion, the Rama and Levush are correct. Regarding grapes and new wine, the great later authority Mahari Weil was uncertain, but in this case Mahari Weil agreed to bless, with sound reasoning, as the Beit Yosef wrote, and as in Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 154. Furthermore, the Agur, who is also a great later authority, ruled in both cases like the Rama.
Until it is completed, etc. The Malbushei Yom Tov wrote as follows: His teacher, the Rama, wrote this here unnecessarily, for this itself is what was mentioned inside in seif 7. In my humble opinion it can be resolved, for he teaches us here that even if he already blessed Shehecheyanu on unripe fruit, he must bless again when its ripening is completed. This refers to what he wrote in the gloss, that one who blessed at the time of seeing has not lost. On this he wrote, 'and one should not bless until completion,' etc., meaning that in this case he did lose the blessing. This appears to me proven from Responsa Rashba, siman 250.
