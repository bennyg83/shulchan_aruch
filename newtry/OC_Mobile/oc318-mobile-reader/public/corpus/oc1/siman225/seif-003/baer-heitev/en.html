Shehecheyanu. It is only optional. See Magen Avraham.
Eating. He should recite the blessing over the fruit before Shehecheyanu. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 236.
He has lost it. A blind person also recites the blessing over seeing, since the object is visible to others. Shevut Yaakov, part 2, siman 31. The author of Yad Aharon disagrees and rules that he should recite it when eating. The Semak likewise writes that one who derives no enjoyment from seeing it, but only from eating it, recites the blessing when eating. See there. One does not recite Shehecheyanu upon seeing fruits produced by grafting one species with another. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 60.
Upon a second sighting. Maharam and the Semak write that one does not recite the blessing then. The same applies according to our practice: if he did not recite it when eating, he does not recite it later even if he eats that fruit again. All the more so, he should not recite it over that same eating while the fruit has not yet been digested. Radbaz, part 1, siman 130. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 230, likewise writes that if he recited Shehecheyanu before the fruit was fully developed, he recites it again when the fruit is fully developed. See the Beit Yosef. When it reaches the size of a white bean, he recites the blessing over it. Radbaz. See Magen Avraham.
