1) [Seif 1] One who sees his friend, etc. All the more so, if he sees someone greater than himself, his father, or his teacher, he recites the blessing. An allusion may be found in the verse, 'sending portions, each person to his friend,' while the Sages say in the first chapter of Megillah, 'Our teacher has fulfilled it for us,' etc. This is obvious, for the reason is simply that he derives enjoyment and joy from seeing him. Therefore, there is no distinction whether the person is greater or lesser than he. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1.
2) There: One who sees his friend, etc. There is no distinction between men and women. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashba. It is obvious that a man recites the blessing upon seeing a woman and a woman upon seeing a man. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, writes the same. It appears that this applies specifically if she is his relative and he is familiar with her. If she is not his relative, he should not recite the blessing, because there is concern that he may come to improper thoughts. I later saw that Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 213, writes the same; see there. See Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, in the later edition, paragraph 1, who writes that a distinguished man should not recite the blessing over his wife, and that the practice is not to recite it even over his daughter; see there.
3) There: He says Shehecheyanu. Although others also derive enjoyment from seeing him, he recites only Shehecheyanu, not HaTov VeHaMeitiv. Beis Yosef in the name of Rashba; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1.
4) There: He recites Mechayeh HaMeisim. Since he recites Mechayeh HaMeisim, he should not also recite Shehecheyanu, because Shehecheyanu is included within Mechayeh HaMeisim. Bach writes this in the name of Maharshal; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3. One recites Mechayeh HaMeisim specifically if he received no letter from the person and did not know about his welfare. If he received a letter or knew about his welfare, he recites only Shehecheyanu. Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 220. Birkei Yosef there writes the same in the name of R' Avraham Azulai.
5) If he is a Jewish Torah scholar, whether he sees him after thirty days and recites Shehecheyanu, or after twelve months and recites Mechayeh HaMeisim, he also recites, 'Blessed is He Who apportioned of His wisdom to those who fear Him.' Bach. We have already written above in siman 224, paragraph 19, however, that over Jewish Torah scholars one should recite this blessing only without the Name and kingship; see there.
6) There: He recites Mechayeh HaMeisim. This means a complete blessing with the Name and kingship, contrary to Panim Meiros, volume 1, siman 66. This is explicitly stated in the handwritten Sefer HaPardes. Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, appears to agree, since he challenges Panim Meiros; see there. See, however, Ben Ish Chai, parashas Eikev, paragraph 13, who writes that the practice is not to recite this blessing even without the Name and kingship. He explains that people are sensitive about having such a blessing recited over them. There, paragraph 14, he writes that the practice is likewise not to recite Shehecheyanu upon seeing a friend; see there. Therefore, in a place with no custom to recite it, one should recite Shehecheyanu without the Name and kingship and contemplate Mechayeh HaMeisim in his heart. Likewise, regarding one who was overseas when his wife bore a son, discussed above in siman 223, paragraph 6, in a place with no custom to recite the blessing he should recite Shehecheyanu over a new fruit and intend to cover the child; see there. The same applies if one did not see his friend for thirty days or longer: he should recite the blessing over a new fruit and cover everything.
7) There: Provided that he is beloved to him, etc. This applies to the blessing Shehecheyanu as well, as is explained by Tosafos on Berachos 58b. Bach and Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, write the same.
8) It is reasonable that with every blessing of praise and thanksgiving, one person may discharge another, specifically when both became obligated, but not otherwise. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. Regarding reciting a blessing upon seeing a mamzer or a person conceived from a niddah relationship, see below, paragraph 56. It is obvious that one should not recite a blessing over seeing a wicked person, since it is forbidden to gaze at his face, as stated in Megillah 28a. How could he recite a blessing over seeing him? This is unlike a wicked king or non-Jew, over whom one recites a blessing as stated in the preceding siman, because there the blessing concerns seeing their honor, whereas here it concerns seeing the face.
