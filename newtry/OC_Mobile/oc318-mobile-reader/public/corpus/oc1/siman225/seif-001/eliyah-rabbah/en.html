If the person seen is a Torah sage in Israel, then whether one sees him again after thirty days and recites Shehecheyanu, or after twelve months and recites Mechayeh HaMetim, one also recites "Who has apportioned of His wisdom to those who fear Him," as stated in the Gemara that I will cite in the next seif katan.
Levush: that it does not fall, etc. It seems to me that one need not bless with another formula, for the blessing of Shehecheyanu also includes others with him. So wrote Lechem Chamudos. This requires study from the beginning of siman 223, where there is a distinction between Shehecheyanu and HaTov VeHaMeitiv when another person is with him, and it is from the Gemara, Berachos 59. Regarding the essence of this law, that he does not bless HaTov VeHaMeitiv, it seems to me that there is proof from the Gemara, page 58, which says that Rav Pappa and Rav Huna were traveling on the road and encountered Rav Chanina. They said to him: Since we have seen you, we bless two blessings over you, 'Blessed is He Who apportioned of His wisdom to those who fear Him,' and Shehecheyanu. Thus, even though there were two of them, they blessed Shehecheyanu.
Levush: and there is no distinction, etc. Meaning, even if he sees a woman whom he loves, he blesses.
Levush: in their hands, no. Meaning, he holds that the explanation is not that the father is exempted from punishment for the son, for the son is still a minor and not subject to punishment. Rather, the father blesses that the son has become exempt from him, so that he is not punished because of him. It appears that the wording 'from his punishment' is directed to this, as Tosafot wrote in Bava Basra 22, that each one would worry lest he be punished through him; see there. On this the Levush asked that he has still not been exempted. However, Lechem Chamudos wrote that the meaning of the blessing is that until now he was obligated to train him in mitzvos and raise him in Torah, and now the father is exempt. This is also explicitly found in the Midrash, Parshas Toldos: Rabbi Akiva said, a person must involve himself with his son until thirteen years; from then on he must say, Blessed is He Who has exempted me, etc. The Yalkut wrote that some say it is a mitzvah to make a bar mitzvah meal for his son like the day he enters the wedding canopy, and it implies that this is on the day he enters his fourteenth year. The Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 7, siman 37, wrote that if the youth expounds Torah, it is a mitzvah meal even if it is not on that day, and he wrote that the main reason for the meal is that one who is commanded and performs is greater. The Rashal wondered how we rely on him regarding Birkas HaMazon, which is according to the Torah, perhaps he has not grown two hairs. He answers that according to the Torah one fulfills the obligation through the blessings of a minor, but the Sages said that he does not discharge an adult, therefore we rely on the presumption that he has grown two hairs. The Magen Avraham in siman 199 challenged him: If so, let the Gemara on page 20 answer that since he does not know how to bless, the Sages did not decree, so that Birkas HaMazon according to the Torah should not be nullified, as stated at the beginning of siman 193. This is not difficult at all, for the Sages upheld their words in a case of sitting and not acting. The beginning of siman 193 is different, for even when both know how to bless, there is only a preferred mitzvah to divide; see there. As for what he further asked, why the poskim rule that question stringently at the beginning of siman 186, one may answer that there it is initially. In any case, many D'Rabbanan doubts are ruled stringently, as explained in the poskim. It also appears to me that since with a minor it must be speaking of one who ate a D'Rabbanan measure, as the Gemara says there, then presumably with a woman, which was taught in the same clause, it also speaks in such a case. As for what he asked that mutual responsibility does not apply to a minor, how can an adult fulfill his obligation through him, it is not difficult at all, for he holds that the concept of mutual responsibility here is D'Rabbanan.
