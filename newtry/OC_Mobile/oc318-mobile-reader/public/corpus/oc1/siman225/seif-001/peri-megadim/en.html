And there is See Taz. So wrote the Levush. The Levush further answered that even for meshaneh ha-beriyot, regarding a lame person and the like, only one who is distressed over them recites; unlike one who sees a black person in seif 8, where certainly even one who is not distressed recites, and one recites over a gentile as well; see there. See Magen Avraham 18, who wrote like the first answer. What the Taz wrote, that one recites only the first time, also refers to meshaneh, etc.; so it certainly is, for he wrote that the change in him is very great. What is difficult from 218 at the end of seif 3, where he ruled that from thirty days to thirty days one recites, while here he ruled like the Ra'avad, and in the Tur he equates them; see there. The Perishah in 218 and Nehar Shalom here answered that one should distinguish between there and here, for here there is not such a new emotional stirring as there; see there.
The Peri Chadash pointed to Even HaEzer 34, and there it says that the Rivash in the name of his teacher would mock saying it without God's Name: what benefit is there? See siman 214, Bach, and this resolves what is here in seif 2 in the gloss and seif 8; see there.
