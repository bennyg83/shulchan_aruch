Thus, if he sees his wife, mother, daughter, or sister, since he is joyful and derives pleasure from seeing them, he recites the blessing. The poskim write that if he sees one of the Sages of Israel, he recites the blessing Who has apportioned of His wisdom, as stated above in siman 224, seif 6, and also Shehecheyanu if he has not seen him for thirty days.

Who revives the dead. In that case he does not recite Shehecheyanu. Chiddushei Aggados explains the reason for this blessing: Each year, on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, a person is judged whether he will die or live. If one sees his friend after one Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur and then does not see him until after the following Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, he has passed through a judgment whether he would die or not. Therefore, he says, Blessed is He Who revives the dead, because his friend was spared from the judgment of death on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.
