He does not recite the blessing again. This applies even if he drinks the wine after forty days, when it has strengthened and tastes different from grape juice. It is nevertheless all one joy, because when he saw or ate the grapes he knew that wine would emerge from them. See the later authorities, who cite an opinion that one must nevertheless recite Shehecheyanu over new wine as well, because it brings greater joy than grapes. Therefore, if he recited Shehecheyanu over grapes, when he later drinks new wine it is proper first to recite Shehecheyanu over another new species or over a garment, thereby exempting the wine as well. If, however, he first recited Shehecheyanu over the wine, Olas Tamid maintains that all agree he does not again recite Shehecheyanu over the grapes. All this applies only when he drinks the wine as grape juice, when it is recognizable as new wine. If he does not drink it until it has become wine, then even if he did not recite Shehecheyanu over the grapes, he does not recite Shehecheyanu over the wine. Although he knows it is new wine, new wine is not visibly distinguishable from old wine. Later authorities.
