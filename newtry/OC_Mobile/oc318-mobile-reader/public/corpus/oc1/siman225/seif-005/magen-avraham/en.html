He does not recite it again. The reason is that it is one joy. When he saw or ate grapes, he knew that wine would emerge from them, and it is all one joy, even if he drinks it after forty days. This resembles the lulav, regarding which the custom is not to recite Shehecheyanu on the second day, although if that day were a weekday it would be neither the time of making the lulav nor the time of performing the mitzvah. The same applies here. My view inclines more toward exemption than obligation. Out of doubt, I refrain from eating grapes until I drink grape juice. Maharil, siman 306. The Radbaz writes that one should recite the blessing. Shelah writes that when drinking good wine, one should bring himself into an obligation to recite Shehecheyanu for another reason. Mateh Moshe likewise writes this. Nevertheless, this appears to apply specifically when he drinks grape juice. If it has already become wine, even though he knows it is new, he need not recite the blessing, because new wine is not visibly distinguishable from old wine, as stated in seif 7.
