That it was perforated. See Taz and what I wrote in seif katan 1. The statement there that a hole passing from side to side outside the seed chambers is not considered through-going does not contradict the present discussion, because there the case is where a thick partition between the chambers was perforated, and that is considered through-going. See Tosafos, Sukkah 34a, s.v. “If it was perforated,” and Rosh there, who cites the view of Behag, corresponding to the first opinion here. The practical halachah requires study. Beit Yosef implies that Rif and Rambam omitted the Yerushalmi's question and require specifically a perforation from one side to the other. According to Mechaber's usual rule, this would imply that the halachah follows them. Taz comments on Bach, who sought to identify three opinions. According to Ran, penetrating only the outer peel until the flesh of the esrog is also called a through-perforation, whereas Taz explains that Ran means a hole reaching the seed chambers. Regarding the comparison to terumah and to the gizzard, see what I wrote in my commentary to Yoreh Deah, siman 49: although the outer membrane is extremely thin, it combines with all the flesh as one membrane, while the second membrane is the hard white layer inside that adheres to the flesh. A through-hole renders the gizzard tereifah even though the pouch containing the food, which peels away on its own and lies separately in a chicken, is not included. This was explained there. See Taz, seif katan 10, on this point. Rambam writes in chapter 11 of Hilchos Terumos, halachah 10, that perforated vegetables are discarded, and Taz understands this as applying also to the peel of an esrog. See there.
