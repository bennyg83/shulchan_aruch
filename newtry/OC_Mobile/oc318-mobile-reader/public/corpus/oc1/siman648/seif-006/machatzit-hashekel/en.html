(Seif katan 6) When most of it split, etc. He was not precise, for specifically, etc. This law is derived from Ran. Ran writes, according to the reading of Beit Yosef and Bach: 'Some explain that a lengthwise split invalidates when most of it is split on both sides. This applies specifically below the chotem, but on the chotem even the slightest split invalidates, etc. Others are stringent and invalidate even the slightest split on one side if it reaches the hollow, or even if it does not reach the hollow, provided most of the thick outer peel split.' Bach writes that according to the stringent opinion in Ran, which is satisfied with a split on one side, the esrog is nevertheless invalid only when that entire side split. Ran still calls them stringent because they are more stringent than the first opinion, which requires most of both sides. Most of both sides is a greater measure than one entire side. No opinion, however, invalidates when only most of one side split. The Gemara compares a split esrog to a severed windpipe, and a windpipe remains kosher if it did not split entirely and a little remained intact above and below. The same should apply to an esrog. These are Bach's words. This requires study, for the Gemara compares them, etc. How can the opinion requiring both sides be satisfied with most of both sides? A windpipe split on both sides remains kosher if a little remains above and below, and there is no distinction in a windpipe between one side and two sides. Our text of Ran reads, 'lengthwise like its width, on both sides,' rather than the Bach's reading, 'lengthwise, most of it, on both sides.' In our reading, the word 'most' does not appear at all.
Although regarding a windpipe, etc. One cannot object that the first opinion requires both sides whereas a windpipe is invalidated by one side. A windpipe has an actual hollow, so if the split reaches the hollow it is considered a through-cut. An esrog has no true hollow, and therefore it is not considered passing through unless it splits on both sides, from one side to the other. We may therefore be lenient. Taz likewise agrees that the entire length must split. There are, however, two opinions in Ran whether both sides are required or one side suffices, as cited in the Shulchan Aruch.
