(Seif katan 6) If cracked on its majority, etc. And he was not precise, for specifically, etc., for this law derives from the Ran. And these are his words: per the version of Rabbi Yitzchak and Bach — there is one who explained it at length on its majority from two sides; however specifically below, but on its pitom even all the more so invalid, etc. And there are those stringent to disqualify even all the more so a crack from one side until its cavity, or even if it does not reach its cavity — as long as most of the thick outer peel is cracked, end of Ran's words; and Bach wrote that there are stringent ones in Ran that suffice with a crack from one side. Nevertheless it is invalid only when completely cracked. And nevertheless he calls them stringent in Ran, for nevertheless they are more stringent than one view that requires majority of two sides, for majority of two sides is a greater measure than an entire crack of one side. But if cracked on majority of one side — there is no one who holds invalid, for in the Gemara the Gemara compares a crack of etrog to a split grain, and in a split grain if it did not crack entirely and a little remains above and a little below it is kosher; and the same with etrog, thus Bach. And to me requires further study, for in the Gemara it compares, etc. — how is it possible to say that per the view that two sides suffice, majority of two sides suffices? For in such a case in a split grain from two sides with a little above and a little below it is also kosher, and there is no difference in grain between one side and two sides. And our version in Ran: at its length in its width from two, etc. — meaning what was in Bach's version "at its majority" and our version instead of the word "on its majority" writes "in its width," and the word "on its majority" was not mentioned at all in Ran:
