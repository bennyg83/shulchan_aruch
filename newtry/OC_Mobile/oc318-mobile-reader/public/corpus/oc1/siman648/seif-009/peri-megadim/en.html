According to the first opinion, however, a majority split on both sides is required, which is more extensive than a complete split along one side. This is unlike Rama's ruling; see there. One may say that an esrog is more stringent than a windpipe, because a windpipe stretches when the animal extends its neck and then heals, unlike an esrog; see Yoreh Deah, siman 34. According to the first opinion and Beit Yosef's reading, a windpipe split along most of both sides is also kosher. The esrog must therefore be invalid because a split extending fully from the bottom is like a windpipe with no complete ring remaining below, for there one requires a complete ring both above and below. The comparison to a tereifah windpipe is thus exact. The Mishnah's unqualified statement that an esrog split is invalid can then refer even to a majority split, meaning on both sides. Necessarily, however, the stringent opinion invalidates a majority split on one side even if a small part remains below and the chotem is intact. In this respect it is unlike a windpipe, which stretches and heals as explained. Taz's reading is: “It split; some explain along its length, along its width, on both sides,” meaning that the opinions differ in the same division found in Behag and Rosh cited in seif katan 6. The Mishnah teaches “it was perforated, it split,” so the split is comparable to the perforation. According to the opinion that a perforation must pass completely through, the split must likewise extend through the full length from one side to the other, beginning below near the uketz and continuing lengthwise until the opposite side. It is not invalid because of a perforation, since this opinion follows Raavad in seif 2 that a through-hole requires missing substance, whereas a split invalidates even without missing substance. A split on the chotem certainly invalidates with even the slightest measure, though perhaps a minute amount must remain outside the chotem and also below it, just as a windpipe requires a margin toward the lung. According to this, “along its width” means through from side to side, which he calls its width. Although a windpipe fully split along one side is tereifah, an esrog is more lenient in this respect. The stringent opinion corresponds to Rosh's view in seif 3 that a perforation reaching the seed chambers is invalid. Similarly, a lengthwise split along one side that reaches the seed chambers invalidates. It is not invalidated because of perforation, since this opinion follows Raavad in seif 2 that a hole requires missing substance, whereas a split does not. Were one to say that a hole reaching the chambers invalidates even without missing substance, what difference would a split along one side make, since it would already be invalid as a perforation? One must therefore say that a split through most of the peel's thickness along one full side invalidates even though it did not reach the seed chambers. According to this, Ran agrees with Tosafos that the entire length must be split, not merely most of it. Ran only cites a dispute whether the split must pass from side to side beginning at the uketz, which is called its width, or whether a complete split along one side invalidates. Beit Yosef, however, reads Ran as saying: “Some explain that it split lengthwise along most of both sides, while some explain along one side.” This can be explained following Taz: the view that defines a perforation as passing completely through similarly requires a split on both sides, while the other view requires only one side. Beit Yosef therefore writes “but Ran,” because Ran disagrees with Tosafos, who require a complete split with nothing remaining above or below. According to Ran's first opinion, a majority split on both sides invalidates. The stringent opinion invalidates a majority split on one side, following the view that a perforation reaching the seed chambers is enough. It presumably refers to the same majority measure and does not follow Bach on this point. Mechaber rules without qualification like Tosafos that a complete split on one side is required, not merely a majority, even though in seif 3 he cites both views, because this resembles the law of a windpipe. It remains difficult why he did not mention that most of the peel's thickness must be split, in accordance with his rulings in seifim 2 and 3. One may alternatively say that Beit Yosef's words “but Ran” refer to the chotem, because Tosafos imply that a split does not invalidate specifically at the chotem. See Magen Avraham, whose reading is: “It split; some explain along its length as along its width, on both sides.” The comparative word “as” means that just as a split around the circumference invalidates only if it forms a complete ring and leaves nothing connected, so too a lengthwise split requires both sides. A windpipe split across its width is invalid when most is split because it stretches, and similarly a lengthwise split in an esrog requires both sides. See below, seif katan 6. Our reading in Ran is: “Some explain along its length or along its width.” See Eliyah Rabbah. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
