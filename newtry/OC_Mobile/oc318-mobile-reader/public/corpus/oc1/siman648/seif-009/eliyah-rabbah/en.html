And there is an authority who says, etc. Ran writes this. Although the Gemara stated it regarding mottling, it appears that the same applies to all disqualifications of an esrog. Yet this is not implied by the other authorities. Moreover, if so, an esrog whose peel was removed from its upper projection should likewise be invalid even when it retains the appearance of an esrog, for it is invalid if nothing at all remains. Rosh writes that it is specifically an invalid color that disqualifies the upper projection, and Tur requires specifically that it no longer have the appearance of an esrog. Bach concludes that even if it lacks the appearance of an esrog upon the upper projection, it is valid. If so, the same should apply here. In truth, I can interpret Raavad and Rosh as holding that if even the smallest amount of peel was removed from the upper projection, it is invalid despite retaining the appearance of an esrog, for the reason given by Ran. Tur and Bach, however, did not understand them this way. Magen Avraham, seif katan 16, likewise writes in the name of Darkei Moshe that a split in the upper projection is valid, for this is the view of Rosh and Beit Yosef. See seif katan 20.
