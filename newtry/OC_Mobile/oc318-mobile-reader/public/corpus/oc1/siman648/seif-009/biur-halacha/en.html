In two or three places. The Kesef Mishneh asked: if two, then certainly three should invalidate. The Taz answered that this is what it means: it compares three to two, namely that even with two we require that they be spread apart from one another at least enough for the measure of a third patch of discoloration, meaning enough for the measure of two small blisters, with some appearance of esrog, like a hairbreadth, separating between this patch and the adjacent patch on one side, and likewise on the other side. But with less than this it is not called spotted. Many later authorities rejected his answer, for how do we know the measure of the blisters by which to measure the gap between two patches of discoloration? They answered that the Gemara asks in this manner in chapter Mefanin, and they answered: as people say. See Peri Megadim, Bigdei Yesha, and Nahar Shalom. Nevertheless, the essence of his reasoning, that it is logical that when the appearance of esrog separates between one patch of discoloration and another by a hairbreadth it does not appear spotted, seems correct in practice.
On most of it. See the Mishnah Berurah. However, on its nose, where we rule that even any amount invalidates there, certainly this applies even on one side.
From the place where it begins to narrow, etc. See the Beit Yosef, that the view of the Rambam and Riatz Gi'at is that its nose means above, near the shoshanta. But in Tur and Shulchan Aruch they wrote plainly like the view of the Rosh, that the sloping part is called the nose; and the Rosh inclines that this is also the view of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rif. Therefore the Tur wrote: from the place where it begins to slope. This is also the intent of the Shulchan Aruch. Now at first glance one might say that this is from half the esrog and upward, but in truth this is not so; for if so, when the boil-like discoloration spreads over most of the esrog, it would automatically also be on its nose. In particular according to Rashi, who wrote that the thickness of the height of the esrog is called its nose, then if the discoloration spreads over most of it, its nose is included. But in the Gemara and in the poskim it is proven that these are two separate matters. It is even more difficult according to the Magen Avraham and other authorities, who wrote that the invalidation on most of it is specifically if it is on two sides and not one side; why should it not be invalid in every case because it is on its nose? Therefore we must say that according to all opinions the nose begins on the esrog after the measure of most of the esrog, and not before. See Sha'arei Teshuvah, who requires concern also for Rashi's view if the discoloration happens to be only in the place of its height, and he presses himself to say that this too is the intent of the Shulchan Aruch. In my humble opinion, the language of the Shulchan Aruch does not imply this; and in the Levush it is explicitly proven that the intent of the Shulchan Aruch is only the sloping part, and not the place of its height, for he copied Rashi's reason but omitted the words 'thickness of its height.' At least in pressing circumstances, one may certainly be lenient even on the first day and bless over it.
