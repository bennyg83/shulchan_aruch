The “others say” is Tur's view, that this is a doubt concerning a Torah law. See Bach, who writes that although Rosh states that these invalidities are fundamentally from the words of the Sages, the matter nevertheless concerns a Torah mitzvah. See there. He evidently means the Torah obligation of taking an esrog, though this requires study. Bach concludes that it may be that all the poskim agree that it is invalid. Mechaber here rules like the first opinion without qualification, that it is kosher, whereas in seif 3 he did not decide the dispute concerning a hole reaching the seed chambers. Levush therefore altered the formulation and writes here too, “some permit and some forbid.” One may explain, as Kappos Temarim writes, that the Gemara sought to resolve the question from an esrog whose interior had decayed, implying that if it had not decayed it would be kosher, but rejected the proof. Mechaber therefore ruled leniently because the proposed proof was rejected and cannot be relied upon. Rama's silence is somewhat difficult, since in Yoreh Deah 36:7 and 37:1 he rules that many authorities validate a decayed organ. If so, the attempted proof here would point toward stringency. Know that where the interior dissolved and decayed, Mechaber may agree that it is invalid, and perhaps Rama would agree as well. This requires study.
