However, as a matter of law one should not invalidate, etc. So the later authorities agreed, that where only the shoshana was removed, one certainly should not be stringent, as is proven from the Mechaber, the Rema, and other poskim. The Ma'amar Mordechai wrote that what appears from the words of the Bach, that its shoshanta and its dad are one matter, and that there is no permission except when the flower falls, meaning the bud that usually falls before it is completed and becomes a pitma, is the opposite of the implication of the poskim; see there. He also strongly challenged his other words there, where he wrote that according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak bar Yakar one must be stringent when a little of the dad was removed; see there. Know further that the main reason some are stringent when only the shoshanta was removed is because of the language of the Rif. However, the Beit Yosef already rejected this and said that there is no proof from the Rif, for it is possible that he too holds like the Rambam, that he does not invalidate when only the shoshana was removed; see there. According to what we have now merited, with the light of Rabbeinu Chananel that has been published, it appears explicitly that all the words of the Rif in our matter are taken from the words of Rabbeinu Chananel. In Rabbeinu Chananel's words it is written explicitly: its pitma was removed, the nose of the esrog and its shoshanta, etc. This is exactly like the words of the Maggid Mishneh in explaining the Rambam's view, and this is also clearly the intent of the Rif, as the Beit Yosef wrote from his broad understanding.
