Its dad. This is the woody projection at the top of the esrog, with the flower upon it. See Taz.
The dad. The wording implies that it is invalid only if the entire dad was removed. Beit Yosef, Bach, and Levush, however, write stringently that even partial removal invalidates it, as does Magen Avraham, and this appears correct to me. Nevertheless, it must at least have been removed down to the body of the esrog. Since the reason is that the esrog is considered deficient, as written in siman 649, seif 5, in Rama's gloss, the removal of an insignificant amount is not considered a recognizable deficiency. See Shnei Luchos HaBris.
Never had one. Such esrogim are recognizable, because in place of the pitom there is a depression formed from the beginning of the fruit's growth. Mabit, Knesses HaGedolah. See Radbaz, siman 111. See Taz, who concludes that if one has before him an esrog that is superior in all other respects but lacks the flower, or from which part of the upper dad was removed, and another esrog that is inferior in other respects but retains its flower, one need not take account of the flower. This is certainly so where there is doubt whether the esrog ever had a flower at all; one should not be stringent. See there.
