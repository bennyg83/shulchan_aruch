Unless it returns to appearance, etc. It appears the explanation: even while still green it is kosher, since it can return to etrog appearance — for he told him it is not kosher except after it becomes like etrog appearance; this is obvious it is kosher, and what does it teach us? And are not all etrogs in the world green at first? And so is Tosafos' language that wrote: and those etrogs that come before us are green like a citron even per R' Yehuda when they return to the appearance of other etrogs, even detached, after staying in a vessel a long time — for certainly their fruit finished, end of his words; and if one thought it is not kosher except after returning to kosher appearance — what is the greatness of a long time, since it returned hadar? Rather certainly the kosher status is immediately even while green, since in the end they will return to etrog appearance even for a long time; and in the Gemara they ask: and R' Yehuda does not require hadar? And we learned: green like a citron — R' Meir permits and R' Yehuda disqualifies — not because he requires hadar, not because the fruit did not finish; and at first glance we should have said: since in the Gemara it only asks on R' Yehuda who does not require hadar, and on this the reason is explained because the fruit did not finish — automatically for us who rule we require hadar, invalid because it lacks hadar; and per this, even if afterward it returns to etrog appearance — invalid because now it is not hadar, unless it already returned; this is not so, for Ran already wrote in the mishnah at the beginning of the chapter that Scripture did not specify anything but anonymously said this mitzvah should be mehudar and transmitted to the Sages, etc., end of his words; therefore per the questioner's reasoning here that the reason in green is invalid per R' Yehuda is because of hadar — meaning we grasp that green is not hadar; but per the answerers that the reason is because fruit did not finish — then we have no proof to say green is not hadar; automatically we should not disqualify it as long as we have finished fruit; and proof for this that Tosafos concluded that certainly the fruit finished and they did not mention this is hadar; but certainly now we are not concerned for green shade because of hadar at all, since we do not find the Sages were concerned for this because of hadar but because fruit did not finish; and therefore permitted even while green since there is finished fruit; and with this the language of Tur that wrote "but if while leaving it," etc., is reconciled for me; and Beit Yosef wrote this wording is not precise, rather it should have been written thus; and that which is invalid means even while leaving it, etc.; and I say he well precisioned: for if he had written per Beit Yosef's language it would be that it is not kosher except after it returned; therefore he wrote "but if it is green" — meaning even while still green it is kosher since it can return afterward; and he did not disqualify it except like field grasses that remain green forever; so it appears to me clear and simple;
