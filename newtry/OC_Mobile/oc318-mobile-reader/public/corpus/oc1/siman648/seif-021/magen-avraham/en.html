The green one. Maharil would invalidate a green one, lest it remain that color, unless it had begun to turn yellow like saffron, end quote. This implies that he holds the reason is that its fruit has not finished developing, and therefore once it turns somewhat yellow like saffron it is valid. But the Bach wrote that it is invalid also because of hadar, and therefore we require that all of it return to the appearance of an esrog, unlike the common practice, which validates it when only part of it has changed, end quote. It is possible that he did not examine the Maharil. Also from Tosafot and the Rosh it implies that the reason is that its fruit has not finished developing, but nevertheless it is hadar, as the Gemara concludes. One can also say that Maharil holds that Tosafot and the Rosh maintain that even a leek-green esrog is valid if we know that its way is to turn yellow, and so it appears from the wording of Tosafot. But we do not rely on this unless it has begun, and so the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies, for he wrote that one leaves it. Therefore one should not overturn the practice. An esrog grafted from a lemon and an esrog is invalid, and it has three signs. First, the grafted one is smooth, while an esrog has small protrusions over its whole body, and they are raised. Second, the grafted one has a protruding stem, while the stem of an esrog is sunken. Third, the inside of the grafted one, namely the pulp, is broad, its juice is abundant, and its middle peel is narrow; in an esrog it is the reverse, for the peel is broad and the inside is narrow and almost dry. These signs are definitive for the grafted esrogim that grow among us. But from the district of Puglia there come esrogim about which we are uncertain, though they cannot bring them to you, for the distance from you is great, etc. A grafted esrog is invalid even in pressing circumstances, end quote of Rabbi Yehudah Padua in the Responsa of the Rama, siman 126. In Olas Shabbos he wrote another sign, that in an esrog the seed stands upright along the length of the esrog, while in a grafted one the seed lies across the width of the esrog. The Levush wrote that the one called Greski is invalid because a transgression was committed with it, like an animal of kilayim, which is invalid for offering, end quote. This is difficult, for if so, the horn of a goat produced through kilayim should be invalid for shofar. Also, why do we need a verse that kilayim is invalid for an offering? Let it be derived from the fact that a transgression was committed with it. See Bava Kamma, folio 77b, and Chullin, folio 115, and what I wrote in siman 586. It seems to me that it is invalid because it is not called an esrog at all. See Tosafot at the beginning of folio 33.
