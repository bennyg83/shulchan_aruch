(Seif katan 17) And every change, etc. According to him, if it split on the chotem, etc. This refers to Ran, who maintains that even the slightest split on the chotem invalidates. Although the reason a split invalidates is because of missing substance, as Darkei Moshe writes in this siman, or because a complete taking is required, as implied by Magen Avraham above in siman 645, seif katan 6, the stringency of the chotem still requires explanation. We find no reason to be more stringent on the chotem than elsewhere except regarding a defect of hadar. Rashi explains that a chazazis of any size invalidates on the chotem because it is more visible there than elsewhere, for a person's eye naturally rests upon that protruding area. Consequently, when a split on another part of the esrog still leaves it hadar, but a split on the chotem removes its beauty because the primary beauty is there and the split is more conspicuous, it invalidates in any amount.
The same applies to every disqualification of an esrog, as Ran writes. Here too the reason explained above must apply. Although those other disqualifications are not inherently based upon hadar, for an esrog may have a defect that invalidates it and nevertheless remain visually beautiful, when that same defect appears on the chotem it is no longer hadar and is invalid in any amount.
This requires study concerning Beit Yosef, etc. Since he writes in seif 5 that a split invalidates, meaning even the slightest amount, etc., all the more so dryness should invalidate, etc. Magen Avraham's words require explanation. In seif 5 the Shulchan Aruch cites some authorities who invalidate even the slightest split on the chotem. Here he teaches concerning dryness that even an authority who validates a slight split on the chotem agrees that the slightest dryness there invalidates. Eliyah Rabbah indeed gives this answer. Although the ruling regarding dryness is also introduced as 'some say,' its intent is that dryness is more severe than a split. In my humble opinion, Magen Avraham means as follows. The Gemara explicitly mentions the chotem only regarding chazazis, which invalidates there in any amount. Rosh extended this to every defect based upon hadar, ruling that such defects invalidate on the chotem in any amount. A defect invalid for another reason does not receive a stricter measure on the chotem. Ran added that even a split invalidates there in any amount. Although a split elsewhere may remain hadar, on the chotem it is not hadar. The same would then apply to every invalidating defect, whatever its underlying reason, if that defect on the chotem removes its beauty. Accordingly, because all agree that a defect rooted in hadar invalidates on the chotem in any amount, the Gemara's mention of chazazis is not exclusive. The dispute between Rosh and Ran concerns a split and similar defects not inherently based upon hadar: whether, when on the chotem, they still leave the esrog hadar. Ran says they do not; Rosh says they do. Magen Avraham therefore asks: since seif 5 cites the opinion that any split on the chotem invalidates, following Ran because it is no longer hadar, it follows that anything not hadar invalidates there in any amount and that chazazis is not exclusive. Certainly every defect inherently based upon hadar, such as dryness, invalidates on the chotem in any amount according to all opinions. Thus the statement here that some say the slightest dryness invalidates appears obvious and universally accepted.
Ran adds that even a split invalidates on the chotem in any amount. Although a split elsewhere may still be hadar, when it is on the chotem its beauty is lost and any amount invalidates. The same applies to every disqualification, whatever its reason. If that defect appears on the chotem, its beauty is lost and it invalidates in any amount. Thus all agree that a defect whose general disqualification is based upon hadar invalidates on the chotem in any amount, and the Gemara's mention of chazazis is not exclusive. Rosh and Ran dispute only a split and similar defects not ordinarily invalid because of hadar: whether the esrog remains hadar when such a defect is on the chotem. According to Ran it does not; according to Rosh it still does. Magen Avraham therefore asks that once seif 5 cites the opinion that a split invalidates on the chotem in any amount, which follows Ran, it is already clear that chazazis is not exclusive and that every defect that removes hadar there invalidates in any amount. Certainly dryness, whose disqualification is based upon hadar, invalidates there according to all opinions. The ruling here should therefore be obvious from seif 5.
