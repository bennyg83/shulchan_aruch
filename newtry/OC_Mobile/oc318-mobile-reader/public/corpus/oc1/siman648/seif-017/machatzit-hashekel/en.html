(Seif katan 17) And any change, etc. And to me — if cracked on its pitom, etc. — meaning per the Ran who holds cracked on its pitom even all the more so invalid; even though the reason of a crack being invalid is because of deficiency as Darkei Moshe wrote in this siman, or because of whole taking as implied from Magen Avraham above siman 645 seif katan 16 regarding lulav — nevertheless this that disqualifies on its pitom all the more so requires a reason, for we do not find reasoning to be stringent on its pitom more than another place, except in disqualification that is because of hadar. And as Rashi explained on this that chazazit disqualifies on its pitom all the more so — and these are Rashi's words there: it appears there to the eyes more than other places on it, for at that thickness a person focuses his eyes, end of Rashi's words. And if so, necessarily it must be that this which disqualifies a crack on its pitom all the more so — even though in a crack the reason is not because of hadar, but because of deficiency or we require whole taking — in these invalidations what difference is there between its pitom and another place? Necessarily it must be that in a crack elsewhere nevertheless it is hadar, but in a crack on its pitom its hadar goes away and it is not hadar, therefore it disqualifies all the more so, for there the essence of hadar depends and there the crack is more visible:

(2) And the same law applies to all etrog disqualifications, so too the Ran. Therefore also in this the above reason must apply, even though those etrog disqualifications are not invalid because of hadar — even though they have deficiency, what disqualifies them? Nevertheless it is hadar; but when that disqualification is on its pitom, then its hadar is gone and invalid all seven:

(3) And requires further study on Rabbi Yitzchak, etc. — since he wrote in his book that a crack disqualifies (meaning all the more so), etc., and all the more so dry, etc.; and the words of Magen Avraham require explanation in seif 5 he wrote in the name of "there are those who say" to disqualify a crack all the more so on its pitom; and it teaches us here regarding dry that even per one who permits a crack on its pitom all the more so, nevertheless dry on its pitom all the more so he concedes invalid; and so is explicitly in Eliyah Rabbah; and even though also regarding dry he wrote the language "there are those who say" — nevertheless the intent is thus that dry is stricter than a crack. And in my humble opinion one can say the intent of Magen Avraham is that in the Gemara it is not explained explicitly the law of pitom except regarding chazazit that disqualifies on its pitom all the more so; and the Rosh innovated: since chazazit is invalid because of hadar, so too all invalidations because of hadar disqualify on its pitom all the more so; but what is invalid because of something else, not because of hadar — also on its pitom requires the measure to disqualify like in another place:

(4) And the Ran added that even a crack disqualifies on its pitom all the more so. That is, in a crack elsewhere still hadar remains; nevertheless when cracked on its pitom it is not hadar and disqualifies all the more so. And the same for all disqualifications — whatever the reason of disqualification may be; nevertheless if that disqualification is on its pitom, its beauty goes away and disqualifies all the more so. If so, since all concede what is invalid because of hadar disqualifies on its pitom all the more so — and the chazazit stated in the Gemara that disqualifies on its pitom all the more so is not specifically; rather on this the Rosh and Ran disagree regarding crack and the like that are not invalid because of hadar — if that disqualification is on its pitom whether it still has hadar or not: per the Ran there is no hadar, and per the Rosh it still has hadar; therefore it is difficult for Magen Avraham, since he wrote in seif 5 in the name of "there are those who say" that a crack disqualifies on its pitom all the more so — and this is from the Ran's reason that there is no hadar; in any case it is proven that whatever is not hadar disqualifies on its pitom all the more so, and chazazit that disqualifies on its pitom all the more so is not specifically; and automatically we hear that whatever is invalid elsewhere because of hadar is certainly for all disqualifying on its pitom all the more so; and if so, what is written here regarding dry that "there are those who say" it disqualifies on its pitom all the more so — it is an obvious matter and invalid for all, for dry is certainly invalid because of hadar and all concede it disqualifies on its pitom all the more so; and we know this from what Magen Avraham wrote in seif 5 that here for all it is invalid, since we already heard from seif 5 that the chazazit the Gemara mentioned is not specifically:
