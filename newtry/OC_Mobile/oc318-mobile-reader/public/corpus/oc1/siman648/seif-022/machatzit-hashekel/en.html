(Seif katan 22) Pickled in vinegar, etc. In strong vinegar, even if it remained only long enough to begin boiling, it is considered cooked. See siman 473, seif katan 14, and siman 527, seif katan 3. Eliyah Rabbah writes that this requires study, because Shach and Peri Chadash conclude at the beginning of Yoreh Deah 105 that even vinegar requires twenty-four hours, and that Tur likewise maintains this. In my humble opinion he overlooked Magen Avraham in siman 447, seif katan 28, who writes that if something remained in a chametz utensil, etc., long enough for water placed upon the fire to begin boiling, it is forbidden by law, as stated in Yoreh Deah 105. Although Shach writes there that vinegar too requires twenty-four hours, one should not be lenient against the Shulchan Aruch, because the Mishnah, etc. Although in siman 104 the Shulchan Aruch writes that vinegar also requires twenty-four hours, that case must concern vinegar that is not very strong. Rashi and Tosafos there likewise indicate that the discussion concerns beer vinegar. The same applies to wine vinegar that is not especially strong. Magen Avraham thus follows his own position, agreeing with the Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh Deah 105 against Shach and Peri Chadash. Although here he writes simply 'vinegar' without specifying strong wine vinegar, that is no difficulty, because the Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh Deah 105 likewise says simply 'vinegar,' yet Magen Avraham explains it as strong wine vinegar, since that is what the poskim ordinarily mean when they mention vinegar.
Know that because of the prohibition of pickling, Taz in seif katan 17 disputes Bach. Rama writes at the beginning of this siman that an esrog from the previous year is certainly dry and invalid, and that the test of passing a needle with thread through it does not help. Bach nevertheless saw people burying the esrog in honey, thereby preserving its moisture, after which the needle-and-thread test is effective. Taz invalidates it because it has been pickled. To defend Bach, one must say that he refers to burying it in congealed honey. The principle that pickling is like cooking does not apply to a congealed substance, as stated in Yoreh Deah 104 regarding a mouse in congealed fat.
