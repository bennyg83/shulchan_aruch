He should examine it by passing a needle through it, etc. This is difficult, for it will then have a through-and-through hole, which is invalid according to the Rosh and Tur, and so the Shulchan Aruch ruled in seif 2 according to the first opinion. One can answer that a through-and-through hole invalidates only when it was pierced from one side to the opposite side, or into the seed chambers. If, however, the hole passes through only one side, meaning through the thickness of the peel, it certainly does not invalidate according to any opinion when nothing is missing. The laws of an esrog are derived from the laws of treifos in an animal, and regarding treifos, if there is a hole through the thickness of an organ, such as the heart or intestines, but it does not reach the cavity, it certainly does not render it a treifah. The same applies here to an esrog. Taz, s.k. 1. However, Machatzis HaShekel explains the words of Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, that even if one pierces only the thick peel from one side to the other, it is called through-and-through. Therefore, one must reverse the needle and not insert it by the eye. Rather, he should insert the needle together with the thread from the side of the eye, without passing the needle from side to side, and then remove the needle with the thread from that same side and see whether the thread contains moisture. In that case there is no through-and-through hole; see there. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, sec. 1, and Levushei Serad. One must take care that the needle not enter the seed chambers. Eshel Avraham there. Regarding an esrog formed in ridges, if one of the protrusions was pierced, one can say that according to all opinions it is valid, because ‘from side to side’ means from one side to the opposite side, not merely through one protrusion. Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 1; Mishna Berurah, Shaar HaTziyun, sec. 7. This applies when nothing is missing. Mishna Berurah there.
