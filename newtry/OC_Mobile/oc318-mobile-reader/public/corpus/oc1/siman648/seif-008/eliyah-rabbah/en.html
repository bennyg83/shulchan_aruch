And if there remains, etc. Some are stringent and invalidate an esrog when most of it is split. Rama. It appears that Levush deliberately omitted this, as all the later authorities write that the halachah does not follow that view. Ran writes: “Along its length, as across its width, from two sides.” Magen Avraham explains that whether it split lengthwise or widthwise, the split must be on two sides. Although an animal's windpipe is rendered a tereifah when most of its width is severed, that is because when the animal extends its neck the cut widens, which does not apply here. I found in Hagahos on Rif that the text of Ran is emended to read: “Either along its length or across its width.” The explanation cited above is directed to this reading.
