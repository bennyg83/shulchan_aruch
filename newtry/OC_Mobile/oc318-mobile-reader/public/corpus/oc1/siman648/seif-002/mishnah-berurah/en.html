If it was the size of an issar. Even if there is no deficiency in it, such as where he inserted a wide peg into it. If the hole is square or long, we consider whether, if one were to round it, it would be the size of an issar; if so, it is invalid.
And if any amount is missing. Meaning, if it is deficient, then even if the hole is extremely small, any amount, it is also invalid. See Ma'amar Mordechai, who agreed that regarding the second day of Yom Tov, it is acceptable to bless over it whenever it is not through-and-through and is not deficient the size of an issar.
And some say, etc. They hold that we require two defects: through-and-through and deficiency. Without deficiency it is valid in every case. Rather, when there is deficiency there is a distinction: if it is through-and-through, it is invalid even with any amount of deficiency; and if it is not through-and-through, it is invalid specifically when it is deficient the size of an issar. We rule stringently, except in pressing circumstances, as the Rema writes afterward.
Even though there is deficiency in them. Meaning, sometimes it appears on an esrog that there was a hole in it while it was still attached, but skin and flesh formed over it from above; in such a case there is room to permit. But if we see that it did not form over, and rather there is a hole and deficiency there, even though it was made by thorns in its manner of growth, it is invalid. This is what he concluded: however, if he sees, etc. The masses err in this, thinking that there is a leniency when the hole was made by thorns.
If he sees that the skin and flesh are not intact, etc. If there is doubt in this, whether the flesh is intact, we go leniently, since the latter view holds that even if there is certainly a deficiency, it is valid whenever it is not through-and-through and the deficiency is not the size of an issar. Therefore, in a case of doubt at least, we may properly rely on this. Even if he is uncertain whether the hole reaches through to the seed chambers, one should also validate it, for whenever it is not clear to us that it reached the seed chambers, one may rely on the words of those who say that even if it reached the seed chambers it is not called a through-and-through hole and does not invalidate unless it has deficiency.
