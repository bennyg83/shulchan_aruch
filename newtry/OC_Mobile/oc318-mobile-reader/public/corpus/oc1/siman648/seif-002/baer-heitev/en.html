Passing through. If the thick peel was perforated from one side to the other, it is considered a through-perforation according to all opinions. Magen Avraham.
The size of an issar. If the hole is smaller than an issar and nothing is missing, such as where a peg was inserted there, it is valid. If any amount is missing, it is invalid even when the hole does not pass through. The alternative opinion is that of Raavad, who maintains that two adverse factors are required, a through-perforation and missing substance. Without missing substance it is valid in every case. Where substance is missing, there is a distinction: if the hole passes through, even the smallest amount missing invalidates it; if it does not pass through, it invalidates only when the missing area is the size of an issar. The accepted halachah is to be stringent, except in pressing circumstances, as Rama writes. Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that if the hole is square or elongated, we assess whether, if rounded, it would equal the size of an issar; if so, it is invalid. The same rule appears in Yoreh Deah, siman 34, regarding the windpipe, contrary to what Knesses HaGedolah writes in the name of Radbaz; see there.
Remains intact. This implies that if there is doubt whether the flesh remains intact, we are lenient. The final opinion, that of Raavad, validates even where something is certainly missing, so long as the hole does not pass through and the missing area is smaller than an issar. We may therefore rely upon that opinion at least in a case of doubt. It appears obvious to me that this applies only where the hole certainly did not reach the seed chambers. Otherwise, even if the flesh remains intact, it is invalid because the hole passes through, as explained nearby. If there is doubt whether it reaches the seed chambers, it is likewise valid when nothing is missing, for the reason stated above, since Raavad validates even a through-perforation when nothing at all is missing.
