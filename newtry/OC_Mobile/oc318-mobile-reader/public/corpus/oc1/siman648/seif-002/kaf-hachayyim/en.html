9) [Seif 2] An esrog that was pierced by a through-and-through hole, etc. If the thick peel was pierced from one side to the other, it is considered through-and-through according to all opinions. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 6; Chayei Adam, klal 151, sec. 2; Beit HaShoevah, sec. 8. See below, sec. 24.
10) Ibid. Even the slightest amount is invalid. This applies even if nothing is missing. Tur; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 4. For example, if it was pierced by a needle and nothing was removed from the esrog, nevertheless, if the hole is through-and-through, even the slightest amount is invalid.
11) Ibid. If it was the size of an issar, it is invalid. If, however, it is smaller than an issar and nothing is missing, such as where a peg was inserted there, it is valid. Tur; Taz, s.k. 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 3.
12) Ibid. If it was the size of an issar, etc. Regarding the measure of an issar, see Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah, Chullin, chapter 3, where he wrote that it has the weight of four barley kernels, with appropriate thickness, and is round in shape. Nekudos HaKesef cites his words in Yoreh De'ah, siman 34, on the Rama's gloss to seif 2; see there. See further there in Shulchan Aruch, seif 2, where he wrote in the name of one authority that we are not expert in the measure of an issar, and therefore measure it as the majority of the width of the windpipe's cavity; see there.
13) Ibid. If it was the size of an issar, it is invalid. If the hole is square or elongated, we assess whether, if made circular, it would equal an issar. If so, it is invalid. The same is stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 34, seif 2, regarding the windpipe, unlike what Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of the Radbaz. Magen Avraham, s.k. 3; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 4; Eshel Avraham, sec. 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 3; Mishna Berurah, sec. 7. Beit HaShoevah, sec. 9, likewise agreed and wrote that even if no other esrog is available, he should take it without a blessing, since blessings are not indispensable; see there.
14) Ibid. If even the slightest amount is missing, it is invalid. Meaning, if anything is missing, even if the hole is extremely small, of the slightest size, it is invalid. See Maamar Mordechai, sec. 3, who wrote that regarding a minute hole on the second day of Yom Tov, one may properly recite the blessing over it, provided it is not through-and-through and the deficiency is not the size of an issar. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, sec. 8. See below, sec. 16.
15) Ibid. If even the slightest amount is missing, it is invalid. This applies specifically when some of the body of the esrog is missing. If only the upper peel, which is like a thin film upon the esrog, is missing, it is valid according to all opinions, as written below, seif 6; see there. So too wrote Mishna Berurah in Biur Halacha.
16) Ibid. Some say that even with a through-and-through hole, a deficiency is required, etc. This is the view of the Raavad, who holds that two defects are required, a through-and-through hole and a deficiency. Without a deficiency it is valid in every case. Where there is a deficiency, however, there is a distinction: if the hole is through-and-through, it is invalid even if only the slightest amount is missing; if it is not through-and-through, it is invalid only if the deficiency is the size of an issar. The established halacha is to be stringent, except in pressing circumstances, as the Rama writes afterward. Taz, s.k. 3; Mishna Berurah, sec. 9.
17) We rule that these are two separate invalidations: it was pierced even though nothing is missing, or even the slightest amount is missing although it was not pierced. This accords with the ruling of Maran in the first opinion, which he wrote without qualification, and it is known that this reflects his view. If it was pierced and nothing is missing, then even in pressing circumstances it should be taken without a blessing in our communities, which have accepted the rulings of Maran. In other places, however, in pressing circumstances one may rely upon the lenient view, take it, and recite the blessing. R' Yosef Navon in Get Mekushar, 124a; Machazik Berachah, sec. 2; Beit HaShoevah, sec. 13. See below, sec. 20.
18) Ibid., Rama's gloss: The custom is to validate holes made on the tree by thorns, etc. This applies when we see that a skin formed over the esrog at the place of that deficiency, although a depression and missing portion remain there. This is what was permitted. If, however, we see that no skin formed and there is a hole and deficiency there, it is invalid even though it was made by thorns during its growth. Many people err in this matter, thinking that there is a leniency whenever the hole was made by thorns. Taz, s.k. 4; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 5; Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 4. R' Yosef Navon there likewise agreed; Machazik Berachah, sec. 3; Beit HaShoevah, sec. 11; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 9; Chayei Adam, klal 151, sec. 2; Mishna Berurah, sec. 10.
19) Ibid., Rama's gloss: However, if he sees that the skin and flesh are not intact, etc. This implies that if there is doubt whether the flesh is intact, we rule leniently. The latter opinion, that of the Raavad, holds that even where a deficiency certainly exists, it is valid so long as the hole is not through-and-through and the deficiency is smaller than an issar. We may therefore at least rely upon this opinion in a case of doubt. Likewise, if there is doubt whether the hole reaches the seed chambers, it is valid, since nothing is missing, and according to the Raavad it is valid even with a through-and-through hole when there is no deficiency at all. Taz, s.k. 5; Eliyah Rabbah there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, secs. 10 and 11; Chayei Adam there; Mishna Berurah, sec. 11. Beit HaShoevah, sec. 12, wrote that this applies specifically in places that follow the rulings of the Rama and rely upon the latter opinion, validating even where a deficiency certainly exists in accordance with the Raavad. In other places that accepted the rulings of Maran the Shulchan Aruch, who invalidates even in pressing circumstances where there is a deficiency, as he ruled without qualification according to the first opinion, an esrog pierced on the tree during its growth is invalid out of doubt when it is uncertain whether the flesh remains intact. Although this is a reversible double doubt, which is permitted even in a Torah matter, nevertheless one should not recite a blessing, since blessings are not indispensable; see there. However, it appears that his statement that this applies only in certain places is not compelling, for the Taz is commenting upon the words of the Rama, and the Rama's words relate to the first opinion as well. Moreover, regarding his statement that one should not recite a blessing, we have already written in many places that where there is an established custom, we do not apply the principle that in cases of doubt concerning blessings one is lenient. See below, sec. 26.
20) Ibid., Rama's gloss: In pressing circumstances one may be lenient, etc. In that case one may recite the blessing over this esrog. Shulchan Gavoah, sec. 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 8; Mishna Berurah, sec. 12. See above, sec. 17.
