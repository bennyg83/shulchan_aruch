From one year. See Taz. Bach invalidates it and maintains that the test does not help. He explains “an old esrog” as one remaining from the previous year. This cannot be proved from Maharil's responsum, which gives the measure of dryness in accordance with Raavad. Had Maharil understood it as Bach does, he would not have needed that measure; he could simply have cited the Gemara in Sukkah 31a, which states that an old esrog is invalid. It must therefore be that he understood the Gemara's “old” esrog as one that had shriveled, whereas Maharil's responsum concerns one that had not shriveled. Maharil states that after a full year it has at least entered a doubt of dryness, whose measure depends on whether moisture remains, and it therefore requires examination. The examination is effective, because when we see that moisture remains, the principle that the Sages knew otherwise does not apply. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 3, follows Bach, and Rama's wording also implies that examination does not help. See below, seif katan 16, that an esrog pickled in honey is invalid, Magen Avraham, seif katan 23, and Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 3. Bach may maintain that the laws of forbidden mixtures differ from those of an esrog. According to this, Mechaber deliberately mentioned specifically vinegar, because soaking in another liquid for a full twenty-four hours does not invalidate it.
