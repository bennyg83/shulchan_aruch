It split entirely. For if any amount remained, it is not invalid, like the windpipe of an animal that split, in Yoreh De'ah siman 34.
And some are stringent, etc. The Ran wrote as follows: Some explained it lengthwise or widthwise from two sides; however, specifically below, but at its nose, even any amount. And some are stringent to invalidate whenever it is cracked from one side until its cavity, or even if it does not reach its cavity, whenever most of the thick outer peel is cracked. End quote. This is the version before us. His intent is clear: the first view holds that with respect to a hole, through-and-through applies only from the two sides of the esrog, meaning from side to side, and this is the third opinion that I mentioned nearby. Do not say that in any event it is invalid because of puncturing, for one can say that this view holds that even a through-and-through hole does not invalidate without deficiency, as above in the name of the Ra'avad. If so, the same applies to a split along the entire length: it is invalid only when the crack goes across the width of the esrog on both sides. But there are those who are stringent, namely Tosafot above, who hold that until the seed chambers is called through-and-through; so too by a crack, if it split along the whole length from one side until the cavity it is invalid. Do not say that this is obviously invalid because of puncturing, for these stringent authorities also hold that puncturing invalidates only when there is deficiency, like the Ra'avad. And regarding those who hold that a through-and-through puncture invalidates even without deficiency, the Ran wrote according to their view that with a crack in most of the width of the peel there is invalidity because of splitting, meaning if it split along the entire length, like the tereifah law of the windpipe, as all the poskim wrote in this matter. The Ran only brings a distinction regarding the width. In the Beit Yosef he brought the text of the Ran in this language: some explained it lengthwise, in most of it, from two sides. Therefore he wrote that the Ran does not hold like Rashi and Tosafot, who require that it split along the entire length, but only most of the length. Based on this, the Rema wrote here: and some are stringent even when most of it split, etc. It appears clear that this is imprecise, and that he had a mistaken text in the Ran before him, in which it said 'in most of it' where in our version it says 'in its width.' There is no logic to be more stringent regarding the length of an esrog than regarding a windpipe. There is a clear proof from the words of the Talmud, where Rava asked: if signs of tereifah developed in an esrog, what is the law? They ask: if it split, we learned it already. Rashi explained that this is like the tereifah of a windpipe that split entirely, so that no ring remained above or below; we learned in the mishnah that a split esrog is invalid, and in such a case it split according to all. If you would think there is invalidity in a split esrog even when some remains above and below, whenever most of the length split, how could he say about this 'we learned it'? On the contrary, the law would not be like the windpipe. And even though regarding 'if it was punctured, we learned it' Tosafot explained that we learned it as valid by an esrog when punctured without deficiency, whereas by tereifot it invalidates even without deficiency, that is understandable, for there he properly said 'we learned it,' even though it is not comparable to tereifot, since the mishnah explicitly says that specifically when it is deficient it is invalid. If so, he properly said 'we learned it' regarding this law here, and the law of deficiency is not learned from tereifot. But regarding splitting, nothing of this sort is mentioned in our mishnah; therefore 'we learned it' refers to what we learned by tereifot, meaning a split esrog is like the split of tereifot, with no distinction between them. Furthermore, if there were a distinction here between esrog and tereifot, the Gemara should have mentioned the distinction and why there is a distinction, as it mentions afterward regarding dissolution of what is inside the esrog, which is not comparable to a lung that poured out like a pitcher and is valid, because there the air does not dominate it and it returns to health, whereas with an esrog, where the air dominates it, it decays. Why did it not say by splitting that there is a distinction? Furthermore, on the contrary, by splitting there certainly is no distinction, for even in a windpipe that split, the air dominates it, since certainly the case there is that the skin was also severed, and there is no distinction there, yet it is valid as long as it did not split entirely. It is obvious that here too it is so. Furthermore, in the mishnah it teaches 'split' and 'peeled' together, and by peeling we say that if some remained it is valid; automatically the same applies to splitting. Even if you wished to press and say that these stringent authorities disagree with Rashi, Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur, we would not listen to a lone stringent opinion. All the more so, the Ran himself never considered being stringent when most of the esrog split but not all of it; rather, their version was mistaken, and the truth is as in our version. This is how it appears to me in practical halachah: to validate an esrog that split but not entirely, where some remains below, and in width it goes only until most of the thickness of the peel and does not reach the seed chambers. This is where it is not at its nose, for at its nose any amount is invalid, as the Ran wrote. This appears clear to me, with Heaven's help.
