He should examine it by passing a needle through it, etc. This examination prescribed by the Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch can hardly be performed without removing even the slightest amount and thereby invalidating it. It is therefore preferable to insert a needle only slightly and withdraw it from the same side into which it was inserted. If the hole fills again after the needle is removed, it is moist; if not, its moisture has already ceased and it has dried. This resembles what is stated in the Yerushalmi at the end of the second chapter of Sheviis regarding gourds that hardened during the Sabbatical year, etc. This is a simple method and does not cause even the slightest deficiency in the esrog. Mor U'Ketziah; Machazik Berachah, sec. 1; Ikkarei HaDat, siman 33, sec. 29. However, Beit HaShoevah, sec. 4, wrote that perhaps an esrog is different, for even if the hole does not refill, its moisture may still not have entirely ceased; see there. The Raavad, in Temim De'im, siman 232, wrote that one should pass a needle through it, and if it contains moisture, the moisture will immediately emerge through the hole; see there. Although it is impossible that inserting the needle will not remove the slightest amount of the esrog's peel, one can say that since that minute amount is pushed to the sides by the pressure of the needle or enters inward, it is not considered missing. This resembles what is written below regarding one who inserted a peg, that if the hole is not through-and-through and is smaller than an issar, it is valid; see there.
