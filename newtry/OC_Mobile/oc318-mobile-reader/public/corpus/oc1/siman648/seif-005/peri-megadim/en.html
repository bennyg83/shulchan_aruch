Rema of Fano writes that in a doubtful case it is kosher because ordinarily, while the esrog is still attached to the tree, its skin and flesh remain intact. We are concerned only when we actually see that they are not intact. Thus, where it is impossible to clarify the matter by examining the interior carefully, it is permitted because the usual condition is that they remain intact. Alternatively, the reason may be that a doubt concerning a D'Rabbanan law is treated leniently; see the preceding seif katan. In his gloss, however, he retracts this and validates it because of a double doubt, as explained. In my commentary to Yoreh Deah I wrote that any opinion relied upon in cases of substantial loss may combine to form a double doubt even where there is no substantial loss. The same may be said where there is doubt whether thorns caused a missing area the size of an issar. His statement that a through-perforation is invalid, as stated immediately afterward, appears to refer to seif katan 4, where the law was derived from a lung: a naturally occurring absence is not considered missing substance, but a naturally occurring through-hole does invalidate and render a lung tereifah. The same would therefore apply here, so that a through-hole caused by thorns is invalid even though its skin remains intact. Yet in Yoreh Deah, siman 43, seif katan 2, Taz validates a naturally occurring hole where the skin remains intact. This requires study, for the same should apply here. If there is doubt whether the hole reached the seed chambers, the esrog is kosher because of a double doubt: perhaps it did not reach them, and even if it did, perhaps the halachah follows the second opinion in all three disputed cases. According to Raavad, a through-hole without missing substance is kosher, and while the esrog is attached, intact skin and flesh are not considered a deficiency. The same applies after it is detached where no substance is missing. In pressing circumstances one may likewise rely upon Raavad even for a hole that truly passes completely through an attached esrog, whereas above it was invalidated when there was no pressing circumstance. It appears that where substance is actually missing there is no double doubt of perhaps it did not reach the seed chambers and perhaps the law requires a hole passing completely through.
