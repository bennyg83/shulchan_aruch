If he sees that it is not, etc. Taz explains that a puncture caused by thorns is no more lenient than other punctures. Whenever the peel and flesh are not intact, it is invalid even if it is known that the puncture was caused by thorns, contrary to the common error. If, however, there is doubt whether the flesh remains intact, we rule leniently, relying in a case of doubt upon the opinion that a puncture which does not pass through requires a loss the size of an issar. If it was punctured as far as the seed chambers, it is invalid even though the peel and flesh remain intact. If there is doubt whether it reaches the seed chambers, it is valid, since the peel and flesh remain intact, and we rely upon the opinion that a puncture must pass from one side to the other.
