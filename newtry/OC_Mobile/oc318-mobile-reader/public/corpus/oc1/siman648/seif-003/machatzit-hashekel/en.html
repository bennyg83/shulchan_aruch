(Seif katan 3) The size of an issar, such as, etc. Meaning, one can find a hole as large as an issar that nevertheless contains no missing substance.
Such as, etc. He derived this from Rambam's wording, who writes, 'If it is the size of an issar or more.' The word 'more' appears superfluous, for this is obvious. He therefore must mean that at times the size of an issar is sufficient, namely when the hole is round, while at other times it must be larger than an issar, such as when it is long or square. In that case its interior must contain an area equal to an issar, and the added length or corners do not combine toward the measure of an issar. It is then larger than an issar when its length or square shape is included. Rambam teaches that even such a case is kosher on the remaining days. Several halachos later Rambam writes that an esrog with missing substance is kosher on the remaining days, and therefore he first wrote 'the size of an issar or more,' because his later ruling refers back to this, meaning that on the remaining days it is kosher even if the hole is larger than an issar. Know also that Rama, citing Terumas HaDeshen, validates holes formed while the esrog was still on the tree, but concludes that if one sees, etc., it is invalid. Taz explains that the distinction is not between damage formed while attached and damage formed after detachment. Rather, Terumas HaDeshen distinguishes between a case in which the outer skin remains intact but is pressed inward, creating the appearance of a depression, which is kosher, and an actual loss of skin or flesh. Such a depression almost never occurs after detachment. Terumas HaDeshen therefore discussed a case that occurred while attached, where a thorn or branch pressed into the esrog without piercing the skin. Taz nevertheless writes that where it is uncertain whether the skin and flesh remain intact, one may rely upon the second opinion, which requires a loss the size of an issar. Since in pressing circumstances Rama permits reliance upon that opinion even where a loss is certain, in a case of doubt one may be lenient even outside pressing circumstances.
