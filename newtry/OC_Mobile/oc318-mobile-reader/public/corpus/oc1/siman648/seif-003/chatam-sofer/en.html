(There, in Magen Avraham se'if katan 23) In the paragraph beginning And in the Levush, etc. that kilayim is invalid. Note: this requires investigation, for with living creatures a verse was needed, since that with which a transgression was performed is permitted because the Merciful One excluded only 'these,' as Tosafot wrote in Chullin 115a, s.v. choresh, etc.; see there. That does not apply to an esrog, which is in the category of seeds, as Tosafot wrote there. See what the Taz wrote below at the end of siman 649. Regarding the essential reason for the invalidity of grafted ones, see Responsa Alshich, siman 110, and see Responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 183 and siman 207; and see Chatam Sofer, Yoreh De'ah, siman 78. The reason for the invalidity is that in any case another species is mixed into it, and it is like one who is half slave and half free, as is explained at the end of the words of Responsa Alshich. See the responsa of the Sema in Responsa Bach, and see Responsa Rema and the Magen Avraham. However, what the Alshich wrote at the beginning of his responsum from the Talmud in chapter Mashuach Milchamah, daf 43b, is puzzling, for what does the nullification of a young tree to an old tree have to do with here? There it speaks regarding nullification of prohibitions, not that the fruit changes and from a nut becomes an apple. See there in responsum siman 183 what I wrote regarding the signs that the later authorities devised for cases of doubt about grafted esrogim.
