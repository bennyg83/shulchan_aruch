If. See Taz and Beit Yosef. The matter depends upon the interpretation of the sugya on 36a, whether the statement of Ulla bar Chanina refers to the first clause or the latter clause, and upon the interpretation of the Mishnah. According to the first opinion, a through-perforation invalidates even where no substance is missing. A non-through perforation invalidates when it is the size of an issar, even where no substance is missing, such as one made by a broad nail. If any amount of the flesh or thick peel of the esrog is missing, as explained below in Taz, seif katan 10, it is invalid on the first day of Yom Tov, as stated below in siman 649, seif 5 in the gloss; with Hashem's help it will be explained there. The second opinion is lenient: where no substance is missing, even a through-perforation does not invalidate. Where substance is missing, any amount invalidates if the perforation passes through; if it does not pass through, the missing area must be the size of an issar. If nothing is missing, even a hole the size of an issar does not invalidate. This is also implied by Raavad's glosses, chapter 8, halachah 6, that actual missing substance is always required. Rama writes that in pressing circumstances one may rely upon the second opinion to validate a non-through perforation missing only a minute amount. This implies that a through-perforation with no missing substance, or a non-through perforation the size of an issar with no missing substance, remains invalid even in pressing circumstances. Thus the two opinions disagree concerning three cases.
