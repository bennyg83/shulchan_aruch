That it was punctured from this side to that side. For this is what the term through-and-through indicates, like an alley that is open through.
Until the seed chambers, etc. Their reason is that the thick peel surrounding the seed chambers is not considered one thing together with the seed chambers; rather, for this matter it is considered a separate entity. Since all of it was punctured from one side, this is a through-and-through hole. Therefore, even though the seed chambers and the other side of the esrog remain whole, it is invalid. Regarding practical halachah, one should be stringent like the latter view; but in pressing circumstances, where it is impossible to find another esrog, one may rely on the first view and may bless over this esrog. This is especially so where nothing is missing, such as if it was punctured by a needle, for there is a double doubt here: perhaps the halachah follows the 'some say' mentioned in se'if 2, that even by through-and-through it is invalid specifically when there is deficiency.
Through-and-through. If the thick peel was punctured from one side to the other not opposite the seed chambers, namely in the place where the esrog narrows below, this is called through-and-through according to both the first view and the second view.
