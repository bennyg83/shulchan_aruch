However if he sees that the skin and flesh are not intact, etc. It is implied that if there is doubt in this whether the flesh is intact, we follow the lenient view, since the latter view which is the Raavad holds that even if certainly there is deficiency it is kosher as long as it is not mefulesh and the deficiency is not issur-size — we may well rely on it in doubt at the very least. And if there is doubt whether it reaches the seed chambers, it is also kosher since there is no deficiency at all, and per the Raavad kosher even if mefulesh as long as there is no deficiency at all. Taz seif katan 5. Eliyah Rabbah there. Rabbi Zalman letter 10 and letter 11. Chayei Adam there. Mishna Berurah letter 11. And see Beit HaShoevah letter 12 who wrote this is specifically in places that follow the rulings of Maharil and rely on the latter view and declare kosher even if certainly there is deficiency per the Raavad; but other places that accepted Maran Shulchan Aruch's ruling disqualify even in time of need if there is deficiency in it, per the anonymous first view — if so, also if pierced on the tree in the way of its growth and we have doubt whether the flesh is intact, we disqualify it out of doubt; and even though it is a double doubt that turns — permitted even in certainty — nevertheless regarding blessing one should not bless, since blessings do not impede; see there. However it appears that what he wrote this is specifically in places, etc., is not necessary, for Taz returns on the words of Maharil zatzal, and the words of Maharil are on the first view; and also what he wrote one should not bless, etc. — we already wrote in several places that where there is a custom we do not say stam kula.
