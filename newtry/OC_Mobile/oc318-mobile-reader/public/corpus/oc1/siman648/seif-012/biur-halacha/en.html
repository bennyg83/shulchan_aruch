And the pitma is included, as is proven in the Beit Yosef, for he takes stringently like the view of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rif, that the sloping part is also included in the nose. Nevertheless, he did not exclude from this the view of the Rambam and Riatz Gi'at, that the dad is called the nose; and so it also appears from the language of Rabbeinu Chananel to one who studies it. So wrote the Machatzit HaShekel at the end of the siman. However, what he wrote, that the pitma explained in the Rema's gloss in se'if 7 is what they call the shoshanta, was written imprecisely, for we take as practical law that the pitma is the dad upon which the shoshanta sits, and not the shoshanta itself. Perhaps he used the Rema's language only as a sign, meaning the place of the pitom. This also appears from the proof he concluded from the words of the Rambam.
Change. This implies specifically a change of appearance, and not other invalidations. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Rosh, unlike the Ran, who invalidates also when it is split in any amount at its nose. According to him, if it split at its nose it is invalid all seven days, for this is not beautiful, and the same applies to all invalidations of an esrog. [Magen Avraham] Now according to the Ran, whether it split, was deficient, was peeled, or the color of the esrog remained, all is invalid at its nose in any amount and invalid all seven days. However, the halachah is not so, but rather like the first view, that only change of appearance and boil-like discoloration invalidate at its nose in any amount, and not other invalidations. Nevertheless, regarding any amount of dryness, and likewise other invalidations of beauty, the Eliyah Rabbah is stringent like the 'there is one who says' brought in the Shulchan Aruch. [Bikkurei Yaakov] However, according to what the Gra wrote in se'if 10 regarding the reason of the first view that permits a split at its nose, see there, automatically according to the view of the stringent authorities there, who invalidate even by most of it, so too at its nose any amount invalidates. This is also the view of the Ramban, brought in the Beit Yosef in siman 645. Therefore it appears that one should be stringent also regarding a split at its nose, except in pressing circumstances.
And there is one who says that the same applies to dryness, etc. This is even according to the first view in se'if 5, which holds there that regarding a split we do not invalidate at its nose more than in other places. Here, regarding dryness, it is more severe, for the reason dryness invalidates is because of beauty, and at its nose, which is more visible to the eye of the observer, even if any amount dried, it is no longer beautiful. See the Magen Avraham, whose view is that the first view also holds this way, and that this 'there is one who says' comes only to exclude the view of the Shiltei Gibborim, according to whom one should not be more stringent regarding dryness at its nose than in other places. However, in the Gra's commentary he wrote that the first view holds that one should not be more stringent regarding dryness at its nose than in other places.
In any amount. See the Mishnah Berurah, that in one place it is when it covers most of it, or in two or three places even on a minority. So wrote the Peri Megadim. In my humble opinion this matter of two or three places is not clear, for the reason regarding boil-like discoloration is stated in the Gemara because it appears spotted. When it dried in some places, I do not know whether it is considered spotted because of that, unless we see that the appearance of the esrog changed because of the dryness. This requires investigation.
