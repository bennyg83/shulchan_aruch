(There, seif 12) It disqualifies chazazit and any change of appearance all the more so, etc. Note: Here I saw in Shaarei Teshuvah of the Gaon Moharaz zatzal in this siman in the name of responsa Penei Aryeh that tends to permit red appearance. And in my opinion, God forbid, to say thus; for it is true that from the language of Shulchan Aruch in seif 16 that he wrote in his wording "if it is black or white" and did not mention red — it is implied red is kosher; but this is not a single proof, for one can say he wrote thus to include that even in black or white if it is not on its majority it is kosher, and all the more so red. But if it is on its majority or on its pitom all the more so, invalid even if red; and even if one says specifically black or white and not red — nevertheless it appears that this is specifically below, like there we rule that it disqualifies only on its majority or in three places; so too we rule red is kosher in it; but above its pitom, which disqualifies in it all the more so, red also disqualifies. And proof from the language of Shulchan Aruch seif 12 that he wrote in his wording that any change of appearance disqualifies in it. From this language it is implied that any change of appearance disqualifies anything that is not the appearance of an etrog. And perhaps Penei Aryeh also concedes in this that above its pitom it disqualifies. And what Penei Aryeh wrote that in an apple red is common — what is this proof to an etrog? Because it is called an apple should its appearance be kosher? Behold an apple's way is to be green, and for several views invalid on an etrog; and also we see that in white and black, if it is in two and three places it is invalid even though a small amount of it does not disqualify — nevertheless in three places it appears speckled and invalid. If so, so too above its pitom, if it is not like the appearance of lightning to be on an etrog, invalid, for it is like speckled and invalid. And behold in a Torah scroll, even per the view that passing a quill over the Name helps, nevertheless in several names it is invalid because it is like speckled even though the writing independently is the way to be in a Torah scroll; nevertheless if it is not equal to the rest of the writing it is like speckled; so too on an etrog above its pitom, any appearance that is not the way of an etrog to be thus, it is like speckled and invalid. And likewise below from the slope in three places it appears invalid for reason of speckled, not per the view of Penei Aryeh; and so we practice, and our intent is for the sake of Heaven and may it be acceptable before Him, amen. And behold in law: if one robbed from him a good etrog, whether it suffices to return to him an inferior etrog — see in my compositions Bava Kamma chapter Merubah (Bava Kamma daf 78), see there with God's help what I wrote to reconcile the words of Maharam Mintz, see there. And behold in law: if at first it was a beautiful etrog and afterward some change of appearance arose in it due to handling and the like — whether it is disqualified from this or not. Behold as I think I already wrote on this matter and forgot its place. And behold it appears at first glance proof from Rashi in Sukkah daf 31b s.v. "since Rava [said] etrog in the right hand," etc. — if so it is too large, perhaps it will fall from his hand and become disqualified that a hole will occur in it, end of his words. And it is difficult why he states specifically a hole — he should have said there will be a change of appearance in it because it will get dirty and there will be a change of appearance. And in my opinion it is easier for an etrog to have a change of appearance than a hole, and a hole does not disqualify except like an issur-size hole, and a change of appearance disqualifies all the more so; and in any case he should have stated both — a hole or a change of appearance. And necessarily it appears that Rashi holds a change of appearance disqualifies only if it comes from itself, but due to something else it does not disqualify, and specifically through something else, since it is possible to rinse and remove it, it does not disqualify. However, there is room to reject — for behold still this is difficult, he should have stated that its pitom or its stem will be removed. Therefore it appears that Rashi holds the concern is not for disqualification alone — what of it if nothing prohibited will result from it; rather the main concern is that it will become disqualified and he will not know of this and go out with it and be nullified from the mitzvah and recite a blessing in vain; therefore Rashi holds this is only out of concern for a hole, for in this, since we require issur-size measure, perhaps he will imagine there is not issur-size in it and err to declare it kosher and come to prohibition and nullification of mitzvah; but where there is no concern of error and disqualification is obvious and there is no concern of error for this, no need "it will fall and become disqualified."
