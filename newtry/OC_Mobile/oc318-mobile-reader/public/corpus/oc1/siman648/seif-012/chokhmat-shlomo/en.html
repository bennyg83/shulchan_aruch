(There, se'if 12) Boil-like discoloration and any change of appearance invalidate in any amount, etc. Note: I saw in Sha'arei Teshuvah of the Gaon Moharaz, of blessed memory, in this siman, in the name of Responsa Pnei Aryeh, that he tends to validate a red appearance. In my opinion, Heaven forbid to say so. True, from the language of the Shulchan Aruch in se'if 16, where he used the language 'if it is black or white' and did not mention red, it implies that red is valid. But this is no proof. First, one can say that he stated it for novelty: even black or white, if not on most of it, is valid, and all the more so red; but if it is on most of it, or from its nose in any amount, even red is invalid. And even if we say that specifically black or white is invalid and not red, nevertheless it appears that this is specifically below, just as there we rule that it invalidates only on most of it or in three places, so too we rule that red is valid there. But above, from its nose, where any amount invalidates, red also invalidates. A proof is from the language of the Shulchan Aruch in se'if 12, where he used the language that any change of appearance invalidates there. From this language it implies that any change of appearance invalidates, whenever it is not the appearance of an esrog. Perhaps the Pnei Aryeh also agrees to this, that above its nose it invalidates. And what the Pnei Aryeh wrote, that redness is common in apples, what proof is that for an esrog? Because it is called an apple, should its appearances be valid? The apple's normal way is to be green, and according to several opinions that is invalid in an esrog. Also, we see that with white and black, if it is in two or three places it is invalid, even though each small amount by itself does not invalidate; nevertheless in three places it appears spotted and is invalid. If so, here too above its nose, if it is not like the bright appearance that an esrog should have, it is invalid, for it is like spotted and invalid. Indeed, with a Torah scroll, even according to the one who holds that passing the pen over the Name is effective, nevertheless with several Names it is invalid because it is like spotted; even though the writing by itself is fit to be in a Torah scroll, nevertheless if it is not equal to the rest of the writing, it is like spotted. So too with an esrog above its nose: any appearance that is not the normal way of an esrog is like spotted and invalid. Likewise below the slope, in three places, it appears that it is invalid because of spottedness, unlike the view of the Pnei Aryeh. So we practice, and our intention is for the sake of Heaven; may they be acceptable before Him, blessed be He, amen. Now regarding the law if one stole from him a good esrog, whether it is enough to return to him an inferior esrog, see what I wrote about this in my work on Bava Kamma, chapter Merubah, daf 78; see there, with Hashem's help, what I wrote to reconcile the words of Maharam Mintz. Now regarding the law if at first it was a beautiful esrog and afterward some change of appearance developed because of handling by the hand or the like, whether it is invalidated because of this or not. I seem to have written previously about this matter, but I have forgotten its place. At first glance, there appears to be a proof from Rashi's explanation in Sukkah 31b, s.v. keivan de'amar Rava esrog beyamin, etc.: if so, it is too large; perhaps it will fall from his hand and become invalid, for a hole will occur in it. End quote. It is difficult: why did he mention specifically a hole? He should have said that it may have a change of appearance, for it will become soiled and have a change of appearance. In my opinion, it is easier for an esrog to have a change of appearance than a hole, and a hole invalidates only at the size of an issar, while change of appearance invalidates in any amount. At least he should have mentioned both: a hole or change of appearance. Necessarily it appears that Rashi holds that change of appearance invalidates only if it comes from itself, but not if it comes from another matter; especially when it comes through another matter, since it can be washed and removed, it does not invalidate. However, one can reject this, for the difficulty still remains that he should have mentioned that its pitma or stem might be removed. Therefore it appears that Rashi holds that the concern is not only because of invalidity, for what of it if no prohibition will result from it? Rather, the main concern is that it will become invalid and he will not know this, and he will fulfill the obligation with it, thereby failing to perform the mitzvah and making a blessing in vain. Therefore Rashi holds that this applies only with concern for a hole, for since there we require the measure of an issar, perhaps he will imagine that it does not have the required measure and err to validate it, and he will come to prohibition and failure of the mitzvah. But where there is no concern of error, and the invalidity is open and there is no concern of mistake, we are not concerned lest it fall and become invalid. Therefore Rashi explained a hole, and there is no proof from here for this law. Examine it.
