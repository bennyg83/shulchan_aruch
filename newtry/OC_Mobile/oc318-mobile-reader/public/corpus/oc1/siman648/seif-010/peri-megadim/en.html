It was peeled. See Taz and what I wrote in seif katan 6. You can observe that an esrog has an extremely thin outer layer resembling a film. Immediately beneath and attached to it is another discernible layer that is somewhat hard and thick, appearing together with it as the peel of the esrog. Beneath this lies the flesh of the esrog extending to the seed chambers. Taz proves from Ran's wording that “outer peel” refers to all the flesh of the esrog, because Ran writes that it is not like a red date. If the somewhat thick peel had been removed while the flesh of the esrog remained, the fruit would redden in the manner of peeled fruit; see Rashi on 35b. Nevertheless, Ran was not satisfied to explain that only the somewhat thick peel was removed, because that would still be considered missing substance. Rather, only the thin surface film was peeled away. See seif katan 6. This is somewhat difficult, because the Gemara concerning terumah and Rambam in chapter 11 may indeed refer to the somewhat thick peel. With Hashem's help I shall explain the matter of a red date further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 7.
