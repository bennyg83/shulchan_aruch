[10] [Levush] And it is that which did not change, etc. Explanation: in appearance, like a place that was not peeled; and per the Tur who ruled like the Raavad. But Bach expanded that even the Raavad did not forbid where it lacks etrog appearance except in two and three places; but in one place, even on its majority or on its pitom, kosher as long as it is not an invalid appearance, and so he ruled. And I add that even in two and three places it is fit to declare kosher as long as it is not an invalid appearance, for I found in Sefer Tamim De'im siman 130 that the Raavad himself agrees with "there are those who say" that specifically with invalid appearance we require; see there. And in any case on its pitom it seems to me to disqualify, for as I wrote above per the Raavad — even where it has etrog appearance, peeled on the pitom area is also called etrog disqualification; if so, certainly it is fit to be stringent where it lacks etrog appearance:
