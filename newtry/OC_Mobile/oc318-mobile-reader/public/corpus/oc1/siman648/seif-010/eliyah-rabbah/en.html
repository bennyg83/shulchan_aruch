[Levush:] Provided that its appearance did not change, etc. Meaning, it retains the same appearance as the area whose peel was not removed. This follows Tur's understanding of Raavad. Bach, however, writes at length that even Raavad invalidated an esrog lacking the appearance of an esrog only where the peel was removed in two or three places; if it was removed in one place, even over most of the esrog or upon its upper projection, it is valid so long as it does not have an invalid color, and Bach rules accordingly. I add that even in two or three places it is proper to validate it whenever it has no invalid color, for I found in Sefer Temim Deim, siman 230, that Raavad himself agrees with the opinion that an invalid color is specifically required; see there. Nevertheless, regarding the upper projection it appears to me that it should be invalidated, for I wrote above that according to Raavad even when it retains the appearance of an esrog, removal of the peel is invalid because it too is considered a disqualification of the esrog. Certainly, then, it is proper to be stringent where it does not retain the appearance of an esrog.
