In two or three places, etc. The question is raised: if two places invalidate it, why was it necessary to mention three? Taz answers that the statement compares three places to two. With two places, there must be an unblemished space between them; if they are separated by no more than a hairbreadth, the esrog still has a single appearance and is valid. Derishah answers that according to the first opinion in siman 146, even three places do not invalidate it unless the defect spreads over most of the esrog, whereas according to the second opinion the ruling teaches that even two places invalidate it when they cover only a minority. In Lechem Mishneh, Hilchos Lulav HaGazul, I found the explanation that “two” means two places on opposite sides, while “three” means three places on one side. This accords with the Yerushalmi's statement that if the defect covers most of one side it is invalid, meaning a minority of the entire esrog, but only where there are three defective places. Rosh was therefore compelled to say that the Yerushalmi disagrees. In my humble opinion, the Yerushalmi cannot be explained this way, for it concludes, “and its upper projection, over most of it,” while upon the upper projection even two places are certainly unnecessary. It is also surprising that Lechem Mishneh writes that Rosh was compelled to say that the Yerushalmi disagrees. Rosh did not mean that the Yerushalmi disagrees at all. Rather, he explains that the words “one side” in the Yerushalmi mean one place, as in our Gemara, and not literally one side. This is also explained in Tosafos, Sukkah 29b, s.v. “It states categorically,” etc. Lechem Mishneh further writes there that another explanation resolves the apparent contradiction between the beginning and the inference from the end: in two places it is invalid even if the defects cover half, while in three places it is invalid even if they cover only a minority. This is difficult, for the Gemara in Sukkah 34b states regarding the Mishnah's ruling that a minority is valid: “This was taught only when the defect is in one place, but in two or three places it is invalid.” Rava explained: “Because it is mottled and invalid.” Thus, even a minority is invalid when the defect is in two places.
