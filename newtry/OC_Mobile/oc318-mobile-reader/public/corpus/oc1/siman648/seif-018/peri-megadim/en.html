Unless. See Taz. Bach invalidates even a partial green coloration because the esrog must be beautiful, and he treats it like a chazazis. See there. Taz, however, maintains that the conclusion of the Gemara does not rest upon the requirement of beauty. This is why Rabbi Meir validates it and Rabbi Yehudah invalidates it, because its fruit has not fully developed. It appears that a green color like the grass of the field indicates that the fruit has not fully developed, even if one leaves it until it turns yellow, whereas a green color unlike field grass indicates that it will return to the normal color of an esrog. If part of it already has the yellow color of an egg yolk, which is the normal color of an esrog, it is possible that it is kosher even though another part is green like field grass. All of this requires study. One should seek a beautiful and attractive esrog whenever possible. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
