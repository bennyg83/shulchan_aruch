It was removed, etc. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 142, writes at length to permit it when one reattaches it with a needle in a manner that is not noticeable, provided that the needle does not pass into the seed chamber but only through the thickness of the esrog, as stated in seif 3. He concludes: “This is, however, a novel ruling, and I require other halachic authorities to agree with me. On the second day of Yom Tov, however, it appears obvious to me that one may recite the blessing over it.” In my humble opinion, his proof from Sukkah 37 can be rejected. It is only regarding taking the lulav by means of a handle that we say that, because it is attached, it is considered part of the lulav; this does not establish that attaching a missing piece makes the object whole. Moreover, I wrote in my responsa at the end of Sefer Eliyahu Zuta that one cannot derive from the law of eruv in siman 366 that pieces of bread may be joined to form a whole loaf; see there. It further appears to me that there is concern for the prohibition of adding to a mitzvah. Although regarding binding a species other than the four species with the lulav, at the beginning of siman 651, it is not considered adding to the mitzvah, it is nevertheless possible to distinguish between the cases.
