The dried one. See Magen Avraham, who concludes that a withered esrog, known as velk, is valid, provided that it is tested; see there. If one already has a prepared esrog, he is not required to wait for a more beautiful esrog to arrive. Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 37, and Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 72, concur. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 45.
A needle. This is difficult, for the needle will make a perforation that passes through, which is invalid according to the Rosh and Tur, and the Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly in seif 2 according to the first opinion. One may answer that a through-perforation invalidates only when it passes from one side to the opposite side or reaches the seed chambers. If, however, the hole passes through only one side, meaning through the thickness of that side, it certainly does not invalidate according to any opinion when nothing is missing. The laws of an esrog are derived from the laws of tereifos in an animal, and regarding tereifos, if a hole passes through the thickness of an organ, such as the heart or intestines, but does not reach its cavity, it certainly does not render the animal a tereifah. The same applies to an esrog. Taz.
Dried. Bach writes that the thread test is ineffective here, because an esrog from the previous year is presumed to be dried and invalid. Taz writes that if we see that its beauty remains, the thread test is effective; see there. Bach further writes that those who store an esrog in honey so that it retains moisture may not rely upon this to validate it without testing it with a needle threaded with a string. Taz, seif katan 17, however, writes that it is invalid because it is included in the law of pickling; see there.
