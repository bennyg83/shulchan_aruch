Magen Avraham wrote: Even if it is withered, etc. And in Hagahos Ashri, etc., if he cut, etc. Rama cites this below in siman 649, seif 5.
And that which we rule concerning a withered esrog, etc. This means that it is not entirely dry, but only partly so. In other words, the meaning of withered is that it is not completely dry, but only partially dry. We call an esrog withered when the moisture throughout the esrog has not dried out entirely, but some moisture remains, which is what is called velk. The same applies if it dried out completely only in one place, while the rest of the esrog remains fully moist. This too is called withered, because some of its moisture has been lost while moisture still remains in it.
And one cannot say that this applies when the entire esrog has withered, etc. He wishes to reconcile Maharil and Rokeach so that their words do not contradict the Gemara, and also so as not to create a dispute among the poskim. The Gemara on page 37a compares this law to a lung, and the same law applies to a lung, as we rule in Yoreh Deah 36:13 that if even part of a lung has dried out, it is tereifah, meaning that it crumbles under the fingernail. If, however, it shriveled through human agency, there is a distinction: if the entire lung shriveled it is tereifah, but if only part shriveled it is kosher. The reason is that a shriveled lung is not completely dry and does not crumble under the fingernail. A withered esrog likewise does not crumble under the fingernail and resembles a shriveled lung. Although a lung that shriveled naturally is kosher in all cases, as stated there in Yoreh Deah, one must say that a withered esrog is compared to a lung that shriveled through human agency.
