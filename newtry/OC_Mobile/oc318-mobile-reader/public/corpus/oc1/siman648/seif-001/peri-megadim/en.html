Raavad prescribed this test, and according to his view it is understandable, as stated in seif 2 under “others say.” According to Rosh and Tur, however, who follow the first opinion in seif 2, it is difficult, and all the more so according to Mechaber, who emends the text to read “he probes it,” and according to the reading “a thread.” See Beit Yosef and Eliyah Rabbah, seifim katanim 1 and 2. What is written concerning its thickness is that a through-perforation does not invalidate it, as with the heart; see Yoreh Deah, siman 40, Shach, Bach, Pri Chadash, and my commentary there, where it is ruled kosher. Here, however, Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, and Taz, seif katan 6, imply that a through-perforation even where it is not opposite the seed chambers invalidates it. See Taz here, Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2, and below, seif katan 6. One must therefore say that partitions inside the esrog separate the seed chambers. If a perforation passes completely through the true width of the esrog, meaning through the width of a round object, it is invalid. A perforation opposite the seed chambers is likewise termed through-going. At the side of the esrog, however, where it narrows and does not constitute its true width, a perforation from side to side does not harm it according to this view. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. If the esrog is formed in separate ridges and only one ridge was perforated, it may be said that all authorities agree that it is kosher, because “from side to side” means from one side of the esrog to the other, not merely through a single ridge, as explained.
