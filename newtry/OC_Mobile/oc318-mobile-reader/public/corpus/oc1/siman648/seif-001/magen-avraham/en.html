Even if it is withered, which is called welk, it is invalid, Responsa Maharil, siman 5, cited in Darkei Moshe, and so the Rokeach wrote. This requires study, for it is explicit in Sukkah, folio 31, that all of them, if withered, are valid, and the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham cite this. Indeed, in the Mordechai it implies that withered is invalid, for he wrote that it is valid in pressing circumstances. But in Beis Yosef at the end of siman 649 he wrote that the correct version is pickled; see there. This also follows logically, for if the text were withered, how could the Mordechai write dry in pressing circumstances, and the Raavyah explained that the same applies to withered, etc.? That is included in his words, for since dry is valid, withered is included. Rather, as the Beis Yosef wrote, the primary reading is pickled, but withered is always valid. In Hagahos Ashri, chapter 3, he wrote in the name of the Raavyah: an esrog that is withered and speckled, which is invalid, if one cut away what was dry, withered, and speckled, it is invalid, end quote. One must analyze this, for at first he wrote withered, and he concluded dry, withered. Rather, it must be that he is discussing where it dried, and the reason he called it withered is that it is not entirely dry but only partly so. The Shiltei Gibborim likewise wrote: a scab that arises after it was detached is only due to dryness and withering, and it is valid, end quote. It is possible to say that if all of it withered it is invalid, but if part of it withered it is valid, for in the Gemara they compare an esrog to the tereifah laws of a lung, and the law of a lung is also so. Nevertheless, it seems to me to be lenient, since all the poskim wrote that dry is invalid, which implies that withered is valid, provided that one examines it. Needle. He should be careful not to make a hole that is perforated through, namely by inserting the needle forcefully; see seif 2 and 3, Bach.
