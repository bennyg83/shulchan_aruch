By passing a needle through it. So wrote the Rosh and the Tur. It is difficult: this will make a through-and-through hole in it, and it is invalid according to the Rosh and the Tur, and so the Shulchan Aruch ruled in se'if 2 according to the first view. One can say that a through-and-through hole invalidates only when it is punctured from this side to the opposite side, or to the seed chambers. But if the hole on one side goes through, meaning through its thickness, it certainly does not invalidate according to all opinions without deficiency. For the laws of esrog are learned from the laws of tereifot of an animal; and regarding tereifot, if there is a through-and-through hole in the thickness of an organ, such as in the heart or intestines, and it does not reach the cavity, it certainly is not a tereifah. So too here with an esrog.

According to the implication of his language, it appears that this is what he means: certainly it is included in dryness, whose measure is that it does not produce moisture, and therefore it requires checking. There is surprise regarding the Rema, who implies that checking is ineffective, since he wrote about this: and it is invalid. I do not know why checking should not help when it has moisture before us. Perhaps the Rema too means that then it requires checking. In the Gemara, daf 31, it states that an old esrog is invalid, while Rabbi Yehudah validates; and there the reason of the first tanna is because it is not beautiful, as explained there, meaning that it has shriveled and lost its beauty. According to this, if we see that it remains in its beauty, the thread test is effective for it. See below in this siman, where I wrote the law if they preserve it in honey.
