(Seif katan 13) All of them are on one side. This implies that if the blemishes spread, etc. Although the plain meaning of the poskim is that whether the blemish is in one place or in two or three places, it must cover most of the surface of the esrog, and most of its circumference is insufficient, Magen Avraham innovates on logical grounds and from the precise wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which follows Raavad and says 'all of them are on one side,' that most of the circumference suffices. In my humble opinion this applies specifically to two or three places, which is more stringent. There Magen Avraham rules that most of the circumference is enough. It does not apply to one place, which is more lenient, for according to one opinion even a minority in two or three places invalidates, while in one place all agree that a majority is required. Magen Avraham therefore rules stringently that in two places, even according to the opinion requiring a majority, most of the circumference suffices. In one place, however, which is more lenient, Magen Avraham agrees that most of the esrog's surface is required. This is also clear from his placing this comment specifically on the case of two or three places. Otherwise, his words would contradict his resolution in seif katan 15, where he writes that the case there concerns a blemish that is only upon most of the circumference. If even one place were invalidated by most of the circumference, then seifim 9 and 11 could also be discussing most of the circumference, and his resolution would accomplish nothing. It must therefore be that in one place Magen Avraham agrees that most of the surface is required. This also appears from Rosh. Rosh writes concerning Raavad's opinion in seif 9, which requires that mottling spread over most of the esrog: 'This is a sound boundary and measure, for it is unreasonable to say that two or three tiny spots of mottling, one beside another, should invalidate it. The language, however, does not indicate his interpretation, for it implies that two or three places are comparable to one place. Just as one place on a minority is kosher, so too two places on a minority should be kosher, unless we say that the comparison is that the spots in two or three places together occupy only a minority of the esrog, as in one place, but their dispersion extends over most of the esrog.' These are Rosh's words. According to what was explained above, there is no difficulty. One place on a minority is kosher even if it encompasses most of the circumference, and similarly two or three places on a minority that encompass most of the circumference are invalid. Yet this is not a sufficient answer, because the term 'minority' regarding one place certainly includes both most of the circumference and an actual minority of the circumference. The Gemara then says that two or three places are invalid, implying even where there is no majority of the circumference, but only an actual minority. Rosh therefore required his own resolution.
