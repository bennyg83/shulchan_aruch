[Levush:] Blisters, etc. The minimum plural is two; one blister, however, does not invalidate the esrog even if it is upon its upper projection. Mabit, part 3, siman 49. Kenesses HaGedolah writes that this inference does not appear correct to him. Mabit further writes that the rule applies specifically when the blister developed from the esrog itself. If, however, the place became indented and red because thorns pricked it while it grew upon the tree, it is valid. Kenesses HaGedolah and Magen Avraham cite this. This requires study, for Sefer Agudah writes that it is natural for mottling to develop where the esrog lies upon thorns. This indicates that the defect need not arise from the esrog itself.
