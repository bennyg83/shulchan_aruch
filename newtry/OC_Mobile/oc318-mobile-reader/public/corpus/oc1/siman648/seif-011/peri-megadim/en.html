Its dad was removed. See Taz, who discusses this at length and concludes that it is invalid only when the entire woody part upon whose top the flower grows was removed. Otherwise it is kosher. This means that the piston inserted into the esrog was removed and a depression remained at its top. I did not understand Taz's statement that sometimes no depression remains at the top, because if the wood is removed from within the esrog a depression will certainly remain. It cannot refer to an esrog whose flesh slopes inward, because even regarding the uketz by which the fruit hangs from the tree, its removal does not invalidate where the place at which it was inserted remains level with the esrog, even if the surrounding fruit protrudes more. This requires study. See Magen Avraham, seifim katanim 8 and 9, and Be'er HaGolah.
