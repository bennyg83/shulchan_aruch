(There, seif 11) If it is half-and-half in one place, etc. Note: see in Taz that there is a difficulty in the Mishnah's precise wording against itself — at first it states on its majority invalid, which implies that with a minority it is kosher, and afterward it states on its minority kosher, which implies that with a minority it is invalid; see there. And in my humble opinion it would seem to reconcile: that a deficiency is kosher on the other days, and there are differences of opinion below per the Ran brought in the gloss below — it is proven that he holds specifically that its majority must remain intact, then it is kosher, and not when only a minority remains; and from the words of Maharai that Magen Avraham brought it is implied that he holds even if only its minority remains intact it is kosher. And in my humble opinion it would seem to decide that if its majority was removed and only its minority remains such — it is invalid per the Ran, for in this case his reason applies well — it is not called an etrog but pieces of etrog; that is, since its majority was removed and only its minority remains, therefore it is called only a piece of etrog and therefore invalid; but if half remained, since in any case its majority was not removed, it is not called a piece of an etrog since the majority was not removed — in this law with Maharai it is kosher. Therefore one can say there is a difference between the first day of Yom Tov and the other days: on the other days we require specifically that it be on its majority; but with a minority, even if chazazit disqualifies even in a minority, nevertheless it is kosher because it is no worse than removal and is considered in any case as if it does not exist; and since if it had been removed from the etrog it would be kosher even though it was not yet removed, it is kosher because whatever is in his hand is not like something lacking an action, as the poskim wrote regarding a Torah scroll on Shabbos — if it is in his hand to fix it, it is kosher; so too here. But on the first day of Yom Tov, if deficient it is invalid, then necessarily if we judge it as it is now, even with a minority it is not hadar; and if we judge it as if removed, behold again it is deficient and invalid; therefore it is kosher only in its minority, for then there is no chazazit on it. Therefore the Mishnah states the decided matter: one who has chazazit on its majority — invalid even on the second day of Yom Tov, for in this case it is necessarily as above; and one who has it on its minority — kosher even on the first day of Yom Tov, unlike with a minority there is a difference — on the first day of Yom Tov invalid and on the second day kosher. And if so, the implication of the poskim does not seem to show this difference; nevertheless the reasoning supports as written. And further it would seem to say: since etrog laws are compared to treifot, as written in the Gemara and Tosafos daf 36 — if so one can say so too here: since in treifot with two adverse factors it is treifah, so too with etrog; therefore one who has chazazit on its majority is invalid even without joining another adverse factor; but on its minority it is kosher if there is no other adverse factor; but if there is another adverse factor it is invalid even combined with a minority of chazazit; therefore at first it states one who has chazazit on its majority — invalid even without another adverse factor, and afterward it states if chazazit rose on its minority — kosher even though there is still another adverse factor that its pitom was removed, even combined with all these adverse factors it is kosher; but with a minority, if there is no other adverse factor it is kosher, but with another adverse factor it is invalid. And behold Tosafos wrote in this sugya daf 35b s.v. "if chazazit rose on its majority" — in the Yerushalmi it is somewhat implied that only on the first day of Yom Tov it disqualifies, as I explained in the previous chapter. And behold I wonder regarding what they brought this Yerushalmi, since in our Gemara it is implied the opposite from what it asks — on the contrary, in two and three places it appears speckled and invalid, and it concludes that it refers to the latter clause, etc.; and it is difficult, since in the end one must emend and reverse the version from kosher to invalid — why need to answer that it refers to the latter clause? It should have remained as at first that it refers to the first clause, only that the intent is thus: since the Mishnah states specifically chazazit together with piercing and deficiency all the more so, therefore it is implied it is invalid only on the first day of Yom Tov, similar to piercing, as Tosafos wrote at the end of the chapter; therefore it says well "if in one place" — only with a minority then it is not invalid except on the first day of Yom Tov and on the second day kosher; but in two and three places it would be speckled and invalid even on the second day of Yom Tov, for from the language of the Gemara it is proven that in two and three places it is worse than one place on its majority, as it asks in the language "on the contrary" — if so it should answer thus that in two and three places it is invalid even on the second day of Yom Tov; and since it does not answer thus, it is proven that it is obvious to him that chazazit on its majority is invalid all seven days; and if so, why did they bring the words of the Yerushalmi here? Requires further study. And behold per the above it would seem to explain the language of the Yerushalmi that R' Yehuda says in the name of R' Shmuel: all disqualifications do not disqualify except on the first day of Yom Tov alone. There they say: and its majority from one side, etc. And behold at first glance from his words in two and three places it is better and it is kosher, and in our Gemara it is implied the opposite; and see Shirrei Korban who addressed this, and in his explanation there it was forced to reconcile. And per what I wrote one can say this follows the view: since all disqualifications do not disqualify except on the first day of Yom Tov, but on the second day kosher even for chazazit; therefore it says well that on its majority from one side it is that which is not invalid except on the first day of Yom Tov, but in two and three places invalid even on the second day of Yom Tov, for it is like speckled; and one can say, and it is correct. And I saw in the Rosh that he brought this Yerushalmi and explains explicitly the above view; and why did he not explain further that the intent is that even on the second day of Yom Tov it is invalid in two and three places?
