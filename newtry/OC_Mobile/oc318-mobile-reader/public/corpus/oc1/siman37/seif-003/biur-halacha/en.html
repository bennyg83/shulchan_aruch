A minor, etc. — It seems obvious that according to the Mechaber, if for some reason his father did not buy tefillin for him — for example, he could not guard them in purity, and the like — and his son became thirteen years and one day, again there is no mitzvah of chinuch on his father, for he is a man and obligated on his own in all the mitzvot; and if he is a poor man, all Israel is obligated in this, as the Perishah wrote in s.k. 4.

To buy for him — One should examine: if he has two sons, must he buy tefillin for each one, or does he fulfill his obligation by first educating one son and after his prayer giving them to his second son? And likewise if he has one son and manages to borrow tefillin for him, or gives him his own tefillin for Keriat Shema and prayer throughout the time of chinuch — that is, until he becomes a man — does he fulfill his obligation thereby? For possibly what the baraita states [Sukkah 4:43], "his father buys him tefillin," is specifically in their time when they laid tefillin all day, and it is not common for one to lend his tefillin to another — unlike in our time; you know that it does not state this regarding lulav. I again found in Beit Chadash something that implies a bit like our words, and it can be rejected; see there, and it requires study.

And some say, etc. — Pri Megadim wrote, and these are his words: "I do not know — if he is thirteen years old, what obligation is on his father? For he is a man on his own; and if he is poor, all Israel is commanded to sustain him." One could say: interpret it as thirteen years and he did not bring two hairs; and therefore one could say that likewise until most of his years, if he did not bring two hairs, one must educate him; so far. And although then he is obligated by law because of Rava's presumption, as brought in responsa — nevertheless one could say that the mitzvah of chinuch does not cease from his father as long as it is not known that he brought two hairs; but all this is a great stretch. And see below in siman 225, seif 2, that when the son becomes thirteen his father recites "Blessed is He who released me" — that is, from the mitzvah of chinuch, according to what Magen Avraham wrote there; and not one of them omitted to say that it is specifically when it is known he brought two hairs; but certainly regarding chinuch, which is rabbinic, we may rely on Rava's presumption. Truth will guide his way: Pri Megadim did not see the book Ba'al HaItur, part 2, which in his day had not yet been printed; for according to what he wrote there, it is proven that according to him this law is not mentioned in the Gemara at all regarding a rabbinic enactment, and it implies that there was no such enactment at all — only regarding Torah law, that a thirteen-year-old is not obligated in tefillin unless he knows to guard his tefillin; and he brought proof for this from the Mekhilta on the verse "and you shall observe this statute"; see there — and this is even if he brought two hairs, similar to the obligation of mezuzah mentioned there; see there. And regarding Pri Megadim's question, what does his father have to do with this — see in Birkhei Yosef and it will be settled for you. All this is according to the Ba'al HaItur's method; but we hold as all the poskim, and as Beit Yosef wrote, that this law is not mentioned in the Gemara at all regarding an adult — only regarding a minor; the world practiced stringency like the Ba'al HaItur not to lay tefillin.

This minor specifically — Explanation: then it depends on whether he knows to guard his tefillin — that is the Ba'al HaItur's view, as explained there. And that we do not practice today to be strict about this — possibly the Rama did not rule to practice like him except regarding not laying them before bar mitzvah, and not for this; and it is also proven a bit in Darkei Moshe; see there. Or else all this is specifically in their time when they laid tefillin all day and it was hard to guard them; but today, when one lays them only at the time of Keriat Shema and prayer, we rely presumptively that when he becomes thirteen he knows to guard his tefillin, as explained in seif 62 — unless we know he does not know to guard his tefillin.

And so they practiced — See in Mishnah Berurah; and see in Pri Megadim, who sides to rule as Beit Chadash wrote, that in any case a minor of twelve years who studies Talmud and understands may lay tefillin — for regarding him we can certainly rely if he knows to guard himself from the matters mentioned nearby.
