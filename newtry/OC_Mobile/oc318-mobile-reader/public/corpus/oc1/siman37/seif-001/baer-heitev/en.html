Reward. In the Talmud it states that one prolongs his days. In Shmusha Rabbah it states that he is assured to be a son of the World to Come, that Gehenom does not rule over him, and his sins are forgiven. The Rosh wrote: if one is careful with tefillin, the scale of merit tips for him; if negligent in them, the scale of guilt tips for him. There is no greater positive commandment than tefillin. A deaf-mute who speaks but does not hear, or who hears but does not speak, is obligated to lay tefillin; but one who neither hears nor speaks — they do not stop him from laying them if he wants. See Avnei Tzedek, and likewise in Shiurei Kenesset HaGadol siman 38. The Ba'er Heitev before me did not copy this well; see there.

In their bodies. Specifically when he does not lay them because of contempt for the mitzvah; but one who refrains because he requires a clean body and perhaps will not be careful with their sanctity — although it is a sin on his part, for he could easily be careful with them during Keriat Shema and prayer — nevertheless he is not among those who sin against Israel in their bodies. The Rosh.
