Reward. The Gemara states that one who wears tefillin merits long life. Shimusha Rabba states that he is assured of being a member of the World to Come, that Gehinnom has no dominion over him, and that his sins are forgiven. Rosh writes that if one is careful regarding tefillin, the scale of merit outweighs the scale of guilt. If he is negligent regarding them, the scale of guilt outweighs the scale of merit, for there is no greater positive commandment than tefillin. A deaf person who can speak but cannot hear, or one who can hear but cannot speak, is obligated to put on tefillin. One who can neither hear nor speak is not prevented from putting them on if he wishes. Olas Tamid. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 38, writes likewise. The Baer Heitev before me did not copy this accurately. See there.
With their bodies. This applies specifically where he does not put them on because the mitzvah is contemptible in his eyes. If, however, he is afraid to put them on because they require a clean body and he fears that he will not guard their sanctity, then although he is committing a transgression, since he can easily guard himself during Krias Shema and prayer, he is nevertheless not included among the sinners of Israel. Rosh.
