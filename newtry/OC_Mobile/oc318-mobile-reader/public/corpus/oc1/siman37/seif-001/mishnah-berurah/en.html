(1) Reward — that all who lay them prolong days in this world, as it is said, "the the Omnipresent upon them shall live" — meaning those who bear the name of the Omnipresent upon them in tefillin shall live; and he is assured to be a son of the World to Come, and the fire of Gehenom does not rule over him, and all his sins are forgiven. Tur in the name of Shmusha Rabbah.

(2) Who does not lay them — meaning even if his refraining is only at intervals, and how much more if he always nullifies this mitzvah. Pri Megadim brought in the name of Netziv that even one who nullified tefillin one day is also among those who sin against Israel.

(3) Among sinners of Israel — specifically when he does not lay them because the mitzvah is contemptible in his eyes; but one who fears to lay them because he requires a clean body and perhaps will not be careful with holiness as required — although it is a sin, for a person can easily be careful at the time of Keriat Shema and prayer — nevertheless he is not among those who sin against Israel. And how much more if the mitzvah is always beloved to him and he is careful to have a clean body, but now he has illness beyond his control so his body is not clean — according to all opinions he has no status of those who sin against Israel at all. And know that Beit Chadash wrote: even if the mitzvah is not contemptible in his eyes, but he refrains from laying them because of neglecting work or other monetary loss, or from laziness — he is also among sinners of Israel in their bodies, but there is a distinction between them regarding punishment; see there. And this is open rebuke to those people who, from laziness, commonly have their tefillin on the forehead and not on the scalp, and also the head-tefillin is not in its place as required — behold, this is like one who did not lay them at all. Pri Megadim already urged us on this in siman 27, where he wrote that tefillin laid not in their place are like tefillin in one's pouch. And see above siman 27 in Biur Halacha, where we expanded on this.

(4) In their bodies — and its law is a verse in the Talmud, Rosh Hashana 17: even if he is an average person, whose way is for God to tilt the scale toward mercy — since this sin is found among his sins, the scale of guilt tips and he must descend to Gehenom; and if, heaven forbid, his sins also outnumber his merits, he is judged in Gehenom twelve months, and then his body is consumed and his soul burned, and wind scatters his ashes under the feet of the righteous. And see there in Tosafot on one who did not repent. And because the power of the mitzvah is so great, and likewise the punishment — therefore every person should be careful to buy tefillin from an expert scribe who is God-fearing and a Torah scholar; and likewise straps he should buy from a trustworthy person so he can be sure they were made for their sake from pure hides — for one who lays invalid tefillin not only fails to fulfill the mitzvah but blesses many blessings in vain, which is a great sin. Woe that the stumbling block is great in buying tefillin and straps from an unworthy person because they sell cheaply, and most are not square, and there are further corruptions common at the time of writing. Every God-fearing person should take to heart: if regarding his garments and utensils he is meticulous that they be proper — how much more regarding holy objects he should not stint and spare money, but be meticulous to buy those that are certainly kosher even if their price is high. Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Mordechai: a person should seek beautiful tefillin, as it is said, "This is my God and I will glorify Him" — meaning he should write them with a skilled scribe in beautiful script, with beautiful ink, a beautiful quill, and beautiful parchments.
