(1) Reward. Whoever puts them on merits long life in this world, as the verse states, “Hashem is upon them, they shall live,” meaning that those who bear the Name of Hashem upon themselves through tefillin shall live. He is assured of being a member of the World to Come, the fire of Gehinnom has no dominion over him, and all his sins are forgiven. Tur in the name of Shimusha Rabba.
(2) Who does not put them on. Meaning even if he refrains only occasionally. All the more so if he regularly neglects this mitzvah. Peri Megadim cites Nezer HaTzvi that even one who failed to put on tefillin for a single day is included among the sinners of Israel.
(3) Among the sinners of Israel. This applies specifically where he does not put them on because the mitzvah is contemptible in his eyes. If he is afraid to put them on because they require a clean body and he fears that he will not preserve their sanctity properly, then although he is committing a transgression, since a person can easily be careful during Krias Shema and prayer, he is nevertheless not included among the sinners of Israel. Certainly, if the mitzvah is always beloved to him and he is ordinarily careful to maintain a clean body, but at present he is prevented by illness because his body is not clean, then according to all opinions he is not considered a sinner of Israel at all. Bach writes that even if the mitzvah is not contemptible in his eyes, but he refrains because of loss of work, another financial loss, or laziness, he is also included among the sinners of Israel with their bodies. There is, however, a difference among these cases regarding punishment. See there. This is an open rebuke to those whose tefillin commonly rest upon their foreheads rather than upon the proper place of the skull because of laziness, and whose arm tefillin likewise are not positioned correctly. Such a person is considered as though he did not put on tefillin at all. Peri Megadim already warned us of this in siman 27, writing that tefillin worn outside their proper place are like tefillin lying in their pouch. See Biur Halachah above in siman 27, where we discussed this at length.
(4) With their bodies. Their law is explicit in Rosh Hashanah 17a. Even if such a person is of intermediate status, concerning whom the Holy One, blessed is He, ordinarily inclines the scale toward kindness, once this sin is included among his transgressions the scale of guilt outweighs the scale of merit and he must descend to Gehinnom. If, Heaven forbid, his sins also exceed his merits, he is judged in Gehinnom for twelve months. Afterward his body is destroyed, his soul is burned, and the wind scatters his ashes beneath the feet of the righteous. Tosafos there explains that all this applies where he did not repent. Because the power of this mitzvah is so great, and conversely its punishment so severe, every person should be careful to purchase tefillin from an expert, God-fearing, and learned scribe. He should likewise purchase straps from a trustworthy person, so that he can be certain they were processed for the sake of the mitzvah from hides of kosher animals. One who wears invalid tefillin not only fails to fulfill the mitzvah, but also recites many blessings in vain, which is a grave sin. Because of our many sins, this is a widespread stumbling block. People purchase tefillin and straps from anyone simply because they are sold cheaply. Most are not square, and many other defects are commonly found in their writing. Every God-fearing person should consider: if he seeks properly made clothing and utensils, all the more so should he not economize or be sparing with money concerning sacred objects. He should seek items known to be kosher even if their price is high. Darkei Moshe writes in the name of Mordechai that one should seek beautiful tefillin, as the verse states, “This is my God and I will beautify Him.” This means that they should be written by an expert scribe with beautiful script, beautiful ink, a fine pen, and fine parchments.
