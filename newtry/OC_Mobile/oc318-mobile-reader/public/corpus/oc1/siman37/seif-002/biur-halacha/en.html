Their mitzvah, etc. — See in Pri Megadim, who is uncertain whether from the Torah one is obligated all day, or from the Torah it suffices for one moment that he lays them and rabbinically all day; and they nullified this now that we do not have a clean body; and he concludes that the essence of the matter is: if he did not lay tefillin even one day at all, he nullified a positive commandment; but when he lays them for a moment he fulfills the mitzvah — yet the optimal mitzvah from the Torah is for them to be on him all day, etc.; see there. In Yeshuot Yaakov he ruled that from the Torah their mitzvah is all day; see there. And see in the book Avnei Nezer, who proves from his words that one who knows he has a clean body should not exempt himself from the mitzvah of tefillin all day. And it is obvious that his intent is if he knows he can guard himself also from distraction — that is, at least from levity and lightheadedness, as we wrote above in the general rule of siman 28; see there. And see in Maaseh Rav, who wrote that if he fears arrogance before people, it is permitted with the hand-tefillin alone; nevertheless it is good to make a small head-tefillin, and the straps should also be covered, rather than going with hand-tefillin alone alone; so far. Fortunate is one who fulfills as required, as our Sages said in Megillah 28: his disciples asked Rabbi Eliezer, "By what did you merit long life?" He said, etc., "and I did not go four cubits without Torah and without tefillin."

At the time of Keriat Shema and prayer — Magen Avraham wrote in a responsum that he established laying them a second time at Mincha; and on erev Shabbat he should not lay them — Shlah.
