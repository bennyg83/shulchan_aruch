(א) To seal a hole in its wall — meaning they wrap around the faucet a piece of garment or flax combings and plug with it the hole in the barrel wall:
(ב) Some permit — to remove it on Shabbat or return it:
(ג) Even though, etc. — that through sealing the faucet in the hole or removing it, beverage absorbed in the combings is squeezed:
(ד) Vessel — for automatically the squeezed wine goes to waste since the hole in the barrel wall is on its side; but if there was a vessel beneath it into which the dripping drop drips, and likewise if the plug was in sealing the hole above so the squeezed wine descends into the barrel — in all this it is a Torah prohibition to tighten or remove the plug even according to this view:
(ה) That psik reisha is forbidden — d'rabbanan, but liability there is none in psik reisha de'lo nicha lei according to all; and all this is regarding Shabbat where we require melacha intent, but regarding other Torah prohibitions Rosh's view is psik reisha de'lo nicha lei forbidden according to all, and its prohibition is from the Torah:
(ו) In something — meaning they are lenient on the measure more than the first view and are lenient even when there is a vessel beneath:
(ז) And one should hesitate about it — for handle-hold of sponge is different, for there is only wiping, unlike here where they tighten the faucet forcefully in the hole — there is certainly squeezing, therefore fit to practice not to place a vessel beneath and let the squeezed go to waste and rely on the first view here; nevertheless there must also be a long faucet for two benefits, end quote Taz, and likewise Ahavat Chesed and Mordechai's statement. And know this entire seif is in a wine barrel as Shulchan Aruch mentioned at the beginning of the seif, and likewise other beverages but not water; and the reason: in all beverages besides water, several early authorities hold there is no whitening concern and squeezing prohibition is only on account of extracting like squeezing olives and grapes, therefore from the Torah for all one requires that he need the beverages that come out similar to extracting, and only d'rabbanan the latter view forbids as I wrote in Biur Halacha; but in a water barrel squeezing prohibition is on account of whitening the plug, for squeezing it is a toldah of whitening and forbidden in every manner; and Taz's view is that red wine is similar to water regarding laundering, and according to his words Shulchan Aruch did not deal here except with red wine that does not whiten and its prohibition is only extracting, therefore we require that he be pleased with what comes out in the squeezing. If beer and other beverage spilled on the tablecloth on the table and he wants to scrape them with a spoon or knife to clean the tablecloth — he should be careful not to scrape forcefully lest he come to squeezing, and only remove the floating beverage from above [and if the beverages are colored he should be careful that other places on the tablecloth not become dyed through his scraping, as in seif 20]; and all the more if water spilled on the tablecloth — certainly he should be very careful in this, for squeezing water is a toldah of whitening as above. One who squeezes hair or hide is exempt but forbidden [Rambam], and therefore one who immerses on Shabbat should be careful not to squeeze his hair to dry them:
