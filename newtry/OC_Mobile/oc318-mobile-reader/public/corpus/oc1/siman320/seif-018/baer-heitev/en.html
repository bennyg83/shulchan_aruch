(א) Forbidden. But there is no liability according to all. Rosh wrote in chapter 14 of Shabbat: even for the Aruch who holds that psik reisha that is not pleasing to him is permitted — nevertheless in other prohibitions he admits it is forbidden; see Magen Avraham. And see in Berachat HaZevach:

(ב) The barrel. And even for one who says psik reisha that is not pleasing to him is forbidden — nevertheless here permitted because squeezing liquids is a toldah of dosh and it is forbidden only when the liquids that emerge do not go to waste. Poskim. And Taz wrote: nevertheless there must also be a long spigot so that there are two reasons for leniency; but nevertheless we see our practice per the first view that they squeeze salad greens on Shabbat — and therefore because what is squeezed goes to waste. Ran and Shach explained that there are two types of squeezing: one due to being a toldah of laundering, namely whitening — therefore the prohibition is only in water but not in other beverages; and for halacha Tur and Shulchan Aruch ruled in siman 319 that wine is like other beverages; only here Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Ran that wine launders. And it appears to me to distinguish that white wine launders like water, but red wine does not launder — on the contrary it reddens the garment. And further, squeezing is also due to being a toldah of dosh and threshing, and the prohibition is only when it is pleasing to him in what emerges from it, like in dosh and threshing; except per the other view that here it is forbidden in any case d'rabbanan even when not pleasing to him; and therefore if beer or other beverages that do not launder spilled on the tablecloth on the table and he wishes to clean it — in any case he should not draw up from there into one palm and pour outside, for even though he will throw it to waste — nevertheless here at the time he draws into the palm he commits a full prohibition and it is a toldah of threshing; and in this all agree to the prohibition, and it will not help that he afterward pours the palm outside — this is crooked and cannot be corrected; only with a knife there is permission to push off from the tablecloth and scrape by means of the beverage that spilled on the tablecloth and let it flow to waste — only he should not scrape with force, for otherwise he commits a d'rabbanan prohibition per the other view; and one must be careful in what he can be careful.
