(א) There are those who permit — Brief explanation of the matter: for it is known the view of Rabbenu Tam and his supporters that there are two matters of squeezing — namely squeezing from water, its prohibition is due to libun and not due to dosh, for water goes to waste and is not comparable to dosh; and in other beverages it is the reverse: regarding libun there is none, for they do not launder in them; rather the prohibition is due to dosh, comparable to squeezing olives and grapes; only specifically when he needs the squeezed liquid, comparable to threshing; and therefore here in this matter that the barrel is of wine — its prohibition is only due to dosh; and automatically when the squeezed liquid goes to waste it is not in the category of dosh at all. And this is the view of many of the Rishonim [Rashba, Rivash, and the Rav HaMagid]; however the view of R' Yehuda and several others with him that nevertheless it is forbidden d'rabbanan. And all this would apply if he intended to squeeze; but in truth he does not intend to squeeze and it is only psik reisha that is not pleasing to him in that squeezing, for it goes to waste by Torah law — certainly he is not liable in any matters of Shabbat in psik reisha that is not pleasing to him, as we say Shabbat 103; and the Aruch disagrees with the other poskim regarding d'rabbanan prohibition — for the Aruch permits entirely in psik reisha that is not pleasing to him; and for the other poskim — forbidden. And behold from Shulchan Aruch's words it appears he agrees with the other poskim that psik reisha that is not pleasing to him is forbidden d'rabbanan; and nevertheless he concluded at the end of the seif "good to practice according to them," etc., and left them on their view to be lenient in this matter because the spigot is long, see there; so too Taz; and Magen Avraham wrote because in our matter there is another leniency that the liquid goes to waste and is not comparable to dosh, as stated. And know that all the words of this seif are only for the poskim who hold other beverages do not launder; but for the poskim who make no distinction between water and other beverages — and in particular wine, for Ran's reasoning that it is comparable to water regarding laundering, as Beit Yosef wrote [and see Taz who agrees with Ran regarding white wine] — closing the spigot would be forbidden by Torah law due to the prohibition of libun, for the spigot is whitened thereby [and what Ran wrote that there is no manner of laundering in a spigot — for this reason the early authorities permitted; they did not mention this reasoning, only that they wrote due to no libun in other beverages, see in Bahag]; and there is no distinction whether the squeezed liquid goes to waste or not; and this matter is not applicable at all to psik reisha that is not pleasing to him — for this applies only if we say his liability is due to dosh. And what Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 21 that there is no liability according to all — this is regarding psik reisha that is not pleasing to him; but in this matter itself it is as we wrote:

(ב) That is not pleasing to him — Behold in our matter he is not at all pleased with the squeezing; on the contrary he would wish it not squeeze, for it goes to waste; but in truth even only where he does not benefit at all from the action done through him and he has no interest in it — it is also called psik reisha that is not pleasing to him, since he does not intend for the action — so Tosafos proved in Shabbat 75 regarding that which Shabbat 103 states in the Gemara that he acts on another's land:

(ג) Good to practice according to them, etc. — This is with the combination of the reasoning of a long spigot, as I wrote in Mishna Berurah; for otherwise Shulchan Aruch's ruling at the beginning of the seif is not like the Aruch. Taz wrote: if beer or other beverages that do not launder spilled on the tablecloth on the table and he wishes to clean it — in any case he should not draw up from there into one palm and pour outside, for even though he will throw it to waste — nevertheless here at the time he draws into the palm he commits a full prohibition and it is a toldah of threshing [meaning there is a Torah prohibition here]; and in this all agree to the prohibition, and it will not help that he afterward pours the palm outside — this is crooked and cannot be corrected; only with a knife there is permission to push off from the tablecloth and scrape by means of the beverage that spilled on the tablecloth and let it flow to waste; and good [in the Taz before us it only states "and therefore," and Acharonim wondered at him; but I saw in Sefer Tosefet Shabbat and Sefer Shulchan Atzei Shatim who copied Taz's words — it appears it must read "and good," meaning that according to Taz's view with a knife one may be lenient even when scraping with force, for he holds the custom is like the Aruch; and in this manner forbidden with the palm] that he should not scrape with force, for otherwise he commits a d'rabbanan prohibition at least per the other view; and one must be careful in what he can be careful, end of Taz's words; and Acharonim disagree and hold there is no distinction between palm and knife — that even with the palm, even when taking with force, there is no Torah prohibition since his intent is to throw it outside and he does not squeeze for the sake of the beverage; therefore there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition per the other view, like scraping with a knife; and so it appears in Peri Megadim that there is no Torah prohibition here; however l'chatchila certainly one must be very careful not to scrape with force even with a knife, and all the more so with the palm — for we rule like the other view; rather he should remove the floating liquid from above without difficulty — and in this manner even with the palm permitted according to all. Further Taz wrote that if water or white wine spilled on the tablecloth — forbidden to scrape even with a knife, for it is laundering; and his intent is when he scrapes with force, for then certainly he comes to squeezing the tablecloth, which is a toldah of laundering, and there is a Torah prohibition here due to libun of the tablecloth [so it appears in Sefer Tosefet Shabbat and Sefer Shulchan Aruch in explanation of Taz's words]; and in Peri Megadim he is uncertain about Taz's intent — it is possible he holds that in water, even without force, forbidden to scrape, for at the time of scraping when he wishes to pour it to the ground it becomes moist on the tablecloth in the place that was dry initially, and its dissolution is its laundering, see there; and the second reason he wrote there is very strained; nevertheless it appears that in a time of need one should not be stringent without force, see above in siman 302 seif 9 in the gloss and what is written there in our explanation that the view of most poskim is that in a garment that has no soiling we do not say its dissolution is its laundering; and also that he does not intend to launder, see there:
