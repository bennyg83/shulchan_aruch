(א) (s.k. 20) Psik reisha, etc., Rosh wrote, etc., nevertheless in other prohibitions he concedes forbidden, for specifically on Shabbat permitted because on Shabbat we require melacha intent, therefore where it is not pleasing to him it is not melacha intent, unlike other prohibitions. From what they challenge in Zevachim daf 91b on this that Shmuel said one who vows wine shall sprinkle from a sprinkler on the altar fire on the altar, and they challenge and behold it is written it shall not go out, and through sprinkling he extinguishes, and they answer partial extinguishing is not called extinguishing, or extinguishing of a mitzvah is different, and they challenge and the baraita teaches wine according to R' Akiva for basins, etc., another baraita teaches wine of libation for basins or is it only for coals, it says it shall not go out, and they answer there is a dispute — this R' Yehuda who holds davar she'eino mitkaven forbidden, this R' Shimon who holds davar she'eino mitkaven permitted — now the Rosh inferred from the first questioner who challenged and behold he extinguishes, and if it were not established that it is davar she'eino mitkaven, and if still forbidden because it is psik reisha — nevertheless it is psik reisha de'lo nicha lei, or in other prohibitions even psik reisha de'lo nicha lei forbidden, for they conclude for R' Shimon permitted that it is davar she'eino mitkaven, and perforce one must say that also in other prohibitions and psik reisha de'lo nicha lei permitted. And also in chapter HaBoneh the Rosh brings proof from this, etc. — that is for the Gemara's conclusion that establishes it like R' Shimon that davar she'eino mitkaven permitted, and even though it is psik reisha, perforce like the Ikkur where it is not pleasing permitted, behold perforce even in other prohibitions the Ikkur holds permitted, for he brings proof from other prohibitions. See there what he answered and wrote two answers, and the answer that seemed to him primary is from the answerer's words there is difficulty for the Rosh, for the answerer's intent in what remains in the first answer of R' Shimon that it is like partial extinguishing or extinguishing of a mitzvah — this permission alone does not suffice except combined with R' Shimon's view that davar she'eino mitkaven permitted, and even though it is psik reisha nevertheless not pleasing, and even though in other prohibitions even when not pleasing forbidden — nevertheless the prohibition is lighter and the reasoning of partial extinguishing or extinguishing of a mitzvah helps to permit, unlike for R' Yehuda who holds davar she'eino mitkaven forbidden — the reasoning of partial extinguishing or extinguishing of a mitzvah alone does not help to permit; but the questioner who did not establish in his mind partial extinguishing or extinguishing of a mitzvah is different challenges and behold he extinguishes, even though certainly it was established in his mind that one can say Shmuel holds like R' Shimon that davar she'eino mitkaven permitted — nevertheless there is difficulty for him that it is psik reisha, and even though not pleasing nevertheless in other prohibitions forbidden, except combined with the reasoning of partial extinguishing or extinguishing of a mitzvah, and this still was not established in his mind until now. And in my opinion he answered nothing, for the Rosh brings proof, etc., that on Shabbat psik reisha de'lo nicha lei permitted from the answerer's words as above, and according to Beit HaZahav on the contrary it implies there forbidden except combined with reasoning of partial extinguishing or extinguishing of a mitzvah, or he does not hold like Beit HaZahav and does not hold to distinguish in chapter HaBoneh between Shabbat and other prohibitions — and the difficulty returns. As Rambam wrote, etc., and as is evident in Menachos — meaning the Rosh comes to prove the Ikkur concedes in other prohibitions, his proof is according to Rambam, for who will tell us that Rambam holds like the Ikkur or the Ikkur like Rambam; therefore Magen Avraham wrote Rambam's words who ruled not like Shmuel are compelled in the Gemara in Menachos, and if so certainly also the Ikkur holds here like Rambam that we do not rule like Shmuel, and the proof from Menachos Kesef Mishneh wrote there chapter 16 of Moed Katan from the anonymous Mishnah in Menachos daf 74b that taught in this the altar's strength is greater, etc., they reject from Shmuel, and as the Gemara says there. And therefore he properly brings proof from the questioner, for we rule like the questioner, and that he brings proof in chapter HaBoneh from the Gemara's conclusion even though we do not rule like him — one must say it is known that one should minimize dispute between Amoraim, and since it appeared to Shmuel that even in other prohibitions he holds psik reisha de'lo nicha lei permitted — therefore we do not rule like him in other prohibitions as is evident from the anonymous Mishnah in Menachos above, nevertheless to minimize dispute one can say that at least on Shabbat the Mishnah also concedes because melacha intent is required; and per the poskim who disagree with the Ikkur and as the Rosh wrote in chapter HaBoneh one can reject his proof from this Shmuel that one can say there is no psik reisha, for one can sprinkle in small drops that do not extinguish, and even if he sprinkles large drops — still it is not psik reisha, and likewise Rashi in Zevachim:
