(א) And grapes. For a Torah-level prohibition one is liable only for treading olives and grapes alone — Rambam; and see siman 67:

(ב) To squeeze them. See Taz, who raised that which other fruits are permitted to squeeze — namely in the case where we say in the Gemara without specification that he intends to sweeten; and even though he benefits from what flows from the liquid — nevertheless his primary intent at the time of squeezing was only for the sweetening produced through the squeezing, which is the manner of squeezing other fruits that are not designated for liquids; but when he intends to extract liquid from them for the need of the beverage and reveals his intent that he squeezes for the sake of the liquids that emerge — certainly forbidden according to his words even in other fruits, end of his words:

(ג) To squeeze. See Magen Avraham, who raised that specifically in a matter where if the entire world had such abundant fruits they would squeeze them; but otherwise even in one place we say their intent is nullified; and if so, these pears that they call pears in a foreign language — forbidden to squeeze in Ashkenaz countries because they practice squeezing them for beverage; and it is possible that if the entire world had an abundance they would also squeeze. And Bach wrote that it is forbidden to squeeze except to sweeten the fruit, but for their water forbidden — as is written siman 67. And so too Semak, and so the world practices, end of his words; and Magen Avraham wrote: certainly where it is customary it is customary; nevertheless it appears to me that where he does not squeeze it except to dip food in it — permitted, for it is not called a beverage — as Beit Yosef wrote, see there:

(ד) Things. Such as one who puts meat in gravy or bread in wine and sucks it:

(ה) They forbid. Even in other things — Beit Yosef, Hagahot Maimoniyot, Ra'avad; and Bach wrote in the name of Rashal that in a beverage absorbed in bread or meat, since there was beverage upon it beforehand — it is close to a chatas; and therefore forbidden to suck it; but to suck grapes in his mouth and throw away the pits — this is not separating but thus is the manner of eating grapes and permitted by law; nevertheless one who is stringent — praiseworthy, end of his words. And nevertheless from Rama's words it appears that other things are more lenient than grapes, as stated above; and it is possible that since their manner of eating is thus — permitted — Magen Avraham, see there. And Taz wrote: in my humble opinion this matter is similar to the law written in siman 319 seif 16 — namely the view of Rosh that one may drink through a cloth, etc.; just as there there is no prohibition of borer in what he does with his mouth, so too here there is no prohibition of dosh in what he sucks with his mouth; what difference between the melacha of borer and the melacha of dosh? And since he already ruled in siman 319 to be lenient like Rosh — Rama should not have brought here the view of those who are stringent; also we have not seen anyone careful in these matters. Only in this one must be careful not to squeeze meat absorbed in gravy for the need of the beverage — as stated above regarding squeezing other fruits, see s.k. 2, end of his words (and in Eliyah Rabbah he disagrees with Taz and ruled to forbid regarding grapes and to be stringent for one who fears Heaven; but in other fruits and likewise meat and bread — one should not be stringent as Yere'im himself permitted, see there):
