[1] And all other fruits, etc. Bach writes that common practice is to prohibit squeezing them, and Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah writes likewise, unless one squeezes them merely to sweeten or soften the fruit itself.
[2] But to suck, etc. Beit Yosef cites Shibbolei HaLeket: "but sucking with the mouth is initially prohibited." Taz thinks this is a scribal error. He did not examine carefully, for it refers to sucking milk directly from an animal, as stated in siman 328, seif 33, where it is prohibited because this is the normal manner.
[3] And some prohibit, etc. Taz rules like the first, lenient view and compares this to siman 319, seif 16, where one drinks through a cloth. One can distinguish between selecting and squeezing. See siman 321, seif 12. Taz also writes that no one is seen to be careful about this, implying he is lenient even with sucking broth from meat or wine from soaked bread. This differs from Maharshal and Bach, who distinguish those cases. All the authorities make clear that grapes are more stringent, and a later authority should not invent a distinction against the major early authorities. Sefer Yere'im, p. 38, in the name of Rabbi Binyamin likewise appears to permit bread and meat while prohibiting grapes. Beit Yosef in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket appears to permit even grapes for a God fearing person. In any event, for all other fruits, and for meat and bread as above, there is no reason to be stringent, as I found explicitly in Sefer Yere'im, which is the source of the law.
[4] [Levush] It is permitted to squeeze them, etc. Some prohibit, and likewise prohibit squeezing apples (Malbushei Yom Tov). Riaz in Shiltei Gibborim, chapter Chaviyot, prohibits even squeezing fruit merely to dip food into the juice. Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman writes that pears called birnen in Ashkenaz may not be squeezed because in Ashkenaz the custom is to squeeze them for juice, and perhaps if they were plentiful everywhere people elsewhere would also squeeze them. It appears to me that squeezing lemons or apples directly into another beverage or into food is plainly permitted according to all. Even not into food or drink, lemons may be permitted because Rabbenu Yerucham writes that this is the custom, and Tanya and Shibbolei HaLeket appear to imply that apples are worse and prohibited even according to those who permit lemons. See Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. If an apple is cooked, it may be squeezed because one is extracting all of the food substance itself (Taz). Salted preserved lemons have the law of pickled and cooked vegetables in seif 7.
[5] [Levush] It is not recognizable, etc. Therefore one may place grapes into wine so that they burst and release their juice, just as with snow in seif 9 (Bach; Magen Avraham). Taz writes this is entirely prohibited because squeezing is a derivative of threshing and is forbidden even with an unusual method. There is no difficulty, because here it is not even an unusual squeezing act; the juice comes out by itself. Taz's further objection that juice flowing by itself is also prohibited as a derivative of threshing is likewise no difficulty, because here it becomes nullified in the preexisting wine. Moreover, liquid that emerges by itself is prohibited only by rabbinic decree. See the beginning of this siman.
[6] Into the pot, etc. But squeezing into beverages is prohibited, as seif 6 also implies. This requires study from the end of seif 1, which permits squeezing into another beverage based on Beit Yosef in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket. One must say lemons are different.
[7] And Rabbenu Tam prohibits, etc. One who relies on the first view loses nothing (Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah). Magen Avraham writes that according to siman 202, where we are not expert in determining what is called unripe fruit, Levush therefore says that so long as it is unfit to eat, squeezing its liquid is prohibited. The same applies to other fruits not fit to eat.
