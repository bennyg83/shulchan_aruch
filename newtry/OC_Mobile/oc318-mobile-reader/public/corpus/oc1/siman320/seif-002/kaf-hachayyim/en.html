15) [Seif 2] Olives and grapes that were crushed before Shabbos, etc. This means even if he placed them there for drinking, and certainly applies to mulberries and pomegranates. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 10. Bach writes that they must have been thoroughly crushed and require only grinding; if they still require pressing, the liquid that emerges is forbidden. Olas Shabbos there writes the same. This follows Tur, who writes that they were thoroughly crushed. According to Shulchan Aruch, however, they are permitted even if they still require pressing, as stated above in siman 252, paragraph 59, where the distinction between Tur and Shulchan Aruch is explained; see there. Bach's question why Shulchan Aruch omitted the word 'thoroughly' is therefore unfounded, since Shulchan Aruch follows his own position stated in siman 252. Nevertheless, one should initially be stringent like Tur.
16) There: That were crushed before Shabbos, etc. There is no longer reason to decree lest one squeeze them by hand, because even if he does so, he incurs no liability once they have already been crushed and the liquid emerges on its own. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 7. See above, siman 252, seif 5, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
17) There: That were crushed before Shabbos, etc. Regarding whether one may place raisins in water and prepare sherbet on Shabbos itself, we wrote above in siman 252, paragraph 57, that the matter is disputed and that one should be stringent and soak them before Shabbos; see there. Regarding straining, see above, siman 319, and our words there in paragraph 108.
18) There: It becomes nullified in the wine in the vat. This does not resemble an item that will later become permitted, which could be left until after Shabbos and is not nullified even in a thousand. That rule applies when the forbidden substance first exists distinctly and afterward becomes mixed with permitted food. Here the liquid was never recognizable; as each small amount emerges, it mixes with the liquid that emerged the previous day. Beis Yosef in the name of Mordechai; Taz, paragraph 3; Magen Avraham, paragraph 5. Accordingly, if the grapes lie separately and the wine flows down an incline into wine already present before Shabbos, all the wine may not be used on Shabbos because it is an item that will later become permitted. Before mixing, the newly emerged wine existed distinctly and was recognizable. Sefer HaTerumah, siman 223.
19) There: It becomes nullified in the wine in the vat. It appears that even if whole grapes were placed in wine on Shabbos itself and split that Shabbos, the wine is permitted, just as one may place snow or hail in a cup or bowl on Shabbos below in seif 9. Bach. This implies that placing them there initially is permitted, like snow and hail in seif 9, and Magen Avraham there writes the same. Taz there, however, disagrees with Bach and writes that this is absolutely forbidden, because squeezing is a derivative of threshing and is forbidden even when performed unusually. The permission applies only after the fact; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4, rules the same. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 8, however, challenge Taz's words; see there. Chayei Adam, klal 14, likewise rules that it is permitted initially.
20) There: It becomes nullified in the wine in the vat. This implies that sixty times the forbidden liquid is required, in accordance with the law of a liquid mixed with the same type of liquid. Even for a rabbinic prohibition, sixty is required, and we do not say that each first portion becomes nullified as it enters. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3. Since we do not know how much wine will emerge from the grapes, the wine in the vat must amount to sixty times all the grapes, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 98, seif 4. Otherwise, it may not be drunk on Shabbos.
