8) There: All other fruits may be squeezed. Beis Yosef cites a dispute. Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur imply that all other fruits may initially be squeezed even for their juice. Rashi, Tosafos, and Hagahos Semak permit squeezing only to sweeten the fruit, but forbid squeezing for its juice; see there. The Shulchan Aruch's unqualified words imply that squeezing even for the juice is permitted. Bach, however, writes that one should not rule permissively concerning squeezing any fruit for its juice, contrary to the unqualified ruling in Shulchan Aruch and the gloss that follows it. He writes that the universal practice is to forbid squeezing every fruit for its juice. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, cites him and writes that where this practice exists it certainly remains binding. Nevertheless, when one squeezes fruit only to dip food into the juice, it is permitted because the juice is not considered a beverage, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Shibolei HaLeket and Rosh; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, at the end of paragraph 1, likewise writes that the first view is primary, but where the stringent practice exists one should not deviate. Taz, paragraph 1, distinguishes that even according to the Shulchan Aruch, who permits squeezing other fruits, this applies only without a specific intention, whereas squeezing them specifically to extract juice is forbidden. Chemed Moshe, Nehar Shalom, and Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, have already challenged this distinction; see there. In practice, although the first view, that of the Shulchan Aruch and those who agree, is primary and only a place with a stringent practice must preserve it, one should initially account for the prohibiting view. Chayei Adam, klal 14, paragraph 3; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Yisro, paragraph 3.
