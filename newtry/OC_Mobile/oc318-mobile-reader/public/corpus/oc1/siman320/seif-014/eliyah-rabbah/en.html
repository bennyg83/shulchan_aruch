[14] [Levush] Rosh prohibits, etc. Maharshal and Bach infer from siman 326, which prohibits warming one's hands after washing even though he does not intend to warm the water. Taz rejects their proof and permits because it is impossible to avoid, since the whole ground is covered with snow. In my humble opinion there is no proof in any case, because there the water reaches yad soledes bo, creating a cooking issue, and it also resembles washing the hands. Shiltei Gibborim at the end of chapter Bameh Tomnin explicitly indicates that whoever permits melting in the sun permits this too. Therefore one may be lenient unless it can be avoided without effort. Responsa Rashba, siman 623, permits urinating on vegetation because the urine burns the plants. See siman 336.
[15] [Levush] Because the freezing, etc. This implies it is permitted even in a river or well, contrary to Magen Avraham.
[16] With salt, etc. See siman 326, seif 6. One may rub his hands with lemon peel or with the lemon itself once it was already squeezed into food. But cutting a lemon and rubbing his hands with it without first squeezing it into food is prohibited (Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah).
[17] [Levush] And see above siman, etc. Meaning that according to those who permit there, salt is likewise permitted because there is no nolad, as Shiltei Gibborim also implies. This requires study, because here one directly crushes it by hand, like crushing snow in seif 9, which all prohibit. According to Ramban there, however, since it is food, even direct crushing is permitted. Taz writes that one may pour water onto salt and afterward wash his hands in it, as stated regarding snow and hail, provided the resulting salt water is not prohibited under siman 321. See siman 318, seif 9.
