[18] A sponge, etc. It is known as a wool-like substance formed at the seashore that absorbs water (Rambam, commentary to the Mishnah). Aruch explains that it is placed over the eye of a great sea creature so it will not see the world and destroy it.
[19] A decree, etc. This requires study. Since a sponge is made for this purpose, why must we decree lest one squeeze it, as explained in seif 15? Furthermore, the Gemara on Shabbos 143a says squeezing is an inevitable result, so the reason is not merely a decree (Magen Avraham). The first question is no difficulty, because although made for this purpose and one does not need the squeezed liquid, the sponge is nevertheless squeezed between the fingers. Magen Avraham himself writes similarly in siman 302, seif katan 27. As to the second question, one can say the Gemara discusses a wet sponge, whereas with a dry sponge used to wipe liquid, squeezing is not inevitable. This enables me to preserve the Mishnah reading, "the Sages say, in either case one may wipe," which Tosafot rejected because of inevitable squeezing. According to my distinction it is understandable. One cannot ask why Taz then prohibits a dry sponge when squeezing is not inevitable. The answer is that the Sages did not distinguish among sponges used for wiping, and Taz does not accept the alternative Mishnah reading. See siman 302, seif katan 22. More plausibly, Rambam, chapter 22, first states the sponge law and then says, "one may not cover a barrel of water, a decree, etc." Tur copied the expression "a decree" by force of the surrounding wording and applied it also to the sponge.
