(א) Since it is not fit — At first glance, behold in siman 202 he ruled that one blesses on unripe grapes, etc.; and every thing that is not fit for eating — one does not bless on it; also in Okatzin chapter 6 Mishnah 3 R' Akiva holds it receives tum'at okhalin; and it is possible he did not mean specifically that it is not fit for eating at all, but through difficulty; and therefore it is called refuse relative to what he squeezes; and if so it is d'rabbanan [Chamad Moshe]:

(ב) To eat — See in Mishna Berurah s.k. 20 what we wrote that if his intent is to eat immediately — the view of Taz and Elyah Rabbah to be lenient for the reason that in borer too we are lenient for immediate eating when it is food from refuse; and see in Peri Megadim who hinted that in Tosafos it states the reason regarding milking is because dosh takes food from refuse; and in our matter too the animal is like refuse and when he takes food from it it is considered dosh; and so too regarding unripe grapes; and according to this the leniency is entirely concealed, for in dosh there is no difference between immediate and later; and see in Sefer Chamad Moshe who sided that the intent of Ran and Tur who wrote like borer — food from refuse — is also because for this reason it is considered dosh, as Tosafos wrote; only they abbreviated in their language [afterward I found in Nishmat Adam that he also wrote thus]; and it appears this is the truth, for I saw in Or Zarua who copies Rabbenu Tam's language at length — he also writes there explicitly that it is like dosh that separates food from refuse, see there; and why should we say Tur argues on this and writes in the name of Rabbenu Tam in another matter? And apart from this, in Milchamot he also wrote that the view of the Geonim who forbid milking on Shabbat into a pot is because it is considered dosh, see there; therefore certainly proper to be stringent like the view of Magen Avraham even if we hold there is no concern of borer here. However, if the unripe grapes are fit for eating through difficulty — for borer there is only a d'rabbanan prohibition, and as above in siman 319; and dosh too appears not so applicable in this manner d'oraisa, for it is not comparable to separating from straw — it is reasonable there is room to rely to be lenient in a time of need like the view of Taz and Elyah Rabbah:
