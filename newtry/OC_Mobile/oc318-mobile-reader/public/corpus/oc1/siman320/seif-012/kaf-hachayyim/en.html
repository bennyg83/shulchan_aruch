Beis Yosef writes in the name of Shibolei HaLeket that sucking meat or bread, or sucking juice from fruits or grapes with one's mouth, does not resemble squeezing at all. In the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 21, however, he writes that one should take care not to squeeze meat that has absorbed broth for the purpose of obtaining the liquid. One who places meat in broth, or soaks bread in wine, brings it to his mouth, and sucks out the liquid acts impermissibly, and there is concern that he may be liable to bring a sin offering. Certainly one should not suck grapes or similar fruit with his mouth. These are its words. Rema therefore writes in the gloss that some forbid sucking with the mouth. Maran's view in Beis Yosef appears to follow those who permit, as Birkei Yosef writes in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1. Bach writes in the name of Maharshal that a distinction should be made. Broth absorbed in meat or wine absorbed in bread already possessed beverage status, so sucking it is close to incurring liability to bring a sin offering and is forbidden. Liquid that emerges from grapes before it possessed beverage status, however, such as when one sucks grapes in his mouth and discards the seeds, does not constitute extracting. This is the ordinary manner of eating grapes and is legally permitted, although one who is stringent will receive a blessing. Bach writes that this is the accepted ruling: meat and bread are forbidden, while grapes are permitted. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, however, writes that Rema implies that other things are more lenient than grapes and that Rashi's commentary also implies this; see there. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 9, likewise is stringent specifically regarding olives and grapes, and this is the view of Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; see there. Since this matter is disputed, one should take care not to suck meat or bread that absorbed liquid, nor olives or grapes. Other fruits appear subject to leniency.
