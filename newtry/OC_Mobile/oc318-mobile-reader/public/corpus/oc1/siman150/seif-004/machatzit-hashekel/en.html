Seif katan 4: One does not, etc. It should read: See Choshen Mishpat, siman 154, seif 32. This refers to where they acquired rights to the windows from ownerless property, meaning they opened the windows toward a place that was then ownerless and someone later acquired it. If they opened toward an individual’s courtyard, whether a prescriptive right is effective for visual intrusion and whether three years are required depends on the dispute among the poskim explained there, seif 7 and elsewhere.
Rama wrote in a responsum, etc. “But if the congregation,” etc., “he may object.” Rama concludes there: “Nevertheless, I say that if opening the windows does not cause that individual great harm, he should not insist upon the strict law, but should go beyond the letter of the law to honor our God and beautify His house. One who is not concerned for his Creator’s honor,” etc. “They may compel him to go beyond the letter of the law, as Mordechai writes in HaMafkid. Although some poskim disagree, everything depends on the degree of harm and the judge’s assessment.”
Otherwise, there is concern that he will claim, etc. and object to construction. This does not mean that his claim would necessarily prevail, for in Choshen Mishpat we rule that since the opening caused him no harm when it was made, no prescriptive right is established, unlike Rabbeinu Yonah and Rivash cited by Rama and Sema there. Nevertheless, the congregation may object lest he later claim a prescriptive right and force them into litigation. Even though one may object and no right is established, if he did not object the owner may say: “Since you did not harm me then, I did not object.” See there. This is likewise Magen Avraham’s meaning here when he writes that the individual may object to construction and force them into litigation. This resolves Eliyah Rabbah’s challenge from the end of siman 149, where communal property cannot be acquired by possession, unlike Magen Avraham. There is no contradiction, for although one cannot establish possession against communal property, he may still force the congregation into litigation, which they wish to avoid.
