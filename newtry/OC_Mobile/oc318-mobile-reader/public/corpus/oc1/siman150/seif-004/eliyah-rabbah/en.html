[4] Opposite a window, etc. Sefer Chassidim, siman 816, relates an incident in which a man’s children died because of this, and another involving one who had marital relations near a synagogue. Knesses HaGedolah writes in the name of Maharam, siman 554, that a distance of eight amos from a synagogue suffices. Responsa Rama, siman 32, states that the congregation may not object to one who opens windows from his house toward the synagogue courtyard, since many people commonly pass through it and it is not used for private matters. If, however, it is sometimes used for private activities, even by many people, they may object. If the congregation wishes to open synagogue windows toward his courtyard, he may object. If the harm to Reuven is not great, he should go beyond the letter of the law, and he may be compelled to do so. Everything depends on the extent of the harm and the judge’s assessment. See Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 150, concerning one who establishes possession against communal property, and siman 154, seif 16 in the gloss, unlike Magen Avraham. Examine carefully.
