Fortresses and towers. Ran reads: “fortresses and towers constructed for strength.” Aruch likewise explains karkushei and avarurei as fortifications. According to this, there is no source to permit a sloped roof that is not usable on top, because the Gemara permitted only a fortress with no dwelling above at all, constructed solely to strengthen the city. This is also somewhat implied by Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah. See also Meiri, who writes similarly.
It may not be taller than the synagogue. This does not mean that it is forbidden only when the body of the house, excluding the roof, is taller than the synagogue. Rather, even if the house itself is not taller, but together with the lower portion of the roof, where one can use the upper story while standing there, it rises above the synagogue, it is forbidden. The slope does not lessen this, since the place can in any event be used, and that area beneath the roof is considered part of the upper-story walls. This is evident from the Rosh there.
