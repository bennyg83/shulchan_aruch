[2] And to purchase for them a Torah, etc. Nowadays they are also obligated to purchase a Shas so that children and adults may study from it. Magen Avraham. However, Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 163 writes that since books are readily available among us, the custom is not to compel purchases other than a Sefer Torah. Nevertheless, to prevent neglect of Torah study, beis din may compel the city’s residents to lend books for study. In siman 292 Rama writes that borrowers must pay for any damage to the books. Reuven has books to sell, and his brother Shimon wishes to purchase them, but Shimon does not lend his books, while another purchaser does lend them. It is preferable to sell to the non-Jew. Sefer Chassidim, siman 927.
