In that city. See Mishnah Berurah and what I wrote in the name of Magen Avraham. I did not copy the words of Perishah and Peri Megadim, who question the basic law, because no one explicitly disputes Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Tosafos. On the contrary, Beit Yosef and the lengthy Darchei Moshe clearly imply that they rule accordingly. Meiri in Megillah, chapter HaKorei Omed, likewise states this explicitly, and Rabbeinu Yonah in the first chapter of Berachos explicitly writes according to Rashi and Rabbeinu Yonah that in Babylonia synagogue entrances were on the west and worshippers bowed eastward, as in our lands. Chayei Adam also records Magen Avraham’s ruling as practical halachah. Know that regarding the essential law of the Tosefta, Meiri writes that concern applies only to the entrance through which most of the congregation enters; other entrances may be made on any side. Know further that the Shulchan Aruch’s law applies only to the synagogue entrance itself. There is no concern regarding the entrance to the vestibule, as Chasam Sofer writes in a responsum, and this is obvious.
Which is on the side, etc. See Mishnah Berurah. This law has a source in the Tosefta, as explained in Biur HaGra. It requires study in places where, because of crowding, it is absolutely impossible to place the aron on the eastern wall and they are forced to place it on the north or south: toward which direction should the congregation pray? Should they face east because that is toward Jerusalem, or is it preferable to face the sacred area, namely the aron?
In the middle of the synagogue. This is explicit in Rambam and Tur, and it is an ancient custom. Its source is Sukkah 51b, as the Gra writes. Regrettably, in some places people breached this ancient custom and began placing the bimah close to the aron because they wished to follow the ways of non-Jews, as is done in their temples. Of them one may say, “Israel forgot its Maker and built temples.” The later poskim have already written at length condemning these people. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 28; Responsa Macholas HaMachanayim; and Responsa Imrei Eish.
