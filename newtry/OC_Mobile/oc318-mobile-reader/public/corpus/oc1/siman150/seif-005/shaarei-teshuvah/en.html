And see in responsa Noda B'Yehuda Even HaEzer siman 16 regarding one who bought a house and courtyard from a gentile and windows in the synagogue open to that courtyard and wants to build near the synagogue — must distance at least more than four and not more than eight; and the difference between four and eight the city's sages decide per what their eyes see as the need of the synagogue; and regarding the chazakah that the congregation held — they negotiated per the matter; and he concluded that the one who wants to build is called "motzi" as Semag wrote, and therefore the law is with the congregation. And see in Be'er Heitev thus in the name of Mahari Zayin; and he wrote there this is when it is clear it was already dedicated to the synagogue; but in a house in a courtyard that they held to pray, and in the meantime Reuven bought the entire courtyard, and after many years they want to build a synagogue opposite the windows and he claims it is his purchase and they claim they held it and it was holy — the law is with Reuven until individuals bring proof it was dedicated to the synagogue — see there; and it appears his reason is because of chazakah with which there is no claim — and I have doubt on this.
