Seif katan 1: And Writings, etc. “So that they may read from them,” etc. This is the wording of Shulchan Aruch, Choshen Mishpat, there. Otherwise, while a Sefer Torah is needed for public reading and Prophets may be needed for reading the haftarah, we find no haftarah from Writings. For what purpose would they purchase them, other than so that anyone who wishes may read and study from them?
See Yoreh Deah, end of siman 270, where this is explained regarding the mitzvah of writing a Sefer Torah. Nowadays, when the Oral Torah may be written, the primary mitzvah is to write and study Mishnah, Gemara, and poskim. See Shach and Taz there. Sema, siman 163, however, somewhat implies the opposite of Magen Avraham. He writes: “Perishah states that nowadays, meaning since printing became available and books are common among us, the custom is not to compel purchase of books other than a Sefer Torah. Nevertheless, to prevent neglect of Torah study, beis din may compel residents of a city to lend books for study,” etc. Maharam cites this below, at the end of siman 292, and Rama concludes there: “Provided they compensate him for damage to the books.”
