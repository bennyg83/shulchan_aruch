To build, etc. See Birkei Yosef, who cites an opinion that a synagogue should not be built by non-Jews. In particular, when making a dome-like structure before the heichal for ornamentation, one should certainly be careful that it be done by Jews. Nevertheless, there is no concern in having non-Jews repair the synagogue roof.
And to purchase for them Tanach. See Mishnah Berurah. It is obvious that the same applies if the community has no place for public Torah study, whether because the synagogue is too cramped for this or for another reason: the residents may compel one another to build a beis midrash. What benefit is there in purchasing books if they have no place to study? This resembles what the Rosh wrote in responsa, principle 13, in a similar matter. Moreover, if residents may be compelled to build a synagogue for prayer, all the more so may they be compelled to build a beis midrash dedicated to Torah, whose obligation is greater. See Shabbos 10a, where Rava saw Rav Hamnuna, etc., and said: “They leave eternal life and engage in temporal life.” See Rashi there. See also siman 153, seifim 1 and 6.
