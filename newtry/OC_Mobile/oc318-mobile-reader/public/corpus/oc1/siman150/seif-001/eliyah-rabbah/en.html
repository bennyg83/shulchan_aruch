[1] They compel, etc. The assessment is according to wealth and not per capita, but synagogue rent is assessed per capita. So write Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid in the name of Maharam Padua, siman 42. It appears to me from the responsum itself, however, that rent is assessed half according to wealth and half per capita. Regarding a city with one synagogue whose residents wish to divide and build another synagogue, see Knesses HaGedolah, who cites authorities that if there is concern that taxes or the ruler’s scrutiny will cause destruction, they may not divide. See Magen Avraham at the end of siman 154, Responsa Rashba, siman 500, concerning one who wishes to enlarge a synagogue and thereby lower the status of the seats, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 162, seif 7.
