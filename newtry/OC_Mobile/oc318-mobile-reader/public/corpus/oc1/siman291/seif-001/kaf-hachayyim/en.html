1) [Seif 1] One should be exceedingly careful to fulfill the third meal. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi said in the name of Bar Kappara: Whoever fulfills the three meals is saved from three calamities, the travail of Mashiach, the judgment of Gehinnom, and the war of Gog and Magog. Rabbi Yosi said, 'May my portion be among those who fulfill the three Shabbos meals.' Tur. The sacred Zohar, parashas Yisro, folio 88a, likewise states: A person must further delight himself three times, because supernal faith depends upon these, upon the holy Ancient One, Zeir Anpin, and the field of apples. A person must delight and rejoice in them. One who omits one of these meals causes a defect above, and that person's punishment is great, etc. Rabbi Shimon said: Regarding one who completes the three Shabbos meals, a voice issues and proclaims, 'Then you shall delight in Hashem.' This is one meal, corresponding to the holy Ancient One of all holy beings. 'I shall cause you to ride upon the high places of the earth.' This is the second meal, corresponding to the field of holy apples. 'I shall feed you the inheritance of Yaakov your forefather.' This is the perfection completed in Zeir Anpin, and corresponding to these a person must complete his meals, etc.; see there. Thus, each of the three meals has a supernal root and an esoteric reason. One who neglects any of them receives great punishment, while one who fulfills them receives great reward. See above, siman 242, paragraph 2.
2) One who has food for two meals, but dividing it into three meals would leave him without enough to be satisfied at each, should preferably eat two satisfying meals. Sefer Chassidim, siman 861. This is also implied by Shabbos 118, which says, 'Make your Shabbos like a weekday,' as stated in siman 242. Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. In light of what we wrote above from the sacred Zohar, however, that each of the three meals has a supernal root and one who neglects any of them damages the supernal worlds and receives great punishment, it appears that the reverse is preferable: he should divide the food into three meals rather than eat two to satisfaction. Note this well. See above, siman 242, paragraph 16. Magen Avraham writes that even before midday he may divide the meal into two, because some say this fulfills the obligation; see there. This is not the view of Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, however, as stated below in seif 2. According to the sacred Zohar and the Arizal, this is likewise ineffective. Each meal must be eaten at its proper time: the first at night, the second in the morning, and the third after Minchah, because at that time the holy Ancient One is revealed, as is known. See Shaar HaKavanos, folio 75d. Responsa Min HaShamayim, siman 14, printed at the end of Radbaz, volume 5, writes the same; see there.
3) There: One should be exceedingly careful, etc. The phrase 'exceedingly careful' is used because in the morning a person desires to eat and drink, delights in abundant foods, fruit, and delicacies, and seeks to complete the count of one hundred blessings. He may fill his stomach so fully that when the time for the third meal arrives, his morning meal has still not been digested. The Shulchan Aruch therefore says that he must be exceedingly careful and look ahead, not filling his stomach to such an extent, so that he will desire to eat the third meal at its proper time. Bach. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, writes that perhaps the intention is that this meal requires special care because it demonstrates the honor of Shabbos. It is something one does not ordinarily do during the week, when people principally eat once at night and once during the day. With the third meal, one magnifies the honor of Shabbos by making an additional meal. One who takes care to fulfill it is rewarded even for the night and second meals as though he ate them specifically in honor of Shabbos. If he is negligent about the third meal, however, this shows that he did not eat the first two in honor of Shabbos; see there.
4) There: One should be exceedingly careful, etc. If according to the plain meaning the halachic authorities wrote radiant warnings commanding fulfillment of the third meal, one must be ten times more careful according to the masters of the esoteric teachings, who speak gloriously of it. The matters relate to the Ancient One, and it is a time of favor. Go and observe Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai on the day of his passing, who rejoiced that throughout his life he had never neglected the three meals. From this, a discerning person will understand the precious splendor and greatness of this meal, the force of its obligation within the mystery of the great holiness, and will open his eyes and heart to fulfill it in accordance with halachah and rejoice in it. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1.
5) There: An egg-sized amount. This is not precise; rather, one should eat slightly more than an egg-sized amount, because an egg-sized amount is considered a snack, as stated at the end of siman 232. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. See what the later authorities write concerning this. Nevertheless, when possible, one should be stringent and eat more than an egg-sized amount. If that is impossible, even an olive-sized amount suffices, as Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, writes in the name of Mateh Yehudah; see there. Furthermore, since he must recite Birkas HaMazon afterward, as stated above in siman 184, seif 6, he fulfills the third meal after the fact. See above, siman 274, at the end of paragraph 14.
6) There, gloss: One who did not eat on Shabbos night, etc. The same applies to one who did not eat on Yom Tov night: he should eat two meals during the day. He likewise recites the nighttime Kiddush. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. This means that siman 271, seif 8, states that if one did not recite Kiddush at night, he recites it during the day but omits Vayechulu; see there. Magen Avraham teaches that just as Yom Tov is equivalent to Shabbos regarding making up the meal during the day, it is likewise equivalent regarding making up the nighttime Kiddush during the day. Machatzis HaShekel.
