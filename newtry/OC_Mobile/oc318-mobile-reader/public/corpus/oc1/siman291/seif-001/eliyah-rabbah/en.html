[1] [Levush] And it is from the Torah, etc. See siman 167, seif katan 28. Responsa Maharil, siman 99, likewise writes that the three meals are rabbinic, and that the verses containing the three occurrences of "today" are an asmachta.
[2] [Levush] Whoever fulfills, etc. The Friday night meal corresponds to the Shabbos of Creation and saves one from the birth pangs of Mashiach. The morning meal corresponds to the Shabbos of the giving of the Torah and saves one from Gehinnom, for Gehinnom does not rule over those who study Torah. The third meal corresponds to the Shabbos of the future and saves one from Gog and Magog (Bach). Abudraham writes that because one who eats excessively must distance himself from the next meal, when he sees that there is a mitzvah to eat three meals he will not eat at any meal beyond the amount that allows him to eat when the next meal arrives. His heart will therefore remain free for Torah study, and he will subdue his inclination by removing the food while he still desires it. If he does this on the day of rest, all the more so will he do so on a workday. Mateh Moshe gives ten kabbalistic reasons: 1. corresponding to the great Shabbos, the Shabbos of Creation, and the Shabbos of the giving of the Torah; 2. corresponding to Avraham, Yitzchak, and Yaakov, right, left, and body; 3. corresponding to the three letters associated with chesed; 4. corresponding to Eden, river, and garden; 5. corresponding to the threefold "Holy, holy, holy," and one who omits one diminishes one sanctity, God forbid; 6. corresponding to the First, Second, and Third Temples; 7. corresponding to the Divine Name formed by yod, heh, vav, which comes on Shabbos to rejoice with Israel, and therefore there is a Mussaf offering; 8. corresponding to keter, chochmah, and binah; 9. corresponding to chesed, gevurah, and tiferet; 10. corresponding to nefesh, ruach, and neshamah. One who omits one of these, God forbid, is regarded as destroying the soul.
[3] With an egg sized amount, etc. This is not exact. Rather, it must be slightly more, because an egg sized amount is considered a casual meal, as stated at the end of siman 232 (Magen Avraham). Bach, who writes that an egg sized amount is required initially because of Birkat HaMazon, does not appear to agree, and the same is implied by Mordechai in the chapter Kol Kitvei and Semag, positive commandment 27. Malbushei Yom Tov goes further and questions why an egg sized amount is required at all, since the nighttime Shabbos meal cannot be more stringent than the rule in the chapter Kol Kitvei that a person should always set his table on Friday night even if he needs only an olive sized amount.
