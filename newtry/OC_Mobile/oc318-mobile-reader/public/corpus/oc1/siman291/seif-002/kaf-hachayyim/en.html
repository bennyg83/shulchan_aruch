2) One who has food for two meals, but dividing it into three meals would leave him without enough to be satisfied at each, should preferably eat two satisfying meals. Sefer Chassidim, siman 861. This is also implied by Shabbos 118, which says, 'Make your Shabbos like a weekday,' as stated in siman 242. Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. In light of what we wrote above from the sacred Zohar, however, that each of the three meals has a supernal root and one who neglects any of them damages the supernal worlds and receives great punishment, it appears that the reverse is preferable: he should divide the food into three meals rather than eat two to satisfaction. Note this well. See above, siman 242, paragraph 16. Magen Avraham writes that even before midday he may divide the meal into two, because some say this fulfills the obligation; see there. This is not the view of Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, however, as stated below in seif 2. According to the sacred Zohar and the Arizal, this is likewise ineffective. Each meal must be eaten at its proper time: the first at night, the second in the morning, and the third after Minchah, because at that time the holy Ancient One is revealed, as is known. See Shaar HaKavanos, folio 75d. Responsa Min HaShamayim, siman 14, printed at the end of Radbaz, volume 5, writes the same; see there.
