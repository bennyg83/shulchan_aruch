In all your ways know Him — in responsa Dvar Shmuel siman 138 he was asked: which is the straight path a person should choose — to engage in Torah and expand with students all weekdays and enjoy from others, or enjoy from the toil of his hands and clean work all weekdays and engage in Torah alone every Shabbat [and his intent is not that he not learn at all on weekdays, for it is obvious a person is obligated at least to establish times for Torah every day as explained in siman 155 and 238 and Yoreh De'ah 246; rather his intent is on the extra time of the day how he should conduct himself; and also his question is specifically if his occupation is clean of mixtures of theft, interest and onaah — for otherwise this is no doubt at all, for such occupations are not called enjoying from the toil of one's hands but from others' toil; and furthermore it is better to be ashamed in this world and receive a hundred gifts than transgress once a Torah prohibition of do not steal] — and I will excerpt briefly the essence of his answer to the questioner: did not his clear eye see what is written in Tur Yoreh De'ah 246 in Bach and Bach and Taz and Shach in the name of Sefer Yam Shel Shlomo; and from all of them Mahari Karo extended at length in his book Kesef Mishneh Laws of Torah Study chapter 5 etc.; but it appears in my humble opinion that even Rambam would agree in our case to permit, for we do not judge impossible from possible; and since per the need of the time and place this man desiring life cannot maintain his study in his hand to merit the many except through support of his needs by others — behold he is like all the multitude of judges and sages who received wages from the contribution of the chamber as we learn in Kesubos chapter 2 'judges of decrees'; and Rambam ruled thus in Laws of Shekalim chapter 4 and these are his words: correctors of books in Jerusalem and judges who judge robbers take their wages etc.; and if it did not suffice for them even though they did not want — they add for their needs and their wives and children and household; and how can it enter the mind that the rabbi would rule in such a case that it is better for a person to hold foolishness and lack of wisdom all his days, which causes much harm and stumbling — Torah study leading to action — and to withhold good from its owner because he enjoys from his brother; and see there further what he extended on this matter; and it is wondrous that Ba'er Heitev did not excerpt except the question and not the answer.
