That all one's intentions should be for the sake of Heaven. It contains 1 seif: If one cannot study without sleeping in the afternoon, he should sleep. {Rama: When he wakes from his sleep, he need not recite Elohai Neshamah. [Beit Yosef] Some say that before sleeping he should recite Vihi Noam. [Kol Bo]} He should not prolong his sleep, because one may not sleep during the day more than the sleep of a horse, which is 60 breaths. Even in this short sleep, his intention should not be for bodily pleasure, but to strengthen his body for the service of Hashem. Likewise, with everything from which he derives pleasure in this world, he should not intend his own pleasure but the service of the Creator, as it is written, 'In all your ways know Him,' and the Sages said, 'Let all your deeds be for the sake of Heaven.' Even optional matters, such as eating, drinking, walking, sitting, standing, marital relations, conversation, and all bodily needs, should all be directed toward serving his Creator or toward something that enables His service. Thus, even if he is thirsty or hungry, if he eats or drinks for his own pleasure it is not praiseworthy. Rather, he should intend to eat and drink what is necessary for his vitality in order to serve his Creator. Likewise, even sitting among the upright, standing among the righteous, or walking in the counsel of the wholehearted is not praiseworthy if he does so for his own pleasure and to satisfy his desire, unless he acts for the sake of Heaven. The same applies to lying down. It goes without saying that when he can engage in Torah study or mitzvot, he should not entice himself with sleep for pleasure. Even when he is tired and needs sleep to rest from his fatigue, if he sleeps for bodily pleasure it is not praiseworthy. Rather, he should intend to give sleep to his eyes and rest to his body as required for health, so that his mind will not become confused in Torah study because of lack of sleep. Likewise regarding marital relations: even at the time required by the Torah, if he acts for bodily pleasure it is disgraceful. Even if his intention is to have children who will serve him and take his place, it is not praiseworthy. Rather, he should intend to have children who will serve his Creator, or intend to fulfill the prescribed mitzvah as one who pays a debt. Likewise regarding conversation: even when speaking words of wisdom, his intention must be for the service of the Creator or for something that leads to His service. The general principle is that a person must direct his eyes and heart to his ways and weigh all his deeds on the scales of reason. Whatever he sees will bring him to the service of the Creator, he should do, and whatever will not, he should not do. One who conducts himself in this manner serves his Creator constantly.
