One may not make a bonfire, etc. The same applies if one wishes to heat the winter-house oven at that time; it too has the law of a bonfire, unless the oven is sealed with clay, in which case it is permitted. Tosefes Shabbos. It is implied that, when enough time remains before nightfall for the flame to rise on its own, it is permitted even with an oven that is not sealed with clay. Nevertheless, it appears to me that, l'chatchila, it is proper to refrain from heating it close to nightfall even in this manner. Rather, one should see to it that the heating itself is also completed during the weekday, because it commonly happens that charred logs remain afterward in the oven, and one is then compelled ultimately to have a non-Jew extinguish them so that the oven will not cool while waiting for them to burn out completely. It is improper to bring oneself into this situation l'chatchila. This is not comparable to the permission to have a non-Jew heat an oven on Shabbos l'chatchila in cold countries, for there he has no other solution, since what he heated yesterday has already cooled. Here, however, he could have heated it earlier, unless circumstances beyond his control prevented him.
Until it catches fire, etc. Otherwise, we are concerned that he may stoke the coals and move the wood after nightfall.
Without assistance. Meaning that one will not need to say, 'Bring thin pieces of wood and let us place them beneath it so that it will ignite.'
Through most of its thickness. The fire must penetrate inward through most of its thickness, and it must also spread over most of its outer circumference.
It is forbidden to benefit, etc. Meaning, even if it later became fully kindled, as written at the end of siman 254 regarding dyeing vats and lupines: whenever one violated an enactment of the Sages, it is forbidden to benefit from the result until Motzei Shabbos.
And when it was kindled to the required degree. Meaning, while it was still daytime, before Shabbos.
He may warm himself, etc. The Taz wrote in siman 275 that one must nevertheless take care not to sit close to the ends of the charred logs, because there is concern that he may touch them in order to draw them nearer to the bonfire. The Eliyah Rabbah and Tosefes Shabbos, however, hold that we are not concerned for this, and it is permitted to make use of the light even close to the bonfire or oven, unless the activity requires close examination or one wishes to read by its light. In those cases we are concerned that he may tilt the lamp, as explained below in siman 275.
And even, etc. With a bonfire, because the fire is extensive, each piece of wood kindles the other.
