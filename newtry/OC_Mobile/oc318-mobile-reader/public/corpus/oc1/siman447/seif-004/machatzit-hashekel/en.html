Se'if katan 4: And specifically, etc. That the oven is small, meaning that it does not hold twelve esronim of matzah, for we require that it not be a tefach thick, as below in siman 460 se'if 5. The Pri Chadash wrote in Yoreh Deah, in Kuntres Acharon, siman 97, that the floor of the oven must hold this measure; and in Pleisi, siman 108, he wrote that the sides must hold this measure, for in the days of the Sages of the Talmud they would attach the bread to its walls. The Rema wrote there that in a place of loss, meaning great loss, one may be lenient with chametz. Therefore with chametz, etc.: this the Magen Avraham wrote from his own reasoning. For one could say that in truth, if it is completely sealed, there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions, and in both cases we permit only in a place of great loss. The Rema did not write to be more stringent with chametz than with other prohibitions except where it is slightly open at the place where the smoke exits; in such a case, with other prohibitions it is permitted even not in a place of loss, but with chametz we require specifically this kind of great loss. There is no distinction with chametz between whether it is open at the place where the smoke exits or not. Rather, it seemed reasonable to the Magen Avraham that just as we are more stringent with chametz than with other prohibitions where it is open at the place where the smoke exits, so too one should be stringent if it is completely sealed: where with other prohibitions we permit only in a place of great loss, with chametz we go down one level and forbid even in a place of great loss.
And furthermore, Tosafos in Avodah Zarah are stringent, etc. That is, regarding chametz; see what the Shach wrote in siman 108, s.k. 13. With this the proof of Maharil is rejected. Maharil wrote in siman 186: it seems that you were uncertain, since chametz forbids by a mashehu, whether aroma is a halachic factor, etc. It appears that there is no distinction according to the one who says aroma is not a halachic factor. A proof is that Levi permitted kosher-slaughtered meat roasted with neveilah meat in every case, even where both are one type, even according to the one who says that one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu. So too it is proven there in Perek Keitzad Tzolin, where the Gemara asks, according to the view that the concern is mixture of flavors: what difference is there between kid and kid, and what difference is there between kid and lamb? Let it say not only kid and kid, which is one type with its own type, where according to Rabbi Yehuda and Rebbe, etc., one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu, but even kid and lamb, which are one type with another type. Rav too holds that one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu. End quote from Maharil. His intent is that we say in Perek Keitzad Tzolin, folio 76: it was stated, fatty kosher-slaughtered meat that one roasted with lean neveilah meat; Rav said it is forbidden. What is the reason? Aroma is a halachic factor. Levi said it is permitted; aroma is not a halachic factor. They raised an objection: one may not roast two Pesach offerings together, because of mixture. What, is this not mixture of flavors, and it is difficult for Levi? No, it is because of mixture of the bodies. The reason for mixture of their bodies or of their flavors is that through this the Pesach is eaten not by those appointed for it. The Gemara derives this from what the latter clause teaches, even a kid and a lamb. Granted, if it is because of their bodies, that is why it teaches even kid and lamb; but if it is because of mixture of flavors, what difference is there between kid and lamb and kid and kid? Rather, what, etc., mixture of flavors, and it is permitted? Let this be a refutation of Rav. End quote from the Gemara. Maharil inferred from the questioners' challenge to Rav, asking why it says even kid and lamb, for what novelty is there in kid and lamb more than in kid and kid, if the reason is mixture of flavors? If, even according to us who rule like Levi, nevertheless with chametz aroma is a halachic factor because Levi concedes that at least a mashehu exists, while Rav holds that aroma is a full flavor, then kid and lamb would indeed be a novelty: not only kid and kid, which is one type with its own type and the baraisa follows Rabbi Yehuda, who holds one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu, so that it is forbidden even if aroma is not a halachic factor, since at least a mashehu exists; but even kid and lamb, which are one type with another type, are forbidden because it is a full flavor. Rather, one must say that the Talmud holds that if aroma is a halachic factor, it is a full flavor, and if it is not a halachic factor, there is not even a mashehu of flavor. If so, it properly asks: what difference is there between kid, etc. About this Magen Avraham wrote that according to the Rif his proof is rejected. At first glance one could answer Maharil's proof: even if we say Levi concedes that at least a mashehu exists, nevertheless the Gemara properly asks what difference there is between kid, etc.; although kid and kid is one type with its own type, the baraisa is speaking initially, for it teaches, one may not roast, and regarding the initial law there is no distinction between its own type and another type. Whatever is forbidden after the fact with its own type because it forbids by a mashehu is at least initially forbidden with another type, for we hold that one may not nullify a prohibition initially. One must say that Maharil was troubled by this: Tosafos there rejected Rashi's explanation, for Rashi holds that Levi disagrees only after the fact but admits that initially it is forbidden. If so, what does the Talmud ask against Levi from the baraisa, one may not roast two Pesach offerings, since the baraisa speaks initially? Rather, perforce Levi permits even initially. Maharil would then have a difficulty according to the questioner's view, that Levi concedes that at least a mashehu exists, and therefore with chametz aroma is a halachic factor. If so, this itself is difficult: why does Levi permit initially; do we not say that one may not nullify a prohibition initially? If so, there is no challenge to Levi at all from the baraisa of one may not roast two Pesach offerings. Perforce the Talmud must hold that this is not called nullifying a prohibition, since his intent is not to nullify, as below in siman 453 se'if 3; see there in the Taz, and in Yoreh Deah siman 84, s.k. 45 in the Shach, and in many places. According to this, Maharil's proof is sound: if there is certainly a mashehu, kid and lamb would properly be a novelty, since they are not the same type, and one might have thought that aroma is only a mashehu and that even initially, with another type, it is permitted because his intent is not to nullify; therefore it teaches us that it is a full flavor. This is a proper rejection of the questioner's view according to Maharil, namely that Rav holds it is a full flavor, while Levi, whom we rule like, nevertheless holds that it is at least a mashehu of flavor. But according to the Rif, even Rav himself holds there is only a mashehu of flavor; if so, certainly according to Levi there is not even a mashehu. For if not, what is Levi's reason? Do not say it is because Levi holds that one type with its own type is nullified, for then their dispute would not depend at all on the law of whether aroma is a halachic factor, but on whether one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu or not, and they should have disputed generally about one type with its own type. Rather, perforce Levi holds that even according to the one who says one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu, aroma is nevertheless not a halachic factor, for there is not even a mashehu of flavor. The Rif, however, holds that we rule like Rav against Levi for the reasons the Rif wrote there; yet one cannot rule like Rav in this case, since Rav concedes it is only a mashehu of flavor. Rather, Rav follows his own view that one type with its own type forbids by a mashehu, and kosher-slaughtered meat with neveilah is one type with its own type. If so, for us, who hold that one type with its own type is nullified, one cannot rule like Rav in that case; but regarding chametz, where we hold that it forbids by a mashehu, we rule like Rav that aroma is a halachic factor. According to this, Maharil's proof is rejected. For according to the Rif one must say that when the Talmud asks Rav, why even kid and lamb, it should have said: if the reason is mixture of flavors, why is kid and lamb, which is one type with another type, forbidden, since even according to Rav aroma is only a mashehu? Perforce one must say that nevertheless, since the baraisa speaks regarding the initial law, as it teaches, one may not roast, etc., regarding the initial law even another type is forbidden according to Rav, because one may not nullify a prohibition initially; perforce one must say that this is called intending to nullify. Even so, it properly asks against Levi, because according to Levi it must be permitted even initially, for according to Levi there is not even a mashehu of flavor, as above. According to this, Maharil's proof is gone, for the Gemara properly asks what difference there is between kid, etc.; although one is one type with its own type and the other is another type, since the baraisa speaks initially, and regarding the initial law there is no distinction between its own type and another type, for one may not nullify a prohibition initially, as above.
In the Mordechai, etc. Rather, he proved, etc. This comes to answer the difficulty of the Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 108, s.k. 14, who wrote regarding the Rema there, and some say one should not distinguish, meaning that chametz too, regarding aroma, is equal to other prohibitions. These are the words of Darchei Moshe in the name of the Mordechai. The Shach wrote: this requires examination, for in the Mordechai, Perek Kol Shaah, it says only that aroma does not apply to chametz bread baked with matzah, because we do not find an aroma of bread with bread, etc. End quote. For this Magen Avraham wrote that the Rema was precise from the language of the Mordechai, whose words in Perek Kol Shaah, siman 570, are as follows: an incident occurred on Pesach in which a non-Jew threw a chametz cake into an oven full of matzos, etc.; but if it did not touch, it is permitted, for we hold aroma is not a halachic factor. So too Rabbeinu Tam answered, etc.: we have not found that the aroma of forbidden bread forbids permitted bread, etc. End quote. The Shach understood that the Mordechai and Rabbeinu Tam stand in one approach, permitting because the aroma of bread with bread does not forbid, but he would concede that the aroma of roasting forbids on Pesach. But Magen Avraham wrote according to the Rema's view, because the Mordechai wrote, for we hold aroma is not a halachic factor, which implies even a complete aroma. Otherwise he should have said that the aroma of bread does not forbid, as Rabbeinu Tam wrote, and he would not have written, so too Rabbeinu Tam answered, etc. Rather, Rabbeinu Tam also permitted, but not for the Mordechai's reason; Rabbeinu Tam permitted because the aroma of bread, etc., does not forbid. According to this, the Rema properly proved that according to the Mordechai there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions, and even with chametz, even a complete aroma is not a halachic factor. What Rabbeinu Tam wrote, that we have not found the aroma of bread, etc., implying that a complete aroma does forbid, is not because of the stringency of chametz, but because Rabbeinu Tam holds that even with other prohibitions aroma is a halachic factor, like Rav, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 3. About this Magen Avraham wrote that nevertheless even in the Mordechai it is not explicit that he permits a complete aroma with chametz. Granted, his language implies this, but it can be rejected as the Shach wrote, that the Mordechai's intent is like Rabbeinu Tam: when he wrote that we hold aroma is not a halachic factor, he meant a weak aroma, like the aroma of bread with bread, where we hold it is not a halachic factor; but with a complete aroma, one may say that he concedes it forbids on Pesach, even though with other prohibitions we rule like Levi.
And here both are equal. That is to say, the Rif and Tosafos in Avodah Zarah are equal here, as written above. In any case, one should not be lenient when it is completely sealed, etc. Chok Yaakov disagrees and permits even when it is completely sealed in a case of great loss; see there.
And all this is with roasting, etc. And similar cases, where it was not entirely, etc. That is to say, since the Rema brought proof to forbid cooking from the fact that Mahariv forbade hot foods insulated for Shabbos, it seemed to him that cooking and insulating hot foods for Shabbos are the same. But according to our text in Issur VeHeter, Issur VeHeter also explicitly distinguishes between cooking and insulating hot foods for Shabbos. If so, one may say that Mahariv too permits cooking, and forbade only one who insulates for Shabbos. Perhaps the Rema also intended this, for he wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 108 regarding what the Beis Yosef wrote: when does this apply? With roasting; but if he comes to cook them in a pot, each one by itself, etc., it is permitted. About this the Rema wrote: specifically when the oven is somewhat open, but if it is closed on all sides in the way that one insulates hot foods for Shabbos, it is forbidden. End quote. His intent in writing, in the way that one insulates, etc., is not that it is merely closed on all sides, while he would hold that even if one cooked them for an hour or two it is forbidden. Rather, by writing, in the way that one insulates, etc., he means that it is both closed and remains there for a long time, and because of this there is a strong aroma, as the Magen Avraham wrote. But with cooking, the Rema concedes that it is permitted.
And although the Beis Yosef in Yoreh Deah, etc. He wrote in the name of the Rashba that the aroma of cooking does not forbid. He brought proof from the fact that we ask against Rav from the baraisa, one may not roast two Pesach offerings, etc., which must be because of mixture of their bodies, since it teaches even a kid and a lamb. This implies that for mixture of their flavors we are not concerned, because aroma is not a halachic factor, as above. Rabbi Yirmeyah answered that he roasted them in two pots. The Gemara asked: could one think two pots? A Pesach offering requires roasting by fire. It answered: something like two pots. Rashi explained: a spit from this side, etc., and a large mound of coals or ash in the middle. Since it answered, in two pots, this implies that in a pot there is no aroma, and therefore cooking does not forbid, because there is no aroma. According to this, pot-roasting is forbidden, because although it is in a pot, nevertheless there is aroma, since it is roasting. The Beis Yosef objected: according to the conclusion, something like two pots necessarily speaks of actual roasting, for it requires roasting by fire, and there is aroma, and nevertheless it is permitted; necessarily the reason is because there is a mound interrupting. So too according to the initial assumption, that he roasted them in two pots, this means pot-roasting, and there is aroma; necessarily one must say it is because there is an interruption of a pot. If so, even pot-roasting is permitted. Magen Avraham rejected this: the interruption of a pot helps according to the conclusion because there is a large mound, so that before the aroma reaches above the mound, the aroma disperses; but the interruption of a pot alone does not help, because the aroma exits upward. Also according to the initial assumption that he roasted them in two pots, it does not speak of pot-roasting, but of actual cooking, and the reason is that cooking has no aroma. About this the Gemara asks: two pots, meaning cooking, could one think so, for it requires roasting by fire? It also could have said: the baraisa teaches one may not roast, so how can you say it is cooking in two pots? Rather, one may say that this too is included in its question, two pots, could one think so, for it teaches one may not roast, etc. But when it answered that he roasted them in two pots, which according to the Magen Avraham's intent means that he cooked them in two pots, one must say that the word roasted is not precise. Since the hole is in a high place, the aroma spreads upward; see there, where he brought proof. And perhaps one may rely, etc., in a case of loss, etc. People ask: is it not obvious to him above that even if the hole is not in a high place, but it is open at the place where the smoke exits, the Rema permits above, and so the Magen Avraham agreed in a case of loss? They answer that above we require specifically great loss, as the Shach wrote; but when the hole is in a high place, then even if there is only a small loss one may be lenient.
And what the Olas Shabbos wrote, that with chametz, etc. The Rema wrote there in Yoreh Deah that if either the forbidden item or the permitted item is a sharp substance, even with other prohibitions it is forbidden. The Shach wrote: even in a large oven whose mouth is open from the side. If so, one may say that on Pesach it is forbidden even in a case of great loss. About this the Olas Shabbos wrote that with chametz we do not find that the prohibited item would be sharp. About this the Magen Avraham wrote that it can be found with borscht. The reason he did not write beer-vinegar is that he follows his own reasoning in s.k. 28, that beer-vinegar is not so much a sharp substance, as the Pleisi wrote there.
