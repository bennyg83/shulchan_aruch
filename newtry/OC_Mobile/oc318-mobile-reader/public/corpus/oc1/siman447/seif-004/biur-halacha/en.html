[4] And was nullified in sixty. The same applies when there was not sixty, but it imparted an impaired flavor. One should analyze whether in this case there is perhaps no dispute about reawakening even according to Rema in se'if 10, for there he was concerned for that view only as a mere stringency; see there. Know that what the Mechaber wrote, "and it was nullified in sixty," he stated the case for a liquid item, whose nullification is in sixty. The same applies to a dry item that became mixed, as Eliyah Rabbah proved from Responsa Rashba, siman 485. Even if it was nullified only one in two, so is proven from Magen Avraham, se'if katan 11, and it is obvious. And the dissenting view that holds it reawakens applies also to dry mixed with dry, meaning that even if it was nullified before Pesach one in a thousand, it reawakens on Pesach and is forbidden.
[4] For it is liquid, etc. I did not copy what Taz wrote, "it appears to me that if liquid became mixed with liquid, etc.," because many poskim disagree with this, as I wrote in siman 453:3; see there in Mishnah Berurah.
[4] With liquid. See Chemed Moshe, who proved that what Rema wrote, "liquid with liquid," is not precise that both were liquid. Rather, if only the chametz was liquid, even if the permitted matter was dry, it also does not reawaken; see there.
[4] Perhaps crumbs remained in it. Although it is a doubtful smallest amount, nevertheless they were stringent about it, because this doubt is close to certain, since presumably crumbs remained in it [Olas Shabbos]. It appears obvious that this law of Rema is not connected to the dispute about reawakening. Even if we hold generally that it does not reawaken, it is still forbidden here, for it resembles the case above in se'if 3 while the wheat kernel is inside it, which according to everyone is forbidden, as Magen Avraham wrote there in se'if katan 8.
