Before Pesach. Even after six hours.
To forbid in any amount. There was a case in which one piece of dough was found in the basket into which baked matzos were placed when they came out of the oven. They emptied the basket and it was not known which matzos the dough had touched. The poskim ruled that one against two is nullified, since the mixture occurred before Pesach, and it is permitted to heat the matzos on Pesach because there is only mere taste. However, all matzos known to have touched the dough require peeling, since there is no nullification for a known prohibition.
And some disagree. According to them, what is written in seif 2, that it is nullified in sixty, means that one must eat it before Pesach, or that it speaks where there was only taste alone, as above in letter 20.
And the practice follows the first opinion, etc. It is permitted even if they reheated it on Pesach, as the later authorities write.
For it is liquid mixed with liquid. Such as beer mixed with wine and the like. Flour mixed with flour is also considered liquid with liquid. See below, siman 453, letter 17 in the Mishnah Berurah. The later authorities wrote that with something that is liquid with liquid, even if the mixture became known only on Pesach, we also say that it had already been nullified.
However, with a dry item that became mixed. Before Pesach, whether chametz became mixed with matzah one against two, or it became mixed with sixty or more, we say that it reawakens on Pesach because the chametz is present; therefore one must eat it before Pesach. If it was one against two, it is forbidden even to keep it D’Rabbanan. If there was sixty, he may cook it before Pesach until it dissolves and is considered liquid, and then it is even permitted to eat it on Pesach. Several later authorities hold that even dry with dry, where we say it reawakens, is only when he bakes or cooks them on Pesach, for then the chametz again gives taste into the rest; but to eat it as is, it is permitted.

It is forbidden on Pesach. This means to drink it, but it is permitted for benefit, and certainly one may keep it, since it is only a mere concern, as the later authorities write.
For we are concerned lest, etc. Meaning, because of the taste that is in the wine from the bread that soaked in it, we would not forbid, for the taste was already nullified in sixty before Pesach and does not reawaken. Rather, we are concerned that crumbs perhaps remained at the time the bread was removed. See the later authorities, who agreed that we are stringent only where he did not filter it before Pesach. Although he filtered it on Pesach, that does not help, for it already became forbidden through the crumbs immediately at the start of Pesach when they gave taste in the wine. But if he filtered it, it is permitted, even if it soaked for twenty-four hours, provided there is sixty in the wine against all the bread. This applies specifically to clear wine that is filtered through a strainer with very fine holes, so that bread crumbs cannot pass through. But if the bread fell into mead, which is sometimes thick and passes only through a strainer whose holes are not so fine, we are concerned that the crumbs may also pass through there.
Crumbs remained in it, etc. Even if one took from that barrel of wine before Pesach after removing the bread from it and mixed it into another barrel of wine, all of it is also forbidden, lest crumbs also fell there.
And they impart taste on Pesach. Although regarding cold there are many who permit below in siman 467, seif 12, here all agree that it is forbidden, for the case is where twenty-four hours passed from when the bread fell until now, as the Magen Avraham writes. According to what is explained below in siman 467, seif 12 in the Mishnah Berurah there in the name of the later authorities, that if a slice of bread fell into water on Pesach, all agree it is forbidden because its way is to crumble and dissolve in the water, and even filtering the water does not help; here too, even if twenty-four hours did not pass from when the bread fell into the water, it is forbidden unless he filtered it before Pesach, for then what dissolved was nullified in sixty. If one drew off the scum of mead with a sieve in which flour had been sifted, it is permitted to drink it on Pesach, for it is nullified in sixty, since it is liquid mixed with liquid, as the later authorities write.
