This is not comparable to the wheat kernel above in se'if 3, where if they heated it while the wheat was inside, the law is different because the actual substance of the wheat is visibly there; see Chok Yaakov. Pri Chadash wrote: an incident occurred where one piece of dough was found inside the basket into which they placed the baked matzos when they came out of the oven. They emptied the basket, and it was not known which matzos the dough had touched. I ruled that one in two is nullified, since the mixture occurred before Pesach, and the matzos may be heated on Pesach according to everyone, since there is only flavor. However, all the matzos that are known to have touched the dough require peeling; see there. So too is written in Responsa Olas HaGer, siman 99.
[4] With liquid. It appears to me that if liquid was mixed with liquid before Pesach in less than sixty, he may add to it before Pesach until there is sixty, so that it will be permitted on Pesach. This does not involve the prohibition of nullifying a prohibition ab initio; Taz. See Bach, siman 453, and Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah there. Flour mixed with flour is called liquid with liquid. If it was nullified before Pesach, one may bake matzos from it even on Pesach. Terumas HaDeshen and Bach.
[4] Forbidden on Pesach. Meaning to drink it, but it is permitted for benefit, and all the more so it is permitted to keep it. Anything that is permitted to keep is permitted for benefit; see Chok Yaakov. If one took from that wine after removing the bread from it and mixed it with other wine, all of it is forbidden; Rashba and Magen Avraham. This is specifically if he did not strain it before Pesach, but if he strained it, according to everyone it is permitted; Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov, see there. Taz wrote that straining does not help, because perhaps small particles pass through the holes of the strainer. If he strained it through a thick cloth, it appears to me that one may permit it. The Bach wrote in a responsum, siman 107, that if he drew off the scum of mead with a sieve in which flour had been sifted, it is permitted to drink it on Pesach, because it is nullified in sixty, since it is liquid with liquid; see there.
