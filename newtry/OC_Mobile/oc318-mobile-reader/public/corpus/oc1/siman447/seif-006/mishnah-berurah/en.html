On Pesach. Even if the salt is lying in the mortar during Pesach itself.
For it does not emit through cold. Meaning, if they placed a hot item in it, it would certainly be forbidden, because it would draw out the absorbed taste of the vessel. But placing salt there, although we generally say that salty is like hot, with respect to drawing taste out of vessels it does not draw out through cold. This is specifically when dry, but with cold moist food and through soaking, he is concerned above in seif 5 to be stringent. If the salt was pounded in it, where there is sharpness and the pressure of pounding, it draws taste out of the vessel even if the salt was entirely dry, even if the mortar was not ben yomo, and even before Pesach. The later authorities wrote that with our mortars, whose primary use is for spices and the like, so that there is no chametz but only a concern for the taste of some small amount, if it was pounded before Pesach and became nullified, one may permit salting with it.
