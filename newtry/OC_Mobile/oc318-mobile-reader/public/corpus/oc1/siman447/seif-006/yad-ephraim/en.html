"And so the language in Hagahos Ashri implies; if so, there is no proof to forbid a second vessel, etc., for he wrote in Darchei Moshe in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos, etc." This is the correct reading. It appears to explain as follows: if we say that cold is completely permitted, there is a proper proof from Mordechai regarding a second vessel. Although it refers to where they poured the water into the utensil, nevertheless, since he wrote that it is a second vessel, it must speak of where they placed the chicken into it afterward. Or even if you say that the chicken was there at the time of scalding, nevertheless, although the bowl emits taste because of the pouring, this emitted taste does not have the power to go back and be absorbed into the chicken except through the hot water inside it, and this is only a second vessel. It appears to me that this is the view of the "some who permit" mentioned in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, end of siman 68, regarding if they poured in order to scald a chicken in a dairy pan. See there in Shach in the name of Levush, who wrote the reason because the stream was interrupted. In my humble opinion, this is not needed; rather, the reason is that the return of the emitted taste into the chicken is no longer because of pouring, but is like a second vessel. The Beis Yosef ruled there to permit in a second vessel. If so, there is a proper proof from Mordechai regarding a second vessel: although the pouring acts to make the utensil emit taste, nevertheless regarding forbidding the chicken there is only a second vessel, and it is proven that a second vessel forbids. This is not so if we say that cold is forbidden. Then there is no proof regarding a second vessel, because once the utensil emitted taste because of the pouring, the chicken automatically became forbidden even though it is only a second vessel, for even if it were merely cold it would also be forbidden. If so, from where did Rema know to forbid a second vessel when there is no liquid there? Afterward he wrote: and furthermore, for he wrote in Darchei Moshe, etc. One must say that this is another gloss, meaning that Darchei Moshe held that it is another gloss, and therefore he wrote here in the gloss two views. According to what I wrote, this is not difficult at all, because one can say that there the reason is that the utensil emits through pouring, and automatically the chicken becomes forbidden, since it is no worse than cold. What he wrote in the second gloss, that one need not be concerned for a second vessel, refers to where there is no liquid. About this he wrote: therefore it appears to me that what Mordechai wrote to forbid with cold is because of cracks, etc. Without that, there is no prohibition with cold. If so, Mordechai properly proves that even a second vessel forbids, for he forbids scalding the chicken even though the chicken is in its feathers and has no cracks, and pouring helps only to cause emission from the utensil, as I wrote. See my Responsa Beis Ephraim, Orach Chayim, part 1, siman 47; there you will find this matter explained more fully. Many were confused about this and amended and switched the approaches, but one who examines will see that this explanation is correct and illuminates the eyes, with Hashem's help.
