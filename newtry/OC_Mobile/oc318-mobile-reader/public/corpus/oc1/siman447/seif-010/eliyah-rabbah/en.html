Levush: Intentionally, etc. This implies that even where there is sixty, if it was done intentionally it is forbidden, for he is referring to the law of Dofus; this requires examination, because in se'if 11 he wrote that even initially one may mix it and keep it. But the Tur is not difficult, for one can explain that he speaks where there is not sixty; and in truth, according to the Tur, perhaps even if it became mixed by itself it is forbidden in this case. Although where there is not sixty it implies that it is forbidden for benefit after Pesach, one may say that he relied on Almori being permitted for benefit, since the bread was charred. But the Levush requires examination. I then examined Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 46a, and it also implies that he speaks where there is sixty, and nevertheless he wrote that one must destroy it before Pesach, for it is intentional, since one must make it and prepare it with chametz. End quote. Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of siman 442 that it implies there that even mixing it intentionally with intent to keep it is forbidden. End quote. That is because since one must make it and prepare it, it is called intentional. According to this, with something that one does not need to make, perhaps it is permitted initially to mix it with sixty and keep it; so one may explain the words of the Levush and also the Tur. With this one can perhaps answer the difficulty from the Tur's ruling in siman 442 regarding triaca, that it is forbidden, for that too requires preparation; examine it. See s.k. 9 there, where I brought other answers. One can also say that what he permits below refers to where it was already mixed, except that there is not sixty, and it is permitted to mix in more; but here Almori speaks where there is actual chametz, which is forbidden to mix in. This requires examination. More than this, the Taz wrote that a liquid mixture may be mixed into sixty even in order to eat it on Pesach, and he brought proof from Sukkah, siman 626. This too requires examination, for in Responsa Rashba, siman 451, it is explained that it is forbidden, because it is included in the rule that one may not nullify a prohibition. Along the way I will mention what Magen Avraham wondered in siman 442, s.k. 13, about the Rosh at the beginning of Perek Kol Shaah regarding Almori: it implies that even according to the second view it is permitted for benefit; if so, what is the dispute with the first view? Therefore he explained that according to the second view, with which the Rosh agreed, it is forbidden for benefit, and he wondered at the Tur, who permits it; see there. I found in Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 45, this language: one view says that chametz that passed through Pesach is permitted for benefit, and when mixed it is permitted for eating; another says it is permitted, but most halachic decisors forbade it, etc. End quote. It is known that his words are taken from the Rosh, and there is no doubt that this text was before the Tur. If so, it appears to me that even according to those who forbid, it is permitted for benefit.
