10) A person cooked fish in a chametz frying pan that had not been used for many days. The spoon used to stir the fish was subsequently used to stir another dish, and some of that second dish was added to a third dish. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, volume 1, siman 21, writes that one may not rule leniently in this case, following Rema below in se'if 10, who prohibits a detrimental absorbed flavor during Pesach. See there. An exception applies when another basis for leniency exists. Here there is an additional basis to permit a detrimental absorbed flavor during Pesach after two months have passed. This implies that even the food cooked directly in the frying pan is permitted after the fact. This is subject to the strict condition that the cooked food not be sharp. See the glosses to Yoreh De'ah, siman 93, and siman 122, se'if 3. From this one may infer that even one who is stringent regarding a pot lid that was not used that day may permit it after two months. Initially, however, with all prohibitions it is forbidden to use utensils that were not used that day without kashering them, even if they were not used for many years, and this is certainly true of chametz. See there. Sha'arei Teshuvah, subsection 2, cites this ruling. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 75, however, permits it only when twelve months have passed since chametz was cooked in the utensil. Rabbi Naftali Kohen agrees, as stated there in siman 76, and rules likewise in siman 80. See there. This will be further explained below in siman 451, subsection 4. See there.
