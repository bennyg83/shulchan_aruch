10) One person cooked fish in a chametz frying pan that had not been used for many days. The spoon used to stir the fish was afterward used to stir another cooked dish, and some of that dish was added to another dish. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 21, writes that one should not rule leniently in this case, according to Rama below, seif 10, who prohibits detrimental absorbed flavor on Pesach, unless there is an additional ground for leniency. Here there is another ground: after 2 months, detrimental absorbed flavor may be permitted after the fact even on Pesach, and it appears that even what was cooked directly in the frying pan is permitted after the fact. This is only when the food was not sharp. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 93 in Rama and siman 122, seif 3 in Rama. From this one may infer that even one who is stringent regarding a pot cover that is not ben yomo may permit it after 2 months. Initially, however, regarding all prohibitions one may not use utensils that are not ben yomo without kashering, even if they have not been used for many years, and certainly regarding chametz. Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2, cites this. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 75, permits only when 12 months have elapsed since chametz was cooked in the utensil, and Rabbi Naftali Kohen agrees, as stated in simanim 76 and 80. This will be explained further below in siman 451, letter 4.
