Se'if katan 2: And there is no sixty in Yoreh Deah regarding roasting. For the Beis Yosef holds that it forbids only the amount requiring removal by peeling; but if one of them is fatty, it spreads through all of it. Not only when the forbidden item is fatty, but even when the forbidden item is lean and the permitted item is fatty, it nevertheless forbids all of it, for the permitted item goes and strengthens the forbidden item, etc., and sixty is required. The same is true with chametz: in such a case it forbids by a mashehu, since it spreads through all of it. Although chametz in itself is not found to be fatty, nevertheless the permitted food strengthens it. In a place that was not folded: that place forbids only because it absorbed from the folded place.
Even if the permitted item is fatty, etc. For certainly an absorbed fatty prohibition emerges from one piece to another even without gravy, but a lean prohibition does not. As for why we do not say in such a case that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., the Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 105, s.k. 19, that this applies specifically to a prohibition in its own right, which at least forbids in its own right, even when lean, to the amount requiring peeling; the fattening effect of the permitted item then causes it to travel until it spreads throughout all of it. This is unlike an absorbed lean prohibition, which in itself does not spread at all; we do not say that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc. See the later authorities there.
And so wrote the Mishnah Berurah, etc. He had to bring proof, lest you say: granted, with other prohibitions we say that an absorbed lean prohibition does not emerge, etc.; that is because with other prohibitions it does not forbid until it imparts flavor, and an absorbed lean prohibition does not emerge in so great an amount as to impart flavor. This is unlike chametz, which forbids by a mashehu; one might have thought that even an absorbed lean prohibition at least emits a mashehu and would forbid as chametz. Therefore he brought proof that even on Pesach we say that the absorbed prohibition, etc., does not emerge, so that even a mashehu does not emerge.
To permit the other pieces that touched the piece on which the wheat kernel was found. We do not say that the other pieces become forbidden because they absorb from the piece on which the wheat kernel lies, since that piece on which the wheat kernel lies is forbidden only because it absorbed the flavor of the wheat kernel, and an absorbed prohibition does not emerge, etc. So too it appears in siman 467 se'if 9, that specifically, etc. That is to say, he brought there three views regarding a roasted chicken in which a wheat kernel was found, etc. One view says to cut away the place to which it spread, etc. A second view says the whole chicken is forbidden, because by turning the spit the flavor spreads through the whole chicken. A third view says that all the chickens roasted with it on the spit and touching one another are forbidden, because by turning the spit the flavor spreads from one to another. Magen Avraham inferred from the third view that only through turning the spit are they all forbidden, which implies that if he did not turn it, even though they touch one another, the other chickens are permitted. Now, if all the chickens are lean, this is understandable, for an absorbed lean prohibition does not emerge, etc., even with chametz. But since the ruling is stated without qualification, it implies that even fat chickens are permitted if he did not turn the spit; for if not, he should have explained this explicitly. Why should we not say that since a fat chicken strengthens the lean chametz prohibition, it must be that even with chametz we do not say that an absorbed lean prohibition emerges? Nor do we say that the permitted fat goes and strengthens it so that at least a mashehu emerges and forbids as chametz. People wonder about this proof from the unqualified language, that it must therefore speak even of a fat chicken: for otherwise, how did Magen Avraham himself prove below in siman 467, from two proofs, that it necessarily speaks there specifically of a lean chicken? The first proof is in s.k. 34, from the first view, which holds that it is enough to cut away the place to which it spread. His language is: it is difficult, for he wrote in siman 447 se'if 1, and there is no substance there that causes spreading, etc.; this implies that if there is a substance that causes spreading, it forbids all of it. One must say that here too it speaks of a lean chicken. End quote. The second proof is in s.k. 30, from the third view, which requires specifically, in order to forbid all the chickens, that all the chickens touch one another. Magen Avraham wrote as follows: but if they are not touching, they do not forbid one another, and we do not say that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 32 se'if 5. There, because of doubt, we say that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil; if so, even if the chickens do not touch one another, they nevertheless touch the spit on which they were roasted, and the absorbed matter would spread from the place where the chicken with the wheat kernel lay until the end of the spit, and the others would be forbidden by the spit. For there it speaks of cooking; that is to say, with roasting it is obvious to us that the absorbed matter does not travel through the whole utensil. If so, if the chicken on which the wheat kernel lies is fat, even if it is not touching, it forbids everything. End quote. Thus he proved from two proofs that it speaks there of a lean chicken, even though it is stated there without qualification. In my humble opinion this is not difficult, and the words of Magen Avraham are simple. Certainly it is not the way of the halachic decisors, and especially of the Shulchan Aruch, to state matters without qualification. But according to the first view, which holds that removing the place to which it spread is enough, and which necessarily speaks of a lean chicken from what the Beis Yosef wrote here, and there is no substance there that causes spreading, etc., one may say that for this reason he stated it there without qualification and did not explain that it speaks specifically of a lean chicken, because he relied on what he wrote here in siman 447, and there is no substance there that causes spreading. So too, according to the third view, which wrote that it is specifically when they are touching, and which necessarily speaks of a lean chicken, for otherwise we would say that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil, even though it is stated without qualification there is no difficulty, for one may say that he relied on what he wrote in Yoreh Deah, that the absorbed matter travels through the whole utensil. But if it would enter your mind that with chametz we say that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., then necessarily the reason he needed there, through turning the spit, etc., implying that if he did not turn it they are permitted, would speak specifically of a lean chicken; this he should have explained and not left unqualified, for this law, that chametz differs from other prohibitions and we say regarding it that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., is not mentioned anywhere. On the contrary, in truth Magen Avraham holds that chametz is equal to other prohibitions in this regard. If with chametz we said that the permitted item goes and strengthens it, etc., he should not have stated the ruling in siman 467 without qualification, but should have explained that if it is fat, even if he did not turn it, etc., they are forbidden. Rather, it must be that in truth chametz is equal to other prohibitions in this regard.
And cooked without, etc. Its law is like roasting, for we say that an absorbed prohibition does not emerge, etc., even if the permitted item is fatty. If so, the piece on which the chametz is not lying is permitted even though it touched the piece on which the chametz is lying. However, etc., as the Rema wrote regarding salting, to forbid the whole piece on which the chametz lies even when it is lean, and to require peeling from the piece that touched that peel; the reason is explained below in siman 466. Know that regarding what the Rema wrote, that redemption is not sufficient, etc., Chok Yaakov wrote as follows: in a case of very great loss or pressing need, one who is lenient to permit it for benefit and to cast into the sea the value of the benefit from the mashehu of chametz mixed into it has not lost, etc. See what the Pri Chadash wrote.
