(א) (s.k. 2) And there is no sixty in Yoreh De'ah for roasting. Beit Yosef holds it forbids only the amount removable in one act of removal; but if one of them is fatty it permeates throughout; and not only when the forbidden is fatty — even when the forbidden is lean and the permitted fatty it nevertheless prohibits the whole, for the permitted goes and enhances the forbidden, etc., and sixty is required; the same for chametz: in such a case it prohibits any amount since it permeates the whole although chametz itself is not normally fatty — nevertheless the permitted enhances it. Where it was not folded — that place only prohibits because it absorbed there; where folded — the whole prohibits any amount.

(ב) Efi' the permission of fat, etc. Dodai, the prohibition of the fat that is swallowed comes out piece by piece without sauce, but not a lean prohibition. And from May dela Amarinan, as the 3rd exhortation, and the sentence of the 20th century, he wrote that the Sach B.D. C.K.S.K.19, with the prohibition on his own part, the KPF forbids on his own part, the fattening factor of the permit that led him to the extent that the prohibition of the khust, which is consumed on the other hand, does not oscillate at all in the latter.

(ג) And so too wrote the Mishna Berurah, etc. He found it necessary to bring a proof, lest one say: granted, regarding other forbidden substances we say that a lean absorbed forbidden substance does not come out, etc. — this is because regarding other forbidden substances, it does not prohibit unless it imparts flavor, and a lean absorbed forbidden substance does not come out in sufficient quantity to impart flavor. Which is not so regarding chametz, which prohibits any amount — one might think that even a lean absorbed forbidden chametz substance comes out at least some amount and would be prohibited as chametz. Therefore he brought a proof that also on Pesach we say that an absorbed forbidden substance does not come out, etc. — that even the slightest amount does not come out.

(ד) To permit the other pieces that touched the piece on which the wheat kernel was found — and we do not say that the other pieces are forbidden because they absorb from the piece on which the wheat kernel rests — because that piece on which the wheat kernel rests is forbidden only because it absorbed the flavor of the wheat kernel, and absorbed forbidden flavor does not exit, etc. And so it appears in siman 467 seif 9, that specifically, etc. — meaning, [the Mechaber] brought there three opinions regarding a roasted chicken in which a wheat kernel was found, etc.: [opinion 1:] cut away the place where [the flavor] spread, etc. Opinion 2: the entire chicken is forbidden, for via turning the spit the flavor spreads throughout the entire chicken. Opinion 3: all the chickens that were roasted with it on the spit and touch one another are forbidden, for via turning the spit the flavor spreads from one to another. And Magen Avraham infers from opinion 3 that only via turning the spit are they all forbidden — implying that if [the spit] was not turned, even though they touch one another, the rest of the chickens are permitted. Now, if all the chickens were lean, it works out well, for absorbed lean forbidden flavor does not exit, etc., even with chametz. But since [the ruling] is stated without qualification, it implies that even fat chickens are permitted if [the spit] was not turned — for if not, he should have explicitly clarified this — and why should we not say, since a fat chicken fattens the lean chametz [prohibition absorbed into it], that therefore we do not say even regarding chametz that absorbed lean forbidden flavor exits. And we also do not say that [the fat] goes and fattens [the lean chametz] such that at least a mashehu exits and would render [it] forbidden in chametz. And people wonder at this proof from the unqualified [ruling, since it necessarily] speaks even of a fat [chicken] — for without this, from where did Magen Avraham himself prove below in siman 467 from two proofs that necessarily it speaks there specifically of a lean [chicken]? Proof 1, in small section 34, from opinion 1, which holds that it is sufficient to cut away the place where [the chametz] spread — and his language [is as follows]: "It is difficult, for [the Mechaber] wrote in siman 447 seif 1, 'and there is no substance there that causes it to spread' — implying that if there is a substance that causes it to spread, it renders the entire [piece] forbidden; and one must say that here [in siman 467] it also speaks of a lean chicken." End of his words. Proof 2, in small section 30, from opinion 3, which requires specifically, in order to render all the chickens forbidden, that all the chickens be touching one another — and Magen Avraham wrote as follows: "But if they are not touching, they do not render [each other] forbidden, and we do not say that the absorbed [flavor] travels through the entire vessel, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 32 seif 5 — (for as a matter of doubt, we say that the absorbed [flavor] travels through the entire vessel, and if so, even if the chickens are not touching one another, nevertheless they do touch the spit on which they were roasted, and the absorbed [flavor] would spread from [the part of the spit] where the chicken with the wheat kernel rested to the end of the spit, and the others would be forbidden via the spit) — for there [in Yoreh De'ah] it speaks of cooking, meaning, but with roasting it is obvious to us that the absorbed [flavor] does not travel through the entire vessel; and if so, if the chicken (on which the wheat kernel rests is fat), even if [the other chickens] are not touching [it], [the fat chicken] renders everything forbidden." End of his words. Behold, he proved from two proofs that [the ruling] there speaks of a lean [chicken], even though it is stated without qualification there. And in my humble opinion this is not a difficulty, and the words of Magen Avraham are straightforward — for certainly it is not the practice of the legal decisors, and especially in the Shulchan Aruch, to leave matters without qualification. But [it can be explained as follows]: according to opinion 1, which holds that it is sufficient to remove the place where [the chametz] spread — and necessarily [that ruling] speaks of a lean [chicken], from what Beit Yosef wrote here [in siman 447]: "and there is no substance there that causes it to spread," etc. — one may say that for this very reason [the ruling in siman 467] is stated without qualification there, and [the author] did not explain that it speaks specifically of a lean [chicken], for he relied on what he wrote here in siman 447: "and there is no substance there that causes it to spread." And similarly, according to opinion 3, which wrote [that it is forbidden] specifically when [the chickens] are touching — and necessarily [that ruling] speaks of a lean [chicken], for if not, we would say that the absorbed [flavor] travels through the entire vessel — even though [the ruling] is stated without qualification, there is no difficulty, for one may say that he relied on what is written in Yoreh De'ah that the absorbed [flavor] travels through the entire vessel. But if it would enter your mind that regarding chametz we say that the permitted [fat] goes and fattens [the lean chametz], etc. — then necessarily, that which required there the reason of via turning the spit, etc., implying that if [the spit] was not turned [the other chickens] are permitted, speaks specifically of a lean [chicken]; and this he should have explained and not left without qualification — for this law, that chametz is different from other prohibitions and we say regarding it that the permitted [fat] goes and fattens [the lean chametz], etc., is not mentioned anywhere. On the contrary, in truth Magen Avraham holds that chametz is equal in this regard to other prohibitions, and if it were so that regarding chametz we say the permitted [fat] goes and fattens [the lean chametz], etc., he should not have left [the ruling] without qualification in siman 467, but rather should have explained that if [the chicken] is fat, even if [the spit] was not turned, etc., [the other chickens] are forbidden. Necessarily therefore, in truth chametz is equal to other prohibitions in this regard.

(ה) And [a case of] cooked without [a substance that causes it to spread], etc., its law is like roasted, for we say that absorbed forbidden [flavor] does not exit, etc., even if the permitted [part] is fat. And if so, that piece on which the chametz is not present, even though it touched the piece on which the chametz rests, is permitted. However, etc. — as the Rama wrote regarding salting — to render the entire piece on which the chametz rests forbidden, even if [the chicken is] lean, and to require [removing] the outer layer from the piece that touched that [piece]. And the reason is explained below in siman 466. And know that regarding what the Rama wrote — "and redemption does not suffice," etc. — [the author of] Chayei Adam wrote as follows: "In a case of very great financial loss, or in a time of pressing need, one who is lenient to permit it for benefit and to cast into the sea the monetary equivalent of the benefit derived from the mashehu of chametz mixed in it has not [acted incorrectly]," etc. And see what the Pri Chadash wrote.
