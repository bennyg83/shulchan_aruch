"And so it sounds in siman 447, etc., and he did not distinguish whether the chicken was fat, etc." One must say that although Magen Avraham in siman 467 establishes that case as dealing with a lean chicken, for otherwise it is difficult according to the first view, which does not hold that all of it is forbidden; see there. Nevertheless, his intent is not that he holds it speaks only of a lean chicken, for that is a great strain, to write so without qualification. Rather, because in the first view he stated "cut off the place of spreading according to estimation," he thereby gave a rule that everything depends on the nature of the spreading. With a lean chicken, where the spreading is small, removal suffices; but with a fat one, where through fattening it spreads throughout all of it, it truly forbids all of it, since the place of spreading is the whole thing. The later views disagree with this and hold that in every case, even with a lean one, it is forbidden because of turning the spit.
