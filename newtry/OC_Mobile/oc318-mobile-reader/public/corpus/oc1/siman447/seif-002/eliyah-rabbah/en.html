Levush: For the custom is to burn, etc. So wrote the Rema in the name of the Mordechai and Piskei Maharai, siman 164. My brother-in-law in Chok Yaakov objected that the Mordechai is referring to the wheat kernel and not to the chicken in which the wheat kernel was found, and he explained the words of Maharai at length in this way: if it referred to the wheat kernel, then should one burn it on Yom Tov? Therefore he explained that less than a kezayis does not require removal; see there at length. But this difficulty is against him, for in Agudah, regarding that incident in the Mordechai, he wrote that it speaks where he had nullified it, and therefore he did not rule to burn it on Yom Tov. Aside from this, the interpretation of Maharai is forced. Rather, the explanation of Maharai appears clear to me: he responded to one who asked whether to burn it or throw it into a ownerless place, and he answered that the custom is to burn it. He explained the Mordechai's words as speaking of custom. He wrote about this, that it does not appear that it refers to the wheat kernel itself and speaks as a matter of law, for even the wheat kernel does not require this, since less than a kezayis does not require removal. If so, it speaks of custom, and the same applies to the chicken. So too in Agudah, Pesachim chapter 2, it is explicit that we burn the chicken on Chol HaMoed; he certainly explained the Mordechai this way. Although the Mordechai also wrote the law of the chicken in chapter 2, this is the Mordechai's way, as is known: here he wrote in the name of Ri, and later in the name of other halachic decisors. So too it is explained in Sefer Amarkal on parchment, and so wrote Shibbolei HaLeket at the end of siman 63 and Tanyah. Therefore one should not move at all from the ruling of the Rema and Levush in this matter.
