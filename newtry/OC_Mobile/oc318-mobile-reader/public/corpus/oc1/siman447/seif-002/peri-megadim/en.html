[2] And there is not. See Taz. According to his approach in Yoreh De'ah 105, note 10, he raised many difficulties because we do not say that something permitted once becomes forbidden. There one can say it refers to lean fat, as written in Darchei Moshe. Chametz, however, is entirely a lean prohibition. Therefore, even fatty meat next to it, hot without liquid, does not forbid more than the amount requiring removal. Perforce, "and there is no item that spreads" means as follows: with other prohibitions, the place of contact and removal apply where there is no fatty item. This is forced. But Magen Avraham explained that it refers to chametz. See Yoreh De'ah 105 in Nekudas HaKesef there, and see Ateres Zekenim, who explained like Taz. With Hashem's help this will be explained in Magen Avraham. See siman 105 in Yoreh De'ah, Shach 37, and 467:14 regarding salting.
