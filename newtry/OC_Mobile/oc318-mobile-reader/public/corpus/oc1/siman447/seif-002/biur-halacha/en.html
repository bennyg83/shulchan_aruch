Chametz is not considered something that will become permitted because the following year it becomes forbidden again, like the first view brought in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 102 in the gloss; see there. So ruled Toras Chatas and Sefer Apei Rabravei, unlike what the Shach wrote there in se'if katan 13, that the Mechaber refers only to a different type [Eliyah Rabbah]. So too Levush here and Magen Avraham in se'if katan 40 wrote without qualification. The Gra in Yoreh De'ah there likewise wrote that the Mechaber here wrote without qualification like the first view there, that chametz is not called something that will become permitted.

[2] It is also permitted. Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Shibolei HaLeket: se'or or chametz that was cooked with cooked meat or roast meat is impaired flavor and is permitted. This is the end of his language. He abbreviated Shibolei HaLeket's wording, and there is room to err; therefore I will copy his language from note 217: se'or or chametz that fell into a pot or was cooked with roast meat is known to impart an impaired flavor, and a small amount the reading is "that was" in that food is permitted. Although that food has chametz flavor, actual chametz that is fit to leaven and is not impaired, being fit for its purpose, is what is forbidden, etc.; since its taste is impaired and impairs this food, it is permitted. This is the end of his language. It implies from his wording that he was precise to write cooking with roast meat, but with cooked meat it is not impaired, unlike what is implied from Magen Avraham, who does not distinguish in this. Even if it fell into a cooked food, we do not say of every dough that fell into cooked food that it is impaired, but only of something very sour, which is not normally placed into a cooked food; in that case we say that presumably it impairs. This resolves Baer Heitev's difficulty from Yoreh De'ah, siman 323, where it states that if challah dough fell into a cooked food, it forbids it. See Chok Yaakov, who challenged Magen Avraham, and Eliyah Rabbah resolves him by saying that presumably it is also impaired. According to what we explained as Shibolei HaLeket's intent, that he does not speak of every chametz but only of something extremely sour, Chok Yaakov's difficulty is resolved, and perhaps he too concedes to this as a matter of law.
