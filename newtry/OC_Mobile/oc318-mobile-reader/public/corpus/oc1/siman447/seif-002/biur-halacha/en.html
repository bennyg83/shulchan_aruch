(א) chametz that became mixed etc. like other prohibitions — from the Mechaber's stam wording it is implied that even in its own kind its law is so, that sixty suffices and it is not called chametz — a thing that has a permitted form because next year it returns and becomes forbidden, Per the first view brought in Yoreh De'ah siman 102 in the gloss — see there; and so ruled Taz and in Sefer Api Ravrevi; and not like Shach there subsection 13 who wrote the Mechaber refers only to not in its own kind [Rabbi Akiva Eiger], and so stam Levush here and Magen Avraham subsection 40, and so Gra in Yoreh De'ah there — the Mechaber is stam here like the first view there that chametz is not called a davar sheyesh lo matirin.

(ב) And imparts taste for detriment, etc. — and likewise regarding reicha, all agree we are not stringent b'eyna like other prohibitions [Acharonim]:

(ג) Also permitted. Shibolei Leket wrote: chametz or chametz that fell into a pot or was cooked with roasted meat — it gives taste for detriment and is permitted, end of his words. Behold Shibolei Leket abbreviated the language and there is room to err; therefore I will copy his wording: in sign 217, chametz or chametz that fell into a pot or was cooked with roasted meat — we know in it that it gives taste for detriment in that food and is permitted, even though that food has chametz taste, for chametz b'eyna is fit for its purpose and is not spoiled, for it is visible for its matter that it is forbidden, etc., since its taste is spoiled and spoils in this food it is permitted — end of his words. And it is implied from his language that he was precise to write cooked with roasted meat, but with cooked meat it is not for detriment [and not like Magen Avraham implies that he does not distinguish in this]; and even if it fell into a dish, not in every dough that fell into a dish do we say it is for detriment, but in something very sour that is not customary to put in a dish — in this we say it is presumably spoiled [and with this the difficulty of Beit HaTovim from Yoreh De'ah siman 323 is resolved, where it says if dough of challah fell into a dish it forbids]. And see Chok Yaakov who challenged Magen Avraham, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger resolved it that presumably here too it is for detriment; and according to what we explained, Shibolei Leket's intent is not in every chametz, but in that which is very sour. And perhaps he too agrees in this for practical law. And regarding grain kernels found in a well of water on Pesach whether this is called taste for detriment, see Magen Avraham and Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Peri Megadim, and it will be explained below in siman 467 seif 13. And regarding dough of chametz that fell into a well see below siman 467 seif 12 in Mishna Berurah s.v. ein.
