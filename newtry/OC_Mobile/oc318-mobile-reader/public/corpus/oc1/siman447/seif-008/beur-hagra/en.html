Olives. He discusses olives and other sharp foods, whether a new knife is required, and how water used in pickling may weaken the sharpness.
Even if they were not careful, etc. This follows from the fact that at the end of the second chapter of Avodah Zarah we permit their pickled olives and are not concerned for the absorbed taste of non-Jewish utensils, as stated there regarding their oil and honey: what should we suspect, etc. See Yoreh Deah, siman 114, seif 8, and so too olives, etc.; and in the gloss there: but if they were pickled, etc. See Yoreh Deah, siman 122, seif 3 in the gloss; therefore, but, etc., and what is stated there.
If, etc. As written in Chullin 113a, and he shakes it off. This means specifically initially, and so the language of the Gemara also implies. But after the fact, even if he placed it in a vessel that was not perforated, it is permitted, since there is water.
