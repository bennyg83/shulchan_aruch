(א) With a new knife. But if the knife is old, even if it is not ben yomo it is forbidden, for it is sharp; and even before they were pickled — not like Shach and Avodat HaKodesh, who wrote specifically that they were cut after they were pickled, then they are sharp; for on the contrary Tur wrote that when one places water on them their sharpness is nullified, but rather it deals with [a case] before they were pickled, and so too the Tosafot; and nullification does not apply, for we measure against the entire knife; and if so, all the olives returned to chametz (Tur) — he explained that each one absorbed a bit from the knife, and if so each one became absorbed with chametz and did not discharge again; and even though it was ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 106 that the prohibition returns and discharges — this applies in cooking, but in pickling it does not discharge, as was said regarding hag'alah, for we require specifically that it was cooked, as above; and the above section wrote that this law is according to one who says it returns and revives, and we do not hold thus, as written in seif 4 — and this is not so, for Tur wrote plainly that it does not return and revive, and afterward immediately the law of olives; therefore the words of Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 96 are primary, and so too here Bach, and see Yoreh De'ah siman 111 in the words of the later authorities; and it appears to me that this is comparable to a piece of meat that fell into several pots one after another, which do not combine; but the brine of the olives is permitted for everyone, for the chametz is nullified in them in sixty; and even though we hold chanan, and if so we would require sixty against all the olives — nevertheless here, since before Pesach it is still permitted, we do not say chanan, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 94 seif 6; and even per what was written in seif 5 that chametz, since its name was placed upon it, is not nat bar nat — nevertheless for this matter it has the law of permitted, that we do not say chanan, for many poskim do not say chanan in any prohibition except meat in milk: and if the olives were cooked before Pesach, even the olives themselves return to being permitted, for chametz returns and is discharged and the taste is nullified in sixty; and even though in Shulchan Aruch siman 106 it is written that that piece is forbidden — nevertheless behold Maharam Lublin wrote in his responsum: specifically where it absorbed a prohibition, but where it absorbed something permitted, even the piece itself is permitted, and so too Taz; however it appears to me that even though it is permitted to keep them, nevertheless it is forbidden to eat them on Pesach, for the plain meaning of Shulchan Aruch and the gloss in siman 106 implies the prohibition is not fully discharged from it; and if so, even though Taz and Maharam are lenient with meat — that is even if we say meat remained in it, all the more so we are not concerned; not so with chametz, which forbids in any amount; and furthermore, for otherwise some say it returns and revives, as written in seif 4.

(ב) If it is not ben yomo.
