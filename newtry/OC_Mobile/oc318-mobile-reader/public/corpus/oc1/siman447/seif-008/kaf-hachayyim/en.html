Even the slightest amount, etc. If some of a food that was itself prohibited by the slightest admixture becomes mixed into another dish, creating two successive minute admixtures, Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 92, subsection 16, concludes as follows: if a solid piece became prohibited by the slightest admixture, fell into another pot, and was removed, what it exudes does not prohibit the second pot in the slightest amount, because we do not apply the rule of the slightest amount twice. If, however, one liquid became mixed with another in this manner, it remains prohibited forever by the slightest amount. See there. Peri Chadash agrees with Taz. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, likewise rules leniently where a solid piece prohibited by the slightest admixture fell into another dish, there is a majority against it, the piece is identifiable, and it was removed. If it cannot be identified, everything is prohibited. See there. Shach in Nekudos HaKesef there, however, challenges Taz's permission of a solid piece prohibited by the slightest admixture that fell into another pot. He writes that all halachic authorities customarily prohibit all the mixtures. See there. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1 in this siman, agrees and writes that the ruling is contrary to Taz. Magen Avraham, siman 467, subsection 13; Chok Yaakov, subsection 5 in this siman and subsection 27 in siman 467; Chok Yosef, subsection 6; and Mateh Yehudah write likewise. Mateh Yehudah writes that this is evident from the words of Maran and Rema, and that this is the custom in Algiers. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 16; Chayei Adam, rule 121, subsection 8. Regarding a doubtful prohibition involving the slightest amount, however, Beis Yehudah, page 109, writes that the second mixture is permitted and agrees with this ruling. See there. Beis David, siman 163, likewise writes that if it is uncertain whether any chametz entered the second mixture, since perhaps all the chametz remained in the first mixture, all authorities permit it. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 4, cites this, but writes that Magen Avraham, subsection 26, implies that it is prohibited even where the chametz may have remained in the first mixture. See there.
