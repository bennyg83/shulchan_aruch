[8] If it was not used that day, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, that if it was used that day it is forbidden, and so it is in the Tur. See Eliyah Rabbah and other later authorities who noted that through pickling it is never considered used that day, because the twenty-four hours of absorption of the prohibition or chametz is completed before the twenty-four hours of the pickled item that was later placed there. So too Magen Avraham wrote above in se'if 5, and he wrote there that it is possible to forbid through pickling only where it was simultaneous, so that at the very moment they removed the chametz, they placed the pickled item there. This is forced, for if so the Tur and Mechaber should have explained this and not written without qualification. Chok Yaakov wrote that in our case it is forbidden because of twenty-four hours, since there is some sharpness here. This too is forced, because the Tur wrote without qualification that with water their sharpness is nullified. Nevertheless, as halachah it appears that one should be stringent, for the Beis Hillel in siman 69 and Pri Chadash answered the difficulty of Taz and Shach by saying that a utensil standing with liquid never has its taste impaired; only when it is empty does it become impaired. According to them, the words of the Shulchan Aruch are halachically correct as written. So too wrote Sefer Ma'amar Mordechai. Although several later authorities copied the words of Taz and Shach as halachah, nevertheless it does not appear appropriate to be lenient ab initio, especially here where Chok Yaakov also wrote to forbid, and Mekor Chayim copied him. However, in pressing circumstances it appears that one may rely leniently even in this, for aside from this, the view of Chok Yaakov above in note 19 is that one forbids through pickling only in a utensil in which chametz is used abundantly, for then one must measure against all of it; otherwise we say that there is sixty against its emission. See there.
[8] It is permitted according to everyone. Meaning that although where they were not careful to cut them with a new knife there is room to permit according to some poskim, nevertheless several forbid because they hold that the sharpness of the olive improves the absorbed taste and makes it favorable, and they forbid. But when they were careful with the knife, even if they were not careful with the pot, there is no concern for a utensil that was not used that day, because through the water they put into the pot its sharpness is nullified [Ma'amar Mordechai]. This is obvious.
