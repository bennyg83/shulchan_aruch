It appears that this is the explanation: according to what I wrote, that Mordechai forbids because of pouring from a first vessel, but permits a second vessel even with actual chametz, the difficulty returns to its place: why does Mordechai forbid even cold? About this he said, "therefore it appears that Mordechai speaks where there are cracks." To me, the small one, Shimon Hirsch, it appears that this is what he means: it is proven from Hagahos Maimoniyos that he forbids because of pouring. For if it is because of a second vessel, then the words of Hagahos Maimoniyos contradict one another, since in chapter 1 the glosses wrote that one need not be concerned for a second vessel. In truth, in the gloss before us this is not in chapter 1; only the Beis Yosef brought that one need not be concerned for a second vessel in the name of the glosses. See there, but he did not explain which gloss he meant. If so, who are the "some who are stringent" about a second vessel that Rema brought? Hagahos Maimoniyos in the final chapter, and the Mordechai, who forbid scalding in a second vessel, do so because of pouring, and where do we know to forbid a second vessel? One cannot say that the need for pouring applies to extracting absorbed taste from a chametz utensil, and here through pouring he holds it releases taste, and from this there is proof to be stringent in a second vessel if the chametz is present. This is not so, for Mordechai forbids actual chametz even with cold food, and Hagahos Maimoniyos also forbids cold food when there is liquid. One must say that the "some who are stringent" refers to a chicken and where there is no liquid; if so, from where do we know that a second vessel forbids? To this he answered that Mordechai forbids the cold case only where there are cracks. Now one can properly say that the "some who are stringent" is Mordechai, and that when actual chametz is present, they forbid in a second vessel from the fact that he forbids pouring to extract taste from a utensil. If you will ask: if so, cold should also forbid where the chametz is present. One can say that cold does not forbid meat except where there are cracks, but the wheat kernel found in a chicken, which has no cracks, does not forbid the cold food. This is easy to understand.
