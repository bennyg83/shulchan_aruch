[8] Olives with which they were careful, etc. The Tur wrote: olives that were pickled during the rest of the year, according to Baal HaTerumah, who forbids radish and onion cut with a knife of idolatrous use, comparing them to asafoetida, etc., so that we measure against the whole knife, if so all the olives become chametz, etc. This is difficult for me: why should we not measure sixty against the knife? Certainly among all the olives there is sixty against it, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 96, regarding onions, se'if 2. Here too, when the olives absorbed from the knife, it was still permitted, and it is not comparable to a knife of idolatrous use in siman 96. Also at the end of his words the Tur wrote: it appears to me that even according to Baal HaTerumah, if they were careful with cutting by knife, etc. This implies that if they were not careful with the knife, there is no permission through nullification against the knife. It appears that this case of cutting olives must refer to before they were pickled, for then they are sharp, since the Tur wrote that through the pickling water their sharpness is nullified. Therefore the Tur compares them to onions. In Issur VeHeter HaAruch, klal 38, law 15, he wrote: furthermore, even regarding the prohibition of chametz, Orach Chayim does not forbid olives cut with a chametz knife unless they were cut after they were pickled; but if they were cut before they were pickled, he permits there eating them on Pesach. This is not found in our Tur; on the contrary, it is the opposite, as I wrote. Perhaps he cites Sefer Orchos Chayim, not Orach Chayim. Therefore it is understood here why sixty does not help to nullify, although in total there is sixty against the knife: since he takes each one separately before its pickling, and each individual one does not have sixty against the knife, it remains with what it received, and joining them does not help, for there is nothing here to join them and carry the taste from one to another. This is unlike there with onions, where the cooking liquid carries taste from one to another. But here, with pickling, each one is pickled separately through its placement in water; therefore joining does not help. It is not comparable to unripe grapes mentioned in the previous se'if, where all of them help to nullify the released taste of the mortar, as I wrote nearby, because there they were all pounded and mixed well; just as they received together, so too joining them helps to nullify the released taste. This appears correct to me. What he wrote here to permit even though they were not careful to pickle them in a new pot is because of the reason the Tur wrote, that their sharpness is nullified at the time of pickling, since at the time of pickling they put water in them, and one can no longer say that it strengthens them. Still, one must be careful about inserting the knife, for the reason we mentioned. In the Tur it is written: "and so it appears, etc." At first glance it is difficult: how does he bring proof from something cut on Pesach itself for what he wrote before regarding olives of the whole year? One can say that he brings proof only for this: that olives, because of their sourness, have the same law as asafoetida mentioned in the Gemara. For onion too resembles asafoetida, and the Rosh forbids if it was cut with an ordinary knife even though it is impaired flavor, while according to the Rosh impaired flavor is permitted, as the Tur wrote in his name. Rather, certainly it is forbidden because it is a sharp item.
