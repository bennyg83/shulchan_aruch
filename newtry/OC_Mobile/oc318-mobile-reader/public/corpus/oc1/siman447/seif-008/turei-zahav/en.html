Olives for which they were careful, etc. Tur wrote: olives pickled all year — according to Beit HaTov who forbids radish and onion with an old forbidden knife, comparing them to a wheat kernel, etc., for in the entire knife we measure — and therefore all the olives became chametz, etc. It is difficult: why do we not measure sixty against the knife, for certainly all the olives have sixty against it, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 96 regarding onions seif 2; for here too when the olives absorbed from the knife it was still permitted; and do not challenge the old knife of siman 96, for Tur at the end wrote: and it appears to me that even according to Beit HaTov, if they were careful to cut with a new knife, etc. — implying without care in the knife there is no leniency through nullification like the knife size; and it appears this cutting of olives speaks before they were pickled, when they are still sharp; or Tur wrote that through water at pickling their sharpness is nullified. And in Or Zaru'a Orach Chayim general rule 38 he wrote: and moreover regarding chametz prohibition, olives cut with a chametz knife do not forbid b'eyna except if cut after pickling; but if cut before pickling, he permits eating them on Pesach — not found so in our Tur, rather the opposite as above; perhaps he brought from a different Orchos Chaim, therefore it works here that sixty does not help to nullify whether all sixty against the knife, since each takes individually before pickling and there is not sixty in each individually against the knife — it stands in what it received and combination does not help, for there is nothing to combine them and carry taste from one to the other, unlike there with onions where cooking gravy carries from one to another; but here in pickling each is pickled individually through placing in water — therefore combination does not help. And do not challenge unripe grapes mentioned in the previous seif, that there the combination helps to nullify mortar discharge, as below, for there all were pounded and mixed well together as they received together — therefore combination helps to nullify discharge, as above — correct. And that which is written here to permit even if they were not careful to pickle in a new pot is because Tur wrote their sharpness is nullified at pickling time since water is placed on them — and one should not say afterward they become sharp again; rather one should be careful about inserting the knife for the reason we mentioned. In Tur: and so it appears, etc. — it is difficult how he brings proof from what was cut on Pesach itself to what was written above about olives of the whole year; one may say he brings proof only that olives' law is equal because of sharpness to a wheat kernel mentioned in the Gemara; for onion is also similar to a wheat kernel and Rosh forbids if cut with stam knife even though lifgam, as Tur wrote in his name; rather certainly forbidden because it is davar charif:
