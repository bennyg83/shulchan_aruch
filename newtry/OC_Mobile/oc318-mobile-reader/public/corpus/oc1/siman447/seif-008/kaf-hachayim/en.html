In any amount etc., and if a prohibition forbidden in any amount became mixed in another dish — which is two any-amounts — Taz concludes in Yoreh De'ah siman 92 subsection 16: if a piece was forbidden in any amount and fell into another pot and they removed it, its discharge does not forbid in any amount, for we do not say two any-amounts; but wet in wet that became mixed this way is forbidden forever in any amount — see there. And so Peri Chadash agreed with Taz; and so Rabbi Akiva Eiger concludes sign 1 to be lenient regarding a piece forbidden in any amount that fell into another dish when there is majority against it and they recognize the piece and removed it; but if they do not recognize it, all is forbidden — see there. However Shach in Nekudat HaKesef there challenged Taz's words regarding permitting a piece forbidden in any amount that fell into another pot, and wrote that so all decisors practice to forbid all mixtures — see there. And so agreed Erech HaShulchan in this siman sign 1 and wrote and not like Taz — see there. And so Magen Avraham in siman 467 subsection 13. Chok Yaakov in this siman sign 5 and siman 467 sign 27. Chak Yosef sign 6, Mateh Yehudah, and wrote that so is proven from Mechaber and Rama's words, and such is the custom in the city of Algezaid — see there. Razah sign 16. Chayei Adam general rule 121 sign 8. However in a doubt of rabbinic prohibition in any amount, Rav Beit Yehudah page 109 wrote a mixture of two is permitted, and so he agreed — see there. And so Rav Beit Din siman 563: if there is doubt that none of the chametz entered mixture B but all chametz remained in mixture A, according to all it is permitted — see there. And Beit Din Erech HaShulchan sign 4; however he wrote that from Magen Avraham's words subsection 26 it is implied that even if there is doubt perhaps the chametz remained in mixture A, we forbid — see there.
