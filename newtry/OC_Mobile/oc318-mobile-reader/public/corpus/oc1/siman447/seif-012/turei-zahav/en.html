[12] Lest it fall, etc. If kartin fell into food, the food is forbidden, as I wrote above regarding noksheh chametz, such as theriac and the like, in siman 442. But it is permitted to keep it until after Pesach, as appears to me. In the Tur he brought earlier in this siman: so too wrote Baal HaItur, specifically actual chametz; but the absorbed discharge that comes out of the walls of a utensil, regarding honey and oil of non-Jews on Pesach, etc., is nullified. For many days our master practiced prohibition with it, and one who is stringent will receive blessing. This is the end of his language. This is difficult: how is it nullified? It requires sixty to nullify it, and what is inside the utensil does not have sixty against the entire utensil, as the Beis Yosef wrote. It appears that HaItur follows his own reasoning, for in Yoreh De'ah, siman 93, he also holds that there is sixty against the emission of the utensil, as the Beis Yosef wrote according to his view there. Although the Tur does not hold this reasoning, he nevertheless brought his words because of other matters mentioned by HaItur, such as meat with whose salting one was not careful. It is also possible that he holds like what Mordechai wrote in chapter Kol Sha'ah in the name of Or Zarua, to permit rinsing a beer barrel with water, drying it, and placing wine and honey in it for Pesach, because the beer taste is nullified in sixty before Pesach, and we do not say that we measure against all of it, since it absorbed permitted matter. Rema ruled this way in siman 451.
