There: if there are not sixty one transgresses on it, and so Tur etc. — meaning Tur there wrote that if there is taste one is liable d'oraisa — see there in Magen Avraham subsection 1; and therefore he wrote that in min b'mino where there is no taste, if so one does not transgress on it, as also explained in that siman — permitted after Pesach if one retained it; but to mix and retain it, perhaps it is forbidden in Hagahot Maimoniyot, for even though everything one does not transgress d'oraisa from the Torah is permitted to mix and retain, this is specifically such as made for preservation and there is not in any case what is here that is less than sixty and against it in not its kind gives taste — therefore even though it is min b'mino it is forbidden to mix intentionally to retain, because of a decree lest one do so not in its kind; and he wrote perhaps therefore Mahari wrote that if one nullifies etc., for Tur challenged him: if it is a thing on which one transgresses d'oraisa, nullification does not help at all; and if one does not transgress, even to mix l'chatchila to retain is permitted; on this Magen Avraham wrote perhaps it deals in such a case where there is taste-giving amount, only it is min b'mino; and in such a case it is forbidden l'chatchila, as it appears to me; and see in siman 442 until "and if it became mixed less than a kezayit" etc. — these are not Bach's words but Magen Avraham's own words; and what he wrote "and if it became mixed" etc. — for this he intended in the gloss, it is from Bach who wrote that from Rama's language it is implied it deals even if he knew beforehand and transgressed and retained, redemption helps, and this means it deals when there is not a kezayit of chametz; not so Hagahot Maimoniyot who wrote we require it was not known, dealing when there is a kezayit of chametz from the chametz.
