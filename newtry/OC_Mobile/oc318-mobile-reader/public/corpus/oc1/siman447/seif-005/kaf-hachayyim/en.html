5) There. Even the slightest amount, etc. This is not necessarily literal; an amount less than one sixtieth is called the slightest amount. Alternatively, it means the slightest amount that improves the mixture and causes it to sell for more than it would previously have sold. Otherwise, the Shulchan Aruch writes below in siman 467, se'if 10, that a cooked chicken in which a split wheat kernel is found during Pesach may be sold, whereas here it states that the slightest amount prohibits benefit. The distinction must therefore be as explained. Peri Chadash. Ma'amar Mordechai there, subsection 16, writes that in the case of the wheat kernel, the Shulchan Aruch relies upon Rosh's opinion that wheat kernels, even when split, are not definite chametz, and therefore rules leniently regarding benefit.
