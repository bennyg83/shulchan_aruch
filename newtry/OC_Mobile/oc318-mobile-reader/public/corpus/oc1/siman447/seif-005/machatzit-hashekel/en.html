And they answer: since it is not yet the time of its prohibition nor the time of its removal, perhaps he will forget. And they challenge: let him nullify it at the sixth hour when he is occupied burning it; and they answer: since from the sixth hour it is forbidden d'rabbanan, it is not in his possession and he cannot nullify, for R' Giddel, R' Chiyya bar R' Yehudah — one who betroths a woman from the sixth hour onward, even with Kordani wheat (which is chametz nokshah), we are not concerned for the betrothal. Rashi explained from the sixth hour and onward — from the beginning of six; and Rabbenu Tam proved that R' Giddel speaks from the end of six, i.e., the beginning of seven when chametz is forbidden from the Torah; and nevertheless he properly brings proof that it is not in his possession even at the beginning of seven when full chametz is forbidden from the Torah; and nevertheless Kordani wheat is nokshah, and he holds nokshah prohibition is only d'rabbanan; and therefore he derives full chametz from the sixth hour, whose time causes it to be d'rabbanan, from nokshah from the seventh hour onward, when full chametz is forbidden from the Torah but Kordani wheat which is nokshah is d'rabbanan. Maharil holds like Rabbenu Tam's explanation, and see the Gemara compares a matter that has leniency in itself to another matter that has this leniency from the time; therefore Maharil holds nokshah on Pesach (as Maharil holds) like Poskim that nokshah is forbidden from the Torah — nevertheless it has no karet, similar to full chametz from erev Pesach from the sixth hour onward, where one may also say this leniency that it has no karet. And this is from the time, for it is still before Pesach; and as full chametz from the sixth hour onward is nullified in sixty because it has no karet from the time — so too nokshah on Pesach, which has no karet in itself, is also nullified in sixty. That which Maharil compared chametz from the sixth hour onward which is d'rabbanan — is borrowed language, for he holds like Rabbenu Tam who explains from the seventh hour, i.e., beginning of seven when forbidden from the Torah; and Maharil who wrote from the sixth hour onward that it is d'rabbanan means from the beginning of six, for he relies on the Gemara and they are nullified at six — meaning at the beginning of six; and on this they answer since from the sixth hour it is forbidden d'rabbanan, it is not in his possession:
