From the fact that he asks, "does it enter your mind," there is proof that this is how he explained the words of the Talmud. If we explain it simply, that the questioner understood "they roasted them in two pots" to mean pot-roast, then why does he ask simply, "does it enter your mind?" According to the first tanna of Rebbi on page 41, pot-roast is permitted. Rather, perforce the questioner held that when it answers here that they roasted them in two pots, it means cooking; for otherwise, it should still be forbidden because the smell exits upward. Therefore he asks, "does it enter your mind," because cooking is certainly impossible for the Pesach offering. However, according to what Tosafos wrote on page 41, that even according to the first tanna, pot-roast is invalid, and there is also a positive commandment of "only roasted by fire," then even if it speaks of roasting them in two pots, the question "does it enter your mind" is properly asked simply.
