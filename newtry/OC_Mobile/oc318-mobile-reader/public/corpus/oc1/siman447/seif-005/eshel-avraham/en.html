And in karpas. See explained well what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham, and from this R' Mendel wanted to forbid even to retain them. And in responsa Shev Yaakov 19 he learned advocacy for the custom of Israel that we are not at all strict to sell them on Pesach, even those made with garlic cut with a chametz knife — from that which is in the gloss itself: "and likewise if a minute amount of these things became mixed" etc., that one need not be stringent in the mixture — see there. And Magen Avraham wrote the reason is ta'am bar ta'am, and also perhaps the vessel was not ben yomo. Nevertheless this requires study, for in any case one may rely b'dieved on stam vessels that are not ben yomo and ta'am bar ta'am etc., and he concludes that what Rama wrote "if a minute amount became mixed" is only regarding fat, for when much became mixed it is certainly forbidden because of concern for chametz crumbs inside; but cooked wine etc. — see there. It turns out that even if there are not sixty against the chametz in the vessel in which it was cooked, it is permitted because it mixed on Pesach, since stam vessels are not ben yomo and it is ta'am bar ta'am — automatically they are permitted to retain, for after all prohibition in any amount even in ben yomo is lighter than a vessel that is not ben yomo and where there are not sixty against the prohibition — see Yoreh De'ah siman 99 and siman 102 seif 5; and even though Taz there argues to be stringent, nevertheless at least the reasoning is clear, and if so since ta'am bar ta'am in not ben yomo even without sixty against prohibition is permitted, all the more so in any amount even in ben yomo where it is ta'am bar ta'am, permitted on the last day of Pesach, and likewise to retain them. And one could say that its sharpness neutralizes chametz taste; nevertheless regarding absorption it is only any amount — see Tosafot Pesachim. And even though we hold stam knives have fat smeared on their surface, nevertheless chametz is not usually on the surface; and see in Darkei Moshe what he wrote in the name of responsum of Rosh regarding an onion cut with a knife that he was not concerned at all that chametz was smeared on the surface; and Magen Avraham subsection 37. And furthermore otherwise there is reason for the custom because many Poskim hold that garlic and the like are not called a sharp thing — see Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 96; and there is at least room to rely on them and retain it, especially regarding rabbinic prohibition in any amount — see there.
