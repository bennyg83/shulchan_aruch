The reason is that the concern for leavening in salt that was salted is nullified in sixty before Pesach and does not return and revive; and if so, the author did not need to write it is permitted, since above he wrote there are those who disagree and hold it returns and revives — it appears the Shulchan Aruch ruled like the lenient view, for here there were two leniencies: perhaps there was no chametz in the salt at all; and if there was, say it was nullified before Pesach Per the lenient view; and those who disagree whom Rama brought is R' Yitzchak Gaon whom Tur brought — who forbids cheese milked by an Israelite before Pesach if not known they were careful about leavening, because R' Yitzchak Gaon holds chametz returns and revives on Pesach; and if so, why did Rama write the custom is to be stringent, since in seif 4 he wrote the custom follows the first view? One must explain the opposite: the reason for the custom to be stringent here is that the salt is visible on these items, and perhaps there is chametz visible among the salt, therefore rinsing does not help:

(ג) Three times before Pesach. Meaning, in a vessel that is not chametz l'chatchila; b'dieved it helps even in a chametz vessel, for cold does not discharge — and Rama mentioned only meat; and it appears the same law applies to fish, that they may be permitted through rinsing like meat, except I have not seen practice to permit fish through rinsing. One may give a reason: Semag wrote — however people are accustomed not to eat aged cheese or salted fish, perhaps because they are accustomed to eat them without rinsing; but salted meat requires rinsing — end of his words; it appears this is because since people are accustomed to eat fish without rinsing, there is concern they will eat them without rinsing, unlike meat. Nevertheless, once there was a shortage of meat and I permitted rinsing fish for Pesach, for it appears to me they did not forbid fish except those salted recently, unlike fish salted in barrels that cannot be without rinsing — this is preferable to meat; but cheese plainly rinsing does not help and is like korkshita. Moharach wrote in siman 451 that if meat was given after they kashered it in a vessel used with chametz for twenty-four hours, the meat is forbidden for Pesach, for it is like pickled like cooked; and I wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 that one may not forbid because of pickled like cooked in a prohibited vessel, for it is lifgam after the twenty-four-hour period which is pickling time, and lifgam is permitted before Pesach; and it appears to me dry meat hanging in a room where flour is managed is chametz b'eyna and rinsing does not help:

(ד) But in fat from rendering. Beit Yosef challenged: behold we rule stam vessels are not ben yomo, and he elaborated; and it appears to me that here we do not say so, for I already wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 122 in the name of Darkei Moshe in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot that an Israelite has remedy through asking, and if he does not know he is penalized; and it appears there is no penalty except for that Israelite whose pot it is, who should have known and did not; but not for another — end of Darkei Moshe. And it appears that although this view is not agreed for halacha as Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 8, nevertheless for Pesach all hold thus, for every person is accustomed to be careful in everything needed for Pesach when preparing to guard from leavening; therefore one who rendered fat in every cooked item should have been careful that the pan is certainly not ben yomo — therefore he is penalized to forbid his cooked food from eating on Pesach, as above — this is correct. And it appears that in preserves, which are sharp and acidic, this is forbidden even in other prohibitions even if not ben yomo; therefore one should be stringent even on the last Yom Tov:
