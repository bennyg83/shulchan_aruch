[5] It is forbidden by law if they were not careful, etc., and did not melt, etc. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 21, who explains Rema's words that it is permitted by law only if they were careful about both matters. This requires explanation: why with cheeses did he not suspect, after the fact, because of a chametz crumb, but only as a stringency ab initio? I saw in Sefer Mateh Yehudah that he wrote at length about this, and his view is that the main reason Rema wrote it is forbidden by law is only because of concern for emission from a chametz utensil, but he forced Rema's language greatly; see there. In my humble opinion, only with fat did Rema become stringent even after the fact because of chametz crumbs, similar to what Chok Yaakov wrote in se'if katan 30 in the name of Maharit, that the way is to gather, etc.; see there. Therefore chametz crumbs are common in it. But regarding other items, Rema in truth mentioned only concern for a chametz utensil. So too it is proven in Darchei Moshe, who permits preserves if they were made in a designated utensil, and he wrote explicitly that we are not concerned for chametz crumbs. The later authorities also copied this without qualification. From Magen Avraham in se'if katan 26 it also implies that only with fat are we concerned for crumbs even after the fact, but not with cooked wine and the like; see there. However, from Chok Yaakov in se'if katan 33 it sounds that he does not distinguish in this, and see Shulchan Aruch HaRav, note 47. This requires examination.
[5] And likewise any item, etc. Know that some poskim hold that specifically regarding fat, where the pan in which it is melted is constantly used, and likewise regarding pots that are constantly used, we presume it was used that day until you know that it was not. But with other utensils we say that presumably it was not used that day. This is the view of Maharit, and Kenesses HaGedolah agreed with him, and so did Pri Chadash. Nevertheless, from the unqualified language of the Shulchan Aruch and the later authorities it sounds that because of the stringency of chametz we are stringent in every case. Regarding mixtures, Magen Avraham in se'if katan 26 is lenient with wine and other matters except for fat, because crumbs are not normally mixed in them, even if they became mixed in less than sixty. The view of Chok Yaakov in se'if katan 32 sounds that with all items one should be lenient only in a case of great loss. It appears that one may rely leniently on Magen Avraham's view, since Maharit and those who assist him are lenient with ordinary utensils even without a mixture, as above. Likewise with fat, one may rely on Chok Yaakov's view to be lenient in a case of great loss, for several later authorities copied his words as halachah [Shulchan Aruch HaRav, Magen Elef, and Mekor Chayim]. It also sounds there in the Biur HaGra that the core law is a stringency; see there.

I heard people say the reason: perhaps it is counterfeit, from something over which boiling water was already poured and then dried. But in this case both reasons of Rabbeinu Tam apply brought in Beis Yosef: what basis is there to presume prohibition, and also, we taste it all year, etc. In this case there is no concern that the counterfeiting was done on Pesach and that there is a smallest amount in it, for it comes from a distant land. [5] He is obligated to destroy it. Regarding the wine, whether it is permitted to drink it: at first glance the matter is simple. Before two months, when it has already dried and does not impart flavor to the wine, he should empty the wine from the barrel, and it is permitted to drink it, and all the more so to keep it. If it is within two months, when it imparts flavor into the wine and Rema forbids drinking it, regarding keeping it depends on the two answers in Magen Avraham, se'if katan 30. According to the first answer it is forbidden, and according to the second answer it is permitted. It appears that the second answer is the main one.
