Dried meat, cheese, and fish that were salted before Pesach and one was not careful with them — it is permitted to eat them on Pesach. However, salted fish that are soaked in water on Pesach in a chametz vessel, one should be stringent to be wary of them, because on Pesach they absorb from the discharge of the vessels, and chametz on Pesach [forbids] in any amount. {Rama: And some disagree and are stringent; and in these lands the custom is to be stringent lechatchilah not to eat cheeses, fish, and dried meat. But if they rinsed the meat three times before Pesach, the custom is to eat it; and for intestines, rinsing does not help, and therefore one should not soak the dried [ones] (Mahari Bruna). Bedieved, one need not be stringent with these. But fat that was melted in a chametz vessel is forbidden by law, if they were not careful during its preparation regarding chametz, and if they melted it in a chametz vessel that was used within the day (Mordechai, beginning of chapter "Kol Sha'ah"; Semak; Hagahos Maimoni, chapter 1). And likewise anything cooked in a chametz vessel — such as cooked wine, or preserves, and the like — is forbidden on Pesach; but on the last Yom Tov there is room to be lenient with it (Piskei Mahari, siman 177). And the same applies if a minute amount of these items became mixed into the food — one should not be stringent to forbid the mixture, as above. Some are stringent about drawing from wine-vinegar that is used throughout the year, for we are concerned lest one put into it some of what remained from a meal, and at times there are bread crumbs in it (Maharil); but in these places, where wine-vinegar is not common, I have not seen [anyone] be stringent about this. Some are further stringent lechatchilah not to fill [vessels] with wine or wine-vinegar within thirty days of Pesach in a chametz vessel; and if they filled it within thirty days, the custom is not to drink it on Pesach (Responsa of Mahari Bruna); but one who is lenient loses nothing, and all the more so in a place where wine and vinegar are not common — so it appears to me. A wine barrel whose boards were sealed with dough: if it is within two months before Pesach, it is still soft and imparts taste on Pesach, and it is forbidden to drink it; and if they put it there before then, it has already dried out and does not impart taste on Pesach, and it is permitted (Mordechai, beginning of chapter "Kol Sha'ah"; Semak; Hagahos Maimoni, chapter 1). However, if there is a kezayis of dough in one place, one is obligated to remove it, even though it is made to strengthen [the barrel] (Piskei Mahari, siman 149), as above, siman 442, se'if 7.}
