That which causes spreading, etc. This implies that if there is a substance that causes spreading, even though the prohibition is lean, nevertheless the permitted fattening substance strengthens it. So too, matzos smeared with oil that touched the place that was not folded are permitted, for an absorbed prohibition does not emerge, etc. Magen Avraham. Chok Yaakov objected that this is not called an absorbed prohibition, since in any event the prohibition in this matzah is from itself. End quote. I say that this reasoning is explicit in the Maharshal, whom I cited in siman 460, s.k. 16, that it is considered an absorbed prohibition. Now, what Magen Avraham wrote accords with the answer of the Maharshal and the Perishah that the Shach wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 105, s.k. 18. But the answer of Darchei Moshe, that with a completely lean prohibition we do not say that the permitted substance strengthens it, is difficult from here, for chametz is completely lean, and nevertheless he wrote, and there is no substance there that causes spreading. In Chok Yaakov he answered that he holds that only there, with salting, one may distinguish regarding something completely lean, unlike heat. End quote. I objected to him that there in Yoreh Deah it explicitly speaks of items roasted together, and nevertheless he permits when it is completely lean. In my humble opinion, Darchei Moshe explains that what is written here, and there is no substance that causes spreading, means gravy. Also regarding what the Taz wrote, that it refers to what he wrote that challah that is hot with hot without gravy, namely with meat, etc., there is no difficulty at all, for it does not refer to chametz at all. However, in my work on Yoreh Deah there I raised many other difficulties against Darchei Moshe, and therefore the main ruling follows Magen Avraham. More than this requires examination: in siman 461, s.k. 12, Magen Avraham forbids even where it touched on one side, and so too his conclusion implies, since he concluded that its law is as the Rema wrote in siman 467 se'if 14 regarding salting. End quote. It appears to me to be lenient in this, for one should not be so stringent with folded matzah, since it is not mentioned by the early halachic decisors, as I wrote in siman 461.
