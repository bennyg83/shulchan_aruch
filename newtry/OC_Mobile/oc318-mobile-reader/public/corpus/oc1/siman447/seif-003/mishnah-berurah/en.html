(18) That was found on erev Pesach. Even after the sixth hour.
(19) It is permitted. It may even be eaten on Pesach, provided that it cooled before Pesach, as explained below.
(20) It is nullified in sixty. The text should read, “it is nullified in sixty.” I found this version in the Shulchan Aruch printed with Nachalas Tzvi and in the first edition printed by the Mechaber himself. The reading in later editions is corrupt. The poskim write that the same applies when a kernel of wheat is found on erev Pesach in matzah baked that day. It is nullified in sixty even if the kernel was not removed, provided that the matzah cooled before Pesach. The matzah may then be eaten, but the kernel must be removed, even during Pesach.
(21) While the kernel remains inside it. If the kernel was removed and the chicken was then reheated, it is permitted. Although some say in seif 4 that a prohibition becomes significant again on Pesach, that applies where actual prohibited substance remains in the mixture, such as beer mixed with honey. Here only absorbed taste remains.
(22) It again imparts taste. Even according to the opinion in seif 4 that a prohibition does not become significant again, that applies only when the prohibited substance is not visibly present. Here the chametz itself remains present.
(23) Regarding reheating in a kli sheni, etc. For example, boiling water was poured into a bowl and the chicken was placed in it.
(24) There is no concern. This opinion holds that a kli sheni cannot cause taste to be released and absorbed. If a boiling piece of meat was placed in a chametz bowl, one should be stringent, since the poskim write in Yoreh Deah siman 94 that a thick food of this kind is always treated as a kli rishon as long as it remains at yad soledes bo heat. It is possible that the same applies if it was transferred from a bowl to a plate.
(25) On Pesach. Although the primary ruling in Yoreh Deah siman 68 is that a kli sheni neither releases nor absorbs taste, regarding Pesach one should be concerned for the stringent authorities there who hold that it does.
(26) It is proper to be stringent, etc. If the kernel did not split but merely softened, and there would be substantial loss or diminished Yom Tov joy, one may rely on the lenient view.
(27) If it is at yad soledes bo heat. Although the Rama in siman 467 seif 12 appears to forbid meat that soaked even in cold water when a kernel of wheat was found there, Magen Avraham explains that the case there concerns meat containing cracks, which becomes prohibited through the water entering those cracks. Here the meat has an intact surface, and therefore becomes prohibited only through hot water, which is considered hot only at yad soledes bo heat. The water itself, however, is prohibited because of the kernel. Bigdei Yesha disagrees. In his view, the plain meaning of the Rama here is that cold liquid does not cause absorption at all. Rather, the Rama here speaks of the strict law, under which it is proper to consider the stringent opinion, whereas the ruling there reflects a custom to be stringent even with cold liquid. Regarding a custom, one who is lenient in pressing circumstances has not lost.
