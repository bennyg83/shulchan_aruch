In Yoreh De'ah 108:1 in the gloss, the Rav brought a dispute where the oven does not hold twelve esronim, is closed, and both items are uncovered: whether it is forbidden with chametz or permitted. In a case of loss it is permitted (see Chok Yaakov here, the Heitev Moshe, and my Pri Megadim there). But where the oven is large or one item is covered, not only where it is open, in which case with other prohibitions it is permitted ab initio and only the custom is to forbid ab initio, as the Rav wrote there, so we learn that there is not even a smallest amount, for otherwise one may not nullify a prohibition ab initio even without intent to nullify when there is no pressure and the bees' legs in honey are impossible otherwise. If so, the same applies to chametz, that at least after the fact it is permitted. Even in a large oven that is closed, where ab initio it is forbidden there, see Taz there, note 2, and perhaps the same applies in a small open oven: after the fact it is permitted even with chametz. What he wrote, that smell is not relevant to bread, see Magen Avraham 461:1, that smell with no fattiness at all does not forbid. See Toras Chatas, klal 38, law 3, where the reason is that both are lean. This implies that chametz bread with a cooked food forbids the cooked food, because it fattens, etc., and afterward even forbids matzah in the oven. See Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah 461 in Hagahos Beis Yosef, note 10, and see Mishneh LeMelech, Hilchos Ma'aseh HaKorbanos, chapter 13, halachah 2, and Pri Chadash 461. The Levush in 461:5 wrote that such a dry smell does not travel far, implying that the reason is that it is dry, and even if there is oil in the oven, it does not fatten the prohibition. Even according to the view that it fattens with prohibition, it does not do so with smell. This is well understood, for Rabbeinu Tam brought proof from the loaves of the thanksgiving offering, even though they had oil in them. Minchas Yaakov in Toras Chatas, klal 35, note 11, says oil is not fat and does not fatten. In my Pri Megadim to Yoreh De'ah I wrote at length that one can say oil does fatten. According to the Levush's words, it is well understood. So too it appears from the incident brought by Mordechai in chapter Kol Sha'ah, copied by the Beis Yosef in 461. See Taz 104:10, that permitted matter does not fatten forbidden matter; nevertheless with smell it is stronger and fattens in the oven, see there, and in 108:1. See Shach 108:15, and this requires examination. With Hashem's help this will be explained further in Magen Avraham 4.
