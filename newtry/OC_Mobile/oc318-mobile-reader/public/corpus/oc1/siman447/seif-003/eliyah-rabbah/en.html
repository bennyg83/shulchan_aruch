Levush: And the utensils are kept, etc. Even if a food that was entirely chametz was cooked in them, he should merely put them away in a room where they are not used, and that is enough. If they became mixed with Yom Tov utensils, the Bach wrote that since some say chametz is also a davar sheyesh lo matirin, it is not nullified. End quote. So wrote Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah and Magen Avraham in his name. What Chok Yaakov wrote, that there is a scribal error, as is proven in the Bach, and one must read, since some say that a utensil, etc., is not compelled; to one who examines it, this is clear. According to this, for us, who hold that chametz is not a davar sheyesh lo matirin, it is nullified. However, Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote another reason, because Maharil and Ra'ah forbid even with other prohibitions in utensils, and so wrote Magen Avraham. In my work on Yoreh Deah siman 102, I wondered at Maharil from Tosafos, Bava Metzia folio 13, and from Avodah Zarah folio 73, s.v. tevel, etc. Furthermore, even according to Maharil it implies that earthenware utensils, for which hagalah is ineffective, are nullified; see there at length. Magen Avraham wrote: on Pesach itself it is obvious that it is forbidden, for it is no better than other dry with dry. End quote. But Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that they were not stringent to forbid dry with dry by a mashehu in these utensils as a matter of law; a utensil forbidden by absorbed mashehu is nullified, and even if it absorbed from actual chametz, one may be lenient when it is not ben yomo. Nevertheless, one should remove one utensil from the mixture. As for Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah's question against the Bach, that flavor without substance is not a davar sheyesh lo matirin, it is astonishing, for the Bach wrote explicitly that its flavor is also a davar sheyesh lo matirin, as the Shach wrote in siman 102, s.k. 9.
