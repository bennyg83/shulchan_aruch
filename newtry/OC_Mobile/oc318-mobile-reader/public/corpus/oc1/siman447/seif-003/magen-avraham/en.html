One might say that in siman 467 seif 12 he concludes to forbid even cold, and one must say that there it deals with water that is moist and imparts taste; but here it deals with a case where they removed the chicken from the pot near evening and placed it in a bowl without gravy, and when Pesach entered it was still boiling — then it is not forbidden unless it reached yad soledes bo, since there is no gravy there; and in Hagahot Maimoniyot at the end of chapter 5 of the laws of Pesach he wrote, and this is his language: a peel does not suffice except for something whose prohibition is noten ta'am, but chametz, which is b'mashahu, is forbidden even in roasting (see above seif 1); and so too Rabbenu Shabtai and Mahari of Strasbourg forbid even only through rinsing in a kli sheini — end of his words; and afterward he wrote that wheat found in water, even cold, the water is forbidden — end of his words; if so, they contradict each other, and one cannot say that in the first part it deals with [a case] without gravy, for he wrote rinsing in a kli rishon, and it is impossible to rinse in a kli rishon except in water; and one must say that in cold they forbid only the water, but if meat is soaked in it they do not forbid it, for cold lacks the power to absorb into it so much; but in hot everything is forbidden, and so it appears from his language that he wrote the water is forbidden; but if afterward they cooked a dish in that water, they certainly forbid the dish; but Rama, who wrote in siman 467 to forbid also the meat that remained in the water — if so, one must say as we said;
