The Rav wrote in his responsum, siman 28, that where there is another ground for leniency, one may perhaps rely on what the Mordechai wrote, that one may rely on the She’iltot and be lenient to nullify chametz in sixty like other prohibitions. So too it is written in Responsa Mishpat Binyamin, siman 69, and it is also cited in Magen Avraham below, siman 467, seif katan 3. Nevertheless, in practice it appears from their words that one does not rely on this unless there are many additional grounds for leniency.

By chametz, in any amount. If it became mixed again into a second mixture in any amount, so that there are two any-amount mixtures, it is permitted. So the Taz concludes in Yoreh Deah, siman 93, seif katan 15, and below, siman 467, seif katan 16. The Shach himself wrote similarly in Yoreh Deah, siman 134, seif katan 16. In Nekudot HaKesef he challenged the Taz and labored to reconcile his own words. See what my brother-in-law, the Rav, wrote in Sefer Even HaOzer to uphold the view of the Taz. See Olat Shabbos and Magen Avraham below, siman 467, seif katan 13, where it is clear that their view is primary for halacha, like the Shach. The Shach wrote there in Nekudot HaKesef that this ruling has spread among all halachic decisors to be stringent even with two any-amount mixtures. But liquid mixed with liquid forbids even with two any-amount mixtures, and so the Taz concludes in Yoreh Deah, siman 92. See there; and the Pri Chadash agrees with him in Orach Chaim in this siman.
