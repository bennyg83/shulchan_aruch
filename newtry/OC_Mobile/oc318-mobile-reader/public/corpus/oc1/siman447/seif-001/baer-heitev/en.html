(א) In any amount. By d'oraisa law, chametz is like other prohibited substances, but the Sages were stringent regarding it since there is karet liability associated with it and one is not accustomed to avoiding it throughout the entire year. The Rav wrote in his responsum siman 28 that where there is an additional ground for leniency, it may be possible to rely on what the Mordechai wrote — that one may rely on the Sheiltot, and that there is room to be lenient and to nullify chametz with sixty parts, as with other prohibited substances. And so too it is written in responsa 42, siman 69, and it was also cited by Magen Avraham below in siman 467, note 33. Nevertheless, in practice, it appears from their words that one does not rely on this unless there are many additional grounds for leniency. Chayei Adam. The Ari, of blessed memory, wrote: One who is scrupulous about any amount of chametz on Pesach is assured that he will not sin throughout the entire year.

(ב) For benefit. Chayei Adam wrote that in a case of great financial loss or at a time of pressing need, one who is lenient to permit it for benefit and to cast the monetary equivalent of the chametz benefit mixed therein into the Dead Sea has not lost, for he has grounds to rely [see there], and see in Responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 86. And if a prohibition forbidden in any amount became mixed into another cooked dish — which constitutes two minute amounts — Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 92 infers that if the piece was forbidden in any amount and fell into another pot and they removed it, its discharge does not forbid in any amount, for we do not say two minute amounts; but moist in moist mixed in this manner is forbidden forever in any amount. And see Taz siman 467 note 17. And similarly Shach himself wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 134 note 16, and in Nekudat HaKesef he objected to Taz and labored to reconcile his own words, and Or Zarua ruled to be lenient like Taz — and see Peri To'elet and in Magen Avraham siman 467 note 13 it is explained that their view is the primary halacha like Shach's opinion, and Shach wrote in Nefesh HaChayim that thus spread the ruling among all halachic decisors stringently regarding two minute amounts. And know that moist in moist is forbidden as two minute amounts even according to Taz, as wrote (and in Sefer Or LiYesharim he proved with clear proofs and practical halacha: in a piece forbidden in any amount that fell into another cooked dish — when there is a majority against it, when they identify the piece they remove it; but if they do not identify it, everything is forbidden, and the absorbed quantity in any amount forbids in any amount):

(ג) And to sell. See Shach Yoreh De'ah siman 136 that it is forbidden to sell to a gentile except the value of the prohibition therein — whenever the body of the d'oraisa prohibition itself is moist and mixed within it. Chayei Adam [see there]:

(ו) The permeating. It is implied that if there is a permeating substance, even if only the permitted substance is fatty, we say that it travels and infuses flavor even into lean material, constituting a prohibition that arises from itself. And so too Magen Avraham ruled stringently (and Peri Chadash in Yoreh Deah siman 105 disagrees, on the reasoning that what has been made forbidden cannot render other things forbidden except in a place where the forbidden substance can travel) regarding a prohibition that arises from itself. Therefore he wrote that matzot that were smeared with fat, and one of them touched another at the place where it was folded and became chametz, renders the entire companion matzah forbidden. However, if it touched the other side at a place where it was not folded, it is permitted, for a forbidden substance that is absorbed does not exit from one piece to another without liquid, even if the permitted substance is fatty. And even though such a case constitutes a prohibition that arises from itself, nevertheless, regarding a folded matzah which is only a chumra, Chayei Adam agreed with Magen Avraham. See Magen Avraham. And it appears to me that excision is required — see Chayei Adam. If one transgressed and cooked it without peeling where peeling was required, the Or Zarua wrote that according to what we rule regarding other forbidden substances — that sixty parts against the peel are required — if so, one should forbid it in chametz on Pesach in any amount. And it appears to me that one should at least permit it for benefit. Chayei Adam, and see siman 467, seif 14, in Magen Avraham.

(ז) Scent. The Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 108 that regarding the halacha, in a case of significant financial loss one may rely on the lenient opinions. And this implies that this leniency applies even in a small oven that is completely sealed, but if the oven is large, or a small one that is open, it is permitted even without loss. And Magen Avraham wrote that specifically when the oven is slightly open at the place where the smoke exits, it is permitted in a small oven in a case of significant financial loss — see there. And Chayei Adam is lenient in accordance with the opinion of Rama in Yoreh Deah, unless the permitted food is a sharp substance — see there, as even in a large and open oven it is forbidden even in a case of significant financial loss. And know that 'sharp substance' refers to where the majority of the cooked dish is sharp, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 122, seif 3. And specifically when the permitted food is a sharp substance does it become forbidden, which is not so when the forbidden substance is a sharp substance — one need not be stringent. See Chayei Adam. Rama further wrote that if one of the foods is covered, even with ordinary dough, it is permitted. And Magen Avraham wrote that all of this applies to roasting, but regarding cooking where one cooked it on a weekday for one or two hours and then seals the oven temporarily, one need not be so stringent b'dieved as one is regarding Shabbat insulation. And Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that a large oven, even if it does not hold twelve esronim, is permitted b'dieved, and if there is a hole on the side in addition to the opening, it is permitted l'chatchila, but it is difficult to permit this in front of the masses — end of his words — and it is possible that one may rely on this in a case of loss. A case occurred where the oven was sealed with a board that is placed before chametz all year without rinsing, and the Gaon Maharash of Prague ruled it permitted. Issur V'heter wrote in principle 39 that it is forbidden to smell warm bread on Pesach, even that belonging to a non-Jew.
