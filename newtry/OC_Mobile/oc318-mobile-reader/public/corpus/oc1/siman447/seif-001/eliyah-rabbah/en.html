(1) In any amount, etc. And regarding if a prohibition forbidden in any amount became mixed into another dish — Taz elaborated in Yoreh De'ah siman 92 and concludes: when a piece was forbidden in any amount and returned and fell into another and they removed it, its discharge does not forbid in any amount; but moist in moist mixed this way is forever forbidden in any amount — end of his words. In Nikudat HaKesef he elaborated to distinguish — it is wondrous: all decisors forbid, for anyway that minute taste in the piece since mixed with another force is not its kind relative to other pieces though mixed, but on Pesach forbidden in any amount even not in its kind — end briefly. In my humble opinion it is greatly strained to explain this as not in its kind since all is one meat species; I looked in Tosafot Chullin daf 100 s.v. keshekadim and found per my opinion proven from what Tosafot wrote in the name of Rabbenu Ephraim: we say a piece became nevelah — meaning the permitted became forbidden; if so in other prohibitions the permitted does not become like forbidden at all; and since the prohibition does not impart taste in the piece it has no power to spread — therefore Rabbenu Ephraim explained we say piece became nevelah only in meat and milk; if so in other prohibitions no "piece became nevelah"; and when it does not impart taste, e.g. there are sixty in the piece, Chazal were not stringent to say piece became nevelah — wanted to say the permitted becomes like actual prohibition; if so the prohibition is only due to minute amount and that minute amount has no power to spread — so appears clear in Tosafot; and so in Rashba 101b and Mishmeret HaBayit 94 and Terumat HaDeshen siman 51 and Rosh; and what he relied on in Ran end of perek Gid HaNasheh — even in min b'mino not nullified — we did not hear so; rather my explanation: R' Yehuda was stringent only on actual prohibition minute amount mixed; but if before mixing there is also permitted taste it is not so severe that it should not be nullified — and that is how Ran explained and wrote "another taste of permitted" — end; and if so all the more on Pesach where prohibition in any amount is only rabbinic; and further it appears from Ran's opening "they strained and answered" — therefore I wrote Tosafot's answer seems correct to me and all great ones wrote Tosafot's answer and so we hold. What Nikudat HaKesef wondered on Taz — why only if it fell into another and they removed it; even if they did not remove it does it not forbid other pieces — he elaborated scribal error — end. In my opinion Taz's words as plain meaning: excluding if one does not recognize the piece — then since the piece itself is forbidden as Tosafot there, and moist forbids only the first piece, etc., all forbidden for no nullification on Pesach even dry in dry; and so I proved in Eliyahu Zuta from Tosafot and Rosh at length; I also wondered there on Rosh Avodah Zarah — not written here except what touches halachah. What Nikudat HaKesef wrote from siman 467 note 12 that Rama wrote if mixed with others be lenient from doubt — seems not proof; one may say when the other dish lacks majority against the pieces; or Rama refers to mixture moist in moist — we permit on rabbinic doubt; and even if Rama holds so, nevertheless I proved above Tosafot and great poskim to permit; Taz strengthened his words in Orach Chayim and wrote a peg so it will not move. Conclusion: be lenient when a piece forbidden in any amount fell into another dish with majority against it when they recognize the piece and removed it; but if not recognized all forbidden; and a spoon absorbing from minute amount forbids in any amount.
