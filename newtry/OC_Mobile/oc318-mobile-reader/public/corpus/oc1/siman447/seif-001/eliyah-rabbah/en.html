With a mashehu, etc. Regarding a prohibition that became forbidden by a mashehu and was then mixed into another dish, the Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 92 discusses this at length and concludes as follows: when a piece became forbidden by a mashehu and then fell into another mixture, and they removed it, what it emits does not forbid by a mashehu; but a liquid mixture that became mixed in this way is forever forbidden by a mashehu. End quote. In Nekudas HaKesef he argues at length, saying that this is astonishing, for all halachic decisors forbid it, since in any case that mashehu of flavor in the piece, once it is mixed with another force, is considered a different type relative to the other pieces, for the others are not mixed; and on Pesach chametz forbids by a mashehu even not with its own type. These are his words in brief. In my humble opinion, it is very forced to explain this as one type with another type, since all of it is one kind of meat. I looked at Tosafos, Chullin folio 100, s.v. when it preceded, etc., and in my humble opinion I found proof from what Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim: wherever we say that the piece becomes like a neveilah, the meaning is that the permitted part becomes forbidden; therefore, even if this prohibition itself lacks power to spread, the permitted part that became forbidden spreads. For this reason Rabbeinu Ephraim explained that we say a piece becomes like a neveilah only with meat and milk. Thus, with other prohibitions, the permitted part does not become like the prohibition at all, and since the prohibition does not impart flavor into the piece, it has no power to spread outward. The same law applies according to Rabbeinu Tam: although a piece becomes like a neveilah even with other prohibitions, that is only where it imparts flavor; but whenever it does not impart flavor, such as where the piece has sixty, the Sages were not stringent to say that the piece becomes like a neveilah, meaning that the permitted part becomes exactly like the prohibition. If so, the prohibition is only because of a mashehu, and that mashehu has no power to spread. This appears clear to me in the explanation of Tosafos; examine it. So too it appears from the chiddushim of the Rashba, folio 101b, from Mishmeres HaBayis, folio 94, from Sefer HaTerumah, siman 51, and from the Rosh. As for his relying on what the Ran wrote at the end of Perek Gid HaNasheh, that although one type with its own type is not nullified, we do not derive that where another permitted flavor is mixed with it, end quote, and he understood this to mean that since another force is mixed in it, it is considered one type with another type as above, this is incorrect. Rather, it appears to me that this is the meaning: Rabbi Yehuda was stringent only with an actual mashehu of prohibition that became mixed, but if before it became mixed it also had a permitted flavor, it is not so severe that it should not be nullified. This is why the Ran was precise and wrote another flavor of permitted food; examine it. If so, all the more so the law is so with chametz on Pesach, where the prohibition by a mashehu is only rabbinic. It further appears to me that since the Ran began, they were forced to answer, and then wrote, but it appears to me, this shows that he wrote his own answer, and he considered the answer of Tosafos cited above forced. To me it appears that even with liquids it is permitted, since I have written that all the great authorities wrote the answer of Tosafos, and that is how we rule. As for Nekudas HaKesef's further difficulty from Tur, Orach Chayim siman 467, that a spoon with which one stirred a dish in which a wheat kernel was found, and afterward one stirred other pots with it, renders all of them forbidden, and Olas Shabbos raised the same difficulty: the Taz himself already answered below in siman 467 that there we are concerned that the spoon touched the wheat kernel itself, and the absorbed matter in the spoon did not have sixty against the wheat kernel. Therefore he ruled that if one inserted the spoon into food after removing the wheat kernel, the spoon afterward does not forbid by a mashehu when inserted into another food. End quote. But I wonder at his reasoning, for if so, how do we act every day when a pot contains several pieces of meat and a dairy spoon was inserted into it, where we combine everything in the pot toward sixty, as is explicit in many places? Let us say perhaps it touched one piece, which absorbed by itself and does not have sixty. Rather, certainly everything combines. Therefore, in answer to the above difficulty, it appears to me as I wrote in my work Eliyah Zuta: specifically from a piece a mashehu cannot spread, but from a spoon, which has no flavor of its own, it can spread; the Rema makes a similar distinction in Yoreh Deah siman 105 se'if 7; see there and examine it. Nekudas HaKesef further wondered at the Taz: why did he specify that it fell into another mixture and they removed it; even if they did not remove it, it also should not forbid the other pieces? He explains there at length that it is a scribal error. End quote. In my humble opinion, the words of the Taz are as written, excluding a case where the piece is not recognizable; for then, since the piece itself is forbidden, as Tosafos wrote there, and liquid forbids only the first piece, etc., everything is forbidden, since on Pesach it has no nullification even dry with dry. I proved this at length in Eliyah Zuta from Tosafos and the Rosh there, though I also wondered there at the Rosh in Avodah Zarah, Perek HaSocher; here I wrote only what pertains to practical law. As for what Nekudas HaKesef wrote from siman 467 se'if 12, where the Rema wrote that if it became mixed with others one may be lenient because of doubt, implying that when it is certain it forbids, and so the Magen Avraham wrote there: this does not appear to me to be proof, for one can say it speaks where the second dish does not have a majority against the pieces. One can also say that it does not refer to the piece, but alludes to a liquid mixture, where we permit in a rabbinic doubt. Furthermore, even if you say that this is the Rema's view, nevertheless I proved above that Tosafos and the great halachic decisors permit it. The Taz too strengthened his words in Orach Chayim and wrote that it is a peg that will not move. The practical conclusion is to be lenient with a piece that became forbidden by a mashehu and fell into another dish that has a majority against it, when the piece is recognizable and they removed it. But if it is not recognizable, everything is forbidden; and a spoon that absorbed a mashehu forbids by a mashehu.
