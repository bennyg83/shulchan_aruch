And examine [this] in a place where smell applies. For example, when the oven is small and closed and the forbidden and permitted items are exposed inside the oven, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 108 and in Shach there; and Rama wrote that in a place of loss one may be lenient, and it appears to me only when it is slightly open in a place where the smoke exits; for when completely closed, even with other prohibitions Rama wrote one may not be lenient except in a place of loss, and therefore with chametz, even in a place of loss, it is forbidden. And furthermore, for Tosafot on Avodah Zarah are stringent even in a large open oven - see there; and also the view of Rif, chapter 6 of Chullin, to forbid, for he wrote that Rav said smell is meaningless according to him, because he holds min b'mino b'mashahu, and we hold in this siman it implies that Pesach, which is b'mashahu - for everyone it is meaningful; and also Ran wrote that smell in any amount is meaningful, and with this we reject the proof of Maharil in his responsum - see there; and if so, we have no authority to permit, for in Mordechai no explanation is found to permit except to prove from what Mordechai wrote that we hold smell is not meaningful - from this we learn that he holds chametz equals other prohibitions in this; and furthermore, in Choshen Mishpat siman 25 Sma wrote that everywhere we rule like Rif except in a place where Tosafot disagree, and here both of them equally forbid; therefore at minimum one may not be lenient when completely closed and small, even in a place of loss, and it is enough for us to be lenient when slightly open, for who will come after Rama's ruling. And all this is in roasting; but in cooking, since with other prohibitions it is permitted l'chatchila to cook in one oven, if so from this we learn there is no smell at all, if so with chametz at minimum b'dieved it is permitted; and even though Rama wrote that if the oven is closed, even in cooking it is forbidden even b'dieved - behold, he himself wrote in Darkei Moshe that in the Gemara and in all the poskim there is to permit in cooking, only that since Orchot Chaim and Mahariv were stringent about hot food insulated for Shabbat one may not be lenient - end of his words; and it appears Orchot Chaim of Rama was not as we said before us, and one must say hot food is considered like roasting, and regarding baking, in cooking we hold it is permitted - end of his words; and the truth is with him, for Rashba wrote the reason that cooking does not forbid is because it has no smell, and our eyes see that hot food insulated for Shabbat has a strong smell, and therefore Mahariv holds it forbids; and this is his language in Yesh S..., page 5: and if the oven is closed and the pot is not well covered, that it has a hole through which it can absorb from it to the outside - forbidden, for a covered pot that has no place for the smell to exit - it is known that insulation causes absorption and it is forbidden even in cooking - end of his words; but if they cook on a weekday for an hour or two and close the oven for that period, one need not be stringent except regarding insulation for Shabbat, and it is possible that Rama also intended this; and even though Beit Yosef in siman 97 disagrees with Rashba and brings proof from the Gemara that the reason is because the pot interrupts, therefore Shulchan Aruch did not conclude what Rashba wrote that the smell of what is cooking does not forbid as much; and if so, even in insulation or pot roasting in an oven one should be lenient; nevertheless it appears to me that Rashba's words are correct, for even though the smell does not exit from the sides, nevertheless it exits from above, and behold the proof regarding peshtida - we require that there not be a hole on top; and that which the Gemara answers, like two pots - Rashi explained: a skewer from here and a skewer from here and a large mound of coals or dirt in the middle - end of his words; it implies only regarding a large mound through which smell cannot rise above it, but the interruption of a pot does not count; and that which he answered initially with two pots - the one who asked understood it deals with cooking, and cooking has no smell, therefore he questioned seif 4 - see there; and if so, the law of hot food insulated [for Shabbat] is like roasting, and all the more so pot roasting as is done on Yom Tov; but when one cooked in an oven for an hour, even if one closed the oven one need not be stringent; and he wrote in Yesh S...: and a somewhat large oven, even though it does not hold twelve se'ah, is permitted b'dieved; and if it has a hole from the side besides the opening, it is permitted even l'chatchila since the hole is in a high place and the oven is high, but it is difficult to permit before the masses - end of his words; and it is possible that one may rely on this for chametz on Pesach in a place of loss to permit if the oven is high and the hole open in a high place: what he wrote above that with chametz we do not find [an issur of] davar charif - not precise, for in Arshak it is called sharp as Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah.
