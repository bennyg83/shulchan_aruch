1) [Seif 1] Chametz on Pesach renders a mixture prohibited, etc. By Torah law chametz is like other prohibited substances; the Sages were stringent because it carries the penalty of kareit and people are not accustomed to avoiding it throughout the year. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Rambam, chapter 15 of Hilchot Maachalot Asurot, law 9, gives the reason that it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin. Beit Yosef cites him. Rama writes in responsum 28 that when there is an additional ground for leniency, one may possibly rely on Mordechai, who permits relying on Sheiltot and nullifying chametz in 60 like other prohibitions. Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 69, writes likewise, and Magen Avraham cites this below in siman 467, seif katan 33. In practice, however, their words indicate that this is relied upon only where there are many additional grounds for leniency. Chok Yaakov, letter 1; Chok Yosef, letter 1; Mekor Chaim, letter 1; Chayei Adam, rule 121, letter 8. 2) The Zohar, Parashat Ki Teitzei, 282b, states that those who observe Pesach, when the night of guarding rules over them, must be guarded from even the slightest amount of chametz and se'or, and all their foods and drinks must be guarded. One who guards them from chametz and se'or has his body guarded below from the yetzer hara and his soul guarded above. Of him it is said, "Evil shall not dwell with You," for his body becomes holy and his soul holy of holies. Of the yetzer hara it is said, "No stranger shall eat of the holy," and, "The stranger who approaches shall die." Baer Heitev writes this in the name of the Arizal. At present I have not found it written in the works of the Arizal in our possession except in Mishnat Chassidim, Masechet Nissan, chapter 3, letter 4. 3) There, "whether with its own kind or with another kind," etc. This means both unlike-kind mixtures, where with other prohibitions we ordinarily follow whether flavor is imparted, and like-kind mixtures, where with other prohibitions we measure against 60, as stated at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah, siman 98. Here, regarding chametz, there is no distinction: even the slightest amount prohibits, even for benefit. 4) There, "whether with its own kind," etc. A like-kind mixture is, for example, flour of wheat that became chametz mixed with flour of wheat that did not become chametz. An unlike-kind mixture is flour of wheat that became chametz mixed with flour of another species that did not become chametz, or into a cooked food and the like. See above, siman 442, letter 3. 5) There, "in the slightest amount," etc. This is not necessarily literal; an amount less than 60 is called "the slightest amount." Alternatively, it means even a slight amount that improves the mixture so that it sells for more than before. Otherwise, the Mechaber below, siman 467, seif 10, permits selling a cooked chicken in which a split wheat kernel was found on Pesach, whereas here he states that the slightest amount prohibits benefit. The distinction must therefore be as stated. Peri Chadash. Maamar Mordechai there, letter 16, writes that in the case of the wheat kernel the Mechaber relies upon Rosh that wheat kernels, even split ones, are not definite chametz, and therefore was lenient regarding benefit. See there, and see our words there. 6) There, "in the slightest amount," etc. Whether the substance of the chametz dissolved or remains present and only its flavor was imparted, the slightest amount prohibits. Responsa Rashba, simanim 30 and 53. This is also evident from the words of the Mechaber here and in siman 467. Peri Chadash. Mateh Yehudah likewise agrees that flavor alone prohibits in the slightest amount. See above, siman 442, letter 4. Regarding sale to a non-Jew after deducting the value of the prohibited substance, see below, letter 13. 7) There, "in the slightest amount," etc. This applies even to a slight amount of chametz nuksheh, which is rabbinically prohibited. Peri Chadash. Taz, seif katan 19, likewise writes that if chametz nuksheh falls into food, the food may not be eaten on Pesach but may be retained until after Pesach. Magen Avraham, seif katan 5, however, writes in the name of Likkutei Maharil that chametz nuksheh is nullified in 60 even on Pesach because it carries no kareit. He cites Piskei Mahara'i, siman 186, which prohibits the slightest amount, challenges it, and concludes that one who is lenient like Maharil has not lost. Nehar Shalom, letter 2; Erech HaShulchan, letter 9; Mekor Chaim, letter 7; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 18; Chayei Adam, rule 121, letter 13; and Magen HaElef agree. Fruit juice mixed with water, however, even though it produces chametz nuksheh, prohibits in the slightest amount. Mekor Chaim there. See below, letter 268. 8) There, "in the slightest amount," etc. If something prohibited by the slightest admixture becomes mixed into another cooked food, creating 2 successive minute admixtures, Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 92, seif katan 16, concludes that if a solid piece became prohibited by a minute admixture, fell into another pot, and was removed, what it exudes does not prohibit the second pot by the slightest amount, because we do not apply the rule of the slightest amount twice. With liquid mixed into liquid, however, it remains prohibited by the slightest amount. Peri Chadash agrees with Taz. Eliyah Rabbah, letter 1, likewise permits where a solid piece prohibited by a minute admixture fell into another dish containing a majority against it, the piece is identifiable, and it was removed; if it is not identifiable, everything is prohibited. Shach in Nekudat HaKesef, however, disputes Taz and writes that the halachic authorities customarily prohibit all the mixtures. Olat Shabbos, letter 1, agrees, as do Magen Avraham, siman 467, seif katan 13; Chok Yaakov, letter 5 here and letter 27 in siman 467; Chok Yosef, letter 6; Mateh Yehudah, who writes that this follows from Maran and Rama and is the custom in Algiers; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, letter 16; and Chayei Adam, rule 121, letter 8. Where the first prohibition by the slightest amount is itself doubtful, Beit Yehudah, page 109, permits the second mixture. Beit David, siman 163, likewise writes that if it is doubtful whether any chametz entered the second mixture, since perhaps all the chametz remained in the first mixture, everyone permits it. Erech HaShulchan, letter 4, cites this, though he notes that Magen Avraham, seif katan 26, appears to prohibit even when it is possible that the chametz remained in the first mixture. 9) A wheat kernel found in a pot renders prohibited the spoon with which the pot was stirred. If that spoon was used to stir other pots, all of them are prohibited. Tur, siman 467; Chayei Adam there. Eliyah Rabbah, at the end of letter 1 here, likewise writes that a spoon that absorbed the slightest amount prohibits another mixture by the slightest amount. Although Eliyah Rabbah holds that a solid piece prohibited by the slightest amount does not subsequently prohibit by the slightest amount, he explains that the slightest amount cannot spread farther from a piece, whereas it can spread from a spoon, which has no independent flavor. A piece that exudes also releases its own flavor, yielding only a minute amount of a minute amount; a spoon has no flavor of its own and can release the entire minute amount that it absorbed. 10) One person cooked fish in a chametz frying pan that had not been used for many days. The spoon used to stir it was afterward used to stir another dish, and some of that dish was added to another dish. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 21, writes that one should not be lenient, according to Rama below, seif 10, who prohibits detrimental absorbed flavor on Pesach, unless there is an additional basis for leniency. Here there is another basis: after 2 months, detrimental absorbed flavor may be permitted after the fact even on Pesach, and this implies that even what was cooked in the frying pan is permitted after the fact, provided that the food cooked in it was not sharp. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 93 in Rama and siman 122, seif 3 in Rama. From this one may learn regarding one who is stringent with a pot cover that is not ben yomo that after 2 months it may be permitted. Initially, however, with all prohibitions one may not use utensils that are not ben yomo without kashering even if they have not been used for many years, and certainly this is true of chametz. Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 2, cites this. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 75, however, permits only after 12 months have elapsed since chametz was cooked in it, and Rabbi Naftali Kohen agrees, as stated in simanim 76 and 80. This will be further explained below in siman 451, letter 4. 11) A householder's loaf of chametz that became mixed among loaves of matzah is prohibited for eating and benefit even if mixed with 1,000 loaves, because it is an item counted individually, as stated at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah, siman 110. It appears to me that they remain prohibited even after Pesach. If the loaf was cut or broken, it is permitted after Pesach. Some say that after Pesach it is permitted even while whole, since every chametz mixture is permitted after Pesach despite having been prohibited by the slightest amount, and an individually counted item should not be more stringent. I am stringent, because a mixture containing an individually counted item was treated like the prohibited object itself. Sefer Beit Moed; Birkei Yosef, letter 2. Peri Chadash in siman 448, seif 3, likewise prohibits it even after Pesach. See below, seif 9. 12) There, "even for benefit." It is likewise forbidden to retain it until after Pesach, as stated below in siman 467, at the end of seif 10. Shulchan Gavoah, letter 3.
