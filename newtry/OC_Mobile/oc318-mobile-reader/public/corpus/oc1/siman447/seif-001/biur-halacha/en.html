(ב) And likewise if it touched etc. — at first glance this has no explanation, namely this; and I found in Mateh Yehudah who forced an explanation and wrote that one can say: since in other prohibitions in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 only touching of roasting is mentioned — the way they roasted with its fat or a piece that was roasted with the forbidden substance — and heat of its surface is not mentioned there, therefore he wrote "and likewise if chametz bread touched" etc., meaning: in the first clause, heat of its roasting, and in the second clause heat of its surface — in both only the place of contact is forbidden. And know that heat of its roasting here means there is only touching alone; but that which is below siman 467 subsection 15, that there are those who forbid the entire thing, deals with roasting by a spit, for when one turns the spit the taste spreads through the entire thing, as explained there — end of his words. And in my humble opinion it appears it should read "and therefore."

(ג) And there is no permeating substance etc. alone — see Magen Avraham in subsection 2 at its end, who wrote that the law of its surface is like roasting; nevertheless it appears to me one should rule here as Rama wrote in salting in siman 467 seif 14, that in any case roasting is not better than salting; and see in Peri Megadim who wrote that although the Rav was silent here on what the Mechaber wrote "there is no permeating substance, only the place of contact is forbidden," I do not agree except he relied on 467 seif 14 regarding salting, that they forbid that entire piece b'eyna in sixty, and on Pesach in any amount; and in truth such is Bach's view there in siman 461. In practice one should look, for Peri Chadash below in siman 461 and Gra in this siman and Maamar Mordechai in siman 461 — all their view is to be lenient like Shulchan Aruch's ruling; and therefore their reason is specifically in salting, where the forbidden substance is found on the meat, where sometimes it is fatty [and also because we do not know at which place the contact was, therefore they are stringent on that piece]; not so in our matter with two loaves where fattiness does not apply at all — one need not be stringent. However Eshel Avraham's view is like Bach, and I found several other Acharonim who copied Bach's words; and all this is only because of the stringency of chametz — people are accustomed to be stringent; and it appears that in a place of loss one may rely to be lenient in this. Afterwards I found in Yoreh De'ah siman 105 in Peri Megadim in Sha'ar HaTevunah section 38 who also sides to be lenient in a place of loss regarding a weak forbidden substance that has no fattiness at all.

(ד) And there are those who are stringent etc. — Mateh Yehudah wrote: it appears one should be stringent only in something uncommon, for example if chametz bread was baked on Pesach with fatty meat — in this one should be stringent, because although it is not a common thing in any amount, nevertheless there is a concern; but in something common, such as wheat found in one of the dishes in an oven, there is no reason to be stringent even in a completely sealed oven where reicha applies, for this is less than two rabbinic any-amounts that the Acharonim spoke of — for even from actual fatty meat it is not [according to what we hold fully absorbed] except any amount, and this smell coming from the wheat, even if its entire body became mixed, is only any amount and through smell; and according to Yerushalmi and Ramban to Levi, in truth even any amount is not certain in such a case — it is not worth relying on the question to permit, especially in a place preventing Yom Tov joy; and there is no proof from Tosafot who are stringent even regarding wheat, for they rule like Rav that reicha is a matter; etc. — see there where he elaborated and concludes thus for practical law.

(ה) Where reicha applies — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote "but in a thing where the name reicha does not apply" etc.; and regarding cooking see Magen Avraham who concludes that what the Rav was stringent about in Yoreh De'ah siman 108 seif 2 in the gloss regarding when the oven was completely sealed, even in cooking [unless in great loss], deals specifically with hot foods insulated for Shabbat, for since they remain a long time inside the oven and the oven is sealed on every side, their smell is strong and its law is like roasting; but in ordinary cooking, even if they remain an hour or two, one need not be stringent. And regarding Pesach see what is written in Mishna Berurah.
