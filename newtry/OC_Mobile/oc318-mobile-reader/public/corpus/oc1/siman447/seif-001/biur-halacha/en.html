[1] And likewise if it touched, etc. At first glance this has no explanation, for it is the same thing. I found in Sefer Mateh Yehudah that he struggled with this and wrote that perhaps one can say: with other prohibitions in Yoreh De'ah, siman 105, only touching by roasting is mentioned, such as a thigh roasted with its sciatic nerve, or a piece roasted with a forbidden item. The heat of baking is not mentioned there. Therefore he wrote, "and likewise if a chametz loaf touched, etc." Meaning: at first, heat of roasting; and at the end, heat of baking. In both, it forbids only the place of contact. The heat of roasting here means that there is only mere contact; but that case below in siman 467:15, where some forbid all of it, speaks of roasting by spit, where when one turns the spit the flavor spreads throughout all of it, as explained there. This is the end of his language. In my humble opinion, it should read "and therefore."
[1] And there is no item there that spreads, etc., only. See Magen Avraham at the end of se'if katan 2, who wrote that the law of baked food is like roasted food. However, it appears to me that one should rule here as Rema wrote regarding salting in siman 467:14, for roasting is at least no better than salting. See Pri Megadim, who wrote that although the Rav was silent here about what the Mechaber wrote, that where there is no spreading item it forbids only the place of contact, he did not concede to him; rather, he relied on 467:14, that with salting we forbid that entire piece, before Pesach in sixty and on Pesach in the smallest amount. In truth, this is the view of the Bach there in siman 461. In practice it requires analysis, for the Pri Chadash below in siman 461, the Shulchan Aruch HaRav in this siman, and the Ma'amar Mordechai in siman 461 all hold to be lenient like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. Perforce their reason is that specifically there with salting, where the wheat kernel is found on meat, fattiness sometimes applies, and also because we do not know where the contact was, we are stringent regarding that entire piece. This is not so in our case of two loaves, where no fattiness at all applies, and one should not be stringent. However, the view of Eliyah Rabbah is like the Bach, and I also found some other later authorities who copied the words of the Bach. All this is only because of the stringency of chametz; the custom is to be stringent. It appears that in a case of loss one may rely on leniency in this. Afterward I found in Yoreh De'ah, siman 105, in Pri Megadim, Sifsei Da'as, note 38, that he too inclines to be lenient in a case of loss with such a lean prohibition that has no fattiness at all.
[1] And some are stringent, etc. Sefer Beis Meir wrote: it appears that one should be stringent only in a matter that is not common at all. For example, if chametz bread was baked on Pesach together with fatty meat, one should be stringent, because although it is not a substantial matter, there is nevertheless some smallest amount. But with something common, such as a wheat kernel found in one of the cooked foods in an oven, there is no reason to be stringent, even in a closed oven where smell applies. This is worse than the two smallest-amount cases about which the later authorities spoke, for even from actual fatty meat, according to our ruling like Levi, there is only a smallest amount; and this smell that comes from the wheat kernel, where even if its entire body became mixed it would be only a smallest amount, and through smell - according to the Yerushalmi and Ramban, according to Levi there is actually not even a definite smallest amount in such a case. It is fitting to rely on the She'iltos to permit, especially where Yom Tov joy would be prevented. There is no proof from Tosafos who are stringent even with a wheat kernel, because they rule like Rav, that smell is a real matter, etc. See there, where he wrote at length and concluded so as a matter of law.
[1] Where smell applies. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote: but with a matter to which smell does not apply, etc. Regarding cooking, see Magen Avraham, who concludes that what the Rav is stringent about in Yoreh De'ah 108:2 in the gloss, where the oven was completely closed, even with cooking unless there is great loss, applies specifically to hot foods insulated for Shabbos. Since they remain a long time inside the oven and the oven is closed on every side, their smell is strong and their law is like roasting. But with ordinary cooking, even if they remain there an hour or two, one should not be stringent. Regarding Pesach, see what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah.
