And it imparts a spoiled flavor, etc. Leaven or chametz that was cooked with cooked meat or roasted meat imparts a spoiled flavor. Magen Avraham, in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket. Chok Yaakov wondered what spoilage applies here; see Yam Shel Shlomo itself, where he wrote as follows: leaven or chametz that fell into a pot or was cooked with roasted meat, and it is known that it imparts a spoiled flavor in that food, it is permitted, etc. Thus it is explicit that this applies only where we know with certainty that the chametz spoils it, but in an ordinary case it is not considered a spoiled flavor. End quote. I examined what he wrote there: it says it is known, not and it is known with a vav; so too it is written in Sefer Tanyah, siman 46. If so, it implies that we know without qualification that this is so; examine it. So too it further implies from the end of their words there. Magen Avraham wrote that wheat in a well imparts a spoiled flavor. Chok Yaakov wrote: this is not clear, as he wrote in siman 467 se'if 12. End quote. In my humble opinion, it is different there, for he speaks of wheat in water in a vessel, not in a well. Magen Avraham wrote that barley in a pit imparts a spoiled flavor, and chametz dough in a well imparts a spoiled flavor.
