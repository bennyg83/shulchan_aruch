Beis Yosef wrote: it appears that his reason is because it is something that will become permitted, or because he holds that dry in dry is nullified one in two in a prohibition that depends on imparting flavor, but in a case where they were stringent beyond imparting flavor, there is no difference between dry and mixed, as the Mordechai wrote in chapter Kol Sha'ah. This is the end of his language. According to this, if it became mixed before Pesach, one in two in a dry mixture, since then its measure is based on imparting flavor, its law is like other prohibitions.
[9] And some say that chametz is equal, etc. Automatically, if the chametz is a piece fit to be honored with, or other items that are not nullified with other prohibitions, it is obvious that the same applies on Pesach. Only Rashi, whom the Beis Yosef brings, distinguishes according to R. Akiva between a whole loaf and a slice of a loaf. Beis Yosef wrote that we do not rule like R. Akiva, and therefore we do not distinguish in this way, for R. Akiva's reason is that a loaf is important in itself, while we hold that the matter depends on being fit to be honored with.
