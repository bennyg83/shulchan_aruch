9) A wheat kernel found in a pot renders prohibited the spoon used to stir the pot. If that spoon was used to stir other pots, all of them are prohibited. Tur, siman 467; Chayei Adam, there. Eliyah Rabbah, at the end of subsection 1 in this siman, likewise writes that a spoon that absorbed the slightest amount renders another mixture prohibited by the slightest amount. Although Eliyah Rabbah maintains that a solid piece prohibited by the slightest admixture does not subsequently prohibit another mixture by the slightest amount, as stated in the preceding subsection, he explains there that the slightest amount cannot spread from a solid piece, whereas it can spread from a spoon, which has no flavor of its own. See there. In other words, when a solid piece that has flavor exudes, it also exudes some of its own substance, producing only the slightest amount of the slightest amount. A spoon, however, has no flavor of its own; when it exudes, it exudes all the minute amount that it absorbed. It therefore renders prohibited the pots subsequently stirred with it.
