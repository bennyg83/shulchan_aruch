It is not nullified even in one thousand. Since in a liquid mixture its prohibition applies in any amount, the authorities were likewise stringent with a dry mixture. I concluded in my Minchas Yaakov, klal 39, seif katan 16, that this is the principal reason in halachah, and not because it is an item that will become permitted, as Nekudas HaKesef maintains. The later authorities also reached my conclusion and challenged his position; see there, where I wrote that they erred in several respects. As a matter of halachah, I concluded there that the primary ruling follows the first opinion, which the Shulchan Aruch records without qualification, that dry chametz mixed with dry food is not nullified. This is also the consensus of the later authorities, with no distinction between a mixture of the same type and one of different types. See above, seif katan 3, whether it may be sold to a non-Jew after deducting the value of the forbidden portion. From six hours onward on Erev Pesach, a dry mixture is nullified by a majority like other prohibitions, provided we do not say that the prohibition reawakens, as discussed above. Chametz utensils mixed with Pesach utensils should not be nullified by a majority, because even regarding other prohibitions some authorities hold that a utensil is not nullified, since it can later be permitted through hagalah, unlike earthenware utensils. This is stated in Maharil, responsum 194, and Magen Avraham writes likewise. This is the Bach's intent, except that his words contain an evident scribal error: instead of “some say that chametz,” the text should read, “some say that utensils.” This differs from Magen Avraham, who did not notice the error, and it is clear. My brother-in-law, the author of Soles Belulah, writes that a piece forbidden by an infinitesimal admixture is nullified one in two even during Pesach. See what I wrote above, seif katan 19, regarding utensils. This requires study.
