Seif 9: Dry with dry, etc.; and some say. Avodah Zarah 74a: “What does this tanna count, etc.?” and “This tanna counts two, etc.” The phrase “these are” comes to exclude other matters, as Rashi explains there. The Rosh likewise writes there, section 1, except that he writes that one need not remove one portion, as stated in Orlah, chapter 2: orlah and kilayim are nullified in two hundred, and one need not remove a portion. Removal was required only for terumah because of the theft of the kohanic tribe. See Yoreh Deah, siman 109. He writes that the mixture is permitted even for eating. Tosafot, however, explain there that “to exclude” means only that those matters were not listed, but they nevertheless prohibit in any amount, just as Bava Kamma 5a says “to exclude an informer and one who renders an offering piggul,” even though one is liable for them. A proof is that the Gemara says it comes to exclude an item counted individually, rather than items forbidden in benefit. Its meaning is to exclude neveilah, as explained there, and in Chullin 100a it states that neveilah is not nullified because it is an item counted individually. The Yerushalmi in Avodah Zarah likewise states: Why was neveilah not taught together with them? Rabbi Yose bar Rabbi Bun answered that the Mishnah teaches only an item forbidden in benefit, whereas neveilah is permitted in benefit. They challenged this from chametz on Pesach. The answer is that chametz carries kares, whereas those items do not carry kares. The Raavad adduces proof from Temurah 28a, which asks: Why, regarding mixtures of consecrated items, does the Mishnah teach prohibitions of eating and prohibitions of benefit together, meaning the Mishnah in Temurah there, while regarding mixtures of ordinary prohibited foods it separately teaches prohibitions of eating and prohibitions of benefit, meaning the aforementioned Mishnah in Avodah Zarah, which lists only prohibitions of benefit? The Gemara answers that because it wished to explain the prohibition of wine used for idolatrous libation, it listed all prohibitions of benefit resembling it, etc. This passage is missing from our text of the Gemara but appears in Rashi, although he explains it as referring to chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah. We see that when the Mishnah lists prohibitions of benefit, it includes prohibitions of eating as well, except that it did not list them explicitly, as above. Even among prohibitions of benefit, it lists only those specifically resembling wine used for idolatrous libation. See Ran and Rosh there. The Rif writes that this general rule is not accepted, because the Gemara there says it comes to exclude, etc., whereas we rule that chametz prohibits in any amount. The Ran writes that the statement there follows the opinion that, like Rabbi Yehudah, chametz after Pesach remains forbidden according to the Torah. According to our ruling, however, that follows Rabbi Shimon, it is an item that will become permitted, which is not nullified even in one thousand, as Rambam writes. See Yoreh Deah 102:4. Terumas HaDeshen, responsum 107, discusses this at length and rules like the first view; see also his responsum 262.
