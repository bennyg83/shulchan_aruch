It is not nullified. This is also implied by Toras Chatas, klal 39. Mordechai writes that the reason is that since in a liquid mixture the prohibition applies in any amount, the same is true in a dry mixture. Nekudas HaKesef writes that the reason is that it is an item that will become permitted, and therefore rules leniently when the mixture is not of the same type. See there. He overlooked the words of Mordechai, although Shach himself cites them in Yoreh Deah 102:13. Accordingly, on Erev Pesach it is nullified by a majority, as stated in seif 2. See Yoreh Deah, siman 109, regarding the proper procedure. Nevertheless, all the pieces must be eaten before Pesach, as stated in the gloss to seif 4. All this concerns a piece of bread, but a whole loaf of chametz mixed with matzah is not nullified according to any opinion, because it is an item counted individually, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 110. See Bach, and what I wrote in siman 451:19. A matzah in which a kernel of wheat was found, or which folded over on one side and the folded section was cut away and then became mixed with another matzah, is not considered an item counted individually, because its prohibition is due to absorbed taste. The Bach writes that a chametz utensil mixed with other utensils is nullified by a majority. Nevertheless, since some hold that chametz is considered an item that will become permitted, it is not nullified. The same is written in Nachalas Tzvi. Toras Chatas, however, rules that chametz is not considered an item that will become permitted; the Tur and Beit Yosef likewise write this in the name of the poskim. This is the accepted practice, for otherwise it would not be nullified even on Erev Pesach, contrary to seif 2. Regarding utensils, however, one should be stringent, because some authorities maintain that even utensils containing other prohibitions are not nullified, as Shach writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 102. During Pesach itself it is certainly forbidden, for it is no better than any other dry mixture.
