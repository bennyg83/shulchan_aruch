If so, on the eve of Pesach, dry in dry is nullified in the majority like other prohibitions, though nevertheless one must eat all of them before Pesach; see se'if 4 in the gloss. All this is with a slice, but if a chametz loaf became mixed with matzah, it is not nullified according to everyone, because it is an item counted by number, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 110. If a matzah folded over, or had a wheat kernel on one side, and he cut off the fold or the place of the wheat kernel, and it later became mixed with others, it is not an item counted by number, because it is forbidden due to absorbed taste. If a chametz utensil became mixed with Pesach utensils, one should not be lenient to nullify it in the majority, since even with other prohibitions there are those who say that it is not nullified because it is something that can become permitted through hagalah. See Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov, and Responsa Olas HaGer, siman 99.
