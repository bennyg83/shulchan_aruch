(א) It is not nullified. Since in its wet form its prohibition applies in any amount, they were also stringent regarding it in its dry form. And therefore, on Erev Pesach, dry [chametz] is nullified in dry [food] by a majority, like other prohibitions. Nevertheless, one must eat all of them before Pesach — see seif 4 in the gloss. And all of this applies to a piece [of chametz], but a loaf of chametz that became mixed with matzah is not nullified according to all opinions, for it is a davar shebeminyan (countable item), as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 110. And matzah that became folded, or that had a wheat kernel on one side, and they cut off the fold or the place of the wheat kernel, and it subsequently became mixed with others — it is not a davar shebeminyan, for it is forbidden on account of what was absorbed into it. And a chametz vessel that became mixed with Pesach vessels, one should not be lenient to nullify them by a majority, since even regarding other prohibitions there are those who say that it is not nullified because it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin (something that has a permissible remedy) through hagalah — [see] Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov, and in Responsa Olat HaGershuni, siman 99.

(ב) in this matter. And the consensus of the later authorities follows the first opinion, that dry [chametz] mixed with dry [food] is not nullified on Pesach, and there is no distinction between the same species mixed with the same species and between a different species mixed with a different species. And see above, sub-section 3, [regarding] whether it is permitted to sell to a non-Jew, except for the value of the forbidden portion within it. And if one of them was eaten, it is permitted — see siman 467, sub-section 4, in Magen Avraham and in Taz. The Or Zarua wrote that a piece that was forbidden by the rule of any amount (mashahu) is nullified one in two, even during Pesach — see there, and this requires further study. Chok Yaakov.
