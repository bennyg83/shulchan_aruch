(א) Dry in dry — meaning, a slice of chametz became mixed among matzah slices and is not recognizable; but if a loaf became mixed among loaves, according to all it is not nullified, for it is a davar shebeminyan and is not nullified even in a thousand, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 110:

(ב) Not nullified — since if moist became mixed its prohibition is in any amount, they were also stringent regarding it when dry. And therefore specifically when mixed during Pesach; but if mixed on erev Pesach until night, we hold above in seif 2 that its law is like other prohibitions — and therefore moist is nullified in noten ta'am like other prohibitions, and likewise dry is like other prohibitions and nullified by majority. Nevertheless, they must all be eaten before night, for otherwise we say it returns and revives on Pesach, as proven above in seif 4 in the gloss. Some wrote that Per the first view, dry in dry is not nullified specifically when the prohibition is from the chametz itself; but if the permitted item itself only absorbed a minute amount of chametz, such as matzah in which a leavened wheat kernel was baked and afterward became mixed with others — it is nullified one in two even during Pesach; but many Acharonim disagree. Acharonim also wrote that a chametz vessel mixed with Pesach vessels on Pesach — its law is like dry in dry, not nullified, forbidden to use on Pesach; and even if mixed on erev Pesach one should be stringent not to use them; however, if the chametz vessel was not ben yomo, in a place where Yom Tov joy would be prevented, one may be lenient if mixed on erev Pesach:

(ג) And some say chametz on Pesach, etc. — their reason: specifically in a moist mixture, where in other prohibitions the measure is noten ta'am, the Sages were stringent regarding chametz that it is in any amount; but in a dry item, where in other prohibitions one in two nullifies, likewise chametz on Pesach:
