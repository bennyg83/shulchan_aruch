Some are lenient, etc. The Shach in Yoreh Deah siman 108 challenged the Rema regarding these lenient authorities, and wrote that in Darchei Moshe he says this is implied by the Mordechai in Perek Kol Shaah, but it is not in the Mordechai; rather, the Mordechai says only that aroma does not apply to chametz bread baked with matzah, for we do not find aroma with bread, but with other cooked foods everyone agrees it is forbidden. End quote. So too wrote Olas Shabbos, and he added to wonder at the Rema, for the Mordechai wrote this in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, who follows his own view that chametz on Pesach is not forbidden by a mashehu. End quote. In my humble opinion, Darchei Moshe did not intend Rabbeinu Tam's incident at all, but rather the incident that the Mordechai brought before it, where a non-Jew threw chametz cakes into an oven on Pesach and they ruled that it was permitted because we hold aroma is not a halachic factor. If so, it is proven that he holds chametz is equal to other prohibitions; for if not, let him derive that aroma is a factor, and nevertheless it is permitted because there is no aroma with bread. So too it is explicit in Rokeach siman 291, for otherwise what was his challenge from the mitzvah-hadas, whose fragrance is forbidden, to the incense of non-Jews? Examine it. The incident of Rabbeinu Tam, who distinguishes bread, appears clear to me as following his own view that with every prohibition aroma is a factor, as I wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 97; therefore he needed the distinction of bread. As for practical law, the Rema wrote in Yoreh Deah there that in a case of loss one may rely on the lenient authorities, but the Bach is stringent here. I already answered properly in my Eliyah Zuta what Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wondered against the Bach. Nevertheless, since Maharil, responsum 214, wrote that chametz is equal to other prohibitions, and I proved this from the Mordechai and Rokeach, one may be lenient in a case of loss and for Yom Tov joy. In Yoreh Deah it implies that this applies even in a small, closed oven; but in a large oven, or a small one that is open, it is permitted even without loss, unlike Olas Shabbos. I also proved in my work on Yoreh Deah that the Rema's view is that the leniency in a case of loss is where the oven is somewhat open at the place where smoke exits, but if it is completely closed, it is forbidden even in a case of loss; so too it implies in Magen Avraham. Furthermore, I wrote there that the Maharshal forbids even with other prohibitions, even in a case of loss, when the oven is small and completely closed, and perhaps Maharil also agrees to this. The Rema wrote there in Yoreh Deah that if the forbidden item is sharp, and all the more so if the permitted item is sharp, it is forbidden even in a case of loss. It implies this is so even in a large open oven, as he wrote in Toras Chatas. Although the Maharshal and Bach objected, the Taz and Shach wrote that it is reasonable to forbid. In my work on Yoreh Deah I wrote that they all did not examine the Mordechai in Perek Gid HaNasheh carefully, where he wrote: and an onion that was roasted, etc., that is certainly forbidden, because it is sharp. End quote. At first glance this is astonishing, for he had not previously written the reasoning that aroma is not a halachic factor. Rather, the explanation is clear: it refers to what he wrote earlier, that in a large oven it is permitted, and about this he wrote that with a sharp item it is forbidden. I found a clear proof to this in the responsa of Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 25, as I wrote at length in my work. Know that a sharp item is called such when most of the dish is sharp, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 122 se'if 3. Borscht is considered sharp. The Rema further wrote there that if one of them is covered, even merely with dough, it is permitted, and he derived this from Toras Chatas and the Mordechai. In my work there I wrote and proved from the Mordechai itself and from other halachic decisors that a dough covering helps only with something that is permitted after the fact, for then it is permitted initially with a dough covering; but with a sharp item it is forbidden unless there is a bowl-covering or something similar. All this applies to roasting, but with cooking it is permitted, unless one insulates for Shabbos, so that it remains there all night and is closed on all sides, and so wrote Magen Avraham. Yet he implies that with chametz he forbids even in a case of great loss, and one may perhaps be lenient in this in a case of great loss and for Yom Tov joy. An incident came before my elder, the Gaon, of blessed memory, where they sealed an oven containing foods with a board that they used to seal it all year and had not rinsed; he permitted it. It is forbidden to smell hot bread on Pesach; Issur VeHeter, siman 39.
