7) There. Even the slightest amount, etc. This applies even to the slightest amount of hardened, inedible chametz, which is rabbinically prohibited. Peri Chadash. Taz, subsection 19, likewise writes that if hardened chametz falls into food, the food may not be eaten during Pesach, but may be retained until after Pesach. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 5, however, writes in the name of Likkutei Maharil that hardened chametz is nullified in sixty even during Pesach, since it does not carry the penalty of kares. He cites Piskei Mahara'i, siman 186, which prohibits even the slightest amount, challenges that ruling, and concludes that one who rules leniently like Maharil suffers no loss. See there. Nehar Shalom, subsection 2; Erech HaShulchan, subsection 9; Mekor Chaim, subsection 7; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 18; Chayei Adam, rule 121, subsection 13; and Magen HaElef agree. Fruit juice mixed with water, however, prohibits even in the slightest amount although the resulting chametz is hardened chametz. Mekor Chaim, there. See below, subsection 268.
