If the vessel was clean. Even if it was ben yomo, since unripe grapes are not considered a sharp item capable of drawing out absorbed taste when cold, according to the Rav. I also found this in Issur VeHeter, klal 12, law 6, who wrote as follows: unripe grapes crushed in a chametz mortar are like cold, and do not draw out taste from the mortar. Although they are considered hot because of their sourness with respect to absorbing, nevertheless with respect to drawing out taste they are considered cold. These are his words. However, the Taz understood that the Rav’s view is that unripe grapes are considered a fully sharp item, and he wonders at the Rav for permitting it if they were cut with a clean knife. According to his words it is difficult why he does not wonder at the law of the mortar ruled by the Mechaber and the Rav.
Within Pesach. This implies that before Pesach it is permitted through rinsing, but during Pesach we are stringent. It appears to me that this applies specifically to something made by a non-Jew, for Maharil is discussing fish cut by non-Jews. But if it happened incidentally that one cut a non-sharp item with a chametz knife, rinsing or scraping suffices, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 96.
And to forbid because of doubt. Meaning, to be concerned that perhaps the knife was not clean. Nevertheless this is difficult, since we rule that an ordinary knife is not clean, as written there in Yoreh Deah, and so the Torat Chatat, klal 61, wrote, unless we know clearly that it was rinsed. If so, why should we be lenient with chametz? One may answer that we assume fat is ordinarily stuck to it, but chametz is not stuck to it, because it is not an item that adheres to it so much. Nevertheless, this requires study in practice, for at least some amount exists, unless that item was first rinsed before being mixed, as it appears to me. In Darkei Moshe he brings proof from what the Tur wrote, that the Rosh permitted an onion cut with a knife used to scrape dough from a kneading trough, because that knife had not been used with hot food, and he was not concerned that perhaps it was not clean. These are his words. It seems to me that there is no proof from there, for there it speaks where he knew that it was clean. Know this, for at the very least he should have had to ask whether it was clean; rather, one must say that he was told that it was clean.
