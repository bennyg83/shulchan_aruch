Se'if katan 15: "As he wrote in siman 467, se'if 14, etc." This means that there in se'if katan 33 he wrote that if the brine does not rise over it, it has the law of salting, and its law is as written in Responsa Rema; see there, where Magen Avraham alluded to here. With salting, the other pieces are permitted. Regarding that piece itself, what is outside the brine can also be said to be permitted, as Taz wrote in siman 467, se'if katan 20. Although with other prohibitions we say that perhaps it spreads upward, nevertheless with chametz, which is a lean prohibition, one should not be stringent. See Shach, siman 105. More likely, Magen Avraham refers to what was written above in the name of Rema regarding where it remained for twenty-four hours. There the law of salting is not relevant at all, for the water nullifies the power of the brine; it is forbidden only by the law of soaking. Then, what is outside the brine did not soften and did not absorb at all. Elsewhere I wrote at length about this, on Olas Shabbos at the beginning of siman 105 in the name of Issur VeHeter. What Magen Avraham wrote, "and so Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 83," means there in se'if katan 6, that the brine must rise over both, and then it is considered soaked when it remained twenty-four hours; see there. What Magen Avraham wrote, "and in Derishah, siman 69, and in Be'er Sheva, etc." In my humble opinion a word is missing, and it should read: "And in Derishah, siman 69, he wrote that even to cause emission from a utensil, the law of soaking applies; but in Be'er Sheva, chapter 25, he wrote, etc." Meaning that Be'er Sheva holds that soaking is ineffective to cause emission from a utensil; see Minchas Yaakov, klal 12, se'if katan 14.
