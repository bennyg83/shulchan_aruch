[7] Unripe grapes that are pounded, etc. Semak wrote that this is cold and does not have the power to cause release of absorbed taste; and even if it releases, it releases only a minimal amount, which was nullified before Pesach, like leeks and onions cut with a dairy knife and placed with meat, as R. Baruch explained. This is the end of his language. It appears that his explanation is as follows: we say in Yoreh De'ah, siman 96, that with a sharp item and pressure of a knife there is prohibition, as written there in se'if 1. Therefore he says here that even if it releases taste, meaning through the pressure of the mortar, and there is a sharp item here, since the unripe grapes have sourness, nevertheless only the released taste itself needs nullification, and the entire unripe grape does not become forbidden. He learns this from onions cut with a dairy knife and placed with meat, where the entire onion does not become forbidden; rather, we measure against what is absorbed in them. This is unlike cutting with a knife of idolatrous use, where the entire onion becomes neveilah. Here too, since it was done before Pesach and was then permitted, afterward on Pesach it is permitted, because the small released taste had already been nullified. Therefore, if it was done on Pesach it is certainly forbidden if he cut with a chametz knife, even if it was clean, because of the pressure of the knife and because it is a sharp item. As is written in Yoreh De'ah regarding radish, siman 96, some forbid even with cold. Therefore it requires examination why Rema wrote that an ordinary knife is not clean; as it appears, even if it is certainly clean one should forbid if he cut them during Pesach with a chametz knife.
