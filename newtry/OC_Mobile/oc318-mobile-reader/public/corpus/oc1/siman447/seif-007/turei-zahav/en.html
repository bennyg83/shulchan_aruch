Unripe grapes that are pounded, etc. Semak wrote that it is cold and has no power to discharge; and even if it discharges a minute amount, it is nullified before Pesach immediately, similar to onions cut with a milking knife and placed with meat, as Rabbenu Yerucham explained — end of his words; and his explanation is what we say in Yoreh De'ah siman 96 that with davar charif and pressure of the knife there is prohibition, as there seif 1; therefore he said here even if it discharges — through mortar pressure and there is davar charif, for unripe grapes have acidity; nevertheless one need not nullify it, for the discharge alone [does not make all forbidden], unlike onions cut with a milking knife placed with meat, where not all the onion becomes forbidden but we measure against what was absorbed in them, unlike cutting with a forbidden knife where all the onion becomes nevelah; here too, since done before Pesach when it was still permitted, afterward on Pesach it is permitted since the minor discharge was already nullified; therefore if done on Pesach it is certainly forbidden if cut with a chametz knife. chametz — even if the knife is clean, because of knife pressure and it is davar charif, as in Yoreh De'ah regarding radish siman 96 where some forbid even in cold — therefore it is difficult why Rama wrote stam knife is not presumed clean; for it appears even if certainly clean one must forbid if cut on Pesach with a chametz knife:
