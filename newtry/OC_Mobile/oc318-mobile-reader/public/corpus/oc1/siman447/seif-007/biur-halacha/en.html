[7] Unripe grapes that are pounded before Pesach. This wording implies that during Pesach one should not be lenient. The reason is that he is concerned for what the Semak concluded at the end of his words, "and even if it releases, etc." as his words are brought in Taz. According to this, it is permitted only before Pesach. But Rema decided as law like the beginning of Semak's words, where he wrote that it does not release taste in cold; therefore even during Pesach it should be permitted [Ma'amar Mordechai]. However, it is difficult, for Rema should have written in the language "some say" or "it appears to me," and not written it without qualification. Mateh Yehudah already asked this and remained with the difficulty. In my humble opinion, if we take as law the words of Eliyah Rabbah and Nachalas Tzvi, that before Pesach it is permitted when he pounded them even if the mortar was not clean, this is very well settled: the Mechaber speaks where the utensil was not clean, and therefore we permit only before Pesach. Rema's language also fits very well for one who examines it. Examine this carefully.
