Unripe grapes. He discusses unripe grapes, chametz knives, sharpness, pressure, and how far to extend concern for attached chametz.
If the vessel was clean. It appears to me that this refers to both cases, for even before Pesach it is forbidden if the vessel was dirty. Even if you say it is not a sharp item, nevertheless through pressure some of it enters, and they cannot be permitted.
During Pesach. This implies that before Pesach it is permitted through rinsing, but during Pesach we are stringent. It appears to me that this is specifically with something made by non-Jews, for Maharil is speaking of fish cut by non-Jews. But if it happened incidentally that one cut a non-sharp item with a chametz knife, rinsing or scraping suffices, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 96.
If it became mixed. He discusses unripe grapes, chametz knives, sharpness, pressure, and how far to extend concern for attached chametz.
And to forbid because of doubt. Meaning, to be concerned that perhaps the knife was not clean. Nevertheless this is difficult, since we rule that an ordinary knife is not clean, as written there in Yoreh Deah, and so wrote the Torat Chatat, klal 61, unless we know clearly that it was rinsed. If so, why should we be lenient with chametz? One may answer that we assume fat is ordinarily stuck to it, but chametz is not stuck to it, because it is not an item that adheres to it so much. Nevertheless, this requires study in practice, for at least some amount exists unless that item was rinsed first before it became mixed, as it appears to me. In Darkei Moshe he brings proof from what the Tur wrote, that the Rosh permitted an onion cut with a knife used to scrape dough from the kneading trough, because that knife had not been used with hot food, and he was not concerned that perhaps it was not clean. These are his words. It seems to me that there is no proof from there, for there it speaks where he knew that it was clean. Know this, for at the very least he should have had to ask whether it was clean; rather, one must say that he was told that it was clean.
