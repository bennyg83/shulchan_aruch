(11) A loaf of the homeowner's chametz that became mixed among loaves of matzah — even if it mixed in a thousand, all are forbidden in eating and benefit because it is a davar sheb'minyan (as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 110); and it appears to me that even after Pesach they are forbidden; and if the bread was cut or broken it is permitted after Pesach. And there are those who say after Pesach it is permitted (meaning even if still whole), for they permitted all chametz mixtures after Pesach even though its prohibition is in any amount, and a davar sheb'minyan should not be more stringent than them whose prohibition is in any amount; and in this I am stringent, for they made a mixture of davar sheb'minyan like the body of the prohibition itself. Sefer Beit Moed. Berakh Yaakov sign 2, and so Peri Chadash in siman 448 seif 3 to forbid also after Pesach — see there; and see below seif 9 and in our words there in detail.
