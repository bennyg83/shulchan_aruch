[11] Whether chametz, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote: if it became mixed in less than sixty, etc. According to his approach, this is through a mixture containing a kezayis of chametz. So it is stated above at the beginning of siman 442 in Magen Avraham; see there, that if there is not altogether a kezayis in it, according to everyone he does not transgress bal yera'eh. So too the Gra brought in this siman. I did not copy what Magen Avraham brought in the name of the Bach, that if it became mixed in less than sixty, even if there was no kezayis and he did not transgress bal yera'eh, nevertheless regarding eating it is forbidden after Pesach because the chametz flavor is tasted, and it is forbidden without redemption. This is because the Beis Meir questions it: since he did not transgress bal yera'eh with this mixture, why should it not be permitted for eating? After Pesach, the reason that the chametz flavor is tasted does not apply. So too in the Biur HaGra it is also proven in se'if katan 39 that where the Mechaber requires sixty, he refers to a case where there is at least a kezayis; and in se'if katan 41 it is proven that regarding a mixture over which he did not transgress bal yera'eh, it is permitted for eating and benefit, meaning even without redemption, for the Mechaber does not speak of that. However, Mekor Chayim's view in this matter is like Magen Avraham, that even less than a kezayis is forbidden without redemption. His reason is that many poskim hold that one transgresses bal yera'eh even for less than a kezayis. Perhaps even the Gra holds that one transgresses bal yera'eh even for less than a kezayis, but this is specifically when it is actual substance and not through a mixture. This requires examination in practice.
