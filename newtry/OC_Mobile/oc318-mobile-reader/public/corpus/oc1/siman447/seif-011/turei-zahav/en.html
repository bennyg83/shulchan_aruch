[11] Before Pesach. The explanation of the Tur's words about this is in siman 442. We learn from this that a mixture in which there is no biblical prohibition, although there is a rabbinic prohibition in it during Pesach, may nevertheless be made into that mixture even intentionally in order to keep it until after Pesach.
[11] And less than sixty is sufficient, etc. This means liquid with liquid, where sixty is required. Nevertheless here, after Pesach has passed over it, where the prohibition is only because of the penalty for having transgressed bal yera'eh, and here, since he cast the benefit into the Dead Sea, there is no longer a penalty, therefore it is permitted even for eating, like the view of the Beis Yosef, and not like Hagahos Maimoniyos, who permits only benefit in this case. With dry in dry, it appears that not only one in two is obviously permitted, even according to the first view in se'if 9, which is stringent during Pesach; here, after Pesach, it is permitted even without casting the benefit into the Dead Sea, for here he did not transgress bal yera'eh, since biblically one in two is nullified. Even rabbinically, the prohibition is only because of the stringency of the smallest amount, and there is no reason to penalize him. Moreover, even one in one is permitted at least through redemption, and its law is like liquid with liquid in less than sixty. In Hagahos Maimoniyos regarding this law, he wrote: if chametz wheat became mixed with kosher wheat and it was not known until after Pesach, etc., as the Beis Yosef wrote. One should not learn from this that if it was known before Pesach, redemption after Pesach is insufficient. That is not so. The reason of R. Shimon's penalty after Pesach makes no distinction between whether one transgressed intentionally or inadvertently; in both cases he penalizes. Just as where it became mixed after Pesach unintentionally in sixty it is permitted even if it had been intentional on Pesach, as Rashi, the Rosh, and the Tur explicitly wrote in this siman, so too redemption helps even if it was intentional. Furthermore, what does his knowledge matter? In any case, the mixture itself is inadvertent. Rather, it is obvious that this phrase "it was not known" is not precise, and he mentioned it only as the usual case, because we certainly are not dealing with a wicked person who knows about it and did not destroy it at the time of its prohibition. Above in this siman, se'if 3, I wrote that even ab initio one may add to the mixture before Pesach, since it is still permitted, and the proof is from sukkah. If so, here with dry in dry, if it is one in two, one may add kosher wheat to it and grind it, and its law will be like liquid with liquid, as above in se'if 3, and he may eat it even on Pesach. If he wishes to keep it until after Pesach, he does not need this, and it is permitted even without redeeming it into the Dead Sea. All this appears correct to me. Although my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote some details in this matter unlike what I wrote, after analysis the matters are clear, with the help of Heaven.
