(א) that became mixed. The general principle in this matter is: a mixture that is of a kind in which there is no d'oraisa prohibition — that is, a mixture in which one would not violate bal yeira'eh and bal yimatzeh, as written above at the beginning of siman 442 — even though there is a d'rabbanan prohibition in it during Pesach, nevertheless it is permitted to mix it before Pesach in order to keep it [over Pesach], and this is not called nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, as written above in sub-section 13 (and for this reason chametz is called a davar sheyesh lo matirin) — which is not so if one intentionally mixed it after Pesach, and certainly during Pesach, which is considered as nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, which is forbidden even regarding a d'rabbanan prohibition, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 99.

(ב) On Pesach. Meaning: it became mixed unintentionally on Pesach in sixty and [Pesach] passed; and even if one kept it intentionally, it is permitted, for one did not transgress b'eyna from the Torah in bal yeira'eh, and it is permitted after Pesach even for consumption:

(ג) In sixty. This is Tur's view. But all Poskim wrote that we do not require sixty; therefore it appears to me that one who is lenient has not lost even in less than sixty, provided it became mixed in a majority — even for consumption after Pesach — later authorities. And see in Sefer Even HaEzer that he reconciles Tur's view at length in siman 447 Magen Avraham section 4. And if the prohibition is a davar ham'amid, such as beer yeast that passed Pesach and leavened in dough — Or Zarua forbids; R' Meir of Lublin siman 121 (and see Yoreh De'ah siman 123 seif 15 in Shach there). Or Zarua: my ancestor the Gaon ruled regarding butter in which bread or flour soaked and Pesach passed while it remained — one should take the benefit of the bread or flour to the sea or river and sell the butter to a gentile; Chayei Adam in siman 442 wrote that this speaks when water became mixed with the butter, for otherwise it is fruit juice and does not chametz [see there]. And similarly he ruled regarding folded matzah found after Pesach — one casts the fold and also the benefit of the absorbed prohibition for a prutah, and sells the rest to a gentile; but if there were sixty in the matzah against the fold, one casts the fold to the sea and the rest is permitted even for consumption; and the same applies to wheat found in baked matzah; but if puffed, all of it is forbidden forever even for benefit.
