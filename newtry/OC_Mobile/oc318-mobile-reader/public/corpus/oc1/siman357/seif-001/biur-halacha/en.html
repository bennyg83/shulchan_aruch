(א) A courtyard less than, etc. — This refers when water poured in the courtyard descends under the courtyard wall to reshut harabbim; but if the bottom of the courtyard is built well and water will not flow there, it is certainly permitted; and so is implied in Avodat HaKodesh.

(ב) That the hollow hold two se'ah — The hollow must hold two se'ah before water reaches the hole at its lip through which discharge goes to reshut harabbim; for if the hole is near the pit's ground, as soon as he pours a little water it goes outside through it [Rabbenu Yonasan in his commentary; see Rashi s.v. min hanekev and Rashba; nevertheless for practical law it is close that they too concede to Rabbenu Yonasan, and so explained Bertenura]. However, in the Yerushalmi on this mishnah it does not seem like R' Yonasan's explanation — see there.

(ג) And the water fall from his hand to makom patur — In Beit Yosef in the name of the Ritva: the hollow of the pit is considered reshut hayachid, since the pit's mouth is within the courtyard — the pit is included in holes of reshut hayachid; and from the Shulchan Aruch it seems that even when he makes the pit's mouth outside, as long as it adjoins the courtyard and there is no reshut harabbim between them, the pit into which he pours is makom patur [Peri Megadim]. In my humble opinion his words are not clear at all, for even per the Shulchan Aruch we require making the pit's mouth from inside; and so the mishnah plainly implies regarding pouring water inside the courtyard; and so Avodat HaKodesh and Rabbenu Yeruham imply. Also I do not know whether it is permitted to pour water from outside into the pit after the hollow is mainly covered with boards and above is reshut harabbim — if there is a crack in the pit's covering, will pouring water inside nullify that place above as reshut harabbim? Seemingly on the contrary: if the pit is uncovered it has the name makom patur when not four by four wide, or karmelit when not ten high; but when covered with boards, what helps? Granted for Rambam's explanation who makes a dome above — certainly movement is not permitted; but per what the Shulchan Aruch wrote that he covers with boards, it requires study whether this is permitted in the essential law; and especially the Ritva's reasoning that since its mouth is in the courtyard it is called holes of reshut hayachid — see Mordechai's article and Beit Meir who questioned this.

(ד) He may pour, etc. — meaning permitted to pour in the courtyard even though water may go out under the courtyard wall to reshut harabbim — we are not concerned; all the more if he pours into the pit itself [poskim].
