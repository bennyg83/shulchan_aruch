1) [Se'if 1] A courtyard that is smaller than four amos, etc. The general principle underlying this siman is as follows. Our Sages estimated that every person needs two se'ah of water each day for washing the face, hands, and feet and for other necessary uses. It is already known that on Shabbos it is forbidden to pour from a private domain into a public domain or a karmelis. A person normally pours wastewater into the courtyard adjacent to the house, which belongs to him and is likewise a private domain, so that he transfers from one private domain to another. It is also known that ordinary courtyards contain dust and earth during the summer. Every person wishes to sprinkle and settle the dust in the courtyard so that it does not become airborne, and therefore pours there the wastewater used each day. Our Sages estimated that with two se'ah of water an ordinary person can sprinkle a courtyard measuring four by four amos. The water settles the dust and earth and is absorbed into the ground. Even where the person does not wish to sprinkle the courtyard, one authority maintains that it is nevertheless permitted because the water is used up and absorbed into the ground and does not make the courtyard's ground filthy. Since this is so, the person is not particular that it specifically flow outside. Consequently, even if it later flows outside, that was not the person's intention, and it is not deemed as though the person transferred from a private domain into a public domain. If, however, the courtyard is smaller than four by four amos, the two se'ah of water are excessive and are not absorbed into the ground. The courtyard becomes mire and mud, which repulses the person, and the person is therefore pleased that the water flows outside. If it flows outside, the person's intention has been fulfilled, and it is deemed as though the person poured directly from a private domain into a public domain. A decree must therefore be imposed lest one pour directly from a private domain into a public domain. Accordingly, one must make a pit in the courtyard that can contain two se'ah. When the person then pours into the courtyard, the person is not particular that the water flow outside, since it can remain in the pit without making the courtyard filthy, and this is not deemed transferring from a private domain into a public domain. When there is a pit, it makes no difference whether water was already in it before Shabbos or not, or whether the person uses several se'ah on Shabbos, since the pit serves merely as a reminder. All this is from the words of the Levush. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1.
2) There. Which is adjacent to a public domain, etc. This excludes a courtyard adjacent to a karmelis, where it is unnecessary to make a pit, as stated at the end of the se'if. See there.
3) There. One may not pour into it, etc. This is a Rabbinic decree lest one pour directly from a private domain into a public domain, as explained above.
