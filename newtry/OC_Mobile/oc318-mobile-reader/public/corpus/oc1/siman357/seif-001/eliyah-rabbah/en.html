[1] Into a makom petur, etc. The Ritva writes that one should place the mouth of the drain within the private domain, making it a hole of the private domain.
[2] [Levush] Such as when it is long, etc. The same applies to any other shape that can contain a square of four by four handbreadths, as stated by the Beit Yosef. A soft plastered surface joins when within four handbreadths, according to the Beit Yosef and Rabbi Yonatan. But Avodat HaKodesh, page 16, writes that an upper chamber built between two balconies that opens to both joins them if the balconies are opposite one another. If one is higher and one lower, they do not join. This implies that when they are opposite, they join even if distant from one another.
[3] It does not require, etc. See the end of this siman, where some disagree regarding a drain. The same dispute applies here, as the Beit Yosef and later authorities write.
