(א) A courtyard, etc. — All matters in this siman are as follows: the Sages estimated that every person needs two se'ah of water daily to wash face, hands, feet, and other needs; and it is known that on Shabbat it is forbidden to pour from reshut hayachid to reshut harabbim or karmelit. Everyone pours waste water into his courtyard near his house, which is his domain and reshut hayachid, taking from reshut hayachid to reshut hayachid. The Sages estimated that two se'ah of water are absorbed in ground of four by four amot and do not soil the courtyard; therefore he is not particular that they go outside specifically — and then even if they later go outside it was not his intent; on Shabbat we require melacha machsheves, and it is not as taking from reshut hayachid to reshut harabbim. But if less than four by four, two se'ah are excessive and not absorbed — the courtyard becomes mud and mire; in summer everyone is particular that his courtyard be clean, therefore he wants them to flow outside, and if they go outside his intent is fulfilled — decree lest he pour directly from reshut hayachid to reshut harabbim. Therefore make a pit holding two se'ah; then when he pours in the courtyard he is not particular that they go outside since they can descend and rest in the pit and his courtyard will not be soiled — and even if they go outside it is not as taking from reshut hayachid to reshut harabbim.
(ב) That it adjoins reshut harabbim — excluding when it adjoins karmelit, as below at the end of the seif.
(ג) In summer months — for a person is particular about soiling his courtyard and presumably wants it to go outside.
(ד) And any pit, etc. — And the same: he may change its depth and width provided the hollow holds two se'ah.
(ה) Outside — meaning at the side of the courtyard.
(ו) In order, etc. — And also so there be no obstacle to the public [Darkei Moshe].
(ז) That it be makom patur — for through the covering that place is removed from public use; and in the case the Rama depicted without covering it is also makom patur since less than four by four — only lest the pit's ground rise with mud and mire and be less than three tefachim high and be nullified regarding reshut harabbim.
(ח) He may pour, etc. — even if it was already full from before he may pour into it, for d'oraisa there is no prohibition except pouring directly to reshut harabbim — the Sages fixed this pit as a Shabbat reminder lest he forget and cast directly to reshut harabbim; since there is recognition through making the pit it is permitted; and for this reason they permitted pouring as much as he wishes even though the pit holds only two se'ah and certainly the remainder will go outside — he is pleased so his courtyard not be soiled; nevertheless permitted since he is reminded through the pit and they did not decree about it.
(ט) At all — for we decree lest he come to pour two se'ah, which is a person's daily measure.
(י) Long and narrow — for example eight amot long and two wide; nevertheless there is measure here to absorb two se'ah.
(כ) (יא) In the rainy season — for a person is not particular about soiling his courtyard and does not intend they go outside; and if because others will see a shower pipe and come to permit in summer too, we say: attribute them more to rainwater, and even not during rain they are absorbed from prior rains.
(ל) (יב) Permitted to pour, etc. — meaning even without making a pit at all.
(מ) (יג) Two upper chambers — meaning two projections wide opposite the upper-room openings [each projection two amot wide, four amot long opposite the upper room], with soft plaster on them fit to absorb water poured on them.
(נ) (יד) Adjoining one another — meaning within four tefachim; they combine as if in one place, and thus it is four amot by four amot and permitted to pour on them, and from there water descends directly to reshut harabbim.
(ס) (טו) But, etc., to karmelit — for even when adjoining reshut harabbim the prohibition is only a decree lest he throw directly to reshut harabbim; in karmelit this does not apply, for even if he throws directly there is no Torah prohibition; see below end of this siman that some dispute.
