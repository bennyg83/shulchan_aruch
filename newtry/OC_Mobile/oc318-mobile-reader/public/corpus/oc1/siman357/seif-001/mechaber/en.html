The laws of a courtyard smaller than 4 amot and of a drain. It contains 3 seifim: A courtyard smaller than 4 by 4 amot that adjoins the public domain — one may not pour water into it on Shabbos in the summer, because since it lacks 4 amot, the 2 se'ah of water that a person ordinarily uses each day will not be absorbed there, and it is as if he pours into the public domain. Therefore one must make a pit whose cavity holds 2 se'ah. {Rama: And any pit half an amah by half an amah with a height of three-fifths of an amah holds 2 se'ah. [Rambam]} This is so whether he makes it inside the courtyard or outside; but if he makes it outside, he must cover it with boards so that it will be a makom patur and the water will fall from his hand into a makom patur. Once he makes a pit, he may pour into it as much as he wishes. If it does not hold 2 se'ah, he may not pour into it at all. If the courtyard is 4 by 4, even if it is long and narrow, or in the rainy season even a courtyard of any size, one may pour into it as much as he wishes. A courtyard and an achsadrah — meaning a structure with 3 walls, and a fourth wall where a door is set that does not enclose at all except across the width of the house, and sometimes they make one amah of paneling on each side, so explained the Aruch, and a panel is a bit of straight, even wall — that neither alone has 4 amot combine to 4 amot to permit pouring into them. {Rama: And the same law applies to 2 galleries in front of 2 upper stories with a ceiling above them in which water can be absorbed: if they adjoin one another, they combine. [Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonatan]} When does this apply? Regarding a courtyard that adjoins the public domain. But if it adjoins a karmelit, even if it is very small, it does not need a pit even in the summer (see above, siman 377).
