In two houses, etc. This implies specifically two separate houses. Within one house, even if the groups did not originally enter with intent to join, they can combine for zimun once the obligation has arisen. Darkei Moshe writes this in the name of Or Zarua, and Nachalat Tzvi rules accordingly, unlike the Bach and Magen Avraham. I also saw that Rabbenu Yerucham in Yoreh Deah and Tzeidah LaDerech do not mention any requirement that they initially entered with intent to join.
And some say, etc. So writes the Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbenu Yonah, and so writes Kol Bo. The Taz says that 'public domain' here need not mean a formal reshut harabim sixteen amot wide. Even a private path separating them may interrupt, as a path interrupts for the laws of peah and Shabbos. The Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchot Matanot Aniyim, writes that if the path is not fixed during the rainy season it does not interrupt. See there. This requires study.
[Levush] The zimun blessings for him, etc. The Shulchan Aruch explicitly says 'the zimun blessing.' Magen Avraham writes that if he cannot hear, he answers Amen, as written in siman 139, seif 7. See my explanation in siman 193 that initially they must hear the entire Birkat HaMazon. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that the custom is for many separate groups to make zimun and then each individual recites Birkat HaMazon for himself.
