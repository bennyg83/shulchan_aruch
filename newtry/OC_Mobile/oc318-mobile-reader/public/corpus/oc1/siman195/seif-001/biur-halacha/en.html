Two groups — see Mishna Berurah; and know that from the novellae of Rashba, who wrote the reason that therefore seeing one another helps when they entered from the outset for this purpose — because it is like reclining together — it is proven from this that he holds that two groups, when each group has enough for zimun, combine to discharge one another; and when some of them see one another, the same applies in one group of three where two of them reclined at one table and the third at another table — as long as they see one another they combine; even though for birkat hamazon we require reclining together, or for us at least sitting at one table — here it is different, for it deals with their meal's beginning: they entered with this intent that even if they divide into places, nevertheless their intent is to be connected; and this helps [as with "we will eat bread in such-and-such place," explained in siman 167 seif 2, that it helps even though they did not recline together since they stipulated from the outset — and the same here; and so in Shitah Mekubetzet]. And so in Chiddushei Ramban at the beginning of chapter 3 "who ate" — it implies he explains our mishnah whether or not each group has enough for zimun [and there is a printing error in his words there and it should read "even if not in every group," etc.]. However he himself holds there that if we require reclining, seeing one another does not help — and we do not rule like him in this. But Rashbash in a responsum [brought in Chiddushei Ra'ah] wrote that only with two groups where each group has enough to invite zimun — and we do not require only to combine so that one discharges the other — therefore seeing one another helps; unlike when there was not enough for zimun in every group except through combination — for this no remedy helps; and so is also the view of the Gra [brought in Mishna'ot Altuna printing]. And in truth it is more reasonable like their words, for even in "we will eat bread in such-and-such place" the Mechaber ruled there in seif 2 [and it is from Terumat Yisrael] that only when they were not scattered at the time of eating — and he did not distinguish at all between seeing one another and not seeing — whence it is clear that whoever did not sit together at least does not help the condition of "we will eat bread in such-and-such place" to combine to become obligated in zimun; and how does it help here that they entered from the outset for this? And so is proven in Chiddushei Ra'ah on this sugya, that it deals when there is in each group enough for zimun, see there. And know that Rashbash distinguishes further: even when there is in each group enough for zimun, but through their combination it will newly arise that they bless with the Name — such as when there are five in each group — seeing one another and entering from the outset also does not help, since through this they combine to bless with the Name. And know further that even for Rashba who is lenient — nevertheless this is only when they entered from the outset for this; but without this it does not help, even when seeing one another and in one house, as explained explicitly in Chiddushei Rashba. And even for those who are lenient in one house and do not require entering from the outset — as we wrote in Mishna Berurah — this is only with two groups where each has enough for zimun, not when they do not have enough for zimun, as Magen Avraham wrote.

Under any circumstance — see Mishna Berurah. And behold from Taz it implies that regarding members of the wedding canopy to combine together for birkat hamazon, reshus harabbim does not separate; and it implies from his words that likewise they do not need combination of shames, even when they do not see one another. But in Eliyah Rabbah and Perishah it states that the law of the Rosh is not said at all regarding combination for birkat hamazon — for also members of the wedding canopy we require the conditions explained in this siman; and the law of the Rosh is said only regarding berachat chatanim; and so ruled in Da'at Hakhamim.
