3) There: If some of them can see, etc. If all the wedding guests could not eat in one house with the groom and divided into groups, they all recite the wedding blessings even if they ate in houses that do not open toward the place where the groom eats. It is not necessary to say this when an attendant connects them; even if no attendant connects them, since those in the other houses began eating when the wedding guests began, they are all considered one group for the wedding blessings, because they eat from the meal prepared for the wedding. Tur in the name of Rosh. Hagahos Maimoniyos on chapter 2 of Hilchos Berachos, however, writes in the name of Sefer Mitzvos Gadol that Ritzba ruled to recite the wedding blessings in houses opening toward the house where the wedding meal is held, provided that an attendant connects them, even though the two groups can neither see one another nor hear the person reciting the blessing. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that as practical halachah it appears proper to rely upon Rosh, of blessed memory. The Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly in Even HaEzer, siman 62, seif 11, and Levush likewise rules this way. Levush's words imply that they combine specifically for the wedding blessings, but not for Zimun, for which seeing or an attendant is required as with other groups; see there. Perishah, paragraph 4, Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, and Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 3, write the same. See below, paragraph 10.
3) There: They combine for Zimun. This means that they may combine and make one Zimun together, but they certainly may divide and each group make its own Zimun. Bach; Yeshuos Yaakov.
