(s.k. 2) And such as when they entered, etc. — from the stam of, etc., because Tur wrote in the language of Shulchan Aruch, and the Rav Beit Yosef wrote on it, and these are his words: per the conclusion of the Rosh, who wrote that our master in siman 193 seif 2 — that even though they did not bless berachat ha-Motzi together they are fixed for zimun — in two groups too we do not require that they enter from the outset with intent to combine together; and if so our master need not write stam "and such as when they entered from the outset to combine together," etc. However it is possible that what our master wrote "and such as when they entered," etc., refers only to combining two houses, etc. — that two groups in one house, even if they did not enter from the outset with intent to combine together, combine; and so is clear from the simple sense of the Yerushalmi, end of his words Rabbeinu Beit Yosef. And one cannot say that also in Shulchan Aruch he holds so — for he need not conceal in Tur's language, since Rabbeinu Beit Yosef himself finds Tur's language difficult here, and he should explain alongside that they need not combine together from the outset, but specifically in two houses; rather one must say that in Shulchan Aruch he retracted.

And even according to what is written in siman 193 seif 2, etc. — this is Rabbeinu Beit Yosef's difficulty cited above.

And what he wrote "every group," etc. — see what Rabbeinu Beit Yosef wrote in his first version.

And for the first view — meaning: according to what Rabbeinu Beit Yosef held in his book on Tur — that was his first view, and afterward in Shulchan Aruch he retracted and it is a second view; and "for the first view" means according to his first view, that he compared this that he wrote to that in siman 193 — even though one could say it is different in siman 193 where they are fixed together at one table; nevertheless one must say that he holds: since there is, etc.
