(א) Gezuztraot. Meaning boards projecting from the edge of the ceiling of the upper story to reshus ha-rabbim. Rashi:

(ב) Behold it is like an opening. Meaning through this it is permitted to carry in the two courtyards from one to the other if they wish to make an eruv together; on this Rama wrote that if the distance between them is not four handbreadths, even without a board there is an opening since they can step from one to the other and its law is like a groove in siman 372 seif 16 — wherever there is an opening, meaning if it was mostly closed up to ten handbreadths the closeness between the courtyards over the gezuztraot that they can step from one to the other is an opening; and in Tosafos daf 78 it is written in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that we are not dealing with placing a board from one to the other, and the law of a groove applies to gezuztraot — if there are four handbreadths between them they make an eruv of two and not one like a groove, but in less than four they regularly step from one to the other therefore when there are not four from one to the other they make an eruv of two, and if they wish one, etc., and this is as stated. And in Beit Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh he wrote here that if there is not four handbreadths distance between them they need not a board from one to the other but even without a board they make an eruv of one and not two as long as they are opposite one another — end of his words; behold this contradicts what was said in the name of Tosafos and the words of Rama, and the resolution is that this deals with making an eruv to carry from gezuztra to its fellow and they are very wide — the air gap between them that lacks four handbreadths nullifies the opening and it is similar to a wall that was breached more than ten and they cannot make an eruv except one, unlike the words of Tosafos and Rama here where the gezuztraot are not wide and one does not wish to use them except that through them there will be an opening from courtyard to courtyard and in this they make an eruv as they wish — all this is clear and simple:

(ג) They cannot join together. The reason in Tur: when they place the board from one to the other it comes from the corner of one to the other and it is not considered an opening, for an opening in a corner people do not make; it implies that whenever they are not in a corner the board helps properly even though there is bending; and thus in Tosafos' question that regarding a board it helps even when not opposite one another:
