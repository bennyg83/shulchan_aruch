The law of two balconies on two upper stories. It contains one se'if.
Two balconies that project from two upper stories, one opposite the other, and one placed between them a board four [handbreadths] wide — this is like a doorway, and they may make an eruv together. {Rama: And if the distance between them is not four [handbreadths], then even without a board the law is likewise (Maggid, chapter 3).} And if they wished, each may make an eruv for itself. But if they were not opposite one another — one drawn to the east and one to the west — they cannot make an eruv together. Likewise, if one was higher than the other, it is not considered a doorway. And this applies where they are three handbreadths distant from one another; but if they are within three [handbreadths] of one another, whether in distance or in height, it is indeed well considered like a doorway.
