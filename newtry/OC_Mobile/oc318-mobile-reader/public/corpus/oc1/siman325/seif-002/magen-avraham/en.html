To give it to him. Nevertheless, it is forbidden to place it directly into his hand, as stated in seif 1; see there. It appears to me that this is also the intent of the Mordechai in the name of Tosafos. What he wrote initially, that it is permitted to give it to them even by hand, comes only to exclude the view of those who say that one may not give it to them by hand, but must place it before them without saying anything. Regarding this, he writes that the Or Zarua permits giving it to them by hand, meaning that one may tell him to take it out, since nowadays we have no true reshus harabim; see siman 345, seif 7. It is nevertheless forbidden to place it directly into his hand, since this can be avoided. This appears correct to me. Beit Yosef did not explain it this way, but my explanation appears correct. It is obvious that even if the utensil belongs to the non-Jew, one may not place it into his hand because this constitutes akirah, contrary to Olas Shabbos.
