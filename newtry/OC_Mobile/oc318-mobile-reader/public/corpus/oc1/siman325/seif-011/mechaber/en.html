A Gentile who picked grass for his animal, a Jew that is not acquainted with him may feed his animal after the Gentile leaves by way of barring the animal’s way and preventing it from straying, thus guiding it to the grass. It is forbidden to lead the animal to the grass so that he should not come to lift the grass and feed it to the animal as the grass is Muktze. If he is acquainted with the Gentile it is forbidden. The same applies to all cases where it is possible to add for the Jew, but when there is no reason to be concerned that the Gentile will add for him, for example he lit a light for himself or he made a ramp to descend, because one light or one ramp is adequate for all and therefore even if he knows him it is permitted.
