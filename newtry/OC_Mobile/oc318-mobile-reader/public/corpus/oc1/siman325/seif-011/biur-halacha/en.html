If he knows him — behold the Mechaber ruled stam here, implying that even if the gentile did it not in the Jew's presence it is forbidden, as Beit Yosef wrote that so appears the view of Rif and Rambam, who did not mention Abbaye's words that it requires specifically in his presence; and so appears Tosafos Beitzah daf 3, not like Ran and Maggid Mishneh who wrote that only in his presence is forbidden, for then there is room to decree lest he intend to increase on his account at the time of doing; and Taz and Eliyah Rabbah copied thus; and in truth according to Ran and Maggid Mishneh several Rishonim wrote so explicitly without any disputant [and it is Abbaye's view in the gemara, and it appears to them that even Rava does not argue on him here, and it appears so from the gemara's language "Rava said: even say in his presence," etc. — implying he too concedes that not in his presence there is no prohibition at all, except here with the ramp even in his presence it is permitted on account of "a lamp for one, a lamp for a hundred" — see there]; and it is the view of Ra'avan brought in Magen Avraham, Rashba in his novellae, Meiri, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Ramach brought in Knesset HaGedolah — all wrote that only if he did it in his presence is it forbidden; and at first glance one could rely to be lenient on all these great authorities, especially since Rambam [and Rif] did not mention explicitly the opposite, and Maggid Mishneh is uncertain that perhaps he also holds thus, but nevertheless one should not be lenient, for Tosafos relied on Tosefta Shabbat that proves explicitly that even not in his presence is forbidden — even though there is no concern lest he increase on his account in this melacha, we are concerned lest they become accustomed for another Shabbat — see there; and their words are brought in Or Zarua; and also in Rabbeinu Yerucham he agreed with them [except Rabbeinu Yerucham's view seems to contradict himself, for at first he wrote simply that only in his presence is forbidden, and afterward he brings Tosafos' view in the name of Tosefta that for this reason even not in his presence is forbidden on account of another Shabbat — and Magen Avraham already addressed this]; therefore one should not be lenient, like the conclusion of the aforementioned Acharonim; and in truth there is no practical difference for halacha, for even if we rule like these great authorities to be lenient — behold Magen Avraham wrote that specifically when the gentile does not know the Jew needs him; otherwise it is forbidden to all, even not in his presence; for the essence of requiring "in his presence" is so he should know the Jew needs and increase on his account; and if so, when the gentile knows the Jew needs — forbidden even not in his presence; and when he does not know — also for Taz and Eliyah Rabbah it is permitted [and even though it seems from Taz that he holds according to those who are lenient when not in his presence it is permitted in every manner — it does not seem proper to be lenient with two leniencies together]. [Gloss: and Magen Avraham's words are wondrous to me, for in R' Gamliel's ramp the gentile certainly knew they needed the ramp to descend, and nevertheless Abbaye said therefore they are permitted to descend because he did it not in his presence; and also from the plain sense of the Rishonim who are lenient it implies only in his presence is forbidden and not in his presence is permitted in every manner; and it is a great reasoning in my humble opinion that when the gentile knows the Jew needs, he is not compelled to intend for him — does he intend for all his acquaintances?
