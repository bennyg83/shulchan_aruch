(א) For the need of his animal — and per the view of those who are lenient mentioned above, even when he gathered for the need of a Jew's animal it is permitted, since it can eat while attached if not that there was a river in between that it cannot eat from there, or the grasses were sparse that there is no way to feed while attached; but other poskim did not distinguish in this, and the essential ruling is like them, as above; but if it is a time of distress that the Jew has no grasses, Chayei Adam wrote he can rely on the view of those who are lenient mentioned above and tell the non-Jew to feed it to his animal, even if he gathered for the Jew's need; and likewise by way of the non-Jew drawing water from the river in a place where the animal can itself go and drink and he has no other water to give it — even though the non-Jew filled on behalf of the Jew, permitted; but to tell the non-Jew to gather grasses in every case — forbidden, for it is a d'oraisa melacha; and likewise to tell the non-Jew to draw water from a pit that is reshut hayachid to reshut ha'rabbim in every case — forbidden; but if around the pit is karmelit, he permits there, in the category of s.k. 62 through a non-Jew on account of tza'ar ba'alei chayyim, and specifically once a day and not more; and through a Jew — always forbidden:

(ב) A Jew feeds after him — for muktzeh is permitted in benefit:

(ג) But if he knows him, etc. — from the Mechaber's stam it is implied that even if not in the Jew's presence he gathered — forbidden if he knows him, and so ruled several Acharonim; and nevertheless we require that the non-Jew know at the time of his melacha that this Jew who knows him needs this; for then the decree applies that he will intend to increase on his behalf; and there are poskim who are stringent who hold that even if the non-Jew already did and did not know then that the Jew needs — forbidden if he knows him, for even though there is no concern he will increase on his behalf on this Shabbat, they decreed that through benefiting from his melacha they will accustom him to increase on his behalf for another Shabbat; and at a time of need one may be lenient on this:

(ד) Forbidden — that is, only until evening, and he need not wait bichdei sheya'asu [Magen Avraham]:

(ה) Permitted — for even for those who forbid on account of another Shabbat we are not concerned in this that he will do on behalf of the Jew himself for another Shabbat, for specifically regarding a thing he will do for himself we are concerned lest perhaps he increase on behalf of a Jew; but not that he will do only on behalf of a Jew:
