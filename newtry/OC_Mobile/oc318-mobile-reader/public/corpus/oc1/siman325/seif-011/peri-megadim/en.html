(א) But — Taz: it seems he holds Beit Yosef in his long book brought Maggid Mishneh and Ran's view that Rava concedes to Abbaye that not in his presence is permitted — even if he knows the Jew needs him; and in his presence forbidden when he knows he needs him; and what Beit Yosef wrote "and so the law according to his view" — meaning he is compelled that if not in his presence, even if he knows, it is permitted; and Beit Yosef holds Rava argues on Abbaye, and whoever knows him — not in his presence also forbidden, all who know the Jew needs him; if not so — permitted both in his presence and not in his presence; for otherwise the Mechaber should have explained and distinguished between in his presence and not in his presence; rather he agrees with Maggid Mishneh that all where "if the Jew needs" — permitted even in his presence and if he knows him; and all who know the Jew needs — even not in his presence forbidden, like Rava who argues on Abbaye; and what Beit Yosef wrote that a gentile who roasted fish is forbidden lest they accustom for the coming Shabbat — meaning stam salted fish everyone desires, and it is known he needs it for a Jew — Magen Avraham responsum 28 seems he has another approach in Beit Yosef's words; and see there will be explained. And in a city whose majority are gentiles — permitted; siman 495 Magen Avraham 22: from stam there is no concern lest he increase on his account — it seems somewhat that all not in his presence, "if the Jew needs" — permitted; and it is possible a majority of gentiles is preferable, for he acts with the majority's intent and has no concern at all for a Jew unless it is known they increase for a Jew — and see there will be explained further:
