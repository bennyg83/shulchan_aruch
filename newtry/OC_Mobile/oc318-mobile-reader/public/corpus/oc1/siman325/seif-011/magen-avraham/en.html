(א) For his animal's need. And for Rabbeinu Tam, even if he gathered for an Israelite's need — permitted, since the animal could go by itself and eat there, as stated in seif 11, unless it stands on the other side of the river (Tosafos and Maggid Mishneh); and Maor wrote in a place where there are not many grasses growing attached — forbidden, for there is no way to feed it attached, as it states "she eats and goes" — if so his action helps; Ohel Yesharim s.k. 32 cited the gemara's words here and did not notice his words contradict one another, as Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote siman 347; and they are unnecessary:

(ב) And they are muktzeh. See siman 324 seif 3 and siman 507 seif 5 — not like Ohel Yesharim:

(ג) If he recognizes him — forbidden. And Ran and Maggid Mishneh wrote that if he did it not in his presence — permitted, for we are not concerned lest he increase for him; and so Ra'avan; and if he knows he needs it, even not in his presence — forbidden; and so Rashi — when he did it, R' Gamliel was not there, it implies the main permission when not in his presence is that we say he did not do it for an Israelite's need since he did not know; but if he recognizes him and knows he needs it — we say he did it with his intent; and the same if he is in his presence and does not know the Israelite needs it — permitted; and so the gemara; and Beit Yosef wrote that even not in his presence — forbidden; and so Rabbeinu Yerucham — a non-Jew who salted a salted fish for himself — forbidden even though "lest he increase for him" does not apply since he already salted; nevertheless some forbid, as in Tosefta — if he recognizes him, forbidden lest he accustom him for the coming Shabbat; and regarding muktzeh there is none fit for a cup; and one might say perhaps he will salt it himself since there is trouble, he will remember and remind; and so Tosafos beginning of Beitzah, end quote; and it requires study, for Ri himself wrote that not in his presence is permitted; and also in Tosefta it states thus; therefore it appears specifically regarding salted fish we forbid, for it is something always needed and we are concerned lest he increase for him for the coming Shabbat; but regarding something not constantly needed, such as a non-Jew who made a linen garment for himself — permitted for an Israelite to wear, for there is no concern he will increase for him for the coming Shabbat, as we say in the gemara regarding a hide not constantly needed; and if the non-Jew recognizes him and knows he needs it, even with a artisan who works for pay — forbidden (and see siman 252 seif 4); and so in the gemara regarding turnip of Mechoza; and it appears that which we do not decree regarding a lamp and hide lest he do for him for the coming Shabbat — this is specifically when the non-Jew does the thing for himself we are concerned lest he increase for an Israelite; but we are not concerned he will do melacha for the Israelite alone for the coming Shabbat; and so in Semak: and further it appears regarding an acquaintance one need not wait bekdei she-ye'asu, since it is only on account of a decree lest he increase for him;
