(ב) And even a doubt. Bach wrote: even though the non-Jew brings a gift to an Israelite we do not say it is certain he gathered them for him, rather it is a doubt until it is known he gathered them for him, end of his words; and it requires study; and the simple sense of the gemara does not imply thus, for we say a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite — if there is in its species attached, it implies even though we do not know they were detached today and also we do not know they were detached for an Israelite — we require bekdei she-ye'asu; and since he brought to him, presumably he gathered for him, for if not — why "a non-Jew who brought a gift," it should have said "that he gathered for an Israelite"; and further Tosafos wrote in Eruvin daf 39 — that case of Bar Tavya deals with trapping of itself through traps spread from erev Yom Tov, and not a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite, end quote — it implies that if it were a non-Jew who brought a gift — forbidden even if we do not know he trapped for an Israelite; and it appears to me if the non-Jew brought hot bread to an Israelite on Shabbat — certainly with the Israelite's intent he baked it; and see seif 11; and so from what he wrote in seif 13 in name of Mordekhai — when he gives to an Israelite it is like mostly Israelite:
