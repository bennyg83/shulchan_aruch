Rambam concluded: and this is when it is not on a great highway, as is written — meaning like regarding a grave and coffin that in publicity it is not permitted forever for one for whom they were brought; and the Rav author of Shulchan Aruch omitted this; and perhaps by mistake he gave a stumbling block before the Rav author of Levush, who distinguished here and said there is not so much disgrace in flutes as in a coffin and grave, etc.; from his words it seems flutes in publicity are permitted to eulogize in the time for them to come — and this is not regarding himself, for whom they were brought is forbidden forever, and their law is like a grave and coffin. In Tur at the end of the siman in Rambam's words regarding one who transported four amot in a public domain — if he told a Jew to bring them to him, they are forbidden to him forever and to others "in the time for them to come," etc.; and Beit Yosef wondered whence Tur learned this — it is possible it is derived from what Tosafos wrote, etc.; and it is astonishing — he understood this is Tur's words, whereas it is clear from Tur's language that it is from Rambam's words; though not explicit in his language, we learn so from the reason he gave for not burying forever on account of publicity — behold regarding a living person who passes in publicity and a mitzvah is done for a gentile by melacha on Shabbat, it is not forbidden forever, as Rambam wrote in chapter 6 law 8, only "in the time it takes to do them"; therefore his words imply that on account of honor of the dead they said it should not be a disgrace to say he was buried in prohibition — wherever it is in publicity, even though he said nothing and the gentile does it on his own for this deceased — if so, a fortiori if he says so, he should not be buried and eulogized in prohibition (since he caused the prohibition); and according to Rashi's explanation one cannot learn this, for he explains what the gemara says "on the highway" that it is in publicity — meaning through this (we know it was) not done for a Jew's need [and if so there is] no stringency for a dead person more than a living person; whereas for Rambam, as we wrote — correct:
