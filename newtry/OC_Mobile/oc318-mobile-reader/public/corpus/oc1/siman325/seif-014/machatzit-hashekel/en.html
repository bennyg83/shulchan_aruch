(s.k. 14) Permitted, etc.; and Sefer HaTerumah, etc. — on Shabbat daf 151 we learned: a gentile who brought flutes — an Israelite may not eulogize with them unless they came from a nearby place; and Shmuel explained (and we rule like what was taught accordingly) and we are concerned perhaps outside the wall for us; and Rashi explained we are concerned leniently; and accordingly the mishnah is explained "unless they came from a nearby place" — meaning unless there is a place to rely they came from a nearby place, then permitted; and even if they came within the city immediately in the morning — nevertheless we rely they came from a nearby place at the beginning of the night; and for us outside the wall all night, and in the morning light they immediately entered the city; and accordingly every doubt in this we follow leniency; but Rif explained we are concerned perhaps outside the wall stringently; and what the mishnah teaches "unless from a nearby place" — meaning they must know for certain they came from a nearby place; even if brought to the city in the morning — impossible; and certainly from a nearby place; if from a distant place it would not be possible to hurry their arrival to the city in the morning; nevertheless we are concerned they came from a distant place and went all night and for us outside the wall at the end of the night; and accordingly for Rif everything we doubt in this we follow stringency; therefore Sefer HaTerumah wrote the reason of "some say for evening permitted immediately" and one need not wait bekdei she-ye'asu — we rule like Rashi that Shmuel's "we are concerned perhaps outside the wall" is lenient, as stated; nevertheless on Shabbat day certainly forbidden, for on Shabbat day it is a muktzeh doubt; and regarding muktzeh doubt all agree we follow stringency, as taught "a doubt of mukhan — forbidden"; therefore Magen Avraham challenged, for here Rabbeinu Yehuda brought two views and some poskim are concerned leniently; and in seif 16 (so it should read) he stam ruled regarding a gentile who brought flutes like Rif and did not bring Rashi's lenient view at all; and it is possible regarding a corpse they were stricter, for it is disgraceful for a corpse that they say Shabbat was desecrated on its account;
