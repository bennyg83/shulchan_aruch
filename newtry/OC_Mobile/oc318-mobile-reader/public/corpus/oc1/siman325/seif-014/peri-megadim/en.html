Not — Taz: the Mechaber's words require explanation, and I will explain there's help; also Taz's words I will explain. Behold the general intent of the Mechaber: whatever prohibition of Torah was done by a gentile is forbidden at twilight to the whole world — all the more also to one for whom it was not done; automatically if brought via public domain — forbidden to all — all the more; and if there are flutes in a nearby place and he brought them from a distant place — one requires "all the more" from a distant place that was brought; and see nevertheless; and so in chapter 6 of Moed Katan 16b: two statements — one like Shmuel, and per Geonim' explanation stringently: perhaps outside the wall for us, even in techumim alone we are concerned thus and forbidden in any case to one for whom it was brought "all the more"; and if it is known it was brought from a very distant place — one requires "all the more" from that place, even many mil as a day's walk; behold from a doubt one need not wait "all the more" except outside the techum one mil and not more — for this is the measure of "all the more" in a doubt; see Levush. And what the Mechaber concluded "and this is," etc. — Taz explained responsum 15: this deals both for him and for others; specifically when Torah prohibition was done; but outside the techum alone, in a karmelit for us who hold thus — for others in any case permitted immediately at twilight, as at the beginning. And one must know the Mechaber wrote regarding eulogizing a Jewish priest in a cemetery; and Chiddushei HaRamban also brought there — see there; and Rambam there law 6 wrote regarding flutes specifically brought for a dead Jew — meaning Jew, and one law for them: grave, flutes — that in stam for a gentile he brought; and we follow the majority; and the Mechaber omitted "forever" when brought in publicity; and in Rambam also in law 5 there it seems flutes have the law of a grave — for him in publicity forbidden forever; and I was about to say therefore the Mechaber changed from Rambam's language who wrote flutes for a dead person known, and the Mechaber grasped eulogizing a Jew with them — it seems brought in stam; and as Tosafos Shabbos 151a s.v. nochri wrote: gentiles are accustomed to bring flutes to eulogize with them for several deceased and Jews for ornament; therefore in publicity too "all the more" suffices, and "forever" does not apply except if brought for one person alone; see Chiddushei HaRamban. And therefore also in techumim alone forbidden to all, for one for whom this was brought is permitted for another after "all the more" immediately; see Chiddushei HaRamban there — except if not so, what difference to me him, what difference to me another, for all brought; and if so the Rosh rejected Rashi's explanation of what he wrote regarding flutes forever — if so it should say forever like a grave; rather "all the more," and he did not conclude as Tosafos wrote that flutes are brought for several deceased — it seems he holds even where for one in publicity "all the more" is permitted; and it is different from a grave and coffin that are attached; Magen Avraham 31; and see there will be explained; and especially according to Beit Yosef on the Rosh who explains in publicity like the Geonim, and he brought Tur's words which are from Rambam's words; and Bach also explained thus plainly that for Rambam it is explained: if in publicity with flutes like a grave; except Taz wishes to explain as it appears from Bach's flow that a Jew says to bring them for him that after death they eulogize with them — forbidden to him forever; whence does he learn this? Perhaps specifically when brought for a dead person there is disgrace in publicity, but brought for a living person to eulogize with them after death — whence do we learn this? Therefore he said there is no difference between living and brought for a dead person who died now; and specifically according to Rambam's explanation who explains "highway" as publicity on the latter part — not like Rashi who does not distinguish in a grave between private and publicity that for a Jew even private is forever forbidden; if so for flutes "all the more" is permitted, not like Rashi; and it is different from a grave that will rest in it forever and is attached — not so for Rambam who explains the latter part likewise flutes too in this manner; and what he wrote "this caused the prohibition" — the law is strained; and "on the highway" is for a gentile, and "not on the highway" — this caused the prohibition — this is what appears to me in his intent here; and see there Magen Avraham will explain further at length responsum 16:
