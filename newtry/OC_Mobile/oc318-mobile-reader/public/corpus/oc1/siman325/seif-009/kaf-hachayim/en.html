Even if the objects belong to a gentile, etc. From this language it seems that even if it is twofold benefit — he stands inside and the objects belong to the gentile — nevertheless forbidden. But in Tur it seems specifically when he stands outside and a Jew gives him — then people err to say it belongs to the Jew, therefore forbidden; but if he stands inside and the object belongs to the gentile — permitted to place before him, for the observer who takes it out says it belongs to the gentile; but when the object belongs to the Jew there is concern for those who know it belongs to the Jew, and mar'it ayin that they say he is his agent. Magen Avraham s.k. 4.
