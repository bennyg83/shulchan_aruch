F) There. And it is permitted to place sustenance before him, etc. But not into his hands, for it is akirah. Magen Avraham s.k. 2. Tosafos Shabbos, responsum 3, responsum 7 responsum 2, Chayei Adam general 62 responsum 14. And he means lest the gentile take them out and there will be akirah by the Jew and hanachah by the gentile, and there is a rabbinic prohibition to do akirah lest he also do hanachah, as is explained at the beginning of tractate Shabbos and below siman 347; and even in a doubt where the gentile might eat here — we do not make a rabbinic doubt, and there is no concern for "lest he do with his hands"; and even in a karmelit it seems forbidden, for it is a closely related decree. R' Yosef Karo responsum 2; and so Machatzit HaShekel s.k. 2 forbids even in a karmelit, for sometimes they decree a decree upon a decree — see there; and although Eliyah Rabbah responsum 5 showed a face to permit giving into his hands if the gentile stands inside, and so is Taz's view s.k. 1 — nevertheless it appears one should be stringent like the aforementioned Acharonim; and Machatzit HaShekel wrote there that even if the gentile stays a while in the courtyard for his own need — it is as standing to rest and the first akirah is nullified — perhaps it is forbidden, for they may decree another time he will do akirah and hanachah — see there; but if the gentile takes from his hand — permitted, for then the gentile does the akirah.
