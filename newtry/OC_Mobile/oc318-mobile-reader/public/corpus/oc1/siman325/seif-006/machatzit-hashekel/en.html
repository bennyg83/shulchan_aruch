(א) (s.k. 6) To give, etc. — as stated in seif 1, for then the Israelite does uprooting:

(ב) The intent of Mordekhai, thus: when Pesach falls on Shabbat — some forbid to give chametz for the gentile to take out; some permit to place before him and he takes them; and it appears for us who have no d'oraisa reshut ha-rabbim — permitted to give them even by hand even if known he will take outside:

(ג) And there was an incident in Regensburg that they sent eruv, etc.; and Tosafos wrote forbidden to give into a gentile's hand, for that is uprooting; and likewise to give him his pledge if he does not take it in the manner of wearing, end of his words; and Rabbeinu Yehuda wrote that necessarily what Mordekhai brought — Tosafos' words mean Tosafos disagree and forbid even for us who have no reshut ha-rabbim, for thus he deduced Tosafos' language that it deals in our time (see there in Rabbeinu Yehuda the necessity) — this is not so, for Mordekhai's permission he wrote likewise in name of Or Zarua; and in Hagahot Maimoniyot it is explained Or Zarua did not permit even for his view except in a place of mitzvah such as chametz on erev Pesach or on account of darkei shalom like the incident of eruv they sent, etc.; and likewise Tosafos may agree; and the Tosafos who forbid deal with an unspecified case where there is no mitzvah and no darkei shalom; rather necessarily what Mordekhai brought from Tosafos — to learn from their words even if the object is of a gentile but pledged in an Israelite's hand that the gentile is not permitted to take it without the Israelite's permission — there is maris ayin, as Tosafos wrote; and likewise to give him his pledge, etc.; but Magen Avraham explains Mordekhai's intent in citing Tosafos — from their words it is clear that if the Israelite gives into his hand literally, besides maris ayin there is another prohibition — the Israelite does uprooting; therefore even in a place of mitzvah or darkei shalom permitted to give to a gentile — it was not permitted that the Israelite give by hand, since the mitzvah and darkei shalom can be fulfilled otherwise by commanding the gentile to take it himself; they needed to permit thus maris ayin; but uprooting — why permit, as Magen Avraham wrote afterward:
