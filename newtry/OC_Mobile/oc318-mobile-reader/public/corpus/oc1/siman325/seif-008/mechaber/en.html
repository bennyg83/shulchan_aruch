An item concerning which there is no concern of trapping or of having been detached from the ground, but which was merely brought from outside the techum: if a non-Jew brought it for himself, it is permitted even that same day. If he brought it for a Jew, it may be moved even by the one for whom it was brought, but eating it that same day is forbidden for the one for whom it was brought, and in the evening only after the amount of time required to perform the act has elapsed. {Rama: And some say that the night is not included in this calculation; rather, one must wait on the first weekday the amount of time required to perform the act. [Semak; Mordechai, end of chapter Kol Ketavei]} For others it is permitted that same day. And some say that for the one for whom it was brought it is permitted immediately in the evening. And the permission for a Jew to move it even when the non-Jew brought it for himself applies only within 4 amot, or within the city if it is surrounded by a wall, provided that it was enclosed for residential purposes, meaning that it was inhabited first and enclosed afterward. Ordinary towns are presumed to have been enclosed for residential purposes, whereas ordinary fortresses are not (see above, siman 401).
