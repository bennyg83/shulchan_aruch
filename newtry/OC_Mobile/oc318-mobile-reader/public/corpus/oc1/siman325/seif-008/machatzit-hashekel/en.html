(s.k. 8) And Tur wrote Smag, etc. — there in Shulchan Aruch: Reuven lent to a gentile and Shimon became guarantor for the gentile; afterward Reuven brought Shimon collateral from the gentile and thereby was released from guarantorship; afterward at bein ha-shemashot the gentile came to Shimon in Reuven's presence and asked Shimon, since he needs the collateral, to become guarantor again and return his collateral; and Shimon answered in this language "I am as I was"; and Shimon claimed his intent in saying "I am as I was" was when he had the collateral he was not a guarantor and you should have understood my intent that I certainly did not intend to guarantee, for did you not see my refusal first against the gentile; and he ruled there with Shimon; and Smag explained what Shimon meant "did you see my refusal" — meaning did you not hear him say I am not permitted to guarantee on Shabbat, and this is false, for in truth permitted to guarantee on Shabbat, and all the more since Shabbat had not yet entered, for it was bein ha-shemashim; or that my intent was only to push off the gentile — if so also what he said "I am as I was" — likewise intent to push off the gentile, and my intent was as when he had the collateral; but Magen Avraham's view is the opposite — forbidden to guarantee on Shabbat on account of "mimtzo cheftzecha"; and what he meant "you should have understood, for you saw my refusal" — meaning did you not hear I said first it is forbidden to guarantee on Shabbat; behold you heard I know there is prohibition in the matter, and from that "I am as I was" should be understood as when he had the collateral I am not a guarantor, for how could you intend I be a guarantor — did you not know there is prohibition; and how through the gentile's urging would my mind turn to transgress a d'rabbanan prohibition:
