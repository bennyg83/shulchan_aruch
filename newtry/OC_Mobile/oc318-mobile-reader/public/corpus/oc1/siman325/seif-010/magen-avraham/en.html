(א) He filled water. Ohel Yesharim wrote, and it appears from Tosafos' words that if they filled bein ha-shemashot water — permitted to benefit from them; and the reason — they do not do at all with intent for an Israelite, end of his words; and see siman 362 seif 7 that specifically regarding finding a lost object we say he does for his own benefit — unlike extinguishing, as stated siman 334 s.k. 20 in Beit Yosef; if so here too — if he filled water for his own need, even through a non-Jew — permitted; and if for an Israelite's need, even bein ha-shemashot — forbidden; and if he comes to say it deals with his acquaintance and we do not decree lest he increase for him — this is not hinted in Tosafos' words; therefore it appears he was not precise and did not look at Rosh there who wrote explicitly regarding a lost object the prohibition is not made on account of an Israelite; and so is Tosafos' intent:

(ב) To a karmelit — permitted. Since it is d'rabbanan; see siman 276:

(ג) Since it is possible. But if the Israelite cannot go there, such as there is danger on the roads — forbidden, as stated in seif 11; and in a river even an animal is permitted (Mordekhai): Ohel Yesharim wrote for Rabbeinu Tam that if there is a river within the techum, even if the non-Jew brought water from a pit that is reshut ha-yachid to reshut ha-rabbim — permitted to give his animal to drink, for if he wished he would give it to drink from the river; and his proof from Rosh, see there; and it appears to me this is forbidden for all, for is a non-Jew who brought fruits from outside the techum and the Israelite has similar fruits — permitted to eat them; and his proof from Rosh is nothing, for Rosh concluded "and this is not reasonable at all" — it implies even Rabbeinu Tam concedes here it is forbidden; and so in Ran and in Maor and in Maggid Mishneh; and see siman 276:

(ד) And some permit, etc. I searched in Kol Bo and did not find this; and also in Darkei Moshe he did not write its source; and also in responsum of Mahariv siman 116 he wrote Rabbeinu Tam did not permit except b'dieved but l'chatchila forbidden; and so Maor (see siman 347 seif 5); and in a place of beer drinking some practice leniency to bring without eruv and rely that the non-Jew himself also drinks from it like a maidservant in the house — nevertheless some refrain, and their strength is correct, end quote (and see seif 11 that even an acquaintance is forbidden, all the more here where the Israelite's intent is primary; and see siman 276 s.k. 3; and it is possible this is the "some permit"; and see siman 347 in name of Gemara; and it appears one should not be lenient except for need, as Rama wrote; and to give him money on Shabbat — it appears forbidden, as stated siman 517):
