(א) That the non-Jew does not know him — for if he knows him, even if the non-Jew did for himself — forbidden, for they decreed lest the non-Jew increase drawing on his behalf; and see below in s.k. 66 that even drawing not in the Jew's presence is forbidden; but at least we require that the non-Jew know at the time of melacha that the Jew needs that melacha, for then we can say he will intend to increase on his behalf when he knows him; and see there further what is written on this:
(ב) In all types of use — that is, we need not say for drinking, for uprooting certainly stands for that — forbidden; but even other uses are also forbidden to use [Tosafos]:
(ג) Even another Jew — for since melacha d'oraisa was done in them on behalf of a Jew — forbidden for every Jew, as above in s.k. 26; and even at night at Motzei Shabbat it will be forbidden until bichdei sheya'asu there is time to draw new ones, as above:
(ד) To karmelit — for it is only a rabbinic melacha, permitted for another Jew, as above in s.k. 35:
(ה) Since it is possible — for he is not considered benefiting from the non-Jew's melacha on Shabbat, since it is possible for him to go down into the pit and drink there permissibly without chillul Shabbat; specifically regarding his animal it is forbidden, for it cannot drink from inside the pit; and regarding an animal too, if he brought water from a river where it is possible for it to drink from there itself, or he brought from a pit standing in reshut hayachid where it was possible to bring it there and water it without chillul Shabbat — also permitted, for even though the non-Jew brought by way of reshut ha'rabbim in prohibition, he is not considered benefiting from melacha of Shabbat, since that benefit he could have had without chillul Shabbat; but if this time the non-Jew brought water for his animal from a pit that is reshut hayachid standing in reshut ha'rabbim, that with this water it is impossible to water it permissibly, as above — forbidden, even though there is also a river there where it was possible to water it from there; for at any rate this water he brought from a place where it is impossible for it to drink permissibly:
(ו) To go there — and if the Jew cannot go there on account of danger on the roads — forbidden also according to this view; and it is obvious the same law to rinse utensils in the water or to do other uses with them that were impossible to do there inside the pit — its law is like his animal, and forbidden also according to this view; and for practical halacha the essential ruling is like the first view, which is the view of all the Rishonim, and so is implied from Gra and Taz and Shach; and only for a great need or for Shabbat need one may rely on this to be lenient, and at Motzei Shabbat one may permit immediately in every manner:
(ז) And there are those who permit — Acharonim strained greatly in the reason of the matter, for behold even among those who are lenient nearby we do not find they were lenient except regarding b'dieved, but not l'chatchila to permit telling a non-Jew, even in karmelit, and all the more in reshut ha'rabbim; and also what the one who noted noted on this, Magen Avraham wrote this is not found in Kol Bo; and Gra wrote it is the view of Rabeinu Yechiel brought in Beit Yosef in siman 307, see there:
(ח) Or other things — that is, something that is somewhat a Shabbat need:
(ט) By way of karmelit — and in Darkei Moshe it is implied they practiced to be lenient even in reshut ha'rabbim according to us:
(י) And even though one should be stringent — for on account of shevut d'shevut they do not permit l'chatchila except for a great need or for a complete mitzvah matter, such as that he needs the watering for Kiddush or to bring the hot dish prepared for Shabbat meals:
(כ) One should not protest — and nevertheless he should not give money to the non-Jew for this [Magen Avraham]; and also he should not give him the vessel into his hand and should not receive them from his hand at the time of bringing, so the Jew not perform hotza'ah and hanachah [Acharonim]:
(ל) For Shabbat need — Acharonim wrote that nevertheless one may not be lenient except in wages and the like of things needed on Shabbat that cannot be without them, except with slight distress; but things not so necessary, such as fruits and the like — one may not be lenient, and even if he wishes to enjoy them for honor of Shabbat; and those who send delicacies through a non-Jew to a guest and groom outside the eruv do not act properly:
