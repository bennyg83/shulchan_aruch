And he extended his hand — Taz. And so Bach: the textual reading in Mordechai and Tosafos wrote "this is Mordechai"; and to distinguish between giving by hand and placing before them, or giving into a gentile's hand — or as Eliyah Rabbah responsum 2 wrote: if the gentile is inside it is permitted, for it is the gentile's akirah, a new akirah; and between when the gentile stands outside and extends inward — it is the Jew's akirah — Shabbos 3a in Tosafos s.v. mai ta'ama. And in Beit Yosef where he wrote "and further" and "Tosafos wrote" — it seems they disagree with what was said initially, and not so — permitted even to give into a gentile's hand, for it is the Jew's akirah; for us who have no public domain it is also shevut in place of mitzvah and darchei shalom, permitted as in siman 307 seif 5 (and it appears in my humble opinion that telling a gentile is the same — telling a Jew is permitted to do shevut of shevut in place of mitzvah; and Magen Avraham responsum 16 seems so — where it is impossible, this too is permitted). And Taz challenged for us: why should akirah alone be forbidden — behold it is a decree upon a decree; and behold they often decreed a closely related decree; and if so, in siman 306 and 347 it will be explained on this. And he concluded that for mitzvah need, as in seif 1 that it is Shabbat need — permitted to tell a gentile for us; but here where there is no mitzvah need and when there is no deaf-mute and darchei shalom — forbidden even shevut of shevut. See Levush in what he wrote that it is forbidden if he knows the gentile will take them out, for he does half the taking out; and with a gentile's objects — requires study, for in Beit Yosef the reason is it appears the Jew commanded to take them out, etc.; and even to place before him and tell him — forbidden; and with a gentile's object he does half akirah — Magen Avraham responsum 6; and see there Eliyah Rabbah responsum 2 will explain further on this:
