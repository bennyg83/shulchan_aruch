Before him in the courtyard — see Mishna Berurah what I wrote in the name of the poskim to forbid if he gives into his hand, and that Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with them; and what they relied on from Mordechai in the name of Tosafos who wrote it is forbidden to give by hand on account of akirah — Eliyah Rabbah explained this is specifically when the gentile stands outside, for then one can say the Jew does akirah; not so when the gentile is inside and he and his hand are in one domain — it appears afterward as if the gentile does akirah anew, and it is permitted; and it seems from his words that for Mordechai even when giving on condition to take out — permitted; and this is strained, for from what Mordechai concluded afterward "and likewise to give him his pledge, etc., as a garment" — he cannot bring it inside and give to him — it is proven from this that also for Mordechai it is forbidden in this manner, for even though this is not akirah, it would be forbidden to a Jew because he does half the melacha himself; nevertheless since he gives and takes out, it appears as his agent in this, as the poskim wrote; and nevertheless in our matter it appears to me to be lenient regarding sustenance when he gives stam, even into his hand — one may rely on Eliyah Rabbah, for even according to Bach and other authorities who explicitly explain Mordechai's words — nevertheless Mordechai does not seem to forbid on account of akirah or because it appears as his agent except when it is known he will take outside; there are those who permit in our public domain, or on account of darchei shalom; and on this Tosafos wrote [or per Bach's textual reading — see there] to forbid on account of akirah [and what Bach wrote "and from Or Zarua it appears explained, etc. — I do not know his source]; and so it is proven there that he forbids with pledges, etc. — namely in this manner of taking on condition to take out; but in a matter he gives on condition to eat and he takes out by himself — there is no support at all from there to forbid on account of akirah, for akirah does not apply to a Jew here; and likewise "appears as his agent" does not apply, for in a matter of eating we rely that he will eat immediately, as the poskim wrote, unless it is known he will take out; and from the baraita that cites "one places before" — there is also no proof, for possibly because of "one places before dogs" it used this language, or it is the way of speech; and so is Rambam's language: one prepares for a gentile and places before him; and in the Jerusalem Talmud cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot and brought by the Gra — it is explicit not specifically "before him," for it is the same in his hand, as it writes: one does not give sustenance to a gentile on condition to take out — and it implies only on condition to take out is forbidden, but without condition to take out it is permitted to give in every manner, for he did not mention "before him" at all; therefore it is difficult on the Acharonim who copied simply that it is forbidden to give into his hand; however from Shiltei Gibborim on Mordechai who brought in the name of Tosafos and Magen Avraham copied — it seems even for sustenance it is forbidden to give into his hand [and behold in the Tosafos before us this is not found; and in my humble opinion it is from Tosafos daf 3a s.v. bava — see there; and the intent there is when he stands outside and extends his hand inward — and the same when he stands inside and gives on condition to take out; but from Shiltei Gibborim's brevity it does not seem so]; again I found in Or Zarua who brought that some wished to infer from the baraita specifically "before him" [but also according to them it is not explained on account of akirah; and it seems their view was that by hand is forbidden on account of mar'it ayin]; and he wrote there is no proof, as we wrote above [and know that one examining Or Zarua itself will see confusion in what was copied in Mordechai — the second "yom" in his name; explanation of all Or Zarua's words proves there explicitly, as Beit Yosef explained in his conclusion — see there]; and for halacha it appears one may rely to be lenient like Eliyah Rabbah, for there is no akirah here, and also no mar'it ayin since he does not give on condition to take out, as is implied from the Jerusalem Talmud as we wrote; and all the more in our public domain, which is only a decree upon a decree — see there in Or Zarua that he thought to be lenient for this reason in every manner; except he concludes one may not be lenient for this reason alone if he gives on condition to take out, only where there is darchei shalom:
