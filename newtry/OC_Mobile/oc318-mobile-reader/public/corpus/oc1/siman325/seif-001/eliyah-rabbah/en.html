[1] [Levush] And not in order that they take them out, etc. The Shiltei Giborim writes that if the non-Jew has no permission to eat in the courtyard, or if one gives him a large amount of food that he will certainly take out, it is prohibited.
[2] [Levush] And this is prohibited, as written, etc. This is difficult, because from the Gemara, Shabbos 3, and Tosafot there, s.v. mai ta'ama, it appears that when the non-Jew is inside, even if one places the object directly into his hand, when the non-Jew leaves it constitutes a new act of lifting. This is also difficult regarding what the Beit Yosef cites from the Mordechai in the name of Tosafot. As for the Bach's surprise that the Mordechai says 'and further' and not 'Tosafot,' he did not examine the Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 21, or the Shiltei Giborim to the first chapter of the Mordechai. It also appears to me that the contradiction raised by the Beit Yosef and all the later authorities within the Mordechai can be resolved. What he writes in the name of Tosafot, that placing it in his hand is prohibited because this constitutes lifting, refers to when the non-Jew stands outside and extends his hand inside. What the Mordechai initially permits refers to when the non-Jew is standing inside. Examine this carefully. In any event, the Levush's principal reason here is not this, for the Beit Yosef explains that the reason for the prohibition is that it appears as though the Jew placed it into the non-Jew's hand for the purpose of taking it out.
[3] Even if the objects, etc. The Magen Avraham holds that if an item belonging to a non-Jew is held as collateral, it is permitted to give it to him when he is standing inside. See seif katan 4. Nevertheless, it appears from his words that actually placing it into his hand is prohibited. According to what I explained, this too is permitted.
[4] [Levush] And for us, etc. The Shulchan Aruch did not write this. The Olat Shabbos was stringent even for us, and so too it appears from the Taz and Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, in siman 447, seif 4, they also write to permit it. One may say that chametz on Shabbos involves a mitzvah matter and is therefore treated more leniently.
