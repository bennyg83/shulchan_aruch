The Mordechai writes likewise in the first chapter of Shabbos in the name of the Or Zarua: When erev Pesach falls on Shabbos, some say that it is forbidden to hand the remaining chametz to a non-Jew, since one knows that he will carry it outside. Others say that one may place it before him and allow him to take it. It appears that according to us, who have no Torah-level public domain, it is permitted even to place it directly into the non-Jew's hand, although one knows that he will carry it outside. An incident occurred in Regensburg involving a municipal official who became ill and desired Jewish food, and it was sent to him at the direction of the city's rabbis. He further writes that it is forbidden to hand an item to a non-Jew because this constitutes an act of lifting, and likewise one may not give him his collateral unless he takes it in the manner of wearing it. End quote. This latter statement appears in the Beit Yosef in the form, Tosafos further wrote, etc. I find it difficult that handing an item to a non-Jew is called an act of lifting, for we learned at the beginning of Shabbos that if one placed an item into the other's hand and the other carried it out, both are exempt. Rashi explains that it is nevertheless forbidden initially, lest one come to perform an entire melacha on Shabbos. That decree should not apply according to us, where there is only a karmelis, since it would be a decree upon a decree. It appears that according to our custom, where we are lenient even to send something initially outside the eruv, as the Rama writes in seif 10, we are not concerned with the restrictions mentioned here except regarding matters connected with commerce. Regarding food and drink, the custom is to be lenient. The reason is that these are needed for Shabbos and a Jew cannot go to obtain them himself, so the case is comparable to a mitzvah need mentioned nearby.
