This law applies even for us, who have no public domain d'oraisa but only a karmelit; so too Mordechai chapter 1 of Shabbos in the name of Or Zarua, and this is his language: when Passover falls on Shabbat — some say it is forbidden to give a gentile chametz, since he knows he will take it outside; and some say one places before him and he takes — permitted. It appears that for us, who have no public domain d'oraisa, it is permitted to give them even by hand even if it is known he will take it outside; and there was already an incident in Regensburg where they sent to a sick person food after a Jewish meal, and they sent it on the authority of the city's rabbis. And further he wrote that it is forbidden to give to a gentile, for this is akirah; and likewise to give him his pledge unless he takes it as a garment, eating, and the like; and further he wrote that we copied this writing in Beit Yosef in these words; and Tosafos wrote, etc. — and it is astonishing in what he wrote that when one gives into a gentile's hand it is akirah — behold we learned at the beginning of Shabbos: or he placed inside and took out — both are exempt; Rashi explained: but forbidden l'chatchila lest he come to do a complete melacha on Shabbat; and this decree does not apply to us, for we have only a karmelit, which is a decree upon a decree.
