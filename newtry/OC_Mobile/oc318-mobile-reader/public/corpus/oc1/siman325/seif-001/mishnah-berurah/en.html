(א) To invite a non-Jew on Shabbat — even though on Yom Tov it is forbidden to invite a non-Jew to eat with him, as a decree lest he increase cooking on his account, as below in siman 512 regarding Shabbat there is no concern for this, and it is permitted:
(ב) And it is permitted to give, etc. — even though it is forbidden to exert oneself on Shabbat on behalf of one whose sustenance is not upon him, as above in siman 324, a non-Jew is considered one whose sustenance is upon him regarding that it is permitted to exert oneself on his behalf, because they support the poor of Eretz Yisrael together with the poor of Israel on account of darkei shalom [Tosafos]:
(ג) Before him in the courtyard — the poskim wrote that specifically to place before him and he takes them himself is permitted; but to give into his hand is forbidden, lest he take out to reshut ha'rabbim and it turns out retroactively that the Jew performed the hotza'ah from reshut hayachid, which is forbidden, as below in siman 347. And the view of Eliyah Rabbah is that it is permitted to give him even into his hand when he is in the courtyard, since he does not give on condition that he take them out, for since the non-Jew stands inside when he takes out outside it is considered that he performs the hotza'ah himself; and one may be lenient on this as his words, as stated in Biur Halacha; and if the Jew does not hand to his hand but the non-Jew takes from the Jew's hand — all agree it is permitted when he takes on condition to eat in the courtyard, for even when he takes out the Jew did not perform hotza'ah at all:
(ד) To eat them — even if he does not specify to him that he should eat there, it is also permitted, for presumably he will eat there; only it comes to exclude if he has no permission in his hand to eat them there, or that there is a lot that cannot be eaten there — forbidden to place them before him, for it is as though he gives on condition that he take out, and there is marit ayin that they will say he takes out on behalf of the Jew and as his agent, as explained in siman 347 [Magen Avraham in name of Shach, and so in Or Zarua]:
(ה) And he extends his hand inside — and through this, even if the Jew does not give them into his hand but he receives them from the Jew's hand, the Jew performs no hotza'ah at all — nevertheless forbidden, since it is known he will take them out they will say he takes out as his agent; and all the more to hand into his hand, forbidden on account of hotza'ah, as below in siman 347:
(ו) Or to give him — and even if he does not give them into his hand but places before him in the courtyard, since it is known he will take out — forbidden, lest they say he takes out as his agent, as likewise in s.k. 4:
(ז) Even if the objects belong to the non-Jew — in Tur it states "or even," etc., and they explained it refers to above, that the non-Jew stands outside and extends his hand inside — that in this even if the objects belong to the non-Jew there is marit ayin that they will say they belong to the Jew that he hands to him; but when he stands in the courtyard it is permitted to place before him, for they did not decree so extensively regarding objects of a non-Jew, and we say one who sees him take out afterward will know the objects belong to the non-Jew:
(ח) He designated a place for him — that is, if the object was pledged with the Jew, even if he designated a place for him on the object that he come and take it whenever he wishes — nevertheless forbidden to leave it that he take it on Shabbat, for an object pledged with a Jew is as his own, and what he designated a place does not help, for hiring does not acquire, and it is still in the Jew's reshut, and there is marit ayin:
