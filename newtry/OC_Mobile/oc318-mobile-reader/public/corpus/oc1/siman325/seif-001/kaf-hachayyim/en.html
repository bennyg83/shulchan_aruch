1) [Seif 1] It is permitted to invite a non-Jew on Shabbos. Although we maintain in siman 512 that one may not invite a non-Jew on Yom Tov, this applies specifically to Yom Tov, when we decree lest one prepare additional food for him. On Shabbos there is no concern that one will prepare additional food for him, because all food is cooked before Shabbos. It is therefore permitted to invite him. Levush; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
2) There: To invite a non-Jew, etc. We do not say that this renders a utensil unusable because the remnants of forbidden wine left in the cup prohibit moving it, since one may wash the cup afterward, as stated in siman 323, seif 8. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
3) There: One may place food before him, etc. We regard his sustenance as our responsibility because we are commanded to support him for the sake of peaceful relations. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. This applies only to the prohibition against rabbinic exertion. Regarding performing labor on Yom Tov, which is a Torah prohibition, his sustenance is not considered one's responsibility. Tosafos, Shabbos 19a; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
4) There: One may place food, etc. Similarly, one may finely cut vegetables and onions and select for that meal. Just as this is permitted for a Jew under siman 319, seif 1, it is also permitted for a non-Jew. Nevertheless, one should be stringent that it be for truly immediate use in such a case. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
5) There: One may place food, etc. Raavad is stringent and forbids doing so whenever one can avoid it. The rule that one may invite a non-Jew on Shabbos does not mean that we feed him directly. Rather, we show him the food and he takes it himself, or we serve food to ourselves and he eats together with us. Beis Yosef. It appears proper to be stringent like Raavad when possible. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2.
6) There: One may place food before him, etc. He may not place it in the non-Jew's hands, because this performs the lifting. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam, klal 62, paragraph 14. The concern is that the non-Jew may carry it out, so the Jew will have performed the lifting and the non-Jew the placing. Rabbinically, even performing only the lifting is forbidden lest one also perform the placing, as explained at the beginning of tractate Shabbos and below in siman 347. Even when it is uncertain whether the non-Jew will instead eat it there, one may not actively create a rabbinic doubt or a compounded doubt. This appears forbidden even in a karmelis, because the decree is closely related. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 2, likewise writes that it is forbidden even in a karmelis, since the Sages sometimes impose one decree as a safeguard for another; see there. Although Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, presents grounds to permit placing it in his hands when the non-Jew stands inside, and Taz, paragraph 1, appears to agree, it nevertheless seems proper to be stringent like the preceding later authorities. Machatzis HaShekel writes there that it may be forbidden even if the non-Jew pauses in the courtyard for his own purpose, which is like stopping to rest and nullifies the first lifting, because we decree lest on another occasion he perform both lifting and placing; see there. If the non-Jew takes it from the Jew's hand, however, it is permitted, since the non-Jew performs the lifting. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. See above, siman 307, paragraph 103.
7) There: To eat them. This is not exact; one may give them without specifying a purpose. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2. If he explicitly tells the non-Jew to carry them out, however, it is forbidden. Beis Yosef. Accordingly, if the non-Jew is not permitted to eat them in the courtyard, or if one gives him so much food that he cannot eat it there, one may not give it to him, because this is like explicitly instructing him to carry it out. Bach; Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2.
8) There: And he extended his hand inside, etc. In this case, even placing the food before him is forbidden because it appears that one gives it to him in order that he carry it out. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Tosafos, Shabbos 3a, beginning with the words 'Bava,' also implies this. Certainly handing it directly to him is forbidden because of performing the lifting, as stated below in siman 347.
9) There: Even if the objects belong to the non-Jew, etc. This wording implies that even when both favorable conditions exist, meaning that the non-Jew stands inside and the objects belong to him, it is nevertheless forbidden. Tur, however, implies that it is forbidden specifically when the non-Jew stands outside and the Jew gives it to him, because people may mistakenly say that the object belongs to the Jew. If the non-Jew stands inside and the object belongs to him, it may be placed before him, because one who sees him carrying it out will assume it is his. When the object belongs to the Jew, however, concern exists for those who know this, because appearances may lead them to say that the non-Jew acts as the Jew's agent. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Chayei Adam, klal 62, paragraph 13. When no observers are present, however, all opinions permit it. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 5. See below, paragraph 15.
10) There: Even if the objects belong to the non-Jew, etc. It appears that according to our practice of being lenient and sending even initially beyond the eruv, as stated in the gloss to seif 10, we are concerned about the matters mentioned here only regarding something associated with commerce. Regarding food and drink needed by a Jew, the practice is lenient, because they are needed for Shabbos and the Jew cannot go obtain them himself. This resembles the mitzvah need mentioned shortly below. Taz, paragraph 1. The matter will be explained in our words on seif 10, with the help of Heaven.
11) There: Even if the objects belong to the non-Jew, etc. If one sold merchandise to a non-Jew but it remains in the Jew's house, and the non-Jew comes to take it on Shabbos, one should be concerned and, when possible, be stringent not to hand it to him because of appearances. Magen Shaul, siman 57. See there, where he cites the responsum of his brother-in-law Rabbi Yosef ben Chason, who forbids it, particularly according to the view that a non-Jew acquires by pulling the item, since according to that view the sale is completed on Shabbos; see there. Ikarei HaDat, siman 14, paragraph 5. See above, siman 252, paragraph 17.
12) If a person has a document at the customs house stating that the duty was paid, and on Shabbos he is compelled to retrieve it and hand it to a non-Jew, may he go to the customs house, take the document, and give it to the non-Jew on Shabbos? See Responsa Lev Chaim, volume 3, siman 109, which writes that if the non-Jew did not prepare it before Shabbos and comes to write it on Shabbos for the Jew, it is plainly forbidden. Even if it was already written before Shabbos, however, it is still forbidden; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1.
13) There, gloss: Even if he designated a place for him, etc. Mordechai, chapter 1 of Shabbos, writes in the name of Rabbi Yoel that a Jew may not return a non-Jew's collateral on Shabbos even if he designated a place for it before Shabbos. Beis Yosef cites him. See above, siman 252, seif 1, in the gloss, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
14) There, gloss: Even if he designated a place for him, etc. This means that before Shabbos the Jew designated a place within his property for the non-Jew to leave his collateral until he comes to take it the next day. The prohibition applies even if the non-Jew redeemed it before Shabbos. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4.
15) There, gloss: Even if he designated a place for him, etc. This is because some hold that rental does not transfer ownership. Magen Avraham, paragraph 5. If so, the item has not completely left the Jew's domain, and since the Jew holds it as collateral, it is like the Jew's own object. Yad Ephraim. It is forbidden even if the non-Jew also stands inside. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 6. If it is not collateral and the non-Jew stands inside, it is permitted even without designating a place. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 5. This follows Magen Avraham's understanding of Tur. According to Shulchan Aruch, however, even when the non-Jew stands inside, the objects belong to him, and they are not collateral, one may not give them to him because of appearances, as stated above in paragraph 9; see there. All this applies where there is no shared eruv for the alley. Where there is a shared eruv, it is plainly permitted, since the non-Jew cannot be treated more stringently than a Jew, who may carry out.
