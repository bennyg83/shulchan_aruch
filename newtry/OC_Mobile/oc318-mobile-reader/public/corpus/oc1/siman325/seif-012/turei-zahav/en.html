A gentile did on Shabbat, etc. This is a mishnah in chapter HaSho'el, and we challenge on it in the gemara: why should he not wait "in the time it takes to do them"? Ulla said: when standing on the highway — meaning as is written here; and specifically when the grave is in publicity; and this is Rambam's explanation, for he explains that the questioner challenges on the latter part — why not bury in it forever; but Rashi explained he challenges on the first part — why bury in it for evening; let him wait "in the time it takes to do them"; though the matter is not proven, one may say it was done for a Jew; and the answer: Ulla said on the highway that it is not a Jew's way to be buried there, for certainly it was not done for a Jew; and the Rosh did not bring this law of Ulla at all; and it appears to me his reason is that he holds like Rashi; and if so, wherever there is doubt whether it was done in prohibition or permission we go stringently — meaning like Rav who said above in the gemara "from a nearby place" — Rashi explained: meaning we saw clearly it came from a nearby place, or we saw in his house — meaning otherwise one must wait "in the time it takes to do them" from a doubt; therefore here we require proof it was not done for a Jew, such as in publicity; whereas for Shmuel who is lenient, from doubt "perhaps outside the wall" we do not need proof here. The Rosh rules like Shmuel; therefore the Rosh did not bring Ulla's law — correct. And Rif, who rules stringently in a doubt — therefore he brought Ulla's law, for so is our Rif, not like the Rif that was in Ran's hands; and what Beit Yosef wrote that from the Rosh not bringing Ulla's law it follows that also in Rif it is so — his words are not a matter, for here he argues on him in explanation, where he forbids in a doubt and the Rosh permits in a doubt and holds that even in doubt of d'oraisa melacha one need not wait "in the time it takes to do them," like Semag whom Tur cites in this siman — in a doubt gathered today they are permitted to eulogize immediately; and this is Tur's intent when he wrote "and R' Yosef Karo did not distinguish between in private, etc."; but Rambam wrote, etc., and so in the gemara — except Rashi explained, etc. — meaning the Rosh holds like Rashi; therefore he did not distinguish.
