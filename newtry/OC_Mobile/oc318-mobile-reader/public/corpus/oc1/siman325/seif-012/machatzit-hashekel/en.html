(א) (s.k. 12) He gathered fruits. Rabbeinu Yehuda challenged, etc. — Rabbeinu Yehuda challenged Rosh for permitting bread a gentile baked on Shabbat and we do not say forbidden on account of nolad because it is human finishing; if so it should be difficult why regarding a gentile who brought a gift it is forbidden; and Rosh wrote the reason on account of muktzeh, etc., see there what he wrote (and even though regarding a gentile who brought a gift besides muktzeh there is prohibition of not benefiting from Yom Tov melacha or lest he tell a gentile to do it and therefore must wait from Yom Tov bekdei she-ye'asu — nevertheless he needed the muktzeh reason; and see siman 515); and Magen Avraham posed the difficulty here regarding the gentile who gathered for himself — likewise forbidden for one reason on account of muktzeh like a gentile who baked bread for himself; and behold regarding baking bread Rabbeinu Yehuda brought two views that some permit and Rosh on account of human finishing — why did he stam here on the side of fish forbid; he should also permit on account of human finishing for the view of those who permit bread:

(ב) And see siman 507 where I wrote in name of Rosh, thus: and they forbade, etc.; and his intent — Tur wrote here it is similar to falling fruits and one may not permit more in a gentile gathering fruits than those that fell by themselves; and in siman 507 Magen Avraham in name of Rosh wrote regarding wood that fell from the palm on Yom Tov — our law is like falling fruits; Rosh wrote the reason on account of nolad, a decree lest he climb and detach; and he wrote there, thus: and it implies one reason, end quote — meaning either nolad alone or lest he climb and detach alone we do not forbid except combining both together; if so likewise regarding falling fruits whose law is like wood that fell — likewise forbidden on account of combining both reasons; and if so since Tur compared a gentile who gathered fruits to falling fruits — necessarily what Rosh wrote the reason regarding a gentile who brought a gift on account of muktzeh — the intent is not muktzeh alone but combining concern lest he climb and detach; and if so Rabbeinu Yehuda's difficulty is resolved — it is human finishing; and in general such a case is permitted and we do not forbid on account of muktzeh; nevertheless here with combining "lest he climb and detach" we forbid; and why did Rosh permit bread a gentile baked — there is no muktzeh concern, for it is human finishing; and there is no "lest a Jew bake" as Rabbeinu Yehuda brought in Taz s.k. 4 — baking and cooking are severe and known to all the world and no need for a Jew to do himself; and in Sefer Chemed Moshe he challenged Magen Avraham's words, see there; and it is resolved as stated:
