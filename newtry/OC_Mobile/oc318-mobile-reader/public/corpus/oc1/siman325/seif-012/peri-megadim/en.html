Did — Taz brought the mishnah Shabbos 151a that we say there "when standing on the highway," etc. And the essence of his intent ~{to prove}~ that the Rosh holds like Semag in seif 7 responsum 5 that the Rosh's view in a doubt is to permit, and there is no distinction between d'oraisa melacha or rabbinic; since he omitted Ulla's law, and between Rambam's explanation and Rashi's that he challenges on the first part — he should not have omitted that in private for a gentile one waits "all the more," in publicity for a gentile one does not need "all the more" (in Beit Yosef as it appears — textual error); rather he holds that all this is for Rav, but for Shmuel we are lenient in a doubt — one does not need "all the more" in techumim, the same in d'oraisa melacha one does not need "all the more" in a doubt. And Rif in the textual reading before Taz brought Ulla's law — on the first part, if one requires "all the more"; but the Rosh omitted it because he rules like Shmuel according to Rashi's explanation — we are concerned leniently; not so Rif, who explains concerned stringently — he properly brought Ulla's law on the first part as stated; and as proof from what the Rosh wrote "likewise all the more" — it implies he challenges for himself; and what is the difficulty that on the latter part he challenges — this is not so; rather from "he did for himself" — all the more we learn that even d'oraisa melacha we attribute leniently; and nevertheless what he wrote in a doubt "gathered today" — the Rosh holds one does not need "all the more" — difficult; Beitzah 24b brought Rav Pappa's law: if there is of that kind attached — forbidden, all the more in a doubt; as R' Yitzchak wrote and Ran brought Shabbos 151: their custom is to bring today, and coffin and the like are different — requires study presently; and on Yom Tov likewise on the first day of Yom Tov in a coffin — one may say for the Rosh in a doubt he forbids, and for evening one does not need "all the more" in doubt of d'oraisa melacha; and what he wrote d'oraisa melacha regarding a dead person — if it were not as if, there is room to examine; and one should not expand here; Bach wrote that trapping for a gentile is forbidden to all — all the more requires study that for evening permitted immediately as at the beginning — see there:
