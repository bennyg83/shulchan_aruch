Rather via a karmelit, etc. This ruling is astonishing at first glance, for it seems even for one for whom they were brought it is permitted to eulogize immediately — and this follows the Rosh, who is the first view in seif 8; and it is difficult, for above he rejected it in dispute, and here in stam; and further in siman 495 seif 5 he again ruled that for eulogy we require "in the time it takes to do them"; and there is no reason to distinguish rabbinic prohibition between karmelit and outside the techum, for one is not stricter than the other; therefore it appears it deals with others who need not wait until evening — for regarding himself he must wait "in the time it takes to do them" like outside the techum, except that for others it was permitted above even on that day, and here "on that day" does not apply since one does not eulogize on Shabbat; therefore permission to eulogize immediately; and better to emend here: he must wait until they come, but others are permitted to eulogize immediately — and so the law proves, since he began in singular "one need not," etc., and concluded in plural "permitted"; rather certainly there is an omission here, as I explained:
