(א) For himself — this "for himself" is not precise, for if we knew it was for himself, even on a weekday it is forbidden for a Jew to eat from it, for it is bread of a homeowner and forbidden to all, as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 112, unless in pressing need when no other bread is available at all; rather it deals with bread he makes to sell, and it is a city whose majority are gentiles — he acts with the majority's intent; and what he wrote "for himself" means he did not bake for a Jew [Acharonim]:

(ב) On Shabbat — at first glance it appears if there is doubt whether perhaps baked somewhat from erev Shabbat [meaning enough to brown the surface, as in siman 252 seif 5] — one may be lenient since there are those who permit in every matter, and like what Rama ruled in siman 252 seif 4 — see there in Magen Avraham s.k. 13:

(ג) There are those who forbid, etc. — behold a dispute like this also exists among the Rishonim regarding a gentile who made shoes for payment on Yom Tov whether one may wear them; it is brought in Hagahot Ashri chapter 3 of Yom Tov; and behold Rama ruled above in 252 seif 4 leniently that there is no muktzeh concern since completed by human hands; and here he did not close the law on this matter — one may say because in our matter there is further reason to be stringent, as Mishna Berurah wrote on account that it is a food matter one should be concerned lest he say to bake, or as Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Yeshu'at Shabbos that specifically in a matter begun from erev Shabbat and completed on Shabbat one may be lenient on account of completion by human hands; not so in our matter; and in siman 252 it also deals with the gentile having begun the shoe from erev Shabbat and completed on Shabbat — therefore he was lenient there:
