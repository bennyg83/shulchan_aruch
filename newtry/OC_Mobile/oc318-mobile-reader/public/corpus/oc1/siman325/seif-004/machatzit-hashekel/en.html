(א) (s.k. 4) The objects, etc. — in Tur he wrote "or even," meaning in Shulchan Aruch he wrote "even," which implies it refers to placing before him when the gentile stands in the courtyard; on this he said "even if the objects are of a gentile" and also the gentile stands in the courtyard — nevertheless forbidden; but in Tur he added the word "or" and wrote "or even," etc. — if so one may explain it is a separate matter; and it deals when the gentile stands outside; but when the gentile stands inside — permitted; and since it is reasonable to Magen Avraham that if he stands inside to permit when the objects are of a gentile — therefore he also explains Tur's words thus, not like Shulchan Aruch; and he also has some proof from Tur's words, as he writes afterward — that Tur wrote "one who sees that he gives," etc.; and this is what he means "that one who sees gives to him" — meaning in Tur he added the word "and gives"; but in Shulchan Aruch he wrote "one who sees the gentile," etc., and one may explain one who sees him afterward in the gentile's hand; but in Tur, since he wrote "one who sees that he gives to him," it implies specifically when one sees an Israelite give to a gentile — that is, the gentile stands outside; thereby there is concern the viewer supposes they are of an Israelite since he gives to him; unlike when the gentile is inside, for the viewer does not see the giving, even though afterward he sees it in a gentile's hand and knows he took it from the Israelite's domain — he will say certainly they are of a gentile and the gentile took his own from the Israelite's domain (and also after investigation it will be known the truth is so; and see siman 244 in Magen Avraham); and his household members who see the giving know the objects are of a gentile; but if the objects are of an Israelite, even if the gentile stands inside — forbidden on account of suspicion of his household members; and also there is suspicion of viewers who will investigate and it will become known to them the objects are of an Israelite, as Magen Avraham wrote siman 244; but the word "and gives to him" is not precise, for if so in any case forbidden, for he does uprooting, as stated; and so in Mordekhai — this is what Rama wrote, and even when he designated, etc.; and Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 5 the reason in name of Mordekhai — lest rental does not acquire, meaning if it were clear to us that rental acquires — then through designating a place for him that place is as rented or lent to the gentile, and thus his courtyard was acquired to the gentile before Shabbat and they are the gentile's objects; rather because we are in doubt in Avodah Zarah daf 15 whether rental acquires or not — therefore forbidden; it implies if it were a gentile's object — permitted, and that is only when the gentile stands inside; and see above siman 252 s.k. 5 in Magen Avraham and what he wrote there; and Rama who cited Mordekhai's words per the Mechaber — it appears he holds even if the gentile stands inside — forbidden even with gentile objects; in truth accordingly one need not Mordekhai's reason that it is a doubt whether rental acquires; and Magen Avraham in s.k. 5 who cited Mordekhai's reason — it is according to his view following Tur's opinion:
