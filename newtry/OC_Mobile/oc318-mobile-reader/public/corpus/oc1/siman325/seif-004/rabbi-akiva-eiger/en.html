Magen Avraham s.k. 14 — and in the evening they are permitted immediately. The citation refers to seif 7 "and some say that in the evening they are permitted immediately." And Magen Avraham comes to answer that one should not challenge that the plain ruling in seif 9 is to forbid in doubt — for if so, on that day it is stringent. In my humble opinion, this is because according to Rif who holds that we are concerned lest it is outside the wall — for us this is a stringency when certain; and even in general where we say the bathhouse of Shmuel from the bathhouse — and if so, one who is lenient from doubt is because he explains we are concerned leniently; and if so it is explicit that even regarding a corpse we are lenient in a manner where there is no place to distinguish between a corpse and other matters regarding doubt. Alternatively, the Mechaber's ruling in seif 14 is per the first view here, for such is the Mechaber's way everywhere to rule like the first view. However for practical ruling in this there is room to distinguish regarding doubt from outside the techum — for one can say in general it does not require b'chedi she'ya'asu, as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 19. And because we have five reasons to be lenient — the view of those who hold regarding techumim that even when certain it does not require b'chedi she'ya'asu, and the reasoning that in doubt it does not require b'chedi she'ya'asu — unlike a corpse, where all require b'chedi she'ya'asu, here in doubt we are stringent. Requires further study.
