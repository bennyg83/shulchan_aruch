(א) By way of clothing — for otherwise forbidden like other objects of a Jew, since they are pledged with him, lest they say the Jew commanded to take it out, as likewise in s.k. 8; however if the non-Jew is mute and does not wish to take by way of clothing — permitted in every manner, as likewise above:

(ב) And another places — Agudah wrote the same law if the non-Jew places the money and takes the pledge — also permitted, provided the Jew does not calculate with him and does not touch them [Magen Avraham and Taz and Shach]; and in Eliyah Rabbah it is implied he holds that when he places money it appears like commerce, and according to him one may not be lenient in this except when the non-Jew is mute; and nevertheless it appears that if he can incur loss from this, such as there is no pledge worth the money and the like — one may rely on Magen Avraham and Taz and Shach:

(ג) And he shall not touch — any of the pledges, neither the old nor the new; however regarding a Jew in such a case one may be lenient that he touch it, for with a Jew it does not appear like commerce but like lending; and if the non-Jew comes to take his pledge and the Jew does not trust him — no other Jew may guarantee on his behalf, for it is forbidden on account of "from finding your desire":
