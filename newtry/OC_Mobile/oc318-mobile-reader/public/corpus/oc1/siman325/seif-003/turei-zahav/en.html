There are those who forbid. This is Rabbeinu Tam, and his reason in Tur, and this is his language: lest it was ground today and it turns out it is muktzeh, for during twilight it was not fit; and in Sefer HaTerumah he wrote faces to forbid and to permit. R' Yosef Karo wrote: and it appears to me to permit, for even if it were flour or dough during twilight it would not be muktzeh since it was completed by human hands, and grinding and baking do not forbid it since it is for a gentile — end of his words. And it is explained in Beit Yosef that for Sefer HaTerumah "faces to forbid" is stricter than Rabbeinu Tam, and he holds that even if ground from erev Shabbat it is forbidden, for it was not fit from yesterday as it is fit after baking. And I am crude and do not know the permission for this bread: if it deals with the gentile making it for himself — even on a weekday it is forbidden, for it is bread of a homeowner and forbidden, as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 112; rather certainly he makes it to sell, and it is a bakery — if so it is difficult, for once you permit it he makes it also to sell to Jews; and it is similar to what is written here in seif 6 "or for a Jew," and a gentile must wait, etc.; and on the poskim who did not mention this reason for prohibition one may say it deals with making for himself, and it deals in a time of pressing need when there is no bakery bread at all; in a matter of bread of a homeowner it is permitted only here Shulchan Aruch seems to permit entirely, since he decided that in pressing need one may rely on those who permit — it seems that for those who permit it is permitted even not in pressing need; and further, certainly one is not obligated in hamotzi on bread of a homeowner, as is implied in Yoreh De'ah there, in the prohibition — it must be said it deals here with making to sell, and most buyers in that city are gentiles:
