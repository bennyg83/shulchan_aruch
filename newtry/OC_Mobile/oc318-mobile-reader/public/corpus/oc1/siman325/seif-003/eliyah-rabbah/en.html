[6] In the manner of clothing, etc. Otherwise it appears as though the Jew instructed him to take it out, since it is held as collateral in his possession, as stated by the Magen Avraham. According to this, in the view of the Levush in seif 1, for us even collateral that is not clothing would be permitted. It appears proper to rely on this in the case of a violent non-Jew or the like.
[7] It is preferable, etc. The Agudah writes that the same applies if the non-Jew leaves money and takes collateral, and this is also permitted, provided that the Jew does not make an accounting with him. He writes that he instructed the Jew to fast the following day. The Bach and Knesset HaGedolah cite this. The Magen Avraham, however, writes that the Rama did not take this concern into account because Rabbenu Yerucham is entirely lenient. In my humble opinion, Rabbenu Yerucham discusses clothing, whereas the Agudah discusses an item that is not clothing, or a case where the non-Jew leaves money, which resembles commerce. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that Rabbenu Yonah instructed his wife to allow a non-Jewish woman who had left a basket in the house to take it and carry it with her on Shabbos, and if she could not find it, to give her another basket of her own. End of his words. This accords with what I wrote in seif katan 3. See the end of siman 307 regarding these laws.
