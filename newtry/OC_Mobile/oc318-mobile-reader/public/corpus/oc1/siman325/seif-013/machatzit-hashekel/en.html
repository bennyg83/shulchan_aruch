(א) (s.k. 13) A doubt, etc. — so it should read, and it refers to seif 7; and also s.k. 14 are found in print by mistake and should be marked seif 7, see there; and some say for evening permitted immediately:

(ב) Bach wrote, etc. — rather it is a doubt, etc.; and if so even if he brought a gift and we do not know he gathered for an Israelite — regarding whether he must wait until evening bekdei she-ye'asu depends on the dispute explained here in seif 7:

(ג) And it is simple, etc. — it appears even though we do not know, etc. — presumably he gathered for him; and as Rabbeinu Yehuda in name of Ra'avad — stam we say he gathered them today, since he brought a gift — presumably he brings from the good and fine, and those gathered today are better than those gathered yesterday; and Magen Avraham wrote likewise we say he gathered for an Israelite presumably — for otherwise why say "a gentile who brought a gift," etc.; it appears he wanted to prove with two proofs — one from stam he brought a gift and did not stipulate we need to know he gathered for an Israelite — evidently even stam forbidden; and further if it dealt specifically he gathered for an Israelite — it should have abbreviated "a gentile who gathered for an Israelite"; therefore why did it use "gift" — for in such a case even if we do not know he gathered for an Israelite; and one may reject this proof — perhaps Bach concedes to Ra'avad's view that regarding bringing a gift we say presumably he gathered today, for those gathered today are finer as above; but nevertheless we need to know he gathered for an Israelite; and even so one should not say it used "gift" to convey such a case — that a gift even stam when we do not know they were gathered today — forbidden; and perhaps Magen Avraham holds it is impossible to hear that regarding a gift we do not need to know they were gathered today; and how do we hear this — perhaps in truth it deals when we know they were gathered today; and necessarily it must be he relied that according to this view from stam he brought a gift and did not stipulate we know they were gathered today — from this we also hear we need not know they were gathered for an Israelite, for also in this stam and no stipulation we need to know he gathered for an Israelite; and further Tosafos wrote in Eruvin daf 40 on this case, etc. — that which we rule a gentile who brought a gift to an Israelite regarding something lacking trapping or attached that requires waiting Motzei Shabbat bekdei she-ye'asu — Rashi wrote the reason so he not benefit from Shabbat or Yom Tov melacha; accordingly if brought to an Israelite on the first day of exile Yom Tov — permitted Motzei the first Yom Tov, meaning the night of the second Yom Tov, if he waits bekdei she-ye'asu — in any case he does not benefit from Yom Tov melacha, for if day one was Yom Tov he certainly waited; and if the second day is Yom Tov then day one was weekday and no melacha was done on Yom Tov — obviously permitted; but Rabbeinu Tam wrote the reason — a decree lest he tell a gentile to bring for him on Yom Tov or Shabbat from attached, etc.; therefore even Motzei Yom Tov or Shabbat forbidden until bekdei she-ye'asu; and if so certainly he will not tell the gentile, for he will not benefit at all since he must wait; all the more he can do it permissibly on Yom Tov — why command a gentile to do prohibition on Yom Tov; and accordingly also on two days of exile Yom Tov forbidden until the second Motzei Yom Tov, for otherwise still concern he will tell the gentile to bring on the first day and benefit on the second Yom Tov after waiting on the second night; and Tosafos challenged on this view from the case stated there that Bar Tavya was trapped on the first exile Yom Tov and slaughtered on the second Yom Tov in the house of the Exilarch — for according to Rabbeinu Tam it should also be forbidden on the second Yom Tov; and Tosafos answered Bar Tavya was not that a gentile brought a gift but trapped of itself through traps spread from erev Yom Tov; and on day one forbidden on account of muktzeh, therefore permitted on day two, end quote; and according to Bach it can be resolved it deals a gentile brought a gift only we do not know he was trapped for an Israelite — for according to Bach it is called a doubt; and regarding a doubt Tosafos on Shabbat daf 151 ruled we follow leniency, as stated in the next s.k.:
