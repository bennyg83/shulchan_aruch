If there is a doubt whether it was brought from outside the techum — forbidden. This is the view of Rif, and the reason: lest he traveled all night and stayed outside the techum and came in the morning; and it is difficult, for here is a rabbinic doubt, for techum is rabbinic; and it appears to me they were stringent since one can wait until evening and there will be complete permission — why enter himself into a doubt of prohibition; and it is similar to what is written in Yoreh De'ah that according to some even in a thousand it is not nullified. And again I saw this reason in Hagahot HaMaggid chapter 2 of Yoreh De'ah in the name of Rashba regarding black eggs and he found white ones, etc.; except that nevertheless in what is written here in the latter part regarding fruit in the city — we are not concerned from a doubt since it is more plausible to attribute to the nearby that is available; but it requires study, for we rule like R' Chiya in chapter Lo Yachpor that majority and nearby follow the majority, even in a nearby matter that is available and evident; and if so, here too one should attribute the fruit to the majority that is outside the techum; and one may answer that here it is different, for there is only a rabbinic prohibition — behold in chapter 1 of Beitzah daf 10 they bring R' Chiya's law regarding black eggs and white ones, where there is only rabbinic prohibition; therefore it appears to me that here he cited specifically "two houses" — for here there is no majority for prohibition, for certainly he took only from his own; but if he had two houses outside the techum and one within the techum — it would be forbidden, as I explained:
