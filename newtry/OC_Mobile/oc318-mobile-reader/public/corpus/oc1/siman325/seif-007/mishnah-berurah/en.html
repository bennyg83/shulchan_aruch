(א) Doubt whether he gathered them, etc. — such as the non-Jew said to the Jew to give him fruits on Shabbat and he went to the non-Jew's house and gave him fruits — doubt whether he gathered these on his behalf or thought to gather others on his behalf and these he gathered for himself initially; but a non-Jew who brought a gift to a Jew — there is no doubt, for we say he certainly gathered them on his behalf; and likewise if he brought warm bread to a Jew we say he certainly baked on his behalf [Magen Avraham and Taz and Shach, and Shach not like Bach]:

(ב) And it is not known, etc. — also this does not discuss when he brought a gift, for when he brought a gift [or brought to sell] we consider as certainly detached on Shabbat, for presumably he brought from the best that were gathered today, and they are forbidden at Motzei Shabbat bichdei sheya'asu according to all:

(ג) Forbidden on that day and at night, etc. — and the reason they forbade from doubt even though it is a rabbinic matter that doubt is lenient, because it is davar she'yesh lo matirin that it is possible to wait until Motzei Shabbat. And if there were two doubts together — whether detached on Shabbat and also whether detached on behalf of the Jew on Shabbat itself — forbidden, for even certainly if not on behalf of the Jew he gathered — forbidden on Shabbat on account of safek muchan, as above in s.k. 24; but at Motzei Shabbat one may permit in this immediately on account of double doubt [Taz and Shach and Pri Megadim and Chayei Adam]:

(ד) And some say that at night it is permitted immediately — for they hold the Rabbis did not forbid in this from doubt; and nevertheless on Shabbat itself, since its prohibition is on account of muktzeh, they agree one may not permit even where there is only doubt whether gathered on Shabbat, for in safek muchan they were more stringent than other rabbinic doubts, as likewise above in s.k. 24; and for practical halacha Eliyah Rabbah wrote it is implied from Acharonim one may rely on this "some say" that one need not be stringent until Motzei Shabbat; but in Chayei Adam he wrote one may not be lenient except for the need of a mitzvah; and so is implied from Or Zarua s.k. 18 that one may not be lenient in this; and so ruled Bach in siman 515:
