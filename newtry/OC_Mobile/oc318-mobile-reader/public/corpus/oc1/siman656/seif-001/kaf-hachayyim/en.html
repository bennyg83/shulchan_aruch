1) [Seif 1] If he purchased an esrog, etc. Although a person is obligated to give all his money in order not to transgress one of the commandments of Hashem, as it is written, ‘And you shall love Hashem,’ etc., ‘with all your resources,’ this applies to a negative commandment, so that he should not transgress it through an active deed. If he can exempt himself from doing so, even at the cost of all his resources, he is obligated to give all his money rather than intentionally transgress a single prohibition. With regard to a positive commandment, however, a person is not commanded to give all his money in order not to forgo it, thereby bringing himself to poverty and casting himself upon the community, for a poor person is considered as dead. It is better for him to remain passive, even where the mitzvah will pass and thereby be forfeited, so that he may fulfill many other mitzvos in its place, rather than bring himself to poverty, which is considered as death. Concerning this our Sages said: One who spends generously should not spend more than one fifth. Levush. This is from the words of the poskim cited by the Beit Yosef, and Rama likewise cited their words in the gloss that follows. Rabban Gamliel, who purchased an esrog for 1,000 zuz, acted with a stringency of his own, because mitzvos were beloved to him. Beit Yosef, in the name of the Semag.
