If he bought an esrog. This is when he does not think the others are expensive except because of beautifying the mitzvah [Mabit, part 3, siman 49; and see Yefeh Mareh, first chapter of Peah].
An esrog that is fit, etc. This is specifically regarding an esrog, about which the Torah says 'hadar'; if so, it is not called hadar when it has the lowest possible valid measure. But with shofar and the like, even with the small measure one need not add one third. Nevertheless, it is good to seek a beautiful one, each person according to his generosity [Yam Shel Shlomo]. This does not appear correct to me, for the Gemara says generally 'beautification of a mitzvah,' and Rashi too explained it regarding two sifrei Torah; see there. Rather, he ruled like Rabbeinu Tam.
Exactly the size of an egg. But if it is as large as ordinary esrogim, even though both come before him together to buy, he is not obligated to buy the more beautiful one according to this reasoning (Beis Yosef).
In order to exchange it. But if his fellow does not want to exchange it, only to sell it, he is not obligated to buy it, since he already bought this one (Beis Yosef).
He should take the more beautiful one. But if he already bought one, he need not add in order to exchange it for a more beautiful one according to everyone; so appears to be the opinion of the Shulchan Aruch. So too the Shelah ruled on daf 65. The Yam Shel Shlomo also asked: if we say that he must add one third every time, then if one hundred esrogim came before him one after another, each more beautiful than the other, should he give his whole house? End quote. This does not appear difficult to me, for he need not add one third unless it is one third more beautiful than the other, and if so there is no difficulty, for it is certainly impossible for there to be one hundred esrogim each one third more beautiful than the other.
Need not spend. The Darchei Moshe wrote: even one whose livelihood is not strained need not spend more than one third for beautifying a mitzvah, unlike what the Nimukei Yosef wrote. We say in the Gemara that one who adds will be repaid in the World to Come.
A passing mitzvah. Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that nevertheless he is obligated to spend one tenth of his assets for this, and I do not know his source (Beis Yosef). Perhaps he means the first year from the principal, and from then on from the profit, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 249. The Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that one whose livelihood is strained need not add one third, and perhaps he need not even buy the mitzvah itself, for for him it is like a great fortune, except for Chanukah lights and the four cups. End quote. The Nimukei Yosef wrote that one must add from his toil and strain his life in order to beautify the mitzvah, and the Yam Shel Shlomo disagrees with him.
He must give all his money. See Yoreh Deah siman 232:12, that regarding an oath, financial coercion is called coercion, for we say that he did not vow with this in mind; and see Bach and Taz there. It is somewhat implied in Gittin 50 and in the incident of Bar Kamtza that it is permitted to violate a negative commandment because of fear of the government; see there. If he has only five selaim and must redeem his son and buy an esrog, he should buy an esrog, for this is a passing mitzvah and that is a mitzvah that is not passing (Kiddushin 29).
