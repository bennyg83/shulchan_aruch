(א) If — Taz Bava Kamma 9a: first view is Tosafot and Rosh; second view is Rashi. What Tosafot and Rosh wrote, like a nut for example, they cited because halacha follows an egg-size; Beit Yosef and Bach: seemingly the Rosh's words contradict Bava Kamma, which implies a third is the minimum size of Chazal in limitation — he adds a third of the price (Tosafot explained in Peah). If it is much larger he need not add for beauty at all, even if both are before him; surely if he already bought one he need not exchange. In chapter Lulav haGazul he explained even if he already bought one he must beautify and add; Taz answered: on the contrary, whoever did not buy at all and both are before him and both are much larger than an egg, he need not take the nicer and more beautiful at all, only if one is egg-size he must add if the second is larger — add a third of the price; but if at first only one very large etrog came and he bought it, when a nicer one comes he must exchange for a third of the price of the first to fulfill "this is for Me and I will adorn it," especially etrog where "hadar" is written; the same for other mitzvot. Nevertheless I did not understand — if so, when two come before him and both are large etc.

(ב) If Reuven caused Shimon to lose a beautiful etrog worth much, he need only give an etrog with which he fulfills, and what is worth more than a smaller one after the festival he must pay — Acharonim in the name of R' Moshe Mintz 113, see there. See Mishneh Lamelech Hilchot Mechira 17 from Hilchot Mechira; he left in doubt in theft and Asufat — Choshen Mishpat 120 wrote plainly: one who snatches an etrog from Reuven must pay according to its value now, and surely from an etrog seller, where all agree he pays according to value during the festival, see there. If he disqualified it unintentionally, one could say he exempts himself with a small etrog even for responsa Choshen Mishpat; it seems reasonable for a snatcher when the victim seized him he can say I am certain and pays according to value during the festival. If he stole a valuable etrog and returned it after the festival, one could say he can exempt himself like Choshen Mishpat — they say in prohibitions of benefit, here is yours before you, since he returned the theft itself; surely here where he returned the etrog itself after the festival even though now worth little — requires study; perhaps say Choshen Mishpat and similar 63, Shach 37:36, and they did not make a regulation here unlike one who stole merchandise from his fellow on market day worth a thousand and after the market it is worth little.
