(35) And he still eats the king's portion, etc.—per what is explained here that even if the treasurer was removed and another appointed in his place—certainly he has no rule over the cities now; nevertheless since he still receives portion from the king he is considered the king's servant and they can rent domain from him—so we are not strict whether one who rents from him has rule over the city but only that the king himself has rule over them and this is from his servants one can rent from him (and Magen Avraham brought this law in the name of Ritva that every small servant is no worse than hired worker and gleaner); therefore the law of the gloss on treasurer removed is puzzling—for he has no rule over the city and no service from the king (and know what is written on this in Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba's teshuvah is a typo—Beit Yosef wrote this himself and it is not in Rashba's teshuvah); perhaps one can say since he still receives portion his first appointment is not voided and it is strained; however all this for renting from him lekhatchila; but for whether what he rented while treasurer is nullified—even if he did not rent for a time but unspecified—in this it is reasonable per Rama's ruling one can say even if the king removed him from being treasurer nevertheless he does not wish to nullify what he already did and proof he still gives him portion—and see Beit Yosef and examine:
