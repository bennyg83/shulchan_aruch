(57) One within the other—and foot traffic of the inner ones is via the outer courtyard:

(58) Forbids—the outer courtyard until they rent from the gentile; the reason in all these we consider all dwell in the outer courtyard since their foot traffic is there and they are commonly found there; they decreed lest they learn from his deeds; nevertheless Israel in the inner one is permitted to carry in his courtyard from house to courtyard—for a gentile does not forbid a lone Israelite and the outer courtyard does not forbid him for he has no foot traffic on it and the three are not commonly in the inner courtyard—the Sages did not decree here:

(59) And there is one who says, etc.—although everywhere one Israelite with a gentile is not forbidden even in one courtyard—here it is worse in a courtyard within a courtyard lest people not know who dwells there and think Israelites also dwell there besides the gentile; and when they see carrying in the outer they will say it is permitted without rental in a gentile's place; nevertheless for law we do not rule thus for many later ones rejected this from law:
