Two courtyards, etc. For foot traffic of two Israelites and gentile is found there and the inner one is permitted in his place (Rambam chapter 2); and per Maharam Me'ir there are disputants who permit; and see in the Gemara and in Hagahot Alfasi that since the inner ones are permitted in their place they do not forbid outside their place and need no eruv at all in the outer—unlike when gentile is in the inner and two Israelites in the outer—then the two outer ones need eruv and we fear they will think more Israelites dwell in the inner and then it is a forbidden foot in its place forbidding outside; and if they made eruv and rented from the gentile—it is as if the gentile shouts, etc.; therefore they must rent—unlike when there is only one Israelite in the outer—see there; and Tur and Rabbeinu Yehonatan hold in all cases one may decree; and so Me'or:

And there is one who says, etc. This Rabbenu Yehudah deduced from Rambam's language chapter 4; and it appears to us it is impossible to say thus, for if so why need he write in chapter 2 the law of Israel and gentile in the inner, etc.—behold even a gentile alone forbids; and so Tosafos proved in the Gemara—see there; and further Rambam concludes until they rent his place—it implies he deals with many; and further why need we fear they will think others dwell there, since no other Israelite is seen in the courtyard; granted in the beginning it deals where there is another Israelite in the inner or outer one; but here where there is no person, why need we fear for this; therefore it appears Rambam refers when two are in the outer one, only that he was terse as is his custom:
