(52) Dwell in his house—in separate rooms for each:
(53) Their dwelling is not considered—although the Sages considered their dwelling and they become like owners through them to rent domain even without the gentile's knowledge—the Sages said this only to be lenient but not that their dwelling be considered a dwelling forbidding one another when they did not make eruv—for in truth their dwelling is not designated for them since the gentile has permission there and can remove them:
(54) One upon the other—and the same they do not forbid courtyard members for they need not join in eruv:
(55) For rental—a typo; it should read and his hired worker—meaning if courtyard members rented domain from the gentile they need not rent from the hired worker and gleaner whether Israelite or gentile; and it is obvious; rather per what we hold their dwelling is considered [that they rent domain from them] one might think like two gentiles in one house requiring rent from each—and it teaches for leniency not stringency:
(56) From the master of the house—and the same if they rented domain from one of the hired workers it suffices and they need not rent from all:
