(1) Does not forbid him—for by Torah law a gentile's dwelling is not considered a dwelling; rather the Sages decreed lest he dwell with him and learn from his deeds—and they decreed only in what is common, namely where many Israelites dwell; but not in one house for it is not common to dwell among gentiles who are suspected of bloodshed:

(2) In two houses—but in one house even if many people each eat separately they are considered as one:

(3) And forbid one another—to exclude where they do not forbid one another such as father and sons who receive sustenance from him so they need not make eruv even if each dwells in a separate house—as explained in siman 380—see there; in such case the gentile does not forbid them and they need not rent his domain:

(4) Then the gentile forbids them—so their eruv among themselves does not help until they rent the gentile's domain:

(5) And it does not help, etc.—like an Israelite who did not make eruv with courtyard members who nullify domain to permit the rest to carry—as explained above siman 380; and the reason it does not help is because the Sages wished to distance Israelite dwelling from the gentile—and therefore they were stringent that he has no remedy until he rents from him; and since the gentile will not easily wish to rent he leaves dwelling with the gentile so carrying not be forbidden:

(6) Rather they must rent from him—and regarding whether lending helps depends on the two views in seif 12; per the first view there it does not help:

(7) For he did not rent, etc.—and in Avodat HaKodesh for Rashba he wrote another reason for this law—that it is not an Israelite's way to rent or lend a house to a gentile in a courtyard where he dwells and they did not decree on what is not common; and see Biur HaGra here and above siman 380 that his view is specifically when the lessor conditioned he can remove him anytime; and where he cannot remove him certainly he forbids—and see what we expanded in siman 384 in Biur Halacha:

(8) But if the house, etc.—meaning do not say eruv rental needed for permitting carrying is included in house rental—for it is evident he did not rent the house intending Shabbat carrying be forbidden and he need not rent domain again—the Torah teaches this only for a lessor not a lessee; therefore he must rent domain from him:

(9) And gentile, etc.—meaning the gentile lessor:

(10) In the house with him—and it deals where the gentile has a designated room; if the Israelite has permission in the whole house he is no worse than the gentile's hired worker and gleaner and need not rent his domain—see seif 15 there:

(11) Israel who lent, etc., in a place where gentiles dwell—meaning Israel has two houses in a courtyard where gentiles dwell—he dwells in one and lent or rented the second to another Israelite and has possession there, i.e., left vessels not carried on Shabbat; though they need not make eruv between them as siman 380—they are still not considered as one and must rent from the gentile—for possession is no better than if they made eruv among themselves which does not help without renting from the gentile as we wrote in s.k. 4—see Biur Halacha what is written on this:
