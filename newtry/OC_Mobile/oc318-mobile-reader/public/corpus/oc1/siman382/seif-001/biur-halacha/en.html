(1) Then the gentile forbids them—it is simple that he does not forbid them except to carry from their houses and from the gentile's house to the courtyard or to the alleyway; but in the courtyard itself or in the alleyway, vessels that rested there are certainly permitted to carry, as with an Israelite without a gentile, and we do not find that a gentile comes to be stringent; on the contrary, by law the gentile should not forbid at all—rather where there are two Israelites the Sages equated the dwelling of a gentile to the dwelling of an Israelite; and so is the plain meaning of the braita daf 62 end of amud 1, and so is Rashi's language there in the mishna and daf 62a s.v. lema dira, and so in Rashi 64a s.v. makhrzinan—see there he mentions only carrying from house to courtyard; and so is explained explicitly in this siman seif 20 in the gloss, and as Mahari'l expanded in this in Beit Yosef—see there; and so too in Biur HaGra siman 39 and Gra siman 7; and we wrote all this to exclude the words of Mahari'l in teshuvah siman 156 [brought in Magen Avraham siman 363 s.k. 27 and in Chiddushei Ra'ah in this siman] who wrote that where there is a gentile the Sages made the courtyard like karmelit that it is forbidden to carry beyond four cubits. And for the law it appears simple that we do not be stringent in this, for Magen Avraham himself wrote in siman 363 s.k. 27 that we have only the words of Shulchan Aruch who does not hold thus—see there; and so Gra in his chiddushim wrote that in the plain meaning the gentile is not more stringent than an Israelite, as we wrote above; and so is the opinion of Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav siman 5 alone—all the proofs I brought above:

(2) And it does not help for the gentile to nullify, etc.—the same if the Israelites nullified their domain to the gentile [Rambam]:

(3) They must rent, etc.—Shulchan Aruch did not explain whether rental from the gentile must be before the eruv specifically or it is fine even afterward; and what they said in the Gemara eruv does not help in a gentile's place [daf 62] is when they did not rent from him at all—unlike where they rented afterward, for at least at the time eruv takes effect at twilight he was already rented from the gentile it helps. And from what they said in the Gemara (daf 66a) just as five dwelling in one courtyard one makes eruv on behalf of all, so too rental one rents on behalf of all—it appears from this language they make eruv together first and become as one and afterward one rents on behalf of all—as for one who makes eruv on behalf of all it is specifically in this manner [and as Rashi explained there and as in Shulchan Aruch siman 372 seif 3 in the gloss]; however in the poskim [Rambam, Semag, Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh] it is explained on this law conversely—they rent first and afterward make eruv—and it appears they wrote the language precisely to teach us rental must come first then eruv—and so appears in BeHag brought in Semag: if the gentile is the case they need to buy domain from every gentile in it as when the whole city is in Israelites' domain and then they make eruv—and so appears from Teshuvah Batzitz siman 45 that after they rent from him the Israelites make eruv with bread. However one can say they were precise that rental must precede only lekhatchila—for lekhatchila certainly one should not make eruv before rental lest the gentile not wish to rent afterward and obviously one may not bless on the eruv for presumably we say the gentile will not wish to rent as in the Gemara (daf 62) a gentile does not rent and the eruv is nullified for eruv does not help in a gentile's place; but b'dieved if they already made eruv perhaps they agree it helps since they rented in the end—and at first glance proof from seif 7 that if they rented for a known time when time ends they must rent again and make eruv for the first eruv does not return and revive—proves the first eruv was entirely nullified because now it is before rental [and eruv does not apply at all when they have not yet rented from the gentile]; but in truth there is no conclusive proof—one can argue there it is different because they rented initially for a known time and the eruv too was only for that time—when rental time ends eruv does not help and it is as if they stipulated no eruv except for that time therefore need new eruv afterward [and for this reason perhaps even per Ra'avad who disagrees on siman 383 that if they made eruv first and a gentile came on Shabbat the eruv returns after they rent—it is only there where it is uncertain the gentile will come on Shabbat and the eruv was made initially valid for the whole Shabbat—unlike here where rental time certainly ends therefore eruv is nullified when time ends and does not revive after new rental—and this resolves Magen Avraham's question there]. Unlike here when they gathered bread and placed it for eruv intending to rent from the gentile before Shabbat and did so—perhaps it helps for all—for the eruv was made for this Shabbat [[[Shulchan Aruch Orach Chayyim 383|and siman 383]] where it is explained eruv made before rental is nullified when the gentile comes is no proof—for there he came on Shabbat and did not rent before Shabbat so at twilight he was not rented from the gentile and since he was to come on Shabbat retroactively the eruv was not fit to stand as the poskim wrote there—unlike here where at twilight rental was already made as required so the eruv then hung too]]; afterward I found in Nehar Shalom siman 383 also argues that in this manner even if they made eruv first first it helps—and so from Chayei Adam general laws eruv 1 and from Etzai Almogim in this siman 9—see there. And also apart from this it is reasonable b'dieved the eruv helps—for at least they were partners in the eruv bread at twilight—and it is stated siman 366:11 that even if they did not place the bread for eruv it also helps when they are partners in the bread; and also for seif 7 below one can say what Shulchan Aruch writes they must return and make eruv is only lekhatchila to place now for eruv that bread or another and bless as enacted—because that eruv bread was nullified when time ended; but b'dieved if the day was sanctified and they made no new correction they can rely on that bread for all are partners in it—and I found in Beit Meir the question remains on that in seif 7—for at least they were partners in the bread before Shabbat—and the proof they brought from Rashi chapter Hader [and it is the law of Shulchan Aruch siman 383] speaks only of rental on Shabbat and at twilight the eruv was not fit to stand as in Tosafos and obviously on Shabbat one cannot make eruv for weekday—but if they rented before Shabbat this reasoning applies—requires study there; per our words his question is settled—for b'dieved Shulchan Aruch agrees it helps and speaks only of the mitzvah of eruv lekhatchila. The upshot: lekhatchila for all rental must precede eruv; if one wishes to make eruv first it is forbidden to bless on it; however b'dieved it is an eruv even if rental came after since at twilight rental was already done:

(4) From him—and then the gentile is as a guest with them [Rambam]; know that in Rabbeinu Yehonatan it is explained even a gift does not help—only rental—and they copied in Chayei Adam; and it appears simple it deals with a weak gift as we discuss in rental; but if he gave him a complete gift with a complete written document in courts it is simple it helps; and the reason they did not require gift is because they did not wish to be so stringent—for a gentile will not give a complete gift in any manner:

(5) Does not forbid him—and it is simple it is not specific that he does not forbid him but also others dwelling there he does not forbid—for if not what did they accomplish by saying he does not forbid him—for since he forbids others in carrying he too is forbidden as others forbid him (Mordechai's essay and so in Birkei Yosef); therefore if the master does not dwell there himself but rented his courtyard to other Israelites and also to a gentile—perhaps here Rama's reason does not apply, etc., and one can say the gentile forbids them—and so in Chemed Moshe siman 384—see there; and so I found in Birkei Yosef in this siman argues thus—and so in Chayei Adam wrote also when he does not dwell in the courtyard himself they say he did not rent to forbid Israelites dwelling with the gentile; and it appears to me primary as Chemed Moshe and Birkei Yosef since Rama's law is puzzling as all the later ones questioned and as we wrote in Biur Halacha below siman 384—and it suffices for us to be lenient when he himself dwells in the courtyard and his proofs in Nishmat Adam can be easily rejected—for there Tosafos too dwelt with the youths and if the youths forbade one another he too would be forbidden—in our case there is no reliance at all; and our words are when he does not dwell there himself—and what the Noda B'Yehuda wrote in Rashba's words is not clear at all:

(6) Even though he has possession, etc.—see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote on the reason; and see in Biur HaGra s.k. 6 who wondered at this law—for nevertheless they do not forbid one another through possession without any eruv; and the principle is wherever they do not forbid one another the Sages did not require renting from the gentile—and so wondered in Ma'amar Mordechai and Etzai Almogim and they remain in doubt—and see in Damasek Eliezer s.k. 6 who somewhat settles Rama's words:
