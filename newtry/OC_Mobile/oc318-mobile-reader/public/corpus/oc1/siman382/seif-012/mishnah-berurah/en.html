(36) One of the courtyard members—the same even if not from courtyard members; customary language:
(37) To place anything there—and he acquires the place by placing something there erev Shabbat; per many poskim it helps even if he removed it afterward since he already acquired the place to place when he wishes:
(38) For it is, etc.—since he has partnership with him in his domain:
(39) And hired worker, etc.—but without rental it does not help—for the Israelite standing in his place is no better than him; unlike five dwelling in the courtyard in seif 9 where one's rental suffices for all—for there that one at least rented from the gentile; here there was only borrowing from him—it does not help for the Sages permitted for gentiles only rental not that borrowing standing in his place to rent like hired worker and household member:
(40) Need not rent—this view holds rental does not permit for a gentile—borrowing also helps for all like seif 9 for rental; therefore eruv with courtyard members suffices:
(41) Rather, etc.—lekhatchila one should be concerned for the first view:
(42) Gives his eruv and suffices—at first glance it implies only if he is from courtyard members as the Mechaber deals; but in Rashba's chiddushim it appears even if he does not dwell in the courtyard the Sages required he contribute to eruv because of this gentile's house we consider as if he dwells there:
