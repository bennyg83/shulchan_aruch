It appears to me Beit Yosef's view that even lekhatchila when they do not know he does not wish to rent they rent from his wife or agent; but Bach ruled like Or Zarua that lekhatchila one must ask the master; and so I saw in teshuvah Mahari'l siman 230. But it requires study—for Beit Yosef wrote so is implied in Rambam and Rashba though Bach brought proof from Rokeach in the name of Yerushalmi—see there; I examined Yerushalmi chapter Hader and nothing is implied. Also what Bach wrote Rambam chapter 2 holds thus—for he wrote in general they rent from his wife or agent and gleaning without his knowledge and did not write lekhatchila they ask but can rent immediately from his wife; perhaps his intent is "without knowledge" means he protests—and when he does not protest one cannot rent from his agent; and "they ask him" means his agent—and this is strained—that "without knowledge" also means without his knowing and this is lekhatchila one asks his agent. Also what he wrote Rashba in teshuvah holds thus is very strained; and further in Sefer Avodat HaKodesh daf 104: they rent from the wife of a gentile without his knowledge, and even if he refused—how did they say rent and he did not wish and they went to his wife, etc., until here—it implies in the beginning when they do not know he refused it is obvious they rent from his wife; and one cannot say it deals where he is not in the city—for then all agree they rent from his wife; for if so it is missing "and even if he refused how," for he should have explained "even if he is in the city and refused"; and so in Shibolei HaLeket and Sefer Tanya: and even from his wife and sons, etc.; and if the gentile does not wish—it appears implied in the beginning even when they do not know if he wishes they rent from his wife, sons, and agent. And further in siman 367 it is implied a man's wife makes his eruv for everyone even when it is not known if he wishes; and so in the Gemara daf 80—for if not, difficulty: what does it challenge from women who made eruv without knowledge that their eruv is not an eruv—perhaps there she did not know her husband does not wish; however one can say b'dieved Or Zarua agrees rental and eruv help and only lekhatchila he speaks. Mahari'l wrote in laws of eruvin of courtyards: it is taught in chapter Halon—a man's wife makes eruv for him without his knowledge. Mahari'l said: in Alfasi he ruled the same law—that one can rent from the wife of a gentile even without the gentile's knowledge—until here. And one can wonder—for this is in the Gemara daf 80: that innkeeper, etc., he does not rent, etc., they came before Rav Yehuda, he said thus said Shmuel: a man's wife makes eruv for him without his knowledge; and it appears in the Gemara one can say he resolves to prohibition from that Shmuel said she makes eruv—it implies regarding gentiles she does not rent; but in Alfasi he concludes: so they said—the wife of a gentile rents without his knowledge—behold he ruled to permission. And in Shiyurei Kenesset HaGedolah he wrote on what Beit Yosef brought this law in the name of Mordechai—this is his language: in derashot Mahari'l he brought this in the name of Alfasi, and perhaps in Hagahot Alfasi he speaks—and this is Mordechai—until here; and with due respect he did not examine Alfasi, and what Beit Yosef brought from Mordechai is that Mordechai also included the law of rental and gleaning, and this is clear; also one can wonder he did not notice it is from the Gemara:
