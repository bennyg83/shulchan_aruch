The first rental suffices. It appears the reason the first gentile did not remove himself since after the second's time his rental returns to him—and it is as if he did not move; therefore the first rental was not nullified; and though for eruv if the eruv-maker rented to another he certainly forbids and needs new eruv—for there since he rented to another and uprooted his dwelling it is as if he withdrew from partnership—no withdrawal is greater; unlike gentile's rental to another we did not see retraction from what he already rented domain to Israel—for it is for permitting carrying; and do not challenge my proof below on death of gentile from death of eruv-maker—and here we distinguish: there we only say the heir does not inherit the eruv the deceased gave—he stands in the deceased's place as if he gave the eruv but the heir stands in place of the deceased; here the heir is like another person; but here you come to learn that just as rental to another nullifies partnership so gentile's rental to another—this is nothing—for one rental to another is not comparable to the other; as stated it is correct:
