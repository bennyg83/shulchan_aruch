(65) Where Israel and gentile dwell—meaning one Israelite:
(66) One house of Israel—of the second Israelite:
(67) And is not open to the courtyard—but to another courtyard or alleyway; if it were open to this courtyard there would be two Israelites in a gentile's place and the law is obvious they must rent and then make eruv; here only one Israelite remains in the gentile's place and needs no correction to carry in the courtyard; and it teaches the second Israelite is forbidden to carry via his window to this Israelite's house and then to the courtyard even if they joined via windows between them; and though because this Israelite also carries via the second's house it would not be considered two in a gentile's place to forbid—for that applies only when both carry via their doorways to the courtyard—they decreed for another reason: if permitted this Israelite might rely and dwell with the gentile not fearing being alone since another Israelite is commonly here and learn from the gentile's deeds:
(68) But if open doorway—it is permitted; for even without eruv he will not rely to dwell alone since the other Israelite is commonly here via his doorway; therefore they did not decree here:
