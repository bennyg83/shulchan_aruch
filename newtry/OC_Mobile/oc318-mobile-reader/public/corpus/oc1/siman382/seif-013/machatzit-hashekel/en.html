(s.k. 13) Two courtyards, etc.—and see Choshen Mishpat, etc., and in the Gemara—meaning plain sugya permits (and so Hagahot Alfasi there); Gemara 65b: R' Yitzchak said Rav said Israel and gentile in inner and Israel in outer—a case came before R' Yehuda and he forbade; Rava and Rav Sheshet wanted to say he holds like R' Meir that even gentile with one Israelite the gentile forbids and requires rental; R' Yosef said if like R' Meir why need Israel in outer—for inner alone needs rental; and your incident was so and he required inner in his place and said permitted—for inner alone per Rashi in outer courtyard has no foot traffic on inner; and they challenge: rather like Ra'avyah who holds gentile does not forbid until two Israelites forbid one another; here they did not forbid one another for inner is permitted foot in its place and does not forbid outside as we hold siman 378; rather like Ra'avyah and R' Akiva who hold permitted foot in place forbids outside—and if they made eruv the gentile now forbids them; and R' Elazar said to R' Eliezer the same reversed—end quote Gemara:

(s.k. 13) And need no eruv at all—for we hold like Rabbenu Akiva's view permitted foot in place does not forbid outside; and since two Israelites do not forbid one another since we hold like Ra'avyah as above—the gentile does not forbid them and need not rent from gentile:

(s.k. 13) Unlike when gentile in inner, etc.—this is the third law the Mechaber wrote; though Mechaber wrote on third law "and even gentile in inner," etc.—it appears there is more reason to forbid in the first two laws than the third; Magen Avraham wrote there is no such thing—on the contrary in third law gentile inner and two Israelites outer even for us the gentile forbids as 75b:2 gentile is like many; the reason we fear they think more Israelites dwell inside; and if they made eruv and rented—gentile would shout, etc.; therefore must rent—unlike only one Israelite in outer—see Magen Avraham:

(s.k. 13) And impossible they made eruv, etc.—Gemara there challenges: what is different from Israel—if only one Israelite in inner he does not forbid outer per our view permitted foot does not forbid; whoever knows he is alone knows this; whoever does not know may think many in inner or permitted foot forbids outside—judge them favorably they presumably made eruv and do not forbid one another; unlike gentile in inner we fear they think many in inner; and answer: granted for Israel in inner who knows only one Israelite inside and knows permitted foot does not forbid—he knows; whoever does not know except thinks many in inner or permitted foot forbids—judge favorably they made eruv; unlike gentile in inner we fear many in inner; and if they made eruv and rented—gentile shouts, etc.; if not they made eruv and rented—eruv helps in gentile's place without rental—Magen Avraham:

(s.k. 13) And will say eruv, etc.—meaning since no shout they certainly did not rent; therefore eruv helps in gentile's place without rental:

(s.k. 13) Unlike when only one in outer—first law in Shulchan Aruch Israel and gentile in inner and Israel in outer—must be we do not decree they think many in inner—for then sugya 65b and 75b:2 contradict—there 65 is explained first law forbids only per R' Akiva permitted foot forbids outside; but per Rabbis for us gentile does not forbid thus; why fear they think many in inner; and in 75 it is clear with one Israelite permitted in all cases; therefore Rambam in chapter 4 deals when only one in outer—not as Beit Yosef understood; besides wondering on his concern one who does not know alone—for all see only one Israelite and one gentile—who would think more; therefore clear Rambam does not hold thus but as Rashi and Tosafos—with one Israelite in outer and one gentile inside permitted; Shulchan Aruch "and there is one who says even gentile in inner," etc.—follows Rambam—but not so; no one holds thus plainly:
