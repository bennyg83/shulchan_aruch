For it is not an acceptance. It is implied in the Rosh that one need not fast at all, and the Bach disagreed with him and wrote: why should it not have the law of a vow? And it can be answered that behold it is written in siman 418 and siman 568 that accepting a fast is not a vow, and therefore it does not avail unless he accepted it proximate to the day of the fast, namely at Minchah. However, the words of the Rosh too can be interpreted that for this matter it is an acceptance, that one must complete his vow, and therefore one should be stringent. And the Mordechai wrote that everyone agrees that if he accepted it before the Minchah prayer, it is fine, and all the more so earlier than that; and so was the practice for those who accept the Behab fasts to recite the blessing on Shabbos, and afterward one need not accept, end quote. And see siman 492; it is implied that at that same time each one accepts the fast upon himself. And in the Levush he wrote that answering Amen constitutes acceptance, and one need not accept it at Minchah; and nevertheless he is not obligated to fast, and such is the custom, end of his words. And see siman 568, se'if 2, in the gloss; and it is implied that even if he resolved in his heart to fast, he is not obligated to fast, since it is not at the time of the Minchah prayer; and so the Levush wrote explicitly. But if he uttered it with his lips, it appears to me that one should be stringent, as I wrote above.
