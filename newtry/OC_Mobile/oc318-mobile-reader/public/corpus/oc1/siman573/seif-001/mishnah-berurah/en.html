(1) Megillas Taanis. It records a number of days during the year on which miracles occurred for our ancestors. The Sages treated them like festivals and prohibited fasting on those days, as well as on the days before and after them.
(2) It is permitted to fast on them. Because, through our sins, many calamities later occurred on those days, their special status was annulled, as stated by the Levush.
(3) Except for Chanukah and Purim. These too are among the days recorded in Megillas Taanis, but because of the great and public miracles that occurred on them, they retained their original status.
(4) But on the days before them, etc. The days before and after Chanukah and Purim are no more stringent than the days before and after the other dates recorded there.
(5) And on Yom Tov, etc. See above, siman 494, se'if 3, Mishna Berurah, se'if katan 6.
(6) On Chanukah. All the more so on Purim, which is a day of feasting and joy. See below, siman 696, se'if 8, and Magen Avraham there.
(7) In Nisan he fasts. The practice of avoiding fasting in Nisan is only a custom, as stated by the Bach. Accordingly, the same applies to Lag BaOmer, the days from Rosh Chodesh Sivan until Shavuos, and the days between Yom Kippur and Sukkos. On Isru Chag, the fifteenth of Av, and the fifteenth of Shevat, however, he does not fast, since those days are mentioned in the Gemara, as stated by Magen Avraham. Some are lenient on all days on which Tachanun is not recited.
(8) On the day of his wedding. Even a widower who marries a widow must fast, and the bride must fast as well. One reason is that their sins are forgiven on that day, and each should confess the sins of the past. Another reason is the concern that they may become intoxicated at the time of the kiddushin.
(9) Even on Rosh Chodesh. Although fasting on Rosh Chodesh is forbidden by law, one fasts when the wedding is on Rosh Chodesh Nisan for the reason stated above. The same applies on Rosh Chodesh Av, although l'chatchilah weddings are not held then. One who fasts in these months need not later fast another day in compensation, since he acted with permission.
