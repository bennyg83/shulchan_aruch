[18] With threads of hair, etc. Because water passes through them and they do not interpose in immersion, so there is no reason to decree lest she remove them (Rambam, chapter 19). Shiltei Gibborim writes that a married woman may uncover her wig, even if it is made from her own hair, because the rule that a woman's hair is ervah applies only to hair attached to her body, not detached hair used to cover her natural hair, even if she uses it for beauty so as to appear to have abundant hair. This is unlike woolen threads, although these hairs are attached to something and could present an immersion issue, because the wig is treated as a garment without which she does not go out. Alternatively, she leaves it at home when immersing (Perishah).
[19] And not a young woman, etc. Rambam permits a young woman to wear hair of an elderly woman (Tur). Shiltei Gibborim writes that this is also the view of Rif and Ran. This requires study, for Ran explicitly implies the opposite, as Beit Yosef writes. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 19, objects to Rambam from the prohibition of a gold tooth because its unattractive appearance may cause her to remove it. He also objects from Shabbos 65a, where the Gemara says that had the Mishnah taught only her own hair we might have thought the reason was because it is repulsive. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty. On the contrary, Rambam's reason is precisely that the Mishnah permits even animal hair, which is more degrading, proving that we do not fear removal of hair because of embarrassment. The prohibition for an elderly woman to wear a young woman's hair is because she may remove it to show that it is not her own. Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh wonder how Rambam explains the Gemara's phrase "incidentally it taught a false case." This is not difficult in my view because Rambam follows Tosafot's reading, "not an elderly woman with a young woman's hair, nor a young woman with an elderly woman's hair." The Gemara may therefore mean that a young woman with an elderly woman's hair is permitted, although the flow appears to refer back to the prohibition on an elderly woman. The latter was taught only incidentally and is not false. So it appears to me.
