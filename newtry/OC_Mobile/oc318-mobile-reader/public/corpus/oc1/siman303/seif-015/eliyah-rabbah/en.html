[20] [Levush] And in her sandal, etc. The text is missing: "and with absorbent material in her sandal when it is tied to the sandal" (Malbushei Yom Tov).
[21] [Levush] And therefore, etc. It is surprising that this is an explicit Mishnah on 64b. Sefer HaTerumah and Semag write that it is a baraita. In any case it is clear that everyone accepts the rule, even though Tosafot there challenge Rabbi Porat's explanation based on grinding medicinal ingredients: if so, why may she not replace it? The answer is that since it was already upon her from before Shabbos, we do not fear grinding ingredients, yet Tosafot certainly does not dispute the Mishnah. One must say that even according to the medicinal-grinding reason, if it fell off she may not replace it, as Tosafot explicitly writes. Beit Yosef, p. 102, and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 19, likewise imply this. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam omitted the clause "if it fell, she may not replace it" because he relied on the law of a bandage that was removed. This shows that all agree.
[22] If it fell, etc. Tosafot in Eruvin writes that this applies specifically when it fell onto the ground, but if it fell onto a utensil she may replace it, as with a bandage in siman 328, seif 25. Tosafot Yom Tov notes that the later authorities omitted this point, and Maggid Mishneh cited above also implies it. This requires study, for if so, how did Sefer HaTerumah and Semag infer that "if it fell she may not replace it" cannot refer to absorbent material used for menstruation because she must remove it when relieving herself? She could simply avoid placing it on the ground. According to Magen Avraham, however, Tosafot wrote its leniency only according to Rabbi Porat's reason. According to the reason that replacement looks like a device for carrying, it is prohibited even if it fell on a utensil. This resolves the issue. Certainly, if she does not intend to leave her house it is permitted, and for us, who have no biblical public domain, it may also be permitted.
[23] [Levush] And according to this, etc. Tosafot on Shabbos 64b indeed writes so. Semag, Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah, however, make clear that even according to the concern that she may scheme to carry, absorbent material in the ear and sandal is permitted. Bach, Malbushei Yom Tov, and Nachalat Tzvi rule likewise, and Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch also imply this, even permitting straw in a sandal. The reason is that it is obvious she needs it for that purpose, unlike something placed in her mouth. Mordechai, Rosh, and Rabbenu Yerucham also accept the concern of scheming to carry, yet permit material in the ear and sandal. Semag, Sefer HaTerumah, and Semak even remain uncertain whether pepper may initially be placed in the mouth on Shabbos to counter bad breath. Maharshal explains that foul breath itself demonstrates the need and leaves no room for suspicion of a carrying device, whereas regarding a loose tooth or illness the need is not as obvious to observers. From Tzedah LaDerech I learned that the uncertainty is whether the Mishnah's mention of pepper also refers to the foul-breath case, but certainly the prohibition of placing something in the mouth refers to pepper. This resolves Beit Yosef and Nachalat Tzvi's difficulty.
[24] [Levush] For her menstruation, etc. Beit Yosef. In my humble opinion Tosafot on Shabbos also agrees that this is permitted, because here it is especially evident that she must carry it out. Piskei Tosafot there likewise implies that they prohibited only material in the ear and sandal according to their approach.
[25] On her arm, etc. But on her lower leg she will not remove it, because she would not expose her leg.
[26] [Levush] Because it is not the manner, etc. Rosh at the beginning of chapter 6 writes that if one forgot that it is Shabbos, even if we prohibit him from untying an item he will not leave it in place, since he is unaware of Shabbos. Regarding items that must be untied for a Shabbos need, however, we fear that he may forget Shabbos and come to carry them. Regarding items that need not be untied, we do not fear this, because at the moment of untying he remembers Shabbos, and we do not suspect that in such a short interval he will then forget and carry. Levush's words can be understood accordingly.
