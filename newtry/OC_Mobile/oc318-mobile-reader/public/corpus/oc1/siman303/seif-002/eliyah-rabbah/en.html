[5] [Levush] But with threads, etc. Specifically when they are not an ornament (Nachalat Tzvi).
[6] Because they are loose, etc. Since she does not choke herself with them, unlike a broad necklace, which is wide and does not choke her.
[7] With a broad necklace, etc. See seif 7. We are not concerned there that she may remove it, because then she would no longer appear fleshy.
[8] [Levush] And it is unlike a forehead ornament, etc.; therefore, etc. Levush took this from Beit Yosef, who writes that a forehead ornament differs because it is not ordinarily tied, and when she does tie it she ties it to her hairnet. A crown, however, is always tied, not to the hairnet, but with straps hanging from it that she ties. This requires study, because Shabbos 59a gives a different reason: the women who wear a crown are distinguished women, and a distinguished woman will not remove it to show others. It is therefore entirely unlike a forehead ornament, which all women wear, so that even a distinguished woman may not wear it out, as Tosafot write there. In my humble opinion Levush did not correctly understand Beit Yosef. Beit Yosef's question is the reverse: why is a forehead ornament permitted when tied, whereas a crown would be prohibited even when tied were it not for the reason of a distinguished woman? He answers that tying a forehead ornament provides a clear identifying feature, because it is not normally tied and therefore she will not remove it; moreover, since it is tied to the hairnet she will not uncover her head. A crown, however, is normally tied and is not tied to the hairnet, so we would fear that she might untie it to show others were it not that a distinguished woman will not do so. This is clear.
