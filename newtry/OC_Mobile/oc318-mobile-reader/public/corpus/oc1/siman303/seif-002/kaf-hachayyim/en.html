13) [Seif 2] Because they are loose, etc. Why do we not forbid them here lest she remove and display them? That concern applies only to a novel ornament. Taz, paragraph 4. If the threads around her neck are colored, it is forbidden because she might display them. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 4. See below, seif 20.
14) There: But a katla around her neck is forbidden, etc. Here the reason is that it interposes. A katla is explained in seif 7. There, however, its importance creates concern that she might remove and display it. Why does the present ruling give interposition as the reason? Tosafos explains that since she tightens it so that she will appear fleshy, she will not remove it, because she would then no longer appear fleshy. Below, however, the case involves a katla lying loosely around her neck, which creates no interposition. It is forbidden only because she might remove and display it. Taz, paragraph 5.
15) There: But a katla around her neck is forbidden, etc. The Gemara explains the distinction between threads and a katla. She does not tighten threads forcefully, because she would choke herself. A katla, however, is broad and does not choke her. Taz there.
16) There: But if they are sewn, etc. The reason is that she will not uncover her head to remove them. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, in the name of Or Zarua; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 5.
