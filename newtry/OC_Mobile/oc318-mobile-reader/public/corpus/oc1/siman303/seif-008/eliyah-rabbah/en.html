[10] Covered, etc. In a place where the custom is to uncover the ears, it is prohibited to go out with earrings if women ordinarily remove them from the ear (Rama). Since he writes "in a place where the custom is," this implies there is no distinction between married women and unmarried girls. In either case, where the ears are uncovered it is prohibited and where they are covered it is permitted. Magen Avraham, seif katan 16, however, appears to hold that unmarried girls are prohibited everywhere, and Lechem Mishneh, chapter Bameh Ishah, likewise implies this, contrary to the apparent wording here.
[11] [Levush] It is troublesome, etc. Although colored threads are prohibited for this reason, earrings are more difficult to remove (Tosafot 59a). One cannot say that the colored-thread case concerns a place where ears are uncovered, for then what would the Gemara on 65a prove from the daughters of Shmuel? Moreover, it should have distinguished based on whether it is customary to remove them. Examine this carefully.
[12] And if she goes out, etc. Even if the pins hold the garment in place (Beit Yosef). Bach disagrees and holds that there is no liability for a sin offering, because this is not the normal manner of carrying. Taz and Magen Avraham object that it is indeed the normal manner of carrying. Yet this requires study, for Shiltei Gibborim, p. 126, permits it in the name of Rosh and Tur. Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 15, also implies that even a perforated pin is an ornament for a woman in her veil. Gedulat Mordechai deletes and emends that text, but in my humble opinion this is not compelled.
[13] [Levush] Since it is for modesty, etc. This implies that even when the pin is an ornament, it is initially permitted whether she presses it into the hair beneath her veil or inserts it through the veil so it will not fall, because concern for uncovering her hair prevents her from removing it to show others. This is explicit in Beit Yosef. Accordingly, the latter clause, "if it does not hold it in place, etc.," concerns a decorative pin; if it is not decorative she is liable for a sin offering. This rejects Taz's objection that according to Tosafot a decorative pin is prohibited, because he did not note that Beit Yosef does not accept that reading. It also appears possible that Tosafot on 60a agrees here, because uncovering hair is more immodest than uncovering the leg with a garter. Tosafot's question there is only why the Gemara does not ask from a garter. Rosh, the Remezim, Rabbenu Yerucham, Raavan siman 349, Mordechai, and Agudah likewise permit even pins fastening the opening of her garment, because we do not decree lest she remove them, for then she would uncover her body. Taz's lengthy discussion of Semag is unnecessary. Semag's permissive ruling clearly follows the view that nowadays, when we have no biblical public domain, it is permitted. See seif 18. Bach also writes so. Taz's question why the Gemara does not establish the case as one in which she gathers her hair and the pin is an ornament is no difficulty, because in the time of the Gemara pins were not made in this fashion, as Raavan writes there.
