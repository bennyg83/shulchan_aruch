(א) Woven into her hair — permitted. We are not concerned lest she carry them in the public domain on Shabbat, for it is forbidden to unravel her hair braid on Shabbat; and this is Rabbeinu Tam's view in the Tur, and likewise in Tosafot there — R' Yitzchak wrote: just as there is prohibition in making a braid, so there is prohibition in undoing a braid. Some forbid — Rashi; and it appears he answers this difficulty: when immersion happens to occur she must remove the thread and undo the braid, and she will do this via a gentile — certainly permitted via a gentile as shevut di-shevut.

(ב) That they not be soiled. Rashi wrote: because water dissolves mud and soils her flesh when she ascends from immersion; and although it is not a chatzitzah since water enters them, nevertheless since she removes them because of soiling she may carry them — end of his words. In the Tur it is written that then she must remove them because of mud that is a chatzitzah; and his view appears that even though wet mud is not a chatzitzah as in Yoreh Deah siman 198 seif 14, nevertheless it is explained there that if she is particular it is a chatzitzah; and the Tur holds that since it is forbidden for this particular woman there is no distinction among women — know that otherwise we would permit an old woman to go out with all these forbidden items because of obligatory immersion, and for an old woman immersion does not apply; rather certainly whatever is forbidden for some is forbidden for all, as above — correct per Rashi.

(ג) Then she removes them etc. This is the reason regarding gilded ones.
