1) [Seif 1] A woman may not go out with woolen threads, etc. The concern that she might carry them four amos in the public domain applies specifically to small articles like these, because even during the week she sometimes carries them in her hand. No such decree was made regarding articles that constitute principal garments. Tosafos; Magen Avraham, paragraph 5; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
2) There: A woman may not go out with woolen threads, etc. She may not go out with them during twilight or during the additional Shabbos period either. Even a non-Jewish maidservant may not go out with threads and must loosen them for immersion. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
3) There: Because she must remove them when immersing, etc. This is forbidden even for elderly women who no longer menstruate at all, because the Sages made no distinction. Taz, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. Birkei Yosef cites Orach Chaim, folio 52d, which states that some say this applies only where women immerse in a river, but in places where they immerse in mikvaos inside buildings there is no concern. The unqualified words of the halachic authorities, however, imply that no distinction is made, and it is forbidden in every case.
4) There: If they are braided into her hair, it is permitted. This is because undoing her hair braids is forbidden, as stated in seif 26. Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
5) There: Some forbid it. This is Rashi's view. Bach writes that Rashi maintains we are concerned that she might loosen the braid enough to remove the threads from it, enabling her to immerse, and then carry the braiding threads four amos in the public domain; see there. Taz there explains that we are concerned that when she needs to immerse, she might tell a non-Jew to undo her braid, which would be a rabbinic prohibition compounded with another rabbinic prohibition and is permitted for the sake of a mitzvah; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2, challenges Taz, writing that a compounded rabbinic prohibition is permitted only when no other option exists, whereas here she can undo her braid while it is still daytime; see there. As a matter of practical halachah, Bach rules that one should be stringent like Rashi, and the later authorities cite this. We already wrote above in siman 13, paragraph 7, that although when Maran first gives an unattributed ruling in the Shulchan Aruch and then cites a dissenting opinion, he intends to rule like the unattributed view, when the dissenting view is stringent one should nevertheless account for it ab initio; see there. Here too, Maran intends that one account for the opinion introduced as 'some forbid' ab initio.
6) There: If they are woven, it is permitted, etc. These are not tightly fastened and do not interpose. Levush Serad. Although Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, seif 3, states that only hollow threads do not interpose, Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3, answers that this requirement is merely a stringency. By strict law, we follow Rambam and Rashi, who do not require them to be hollow. It suffices to apply the stringency during the week and not on Shabbos; see there. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 2, gives a similar answer, but writes that Darkei Moshe implies that here too they must be hollow. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, writes the same.
7) There: If they are woven, it is permitted, etc. This applies even if they are not braided into her hair. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
8) There, gloss: Provided that they are not soiled, etc. This is also stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, seif 4 in the gloss. The Shulchan Aruch does not state it because Beis Yosef rules like Rambam and the first version of the Gemara, that whenever the threads are woven no decree was made, without distinguishing between soiled and unsoiled threads; see there. We nevertheless maintain the stringent view of Rema, because the later authorities agree and because Maran's view in Beis Yosef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, also appears to incline this way; see there.
9) There, gloss: Provided that they are not soiled, etc. Rashi explains that the water dissolves the mud and soils her flesh when she emerges from immersion. Although the threads do not interpose because water enters them, she removes them because of the dirt and might consequently carry them, so she may not go out with them. These are his words. Tur writes that she must remove them because the mud interposes. Bach questions this, because it does not interpose when water enters it. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, Bach therefore explains Tur as follows: since she must remove them because of the mud and is particular to remove the thread, the thread itself interposes if she does not remove it; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, writes the same. See Taz, paragraph 2, for another explanation of Tur's view.
10) There, gloss: So that they will not become soiled, etc. This reason refers to gilded threads. Taz, paragraph 3. This is also Tur's text. Magen Avraham, however, reads 'become soiled,' applying it to the beginning of the ruling as well. Levush Serad. In any event, since she must remove them when immersing, she may not go out with them on Shabbos, because we are concerned that she might carry them four amos in the public domain.
11) Regarding whether a woman may go out with an expert amulet when its removal entails no danger, it can be said that she may not. She must remove it in water, even if it is sewn into leather, because the ordinary practice is to remove it so that it does not become dirty in the water and its letters are not blurred. The mitzvah of immersion does not ordinarily apply to a man, because it was uncommon even in the Talmudic era, and all the more so today, except for one who regularly immerses after a seminal emission. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2.
12) Regarding every article with which our Sages forbade a woman to go out because it interposes, it is possible that the prohibition also applies to an androgynos, for whom immersion is common because uterine blood renders such a person impure like a woman. Regarding a tumtum, whose status as male or female is uncertain, it can be said that since the law is rabbinic, the uncertainty is treated leniently. No decree was made for a male even regarding an article that interposes, because mitzvah immersion was not so common for him, even in the Talmudic era when people ate their ordinary food in taharah. A woman, however, even an elderly woman or virgin, may not go out with it, as Taz writes in paragraph 2. This certainly applies to a virgin because of immersion for ordinary food and offerings, as stated here. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 5. See above, paragraph 3.
