Whatever the Sages forbade going out with into the public domain, it is forbidden to go out with into a courtyard that has no eruv — except for a kavul and a wig, that is, a plait of hair that she has plaited into her own hair. And some say that whatever they forbade going out with, it is even forbidden to adorn oneself with it in the house, and all the more so to go out with it into a courtyard that has an eruv — except for a kavul and a wig. And some say that it is all permitted to go out with in a courtyard, even one that has no eruv. And nowadays our women are accustomed to go out with all ornaments; and some have said that by law they are forbidden, but since they will not heed [correction], it is better that they act unwittingly than deliberately; and some have found merit for them, to say that they act thus in accordance with the latter opinion that I wrote, that they did not forbid going out with ornaments into a courtyard that has no eruv. And now that we have no full-fledged public domain, all of our [thoroughfares that would be a] public domain have the status of a karmelis, and its law is like a courtyard that has no eruv, and it is permitted. {Rama: And some say a further reason to permit: that nowadays ornaments are common, and [women] go out with them even on weekdays, so there is no concern lest she remove and display [them], as there was in their days, when they were accustomed to go out with them only on Shabbos, and they were not common (Tosafos; Hagahos Alfasi, chapter "Bameh Ishah").} However, a ring bearing a seal for a woman, or one without a seal for a man — concerning which the Mishnah taught that [carrying it out] renders one liable for a chatas — is forbidden even in a karmelis, even for us; and the same applies to whatever was stated to carry a liability for a chatas. And there is one who says that in the present time, when men are accustomed to go out with a ring that has no seal, it is for them like an ornament, and it is permitted; and according to this, it is possible that since women are now accustomed to go out with a ring that has a seal, it is for them like an ornament, and it is permitted. Nevertheless, one must warn women not to go out except with the pins that they need to hold up their kerchiefs, and no more — for in this, from which they derive no benefit, they will heed us.
