If he began to eat, etc. That is, rich matzah; meaning that even though he began at a time of permissibility, nevertheless he must stop, and he is permitted to eat only until twilight (bein hashemashos), and afterward he must wait until it is certainly night, as explained in siman 472; and he spreads a cloth over the table and recites Kiddush; and this is what the Mechaber wrote, that his law is like on Shabbosos, etc. And according to the conclusion of the poskim above there, if he drank wine at the outset within the meal, he need not recite the blessing on wine again, only Kiddush alone; and afterward he says the Haggadah as is customary; and when he reaches eating he recites only the blessing on eating matzah, and not haMotzi, since he is within the meal. [However, according to our custom that we recite a blessing on each and every cup, because each cup is a mitzvah of its own and Chazal instituted a blessing over them, it appears that even in this case one must recite a blessing on each and every cup, and one is not exempt by what he recited earlier on the wine at the meal.] And the Acharonim wrote that according to the custom of the Rama above in siman 444, that we do not practice eating rich matzah even on erev Pesach, the entire core ruling of the Mechaber does not apply at all, for he did not eat bread at the outset, and it is not apt to say that he began a meal.
To bathe. After midday he should go to the bathhouse and immerse in honor of the festival; and after the Minchah prayer it is proper that he occupy himself with the laws of the Korban Pesach, and the Holy One, Blessed be He, will credit it to him as if he fulfilled it in practice; and in the Shalah he copied from Seder HaYom what every person should learn; and one does not provide the consolatory meal for a mourner on erev Pesach.
