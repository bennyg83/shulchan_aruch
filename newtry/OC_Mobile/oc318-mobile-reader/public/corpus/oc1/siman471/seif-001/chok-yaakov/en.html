From the tenth hour onward. That is, immediately after nine hours, which is one quarter of the day; for at nine and a half hours is the time of Minchah Ketanah; and at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim it says one should not eat near Minchah, and that means near Minchah Ketanah, as the Gemara concludes there on folio 107b; and so is the consensus of the Rif, the Rambam, the Rosh, the Tur, and all the other poskim. However, the Ran, although at the beginning of the Mishnah he also explained thus, nevertheless at the end he wrote that one should be stringent from the time of Minchah Gedolah, that is, from six onward; and so wrote Maharil in the laws of Shabbos HaGadol; and see in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, who labored at length to force and resolve the sugya according to the Ran; and see above, siman 639; and my brother-in-law in Ateres Zekenim, siman 111, also labored at length to be stringent in this; see there. And regarding the practical law, the Rav ruled below in siman 639, se'if 3, in the gloss, that on erev Sukkos one should not eat from midday onward, so that he eat in the sukkah with appetite, parallel to eating matzah (Maharil), end quote; and here he left it unelaborated like the view of the Mechaber on erev Pesach from the tenth hour itself; and the Acharonim already wonder at this and wish to force a distinction, that this case is different since one may eat only rich matzah, which does not satiate so much; but that is only prophetic speculation: from where do we know this distinction, for Maharil said "parallel to eating matzah"? And on the contrary, Maharil in a responsum, siman 99, wrote, and this is his language: that all the more should one be particular on erev Pesach, since one must hurry for the children and the afikoman to eat before midnight; therefore one should not eat rich matzah for the third meal after Minchah, which is not so on other eves of Yom Tov and Shabbos, when they wait long until dark, end quote. And furthermore, according to this, what does the Gemara ask at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim, "A person should not eat from Minchah onward: why mention specifically erev Pesach; even erev Shabbos and Yom Tov as well"? Perhaps it mentioned erev Pesach for a novelty, that even though he eats rich matzah, which does not satiate so much [still he may not]. And in truth, according to what Tosafos wrote there, s.v. "why mention specifically," it is possible to resolve that difficulty; see there. However, as for the core wonderment of the Acharonim against the Rav for omitting here the words of Maharil, it appears to me simply that there is no difficulty, and the Rav did well; for side dishes are permitted to eat, as is plain in the Gemara and all the poskim, that only bread is forbidden to eat, that is, rich matzah, for chametz is in any case forbidden all day; and so Maharil wrote explicitly in the laws of Shabbos HaGadol; and since the Rav already ruled above in siman 444 and siman 462 that we do not practice at all to eat rich matzah, then for what purpose should the Rav rule here like Maharil not to eat from midday, for chametz and matzah are in any case forbidden all day, and rich matzah we do not practice to eat at all? But on erev Sukkos, where it is possible to eat bread, he needed to write it. And side dishes are permitted in every case, whether on erev Pesach or on erev Sukkos, as is plain in the Gemara and all the poskim; and so wrote Maharil himself for this resolution; and so too intended the exceptional Moreinu HaRav R' Wolf Segal Horowitz, the dayan, may his lamp shine, that it is clear and correct without any doubt at all. However, the Kol Bo, siman 49, wrote that side dishes are forbidden to eat from Minchah Ketanah, that is, from the tenth hour onward; and that is astonishing against the implication of the Gemara and all the poskim; and it is possible to force and resolve the sugya according to that, and thereby also resolve what the Acharonim asked on the Ran and Maharil; and one may also resolve several difficulties that Tosafos asked at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim; see there; but the implication of all the poskim is not so, and this requires further study.
A little fruit or vegetables. In the Tur he wrote "and kinds of cooked dishes"; his intent is as the Rosh wrote in explaining the language "tardeima" in the Gemara, that it refers to kinds of cooked dishes, and not like the explanation of the Rashbam that it refers to fruit; but nevertheless, practically they do not disagree, for it is permitted to eat everything except bread, that is, rich matzah; and so is the implication of the Tur's language; and not like the Beis Yosef, who wrote that the Tur disagrees with the Rosh in this; and in truth there is no dispute here at all; and so it is implied explicitly in the Kitzur Piskei HaRosh; see there; and so is the implication of most poskim, that only bread is forbidden; and so it is explained explicitly from Tosafos and the Rosh at the beginning of Arvei Pesachim regarding what they asked from "he should not eat"; see there. And this is the language of the Kol Bo, siman 49: side dishes, such as fruit and vegetables and kitniyos and meat and cheese and eggs and the like, end quote. And it was already explained above that we practice a prohibition regarding kitniyos; also cooked dishes of the five species, which the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rosh, must mean with fruit juice; and we practice a prohibition in all this, as explained above in siman 444.
Or vegetables. Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: and the Tosafos in Eruvin, folio 55b, wrote that raw vegetables stimulate the appetite, but cooked ones satiate even with a light amount; see there; end quote. And in truth there is no proof from there at all; and one who looks there [sees] that there it refers to a fixed manner of eating, that is, when one uses them as a relish with bread; but vegetables alone are permitted to eat, whether raw or cooked, as is the implication of the unelaborated language of the poskim.
One who drinks much wine. The Tur permits drinking even a little wine; and see in the Beis Yosef how he labored to resolve the sugya of the Gemara according to him; and see in the Bach and the Taz, who explained it in another manner; nevertheless, the main [rule] is to be stringent like the view of the Shulchan Aruch; and so ruled the Bach; and in any case he should not drink too much, and through this the appetite for food will be spoiled; and so wrote the Taz; and see above, siman 477.
