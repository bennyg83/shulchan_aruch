Matzah ashirah. This means matzah kneaded only with fruit juice. If it was kneaded with fruit juice and water, one may fulfill the obligation with it in pressing circumstances. This is proved from Menachos. It is therefore forbidden to eat it on Erev Pesach. Maharal in Gevuros Hashem and Bach. Pesachim 38 asks regarding the ruling that one may fulfill the obligation with the thanksgiving offering loaves and the Nazirite’s wafers: Are they not matzah ashirah? The Gemara answers that the quantity of oil was only a reviis divided among ten large loaves made from seven tenths of an ephah, and this is not considered rich. This implies that if there is a large amount of oil, even though it is mixed with water, the result is matzah ashirah, and one does not fulfill the obligation with it even in pressing circumstances. Otherwise, why did the Gemara ask? Perhaps the Mishnah meant only that one fulfills the obligation after the fact. Rather, even after the fact one does not fulfill the obligation. This is also implied by siman 168, which rules that Bircas HaMazon is not recited over something kneaded with fruit juice. At the end of siman 188 it states that on Yom Tov one must eat a kezayis of bread over which Bircas HaMazon is required. What special novelty, then, exists regarding the first night of Pesach? Rather, as Ran writes in the second chapter of Sukkah, during the day one may eat matzah ashirah. This must refer to a product containing only a small amount of oil, because if it contains much oil, Bircas HaMazon is not recited over it. On the first night, however, even a small amount of oil disqualifies it, unless the amount is exceedingly slight, as in the thanksgiving offering. Furthermore, fruit juice alone does not cause fermentation. Why, then, would the verse “bread of affliction” be needed to exclude it? It would be obvious that one cannot fulfill the obligation with it, as stated in siman 453. It must therefore be that even when mixed with water one does not fulfill the obligation because it is matzah ashirah. See Beit Yosef, siman 462, in the name of Maggid Mishneh. Matzah with which one may fulfill the obligation. It appears to me that folded or swollen matzah should not be eaten throughout Erev Pesach, because perhaps it is valid matzah. See the end of siman 461. The wording implies that matzah may be eaten on the thirteenth of Nisan. It is permitted to feed him. Although it is forbidden to feed a minor a prohibited item directly, as stated in siman 343, one may directly feed him something whose only issue is the cancellation of a positive mitzvah, as stated in siman 269. A child old enough to understand, however, may not be fed matzah, because one cannot say to him “because of this” after he has already filled himself with it. Terumas HaDeshen. There is no distinction between a boy and a girl. And it was kneaded with wine and oil. This remains so even if it was later baked a second time. If it was crumbled and cooked, however, it may be eaten, and the appropriate blessing is recited, because it is a cooked dish and has dissolved.
