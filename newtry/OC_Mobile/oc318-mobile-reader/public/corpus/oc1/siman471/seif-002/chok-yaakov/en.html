Rich matzah. And if it was kneaded in fruit juice with water, it is likewise rich matzah, and one does not fulfill with it; and so concluded Magen Avraham; and I made supports for his words in siman 462, s.k. 2; see there regarding in what manner the fruit juice is nullified within the water. And Magen Avraham further wrote, and this is his language: that puffed and doubled matzah one should not eat on erev Pesach all day, lest it be [true] matzah, end quote; and see above, siman 461.
It is forbidden to eat. In the Yerushalmi: that anyone who eats matzah on erev Pesach is as if he had relations with his betrothed, etc. And the reason the Levush wrote: that it compares to his betrothed, for just as one who has relations with his betrothed shows the greatness of his desire, that he is lustful and steeped in promiscuity so that he cannot restrain himself to wait until he brings her under the chuppah, when they recite the seven blessings, so one who eats matzah on erev Pesach shows gluttony, that he cannot wait in eating matzah until night, when they recite seven blessings; and so wrote the Kol Bo and Mahariv. And Mahariv wrote in siman 193 that the seven blessings of matzah are these: wine of Kiddush, and Asher Gealanu, wine of the poor man's bread, haMotzi, and eating matzah: behold seven blessings; and he does not count Shehecheyanu, for one says it even in the marketplace; and he also does not count the blessing of dipping, for its obligation is for the children; and according to the view of the Asheri that one does not recite a blessing on the second cup, perhaps he counts Asher Gealanu as two blessings; and he does not count the blessing of Hallel even according to the one who recites it, because there are places that do not say it at the table, end quote. And in the Shalah, siman 59, and in the Tanya, he includes Zeman and dipping and excludes Asher Gealanu; and Nimukei Yosef [explains], because it is impossible that he did not wash his hands when he ate by day, end quote. Even though he counts the blessing of haMotzi, for which it is also impossible that they did not recite haMotzi by day when he ate, it appears to me that [the difference] is because he does not recite [haMotzi] when eating matzah by day, since it is forbidden, and it is forbidden to recite a blessing over eating something forbidden, as explained above in siman 196; see there. And Asher Gealanu he does not count because it is not of the topic of eating and drinking; and so too the Levush likewise omitted the blessing of Asher Gealanu and the blessing of wine of the second cup; and one must say that there the blessing of wine is one matter. And in my humble opinion there is no need for all these forced resolutions; even though there are more than seven blessings here, nevertheless he said seven blessings in order to compare and equate to the seven blessings of a betrothed woman; for even though here there are more, nevertheless seven blessings are included, just as a maneh is included in two hundred; and it is straightforward.
Day of the fourteenth. But on the night of the fourteenth it is permitted; and so it is explained explicitly in the Ran, chapter Elu Ovrin; and so is the implication of the Yerushalmi and the poskim and the Bach; and not like the implication of Magen Avraham; and so wrote my brother-in-law the Rav in Ateres Zekenim; and in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote that many in Constantinople practice not to eat matzah from Rosh Chodesh.
Lest he come to eat from them. Since he is occupied with it, as R' Yehudah was concerned regarding chametz, and the Rabbanan regarding chadash; end quote of Maharil. And even so, the Rav ruled that one need not be concerned for this, for it is not comparable to chadash and chametz, regarding which we are concerned for a Torah prohibition, which is not so of eating matzah on erev Pesach, which is only a rabbinic prohibition (see Maharil, siman 99); also, since one is not accustomed to them all year, we are not concerned that he will come to eat from it; and it is straightforward.

But if it was not kneaded in wine and oil, even if he did not again knead them and bake them, but only ate it as is, he fulfills his obligation if he eats a kezayis from it, as stated in the Gemara of Berachos, folio 37b; see there. And it is not comparable to soaked matzah that dissolved, regarding which he ruled above in siman 461 that one does not fulfill with it; for that case is different, since it was soaked so much that it dissolved, it is as if the form of bread departed from it; which is not so when it was crumbled by hand; and the like of this is explained above in siman 169 regarding a blessing; see there.
It is not called rich matzah, and it is forbidden to eat it on erev Pesach. And if so, it is implied that one fulfills with it at night, since it is not called rich matzah; and so is the implication of the Rav's language at the beginning of this gloss, that specifically matzah with which one fulfills is forbidden to eat. However, Maharil in the laws of Shabbos HaGadol left the matter in doubt as rich matzah; see there. And if it was cooked, it was explained above in siman 461 that one does not fulfill with it, and it is permitted to eat it on erev Pesach; and so is the consensus of the Acharonim.
