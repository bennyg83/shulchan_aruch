[3] And if one wants to return etc. — we are not accustomed to say it except after supplications; and the Bach wrote that it is a mistaken custom, but rather one should say Yehi L'Ratzon immediately after the Shemoneh Esrei prayer; so too wrote Lechem Chamudos and so in Seder HaYom; and it seems one should return and say Yehi L'Ratzon — until here. So too wrote Sefer Tanya. It implies he should say Yehi L'Ratzon after the steps, and the poskim do not imply so; and as the Levush wrote at the beginning of siman 122. And it is further difficult from the Gemara Berachos 29a, where it says: when we said "one who finished returns to the beginning" — we only stated this when he uprooted his feet; but if he did not uproot his feet, he returns to his work, for uprooting his feet — what is it? He has still not said Yehi L'Ratzon. And it is possible that while stepping he says, as Maharshal says, Yehi L'Ratzon; also possible that it is not exact and requires examination. Seder HaYom wrote: Yehi L'Ratzon etc. is auspicious for many matters: its beginning yud and end yud, its words yud yud yud, it has forty-two letters and its secret is a great secret; therefore one must say it quietly with intention, and it helps greatly to have one's prayer accepted and not return empty. Beit Yosef wrote: it is good to say one verse from Torah or Prophets or Writings before saying Yehi L'Ratzon that begins with His Name and ends with His Name.
