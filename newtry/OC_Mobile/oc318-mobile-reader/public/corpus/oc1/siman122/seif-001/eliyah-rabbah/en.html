Elohai Netzor, etc. Rabbi David Abudarham writes that although good and evil are placed in a person's own hands, he asks Hashem to assist him in doing good. 'Let my soul be silent to those who curse me' means: not only do I ask that I merit not to insult another first, but grant me the merit to endure being cursed. This is the quality of those who hear their shame and do not answer. 'Let my soul be like dust' alludes to dust, which everyone treads underfoot yet which rises above their heads as dust, and in the end everyone needs it. So too, one who humbles himself in this world will become foremost in the World to Come. Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 87, states that he would say 'My God, before I was formed, etc.' even on Shabbos. A responsum of Maharshal states that he would say, 'May it be Your will that You save us from the brazen, etc.' The Tur records the following custom: to say Elohai Netzor through 'frustrate their designs,' followed by 'Our King and our God, Healer of all flesh, unify Your Name in Your world, build Your city, establish Your House, perfect Your Sanctuary, hasten the end and the coming of Your Mashiach, redeem Your people, gladden Your congregation. Act for the sake of Your Name, etc. Act for the sake of Your right hand. If not for our sake, then so that Your beloved may be delivered; save with Your right hand and answer us.' Then one says Yihyu LeRatzon. Maharshal followed this practice as well.
He shortens, etc. This means that he shortens Elohai Netzor itself. If there is not enough time even to step backward, he should interrupt.
