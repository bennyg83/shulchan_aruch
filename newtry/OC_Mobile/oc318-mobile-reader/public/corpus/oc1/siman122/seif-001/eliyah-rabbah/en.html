[1] Regarding Elokai Netzor etc. R. David Abudraham wrote: even though good and evil are given over to man's hand, he asks of Heaven to help him do good; and "to those who curse my soul may it be silent" — meaning, not only am I asking that I merit not to curse others first, but rather that I merit to bear my curse; and this is "they hear their insult and do not respond," and "my soul is like dust" — to hint that just as with dust everyone tramples it with their feet and it rises above their heads, yet in the end everyone needs it — so one who humbles himself in this world will be head in the World to Come. He wrote in the responsum of Maharam of Mintz, siman 87, that he would say "Elokai, before I was created" etc. even on Shabbos. And he wrote in the responsum of Maharshal that he would say "Yehi ratzon that You save us from brazenness" etc. And this is the wording of the Tur: "I am accustomed to say: Elokai Netzor... until... confuse their plans; our King and our Heaven and Healer of all flesh, unify Your name in Your world, build Your city, establish Your house, complete Your Temple, bring near the end, the coming of Your anointed, redeem Your people, gladden Your congregation; act for the sake of Your Name... act for the sake of Your right hand; if not for our sake, for the sake of Your beloved that they be released; save Your right hand and answer us — Yehi L'Ratzon." So too Maharshal practiced.
