1) [Seif 1] But between 'Yihyu leratzon' and the other supplications, it is quite proper. That is to say, he may answer Kaddish and Kedushah in the manner that one interrupts during the berachos of Krias Shema, as he writes afterward. And so is proven in the Beis Yosef, for Maran, of blessed memory, took this law from a responsum of the Rashba, who holds that one may not interrupt at all in the Shemoneh Esreh between [its conclusion] and 'Yihyu leratzon,' neither for supplications nor for Kaddish and Kedushah; and even so he holds that one who says supplications after 'Yihyu leratzon,' and the shaliach tzibbur has begun to arrange his tefillah and reached Kaddish or Kedushah, should shorten and conclude; and if he did not shorten, he may interrupt in the manner that one interrupts during the berachos of Krias Shema; see there. And although the Taz, s.k. 1, found difficulty with this and came to distinguish between one who regularly says supplications and one who does not, as seen there, and the Mishbetzos Zahav upheld his words, the plain sense of the matter appears more like what I have written; and so is implied from the words of the Levush and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1. Therefore one should not distinguish between one who is regular and one who is not, and one should not interrupt in their middle except in the manner that one interrupts during the berachos of Krias Shema. And afterward I saw that the Mateh Yehudah, ot 1, wrote so explicitly and rejected the words of the Taz; see there. So too wrote the Maamar Mordechai, ot 2. However, it appears to me that he may also answer 'Yimloch' in Kedushah, and he may also answer Amen after 'HaKel HaKadosh' and after Shomea Tefillah, for since there is a dispute about this even during Krias Shema, during supplications one may be lenient, as they are not so stringent; see above siman 66, seif 3, in the gloss, and our words there, ot 18, and examine this well. And it further appears to me that he may also answer the Amen of other berachos; see above siman 59, seif 4, in the gloss. Only the Amen after Tiskabel, Yehei Shelama, and Oseh Shalom, and 'Baruch Hu uVaruch Shemo,' one should not answer, because they are merely a custom, as we wrote above in siman 66, ot 23; see there. And afterward I saw that the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 18, ot 15, wrote so, and in the Pe'as HaShulchan, that one may answer every Amen, and he wrote that so wrote also the sefer Shulchan Shlomo; see there. This too means specifically that he may also answer the Amen of berachos, but not the Amen after Tiskabel, etc., nor 'Baruch Hu uVaruch Shemo,' for the reason we said, that they are not obligatory. And if he has already finished the supplications but cannot step back because of a person praying behind him, he may interrupt and say even 'Baruch Hu uVaruch Shemo.' Maamar Mordechai there; and so wrote the Chesed LaAlafim, ot 25.
