(1) [Seif 1] But between Yehi L'Ratzon and the other supplications it is fine — meaning that one may answer Kaddish and Kedusha as one interrupts in the blessings of Shema, as stated afterwards; and so it is proven in Beit Yosef that Maran took this law from the responsum of the Rashba, who holds that one may not interrupt at all between the eighteen [blessings] — neither for Yehi L'Ratzon, nor for supplications, nor for Kaddish and Kedusha; and nevertheless he holds that one who says supplications after Yehi L'Ratzon, and the prayer leader began to order his prayer and reached Kaddish or Kedusha — he shortens and rises; and if he did not shorten, he may interrupt as one interrupts in the blessings of Shema; see there. And although the Taz in sk"a questioned this and came out to distinguish between one accustomed to say supplications and one not accustomed, see there — nevertheless Maaseh Tzaddik held to his words; the explanation of the matters is more plausible as I wrote, and so from the words of the Levush and Rabbeinu Zalman letter aleph; therefore one should not distinguish between accustomed and not accustomed, and one may not interrupt in their midst except as one interrupts in the blessings of Shema. And afterwards I saw that Mateh Yehudah letter aleph stated so explicitly and rejected the words of the Taz; see there. And so Maamar Mordechai letter bet; however it seems to me that one may also answer "yimloch" in Kedusha, and one may also answer Amen after HaKel HaKadosh and Shmoneh Esrei, for since there is a dispute in this regarding Shema, regarding supplications one may be lenient that they are not so strict; see above siman 66 seif 3 in the gloss and in our words there letter ches, and examine. And further it seems to me that one may also answer Amen of "after the blessings"; see above siman 59 seif 4 in the gloss — only Amen after "titkabel" and "yehei shlamah" and "oseh shalom" and Baruch Hu and Baruch Shemo one may not answer, because they are only a mere custom, as stated in siman 66 letter kaf-gimmel; see there. And afterwards I saw that Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 18 letter tet-vav and in Paas HaShulchan that every Amen one may answer, and he wrote that Shulchan Shlomo also stated so; see there. And this too is specifically that Amen of blessings one may also answer, but not Amen after "titkabel" etc. and Baruch Hu and Baruch Shemo, for the reason we said that they are not obligatory; and if he already finished the supplications but cannot step backward because of a person praying behind him, he may interrupt and say also Baruch Hu and Baruch Shemo — Maamar Mordechai there; and so Chasdei David letter kaf-hei.
