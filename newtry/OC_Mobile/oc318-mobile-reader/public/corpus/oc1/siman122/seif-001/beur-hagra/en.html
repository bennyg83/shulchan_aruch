For it is an extended prayer, as stated in the first chapter: since the Sages instituted it, and as stated there that David said after the eighteen sections; and furthermore, since they said in chapter 4, we only stated this regarding one who is not accustomed [to say supplications] — from which we learn that Yehi L'Ratzon one says immediately, for otherwise it should have said "only when he did not say Yehi L'Ratzon" may one not interrupt; and this is what the gloss says, "and specifically" etc. Rashba end of chapter 2, and Terumat HaDeshen in chapter 4.

But in a place etc. And it is not necessary [to forbid supplications] — for even in the middle of the prayer it is permitted, and in Shmoneh Esrei it is permitted to ask all one's needs, and how much more so before Yehi L'Ratzon; and as stated in siman 119 s"1, and as Terumat HaDeshen wrote in chapter 4.

And in places etc. And so the forty [amot] at the end of chapter 2, where Mar brei d'Ravina etc. Yehi L'Ratzon etc. forty, which afterwards the Rashba and Terumat HaDeshen say. And although one can push aside that it says afterwards p"1 as stated in s"2, and as Terumat HaDeshen wrote in chapter 4 — nevertheless it is obvious that it is permitted, for it is not better than Shmoneh Esrei, as stated above there.

However etc. — meaning, after Yehi L'Ratzon, even though he did not uproot his feet, nevertheless it is not part of the prayer, and as stated in chapter 4 there: "only" etc. — thus it is like uprooting his feet regarding going back; nevertheless he should shorten in order to uproot his feet; and so Magen Avraham.
