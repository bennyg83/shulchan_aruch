And for Kaddish and Barchu — see Mishna Berurah. And if one began to say certain supplications that one is not accustomed to, and it happened to him to answer a plain Amen — it seems to me that it is forbidden, since he has not yet said Yehi L'Ratzon, and it is not comparable to what the Taz permitted in sk"a, and as we wrote in Mishna Berurah in letter aleph there — for that case speaks of after he already said Yehi L'Ratzon. And in Da'as Chachamim [at the end of the laws of prayer, in the section "in our countries" letter aleph] it implies that it is permitted, but it is not clear; and one can say that the reason is that in our countries, where they are accustomed to say Elokai Netzor before Yehi L'Ratzon, it follows that he holds that once one finished the eighteen blessings, one has already finished the entire prayer; therefore his law is like in a place where they are accustomed to say Yehi L'Ratzon after Yehi L'Ratzon — meaning that the verse Yehi L'Ratzon that Chazal instituted to say after the eighteen blessings [as David said after the eighteen of his psalms] is not inferior in any respect to the other supplications that one is accustomed on one's own to say after one's prayer, regarding which we say that it partially resembles the prayer and one may not answer Amen either in their midst or before beginning to say them; and as we wrote in Mishna Berurah letter aleph. So too here.
