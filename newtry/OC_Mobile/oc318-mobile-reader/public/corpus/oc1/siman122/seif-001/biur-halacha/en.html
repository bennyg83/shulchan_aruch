If one began reciting supplications that he does not ordinarily say, and an opportunity arose to answer a regular Amen, it appears to me that he may not answer, since he has not yet said, "May the words of my mouth be acceptable." This is unlike the case permitted by Taz in seif katan 1, as we explained in Mishnah Berurah, letter 1, because there Taz discusses a case after he had already said, "May the words of my mouth be acceptable." Derech HaChaim, at the end of the laws of prayer, under the heading "In our countries," letter 1, implies that it is permitted, but this is not clear. One cannot explain that his reason is that in our countries, where people ordinarily recite Elokai Netzor before "May the words of my mouth be acceptable," he holds that once the eighteen blessings are completed, the entire prayer is finished, and the law is therefore like a place where people ordinarily recite supplications only after "May the words of my mouth be acceptable." This cannot be correct, because according to all opinions the verse "May the words of my mouth be acceptable," which the Sages instituted after the eighteen blessings, is no less significant than other supplications a person has accustomed himself to recite after prayer. Such supplications retain some status of prayer, and one may not interrupt for Amen, either in the middle of them or before beginning them, as we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, letter 1. The same applies here.
