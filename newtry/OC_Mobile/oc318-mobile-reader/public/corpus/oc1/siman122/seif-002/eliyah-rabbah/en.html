And if he wishes to repeat, etc. Our custom is not to say Yihyu LeRatzon until after the personal supplications. The Bach writes that this is a mistaken custom and that one should say Yihyu LeRatzon immediately after the Amidah. Lechem Chamudot and the Seder HaYom write likewise. It appears that he should repeat Yihyu LeRatzon. Sefer Tanya also writes this. His wording implies that Yihyu LeRatzon should be said after the backward steps, but this does not appear to accord with the poskim, as the Levush writes at the beginning of the next siman. There is also a difficulty from Berachot 29, where the Gemara says that the rule that one who has finished returns to the beginning applies only where he already moved his feet, but if he has not moved his feet he returns to Avodah. What difference does moving his feet make if he has not yet said Yihyu LeRatzon? Perhaps when he steps backward Maharshal means that he says Yihyu LeRatzon then. It is also possible that the Gemara's wording is not exact. This requires study. The Seder HaYom writes that Yihyu LeRatzon is especially effective for many matters. It begins with the letter yod and ends with yod; it has ten words containing ten yods, and forty two letters, corresponding to a great mystical secret. Therefore one should recite it slowly and with concentration. It is very effective in having one's prayer accepted so that it does not return empty. The Beit Yosef writes that before Yihyu LeRatzon it is good to say one verse from the Torah, Neviim, or Ketuvim that begins with the first letter of one's name and ends with the last letter of one's name.
