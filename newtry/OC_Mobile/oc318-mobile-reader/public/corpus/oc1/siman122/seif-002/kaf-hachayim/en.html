(2) In that gloss. And specifically in a place where they practice saying Yehi L'Ratzon immediately etc. — the Bach wrote thus: the enactment of the Sages is to say Yehi L'Ratzon after the Shemoneh Esrei prayer, as stated in the first chapter of Berachos 9b; and so the Rashba in his responsum, that Yehi L'Ratzon is part of the eighteen [blessings] and one is not permitted to interrupt between the end of the eighteen and Yehi L'Ratzon by saying supplications; and Beit Yosef brings it, and presumably such is the view of all the poskim etc., and so in Shulchan Aruch — unlike what the gloss to Shulchan Aruch says, that in these places where they practice interrupting in Elokai Netzor before Yehi L'Ratzon, they also interrupt for Kaddish etc., for he tied the matter to custom which does not exist — since in the Gemara they said the Sages instituted Yehi L'Ratzon after the eighteen, what do we care for a mistaken custom that they did not practice so; and so Beit Yosef in siman 123 regarding the law that one steps three steps etc., what do we care for a mistaken custom; see there, end quote. However Rabbeinu Yonah in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar wrote that even if one says Yehi L'Ratzon after the supplications there is no prohibition in this, for in the blessings themselves, if one comes to say at the end of each blessing something akin to the blessing, one says it — and Yehi L'Ratzon is not better than the blessings themselves, end quote; and Beit Yosef brought it at the end of this siman, and Levush first chapter of Berachos letter nun-dalet, and wrote there that the Tur so practices in these countries — only regarding what Mahari wrote that one may also interrupt to answer Kaddish etc., the Levush wrote there that he argues with the matter, per Rabbeinu Yonah's reason; see there. And Knesset HaGedolah brought it in Hagahos Beit Yosef letter aleph; and the Taz in sk"bet questioned the words of Mahari that wrote one may interrupt; see there. And Kaf HaChayyim letter aleph that they may not interrupt; see there. And so Birkei Yosef letter bet that such is the essential law, that those who practice saying Elokai Netzor before Yehi L'Ratzon may not interrupt to answer Kaddish and the like; see there. And so the Gra. And Birkei Yosef wrote there regarding what the Taz said on the essential custom, that he came out to distinguish between adding in a blessing in a temporary way and a fixed addition — it is not clear, end quote. Therefore it is more correct to say Yehi L'Ratzon before the supplications, in order to satisfy all views, and also so that one may answer Kaddish etc. within the supplications if it happens to him; and so the Levush there, Knesset HaGedolah Hagahos Beit Yosef letter aleph, Eshel Avraham letter aleph, Olas Tamid letter aleph; and see below letter yud-bet.
