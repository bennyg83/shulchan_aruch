2) There, in the gloss: And specifically in a place where the custom is to say 'Yihyu leratzon' immediately, etc. The Rav the Bach wrote, and this is his language: The enactment of the Sages is to say 'Yihyu leratzon' after the Shemoneh Esreh, as stated in the first chapter of Berachos, folio 9b; and so wrote the Rashba in a responsum, that 'Yihyu leratzon' is part of the Shemoneh Esreh, and one is not permitted to interrupt between the conclusion of the Shemoneh Esreh and 'Yihyu leratzon' with the saying of supplications; and the Beis Yosef cites it, and presumably such is the view of all the poskim, etc.; and so he ruled in the Shulchan Aruch, and not like what is written in the gloss on the Shulchan Aruch, that in these places where the custom is to interrupt with 'Elokai Netzor' before 'Yihyu leratzon,' they interrupt as well for Kaddish, etc., for he made the matter depend on custom, which is not so: since in the Gemara they said that they enacted 'Yihyu leratzon' after the Shemoneh Esreh, what have we to do with an erroneous custom in which they did not practice thus? And so wrote the Beis Yosef in siman 123, in the law that one steps three steps, etc., that what have we to do with an erroneous custom; see there. End of his language. However, Rabbeinu Yonah, in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar, wrote that even if he says 'Yihyu leratzon' after the supplications there is no prohibition in it, for within the berachos themselves, if one comes to say at the end of each berachah something akin to the berachah, he may say it, and 'Yihyu leratzon' is no better than the berachos themselves. End of his language. And the Beis Yosef cited him at the end of this siman, and the Lechem Chamudos on the first chapter of Berachos, ot 54; and the Lechem Chamudos wrote there that such is the custom of these lands. However, on that which Moreinu HaRav Moshe [Isserles], of blessed memory, wrote, that they are permitted also to interrupt to answer Kaddish, etc., the Lechem Chamudos wrote there, 'I hesitate concerning the matter, according to the reason that Rabbeinu Yonah wrote'; see there. And the Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah cited him in the Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 1. And likewise the Taz, s.k. 2, raised a difficulty against the words of Moreinu HaRav Moshe, of blessed memory, who wrote that they may interrupt; see there. And so ruled the Pri Chadash, ot 1, that they may not interrupt; see there. And so wrote the Birkei Yosef, ot 2, that this is the essential ruling, that those who are accustomed to say 'Elokai Netzor' before 'Yihyu leratzon' should not interrupt to answer Kaddish and the like; see there. And so wrote the Gra. And the Birkei Yosef wrote further there that what the Taz wrote concerning the essence of the custom, coming to distinguish between one who adds to a berachah in passing and a fixed addition, is not clear. End of his words. Therefore, the most proper course is to say 'Yihyu leratzon' before the supplications, in order to satisfy all opinions, and also so that he may answer Kaddish, etc., during the supplications if it should occur to him. And so wrote the Lechem Chamudos there; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, ot 1; Ateres Zekeinim, ot 1; Olas Tamid, ot 1. And see below, ot 12.
