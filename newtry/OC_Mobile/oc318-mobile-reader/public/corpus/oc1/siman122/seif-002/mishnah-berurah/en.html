(7) It is not proper — see in Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe that they wrote that by law there is no prohibition, for it is not stricter before Yehi L'Ratzon than the blessing Shomea Tefillah, for we hold above in siman 119 that one may ask therein all one's needs; only that ab initio it is more proper to say Yehi L'Ratzon before Elokai Netzor.

(8) One says Yehi L'Ratzon — Seder HaYom wrote: Yehi L'Ratzon etc. is auspicious for many matters: its beginning yud and end yud, its words yud and yud yud, and it has forty-two letters and its secret is a great secret; therefore one must say it quietly with intention, and it helps greatly to have one's prayer accepted and not return empty. Chayei Adam wrote: it is proper and fitting for every person to pray every day separately for his needs and livelihood, that Torah not depart from his mouth, his seed and his seed's seed, that all who issue from his loins be servants of Heaven in truth, and that he not find, Heaven forbid, disqualification in his seed, and everything he knows in his heart that he needs; and if he does not know how to speak eloquently in the holy tongue, he may say it even in Ashkenaz, only that it be from brokenness of heart. And it is better to fix prayers for all matters he needs after finishing all eighteen [blessings], rather than fixing them in the blessing Shomea Tefillah, so that when he needs to answer Kaddish or Kedusha he can answer after saying Yehi L'Ratzon according to all.
