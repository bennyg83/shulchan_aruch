[4] [Levush] Such as saying a kav, etc. According to this, Levush's later phrase "but otherwise it is permitted" means that one may mention the name of a measure so long as he does not add it to an earlier amount already supplied. This also appears to be Rama's meaning, because the item's standard measure and price are known. Olat Shabbat, however, understands the case as saying, "Fill this vessel for me," while mentioning the formal name of a measure remains prohibited. Nachalat Tzvi rules likewise. I do not understand their reasoning. Nachalat Tzvi says Bach interprets it so, but Bach speaks according to Tur, who sees no distinction based on a known price. According to Rama it is certainly permitted. Magen Avraham was later printed and explicitly writes this in the name of Darkei Moshe.
[5] [Levush] With liquid measure, etc. Rama does not distinguish liquid from dry measure. In truth, nowadays even liquids often have a known standard measure and price, and the public relies on this for leniency. Taz objects at length from the Gemara's rule not to mention the name of a measure, showing that merely stating the formal measure is prohibited. There is no difficulty, for the Gemara can concern an item whose standard measure and price are not known. His other proofs can likewise be rejected. Nevertheless, a God fearing person should be stringent, as above. See siman 304, seif katan 31.
[6] And even if there is no, etc. Olat Shabbat writes that Tur says this, whereas Ran says one should not bring them in a basket holding three or four but should bring one or two on the shoulder, implying that the change is required only to lighten the burden. He did not examine carefully, because Tur in siman 510 also writes like Ran. The meaning is that when one can change the manner in a way that lightens the burden, he should do so. When that is impossible, such as with a barrel or sack full of fruit, he should make another change. Thus, if the normal way is on the shoulder, he should carry it before him, or make a comparable change. This also resolves Olat Shabbat's objection at the end of siman 510.
[7] And some say, etc. This is from Tosafot at the beginning of chapter HaMevi. It is explicit there that the leniency applies specifically inside a house where no one sees. Outside, where carrying itself is permitted but observers are present, it is prohibited. Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh writes likewise.
