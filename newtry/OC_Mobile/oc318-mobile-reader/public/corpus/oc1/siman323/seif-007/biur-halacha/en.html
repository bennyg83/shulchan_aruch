(א) Permitted to immerse, etc., and some forbid, etc. — the reason for these two views is that in tractate Beitzah daf 18 we say that according to all it is forbidden to immerse an impure vessel even on Yom Tov; and Rabbah gave the reason — a decree lest he carry it four cubits in the public domain, and a decree of Yom Tov on account of Shabbat; and Rav Yosef said the reason — lest he squeeze, and we decree vessels that are not fit for squeezing on account of vessels fit for squeezing; and Rav Beivai gave the reason — lest he delay the vessel from immersing until Yom Tov and Shabbat when he is free from work, and he will come to mishap that he forgets and uses terumah before immersion; and Rava gave the reason — it appears like fixing a vessel. Rif brought only Rav Yosef's reason and Rav Beivai's reason; and automatically according to these two reasons it is permitted to immerse a new vessel — for regarding lest he squeeze there is nothing to decree, for you do not find a new vessel that requires immersion and squeezing would apply to it that there would be reason to decree on account of vessels fit for squeezing; and also regarding lest he delay — one cannot say specifically in an impure vessel if he delays it and uses terumah and eats it he creates a prohibition by eating impure terumah, unlike a new vessel — even if he uses it without immersion he does not forbid the food, and even though he creates a prohibition in using it without immersion, nevertheless for this they did not decree lest he delay, since in any case he does not eat prohibition; and also this prohibition is only rabbinic even according to those who hold immersion is from the Torah; therefore for Rif it is permitted to immerse a new vessel. But Rosh ruled like Rabbah's and Rava's reasons, and this reason applies also to a new vessel bought from outside the Land that requires immersion, and even glass vessels whose immersion according to all is only rabbinic — likewise these forbidders forbid thus; and many Acharonim, even though we rule that a vessel that became impure in rabbinic impurity — permitted to immerse it on Shabbat and Yom Tov — that case is different for it is not so called fixing, since even in its impurity it was fit to use impure things; but new vessels that were not fit to use at all before immersion — it is properly fixing. And behold from the Mechaber's view it appears he agrees with the first view; and see in Peri Megadim that the difficulty on the Mechaber remains — why does he rule leniently even for a metal vessel? For behold in Yoreh De'ah siman 120 s.k. 14 he rules that it is d'oraisa; and according to Rambam in chapter 23 of Shabbos who forbids immersion of vessels that became impure in d'oraisa impurity on account of fixing — here too it would be forbidden on account of fixing; and in my humble opinion it is possible he holds that here fixing does not apply, since even if he uses the vessel without immersion the food is not forbidden b'dieved according to all, and this resembles what is stated there that they immerse from rim to rim; and Rambam explained in the commentary on the mishnah: a vessel they immersed for mundane use — we rule it is forbidden to use it for terumah until they immerse it for terumah — nevertheless permitted to immerse this immersion on Yom Tov, since essentially the vessel is pure and it is not to remove impurity but for added purity; therefore it is not called fixing here; and likewise immersion in new vessels is not to remove impurity, for it did not become impure, but only because it left the domain outside the Land and came to the sanctity of Israel the Torah required immersion, as is in Yerushalmi that on its account from the Torah there is no prohibition to use it; and even the rabbis who forbade using it until they immerse it — nevertheless the food is not forbidden according to all; therefore this is not called fixing, as it appears to my humble opinion, to explain Shulchan Aruch's view because of the severity of the difficulty; and thereby the difficulty that Yeshuot Shlomo raised on Beit Yosef, as brought in his name in Taz, is resolved; and even though Beit Yosef's words are still somewhat difficult — nevertheless Shulchan Aruch's words at least are not rejected from halacha; and it is possible when he wrote his words in Beit Yosef he held that immersion of all vessels is rabbinic:

(ב) He fills it with water — see in Mishna Berurah what is written that he does not bless — this is from Magen Avraham; and it is difficult to me — if so, with a niddah woman we rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 197 that she may immerse on Shabbat, and the reason is because it appears like drawing water, and not one of the poskim says specifically without a blessing afterward; I found this difficulty in Yeshuot Yaakov and he answered because our custom is to bless after immersion — therefore at the time of immersion it is not noticeable; but this is strained — it works for Rama in Yoreh De'ah siman 200, but for the Mechaber there who rules anonymously like the majority of Rishonim and almost all of them who hold that in every immersion one blesses before immersion except immersion of a convert because the man is not yet visible — he should have mentioned in siman 197 in his Beit Yosef that she should not bless; and also from the outset of the law it is difficult to me how they permitted filling water on this basis — who is preferable to Er who? We learned in Terumot chapter 1 that one does not separate terumah because he cannot bless; but one may answer strained: it deals when he needs it on Shabbat and has no other; but the first difficulty is hard. And were it not that I fear, I would say it is permitted to bless at the time he submerges the vessel in the water and immerses it; and likewise regarding a niddah that she may immerse even with a blessing beforehand per the Mechaber, and the reason is since essentially the immersion is not noticeable at all because it appears he wants to fill water, or with a niddah because it appears like drawing water — the rabbis did not decree on such immersion; and automatically it is permitted to bless as well; unlike all those the gemara challenges in Beitzah 18 "it works with good water," etc. — "it works in summer days," etc. — see there — in all those the recognition is in the immersion itself that it does not appear like drawing water, unlike here. I reconsidered with Hashem's help that Magen Avraham's law depends on great pillars — Tosafos hold like Magen Avraham's view and Rambam holds like our view; namely Tosafos wrote in Yevamos 46b s.v. "fixing of a man," etc., and even though regarding impurity it is permitted to immerse because a person appears like drawing water — here it is different, for he requires two Torah scholars to stand by him at the time of immersion to inform him of the commandments, etc. — behold they hold like Magen Avraham that where it is noticeable for any reason we do not say it appears like drawing water; and in Yoreh De'ah siman 267 Beit Yosef brought Rambam's language who wrote since the matter requires a court one does not immerse him on Shabbat; and Beit Yosef wrote there that therefore Rambam did not say on account of appearing like fixing of a man as is in the gemara, because he holds that according to what is concluded in Beitzah that a person appears like drawing water — this reason is set aside; and even though they require two Torah scholars to stand by him, etc. — nevertheless since it appears like drawing water it does not avail; and therefore according to our view stated above; and the Mechaber copied there in our pure table Rambam's language — behold he holds accordingly; and thereby our difficulty above is resolved regarding why Beit Yosef did not distinguish regarding a niddah for blessing.
