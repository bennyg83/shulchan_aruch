(א) Permitted to immerse a new vessel. Beit Yosef wrote thus in the name of Rif, not as he wrote only the reason of Rav Yosef and Rav Beivai and did not bring the words of Rava and Rabbah; and Rosh wrote that per the reasons Rif brought it is permitted to immerse new vessels on Yom Tov; therefore it appears the reason of Rabbah is primary regarding Rav Yosef, and so we find this reason regarding shofar and lulav in Megillah, and likewise Rava is later; and from these two reasons it is forbidden to immerse new vessels on Yom Tov, end of his words. And I wondered at the holy mouth of Rosh in this — what he wrote that the halacha follows Rabbah regarding Rav Yosef is not a difficulty, for here Rav Beivai also argues on Rabbah; and what he further wrote that Rabbah's reason applies regarding shofar and lulav, etc. — I wondered at this, for there it is permitted on Yom Tov except that on Shabbat they decreed thus; but here that they forbade also on Yom Tov as is in the gemara, and Rabbah holds a decree of Yom Tov on account of Shabbat in this — Rav Beivai does not hold thus, for he has no decree at all, as Rav HaMaggid wrote there; and further it appears to me that also from Rava there is no proof of prohibition in new vessels, for on the contrary since we permit in the baraita there the offspring of impurity to immerse on Shabbat since it is rabbinic, and new vessels too their immersion is rabbinic, as Rav HaMaggid wrote in chapter 4 of Yom Tov in the name of Rambam and chapter 7 of Maggid Mishneh — if so immersion of new vessels is also permitted; and even though Rav HaMaggid wrote there that one may also say the reverse, that immersion of new vessels is worse than impure vessels and even one who is not concerned for Rava's reason on account of fixing — this is in vessels that were also fit to become impure before, unlike new vessels that were not fit at all initially — nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition on account of fixing; nevertheless there is no proof from Rava's reason who said fixing — how does he hold regarding new vessels? For it is possible he concedes there it is permitted since they are rabbinic; it turns out Rif's words are clear that he did not bring this of Rava, for according to him there is reason for prohibition and permission in new vessels; and regarding those reasons of Rav Yosef and Rav Beivai it is certainly for permission since he does not hold at all concern of fixing; and it appears to me that Rambam also holds thus for permission in new vessels, since he wrote in chapter 23 of Shabbos that one may not immerse impure vessels on Shabbat because it is fixing a vessel, etc. — since he specified impure vessels it implies full impurity from av hatumah, which would be full fixing, unlike new vessels whose immersion is rabbinic — this is included in what he wrote in chapter 4 of Yom Tov: a vessel that became impure in impure liquids that are offspring of impurity — they immerse it because it is pure from the Torah, etc.; and if so likewise new vessels are permitted; and even though Rav HaMaggid was uncertain regarding new vessels according to Rambam, as he wrote in chapter 4 of Yom Tov — nevertheless the matters appear to lean toward permission, since he did not specify in chapter 23 of Shabbos vessels generally which would include everything but relied on what he wrote in chapter 4 of Yom Tov that specifically impure vessels that are d'oraisa are forbidden; it turns out Rambam holds properly also as he wrote Rava's reason on account of fixing and did not bring it because one should not err to say that also in new vessels it is so; and according to Rosh's view by necessity he did not bring it at all and relied on what he wrote in Shabbos — it turns out he wrote properly according to Beit Yosef that for Rif and Rambam immersion of new vessels on Yom Tov is permitted. And I saw in Rav Rashal that he wrote in chapter 2 of Yom Tov seif 9: the Karo wrote that since for Rif and Rambam it appears permitted to immerse new vessels on Yom Tov — thus we practice; and he erred a great error, for Rambam wrote in chapter 23 of Shabbos that one does not immerse vessels because it is fixing; and accordingly new vessels too are forbidden; and likewise one may wonder at Rav HaMaggid who appears uncertain in Rambam's words whether he holds the reason of fixing and did not examine chapter 23 of Shabbos, end of his words. And I saw a great man, but we do not see any refutation at all; and all the more he should not have been lenient in honor of our great rabbi Beit Yosef and Rav HaMaggid to attribute to them such an error that they did not examine chapter 23 of Shabbos — and this deficiency one should not attribute except to the small, but not to the great ones of Israel like them, for they certainly saw and saw, and Rav HaMaggid himself wrote in chapter 23 that his intent was to write the law of immersion of new vessels in chapter 4 of Yom Tov; rather certainly he wrote properly for Rambam, since he specified impure vessels it implies d'oraisa in this — Rav HaMaggid was uncertain regarding new vessels but was not uncertain whether Rambam holds the reason of fixing — he certainly holds as he wrote in Shabbos regarding impure vessels; rather in new vessels Rav HaMaggid was uncertain whether we follow d'oraisa or where fixing applies more; and if so even according to one who is not concerned for fixing in impurity he concedes in this since it was not fit at all initially; and all the more for one who holds the reason of fixing; and Beit Yosef is satisfied in this direction that specifically d'oraisa, but new vessels that are rabbinic their law is like offspring of impurity that is permitted on Yom Tov — all this appears clear and simple in my humble opinion after examination:
