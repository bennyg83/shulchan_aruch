(א) And Taz did not expand; and according to his view in the previous letter that certainly by measure, even without "such and such," etc., also forbidden; and the distinction that exists between money and measure — apparently the buyer does not violate, for he says and informs how much he needs and the seller automatically counts for him; but the measure of wet and dry goods where he is precise in measuring — on the seller is the prohibition. And Magen Avraham letter 2: in seif 4 the permit for the seller that he not limit himself precisely; and in seif 4 the permit for the buyer; and if so certainly when he does not limit himself and adds a little or lacks a little — and likewise Levush who boiled above and does not wait until this finishes — the permit for the seller. But the implication of Taz's language implies the prohibition of measuring also on the buyer, for what is the reason they did not cite Tosafos Beitzah 29b s.v. shelo the sum of a measure — for even without a sum it is forbidden for the buyer to mention measure; and one may say for the sum of a purchase that he informs in a dinar of nuts that is known how much he wants and how much he needs. And see Levush implies he distinguishes between the measure of wet goods that requires intent and measurement — forbidden to mention the name of a measure; unlike the measure of dry goods, nuts and the like — see there:

(ב) And know that what we say in the gemara there 29a "a vessel designated for measure" — Rashi and Rambam disagree: Rashi explains "designated" that it was made with precision and estimation that it was not made with precision; and according to this, even though he retracted in his composition chapter 4 from 129 — both of them — and ruled like Rava, nevertheless he did not retract from his explanation of "designated" in a case where perhaps he has not yet measured; behold he measures with it within the category that he should not mention the name of a measure — requires study:

(ג) And from the words of Or Zarua I learned that what Rabbenu Chananel wrote "and likewise the sum of a measure" — that it is a good textual reading in the baraita, and that is when he reminds him "such and such vessels I took — give me more such vessels" and it will total such and such — forbidden; but if not so — permitted; and that is to say "fill this vessel for me" ten times — permitted; but to mention the name of a measure explicitly — "give a quart" or "half" — forbidden without a sum. And in what is written in the mishnah a man says "fill a vessel" but by measure without a sum forbidden; and that which the mishnah does not explain "but not by measure" — to pour from the seller's vessels to the buyer, for he speaks of that; but by measure forbidden to mention the name of a measure even if he goes with the vessel — forbidden. And possibly therefore the mishnah says "to his fellow" and Rambam "to the shopkeeper"; and nevertheless it implies that the mishnah's version is "to the shopkeeper," for an ordinary person presumably does not give his vessels to bring to his fellow, rather he has them only for his own use; and by a shopkeeper you find that he has many vessels, or the reverse — for the shopkeeper does not ask for his vessels to bring the buyer to his house because many buyers will come and he pours for each; but an ordinary person may ask him for the need of the hour:

(ד) And with this is resolved in Yerushalmi the version in mishnah 1 "he says to the shopkeeper" (and that is Rambam's textual reading as Maggid Mishneh wrote); and in mishnah 2 "he says to his fellow"; and in the Bavli the matter is reversed. And there, in the gemara R' Huna: whether lacking or extra — this means even though he pours into his vessels, permitted in them lacking and extra that the buyer says and the seller does so. And there R' Eliezer said to Ze'eira bar Chama: "fill this vessel for me" — and tomorrow they do not contain it; Rosh Hashana explains, and as is written in seif 4. And the Gaon the commentator (s.v. davar) explained "but not by measure" that he should not fill it actually — if so R' Yehuda forbade "he should not fill it" if lacking and extra — why; and why not as is written; and one may say also what is written "and tomorrow they contain" — this too is forbidden; why not according to its plain meaning for permission, as Maggid Mishneh wrote and as the Mechaber wrote in seif 2 here; and that which is difficult — a matter that is forbidden to say "tomorrow I will do such-and-such object" forbidden on Shabbat — one may say for the need of Shabbat permitted, as they rule regarding pledging charity simanim 306 and 307; requires study:
