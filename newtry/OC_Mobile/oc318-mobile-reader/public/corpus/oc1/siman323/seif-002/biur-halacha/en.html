Permitted to say, etc. — in order to understand well the Mechaber's and Rama's words I will precede here with a brief introduction, namely that there are three matters — they are six, meaning money and measure and count, which are sum of money and sum of measure and sum of count; and behold with count the law is already decided simply that count is permitted, such as to say give me nuts by count; but to make with him a calculation included in this, such as how many nuts you already have in my hand fifty, give me fifty more and it will be together one hundred — this is sum of count and forbidden, for it resembles buying and selling; and with measure likewise sum of measure and sum of money — that is, that he should say how much money I already owe such and such, give me such and such more and I will owe you together sixty-five such and such; and likewise regarding measure how many measures I owe you already such and such, give me another such measure and I will owe you in total such and such — this is forbidden to all, for it resembles buying and selling; but with measure or with money themselves — that is, that he should say give me nuts for one single coin or for two single coins — the Mechaber's view is that it is forbidden, for it is clear in the gemara that it is forbidden to fix prices; and likewise forbidden to mention the name of measure; and Rama brought in Hagahah the view of Hagahot Ashiri who holds that with money in this manner, as we say that for a mere sign he mentioned a single coin to inform him how many nuts he needs, and it is only like saying to him a count of nuts, and it turns out that the language of sale in money was not mentioned here but only giving how much he will give; and what was mentioned in the gemara that it is forbidden to fix prices — this is in a thing whose purchase is not known, that the thing has no fixed amount and definite pricing of money, for then we cannot say that what he mentioned to him in money is only to inform him how much he needs and not to fix prices for him for the purchase, since he does not know how much he will give him for this amount of money; and Rama wrote in Darkei Moshe that according to Hagahot Ashiri's view we can say likewise regarding measure in this manner — they only forbade mentioning to him the name of measure except in a thing whose measure is not known — that is, when he says give me a measure of nuts or another thing they are not accustomed to measure, for then we cannot say his intention is only for a sign to inform him how much he needs, since he does not know how much the measure holds and does not know if he will need more or not; and therefore what he mentioned to him regarding measure is that he should measure him specifically; but in wine and beer and the like that they are accustomed to measure — his intention is only to inform him how much he needs and not that he should measure him specifically; and therefore it is permitted; and accordingly he wrote here in Hagahah "and likewise with sum of measure," etc.:
