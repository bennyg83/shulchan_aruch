8) There, gloss: Some are lenient and permit saying, etc. Rosh writes in Responsa, klal 22: Regarding the practice in your place of measuring wine on Shabbos, before Shabbos they write that one person deposited a certain amount of money and another person a different amount. The next day, when the wine is measured, the wine seller takes a needle and makes a number of holes corresponding to the measures he gives. Know that they commit two wrongs. First, they measure, whereas the Mishnah teaches that one may say to another, 'Fill this utensil for me,' but not with a measure. The Gemara explains that this means not with a utensil designated for measuring. Second, it is forbidden to look at the writing, for the Mishnah teaches that one may count his guests and portions orally but not from writing. The Gemara asks why, and Rav Bibi answers that it is a decree lest he erase, while Abaye says lest he read ordinary documents. These are his words. Beis Yosef, Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2, and Magen Avraham, paragraph 5, cite him. Olas Shabbos writes there that according to this, written tokens called zeichen in German, which record how much beverage one must give, are likewise forbidden. Lead tokens are also forbidden, because the author writes above in siman 307, seif 12, that even engraved writing on a tablet or ledger may not be read. These are his words. Magen Avraham there writes that the responsum of Rosh implies that one may likewise not write sums of money upon slips and, when a person takes wine, place the slip where that person's name is written. Reading such slips is forbidden as reading ordinary documents. The custom is therefore to use a seed as a marker or some other object. Charity collectors may possibly use slips, as stated in siman 307, but it is nevertheless preferable, whenever possible, to use a seed, as the early authorities practiced. These are his words. This means placing one seed for every measure where the person's name is written. Machatzis HaShekel.
