(37) That he drank in it, etc. — wine; even though without rinsing forbidden to use it, nevertheless it is not called fixing through this, for the body of the vessel itself is permitted and wine forbids it, and when removing it he does not fix it but like removing something repulsive from it. And if he knows he will not use it today — forbidden to rinse it, as explained in seif 6 in Mishna Berurah; and nevertheless permitted to move the cup even without rinsing, and even if there are crumbs of bread in the cup in which the non-Jew soaked wine — nevertheless permitted to move the cup and it is not a base for something forbidden, for the crumbs are nullified relative to the cup. Terumat HaDeshen wrote one should not nullify a prohibition on Shabbat, for there is no greater fixing than this; and even though in Yoreh De'ah siman 99 seif 6 in the gloss he holds even on a weekday one should not nullify any prohibition by adding to it until sixty, even if it is a rabbinic prohibition — nevertheless a practical difference emerges regarding dry in dry where one became mixed in two, that on a weekday one may add to it until sixty in order to cook it afterward, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 109; and on Shabbat and Yom Tov forbidden because it is called fixing; and where one nullifies the prohibition of a rabbinic prohibition — permitted, as in siman 507 seif 2. And permitted to estimate sixty even if it became mixed on erev Shabbat and he did not know until Shabbat. And specifically to estimate in mind and with the sight of his eyes — behold to measure not, even though measuring for a mitzvah is permitted, here it appears like fixing by doing an action with his hands [Peri Megadim]. Magen Avraham wrote dry in dry where one became mixed in two — permitted to throw one of them away to permit the others, since it is only a mere stringency of law that whatever is not eaten by one person is permitted — therefore this is not like fixing. A non-kosher knife — if he wants to cut bread with it and the like when cold that requires rinsing — it implies rubbing well is not mere rinsing; one should not do this on Shabbat, for it is like fixing [Peri Megadim, see there further]:
