3) The synagogue treasurers have a blank ledger in which the names of individual congregants are written, each on a separate page. When a person purchases the honor of reciting Kaddish or something similar on Shabbos, the treasurer ordinarily places on that person's page pieces of paper marked with large dots. He counts out dots equal to the price of the Kaddish and accumulates the pieces there. The next day he removes and counts them, thereby learning the amount to record. He does the same with the names of assistants so that he will not forget and cause the synagogue a loss. See Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1, who discusses this at length and writes that one should not protest, because they have grounds upon which to rely since these are calculations for a mitzvah. When there are not many purchases and it is possible to pay attention and remember who pledged and how much, however, it is preferable to refrain; see there. See below, paragraph 8.
