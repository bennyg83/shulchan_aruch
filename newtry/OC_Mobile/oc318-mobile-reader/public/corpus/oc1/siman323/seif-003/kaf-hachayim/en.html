(3) Treasurers of the study halls who have a ledger with sections in which they write the individual names of those who donate kiddush, etc. — each name on a page separately; and when on Shabbat someone buys saying kaddish and the like — such is the treasurer's way: he places on the page where that individual's name is written pieces of cardboard on which large dots are marked and tallies the number of dots until the sum of the kaddish purchase price and stores them inside; and the next day he takes out by number and knows thereby how much the kaddish costs and writes it in its place; and likewise he does with the names of those who assist him so he not forget and the study hall lose. See Pischei Teshuvah letter 1 what he expanded on this and wrote one should not protest against them, for they have whom to rely on on account that accounting for a mitzvah is considered [important]; but when there are not many sales and it is possible to give them notice and remember who donated and how much — better they refrain from this; see there.
