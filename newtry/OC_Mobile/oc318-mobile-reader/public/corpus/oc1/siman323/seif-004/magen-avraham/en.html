(א) And likewise with a sum of money. But from the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch it implies it is forbidden to mention money at all, for there it writes money and not sum of measure and not sum of number — it implies money is forbidden even without a sum and accounting:

(ב) That its purchase value is not known. Meaning it has no fixed amount and lacks determination of price — then forbidden to say give me for such-and-such plain coins or two, even if he takes what they give him, for then one cannot say his intent is only to say what he needs, since he does not know what he will give him. Darkei Moshe wrote, and it appears to me likewise regarding measure — they only forbade mentioning the name of a measure in a thing whose measure is not known; but in a thing whose measure is known, only informing the shopkeeper how much he needs — permitted, end of his words. Meaning specifically when he says money, give me a measure of walnuts or a thing people are not accustomed to measure — then he does not know how much the measure holds and does not know if he will need more or not, and if so his intent is not to say how much he needs; but regarding wages and the like his intent is to inform how much he needs and it is permitted [and Eliyah Rabbah did not look at Darkei Moshe]. And this is the permit for the buyer and the permit for the seller, so he holds. And it requires further study why they do not practice so now. And Shulchan Gadol wrote it is permitted to say fill this vessel for me until the handles, see there. What people practice — they write on erev Shabbat so-and-so left such-and-such money and so-and-so such-and-such, and on Shabbat when giving wine the buyer takes a needle and makes holes per the number of wine he buys — forbidden, for forbidden to look at that writing in which the sum of money is written, as in siman 307 seif 12 (Beit Yosef in name of Rosh general rule 22). And it implies forbidden also to write on notes a sum of money, and when buying wine he places the note where the man's name is written — forbidden to read in those notes, for it is lay documents; therefore they were accustomed to take a pit for a sign or other thing. And charity collectors perhaps are permitted with notes as in siman 307; nevertheless good to be stringent where possible with a pit, and so the ancients practiced. And it implies if he wrote only the man's name it is permitted to read and make holes, for it is not called lay documents but a document in which is written which matter; nevertheless it requires further study on Rosh, for it implies in chapter HaBoneh that even a mere mark is forbidden to make for a sign, and likewise Rambam chapter 1 — one who marks marks on a wall is liable; and if so all the more forbidden to make a hole for a sign.
