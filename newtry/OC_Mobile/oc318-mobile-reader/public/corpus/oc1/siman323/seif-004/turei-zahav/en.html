(א) And not a sum of measure, etc. — in the first chapter of Beitzah we say: a person goes to a shopkeeper and says to him give me twenty eggs, etc., provided he does not mention to him a sum of measure; Rashba says provided he does not mention to him a sum of purchase. Tosafos wrote — Rashi's explanation: sum of measure — a kav or two kavin; sum of purchase — that he should not say give me nuts for one dinar or two. And it is difficult — it is obvious that it is forbidden; and further why did he say sum of measure — he should have said measure and no more; therefore it appears to us like the version of R' Chananel who reads sum of count, and the explanation is that he should not say give me ten nuts and you already gave me thirty, and if so I owe you forty altogether; but Rashba says provided he does not mention to him a sum of purchase — that he should not say to him give me nuts for six coins and you already gave me such and such, and if so I owe from the whole twelve coins; but sum of count is permitted, end of his words. And their words are astonishing and impossible to settle, for at first they challenged that it is obvious it is forbidden when he mentions one dinar or two, and afterward they wrote specifically if he says give me nuts for six coins and you already gave me, etc. — which implies that without "and you already gave" there is no prohibition; and in Beit Asher it is written this matter in different language: sum of purchase — that he should not say to him take such and such to complete a dinar; and this is the essence; and likewise in chapter "one who asks" (Shabbos daf 150) Tosafos themselves wrote thus: provided he does not mention to him a sum of purchase — if they would give one hundred nuts for a dinar and he already took ninety, he should not say give me twenty nuts for I will owe you ten nuts, which appears that he is completing the purchase; rather he should say simply give me twenty nuts, end of his words. And if so Tosafos' own words contradict themselves in their place and not in their place; and it appears to me there is a scribal error in their words in chapter Beitzah, and it must read give me nuts and you already gave me such and such, etc.; and so I saw that Maharam of Lublin emended thus. And Rif wrote sum of purchase — such as if he owes him money he should not say give me in money and you have money owed me from me, behold you have two coins in my hand; and Ran wondered at him — if he said give me in money it is obvious it is forbidden even without making a calculation, for this is buying and selling; and he corrected Rif's words that he did not specifically say in money but rather the explanation is in order that you should have two coins in your hand, etc.; and Rashal in chapter Beitzah wrote that Ran's explanation is strained and the words are as their plain meaning; and so Hagahot Ashiri wrote in chapter "one who traps" that sum of purchase was not except if at first he owed a single coin and says give now in a single coin and behold you have in my hand two single coins — this is sum of purchase and forbidden; and that which we say above that one does not fix prices on Yom Tov — this is specifically in a thing whose amount of sale is not known, end of his words. And Rashal wrote proof for this from Tosafos in chapter Beitzah that I brought above; and I wonder that he did not examine at the beginning of their words that they hold it is obvious for prohibition like Ran's challenge, and as we wrote that by necessity there must be a scribal error there; and according to this Hagahah Rama wrote to justify the custom that they now mention sums of money on Shabbat since they are not coming to calculation; and one must answer according to Hagahot Ashiri that even though with measure it is forbidden even without a sum — that is, calculation — as in the complete sugya above where when he measures and shows the limitation he immediately creates a prohibition, unlike here where even though he said give me nuts in a single coin for a mere sign he said thus how much he needs to give, and it is like saying a count of nuts since the amount of their sale is known, and it turns out that the language of sale was not mentioned here but only giving how much he will give; and according to this he gives, and this does not apply to measure where the measuring itself is a transgression, as stated, to answer according to Hagahot Ashiri; but we already mentioned Tosafos' view that it is obvious that it is forbidden when mentioning money, and with measure it is obvious forbidden to all, as we explained in the commentary on the mishnah; also Rif only read sum of count and sum of purchase and did not mention sum of measure, for the halacha is already that even with measure without calculation it is forbidden; and for this reason R' Chananel also did not read measure in the baraita, and Rashi who reads sum of measure does not explain it regarding this calculation that he should not mention measure at all; and so it is clear from Tur's words who wrote here like Shulchan Aruch in this seif that he should not mention to him the name of money nor sum of measure nor sum of count to say such and such you have in your hand give me such and such, etc. — it implies that it refers to count that he will give him per the count; but if regarding measure — what is such and such, language of doubling; rather he should have said such you have and such you will give me; and all the more in Shulchan Aruch's language that concludes here to say that you have fifty nuts, etc. — behold he began with sum of measure and left it and explains with sum of count in fifty nuts and did not explain with sum of measure thus; rather certainly sum of measure means only mentioning a kav as Rashi, as we wrote in the commentary in hints: that he should not mention to him the name of money nor sum of a kav or two kavin nor sum of count to say such you have in my hand give me two and it will be such, end of his words — behold it is clear as we wrote. And if so one may wonder greatly at Rama who joined to this sum of measure to say that likewise there is no prohibition except when making a calculation — and this did not enter the mind of any early or later authority to contradict what is explicit in mishnah and gemara; also Rashal who brought this leniency of Hagahot Ashiri regarding purchase and count — nevertheless regarding measure he wrote that in this generation one does not protest when they mention the name measure; by necessity it appears to me that although it is impossible to protest this thing to not mention the name measure since they have already become entrenched in this, and Rama is not sufficient to justify the custom even according to his view, for we already see every Shabbat the bad custom that he makes calculation with him how much he already bought and how much now, and likewise they are very meticulous in measuring that he should not lack even the slightest amount; and if so there is no proof from this custom; and better they should be unwitting, etc.; but there is obligation in the matter not to mention measure at all but fill this vessel for me as the mishnah implies; and regarding mentioning money, in this too there is a bad custom that they calculate how much he bought and how much he buys now, and the righteous should not walk in it to mention money at all, for this is the essence of buying and selling, as Tosafos and Rosh and Ran and Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote; Hagahot Ashiri alone in these two matters; and this suffices to save from punishment one who mentions the name of money; but one should not do so, for it is possible to do with permission that he can say by way of count give me such and such and they will not come to calculation until tomorrow; and in a thing that has no count, such as meat, he can say give me a piece and tomorrow we will weigh them, as is written in seif 2, as it appears to my humble opinion:
