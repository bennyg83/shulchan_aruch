(א) To say to a shopkeeper, etc. — specifically an Israelite shopkeeper; but a non-Jew — forbidden to buy eggs from him lest they were born today and are muktzeh; and likewise pomegranates lest they were picked today from the attached [below siman 517 seif 1]:

(ב) The name of money — such as saying give me for such-and-such plain coins; and all the more when he mentions to him a sum of money — meaning he makes an accounting with him that includes also what he already owes him — obviously forbidden:

(ג) And not sum of measure — see in Biur HaGra who wrote it is a textual error and it should read and not the name of a measure — meaning he mentions to him give me such-and-such a measure; and all the more sum of measure — meaning he makes an accounting with him that includes from how many measures he already took from him:

(ד) And not sum of number, etc. — for in such a manner it is the way of commerce and sale; but number stam was already explained above that it is permitted:

(ה) And likewise with sum of money, etc. — he disagrees with the Mechaber, for he holds stam the name of money is forbidden to mention; and he holds that just as with number — specifically sum of number is forbidden and number stam is permitted to mention — so is the law regarding money; and it should have said "and some say," except that such is his way sometimes:

(ו) And likewise with sum of measure, etc. — meaning specifically sum of measure when he makes an accounting with him that includes also from his measures he already owes him; but the name of a measure stam Rama holds permitted to mention, for his mentioning measure is only as a mere sign to inform him how much he needs and not that they measure precisely; and that which the gemara forbade mentioning the name of a measure — is in a thing people are not accustomed to measure with a measure, for then one does not say he mentioned it only as a mere sign; and the Mechaber above who did not distinguish in this holds in every manner forbidden also in this:

(ז) But otherwise permitted — and this leniency is only for the buyer who does not transgress a prohibition in what he mentions the name of a measure, as explained; and the permit for the seller that it be permitted to measure for him and give in the buyer's vessels is explained above in seif 1 that he may add or diminish a little:

(ח) And even though forbidden, etc. — he returns to the beginning of the gloss and says that which we rule forbidden to mention money at all is in a thing whose purchase value is not known — meaning that thing has no fixed amount and determination of price; then we cannot say what he mentioned to him in plain coins is only to inform him how much he needs and not to fix a price for the purchase, for he does not know how much he will give him for this measure of money; but a thing whose purchase value is known — we say he mentioned it only as a mere sign to inform him how much he needs; and it turns out sale in money was not mentioned here but mere giving of how much he will give:

(ט) Already spread, etc. — and behold several Acharonim hold one should be careful by law not to mention the name of money, and all the more the name of a measure in every manner, as the Mechaber's view; nevertheless one should not protest the hand of one who practices leniently, for he has on whom to rely; but it is fitting and very proper to be stringent in a matter not to mention the name of a measure or money, and especially since he can do so permissibly — he can say fill this vessel for me and tomorrow we will measure it; and in a matter where number applies he can say by way of number give me such-and-such, and he will not mention the name of money at all and they will not come to accounting until tomorrow. What people practice — they write on erev Shabbat so-and-so left such-and-such money and so-and-so such-and-such, and on Shabbat when giving wine the seller takes a needle and makes holes in paper per the number of wine the buyer takes — forbidden to do so, for forbidden to look at that writing in which the sum of money was written, for it is lay documents. Also making holes in paper is likewise not proper l'chatchila; and likewise forbidden to write on notes on erev Shabbat a sum of money, and on Shabbat when buying wine the seller places the note where the man's name is already written by him — forbidden to read in those notes, as explained; therefore they were accustomed to give a pit or other thing on the man's name for a sign. And charity collectors perhaps are permitted with notes to give on the man's name for remembrance, since it is heavenly property, as in siman 307; nevertheless good to be stringent where possible with a pit and the like, and so the ancients practiced [Acharonim]:
