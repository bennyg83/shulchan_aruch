(49) Of water. Although water is fit to be drunk cold and it would therefore seem that its law should be like fruit that is eaten raw, discussed above in seif 4, water is nevertheless not as pleasant cold as fruit is raw. Heating improves it greatly, and therefore the decree against stoking the coals applies.
(50) Into the oven. In our ovens, it is permitted when the oven is swept out and covered with ash, or when its opening is sealed with clay, as explained above.
(51) On erev Shabbos close to nightfall. This is forbidden unless there is enough time for the water to become at least somewhat warm before bein hashmashos, even if it will not become fully heated. It is likewise forbidden to place water in pots inside ovens on erev Shabbos close to nightfall. Although his intention is only to prevent the pot from cracking from the oven's heat, and one might ask why he would stoke the coals, nevertheless it is customary to use that water to rinse utensils. We are therefore concerned that he may stoke the oven so that the water will become hot. He must consequently be careful to place it early enough that it becomes somewhat warm while it is still day.
