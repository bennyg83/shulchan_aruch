Its surface. Before nightfall, for once this has occurred we are no longer concerned that he may stoke the coals.
That adhere to the oven. The later authorities agree that it is likewise sufficient if the surface facing the fire forms a crust. It is possible that the Mechaber also holds this way. He mentions the surface adhering to the oven because that surface forms a crust more readily. There is no distinction whether the bread is baked on the oven floor or in baking pans. See the Rama's gloss below. See also Biur HaGra on the adjacent gloss, who explains that this applies specifically to thin bread. If the bread is thick, it does not bake quickly, and both the lower surface facing the oven and the upper surface facing the oven cavity must form a crust.
And strands are not drawn out. Even if the bread is thick, this measure is sufficient according to all opinions, because when strands are no longer drawn from it, its surfaces have certainly formed a proper crust both above and below.
This is called the formation of a crust. All this applies when the stove or oven is neither swept out nor covered with ash. On a stove, or in our ovens, when it is swept out and covered with ash, or when the oven opening is sealed with clay, it is permitted in every case to place the bread in the oven close to sunset.
Above and below. 'Above' means the side facing the oven cavity, and 'below' means the side facing the bottom of the vessel in which it is baked. See Magen Avraham and Taz, who write that according to the Mechaber's view above, it is sufficient if a crust forms in only one place. Biur HaGra, however, writes that because it is thick and does not bake quickly, all opinions require a crust both above and below. Dagul Merevavah also writes that all opinions agree in this case, but for a different reason: the upper and lower portions of a pasty are not a single mass, since the filling between them separates them. They are therefore like two cakes, each of which requires its surface to form a crust. Chemed Moshe writes likewise.
And what is inside it must be cooked. This refers to the meat, fish, or similar food used to fill the pasty. Otherwise, even if its surfaces formed a crust, we are concerned that he may stoke the coals so that the filling will cook.
If intentionally, it is forbidden. It is forbidden to remove it from the oven even with an unusual method, even if he has nothing to eat. Even if a non-Jew removed it without his knowledge, he may not eat it.
B'chedi she'ya'asu. This waiting period applies to eating it. Removing it from the oven is plainly permitted immediately on Motzei Shabbos.
Unintentionally, etc. Why do we not impose a penalty even when it was done unintentionally, as we do in se'if 8, where no distinction is made between an unintentional and intentional act? Since he has no other bread to eat, and it is impossible to fulfill the mitzvah of three meals without bread, it is permitted. There is no comparable obligation to eat a cooked dish on Shabbos. Indeed, if he has nothing else at all to eat except the cooked dish that he unintentionally left on the fire, he may eat it, since it is forbidden to fast on Shabbos, as stated in siman 288. It follows that the entire leniency is because he has no other bread to eat. Therefore, even in our ovens, concerning which it is explained below in se'if 7 that removing bread from them does not involve the prohibition of removing bread from an oven, he may nevertheless take from the bread only what is needed for three meals.
Food for three meals. If one loaf that he removed contains enough for three meals, he may not remove any more, even though he will not have a whole loaf and lechem mishneh for each meal. This is the ruling of the Magen Avraham.
He should remove it with a baker's peel. Removing bread from an oven is not a complete shevus; it is forbidden only because it resembles a weekday activity. Therefore, where it is impossible to use an unusual method and it is needed for Shabbos, the Sages permitted it.
He removes it in the usual manner. Eliyah Rabbah writes that one should be stringent in accordance with the poskim who maintain that even when the bread was placed in the oven permissibly, it must be removed specifically with an unusual method.
But if it is sealed with clay. This refers back to the beginning of the se'if: it is permitted to place the bread in the oven, because we do not decree lest he break the clay seal and stoke the coals. See se'if 7 regarding how he should conduct himself when removing it.
To bake it. For even without stoking, it will bake properly. See Magen Avraham, who writes that regarding our ovens, since we rule below in se'if 7 that one may remove bread from them on Shabbos and eat it even when he already has enough for three Shabbos meals, we are concerned that he may change his mind and decide to eat from the bread in the oven on Shabbos, and will stoke the coals so that it bakes more quickly.
