In the gloss to se'if 5: 'until its surfaces form a crust.' Note: The words of the Even HaOzer here are astonishing, for the passage in the Gemara, Menachot, at the beginning of the chapter 'Shtei HaLechem,' escaped his notice; there the mold was attached to the oven and heated together with it. His inference from the expression 'with bread' is also unfounded, for in the versions of the Semak and Terumat HaDeshen, and likewise in the Semag cited by the Beit Yosef, I did not find this word at all. Moreover, everything he wrote on this matter is merely an ingenious conjecture. The truth is that in a filled pastry, when the two breads are divided into an upper and a lower layer, they are regarded as two separate loaves. One must therefore assess the lower one by its lower surface and the upper one by its upper surface, because the lower surface of the upper layer is not baked by the heat of the fire, but by the heat of the filling within it. This applies even to an ordinary cake containing almonds or sesame seeds, which separate between the upper and lower layers, as written below in siman 460. The Chemed Moshe likewise wrote this here. Nevertheless, when it is baked in a mold the matter requires consideration, because what the Magen Avraham wrote in se'if katan 16 concerns a pot standing directly on the fire, whereas when it is baked in a swept oven the matter requires consideration.
