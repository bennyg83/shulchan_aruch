Its attached surfaces. It seems to me that he holds that this is more easily considered crusted, for he holds that it is sufficient if it crusts on whichever side. Therefore, regarding a cake too, it is enough if the surface facing the fire crusts, for there it is obvious that the side facing the fire crusts first. See the Remazim, and you will find that the Tur follows the view of the first Tanna, as Tosafot wrote in explaining Rashi, that it is enough for whichever side to crust. See there and examine it carefully. This is also the view of the Rambam.
Above and below. This requires study, for this is not like what the Beit Yosef wrote, since he holds that crusting on one side is sufficient, as explained above. If so, he should have written, and some say. It is obvious that in all this, even if he placed them raw close to nightfall it is forbidden, for they cook quickly, as written at the beginning of this siman. It is also obvious that on a stove that was swept or covered with ashes, everything is permitted, as written in siman 253. A cake or types of dough baked in a pot have the law of bread, as is implied in the Bach, provided they are not filled.
Unintentionally, etc. The reason we do not penalize even an unintentional violation as written in seif 8 is that here, since he has nothing to eat, it is permitted, as the Beit Yosef wrote. If so, even in our ovens, more than three meals is forbidden. The Bach wrote that the reason is that it is uncommon for one to place bread in an oven close to nightfall, for hot bread is not so good. According to this, in our ovens, more than three meals would be permitted. But it seems to me that one should be stringent, for the Kesef Mishneh wrote in the name of the Ramach that wherever it was baked in violation, it is forbidden in every case. If so, it is enough for us to be lenient for the food of three meals.

Plastered with clay. According to the Beit Yosef it is forbidden, as written in seif 1, for here the reasoning that it will harden does not apply.
Only for after Shabbos. Meaning that he has food for three meals, and therefore it is forbidden for him to remove it from the oven on Shabbos, except for after Shabbos, and therefore it is permitted there. If so, in our ovens it is forbidden even though he is baking it for after Shabbos, just as we forbid this with all cooked foods and do not distinguish between whether they are wanted for Shabbos or for after Shabbos. We decree lest he change his mind and eat it on Shabbos and stoke. The same applies to bread. See siman 257, seif 1 in the gloss.
