One may not place bread in an oven close to nightfall unless there is time for a crust {i.e., that a crust and skin form on the surface of the loaf from the fire} to form on its surface that adheres to the oven; nor a cake upon coals unless there is time for its surface facing the fire to form a crust. {Rama: And whenever one breaks it and the strands do not stretch, that is called the forming of a crust (Hagahos Maimoni, third chapter); and a pashtida or a pladin requires that its surface crust above and below, and that what is inside it be cooked to the [degree of] ma'achal ben Derosai (Beis Yosef in the name of Semag, Sefer HaTerumah, and Hagahos Maimoni).} And if he placed them close to nightfall and their surfaces did not crust: if intentionally, it is forbidden until after Shabbos the time it would take to make them; and if unintentionally, if he has nothing [else] to eat, he is permitted to remove from it food for three meals, and he may tell others who have nothing to eat, "Come and remove for yourselves food for three meals." And when he removes it, he should not remove it with a baker's peel {i.e., a shovel or winnowing-fork; the Aramaic for the peel is pala in the vernacular}, but rather with a knife or the like, so that he not act in the manner he acts on a weekday; and if it is impossible to remove it with a deviation, he may remove it with the peel. And if he placed it [with enough time] for its surface to crust, since he did not perform a prohibition, and it is a Shabbos need, he may remove it in his usual manner; but [if] not for the needs of the day, it is forbidden even with a deviation. {Rama: And all of this is with an oven that is not plastered with clay (Hagahos Ashiri); but if it is plastered with clay, or he is not baking for the needs of Shabbos but only for after Shabbos, so that he has time to bake it, it is permitted, for we do not decree in such a case lest he stir the coals (Kol Bo).}
