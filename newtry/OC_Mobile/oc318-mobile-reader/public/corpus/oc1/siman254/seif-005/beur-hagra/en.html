Se'if 5: Its surfaces, etc., but not, etc. The Rambam wrote this regarding both bread and a cake, referring to the surfaces attached to the oven or to the fire. The Beit Yosef distinguished between them and held that the Rambam ruled like R' Eliezer, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote, but he gained nothing by this distinction, as explained by the Taz. It appears to me that the Rambam ruled like the first Tanna, as is evident from his commentary to the Mishnah, for it is not his practice to rule like a lone opinion merely because the Gemara raised a question according to that opinion, as I have written in many places. Rather, he was troubled why the Gemara did not raise the question according to the first Tanna. He therefore explained unlike Rashi and Tosafos, that this is neither necessarily a leniency nor necessarily a stringency, for sometimes one surface forms a crust first and sometimes the other. The Gemara did not ask according to the first Tanna because according to him one surface certainly suffices, since he stated without qualification that its surfaces should form a crust. This is why the Rambam wrote, 'in the oven or by the fire.' The Tur wrote likewise.
Whenever it can be broken. See Menachos 78b, and Tosafos there, s.v. 'he breaks it.'
And a pie. So wrote the Semag and Sefer HaTerumah. They rule like R' Eliezer and explain, in accordance with Tosafos, that his statement is lenient, as is explicit in their words there regarding the blessing of Hamotzi. Only with regard to a pie does R' Eliezer agree, as Tosafos wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: 'R' Eliezer agrees regarding the showbread,' etc. That is because it is thick and does not bake quickly, as stated in the second chapter of Pesachim and at the end of the second chapter of Beitza; the same applies here. It should not have been necessary to mention specifically the lower surface, but they adopted the wording of Tosafos in the name of the Yerushalmi, for here even the upper surface must crust. There is no difficulty regarding the Rama, for perhaps he wished to satisfy Rashi's explanation as well. This applies specifically to a pie, whereas for other items the lower surface suffices either way. See Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 7 of Hilchos Berachos, and Hagahos Ashri, chapter 6 of Berachos, s.v. 'we say regarding the portion,' and above siman 167, se'if 1. It appears more likely that they too agree that the upper surface may sometimes crust first, but ordinarily the lower one crusts first. Therefore, here, where a full crust is required, both surfaces are needed, whereas elsewhere, where we rule like R' Eliezer, there is no concern. In this way their words accord properly with the Rambam's ruling that one surface suffices in every case, although they differ in explanation, as stated above.
And it should bake. This follows the approach of the first opinion in siman 233, se'if 1.
If he placed it, etc., if. See there, folio 117b: 'Our Rabbis taught: If he forgot,' which implies that if he acted intentionally it is forbidden.
Until. See there, folio 18b.
If there is no, etc., for they do not have, etc. Ran. This is derived from the statement on folio 3b, 'If one attached bread,' etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'they permitted,' and 'although,' etc. The Rif did not accept their resolution, as the Ran explains. On the contrary, the chapter Kol Kisvei proves that there is no rabbinic prohibition at all, but only a weekday-like activity, as stated there, 'whatever can be done,' etc.; otherwise the Gemara's question there would not begin. The Rif therefore answers that there they permitted it because he had nothing to eat in honor of Shabbos, whereas here the bread has not yet baked and is not yet fit to eat. See the Rif and Ran there.
And if it is impossible. For he said, 'whatever can be done,' etc., because one must do as much as possible, as stated on folio 128b. They also said there, 'and if he cannot do so with her hair,' etc.
If he placed it with sufficient time. This follows from the statement there on folio 37b that if its surfaces have crusted it is permitted, and from the end of the second chapter, 'and he waits long enough for it to roast,' etc. Necessarily this refers to a Shabbos need, and necessarily he removes it from the oven on Shabbos, just as with the shofar blasts, as the Ran wrote. Furthermore, from the statement there, 'if he forgot,' it follows that intentional placement is forbidden.
And a Shabbos need, etc. If it is not needed for Shabbos, it resembles blowing the shofar, as stated there. Regarding blowing the shofar, we say in Rosh Hashana 33a, 'but one may train,' etc. This is not difficult, for, as Tosafos explain there, a minor who has reached the age of education may do so because there is some need of the day, whereas one who has not reached that age may not. The Rif wrote similarly at the beginning of Shabbos, as explained in Milchamos, from the fact that removing bread was permitted as stated above. The Rif answered that it was permitted only for a Shabbos need, unlike the case involving Rav Bibi, etc. See Milchamos and the Ran at the beginning of Shabbos.
Even with an unusual manner. Since the Gemara there resorted to a strained answer and did not permit it through an unusual manner, it follows that it is forbidden even with an unusual manner. The unusual manner is required only as an added restriction where the act itself is permitted, as stated, 'as much as possible.'
All this applies in an oven. This follows his approach in se'if 1, according to Rashi's explanation there.
Or if he is not a baker. Although regarding other matters there is no distinction, for we say this only where the food is completely raw, since he necessarily removes his attention from it, otherwise the Gemara should have distinguished between partially cooked and uncooked food. Here, however, since he is baking for Motzei Shabbos, he necessarily has bread for Shabbos and may not remove any at all. Therefore he removes his attention from it, and it is comparable to completely raw food. Kol Bo.
