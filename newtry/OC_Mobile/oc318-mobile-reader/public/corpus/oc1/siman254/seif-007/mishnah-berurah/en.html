In which there is no act of removing bread from the oven. The melacha of removing bread from an oven applies only to their ovens, in which they would attach the bread to the oven walls. In our ovens, where the bread rests on the oven floor, removing bread from the oven does not apply.
To remove more. The Magen Avraham writes that this refers to a case in which he placed the bread in the oven permissibly. In their ovens, he was forbidden to remove any bread that he did not need for Shabbos, as explained above in se'if 5, even if he did so in an unusual manner. In our ovens, however, where the melacha of removing bread from an oven does not apply, he may remove all the bread, even if it is far more than the amount needed for the three Shabbos meals. All this applies when he intends to eat at least some of it on Shabbos. Otherwise, it is forbidden to remove it for his weekday needs. Even ordinary moving is forbidden when done for a weekday need, as the poskim write that one may not bring wine on Shabbos for Motzei Shabbos. It appears that this is permitted through a non-Jew. The Eliyah Rabbah is lenient and permits him to remove it himself from our ovens where there would otherwise be a loss because the bread would spoil. The Magen Avraham, however, is not satisfied with this, because he can eat some of the bread on Shabbos.
