Se'if katan 7: Upon the coals, near the coals, etc. This is from the Mordechai. Although Magen Avraham wrote in siman 253, in the name of the Mordechai, se'if katan 18, that if the pot is not buried in the coals but merely rests upon them, it may be removed even by a Jew, that applies b'dieved. The Mishnah's statement, however, that one may not roast unless, etc., concerns l'chatchila. Therefore it must mean near the coals. According to the Rambam, etc., meaning that the Mordechai may explain 'meat on coals' like Rashi. But according to the Rambam, who explains it as meat directly upon the coals, as stated above and ruled in se'if 2, the Mordechai's question remains: how may one remove the meat from the coals?
