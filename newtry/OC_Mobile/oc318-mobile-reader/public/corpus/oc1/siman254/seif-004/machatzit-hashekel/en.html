Se'if katan 4: It was cooked, etc. A case in which it is forbidden is, for example, goat meat in an oven whose mouth was not sealed.
According to those who forbid at the beginning of siman 253, namely the Beit Yosef's view there that the Mishnah teaches the law of leaving food on the fire, it is forbidden to leave food even when it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai unless the stove is grufa u'ketumah. This is the Rif's stringent view. Since the case here is goat meat in an unsealed oven, there is no reason to be more lenient here than in other cases of leaving food on the fire. Therefore, according to the Rif, even if it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai it is forbidden when the stove is not grufa u'ketumah. According to the lenient view in siman 253, namely Rashi's view that the Mishnah teaches the law of returning food but leaving it is permitted once it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai even when the stove is not grufa u'ketumah, the same applies here and it is permitted.
However, when it is in a pot, namely pot-roasted meat, even kid meat is forbidden according to the Rif, who is among those who forbid at the beginning of siman 253. The Beit Yosef here rules like the Rambam, that kid meat is permitted because a small amount of heat suffices and it does not require stoking. When it is pot-roasted, however, it requires greater heat. Therefore, according to the Beit Yosef, who wrote at the beginning of siman 253 that leaving food is forbidden on a stove that is not grufa u'ketumah even after it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, the same applies to kid meat in a pot. If it is completely raw in the pot, however, Magen Avraham writes at the beginning of this siman that it is permitted, as explained above in siman 253.
