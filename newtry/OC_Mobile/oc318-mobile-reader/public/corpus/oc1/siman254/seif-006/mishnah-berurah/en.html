Even intentionally. For one might have thought, "Feed the wicked man and let him die"; it therefore teaches us that it is permitted. All the more so, when he placed it in the oven unintentionally, it is permitted in order that he not come to incur the prohibition requiring a chatas.
For him. That is to say, specifically he himself is permitted to remove it from the oven, but others are forbidden to remove it for him, whether he placed it there intentionally or unintentionally. This applies even if the person who placed the bread in the oven is not present so that they can inform him, because we do not tell a person, "Commit a minor sin so that your fellow will not come to violate a severe prohibition," when his fellow acted wrongfully by placing it in the oven. Even in our ovens, in which removing bread from the oven is not itself prohibited, the Eliyah Rabbah inclines to rule that another person may not remove it, because the dough is muktzeh and may not be moved. All this concerns baking. Regarding cooking, however, it is obvious that if someone forgot, or transgressed and placed a pot near the fire, another person must also remove it so that his fellow will not come to violate a prohibition. Removing the pot involves no prohibition at all, and consequently there is a mitzvah here to separate him from a prohibition.
To remove it from the oven. This is permitted even with a baker's peel. Nevertheless, if it is possible to do so in an unusual manner, such as with a knife or the like, he should do so. This applies only if doing so will not require him to delay, lest because of the delay a crust form on its surface and he thereby become liable.
