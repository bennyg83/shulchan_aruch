But the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 9 wrote that unintentional is a greater novelty, for since he remembered, there is no liability for a chatas, since we require the beginning and end to be unintentional. Nevertheless, he is permitted to remove it before he comes to a chatas prohibition. See there. There is no need for what the Bach was forced to say. Nevertheless, if it is possible to do it with a change, he should do so, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 5. This applies specifically to him, but others are forbidden to remove it, as Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote, for a person is not told, commit a minor sin so that your fellow will not commit a severe prohibition, since the prohibition was not caused by him, and his fellow also acted negligently by sticking it in the oven. This is from the Bach and Tosafot. In truth, the main matter depends on whether he was negligent. See Eruvin 32b in Tosafot, and see the end of siman 306.

The book Kikayon DeYonah asked why they did not uphold their words in the face of kares, as with an uncircumcised one, sprinkling, and the knife, etc. This is not a difficulty, for there the Gemara lists many cases in which they did not uphold their words in the face of kares, as stated in Pesachim 92.
