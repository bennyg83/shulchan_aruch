Beans and lupines. The same applies to other kinds of legumes, vegetables, and soft foods. None of these resemble meat, regarding which we rule above at the beginning of siman 253 that one may place it shortly before nightfall while it is raw, because it will certainly not cook in time for the evening meal, while for the following day's meal it will cook even without stoking. These foods, however, cook easily, and we are concerned that he may stoke the coals for the evening meal.
Into the oven. This means even when it has been swept out and covered with ash, as written in siman 253, se'if 1. Nevertheless, our ovens are comparable to a stove, for which it is permitted when swept out and covered with ash. If the oven opening is sealed with clay, it is permitted under all circumstances, as the Rama writes above in his gloss to se'if 1.
When they have not been swept out. This refers only to a stove. The plural wording is used in reference to stoves generally. It cannot refer to a kufach, since it is explained in siman 253 that when it was heated with wood, sweeping it out and covering the coals with ash are ineffective.
Until the time of b'chedi she'ya'asu has elapsed. This is so that he not benefit from having violated a rabbinic prohibition. This is from Tosefes Shabbos. See above, siman 253, se'if 1, in the Rama's gloss, that if it was cooked before Shabbos to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai, that is sufficient.
