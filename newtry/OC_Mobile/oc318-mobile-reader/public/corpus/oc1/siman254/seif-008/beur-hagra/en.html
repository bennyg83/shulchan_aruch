Even unintentionally. This is stated in the Yerushalmi and cited by the Semag; see there and see the Beit Yosef. The Yerushalmi, chapter 3, halacha 1, states: 'It was taught: A woman may not fill a pot with beans and lupines, etc. If she placed them there, after Shabbos they are forbidden until the time of b'chedi she'ya'asu has elapsed. R' Acha said in the name of R' Yasa: This applies when it was done unintentionally, in accordance with R' Yehuda. R' Mana said: R' Yasa spoke correctly, for it was taught: One who plants on Shabbos unintentionally may leave it in place; intentionally, he must uproot it. During Shemittah, whether unintentionally or intentionally, he must uproot it. R' Yehuda reverses the rulings: One who plants on Shabbos, whether unintentionally or intentionally, must uproot it. During Shemittah, if unintentionally he may leave it, and if intentionally he must uproot it. Why? Because the benefit of Shabbos remains upon it. Here, however, once you say that he must wait after Shabbos for the time of b'chedi she'ya'asu, it is as though he derived no benefit from Shabbos at all, etc.' See there. We rule like R' Yehuda regarding one who cooks, as explained there concerning R' Yehuda's ruling about cooking, and the same applies to planting. See what I wrote above in siman 301, and what I wrote there. The reason stated above, that these matters do not require, etc., does not accord with Rashi, and he was compelled to explain it that way because the case is comparable to water. Rashi took care to avoid this difficulty there.
