However. See Taz. The first opinion is that of the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 3 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachos 13, 15, and 16; the second opinion in the Rama's gloss is that of the Tur. Both, meaning the Rambam and the Tur, rule like Rav Yirmiyah of Difti according to the latter version in Shabbos 18b: a mature goat whose oven opening is sealed is permitted, and a kid whose oven opening is not sealed is likewise permitted. The Gemara challenges Rav Ashi, who permits even a mature goat when the opening is not sealed, from the Mishnah: one may not roast meat, an onion, or an egg unless there is enough time before nightfall for them to roast to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. The Gemara answers that the Mishnah concerns meat placed directly on coals. The Rambam explains the permissive cases of a kid without sealing and a mature goat with sealing as follows: a kid is tender, cut into pieces, and roasted. Needless to say, when it is near the fire, it is permitted whether raw or somewhat cooked, because stoking would harm it by scorching it. This is unlike food in a pot, where even kid meat cooked to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai is forbidden according to the Rambam and Rif in siman 253, se'if 1. There the pot intervenes, whereas here the meat is beside the fire and stoking would harm it; therefore it is permitted when raw or somewhat cooked, and all the more so at the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. With a mature goat, and certainly beef, it does not scorch readily. Near the fire its law is more stringent than food in a pot: in a pot raw meat is permitted, whereas near the fire raw meat is forbidden, and certainly meat at the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai is forbidden according to their view in siman 253, se'if 1. If the opening is sealed with clay, it is permitted, because the draft is harmful even to a mature goat and to beef. Furthermore, since it is sealed so thoroughly, he is concerned about the great loss of heat and will not uncover it. They do not accept Rashi's reason there that the seal itself serves as a reminder. A sealed kettle in siman 252 may be more tightly closed, or perhaps it is not normally stirred as one would stoke coals; see Magen Avraham, letter 5. The Gemara's answer of meat placed on coals, according to Rav Ashi, means that it is placed directly on the fire, as opposed to merely near the fire. The same applies when it actually touches coals at the side; see Magen Avraham, letters 7 and 12, where I explained this. Meat on coals therefore has both a leniency and a stringency compared with meat near the fire. Its leniency is that once it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai it is permitted, because further stoking would scorch it, and it is therefore like mitzamek v'ra lo, as the Rambam explicitly writes there in halacha 16; see there. Near the fire, by contrast, meat at ma'achal ben Derusai is forbidden according to the first opinion in siman 253, se'if 1. Its stringency is that when it lies directly on the fire, actually touching it, even a kid whose opening is sealed is forbidden; see Magen Avraham, letter 9. Since he placed it directly on the fire, he is not concerned about slight scorching. Near the fire, however, a kid without sealing and a mature goat with sealing are permitted because the draft harms them, whereas directly on the fire the draft does not harm them, or for the other explanations given. Although Rav Ashi gives the answer of meat on coals, we should not multiply disputes, and the same distinction applies according to Rav Yirmiyah, both for permission and prohibition. Moreover, on folio 20a the Gemara asks how much roasting is required and answers: ma'achal ben Derusai. According to the first opinion in siman 253, food at ma'achal ben Derusai is forbidden; rather, here the food is lying on the coals, and is therefore permitted at ma'achal ben Derusai or even less. Yet when it is completely raw, even kid meat is forbidden, for the Mishnah states simply 'meat': since he placed it on the coals, he is not concerned that it may scorch. The second opinion in the Rama's gloss is that of Rashi and the Tur. We rule like Rav Yirmiyah according to the latter version: a kid without sealing is permitted because the oven is at least covered with a board, so the draft harms it; and a mature goat with sealing is permitted because the seal serves as a reminder. He will not do both acts. Meat directly on coals, where there is no covering at all, is forbidden even according to Rav Ashi. According to this, there is no distinction at all between meat near the fire and meat directly on it.
Now. According to the Rama's second opinion here, there are apparently several leniencies. First, roasted food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai may be left near the fire, even though it is forbidden according to the first opinion in siman 253, se'if 1, as the Taz here and Magen Avraham, letter 6, write. Second, food in a pot whose opening is sealed is permitted, as Magen Avraham, letter 5, writes, because the seal serves as a reminder, following Rashi's explanation. Third, meat placed directly on the fire is permitted when sealed, according to the Rama, as Magen Avraham, letter 9, writes. Fourth, regarding a kettle in siman 252, according to Magen Avraham, letter 1, and the second answer of Tosafos in Shabbos 18b, s.v. 'in a kettle,' the Rama permits it even when it is merely sealed. According to the first opinion, whose reason is that the draft is harmful when the opening is sealed with clay, all these cases are forbidden. The second opinion does, however, have one stringency: whenever the oven is open and uncovered, even a kid cut into pieces is forbidden, because it does not accept the rationale that the meat will scorch. It is permitted specifically when covered, because the draft harms kid meat even without sealing. If so, it is certainly very difficult to understand why the Rama writes, 'and some are stringent,' when this opinion contains mostly leniencies. The Taz, of blessed memory, mentions only this leniency: according to the first opinion here, food at ma'achal ben Derusai near the fire is forbidden, while according to the Rama roasted food at ma'achal ben Derusai near the fire is permitted. The other leniencies are less certain. The Rama's wording, 'and directly upon the fire everything is forbidden,' is also difficult. According to the Mechaber in se'if 2, even kid meat is forbidden there, perhaps even when sealed. He should instead have said, 'and near the fire everything is forbidden.' In Nechalas Tzvi I saw that he sensed this difficulty and explained the words as though they read 'near the fire.' Know also that even though the Tur in siman 253 and the Rama in se'if 1 rule like the second opinion there, that leaving food is permitted once it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, this refers to food at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'ra lo, or to food fully cooked that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo. Food merely at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo remains forbidden. We already inferred this there from the Mechaber's wording, 'or mitzamek v'yafeh lo,' which reflects the approach of the Baal HaMaor; see there. Accordingly, the Taz was correct to write that the Rama requires further study, because the latter opinion indeed introduces the leniency of permitting food at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo near the fire. The Tur likewise writes in siman 253 that food at ma'achal ben Derusai, and food fully cooked that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo, may be left, implying that food merely at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo is forbidden. Nevertheless, here he writes without qualification that meat, etc., may not be roasted unless it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, and he does not limit this to meat placed directly on the fire. This implies that even near the fire, meat at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo is permitted. I am aware of the dispute whether an egg is mitzamek v'yafeh lo. See the Baal HaMaor and Milchamos in the first chapter and in Perek Kirah, where the Ramban brings proof that eggs are mitzamek v'yafeh lo from Shabbos 38a, which implies that contracted eggs improve; see there. The Taz therefore correctly writes that the Rama requires further study, because the second opinion in siman 253, se'if 1, contains the leniency just described. He alludes to this by writing that ma'achal ben Derusai alone is permitted, meaning even when the food is mitzamek v'yafeh lo. In our novellae to Perek Kirah we wrote at length that the Baal HaMaor holds eggs are mitzamek v'ra lo, and that the passage concerning eggs contracted in wine may mean that the wine improves, not the eggs themselves; see there. The Tosefes Shabbos and Chemed Moshe strongly criticized the Taz, of blessed memory, but according to what I have written his words remain sound and established.
In the sacred words. The Levush writes as follows: 'When it is sealed with clay, we are not concerned that he will perform two acts; and some say that when it is sealed everything is permitted, as we stated,' etc. He omits the word 'stringent' and, under the first opinion, writes that we are not concerned that he will perform two acts. To my humble understanding, these words are exceedingly difficult. According to the first opinion, the reason for permitting an opening sealed with clay is that the draft is harmful, as the Mechaber writes, and so the Rambam explicitly states in chapter 3 of Hilchos Shabbos, halacha 13. This requires further study.
I will speak once more. The Mishnah in Shabbos 19b states: one may not roast meat, an onion, or an egg unless there is enough time before nightfall for them to roast, meaning to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. It lists these three items deliberately. Meat teaches that although completely raw meat may be left in a pot, raw meat may not be left for roasting. An onion, although edible raw, is treated like food that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai; onion is different, as Magen Avraham explains in letter 13. An egg teaches that although it is mitzamek v'yafeh lo, it may nevertheless be left for roasting near the fire or directly on the fire. Although food at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo is forbidden when cooked in a pot, it is permitted when roasted. Know that although the Tur in siman 253, and likewise the Shulchan Aruch as indicated by Be'er HaGolah under the second opinion, cite Rashi and Ri, one may say that they did not rule entirely like them. Rather, they agree only concerning food at ma'achal ben Derusai that is mitzamek v'ra lo, or food fully cooked that is mitzamek v'yafeh lo, as I have written, following the approach of the Baal HaMaor, of blessed memory. With God's help, I will explain further in siman 259 concerning the laws of leaving food and insulation. The references to Semag, Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah may relate to meat placed directly on coals, but they rule like Rav Ashi. Since the paths of wisdom are hidden from me, I was compelled to elaborate to this extent.
I now return. The earlier words of the Levush can perhaps be reconciled. He may hold that even according to the Rambam and the Mechaber, sealing with clay permits the food because it serves as a reminder, like a sealed kettle. See Lechem Mishneh to chapter 3 of Hilchos Shabbos, halacha 11, and the Ran regarding the normal practice of stirring a kettle; see there. The Rambam likewise rules without qualification regarding a sealed kettle. When the Mechaber gives the reason that the oven will cool, he states an even stronger reason: this applies even when the oven is full of coals. One might otherwise say that opening and closing the oven involves kindling and extinguishing; see Terumas HaDeshen, responsum 99, Responsa Rama 102, and Magen Avraham 253:18. In such a case sealing alone would not suffice on the ground that a person will not perform two acts, as the Levush writes. We would still be concerned that he might forget and open it, thereby kindling. The Mechaber therefore says that he will not open it because the oven would cool. He understands this similarly to the first version, that the seal would harden, etc. The Levush wrote this because of the opinion introduced as 'some are stringent.' It follows well that these leniencies apply even according to the Mechaber. As for the leniency once the food reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, he holds that the Rama already decided in favor of the second opinion in siman 253. Thus the words of the Levush are clear as a garment, and his clothing is white as snow. With God's help, this will be explained further in simanim 257 and 259.
