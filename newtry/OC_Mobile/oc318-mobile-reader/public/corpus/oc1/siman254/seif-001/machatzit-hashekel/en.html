Se'if katan 1: Which is near, etc. To understand the words of the Shulchan Aruch, one must first set out the relevant statements of the Gemara and poskim. Shabbos 18b states: 'Now that the master said that anything harmed by air will not be uncovered, there is no concern that one may stoke the coals.' Therefore kid meat is permitted even when the mouth of the oven was not sealed with clay. With regard to a goat, if the mouth of the oven was sealed, all agree it is permitted. If it was not sealed, Rav Yirmiyah of Difti forbids it and R' Ashi permits it. The Gemara challenges R' Ashi from the Mishnah: one may not roast meat, an onion, or an egg unless they will roast before nightfall to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. According to R' Ashi, however, it should be permitted in every case, so why did the Mishnah require that they roast before nightfall? The Gemara answers that the Mishnah concerns meat placed directly on coals, where R' Ashi agrees that it is forbidden. Tosafos and the Rosh write in the name of Halachos Gedolos that although completely raw food may be placed shortly before nightfall, as stated above in siman 253, the present discussion concerns roasting, for which even completely raw meat is forbidden. Some poskim rule like R' Ashi, but most rule like Rav Yirmiyah of Difti, and the Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly. Rashi and those who follow his approach disagree with the Rambam and those who follow his approach regarding the explanation of the passage. The Rambam holds that the permission based on the food being harmed by air was not needed for kid meat, because its flesh is tender and a small amount of heat suffices. Therefore we are not concerned that he may stoke, since stoking would scorch it from excessive heat.
The permission based on its being harmed by air was needed for goat meat, whose flesh is tough. Since there is concern that one may stoke, Rav Yirmiyah of Difti permits it only when the mouth of the oven is sealed with clay. Then there is no concern that he will stoke, for he would first have to uncover the oven. Before opening it, the heat is concentrated, while opening it allows air to enter, cools the oven, and causes the meat to toughen, as the Shulchan Aruch explains. According to this, kid meat is permitted even when the oven is completely open and uncovered, because there is no concern that he will stoke: it does not require stoking, and if he does stoke it will scorch and be ruined. According to R' Ashi, who treats kid and goat meat alike and permits both in every case, the Mishnah's rule that one may not roast meat, etc., is explained as meat placed directly upon the coals. The Rambam explains that it lies upon the coals themselves. In such a case even kid meat is forbidden lest he stoke. Although stoking would scorch it, we see that he prefers rapid roasting despite scorching, for he placed it directly on the coals, where it will certainly scorch. This is the Rambam's approach and that of the Beit Yosef here. Rashi, however, holds that the statement that anything harmed by air will not be uncovered refers to the permission of kid meat. For this reason we are not concerned that he may stoke, because air harms it. Accordingly, kid meat too is permitted only when the oven is closed, for if it is open, why should we not be concerned that he may stoke when air is entering in any event? Goat meat, however, is not harmed by air and is forbidden even when the oven is closed, because we are concerned that he may uncover the oven and stoke. Therefore, according to Rav Yirmiyah of Difti, the mouth of the oven must be sealed with clay, which serves as a reminder and prevents him from stoking. According to this, when the Gemara answers R' Ashi that the Mishnah concerns meat on coals, it means that it is roasted outside the oven upon coals. This is not necessarily limited to direct placement on the coals; the same applies when it is near them. Tosafos write that the same law applies if the meat is inside the oven but the oven is completely uncovered, for although R' Ashi does not require a clay seal for goat meat, he at least requires a cover. It cannot be better than kid meat, which requires a cover; otherwise the reason that air harms it would be irrelevant. The Rama in his gloss rules like Rashi.
I now return to the words of Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1. The phrase 'near the fire' implies that pot-roasted meat is permitted, for 'near the fire' implies without a pot. Pot-roasting does not have the law of roasting and is permitted when the meat is completely raw. In a pot it does not cook quickly, and when completely raw stoking is of no use, as explained above at the beginning of siman 253.
