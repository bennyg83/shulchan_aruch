Se'if 1: Although. Tosafos there, s.v. hai. Halachos Gedolos writes that the discussion concerns roasting, as is evident from the challenge from the Mishnah, 'One may not roast,' and likewise below, 'One may lower,' etc. Therefore, mature goat meat in an oven whose opening is not sealed is forbidden even when completely raw.
That is near the fire. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch is precise. It means to exclude meat placed directly on coals, which is forbidden in all circumstances, as stated in se'if 2. When the Tur writes 'near the fire,' he means inside an oven, as Rashi explains.
Because it cooks quickly. The Tosafos cited above.
With beef. Rambam; and so it is stated in the Yerushalmi, chapter 1, halacha 5.
Or mature goat meat. This is explained according to R' Yirmiyah and the latter version, since the Gemara says there, 'This verse,' etc. Likewise, there on 20a it states, 'But the Master said: a kid,' etc. This is the view of the Rif and Tosafos there. Sefer HaTerumah and Mordechai, however, explain according to the Geonim, who rule like Rav Ashi. Nevertheless, Sefer HaTerumah writes that uncut meat is forbidden. Even Rav Ashi agrees, according to their text, which reads: 'But did Rav Ashi not say, whether sealed or,' etc.; 'there it is cut,' etc. They do not have the word 'kid' in the text. They explain that uncut meat does not cook quickly, and therefore Rav Ashi agrees that it is forbidden. This refutes the proof of Tosafos. Their second proof, from the earlier answer that the draft is harmful to it, etc., is likewise no proof, as explained above, because that question was raised only in a case where stoking is unnecessary, specifically cut meat.
And poultry. Sefer HaTerumah and Hagahos Maimoniyos.
For they are. There, 20a.
And there is no distinction. Meaning, in a case where it is permitted, it is permitted even when the meat has begun to cook but has not yet reached ma'achal ben Derusai. This follows from the Gemara's challenge from the Mishnah, 'One may not roast.'
And all this is while the cavity. This follows from what is said regarding the Pesach offering: there it is not cut into pieces, and the verse states, 'with its head upon its legs.'
And some are stringent. This follows Rashi's approach, that a kid is permitted because the draft is harmful to it. Accordingly, the prohibition stated in se'if 2 regarding meat on coals is not limited to meat directly on the coals. The same applies near the coals, and even inside an oven whenever it is open, because the rationale that the draft is harmful does not apply when the oven is open regardless. This is the meaning of, 'and on the fire, where it is exposed': whenever it is exposed, it is called meat on coals. The wording is intended to prevent one from understanding the Gemara's phrase, 'meat on coals,' as referring only to meat directly on the fire. The Tur likewise writes, 'near the fire.' Tosafos there, s.v. hasam, proves this as well. They ask: according to Rav Ashi, who permits it, how can the Mishnah state, 'One may not place bread,' etc., and, 'One may lower,' etc., since otherwise it would be forbidden? They answer that the Mishnah concerns an open oven, which is like meat on coals. The Shulchan Aruch does not write that it is permitted once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, as stated in se'if 2, because it rules according to the first opinion in siman 253, se'if 1; without that concern it is permitted.
