1) [Seif 1] Although it is permitted to leave raw meat, this applies specifically in a pot that does not cook quickly. He has diverted his attention from it until the next day, and we are not concerned that he might stoke the coals. Tur and Levush.
2) There: But regarding roasted meat near the fire, etc. This implies that pot-roasted meat is permitted. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
3) There: It is forbidden to place it shortly before nightfall, etc. This applies unless it was fully roasted while it was still daytime and continued cooking causes it to contract and deteriorate, as stated in siman 253, seif 1. Beis Yosef writes the same. In seif 2 it is sufficient that the food be cooked to the degree of maachal ben Derusai specifically because it was placed directly upon coals. Here, however, where it is not directly upon coals but near the fire, maachal ben Derusai does not suffice, as stated below in paragraph 5. Magen Avraham, paragraph 10, and Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2, write the same. This applies according to the first opinion recorded in siman 253, seif 1. According to the opinion introduced there as 'some say,' however, anything cooked to the degree of maachal ben Derusai may be left in place. Accordingly, roasted meat near the fire may likewise be placed there shortly before nightfall once it is cooked to the degree of maachal ben Derusai, as Beis Yosef writes; see there.
4) There: Because it will cook quickly, etc. The same applies to all types of legumes that cook quickly. Rabbeinu Yerucham; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2. See below, seif 8.
5) There: But the meat of a kid or a fowl, etc., is permitted. This means that it is permitted even if it has not reached the degree of maachal ben Derusai, since we are not concerned that he might stoke the coals. Seif 2 forbids food before it reaches the degree of maachal ben Derusai, apparently even kid meat, because there it is directly upon the fire. We see that he is unconcerned that the meat might be singed, since he places it upon the coals, and therefore we are concerned there that he might stoke them to hasten the roasting. After it reaches the degree of maachal ben Derusai, however, even beef is permitted, because it is fit to eat and he would not stoke the coals and thereby ruin it. Near the fire there is a distinction between kid meat and other meat, as stated in the Shulchan Aruch. This is Rambam's view according to Maggid Mishneh's explanation. Tur, however, does not distinguish between food directly upon the fire and food near the fire, but only between cooking and roasting. Roasted food, whether near or directly upon the fire, entails both a leniency and a stringency relative to cooked food. The stringency is that before it reaches the degree of maachal ben Derusai it is forbidden according to all opinions, lest one stoke the coals, even if it was entirely raw. The leniency is that, according to Rif and Rambam in siman 253, cooked food that improves through continued contraction may not be left in place, whereas here it is permitted as soon as it reaches the degree of maachal ben Derusai. Taz, paragraph 1; see there. A practical difference is that according to Tur, there is no distinction between beef and kid meat, whether directly upon or near the fire. In either case, if it has been cooked to the degree of maachal ben Derusai, it may be left near the fire shortly before nightfall, while if it has not, it is forbidden. According to the Shulchan Aruch, when the meat is directly upon the fire there is no distinction, as stated in seif 2: whenever it has reached the degree of maachal ben Derusai, it may be left there. When it is near the fire, however, beef may not be left there unless it is fully cooked and continued cooking causes it to contract and deteriorate, as stated above in paragraph 3, while kid meat may be left there even before it reaches the degree of maachal ben Derusai. This follows the first opinion in siman 253, seif 1. According to the opinion introduced there as 'some say,' even beef may be left near the fire shortly before nightfall once it reaches the degree of maachal ben Derusai, as stated above in paragraph 3; see there. We already wrote there in paragraph 31 that the custom is to be lenient like the opinion introduced as 'some say'; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, writes the same in this siman.
5) There: That are cut into pieces, etc. If they are whole, however, they have the same law as beef and goat meat, as stated below in paragraph 15. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, writes the same.
6) There: Because if he stokes the coals, etc. For this reason it is permitted even if the mouth of the oven is entirely uncovered, because if he stokes the coals the meat will be singed. Beis Yosef; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
7) There: Because if he comes to open it, etc. Since it is sealed, its heat is intense; when he opens it, the wind blows inside and cools the oven. Beis Yosef; Olas Shabbos there.
8) There: And the meat will become tough, etc. This implies that if it is in a pot, sealing the oven with clay does not help, since the meat will not become tough. See the gloss below. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3. This means that according to the gloss, which follows Rashi's approach, if it is sealed with clay it is permitted even in a pot. Machatzis HaShekel. See below, paragraph 13.
9) There: And the meat will become tough, etc. Raw squash, since exposure to the wind is harmful to it, has the same law as kid meat. Shabbos 18b, cited by Beis Yosef. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 9, however, writes that this applies specifically according to Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who maintain that the principal reason regarding kid meat is that exposure to the wind harms it. According to Rambam, the reason regarding kid meat is not harm from the wind but singeing. This reading is therefore incorrect according to him, and Rambam omitted this case; see there. Accordingly, the Shulchan Aruch, which adopts Rambam's reason of singeing, likewise rejects this ruling. Rather, squash is like other produce, and the Shulchan Aruch therefore omitted it. See below, paragraph 18.
