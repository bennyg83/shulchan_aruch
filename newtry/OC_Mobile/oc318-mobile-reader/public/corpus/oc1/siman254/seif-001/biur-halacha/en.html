Although, etc. In order that the words of the Mechaber and the Rama in these two se'ifim be thoroughly clear to you, I will first present the words of the Tosefes Shabbos, who briefly copied the words of the Gemara and the poskim. We learn in the Gemara, Shabbos 18b: Now that you have said that anything harmed by a draft will not be uncovered, a kid is permitted whether the oven opening is sealed with clay or not sealed. A mature goat, meaning an adult goat, is also permitted when the opening is sealed. Regarding a mature goat when the opening is not sealed, Rav Yirmiyah forbids it and Rav Ashi permits it. The poskim write that we rule like Rav Yirmiyah. The Gemara asks: according to Rav Ashi, who permits it, did we not learn that one may not roast meat, an onion, or an egg unless there is enough time for them to roast before nightfall? It answers that there the case is meat placed directly on coals. The commentators write that according to the conclusion Rav Yirmiyah also agrees with this answer. Tosafos and the Rosh write that the passage concerns roasting. Therefore, a mature goat in an unsealed oven is forbidden even when completely raw, although one may place completely raw meat in a pot shortly before nightfall, because meat roasted without a pot cooks quickly enough to become edible at night, and one may therefore come to stoke the coals. According to the Rambam and the Mechaber, the principal reason that a kid is permitted whether the opening is sealed or not is that it requires only a little heat. We therefore do not suspect that he will stoke, because if he does so the meat will scorch. For this reason, even if it is outside the oven near the coals there is no concern that he may stoke. This applies specifically when it is near the coals. If it is placed directly on the coals, even a kid is forbidden, because when it lies directly on the coals he plainly does not care if it scorches, since he wants it to roast quickly in any manner possible. If it is placed directly on the coals and has roasted before Shabbos to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai, it is permitted even according to the first opinion at the beginning of siman 253, which holds that ma'achal ben Derusai does not suffice for food in a pot, as explained there. Roasted meat lying on the coals is certainly permitted once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai, because after that point he will certainly not stoke, since he is concerned that the roast will be ruined by scorching. Regarding beef and mature goat meat, the Rambam and the Mechaber hold that it is permitted when the oven opening is sealed with clay and the meat is inside the oven, because he will certainly not open the oven to stoke. If he opens it, the draft will enter, toughen the meat, and ruin it. Therefore, if the roast is in a pot, as in pot roast, so that the draft will not toughen it, it is permitted when completely raw, as written at the beginning of siman 253. If it has cooked somewhat, however, it is forbidden even as pot roast, because it will not be toughened and there is concern that he may open the oven and stoke. According to this, the Gemara's answer that the case is meat on coals means that it is placed directly on the coals, as stated in se'if 2. In that situation he is not concerned about scorching and wants only that it roast quickly. It is therefore permitted only once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai. Once it reaches ma'achal ben Derusai it is permitted according to all opinions, including the Rambam and the Mechaber at the beginning of siman 253, as explained. All this follows the Mechaber's view here. According to the Rosh and the Tur, whose view the Rama cites in his gloss, the principal reason a kid is permitted is that a draft harms it, and he will therefore not uncover the oven. It is permitted even when the oven opening is merely covered and not sealed with clay. A mature goat, however, is permitted only when clay is spread around the covering. In that case there is no concern that he will stoke, because he will not trouble himself so much as to break the clay seal around the oven opening. For this reason it is permitted even when the meat is in a pot. They explain that the Gemara's answer concerning meat on coals means that the oven is entirely uncovered and completely open. It is therefore forbidden even for a kid. Once it has reached ma'achal ben Derusai, however, it is permitted even according to the first opinion at the beginning of siman 253, and even if it is not on the coals but merely near them, because with roasted meat that has reached ma'achal ben Derusai there is no concern that he may stoke. This clarifies all the words of the Mechaber and the Rama. End of the words of the Tosefes Shabbos.

Know that all this follows the Shulchan Aruch, which rules like the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, who follow Rav Yirmiyah in the Gemara and forbid a mature goat in an unsealed oven. Many other rishonim, however, including Rav Hai Gaon, Rabbeinu Chananel, the Baal HaMaor, the Rashba, Sefer HaTerumah, and the Mordechai, rule like Rav Ashi according to the second version, that a mature goat in an unsealed oven is permitted. The Bach therefore writes that b'dieved, if one transgressed or forgot and left it in a covered oven even though the opening was not sealed with clay, it is permitted, since some authorities permit this even l'chatchila. The same applies to every comparable case, as stated by the Bach, Magen Avraham in se'if katan 11, and Eliyah Rabbah. It appears that the same applies below, where the Rama concludes, 'and the custom follows this view.' That is, when the meat is over an exposed fire, even a kid or poultry is forbidden. This prohibition too applies only l'chatchila. B'dieved one may be lenient regarding a kid or poultry beside the fire even when the fire is completely exposed, since the Mechaber permits it l'chatchila according to the Rambam's approach.
And over an exposed fire. Even if it is inside an oven, so long as the oven is uncovered it is considered as though he were roasting it openly beside the fire. The rationale that a draft harms it does not apply, because the oven is already open. This is the explanation of the Magen Avraham and the Gra in the words of the Rama.

Meaning, even kid meat is forbidden until it has roasted before Shabbos to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai according to this view, as stated by the Taz, Magen Avraham, and Gra. Even for beef this degree suffices. According to them, there is no difference whether one roasts it beside the fire, meaning near it, or directly over the fire. According to the Rambam, however, whose view is that of the Mechaber in se'ifim 1 and 2, when the meat is directly over the fire, not necessarily literally atop the coals but likewise at their side whenever it touches them, as the Peri Megadim explains, kid meat and beef have the same requirement of ma'achal ben Derusai, as stated in se'if 2. When it is not directly over the fire but merely beside an exposed fire, and the same applies in an oven whose opening is covered but not sealed with clay, since these cases are equivalent according to them, beef is forbidden until fully cooked, as written in the Mishna Berurah, se'if katan 4, whereas kid meat is completely permitted, as explained above in se'if 1. The Rama therefore did not state explicitly that according to this opinion there is a leniency when the meat roasted before Shabbos to ma'achal ben Derusai, permitting even beef. He had no need to do so, because the Rama already wrote above, at the end of siman 253, se'if 1, that the custom is lenient even regarding food in a pot and regards ma'achal ben Derusai as sufficient. This is the explanation of the Gra and other acharonim in resolving the Taz's question.
