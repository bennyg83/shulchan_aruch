An onion and an egg. The same applies to other foods that are not eaten raw, as explained below.
On top of the coals. This means that they are touching the coals, and after they have roasted he must wait until the coals are extinguished and then remove them. The reason we are concerned here that he may stoke the coals even with kid meat is as the Mechaber explains afterward: since he placed it, etc. If, however, he roasts it near the coals, the Mechaber's view was already explained in se'if 1: regarding tender kid meat and poultry, we are not concerned at all that he may stoke, because doing so would scorch the meat. It is therefore permitted to place them there close to nightfall even if they will not roast before nightfall to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. The same applies to an onion and an egg. Regarding beef and goat meat, which are tough, they must be fully roasted before nightfall; otherwise, we are concerned that he may stoke, because the meat will not be harmed by stoking when it is not resting directly on the coals. The Rama follows the poskim who make no distinction between placing food on the coals and placing it near them. Whether it is beef or kid meat and poultry, it must roast before nightfall to the degree of ma'achal ben Derusai. If the oven is sealed with clay, however, according to the Rama it is permitted in every case to place it there close to nightfall, for we are not concerned that he will break the seal and stoke the coals. He already wrote in se'if 1 that the custom follows this view.
