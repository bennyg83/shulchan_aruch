These foods do not require extensive cooking, etc. Peri Megadim, part 1, end of siman 84, writes that some people place a pot of tea close to nightfall upon an iron utensil, put beneath it a strong liquid called spiritus together with a wick, and allow it to burn throughout the night. On Shabbos morning they have a non-Jew remove the pot from the fire. This is an absolute prohibition, for it has the same status as a stove or small stove that was neither swept nor covered, and resembles the case of filling a vessel, etc. One cannot argue that stoking is inapplicable because there are no coals here, only burning liquid. We see that when the liquid is stirred, the flame rises higher, and there is therefore concern that one may stoke it. The sages of the generation are consequently obligated to protest against this practice.
