The Levush corrected this in accordance with what the Beit Yosef writes, that the Rambam and Tur rule like R' Eliezer and follow Rashi's stringent explanation: thick bread requires that the surface adhering to the oven form a crust, whereas a thin flat cake requires the surface facing the fire to form a crust. The Prisha likewise explains this; see there. Alternatively, when it lies directly on coals, this degree suffices because it scorches, and so it appears from the Levush. According to this, the ruling applies specifically when it lies on or touches the coals, as in se'if 2 concerning meat on coals. If it is merely near the fire, its required degree is like that of bread, etc. One must then say that a flat cake need only reach ma'achal ben Derusai, whereas bread requires more; this is somewhat difficult, but it can be maintained. See Olas Tamid and Nechalas Tzvi. The Taz, of blessed memory, was not satisfied with this explanation, because the Mishnah in Shabbos 19b and 20a mentions both bread and a flat cake under one rule: until their surfaces form a crust. R' Eliezer says: until its lower surface forms a crust. There is no concern that the Mechaber uses the masculine form for bread and the feminine form for a flat cake, since Scripture uses 'bread' in both feminine and masculine forms. Bread appears in the feminine in Mishlei 17:1 and 23:8, and in the masculine in I Melachim 14:3, Tehillim 104:15, and Bamidbar 4:7. Do not challenge this from 'the two loaves of waving' in Vayikra 23:17, for the feminine form refers to the waving, which is described as a new meal offering; see Rashi there. According to the Beit Yosef, one may answer that since the Mishnah teaches both cases, it follows that their laws differ. According to Magen Avraham, letter 15, the matter is clear. The words 'and even Tosafos, the question' contain a scribal error and should read: 'and Tosafos explain the question according to Rashi,' etc. Tosafos on Shabbos there, s.v. 'they asked,' mean as follows. At first glance, the Yerushalmi appears to support Rashi, that the oven side is the stricter one, for it states there that R' Eliezer concedes regarding bread that its surface must form a crust while it is in the oven. The Yerushalmi understands that R' Eliezer argues leniently, requiring only the lower surface facing the fire, whereas the Bavli understands that he refers to the oven side and is stringent. The halacha follows the Bavli and Rashi. Tosafos therefore explain that the Yerushalmi's phrase 'in the oven' does not refer to the surface adhering to the oven. Rather, it means that R' Eliezer concedes that the bread must form a proper crust while still in the oven, and that it may not be removed before then. Thus, regarding Shabbos he argues leniently, as in the Bavli, but not according to Rashi: the oven side is actually more lenient, and according to R' Eliezer that side alone suffices. The Rambam and Tur therefore write 'the oven side or the fire side,' because either one suffices. Had they written only 'the oven side,' one might have understood it stringently like Rashi; they therefore teach that this is not so. The Rama cites the dissenting opinion, as Magen Avraham, letter 16, explains. See siman 461, se'if 3.
