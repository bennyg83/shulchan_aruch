Magen Avraham, s.k. 13. 'It is forbidden to roast raw apples.' I do not understand the a fortiori inference. Here the case is more severe, because hastening their cooking constitutes full cooking by Torah law. See what I wrote in the margin at the beginning of siman 368. It is only rabbinic here because apples are eaten raw and do not require cooking, as the Rambam writes in chapter 9, halacha 3. Nevertheless, the act of cooking itself is a complete act of cooking, such that in an ordinary case one would be liable. By contrast, placing fruit near an oven before it has been kindled is also only rabbinic in an ordinary case, because when one places the fruit there no fire is yet present. This accords with the ruling that when one person places water and another supplies the fire, the one who placed the water is exempt. That case is therefore more lenient than ours. What a fortiori inference is there?
