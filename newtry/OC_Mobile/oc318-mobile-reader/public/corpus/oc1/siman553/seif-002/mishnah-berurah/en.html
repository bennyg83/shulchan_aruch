(3) And its twilight is forbidden: that is, both at its entry and at its exit; but one need not add upon them. And see above in siman 261, where we explained that twilight is called from when the sun has set; and the same applies in our matter.
(4) And one is permitted washing, etc.: we have already written that if after interrupting his meal he accepted upon himself not to eat further, then washing and anointing are automatically forbidden as well; and specifically when he accepted the addition from plag haminchah and onward, for before that it is not an acceptance, and what he accepted explicitly he accepted and nothing more. And likewise, when he accepted upon himself the prohibition of eating meat and wine from the seventeenth of Tammuz and onward, washing is not included in the prohibition.
(5) Before he says Barchu: that is to say, even though their custom was to pray Maariv while it was still day, nevertheless after Barchu it is regarded as night for this matter. And in the Maharil he wrote that one should remove the shoes before going to the synagogue. And in any case he should see to remove them before twilight.
(6) And if it is Shabbos, they remove, etc.: for it is forbidden to put any sign of mourning on Shabbos. And he should not touch them with his hands, for then he would need to wash his hands. And after the fact, if he touched them, he should clean his hands with anything that cleans.
(7) Before Barchu: because of confusion of mind, and so that affliction not appear on Shabbos, he first says Hamavdil, without Shem and malchus (Peri Megadim).
(8) And they adopted the custom, etc.: for Torah gladdens the heart; and nevertheless all this is not by law, for by law on erev Tisha B'Av all five afflictions are permitted; and the main reason is that one can learn the things that are permitted on Tisha B'Av. And from many later authorities it appears that they adopted this custom and did not challenge it, for even if it falls on Shabbos, several later authorities agreed to conduct oneself as the Rama wrote, as I will write below. However, there are some later authorities who greatly questioned this custom. First of all, regarding the Maharshal, they wrote of him that he himself learned after midday and also permitted others in this. Also the Gra in his commentary wrote that it is an excessive stringency; and likewise the Maamar Mordechai in his work elaborates on this and wrote that it brings many learners to bitul Torah, for they become slack about learning the things permitted on Tisha B'Av, since a person learns only what his heart desires; and therefore his view is to be lenient in this, and he wrote that so he himself practiced; see there. And likewise the Chayei Adam wrote that it is a mere stringency; and therefore it appears that one who wishes to be lenient in this, we do not rebuke him.
(9) And therefore if it falls on Shabbos: that is, even erev Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos, one does not say Pirkei Avos, for it is like Torah learning; and that is after midday, for before midday Torah learning is permitted even when it falls on Shabbos.
(10) They do not say, etc.: and the Taz greatly questions refraining from learning on Shabbos, and concludes that one who learns on Shabbos after midday has not lost his reward; and that is even when Tisha B'Av itself falls on Shabbos, and all the more so when erev Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos. And it appears that one may rely on this, since even when it falls on a weekday several later authorities are lenient, as above.
