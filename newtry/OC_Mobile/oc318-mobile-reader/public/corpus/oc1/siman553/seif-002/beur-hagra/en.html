Se'if 2. Tisha B'Av, etc. As stated regarding a communal fast: they eat and drink while it is still day, etc.; and the night is lenient only regarding melacha, as stated there on folio 13a: when they said it is forbidden in melacha, etc., and when they said, etc., implying that in other matters day and night are equal. And see Tosafos there, folio 12b, s.v. Mah, etc. And they said there is no communal fast, etc.; and in chapter 4 of Pesachim we say that its twilight is forbidden; and we say that whatever is because of Tisha B'Av is forbidden in washing; and in the Tosefta, anointing as well; and according to all the interpretations, at night at least it is forbidden.
And it is permitted, etc. Not like the Ramban, who explained what is stated there, 'whatever is because of Tisha B'Av,' etc., that from the seudah hamafsekes and onward washing and anointing are forbidden, as stated in the Tosefta as above, and he wrote that we hold like the first Tanna. But the Raavad explained that it refers to after he interrupted and accepted upon himself the prohibition of eating, that then he is forbidden in all the afflictions; and this is the meaning of 'whatever is,' etc.: that is to say, one who interrupts because of Tisha B'Av is forbidden; and whatever is not, etc., but only because of a communal fast that is not Tisha B'Av, even though he interrupted eating, the rest is permitted. And even according to the Ramban's explanation, one may say that the halacha follows R' Yehudah son of R' Yosi, since [the other view] was silent in the Mishnah; and even though the Ramban raised a difficulty against this, nevertheless the custom follows R' Yehudah son of R' Yosi, and so the plain sense of the Mishnah indicates, that only meat and wine are forbidden, and from its specifying those it indicates that the rest is permitted.
Until twilight. That is to say, one need not add [to the fast], as the Ramban wrote, from the fact that it says twilight is forbidden.
And if it is, etc. For any sign of mourning is forbidden on Shabbos, as stated in chapter 3 of Moed Katan; and even regarding one who interrupts while it is still day, they raised a difficulty at the end of chapter 3 of Eruvin and said, for he ate and drank the whole day, etc., which is not so regarding removing the shoes.
Except for the chazzan, etc. As stated at the end of chapter 5 of Berachos, because of confusion of mind; and so as not to practice affliction on Shabbos, he said only, etc.
And they adopted the custom, etc. Because of joy, as written in siman 554, se'if 1; and it is an excessive stringency, for he does not practice any of the things practiced on Tisha B'Av, washing, etc., as above.
