N.b.: see Chatam Sofer Orach Chayyim siman 556 [where he was asked whether it is proper to gather congregations publicly on Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbat and is postponed, per Israel's custom every Shabbat, or not; after explaining the midrash that Rabbi read Megillat Eichah on Tisha B'Av that fell on Shabbat, etc., he concluded: therefore one may permit before chatzot at least public reading such as the haftarah and similar; and so is practiced everywhere I saw — end quote. And the reason for the custom not to study on erev Tisha B'Av after chatzot he wrote there: because whatever one studies from chatzot onward remains in his mind and they meditate on it at night and enter mourning while he is happy; however, even on Shabbat after chatzot it is forbidden — not because of mourning of the mind but because of the night that follows; therefore the custom applies on Shabbat too].
