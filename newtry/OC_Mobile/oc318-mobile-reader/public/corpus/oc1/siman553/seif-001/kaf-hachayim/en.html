(1) [Seif 1] unless he accepted upon himself explicitly etc. — and even if he accepted the fast explicitly, bathing and anointing are not forbidden until night. Rashal in responsa siman 92. And so Levush. But Bach wrote that from when he accepted the fast, if eating is forbidden for him, bathing and anointing are also forbidden; he wrote on Rashal's aforementioned words that they are not clear — see there. Beit Din in Shakh Keneset haGedolah in gloss of Taz. And so Taz s.k. 1. Magen Avraham s.k. 4. Rabbi Akiva Eiger letter 2 from 29 Av and wrote unlike Levush. Maamar Mordechai letter 1. Eliyah Rabbah letter 2. Mishnat Hasidim letter 1. Chayei Adam general rule 134 letter 8. Derech haChayyim letter 4. Mishna Berurah letter 2. However wearing shoes is permitted, since we see they are on his feet and he did not accept this. Rabbi Akiva Eiger there. Eliyah Rabbah there. Mishnat Hasidim there. Chayei Adam there. Derech haChayyim there. Mishna Berurah there. And that bathing and anointing are forbidden from accepting the fast — this is if it is from minchah ketanah and above; but before that, what he accepted he accepted and no more.
