Seif 1 in the gloss: and permitted in bathing, anointing, and wearing shoes until bein hashmashot etc. — note: Taz brought the view of the Ramban who forbids bathing even at a time when eating is permitted, because this is enjoyment for later etc. And behold, the Ramban's words seem to contradict themselves, for in Shabbat chapter R' Eliezer regarding milah he brings the Ramban's view that if hot water spilled after milah it is permitted to circumcise because now it is in permission and afterward pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat — Beit HaMeir and Rashba disagree there — see there; if so it is proven the Ramban holds we follow the present; if so here too why not follow the present that now it is permitted to bathe even though enjoyment comes later without any action, and enjoyment that comes automatically is not forbidden — only that one may not make enjoyment for oneself, and here he does it at the time of permission. However, per what Ran wrote to reject proofs of Beit HaMeir and Rashba — since slaughter is not a fixed mitzvah on Yom Tov like milah on Shabbat — if so it is proven only a fixed mitzvah permits the Ramban to follow the present, but otherwise we follow the outcome; if so all the more here we follow the outcome. And behold, conversely one should not challenge those who permit bathing when eating is permitted even though enjoyment extends afterward — for regarding study we hold like R' Yehuda who forbids study even of what he never learned, and Taz explained below the reason is because even though he is pained now he will be happy later — see there; so why is bathing permitted when eating is permitted even though it is enjoyment for later? However, this needs study: there he learns at the time of prohibition, therefore we properly follow what will be at the end — enjoyment for him; but if he acts at the time of permission we do not look at the outcome, only since now it is at the time of permission it is permitted. Yet it is difficult on those who hold we follow the outcome — if he knows afterward he will need to violate Shabbat, circumcision is forbidden — if so it is proven we follow the outcome; and why is bathing permitted? Only before bein hashmashot we follow the present. Perhaps it is like what Nissim wrote in Bava Kamma chapter 2 regarding lighting a candle on Shabbat though it burns afterward on Shabbat — we consider everything that happens afterward as if done immediately now — see there; therefore it fits here too that everything extending afterward we judge as if done now; therefore if he learns or bathes at the time of prohibition, even though enjoyment is for later, nevertheless it is considered as if done then at the time of action and forbidden; if he bathes at the time of permission, even though enjoyment is at the time of prohibition, nevertheless it is considered as if that enjoyment is immediate and now is the time of permission and therefore permitted. In milah it is the reverse: what heats afterward is as if done immediately; now there is no permission since pikuach nefesh is not yet, and milah preparers do not override Shabbat — therefore they forbid there. From Rashi Pesachim on "his bath" — he explained in eating and melacha and did not explain other enjoyments; necessarily either he should list the main thing only eating not melacha, or list all — why specify eating and melacha? From this it appears he holds like the Ramban, therefore specifically eating and melacha are forbidden in bein hashmashot but before then permitted; but anointing and bathing are forbidden even before — it is proven he holds so regarding the Ramban. Yet on the Ramban it is difficult from the sugya there that says there is no difference between Tisha B'Av and Yom Kippur except one's doubt is forbidden and one's doubt permitted — why did the tanna not also count that on Yom Kippur the Ramban admits bathing and anointing are permitted when eating is permitted — otherwise it is difficult on the Tur why he brought the Ramban in laws of Tisha B'Av and not in Yom Kippur laws which are stricter; necessarily on Yom Kippur the Ramban admits permission. Namely, on Tisha B'Av all enjoyment is forbidden because even smelling spices is forbidden — it is from mourning; therefore all enjoyment is forbidden; but on Yom Kippur the reason is not mourning but the Torah's decree of five afflictions — other enjoyment like smelling spices is permitted as Magen Avraham wrote siman 559 and siman 46; if so, when done at prohibition time it is forbidden, but at permission time since the act is in permission even if enjoyment comes later it is only like other permitted enjoyment on Yom Kippur; but on Tisha B'Av where all enjoyment is forbidden, what difference if bathing is at permission time — if enjoyment extends into prohibition time it is also forbidden; therefore the Tur did not bring the Ramban in Yom Kippur laws, only Tisha B'Av. If so, since there is something permitted on Yom Kippur and forbidden on Tisha B'Av, why did the tanna not count this difference between Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av like he counted doubt forbidden/permitted? One could say since the Ramban explained the baraita's intent in the dispute of Tanna Kamma and R' Yehuda like R' Yehuda, and R' Yishmael permits here — one may say this baraita is like R' Yehuda who permits; the Ramban rules like Tanna Kamma who forbids. But per this it is again difficult why the tanna did not count study between communal fast and Tisha B'Av — forbidden on Tisha B'Av, permitted on Yom Kippur; necessarily "there is no difference" only what Yom Kippur is stricter — this alone, doubt forbidden/permitted; but many things Tisha B'Av is stricter. One may say the baraita is not like tannaim but all agree — only what depends on dispute is not counted. This fits without contradicting the sugya; I have no time to expand — perhaps with divine help we will be granted another time to explain more — examine well.
