And it is permitted. See the Taz, letter ches: washing does not require adding [to the fast], while eating does require adding; and this is not so; see Magen Avraham 3. Rather, it is to exclude the view of the Ramban, of blessed memory, in the Tur: once he accepted the fast explicitly, even the Rosh agrees that he accepted washing as well; but regarding shoes, we see that he did not accept them, and it is as if he stipulated that shoes remain permitted. And what they wrote regarding one who accepts [restrictions] during the Three Weeks, that is from the seventeenth of Tammuz: even if in that place they also practice a stringency of washing from the seventeenth of Tammuz, he accepted only what he stated explicitly; and it is unlike here, where from plag haminchah onward he accepted an addition to Tisha B'Av regarding eating, and automatically he accepted whatever belongs to Tisha B'Av, namely the five afflictions; which is not so during the Three Weeks, where that is a separate stringency for what he specified as forbidden, etc. And see siman 551, se'if 16. And what he wrote in the name of the Maharshal: that is, in this he ruled like the Maharshal regarding the Three Weeks; but regarding an addition to Tisha B'Av he did not rule like him; and see Magen Avraham letter daled; and with Hashem's help there it will be clarified further. And specifically when he accepted the addition to Tisha B'Av from plag haminchah, for that is an addition; but before that it is not an addition, and if he accepted it separately, then it is like during the Three Weeks; and see Beis Yosef and Bach; and in siman 261 in the Magen Avraham, letters tes and yud, from when acceptance of an addition to Shabbos takes effect, and the same applies here; and see Magen Avraham letter daled.
