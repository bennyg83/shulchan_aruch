And one is permitted washing, etc. Not like the Ramban, who forbids washing even at a time when eating is permitted; and his reason is that the pleasure of anointing is a pleasure that comes later, and it appears as if he washes for the need of Tisha B'Av. And the Rosh was astonished at this: that he should be permitted to eat and drink, and yet forbidden to wash and anoint. If so, what the Rama wrote here, 'and one is permitted washing,' means as long as eating is permitted; but after he accepted explicitly not to eat, the prohibition of washing also takes effect upon him, for the stringency of Tisha B'Av begins from that hour, and we do not say that by default he accepted half upon himself and half he did not accept. But regarding wearing shoes, it appears that it does not depend on accepting the prohibition of eating, for we see clearly that he does not accept that upon himself, since his shoes are on his feet and he does not remove them; it is as if he says explicitly that he does not yet accept this stringency upon himself. And for this reason that we said he accepts upon himself the stringency of Tisha B'Av before its time: it appears that one who accepts upon himself the prohibition of eating meat and wine during the Three Weeks, washing is not included in the prohibition, for he did not then accept upon himself the stringency of Tisha B'Av, but only a special stringency for that time, and what he accepted explicitly he accepted and nothing more. And the Maharshal in responsa, siman 92, also ruled to permit washing in this matter, and elaborated; see there. So it appears to my humble opinion.
