(1) Even though, etc. As stated in Eichah Rabbati, R' Yehudah ben Beseira, etc., and the Rosh and Tosafos brought it at the end of chapter 3 of Eruvin and at the end of Taanis, and other poskim.
Unless he accepted, etc. As written at the end of chapter 2 of Eruvin; and he says explicitly, to exclude where his intent is merely not to eat, for that is not an acceptance. And acceptance, etc. And so he wrote in siman 608, se'if 3, in the gloss; and his words are very puzzling, and it is an error, for the poskim disagreed only about one whose intent is not to eat, for Maharam and Ravyah wrote that even in such a case it is forbidden, and there is no proof from the midrash, for that was in the morning; and Hagahos Maimoniyos, and so it appears from the words of the Rambam that it is forbidden; and see chapter 3 of Hilchos Taaniyos and the Hagahos Maimoniyos there. And the poskim disagree about this unless he accepted; but they are not discussing whether acceptance in the heart is an acceptance, and its law is like any other fast. And even though the Maggid Mishneh wrote regarding one who accepted upon himself explicitly and uttered it with his lips, etc., he holds that every acceptance requires utterance with the lips; but for us, who hold that even acceptance in the heart suffices, as written above in siman 562, se'if 6, and in Yoreh De'ah siman 258, and in Choshen Mishpat siman 212, it is no worse than any acceptance of a fast, and as Rabbeinu Tam wrote, that any vow in the heart suffices.
