(s.k. 1) One may lower. In the Gemara it implies, etc. — and this is from our mishna in the chapter Meshilin: one may lower fruits through a skylight on Yom Tov; and the Gemara asked there in tractate Shabbat we learned: one clears four or five boxes of straw and grain on Shabbat because of guests or because of neglect of the house — if we say that here too it was not permitted except four or five boxes, or perhaps there where there is no monetary loss; but here where there is monetary loss even many as well, etc., and it was not resolved; and since it says "but here where there is monetary loss" it implies he resolved that the mishna was not permitted except where there is monetary loss:

(ב) And even to finish [the storehouse], etc. And so the Rif and Rambam, etc. — unlike R' Yehuda, for there it still requires further [analysis]. There we learned regarding clearing out, etc., provided he does not finish the storehouse (and Rashi explained: to take everything to expose the floor) — lest he come to level high places. Here what? Perhaps Shabbat is strict but Yom Tov is lenient — even to finish it is permitted. One answer: there, since there is nullification of hekdesh, you said it is forbidden — how much more so here. And also this question was not resolved. And R' Yehuda challenged the Rif and Rambam who cited the Mishnah anonymously — it implies even a large quantity is permitted. And he holds: since these are rabbinic matters, we record the unresolved question leniently; if so, it should likewise permit even to finish, for that too is an unresolved question that was not resolved — see there what he wrote to distinguish between a large amount and finishing. And Magen Avraham holds that in truth even to finish they ruled leniently, and this too is from their writing the Mishnah anonymously — it implies in every case it is permitted even to finish. And so is the view of Shulchan Aruch that he did not mention prohibition to finish; and it cannot be that for the Rif and Rambam it is forbidden to finish — for then he should have been unspecific in Shulchan Aruch and brought their view in explanation; rather he retracted and holds that also the Rif and Rambam permit even to finish, and therefore was unspecific; and so it is unspecific in Shulchan Aruch — it implies even to finish is permitted.
