Hallel — Taz and Magen Avraham bet that one drinks the fourth cup after, for it is fitting for him. What the Mechaber wrote siman 26, all 38 — see Perishah yud alef opposite the Name may He be blessed; and in Pesachim 111b in the Gemara it is explained opposite siman 26 generations. Below letter bet — but one who did not observe mourning before Yom Tov nevertheless says Hallel, and it is simple — responsa Mor U'Ketzia of Lubin siman 73: an onen on night one or night two — another hears Haggadah and he eats matzah and maror and blesses; and Magen Avraham 491 letter chet brought briefly — see there; and it implies he eats meat and drinks wine at night and drinks four cups; and by day — see there Shulchan Aruch Mekubal and siman 416 to repeat the portion he is obligated — permitted; and nevertheless one may say between Haggadah; but if there is no other, it is simple he says Haggadah himself, for it is a positive commandment, as Rambam wrote chapter 7 law 1 from matzah; and see in TaZ in Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah in Tiferet Yisrael it is explained further. And it appears he need not skip the midrashim and says all, like Eizeh Makoman; and it implies there specifically on Yom Tov night an onen is obligated in mitzvot; but on chol ha'moed an onen is exempt from mitzvot even at night — see Shulchan Aruch Mekubal; an onen on Purim is obligated in Megillah at night — one must say specifically Purim and Yom Tov, for on chol ha'moed an onen is exempt even at night. And what Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah — Shulchan Aruch Mekubal that there is no way to bury at night — perhaps on Yom Tov, all that does not darken beyond the techum or is not occupied with burial needs has no status of aninus, as Maharam of Lubin wrote; see Shach there tet and Avnei Nezer alef; and behold an onen on the eighth day of Pesach when chametz became chametzed by itself and remained after Pesach — for a regular person it is forbidden — siman 448; an onen — one may say since exempt from all mitzvot, d'rabbanan or positive commandment, all while actually occupied with burial needs — occupied in mitzvah is exempt from mitzvah — see chapter one who mourns and siman 71; and these are positive and negative commandments in Shema that he is exempt from; but to transgress a positive commandment, Heaven forbid — an onen of Israel like all Israel — responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 1 on yavam onen (what he wrote there that positive commandment is permitted — she was not forbidden an hour, and even if occupied with burial needs one may say positive commandment exempt; nevertheless if he wishes he leaves this mitzvah and performs yibum — see there); and if so there is no difficulty on the eighth — one may say permitted after Pesach even on the seventh if he was compelled that he could not bury until the eighth and similar; if gentiles ate, he may take payment from them, for chametz after Pesach is a penalty; and similarly they did not penalize; and to leave it unintentional or compelled is forbidden as ha'aramah and similar, unlike here that he did not transgress at all positive and negative of Beit Yosef — since he is in Shema, what is it to me whether positive or negative. Nevertheless if he could nullify before it chametzed, one may say he transgressed Beit Yosef, for they did not exempt him to do mitzvah but because of honor of the dead — see beginning of chapter one who mourns; and occupied is exempt from doing mitzvah, unlike nullification alone; see what Rosh wrote beginning one who mourns — when Rabeinu Yehuda was an onen, his students said let us bless before you; he said one does not answer amen — it implies otherwise permitted to hear; and so here. And what he wrote there that one may not answer amen even b'dieved he fulfills without answering amen — he was stringent on himself; and still requires further study.
