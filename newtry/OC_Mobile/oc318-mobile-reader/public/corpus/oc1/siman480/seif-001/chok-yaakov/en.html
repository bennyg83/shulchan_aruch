And he does not recite a blessing over the Hallel beforehand, nor over what they say before eating, nor over what they say after eating; this is the conclusion of the poskim, as explained in the Tur and Beis Yosef, siman 473; see there. And above in siman 422 it is explained that even though Hallel throughout the year is said standing, on Pesach they say it sitting, in the manner of reclining and freedom; see there in the words of the later authorities. And see what I wrote above in siman 473, s.k. 4.
And he completes the Hallel over it. And it appears that where mourning occurred on Yom Tov and he did not observe his mourning before Yom Tov, that he completes the Hallel; even though they do not say Hallel in a house of mourning, on Pesach it is obvious that they say it, for even on Chanukah there are those who say that they say it, as the later authorities wrote above in siman 131; all the more so on Pesach, when one is obligated to say song over the redemption; and also because it would be like public mourning, since everyone says it. And see in Yoreh De'ah siman 341, and see above regarding the law of reclining for a mourner.

And then he should say "Yehalelucha" until "Melech Mehulal Batishbachos." And we follow the opinion of the Tur and the Rosh to say "Yehalelucha" until "me'olam ve'ad ha'olam Atah Kel" after the Hallel, and afterward these twenty-six [verses of] "Ki Le'olam Chasdo," and Nishmas, and he concludes with Yishtabach until "Chai HaOlamim." And see the Taz at the end of siman 486.
And he drinks it. And the piyyutim, namely "Az Rov Nissim" and "Ometz Gevurosecha," they say after drinking the cup; so is the consensus of some of the later authorities. And there are those who say to drink after saying the piyyut "Ki Lo Na'eh," so that he will not be thirsty when he lies down; and so it is in the siddurim, and so is the custom. And the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Maggid Mishneh: on the first night he should say "Az Rov Nissim" and should not say at the end "Vayehi Bachatzi Halailah"; and on the second night he should say "Ometz Gevurosecha" and should not conclude "Va'amartem Zevach Pesach"; end quote. And we do not practice thus; rather they say everything on both nights equally.
And through the merit. And in Panim Meiros, siman 361, he wrote as follows: And in Sefer HaManhig I found a custom in several places that they do not lock the rooms in which they sleep on Pesach night, because in Nisan they were redeemed and in Nisan they are destined to be redeemed, as it is written "leil shimurim," a night kept [guarded] from the six days of Creation; and if Eliyahu comes he will find an open doorway, and we will go out to greet him quickly; and we believe this, and there is support for the matter in the Yerushalmi of Kesubos, chapter HaNosei; and there is great reward in this faith; end quote. And we do not practice thus to leave a doorway open, because damage from thieves is common. And in these lands they practice to pour one cup more than [the number of] those reclining, and they call it the cup of Eliyahu HaNavi.
