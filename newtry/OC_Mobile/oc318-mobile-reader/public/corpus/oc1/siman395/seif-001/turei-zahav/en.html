(א) It is a mitzva to check etc. — so one not come to a prohibition of carrying:

(ב) Or at the time he transfers ownership to them — in the Tur it is written: at the time he collects it from the residents of the courtyard or at the time he transfers ownership to them, and one person acquires for all and says "by law it shall be permitted" etc.; and Beit Yosef explained this means: or at the time he does not collect but one acquires for all; and it requires study: if so, what is "and one acquires for all" that he said afterward — is it not already included in what he said "or at the time he transfers ownership"? And it appears to me the Tur deals with both manners of collecting, only regarding the time of the blessing he gave two types: one blesses at the time of collection, or waits after collection to bless in the synagogue as we do for eruv chatzerot; and in order that you not ask how he acquires — behold there are many shares in this, and acquisition applies only if he gives of his own — therefore one person acquires for all; for even though many have a share in it, nevertheless one blesses and acquires for all — meaning it is possible the main eruv was not made from everyone's, nevertheless those from whom the eruv was made acquire for others from whom it was not made; and one of them does this on behalf of all, acquiring for all on behalf of each one; and similarly they initially gave for the eruv so the acquirer would acquire on their behalf and acquire for others as stated. And in Levush it is written: therefore our custom is to collect flour from each house and bake part of it as one matzah for eruv and the rest they give to the beadle; and he concluded on this — and it appears to me that per our custom it is not an eruv if they forgot to bless, for they did not acquire it for them and the flour of all is not kneaded within it and it is not an eruv until they acquire it for them — end of his words; and we do not agree, for the reason I wrote that each one who gives initially gives flour on condition that the acquirer acquire in it for the entire congregation even one who will not give flour, and the remainder the beadle keeps for himself; and if so the beadle who makes the eruv matzah makes it on behalf of all, for the flour is everyone's; and the acquisition the rabbi does afterward at the time of the blessing is to publicize that the entire congregation acquires in it; and I compare this to the continual offerings that were offered from the congregation and the remainder was for the summer altar — and if so you may ask: behold a portion remains; perhaps one portion remains entirely from one person — and if so he has no share in the continual offering; but certainly since everything was initially mingled together, everything became belonging to each and every one; so too regarding eruv as stated — correct:
