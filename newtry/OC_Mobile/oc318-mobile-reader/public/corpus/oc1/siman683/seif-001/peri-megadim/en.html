We complete. See Turei Zahav. See siman 670 and Beis Yosef there, who gives three answers. In the Gemara, Shabbos 22, the miracle existed on every day, meaning that it was renewed each day. Another reason is that there is a distinction in the lamps and in the reading, with a prince for each day, and it is comparable to Sukkos, where there is a distinction in the offerings, and therefore we complete Hallel all eight days and on the ninth. The addition of lamps is only for those who beautify the mitzvah, and the primary reason is that a lamp is for each day, etc. See Eliyah Rabbah. From there one can learn that A song, a chapter of Tehillim of Asaf is said on Lag BaOmer, the Fifteenth of Shevat, and the Fifteenth of Av; only on a day when Hallel is said do we not say A song, a chapter of Tehillim of Asaf. On Purim we say A song, a chapter of Tehillim because it contains the downfall of Amalek; see siman 693 in Eliyah Rabbah. In the holy community of Prague they also say it on Chanukah and compare the matters to each other; see there. Kol Bo wrote that on Chanukah one does not say it because there is Hallel; if so, on Purim, where Hallel is not said, perhaps it is because the Megillah is Hallel, or because they wanted to establish it as a Yom Tov. But at Shacharis one does not recite the prayer because there is Hallel, whereas at Maariv one does recite it, since on Rosh Chodesh too one prays Maariv. See the laws of Rosh Chodesh. In this respect Chanukah is no stronger than Rosh Chodesh, even though we say, and they established them as a Yom Tov. One does not say E-l Erech Apayim on Monday and Thursday during Chanukah, nor the May it be Your will after the Torah scroll. Magen Avraham; see siman 131 and Levush here.
