In order that. See the Taz. Behold, in Pesachim 115a there is a dispute. According to Hillel, in Temple times the mitzvah ab initio is to eat Pesach, matzah, and maror together (according to Rashi and Rashbam there; but according to the Rambam, of blessed memory, Hilchos Chametz u'Matzah 8:6, he wraps only matzah and maror below), and after the fact he fulfilled with each one separately; and according to the Rabbanan, ab initio we require, from what they hold, each one separately, and after the fact he fulfilled together; so it is in Tosafos there, s.v. "ela," and so wrote the Eitz [Chaim?], letter zayin; see there. And it concludes there that nowadays he eats first matzah by itself and maror by itself, and afterward both together as a remembrance of the Temple according to Hillel. And the Bach wrote that nowadays there is no mitzvah at all, even rabbinically, even according to Hillel, to eat matzah and maror together, since there is no Pesach; see there. And that is according to Rashi and Rashbam, that he wraps all three together, from what they hold for Hillel; which is not so now, when there is no Pesach; for that reason they say "a remembrance of the Temple according to Hillel," etc. And it is obvious that if he spoke between the blessing and the wrapping, he does not go back and bless. However, Tosafos there, s.v. "ela" (the second), wrote that for that reason nowadays each one must be alone and afterward both together: for if he eats both as one, rabbinic maror nowadays comes and nullifies Torah matzah; and if he eats first matzah alone and afterward optional matzah, the matzah nullifies rabbinic maror nowadays. And they wrote afterward: "However, if we held like Hillel," etc., "rabbinic and rabbinic," etc. They mean by this that they wrote that it appears the law follows Hillel in Temple times: he wraps matzah and maror (without Pesach), and likewise nowadays wrapping is required rabbinically; yet even according to Hillel it is impossible now, for rabbinic maror nullifies Torah matzah; for that reason even according to Hillel nowadays ab initio each must be separate and afterward both together, rabbinically, as with a Torah matter, etc. And on this they wrote: "However, if it were clear to us that the law follows Hillel, there would be another remedy: he would eat first matzah and afterward matzah and maror in a wrap, and it would not be optional matzah, for rabbinically wrapping is required," like the Rambam, of blessed memory, that wrapping means matzah and maror without Pesach; and consequently for us wrapping is required rabbinically in the manner of a Torah wrapping; only that we are in doubt which view the law follows, and perhaps like the Rabbanan, and optional matzah comes and nullifies rabbinic maror; therefore he must first eat each separately and afterward both together, lest it be like Hillel. And if one objects that "remembrance of the Temple" is a rabbinic mitzvah: I do not know; for indeed he means rabbinically a remembrance of the Temple; if so he should say the same when eating maror alone: remembrance of the Temple, for he would eat the maror next to the Pesach, etc. And possibly they say "remembrance of the Temple" with the wrapping to inform that now one may not fulfill with wrapping alone, for rabbinic maror would nullify Torah matzah; and because of remembrance of the Temple, for there everyone would fulfill with wrapping alone, since both were according to the Torah, as stated. Nevertheless, even nowadays [when] matzah [after the first kezayit] is rabbinic, it is [still] for wrapping according to Hillel. And specifically ab initio each separately and afterward the wrapping, but not the reverse: for how can he bless "al achilas matzah" when maror nullifies it? Therefore it is impossible except first to fulfill the Torah obligation of matzah (even according to Hillel nowadays) and the rabbinic obligation of maror. And with this the words of the Taz are also clarified; and see Peri Chadash and Chok Yaakov; and what appears to me in my humble opinion I have written.
And I saw in the Mordechai in chapter Arvei Pesachim, in the Seder Pesach, that he wrote: and if you say, how does he wrap an olive-bulk of matzah and an olive-bulk of maror and an olive-bulk of charoses and eat them, for the throat does not hold more than two olive-bulks? One can say that when crushed it holds more. And behold, what is an olive-bulk of charoses? And if you say charoses is a mitzvah according to R' Elazar b'Rebbi Tzadok, and so ruled the Rambam, of blessed memory, Hilchos Chametz u'Matzah 7:11: it need not be a kezayit, since one does not bless on it, for it is secondary to the maror. And if not for fear I would have emended "an olive-bulk of Pesach" in its place; only that in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 138, he copied "an olive-bulk of charoses"; see there; and see Eliyah Rabbah 6. And if the reading is "Pesach," one can say there is a proof for the Rambam, of blessed memory, that they do not wrap Pesach with it, from the Mordechai's difficulty; and from charoses there is no difficulty, for he shakes it off afterward, as stated. Also what the Mordechai wrote there, "now that the law was not stated like Hillel," etc.; see there: it is difficult even if the law were like Hillel, etc. And with this one can explain what Tosafos wrote "However," and possibly this is the meaning: now that the law was not stated like Hillel, for if it were only like the Rabbanan, etc. And know that we are in doubt whether the law follows Hillel or the Rabbanan, and we do both: it is difficult, for the entire matter is rabbinic, for maror nowadays is rabbinic, and all the more so the wrapping; why do we not say a rabbinic doubt is lenient, and if so he would do each separately and no more? And if you say that we would uproot a rabbinic enactment according to Hillel, and in such a case we do not say a rabbinic doubt is lenient: see Maggid Mishneh, Hilchos Megillah 1:11, that for that reason even if he reclined for the first two cups, he still needs afterward as well; and it is not comparable to a city that has a doubt; see there. Still, since after the fact he fulfilled with each alone according to Hillel, the rabbinic enactment is not uprooted; rather it implies that it is clear to us that the law follows Hillel, and we also practice a stringency like the Rabbanan to eat also the maror first. And with this the words of the Rambam, of blessed memory, Hilchos Chametz u'Matzah 8:6 and 7, are somewhat settled; see there in Lechem Mishneh; and God willing, in Magen Avraham 8 it will be explained further.
