(34) Without intention. And even though mitzvos require intention, as above in siman 60, se'if 4, and all the more so here where he does not want to eat, so that he certainly does not intend to fulfill the mitzvah: the commentators answered that regarding a matter of eating, since his throat necessarily enjoyed, it is better and is like one who intends. And know that several poskim disagree with this and hold that according to what we hold, that mitzvos require intention, one should not distinguish between a matter of eating and other mitzvos, and anyone who did not intend in the eating to fulfill the mitzvah did not fulfill; and so ruled the Peri Chadash.
(35) For example, if gentiles forced him, etc. And all the more so where a Jew forced him to eat in order to fulfill the mitzvah of matzah, that he fulfilled.
(36) He fulfilled his obligation, since, etc. And nevertheless, where he says explicitly that he intends not to fulfill the mitzvah, in this case it is possible that it is worse, even though his throat enjoyed, and he did not fulfill.
(37) But if he thought, etc. Since he did not know at all that he was performing the act of the mitzvah; and even without gentiles forcing him, and he erred on his own, he also did not fulfill.
