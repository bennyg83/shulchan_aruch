(s.k. 4) A kezayit, etc. These are the words of Darkei Moshe, etc.: that it suffices when he swallows a kezayit, etc., and he need not swallow the olive-bulk of matzah and the olive-bulk of HaMotzi at once.
He wraps all three, that is to say, Hillel would. Wrap Pesach, matzah, and maror; for we say that the throat does not hold more than a hen's egg, and three olive-bulks are an egg and a half. It is implied that he holds that it suffices when he swallows one olive-bulk, etc., from his writing "the same applies for us [for] one olive-bulk of matzah and of maror," whereas he should have said "the same applies for us [for] two olive-bulks of matzah and maror" (though this is a weak inference). Even though for us, who wrap matzah and maror as Hillel did, he should at least eat also the two olive-bulks of matzah and maror at once, similar to Hillel, one must say that he holds that since for us it is only a stringency, and as will be written below in the name of the Bach, it suffices when he swallows one olive-bulk at once. But the Magen Avraham in fact disagrees in s.k. 1; yet here regarding the two olive-bulks of matzah and HaMotzi, it is possible that the Magen Avraham admits that it suffices if he eats one olive-bulk at once.
And so is implied in Chullin at the end of chapter 7, etc. In my humble opinion one must force this proof, and according to Tosafos there there is no proof at all; but according to Rashi there is some proof. For thus it is stated there: if he divided a limb from a living animal outside (Rashi explains: he divided it before putting it in his mouth, and ate each part alone, one after another), all agree he is exempt; for Rashi wrote: even though all prohibitions combine within the time of eating a peras, Rashi answered that ordinary eating is at once, only that for all prohibitions a law transmitted to Moshe at Sinai revealed to us that they combine within the time of eating a peras; but for a limb from a living animal, since it is a novelty that even bones and sinews combine to a kezayit, you have only its novelty, and he must eat it at once. But if he divided it inside (Rashi explains: after it came near the throat he swallowed it in halves), R' Yehuda said he is liable, for his throat benefited from a kezayit, and Reish Lakish said he is exempt: we require eating in his insides, and that is lacking; end of his words. And the Magen Avraham holds that we hear the law to Moshe from Sinai of combining only regarding a prohibition; but regarding a positive mitzvah, the plain meaning of the verse remains, as it is written "in the evening you shall eat matzos," and eating implies at once; for even regarding a limb from a living animal, since it is a novelty, we say the law to Moshe from Sinai does not apply to it. And granted that R' Yehuda holds that if he divided it inside he is liable because his throat benefited from a kezayit, nevertheless here he would admit to R' Yehuda that we require eating in the insides; for for that reason we hold above in siman 210 that one who sips and spits out need not bless; see there in Beis Yosef and Magen Avraham. Therefore R' Yehuda admits that he must swallow at once. And even though regarding a limb from a living animal he must swallow it whole and not by chewing, this case is different regarding matzah, for ab initio we require the taste of matzah in his mouth, and only after the fact he fulfilled if he swallowed matzah, as written in se'if 3; so one must force it, in my humble opinion.
