"At the dipping."
But if he thinks it is chol. And in this, even according to the opinion that mitzvos do not require intention, he also did not fulfill, as is proven in the Ran according to the reasoning of the Ra'ah, who holds in general that mitzvos do not require intention; and so is stated explicitly in the words of the Ra'ah cited in Sefer Yavin Shemuah of the Rashbatz. And know that Rabbeinu Yeruham in the name of Tosafos disagrees with this, and in their view, if we say in general that mitzvos do not require intention, even if he thinks it is chametz he also fulfilled. And for the practical law, for us one should not be lenient in this, since we rule anyway that mitzvos require intention; only, because we find that the Rambam copied the statement of the father of Shmuel, we are forced to distinguish that a matter of eating is different; and it is enough for us to be lenient for this reason only for what we find explicitly, and not where he did not know at all that today is Pesach, or that this is not matzah, for possibly in that case the reasoning that his throat enjoyed does not help.
He did not fulfill. See Chok Yaakov, whose view is that regarding maror, even if he did not know that it was maror, or he thinks that it is chol, he also fulfilled; and his main reasoning is that a rabbinic matter, according to everyone, does not require intention. And according to what we wrote above in siman 60 in the Mishnah Berurah in the name of several poskim, that one should not distinguish in this way, if so the same applies to maror. And even according to Chok Yaakov, who is lenient regarding maror for the reason that it is a rabbinic matter, nevertheless on the second day of Yom Tov, which is also rabbinic, he admits that one should not be lenient where one ate matzah and thinks it is not matzah, so that people not come to treat it lightly.
