(א) E.g. gentiles forced him, etc. — in Mishnat Hasidim introduction section 3 letter 5 he doubts if forced to eat maror he fulfills, for it is bitter and harmful and he does not enjoy; see Biur Halacha s.v. betibul.

(ב) But if he believes it is chametz — even per those who hold mitzvot need no intention he does not fulfill, as Ran proves per Ra'ah who holds mitzvot generally need no intention, explicit in Ra'ah in Yavin Shemuah to Rashbatz; know R' Yerucham in name of Tosafot disagrees; per my view if we say mitzvot need no intention, even if he thought it was chametz he fulfills. For practical law we do not lighten this since we rule mitzvot require intention; only because we find Rambam citing Abba Shmuel — we must distinguish eating is different and suffices to lighten only for what we find explicitly, not when he did not know today is Pesach or this is not matza, for perhaps enjoyment of throat does not help there.

(ג) He did not fulfill — see Chok Yaakov: even for maror if he did not know it was maror or thought it was chametz he fulfills; his main view maror d'rabbanan never needs intention; per what we wrote siman 60 Mishna Berurah in name of poskim one should not distinguish — same for maror; even Chok Yaakov who is lenient on maror because d'rabbanan nevertheless on second Yom Tov also d'rabbanan he agrees not to lighten on matza when he thinks it is not matza lest they treat it lightly.
