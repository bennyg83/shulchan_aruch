If he ate matzah without intention, etc. Marginal note: behold, in siman 60 the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Radbaz that a rabbinic mitzvah, according to everyone, does not require intention. And behold, at first glance this is puzzling to me from the Gemara of Pesachim folio 114, where Reish Lakish says there, "This tells us that mitzvos require intention"; and it is difficult for me, for the Mishnah there deals with nowadays when there is no Pesach, as it says there that they bring before him two cooked foods, namely one as a remembrance of the Pesach and one as a remembrance of the chagigah; and it also concludes, "and in the Temple they bring before him the body of the Pesach," which proves that initially it deals with nowadays; and if so, maror nowadays according to everyone is rabbinic; and see at the end of chapter Arvei Pesachim; and if so, why is intention needed? And the Gemara concludes that it is a tannaitic dispute; see there. And possibly the Gemara speaks only of something that is essentially rabbinic; but something that is essentially according to the Torah, only that nowadays it is rabbinic, is like a Torah [mitzvah] and requires intention. And while on this subject, a further difficulty for me is from what the Gemara there challenges Reish Lakish from the beraisa that if he ate them without intending, he fulfilled; and it is difficult, for in the Yerushalmi on this Mishnah there it challenges Reish Lakish, who requires intention, from [the ruling that] if he ate matzah without intention he fulfilled, and it answers: here, since Chizkiyah concludes here, and according to the commentary of Korban HaEdah, that there is certainly a presumption that he intended at first, but maror was not eaten while reclining, therefore it requires intention; see there. If so, what does the Gemara here challenge from the beraisa that if he ate them without intending he fulfilled? Granted that the plural wording "ate them" proves it refers even to maror; but what is the difficulty? Let us say it follows Hillel, who wraps them at once, so that he also ate the maror while reclining, and therefore he holds that he fulfills; while Reish Lakish deals according to the Rabbanan, who eat maror alone without reclining, and therefore one can say he did not fulfill. However, this would not be difficult, for one can say that specifically regarding matzah, if he did not intend for the sake of the mitzvah, why would he recline? From this it is proven that he intended; but when he ate them together, there is no proof from reclining either, for one can say the reclining was because of the matzah, and therefore this reclining does not help also for maror; and therefore even when he ate them together, maror requires intention. But according to the Radbaz's view it is properly difficult: since regarding a rabbinic [mitzvah] intention is not needed, then what is the challenge? Perhaps Reish Lakish's reason that nowadays maror requires intention is not because it is essentially according to the Torah, but because he then also eats the matzah, and matzah requires intention, and if so maror too requires intention, so that people not come to treat lightly also a rabbinic [mitzvah], as the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Radbaz in siman 184 in a similar vein. And if so, fine for us, who eat the maror by itself and the matzah by itself: granted that regarding matzah intention is not needed because of eating while reclining, in any event from the reclining it is proven that he intends; so if he did not intend for maror, people come to treat a rabbinic [mitzvah] lightly. But the beraisa follows Hillel, so that he eats both together while reclining, and then his actions do not prove that maror is inferior to matzah so as not to require intention; so granted that in truth reclining does not help as intention regarding maror, in any event treating a rabbinic [mitzvah] lightly does not apply, since there is no distinction of maror more than matzah; therefore we do not require intention for maror since it is rabbinic. And this requires further study for now; examine carefully. And behold, regarding the law if he ate matzah of other species that had in them [enough] within the time of eating a peras, and we hold that he fulfills: if he intended to fulfill with those other species and did not intend to fulfill with a kezayit of the five species within it, did he fulfill or not? At first glance it would seem, according to what is written in Rosh Hashanah regarding one who blew as one merely busying himself, that even according to the opinion that mitzvos do not require intention, in that case he did not fulfill, for this is worse. But according to the truth I wrote in a responsum to the holy community of Iasi, which found its place in Be'er Heitev, siman 448, in my composition on Orach Chaim of the year 5601: there I wrote to prove the opposite, that even according to the opinion that mitzvos require intention he fulfills in this case, for this is counted as intention; see there, and examine carefully.
