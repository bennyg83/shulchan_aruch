There, seif 4. "If one ate matzah without intent, etc." NB: In siman 60, the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Radbaz that according to all views a rabbinic mitzvah does not require intent. At first glance this is astonishing to me from Pesachim 114, where Reish Lakish says: this teaches that mitzvot require intent. This is difficult, for the Mishnah there speaks in our time, when there is no Pesach offering, as it says there that they bring before him two cooked dishes, one as a remembrance of the Pesach and one as a remembrance of the Chagigah, and it concludes that in the Temple they would bring before him the body of the Pesach. This proves that at first it speaks in our time. If so, maror in our time is rabbinic according to all views; see the end of Arvei Pesachim. Why, then, would intent be required? The Gemara concludes that this is a tannaitic dispute; see there. Perhaps the Gemara says this only about something whose essence is rabbinic, but something whose essence is Torah law and is only rabbinic in our time is like Torah law and requires intent. While on this, I find another difficulty from the Gemara there, which asks against Reish Lakish from a baraita that if one ate them without intent he fulfilled the obligation. But in the Yerushalmi on that Mishnah, it asks against Reish Lakish, who requires intent, from the statement that if one ate matzah without intent he fulfilled, and it answers that there, since Chizkiyah concludes so, and according to the Korban HaEdah this means there is a presumption that he intended initially; but maror is not eaten reclining, so it requires intent. If so, what does our Gemara ask from the baraita that if one ate them without intent he fulfilled? Although the plural "ate them" proves that it refers also to maror, what is the question? Let us say it follows Hillel, who wraps them together, and if so he ate the maror too while reclining; therefore it holds he fulfilled. Reish Lakish, however, speaks according to the Rabbis, where one eats maror by itself without reclining, and therefore one may say he did not fulfill. This, however, is not difficult, for one can say that specifically with matzah, if he did not intend for the mitzvah, why would he recline? From this it is proven that he intended. But when he ate them together, the reclining is no proof, for one may say the reclining was because of the matzah, and therefore this reclining does not help for the maror. Thus even when eating them together, intent for maror is required. According to the Radbaz, however, the difficulty remains: since rabbinic mitzvot do not require intent, why does the Gemara ask? Perhaps Reish Lakish's intent is that in our time maror requires intent not because its root is Torah law, but because he is then also eating matzah, and matzah requires intent; therefore maror too requires intent, so that one not come to treat rabbinic mitzvot lightly, as the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Radbaz in siman 184 regarding something similar. If so, this is true for us, who eat maror separately and matzah separately. Although matzah does not need intent because he eats it reclining, in any event the reclining proves that he intends. If he did not intend with maror, he would come to treat rabbinic mitzvot lightly. But the baraita can follow Hillel, who eats both together while reclining; there, his act does not show that maror is lesser than matzah and does not require intent. Therefore, although in truth the reclining does not help as intent for maror, nevertheless there is no treating of rabbinic mitzvot lightly, since there is no distinction visible in maror more than in matzah. Therefore maror would not require intent, because it is rabbinic. This requires study for now. Examine carefully. Regarding the law of one who ate matzah made from other species that contained a kezayit within kedei achilat peras, where we hold that he fulfills the obligation: if he intended to fulfill with the other species and did not intend to fulfill with the kezayit of the five species contained in it, did he fulfill or not? At first glance it would appear, based on what is written in Rosh Hashanah regarding one who blows as mitasek, that even according to the view that mitzvot do not require intent, in such a case he has not fulfilled, for this is worse. But in truth I wrote in a responsum to the holy community of Iasi, placed in Hilchot Pesach siman 448 in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5601, where I proved the opposite: even according to the view that mitzvot require intent, he fulfills in this case, for this is considered intent. See there and examine carefully.
