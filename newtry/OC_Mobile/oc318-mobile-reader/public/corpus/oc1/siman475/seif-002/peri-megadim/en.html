(א) And he eats — Taz. Behold in Berakhot 39b: on Pesach one places the broken inside the whole and breaks; there are three explanations: (1) Rosh Pesachim 116 — motzi on the whole requiring lechem mishneh with the third, and eating matzah on the broken as an impoverished person's way; therefore three matzot needed. (2) Gemara Yerushalmi in Semag — motzi on the broken as impoverished; do not make bundles of bundles; not fitting to bless two blessings on the broken — bless eating matzah on the whole; and Beit Yosef wrote Tosafot wrote thus — see Tosafot Berakhot 39b s.v. hakol. (3) Appears Rif's view in Beit Yosef — on the broken bless both motzi and eating matzah; so Ran; see Tosafot Pesachim on 'what' — therefore Rif on Pesach breaks on one and broken presumably places broken on whole as Berakhot states on Pesach — placed inside whole, meaning on the whole, within it, so it appears he breaks on the whole; nevertheless two blessings refer to the broken per Rif and Yerushalmi; only two matzot needed — one whole and one broken; for us three matzot — two wholes for lechem mishneh and broken in middle; doubtful whether motzi or eating matzah on it; at motzi hold all three, then discard lower third, bless eating matzah on both broken and upper — doubtful; Mechaber ruled eat a kezayit from each — doubtful whether eating matzah on whole or broken; l'chatchila put both in mouth — two kezayit together; not only for Rosh that whole is motzi and broken eating matzah — motzi eats first since he blessed on it earlier and after filling his appetite eats kezayit matzah as Bach wrote; and even per Mechaber who is doubtful, eat both together, for impossible to know which he eats with eating matzah blessing — must eat two kezayit within time of eating a meal portion; if cannot eat quickly, still two kezayit within meal portion time; nevertheless eat from motzi first, for there too he fulfills eating matzah — Bach and Perishah; but from Levush it implies both are eaten together because both are one mitzvah — motzi is not without matzah and matzah not without motzi — see there.

(ב) Maharal of Prague challenged: all not in one gulp even in one gulp does not apply — see Tur he eats together; if eats HaMotzi first without intending eating matzah likewise not in one gulp; see Beit Yosef; already explained; appears since Taz aimed at Levush as written.
