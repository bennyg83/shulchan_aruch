At the first dipping, he recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. Magen Avraham writes that this applies only if he takes a species of maror that is ordinarily eaten raw. Over something not ordinarily eaten raw, such as horseradish, one should recite only the blessing on eating maror, just as no blessing is recited over pepper, as explained in siman 202. In truth, however, the Gemara and all the poskim clearly imply that Borei Pri HaAdamah is recited over every species of maror, even one not eaten raw. The reason must be that through dipping in vinegar or the like it becomes edible. Alternatively, these blessings over enjoyment also function here as blessings over the mitzvah, as explained below in siman 484. One therefore recites Borei Pri HaAdamah and the blessing on eating maror over it at the time of dipping. This is simple and clear. See above, siman 205.
