At the first dipping, Borei peri ha'adamah. The view of the Magen Avraham is that if of the kinds of maror he has only tamcha, which among us is called horseradish (chrein), he does not bless a blessing of enjoyment on it at all, only "al achilas maror," and this is so even in our case, where he eats it before the meal, because it is not the way to eat it as it is. And Chok Yaakov wrote that he does bless on it, since it is fit to eat with vinegar and the like [and so wrote the Chasam Sofer in his glosses to siman 473; see there]; and according to his view one should properly bless on it also Borei peri ha'adamah for this reason. And in truth the Magen Avraham's words are somewhat difficult: for if it is not fit for eating at all, then why do we fulfill with it the obligation of maror, according to what we wrote above in siman 473 in the name of the poskim, that for maror we require something fit for eating [and possibly, because it is fit for eating by means of some preparation, it is called an edible item]? And all the more so according to our custom today, that we eat it grated, it is properly called fit for eating; and we see that many people dip their bread in it all year; and if so, at the very least it does not leave the category of Shehakol, as above in siman 205 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 5; see there. And it is also possible that since the Torah obligated him to eat it on this night, it is significant eating, for the Torah counted it as eating it as it is; and therefore one should bless on it the blessing fitting for it, namely Borei peri ha'adamah; and in any event one must certainly bless Shehakol on it. And so the Gaon R' Akiva Eiger tends in his novellae; see there.
