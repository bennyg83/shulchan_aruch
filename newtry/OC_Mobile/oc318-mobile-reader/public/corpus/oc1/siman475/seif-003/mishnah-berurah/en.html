(29) If he swallowed matzah, he fulfilled. After the fact, for this too is called eating even though he did not chew it and did not sense the taste of matzah. And even though above in siman 461, se'if 4, we rule that one does not fulfill with cooked [matzah], because the taste of matzah was nullified, which implies that the taste of matzah is required: one can say that there it is worse because the bread itself lost the taste of matzah, but here it has taste, only that he did not sense the taste in his mouth. And nevertheless all this is after the fact; but ab initio he should chew it until he senses the taste in his mouth.
(30) For we require the taste of maror. That he sense the bitterness in his mouth, as a remembrance of "and they made their lives bitter."
(31) The obligation of matzah he fulfilled. And we do not say that the maror nullifies the matzah, as above in s.k. 16; for nullification applies only when he chews them and they mix together, but here, since he did not chew them, each one stands by itself.
(32) In siv. That is the bark that grows around the date palm.
(33) Because there is no, etc. And if he wrapped the matzah in a food item and swallowed, he fulfilled, even though there is a separation between the matzah and his throat, since both are kinds of food and that is the manner of eating [Peri Chadash].
