(There, se'if 5.) If he ate a kezayit of matzah, etc. Marginal note: behold, the later authorities were in doubt whether, if a person has only a half-measure of matzah or the like, he is obligated to eat it or not. And the crux of the doubt is whether the mitzvah of eating applies to a half-measure or not. And it appears to resolve this doubt from the words of Tosafos in Zevachim folio 97b, s.v. "and one bone that has no marrow," and these are their words there: that it is not possible with a glowing coal because of loss of consecrated [matter]; if so he does not fulfill the positive commandment "and they shall eat the flesh on this night," for it is impossible that the glowing coal not burn a little of the marrow inside; and even Abaye, who explains there because of cracking, is not because he holds that a glowing coal is possible without burning the marrow, but rather he holds that for a small loss of consecrated [matter] we are not concerned here, etc. And if so it is proven that it is only a small loss; and if what burned were the measure of a kezayit it would not be considered a small loss; and perforce it is less than a kezayit; and nevertheless they wrote that the positive commandment of "and they shall eat the flesh on this night" would not be fulfilled; and it is proven that a positive commandment applies even to less than a kezayit; examine carefully. And while on this subject, it is difficult for me what Tosafos meant by this in writing "even according to Abaye": for ultimately, according to Abaye it is possible to fulfill both of them by means of a glowing coal; and perforce, whoever proves that since a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment in the Temple does not hold like Abaye; and what difference does it make whether we say Abaye was not concerned at all for loss of consecrated [matter], or that he admits to this and only holds that it is a small loss? And possibly their intent is that there, where the challenge is only regarding why they are not numbered on the marrow in the thighbone, that it is possible by means of a glowing coal, in that case it is properly only a small loss, only on the thighbone alone. But here, where the challenge is on every bone that has marrow, if he would do so in many places, in combination it would be a significant loss; therefore all agree to this proof that a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment of the Temple. And see what I wrote on this sugya in the elucidation of the Yerushalmi, Pesachim chapter 7, in my composition on Orach Chaim of the year 5609, folio 135; and in my composition on Yoreh De'ah in the addenda Tuv Taam, edition vav; see there, and examine carefully.
