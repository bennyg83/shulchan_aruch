If he paused between one eating and the next longer than the time required to eat a pras. This is the wording of the Gemara in the final chapter of Pesachim. In truth, however, it is explained below in siman 612 from the Gemara, Tosafot, and all the poskim, as cited there by Beit Yosef, that if the period from the beginning of the first eating until the completion of the final eating exceeds the time required to eat a pras, the portions do not combine. The expression “between one eating and the next” is therefore not precise. Magen Avraham writes likewise, and the matter is clear, contrary to Rosh Yosef, who constructs differing opinions regarding this. The Tur and Rokeach, siman 283, appear to have guarded against this problem by writing, “between one eating and the next, the time required to eat a pras.” In my humble opinion, their intent is that together with the eating itself, the total period exceeds the time required to eat a pras. The reason that the Sages said that if the period from the beginning of the eating until the end exceeds this measure the portions do not combine is that there is then a pause of the time required to eat a pras between the first chewing and the last chewing, and therefore they do not combine to form the required measure. The Tur consequently wrote here with precision, “the time required to eat a pras,” while below in siman 612 it is explained that if the period is longer than the time required to eat a pras they do not combine. Both formulations express the same measure, from the beginning until the end of the eating. Here, when it says “between one eating and the next,” the meaning is identical. This is contrary to Rosh Yosef, who writes that the Tur’s wording here is imprecise. The Mechaber did not wish to alter the Gemara’s wording and relied upon siman 612. See there regarding the length of the time required to eat a pras. Taz writes that although one fulfills the obligation whenever he did not pause for that full measure, the optimal fulfillment is to swallow a kezayis of matzah, and likewise of maror when crushed, at one time.
