And he holds them in his hand — Taz. What he wrote 'he should hold both in his hand' — so Tur; and one may say Rosh Pesachim 116a wrote he breaks from the whole and broken as one and did not mention holding two, for per his view requiring lechem mishneh it is fitting to hold all three at motzi, then set aside the third and bless (motzi and) eating matzah on the whole and broken, intending to fulfill eating matzah through one of them as we will say below letter bet (see Maharil; and discarding the third from his hand is correct not to treat it lightly); if so, why did Tur write 'hold both'? And from what Taz wrote afterward it appears after it is not precise — rather hold all three together or bring them with the plate near him; correct to hold all three in hand as stated; and what Taz wrote in the name of Rashal — he challenged him: why bring from siman 476? Tur wrote here to break from both together for one who wishes to fulfill both; Rashal's intent is halacha is certainly like Rosh that on the whole motzi requiring lechem mishneh one holds all three, says motzi, breaks a bit to show the halacha, and remains somewhat connected — see siman 167 in Magen Avraham hei and Ateret Zekenim in the name of Bach and Rashal in Shabbat — one marks with a knife before the blessing — see there; possibly so here too; since still connected it is considered whole; nevertheless correct custom as Chayei Adam in the name of Maharil: hold all three, bless motzi, discard the lower from hand, intend eating matzah fulfilled through upper or broken, eat a kezayit from each since doubtful, intend on both — as stated; unlike one who holds motzi is primarily a kezayit on the whole — need not eat kezayit from motzi; see Darkei Moshe 476 explaining 'he should not break' meaning not separate entirely; and when eating two kezayit together or one after another if unable together, still need haseiba on both since doubtful — explained further letter bet and see 476.
