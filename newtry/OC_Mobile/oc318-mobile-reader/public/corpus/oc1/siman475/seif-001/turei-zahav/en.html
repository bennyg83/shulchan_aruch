(א) He grasps them in his hand. The reason: some poskim bless HaMotzi on the whole and eating matzah on the broken lechem oni; some hold the reverse — therefore he grasps two in his hand and blesses both blessings, then breaks from the whole. Tur wrote 'he takes the plate,' etc.: meaning one should not remove the whole matza from the plate and grasp it, for then it separates from the third matza and we require lechem mishneh as every Yom Tov; rather they remain on the plate and he brings them near, or takes all three matzot in hand, places his hand on them, then removes the whole and breaks, then breaks from the broken; Rashal wrote first bless HaMotzi and break the whole without fully breaking it first, then grasp the second and bless on eating matzah and break from both — end; I do not know this breaking first without fully breaking; also why divide blessings HaMotzi on whole and eating matzah on broken — he holds one view; but Tur's meaning is fulfill both views, say two blessings consecutively while still on the plate, then break them sequentially; so Tur siman 486 that breaking should be as one.

(ב) He eats them leaning together. Because both blessings relate to each other; even if he had only one matza, we do not make bundled mitzvot — therefore he takes one matza per blessing so eating is as one, as if truly one matza. Maharal of Prague challenged: we say chapter HaIsh Mekadesh whatever is not sequential even in one gulp does not apply — I did not understand the question, for here both are truly one body as explained, unlike HaIsh Mekadesh. This also resolves Maaseh Choshev: why kezayit for HaMotzi — answered siman 167 we do not require kezayit for HaMotzi eating; no question, for here both blessings fall on two matzot without division, both as one eating. One could say: why two kezayit — one suffices from both; answer: since doing so to fulfill both views, each bears name HaMotzi and eating matzah — must eat kezayit from each for eating matzah; otherwise Tur brought first bless HaMotzi on whole and eating matzah on broken yet eat kezayit from each — requires study; also per view that enactment is whatever he eats at that time both blessings apply as one — if he eats kezayit from one and less from other, eating unequal — at start two mitzvot, at end one — as I explained.

(ג) He eats the HaMotzi one first. So Tur siman 486. It appears this applies only to first Tur view blessing HaMotzi on whole and broken for eating matzah — then we know which is HaMotzi and it precedes if cannot eat together; per later words blessing both together — no distinction which is for matza or HaMotzi, therefore Shulchan Aruch and Tur should not write this per that view; only since whole is before him first lest violate mitzvot he eats it first; therefore if he removes broken from hand and only broken remains in hand he should not set it aside but take broken from whole — for this too violates mitzvot in my view.

(ד) And submerge it entirely in charoset. Due to kapa — meaning a worm species inside dies in charoset.

(ה) Third matza. To fulfill one mitzvah in it.

(ו) And dip in charoset. Hillel dipped in charoset, for his main maror mitzvah was here — Hillel held 'on matzot and maror they shall eat it' means all together; Rabanan hold each separately; if fulfilling both views why not take charoset for wrapping? Perhaps Rama brings Avi Ezri in Tur. Tur wrote Rashi and Rosh agreed to dip in charoset; so Rashal ruled; Maharal of Prague practiced; though written in customs and Rama, I saw custom not to dip wrapping in charoset; Maaseh Choshev wrote not to practice so — correct to dip wrapping in charoset too; Avi Ezri: we find only two dippings not three; Rama end siman 476 — one can say two charoset dippings count as one, for we do not do so except from doubt.

(ז) So blessing on eating matzah applies, etc. Meaning for Hillel this is main mitzvah here, therefore related to eating matzah blessing; appears interruption only in unnecessary speech, but necessary matters like 'take wrap' are not interruption; seemingly if he spoke between blessing and wrapping he must return and bless HaMotzi and eating matzah like HaMotzi rest of year siman 167 seif 6; here too must fulfill Hillel for we say in Gemara now law not stated like either — act with stringencies of both; Tur wrote one wishing optimal mitzvah should not speak until wrapping — implies b'dieved no need return and bless.
