And he should grasp them. All three of them: the upper one for HaMotzi, and the third for lechem mishneh, and the broken piece for the title of lechem oni; and he should bless HaMotzi, and set aside the third so that it slips from his hand, and bless over the broken piece while still grasping the upper one "al achilas matzah," and recite both blessings before he breaks them. Maharil and the later authorities.

With salt. And so it is in the writings.
HaMotzi. See what I wrote above in siman 472, s.k. 8. And the Magen Avraham wrote: in any event, ab initio one should swallow a crushed kezayit at once; and after the fact, even if he eats little by little, provided he does not delay between them the time of eating a peras; and likewise regarding maror, even ab initio if it is hard for him to swallow it together; end of his words.
In charoses. To kill the venom within it; and he does not bless until after the dipping, before eating.
Taste. And likewise one should not soak the maror overnight in water, for that is pickled [and loses its bitterness]; Magen Avraham in siman 473, s.k. 14. And Chok Yaakov is lenient on this; see there.
Reclining. And if he wishes to recline, he is permitted.
In charoses. And so ruled the Bach, the Maharal of Prague, the Taz, Chok Yaakov, and the later authorities, to dip in charoses; and the Magen Avraham wrote: where the custom is practiced, follow the custom.
Like Hillel. Our wording is thus: so Hillel did when the Temple stood; he would wrap matzah and maror and eat, etc. And the Taz wrote that one should say he would wrap Pesach, matzah, and maror and eat, etc., and so Rashi explained, and so wrote the Arizal. And Chok Yaakov wrote that nowadays, when there is no Pesach, one should not mention Pesach, for how can one say a falsehood, since he has no Pesach; therefore one should not corrupt our wording; see there.
Divert [by speaking]. And if he spoke, he need not go back and bless again. Bach, Taz, Magen Avraham.
