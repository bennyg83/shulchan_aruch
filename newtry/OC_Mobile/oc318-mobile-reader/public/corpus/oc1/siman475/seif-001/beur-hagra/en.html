Seif 1: He takes the matzot and holds them. See Magen Avraham, and what was stated above in siman 473. This follows the Gemara’s instruction to hold two, and so forth.

He recites the blessing, and afterward. This follows the first opinion in Tosafot, Berachot 39b, that HaMotzi is recited over the whole matzah and Al Achilat Matzah over the broken one, which is the bread of affliction. Both blessings should not be recited over the broken matzah, because mitzvot should not be bundled together. See Pesachim 102b. Rabbeinu Menachem recited both blessings over the broken matzah and maintained that this is not considered bundling mitzvot, just as one recites both Borei Pri HaGafen and Kiddush over the cup, and just as one who has only chazeret recites both Borei Pri HaAdamah and Al Achilat Maror at the first dipping. Ri avoided the dispute by acting as ruled here. Rosh in Arvei Pesachim and in his Pesach order follows the first view, while also writing that one should act so as to avoid the dispute. See Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai.

Afterward he breaks them. As stated there, he breaks one whole matzah together with the broken matzah.

The upper one. As stated in siman 274.

He dips them in salt. The Yerushalmi states that he dips it in salt.

This is not the custom. As stated in Berachot 40a.

He eats them while reclining. See Pesachim 108a.

Together. If he first eats the matzah over which HaMotzi was recited, he has already fulfilled his obligation with it, although the obligation properly relates to the broken matzah, as explained above. See Bach.

If he cannot. Ideally, the entire olive-sized portion must be eaten together, as indicated by Pesachim 114b, which states that if one ate it in halves he fulfilled his obligation only after the fact.

Afterward. See Pesachim 115a.

The third matzah. See Pesachim 117b and Yerushalmi, Shekalim 3:4, regarding the contribution from the three collection boxes: he takes from the first, and even if some remains there he proceeds to the second; he takes from the second, and even if some remains there he proceeds to the third. When the third is depleted, he returns to the second, and so forth.

In charoset. Because of the maror; Hillel necessarily acted in this manner.

Some say. They maintain that there is no concern of harmful residue, as the Yerushalmi states that anything containing bread is not subject to that concern. See Hagahot Maimoniyot.

While reclining. This is because of the matzah. Maror itself does not require reclining, as stated there. Hillel necessarily acted in this manner.

From the time he recites the blessing, so that. See Taz, for according to Hillel one fulfills the obligation only with this combination, and an interruption would therefore be problematic.
