1) [Se'if 1] One washes one's hands, etc. Although one washed for the first dipping, Pesachim 115b teaches that one washes again and recites the blessing Al Netilas Yadayim because attention was diverted while reading the Haggadah. Mordechai writes: 'It appears to me that if one intended during the first washing that it should cover the entire meal, without diverting attention, one need not wash again.' Beis Yosef responds that one should not form this intention, so as not to negate the enactment of the Sages to wash twice on the night of Pesach. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, cites this and writes that Beis Yosef's words are correct and this is the custom; Bach and Magen Avraham challenged him without cause. See there. Chok Yosef, subsection 1, responds to Chok Yaakov and supports Mordechai, Bach, and Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, he concludes that one should not act this way, as Bach himself concludes, because one should not interrupt between washing and HaMotzi. One would also have to recite the blessing immediately and intend that the washing is for eating, and not everyone knows these laws. The custom is therefore to wash twice. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, likewise writes that initially it is improper to act this way.
2) Regarding the practical law, although initially we wash twice, in pressing circumstances when water is unavailable, or for an ill or elderly person who suffers from the cold, one may rely upon Mordechai, who is a great authority. It is possible that Rabbeinu Beis Yosef agrees in such a case, provided that the first washing is performed according to all its laws and with a blessing. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1.
3) There. One washes one's hands, etc. Even if one did not divert attention from guarding one's hands while reciting the Haggadah and Hallel, one must nevertheless wash again for the meal, without a blessing. When washing for dipping a vegetable, one did not intend that the washing should also cover the meal. A washing performed for food dipped in liquid does not cover eating bread unless one specifically intended this at the time of washing, as stated in siman 158. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. See se'if 7 there and our words there, with the help of Heaven. Nevertheless, as stated above, it is initially improper to avoid diverting attention from the hands for this purpose.
4) There. And one recites Al Netilas Yadayim. Regarding the intention for this washing, see above, siman 473, subsection 108.
5) There. One holds them in one's hand, etc. Some authorities maintain that HaMotzi is recited over the whole matzah and Al Achilas Matzah over the broken matzah, which is poor person's bread. Other authorities reverse this. The Shulchan Aruch therefore writes that one should hold all of them while reciting the blessings, thereby satisfying all opinions, as explained in Tur and Beis Yosef. See there. The Arizal's opinion agrees with those who recite HaMotzi over the whole matzah and Al Achilas Matzah over the broken matzah, as stated above in siman 473, subsection 117. See there for the mystical rationale. This is also the opinion of the later authorities, as stated in the following subsection.
6) There. One holds them in one's hand, etc. One holds all three so that there are two complete loaves and a broken piece representing poor person's bread. Magen Avraham, subsection 2. Derashos Maharil, order of the Haggadah, likewise writes: 'One should hold the three matzos. The upper one is for HaMotzi, the third provides the second whole loaf, and the broken one represents poor person's bread. One recites HaMotzi, lets the third matzah slip from the hand, and while holding the broken piece together with the upper matzah recites Al Achilas Matzah. Both blessings should be recited before breaking them.' Chok Yaakov, subsection 2, cites this and writes that the later authorities agree. Shelah, page 142b, likewise writes that this satisfies every opinion and doubt. See there. Mateh Moshe, siman 645; Chok Yosef, subsection 2; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsections 4 and 5; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Tzav, subsection 34; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2, write likewise.
7) There. One recites HaMotzi and Al Achilas Matzah, etc. The reason for this order is that when a frequent mitzvah and an infrequent mitzvah coincide, the frequent one takes precedence. Peri Chadash. The intentions for the matzos were already written above in siman 473, subsection 117; take them from there. One should also intend the mystical intention associated with food, as stated above in siman 157, subsection 20. See there. One should further intend while eating to fulfill the Torah's positive commandment, 'In the evening you shall eat matzos.'
