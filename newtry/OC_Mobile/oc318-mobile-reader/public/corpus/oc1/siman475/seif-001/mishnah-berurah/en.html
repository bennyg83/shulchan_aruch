(1) He should wash his hands, etc. That is to say: even though he washed for the vegetables, as written in siman 473, se'if 6, nevertheless, since in between he said the Haggadah and Hallel, we are concerned that he diverted his attention and touched an unclean place, for the hands are busy.
(2) And he should grasp them in his hand. All three of them: the upper and the lower for lechem mishneh, and the broken piece for the title of lechem oni; and he should bless HaMotzi, and set aside the third so that it slips from his hand, and bless over the broken piece while grasping the upper one "al achilas matzah," and recite both blessings before he breaks them, as the Mechaber concludes, "and afterward," etc.
(3) From both of them together. For ab initio one must eat from both at once, as below; and therefore it is proper that the breaking also be at once, and not one after the other, so that there not be an interruption between the first breaking and the eating.
(4) And the custom is not to dip them, etc. That is to say: even though throughout the days of Pesach one dips in salt, even though it is clean and by law does not need dipping, as explained above in siman 167, se'if 5, nevertheless on the first nights of Pesach they are not accustomed to do so, for it appears more as lechem oni when it is not dipped in salt.
(5) On the first night. That is to say: on the first nights.
(6) And he should eat them, etc. Because he blessed two blessings and his intention was on the whole one and on the broken piece, therefore he must eat from both of them.
(7) While reclining. See above in siman 472, se'if 7, and in the gloss there.
(8) Together. For if he first eats a kezayit of the HaMotzi, namely from the whole one, he is like one who interrupts between the blessing "al achilas matzah" and eating the broken piece on which that blessing stands; and if he eats the broken piece first and afterward the whole one, he is also like one who interrupts, according to those poskim who hold that the blessing "al achilas matzah" stands on the whole one; and therefore he should eat them together.
(9) A kezayit from each one. From the broken piece he certainly needs a kezayit, for we bless "al achilas matzah," and there is no eating less than a kezayit; but as for the HaMotzi piece, we hold that one blesses HaMotzi even on less than a kezayit, as above in siman 210. Rather, because there are poskim who hold that the blessing HaMotzi stands on the broken piece and the blessing "al achilas matzah" stands on the whole one, therefore he needs a kezayit from each. And the later authorities agreed that he need only put the two olive-bulks into his mouth at once and crush them, but he is not obligated to swallow them at once; rather it suffices that he swallow about a kezayit at once and afterward swallow the rest. And after the fact, even if he swallowed the kezayit little by little, he fulfilled, provided that from the beginning of his eating until its end he did not delay more than the time of eating a peras.
(10) Of the HaMotzi first. That is the whole upper one, which by the primary law has the HaMotzi blessing standing on it; and after the fact it is not considered an interruption that he eats it between the blessing of eating matzah and eating the broken piece, since it comes for the need of that kezayit, namely for the need of the HaMotzi blessing that one must also bless over the kezayit of the broken piece. And the poskim wrote that one must also eat the first kezayit of HaMotzi while reclining.
(11) And afterward of eating matzah. And after the fact, if he ate one kezayit, whether from the whole one or from the broken piece, he fulfilled.
(12) A kezayit of maror. And even if he ate a whole stalk with the leaves as its natural whole form, he did not fulfill so long as it does not contain a kezayit [Terumas HaDeshen].
(13) And he should submerge it entirely. In order to kill the venom within it. And there are places where they are not accustomed to submerge it entirely, but only to dip part of it.
(14) And he should eat it without reclining. For it is a remembrance of bondage; and nevertheless, if he wishes to eat while reclining, he is permitted.
(15) The third matzah. In order to fulfill a mitzvah with all three of them.
(16) And he wraps it with the maror. And he needs a kezayit of matzah and a kezayit of maror. And the reason for this wrapping is to fulfill the view of Hillel, who holds that the verse "upon matzos and bitter herbs they shall eat it" implies together. Nevertheless, to fulfill only thereby the eating of matzah and the eating of maror is impossible nowadays even according to Hillel, for since maror nowadays, when there is no Pesach, is only rabbinic, therefore when he eats them together the taste of maror comes and nullifies the taste of matzah, which is according to the Torah even nowadays. And for this reason he must first eat each one separately, and afterward he eats both together, in order to make a remembrance of the time when the Temple stood and they offered the Pesach, and then according to Hillel's view they would eat matzah and maror together.
(17) And he dips it in charoses. For this wrapping is a remembrance of the Temple according to Hillel, and Hillel would fulfill the mitzvah of charoses with this maror in the wrapping, for he would not eat maror at all before this wrapping; and here too one must shake off the charoses, as with the maror he ate earlier [Maamar Mordechai].
(18) That one should not dip it. Their reason is that he already fulfilled the mitzvah of charoses at the first dipping, and also one need not fear the venom in this maror since he eats it with matzah in a wrap.
(19) And so I have seen practiced. See the later authorities who agreed that the primary opinion is the first one; and nevertheless, where the custom is practiced, follow the custom.
(20) And he says. See Biur Halacha.
(21) Like Hillel. And the wording among us in the Haggadah is: so Hillel did when the Temple stood, etc.; he would wrap matzah and maror and eat them together. And some wrote that one should say: he would wrap Pesach, matzah, and maror.
(22) And he eats them together. See above, s.k. 9, where we wrote that he need not swallow them together, only put them into his mouth at once; and the same applies here.
(23) While reclining. Because of the matzah that he eats.
(24) In order that it apply. That is to say: for we make a remembrance of the Temple, and then the primary mitzvah was only with wrapping; therefore one must not interrupt, so that the blessing apply to the wrapping. And nevertheless this is only ab initio; and after the fact, if he spoke in between, he need not go back and bless over the wrapping.
