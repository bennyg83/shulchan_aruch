(א) He washes his hands — see Mishna Berurah; and it is written in Shibolei HaLeket: since the reason depends on distraction, if it is clear to him that he guarded his hands well and did not touch sacred texts or other things that defile hands, he need not wash again, for his hands are pure from the first washing; and if he washed he should not bless lest it be a blessing in vain — end; and similarly Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Mordechai; and although for practical law we certainly must wash again, as above siman 158 seif zayin, and especially when he did not intend from the start for eating — in any case he should not bless, as explained there; and the correct approach is that in this manner he should defile his hands before washing so he can bless.

(ב) And he blesses motzi and on eating matzah, and afterward, etc. — the reason is because there is a dispute: some poskim hold one blesses motzi on the whole and eating matzah on the broken piece, and some hold the reverse; therefore the Mechaber ruled he should hold both in his hand [the lower alone for lechem mishneh] and bless motzi and on eating matzah and break both.

(ג) A kezayit from each — see Mishna Berurah on this reason, words of commentators. However fundamentally it is very puzzling: per all this we derive something new that on the first night one must eat two kezayit and we do not find this anywhere; all poskim requiring three matzot for Seder did not mention this except Rosh and Mordechai; in Talmud only kezayit of sun that ate kezayit is mentioned; though possible to say main measure is kezayit and b'dieved one fulfills — nevertheless such a matter the Talmud should explain; truly even those requiring three matzot it is only for lechem mishneh; requiring intention in first blessing on one matza and second on other matza is because then matzot are bundles — but both matzot are one matter like Shabbat lechem mishneh; fundamentally one may eat whichever he wishes, only because he intended each matza in blessing it is good to taste each; certainly one kezayit combined from both suffices, especially per poskim' explanation and Mechaber's ruling holding both and intending both blessings together — all the more one kezayit from both suffices; R' Yerucham citing his teacher Rosh also only said eat from both, not two kezayit — requires study.

(ד) And says zecher lemikdash — difficult: this statement is interruption between blessing and wrapping; Mechaber himself concludes one should not speak so blessing on eating matzah applies, etc. Forced to say this statement too is meal-related like 'take and bless.' Truly no poskim have this wording Mechaber wrote; they only wrote this wrapping is zecher lemikdash but did not say one says zecher lemikdash; perhaps Mechaber imprecisely wrote 'and says and eats' meaning one must begin eating first then say this Haggadah; lest I say Mechaber should read 'and dips in charoset zecher lemikdash keHillel' per Talmud and all poskim; this 'and says' is extra language — requires study.
