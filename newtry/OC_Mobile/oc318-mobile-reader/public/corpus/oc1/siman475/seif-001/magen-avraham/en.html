He should wash his hands. And even though he already washed for the vegetables, as written at the beginning of siman 473, nevertheless, since he recited the Haggadah, we are concerned that he diverted his attention and touched an unclean place (Gemara). And the Mordechai wrote that if at the first washing he intended to finish his entire meal with it without diverting attention, he need not wash a second time; end of his words. And it appears to me that one should not intend this, so as not to nullify the Sages' enactment who enacted washing twice on this night (Beis Yosef). And I do not know where we find this enactment, for we say: one who washed his hands at the first dipping must wash his hands at the second dipping, and the reason is written alongside it: lest he divert his attention; and if so, when he did not divert attention he need not wash. However, he needed to bless immediately at the first washing; see siman 158, se'if 7, that he must intend that he washes for eating; and see there, se'if 2.
And he should grasp them in his hand. All three of them, so that there be lechem mishneh and a broken piece because of lechem oni (Bach, Maharshal, Shelah).
The upper one. Even if [Pesach] falls on Shabbos, so wrote in Darkei Moshe; see siman 274.
A kezayit from each one. For the broken piece of HaMotzi also requires a kezayit, and so is implied in the Tur; see what I wrote in siman 167 at the end of se'if 1. These are the words of Darkei Moshe: in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 139, it is written that one must eat the kezayit together and swallow, and so wrote Beis Yosef in the name of Tosafos; end of his words. From his wording it is implied that it suffices when he swallows one kezayit at once. And so is implied in Terumas HaDeshen there, who wrote on that which we say one wraps all three at once: the Mordechai asks how the throat can hold so much, for a kezayit is half an egg; and one can say that when it reaches the throat it is crushed, and therefore it holds so much. From here it is proven that one should not swallow the olive-bulk of matzah little by little, for otherwise there would be no difficulty at all, since he could well put all three olive-bulks into his mouth together, chew them together, and swallow little by little; rather, it is a mitzvah to swallow them together for Hillel, and the same applies for us for one olive-bulk of matzah or of maror; end of his words. It is implied that he holds that it suffices when he swallows one olive-bulk at once. And that which the Shulchan Aruch wrote, that he should eat them together, means that he should put them into his mouth at once. And Maharil wrote that one may eat little by little, provided he does not delay in his eating longer than the time of eating a peras. But in se'if 6 it is implied that only after the fact he fulfilled, but ab initio he must eat together; and so is implied in Chullin at the end of chapter 7, that one must swallow together. However, regarding maror one may rely on Maharil if it is hard for him to swallow together; and see siman 172 at the end of se'if 9.
A kezayit of maror. And even if he ate the whole plant intact, it is not called a "whole item" so as to fulfill with less than a kezayit (as written in siman 210), for one does not fulfill with the roots (Piskei Maharai, siman 245).
