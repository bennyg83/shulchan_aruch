And likewise. See Taz, who agrees that one should not recite a blessing on Chol HaMoed. Furthermore, if he recites a blessing, it appears that he intends to perform the mitzvah. If Chol HaMoed is in fact exempt, he may then violate bal tosif. When he does not recite a blessing and wears them because of doubt, bal tosif does not apply, because he acts only from uncertainty. See Magen Avraham, letter 2. As for the practice of removing them before Hallel, nowadays the custom is to remove them after Kedushah in the repetition of the Amidah. Nevertheless, one must intend to listen to the remainder of the repetition. It appears that Rabbeinu Tam tefillin should not be worn on Chol HaMoed, because the Zohar speaks very severely about the matter. Rashi tefillin suffice, and there is also a double doubt: perhaps Chol HaMoed is exempt, and perhaps the halachah follows Rashi.
And behold. Shabbos is called a sign, as it says, “It is a sign between Me and you,” while concerning tefillin it says, “They shall be as a sign upon your hand.” Through this we are identified as servants of Hashem, for a servant bears the seal of the king. Therefore, on Shabbos there is already another sign. Although the word “sign” is not written explicitly concerning Yom Tov, Yom Tov is called Shabbos. Chol HaMoed, however, is not called Shabbos. See Rashi on Menachos 36b, who explains “they themselves are a sign” based upon the verse “It is a sign,” which refers to Shabbos in Shemos 31:13 and 17. Were I not afraid to suggest it, I would say that the verse, “They shall be for signs and for appointed times,” may provide another source. “Signs” refers to Yamim Tovim, which are called signs, while “appointed times” refers to Rosh Chodesh, which is likewise called a mo'ed. See the laws of Rosh Chodesh, siman 419, in Tur and Levush. The verse would then mean that the luminaries serve as signs for Yamim Tovim and as appointed times for Rosh Chodesh. Rashi explains it as referring to eclipses. Regarding Taz's discussion of reciting the blessing quietly, Eliyah Rabbah explains that this was instituted because of the dispute: some say to recite it and others say not to. It is therefore recited quietly, like “Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.” Levush explains that this demonstrates that Chol HaMoed possesses some degree of sign; see Levush. Whether one may interrupt between the tefillin of the arm and the tefillin of the head on Chol HaMoed is discussed in siman 25, Magen Avraham, letter 17.
