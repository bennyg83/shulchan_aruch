(6) They too are a sign. On Pesach the sign is eating matzah, and on Sukkos it is dwelling in the sukkah. The dissenting opinion holds that since performing melacha on Chol HaMoed is permitted by Torah law, there is no sign.
(7) To put them on during Chol HaMoed. Tefillin should be removed on Chol HaMoed before Hallel. Some now have the practice of removing them after Kedushah during the repetition of Shemoneh Esrei. They must nevertheless be careful to listen attentively to the repetition.
(8) That there is no, etc. Since some put on tefillin and others do not, or some refrain from reciting a blessing, one who does recite the blessing should do so quietly to avoid dispute. Likewise, he should not walk through the public domain wearing them on his way to the synagogue. The later authorities agree with the Taz that it is preferable to put them on without a blessing, because the blessing does not prevent fulfillment and in a doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. This is especially so because the Gra writes that the dissenting view has no primary basis in the Gemara. At least regarding the blessing, one must certainly be stringent. Before putting them on, he should also think: If I am obligated, I put them on for the sake of the mitzvah; if I am not obligated, I do not put them on for the sake of the mitzvah. In this manner he satisfies all opinions. Even according to those who hold that Chol HaMoed is not a time for tefillin, he does not violate adding to a mitzvah, because he does not definitely intend the act as a mitzvah. All the more so, there is no concern that he is degrading the sign of Chol HaMoed, because that concern likewise applies only when he intends the act as a mitzvah. Rabbeinu Tam tefillin should not be worn on Chol HaMoed, according to Peri Megadim. The later authorities also write, as cited in Orach Chaim, that it is improper for some worshippers in one synagogue to put on tefillin while others do not, because of the prohibition against forming divided groups. One who does not ordinarily wear tefillin on Chol HaMoed but prays in a beis midrash where they do should also put them on, without a blessing. A congregation whose custom is to wear tefillin should not change its practice.
