Se'if 2: On Chol HaMoed, etc. This means the signs of matzah and sukkah. Halakhot Gedolot and Sefer HaIttim write likewise in the name of the Gaon. The same appears in Zohar Chadash, page 51c.
And some say, etc. This is based on the statement in Moed Katan, page 19a, that if the tefillin are meant to be put on after the festival, what difference is there whether he writes them for himself or for others? This is no proof at all. Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah follow their own views at the end of Eruvin that Shabbat and Yom Tov are times for tefillin, as is also explained there in Moed Katan. Rosh, in Hilkhot Ketanot, rejects this proof, as do Tosafos in Menachot, page 36b, s.v. yatz'u. See Tosafos in Moed Katan, page 19a, s.v. Rabbi Yose, and Eruvin, page 96a, s.v. yamim. Nor is there any proof from the Yerushalmi cited by Tosafos in Moed Katan there. That discussion follows the opinion that Shabbat is a time for tefillin, as it states there: “The Mishnah disagrees with Rav: A person may write tefillin,” etc., “for himself.” The Yerushalmi then reconciles the Mishnah with Rav by explaining that the Mishnah follows Rabbi Yehudah, who says that one may write, etc., because he holds that Shabbat and Yom Tov are times for tefillin. See Nimukei Yosef on that sugya. This alternative opinion has no basis at all. Tosafos in Eruvin there finds a slight implication from the sugya at the end of the first chapter of Beitzah concerning sending garments, whether sewn, etc., where the Gemara says this includes tefillin; see there. That proof too is incorrect, because the Mishnah's text is corrupt. In the Yerushalmi, in the first chapter of Beitzah, the Mishnah explicitly states, “even though they are not needed for the festival.” The same wording must appear in our Mishnah. If so, the aforementioned sugya provides no implication at all. See there.
