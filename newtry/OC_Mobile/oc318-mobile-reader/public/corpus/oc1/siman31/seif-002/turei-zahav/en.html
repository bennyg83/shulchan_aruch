And this is the custom, etc. It appears that one who puts them on on Chol HaMoed without a blessing acts properly. We rule everywhere that blessings do not prevent fulfillment, while the Zohar speaks very severely concerning the punishment for putting them on on Chol HaMoed. Rosh himself concludes, “One who recites the blessing has not lost,” which implies that there is no absolute obligation to recite it. He did not see the Zohar. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillin, likewise rules in the name of Semak that they should be put on without a blessing, because bal tosif applies only where one intends to add. This appears correct. It further appears that one who puts them on on Chol HaMoed should remove them before Hallel, and certainly before Krias HaTorah, because those observances come because of the festival and express the principal sign. This differs from Rosh Chodesh, where removal is not because of the sign, but because of the sanctity of Kesser. Therefore, on Rosh Chodesh they are removed before Mussaf, as stated in siman 25. The custom recorded by Rama, not to recite the blessing aloud, is astonishing. Does Heaven distinguish between private and public conduct?
