It is forbidden. See Kesef Mishneh, who writes that the commentators disagree concerning Chol HaMoed. Some say that it is included among Yamim Tovim and tefillin are not worn then, while others say that tefillin are worn. He writes that the latter view appears from Rambam's words in chapter 7 of Hilchos Shevisas Yom Tov, where Rambam permits a person to write tefillin for himself on Chol HaMoed; see there. This requires study, because Kesef Mishneh himself writes the opposite there in chapter 7 of Hilchos Shevisas Yom Tov: “If he has nothing to eat.” He explains that Rambam's reason is that he maintains tefillin are not worn on Chol HaMoed, because Chol HaMoed is included within Yom Tov. Thus he attributes to Rambam the view that tefillin are not worn on Chol HaMoed, contrary to what he writes here in Hilchos Tefillin. This requires study. Yad Aharon. See Olas Tamid.
Aloud. Taz writes that this is astonishing. Does Heaven distinguish between private and public conduct? It is therefore proper to put them on without a blessing. Sefer Temim De'im, siman 40, likewise rules that no blessing should be recited. Lechem Chamudos explains that many authorities maintain that one should not recite a blessing on Chol HaMoed. Those who nevertheless wished to preserve the strict law and recite a blessing instituted that it be recited quietly so as not to generate dispute, comparable to the quiet recitation of “Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.” Magen Avraham writes that one should not walk with them in the public domain, but should put them on only in the synagogue. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees: since they are worn publicly in the synagogue, what difference is there if one walks with them in the public domain? The writings of Mahari, siman 107, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 25, state that one who puts on tefillin on Chol HaMoed without a blessing may nevertheless not speak between the tefillin of the arm and the tefillin of the head. This refers to ordinary conversation. He may, however, answer Kaddish or Kedushah. See Magen Avraham in siman 25, seifim katanim 15 and 18. Yad Aharon writes that Magen Avraham overlooked Mahari's words, because Magen Avraham permits one who puts on tefillin on Chol HaMoed without a blessing to answer. This criticism is incorrect. There is a distinction between ordinary conversation and answering Kaddish or Kedushah. It is surprising that he thought Magen Avraham overlooked Mahari's words, for Magen Avraham himself cites them in seif katan 15. On the contrary, Yad Aharon overlooked Magen Avraham's words there.
