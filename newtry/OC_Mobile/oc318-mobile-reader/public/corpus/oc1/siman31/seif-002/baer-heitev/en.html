Forbidden. See Kesef Mishnah who wrote that the commentators disagree on Chol HaMoed: some say they are included among Yom Tov days and one does not don tefillin, and some say one dons; and so it appears from the words of our Rabbi who wrote in chapter 5 of Laws of Resting on Yom Tov that a person writes tefillin for himself, etc., see there. And it requires investigation — for he, may he rest in Eden, wrote the opposite there in chapter 5 of Laws of Resting on Yom Tov: "he has nothing to eat" — it seems his reason is because he holds one does not don tefillin on Chol HaMoed since it is included among Yom Tov, end quote. Behold he wrote per the Rambam's view that one does not don tefillin on Chol HaMoed — the opposite of what is written here in Laws of Tefillin; requires investigation — Yad Aharon. And see Orach Teshuvah.

Aloud. And Taz wrote: it is astonishing before Heaven — who divides between hidden and revealed? Therefore it is proper to don them without a blessing. And so too in Sefer Tamim De'im siman 40 that one should not bless on them; and in Lechem Chamudos he wrote the reason that many disagree not to bless on Chol HaMoed, and those who saw fit to establish the law and bless would bless secretly so as not to come to dispute — a reminder in Shechem. And Magen Avraham wrote that one should not walk with them in reshut ha-rabbim, only don them in beit ha-knesset (and in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he disagrees, for since they don in the synagogue publicly, what difference is there if they walk with them in reshut ha-rabbim). In the writings of Mahari, siman 107, and Beit Yosef brought siman 25 that one who dons tefillin on Chol HaMoed without a blessing — nevertheless it is forbidden to speak and converse between the arm tefillin and the head tefillin, see there — meaning mundane speech. But Kaddish or Kedushah is permitted for those who don on Chol HaMoed without a blessing; see Magen Avraham in siman 25 s"k 15 and s"k 18 and investigate. And the author Yad Aharon wrote that Rabbi Magen Avraham omitted these words of Mahari, because he wrote that one who dons on Chol HaMoed without a blessing may answer Amen — and he did not distinguish that there is a difference between conversing in mundane speech or answering Kaddish and Kedushah. It is astonishing in my eyes how it entered his mind that Magen Avraham omitted Mahari's words — behold Magen Avraham brought them in s"k 15, see there; and on the contrary, from him was omitted what Magen Avraham wrote in s"k 15 — wondrous. And above siman 25 s"k 11.
