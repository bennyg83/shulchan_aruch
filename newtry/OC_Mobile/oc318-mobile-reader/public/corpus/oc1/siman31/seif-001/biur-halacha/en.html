It is forbidden to put them on. See Mishnah Berurah. The source of this law is below in siman 308, seif 4, in Taz and Magen Avraham there. Derech HaChayim rules stringently in the laws of muktzeh that tefillin may be moved only for use of their place or for a permitted use, like any utensil whose primary function is prohibited. Birkei Yosef in this siman implies the same. Chayei Adam, however, implies that they may also be moved so that they do not fall, and the same should apply to prevent theft. Peri Megadim in siman 308, Mishbetzos Zahav, seif katan 3, likewise implies this. The entire issue depends upon whether putting on tefillin on Shabbos and Yom Tov not for the sake of the mitzvah is forbidden. Since, as we shall cite below, the Gra is among those who are lenient in this respect, he is consequently also lenient regarding moving them. Therefore, in pressing circumstances one may certainly be lenient, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah.
It would be a degradation. See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, that this applies where one puts them on, etc. This is from Magen Avraham above in siman 29. His words do not clarify whether there is at least a D'Rabbanan prohibition. Bach implies that there is no prohibition at all, even D'Rabbanan, when one puts them on not for the sake of the mitzvah. Otherwise, there is in any event a prohibition of bal tosif. The Gra's commentary in siman 301, at the end of seif katan 7, likewise implies that when one does not intend to wear them on Shabbos and Yom Tov for the sake of the mitzvah, there is no prohibition even D'Rabbanan.
