Seif 2. On Chol HaMoed it is likewise forbidden, etc. Footnote: see Magen Avraham s"k 2 what he wrote — that one who dons on Chol HaMoed does not transgress bal tosif since he does not intend to add — meaning he does because of doubt whether perhaps Chol HaMoed is obligated in tefillin, etc. Behold at first glance this contradicts Mordekhai in first chapter of Megillah, who challenged there: why do we not read the megillah on the 15th also from the power of doubt of the day? And he answered there with one answer that one transgresses bal tosif, and it is proven that even in doubt bal tosif applies. However one may say that from this there is proof for the view of Radbaz that Magen Avraham brought [siman 690] that a rabbinic mitzvah does not require intent — and therefore it comes out well that the intent of Semak and Magen Avraham is: since he does not intend to add, and mitzvot require intent, therefore without intent he does not transgress bal tosif; but in a rabbinic mitzvah that does not require intent, even without intent he transgresses bal tosif. And even though in the Gemara Rosh Hashanah chapter 3 we conclude that even for fulfillment intent is not required, and to transgress not in its time certainly intent is not required — that is only if he does not intend at all for the mitzvah; nevertheless to be transgressive, bal tosif requires intent; but where he intends for the mitzvah only that we say since he does not intend for a complete mitzvah but only from doubt, there is no bal tosif — in this one may well distinguish: specifically if mitzvot require intent, then even in such a case he does not transgress bal tosif; but if mitzvot do not require intent, even for bal tosif such intent is not required — therefore rabbinic mitzvot are inferior to Torah mitzvot: for Torah mitzvot, since they require intent, he does not transgress bal tosif; but rabbinic, since they do not require intent, he transgresses bal tosif. However, since megillah is words of Kabbalah and like Torah law, therefore it requires intent — and again why should we be concerned for bal tosif? And perforce one must hold that even in doubt bal tosif is a concern. However one may say the intent of Mordekhai is thus: behold there he answered with another answer that one transgresses lo ya'avor; and one may say both are one: behold at first glance one may say that since it is only doubt whether he transgresses lo ya'avor, it is appropriate to call it rabbinic doubt and be lenient; and one should not say that conversely it is also rabbinic doubt and lenient — this is not so, for regarding obligation to read, since in any case he became obligated to read and it is doubt whether he is discharged, it is appropriate to call the doubt stringent; not so regarding lo ya'avor, where there is no certainty whether he transgressed lo ya'avor or not — if so it is appropriate to obligate him to read from doubt. But one may say megillah is words of Kabbalah and like Torah law — if so this lo ya'avor is also like Torah doubt and therefore the doubt is stringent, etc. Therefore it comes out well that Mordekchai was uncertain whether megillah is entirely words of Kabbalah and like Torah law or not — and therefore he resolves either way: if it is like Torah law and bal tosif from the power that intent is required and he does not transgress without intent, and behold he does only from doubt — but then the answer is from lo ya'avor, for Torah doubt is stringent; and if its law is not like Torah law and the doubt is lenient, and lo ya'avor is not relevant as above — then automatically, since it is rabbinic and does not require intent, bal tosif is relevant; and either way he resolves. However in truth without this our question stands from what Mordekhai wrote in his words that even when not in time bal tosif does not apply — nevertheless, since there are those established for today, etc. If so it comes out well: behold the Gemara concludes in Rosh Hashanah that to transgress in its time intent is not required, not in its time intent is required — if so, on Chol HaMoed, if it is not time for tefillin at all, then it is not in its time and intent is required, and behold he does not add but only from doubt as Magen Avraham wrote; but there on the 15th, since in any case it is their time for those established, therefore even without intent he transgresses bal tosif — and it comes out well; investigate.
