(1) And Yom Tov — and the second day of Yom Tov is also included for us, diaspora Jews.

(2) It is forbidden to don — and even regarding moving them, some say it is likewise forbidden if not for their body or place, like other utensils whose primary use is forbidden; and some say that so they not fall, or so they not be stolen, it is also permitted to move them from place to place; and in time of need one may be lenient — see Biur Halacha.

(3) That they themselves are a sign — between the Holy One, blessed be He, and Israel, as it is said: "for it is a sign between Me," etc.; and Yom Tov is also called a sign, for on Pesach in Egypt it is written "sign," and all the festivals of the the Omnipresent were equated in parashat Emor.

(4) Another sign — meaning tefillin, as it is written therein: "and it shall be for you as a sign upon your hand."

(5) It would be disrespect — and one also transgresses the negative commandment of bal tosif when he dons them for the sake of the mitzvah; but when donning not for the sake of the mitzvah there is no bal tosif, and also no disrespect unless he dons them publicly — then it is forbidden rabbinically; and some are stringent in all cases unless they are donned in disgrace, such as one who finds tefillin on Shabbat in a field — it is permitted to wear them and bring them to the city by way of wearing as clothing, as will be explained below siman 308.
