(1) And Yom Tov. For those who live outside Eretz Yisrael, the second day of Yom Tov is included as well.
(2) It is forbidden to put them on. Some authorities maintain that even moving them is forbidden unless needed for their permitted use or for their place, like any utensil whose primary function is prohibited. Others maintain that one may also move them from place to place so that they not fall or be stolen. In pressing circumstances one may be lenient. See Biur Halachah.
(3) They themselves are a sign. Between HaKadosh Baruch Hu and Israel, as it says, “For it is a sign between Me and you.” Yom Tov is likewise called a sign, for the Exodus from Egypt is described as a sign, and all the festivals of Hashem are compared to one another in parashas Emor.
(4) Another sign. Meaning tefillin, concerning which it is written, “It shall be for you as a sign upon your hand.”
(5) It would be a degradation. He also violates the prohibition of bal tosif when he puts them on for the sake of the mitzvah. If he puts them on not for the sake of the mitzvah, he does not violate bal tosif, nor is there degradation, unless he puts them on publicly, in which case it is forbidden D'Rabbanan. Some are stringent in every case, unless the tefillin are being treated disrespectfully, such as where one finds tefillin in a field on Shabbos. In that case he may put them on and bring them into the city in the manner of clothing, as will be explained below in siman 301.
