They are. See Taz. The dispute among the authorities mentioned in seif 3 may be explained as follows. One view maintains that the sign of Yom Tov consists of matzah and sukkah. The other view maintains that the sign is specifically the prohibition against labor. One may further say that one view maintains that some forms of labor are forbidden on Chol HaMoed by Torah law, while the other maintains that labor is permitted by Torah law and forbidden only D'Rabbanan. See siman 530, Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2, who writes that the Zohar follows its own view that labor on Chol HaMoed is prohibited by Torah law. See also what Eliyah Rabbah asks in letter 1 from Yoreh Deah, siman 265, Shach, seif katan 24, that one should not remove tefillin before the circumcision because circumcision is called a sign. On the contrary, that would appear to degrade the sign of circumcision. One may answer that during the week we wear tefillin even though we possess the sign of circumcision, as Levush writes in siman 29, because two witnesses are required. Two signs suffice. On Shabbos, circumcision and Shabbos provide the two signs. This follows Semag and Abudraham, whom I cited there.
