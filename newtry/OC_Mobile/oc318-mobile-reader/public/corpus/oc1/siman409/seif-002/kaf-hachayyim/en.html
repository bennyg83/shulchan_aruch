9) [Se'if 2] So that it is possible for him, etc. The rationale of eruv is that the person is regarded as though he established his residence there, as stated above at the beginning of siman 408.
10) There. To eat it during bein hashmashos. This is the time when the eruv takes effect, as stated immediately afterward.
11) There. Except through a transgression. This means a transgression of Torah law. Olas Shabbos, subsection 3. It is forbidden even during bein hashmashos, when it is uncertain whether night has begun. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 7.
12) There. Or if he intended to establish his Shabbos residence in a karmelis. See above, siman 345, se'if 7, where some authorities maintain that nowadays our public domains have the law of a karmelis because six hundred thousand people do not pass through them every day. See there. It is possible that one may rule leniently for a mitzvah purpose and in pressing circumstances when the person cannot place the eruv elsewhere.
13) There. For anything instituted by the Sages, etc. Eruvin 32b explains that even if one places an eruv upon a tree below ten handbreadths while intending to establish Shabbos residence below on the ground, which is a public domain, the eruv is valid despite the two Rabbinic restrictions involved in retrieving it. One would transfer the eruv from the tree, which is a karmelis, into a public domain, and would also make use of a tree, which is attached to the ground. Nevertheless, it is a valid eruv because the Sages did not impose a decree upon a Rabbinic restriction during bein hashmashos. See there. There are, however, certain Rabbinic restrictions that they did impose, as stated in the following se'if.
