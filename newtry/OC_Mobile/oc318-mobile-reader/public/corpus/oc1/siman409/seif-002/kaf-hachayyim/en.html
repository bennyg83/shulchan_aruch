2) There. Because a cemetery is forbidden for benefit. This discusses a built grave. If the eruv was placed on the natural ground of a grave, that ground is not forbidden, as stated at the beginning of Yoreh De'ah, siman 364. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1. What is considered a built grave depends upon the dispute between the Tur and Rabbeinu Yeshayah there. According to Rabbeinu Yeshayah, even earth removed from the grave and subsequently replaced over it to cover the coffin has the law of a built grave, since it was detached and later reattached. Only a niche hewn into rock is called natural ground, because the rock above the niche was never detached, and the sides of the grave certainly have that status. According to the Tur, however, even earth that was detached and subsequently reattached has the status of natural ground because it remains earth; only an actual built grave is forbidden. See there. Perhaps one may rule leniently regarding an eruv in accordance with the Tur, particularly since the primary ruling there also appears to follow the Tur. Machatzis HaShekel. Tosefes Shabbos there, however, writes that Rema rules that earth placed over a corpse becomes forbidden like a built structure, and the same applies here. See there. Shach there, subsection 2, appears to agree with the Tur. It therefore appears that regarding an eruv one may be lenient, as Machatzis HaShekel writes.
