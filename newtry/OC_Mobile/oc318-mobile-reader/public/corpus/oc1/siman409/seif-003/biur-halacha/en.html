(א) That they are soft — so Rashi explained in the sugya: because it is soft it is close to certainty he will snap; and that which is explained there to the contrary that a soft branch they did not decree for snapping — Rashi explained it is a soft branch like a vegetable not yet hardened at all, then it is like a vegetable and has no name of tree; accordingly are Shulchan Aruch's words here. However acharonim questioned Shulchan Aruch's ruling [see Bach]; in Ritva I found he completely disagrees with Rashi's explanation because in the sugya there it implies the opposite, as above — softness is actually a stringency; per him the prohibition is specifically in hard reeds because a thin reed one comes to break when taking his eruv, and a soft branch is permitted because one can bend it; but in a hard one it is forbidden, as his words are explained in Peri Chadash; however Ritva explained there in name of some of his teachers that even in a soft branch what we permit is specifically a species that is only soft, but in a species that is hard even its soft shoots are forbidden — like Rashba's view in Avodat HaKodesh, brought above siman 336 — see there.

(ב) And he would be liable for a sin-offering — seemingly not explained, for the concern is because it is soft he comes to snapping unintentionally as Rashi explained, and if so there is no liability for a sin-offering, which is only for intentional melacha where he erred in liability; Ritva already addressed this and explained not like Rashi but as above that he comes to full melacha to break it intentionally when taking it because it cannot be bent because it is hard; per Rashi it must be that since the essence of melacha has sin-offering liability and it is close he will do it, they decreed even if he will do it unintentionally.
