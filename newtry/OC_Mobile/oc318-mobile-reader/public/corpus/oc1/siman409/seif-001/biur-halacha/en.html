(א) It is not an eruv, per, etc. — the Mechaber ruled stam like Rambam who rules that even for an Israelite it is not an eruv; see Maggid Mishneh, for so is also Ramban's view and like the Geonim that we do not hold like R' Yehuda even for eruv in a place where many disagree on him; so is also Or Zarua — see there. However when we examine we find several Poskim who are lenient for an Israelite because they hold like R' Yehuda that this is not considered a prohibition of benefit [see in the Gemara] — they are R' Meir of Rothenburg and Raosh who brought it, and so Tur brought them, and so is Rashba's view brought in Hagahot Maimoniot, and R' Yonatan, and Hagahot Semak in name of Riaz [only he is lenient even for a kohen because he can go out in a chest and a thrown tent is called a tent to go out before impurity — but we do not hold so]; so Agudah, and Korban Netanel wrote that in pressing circumstances one may rely on them to be lenient.

(ב) Because, etc. — see Mishna Berurah where we wrote that in a cemetery this counsel is impossible; however when he placed the eruv for a kohen in an individual grave [of permanent ground where there is no prohibition of benefit] it is a valid eruv, for although a grave seizes four cubits around it it is only d'rabbanan and permitted to stand there in a thrown tent; therefore he can reach there by placing a box and tower and standing in the air near the grave and taking the eruv from there via some vessel [in Magen Avraham s.k. 5 it implies we require the tower to hold forty se'ah, one cubit by one cubit, three high — this is not so compelling here, for the tower is only because of distance as Tosafot wrote; also what he wrote to take the eruv via plain wooden vessels without a handbreadth width is not compelling in our time since we are all impure from the dead anyway].
