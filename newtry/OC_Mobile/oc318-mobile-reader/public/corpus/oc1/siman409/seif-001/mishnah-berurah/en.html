(א) The giver, etc. — deals even with an Israelite; and if they placed the eruv there for a kohen, the taste inside is also forbidden, because we require that he be able to reach the place of his eruv, and since he is a kohen it is not possible [Tur].
(ב) Forbidden in benefit — and deals with a built tomb; but if they placed it in a ground tomb it is permitted, for permanent ground is not forbidden, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 364; all the more if they did not place it in a tomb but on the ground beside the graves — certainly according to all opinions his eruv is a valid eruv, for there is no prohibition of benefit at all.
(ג) After acquisition — for what is placed there at bein hashemashot, which is the time of acquiring shevitah, we do not care, since one may make an eruv only for a matter of mitzva and mitzvot were not given for benefit [and therefore benefit is not considered from what he can walk thereby outside the techum]; rather, what he wants is that the eruv remain there even after acquiring shevitah in order to eat afterward if he wishes — this is considered benefit, and therefore they forbade acquiring shevitah there.
(ד) In a beit hapras — it is a field in which a grave was plowed and we are concerned d'rabbanan that perhaps a bone the size of a barley grain still remains there.
(ה) It is a valid eruv — for no prohibition of benefit applies here at all.
(ו) Because, etc. — meaning: even though d'rabbanan a kohen is forbidden to walk there lest he move with his foot a bone the size of a barley grain, which imparts impurity by contact and carrying, and if so he cannot reach the place of his eruv — nevertheless it is permitted, for it is possible for him to enter there via a floating chest carried on oxen so that he will not move a bone; specifically in a beit hapras which is d'rabbanan and has no tent impurity, unlike a cemetery where this counsel is impossible for a kohen because he forms a tent over the graves; and even if he enters in a chest holding forty se'ah it is not an interposition between him and the grave, for we hold a thrown tent is not called a tent and whatever is not a tent does not interpose.
(ז) He blows — because it is d'rabbanan they relied to be lenient and walk by blowing with his mouth; and if there is a bone the size of a barley grain there he pushes it aside, and a large bone he must see.
