(6) There. In a floating tower — namely a large box one cubit by one cubit, three cubits high, carried on animals; he can take the eruv in plain wooden vessels without a handbreadth width and bring it to himself — Magen Avraham s.k. 5. And it must be that what Magen Avraham wrote "and he can take, etc." is a matter by itself and does not refer to beit hapras, for in beit hapras even a handbreadth width is not needed, since beit hapras does not impart tent impurity; rather it refers to an individual grave that is not built, where it is permitted (as above opinion 2), and he can go there by placing a box and tower; even per those who say a thrown tent is not called a tent — nevertheless in an individual grave it is permitted, for there is no greater distance than this as Tosafot wrote; therefore we require the wooden vessel not have a handbreadth width so it not impart impurity as a tent — Turei Zahav opinion 5, and Avodat HaRishonim agreed in opinion 5; see Machatzit HaShekel.
