(א) And he recognizes a tree or fence — but if he does not recognize any place and said stam "my shevitah at the end of two thousand from here" — it does not help even per the first view; so is proven from mishnah (daf 49), "if he does not recognize, etc., and he said my shevitah in my place, etc." — see there; so all acharonim.

(ב) At the end of two thousand cubits — from this wording it implies specifically if at the time of declaration he was within two thousand to the base of the tree; but if he was beyond two thousand, even though when the day was sanctified he reached with his feet within two thousand, and also if he would run from the time of declaration he could reach the base of the tree before dark — it does not help; but from Rambam's wording it implies even if at the time of declaration he was beyond two thousand to the base of the tree, since when the day was sanctified he reached with his feet within two thousand and also if he had run from the time of declaration he could have reached the base of the tree before dark — his declaration helps [Sefer Beit Meir — see there who sided with Rambam's halacha].

(ג) But if not, etc. — not so precise, for from the outset it deals when the entire tree is not within two thousand — the branches beyond its base are outside two thousand — only that he specified his shevitah place at its base; it should have said briefly: but if he did not specify the place beneath it, etc.; rather the wording is somewhat inverted as if he said: but if he did not specify the place beneath it — if the entire tree is not within two thousand [as he described from the outset] he did not acquire shevitah; and if all of it stands within two thousand, etc. — to exclude Rambam's view written below.

(ד) From before day — and if he can reach at bein hashemashot, if it helps b'dieved it appears it depends on the dispute explained below siman 415 seif 3, and the Mechaber stated a decided matter.

(ה) And per Rambam one who acquires shevitah at a distant place — distant place is called when he is not sitting in his house and going there to make eruv with feet, but when he comes on the road and nightfall came upon him and he says "my shevitah at a place distant from here" [even though it is within two thousand cubits].

(ו) And so is the view of the Rif — see Mishna Berurah what Avodat HaRishonim wrote that the main view is like the first view, which is Rashba, Raosh, Tur, and Mordechai's view — they saw Rambam's words and disagree; so Agudah brought that they hold so [and what Rabbeinu Yonatan wrote — seemingly no decision from his words, for though he explained like Rashi perhaps he ruled like Rav and like Raavad's view]; I also saw in Ritva and so in Sefer HaMilchamot. Nevertheless this law is still not clear, for Rambam is not alone, for many Poskim stand in his position though not in all matters — I will explain. Namely what the Mechaber wrote that per Rambam when one says stam "my shevitah beneath it" he does not acquire shevitah there and has nothing from the tree and beyond — Rambam is not alone, for Rashi's second explanation is also so, that even per Shmuel he has only from his place until beneath the tree out of doubt, and in any event whether he acquired shevitah at his place or beneath the tree he acquires that area, but from the tree and beyond — no; and though Rashi challenged this explanation in Rashi Chadash before us, he explained so, and it is proven per Rashi Chadash that even if we rule like Shmuel, at least he has nothing from the tree and beyond; Rif's view is proven to hold like Rambam here as Shulchan Aruch wrote [and as Beit Yosef inferred from the statement of Rav Huna that specifically four out of twenty-seven — part of his house specified — he acquires shevitah there; per first view, for Shmuel in every case he acquires shevitah beneath the tree and has four thousand cubits minus the width of the tree as explained in Shulchan Aruch — Rif rules like Shmuel, so necessarily he holds that for Shmuel where part of his house is not recognizable he has nothing from the tree and beyond; it appears he explains like Rashi Chadash; Avodat HaRishonim's words that nothing is proven from Rif are not clear] — see Hagahot Maimoniot who wrote close to Rambam's words also in Sefer HaItim, and so Raavad — per him he certainly does not hold he may not move from his place at all, for he rules like Rav; so Hagahot Semak brought Riaz also rules like Rav; thus Rashi Chadash, Rif, Rambam, Raavad, and Riaz all stand in one position to forbid at least from the tree and beyond — see Or Zarua who also brought views whether halacha is like Rav or Shmuel [and it appears he himself holds there halacha is like Rav — see there]. However what Rambam wrote that he has shevitah at his place two thousand in every direction — whether saying "my shevitah beneath it" without specifying the place, or intending to acquire shevitah more than two thousand away — in this one may say Rambam is alone; not only all those standing in Rashba's position certainly hold he already uprooted his view and has nothing from his feet toward the other side [not toward the tree]; so Raavad, Riaz, and Or Zarua who rule like Rav — certainly so; only Rashi Chadash and Rif remain — in Rashi Chadash it is explained he does not hold so — see there: per Rav where he said "my shevitah beneath it" without specifying his place he may not move from his place; and even per Shmuel he has only from his place to the tree because of doubt, but not beyond; as above; and where he said "my shevitah" more than two thousand away it is explicit in Rashi Chadash 50b that according to all he may not move from his place, as other commentators explain in this sugya [see Biur HaGra who reconciles the sugya with difficulty also for Rambam's position]; also from Rif it is not proven he holds like Rambam here — one may say he stands in Rashi Chadash's position: the difference between part of his place specified or not — where not specified at all he certainly does not acquire shevitah and has only from his place to the tree by force of doubt as above; where part of his house is specified he acquires shevitah there and has even from the tree and beyond; but regarding acquiring shevitah at his place when he did not specify his shevitah, and all the more where he explicitly distanced his shevitah outside the techum — nothing is proven from Rif that he holds so; plainness of the sugya 50b when he distanced his shevitah two thousand and four cubits from his feet proves the opposite, and so in Rashi Chadash there; it appears to me what the Mechaber wrote "and so is Rif's view on this" he wrote intentionally — only regarding the last two rulings [the statements of R' Huna there] that Rif brought in his laws, but in the first two rulings that Rif brought from there — Rambam that he acquires shevitah where he stood when it grew dark — we have no proof from Rif that he holds so; it is possible he holds only that he did not acquire shevitah there to be permitted beyond the tree, but also beyond his feet he holds he is not permitted, like Rashi Chadash above.
