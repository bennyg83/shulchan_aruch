For anything instituted by the Sages, etc. Eruvin 32b explains that even if one places an eruv upon a tree below ten handbreadths while intending to establish Shabbos residence below on the ground, which is a public domain, the eruv is valid despite the two Rabbinic restrictions involved in retrieving it. One would transfer the eruv from the tree, which is a karmelis, into a public domain, and would also make use of a tree, which is attached to the ground. Nevertheless, it is a valid eruv because the Sages did not impose a decree upon a Rabbinic restriction during bein hashmashos. See there. There are, however, certain Rabbinic restrictions that they did impose, as stated in the following se'if.
