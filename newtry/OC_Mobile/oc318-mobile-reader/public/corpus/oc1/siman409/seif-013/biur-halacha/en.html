And specifically for one coming on the road they permitted him — and even if he has bread in his hand it suffices that he say "my shevitah in such-and-such a place," since he left his city with intent not to make eruv [meaning this is called coming on the road]; so Avodat HaRishonim in name of Levush Yom Tov, and like Rashba brought in Beit Yosef, and Beit Yosef wrote so also from Rambam who holds thus [and so Hagahot Semak brought Rashi Chadash and Ritva]; unlike Rashi who holds we require two things: coming on the road and he has no bread with him; in Darkei Moshe he wrote that Rashi also agrees to Rambam and Rashba — see there; and so plainness of the sugya implies that only if he was seized on the road may he say "my shevitah in such-and-such a place," and because the majority do not have bread they did not divide the rabbis' ruling; and a poor person is considered even if he has bread with him, as Ritva wrote; see below siman 410 in Biur Halacha seif 1.
