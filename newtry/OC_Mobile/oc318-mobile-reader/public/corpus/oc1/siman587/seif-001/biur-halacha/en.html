(א) Or into the pit — see what Mishnah Berurah wrote regarding a building most of which is on the ground etc.: those are Magen Avraham's words, and his reason is that the main reverberating sound comes from the depth of the pit; therefore Rosh explained per Rashi that on the ground there is no echoing sound. Nevertheless it is plausible to Magen Avraham even on the ground, as he wrote.

(2) And if he heard kol habarah he did not fulfill — see Mishna Berurah that this matter depends on the depth of the pit and distance from it; and Taz wrote that therefore even one blowing in the synagogue and there are people standing outside somewhat far from it, this too has relevance of distinction per distance from the synagogue — see there [and one should not challenge his words from our mishnah, for we do not find the law of kol habarah except in pit and cistern; and if so it should have been included also in synagogue with them; and regarding one standing outside it at a distance like the latter clause brings "behind the synagogue" regarding intention — and it implies from this that the law of mixed sound in a house is not relevant at all, even at a distance; one can say he wished to inform us the opposite — that even one standing right next to him and heard shofar sound, it can also be imagined he did not fulfill, such as where he did not intend in his heart — strained]. And it appears from Taz who explains kol habarah as a weak sound and sound heard outside the building from afar is not as strong as sound heard from close and inside; but in my opinion kol habarah stated regarding shofar means a mixed sound, and in Gemara language in the sugya it should be fulfilled at the beginning of tekiah before his sound mixes — and such a sound is not heard outside a building on the ground even one standing far from it, but in sound that emerged from under the ground; and so I found explicitly in Rabbeinu Menachem — he wrote on what Rambam wrote pit and cistern: the commentator said pit is in digging and round, and cistern is roofed with covering and square; and the way of these places when a person shouts and speaks or blows in them the sound becomes confused and appears as if another speaks opposite him; and those standing in the pit etc. — because the sound is not confused for them, but those standing outside whom the sound confuses more than in the pit etc. — see there. Afterward I found in Halacha Berurah and Mateh Yehuda who also disagree on Taz — see in Mateh Yehuda who concludes they only said it in a pit, but in level places it is obvious there is no difference for us between standing close or far, for whatever reaches them sound they always hear shofar sound, and if they are farther they also did not hear the sound — see there; nevertheless in practice one should not deviate from Taz's words, for all the Acharonim brought his words as halacha, that one hearing shofar sound behind the synagogue at a distance depends on his assessment whether he heard shofar sound or kol habarah; and from Rabbeinu Menachem's words too there is no proof against Taz's words, for although he explains kol habarah is through confusion of sound, it is possible that through distance of place it is also called kol habarah; and see Gittin daf 89 — they did not hear kol habarah except so candles would burn and beds spread — thus kol habarah is not fixed specifically on confusion of sound but on a mere sound not on a clear matter; and through the pit's hollow where he stands the sound becomes confused and clear shofar sound is not heard even though he is close; and Taz's reasoning and Shaarei Teshuvah that through distance of place outside the building too shofar sound is not clear. Know further that in the Gemara we pose a difficulty on what was thought that beginning of tekiah without end of tekiah is valid — from that one who blows into the pit: if he heard shofar sound he fulfilled, and if he heard kol habarah he did not fulfill; and why should it be fulfilled at beginning of tekiah before his sound mixes — and it implies from this sound-mixing does not come at beginning of tekiah but in the middle; and it is difficult whence we know there was measure of tekiah before the sound began to mix [and per the first view that if there was measure of tekiah even for the conclusion he fulfilled, and per the Gemara's view even without measure of tekiah he fulfilled — our difficulty is only per the first view] — and I found in Ramban and Ritva who addressed this and answered: it is possible since it does not teach "if kol habarah" but "if kol habarah he heard," it implies even if there was also shofar sound at its beginning in the required measure, nevertheless he did not fulfill if he also heard kol habarah; and they further wrote: or alternatively the Sages established sound does not mix in pits except for one who extends greatly and blows in them — end quote; and per this answer the main concern of kol habarah is specifically when extending the blowing greatly, but when extending in each tekiah and shevarim only per the measure the Sages gave there is no kol habarah at all; nevertheless from all the poskim who ruled definitively on this it does not appear so, and therefore they must hold like Ramban's first answer.
