2) Ibid. One who sounds the shofar inside a pit. Or into a cistern. Mishnah, Rosh Hashanah, ibid. Rashi there, and the Rif and Rambam in his Commentary on the Mishnah, explained that a pit is an excavation in the ground itself, while a cistern is an elevated place above the ground surrounded by walls. So too explained the Rav of Bartenura on the Mishnah there. The Rambam and Rav of Bartenura likewise explained this in Keilim, chapter 5, Mishnah 6, and Bava Batra, chapter 4, Mishnah 2, see there. However, the Rashbam there in tractate Bava Batra, 64a, explained that a pit is an excavation in firm ground that holds water without construction, while a cistern is constructed by digging a pit in loose earth and building it a stone wall, end quote. The Tosafos Yom Tov wrote on the Mishnah there in Bava Batra that this also appears to be the Rambam's view in his code, chapter 25 of Hilchos Mechirah, see there. This is also the view of the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, that a cistern is a constructed space below ground, such as a cellar, see there. The Machatzis HaShekel wrote that this is also the view of the Shulchan Aruch, and it appears to be the view of the Rosh, see there. The Magen Avraham wrote there that if most of it is built above ground, even though part of it is below ground, it is permitted. So ruled the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 1; Mateh Ephraim, os 1; and Mishna Berurah, os 2. However, the Eliyah Rabbah, os 1, wrote that regarding shofar one should be stringent in accordance with all the opinions, since the prohibition is according to the Torah. Meaning, one should be stringent even according to those who hold that a cistern is a structure above ground. It appears that even according to this opinion, this applies only where it is made like a pit or cave, for otherwise, according to all opinions, it does not have the status of a cistern.
