Not under obligation. For example, before dawn, and as below in siman 588.
As one who is preoccupied. To practice, or merely as a song, for which he has not fulfilled [his obligation], and as below in siman 589, se'if 8.
And he blew and prolonged. That is to say, he lengthened his teki'ah for his [the hearer's] sake.
He has not fulfilled. For in any case he is missing the beginning of the teki'ah; and the same applies also if the entire teki'ah was under obligation, but he entered midway through the teki'ah, or left the synagogue midway through the teki'ah and thereafter did not hear [it]; he has not fulfilled [his obligation], even though what he heard had the measure of a teki'ah, and it is like one who heard half the measure of a teki'ah.
And some say that he has fulfilled. And the first view is primary, and he must return and blow again; and even so he should not recite the blessing over it, for a doubt regarding blessings is ruled leniently; and it is better that he hear the blessing from another who has not yet fulfilled [his obligation].
And midway through the teki'ah he went outside. That is to say, the blower, and he completed it at the edge of the pit; and it turns out that those standing outside heard only the end of the teki'ah, for the first half was an invalid teki'ah; and if there was the measure of a teki'ah at the edge of the pit, it depends on the two aforementioned views. And the same applies if he heard from the beginning a shofar sound (for example, he was with the blower in the pit, and midway through the teki'ah he went outside and thereafter heard only an echo) he likewise has not fulfilled [his obligation], for we require hearing a valid shofar sound from beginning to end. And the Acharonim wrote that all the people should take care not to clear their throat and make noises during the blowing, so that they hear all the sounds from their beginning to their end; and likewise they should not bring their children near them, also for this reason; and it is better that they be with their mothers in the women's synagogue, for women are not obligated by law in sounding the shofar. Nevertheless, it appears that for children who have reached the age of chinuch, it is a mitzvah to bring them and keep them with him, and he should caution them to listen to the blasts and not confuse the congregation.
