Every labor, etc. See above, siman 416, that there is eruv and carrying on Yom Kippur. The Acharonim wrote that the prohibition of driving a laden animal also applies on Yom Kippur; see Sha'agas Aryeh, siman 78. Know that one makes eruvei chatzeros and eruvei techumin with bread, even though it is forbidden to eat, because it is fit for minors who do not fast, as Rashi wrote in Eruvin 30b; see there in the baraisa. The words of the Mateh Ephraim on this are astonishing, for it appears that this sugya escaped him.
To trim vegetables - See Magen Avraham, who wrote that this speaks of vegetables eaten raw as they are; otherwise, they are forbidden to handle because of muktzeh, since they are not fit even for minors. In my humble opinion this is not compelling, for just as they permitted preparation for weekday because of distress, so too they permitted muktzeh for this reason. Indeed, according to several explanations there is a concern of selecting in trimming vegetables; see the explanations of Rabbenu Chananel, the Rambam, and the Maggid Mishneh. Muktzeh is no better than this. However, I found that Rabbenu Manoach also wrote like the Magen Avraham, but for another reason. Regarding the Rambam's statement, "and it is permitted to gather the spoiled leaves and cut the rest, etc.," he wrote that it speaks of something eaten raw as it is, for otherwise there is a concern of grinding; see there. However, in the Meiri at the end of chapter Elu Kesharim, he also initially wanted to say something like this and ultimately rejected it, for they said in the Talmud that in the house of Rav Yehudah they trimmed cabbage and gourds, which are not eaten raw as they are. See what he wrote further on this. According to this, the words of the Magen Avraham are also rejected for this reason, and it is proved that even something not eaten raw as it is, such as cabbage and gourds, is also permitted to trim.
And to crack nuts - See Darkei Moshe in the name of Mahara of Prague, who at the beginning of his words leaned toward saying that this is specifically to break them and leave them in their shells until evening, but to remove the food would involve threshing; see there. However, from the language of the Tosefta, brought in the Gra, which teaches: one cracks nuts and breaks apart pomegranates, etc., from Shabbos for that Shabbos, but not from Shabbos for Motzaei Shabbos, because it is like preparing from sacred to weekday; and afterward it teaches there: one cracks nuts, etc., on Yom Kippur near dark and is not concerned that he prepares from sacred to weekday, see there, it is proved that just as on Shabbos it is certainly permitted even to crack and remove the food to eat it that day, so too it is permitted in this manner on Yom Kippur for the needs of the night. This is clear. Know further that in the Yerushalmi, chapter 4 of Pesachim, it further says: what is the law of saying about chalutah, make me chalutah? He said to him: it is permitted, etc. This implies that just as they permitted trimming vegetables, they also permitted telling a non-Jew to cook for after the fast. This requires study, for the authorities did not cite it. Perhaps it is because at the conclusion, even with trimming vegetables we are stringent because perhaps people will do it early, and for this same reason there is concern regarding telling a non-Jew.
