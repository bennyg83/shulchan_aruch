Every, etc., and anything forbidden, etc. For we rule that there is eruv and carrying on Yom Kippur, from what is stated in chapter 3: Do you not agree that one makes an eruv for an adult on Yom Kippur, etc.? Rif in the first chapter of Beitzah. His words require great study, for there it speaks of eruvei techumin. They further brought proof that so we conclude in Kerisus 14a, that Rafram's statement is false. See Tosafos there, and see the first chapter of Beitzah, 12a, and see Rashi there, comment beginning "let them disagree, etc."
To trim. The Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh explained in his name that this means to rinse, as written in the Yerushalmi there: When Yom Kippur falls on Friday, one does not blow shofar; at the conclusion of Shabbos one does not recite havdalah, etc. Rabbi Shmuel, brother of Rabbi Berachyah, said: let him recite havdalah, for it permits rinsing pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables. Rabbi Yosa said: is it permitted to rinse pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables at all except from Minchah and onward? Let him recite havdalah from Minchah and onward. What will you say? There is no lamp here, there is no cup here, etc. So too it is in the Tosefta at the end of chapter 13 of Shabbos: one cracks nuts, breaks apart pomegranates, and rinses pickled vegetables on Yom Kippur near dark, and he is not concerned that he is preparing from sacred to weekday. This means as it says there just before this: one cracks nuts, breaks apart pomegranates, and cuts, perhaps the text should read figs, from Shabbos for that Shabbos, but not from Shabbos for Motzaei Shabbos, because it is like preparing from sacred to weekday. But on Yom Kippur they permitted it, as written in the Gemara, because of distress. This means, as the Rashba wrote, that he should not need to prepare in the evening and be distressed. So too it is in Pesachim, chapter 4, halachah 4: When Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, what is the law of rinsing pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables from Minchah and onward? Rav said: forbidden. Rabbi Elazar said: permitted. Rabbi Yaakov bar Acha said in the name of Rabbi Elazar: what is the reason they said one rinses pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables from Minchah and onward? Because of danger. Is the danger of Yom Kippur that falls on Shabbos different? Is Yom Kippur that falls on a weekday different? Rabbi Chananya, colleague of the Rabbis, challenged: our Mishnah disagrees with Rabbi Elazar, for when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos the loaves are distributed in the evening. It is different there, for since it is a light matter, perhaps he will forget and eat. Rabbi Pinchas challenged: our Mishnah disagrees with Rabbi Elazar: when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, "Shabbason," resting, and on a weekday, "Shabbason," resting. If on a weekday you rest, all the more so on Shabbos? It is needed to teach that even things you are permitted to do on a weekday, you rest from them on Shabbos. Which is this? Rinsing pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables, etc.; see there. See Magen Avraham subparagraph 2, "Rashi explained, etc." But in the Tosefta mentioned above it is explicit that they permitted this only to prepare for Motzaei Yom Kippur, for it says there that on Shabbos it is permitted for that day but not for Motzaei Shabbos. And what was written, "and Ran, that they permitted, etc." is a textual error; it should read: "and the Ran explained that they permitted, etc." For all this was written by the Ran, Maggid Mishneh, and Rashba mentioned above, and their proof is from the Yerushalmi and Tosefta mentioned above. This is the main view, and there is no answer against it.
When it falls on a weekday. Rambam. This follows Rav Huna there, and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that he is later than Rabbi Yochanan and the Tanna Kamma. This requires study, for in chapter 6 of Berachos the Rif and Rambam ruled like Rabbi Yochanan against Rav Huna, and so ruled all the authorities. See Tosafos there, 40b, comment beginning "and Rabbi Yochanan, etc.," that only from Abaye and Rava onward do we rule like the later authority. See the Rosh, chapter 19 of Shabbos. And what he wrote that the Tanna Kamma, on the contrary, the Tanna Kamma is Rabbi Yochanan's. One must say that he did not have the reading, "the Tanna Kamma of Rabbi Yochanan, etc., until Rabbi Yochanan said," and necessarily there is a textual error there. Rather, it should read: the Tanna Kamma of Rav Huna: when Yom Kippur falls on a weekday, one cracks, etc. This is what the Maggid Mishneh meant by "and it was taught like them." Also in the Yerushalmi, Pesachim chapter 4, halachah 4, it establishes the Mishnah of Shabbason, resting, regarding trimming vegetables, as it says there: When Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, what is the law of rinsing pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables from Minchah and onward? Rav said forbidden; Rabbi Elazar said permitted, etc. Rabbi Pinchas challenged: the Mishnah disagrees, etc. It is needed to teach that even things you are permitted to do, you rest from them on Shabbos. Which is this? Rinsing pickled vegetables and boiled vegetables. The Ran and Maggid Mishneh explained in the name of the Ramban and Rashba that this is trimming vegetables. See what I wrote above.
