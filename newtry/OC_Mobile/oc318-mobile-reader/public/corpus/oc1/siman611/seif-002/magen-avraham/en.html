Although nowadays there is no practical difference in this, nevertheless there is a practical difference regarding kam lei bidrabbah minei: one who wounds his fellow on Shabbos is exempt from payments, while one who wounds on Yom Kippur is liable, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 429, se'if 2.
To trim vegetables - Rashi explained: to remove the spoiled leaves. They asked that this is selecting. One can say that since these leaves are not complete waste and are not fit to be called waste, and perhaps they are fit to eat with difficulty, on Shabbos this is only a rabbinic prohibition, and on Yom Kippur it is permitted. Although one who curdles or strains is liable because of selecting, there it is with moist items. What Rashi wrote, "and he cuts the rest," does not mean very finely, for if so, that is grinding; see simanim 319 and 321. Some explain that trimming means detaching the leaves from the stalks in order to straighten them, so that they will be ready to cut and crumble. Some explain that it is rinsing vegetables; see the end of chapter 15 of Shabbos. The reason they permitted this is because of distress: he sees it and prepares it and does not eat. This is specifically from Minchah and onward, when he anticipates eating, as Rashi explained. The Ran explained that they permitted it so that he would not have to prepare everything in the evening and be distressed. But before Minchah it appears as though he is preparing for the needs of the day. Some explain that before Minchah we decree lest he come to eat from it; afterward, since it is close to the time of eating, it is like one who has bread in his basket. So too wrote the Maggid Mishneh. See what I wrote at the end of siman 612. One cannot say that it speaks of vegetables not eaten raw as they are, for then they would be muktzeh, since they are not fit even for minors. See what I wrote in siman 308, se'ifim 31 and 32.
