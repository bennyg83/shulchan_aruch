(4) Except that on Shabbos its intentional violation, etc. - Even nowadays, when capital cases are not practiced, there is nevertheless a practical difference regarding kam lei bidrabbah minei: one who wounds his fellow on Shabbos, since he would have been liable to death by human court, is exempt from payments, even if he wounded inadvertently, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 429. This is unlike Yom Kippur, which has only kareis.
(5) And they permitted trimming vegetables - Some explain that this trimming is that he detaches or cuts the leaves from detached stalks in order to straighten them so that they will be ready to cut in the evening. Although he troubles himself for the evening, which is weekday, they permitted it so that he would not need to prepare everything in the evening and be distressed. Others explain that trimming means rinsing the vegetable for the evening's need, and this too is permitted for the reason mentioned above.
(6) And to crack nuts - So that they will be ready for the evening meal and he will not need to trouble himself with them after the fast.
(7) From Minchah and onward - Meaning Minchah Ketanah, which is close to evening. This is the time when people normally prepare their food on weekdays, and it is recognizable that he is doing it for the night. But before Minchah it is forbidden, because it appears that he is preparing for the day.
(8) When it falls on a weekday - But when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, they did not permit it, so that one should not come to do this on every Shabbos of the year, preparing during the day for the night.
(9) And nowadays the custom was to forbid - Because in the later generations they began to spoil the matter and hurry to do these things before Minchah. Therefore the permission of trimming vegetables and cracking nuts was entirely cancelled.
(10) For the need of the night - After Yom Kippur, so that they not need to fast further when they have nothing to eat.
(11) For the Minchah meal - Meaning even if the fire broke out at noon, after they had eaten the morning meal, as above in siman 334.
(12) Play with nuts - Specifically on the table and not on the ground, lest they come to level holes, as written in siman 338 (Magen Avraham).
