(Subparagraph 2) To trim, etc. The spoiled ones. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 1 of the Laws of Resting on the Tenth, law 3, wrote: and they asked that this is, etc. So it should read.
On Shabbos this involves only a rabbinic prohibition, since the vegetable is not attached to the ground.
And what Rashi wrote, "and he cuts, etc." This too is from the words of the Maggid Mishneh there.
But what is written, the word Rashi, is a textual error. Rather, the Rambam wrote this, in these words: What is trimming? That he removes the spoiled leaves, cuts the rest, and prepares it for eating.
But Rashi wrote as follows: to detach the leaves from the stalks in order to leave them so they will be ready to cut and crumble, end quote. These are the "some explain" whom the Magen Avraham brings afterward. The Maggid Mishneh wrote there that the Rambam and Rashi are the same in explaining trimming. That means both explain that trimming means detaching leaves. According to the Rambam, the purpose of detaching is to remove the spoiled ones, while according to Rashi the intent of detaching is to straighten and prepare them. But the language "and he cuts the rest" is the Rambam's language, as above.
See siman 319. And see there in the Magen Avraham, subparagraph 5. "Anticipates eating" is how Rashi explains it. So it should read.
And this is his language: it is permitted, etc. For it is a rabbinic prohibition, etc.; and here, because of the distress of preparing and not eating, it is close to affliction, and is permitted. This is specifically from Minchah and onward, when he longs for and anticipates the time of eating and there is greater distress, end quote. This implies that before Minchah it was not permitted, because then there is not so much distress. So Rashi explained explicitly in the comment beginning "since it is fit."
And the Ran wrote that they permitted it in order, etc. So it should read. See what I wrote at the end of siman 602. And one should not say that it speaks of raw vegetables, etc. That is, at the end of siman 612 the Rema wrote that it is permitted to touch foods, and we do not decree lest he come to eat. With this it is understandable why the Ran needed to say that the reason it is forbidden before Minchah is that it appears as though he is preparing for the needs of the day. Why not say that it is because he may come to eat from it? Rather, the Ran holds like what the Rema wrote at the end of siman 612, that we do not decree. And the "some explain" who wrote that the reason is lest he come to eat, see what the Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 612 to reconcile this, that it does not disagree with the Rema at the end of siman 612. About this the Magen Avraham wrote: and one should not say that it speaks here of raw vegetables that are not fit for eating, and therefore the Ran needed the reason that it appears like preparing for the needs of the day. But actually with something fit for eating, we do decree, unlike the Rema at the end of siman 612.
And see what I wrote in siman 308. That is, he wrote there that specifically raw meat that is soft and fit raw is permitted to move. But something not fit raw, although it is fit for dogs, nevertheless since after Shabbos it will be fit for a person through cooking, his mind is not directed to dogs, and therefore it is forbidden to move it.
