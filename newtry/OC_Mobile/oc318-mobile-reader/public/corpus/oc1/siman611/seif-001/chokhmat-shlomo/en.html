Seif 1: There is no karet liability except for melacha, eating, and drinking. In my opinion: see Magen Avraham who wrote nevertheless all are d'oraisa. In truth this law is not agreed — Rosh and Ran hold only rabbinic. I examined Ran — his proof is from permitting children to wash and feed them, and it says "you shall not eat, you shall not feed them" to warn adults regarding minors, end of his words. In my opinion this does not follow — perhaps the Torah warned only regarding something that is an actual negative prohibition, not regarding what is only a positive commandment, for punishment and warning certainly do not apply here except violating the positive "afflict your souls"; if so there is no proof from this except like running which is a negative prohibition. Also one may distinguish for a minor female — this is a positive time-bound commandment from which women are exempt; we only learn from "man or woman" who were equated to all Torah punishments, and this fits where there is punishment and warning, but where there is no punishment and warning one may say women are exempt; therefore one may say for women these afflictions are rabbinic, therefore an adult may anoint a minor girl — for women it is only rabbinic but for males from the Torah. So it appears at first glance. But apart from this and apart from this, we may ask: the Torah did not say "you shall not eat, you shall not feed them" except regarding a matter where the minor's act is also considered an act — such as eating, for is a minor's eating not eating? It is eating, only he is exempt from commandments; so too entering a tent for a corpse — a minor has an act and entering a tent is considered real entry, only the minor is exempt and the adult may not do so for him. But on Yom Kippur it is not written "eating" but "affliction," and in affliction one may say the matter is contingent on settling the mind; since we hold a minor lacks daat, his eating is not settling the mind, and all the more so his other pleasures are not settling the mind — there is no prohibited act at all, unlike all prohibitions where the act is a prohibited act and only the minor is exempt from commandments. On Yom Kippur where the matter is contingent on settling the mind, since a minor's mind is not considered mind there is no prohibited act; and if an adult ate without his mind settling he is exempt from the Torah, and though forbidden it is only from chazi le'itztareif — this does not apply to a minor, examine and inquire.
