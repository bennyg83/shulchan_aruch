If one was occupied with communal needs, etc. — see in Magen Avraham note 4 what he wrote in the name of Kesef Mishneh in the name of Rambam, and see there in Kesef Mishneh that he concluded per Rambam that he wrote — specifically in the early generations there is no reason; and apparently why did he reject it with both hands — behold Magen Avraham in siman 38 proved from Rashi that if his main intent is only to earn profit he is not called one engaged in a mitzvah; and one could say that from Rambam's words it is implied that in our generations we do not attribute intent for Heaven's sake even if he does not profit from it — and on this Kesef Mishneh wondered; and in any case if he profits from it and we know his main intent is only to earn profit, one must examine greatly per Magen Avraham above whether he is called one engaged in a mitzvah to be exempt from Keriat Shema; and it is possible that here it deals where there is no one who will exert himself except him — it is preferable more so; uncertain — see above siman 38 in Biur Halacha that we proved that if his intent is for both he is called one engaged in a mitzvah:
If one was occupied, etc., and per Raavad one stops, etc. — see in Magen Avraham that he wrote it deals where he began after the half-hour before dawn arrived, when it is forbidden to do all these per Raavad, and he began the matter in prohibition and therefore stops in all these; for in a Torah matter where he began in prohibition even if he began he stops; and Rambam's reason is that he holds they did not decree on all these except near Minchah which is a common matter as below siman 89 seif 7; if so he began the matter in permission and therefore does not stop — and likewise in Biur Gra that this is Rambam's reason; see in Machatzit HaShekel and in Chiddushei Ra'ah that it is proven from their words that if he began in all these after dawn arrived, per all opinions he stops for behold he began in prohibition — not as implied from Levushei Serad; and likewise per this if he began in all these in the evening near the time of evening Keriat Shema he must stop even per Rambam; and there are later authorities who wrote that per Rambam in all cases he need not stop bedieved after he already began if after he finishes there will remain time; and for practical law we have no difference in this for the agreement of most later authorities as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah note 23. And what I further wrote in Mishnah Berurah that if he was occupied in work before sunrise it is called that he began the matter in permission — so is found in later authorities on siman 89 that work is permitted until sunrise; and likewise explicitly in Rambam chapter 6 of Laws of Prayer halakhah 4; and I saw in the siddur of the Gaon of Lissa in matters forbidden before prayer at the end of note 4 that it is implied from his words that ordinary work besides bathhouse and haircut and tanning is like eating that is forbidden a half-hour before sunrise [and what he wrote there note 5 'and if he began to do work' certainly refers to haircut and entering the bathhouse and the like that deals in the prior note so his words do not contradict each other — see there] per Rambam and the rest of the later authorities above — apparently it is not so implied. And what he further wrote in Da'at Chokhmah end of note 6 that if he began after the half-hour its law is like one who began in prohibition — for eating one does not need this for there even if he began in permission he must stop; and tanning and haircut are certainly permitted before sunrise — therefore it refers to other work and is compared to eating that is forbidden a half-hour before sunrise, and follows his view at the end of note 4; and for practical law — uncertain in this as explained:
