The Kesef Mishneh writes in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach that this applies specifically to earlier generations, who occupied themselves with communal affairs for the sake of Heaven. Nevertheless, even now, if one is engaged in communal affairs, even merely to save communal property, and no one else will attend to it, he does not interrupt, because he is engaged in a mitzvah.
He should not interrupt. At a minimum, he should recite the first paragraph and Baruch Shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed if possible. If one is exempt from reciting and nevertheless recites, he is called an ordinary person who acts improperly, according to the Lechem Chamudos. The Darchei Moshe implies that if he intends merely to read as one reads from the Torah, this is permitted. See siman 63, seif 4, and the Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam. See what I wrote at the end of siman 106, contrary to the Olas Tamid. It appears to me that he should nevertheless recite the passage containing the Exodus from Egypt even after the time for Krias Shema has passed. See what I wrote in siman 67.
