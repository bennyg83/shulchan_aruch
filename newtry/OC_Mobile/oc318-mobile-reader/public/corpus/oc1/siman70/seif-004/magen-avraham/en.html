For communal needs. Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of the Rambam, and specifically in the early generations when they engaged with them for communal need; and nevertheless nowadays too, if they engage with them even in saving property, and there is no one who will exert himself except him, he does not stop, for one engaged in a mitzvah [is exempt], end quote.

He should not interrupt. In any case he reads the first paragraph and Shema with its surrounding [blessings] if possible; and if he is exempt from reading and reads, he is called a fool (see Siman 38). And in Darkei Moshe it implies that if he intends like one reading Torah it is fine; and see Mishnah Berurah subnote 3 subnote 4; and in Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam see what is written end Siman 106, not like Or Tzaddik; and it appears to me that nevertheless he should say a passage that contains the Exodus from Egypt even after the time for Keriat Shema has passed, see what is written Siman 67.
