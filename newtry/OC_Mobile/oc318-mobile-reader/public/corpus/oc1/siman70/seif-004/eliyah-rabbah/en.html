The Olas Tamid objects from the Yerushalmi: one who is occupied with communal needs is considered as though he were occupied with words of Torah, yet he interrupts and recites Krias Shema. In my humble opinion, this presents no difficulty. The Beit Yosef means that he is regarded as one who teaches Torah to the public, a case he mentioned immediately beforehand, where Rabbeinu HaKadosh did not interrupt while teaching the public. This is also implied by the Levush, who writes that it is like teaching Torah to the public and that a communal positive obligation takes precedence. The Olas Tamid also overlooked what the Kesef Mishneh writes in chapter 2 of the laws of Krias Shema, that Rambam interprets the Yerushalmi specifically with regard to prayer. Were it referring to Krias Shema, it should have said that one engaged in communal needs interrupts, just as one engaged in Torah study interrupts. Thus the Beit Yosef himself raised this objection and therefore interpreted the passage as referring to prayer. In truth, this creates a major difficulty, for the Beit Yosef's statements contradict one another. I am even more astonished at the Lechem Mishneh there, who writes that the Beit Yosef in siman 70 interprets it as referring to prayer and then argues against him at length. I do not understand his difficulty, and the remainder of his discussion also calls for objection, but this is not the place to elaborate.
[4] He should not interrupt, etc. If he can interrupt for Krias Shema and then return and complete the communal needs without burden, he should interrupt, according to the rulings of Tosafos. Magen Avraham writes that nevertheless he should recite the passage containing the Exodus from Egypt even if the time for Krias Shema has passed.
