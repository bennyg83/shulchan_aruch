Seif 4: If he was, etc. Rambam. Beit Yosef wrote that this is derived from the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 5: Rabbi Yirmiyah said, a person should not stand to pray except after a conclusive halachic ruling. Rabbi Yirmiyah said, one who is occupied with communal needs is considered as one occupied with words of Torah. Rambam explains it in this manner and places these teachings of Rabbi Yirmiyah together. This is also implied in chapter 6 of Hilchos Tefillah, as written above at the end of siman 93. Rosh explains that it refers to the preceding statement, concerning standing to pray from such a state, as he wrote there. Here, however, none of this is necessary, for it is explicit in the Tosefta, chapter 1: Rabbi Yehudah said, on one occasion I was walking behind Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Elazar ben Azaryah. The time for Krias Shema arrived. I thought that they had despaired of reciting it because they were occupied with communal needs. I recited and reviewed, and afterward they began, when the sun was already visible above the mountain tops. The same appears in the Yerushalmi, chapter 1, though there is a scribal error there; see the commentary there. The words of Beit Yosef are surprising, for even one occupied with Torah is obligated in Krias Shema. Even Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his colleagues are exempt only from prayer. Rambam properly derived this regarding prayer, meaning a case like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai and his colleagues, as Rambam wrote there. Lechem Mishneh likewise raised this difficulty, and Kesef Mishneh also rejected that explanation there.
And he should recite, etc. Meaning that in the other cases he need not recite, as it says there, 'I thought,' etc.
