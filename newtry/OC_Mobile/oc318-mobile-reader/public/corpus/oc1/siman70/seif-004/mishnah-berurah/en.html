(16) With communal needs. This concerns a case where no one else can attend to them. He therefore does not interrupt, because he is occupied with a mitzvah, even when he is occupied only with saving the community's property.
(17) And the time arrived, etc. This implies that if he began improperly after the time arrived, he must interrupt. See Peri Megadim, who inclines to the view that in every case he need not interrupt.
(18) He should not interrupt. If he can interrupt to recite Krias Shema and then return and complete the communal matter without difficulty, he should interrupt. Peri Megadim writes that although one occupied with communal needs is exempt from Krias Shema, if he stopped and recited it he has properly fulfilled the obligation. He is not absolutely exempt from Krias Shema; rather, he is presently occupied with another mitzvah.
(19) He should complete their affairs. Nevertheless, he should do what he can and recite at least the first verse together with "Blessed is the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever" at the proper time. This is brief and can be done even while attending to communal needs.
(20) If time remains. If no time remains, what can he do, since he was occupied with a mitzvah? Nevertheless, he must mention the Exodus from Egypt even after the time for Krias Shema has passed, because this is an independent mitzvah whose time extends throughout the day. If the time has already passed when he may recite Emes VeYatziv, as explained in siman 58, he should therefore recite another passage that mentions the Exodus. See the Mishnah Berurah there. See also below, siman 93, seif 4, regarding prayer, and the Mishnah Berurah there. Know that if his involvement in communal needs continues until after midday, when according to all opinions he is exempt even from prayer, he is nevertheless forbidden according to all opinions to eat before fulfilling the mitzvah of tefillin, whose time extends throughout the day. According to Peri Megadim in siman 89, he is also forbidden to eat before praying Minchah. Therefore, when one half-hour after midday arrives, he should pray Minchah and then eat. See Machaneh Yisrael, chapter 7.
