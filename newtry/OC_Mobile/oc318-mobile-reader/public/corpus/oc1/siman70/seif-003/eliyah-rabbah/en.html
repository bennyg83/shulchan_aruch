[3] For communal needs, etc. For a communal mitzvah is greater; and Olas Tamid challenged this, and this is his wording: Yerushalmi—one engaged in communal needs is as if engaged in words of Torah—he interrupts and reads, until here his words. It is not difficult at all to my humble mind, for Beit Yosef's intent is "as if engaged in words of Torah for the many" that he mentioned above in Beit Yosef—that Rabbeinu HaKadosh did not interrupt when he taught the many; and likewise it implies in Levush that he wrote it is like one learning Torah for the many, and a communal mitzvah is greater. Olas Tamid also overlooked what Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 2 of Hilchos Keriat Shema, that Rambam explains the Yerushalmi regarding prayer specifically—for if it were Keriat Shema he should have said he interrupts when engaged in communal needs as for words of Torah, until here—behold Beit Yosef raised his own difficulty and therefore explains it regarding prayer. But in truth this is a great wonder, for Rabbi Beit Yosef's words contradict themselves; and I have an even greater wonder on Lechem Mishneh there, who wrote that Beit Yosef in siman 70 explained it regarding prayer and expanded to challenge him—see there; and I do not know what came over him—the rest of his words are wondrous as well—but I have no business to expand in this book.
