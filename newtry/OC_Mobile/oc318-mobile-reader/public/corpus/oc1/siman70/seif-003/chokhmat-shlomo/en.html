Seif 3. One who marries a virgin is exempt from Krias Shema, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham, who wrote that even if he wishes to be stringent with himself, he is not permitted to do so, because it appears arrogant. He discusses whether the halachah here follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. See the laws of fasts and the laws of Tisha B'Av, siman 554, where, when it can be said that he simply has no work, there is no concern. I have seen in Tosafos, chapter Eizehu Neshech, 67a, s.v. Ravina ate by deduction, as follows: Rabbeinu Tam says that Ravina did not wish to assume the title and conduct himself as a Torah scholar. This proves that Ravina held that the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, that not everyone who wishes to assume the title may come and assume it. Yet even according to this, the matter is very difficult, for the Gemara explicitly states in the fourth chapter of Pesachim that where an observer can say that he simply has no work, we are certainly not concerned for arrogance. Here, who knows whether he has a loan arranged by deduction and that he is stringent because he is a Torah scholar? This is certainly no worse than refraining from labor on Tisha B'Av, where we are not concerned for arrogance, and all the more so here. This presently requires great study. One cannot say that Ravina follows the Sages in the fourth chapter of Pesachim, who hold that even on Tisha B'Av we are concerned for arrogance. That cannot be, for the Gemara there raises a contradiction between the Sages' positions and answers that there, since everyone else works and he does not, it appears arrogant. This answer applies to labor on Tisha B'Av, where most people work and the matter is visible and public, so that if he refrains it appears arrogant. But here, do most people eat through a deduction arrangement? Certainly not. Even where they do, is it evident whether the arrangement is by deduction or not? Therefore arrogance certainly does not apply here. This is even clearer than Krias Shema, concerning which the Sages hold that arrogance does not apply. Thus, according to both the Sages and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, arrogance should not apply here. Perhaps Ravina follows the Gemara's other answer, which reverses the positions, and holds that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is concerned for arrogance in every case, and therefore he was concerned for arrogance even here. It requires study why Tosafos and the commentators did not take note of this. Examine this carefully.
