(10) The virgin. He is preoccupied with a mitzvah, because his thoughts are occupied with consummating the marriage. This does not apply when marrying a widow, for he is not similarly preoccupied.
(11) From Krias Shema. This includes its blessings. The same applies to prayer, as explained below in siman 106.
(12) Three days. And four nights. For example, if he married on Wednesday afternoon, he remains exempt through and including Motzaei Shabbos. At times the exemption may extend through four days, such as when he entered the chuppah on Wednesday before reciting the morning Krias Shema.
(13) If he did not consummate. After consummation, when he is no longer preoccupied, he is obligated. From the end of this period onward he is obligated even if he did not consummate the marriage, because once three days have passed without consummation he abandons his immediate expectation of it and is no longer preoccupied.
(14) He recites. Meaning, he is obligated to recite Krias Shema, together with its blessings, in the usual manner, and he also prays. Since he is obligated in Krias Shema, he is obligated in prayer. If he does not recite it, he appears arrogant, as though claiming that he is able to concentrate at all times. Regarding tefillin, see the Mishnah Berurah above in siman 38, seif 7.
(15) He should recite Krias Shema. It is explained there that one who is only mildly intoxicated recites Krias Shema and prays, because in our times we do not in any event attain such complete concentration. If, however, he is unable to speak before a king, he may not recite Krias Shema or pray until the effect of the wine has passed.
