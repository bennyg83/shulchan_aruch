(10) The virgin [bridegroom]—because he is preoccupied with preoccupation of a mitzvah, as he thinks about the matter of virgins; unlike with a widow, he is not preoccupied.

(11) Kriat Shema—and its blessings; and the same applies to prayer, as will be explained below in siman 106.

(12) Three days—and four nights; for example, if he married on the fourth day at noon, he is exempt until Motza'ei Shabbat, and until included. And sometimes four days may be envisioned—for example, if he entered the chuppah on the fourth day before he had recited the morning Kriat Shema, he is exempt four days.

(13) If he did not do [so]—but after the act, when he is no longer preoccupied, he is obligated; and likewise from this time onward he is obligated even if he did not consummate the marriage, for since three days have already passed and he did not do the act, he despairs afterward of the matter and is not preoccupied at all.

(14) He reads—meaning, he is obligated to read with its blessings as required, and also to pray; for since he is obligated in Kriat Shema, he is obligated in prayer; and if he does not read, he appears arrogant, as though he shows that he intends [to read] at every hour. And regarding tefillin, see above in siman 38, seif 7, in the Mishna Berurah.

(15) He should read Kriat Shema—there it was explained that in slight intoxication he reads and prays; for in our time in any case we do not have such intention. But if he cannot speak before the king, he should not read and pray until he removes the wine from upon him.
