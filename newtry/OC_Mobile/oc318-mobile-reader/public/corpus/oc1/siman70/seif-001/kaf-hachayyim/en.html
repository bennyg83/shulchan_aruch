1) [Seif 1] Women and slaves are exempt from Krias Shema, etc. It appears to me that women are also exempt from the blessings of Krias Shema, for those blessings likewise have a fixed time, as stated at the end of siman 58. Nevertheless, it appears to me that they are obligated to recite Emes Veyatziv, because remembering the Exodus from Egypt is a Torah obligation, as stated in siman 66. They must therefore place redemption immediately before prayer, for they are obligated in prayer, as stated in siman 106, seif 2. Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Siddur Beis Yaakov, in the laws of the evening Krias Shema, paragraphs 1-2, writes that women are likewise obligated to recite Emes Ve'emunah at night. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, however, writes that women are obligated to remember the Exodus only by rabbinic law; see there. Shaagas Aryeh, siman 12, likewise writes that women have no Torah obligation to remember the Exodus; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, cites his ruling. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. What Siddur Beis Yaakov writes there (that the primary view follows Magen Avraham) is not compelling, for what connection does one matter have with the other? See there and analyze this carefully. Nevertheless, even according to the view that women are exempt, if they perform the mitzvah they receive reward, although not like one who is commanded and performs it, as our Sages state in Kiddushin 31a; see there. See what we wrote on this subject in our sacred work Chukei Chaim, in the first discourse concerning the purchase of a Torah scroll; see there. If women wish to recite the other blessings of Krias Shema, as well as Pesukei DeZimra and its blessings, they are permitted to do so. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Women who know how to study therefore customarily recite the entire order of prayer like men, no less than from the passage of the Akeidah through the conclusion of Aleinu.
2) There: Women and slaves are exempt, etc. A person who is half slave and half free is obligated according to the final Mishnah, which rules that his master is compelled to free him. Where the master is a minor and therefore cannot be compelled, or where the slave had already fulfilled the mitzvah to be fruitful and multiply while he was a non-Jew, before becoming a slave, he is not compelled to free him; according to the Yerushalmi, such a half slave is exempt from Krias Shema. Hagahos Rabbi Akiva Eiger. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, writes that our Gemara implies that the Yerushalmi's ruling is rejected. The Mishnah states simply that "slaves" are exempt, implying only complete slaves, whereas one who is half slave and half free is obligated, as the Gemara concludes in Chagigah; see there. See below, siman 589, seif 5. A tumtum and an androgynos are obligated because of doubt, as stated above in siman 17, seif 2, and below in siman 589, seif 4.
3) There: Women and slaves, etc. A mute person who can hear but cannot speak should listen to another person, intend to fulfill his obligation, and thereby fulfill it. Shaagas Aryeh, siman 6. A person who can speak but cannot hear can fulfill his obligation through his own recitation. Shaagas Aryeh, siman 7.
4) There: Because it is a time-bound positive mitzvah. Women are likewise exempt from the blessing over the new moon because it is a time-bound positive mitzvah. Although women perform other positive mitzvos, such as sukkah, they do not perform this mitzvah because they caused the moon's diminishment. Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 426 in the name of Shelah, and in the present siman, seif katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah there, paragraph 7; Solet Belulah in the present siman, paragraph 1. Chida likewise rules in Machazik Berachah, siman 426, paragraph 4; see there. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 231c; Siddur Beis Yaakov, in the laws of the blessing over the moon, paragraph 18. See further below, siman 426, paragraph 1.
5) There: It is proper to teach them to accept the yoke of the Kingdom of Heaven. This means the first verse, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Ohel Moed. Levush likewise writes that it is proper to teach them to accept the Kingdom of Heaven, as Maran states. So too Pri Chadash, paragraph 1; Solet Belulah in Mekor Chaim, paragraph 1; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Yad Aharon in Hagahos Beis Yosef; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1; Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 94a; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; and Siddur Beis Yaakov, in the laws of the evening Krias Shema, paragraph 1. This is contrary to Bach, cited by Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, who writes that they are obligated to accept the Kingdom of Heaven; see there. Rather, it is only proper and fitting to teach them, etc. It is likewise proper and fitting to teach them to recite "Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever" together with the first verse, for it is included with the first verse, as stated above in siman 66, seif 1, in our comments there, paragraph 13, and in siman 61, paragraph 45. Levush, Solet Belulah, Ateres Zekeinim on this seif, and Shalmei Tzibbur there write likewise; Kesher Gudal, siman 11, paragraph 3.
