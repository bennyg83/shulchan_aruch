1) [Seif 1] Women and slaves are exempt from Krias Shema, etc. It appears to me that women are also exempt from the blessings of Krias Shema, for those blessings likewise have a fixed time, as stated at the end of siman 58. Nevertheless, it appears to me that they are obligated to recite Emes Veyatziv, because remembering the Exodus from Egypt is a Torah obligation, as stated in siman 66. They must therefore place redemption immediately before prayer, for they are obligated in prayer, as stated in siman 106, seif 2. Magen Avraham, seif katan 1; Solet Belulah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Siddur Beis Yaakov, in the laws of the evening Krias Shema, paragraphs 1–2, writes that women are likewise obligated to recite Emes Ve'emunah at night. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, however, writes that women are obligated to remember the Exodus only by rabbinic law; see there. Shaagas Aryeh, siman 12, likewise writes that women have no Torah obligation to remember the Exodus; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, cites his ruling. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. What Siddur Beis Yaakov writes there (that the primary view follows Magen Avraham) is not compelling, for what connection does one matter have with the other? See there and analyze this carefully. Nevertheless, even according to the view that women are exempt, if they perform the mitzvah they receive reward, although not like one who is commanded and performs it, as our Sages state in Kiddushin 31a; see there. See what we wrote on this subject in our sacred work Chukei Chaim, in the first discourse concerning the purchase of a Torah scroll; see there. If women wish to recite the other blessings of Krias Shema, as well as Pesukei DeZimra and its blessings, they are permitted to do so. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Women who know how to study therefore customarily recite the entire order of prayer like men, no less than from the passage of the Akeidah through the conclusion of Aleinu.
