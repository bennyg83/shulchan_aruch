(Seif katan 1) Women, etc. They are likewise exempt from the blessings of Krias Shema. Meaning, although the blessings of Krias Shema are not intrinsically connected to Krias Shema, as the Magen Avraham wrote above in siman 58, seif katan 7, and in siman 60, and as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 46 in the name of a responsum of the Rashba, bringing proof from the fact that we do not recite the blessing, "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to recite the Shema," and he also cited this in the Beit Yosef in siman 60, one might therefore have thought that although women are exempt from Krias Shema, they are nevertheless obligated in its blessings. The ruling teaches that for the very reason they are exempt from Krias Shema, namely because it is a time-bound positive mitzvah, they are likewise exempt from its blessings, since the blessings too are time-bound, namely until the end of the fourth hour, as stated at the end of siman 58.
And this is also evident from what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote. He required a proof so that one should not say that, since blessings are D'Rabbanan, the category of a time-bound positive mitzvah does not apply to a Rabbinic matter. This is in fact Rashi's view, as will be explained below.
Although prayer, etc. This refers to the Mishnah on page 20 that women are obligated in prayer.
We treat it as such, or alternatively, because it is a plea for mercy. This is the correct reading. Meaning, since prayer is a request for one's needs, a woman too should appropriately ask these things from Hashem. Do women not also require life and their other needs? It follows that Rabbeinu Yonah maintains that women are exempt even from a Rabbinic time-bound obligation. One cannot say that he holds prayer to be a Torah-level positive mitzvah, as discussed below in siman 106, seif 2, because the Torah-level mitzvah of prayer is not time-bound, as explained there. His question must therefore concern the Rabbinic obligation of prayer, which the Sages fixed at particular times. The Magen Avraham's proof is consequently sound. This also appears in the Gemara on page 20b according to our text. The Gemara asks regarding the Mishnah's ruling that women are obligated in prayer, "Is this not obvious?" It answers that one might have thought that, since it is written, "Evening, morning, and noon I shall speak," prayer resembles a time-bound positive mitzvah. The Mishnah therefore teaches that prayer is a plea for mercy. Rabbeinu Yonah evidently did not have this reading. Tosafos had this reading and wrote: "Rashi does not include this text, since prayer is D'Rabbanan, and what relevance does a positive mitzvah have to it? Nevertheless, one may answer that Hallel too is D'Rabbanan, yet women are exempt because it is a time-bound positive mitzvah."
