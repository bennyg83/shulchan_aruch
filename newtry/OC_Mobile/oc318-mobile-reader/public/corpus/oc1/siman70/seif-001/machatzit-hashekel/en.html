(Subnote 1) Women, etc. — also from the blessings of Keriat Shema. Meaning: the blessing of Keriat Shema has no connection to Keriat Shema itself, as Magen Avraham wrote above Siman 58 subnote 7. And in Siman 60 Rav Bachya likewise in Siman 46 in the name of responsa Rashba, and he brought proof from that we do not bless “Who commanded us concerning Keriat Shema” to read the Shma. And he brought it also in Siman 60 in Beit Yosef. If so, one would think they are exempt from Keriat Shema but nevertheless obligated in its blessings — this teaches that for the same reason they are exempt from Keriat Shema, for it is a positive time-bound commandment, for the same reason they are exempt from its blessings, for the blessings too are a positive time-bound commandment — that is, until four hours, as stated at the end of Siman 58.

And likewise from what Terumat HaDeshen wrote: he needed proof lest one say that since the blessings are rabbinic and in rabbinic speech the category of positive time-bound commandment does not apply to them — whereas in truth that is Rashi’s view, as I will state below.

And even though prayer, etc. — this bears on what we learned on daf 4, that women are obligated in prayer.

We judge it — or alternatively because it is mercy, as I need to write. And the meaning is: since it is a request for His needs, it is also fitting that a woman should request from Him, may He be blessed — is it that women do not need life and other needs? From this we hear that Terumat HaDeshen holds that even rabbinically, in a matter that is time-bound, women are exempt; it is not that he holds prayer is a Torah-level positive commandment — and as I wrote in Siman 106 seif 2, for behold “the mitzvot to perform” from the Torah regarding prayer are not time-dependent, as I wrote in Siman 106. Rather, necessarily his question was on rabbinic prayer that has a fixed time rabbinically, and therefore he properly challenged Magen Avraham. And so too in the Gemara 20b according to our version, for it challenges on what we learned that women are obligated in prayer — it is simple, and it answers: one would think that since it is written “evening and morning and noon I speak,” it is like a positive time-bound commandment — what does it teach that they are mercy? (And therefore Terumat HaDeshen did not have it thus in their version.) So is the version of Tosafos, and Tosafos wrote as follows: Rashi does not have this text, for prayer is rabbinic — what positive commandment applies to it? And nevertheless one could say that behold Hallel is rabbinic and women are exempt for that reason, for it is a positive time-bound commandment, end quote.

Also from Birkat HaLevanah, etc. He brought in Siman 426 in the name of Shelah: even though on other positive time-bound commandments women may be strict upon themselves to fulfill them and to bless upon them, as above Siman 17 — nevertheless for Birkat HaLevanah they are not permitted to bless; see there the reason.
