[1] It is proper, etc. This is Beit Yosef's wording citing Ohel Moed that they are obligated in the first verse. Olas Tamid wrote, and I wonder, for Shas (Berachos 20a) asks: it is obvious that women are exempt from Keriat Shema because it is a positive time-bound mitzvah; and it answers—what might you have thought? Since it contains acceptance of Heaven's kingship, it would imply that even accepting Heaven's kingship of the first verse they are exempt—until here. Bach already answered it that this is what Shas means: since in any case they are obligated to accept Heaven's kingship of the first verse, it would imply they are likewise obligated in reading all three paragraphs—and Nachalas Tzvi explained likewise. Even without this it seems to me there is no wonder on Ohel Moed from Shas, for I found in Rashba's chiddushim that some textual readings do not have this text; rather we read "What is different about Keriat Shema and tefillin?" and it answers they are positive time-bound mitzvos and women are exempt—and this is the correct reading, until here; and I likewise saw in Knesset HaGedolah; if so one should say Ohel Moed had that reading. Lechem Chamudim wrote on page 19 that it appears one should explain that Ohel Moed does not mean obligated by Torah law, for if so according to us, who hold Keriat Shema by Torah law is only the first verse, what would women's exemption be more than men's? Rather to obligate them by rabbinic law only; therefore Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote in this language "and it is proper" to teach so, until here. It does not appear so to me, for the expression "and it is proper" does not suit a rabbinic obligation; moreover there is the passage of tzitzis by Torah law as above siman 67—if so one should say that in this women are more exempt than men. (However I found in Magen Avraham—in this wording it appears to me that in any case they are obligated to say emes ve-yatziv, for mention of the Exodus from Egypt is by Torah law; therefore they must connect redemption to prayer, until here his words. But it is somewhat difficult from what he himself wrote in siman 106 that most women do not pray regularly because they say in the morning near netilah some request—in this they certainly do not connect redemption to prayer nor mention it.) One can also say that what it calls exempt from Keriat Shema means exempt even from rabbinic obligation, like the Tanna taught there they are obligated in prayer which is only rabbinic, and as Tosafos wrote s.v. bi-tefillah, etc., that they are exempt from Hallel which is rabbinic. Therefore it appears to me that certainly Ohel Moed speaks of an obligation by Torah law, as Bach and Nachalas Tzvi also ruled; and that Shulchan Aruch wrote the wording "and it is proper" is because it brought in Knesset HaGedolah that it found a gloss in Beit Yosef who wrote about Ohel Moed "I do not know his source for this wording and I do not see that the poskim hold so," until here; therefore Shulchan Aruch wrote only the wording "and it is proper." Incidentally I saw to bring a new matter that the author of Halachot Gedolot wrote in this wording about mezuzah: obviously they are obligated, for it is not time-bound—one might think to say that since it is written in it "when you lie down and when you rise" it should be like a positive time-bound mitzvah, it would imply—until here. And Shas has "what might you have thought—since it is equated to Torah study it would imply"; and this requires study.
