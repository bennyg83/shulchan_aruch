[1] And it is proper, etc. — Beit Yosef's wording in the name of Ohel Moed: they are obligated in the first verse. And Olat Tamid wrote — and I wonder, for the Talmud challenges [Berakhot] 20a: it is obvious that women are exempt from Keriat Shema for it is a positive time-bound commandment; and it answers — lest you say since it contains acceptance of the Kingdom of Heaven we are informed — behold even from acceptance of the Kingdom of Heaven of the first verse they are exempt — until here. And Bach already resolved for it: thus the Talmud says — since in any case they are obligated to accept the Kingdom of Heaven of the first verse, lest they also be obligated in reading all three sections we are informed; and so Nachalat Tzvi explained. Also in any case it appears to me there is no wonder on Ohel Moed from the Talmud, for I found in Chiddushei Rashba that there are versions that do not read thus; rather we read: what is different about Keriat Shema and tefillin; and it answers — they are positive time-bound commandments and are exempt; and this is the correct version — until here; and so I saw in Kenesset HaGedolah; and if so one can say Ohel Moed reads thus. Lechem Chamudot 19a — it appears to explain that Ohel Moed speaks not of Torah obligation; for if so for us who hold Keriat Shema from the Torah is only the first verse — what are women exempt more than men; rather to obligate them rabbinically only; and therefore Shulchan Arukh and Levush wrote this wording 'and it is proper to teach' — until here. And it does not appear so to me, for on rabbinic obligation the wording 'and it is proper' is not appropriate; and further behold parashat tzitzit is from the Torah as above siman 67 — if so one can say that in this women are exempt more than men. (However I found in Magen Avraham this wording: it seems to me that in any case they are obligated to say Emet veYatziv for remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt is from the Torah; and if so they need to adjoin redemption to prayer — until here his wording. Only somewhat difficult from what he himself wrote in siman 106 that most women do not pray regularly because they say in the morning near washing some request — behold in this they certainly do not adjoin redemption to prayer and also do not mention.) And further one can say that what it says exempt from Keriat Shema means even from rabbinic obligation women are exempt, as it taught there they are obligated in prayer which is only rabbinic; and as Tosafot s.v. batefilah wrote, etc., that they are exempt from Hallel which is rabbinic. Therefore it appears to me that certainly Ohel Moed speaks of Torah obligation as Bach and Nachalat Tzvi also ruled; and that Shulchan Arukh wrote the wording 'and it is proper' because he brought in Kenesset HaGedolah that he found a gloss of Beit Yosef who wrote on Ohel Moed this wording: I do not know from where he has it and he does not see that the view of the decisors is so — until here; therefore Shulchan Arukh wrote only 'and it is proper.'
