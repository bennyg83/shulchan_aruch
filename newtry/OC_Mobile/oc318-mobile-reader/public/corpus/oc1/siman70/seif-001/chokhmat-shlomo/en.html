Seif 3. One who marries a virgin is exempt from Keriat Shema, etc. Marginal note: see Magen Avraham, who wrote that even if he wishes to be strict on himself he is not permitted, for it appears presumptuous; and he debated whether the halachah follows Rabbi Shimon ben Gamliel in this; and see Hilchos Taanis and Hilchos Tisha B'Av (siman 554), where one can say it is melacha he lacks time for. I saw in Tosafos Pesachim s.v. Ravina achal b'nekiyasa, and Rabbenu Tam's language is that Ravina did not wish to take the title and make himself a Torah scholar, so conclude. From this it is proven that Ravina held the halachah follows Rabbi Shimon ben Gamliel—not everyone who wishes to take the title may come and take it. Even so it is very puzzling, for it is explicit in Shas Pesachim chapter 4 that when the observer says it is melacha he lacks time for, we certainly are not concerned for presumption; and here who knows whether he has a loan in nekiyasa, and that he is strict because he is a Torah scholar—behold it is more lenient than melacha on Tisha B'Av, where we are not concerned for presumption in that case; how much more so here—and this requires much study now. One cannot say that Ravina held like the rabbis in Pesachim chapter 4 who hold that even on Tisha B'Av we are concerned for this presumption, for Shas there asks one set of rabbis on another and answers—there, since everyone did it and he did not do it, it appears presumptuous; granted for melacha on Tisha B'Av the majority of the world works and it is something noticeable and well known, therefore if he does not do it it appears presumptuous; but here, is the majority of the world eating in nekiyasa? Certainly not; and if they eat, is it recognizable whether he is in nekiyasa or not? Certainly there is no presumption concern here; and Keriat Shema, which the rabbis hold has no presumption concern in this—therefore for both the rabbis and Rabbi Shimon ben Gamliel there it does not apply. Perhaps Ravina holds the other answer there that answers the opposite, and he held Rabbi Shimon ben Gamliel is concerned for presumption in every matter, and therefore here too we are concerned for presumption—and requires study why Tosafos and the commentaries did not notice this; put your mind to it.
