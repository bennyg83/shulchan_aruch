(1) They are exempt—even rabbinically.

(2) Kriat Shema—and they are also exempt from the blessings of Kriat Shema, for these too have a fixed time, as above in siman 58, seif 6. But the blessing of Emet veYatziv, which was instituted for the matter of remembering the Exodus from Egypt, and likewise the blessings that follow it in the evening—they are obligated to recite them, for the mitzvah of remembering the Exodus from Egypt applies by day and by night; and if so, they must juxtapose redemption to prayer, for in prayer they are obligated, as below in siman 106, seif 2. Thus wrote the Magen Avraham; and see in the Peri Megadim, who wrote that according to one who holds that remembrance of the Exodus at night is only rabbinic and a support from a verse—if so, it is automatically a positive time-bound commandment, and they are exempt from the Torah and obligated only rabbinically; and likewise in Yeshuot Yaakov—see there. And even if one would say that it is a Torah mitzvah by day and by night, in Sefer Sha'agat Aryeh, siman 13, he leans that women are exempt because the daytime remembrance is an independent mitzvah: if he did not mention it by day, he need not mention this remembrance at night; and what he mentions at night is an independent mitzvah—see there. And regarding pesukei dezimra, see above in siman 52 in the innovations of R' Eliezer Ashkenazi, for it is evident from his words that they were instituted primarily for prayer; if so, they are obligated. And regarding the blessing of the morning—this seemingly depends on whether one would say that these blessings have a [fixed] time; see above at the end of siman 52 in the Mishna Berurah and in Biur Halacha, and it is uncertain; but from the conclusion of the language of the Taz in siman 46, seif 4—and especially from the Levush there, see there—it implies that for blessings they bless the morning blessings like men. However, all this we wrote regarding obligation; but it is obvious that they may take obligation upon themselves and bless, even the blessings of Kriat Shema, as above in siman 17, seif 2, in the gloss.

(3) A positive time-bound commandment—for the verse ties it to the time of lying down and the time of rising.

(4) And it is proper—this implies from the law that they are exempt; and the Bach ruled that by law they are obligated to accept the yoke of Heaven with one verse—see there; but many later authorities disagree on this.

(5) Shamayim (heaven)—in Nachalat Tzvi he explained that the author's intent is one entire paragraph, and the Rama added on him that in any case [they recite at least] the first verse; but in the Levush it implies that the author's intent as well is only one verse—and this is what the Rama explained.
