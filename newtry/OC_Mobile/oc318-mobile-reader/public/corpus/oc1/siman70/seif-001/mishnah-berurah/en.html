(1) Are exempt. Even on a D'Rabbanan level.
(2) From Krias Shema. They are also exempt from the blessings of Krias Shema, since those blessings likewise have a fixed time, as stated above in siman 58, seif 6. They are, however, obligated to recite Emes VeYatziv, which was instituted concerning the remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt, and the blessings following the evening Krias Shema, because the mitzvah to remember the Exodus applies by day and by night. Consequently, they must juxtapose redemption to prayer, since they are obligated in prayer, as stated below in siman 106, seif 2. This is the Magen Avraham's ruling. See Peri Megadim, who writes that according to the view that remembering the Exodus at night is only D'Rabbanan and the verse is merely an asmachta, it is a time-bound positive mitzvah. Women are then exempt on a Torah level and obligated only D'Rabbanan. Yeshuos Yaakov writes similarly. Even if we say it is a Torah-level mitzvah by day and by night, the Shaagas Aryeh, siman 13, suggests that women are exempt because the daytime remembrance is a separate mitzvah. If one failed to remember it by day, one need not compensate for that remembrance at night; the nighttime remembrance is an independent mitzvah. Regarding Pesukei DeZimrah, see Rabbi Akiva Eiger's novellae above in siman 52, from which it appears that they were principally instituted for prayer, and women are consequently obligated. Regarding Bircos HaShachar, this would apparently depend on whether these blessings have a fixed time. See the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah at the end of siman 52. This requires study. The unqualified language of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 46, seif 4, and especially that of the Levush there, implies that women recite Bircos HaShachar like men. All this concerns obligation. It is obvious, however, that women may voluntarily assume the obligation and recite even the blessings of Krias Shema, as stated above in siman 17, seif 2, in the gloss.
(3) That is time-bound. The Torah made it dependent on the time of lying down and the time of rising.
(4) And this is proper. This implies that they are exempt according to the strict law. The Bach ruled that they are legally obligated to accept the yoke of Heaven by reciting the first verse. Many Acharonim disagree.
(5) Heaven. Nachalas Tzvi explains that the Mechaber intended the entire first paragraph. The Rama adds that they should at least recite the entire first verse. The Levush, however, implies that the Mechaber too intended only the first verse, and this is what the Rama clarifies.
