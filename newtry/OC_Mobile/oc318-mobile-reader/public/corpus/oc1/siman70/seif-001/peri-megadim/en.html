What the author wrote in seif 2, according to Rashi: when he reaches the age of education he is exempt—that is, when he is already six or seven; but from age twelve his father is obligated to educate him, as with tefillin—see the Bach here and in siman 37, Magen Avraham, sign 3. For the author as well, specifically from age twelve; but less than that, all the more so for Kriat Shema; and here he wrote only that it is fitting to conduct oneself according to Rabbeinu Tam. However, in Eliyah Rabbah, sign 2, he wrote that tefillin and tzitzit—their time is all day—see there. And truly regarding tzitzit (in siman 17) there is proof that there, even less than age twelve, whoever knows how to wrap himself [is obligated]. And what Rashi wrote in Berachos 20b, that minors are obligated in prayer of mercy—according to R' Yehuda, the morning prayer is until four hours; perhaps it is not common; but per the Rabbis, in any case until chatzot—see siman 58 in the Beit Yosef.

Kriat Shema—in sign 17 he brought the words of the Beit Yosef, and there is a practical difference nowadays in a case where the groom wished not to read—the choice is in his hand (for according to Shulchan Aruch there is no ruling whether he is obligated to read; and the Magen Avraham wrote that it implies he must read now); therefore he wrote this. And regarding a widow—even though he is engaged in preparing the marriage, it is not considered preoccupation but only a mitzvah by itself, and he is exempt. And from what the author wrote three days and did not write four nights—he holds until Motza'ei Shabbat, and until included, and he is obligated in Kriat Shema; and see Peri Chadash likewise, that the view of Mahari Abuhav and the author is so; but the Rosh and Ra'avyah explained until included. And indeed in Mahari Abuhav one could say so; but in the Beit Yosef it was laid down in his answer that four nights as well is fine—so why did he return here? And in Eliyah Rabbah, sign 3, and until included—see there. And what he wrote in sign 5, from here onward he forgives her the covenant of blood—not because she is not preoccupied anymore. And now they sanctify under the chuppah; and also in their time with a Jewish wife there is room for concern (Kesubos 9a), but nevertheless he wants to know if she is not unrestrained; and the same applies to one who married a woman who entered the chuppah and was not consummated, as stated. And there is a practical difference nowadays: according to one who holds that if he wishes he does not read—even the covenant of blood; see Ra'avyah and Tosefos, chapter 2, mishnah 5 of Berachos, Kriat Shema on Ra'avyah, four nights—one could say on the contrary, three days and not "until" included. And what he wrote there regarding the reason applies all the more so to her as well, and she waits—see there.
