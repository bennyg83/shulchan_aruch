What the Mechaber wrote in seif 2, that according to Rashi a minor who has reached the age of education is exempt, means one who is already six or seven years old. But at age thirteen one is obligated to train him, as with tefillin. See Bach here and Magen Avraham, siman 37, seif katan 3 and 4, that according to the Mechaber as well, this applies specifically at age thirteen, but not below that age, all the more so regarding Krias Shema. Here he wrote only that it is proper to follow Rabbeinu Tam. However, Olas Tamid, seif katan 2, wrote that tefillin and tzitzis apply throughout the entire day; see there. The truth is that there is a proof from tzitzis, in siman 17, for there even one younger than thirteen is included once he knows how to wrap himself. What Rashi wrote in Berachos 2b, that minors are obligated in prayer because it is a request for mercy, can be understood according to Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that Shacharis may be prayed only until the fourth hour, since the child may not commonly be available then. According to the Sages, however, one may in any event pray until midday. See siman 106 in Beit Yosef.
From Krias Shema. See Taz, who cited the words of Beit Yosef. A practical difference nowadays may be that if the groom wishes not to recite Krias Shema, he has permission to refrain. The Shulchan Aruch does not decisively establish whether he is obligated to recite, though in Magen Avraham I will write that it implies that he must recite nowadays. Therefore, Taz wrote this qualification. Regarding a widower marrying a widow, even though he is occupied with preparations for the wedding, this is not considered anxiety, but merely involvement in a mitzvah; see Beit Yosef. From the Mechaber's wording, 'three days,' without writing 'four nights,' it appears that he holds that the exemption lasts until Motzaei Shabbos but not including it, so that he is obligated on Motzaei Shabbos. See Peri Chadash, who likewise wrote that this is the opinion of Mahari Abuhav and the Mechaber. Rosh and Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura, however, explained that Motzaei Shabbos is included. In truth, Mahari Abuhav may be understood this way, but Beit Yosef accepted his resolution that four nights is also accurate, so why did the Mechaber alter the wording here? Olas Tamid, seif katan 3, writes that Motzaei Shabbos is included; see there. What he wrote in seif katan 5, that from then onward he should forgive her concerning the claim of a severed member, is difficult to understand, unless the intent is merely that he is no longer anxious. Nowadays they perform kiddushin under the chuppah, and even in their time, regarding the wife of an Israelite there is a double doubt, as explained in Kesubos 9a. Nevertheless, he wants to know whether she was wanton. The same applies where he married a woman who entered the chuppah but with whom relations were not consummated, as stated. A practical difference nowadays, according to the view that if he wishes he need not recite, is also in the case of one with a severed member. See Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura and Tosafos Yom Tov on Berachos, chapter 2, mishnah 5. What Tosafos Yom Tov wrote concerning Rav Ovadiah's phrase 'four nights' may be understood oppositely, that one should not mistakenly say that Motzaei Shabbos is excluded. See what I wrote there concerning the reason that the same certainly applies to her and that he should wait; see there.
