(21) Engaged in eating—and what is called a beginning, see below in siman 235, seif 2.

(22) He finishes and afterward [reads], etc.—that is, when he estimates that time will remain for him to read; and what he wrote, and if he was afraid—meaning, he is afraid that perhaps his estimate is not accurate.

(23) And per the Ra'avad he stops, etc.—and most later authorities agreed, and almost all of them, that the main matter depends on whether it is a Torah matter: whether he began in permissibility or in prohibition; and if so, accordingly—in work or in all these matters, except eating—if he began before dawn, he need not stop if time will remain for him after he finishes to read Kriat Shema and pray; for we rule below in siman 89, seif 7, that they did not decree near dawn; and if so, he began in permissibility. But if he began after dawn rose, for he began in prohibition—he stops for Kriat Shema, which is from the Torah, but not for blessings and not for prayer; and afterward, when he finishes the work, he reads Kriat Shema with the blessings and prays—but only if time will still remain for him; but if he sees that the time is passing, he must stop immediately. And wherever he does not stop, even if the time of prayer with the congregation will pass, he is not obligated to stop. And regarding eating—it implies from the author in siman 89, seif 5, that he holds for the main law that for eating one must stop immediately from when dawn rises, even if he began in permissibility; and no advice helps in such a case that he did not pray.

(24) And he reads—it implies that after he reads Kriat Shema, even though he has not yet prayed, it is permitted to finish all these matters that he began, as we wrote in the previous paragraph; and this wording is not precise specifically regarding if he was engaged in eating—for regarding eating we already wrote that he must also pray beforehand; and he cited it because of other matters, or alternatively because of the evening Kriat Shema he cited it, for all these laws apply in it as well. And regarding the evening—certainly if he began in all these five matters within the half hour before the stars emerge, he stops immediately; and when the stars emerge he reads Kriat Shema without its blessings, and afterward finishes his eating or other matter, and afterward reads Kriat Shema and its blessings and prays, as below in siman 235, seif 2—see the Peri Megadim there.

(25) Time remaining to read—a decree lest he prolong in them and the time of Kriat Shema pass; and it is not comparable to the Minchah prayer, for we rule in siman 232 that if he began in all these before he prayed, he need not stop—for since Kriat Shema is from the Torah, they were stricter in it.

(26) Siman 235—meaning, that there the distinction between beginning in permissibility and beginning in prohibition is explained; see there further.
