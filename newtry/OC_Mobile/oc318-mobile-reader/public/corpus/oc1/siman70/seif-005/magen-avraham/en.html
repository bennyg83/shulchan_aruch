If he was occupied with eating, etc. It must be that the case is where he began in a forbidden situation, for if he began in a permitted situation, he ruled in Siman 235 subnote 2 that he need not interrupt; rather the case is where he began in a forbidden situation — and even so according to Rambam he need not interrupt, since something uncommon to begin near dawn they did not decree, as stated Siman 89 subnote 7; and it is proved from here that Rambam holds that even for prayer he need not interrupt, for if he had to pray, he would also have had to read Keriat Shema in order to link redemption to prayer — rather certainly he need not interrupt; and therefore Tur wrote Siman 89 in his [Rambam’s] name that he holds he need not interrupt. And Rav Bachya wrote there that it is a printers’ error in Tur, and it does not appear to me as he wrote; and even though for lulav he must interrupt, as stated Siman 652 — lulav is different, for its time is the whole day, and in the middle of the day eating and drinking are common, not so for Keriat Shema, as stated above; and Bach ruled that Rambam speaks where he began in a permitted situation and Raavad speaks where he began in a forbidden situation, and they do not disagree — and so one should rule.
