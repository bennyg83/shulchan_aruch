If one was occupied in eating, etc. — it should read it deals where he began in prohibition; for if he began in permission he ruled in siman 235 seif 2 that he need not stop — rather it deals where he began in prohibition; and nevertheless per Rambam he need not stop since a matter that is not common to begin near morning they did not decree as written siman 89 seif 7; and it is proven from here that Rambam holds that even for prayer he need not stop — for if he needed to pray he would also need to read Keriat Shema in order to adjoin redemption to prayer; rather certainly he need not stop; and therefore Tur siman 89 wrote in his name that he holds he need not stop; and Rabbeinu Yosef wrote there it is a scribal error in Tur — and it does not appear so to me as I wrote; and although for lulav he must stop as written siman 252 — lulav is different for its time is all day and in the middle of the day eating and drinking is common, not so in Keriat Shema as explained; and Bach ruled Rambam deals where he began in permission and Raavad deals where he began in prohibition and they do not disagree, and so one should rule:
