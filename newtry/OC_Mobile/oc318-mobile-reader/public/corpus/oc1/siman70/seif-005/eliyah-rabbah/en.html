[5] And according to Ra'avad, etc. Levush wrote, and in Levushi Yom Tov he wrote, and see below siman 235 seif 5 that the unspecified ruling follows Ra'avad, until here. In my humble opinion here it speaks when he began before the time of obligation arrived, as Beit Yosef wrote in the second answer; therefore Levush was clever and wrote "and the time for Keriat Shema arrived," which implies he began before the time. But in siman 235 it speaks when he began after the time arrived, as he wrote explicitly: if he began eating after the time for Keriat Shema arrived he interrupts, etc.; if so it is clear that on the contrary the unspecified ruling follows Rambam. Bach understood Shulchan Aruch's words as ruling like the first answer and therefore wondered that the main point follows the second answer. He was not precise, for Shulchan Aruch copied Rambam's wording, and Beit Yosef already proved—and likewise from Rambam's own wording—that it speaks when he began before the time arrived; likewise Bach in chapter 2 of Hilchos Keriat Shema concludes so. From this it is likewise difficult against Magen Avraham, including what he wrote that one can wonder at Beit Yosef—wonder of wonders—that even Ra'avad agrees that if he began before the time he does not interrupt, for no one disagrees on this, except that Ra'avad did not bother to parse Rambam's words and thought it speaks after the time arrived, until here. I say his words are astounding—that Ra'avad would err in what is clear and proven from Rambam; and what he wrote that no one disagrees on this is not so, for below siman 652 he wrote there is proof from Tosafos Sukkah 38a s.v. mai, etc., that even if he began before the time he interrupts. I also wrote in this siman that Rambam and Rabbeinu Yerucham hold thus—that even if he began learning before the time he interrupts for Keriat Shema; however in this one can say eating is stricter than words of Torah, for for words of Torah he does not interrupt, for it is not an interruption since Keriat Shema is also words of Torah, which is not so for eating, and for other matters he does not interrupt as Kesef Mishneh wrote there. Likewise below siman 489 regarding counting the Omer and siman 652, Shulchan Aruch plainly [rules] that if he began before the prohibited time he does not interrupt; and likewise Beit Yosef wrote in siman 235 in the name of Mordechai and Ran; and likewise it implies in Shulchan Aruch and Levush there; and such is the main point and Rambam's view; and likewise Magen Avraham's view.
