[5] And according to the Raavad, etc. The Levush writes this, and the Malbushei Yom Tov comments: See below, siman 235, seif 5, where the ruling is stated anonymously in accordance with the Raavad. In my humble opinion, here the case is one in which he began before the time of obligation arrived, as the Beit Yosef writes in his second answer. The Levush therefore carefully writes, and the time for Krias Shema arrived, implying that he began beforehand. In siman 235, however, the case is one in which he began after the time arrived, as it explicitly says: If he began eating after the time for Krias Shema arrived, he interrupts. It is therefore clear that, to the contrary, the anonymous ruling follows Rambam. The Bach understood the Shulchan Aruch as ruling according to the Beit Yosef's first answer, and therefore objected that the second answer is primary. He was not precise, for the Shulchan Aruch copies Rambam's language, and the Beit Yosef already proved, as did the Bach himself from Rambam's language, that Rambam refers to one who began before the time arrived. The Bach likewise concludes this way in chapter 2 of the laws of Krias Shema. This also creates a difficulty with Magen Avraham, who writes in astonishment against the Beit Yosef that even the Raavad agrees that if one began before the time he does not interrupt, for no one disputes this, and that the Raavad merely failed to analyze Rambam's words and thought that Rambam referred to one who began after the time arrived. I find his words astonishing. Would the Raavad err regarding something clear and demonstrable from Rambam? Moreover, his assertion that no one disputes this is incorrect, for below, siman 652, I wrote that Tosafos in Sukkah 38a, s.v. What, implies that even if one began before the time, he interrupts. I also wrote in this siman that Rambam and Rabbeinu Yerucham hold that even if one began studying before the time, he interrupts for Krias Shema. One may nevertheless distinguish that eating is more severe than Torah study, for with Torah study he does not interrupt, since it is not truly an interruption, as Krias Shema itself consists of words of Torah, whereas with eating and the other activities he does not interrupt, as the Kesef Mishneh writes there. Below, in siman 489 regarding counting the Omer and in siman 652, the Shulchan Aruch likewise rules anonymously that if one began before the prohibited time, he does not interrupt. The Beit Yosef in siman 235 cites the Mordechai and Ran to the same effect, and this is also implied by the Shulchan Aruch and Levush there. This is the primary ruling, in accordance with Rambam's reasoning, and Magen Avraham agrees.
