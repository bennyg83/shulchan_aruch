And according to Ra'avad, etc. As written in Sukkah 38a: for a matter of Torah law one interrupts even when there is time left; and Shabbos 9b that one interrupts for Keriat Shema, etc.; and in the Gemara it asks—the first clause taught him this, etc.—but from interrupting for Keriat Shema it does not ask. But Rambam holds that the sugya there and there is like Rabbi Yehudah according to Rabbi Yosef, who says one interrupts for Kiddush; but Rabbi Yosei holds one does not interrupt; and we likewise rule that Havdalah does not establish a binding obligation—hence one does not interrupt even for words of Torah—those sugyos are rejected; and this is what it says here, that he does not interrupt even though it is words of Torah—to exclude those sugyos; and the same applies that he may begin near Shacharis, except that it teaches a greater novelty, as I wrote; and regarding prayer it writes that he begins—for cases where one interrupts, it goes without saying that even for Minchah one does not interrupt; and likewise Rambam does not hold a distinction between whether there is time left or not; therefore he omitted it also in Minchah prayer, and likewise in lulav he omitted the entire sugya there; but since there is no compelling proof to reject a sugya of a distinction between whether there is time or not, therefore he wrote "if he was afraid," etc.; and in that sugya Rabbi Zeira said—perhaps it is a mitzvah to interrupt, etc.; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 10 seif 1, and if he peeled, etc.; and Ra'avad's view follows Tosafos, and it appears that when he began in permission those sugyos above apply, and here it speaks when he began in prohibition.
