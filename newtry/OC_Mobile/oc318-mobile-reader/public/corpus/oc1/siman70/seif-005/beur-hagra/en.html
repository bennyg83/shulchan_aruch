Seif 5: If he was engaged, etc. It is even permitted to begin shortly before Shacharis, for the prohibition was stated only shortly before Minchah, when this activity is common, as stated in chapter 6 of Hilchos Tefillah and in Shulchan Aruch, siman 89. This is inferred from the Mishnah's wording, which specifically teaches shortly before Minchah.
And according to Raavad, etc. As stated in Sukkah 38a, regarding a Torah-level obligation one interrupts even when sufficient time remains, and in Shabbos 9b, one interrupts for Krias Shema, etc. The Gemara there challenges, 'Was this not already taught in the first clause?' but does not challenge from the ruling that one interrupts for Krias Shema. Rambam, however, maintains that the discussions there and there follow Rabbi Yehudah in Pesachim, who says that one interrupts for Kiddush. According to Rabbi Yose, who holds that one does not interrupt, and since we also rule that Havdalah does not establish a meal as concluded, it follows that one does not interrupt even for a Torah-level obligation. Those discussions are therefore rejected. This is why Rambam writes here that one does not interrupt even though Krias Shema is a Torah-level obligation, contrary to those discussions. The same applies that one may begin shortly before Shacharis, but Rambam teaches the greater novelty here. Regarding prayer he wrote that one may begin, for it is unnecessary to say that one does not interrupt, since one does not interrupt even for Minchah. Rambam also does not accept a distinction between whether sufficient time remains or not. He therefore omitted this distinction regarding Minchah as well, and regarding lulav he omitted the entire discussion there. Still, because there is no decisive proof to reject the distinction between a case where sufficient time remains and one where it does not, he wrote, 'If he was afraid,' etc. In the discussion there, Rabbi Zeira said, perhaps it is a mitzvah to interrupt, etc. See Yoreh Deah, siman 10, seif 1, regarding one who skinned, etc. Raavad's view follows Tosafos and the other poskim, that those discussions concern one who began permissibly, whereas here the case is one who began impermissibly.
