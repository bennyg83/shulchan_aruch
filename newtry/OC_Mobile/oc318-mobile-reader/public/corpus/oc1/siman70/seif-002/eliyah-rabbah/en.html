Levush wrote: one occupied in Torah is obligated to stop for Keriat Shema and its blessings; however even though the time of Keriat Shema arrived, as long as he still has time to read he may study and read afterward, etc. Difficulty — for below siman 89 seif 6 he wrote it is forbidden to study when the time of Keriat Shema and prayer arrived. And one can say that here it deals where he already began — we say he need not stop; and so he wrote at the end of siman 106 that if he has time he does not stop — strained and requires examination. And further difficulty — behold we learned Shabbat 11a they interrupt for Keriat Shema and it deals even where there is time; and so Rosh and Ran wrote chapter 1 of Shabbat and chapter Lulav HaGazul; and Beit Yosef, Shulchan Arukh, and Levush ruled in siman 252. However one can say that the laws here and end of siman 106 deal where he began before the time of prohibition and therefore he does not stop as below end of siman 252 — strained. Also difficult — from Rambam's wording chapter 2 of Laws of Keriat Shema and Rabbenu Yerucham page 24 who wrote 'if one was occupied in Torah and the time of Keriat Shema arrived he stops' — it is implied that even if he began before the time of prohibition he stops; and in this vein Beit Yosef sharpened at the end of this siman; also below end of siman 252 I placed a spark to the law that if he began in permission — see there and see what Sefat Emet wrote on this. And it appears to my humble opinion per Levush's view that he saw Beit Yosef's words at the end of siman 106 who brought Ba'al HaMaor's words that Ran brought who wrote they do not interrupt where there is time; but to his honor he did not go to its depth, for perforce Ba'al HaMaor deals only with prayer which is rabbinic, but in Keriat Shema which is Torah law they interrupt even where there is time as we said.
