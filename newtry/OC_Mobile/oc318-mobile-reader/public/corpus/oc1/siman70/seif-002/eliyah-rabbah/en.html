Levush wrote that one engaged in Torah must interrupt for Keriat Shema and its blessings; however even when the time for Keriat Shema arrived, as long as he still has time left to read, he may learn and read afterward, etc. It is difficult, for below siman 89 seif 6 he wrote it is forbidden to learn when the time for Keriat Shema and prayer arrived. One can say here it speaks when he already began—we say he need not interrupt; and likewise he wrote at the end of siman 106 that if he has time left he does not interrupt—but it is forced and requires study. It is further difficult: behold we learned in Shabbos 11a that one interrupts for Keriat Shema, and it speaks even when there is time left; and likewise wrote Rosh and Ran in the first chapter of Shabbos and in chapter Lulav Hagazul; and Beit Yosef, Shulchan Aruch, and Levush ruled in siman 652. However one can say that the halachos here and at the end of siman 106 speak when he began before the prohibited time, therefore he does not interrupt, as below at the end of siman 652—but it is forced. It is also difficult from Rambam's wording in chapter 2 of Hilchos Keriat Shema and Rabbeinu Yerucham page 24, who wrote: if he was engaged in Torah and the time for Keriat Shema arrived, he stops—it implies that even if he began before the prohibited time he stops; and in this vein Beit Yosef inferred at the end of this siman; likewise below at the end of siman 652 I analyzed the law carefully regarding that he began in permission—see there—and see what Soles Belulah wrote on this. What appears to my humble mind follows Levush, who saw Beit Yosef's words at the end of siman 106 where he brought Baal HaMaor, who brought Ran, who wrote that one does not interrupt when there is time left; but with forgiveness of his honor, he did not plumb its depth—for necessarily Baal HaMaor speaks only of prayer, which is rabbinic, but for Keriat Shema by Torah law one interrupts even when there is time left, as we said. Know that if not so, Ran's words in the first chapter of Shabbos itself would contradict themselves; it would also contradict his words in chapter Lulav Hagazul; and likewise it is clearer for one who studies Ran himself in the first chapter of Shabbos that Baal HaMaor speaks specifically of prayer, and Beit Yosef brought it briefly; and see below siman 93.
