The Levush writes that one who is engaged in Torah study must interrupt for Krias Shema and its blessings. Nevertheless, even when the time for Krias Shema has arrived, as long as sufficient time remains to recite it later, he may continue studying and recite it afterward. This is difficult, for below, siman 89, seif 6, he writes that it is prohibited to study once the time for Krias Shema and prayer has arrived. One may answer that here he discusses one who already began, and we say that he need not interrupt. He likewise writes at the end of siman 106 that if sufficient time remains, he does not interrupt. This is strained and requires study. It is also difficult, for the Mishnah in Shabbos 11a states that one interrupts for Krias Shema, and this applies even where sufficient time remains, as the Rosh and Ran write in the first chapter of Shabbos and in the chapter Lulav HaGazul, and as the Beit Yosef, Shulchan Aruch, and Levush rule in siman 652. One may nevertheless answer that the rulings here and at the end of siman 106 discuss one who began before the prohibited time, and therefore he does not interrupt, as below at the end of siman 652. This too is strained. It is also difficult that the language of Rambam in chapter 2 of the laws of Krias Shema, and of Rabbeinu Yerucham on page 24, who write that if one was engaged in Torah study and the time for Krias Shema arrived, he interrupts, implies that he interrupts even if he began before the prohibited time. The Beit Yosef similarly infers this at the end of this siman. Moreover, below at the end of siman 652 I challenged the ruling concerning one who began permissibly. See there, and see what the Soles Belulah writes on this matter. In my humble opinion, the Levush understood the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 106, who cites the Baal HaMaor as brought by the Ran, as ruling that one does not interrupt when sufficient time remains. With due respect, however, he did not reach the depth of the matter, for the Baal HaMaor must refer only to prayer, which is D'Rabbanan. For Krias Shema, which is D'Oraisa, one interrupts even when sufficient time remains, as stated above. Proof of this is that otherwise the Ran's own words in the first chapter of Shabbos would contradict one another, and they would also contradict his words in the chapter Lulav HaGazul. This is even clearer to one who studies the Ran himself in the first chapter of Shabbos, for the Baal HaMaor refers specifically to prayer. The Beit Yosef cites his words only briefly.
