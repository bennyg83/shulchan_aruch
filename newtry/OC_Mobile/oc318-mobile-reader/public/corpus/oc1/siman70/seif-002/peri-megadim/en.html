Regarding finish—in sign 17 he expanded. Know that the author in seif 4 was engaged in communal needs and the time arrived, etc.; and in seif 5 he was engaged and did not write and if he can stop—one could say this refers back to the previous [seif], and if he began in permissibility specifically he does not stop for these five; and from this, in prohibition—even in communal needs he stops, for he did not begin according to law (thus in the Magen Avraham and Beit Yosef here); and the Magen Avraham in sign 6 rejected this. And the Taz challenged the second answer—for certainly the Rambam, may his memory be blessed, holds regarding those five that even in prohibition he does not stop as long as there is time remaining; for in his explicit explanation of the mishnah he explained that they stop for Kriat Shema and not for prayer—a matter by itself; and words of Torah—but at the beginning, even for Kriat Shema they do not stop (and not like Rashi, who explained and they stop with a vav; and words of Torah are different, for this is fixed and that is fixed—better to read Kriat Shema and words of Torah). And therefore the mishnah, when he began in prohibition—from when he challenges lulav, where there it is certainly in prohibition, there is no mishnah of Shabbat when he began in prohibition; and nevertheless the Rambam explained in the mishnah that judgment and the like—they do not stop—contrary to the words of the Beit Yosef in the second answer. And the Ran, who wrote that if he began in permissibility he does not stop, and the mishnah of Shabbat in prohibition—and they stop for Kriat Shema (with a vav, and it refers to the beginning as well; and in our innovations we expanded on mishnah Shabbat 9:2 and 11:1)—also what the Beit Yosef wrote, and so it appears from what the Rambam wrote; and if he stops, it is praiseworthy according to one who holds from a rabbinic decree in Sukkah—for there it is with lulav when he began in prohibition; and also in the conclusion it deals with when there is no time remaining there, as the Taz wrote afterward. Therefore it seems that indeed there is an answer to distinguish between whether there is time remaining or not; and certainly the answer of Torah versus rabbinic is rejected, and there is no distinction—even in Torah matters the Rambam holds that even in prohibition he does not stop; and therefore one who stops is praiseworthy, since there is room to distinguish between Torah and rabbinic.

Nevertheless, what the author wrote in seif 4 regarding communal needs and the time arrived—it implies here in prohibition he stops; but in Kesef Mishnah he wrote that he learned this from intercalating the month; and in the Beit Yosef he wrote from the Yerushalmi that communal needs are like one engaged in Torah (of many people); and in Torah study of many it implies in chapter 89, and all the more so—even in prohibition he does not stop. And in the Gemara Shabbat 11a, which challenges: and it was taught, just as they do not stop for prayer, so they do not stop for Kriat Shema—for it was taught regarding intercalating the year; and what is the challenge? Perhaps in permissibility; but rather, just as for prayer we mean in prohibition, and on this it answers: regarding intercalating the year, in prohibition they do not stop—and why did the author write and the time arrived? Also in Kesef Mishnah, chapter 2 of Kriat Shema, and if he can stop refers back [to the previous case]; and if so, all the more so communal needs—and it is difficult as we said.

See Netsiv, who wrote to distinguish between Kriat Shema, whose time is fixed, and lulav, which is all day; and in our innovations we wrote similarly. And what he wrote—that the Rambam learned from when they established that they stop for Kriat Shema in Torah matters and not those of the beginning—we wrote in our innovations; and they do not stop for prayer is superfluous—see there. And Eliyah Rabbah here, and Eliyah Zuta in chapter 89, and all the more so chapter 89 and 232, will explain more on this; and here there is no need to expand. And see Levush, seifim 4 and 5, who was clever in this—he brought it in the Magen Avraham, sign 6.
