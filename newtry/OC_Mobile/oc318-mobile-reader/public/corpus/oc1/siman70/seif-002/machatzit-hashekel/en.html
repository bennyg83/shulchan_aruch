(Subnote 2) Three days, etc. And if he entered the chuppah — meaning: on Tuesday morning begin the three days that the Sages exempted him from the time of the chuppah until Motzei Shabbos; and we learned a virgin is wed on Wednesday, and ordinarily the chuppah is Tuesday night — therefore until Keriat Shema it is three days and three nights; hence if he entered the chuppah on Tuesday before he read Keriat Shema of Shacharit, he is exempt also from Keriat Shema of Shacharit of Tuesday.

For it appears as yuhara, for everyone cannot have intent in the time of the wedding, and it would appear that he can have intent.

For all agree the halacha follows Rabban Gamliel, as Rabbeinu Bachya ruled in the name of Rabbi Yochanan in Gittin 75a and in more places in Shas; and the dispute of Rosh, Rif, and Rambam depends on the two resolutions of the Gemara — and that is in Berachos 17b, where the Gemara concludes on what we learned there: a groom, if he wishes to read Keriat Shema, reads. Rabban Gamliel says: not everyone who wishes to take the Name may take. And it challenges: should one say Rabban Gamliel is concerned for yuhara but the Rabbis are not concerned? But behold we heard them the opposite, for we learned: a place where they are accustomed to do melacha according to local custom on erev Pesach they do; a place where they are accustomed not to do, they do not do; and everywhere Torah scholars are idle. Rabban Gamliel says: a person should always make himself like a Torah scholar — Rabbi Yossi says the practice is inconsistent (and that is this [matter of] Keriat Shema: Rabban Gamliel holds one may read, and the Sages say not everyone who wishes to take, etc.). And Rav Sheishes son of Rav Idi said: there is never a contradiction between rabbinic rulings; Keriat Shema here according to Rabban Gamliel and Keriat Shema there according to Rabban Gamliel is not difficult — the matter of Keriat Shema depends on intent. And we are witnesses that he cannot have intent; therefore if he reads it is yuhara. But Bava Batra that sees melacha — it is not so, and how many idlers are there? Therefore Rosh ruled like Rav Sheishes son of Rav Idi, who said: never substitute; and he answered Rabban Gamliel “not everyone who wishes,” etc.; therefore Rosh ruled like him that he is not permitted to read. But Rif and Rambam ruled like Rabbi Yossi that the practice is inconsistent. And Rabban Gamliel holds one may read and is not liable for yuhara; therefore they too ruled one may read like Rabban Gamliel.

There is a question on Choshen Mishpat end Siman 163, for we learned in the first chapter of Bava Batra: we compel the city’s people each other to build for the city a wall, doors, and a bar — Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: specifically a city adjacent to the border, since it stands on the border of the country, it needs extra guarding from enemies. And it is explained at length there, end Siman 163, that all agree we compel even if it is not adjacent to the border, and that is like the first Tanna.

And Tosafos Yom Tov, etc. — for he asked from the text itself: it is impossible to say the Rambam ruled like the inverted practice. For Rabban Gamliel is not concerned for yuhara — if so, all the more so the Rambam should rule like Rabban Gamliel regarding chapter 4 of Pesachim mishnah 5, that each person should make himself like a Torah scholar; but behold the Rambam in chapter 5 of Taanis only brought that everywhere Torah scholars are idle, and did not copy that each should make himself like a Torah scholar. Or the Rambam did not have this [text] at all; and we do not rule like Rabban Gamliel except where his reason is plausible, as they said in Kesubos 49b on this principle — they are Amoraim according to Rabbi Yossi, and likewise according to him Rif end of Get Pashut — and therefore also Rif and Rambam could hold they rule like Rav Sheishes son of Rav Idi, and even so they did not rule like Rabban Gamliel because his reason was not plausible to them in this. And likewise Shach in Choshen Mishpat Siman 171 subnote 9.

And in Beit Yosef Choshen Mishpat Siman 231: he brought that the first Tanna holds the wholesaler wipes his measures once every thirty days because he sells and measures frequently and much sticks to his measures, and thereby his measure shrinks and wiping is needed; but a homeowner who measures only occasionally and does not have much stick — therefore it suffices that he wipe once every twelve months. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says the matters are reversed: a wholesaler, since he measures frequently, what sticks does not dry on him, therefore wiping once every twelve months suffices; but a homeowner who measures only occasionally, thereby what stuck dries from measuring, therefore he needs wiping once every thirty days; and Rambam ruled like the first Tanna. And Rav Bachya challenged: behold we rule like wherever Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel taught in our Mishnah, etc. And Rav Bachya himself answered that we do not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel except where his reason is plausible.
