(Seif katan 2) Three days, etc. This applies if he entered the chuppah. Meaning, if he entered the chuppah on Wednesday morning, his exemption extends for four days, because the Sages exempted him from the time of the chuppah until Motzaei Shabbos. The Mishnah teaches that a virgin marries on Wednesday, and ordinarily the chuppah takes place Wednesday evening. From then until Motzaei Shabbos there are three days and three nights. Consequently, if he entered the chuppah on Wednesday before reciting the morning Krias Shema, he is exempt even from the morning Krias Shema of that Wednesday.

According to everyone, the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as Rabbah bar bar Chanah ruled in the name of Rabbi Yochanan in Gittin 75a and elsewhere in the Gemara. The dispute among the Rosh, Rif, and Rambam depends on the Gemara's two resolutions. In Berachos 17b, the Gemara discusses the Mishnah: "A groom who wishes to recite Krias Shema may do so. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says: Not everyone who wishes to assume the reputation may do so." The Gemara asks whether Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is concerned about arrogance while the Sages are not, although elsewhere they apparently hold the reverse. The Mishnah states that in a place where people customarily perform labor on Tisha B'Av they may do so, and where they customarily refrain they may not; everywhere, Torah scholars remain idle. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says that every person may always conduct himself as a Torah scholar. Rabbi Yochanan answers that the positions must be reversed. Thus, regarding Krias Shema, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits him to recite it, while the Sages say that not everyone who wishes to assume the reputation may do so. Rav Sheisha son of Rav Idi answers that the positions need not be reversed. The Sages' positions are consistent, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's positions are also consistent. Krias Shema depends on concentration, and we know that the groom cannot concentrate; if he recites it, this is arrogance. Regarding Tisha B'Av, however, an observer can say that he simply has no work to do, for there are many idle people. The Rosh therefore ruled according to Rav Sheisha son of Rav Idi, who said not to reverse the positions, so Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that not everyone may assume the reputation. The Rosh consequently ruled that the groom may not recite Krias Shema. The Rif and Rambam, however, ruled according to Rabbi Yochanan, who reversed the positions. According to that resolution, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits the groom to recite Krias Shema and is not concerned about arrogance, and they therefore likewise ruled that he may recite it.

In Choshen Mishpat, at the beginning of siman 163, the Mishnah in the first chapter of Bava Basra teaches that the residents of a city may compel one another to build a wall, gates, and a crossbar for the city. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says this applies specifically to a city near the frontier, because it stands at the country's border and requires additional protection from enemies. Yet the halachah is ruled there, at the beginning of siman 163, that the residents may compel one another in every city, even one that is not near the frontier, in accordance with the first Tanna.

Were that so, the Rambam should certainly have ruled like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel regarding Tisha B'Av, that every person may conduct himself as a Torah scholar. Yet in the Laws of Fasts the Rambam records only that Torah scholars everywhere refrain from work, and does not state that every person may conduct himself as a Torah scholar. Rather, the Rambam does not accept the general rule that the halachah always follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. We follow him only where his reasoning is persuasive, as the Gemara in Kesubos 77 says regarding this rule that it is a dispute among Amoraim concerning Rabbi Yochanan's view. The Rif likewise writes this explicitly at the end of the chapter Get Pashut. It is therefore possible that the Rif and Rambam agree with Rav Sheisha son of Rav Idi and nevertheless did not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel here because his reasoning did not appear persuasive to them. The Shach writes similarly in Choshen Mishpat, siman 171, seif katan 9.
And in Beit Yosef, Choshen Mishpat, siman 231. The first Tanna holds that a wholesale merchant must clean his measuring vessels once every thirty days. Since he sells and measures frequently, much residue adheres to the vessels, reducing their capacity, so they require cleaning. A private householder, who measures only occasionally and therefore accumulates less residue, need clean them only once every twelve months. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel reverses the rule. Since the wholesale merchant measures frequently, the residue does not dry, so cleaning once every twelve months suffices. A householder measures only occasionally, causing the residue from each measurement to dry, and therefore he must clean the vessels once every thirty days. The Rambam ruled like the first Tanna. The Beit Yosef asks that we ordinarily rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel whenever he appears in our Mishnah. The Beit Yosef himself answers that we follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel only where his reasoning is persuasive.
