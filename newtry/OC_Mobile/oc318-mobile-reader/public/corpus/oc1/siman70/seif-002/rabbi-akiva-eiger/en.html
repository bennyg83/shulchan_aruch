Magen Avraham, comment 6—lulav is different, for its time is all day. It is not clear to me, for it implies from the language of the Rambam here: he was engaged, etc., and the time of Kriat Shema arrived—he finishes. It implies that if he began afterward, when day broke, he stops; and as implied from the language of the Magen Avraham, who wrote that it is not common to begin near dawn—they did not decree. It implies that only near [dawn] it is not common, and they did not forbid eating near [dawn]; but if he began afterward, he stops. Behold, even though the law applies that if he began, he stops when he began by day—nevertheless near [dawn] they did not decree. If so, what of it that lulav, whose time is all day, eating and drinking are common? And what is the difference between Kriat Shema, to which three hours of eating and drinking apply, and lulav, to which all day eating and drinking apply? Therefore it seemed to me that for the Rambam, with lulav as well, one does not [take it] half an hour before day—for since it is not common to eat then. And that which is in the gloss in siman 491—that is because they were concerned for the view of the Rosh, that also for Kriat Shema, if he began near day, he stops.
