Magen Avraham, seif katan 6. Lulav differs because its time extends throughout the day. I do not understand this. Rambam's language here, if he was occupied, etc., and the time for Krias Shema arrived, he completes what he is doing, implies that if he began afterward, once daylight had arrived, he must interrupt. This is also implied by Magen Avraham's language that beginning close to dawn is uncommon and therefore the Sages did not decree against it. This indicates that it is only close to dawn that beginning is uncommon and eating was not prohibited, but if he began afterward he must interrupt. Thus, although the rule that one who began must interrupt applies when he began during the day, the Sages nevertheless did not decree during the period immediately before dawn. What difference, then, does it make that eating and drinking are common throughout the entire day with regard to lulav? How does Krias Shema, for which eating and drinking are relevant during its three-hour period, differ from lulav, for which eating and drinking are relevant throughout the day? It therefore appears to me that according to Rambam, even with regard to lulav the Sages did not enact a decree during the half hour before daybreak, because eating at that time is uncommon. The gloss in siman 651 follows the position of the Ravyah, who holds that even regarding Krias Shema, if one began close to daybreak he must interrupt.
