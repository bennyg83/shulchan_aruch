There: This rule applies to the order of the verses, and all the more so to the order of the words. Pri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav at the beginning of the siman; Petach HaDevir, letter 1.
There: If one placed one paragraph before another, then although he was not permitted to do so, he fulfilled his obligation. Pri Chadash, letter 1; Pri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav at the beginning of the siman; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, se'if 1; Siddur Beit Yaakov, letter 1. Above, siman 61, letter 16, the Kavanot are cited for the esoteric reason that the paragraphs must be recited in precisely this order. Ruach Chayim, letter 1, writes that one must recite them again.
The reason is that these paragraphs are not adjacent to one another in the Torah, yet initially they were arranged according to their order in the Torah. The paragraph of Shema comes first because its principal subject is the acceptance of the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, and it appears in the portion of Vaetchanan, before Vehayah Im Shamoa, which appears in the portion of Ekev. Vehayah Im Shamoa therefore follows it, since both are in the same book of the Torah. We then return to the beginning of the Torah for the paragraph of Vayomer, because the verse concerning the Exodus must be recited every day: “So that you shall remember the day of your departure from the land of Egypt all the days of your life.” Levush.
