(1) Return, etc. Nachalat Tzvi wrote this — his language: Beit Chadash and Levush wrote that certainly on the first verse he knows that with intention he read them; for if he does not know whether he read it with intention, it is obvious he returns to the beginning of Keriat Shema — until here. And his words are astonishing, for from Levush it is clear that even if he does not know whether he read with intention he returns to v'ahavta, for we establish the first verse on its presumption; and in fact Beit Chadash ruled that even in doubt he returns to the beginning of Keriat Shema — not like Levush; and so Lechem Chamudot on folio 14 expanded on this and wrote: whence do we invent from our hearts to establish a presumption? — see there. And in my humble opinion Levush is reasonable, for it is similar to one who returns the wind below at the end of siman 114: if in doubt we say establish him on the presumption in what he was accustomed — certainly they said so; see there. Also we have established that we lash for presumptions and burn for presumptions. Again the book Taz was printed and I saw that he ruled thus too, for according to one explanation it is forbidden to say the section and repeat it — and his words are astonishing, for it is possible to read in a whisper or to wait a little as written in siman 61 note 13, and as he himself wrote there and at the end of siman 63; and it is strained to say that in doubt whether he had intention we do not rely on this.
