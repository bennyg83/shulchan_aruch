(Note 1) Returns to the head of the section, etc. — returns to v'ahavta. And if 'I do not know,' etc. — it is obvious he returns to the head — meaning he returns to the head of the section that Shulchan Arukh wrote — that is, to v'ahavta; and 'read' v'ahavta — the head of the section. And it deals when he knows he read Shema with intention; for it is not dealing when he is in doubt whether he read Shema — for then the head of the section would mean simply that he returns to Shema; if so there is a difficulty: since he is in doubt whether he said Shema, then even if he said Shema, he presumably did not have intention in it; for if he had intention in it, it would not be possible to fall into doubt whether he read it. And since he did not have intention, why specify that he is in doubt whether he said Shema? Even if he said Shema, since he did not have intention he must return and say Shema. And if so he must read the whole section so that it not be reading out of order — and this is proof for the words of Magen Avraham (in siman 63 note 6). Or it deals when he knows he read Shema with intention and is in doubt from v'ahavta onward, and he returns to the head of the section — that is, to v'ahavta.

And see Levush, who wrote the opposite — that from the unstated case we say he read with intention. And HaLev Chadash wrote his proof is from Rambam; and HaLev Chadash rejected his proof and his reasoning and agreed with the words of Beit Chadash — see there.
