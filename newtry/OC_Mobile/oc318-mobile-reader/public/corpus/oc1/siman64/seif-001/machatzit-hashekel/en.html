Seif katan 1: He returns to the beginning of the paragraph. He returns to VeAhavta. If he does not know, it is obvious that he returns to the beginning. Meaning, he returns to the beginning of the paragraph mentioned by the Shulchan Aruch, namely VeAhavta, which is called the beginning of the paragraph. The case is one where he knows that he read Shema with concentration. One cannot explain that he is uncertain whether he read Shema, and that the beginning of the paragraph means literally that he returns to Shema. For if so, there is a difficulty: since he is uncertain whether he said Shema, then even if he did say Shema he necessarily did not concentrate, because had he concentrated, it would be impossible for him to be uncertain whether he read it. Since he did not concentrate, why specify that he is uncertain whether he said Shema? Even if he certainly said it, since he did not concentrate he must repeat Shema. He must then read the entire paragraph so that he not read out of sequence. This supports the Magen Avraham in siman 63, seif katan 6. Rather, the case must be that he knows he read Shema with concentration, but is uncertain from VeAhavta onward; he returns to the beginning of the paragraph, meaning VeAhavta.
See the Levush. He writes the opposite, that ordinarily we presume he read with concentration. The Lechem Chamudos writes that the Levush's proof is from the Rambam. The Lechem Chamudos rejects both his proof and his reasoning, and agrees with the Bach; see there.
