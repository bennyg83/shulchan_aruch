When does this apply? To the order of the verses. If he read the verse Vehayu HaDevarim before VeAhavta, he should go back and read only the verse Vehayu HaDevarim, but not VeAhavta. Although when he originally read it, he read it after Vehayu HaDevarim, now it is in its proper order. A proof is from Yoma 62, in the Mishnah concerning one who brought the blood of the goat before the blood of the bull; see there. Accordingly, if we say that only the first verse is D'Oraisa, the exclusion derived from Vehayu could apply only where he recited the words of Shema itself out of sequence, for if he reads the verse Shema at the end, he certainly fulfills his obligation. One should not force the explanation that our case is worse because at the time of reading the paragraph was recited in reverse order and he is not now correcting it. This is not so. On the contrary, it is reasonable that our case is better, since it is no worse than one who did not read the other verses at all.
Although he is not permitted to do so, he has fulfilled his obligation. See Biur HaGra in seif 2, who writes that the Tosefta seemingly proves against the Rambam. Meaning, on a Rabbinic level he has not fulfilled his obligation in any case. The Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav likewise questions whether perhaps on a Rabbinic level he has not fulfilled his obligation. Know that the Peri Megadim leaves the fundamental law requiring study: Why, regarding writing the paragraphs, do the Shulchan Aruch in Yoreh Deah siman 288 and the Rambam himself rule that they are invalid when written out of sequence, even though there too the paragraphs are not adjacent to one another, whereas here he fulfills his obligation? A similar question is raised by the Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 288 regarding the Rambam's statement that they are not adjacent in the Torah, meaning that their order is only a Rabbinic enactment. It appears to me that this can be resolved simply, and that this is the intent of the Gra. Since Vehayah Im Shamoa in Parashas Eikev is written in the Torah after Shema in Parashas Vaeschanan, if one writes Vehayah Im Shamoa first, it is considered not written in its proper order, and is therefore invalid there. In our matter, however, the order is only an enactment of Chazal. The reason is straightforward. The four paragraphs of tefillin and the two paragraphs of a mezuzah are indispensable to one another, as stated in the Mishnah in Menachos 27. Therefore, if one omits one verse or one paragraph, it is as though he wrote nothing at all. Thus, when the Torah revealed that they must be in order, as stated in the Mechilta, presumably because it says, They shall be as frontlets between your eyes, the reference is to all four paragraphs together, which are regarded as one paragraph divided only into verses. In our matter, by contrast, if one read only the first paragraph, he certainly at least fulfills the mitzvah of accepting the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, even if he did not read the second paragraph concerning acceptance of the yoke of mitzvos. Even if both are D'Oraisa, they do not invalidate one another, since each contains its own separate command, and each paragraph teaches a distinct subject. We therefore say that the word Vehayu refers only to the verses within each paragraph. This applies even if both paragraphs are D'Oraisa, and certainly if only one paragraph is D'Oraisa, Vehayu refers only to the verses within that paragraph. Nevertheless, on a Rabbinic level he certainly has not fulfilled his obligation if he altered the order of the verses in the second or third paragraph.
