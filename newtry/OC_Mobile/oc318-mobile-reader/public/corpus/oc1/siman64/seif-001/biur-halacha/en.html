In the order of the verses — if he read a verse and 'these words' [v'hayu] came before v'ahavta, he returns and reads only the verse 'these words' and not v'ahavta, even though at the time of reading he read it after 'these words'; nevertheless now they are in order. And proof from Yoma 62 in the Mishnah: he preceded the blood of the goat to the blood of the bull, etc. — see there. And accordingly, if we say that only one verse is from the Torah, the limitation of v'hayu would not apply except if he changed the words of Shema out of order; for if he reads the verse Shema at the end, he certainly fulfills, and it is strained to say that here it is worse since at the time of reading the section was read in reverse and now it is not fixed — for on the contrary it is reasonable that here it is better, since it does not lack as if he did not read the rest of the verses at all.

Even though he is not permitted, he has fulfilled, etc. — see in Biur HaGra in siman 62 that he wrote it appears proven from the Tosefta not like Rambam and R' Yehudah that rabbinically he does not fulfill in any case; and so Pri Megadim in note 7 doubts this, that perhaps rabbinically he does not fulfill. And know that Pri Megadim left in doubt the main law — why in writing the sections did Shulchan Arukh rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 288, and also Rambam himself who invalidates not in order even though there too the sections are not adjacent one to another, yet here he fulfilled; and similar to what he wrote on what Rambam wrote that they are not adjacent in the Torah — meaning it is only a rabbinic enactment — Taz also challenged in Yoreh De'ah siman 288. And it seems to me simply to resolve that this is the intent of Biur HaGra, and the intent is: certainly since in the Torah it is written 'and it shall be if you listen' in parashat Eikev after Shema in parashat Va'etchanan, if one writes v'hayah before [Shema] it is called not as they were instituted; therefore there it is invalid, unlike in our matter which is only an enactment of Chazal. And the reason seems to me simple: there, since the four sections in tefillin and the two sections in mezuzah impede one another, as stated in Menachot 27 in the Mishnah — if so, if one lacks one verse or one section, it is as if he did not write at all; therefore we say that what the Torah revealed to us is that we require them in order, as stated in the Mechilta; and presumably the reason is also because of 'and they shall be totafot between your eyes' — the intent is on all four sections together, which together are considered only one section divided only into verses; unlike in our matter, if he read only the first section he certainly has at least the mitzvah of accepting the yoke [of Heaven], even if he did not read the second of accepting the yoke of mitzvot — even if we say both are from the Torah they do not impede one another, for in each is written a command in itself and each section teaches a unique matter in itself; therefore we say the intent of the verse v'hayu is only on the verses in each section; and all this even if both are from the Torah, and all the more if we say one section is from the Torah — certainly v'hayu applies only to the verses in that section. And nevertheless rabbinically he certainly does not fulfill even if he changed the verses in the second section.
