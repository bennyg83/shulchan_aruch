Taz seif katan 1 According to one explanation. Certainly, if he knows that he read the first verse with concentration, even without this reason he need begin only from VeAhavta, as written in the Shulchan Aruch regarding one who omitted a verse in the middle: he returns to the beginning of that verse, not to the beginning of the paragraph. Rather, the Taz concerns one who is uncertain whether he read the first verse with concentration. The Taz does not agree with the Magen Avraham in seif katan 1, but with the Levush, who writes that we presume he read the first verse with concentration, since everyone knows this law, but has now forgotten; see there. Ordinarily, however, we do not rely on a presumption where the matter can easily be clarified. Here he should therefore have begun with Shema. The Taz consequently gives this reason, that according to one opinion repeating it involves a prohibition, and for that reason we rely on the presumption. This is clear.
