{Rama: Taz note 1 — for on the one hand.} And certainly if he knows he read the first verse with intention, without this reason he need not begin only v'ahavta, as Shulchan Arukh wrote: if he skipped a verse in the middle he returns to the head of that verse and not to the head of that section. Rather, Taz deals when he is in doubt whether he read the first verse with intention, and Taz does not hold like Magen Avraham note 1, but like Levush, that there is a presumption he read the first verse with intention, for every person knows this law — only that now he forgot; see there. Only we do not establish a presumption in a place where it can be clarified easily, unlike here where it was fitting to begin Shema; therefore Taz wrote this reason — because according to one explanation there is a prohibition, therefore we rely on the presumption — and let it be clear.
