He is at Uchsavtam. The reason it does not say at Ukshartam is that there is a distinction: the first Ukshartam has a kamatz and is singular, while the second has a segol and is plural. The Taz gives another reason: one does not ordinarily become confused in the middle, because he can recognize from the amount of time since he began reading. At the end, however, a somewhat longer period has elapsed, and he may therefore sometimes become confused, thinking that he has finished the second paragraph.
If he began. Meaning, he afterward became uncertain whether he said Lemaan Yirbu after Uchsavtam in the first paragraph. Even so, he need not return, since presumably he followed his usual practice of saying Lemaan Yirbu after Uchsavtam in the second paragraph. All the more so, if he finds himself at another word in the middle of the paragraph, we presume that he said everything preceding it and did not omit a verse or word. Later authorities.
He need not return. All this applies when he is reading alone. But if he is reading with the congregation and sees that the chazzan is at Uchsavtam in the first paragraph, or near it, he should return to the first Uchsavtam, since it is evident that he erred and did not continue according to his habitual sequence. Conversely, if he is at Uchsavtam, where the Shulchan Aruch rules that he returns to the first Uchsavtam, but he sees that the chazzan and congregation are at Uchsavtam in the second paragraph, or near it, either shortly before or after, he should also afterward say the verse Lemaan Yirbu. Presumably he too read the second paragraph with them, and there is a strong indication of this. The law regarding one who altered the text of a blessing from the formula established by the Sages, which the Magen Avraham cites in the name of the Rambam, will be explained, with Hashem's help, in the Mishnah Berurah in siman 68; see there.
