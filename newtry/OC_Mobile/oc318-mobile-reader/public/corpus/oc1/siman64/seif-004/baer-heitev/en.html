In uchtavtem. And that which [the Mishnah] did not say in ukshartem — because there is a difference: in ukshartem the first [occurrence] is with kamatz, which is singular, and the second is with segol, which is plural — see Tosafot Yom Tov. And Taz wrote the reason that in uchtavtem it is the end of the section, so one can err — see there.

Your days. And all the more so in the middle of the section there is a presumption that he said everything above it — in Beit Yosef, Rashba. Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema: the principle of the matter — whoever changes from the formula the Sages established in blessings, he has erred and returns and blesses in the formula. And Kessef Mishneh wrote in chapter 1 of Berakhot: specifically if he opened a blessing in a place they did not enact to open, or concluded in a place they said not to conclude; but if he changed the wording of the blessing and did not say it literally but said another formula on the subject of the blessing, he has fulfilled — see there; and see siman 59.
