At Uchsavtam. The reason it does not say at Ukshartam is that there is a distinction: the first Ukshartam has a kamatz and is singular, while the second has a segol and is plural. See Tosafos Yom Tov. The Taz writes that the reason Uchsavtam is used is because it is at the end of the paragraph, where one may become confused; see there.
Your days. All the more so, if he finds himself in the middle of the paragraph, we presume that he recited everything preceding that point. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba. The Rambam writes in chapter 1 of Hilchos Krias Shema: The general rule is that anyone who departs from the formula established by the Sages for blessings has erred and must repeat the blessing according to the prescribed formula. The Kesef Mishneh writes in chapter 1 of Hilchos Berachos that this applies specifically if he began with a blessing where the Sages did not institute an opening blessing, or concluded where they said not to conclude with a blessing. But if he altered the wording of the blessing and did not recite the exact text, but instead used another formulation concerning the subject of the blessing, he has fulfilled his obligation. See there, and see siman 59.
