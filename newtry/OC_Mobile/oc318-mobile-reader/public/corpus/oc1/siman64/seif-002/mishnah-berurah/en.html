And he erred in it. The Peri Chadash writes that this applies specifically where he erred, but if he acted deliberately and intentionally, he returns to the beginning, as with prayer below in siman 114, seif 7. The Magen Avraham below in siman 104, seif katan 6, is uncertain about this; see there. This matter will be explained, with Hashem's help, in the Mishnah Berurah in siman 65; see there in seif katan 1 and in Biur Halachah.
One verse. If he omitted one word, some say it is sufficient to return to that word and continue, where it begins a separate clause, such as if he omitted the word Vedibarta from the verse Veshinantam. The Eliyah Rabbah, however, rules based on several Rishonim that he must always return to the beginning of that verse.
And completes the paragraph. For if he says only that verse, he will be reading out of sequence. Even if he did not remember that he omitted a verse from the first paragraph until after he had also recited the following paragraph, it is nevertheless sufficient for him to recite from the omission until the end of the first paragraph, since the order of the paragraphs is not indispensable.
To the beginning of the paragraph. Because of the uncertainty, even if this occurred in the second paragraph, and even according to the opinion that the second paragraph is Rabbinic, since he is engaged in Krias Shema he must correct the entire reading. If it occurred in the first paragraph, the law depends on whether he remembers having said Shema with concentration, together with Baruch Shem Kevod Malchuso LeOlam Vaed. If he does, he returns to VeAhavta. If he does not remember having said Shema with concentration, he returns to the beginning in any event, because reading it without concentration is equivalent to not having read the verse Shema at all. Consequently, he must read this entire paragraph so that the reading not be out of sequence. He should read it quietly, or wait briefly, so that he not appear to be saying Shema, Shema.
