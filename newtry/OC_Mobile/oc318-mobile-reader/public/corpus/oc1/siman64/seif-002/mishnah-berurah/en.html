(ד) And he erred in it — Peri Chadash wrote: specifically when he erred; but if deliberately and with intent, he returns to the beginning, as regarding prayer below in siman 114 seif 7. And Magen Avraham below in siman 104 note 6 is in doubt about this — see there; and in siman 65 this matter will be explained in Mishnah Berurah with God's help — see there in note 1 and in Biur Halachah.

(ה) One verse — and if he skipped one word, some say that in this case it suffices that he return to that word and complete, if he is at a place where a matter begins independently, for example he skipped from the verse v'shinantam the word u'dibarta; but in Sefer Eliyahu Rabbah he ruled according to many early authorities that he must always return to the beginning of that verse.

(ו) And he completes the section — for if he says only that verse, it would be reading out of order. And even if it was not remembered that he skipped the verse from the first section until after he also said the section after it — nevertheless it suffices in what he will say from the skip until the end of the first section, for the order of the sections is not indispensable.

(7) To the head of the section — because of doubt, even if this was in the second section, and even per the view that the second section is rabbinic, since he is engaged in Keriat Shema he must fix everything. And if this was in the first section, it depends: if he remembers that he said Shema with intention, he also returns to v'ahavta as ruled in siman 63; and if he does not remember that he said Shema with intention, in any case he returns to the beginning, for since he did not read it with intention it is as if he did not read the verse Shema at all, and consequently he must read this whole section so that the reading not be out of order; and he should read it in a whisper or wait a little so that he not appear as one reading Shema twice.
