[א] Return, etc. Nachalat Tzvi — this is his language: Bach and Levush wrote that certainly the first verse — he knows he read them intentionally; if he does not know whether he read it intentionally, obviously he returns to the beginning of Keriat Shema — until here. And his words are astonishing, for from Levush it is clear that even if he does not know whether he read intentionally he returns to "and you shall love," for we establish the first verse on its presumption; and in truth Bach ruled that in doubt he also returns to the beginning of Keriat Shema — not like Levush; and so Lechem Chamudot page 14 elaborated on this and wrote: from where do we invent from our hearts to establish a presumption? — see there. And in my humble opinion Levush is reasonable: it is similar to "who returns the wind" below end siman 114 — if in doubt we say establish it on the presumption in what he was accustomed; certainly they said — see there; also we have the principle "they lash for presumptions and burn for presumptions." Again was published Sefer Taz and I saw he also ruled thus from the reason that for one explanation it is forbidden when one says the section and repeats it; and his words are astonishing, for it is possible to read in a whisper or wait a bit as he wrote in siman 61 comment 13 and as he himself wrote there and at end siman 63; and it is strained to say in doubt if he intended we do not rely on this.
[ב] Return to the head, etc. Meaning it does not suffice like one who reads what he skipped — for if so it is like reading out of order.
[ג] That verse, etc. Or a word. Malbush Yom Tov — this is his language: the wording of Rosh and of Tur is not so; and it seems he always returns to the beginning of the verse. And in Kesef Mishneh I found it seems somewhat like the words of Levush — until here his language. However from Tosafot [Berakhot] 16a and Agudah and Semak and Avudraham it seems that even if he skipped a word he begins from the beginning of the verse.
[ד] [Levush] He erred and "you wrote them," etc. For if "you bound them" — David did not mention that he erred, for they differ from one another in their cantillation and vocalization; and Taz wrote there is no way to err in the middle that he could feel according to the time he began.
