Even if it was mixed before Pesach. For it is like kitniyos species; even though it was clarified above in siman 453, s.k. 6, that kitniyos species do not forbid the mixture, and even where they are the majority they are nullified in an ordinary case: here we are dealing with a case where there is no real mixture at all; and as the Terumat HaDeshen wrote, brought in the Magen Avraham, that they make the mustard with wine and crush it and grind it, and it is not recognizable like kitniyos species, but the mustard itself is the main thing and is recognizable and visible; therefore it is not called a mixture at all. And regarding what the Maharil wrote, that wine into which mustard was placed when it was still grape must, preventing its fermentation so that it remain in its sweetness, that one may not drink it on Pesach: one must say that there too it is not called a mixture, since it is done for its taste, so that it remain in its sweetness. And one may further say that only if it fell into the cooked dish did the Rav write above that its mixture does not forbid; which is not so here, where he mixes [it] ab initio in order to prevent its fermentation, and it is possible to leave it until after Pesach: we do not permit drinking it during Pesach.
