(1) Other kinds of dipping sauces. These are sauces into which meat is dipped, containing vinegar or another sharp substance, and flour is commonly added to them. The flour can also become chametz through the sharp liquid.
(2) That contain water. If they contain no water at all, they are fruit juice and do not cause leavening even if the liquids themselves are sour, as explained in siman 462. According to the Rama there, se'if 4, who records our custom to be stringent even with fruit juice alone, dipping sauces made from wine vinegar mixed with flour are likewise forbidden even when they contain no water.
(3) It should be eaten immediately. The sharpness of the mustard prevents it from becoming chametz quickly.
(4) And it must be destroyed. Burning is not literal, as explained in siman 445.
(5) And the custom, etc. The reason is that mustard is gathered into a pile like grain and is treated like kitniyos, which custom forbids, as stated by the Levush.
(6) Even if it was mixed, etc. This does not mean that mustard was mixed with flour, for that is obviously forbidden whether the mixture occurred before Pesach or during Pesach. Rather, it means that it was mixed with wine. The novelty is that even though the mustard is crushed when mixed and no longer appears like kitniyos when Pesach arrives, it remains forbidden. Nevertheless, b'dieved, mustard that fell into a cooked dish does not forbid it. It is also permitted to retain mustard until after Pesach when it contains no flour. Wine into which mustard was placed while fermenting, so that it would remain sweet and not spoil, may be retained, but it may not be drunk.
