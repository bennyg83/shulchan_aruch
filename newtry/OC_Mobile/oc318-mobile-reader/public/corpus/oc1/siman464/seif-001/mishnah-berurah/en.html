(1) Other types of condiments—with which one dips meat, and they contain vinegar and other things that have acidity, and people are accustomed to put flour in them, for flour also becomes chametzed through acidity.

(2) That contain water—for if they have no water at all they are fruit juice and do not cause chametzing, even though they are sour things in themselves, as written above in siman 462. And according to what Rama wrote above in siman 462 seif 4, that we are stringent with fruit juice alone as well—even if they have no water at all—only types of condiments made from wine vinegar with flour are also forbidden.

(3) He must eat immediately—for the strength of mustard does not let it stand long before chametzing.

(4) And must destroy it—not specifically burning, as explained above in siman 445.

(5) And the custom, etc.—and the reason is because mustard is a thing that is preserved and is like legumes which people forbid [Levush].

(6) Even if mixed, etc.—it does not mean mixed with flour, for in that it is obvious it is forbidden whether before Passover or on Passover; rather it means mixed with wine. And the novelty that even though at the time of mixing they crush it and it does not appear like legumes, when Passover enters it is nevertheless forbidden. Nevertheless b'dieved if mustard fell into a dish it does not forbid it. And likewise it is permitted to leave mustard until after Passover since it is without flour. And wine into which mustard was placed while still fermenting so that it stands in its sweetness and does not spoil—it is permitted to leave it, but not to drink it.
