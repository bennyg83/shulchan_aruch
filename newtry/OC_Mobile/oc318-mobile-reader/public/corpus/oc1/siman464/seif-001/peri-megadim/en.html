Even—Peri Megadim Eshel Avraham: possibly his intent is that he explains what Be'er Heitev wrote: even if mixed before Passover, as Maharil wrote—wine into which mustard was placed to preserve it in its sweetness may not be drunk on Passover but may be left until after Passover; but if it fell into a dish where one cannot leave until after Passover, its mixture does not forbid—see siman 453; and Chok Yaakov here. Preserving—one can say it is as in its own form—see there. And what he wrote mustard without flour is permitted to leave—the same applies with flour without water that it is permitted to leave; and possibly for one who is concerned for Rashi in siman 462 one should not leave it, and it is good to sell to a gentile; however it appears one need not be so careful to sell, for in time of need it is permitted even to knead with them and eat. And what he wrote in the name of Rokeach that mustard should not be ground in a chametz bowl—for it is sharp and presses out and absorbs, and Beit Yosef and Noda B'Yehuda siman 447 on this. Nevertheless our wheat flour for strawberries is chametz and must be sold even without water. And if the explanation of mixed before Passover is an actual mixture but that one can leave it after Passover—it is l'chatchila; one should not learn from less than sixty being permitted during Passover, for one can say it preserves or is recognizable; however since he did not explain plainly mixed less than sixty, it implies on Passover b'dieved even less than sixty is permitted—Chok Yaakov in 453 and here; and if so in Magen Avraham (1) it will be explained further.
