That it is not called bread. See in the Mishnah Berurah. And it was not explained how much is called "much mursan," and also in what case it deals: whether he mixed other mursan, or even with its own mursan, whenever there is much, the status of bread is nullified by that. And it is not comparable to that of se'if 1, where so long as they have not been separated they do not diminish the power of the bread, and on the contrary they even join to complete the measure; for there it deals with there having been little mursan; see in Machatzit HaShekel what he wrote on this. And I found that the Rashba wrote on this, and this is his language: that what Resh Lakish said in the Yerushalmi, "all that he did not mix mursan into it," one should not explain that whenever there is mursan in it it is not liable for challah, for behold we learned: five quarters they are, and their sovin and their mursan; rather, to say that sometimes their mursan is so mixed into it that it is not fit for shepherds; alternatively, one can explain that he mixed mursan into it after it had been removed from it, because it is not the way of people to do so, and as we learned in the mishnah: he removed their mursan, etc.; end quote. Brought in the Beis Yosef, Yoreh De'ah siman 330. And from his words it is explained that the measure of much mursan is whenever the shepherd does not eat of the bread because of them. And it appears that this law does not depend on taste-as-essence like that of siman 453 regarding rice; for that case is different, for rice too is a kind of food, and therefore if there is in it the taste of grain, and all the more so in the measure of eating a pras, one fulfills with it, and according to several posekim even with one kezayit, as we explained there; which is not so here: even if he ate a whole loaf he does not fulfill, for the sovin and the mursan ruin the bread, and through them it is not fit for eating. And it is also not comparable to that of siman 455, se'if 6, regarding spices, where giving taste suffices; for here it is different, for it is not the way of people to eat such bread, as the aforementioned Rashba wrote. And in the Peri Megadim it is implied that also in our matter the measure of eating a pras suffices; and in my humble opinion it appears as I wrote.
