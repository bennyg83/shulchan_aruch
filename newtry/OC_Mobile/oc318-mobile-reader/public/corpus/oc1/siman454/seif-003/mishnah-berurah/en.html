(11) There is none in this time, etc.: that is to say, from the side of the law, if he scalds the flour, namely that he puts it into boiling water that is boiling with a strong boil upon the fire before the kneading, and afterward he kneads it and makes dough from it, we are not concerned to say that perhaps the flour already became chametz while it was in the boiling water before he began to work it by hand in kneading, because the boiling of the boiling water hastens to cook the flour first, and once it has been cooked it never again comes to chimutz, even after the boiling water has cooled from its boil. Nevertheless the Geonim forbade us all kinds of chalita in boiling water, because we are not expert in the chalita of boiling water, and we are concerned that perhaps he will not boil the water beforehand properly and thoroughly.
(12) Every kind of chalita: that is, whether he puts water upon the flour or flour upon water, and even if the water is boiling strongly, standing upon the fire, and he stirs the flour within the water.
(13) Forbidden: both in eating and in benefit; and likewise it is forbidden to keep it, for since we are not expert we are concerned that it is absolute chametz; and if it became mixed into a cooked dish it forbids in any amount. Nevertheless the Acharonim wrote that with chalita one should not forbid the cooked dish in any amount except for eating, and not for benefit; and likewise, to keep it is permitted in such a case. The Acharonim further wrote: one who has an illness in his stomach, and his remedy is that they chalut barley or oats and place them on his stomach: if this illness occurred on Pesach and there is danger in it, it is permitted for a Jew to do it by way of chalita, namely that they boil the water well and afterward put the barley into it; for even where there is danger, wherever it is possible on a side of heter he should do it on a side of heter. And if there is no danger in it, it is forbidden even by way of chalita, for since we are not expert, when he does chalita there is concern of bal yeira'eh. And if a non-Jew does this from his own, or if the Jew transfers ownership of the barley to him before the chalita, it is permitted even where there is no danger. And if it is possible to chalut the barley in fruit juice, it is permitted even by a Jew even where there is no danger, for in fruit juice it does not become chametz; and see in the Biur Halacha. The Chayei Adam wrote: when soldiers pass through on Pesach and compel a Jew to cook kinds of groats for them, if he can appease them that he cook buckwheat for them, how fine; and in any event he should clear them of grain kernels that are commonly mixed in them. And if he cannot appease them with this, and they give him barley groats: if he can save himself with money, he is obligated to give according to his ability; and if not, then the Jew should at least be careful to do chalita, namely that he first allow the water to cook until it is well cooked, and then put in the groats little by little so that the water not cool; and also with buckwheat it is good that he do so. But oat groats are absolute chametz, as is known; end quote.
