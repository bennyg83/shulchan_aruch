That it is not. See Taz: the Mechaber ruled like the Rambam of blessed memory: even though it is fit for human food, clean fine flour, all that he made for a wild beast or for cattle, and was careful about chimutz, even so one does not fulfill with it, because he did not guard it for matzah of the mitzvah; and like that of Pesachim 38b, challah of a todah, etc.; and Rav Huna, Pesachim 40a, doughs of non-Jews, etc., provided that he eat a kezayit of matzah at the end: the same law applies here. And the Rav [Rama] ruled as the Tur wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 330: that when the shepherds do not eat from it, that is when it is not fit from [having] much sovin, that is when one does not fulfill with it; but if they are fit for eating, he fulfills. And challah of a todah is worse still, for he guarded it for the sake of a sacrifice, not for the sake of matzah of the mitzvah; and with doughs of non-Jews: ideally [not], but after the fact, even if he did not guard at all from kneading onward, so long as it did not become chametz, he fulfills with it. And that the Rav did not gloss in Tur Sof [siman] matzah that three deaf-mute/idiot/minor kneaded, one fulfills with it: perhaps he relied on here; and with Hashem's help there it will be explained further. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 330, se'if 8 and se'if 9, which are doubled. And apparently in se'if 8 for the reason of "your doughs" and not of a wild beast, as Bertinoro wrote in chapter 1 of Challah, mishnah 8; and see Beer HaGolah (9); and in the Rambam of blessed memory in his commentary on the Mishnah he wrote "when you eat of the bread of the land," and that is even though it is fit for a person, since he made it for a wild beast he is exempt. And se'if 9 deals: even though he made it also for shepherds, if they are not fit for them it is exempt. And this is not [right], for if so what is this that he concludes, "and specifically when it is recognizable that it is for dogs"? Rather both cases deal with what is fit for a person, as Beer HaGolah wrote there. And behold from "your doughs" it is difficult to exclude if he made for a non-Jew like for a wild beast, for in every place it excludes "from you" and not for a wild beast and not for a non-Jew, regarding Yom Tov; but with "when you eat of the bread of the land" it is fine. And see Beis Yosef there, who brought the Yerushalmi of Challah, a dispute of Rabbi Yehoshua and Rabbi Yochanan and Rava; see there. And if we say that Rabbi Yochanan, who said if he made them like blocks he is liable, and like molded [loaves] (like boards, and it is recognizable for a wild beast) he is exempt: that is, for this we require specifically that he change the form of the bread; but in the form of bread, even if he made it for cattle he is liable; so it is fine that for a non-Jew in the form of bread he is liable. And in Eshel Avraham he asked why she is exempt from challah with clean [flour]; and we already wrote from "your doughs" or from "when you eat of the bread of the land." And behold the Rav who glossed according to the Tur and the Rosh: specifically when it is not fit for shepherds; but if fit for shepherds he fulfills with it even though he made it for dogs, that is in the form of bread that is liable for challah for this reason; and according to the Mechaber one does not fulfill with it, because it is not guarded for matzah; and that is what the Magen Avraham letter 3 [wrote]; with Hashem's help there it will be explained; and see Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah there in Kenesses HaGedolah, that according to the Rambam of blessed memory even regarding challah he is exempt even in the form of bread: it implies that he ruled like Rava and also disagrees with Rabbi Yehoshua; see Levush, who wrote: eaten under duress is not guarded for matzah; and further that it is not human food: this requires further study, for according to the Mechaber's reason even clean fine flour one does not fulfill with it; and see Eliyah Rabbah letter 3. And nevertheless for the practical law: if he ate matzah made for a person and it is eaten only under duress, whether he fulfills with it: see Rambam, chapter 14 of Hilchos Maachalos Asuros, law 10, that not in the normal manner of eating he is exempt; and the same applies in a positive commandment; see Maggid Mishneh there, law 12, and below siman 612. And in the mishnah there: either way it renders impure through a tent: therefore [it must be] fit for a person under duress; or one can say it already received impurity; and see in Bertinoro's explanation and in the Rambam and Tosafos Yom Tov. And that they are not baked on Yom Tov likewise deals even with clean fine flour if he made them only for dogs; see Lechem Mishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov.
