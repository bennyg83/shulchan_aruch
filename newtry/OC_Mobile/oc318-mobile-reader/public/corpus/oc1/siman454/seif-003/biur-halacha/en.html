See in the Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that since we are not expert we treat it as absolute chametz; and so it is in truth explained in Hilchos Ritz Giat in the name of Rav Sherira, and this is his language on folio 91 there: even though the Sages said chalut, etc., and boiling water with a strong boil are those that bubble, etc.: we do not rely on this to act in practice, for we do not know how it will be guarded that it not come to chimutz, and perhaps it will cool from this corner or from the edge and come to chimutz; and we have not heard in the two yeshivos of anyone who permitted to do so except for a sick person and for a woman in childbirth alone, according to experts who say that he has a remedy in this and if not he comes to danger, or at a time when they say if they feed him matzah he will be endangered, etc.; and he has nothing to eat but chalut in boiling water: they feed him immediately, and he must eat immediately, and they leave over nothing of it at all; and see there that he also wrote a responsum of Rav Hai Gaon on this, and this is his language there: chalita in boiling water is certainly matzah, etc.; however, all the early Rabbis of many many generations say: this chalita, we do not know how it is, and they did not permit at all to do chalita with dough; and many many labored to permit for them barley water, that they boil boiling water and cast barley into it, whether in dough or in chalita, and the Geonim did not permit for them, for they say we do not know the permitted chalita how it is, etc. And regarding a sick person the early Geonim ruled: when the illness is hard upon him and he cannot taste anything on Pesach and he is endangered, it is permitted to do for him what he needs according to physicians, for with everything one heals except idolatry; end quote there. And so wrote Maharam Chalawah on the sugya of chalita; and see there: after bringing the aforementioned words of Rav Hai he wrote: and Rav Alfasi explained his words further and forbade for everyone like other issurim that stand on their law, that for a sick person who has danger it is permitted and not for another; end quote there on folio 39b; see there. And from these words it is proven that one must forbid chalita even after the fact and even in benefit and in keeping, since it is like other issurim; and it is also explicit that for a sick person without danger we are not lenient. And we elaborated on this to exclude the words of the Magen Avraham who is in doubt here that possibly it is only ideally as a mere matter; and had he seen the words of the aforementioned early authorities he would not have been in doubt here. And as for the proof he brought from liver, see in Eliyah Rabbah who rejected this. And further I wonder at the Radbaz, part 1, siman 241, whom they asked whether it is permitted to give a sick person barley water to drink on Pesach; and see there that he argued that possibly the Geonim's stringency was only with dough that hastens to become chametz before it is cooked by the boiling of the water, but with barley that is hard they did not decree on chalita; and he further wrote that even if they decreed also in such a case, nevertheless for a sick person, etc., we rely on our expertise, for we see that the barley is dry and strong, etc.; and even for a sick person without danger I see to permit a matter like this, etc.; see there. And in the aforementioned responsa of the Geonim it is explicit that also with barley water this enactment applies, and it is also explicit that for illness without danger they did not permit. And see in Machatzit HaShekel what he wrote on this, that necessarily it deals with where there is no danger; and his words are astonishing. And see in Shaarei Teshuvah who also leans to be lenient to place chaluted barley on the stomach even without danger, and he supported his pegs with the words of Chok Yaakov; and as it appears he held that Chok Yaakov permits keeping it after Pesach even intact; and that is not the intent of Chok Yaakov, who refers only to a mixture of any amount, as we copied in the Mishnah Berurah, and so the Shulchan Aruch HaRav copied. And with chalut intact it is certainly forbidden to keep it, as is explained in the aforementioned responsum of Rav Sherira who wrote, "and they leave over nothing of it at all." Therefore for the practical law one should not be lenient against all the aforementioned Geonim, and one should not give a sick person barley water to drink unless he is in danger; and likewise regarding chaluting barley and placing them on the stomach, as we copied in the Mishnah Berurah. Except that one can say that the Geonim themselves were stringent only regarding drinking, where there is concern of kares; but regarding placing on the stomach, where there is in this only concern of a mere negative commandment of bal yeira'eh, possibly they would not have been stringent in this and for every sick person they permitted; but it is difficult to say so, for what difference is there between a negative-commandment issur and a kares issur? And this requires further study.
