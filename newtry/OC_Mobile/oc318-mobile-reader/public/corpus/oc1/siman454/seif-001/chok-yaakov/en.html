With bread of sovin and not with bread of mursan. That is, sovin is mursin, except that sovin is the coarse husk of the produce and mursan is the fine; and so Rashi explained at the end of chapter 7 of Shabbos; and Bertinoro in chapter 2 of Challah and chapter 11 of Terumos explained the opposite. And the Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: it is somewhat implied that it can come to chametz, since according to the Rif one fulfills with them the obligation of matzah; and one can say that since it is of a species that can come to chimutz, one fulfills his obligation with it. And I have seen those who rule that a husk they call "shprayr" does not cause chimutz; however, that is a husk of straw, but the husk of the wheat itself possibly causes chimutz, and also it is impossible that some substance of the wheat did not adhere to it; end quote of the Magen Avraham. (And see chapter 11 of Terumos, mishnah 5: it is explained that sovin of new wheat has much flour mixed in them, which is not so of old ones, in which no flour at all is mixed; see there in Bertinoro's explanation.) And I wonder at all his words here: what is he in doubt about, and he hangs himself on the view of the Rif, which is a solitary [view] against the view of all the posekim, and we do not hold like him; and there are those who explain that the Rif too refers to dough mixed with the sovin and mursan that are in it; see in the Beis Yosef. Also in Rashi's explanation one can explain so, as the Nekudas HaKesef wrote. And in truth a complete mishnah we learned: one does not soak mursin for chickens, and a woman does not rub mursin, and it certainly causes chimutz, as is explained below in siman 465; and how did all this escape him? And if a stalk of straw is found within dissolved food and there is no grain at all in the stalk: is there concern that perhaps there was grain there and it fell into the dissolved cooked dish and is not recognizable? See in my books Toras HaShelamim, question 67, and below in siman 465 in the Magen Avraham, s.k. 5, that we do not establish a presumption of issur; and regarding an issur of any amount that is rabbinic, we say that all of them fell at the time of threshing; see there.
With the sovin and mursan that are in it. And if he separated the mursan from it and then mixed them back with them, the Magen Avraham wrote that he is in doubt whether it joins toward a kezayit; and see in Yoreh De'ah siman 324; end quote. It appears to me that there is no doubt here at all: since regarding challah it does not join, the same applies regarding matzah, as is explained in the Beis Yosef that this depends on that; and so is the implication of the Maggid Mishneh; and so wrote the Rashba in Piskei Challah, shaar 1, siman 1; and so is the implication of the language of the posekim who wrote "with the sovin and mursin that are in it" specifically. And the Ran wrote that the same law applies, that one fulfills with wheat from which worms ate.
