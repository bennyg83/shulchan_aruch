1) [Se'if 1] One does not fulfill the obligation with bran bread, etc. The verse states, 'For seven days you shall eat matzos with it, bread,' and these are not called bread. Only something fit for human consumption is called bread, as it is written, 'For a person does not live by bread alone.' Furthermore, regarding challah the verse states, 'When you eat of the bread of the land,' implying that only what people themselves eat is called bread. For this reason, one fulfills the obligation of matzah only with a substance that is obligated in challah. Levush.
2) There. Neither bran bread nor coarse-bran bread. Bran is the wheat husk that falls off during pounding, while coarse bran is what remains in the sieve. Rashi, Shabbos 76b, as cited by Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Rabbeinu Ovadiah's commentary to chapter 7 of Shabbos writes likewise, but notes that Rambam explains the terms in reverse: coarse bran is thicker and inferior to bran. Rambam's commentary to chapter 2 of Challah likewise explains that bran is the fine material and coarse bran is the thick material, as does his commentary to chapter 11 of Terumos. See there. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, cites these views.
3) Magen Avraham writes there: 'I have seen authorities rule that the husk called shpreirer does not become chametz. This, however, is a husk from straw. The husk of the wheat kernel itself may become chametz, and it is also impossible that no wheat substance adheres to it.' These are his words. Chok Yosef, subsection 1, cites them. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1, however, expresses surprise at Magen Avraham's uncertainty. An explicit Mishnah states that coarse bran may not be soaked for chickens and that a woman may not rub herself with coarse bran, because it certainly becomes chametz, as explained below in siman 465. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, resolves the difficulty by explaining that Magen Avraham was uncertain only about the wheat husk, which is bran, as Rashi explains at the beginning of his discussion: bran is the wheat husk that falls off during pounding. See there. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur likewise writes that Magen Avraham refers specifically to bran. There is no uncertainty regarding coarse bran, which certainly becomes chametz. See there. Chayei Adam, rule 122, subsection 2, writes likewise. This will be further explained below at the beginning of siman 465. See there.
4) If an empty ear of straw is found in a soft, dissolved food and it contains no grain, we do not presume that a prohibition occurred. Since the prohibition of the slightest amount is rabbinic, we assume that all the kernels fell out during threshing. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, siman 465, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there, subsection 6; Chayei Adam, rule 122, subsection 2.
