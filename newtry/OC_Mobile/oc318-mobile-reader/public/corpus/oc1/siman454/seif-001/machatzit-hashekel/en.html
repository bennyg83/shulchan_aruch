(s.k. 1) Sovin etc. — proves somewhat etc. per Rif's view. Meaning as Ran understood Rif's words that he wrote to explain the baraita daf 37: they taught one fulfills with bread of hadra'ah — i.e. bread of sovin; and Ran holds the dough was entirely sovin. But he challenged him: it is not bread for challah — why fulfill with it for matzah? Yet we do not rule like Rif (and Rosh ch. 2 that also Rif's intent is as Shulchan Aruch: bread of sovin means sovin in it, and it is impossible it not reach chimutz — as explained siman 453 one does not fulfill):

And it is possible etc. Kaf HaChayyim wrote: I wonder on this — did we not learn a complete mishnah: one may not soak mursan for chickens, nor may a woman speak meat with mursan, and it certainly causes chimutz as below siman 465 — end quote in brief. In Avot of Rabbi Natan it says Magen Avraham was uncertain regarding sovin that are inferior to mursan per Rashi's explanation; and although Rambam explains opposite to Rashi that mursan is thick and sovin thin — nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote his words per the explanation he brought — see there. In my humble opinion Magen Avraham certainly agrees sovin and mursan chametz, for it is impossible a little flour not adhere to them, as he concludes this s.k. Therefore one does not soak for chickens nor does a woman speak with them; rather he was uncertain whether mursan and sovin themselves chametz, therefore Maharil inferred Rif holds one fulfills with bread of sovin — so sovin themselves chametz. Nafka mina: if sovin or mursan fell into a dish after noon until night, then it is batel as written above siman 447. Against a little flour adhering to them — some say if sovin themselves do not chametz, the dish is permitted. What he concludes "and also impossible not adhere" — his intent: one must distinguish between shafiryar and wheat hull itself; those who permitted shafiryar would not permit wheat hull itself, for Magen Avraham knows those mursan they permitted even on Pesach forbid in any amount, or on erev Pesach and there is no view; if so with wheat hull itself — even though it does not chametz by itself, nevertheless forbid because of adhering flour, as proven from "one does not soak mursan":
