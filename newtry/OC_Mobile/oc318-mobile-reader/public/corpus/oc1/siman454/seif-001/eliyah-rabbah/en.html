[1] Sovin, etc. The Magen Avraham wrote: I have seen those who rule that a husk they call "shprayr" does not cause chimutz; however, that is of the husks of straw, but the husk of the wheat itself possibly causes chimutz, and also it is impossible that some substance of wheat did not adhere to it; end quote. And my brother-in-law in Chok Yaakov wondered: what is he in doubt about, behold a complete mishnah: one does not soak mursan for chickens, for it causes chimutz, as is written in siman 465; end quote. And in my humble opinion, to resolve: the Magen Avraham is not in doubt at all about mursan, but only about the husk of wheat, which is sovin; and as he wrote at the beginning of his words, this is his language: Rashi explained, sovin is the husk of wheat that falls at the time of pounding, and mursin remaining in the sieve; end quote. And even though Bertinoro at the end of chapter 7 of Shabbos wrote on this explanation of Rashi, this is his language: and the Rambam explained the opposite, that mursan is thick and inferior to sovin; end quote: nevertheless nothing is difficult for the Magen Avraham, for he wrote thus according to Rashi, since he brought only Rashi's explanation. And in passing I will mention that apparently there is a difficulty on Rashi's explanation from the mishnah of chapter 11 of Terumos, which taught: mursin are permitted; sovin of new ones are forbidden and of old ones are permitted. And according to Rashi's explanation, why should mursan be permitted and sovin forbidden, behold sovin is inferior to mursan? Also there is a difficulty for me on the Rambam's explanation: if mursin are inferior to sovin, if so the mishnah of chapter 7 of Shabbos taught that mursin and sovin do not join [in this], and needless to say [in that]; and Tosafos, Pesachim folio 39, make clear that it did not teach so at all. And with this one can resolve the first difficulty on Rashi: that he holds that sometimes they call sovin what they call mursan and vice versa, and he inferred so from the force of the difficulty mentioned above. And with this is also resolved why Bertinoro in Terumos did not bring Rashi's explanation and stated as the Rambam's explanation, while in Shabbos it is implied like Rashi's explanation, as I explained; and now also it is fine that he stated likewise in chapter 2 of Challah like the Rambam, because there it taught that sovin and mursin join, and according to Rashi it is difficult, for it would be "this and needless to say this"; and Tosafos Yom Tov did not go into this. And for the practical law it appears that a hard husk certainly causes chimutz, as we said; and with a thick husk, all that is new, namely all that is within thirty days, also causes chimutz, but after thirty days no flour at all is mixed in them, as Bertinoro explained in chapter 11 of Terumos. However, in Shibolei HaLeket, siman 62, in the name of Rashi it is explained that even sovin causes chimutz. Chok Yaakov wrote: if a stalk of straw is found within dissolved food and there is no grain at all in the stalk, we say that all of it fell at the time of threshing, and we do not establish a presumption of issur; see Magen Avraham and the end of siman 465, even if grain is found in the remaining stalk; and if three are found, this requires further study.
