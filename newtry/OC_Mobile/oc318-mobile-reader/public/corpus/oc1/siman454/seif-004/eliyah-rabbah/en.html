One who has an illness in his stomach that they call "heyb mutter," whose remedy is to chalut barley or oats: he wrote in the responsum mentioned above that if there is danger it is permitted for a Jew to do it, and if non-Jews make it from theirs it is permitted even without danger, for placing it on his stomach is not in the normal manner of their benefit; and the same law applies if it is possible with fruit juice: permitted even by a Jew without danger, as above; and even with danger, wherever possible on a side of heter he should do so. And the meaning of chalutah is that he boil the water and afterward put the barley into it; and it is obvious that where there is danger and it is impossible by way of this chalita, it is permitted in every manner, even in the manner of their benefit, as a remedy of eating. The general rule of the matter: not for a remedy, even not in the normal manner of their benefit, and even of non-Jews, and even regarding all the issur, it is forbidden; for a remedy it is permitted so long as it is not in the normal manner of their benefit, whenever it is of non-Jews, or a Jew transfers ownership to him, or with chametz that Pesach passed over; and in the normal manner of their benefit there is no heter except where there is danger, even for a rabbinic [issur]; and see in the Acharonim at the beginning of siman 466.
