With stolen matzah. And it is explained in the Yerushalmi that specifically with stolen matzah one does not fulfill, because the matzah itself comes through a transgression; which is not so if he carried matzah from a private domain to a public domain: he fulfills his obligation on Pesach, because he transgressed, and the matzah itself is not [involved] in a transgression. And so wrote in Toras HaAdam of the Rashba, folio 21, column 3; and so is the implication of Tosafos in Sukkah folio 30, s.v. "because," to distinguish such a case; and see there. Nevertheless, one who carried them out: whether it is forbidden for him to eat, like the law of one who cooks on Shabbos, see above at the beginning of siman 318 and in siman 307, se'if 19. And the Taz wrote that one fulfills with borrowed matzah, since it does not return intact. And the Acharonim wrote, and so it is in the Rokeach, that it is good to say at the time of milling or at the time of baking, "Anyone into whose hands my flour or my matzah arrives, behold it is his as a gift."
And regarding blessing, see above siman 649. His intent is upon what he ruled there, that even though he acquired it through ye'ush and a change, even so he should not recite a blessing over it because of "one who cuts blesses," and there is no distinction between change of name and change of deed, and the like; as we say at the end of that se'if regarding challah: behold one who stole a se'ah of wheat and milled it and baked it and separated challah from it: this one does not bless but reviles; even though there is change of deed and change of name. And so is the implication of most of the posekim, and in Tosafos in Sukkah folio 30, and in Merubah folio 69, and in HaNizakin folio 55, and in the Rosh, chapter HaGozel Etzim; and so is the agreement of the Bach and the Acharonim above in siman 196; and not like the Magen Avraham in siman 649, s.k. 2, who wrote to distinguish between change of name and change of deed. And in truth that is not so. And as for the core difficulty that the Magen Avraham raises below from the sugya of the Talmud in Sukkah folio 30 regarding onvankeri (Rashi explained: merchants of every thing for profit), where the Talmud asks, "and let him acquire them by change of name and change of deed": for from the outset that it is forbidden to bless, nothing is difficult at all, for that it is forbidden to bless over it means in a case of absolute theft, as we say, "behold one who stole a se'ah of wheat"; which is not so there, for it is only a remote concern, and because of a mere stringency of mitzvah they are stringent because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. And know that this is so: for if not so, why did Rav Huna command specifically regarding the hoshanah of the mitzvah and not regarding all other things that people take from them to be cut? And further, when the Talmud asks, "and let them shear the merchants themselves," what is the difficulty: for Rav Huna commanded how they should act ideally, and how would he instruct them to commit theft? Rather, certainly there is no theft here at all, only that because of a stringency of mitzvah we are stringent. (And see in the Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 319, and in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, and Shaar Efraim siman 2, regarding the law of the prohibition of theft.) And in all this it appears that the Taz erred below in siman 664, s.k. 16, for he understood that it is absolute theft, and therefore raises a difficulty from that of HaGozel Basra regarding that of Rav Ashi, etc.; see there; and he did not notice the contradiction of this sugya itself, which proves that it is not theft at all, only a mere stringency of mitzvah, as I wrote. And as for the core difficulty that the Taz raises, why the posekim omitted entirely this ruling of Rav Huna; see there, where he forces a resolution; and in my humble opinion the main reason of the posekim is from what we say in Gittin, chapter HaNizakin folio 85: there is no law of anparas in Babylonia; and the posekim explained that since there are judges and the law of the kingdom, and they did not claim, they forgave regarding them, and it is not theft at all, and it is permitted to take, even though there is no law of ye'ush in land, as is explained explicitly in the Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Gezeilah, and in the Tur, Choshen Mishpat siman 256; see there in the Beis Yosef. And with this is resolved what the Shach hints there in Choshen Mishpat in siman 107 and in siman 371; and so it is necessary according to the Rambam, and not according to Tosafos in chapter Chezkat HaBatim. And I did not wish to elaborate here, for I explained all this in my book on Choshen Mishpat, for there is its place; and see in the Shach to Choshen Mishpat siman 95 regarding the law that whatever stands to be cut is as if cut.
