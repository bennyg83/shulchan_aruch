(14) A person does not fulfill his obligation: that is, even after the fact he must go back and eat; and from the terse wording it is implied that he must go back and recite the blessing; and see in the Biur Halacha. And see in the responsa Shaagas Aryeh, siman 94, who wrote that even on the second night of Yom Tov one does not fulfill with stolen matzah.
(15) With stolen matzah: the reason is that we learn from challah by a gezeirah shavah of "lechem"-"lechem": just as there a person separates challah only from his own dough, for it is written "your doughs," so here likewise. And specifically stolen; but if he borrowed matzah he fulfills with it, for behold a loan for the purpose of eating it and not returning it intact, if so it is truly his. The posekim wrote that one should be careful when they bake many in one oven and many times the matzos are exchanged: it is proper that they say, "Anyone into whose hands his matzah arrives, it shall be his as a gift," for if not so there is concern of stolen matzah. And they further wrote that it is good to say so also at the time of milling, for sometimes the flour is exchanged. And there is one further detail by which people commonly stumble, for example if he bought matzah and brought it into his domain: if the seller revealed his mind at the time of sale that he is pressed for money and cannot sell on credit, and therefore he goes in and out after him for the coins and puts him off with "go and return" and does not give him [payment], by law he did not acquire, as is explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 190, se'if 17; and consequently afterward he does not fulfill with them the obligation of matzah according to the Torah.
(16) He fulfills with it: and nevertheless ideally he should not take it in order to fulfill with it, for behold he cannot recite a blessing over it, as below, and as above in siman 11, se'if 6, in the gloss.
(17) That he acquired it through a change: that is to say, through a change of deed; and likewise where he stole the matzah and gave it to another, that other fulfills with it, for it is a change of domain [and specifically where there is also ye'ush of the owners, for change of domain alone does not acquire]; and that other is permitted to recite a blessing over it, for the mitzvah did not come to his hand through a prohibition, for the robbed party had already despaired of it before it came to his hand. And if the other returned and gave it to the thief, the thief too fulfills with it, for it is ye'ush and change of domain. And regarding maror, whether one fulfills with stolen after the fact, see in the Biur Halacha.
(18) And regarding blessing, see etc.: that is to say, that there it is explained that regarding blessing, in every case one should not bless, even in a place where he acquired, for it is "one who cuts blesses, reviles Hashem," both for the blessing of eating matzah and for the blessing of Hamotzi itself; and regarding Birkas HaMazon, see above in siman 196, se'if 1, in the Mishnah Berurah.
