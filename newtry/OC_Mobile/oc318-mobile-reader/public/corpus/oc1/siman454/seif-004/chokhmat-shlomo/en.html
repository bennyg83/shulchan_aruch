Se'if 4. A person does not fulfill his obligation with stolen matzah, etc. Marginal note: see the Magen Avraham in what he wrote in the name of the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rokeach, that it is good to say, "Anyone into whose hands my matzah arrives, behold it is mine as a gift." But according to what I wrote in my work on Orach Chaim of the year 561, in siman 655 in Machatzit HaShekel on the sugya of the willow for seven [days], and I resolved there the difficulty of Tosafos, s.v. "and they brought rods of willow," and I wrote in one approach according to what is in the Yerushalmi there at the end of chapter 3 of Sukkah, and I proved from there that where it was of his own free will without any compulsion of hefker, it is certainly hefker, and all the more so where he receives something else in its place; see there. If so, regarding a lulav, since he has no other option, it is like hefker under duress, and since it is not hefker with a whole heart, for he relies that perhaps his own will reach his hand, therefore it is not hefker, and therefore a declaration is required. But here, since he is not forced in that way, if so it is with a whole heart; therefore, of itself, since he knows it may be delayed, he despairs of it, and so it becomes hefker automatically, and no declaration at all is needed here. So it appears correct to me, with Hashem's help; and examine this carefully.
