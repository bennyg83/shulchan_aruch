With matzah. See Taz; see Peri Chadash (4) and Chok Yaakov letter 5: one who carried matzah from a private domain to a public domain and ate it: he fulfilled, as is implied in the Yerushalmi, for this is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since without a transgression too he could fulfill the positive commandment; see there. And whether it is permitted for the one who carried it out to eat: Chok Yaakov pointed to Shiyarei [Kenesses HaGedolah] and Shulchan Aruch; and there in Shiyarei we wrote regarding a Jew who committed a rabbinic prohibition: it is implied in the Levush to mix so that they make; and in Eliyah Rabbah there, letter 4, and there we wrote of this. And possibly regarding carrying out from a public domain to a private domain and the like, this requires further study. And it implies: if he baked matzah on Shabbos and ate [it] on Motzei Shabbos, he does not fulfill with it, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and possibly nevertheless he could have baked toward evening.
Behold. One who ate matzah of a prohibition of their [rabbinic] words does not fulfill with it, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, like challah of outside the Land and the like; see Pesachim 35b. And behold according to Rabbi Shimon there, even if he has only matzah of tevel he does not fulfill with it; which is not so for us, that an issur that is only by inclusion [rabbinically / in a mixture], he fulfills with it only when he has no other matzah but this: one can say that he fulfills with it; but when he has other matzah he does not fulfill with it, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. However, in Tosafos, Kiddushin 38a, s.v. "that he brought near," it is explained that he does not fulfill, for a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment from before Sinai, or rabbinically the first kezayit on account of the second kezayit; see there. And nevertheless regarding a prohibition of their words, one can say that it is a decree upon a decree; and according to one resolution one can say: whatever according to the Torah does not override, the same applies regarding their words: they upheld; see Magen Avraham, Temurah 46b; and in the Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchos Chametz u'Matzah, law 7: whoever does not recite Birkas HaMazon does not fulfill with it: that is, even for a rabbinic prohibition one can say when he has other matzah that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. And possibly even wine of the four cups or maror that is rabbinic, in such a case a rabbinic positive commandment comes and overrides a rabbinic negative commandment, as the Magen Avraham wrote in Temurah letter 2; and it is called "in its time," and also he can eat a kezayit and a revi'is at once; see in the Yerushalmi and in siman 475; with Hashem's help it will be explained further. And see Yoreh De'ah 119 regarding one who is suspected, that people do not lodge with him on Pesach in such a case, and he has no other matzah: what is the law? And this requires further study. And with Hashem's help in siman 475 and 485 I will explain further.
