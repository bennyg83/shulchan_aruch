A person does not fulfill his obligation with matzah, etc. See in the Mishnah Berurah; and see in the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger and in Shaagas Aryeh, who brought the view of the Ritva that his opinion is that even with stolen matzah there is shinui maaseh, for since he chewed it he acquired it by shinui maaseh, and the mitzvah is fulfilled afterward with the swallowing of the matzah, for behold one who swallowed matzah fulfilled; and if so it is similar to one who stole wheat or flour and made it into matzah, who fulfilled. And even though for the practical law we certainly hold like the view of the Shulchan Aruch, which is the view of most of the posekim who hold that he did not fulfill and must go back and eat other matzah of his own (and possibly they hold that this is called that the acquisition comes through the mitzvah itself, for we hold in siman 649 that it does not help; for even though if he swallowed without chewing he fulfilled, nevertheless ideally chewing is required so that there be the taste of matzah in his mouth): nevertheless, regarding whether he must go back and recite a blessing when he eats it, possibly one should be concerned for the view of the Ritva that he already fulfilled after the fact, and therefore he should not bless afterward. I found that Magen Elef agreed so. And regarding maror, whether one fulfills after the fact with stolen: the view of Kenesses HaGedolah in the name of Mahariv is that he fulfilled, and the Peri Chadash disagrees with him, for matzah and maror are equated to each other, as is explained in chapter Kol Shaah. And see in Shaagas Aryeh, siman 94, whose view is that also with stolen matzah the disqualification is because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression (and that is also the view of the Levush), and therefore regarding maror, which is only rabbinic, it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression in a rabbinic [matter], for according to the Mechaber in siman 649, se'if 5, he fulfills after the fact; and so wrote Mekor Chaim; see there. However, according to the Rama there in the gloss, who wrote that we practice like the posekim who hold that also in a rabbinic [matter] a mitzvah that comes through a transgression is invalid, certainly also with maror he did not fulfill. [And I saw a wondrous thing: that Kenesses HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahariv in siman 193 that one fulfills with stolen maror; and I looked there and it is explained to the contrary, and this is his language there: Moreinu HaRav R' Anshil of Marpork was in doubt: since maror is equated to matzah and stolen is invalid, if so a Jew should not uproot maror (himself) from the ground, just as with myrtle, of which he said to them, etc., to those who sell myrtle, etc.; see there in chapter Lulav HaGazul. And it appears to me that it is not comparable, for there regarding myrtle it continues to live in the ground, therefore it is like ground; see there. Behold it is explained explicitly from Mahariv that he too admits that if one steals maror from his fellow's hand he did not fulfill, and only regarding cutting from the ground he holds that it is not called attached beforehand, and it is not comparable to myrtle.] And in any event regarding blessing one must certainly be careful, when he takes other maror to eat, not to recite a blessing again anew.
And regarding blessing, etc. The posekim wrote that specifically with stolen one does not fulfill, because the matzah itself comes through a transgression; which is not so if he carried matzah from a private domain to a public domain when Pesach fell on Shabbos: he fulfills his obligation with it and also recites a blessing over it, because he transgressed a transgression and the matzah itself is not involved in a transgression. And see above siman 318, se'if 1, in the Mishnah Berurah and in the Biur Halacha.
See below at the beginning of siman 649. See Mishnah Berurah. See in Chok Yaakov who brought the view of Magen Avraham to disagree here, that he must bless since he already acquired; and Chok Yaakov disputed him and agreed with the Mechaber's view; and in Mekor Chaim and Magen Elef they decided like Magen Avraham; see there. And likewise Beis Meir hesitates here and wrote that several posekim hold that this "this one does not bless but reviles" does not come to exclude that he need not bless over it, but to say that he mentions sin in his blessing, and in any event he must bless; see there. And see above siman 11, where we wrote that the view of the Taz and the Gra is like the Shulchan Aruch and Rama; and so I found that the Yaavetz in siman 196 decided so for the practical law; and so is the view of the Shulchan Aruch HaRav in this siman; and so one should rule, for a doubt of blessings is to be lenient.
