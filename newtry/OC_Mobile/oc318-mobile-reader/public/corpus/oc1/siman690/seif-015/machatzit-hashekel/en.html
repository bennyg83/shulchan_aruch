Seif katan 15: He must, etc. The Rashba wrote, etc., whether the listener must have intent, etc.; "for I heard that one sage, etc." That is to say, the questioner knew that one must hear every word. This is possible even by heart. But he was uncertain whether one must concentrate on every word, and this doubt arose for him because he heard that the sage held a megillah in his hand. Perforce the reason is that he held that one must concentrate on every word; this is difficult when one hears by heart, and it is easier to concentrate when one hears from the writing. About this the questioner wondered, for perforce one need not concentrate on every word, for is it not true that one has fulfilled, etc.? Furthermore, women and unlearned people, etc., who do not know how to read Ashuris from the writing, how do they fulfill their obligation? For by heart it is impossible to concentrate on every word, and for them, since they do not know how to read Ashuris, it is impossible from the writing. The Rashba added a proof for him from the case of one who was passing by, etc.: if he stood, etc. Perforce he was not holding a megillah in his hand in order to listen, for if so, what doubt is there? He certainly intended to fulfill his obligation. Rather, perforce, even if he hears by heart he has fulfilled, even though he did not concentrate on every word. So wrote the Tur in siman 59, that for this reason each person should recite the blessings himself and not rely on the prayer leader to discharge his obligation. Although we hold that one who hears is like one who answers, nevertheless there is concern lest he not hear one word. Thus one word prevents fulfillment even for one who hears.
