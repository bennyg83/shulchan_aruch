And he recites the blessing, etc. The Magen Avraham wrote as follows: if he wishes, he recites the blessing and afterward rolls it, and so it is in Seder HaYom. In truth, this is also written in Knesses HaGedolah, siman 8, seif 2. But I wonder how he was not concerned for what is written in Maharil, in an incident where the reader began the blessing before rolling it, and Maharil rebuked him and silenced him until they rolled it into one roll and placed it before him. Then he resumed and recited the blessing. So too the Mateh Moshe ruled simply. I further saw that the Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah retracted and ruled in accordance with Maharil. Also, Maharil brings a proof from the language of the Semag: he goes back, rolls it, and recites the blessing. In the Mordechai and Agudah I saw this stated even more explicitly: he rolls all of it and afterward recites the blessing. It seems to me that the incident of Maharil speaks where he had only begun, 'Blessed are You,' but once he has mentioned the Name, there is no logic to silence him, for in Responsa Maharil, siman 57, he wrote that the reason is that it is disgraceful for the Megillah to lie open unnecessarily after the reading has been completed. If so, because of disgrace one should not cause a blessing in vain. See siman 135, and this requires study.
