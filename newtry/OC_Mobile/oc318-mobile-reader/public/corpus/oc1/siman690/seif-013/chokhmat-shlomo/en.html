"If he was writing it, meaning that he read a verse in the megillah from which he was copying and wrote it, if he directed his heart to fulfill his obligation, etc." Note: In my humble opinion a new law appears, and it is correct with Hashem's help: even if he directed his heart, this is only for an individual, where if he directed his heart to fulfill his obligation it helps; but in a congregation this does not help. This is because it is similar to what is written in the Gemara and in Shulchan Aruch below, siman 691, that we do not read publicly from a megillah written among the Writings, and if he read he did not fulfill his obligation unless there is a recognizable sign that it is longer or shorter. If so, it is proven that in a congregation intent of the heart to fulfill does not help; rather, it must be recognizable that he is reading for the sake of the megillah. If so, here too, when he writes it, expounds it, and corrects it, in actual practice it is not recognizable whether he is reading for the sake of the mitzvah or in order to write, expound, and correct. If so, this is no better than a megillah written among the Writings when it is not recognizable. Perforce, the reason there is that since it is not recognizable, it is not known to them whether it is a megillah. The same law applies if it is not recognizable whether he read it for the sake of the mitzvah; this does not help in a congregation. See Magen Avraham, siman 691, who wrote that any ten are called a congregation; see there. With this the words of the Kol Bo, brought by the Korban HaEdah on the Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halachah 2, are properly settled. Regarding "if he was writing it," the Yerushalmi says: this teaches that on Purim work is permitted. It answers: say that it was on the fourteenth in walled cities; end quote of the Yerushalmi. The Korban HaEdah brought in Shirei Korban the words of the Kol Bo, who cited the Yerushalmi's question and answered that it was a pressing circumstance. The Korban HaEdah wondered: why was he not satisfied with the Yerushalmi's answer that it speaks of the fourteenth in walled cities? He left this requiring analysis. According to what I wrote, it works well. This law appears clear to one who examines Rashi's explanation, the Ran, and Beis Yosef, who brought them, that the reason it is invalid when written among the Writings is because it is not recognizable and it is like reading Scripture, and there is no publicizing of the miracle. If so, here too, when he wrote it, expounded it, and corrected it, it is not recognizable that it is for the sake of the mitzvah, and there is no publicizing of the miracle. According to this it works well. Since we hold that a megillah in its proper time may be read by an individual, and not in its proper time requires ten, and it is proven in the Gemara, page 5, that even in walled cities on Friday it is also considered not in its proper time and requires ten, if so it is difficult: how can one say that he was writing it on the fourteenth in walled cities? At that time we require ten, and if so his directing his heart does not help. But the Yerushalmi follows its own view. One who examines the Yerushalmi in the first chapter of Megillah, halachah 3, will see this language: Rabbi Avina said in the name of Rabbi Asi: anything they said should be postponed to the day of assembly, and specifically with ten. If so, it is proven that anything not postponed to the day of assembly, even if postponed to Friday, nevertheless does not require ten. Therefore the Yerushalmi can answer that the mishnah speaks of the fourteenth in walled cities. But the Kol Bo follows the view of our Gemara, which holds on page 5 that even in walled cities on the fourteenth ten are required. Therefore it is impossible to explain that it speaks of the fourteenth, since ten are called a congregation, and therefore he was forced to explain it differently. Analyze this carefully: even if the others have already fulfilled their obligation, nevertheless, since ten are required and publicizing is required, the case of one who was writing it does not help. Analyze this. One should not challenge our words by asking: if so, according to Rabbi Asi, who holds that whether in its proper time or not in its proper time it requires ten, how will he explain the mishnah of "if he was writing it, etc."? This is not difficult. Rabbi Asi will hold that regarding a megillah written among the Writings as well, one fulfills in a congregation, and he disagrees with Rabbi Yochanan. For in any event he disagrees with Rabbi Yochanan: Rabbi Yochanan holds that it is permitted even for an individual, while Rabbi Asi holds that we require a congregation, as Tosafos wrote under "if so." So too, in this matter he disagrees with Rabbi Yochanan. On the contrary, according to this it works well, for this depends on that. Rabbi Yochanan follows his own view, that in a congregation one has not fulfilled with a megillah written among the Writings; perforce, according to him, it is proven from the mishnah of "if he was writing it" that one fulfills even individually, as I wrote. But Rabbi Asi, who holds that one fulfills in a congregation with a megillah written among the Writings, if so there is no proof from the mishnah of "if he was writing it" that one fulfills individually, and therefore he holds that we require a congregation. For us, since we rule like Rabbi Yochanan that one does not fulfill with a megillah written among the Writings, and we rule that not in its proper time, even on the fourteenth, requires ten, it is impossible to answer like the Yerushalmi. The words of the Kol Bo therefore work well, with Hashem's help, properly. Analyze this carefully.
