(there seif 13) There was one who wrote while reading a verse in Megillah that he copies from it and writes it if he set his heart to fulfill yotzei, etc. NB it appears in my humble opinion a new law and it is correct, may it be so: even if he set his heart it helps only for an individual in this if he set his heart to fulfill; but in congregation this does not help. And it is similar to what Talmud and Shulchan Aruch wrote below siman 691: one does not read in congregation Megillah written between the lines; if he read he does not fulfill yotzei unless there is recognition it is long or short. If so proven in congregation intent of heart to fulfill does not help — only that it be evident he reads for Megillah; and if so here too if he writes, expounds, proofreads — in act it is not evident if he reads for mitzvah or to write and expound and proofread — therefore not better than Megillah written between lines that is not evident — perforce the reason: since not evident they do not know if it is Megillah — likewise if not evident if he read for mitzvah it does not help in congregation; see Magen Avraham siman 691 who wrote every ten is called congregation — see there. And with this we placed on correct everyone's words in Kol Bo who brought Korban Eidah in Yerushalmi chapter 2 halacha 2 regarding "there was one who wrote it" — you said Purim is permitted in doing melacha and answered: said on the fourteenth in walled cities — end Yerushalmi; and Korban Eidah in Shirrei Korban brought everyone's words in Kol Bo who brought Yerushalmi's difficulty and answers everyone in Kol Bo that it was time of pressure; Korban Eidah wondered why he was not satisfied with Yerushalmi's answer that it deals with the 14th in walled cities and left in requires study; and per my words it will come out well: behold the above law appears clear to examiner in Rashi and Ran and Beit Yosef who brought them: reason invalid between lines is because not evident if like reading Scripture — no publicizing miracle; and if so here too if he wrote, expounded, proofread — not evident it is for mitzvah — no publicizing miracle; and per this it comes out well: since we hold Megillah in its time for individual, not in its time with ten; and proven in Talmud daf 5 that even in walled cities on erev also considered not in its time and is with ten; and if so difficult how say he wrote on the 14th in walled cities — behold then we require ten and then intent does not help. But Yerushalmi per its method: examiner in Yerushalmi first chapter Megillah halacha 3: R' Avina in name of R' Asi: all they said postpone to day of entry, and specifically with ten — if so proven whoever is not postponed to day of entry even though postponed to erev — nevertheless does not require ten; therefore answers well Mishnah deals with the 14th in walled cities; but Kol Bo follows our Talmud's method who holds even in walled cities on the 14th ten required — impossible to explain it deals with the 14th since ten is called congregation — therefore must explain another matter; examine: even if others already fulfilled yotzei — nevertheless since we require ten and require publicity — "there was one who wrote" does not help; examine. And do not challenge on our words: if so for R' Asi who holds whether in its time or not in its time with ten — how explain Mishnah "there was one who wrote," etc.; this is not — R' Asi holds Megillah written between lines also fulfills in congregation and argues on R' Yochanan; for without this he argues on R' Yochanan: R' Yochanan holds permitted even for individual and he holds congregation required as Tosafot s.v. if so — also here he argues on R' Yochanan; and on the contrary here it will come out well: this depends on that — R' Yochanan per his method holds in congregation not fulfilled written between lines — perforce from Mishnah "there was one who wrote" fulfills even individual as said; but R' Asi holds fulfills in congregation written between lines — therefore no proof from Mishnah he wrote fulfills individual — therefore holds congregation required.
