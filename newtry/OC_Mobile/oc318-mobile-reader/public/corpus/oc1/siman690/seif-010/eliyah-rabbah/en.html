In one breath, etc. Initially, the practice is to say in one breath from the beginning of 'five hundred men and Parshandasa' until 'the ten,' as the Rama writes, and so the Bach ruled. The reason is given in Darchei Moshe and Mateh Moshe in the name of Maharil, that the ten sons of Haman were officers over those five hundred men. Regarding the other verses of the Megillah, the Mateh Moshe wrote that one should pause only long enough to take a breath, whether in the middle of a verse or between verses, because one is obligated to read it like a letter. When the shaliach tzibbur reaches 'on that night the king's sleep was disturbed,' he should raise his voice more than he has until now, for there begins Esther's redemption. When he says 'this letter,' he should shake the Megillah there.
