Seif katan 10: He does not fulfill in a foreign language. For he holds that what is stated in the Gemara, "but we read, etc.," means as the Ran explains, unlike Olas Shabbos. His words require explanation, for Olas Shabbos asked against Ramban, who holds that one does not fulfill in a foreign language if he knows Ashuris, from the fact that the Gemara sets mishnayos against each other on page 18. It teaches at the beginning of chapter 2: if he read it in translation or in any language, he has not fulfilled; and then it teaches: but we read it for foreign-language speakers in a foreign language. If it is impossible to explain like the Ramban, then perforce that which it says we read it for foreign-language speakers speaks of one who does not know the Holy Tongue. If so, perhaps the first clause, which teaches that he has not fulfilled, speaks when he knows the Holy Tongue.
Therefore Olas Shabbos wrote that the primary view appears to him to be like the poskim who say that he has fulfilled even if he knows the Holy Tongue. If so, I do not know what Magen Avraham added by bringing the words of the Ran and writing on this, unlike Olas Shabbos. How is the difficulty of Olas Shabbos resolved through this? Perhaps one can say that his intent is as follows. The conclusion of the Gemara there is that the later clause, "but we read, etc.," is comparable only to what was taught: Egyptian for Egyptians, Hebrew for Hebrews, Aramaic for Arameans, Greek for Greeks, he has fulfilled. Olas Shabbos understood that the answerer's intent is that the later clause, "but we read, etc.," speaks when he understands that foreign language, and the first clause, which teaches that one who read it in any language has not fulfilled, speaks when he does not understand that language. Therefore he asked properly. However, Magen Avraham has a different approach to explaining the answerer. If we make a slight precision, why did Magen Avraham write, "that which is stated in the Gemara, but, etc."? He should have said "that which is stated in the mishnah," for that is the language of the mishnah. However, the Ran brought that the Ramban disagrees with Rashi, who holds that if he understands the foreign language, even if he understands the Holy Tongue, he has fulfilled. Why? Since he can read in the Holy Tongue. And he wrote that this is also implied by the language "foreign-language speakers," meaning one who does not know the Holy Tongue, as we say elsewhere: those foreigners who came before Rava, he set up an interpreter between them, etc. This implies that "foreign-language speaker" means one who does not know the Holy Tongue. So too, what was taught, Egyptian for Egyptians, means for Egyptian foreign-language speakers who do not know the Holy Tongue; these are his words. If so, according to the Ramban one can say that just as "foreign-language speaker" means one who does not know the Holy Tongue, likewise Egyptian, Aramean, and the like also imply one who does not know the Holy Tongue, but only Aramaic or Egyptian and the like. If so, one can say that the answerer in truth answered according to the question of Olas Shabbos: it is comparable only to what was taught, Egyptian for Egyptians, etc., meaning that the later clause speaks of foreign-language speakers in their foreign language, where he is foreign-speaking and does not understand the Holy Tongue, like the baraisa. The first clause in truth speaks even when he understands the foreign language, since he understands the Holy Tongue he has not fulfilled. But Magen Avraham, whose way is to be brief, included all those mentioned in the baraisa, Egyptian, Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek, in the word "foreign-language speakers." If so, Magen Avraham's intent in writing, "that which is stated in the Gemara, but we read for foreign-language speakers, etc.," means: that which the Gemara brought from the baraisa of Egyptian, etc., speaks of one who does not know the Holy Tongue.
If so, in truth the answerer answered according to the difficulty of Olas Shabbos. And if we want to emend the words of Magen Avraham, what he wrote, "this means one who does not know the Holy Tongue," should read "this means one who knows the Holy Tongue," and the word "does not" is a scribal error. This works better, and Magen Avraham's intent is not to answer the difficulty of Olas Shabbos, for Olas Shabbos indeed asked well against the Ramban. Rather, regarding what Olas Shabbos ruled as practical law against the Ramban because of the above difficulty, on this Magen Avraham disagreed, for according to us one can answer the difficulty of Olas Shabbos. The Ran there wrote on the Ramban's words, in what he wrote against Rashi, asking: since he understands the Holy Tongue, how can we permit him other languages? Against the Sages who disagree with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel there is no difficulty, for they hold that all books may be written in any language; only megillah, because it says concerning it "according to their script and according to their times," requires specifically the Holy Tongue. If so, we establish the exclusion in the more reasonable case, and it comes to exclude other languages only when he does not understand that language; but when he understands the language, even though he knows the Holy Tongue, the external reasoning remains that he fulfills. But Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that even the other books were permitted to be written only in Greek, and according to him the derivation from "according to their script and according to their times" is not needed. However, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel presumably does not disagree with the baraisa of Egyptian for Egyptians, that if he understands that language he fulfills. Perforce one must say that according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, based on reasoning, megillah is more lenient in this than other books, since its essence is publicizing the miracle. Since he understands that language, it is fitting that he fulfill with it, for it contains publicizing of the miracle. If so, according to him the law is with the Ramban: one who understands the Holy Tongue does not fulfill in a foreign language, since it was permitted to him only because of publicizing the miracle. Since he understands the Holy Tongue, and there is also publicizing of the miracle for him in the Holy Tongue, the other languages remain prohibited. The Ran wrote further that the language of the mishnah implies that even if he knows the Holy Tongue, nevertheless he fulfills in a foreign language that he understands, for it teaches, "but we read it for foreign-language speakers in a foreign language," and it does not teach, "the foreign-language speaker reads it in a foreign language," parallel to the first clause, which teaches, "if he read it in translation." This implies that it speaks where someone else, who is not foreign-speaking, reads it for the foreign-language speaker. If it were impossible for one who knows the Holy Tongue to fulfill in a foreign language, then the reader, who is not foreign-speaking and understands the Holy Tongue, does not fulfill for himself in a foreign language, and in such a case he cannot discharge another, even one who does not understand the Holy Tongue, as stated in the Yerushalmi; these are his words. If so, for us, since we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, one can say like the Ramban, and the Ran too agrees with him, and for us the difficulty of Olas Shabbos is not difficult. For the Gemara should not have answered that the later clause speaks of one who does not understand the Holy Tongue and the first clause of one who understands the Holy Tongue, because the Gemara proved that the mishnah holds that even one who understands the Holy Tongue fulfills, from the fact that it teaches, "we read it for foreign-language speakers," and does not teach, "the foreign-language speaker reads in a foreign language," as the Ran wrote. Perforce, the reader is not a foreign-language speaker and understands the Holy Tongue, and nevertheless he discharges his fellow. Perforce one must say that one who understands the Holy Tongue also fulfills in a foreign language that he understands, as the Ran wrote, and this is because the mishnah follows the Sages. But for us, since we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, one can say like the Ramban, that he does not fulfill in a foreign language when he understands the Holy Tongue, for that is reasonable, as the Ran wrote and as above. According to this, Magen Avraham's words are only to disagree with the ruling of Olas Shabbos, for according to us it is not difficult, and according to us one should rule like the Ramban, as above. This is the explanation of Magen Avraham's words: he holds that what is stated in the Gemara, meaning in the mishnah, "but we read, etc.," means one who knows the Holy Tongue. This is from the fact that it teaches "we read for foreign-language speakers" and does not teach "the foreign-language speaker reads," as the Ran wrote. Perforce one must say that the mishnah holds that he fulfills even if he knows the Holy Tongue, and therefore it challenges properly; this is because the mishnah follows the Sages. But for us, since we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, one can say like the Ramban, for this is reasonable, as above.
