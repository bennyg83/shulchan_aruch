Section 10: One who knows. They asked: do we not say in the first chapter, 9a, that regarding megillah one fulfills only in the Holy Tongue? They explained "for foreign-language speakers" as referring to those who do not know the Holy Tongue, like in the first chapter of Makkos, "those foreign-language speakers, etc." Because they do not know, it was permitted to them with difficulty, so that they should have actual knowledge, and also be able to read for themselves. So too it is in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 2 of Megillah, end of halachah 1: Rabbi Abahu said in the name of Rabbi Elazar: one who knows Ashuris and knows a foreign language fulfills with Ashuris, meaning only with Ashuris, for with Ashuris one fulfills even if he knows nothing at all. With a foreign language he fulfills in the foreign language. If one knows Ashuris and knows a foreign language, what is the law regarding discharging others in the foreign language? That is to say, the others know only the foreign language. It remains unresolved, like this rule: anyone who is not obligated in the matter does not discharge the public from their obligation. This is the precise text. See the Ran and Maggid Mishneh, who say that so ruled the Ramban and the Rashba. So too it is in Sotah at the beginning of the chapter "These are said": Rabbi Levi bar Chisa went to Caesarea and heard them reciting Shema in a foreign language, apparently some foreign language, and in the Aruch he wrote that it is Greek. He wanted to stop them. Rabbi Yose heard and became angry. He said: do I say that one who does not know how to read Ashuris should not read it at all? Rabbi Berechyah answered: regarding Megillas Esther, if he knew how to read Ashuris and a foreign language, he fulfills only with Ashuris. That is to say, what are you answering from one who does not know to one who does know? He properly stopped them, etc.; see there. So is the main view, because the plain meaning of the Yerushalmi is because he also knows the Ashuris language, and so too is the plain meaning of the mishnah, which calls them foreign-language speakers, and from the fact that it says "and the foreign-language speaker, etc.," and the Gemara asks, but he does not know what it is saying. Even according to those who rejected the Yerushalmi and explained that the Yerushalmi speaks where it is written in Ashuris language, unlike our Gemara, as written below, nevertheless from them we learn for us where it is written in a foreign language. And our Gemara, which says, "and he read them, etc., like an interpreter who translates, he has fulfilled," means in the language of translation, as written below. Certainly the explanation is so. But nevertheless there is no proof from this, for only regarding part of it did he fulfill, as one fulfills with what he reads by heart. And although the Yerushalmi does not imply so, for it asks there how one fulfills in translation, and answers: Shmuel follows his own view, etc.; Rabbi Abahu, etc. This implies that according to Rabbi Abahu one has not fulfilled in this case. One can say that our Gemara disagrees with this, for it does not ask anything from translation. But one can say that our Gemara holds like Shmuel, that this baraisa of "if the scribe omitted, etc." is Shmuel's, as stated there in the Yerushalmi. According to this, we rule like Shmuel, and this is the second view, that he fulfills. But to explain the Yerushalmi as they explained, and to distinguish that if it is written in a foreign language even Rabbi Abahu agrees, is not clear to me, as above. For certainly the Yerushalmi disagrees with our Gemara and validates it for foreign-language speakers even if it is written in Ashuris; nevertheless the same law applies for us if it is written in a foreign language, as above.
And some say that he fulfills. This is because they explain the Yerushalmi as speaking where it was written in Ashuris and he read it in a foreign language, as above, for they hold that the Yerushalmi says that even so he fulfills, and specifically when he knows only the foreign language, as the Ran wrote in the responsum cited above. It teaches in the Yerushalmi there: Shmuel taught: if he erred and omitted one verse, and the interpreter translated it, he has fulfilled. We say: in any language he has not fulfilled, and you say this? Shmuel follows his own view, for he said: if it was written according to its law, the foreign-language speaker fulfills with it in a foreign language. Rabbi Abahu in the name of Rabbi Elazar: one who knows, etc., as above. The Yerushalmi holds that this is what the mishnah means: by heart, he has not fulfilled; in any language, etc., also refers to where it is written in Ashuris and he read it in translation, in any language, which is also by heart. It answers that Shmuel follows his own view, for he holds that if it is written according to its law, meaning in the Holy Tongue, and he read it in a foreign language, he has fulfilled, since he translated from the writing, unlike our Gemara; and the mishnah is not because of by heart, but because he does not know the foreign language. It said "written according to its law" as a novelty. But that which Shmuel taught is even according to our Gemara, as written below. Rabbi Abahu disagrees with him and holds that in such a case, where it is written in Ashuris and he reads in a foreign language, he does not fulfill with it, except specifically where he does not know the Holy Tongue. About this it says: one who knows, etc., what is the law regarding discharging, etc.; like this rule, anyone, etc. It emerges that according to our Gemara, which holds that even for one who does not know the Holy Tongue, nevertheless in such a case he has not fulfilled because it is by heart, he also cannot discharge others. This is what the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch wrote in section 9: but if it was, etc.; and since he did not fulfill, etc. The Yerushalmi in Sotah follows the view that Shema may be said only in the Holy Tongue, and compares it to megillah in the above case. It says there: Rabbi says, I say that Shema may be said only in the Holy Tongue. What is the reason? "And these words shall be." Rabbi Levi, etc., as above. So too it appears there in the Yerushalmi that it holds that what is written there, by heart he has not fulfilled; in any language, etc., means that it is written in Ashuris and he read it in translation, etc., which is also by heart, as above. It says there above: and this is where it is written in a foreign language, meaning what is written, "but we read it for foreign-language speakers in a foreign language." What are we dealing with? If it was written in Ashuris and he translated it in a foreign language, that is what we learned, "in any language." That is to say, it said in the first clause that he has not fulfilled in such a case. If it was written in a foreign language and he translated it into Ashuris, meaning even if he translated it into Ashuris, that is what we learned: what is the difference between books and Megillas Esther? The books may be written in any language, and the megillah may be written only in Ashuris. Let it be resolved that it was written in Greek, meaning Greek, as in the Gemara, where Greek is valid. Shmuel taught, etc. It appears that the question against Shmuel is also not because of by heart, for even at first he held that it is not by heart, as written: if, etc.; if, etc. Rather, it is because one does not fulfill in a foreign language except Greek, as written: when it was written in the Holy Tongue and he read it in a foreign language, this is "in any language" and is valid only in Greek, and he read in the Holy Tongue, as above. The Ran's explanation is astonishing, for if the question was because of by heart, it should have challenged from the first clause, that if he read by heart, etc. Furthermore, what does it answer? In any case, it is by heart. Rather, certainly it knew that part of it is like from the writing, as in our Gemara, but it challenged from another language, for it held that what is written "but we read, etc." refers specifically to Greek foreign language, as above, like the initial assumption in our Gemara. To this it answered: Shmuel follows his own view, etc., like the conclusion of the Gemara that the mishnah speaks of every foreign language.
And if it is written. This follows from the baraisa of Shmuel mentioned above: if he erred, etc., and translated it, etc., although he read the entire megillah in the Holy Tongue. According to Rabbi Abahu this is specifically when he does not know the Holy Tongue and he read the entire megillah in another language. According to our Gemara it is specifically when it is written in those languages, for otherwise it is by heart. So too one can explain our Gemara there: The Sages taught: if he omitted, etc., and the reader read them like the interpreter who translates, etc. According to Rashi's explanation, all this is extra language. Rather, the explanation is that he read them in the language of translation, because they knew the translation by heart; therefore it used such a case and says he has fulfilled, because the part he omitted is like written, since he did not omit most of it. This is what it means by "like the interpreter, etc." For otherwise, written words are forbidden to be recited by heart in order to discharge the public from their obligation, according to everyone. Perforce it speaks even where they understand the Holy Tongue, for otherwise the baraisa of blurred letters should have said the same. In any case, learn from this that if part of it is written in translation, one may read it in such a manner. So too one can say that even according to Rabbi Abahu and Rabbi Elazar, they disagree only with Shmuel, but not with what Shmuel taught, for in such a case Rabbi Elazar agrees, as above. According to this explanation there is support for the Rambam that he fulfills, as above.
