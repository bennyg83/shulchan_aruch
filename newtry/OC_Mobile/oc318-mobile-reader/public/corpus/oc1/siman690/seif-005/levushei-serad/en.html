Magen Avraham, seif katan 8. That after the fact it is valid, etc. He resolves the Taz’s difficulty in seif katan 3. The distinction, when the scribe omitted text, between most of the Megillah and half of it is derived from reconciling two baraisos in Megillah 18a: the invalidating baraisa concerns omission of most, while the validating baraisa concerns omission of part. Regarding one who was writing it, cited in the Taz, seif katan 3, the Gemara asks: if he wrote and read it verse by verse, how did he fulfill his obligation, when Rav Chama ruled like the opinion requiring the entire Megillah, and even the opinion beginning from “A Jewish man” requires the whole Megillah to be written? Rashba proves from this that if it is missing from the beginning through “A Jewish man,” it is invalid, necessarily even after the fact, for otherwise what is the Gemara’s question? Taz objected that in a verse-by-verse case most is missing. Magen Avraham answers that the Gemara necessarily knew Rav Chama invalidated even after the fact; otherwise it could ask whether even omission of most is valid. One cannot say the invalidity follows from reconciling the baraisos, for then the Gemara should have challenged from the baraisa rather than citing Rav Chama. It therefore knew Rav Chama invalidated after the fact. His ruling must include even a minority omission, since from the beginning through “A Jewish man” is only a minority. This is clear.
