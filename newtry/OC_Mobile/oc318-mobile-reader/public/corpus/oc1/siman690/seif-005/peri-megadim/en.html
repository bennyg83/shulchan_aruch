And read. See Taz, and see Beis Yosef, who wrote that since the baraisa in Megillah 18b was stated in this language, etc. The Taz explained that there is a need to teach us this: not only where he read the third verse and afterward the second, which is out of order because the subject changed; even if he skipped one verse and read afterward and finished the entire megillah, where the subject did not change because of the one verse that he skipped, nevertheless he must go back and read in order, as stated. The Mechaber follows his own approach in Beis Yosef and omitted this, because he holds that it is the same matter. The Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 2 of Megillah, halachah 1, also did not bring the baraisa; see Maggid Mishneh there. However, in the Gemara there, 18b, if he found the congregation had read half of it, he should not say, I will read half with the congregation, etc. The Rambam, of blessed memory, mentioned this there in halachah 2, but the Taz omitted it, and I do not know why, for one can say that the Sages permitted reading with the congregation, and it teaches us that he has not fulfilled whenever he read out of order. Know that even one verse out of order, even after the fact, he has not fulfilled, for one must read all of it, as explained. The same applies to one word out of order: he must go back and read in order. The case of one verse is a novelty, and so too "I will read half" teaches that one should not say that since it is a separate subject, it is acceptable. See the Ran, of blessed memory, at the beginning of the chapter of one who reads out of order.
