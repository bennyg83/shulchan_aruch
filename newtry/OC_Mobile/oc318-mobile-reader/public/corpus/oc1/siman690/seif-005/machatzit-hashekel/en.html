Seif katan 5: Even a minority; see Keneses HaGedolah, etc. On page 18b it says: The Sages taught: if the scribe omitted letters, words, or verses, and the reader read them by heart, he has fulfilled. It challenges this from what was taught: if it had blurred letters, if their outline is recognizable it is valid, and if not it is invalid. It answers: this is not difficult; this case is where all of it is so, and the same law applies to most of it, which is like all of it, and then it is invalid; that case is where part of it is so, and then it is valid. The Rashba asked from that which Rabbi Chelbo said in the name of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the name of Rav: even according to the one who says that one need not read the whole megillah from its beginning, and it is enough if he begins from "There was a Jewish man, etc.," nevertheless it must be written in its entirety before him. This implies that if it is missing until "There was a Jewish man," it is invalid. Why? You said that if part of it is missing, it is valid. One must say, if the Rashba does not disagree with the Orchos Chaim brought by Rama, that if a complete matter is missing, it is invalid even if only part is missing; and perforce they do not disagree, for in Shulchan Aruch he brought the law of the Rashba and also the law of Orchos Chaim. Perforce one must say that since we are dealing with the one who says that one need read only from "There was a Jewish man" and onward, what is missing until "There was a Jewish man" is not treated as a missing complete matter; this is easy to understand. The Rashba wrote that one cannot say that Rabbi Chelbo speaks initially, as the language "it must" also implies, and that the baraisa speaks after the fact, for it teaches "he has fulfilled." This is not so, for perforce the Gemara knew that Rabbi Chelbo was speaking also after the fact, from the fact that it challenges what we learned, namely below in section 13, that if he was expounding it or writing it, if he directed his heart, he has fulfilled. It asks: what are the circumstances of writing it? If you say that he wrote verse by verse and each time read the verse that he wrote, but Rabbi Chelbo said that it must be written in its entirety. If Rabbi Chelbo spoke only initially, what is the challenge to the mishnah of one who was writing it, which speaks after the fact, for it teaches "he has fulfilled"? Rather, certainly the Gemara knew that Rabbi Chelbo speaks even after the fact, and the difficulty returns. From this the Rashba proved that at its beginning, and automatically the same at its end, even if only part is missing it is invalid. In Keneses HaGedolah he wrote that one can say there is no distinction, and that a minority is valid even at the beginning and one fulfills with it after the fact, while Rabbi Chelbo speaks initially.
And that which the Gemara challenged from the mishnah of one who was writing it, as above, even though the mishnah speaks after the fact and after the fact Rabbi Chelbo agrees that he has fulfilled, is specifically where part is missing; but where all of it or most of it is missing, even after the fact he has not fulfilled. And when one writes the megillah, when he reads one verse, the entire megillah is still missing; and so too when he writes more. In my humble opinion this is nothing, etc. From where did he know to challenge, etc.? That is to say, the questioner certainly did not challenge from the force of the baraisa of blurred letters mentioned above, because it follows perforce that where most or all is missing it is invalid; if so, the Gemara should have brought the baraisa. And from Rabbi Chelbo there is no proof, for one can say that even if most is missing it is valid after the fact. Rather, one must say that since it did not bring the baraisa, and from Rabbi Chelbo too there is no proof, it challenged from reasoning, that where most is missing it is reasonable that even after the fact he has not fulfilled. About this Magen Avraham wrote: if so, it should not have brought the words of Rabbi Chelbo; this is how the text should read. Since from Rabbi Chelbo there is no difficulty, only from reasoning.
