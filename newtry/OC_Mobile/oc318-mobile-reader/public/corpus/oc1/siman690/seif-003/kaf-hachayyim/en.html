11) [Seif 3] He must read all of it. For the Gemara decided the halacha according to the one who says all of it. Beit Yosef.
12) Ibid. One must read all of it. It is clear that the reading is indispensable, even regarding a single verse; if he did not read it, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Mateh Yehudah. It is explained in Responsa Rashba, siman 467, that even if the reader omitted one word, or a listener did not hear even one word, he has not fulfilled his obligation; see there. So too wrote Magen Avraham, s.k. 15; Eshel Avraham, os 76. See further below, os 16 and os 82.
13) Ibid. And from a written text. It is explained in the Gemara that even if he reads every verse from a Megillah, but the Megillah was not valid, this is also considered reading by heart, and he has not fulfilled his obligation. Mishna Berurah, os 6.
14) Ibid. And l'chatchilah it must be written entirely before him. Not even one letter may be missing from it. Chayei Adam, klal 155, os 16.
15) Ibid. But b'dieved, etc. If he has no other Megillah, he may read from it l'chatchilah. Mishna Berurah, os 8.
16) Ibid. If the scribe omitted, etc. It appears to me that one need read them by heart only when the omitted letters change the meaning of the reading. For example, in the verse, 'And these days of Purim shall not pass,' if the word 'not' was omitted, the meaning of the reading is impaired; likewise the mem of 'and gifts to the poor,' and similar cases. But if he omitted other letters or other words that do not impair the meaning of the reading, he has fulfilled his obligation even though he did not read them at all. Riaz, cited by Shiltei HaGibborim in the first chapter of Megillah; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos HaTur; Mishbetzos Zahav, s.k. 4. However, the Magen Avraham wrote there regarding the words of the Riaz mentioned above that below, seif 14, it does not appear so. So too, the Pri Chadash wrote below in seif 14 that whenever anything at all is omitted from its reading, even one word, he has not fulfilled his obligation. He wrote concerning the words of the Riaz mentioned above that they are not convincing; see there. So too was written above, os 12.
17) Since not everyone is proficient in determining what impairs the meaning of the reading, and all the more so because some say that one must always go back, therefore, whenever he omitted even one letter, he should go back and read without a blessing. If he omitted something and knows that it impairs the meaning of the reading, he should go back and read with a blessing. Accordingly, if he read, 'And Mordechai sat,' etc., or, 'And Haman fell,' even if he already finished the entire Megillah, he must go back and read it without a blessing. Chayei Adam, klal 155, os 16. However, regarding what he wrote, 'If he omitted something and knows, etc., he should go back and read with a blessing,' one must be concerned for a possible blessing in vain, for perhaps he fulfills his obligation according to the opinion mentioned above. Rather, he should only go back and read without a blessing. Likewise, in every matter where he is uncertain whether he fulfilled his obligation, he should go back and read without a blessing.
18) Ibid. Even up to half of it, etc. This implies that it is invalid only when the majority is missing, but if half is missing it is valid. Such is the view of the Rashba, Ran, Meiri, and Tur; see there. It is possible that they hold as the Tur wrote in siman 678 in the name of his brother, R' Yehudah, regarding an esrog; see there. However, the Rambam's view is that if half is missing it is invalid, for he holds like the Ra'avad there. Erekh HaShulchan, os 3. Such is also the view of Orchos Chaim and the Ra'ah, in accordance with the Rambam, that if half is missing it is invalid, as Birkei Yosef wrote in os 5; see there. Therefore, one should be stringent.
