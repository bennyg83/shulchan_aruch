As for what he asked, that the Gemara's challenge is appropriate, in Megillah 17a and 18b, regarding reading by heart, and the Sages taught that if part is missing, etc., and there is the statement of Rabbi Chelbo, and it challenges the case of if he writes it, etc.; see there and in Beis Yosef. Since "he writes it" implies from the beginning, if so he has not fulfilled, for until halfway the majority is not written, and in such a case it is invalid even in the middle, as it concludes and as the Mechaber wrote here, that up to half is valid, but more than that is not. And if you will say that after he wrote half, he wrote one additional verse, so that most is now written, and he fulfills by reading the second half: this is not so, for certainly he must read all of it after the fact, and whenever he omitted one verse he has not fulfilled. Rather, wherever most is written and he completes the reading by heart, it is as written regarding megillah; but here, where the reading until half was done invalidly, because most was missing, how can he afterward fulfill with the first half? I saw that the Pri Chadash answered that he fulfills with the second half, and his intent is that the reason he does not fulfill with the second half is because he had not yet written the final verse. Once the final verse is written he fulfills. But what can you say? That the reading is not validated retroactively. The same applies to the first half: through what should it become validated? For that which the minority may be by heart applies when the majority is already written. This requires analysis.
And know. The Mechaber here wrote the language of the Tur: but if more than half of it is missing, it is invalid. This implies that if half is missing, it is valid, unlike the Rambam, of blessed memory, from whose words it implies that at half it is invalid. In the Gemara, 18b, it contrasts that which the Sages taught, that if it was missing it is valid, with that which was taught, that if it was missing it is invalid, and answers: this is where part is missing, this is where all is missing. Both the Rambam and the Tur explain that "all" is not precise and means most; one reads "all" as most. The Rambam, of blessed memory, explains: if all is present, meaning most is present, it is valid, but if only part is present, it is invalid. The Tur explains the opposite: if all is missing, meaning most is missing, it is invalid, but if part, even half, is missing, it is valid. What Beis Yosef wrote, that so it is in the Tur, meaning from the language "all," whose explanation is most, in this the Rambam and the Tur are equal. See Be'er HaGolah, os 8, and what he wrote there, "and according to the explanation of the Rambam, of blessed memory," is difficult; see Pri Chadash. According to the Rashba and Orchos Chaim, one could have answered regarding the two baraisos: this one is at the beginning, this one is in the middle, or where a complete matter is missing; and see Pri Chadash, who sensed this; see there. However, the distinction from reasoning is that whenever most is missing, it is like all is missing according to the Tur's explanation, and it is not because of the contradiction between the baraisos. I will explain further: the Mechaber wrote that initially all of it must be before him, and after the fact, if the scribe omitted in the middle, etc.; he did not conclude regarding the beginning and end. The Rav is the one who added this, for Beis Yosef was uncertain about it. Therefore in Shulchan Aruch he wrote what was clear to him, that in the middle, if he omitted a minority, it is valid, while regarding the beginning and end, and omitting the beginning and end, the Rashba's question is not so compelling. See Magen Avraham on this, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. Be'er HaGolah, os 6, marked the statement of Rabbi Chelbo in the name of Rabbi Chama in the name of Rav. According to the Rashba, that statement of Rav is after the fact regarding omission of the beginning, while from the baraisa where the Sages taught that if the scribe omitted and he read like an interpreter, he fulfilled, which is after the fact and not initially, he should have marked it.
