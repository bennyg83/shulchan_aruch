Seif katan 5. "To include that of Rav Chelbo in the name of Rav Acha." That is to say, the Keneses HaGedolah wondered about the Rashba's proofs that if the beginning is missing, even a minority, it is invalid. For if not, the baraisa, which says that if he omitted up to half it is valid, would be difficult according to Rav Chelbo. And one cannot answer that Rav Chelbo was speaking initially, for this is not so: if so, why does the Gemara challenge the mishnah of "if he was writing it, etc."? For when he reads the first verse that he wrote, most of it is still missing, and where most is missing he certainly has not fulfilled. The Taz in seif katan 3 also raised this type of difficulty. About this Magen Avraham wrote that this is not so, for the Rashba proved well: if it were so that there is room to explain that Rav Chelbo spoke only initially, but after the fact one has fulfilled, then even regarding where most is missing there is no proof from Rav Chelbo to invalidate after the fact. Perhaps he spoke only initially, that it must be written in its entirety, while after the fact even where most is missing it is valid. I would then have no proof that where most is missing it is invalid except from the baraisa, which teaches that if the scribe omitted up to half, he has fulfilled, from which one can prove that more than half he has not fulfilled. If so, the Gemara should not have brought Rav Chelbo in the name of Rav Chama; it should have brought the baraisa, from which it is proven that specifically up to half he has fulfilled, but otherwise not. And although one can reject this and say that there is no proof from the baraisa, for one can say that the same law applies if he omitted more than half and needed to read it by heart, unlike this case, where he reads all the verses from the writing, except that at the time of reading it was not all written before him, nevertheless from Rav Chama as well nothing is proven, for one can say that he spoke only initially. Only if you say that the Gemara's challenge is based on reasoning, that with more than half there is no distinction between reading each verse from the writing or reading by heart, then in any case it should not have brought Rav Chama at all. Therefore the Rashba proved well that the Gemara explains that Rav Chama spoke after the fact. However, in my humble opinion, even without this, the difficulty against the Rashba is not difficult at all. He proved well that Rav Chama spoke after the fact, for if it were so that Rav Chama spoke initially, what does the Gemara challenge from the mishnah? Granted, when he first wrote the megillah at the beginning he did not fulfill, since most of it was missing. Nevertheless, once he reached half, from there onward most of it was written before him, and let us say that he fulfilled by reading the second majority of the megillah while most of it was written before him, and the first half that he read is no worse than if he had omitted it and read it by heart. Rather, perforce, if he omitted its beginning, even if he did not omit most, he has not fulfilled; and the same applies to omitting its end. See at the end of the book, in the collected writings of Pri Chadash, what he wrote on this. The reference mark.
