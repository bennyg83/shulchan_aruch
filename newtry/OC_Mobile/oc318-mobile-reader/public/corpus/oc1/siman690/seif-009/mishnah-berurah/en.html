27) If it was written in translation, etc. Even if the script was also Ashuris script, one who does not know the language has not fulfilled.
28) But if it was written, etc. That is to say, when do we say that he fulfills because he knows the language? Specifically if he reads in the language written in the megillah. But if he translates this language into another language, even though he knows both languages together, he has not fulfilled. The reason is as below.
29) In Hebrew script. This means in the Hebrew language.
30) And he read it in Aramaic for an Aramaic-speaker. It teaches us that although the megillah is before him, and he examines each word and translates it for the listener, and one might have thought this is better than reading by heart, it teaches that even in this case he has not fulfilled.
31) He has not fulfilled. This means the listener. Since the reader read it by heart, and it is not considered reading even for himself, the listener is no better than he is.
32) But one need not be concerned, etc. That is to say, we are not particular that it be specifically the script of the language that he is reading. Even if the language was Egyptian and the letters were written in Aramaic script, or the like, it is also permitted. Nevertheless, we require that the reader know that script, for otherwise it is included in reading by heart, since he does not know it. But several poskim disagree and hold that a megillah in any language is not valid to read from, even for a foreign-language speaker, unless its script is Ashuris; see Biur Halacha. Peri Megadim wrote: if it was written in the script and language of translation or the like, and the reader does not understand the translation, and he read it for a listener who understands the language of translation, he does not fulfill his obligation even according to the Mechaber. So too, if the reader understands that language and it is written before him in that language, but the listener does not understand that language, the listener does not fulfill, for since he does not understand, there is no remembrance of the miracle for the listener. However, nowadays the custom is to read everything in the Holy Tongue and to write it in the script of the sacred letters, Levush.
