Section 9: If it was, etc. The Rambam ruled that Greek foreign language is valid for everyone, like Rav and Shmuel. The Rif omitted what is written there, that Greek foreign language is valid for everyone. See the Rosh there, who wrote the reason he omitted it: because they hold like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and we do not rule this way, but like the Sages. His words are astonishing and at first glance are not understandable, for Rabbi Yochanan ruled in the first chapter there, 9b, like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Rather, one can say that we do not rule like Rav and Shmuel, who ruled like him even regarding megillah; and likewise the plain flow of the sugya there, 9a, is unlike Rav and Shmuel, for it says there: if it is Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, there is, etc.; rather, it is not difficult: here with megillah, etc. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel came to be stringent. So too Rav Papa and Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak hold there. And that which Rabbi Yochanan ruled that the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel is because Rabbi Yochanan holds that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagrees only regarding other books, but not regarding megillah. See Milchamos. The Ran rejected his words and wrote that they omitted it because Greek has already been forgotten and its memory lost, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchos Sefer Torah. So too the Rambam ruled here like Rav and Shmuel, and so ruled the Baal HaMaor; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that many poskim ruled this way. Nevertheless, the words of the Ramban are correct, for certainly the sugya there on 9a is unlike Rav and Shmuel, as above. So too in the Yerushalmi there: what is the difference between books and Megillas Esther? The books may be written in any language, but Megillas Esther may be written only in Ashuris. But the Rambam holds like Rav Ashi there, as Tosafos wrote there under "here, etc.," and we rule like Rav Ashi.
