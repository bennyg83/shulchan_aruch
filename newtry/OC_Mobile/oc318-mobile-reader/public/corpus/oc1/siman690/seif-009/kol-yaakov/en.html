1) Seif 9 in the gloss: But one need not be concerned in which script it is written. So too is the opinion of Maran, as is proven in Beis Yosef, and he wrote similarly in Yoreh Deah siman 488. Rather, Maran used the language of the Rambam, as is his way, for such are the words of the Rambam, Hilchos Megillah, chapter 2, halachah 4. So too is the opinion of the Rashba, brought by Maamar Mordechai in os 6, and so too is the opinion of the Radbaz in Leshonos HaRambam, siman 126. But according to the Riaz, brought by Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 2 of Megillah, and by the Tashbetz in responsa, part 1, siman 8, and according to the Tosafos Yom Tov and Pri Chadash, os 9, they hold that although they validated other languages for the megillah, the writing must be Ashuris, which is the script of the Holy Tongue, and not other scripts. Regarding practical law, the main view appears to be like the view of Maran and those with him, that it is valid. So wrote the author of Chikrei Lev, part 1, siman 134; Erech HaShulchan, os 9; and Beis Oved, os 16. However, initially one should be concerned for the words of Riaz, Tashbetz, Tosafos Yom Tov, and Pri Chadash, not to fulfill one's obligation except with a megillah whose writing is Ashuris script and Ashuris language. So wrote Chikrei Lev there and Beis Oved there. And what Biur HaGra, os 20, wrote on the words of the gloss, that it is an error, is astonishing, for great poskim hold this way: the Rashbam, the Rashba, Maran, and the Radbaz. And as for what he challenged from the mishnah, "until it is written Ashuris, etc.," see what Tosafos Yom Tov, Shiyarei Keneses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur os 1 in siman 691, Shulchan Gavoah os 16, Maamar Mordechai there, Erech HaShulchan there, and Chikrei Lev there answered.
2) Some say that this rule, that even in another script it is valid, applies specifically when it is also written in another language. But if he writes it in the Holy Tongue, he must write it specifically in Ashuris script, and if he wrote the language in the Holy Tongue but the script is not Ashuris, it is invalid. This is the Radbaz in Leshonos HaRambam, siman 126, and Beis Oved, os 17, wrote similarly in the name of Chasdei David. But Maran in Beis Yosef rejected this view and wrote that we are particular only that it not be written in another language, so that it should not be like one reading by heart; but whenever it is written in that language, even if it is written in another script, we have no problem with it. Beis Oved wrote there that the view of Maran is primary.
