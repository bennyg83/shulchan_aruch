48) He must intend, etc., and he must, etc. This is indispensable even after the fact. This intent is before he begins to read: one should intend for the entire reading, this one to fulfill and this one to discharge, and afterward he need not intend throughout the entire reading. The listener must turn his ear and hear every single word from the reader's mouth. If the reader omitted even one word, and likewise if the listener missed hearing one word, they have not fulfilled, and he must go back.
49) Presumably his intent, etc. Regarding the listeners, the law changes: those standing in the synagogue are presumed to intend, but one passing behind the synagogue must explicitly intend to fulfill his obligation, Pri Chadash.
50) We are not exacting regarding its errors. This law is not speaking regarding writing, for there the law is as in section 3. Here it speaks regarding reading, meaning even if he read it with several errors, since in any case he read all of it.
51) Another. For example, if instead of "sitting" he read "sat," or instead of "fallen" he read "fell," for this is like skipping that word completely, and he must go back and read it in order.
