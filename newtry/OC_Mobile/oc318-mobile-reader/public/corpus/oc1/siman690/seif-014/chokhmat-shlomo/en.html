There, section 14. "The one who reads the megillah must intend to discharge the listener, etc." Note: See Magen Avraham, seif katan 15, who proved that even if the listener did not hear one word, he has not fulfilled, and he wrote that this is not implied in the Bach. This implies that the Bach holds that if the listener did not hear one word, he has fulfilled. In my humble opinion there appears to be a proof for the Bach from the Yerushalmi, and this is its language: It was taught: we are not exacting regarding its errors. Rabbi Yitzchak bar Abba, etc., were sitting before Rav; one said one form of "Jews" and one said another form of "Jews," and he did not make either of them go back; end quote. Beis Yosef in this siman asked that it should have said that he did not make the one who read the longer form go back, but the one who read the regular form read correctly, so why would there be a need to make him go back? He forced his answer. The Korban HaEdah was also forced in this, and his words too are astonishing. Granted, there is a place in the megillah where the longer spelling is written; nevertheless, the reading in every place is the regular form, and if so he read properly, for even in the Torah we follow the read form. Therefore the intent of the Yerushalmi appears to be that it teaches two things: it speaks where the one who read the longer form had a valid megillah, and the one who read the regular form had a Chumash or an invalid megillah. It is explicit in Shulchan Aruch that it is forbidden to assist the cantor from a Chumash, and the later authorities, brought in Magen Avraham, wrote that perhaps the listener will set his mind on this one who reads from the Chumash. Magen Avraham wrote that the reader himself may listen even though he is assisting. It also appears that even regarding others, this is only a precaution initially; after the fact, since we hold that regarding megillah even ten may read at once, because it is beloved they set their minds to it and hear. Nevertheless, it appears that this is only if their reading is the same. But where it is not the same, it is forbidden. According to the initial assumption there in Rosh Hashanah that two voices from two people are not heard, and although the conclusion is that they are heard, that is specifically there, where the one who blows with the trumpets knows that the mitzvah of the day is with the shofar and sets his mind upon it. But this one who reads the regular form thinks that the reader of the longer form is reading in error; if so, one does not set his heart on an error, and he did not set his mind on it. Therefore even he himself has not fulfilled. Either way, he did not set his mind on the regular form of the second reader, and his own reading was from an invalid scroll. Therefore the Yerushalmi teaches that he did not make either of them go back, because the one who read the longer form, even though if the reader omitted a word he has not fulfilled, nevertheless in this case we are not exacting regarding an error; and the second, who read the regular form, even though it was from an invalid scroll, is only a listener, and regarding a listener, if he omitted a word there is no objection. It therefore teaches one case that contains two points, and it works well. From this there is proof for the opinion of the Bach. Analyze this well.
