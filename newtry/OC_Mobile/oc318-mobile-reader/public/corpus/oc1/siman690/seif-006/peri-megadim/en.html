Although. See Taz. In the Gemara: just as with women and unlearned people, and "the couriers." See Ran, who proved from here that anyone who skipped one word and did not say it at all by heart has not fulfilled even after the fact. For if not, what proof is there from "the couriers," which we do not understand, and it would be like after the fact, etc.? Therefore every God-fearing person should have a valid megillah in his hand and read word by word quietly, for it is impossible to hear everything from the prayer leader, especially when the children confuse matters, as I wrote above. Know that what the Mechaber wrote, that a foreign-language speaker who heard the megillah in the Holy Tongue and in the sacred script, is the language of the Rambam, of blessed memory, in chapter 2 of Megillah, halachah 3. The Tur wrote only "in the Holy Tongue." In the mishnah of one who reads out of order, it concludes: until it is written Ashuris and in ink. See Tosafos Yom Tov and Pri Chadash on this. According to what Beis Yosef wrote, that if it is written in a foreign language, even though the script is not special to that language, he has fulfilled, and it also does not require Ashuris script, the same law would apply to Egyptian script and Aramaic language, that he has fulfilled. If so, for what need did the mishnah require Ashuris script? And the Gemara concludes on 19a that it is written "according to their script and according to their times." See Pri Chadash whether the verse is "according to their script and according to their language" or "according to their script and according to their times." In our novellae we wrote about this. Therefore we are forced to say that since ordinarily one writes in the Holy Tongue, with which everyone fulfills even one who does not understand the Holy Tongue, and now we do not know the meaning of the word "the couriers," as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Rivash, of blessed memory, in a responsum, and although Beis Yosef found a remedy, to write in a foreign language and read "the couriers" in the Holy Tongue, that remedy is only after the fact, for initially one must read all of it from writing. However, if he wrote all of it in a foreign language and "the couriers" in the Holy Tongue, initially it is proper according to Beis Yosef, who holds that it may be written in two languages. Nevertheless, the mishnah properly said Ashuris for the whole world, who do not understand and fulfill with Ashuris; likewise we require Ashuris script for this, as stated. What Tosafos Yom Tov and Pri Chadash wrote is therefore settled. But in truth, in other languages, for one who understands that language, even though it is written in the script of that language, he fulfills, as we wrote. With Hashem's help, it will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
