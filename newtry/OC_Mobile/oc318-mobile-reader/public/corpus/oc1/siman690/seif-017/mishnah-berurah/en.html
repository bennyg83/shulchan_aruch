55) That the reader, etc. But the individuals listening with megillos do not need to spread them out, Bach and Maharil.
56) And spreads it out like a letter. This means that he spreads it all out and does not leave it rolled; rather, he folds it sheet upon sheet. He should be careful not to drag it on the ground, Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Chadash. See Peri Megadim, who wrote that this is not specifically on the ground; the same applies if it hangs over the table or lectern, for this too is a disgrace to sacred writings. He wrote further that the spreading should be before he begins to bless, so that there should be no interruption between the blessing and the reading.
57) He goes back and rolls all of it and blesses. That is to say, he rolls all of it and places it before him on the bimah, and afterward blesses. The reason is that it is disgraceful for the megillah to be lying that way. Even if he began the blessing and said "Blessed are You," since he has not said Hashem's Name, he stops in the middle, rolls it, and afterward blesses.
58) Aloud. Because of joy. And the reason he goes back and reads those verses is to make them heard to the congregation, for the mitzvah initially is to hear the entire megillah from the writing.
59) That they strike Haman. Maharil was not particular to strike Haman. The Gaon Yaavetz writes about his father, the Chacham Tzvi, that he would strike, stamp with his foot, and slap with his sandal when he reached the mention of Haman. Peri Megadim wrote that their gain is offset by their loss, for they cause much confusion.
60) When they read the megillah in the synagogue. The cantor must be silent then, while they strike, so that everyone will hear his reading. Because mistakes through the striking are very common, and the children are accustomed to strike several times while the cantor goes back to read, therefore it is good and proper that each individual read a verse or two from the Chumash while they strike Haman, for then, even if he does not hear from the cantor, he has fulfilled, as written in section 3, Magen Avraham. So too Peri Megadim wrote that it is very proper that each person hold a Chumash during the reading, and every word that he does not hear from the cantor he should read from the Chumash, Magen Avraham.
