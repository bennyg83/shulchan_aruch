"They wrote further that the children had the custom to form an image of Haman on pieces of wood and stones, etc., and from this the custom spread that they strike Haman when the megillah is read in the synagogue, etc." Note: See Magen Avraham, seif katan 19, who wrote what he understood from Maharil, that he was not concerned to strike Haman, etc. In my humble opinion this has a source in the Gemara in the first chapter of Megillah, where Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak began that passage from here: "Had Hashem not been with us, let Israel now say; had Hashem not been with us when a man rose against us," a man and not a king. At first glance, what does he need with this, and who does not know that Haman was an ordinary person and not a king? But it appears based on the words of the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 of Megillah, and this is its language there: Rav said: one must say, "Cursed is Haman, cursed are his sons." Rabbi Pinchas said: one must say also, "And Charvonah is remembered for good." Rav Berechyah, etc., when he would reach that verse, "whom Nevuchadnezzar exiled," would say, "May Nevuchadnezzar's bones be ground," for every mention of Nevuchadnezzar in Yirmiyah refers to when he was alive, but here he was dead; end quote of the Yerushalmi. Korban HaEdah in Shirei Korban brought in the name of some who explain that it does not distinguish between Haman before his hanging and afterward, and that it disagrees with Rabbi Yochanan nearby. He wrote that he did not know what forced him to this, for Rav's intent can also be after they read the megillah, after Haman was hanged; see there. I say that I do not know what forced both of them to this, for one case is not comparable to the other. Perforce the intent of Rabbi Yochanan is that he did not say this during Nevuchadnezzar's lifetime but after his death he would say it, because during his lifetime one must apportion honor to royalty, but after his death one need not apportion him honor. So it is in the Midrash on Parashas Vaera, on the verse, "and He commanded them to the children of Israel and to Pharaoh king of Egypt," to apportion honor to royalty; see there. If so, according to this it works well: this is reasonable for Nevuchadnezzar, who was a king, but Haman was not a king, so one need not apportion him honor. Therefore even during his lifetime it is permitted to say, "Cursed is Haman." This is what Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak teaches: "Had Hashem not been with us, etc., when a man rose against us," a man and not a king, and one need not apportion him honor. It is therefore proper to say immediately, "Cursed is Haman." But since it is forbidden to interrupt with speech during the megillah, they strike him, which is not an interruption. If so, this is based on the law of the Gemara and the Yerushalmi, to say even during his lifetime, "Cursed is Haman," and this striking is in place of saying it.
