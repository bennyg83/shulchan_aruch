And he spreads it like a letter. Even the portion that he has not yet read. Maharil, siman 56. When the chazzan read and Maharil held a valid Megillah in order to follow, he kept it rolled. Bach.
And he rolls all of it. He places it in its place and then recites the blessing. This differs from siman 284, seif 6, where the Haftarah is not removed until after its blessing, because here the blessing does not refer to the Megillah itself. Maharil states that he understood from Maharil that he was not concerned to strike at Haman’s name. In any event, when people strike at Haman’s name, the chazzan must remain silent so that everyone can hear his reading. Each individual should read one or two verses from a chumash while the striking continues, for then even if he does not hear them from the chazzan he fulfills his obligation, as stated in seif 3.
And he recites the blessing. If he wishes, he may recite the blessing first and then roll it.
That the children customarily, etc. Levush writes that when Haman is mentioned one should say, “The name of the wicked shall rot.” See Rabbah in Megillah concerning Nevuchadnetzar.
And the custom should not be abolished, etc. Rama writes in responsum 21, citing Maharik, shoresh 144, that a custom found in any posek should not be abolished. Even under pressure one should not change a custom, as the Sages said regarding the people of Beisan. Even if the custom contains an element of prohibition it is not abolished, and even a local custom can override the ordinary ruling. If circumstances have changed from those existing in earlier times, however, the custom may be changed in accordance with present conditions. Maharaash, in his commentary to Semag, writes that the principle that custom overrides halachah applies even when most opinions prohibit and the Talmud supports them, provided that the custom rests upon the Pesikta or other early works, for our ancestors certainly received it through an authentic tradition. This applies specifically to a custom established by venerable authorities. A custom having no Torah basis is merely an error in judgment. A decree enacted by formal vote requires another formal vote to permit it even when its reason is known and has ceased. One beis din cannot annul the enactment of another unless it is greater in wisdom and number. A measure enacted only because of a concern does not require a new vote once that concern has passed, as explained in Yoreh Deah 116 and Tosafos in Beitzah. Tosafos in Yevamos 79 write that in monetary matters a later court may annul the words of an earlier court because ownerless property declared by beis din is ownerless. At first glance Gittin 36 regarding prozbul appears otherwise, though there the matter also has a prohibitory aspect because people would refrain from lending. Tosafos there add that an enactment whose prohibition did not spread among most of Israel may be annulled. See the end of siman 15 and Nachalas Shivah for many laws of customs.
