And spreads out. This applies specifically to the prayer leader; the listeners who have a megillah do not need to spread it out. Bach and Maharil; see Magen Avraham.
And rolls it. He places it in its place and afterwards blesses; Maagalei Tzedek, Mateh Moshe, Maharil. If he wishes, he may bless and afterwards roll it; Keneses HaGedolah, Seder HaYom. Maharil wrote: when he says "on that night the king's sleep was disturbed," he should raise his voice, for there is the essence of the miracle. When he says "this letter," he should shake the megillah; Mateh Moshe.
That they strike. Maharil was not particular to strike Haman. In Levush he wrote that when they say Haman, one should say, "the name of the wicked shall rot." In any case, when they strike Haman, the cantor must be silent so that everyone will hear his reading. Each individual should read a verse or two from the Chumash while they are striking Haman, for then, even if he does not hear from the cantor, he has fulfilled, as written in section 3.
Custom. Rama wrote in responsa, siman 19, in the name of Maharik, root 144, that if a custom is found in any posek, one should not abolish it, and even in pressing circumstances one should not change a custom. Even if there is some side of prohibition in the custom, one should not abolish it, as Maharik wrote in root 9. Even regarding the custom of one place, we say that it nullifies the law. However, if the matter changed from how it was in the time of the early authorities, they may change the custom according to the time; end quote. Mahara Ashkenazi, in his glosses to Sefer Mitzvos Gadol, wrote that what we say, that custom uproots law, means that even if most opinions hold that it is forbidden and the Talmud supports them, but the custom is built on the basis of the Pesikta or external books, we say that custom uproots law, for certainly our fathers received it from one another, man from man; end quote. See Maharik, roots 9 and 354, and the book Beis Shmuel. This applies specifically to a custom established by veteran authorities, but a custom that has no proof from the Torah is only like one who erred in judgment; Masas Binyamin, chapter 14. A matter that was decreed by a vote, even if the reason for it is known and the reason has ceased, requires another vote to permit it, as in the Gemara, first chapter of Beitzah. A court cannot nullify the matter of another court unless it is greater than it in wisdom and number. But in every matter that is only because of a concern, and the concern has passed, another vote is not needed, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 116, and in Tosafos in Beitzah; see there. In Yevamos, page 78, they wrote that in monetary matters they can nullify the words of another court because property declared ownerless by the court is ownerless. See there in Tosafos, that a matter whose prohibition did not spread among most of Israel can be nullified; see there. See Magen Avraham, and see what Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 9. See the book Nachalas Shivah for many laws regarding customs, and see the book Beis Shmuel.
