Section 17: The custom. Because it is called a letter, as stated there, 19a. See what I wrote in siman 691, section 2.
And when he finishes, etc. There, 32a, Tosafos under "he rolls it." But afterward, etc.
He goes back and rolls it, etc. This is unlike Magen Avraham in the name of Seder HaYom. See Keneses HaGedolah, that there is no substance to his words. This is the language in Maharil: Once, the reader began the blessing "HaE-l HaRav" after the reading before he had rolled the megillah-letter, and Maharil rebuked him and silenced him until they rolled the megillah-letter into one roll and placed it before him; then he went back, began, and blessed. So too the language is: he goes back and rolls it and blesses; end quote. The author of Magen Avraham did not see the book of Maharil, only what was written in Keneses HaGedolah, as is known, and the distinction of Seder HaYom has no dawn.
Some wrote, etc. Hagahos Maimoniyos and Mordechai wrote that this is because of joy and to awaken the children so that they hear the reading of the megillah. In Sotah 30b, Rabbi Nechemiah says: like a scribe who leads Shema, etc. The Geonim wrote: from here they practiced saying verses of redemption, the whole congregation together, such as in the blessing of Shema, where the prayer leader discharges them, and when he reaches "Who is like You" and "May He reign," everyone says them together aloud. It is not forbidden because of written words being recited by heart, as written in Taanis 28a: Rabbi Yosef said, but can an individual, etc.? Rather, all enter and read it by heart, like one who recites Shema.
They wrote further. In the Midrash: "You shall surely erase the memory of Amalek," even from wood and stones.
