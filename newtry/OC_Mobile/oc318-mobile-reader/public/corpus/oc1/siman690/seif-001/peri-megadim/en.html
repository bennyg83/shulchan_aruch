Whether. See Taz. In the Ran the text is: standing or sitting, he has fulfilled; if two read it, they have fulfilled. So too it is in the Tur, and he holds that the "they have fulfilled" of the latter clause is by way of the first clause. The Taz resolves this for the Rambam, of blessed memory, that in this matter there is a distinction between an individual and a congregation. Know that one can say from what the Mechaber wrote here, like the Rambam, of blessed memory, that initially one must read publicly while standing because of the honor of the congregation. If so, perforce regarding the Torah, even after the fact he has not fulfilled while seated, and must go back and read standing. For in Megillah 21, regarding that mishnah, it says: The Tanna taught: this is not so with the Torah. And if in a congregation, regarding megillah, he must initially stand, while as an individual, even regarding Torah when he reviews his weekly portions, he need not stand, then this requires analysis according to what Magen Avraham wrote in 141:1, that after the fact he has fulfilled; see there, and see what I wrote there. In the book Chemed Moshe he asked from what is written here, that after the fact he has fulfilled, while women, even after the fact, do not discharge others; see siman 684 in Magen Avraham 5. One can answer that the honor of the congregation is not comparable in each case, and women do not discharge even after the fact. See Magen Avraham 141, regarding what he wrote from the king: if it were so that even after the fact he has not fulfilled, how does the honor of the king help? One can say that this is because from the Torah we have no reading at all, only the enactment of the Prophets, and the verse "stand with Me" is an asmachta; the Sages waived it for a king, etc. See siman 685. And rely on what I wrote at the beginning of the chapter.
