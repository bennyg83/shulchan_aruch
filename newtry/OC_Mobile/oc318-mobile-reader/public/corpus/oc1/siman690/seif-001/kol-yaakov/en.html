1) Section 9 in the gloss: But one need not be concerned in which script it is written. So too is the opinion of Maran, as is proven in Beis Yosef, and he wrote similarly in Yoreh Deah siman 488. Rather, Maran used the language of the Rambam, as is his way, for such are the words of the Rambam, Hilchos Megillah, chapter 2, halachah 4. So too is the opinion of the Rashba, brought by Maamar Mordechai in os 6, and so too is the opinion of the Radbaz in Leshonos HaRambam, siman 126. But according to the Riaz, brought by Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 2 of Megillah, and by the Tashbetz in responsa, part 1, siman 8, and according to the Tosafos Yom Tov and Pri Chadash, os 9, they hold that although they validated other languages for the megillah, the writing must be Ashuris, which is the script of the Holy Tongue, and not other scripts. Regarding practical law, the main view appears to be like the view of Maran and those with him, that it is valid. So wrote the author of Chikrei Lev, part 1, siman 134; Erech HaShulchan, os 9; and Beis Oved, os 16. However, initially one should be concerned for the words of Riaz, Tashbetz, Tosafos Yom Tov, and Pri Chadash, not to fulfill one's obligation except with a megillah whose writing is Ashuris script and Ashuris language. So wrote Chikrei Lev there and Beis Oved there. And what Biur HaGra, os 20, wrote on the words of the gloss, that it is an error, is astonishing, for great poskim hold this way: the Rashbam, the Rashba, Maran, and the Radbaz. And as for what he challenged from the mishnah, "until it is written Ashuris, etc.," see what Tosafos Yom Tov, Shiyarei Keneses HaGedolah, Hagahos Tur os 1 in siman 691, Shulchan Gavoah os 16, Maamar Mordechai there, Erech HaShulchan there, and Chikrei Lev there answered.
