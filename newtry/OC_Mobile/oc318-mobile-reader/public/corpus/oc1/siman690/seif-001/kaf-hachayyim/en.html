1) [Seif 1] A person may read the Megillah whether standing or sitting. This implies that even l'chatchilah he may read it while sitting, as the Tur wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi. So too wrote the Maggid Mishneh, and so ruled the Ran; this is the primary ruling. Bach.
2) Ibid. Whether standing or sitting. The blessing should be recited while standing. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Eshel Avraham, os 1. One who listens is considered as though he responds and as though he recites the blessing; therefore, the listener must stand like the one reciting the blessing. Machatzis HaShekel. So too wrote Beit Oved, os 9. So too wrote Rema MiPano, siman 102, that the blessing over the Megillah is recited while standing, and such is the common practice; see there. This implies that whether one reads for the congregation or reads privately in his home, the blessings must be recited while standing. So too wrote Eshel Avraham there; Beit Oved there; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Tetzaveh, os 4. However, Knesset HaGedolah, in Hagahos HaTur, siman 292, cited the words of Rema MiPano mentioned above and wrote: We have not practiced this way. So too wrote Tefillah LeDavid, 86a; Orah V'Simchah, 14a and 14b; Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 31, os 76; and Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, os 2, who wrote that their custom is for the one reciting the blessing, who is the shaliach tzibbur, to bless and read the Megillah entirely while standing, but the congregation did not customarily stand even during the blessing; see there. Such is also the custom of Beit El, may it be rebuilt and established speedily in our days, that the congregation does not stand even during the blessing.
3) An individual who reads while sitting, if he is in his home, also recites the blessing while sitting, for the blessing follows the manner in which the mitzvah is performed, as Rav Shemen HaMaor wrote above in siman 8, 3c, and in this siman, 53d; see there at length. Yafeh LaLev there.
4) Ibid. But he should not read publicly while sitting, etc. This implies that the concern applies only to sitting and not to leaning. Mateh Yehudah. The Mishna Berurah cited his words in Biur Halacha and wrote that in a pressing circumstance one may rely upon this to be lenient; see there.
5) Ibid. Because of the honor of the congregation. If the congregation wishes to waive its honor, it may do so, unlike with the reading of the Torah. Mateh Yehudah.
6) Ibid., Rama's gloss. It is forbidden for the chazzan to read, etc. This refers to a place where the custom is that the chazzan does not regularly read the Megillah. In our countries, however, where the chazzan always reads the Megillah, there is no need for them to tell him to do so, for he was appointed for this purpose from the outset, as written in siman 139, seif 3. This also appears from the Mordechai; see there. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah, os 2; Eshel Avraham, os 2; Mishna Berurah, os 3.
