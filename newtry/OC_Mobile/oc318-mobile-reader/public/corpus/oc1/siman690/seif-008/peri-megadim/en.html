If he read it. See Taz. He explained what Tosafos wrote in Taanis 12b, under "dozing and not dozing," that one must emend, and so too it is in the Ran, of blessed memory, in the chapter of one who reads, regarding dozing; in Megillah both explanations work well, but in Eruvin and Taanis it is impossible to explain it as the end of sleep; see there. The text has a scribal error: it says 11 and should say 12, and in the Ran it should say chapter 2. What the Taz concluded, that regarding the law there is no distinction, means that even at the end, when he awakens from sleep and is dozing and not dozing, he has also fulfilled, unlike the Bach, who ruled as Tosafos wrote, that only at the beginning of sleep is it so, but at the end of sleep he has not fulfilled. Tosafos wrote only that the explanation in Eruvin and Taanis cannot be so, and I do not know why he needed to emend, for even if the text is as before us, he can explain it that way; perhaps by the way he also emended this. What is written in Shulchan Aruch, that if he heard it while dozing he has not fulfilled, is Yerushalmi, as Be'er HaGolah wrote. The reason is that when he reads with his mouth it is proper, unlike hearing, where one can say that he did not hear well while awake and not awake, dozing, etc. However, Beis Yosef brought the Yerushalmi: Menashe was sitting before Rabbi Zeira and dozing. He said to him: go back, for you did not have intent. This means that he was listening, so it implies that the reason is that mitzvos require intent, and although he hears, he does not have proper intent, unlike one who reads with his mouth. This requires some analysis, for one who is dozing does not know how to respond with reasoning, and Rashi, of blessed memory, explained this as understanding of the heart; how then can he have proper intent? See siman 63: regarding mitzvos requiring intent, there are two types of intent, and it is settled.
