If he read it - See Taz. He explained what Tosafot wrote in Taanit 12b, s.v. "nim velo nim," that the text must be emended; and so it is in Ran, chapter HaKorei. One who is dozing: with Megillah both explanations work well, but on the eve of Pesach and in Taanit it is impossible to explain it as the end of sleep; see there. The reference "11" is a scribal error and should be "12," and "Ran, chapter 2" should be corrected. What the Taz concluded, that for practical law there is no distinction, means that even at the end, when he awakens from sleep and is dozing but not dozing, he has also fulfilled his obligation, unlike Bach who ruled according to what Tosafot wrote that this applies specifically at the beginning of sleep, but at the end of sleep he has not fulfilled. Tosafot wrote only that this explanation cannot be used for the eve of Pesach and Taanit. I do not know why he needed to emend it, for even if we read as before us it can be explained so; perhaps incidentally he emended this too. What Shulchan Aruch wrote, that if he heard it while dozing he has not fulfilled his obligation, is from the Yerushalmi, as Beer HaGolah wrote. The reason is that when he reads with his mouth it is proper, but when he hears, one can say he did not hear well while half awake and half asleep. However, Beit Yosef brought the Yerushalmi: Menashe was sitting before R. Zeira and dozing; he said to him, "Go back, for you did not have intent." This means he was listening, so it implies the reason is that mitzvot require intent, and even though he hears, he does not have proper intent, unlike one who reads with his mouth. This requires some study, for one who is dozing and does not know how to answer with reasoning, as Rashi explained, lacks mental comprehension; how can he have proper intent? See siman 63: regarding mitzvot requiring intent, there are two kinds of intent, and with this it is resolved.
