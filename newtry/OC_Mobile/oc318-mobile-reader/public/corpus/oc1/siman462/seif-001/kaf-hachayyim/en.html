1) [Se'if 1] Fruit juice without water, etc. There are many opinions. According to Rashi, dough leavened with fruit juice does not incur kares, but it is hardened, inedible chametz, and it is forbidden to knead even with fruit juice alone. According to Rav Alfasi, one may knead even with fruit juice mixed with water from after the first night onward. Some prohibit kneading with fruit juice mixed with water even after the first night, but permit smoothing the dough with it while shaping from after the first night onward. Rabbeinu Yitzchak explains that fruit juice without water does not cause leavening at all, and one may knead with it after the first night. On the first and second nights, however, one cannot fulfill the matzah obligation with it because it is matzah ashirah. Tur. All this will be explained below. Regarding fruit juice without water, the Shulchan Aruch rules here like Rabbeinu Yitzchak that it does not cause leavening at all. This is the opinion of Rambam, chapter 5, halachos 2 and 20; Rabbeinu Tam in the Gemara there; Rosh, chapter Kol Sha'ah; and Maggid Mishneh there in chapter 5 in the name of the Geonim, as cited by Beis Yosef. See there. Responsa Rashba, simanim 4 and 333, likewise writes that fruit juice without water does not cause leavening at all and is permitted even initially. See there. Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Kol Sha'ah, likewise explains Rif this way. See there. Rashba's statement in siman 454, 'This is also our prohibitive custom,' can be explained as referring specifically to fruit juice mixed with water, or to dough kneaded with water and smoothed with fruit juice. He is discussing Rif, who permits these cases, while also citing the prohibiting opinion. See there. Fruit juice alone, however, is initially permitted, as Rashba writes in simanim 4 and 333. Sefer HaManhig, siman 33 in the section Eilu, however, cites the prohibiting and permitting authorities and concludes: 'There are now three disputes. According to Rashi, it is forbidden to knead even with fruit juice alone, because one violates the prohibition against consuming any leavened substance. According to Rif, even fruit juice mixed with water does not cause leavening. According to Rabbeinu Gershom and Rabbeinu Tam, both kneading and smoothing with fruit juice mixed with water are prohibited. We should not place our heads between these great mountains, and should be stringent according to all opinions.' These are his words. Maharanach, volume 2, siman 74, likewise discusses all the positions at length and concludes that one who fears Heaven should take into account the opinion that fruit juice without water causes leavening. Such a person should not knead with fruit juice without supervision, but should guard the dough and observe every precaution required for dough kneaded with water alone. See there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, cites this and writes: 'People are not careful about this. My father, of blessed memory, was careful not to eat matzah kneaded with fruit juice until the last day of Yom Tov of Pesach, because he was concerned that fruit juice without water causes leavening. Perhaps he acted according to Maharanach's ruling. I adopted his practice. The author of Tomes Yesharim, siman 108, inclines to be stringent not to knead with fruit juice even under supervision, including fruit juice alone without water. See there, where he discusses all the authorities.' These are his words. Peri Chadash, however, cites Maharanach and nevertheless rules like the Shulchan Aruch. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1, similarly cites both sides and writes that the practical law follows the Shulchan Aruch because most authorities and commentators permit it. He writes that this is the custom in all Sephardic cities. See there. Nevertheless, even where the custom permits kneading with fruit juice alone, one should initially take the prohibiting authorities into account and guard it with all the precautions governing dough kneaded with water, since doing what is better can cause no harm. Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 2, writes that his teacher acted this way and that he followed his teacher's practice. See there. The Ashkenazic custom is not to knead with fruit juice at all except in pressing circumstances, as stated below in the gloss to se'if 4. See there.
2) There. Fruit juice, etc. Fruit juice includes wine, milk, honey, olive oil, apple juice, pomegranate juice, and similar wines, oils, and beverages. Rambam, there. Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman writes that even apples and pomegranates that are sour produce liquid classified as fruit juice, contrary to Shiltei HaGibborim. See there. Chok Yaakov, subsection 7; Mekor Chaim, subsection 1; Chok Yosef, subsection 5; Chemed Moshe, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2; Chayei Adam, rule 126, subsection 7, write likewise. We already stated in the preceding subsection that all fruit juices are subject to a dispute whether they cause leavening. Therefore, anyone who fears Heaven should personally be stringent with every type of fruit customarily used for kneading and take every possible precaution to work quickly, satisfying the dissenting opinion. This is certainly required for a substance whose classification as fruit juice is itself disputed, and the point need not be repeated.
3) Vinegar is likewise included among fruit juices. Tosafos, Pesachim 35b; Tur and the Shulchan Aruch below in se'if 6; Chok Yaakov, subsection 7; Peri Chadash below in siman 466, se'if 5; Beis Yehudah, siman 226; Chemed Moshe, subsection 2; Chayei Adam, there; Chikrei Lev, Yoreh De'ah, volume 1, siman 183, page 256b. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 2, however, cites the first Rabbi Yeshayah as prohibiting, but writes that the authorities do not agree, as Maran states in se'if 6. See there. Ruach Chaim, subsection 6, writes that if one kneaded with vinegar after the fact, the dough should not be prohibited, but initially one should not act contrary to Rabbi Yeshayah's prohibition. See there. See below, subsection 60.
4) Orchos Chaim and Kol Bo write in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak that milk is not fruit juice and causes leavening. Beis Yosef's question is readily answered by consulting Baal HaMaor in the chapter Kol Sha'ah, who holds this opinion. Raavad in Sefer Tamim De'im, Ramban in Milchamos, and Ran there, however, demonstrate that milk and other beverages have the same status as wine, oil, and honey. This is also the opinion of Rokeach, siman 281; Rabbeinu Shimshon; Ri'az; Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah; and Tashbetz, page 30. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 3. Beis Yosef likewise writes that all the authorities consider milk fruit juice. Rema writes likewise below in the gloss to siman 466, se'if 5. Magen Avraham, subsection 4. Rambam's opinion was cited above in subsection 2. This is also the opinion of the later authorities: Chemed Moshe, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there; Chayei Adam, there. Whey likewise has the status of fruit juice. Chemed Moshe, there. Peri Chadash below in siman 466, se'if 5, writes likewise. Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 211, however, writes that whey causes leavening. See there. Bee honey likewise has the status of fruit juice. Sefer Mitzvos Gadol; Darkei Moshe, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6. Chemed Moshe writes there that this applies to both bee honey and date honey. This means honey that flows naturally from dates, not honey produced by cooking dates in water. The latter may have the status of fruit juice mixed with water, as stated below in subsections 24 and 25. See there.
5) The custom is to heat oil before using it to prepare matzah ashirah, in order to absorb the small amount of water contained in the oil. Me'am Lo'ez, parashas Bo, page 58a, writes that this is the custom of the holy city of Tzefas. See there. If one kneaded without heating the oil, it appears that the dough should be prohibited when the oil is new from that year, because oil cannot be entirely free of water. Once more than a year has passed, the custom is not to heat it, because people say that over the course of a year the water in the oil is absorbed into the vessel or settles at the bottom of the container. It appears preferable, even after a year, not to knead with unheated oil remaining near the bottom of the container. This is certainly so when the oil is stored in glass rather than earthenware, because glass does not absorb the water. Me'am Lo'ez explains there that three days suffice for the water to settle at the bottom. See there. Ruach Chaim, subsection 2, concludes that if one kneaded with new oil without first heating it and a year had not passed, the dough is prohibited, contrary to Lev Meivin. See there. Accordingly, every oil produced by soaking kernels in water, such as sesame oil and the like, must first be heated unless a year has passed. Nevertheless, the unqualified language of the early and later authorities permitting kneading with oil implies that even oil by itself may be used without heating, because oil naturally floats above water. Therefore, after the fact, it appears that one may be lenient like Lev Meivin. Regarding the oil remaining at the bottom of the container, however, it may be proper to be stringent because it might contain traces of water. Even according to the stringent opinion, prohibition after the fact applies only if the dough was not baked immediately. Se'if 2 explains that even fruit juice mixed with water is permitted if baked immediately. See there.
6) Regarding whether oil heated over fire must be left for one or two days to cool, Ikarei HaDat, siman 18, subsection 63, writes in the name of Pachad Yitzchak, subsection 40, that it is permitted even while hot and even if it contains pepper. See there. Ruach Chaim cites this and writes that it certainly need not be left for one or two days once it has cooled, and that this is clear. See there. Be'er HaMayim, siman 18, however, discusses the issue and appears to conclude that hot oil causes leavening. See there. Sedei Chemed, ma'areches chametz umatzah, siman 13, subsection 6, cites this. See there. One should therefore be stringent not to knead with it until it cools. See also below, siman 466, subsection 54.
7) Human milk has the status of fruit juice. Admas Kodesh, volume 1, siman 8; Birkei Yosef, subsection 3; Sha'arei Teshuvah, subsection 10. Accordingly, Admas Kodesh rules there that if a nursing woman kneaded dough for Pesach and milk dripped from her body into it, the dough has the status of fruit juice mixed with water if it is not baked immediately. See there. See Olas Shabbos there, which writes that the matzah may be eaten with meat. See there. See below, subsection 22, regarding a small amount that fell in when the dough was not baked immediately.
