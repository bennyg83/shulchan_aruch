(21) A wheat kernel that is found, etc.: and the case is that they cooked the honey and the wine, or that it was pickled for twenty-four hours; for with mere soaking in cold, according to many poskim, even if it is found in plain water it also does not forbid, as below in siman 467 se'if 12; see there.
(22) It is permitted: the honey and the wine; and we do not say that they became leavened through the wheat, even if it softened and cracked there, for fruit juice does not cause leavening.
(23) And provided that water did not mix into them: for if it mixed, on the contrary they cause leavening more, as above in se'if 2. And that which is explained above in se'if 3, that it is permitted to knead with wine even though it is impossible for him without a drop of water: that case is different, for the water fell before the fermentation of the wine, and through the fermentation of the wine the water turned into wine; which is not so where it mixes afterward. And regarding a wheat kernel found in mead (called mead), or in fruit brandy (made from fruit), or in date beer and fruit beer, and it is known that no water mixed into them after they were made: whether it forbids or not, the Acharonim were in doubt about this: whether to compare them to raisin wine, where through fermentation the water turns into wine, and so too here through cooking and fermentation of the honey the water turns and becomes fruit juice, and likewise for the other things above; or perhaps grapes are different, for their power is strong to turn water into wine, and likewise with raisin honey above, through cooking their substance changes entirely and they become a solid thing; which is not so with all these, for even after making and cooking them their essence is water. And therefore one should be stringent where it fell into them, not to drink them; but to keep them until after Pesach or to sell to a non-Jew, one may be lenient. However, if it is known that the wheat was not in water for twenty-four hours, one need not be stringent even for drinking, for cold does not impart taste. And likewise if the wheat did not crack but only softened, one may be lenient even if he does not know how long it stayed [as I explained below in siman 467 se'if 12 in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 55], unless it is known to him that the wheat stayed twenty-four hours in water and became pickled. The poskim wrote that fat is also fruit juice; therefore a wheat kernel that fell into fat, even hot, does not forbid, for fruit juice does not cause leavening, even if the wheat cracked; provided that water did not mix into the fat after they melted it. And even though without this there is in every fat a mixture of water, for they rinse it at the time of salting, nevertheless one need not be concerned for this, because when fat is cooked thoroughly in a pot the water is burned off in the cooking and goes away entirely; and therefore even if it is known that he expressly mixed a little water into the fat before they melted it, one need not be concerned. And all this is where it is known that it fell after they melted the fat; but if a wheat kernel is found in fat and it is not known whether it fell before or afterward: if they poured the fat from the pot in which they cooked it into another vessel and there the wheat was found, even though the fat was still hot, it is permitted, for we do not presume a prohibition, and we say it was found in a secondary vessel and was there also beforehand, and when they poured the fat upon it there was already no water in it. And likewise even if the wheat was found in the very vessel in which they melted it, only that it was after they removed it from the stove and set it elsewhere, and there the wheat was found in it, even while the fat was still hot, it is permitted, for we do not presume a prohibition from place to place, and we say the wheat fell in this place after they had already melted it and everything became fruit juice. But if the cracked wheat is found in the first vessel in which they melted the fat on Pesach, and it stands in its place, the fat is forbidden, for we are concerned that perhaps it fell before the melting or at the beginning of boiling when rinse-water was still present on the fat, and immediately it leavened, like the law of water with fruit juice that hasten to leaven, and it forbids the fat. And if he says clearly that he knows he did not melt immediately after rinsing, but that the water had already dried beforehand, then even if the wheat is found before the fat was thoroughly cooked, it is also permitted, for there is only fruit juice alone. And if they made with fat dumplings (called knaidlach) from ground matzah and a wheat kernel was found in them: if he mixed into them only fat and eggs, without water, obviously it is permitted, for they are fruit juice and do not cause leavening; but if there is also water in the dumplings, even a little, it is fruit juice with water and forbidden. And the same applies to matzah that they soak in water after baking and squeeze out, and afterward knead with fat: if a wheat kernel fell into it, one should be stringent and forbid it, because it is impossible to squeeze it so thoroughly, and water remains in it visibly.
