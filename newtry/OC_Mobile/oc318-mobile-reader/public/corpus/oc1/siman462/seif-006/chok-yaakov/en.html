With honey or wine. The later authorities write in the name of Tashbetz that raisin wine or raisin honey made with water, whose taste has been completely transformed, has the status of fruit juice alone. This is also the conclusion of Mabit and Maharit, Orach Chaim, siman 2. See Magen Avraham, who remains uncertain regarding a kernel of wheat found in honey-water, called mead, and similar drinks, whether such liquid is classified as fruit juice with respect to foreign substances, since its taste was changed through cooking. Taz records an incident in which a servant brought a vessel of mead to drink and a kernel of wheat was found in it. He permitted the mead because cold liquid does not cause it to prohibit; see his extended discussion in siman 467, seif katan 14. Regarding a kernel of wheat found in goose fat, Shelah prohibits it, while my learned brother-in-law writes in Soles Belulah in the name of my grandfather, the gaon, that it should be retained until after Pesach. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 81, permits it, and Magen Avraham also argues at length for permission. Still, if matzah was soaked in water, then squeezed out and kneaded with fat, and a kernel of wheat fell into it, one should be stringent, since some water remains and it is impossible to squeeze it out completely.
In which no water was mixed. The later authorities express great surprise at this ruling, because it appears to contradict the Mechaber's own ruling in seif 3 that a small amount of water is nullified. One cannot answer that here he refers to a large amount of water, for that is contrary to the unqualified wording of the Mechaber. Moreover, the source of the ruling is Rambam and Semag, who speak even of the smallest amount. Because of this question the later authorities entered into forced distinctions of various kinds. In my humble opinion there is not even the slightest difficulty. Above we said only that a small quantity of water does not cause the dough to leaven more rapidly, as fruit juice mixed with water ordinarily does, because the water is nullified within the fruit juice and one may therefore knead with it initially. Nevertheless, the dough still requires guarding and active handling so that it should not become chametz, as I explained above in seif katan 6, because perhaps even a small portion may become chametz at the place where the water is found. Accordingly, if a kernel of wheat is found in honey or wine containing a small admixture of water, we certainly suspect that the kernel became chametz, just as where a kernel is found in water. Dough may be kneaded with water only through guarding and handling. This is simple and clear. Rosh writes similarly at the beginning of Perek Eilu Ovrin regarding the view of Rif and those who follow him concerning fruit juice mixed with water, and this accords with Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchos Chametz; see above, seif katan 4. I am astonished at all the great later authorities: what happened to them, and why did they seek to divide matters that are equal?
