And provided that it did not mix. It is astonishing, for in se'if 3 he wrote that even if water mixed into them it is nullified; and it is possible that here the case is that much water mixed into them; and that is forced (see what I wrote in siman 463 se'if 2). And it appears to me that these are different views; and these are the words of Terumat HaDeshen siman 113: wheat that is pickled in must certainly became leavened, for it is impossible that a little water did not mix into it; until here. And these are the words of the Rambam: and that is provided that no water in the world mixes into them; and if any water mixes into them they cause leavening; and so wrote the Semak, that one should not make pancakes in milk lest perhaps he mix water into it first; and so is implied in the Yerushalmi that the Rif and Rosh brought at the beginning of chapter 3: and all of them through water, as the Tur wrote at the beginning of siman 442. And one cannot say that the case is that the water is much, for presumably they do not mix much water into vinegar; and likewise regarding that of the Yerushalmi that the Rosh brings in chapter 2, that he commanded to destroy the wine in which wheat was found: that is, he thought perhaps water mixed into it. And afterward I found in Kenesses HaGedolah that he wrote that what he wrote in se'if 3 deals with where water mixed while it was still must, for since it ferments afterward it is as if it was cooked and the taste of the water changed to be wine, like raisin wine that does not cause leavening for this reason, as the Mabit wrote, Even HaEzer siman 272, that they are like rainwater that changed within the grapes; and a proof from what we say in Bava Basra chapter 6: raisin wine, if he brought it for libations, is valid, and diluted wine, if he brought it, is invalid; which implies that raisin wine is not called diluted; end quote. And so wrote Rav Bezalel in Bedek HaBayis in the name of R' Shimon son of the Rashbatz; and Maharit wrote, part 2 se'if 2, that the same law applies to honey made with raisins with water, that it is fruit juice; end quote. One must say that what he wrote above in the name of Terumat HaDeshen to forbid means that the wheat fell into it while it was still must, when the water had not yet changed from its original nature; but after it fermented with the water they changed from their original nature. And accordingly it requires further study regarding mead called mead, if a wheat kernel fell into it, whether it causes leavening, for here too the water changed from its original nature through cooking with the honey and fermentation; and also regarding beer and brandy there is room to doubt whether it causes leavening; and the practical difference is: if there was a wheat kernel in beer or brandy in cold and they removed it from it, and afterward it fell into fruit juice: if it was already leavened it forbids the fruit juice, and if it does not cause leavening it does not forbid the fruit juice; and see siman 467 se'if 12. However, one may say that the wheat kernels themselves absorb from the beer and brandy, as it is in chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah: because of their sharpness they are like cracked; and so wrote the Mordechai in Pesachim: if milk fell on wheat it is forbidden to eat them with meat. It is written in the Shelah: a wheat kernel that fell into hot goose fat is forbidden, for it causes leavening by force of the water with which it was rinsed; and for this reason Rokeach forbade a roasted hen on which a wheat kernel was found, etc.; end quote. And it appears to me that one should not be stringent in this, for the water with which it was rinsed already dried; and all the more so when it was cooked by the fire; and so is clearly implied at the beginning of siman 463, where he counts water that issues from the stalks as fruit juice, even though much rainwater fell upon it, as above regarding wine. And what the Tur wrote regarding dry meat means that when it sweats it expels the water and it is present visibly. And what Rokeach wrote regarding a hen means because it was washed in water, and one may say that the wheat was inside it from life and the water leavened it; and he concludes, and these are his words: And R' Shimon annotated thus: and this wheat, how did it become leavened? And if you say when she washed it in water, they washed and fell, and there is not in them [enough] to leaven, and the fat of the fowl is fruit juice and does not cause leavening; end quote. And he also discusses there whether we say it was digested; but according to all, fat does not cause leavening, for we hold that nothing causes leavening except with water; and so he wrote in Sefer Tzemach Tzedek. But with fried dishes where they soak the matzah in water and squeeze it and afterward knead it in oil, and a wheat kernel fell into it, one should be stringent, for water remains in it visibly, for it is impossible to squeeze them so thoroughly.
