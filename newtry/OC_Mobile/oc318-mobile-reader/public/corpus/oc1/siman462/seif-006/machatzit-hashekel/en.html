And these are the words of Terumat HaDeshen, etc. He brought a proof that there are views that hold that the water is not nullified even if it is little.
Lest he mix water into it first. It is implied that if he mixes it is forbidden, even though it is a little water, as the language "mix" implies. And "all of them through water" means that which is taught at the beginning of chapter 3, "These pass over," and it listed four kinds of the country, and among them Edomite vinegar; and it is a mixture of chametz because the Edomite wine did not cause leavening until they put barley into it; and they asked in the Yerushalmi: what of it? Behold wine is fruit juice and does not cause leavening; and they answered: through water, for they also mix water into it, and it is fruit juice with water that causes leavening.
That he commanded to destroy, etc. And the Rosh wanted to say because he holds that even fruit juice alone causes leavening. And that companion holds that the concern is perhaps water mixed into it and it is fruit juice with water; but fruit juice alone he admits does not cause leavening; and presumably it is a little water. Nevertheless we hear that there are several views that hold that the water is not nullified; and so one may say that the Tur holds and disagrees with Semag of se'if 3; and if so it is difficult regarding Rav Bezalel who copied both without a dispute.
Rainwater. That is to say: for upon all grapes while attached, rain falls upon them and is absorbed within the grapes, and nevertheless it is not "diluted," for the water changes and becomes wine; from what he wrote above in the name of Terumat HaDeshen to forbid wheat that is pickled in must.
And also in beer, etc. And the practical difference, etc.: that is to say, for even without this the wheat absorbed from beer and brandy, which are chametz in cold where the wheat did not absorb. Therefore there is a doubt whether the wheat itself became leavened.
He wrote in the Shelah, etc. A roasted hen: even though the juice and fat of the hen are fruit juice, with what did the wheat become leavened? Rather necessarily because of rinse-water on the hen. This law is brought in siman 467 se'if 15. And what the Tur wrote regarding dry meat in siman 466 in the name of Rokeach: dry meat that sweats at the time of rains causes leavening by force of the water with which it was rinsed; end quote.
And he concludes, meaning Rokeach, regarding the law of a hen; and from what he concludes, Magen Avraham brings proof for his words, for R' Shimon was truly puzzled on this, and one must say that Rokeach holds that even so we are concerned that there was water when they rinsed it. And so he wrote in Sefer Tzemach Tzedek; and Peri Chadash brought it in siman 467; and also the words of the Shelah.
And he wrote in the name of Tzemach Tzedek that one need not be concerned that perhaps the wheat fell into the fat before cooking and boiling, when the water was still in its original state and had not changed and it leavened; for we say: here it was found and here it was, after it had already been brought to a boil.
And it appears somewhat from the words of Peri Chadash that specifically if the wheat was found in a pot into which they poured the fat from the pan in which they boiled the fat, for that is from place to place; but if it was found in the pan in which it was boiled, we do not say "here it was found," etc., for from time to time we do not say "here it was found," as is written in siman 467 s.k. 16.
Nevertheless, according to what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 467 s.k. 25 at its end: even if it was found in the pan one should not forbid unless it was found in the pan while it still stands on the stove on the fire; but if they already removed it from the stove, even though the wheat was found in the pan, since the place where the pan stood changed, it is from place to place, and we say here it was found and now it fell after it was brought to a boil, and it is permitted.
