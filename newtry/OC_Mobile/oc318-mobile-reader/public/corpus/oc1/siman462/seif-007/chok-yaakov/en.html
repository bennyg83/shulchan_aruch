Magen Avraham discusses the matter at length and concludes that, after the fact, if a kernel of wheat is found in salt during Pesach, one should remove a layer equal to the thickness of netilah and retain the remainder until after Pesach. If the salt was used during Pesach, the food should not be prohibited after the fact unless some of that removed layer was placed into the food. All this applies where the kernel was moist, meaning that it had softened. If it remained dry, the salt is permitted in every respect. Bach writes this. Taz concludes: The ruling stated here concerning salt plainly applies equally to spices. I do not know the source of this ruling. On the contrary, Tur and the other authorities imply that it applies specifically to salt, which is a derivative of water and, according to some authorities, is not classified as fruit juice and can cause leavening. Other spices, however, are derived from produce and therefore do not cause leavening at all; moreover, they are dry, as Magen Avraham writes. Initially, one should nevertheless inspect them before placing them into cooked foods, so that no kernel of wheat or barley is placed into the dish. This is self-evident. Rokeach, siman 471, writes: A person must inspect his salt, spices, and peppers to ensure that there are no moist kernels of wheat among them.
