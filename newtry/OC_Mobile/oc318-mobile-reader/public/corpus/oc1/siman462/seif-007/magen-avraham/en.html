The salt before Pesach. For then even if a wheat kernel is found in it it is nullified in sixty, which is not so on Pesach, when it is in any amount (Maharil; Bach). And it requires further study if a wheat kernel is found in a barrel of salt on Pesach, whether they forbid the entire barrel; for even according to those who forbid at the end of siman 467, that is regarding salting meat, where the salt dissolves and absorbs everything; but here the salt is dry and does not absorb. However, in Beis Yosef at the end of siman 95 he wrote in the name of the Rambam that it is forbidden to place a jar of salt next to a jar of kutoach, because it draws from it; and it is possible that kutoach is different for it is a sharp thing. However, in Tosafos and Rosh chapter 8 of Chullin they wrote that salt placed in a meat bowl, etc., because there is no salting effect for wood; which implies a bit that if it was lying next to food it absorbs; but above in siman 447 se'if 7 it is implied that it does not absorb; therefore it appears to me that ab initio one should be stringent not to use the entire barrel, and keep it until after Pesach; and so must one write; but a netilah's measure around it he should throw away. And the same applies if it is found on erev Pesach: he should throw away around it a netilah's measure; but if they put some of the salt into the food, one should not forbid after the fact unless they put from that netilah's measure; then one should forbid after the fact, for in siman 467 he ruled that even regarding salted meat one forbids only that piece; see there; and all the more so here, where fat does not apply at all and it is dry, that one should not forbid beyond a netilah's measure, as is written in siman 461. And the Tur holds that salt is fruit juice. And he wrote in Darkhei Moshe that our salt which they cook from water certainly causes leavening; end quote. If so it is proven that their dispute is regarding salt dug from the ground; but in the Tur it is implied at first glance that salt dug from the ground, according to all, does not cause leavening. The Bach wrote: if the wheat did not become moistened and it is dry as originally, even if it imparts taste it is permitted. Maharshal wrote: one should sort the spices; and it is obvious that after the fact it is permitted if a wheat kernel is found in them, for they are dry.
