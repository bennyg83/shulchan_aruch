[7] [Levush] A little water, etc. He wrote in the Shulchan Aruch: it is permitted to knead with wine even though it is impossible for him without a drop of water that falls at the time of the vintage, since the water was already nullified in the wine before they kneaded the dough; until here. And that is Hagahos Maimoniyos from Semag in Beis Yosef; and Levush who omitted it and on the contrary wrote that even a little water causes leavening appears to hold like one sage that Kenesses HaGedolah brought, who wrote from Semak that it is a solitary view and Rambam forbade; and in Kenesses HaGedolah he dealt at length to disagree with him, for Semak deals specifically at the time of vintage when it was nullified through fermentation, and "before they kneaded the dough" is not exact; and so wrote Maharit there, that specifically at the vintage they were nullified even though water is more than an imparting of taste; and so appears the conclusion of Magen Avraham. And in truth I am astonished at that sage, for in Semak folio 31 he also ruled like Rambam; and more astonishing is Kenesses HaGedolah who did not notice this; and I saw in the commentaries of Maharshal folio 66 that he wrote that Semak rules like Rambam and did not write that it is nullified except in the name of Riva, and he does not hold so; and that is forced. Also according to this it is astonishing regarding Hagahos Maimoniyos who ruled in the name of Semag that it is nullified, and likewise Beis Yosef and Shulchan Aruch. And also Kenesses HaGedolah's answer is not clear to me, for the language "since they were already nullified in the wine before they kneaded the dough" implies that even in absolute wine it is nullified; and so is implied in Shelah folio 141; and so my brother-in-law agreed with me in Sefer Chok Yaakov. But what he answered regarding the main difficulty, that a little water does not cause it to hasten to leaven and it is permitted to knead with it ab initio, but nevertheless the dough needs guarding, for in any case there is a concern of leavening in the place of water; and if so a wheat kernel found in it is forbidden like with water; until here are his words. And it is not clear in my eyes, for either way: if it was nullified there is no leavening in it at all, and "place of water" does not apply, for everything mixes together; and for this reason wet with wet does not revive again in siman 447 se'if 4; and from the Rosh chapter "These pass over" there is no proof at all, for he does not deal with a case where it was nullified. And furthermore it is difficult: for in Semag there it is implied that if he kneaded with fruit juice into which any amount of water mixed, he must burn it immediately; rather the main point is as I explained in Eliyahu Zuta, that this is the language of the Rambam and Semag: when do we say this? When water did not mix into them at all in the world; but if any water mixed into them they cause leavening; until here his language. And all the great authorities mentioned above understood "any amount" as a tiny amount. And in my humble opinion it is difficult: if so he should have said "even any amount" [and Maharshal copied thus, and it is not there]; also what is the language "any water in the world"? Rather it appears to me that this is its meaning: "any water in the world" means from anything called water that has the law of water, such as dew and other things in siman 466, or whether hot water or cold, drawn or natural; and for this he concluded: but if water of any amount mixed into them, that is, whatever water in the world, they cause leavening; but regarding a little that was nullified he admits that it is permitted. And likewise in the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter "They make partitions" he used the language "any amount" in the way I explained. But it requires further study in Rokeach at the end of siman 290, who forbids a drop of water in milk; and so he wrote in Terumat HaDeshen siman 113. And perhaps he deals with where water fell after the wheat fell, and in this it is forbidden like at the time of kneading above; and it still requires further study. And at first glance one should compare it to what the Rambam wrote at the end of the Laws of Tum'as Ochlin: fruit juice that mixed with water, everything is liquid and makes susceptible; until here; and in siman 466 we learned leavening from making susceptible; but Raavad there disagrees, that we follow the majority.
