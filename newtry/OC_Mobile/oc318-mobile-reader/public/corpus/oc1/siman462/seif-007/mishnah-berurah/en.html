(24) The salt. This refers to sea salt, which is treated like water. Regarding salt mined from the ground, several acharonim incline to the view that it is not considered water but fruit juice, which does not cause leavening. Nevertheless, it is proper to inspect even such salt.
(25) And it becomes chametz. The wheat kernel becomes chametz and then releases the taste of chametz into the salt. If the salt was not checked and wheat kernels were found in it, the acharonim agree that if the kernels are dry, the salt is permitted even on Pesach. If they were found softened and it is erev Pesach after midday, one must remove a netilah, meaning a thumb's thickness, around the place where the kernels were found and discard it, while the remainder is permitted. If they were found on Pesach, l'chatchilah one should not use any of the salt in that vessel. He should discard a netilah around the kernels and keep the remainder until after Pesach. B'dieved, if the salt was used, the food is not forbidden unless some of the salt from the netilah area was placed into it, in which case the entire food is forbidden. The acharonim write that spices too should be inspected and any grain kernels found among them should be discarded before they are added to food and before they are crushed, lest they become mixed into the spices. The spices themselves clearly remain permitted even if many kernels were found among them, since this is a dry mixture with another dry substance.
