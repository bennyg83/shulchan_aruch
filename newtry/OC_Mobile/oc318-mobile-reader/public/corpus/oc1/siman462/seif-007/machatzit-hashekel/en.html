(Seif katan 7) The salt, etc. "For then," etc.; meaning, he comes to explain what difference there is whether he sorts it before Pesach or waits until Pesach.
And it requires study if it is found, etc. Meaning, the law of the Shulchan Aruch is that initially it is better to sort it, but he is uncertain after the fact if it was found on Yom Tov whether one should forbid the salt in a case like this even in a minute amount.
For even according to those who forbid, etc. Meaning, many poskim hold that salting forbids only the amount of netilah with a lean item, and even according to us, who are stringent above in siman 467 regarding salting, that is, etc. However, in Beit Yosef, Yoreh De'ah, he wrote in the name of Rambam, etc.; this law is an explicit Gemara in Chullin 112, and so we rule there in Yoreh De'ah siman 95. But we hold the reason is lest some kutach fall into the salt without him knowing, and he will put the salt into meat. Rambam, however, according to his text in the Talmud, gave another reason: because it draws, etc. Thus, although salt is dry, it draws from what is near it; all the more it draws from wheat placed inside it.
However, see Tosafot Chullin 113, s.v. tahor, etc.
But above in siman 447 seif 6 it implies that it does not absorb. I do not know the implication there; on the contrary, it somewhat implies the reverse. It is written there that salt placed in a mortar is permitted for salting meat with it on Pesach. There too the reason is explained in Sefer HaTerumah, cited by Beit Yosef there on Mordekhai's words, which permits salt placed in a mortar because it does not draw out what is absorbed in the vessel. Although Pri Chadash there wrote that an ordinary mortar has actual chametz in it, see there, nevertheless those who came after him disagreed, and there is also no hint in Magen Avraham's words that he intended this, as though he held it obvious that an ordinary mortar has actual chametz in it, contrary to what is implied in Beit Yosef there. Therefore my heart tells me that this is a printing error and the words were reversed; so it should read: "a sharp item. But above in siman 447 seif 6 it implies that it does not absorb."
However, from Tosafot and Rosh, etc. It somewhat implies, etc., that next to food it absorbs; therefore it appears to me, etc.
Meaning, at first he wrote that from Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam it implies that it absorbs; although he wrote as a possibility that kutach is different, etc., nevertheless he holds as primary that Rambam's words imply it absorbs and there is no distinction between kutach and another thing. About this he wrote: but above in siman 447 it implies that it does not absorb, from the fact that he permits salt placed in a mortar. Then he wrote: however, from Tosafot and Rosh, etc., it implies that next to food it absorbs.
According to this, also in siman 447 regarding the mortar, one can say the reason is that salt does not have the power to draw out from the vessel, as we rule in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 95 and in siman 105 seif 13. In any case, Magen Avraham has no complete proof, neither to prohibit nor to permit; therefore he ruled that initially one should be stringent, etc.
But the amount of netilah is forbidden by law.
The same applies if it is found on Erev Pesach. Meaning, then it is nullified in sixty; nevertheless the amount of netilah is forbidden, for by law they do not spread further, and what does the sixty opposite it help?
And as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 105. This is not specifically Erev Pesach; the same applies before Pesach. He mentioned Erev Pesach because then one must throw away the amount of netilah, whereas before Pesach it can be eaten before Pesach.
And the Tur, etc. Darkei Moshe wrote, etc., that their dispute is proven. Meaning, the Tur wrote as follows: I found written that if moist wheat was found in salt, it ferments, because salt is derived from water and is not included among fruit juices. This does not appear to me, for we permit vatika with oil and salt, which is the law explained in siman 463 seif 2; thus salt is included among fruit juices, end quote.
But in Tur it appears at first glance, etc. Since he wrote "for salt is derived from water," it implies that those who forbid themselves forbid only salt that is cooked, etc., which is derived from water. According to this, with salt dug from the ground, those who forbid would agree it is permitted. One must then explain why Tur challenges from vatika: he was not satisfied to say that the Talmud speaks of salt dug from the ground, because he holds that ordinary salt mentioned in the Talmud is salt cooked from water. Rashal wrote, etc.; and it is obvious that after the fact it is permitted, unlike what is implied in Turei Zahav; see there.
