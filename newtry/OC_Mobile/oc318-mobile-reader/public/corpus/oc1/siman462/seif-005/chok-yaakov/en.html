So that it should not become obligated in challah. This implies that if he kneads the full measure it is obligated in challah, as ruled in Yoreh Deah, siman 329. Nevertheless, initially one should be careful on Pesach to knead less than the requisite measure, because according to some authorities there is uncertainty whether such dough is obligated in challah. Why should one knowingly enter into a possible doubtful blessing according to those authorities? Although we rule like the authorities who obligate it in challah, initially it is proper to be cautious. Furthermore, when kneading with liquids other than the seven liquids represented by the mnemonic yad shachat dam, meaning wine, honey, oil, milk, dew, blood, and water, there is no remedy for the challah, as Semak writes and Beit Yosef cites. It is therefore appropriate to enact the same precaution even regarding other liquids. This differs from the rest of the year, when there is another remedy, namely mixing water into the dough, as Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 329, writes. This is clearly the Mechaber's intent, and so it appears in the glosses to the Levush. The later authorities labored over this without need, for the Mechaber's words are entirely correct. In Yoreh Deah, where he ruled without qualification, he relied upon what he wrote here.
