(9) In order to permit a cascading stream (nitzok): for regarding yayin nesech a cascading stream is a connection; that is, if a Jew pours wine from his jug into a non-Jew's jug and there is liquid dampness in it, the wine in the Jew's jug becomes forbidden, for the column of the cascading stream connects the wine in the upper vessel to the wine in the lower vessel, and we view it as if the forbidden wine is mixed into the permitted; but when there is also water in the Jew's vessel in a measure that can nullify the wine in the non-Jew's vessel (that is, sixty times, and according to one view six times suffices; see Yoreh De'ah siman 126), it is permitted, for regarding every case of its own kind and its own kind and what is not its own kind, we say: remove its own kind as if it is not present, and what is not its own kind outweighs it and nullifies it.
(10) Since they were already nullified, etc.: most Acharonim agreed that in general nullification does not help for water mixed into fruit juice, and whenever there is in fruit juice even any amount of water, its nature has already changed and it can leaven dough and further hastens to leaven; and therefore one must be stringent ab initio not to knead with them, as above. And here it is different, for the water mixes into the wine before its making is finished, that is, while it is still must, and afterward the wine ferments and the water too turns into wine; and therefore everything is considered wine. And accordingly, if the water mixed into wine after fermentation, certainly that wine no longer has the law of fruit juice.
(11) Before they kneaded the dough: for if any amount of water mixed into fruit juice at the time of kneading, according to all the water was not nullified; and the Mechaber's language is not so precise according to the conclusion of most Acharonim above, for even before kneading it was not nullified, for the whole reason of nullification is only because it mixed into wine while the wine was fermenting and the water turned into wine, as above; and see Biur Halacha. And the Acharonim wrote that raisin wine or raisin honey, even though made with water, since their taste changed entirely they are like plain fruit juice, for the water in which the raisins soaked changed in nature; and specifically after their fermentation already finished, as above regarding grape wine [for otherwise it is not called fruit juice], and also that no more water was poured into it afterward; and specifically when the raisins are many, for if they are one of six in water, obviously this is considered fruit juice with water and causes leavening. And there are among the Acharonim who are stringent with raisins in every case and hold that raisin liquid is not considered fruit juice; and therefore every scrupulous person should be stringent not to eat matzah kneaded with them, only to sell or to keep until after Pesach; and see what we wrote below in se'if 6 regarding mead.
