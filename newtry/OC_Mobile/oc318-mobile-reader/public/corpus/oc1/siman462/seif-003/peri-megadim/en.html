Before See Taz, what he wrote: if it is dry it expelled nothing; see Yoreh De'ah 91, that salting does not expel and transfer taste except when somewhat moist, not when completely dry. However, what he wrote "if it did not soften" is difficult, for when it did not soften, even if the salt is moist and even in a cooked dish of this sort there is no prohibition; see siman 467 in Taz note 10. And what he wrote regarding spices is difficult: who explained them? And furthermore, when dry there is no expelling and transferring; and in Eliyah Rabbah 9 he wrote that the case is that the wheat is moistened; and nevertheless if they are dry there is no expelling and transferring of sharpness without pressure. And one may say that nevertheless one must sort the spices lest there be a wheat kernel and it leaven the dish; and God willing in Magen Avraham 7 this will be explained further.
Says the writer: I saw fit to note a small note, for Peri Chadash here ruled that salt is fruit juice, unlike the Mechaber; and also what he wrote, that the Mechaber in Kesef Mishnah se'if 12 said that with fruit juice one does not wash hands, whereas with salt one does wash, and he found in the Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 10 of Tum'as Ochlin, that salt is not a derivative of water and is like fruit juice that do not make susceptible; end quote; the Mechaber there is consistent with his view here. And I, the poor one, find that this requires further study for me regarding what we learned in Mikvaos chapter 6 mishnah 1: snow, hail, etc., and salt, etc.; apparently salt is water; and so he ruled in Yoreh De'ah 201 se'if 30; and so ruled the Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 6 of Mikvaos, laws 3 and 4; and for a mikveh we require water, and the verse writes only a spring and a pit, a gathering of water, for we require a derivative of water, as is written in Yoreh De'ah se'if 33 there. And if you say that salt only completes [the measure], still, all fruit juices do not complete at all, as is clear in the Rambam there law 4 and in Yoreh De'ah there. It is true that the Tur in Yoreh De'ah there omitted salt, and Beis Yosef wrote why. And according to his view here that salt, even from water, is fruit juice, see Magen Avraham 7; and he learned from a Tosefta that Kesef Mishnah brought in chapter 10 of Tum'as Ochlin law 3, that salt is like fruit juice. But the words of the Rambam, of blessed memory, contradict one another. Also the Mechaber in Yoreh De'ah there began with five: snow, hail, frost, salt, soft mud, and concluded with three: snow, hail, frost, that an entire mikveh from them is valid; granted that he omitted mud as Beis Yosef wrote from Sukkah 19, soft mud proves that it joins; but why did he omit salt, to say that if he made the entire mikveh, etc.? Also there he brought soft mud, and in Kesef Mishnah se'if 12 he omitted it; see Beis Yosef. Also regarding salt he wrote in Kesef Mishnah that if he made the entire mikveh from them, one immerses in it for netilas yadayim. And according to what Magen Avraham wrote in note 7 in the name of Darkhei Moshe to distinguish between salt from water and salt that they dig, that of Mikvaos deals with salt from water, and that of Tum'as Ochlin deals with dug salt; nevertheless that of Yoreh De'ah 201 se'if 30 and Orach Chaim, Kesef Mishnah se'if 12, remains difficult. And from the language of the Tur it is implied that even salt from water is fruit juice; and God willing in Magen Avraham note 7 this will be explained further. What the Mechaber wrote, a wheat kernel in honey, even without sixty is permitted: see Levush and Olas Shabbos, and it is implied even if it became established it is permitted; and in the introduction to siman 467 and in Taz there note 10 we wrote about this.
