[3] He should bake immediately, etc. And if it was not baked immediately: since it did not wait the measure of a mil without activity, and the measure of chametz is not recognizable in it, and the like, my brother-in-law wrote in Sefer Chok Yaakov that one should permit it for benefit. And regarding the proof he brings from Kol Bo in the name of Raavad concerning salt, one may distinguish, for one may say that this is worse, for there are poskim who say he should burn immediately, as the Tur wrote; also it appears to me that the Kol Bo's view there is that it is forbidden for benefit, and that he mentioned eating is to exclude what he wrote there that it is permitted for eating only that one does not fulfill on the first night; see there and examine carefully. But one may rely in this on the Rif and the Rambam and Maggid chapter 5, who ruled that it is permitted to knead, for it does not hasten to leaven more than other dough; and even though we do not hold like them, nevertheless one may say somewhat like the reasoning of Terumat HaDeshen in siman 113: they eat and we do not keep; and even though from Rabbeinu Yeruham it is implied that from the Rambam he does not hold like the Rif, this requires further study; and Maggid and Beis Yosef wrote that he holds like the Rif, as we said. And furthermore it appears to me that possibly the Rosh and Tur who wrote "bake immediately" are not exact, but rather like when kneading with water alone, to exclude what he wrote regarding fruit juice that even if it waited the whole day it does not leaven; and they disagreed with the Rif only regarding that he permits to knead ab initio; examine carefully; and so it appears in Sefer Tzidah LaDerech. And furthermore, according to Rashi's version on folio 36, "and the Sages say he may eat," obviously it is permitted; and so is implied in Tosafos folio 35 s.v. Fruit juice, etc.; and even for eating it would appear to permit in a case of great loss.
