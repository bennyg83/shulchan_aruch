Forty-three eggs. That is, with their shells; not like Rabbeinu Yerucham cited in the Beis Yosef; and so wrote the Bach; and so he wrote at the end of the responsum Chut HaShani in the kuntres of calculation; see there.
And likewise one should measure it. And see in the Tur, who wrote further to measure by another method, except that this measure is better; and in any case one should not measure by weighing liquid, by the consensus of the Rishonim and Acharonim; and see in my book Minchas Yaakov, klal 85, s.k. 58, and in Solet LeMinchah there.
One should fill a vessel with water. And Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language: and I found written in the name of Maharich that it is the measure of three quarts and a kvarterl, and Mahariv wrote that it is three zeidelach; end quote. And the Shach wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 354, and this is his language: and I measured it, and it is three quarts less a little, and so they practiced; and so wrote Mahariv, peirush 193, in the laws of Pesach regarding the measure for challah, that it is a vessel that holds a little less than three zeidelach, and a zeidel is a quart; end quote. And a quart in the lands of Poland is more than our zeidel. And my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Sefer A.Z. in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov, and this is his language: and I measured, and it is a Prague pint, which is two and a half Ladmor quarts, and which is three Viennese achterling; end quote. And so I also measured it, and it is a Prague pint. And from the words of all of them [it follows] that one should not measure this according to received tradition alone, for the measures change according to place and time, and every leading authority of his generation should measure according to his place and his time. And see above at the beginning of siman 457, where it is explained that even if one kneaded half a zeidel and a little more, there is no concern.
Forty-three eggs. And in the Rokeach, siman 274, he wrote that even though the measure for challah is forty-three eggs and a fifth of an egg, nevertheless when one comes to measure, one should measure by means of forty-four average eggs.
To fill it with flour. To exclude the one who holds to fill [it] with grain of this measure from which more flour than this measure is made, or to fill it with water and knead with that water as much flour as possible; and these opinions are rejected; they are brought in the Beis Yosef in this siman; see there.
Leveled and not heaped. See in the Beis Yosef what he challenged regarding the proofs of the Riva in the Mordechai, in that he brought a proof from the measure of the Kohen Gadol, which was heaped within it. And the Bach and the Taz marvel at the Beis Yosef in this, according to the explanation of Rashi and Tosafos there. And in truth it appears that the intent of the Beis Yosef is to explain in a different approach: that what it means is that all the measures were heaped, meaning that all of them were a full issaron and afterward they heap them from the outside; which is not so regarding the measure of the Kohen Gadol, which was heaped within it, meaning that it held a full issaron and also what one can heap from the outside upon an issaron all entered within it. And upon this the words of the Beis Yosef settle well; see there. And regarding the law there is no dispute here, as the Mechaber ruled here, that the measure must be leveled and not heaped.
For if so he will not knead it well. This implies that even if he wants to take only the measure of an issaron, nevertheless he should not afterward pack the flour down, so that it not be poorly kneaded and come to chimutz within a cooked dish; and so he wrote there in the name of the Ra'ah; see there. (And see in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah what he wrote here, and his words are confused, and what caused this was that he did not see the Maharil itself, as is clear within his words; see there.) And see in Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter Kol HaMenachos, in the section of matzah, who wrote that one should not weigh heavily upon the fine flour with the measure, for if so it turns out to be extra; end quote. This implies that if he first takes less than the measure in a precise amount, there is nothing [wrong] in that, and he afterward packs the flour. But it is possible that there is no proof from there, for those were made by kohanim, who are diligent, and we do not concern ourselves that he will not knead well even if he packs the flour; and this is understandable.
At the time of putting the flour. And likewise at the time of putting water into the flour, and at the time of making the matzos (Maharil); and likewise at the time of drawing [water], as written above in siman 455.
