Leveled and not heaped. In Mordechai in the name of Riva it is written, and these are its words: we learn from the High Priest whose measure was likewise a tenth eiphah and was heaped inside, as we say in chapter "two measures" — end quote — meaning one should measure only as much flour as enters the vessel without heaping, for the vessel itself holds a tenth eiphah which is 43 eggs and a fifth; and if he heaps it will be excess beyond this measure. And you may ask likewise here too one needs this excess of heaping — it is not so, for we learn from the High Priest, for there it says all measures were heaped except the High Priest's that was leveled because it was heaped inside — meaning all was equal regarding the flour except the vessels were divided: for other measures the vessel itself does not hold the measure of challah except with the heaping specifically; but the High Priest's measure was large holding by itself the measure of challah — if so he was not permitted to heap for it would be excess; and Rashi and Tosafot explain there the reason because one must divide half in the morning and half in the evening and if it were like other measures that he would need to heap — some fine flour would spill to the ground. The general principle from this — one should not take more than the measure of challah in all measures, except there is a distinction in the vessel itself — if so our vessel which itself holds the measure of challah one should not heap it lest there be excess like the High Priest, for there is a prohibition on Pesach in having excess because of chimutz which is not so all year — so far this is simple; and Beit Yosef wrote there is no proof from the High Priest — on the contrary there is proof to the contrary from there to say that measure was so large that when leveled it contained a heaped isaron — end quote — his words have no understanding at all, for what heaped isaron he says — behold all were equal as written; and Ma'aseh Rav wrote Riva did not explain the reason as Rashi and Tosafot I mentioned and I did not feel in this — certainly he holds thus and he does not learn from there except that there should not be excess beyond the measure when the vessel holds by itself the measure of challah — and this is simple per my humble opinion.
