One may not knead a large dough, for the one who kneads it cannot save it from becoming chametz at the time of kneading; and from the beginning of kneading until the end of kneading is the measure of a mil, [so] the dough is forbidden, for it has already come to chimutz before the helpers act upon it at the time of shaping; and so it is implied in the Ran. And it appears that even if there was not the measure of a mil from the beginning of kneading until the end of kneading, if one leaves it without some working of it at the time of shaping, that joins to the measure of a mil and it is forbidden. And even though our Rabbis, of blessed memory, [taught] to be very particular not to knead more than the measure for challah, even so it is better that one reduce it, as is written in siman 457; and that is when one kneads more than one dough, for then one can bring the doughs together; but if one kneads only one dough, one should not be so exacting, lest there not be there the measure for challah, and when one recites the blessing over separating challah the blessing will be in vain (Beis Yosef, Orach Chaim).
