1) [Se'if 1] One may not knead a large dough for Pesach, etc. The Sages determined that when a dough exceeds one issaron, working it by hand through kneading and shaping does not protect it from becoming chametz, because the hands cannot adequately work so large a dough. Even if one intends to work it so quickly that less than the time needed to walk a mil will pass from the beginning of kneading until baking, it is initially forbidden to knead more than one issaron. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. Even if one intends to divide it immediately after kneading and give it to many assistants, there is concern that it will become chametz during the kneading itself. There is also concern that it will not be thoroughly kneaded, leaving flour that will later become cooked. Similarly, if one kneads several small doughs, then joins and shapes them together, it is forbidden because of concern that they will become chametz during shaping. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1. See below, subsection 22.
2) There. One may not knead a large dough for Pesach, etc. See Tur and Beis Yosef, who cite an opinion that this applied only to the early authorities, whose ovens were small and had to be reheated for each dough. With our large ovens, it is preferable to knead a large quantity together while people continually work the dough so that it does not become chametz. The other Geonim, however, were stringent. See there. Ri'az likewise writes that even if many women bake in many ovens, they may not knead more than the quantity obligated in challah at one time on Pesach. A large amount of dough might become chametz even during a short delay. Rather, each woman should knead separately the quantity obligated in challah. Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Kol Sha'ah, cites this. Sefer HaZichronos, page 24; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Peri Chadash; Chemed Moshe, subsection 3, write likewise. Even if one has many assistants, one should not knead more than this quantity. Bach; Chemed Moshe, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 5. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there that people now rely upon the first opinion, and we lack the authority to protest because they have an opinion upon which to rely. Every individual, however, should be stringent personally. See there. See below, subsections 21 and 25.
3) Nowadays, the custom is that one woman begins kneading and rolling, then gives the dough to others to smooth and finish forming the pieces, and they bake them one after another. Since no individual handles more than the prescribed quantity and the oven is large, there is no concern in kneading many prescribed quantities one after another in this manner. This is the custom. Tashbetz, Ma'amar Chametz, page 31; Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1.
