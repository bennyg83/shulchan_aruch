Leveled. See the Taz. Behold, in Menachos 87a, according to Rabbi Meir there are two issaron measures: one heaped and one leveled; and according to the Rabbis, one issaron, and leveled, like Rav Chisda, and like the explanation of Rashi and Tosafos: that between this and that there was only a precise issaron of flour, forty-three eggs and a fifth of an average chicken egg; rather, for the other menachos the [measure called] issaron was a vessel holding two thirds of an issaron with a heap of a third, for example; and for the Kohen Gadol the vessel itself holds forty-three eggs and a fifth, and there was no heap at all. And on folio 90a: all the measures were heaped, and this one of the Kohen Gadol was heaped into its inside; that is to say, the heap that was proper to be was within the vessel; and like Rabbi Meir, as Rav Chisda said there, and according to his approach on folio 87b; and the meaning of "leveled" will be language of removal; see what I wrote above. And the Rambam, of blessed memory, in Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, chapter 1, halacha 16, ruled that it is one issaron, like the Rabbis, and he did not explain whether leveled; see the Mishneh LaMelech there. And in truth he stated anonymously like Rabbi Meir; and we hold [that when there is] a dispute and afterward an anonymous Mishnah, the halacha follows the anonymous Mishnah. And one may say [this] from the fact that it said "its heap into its inside" and did not say "except for this one, which was leveled" ("heaped" and "leveled" are passive forms: heaped and leveled, closed). And the Rambam, of blessed memory, in his commentary on the Mishnah there [says] this is according to Rabbi Meir; and the matter of "its heap into its inside" [is] that they put into that issaron forcefully when it is leveled, an amount that one heaped issaron holds: this needs explanation. And it would appear at first glance like what the Beis Yosef wrote, that flour packed and compressed holds more than if it were loose; and if so, it is a third view, that the two issaron [amounts] of the Gemara are one for the Kohen Gadol and one for all the menachos, and both [are] in one measure that holds two thirds of an issaron, and upon them the heap of a third; except that of the Kohen Gadol [is packed] like its outside so that it not fall to the ground, like Rashi and Tosafos on 87a: he presses forcefully and packs so that all of it is held within the measure; and according to this, "its heap into its inside" settles well. However, it is forced to say there are three disputes, and to say that this Mishnah is according to Rabbi Meir, that is, according to his approach, and it is not [explicitly] Rabbi Meir: that is forced. However, Rav Chisda according to his approach said regarding Rabbi Meir and the Rabbis that all [measures] of the Kohen Gadol are leveled, and according to the Rabbis one and leveled, according to his approach; and he said here that it is Rabbi Meir. Which is not so if we say according to the Rabbis [that there is] one and heaped for all the menachos, and for the Kohen Gadol "its heap into its inside" means packing from pressing the flour, and from within that settles the Mishnah like the Rabbis: all the measures were heaped, except that of the Kohen Gadol: its heap was necessarily by pressing the flour and packing into the Torah [measure]; and therefore he ruled so in Hilchos Klei HaMikdash, for such is his way at times, or he found so in another place. And behold, the Rosh, of blessed memory, holds that the measure for challah is forty-three eggs of grain, and when milled much is added, and one must heap so that there be the measure of challah from the omer; see Beis Yosef. And according to this, the same applies regarding a minchah of a tenth of an ephah: even though "solet" means a tenth of an ephah of grain that is milled into fine flour; if so, all the measures were heaped, and that of the Kohen Gadol had its heap into its inside so that the vessel itself hold this measure; if so, there is no proof to refute the explanation of the Rosh, of blessed memory, and this is not a corrupt explanation, as said; and see Chok Yaakov, note 6, and the Peri Chadash. And see the Bach, and this requires some further study there. However, for the practical law we hold [that it is] flour only, forty-three eggs and a fifth, both for challah and for Pesach. And in Yoreh De'ah 324 the Mechaber wrote that the measure should not be heaped: at first glance, that there is no need for a heap is not a warning; but on Pesach a warning applies, namely that one should make the measure hold the forty-three eggs leveled, not holding two thirds and with the heap [making] an issaron like [that of] the Kohen Gadol, so that one not scatter and it be less than the measure; and this would be the explanation of the words of the Riva in the Mordechai; and this is forced; see the Peri Chadash and in Chok Yaakov. However, the language of the Riva in the Mordechai, that they do not heap a Pesach measure, because since it is a tenth of an ephah, every measure of the Kohen Gadol which was likewise a tenth of an ephah had its heap within it; which is not so regarding all the measures that were heaped: it appears clear like what the Taz, of blessed memory, wrote, that this is the meaning: our women who make the vessel itself hold an issaron are forbidden to heap for Pesach, for it is excess and it becomes chametz; and he took an analogy and a proof from the Kohen Gadol, whose measure holds an issaron: it is forbidden to heap, for there would be too much flour; which is not so regarding the other measures, which hold only two thirds of an issaron, and therefore they were heaped. From now, our measures, which themselves hold an issaron, it is forbidden to heap; and the same applies regarding challah, one is obligated with this measure; and he brought no proof at all whether flour or grain, for that was obvious to him; and everything settles well, that we not learn from the other measures, for the proof is only that one should not add beyond an issaron, as said; and the difficulty of the Bach, of blessed memory, settles well: that there there was no heap so that it not fall to the ground, for he brings no proof about that at all, only not to add more than the measure; and indeed, if the measure is only two thirds of an issaron, one heaps; and it is more correct to be leveled; and that is what [he meant by] "and you did not feel [this]," that is to say, according to him this is no difficulty, as said. What remains to consider: according to the one who says that by human hands one cannot be precise, and eggs are large and small, one may answer easily; see Chullin 28b, s.v. lefi. And behold, for challah it is proper to add if one has [enough], to fulfill the view of the Tur and the doubt of the Rosh regarding challah, siman 324; and on Pesach one should take a precise measure holding about forty-three eggs and not more, and afterward take challah by combining in a basket; and it should not be heaped at all, for one will scatter the flour and soil it with water, and all the more so on Pesach; and God willing, in Magen Avraham more of this will be explained.
