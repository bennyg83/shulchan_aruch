It is permitted. See the Taz, what he wrote: like one who is somewhat free from working; that is to say, according to the one who says that ab initio one should not leave [dough] without working even for a moment, and when one kneads more than the measure for challah, working is not sufficiently effective in it and it is like free from working; and even if from beginning to end it does not remain idle the measure of a mil, even so ab initio it is in any case forbidden; and see siman 459. And there is another concern: lest they work it for more than the measure of a mil, and in truth in a large dough working is of no benefit, and there is concern regarding after the fact as well; and God willing, in Magen Avraham letter 6 it will be explained.
He wrote. In Kenesses HaGedolah, in the glosses of the Beis Yosef: some cover the amilah with metal, and some with smooth stone, and the sages of the generation praised [this] in Sefer HaZichronos, folio 52; end quote. And regarding metal, [it should be] without nails, for otherwise the dough sticks there; also from copper it appears one should refrain; see siman 455 in Magen Avraham letter 9. And the world practices lightly in making matzos, and it is proper to supervise this; and God willing, in [siman] 459 more will be explained.
Related to letter 1: regarding the chavitim of the Kohen Gadol one may say [he] packs, for there is no concern for flour that remains in it, since it is entirely [burned]; which is not so regarding the other menachos, lest there be flour in it and he put it into a cooked dish, for he is permitted to eat it thus, and it will become chametz; see Darkei Moshe 2, and understand.
