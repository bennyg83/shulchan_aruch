(6) If he kneaded etc.: and the reason is that even though we say that working is of no benefit in a dough this large [to prevent] it from coming to chimutz, nevertheless it is no worse than if he left dough without any working at all, for we hold that there is no prohibition so long as it did not remain idle the measure of a mil.
(7) It is permitted after the fact: even if he transgressed and kneaded more deliberately. And if he left it the measure of a mil during his kneading before the helpers began to assist at the time of shaping, several later authorities inclined that according to the aforementioned reasoning one should forbid even after the fact, for since the Sages measured that because of its great size it is impossible for one person to work the entire dough properly, it is like dough left without any working at all. And there are poskim who were lenient in the essence of this law in our days; and according to their view the Sages were concerned about this only in their days, when one person would knead and shape, and they would shape by hand without wooden rollers, and the matter would be delayed a great deal; and also their ovens were small, so that they could not put many matzos into the oven at once, and many matzos would remain on the board without working; and therefore the Sages prevented kneading a large measure at one time. But in our days, when they bake matzos with many helpers, and also our ovens are very large, and they knead and shape and bake with great zeal, one should not be stringent even when kneading a large measure at once, for certainly it will not come to chimutz; and the world practiced ab initio according to this view, and one should not protest against them since they have upon whom to rely. Nevertheless, every God-fearing person should see to be stringent according to the first view even in our days; and especially in small settlements that do not have many workers, certainly one should be careful not to knead more than the measure explained here. The poskim wrote that we are particular not to knead more than this measure when he kneads many doughs that can be combined for challah afterward, as below in siman 457; but if he comes to knead only one dough, one should not be so exacting, lest there not be in it the [required] measure, and when he recites the blessing it will be in vain.
