(6) If he kneaded etc. — and the reason that even though we say hand-work is not useful in such a large dough it will not come to chimutz, nevertheless it is no worse than if he left dough without any hand-work at all — we hold there is no prohibition as long as it did not remain the measure of a mil.

(7) Permitted b'dieved — even if he transgressed and kneaded more intentionally. And if he left it the measure of a mil in his kneading before the helpers began to assist at shaping — some Acharonim sided that per the aforementioned reasoning one should forbid also b'dieved, because since the sages measured that due to its great size it is impossible for one person to deal with the entire dough properly — it is like left without any hand-work at all. And there are poskim who lenient in the essence of this law today, and per their view the sages were not concerned in this except in their days when one person would knead and shape and they would shape by hand without wooden implements and the matter would delay greatly; and also their ovens were small that they could not put many matzot into the oven at once and much matzah would remain on the board without hand-work — and therefore the sages prevented kneading a large measure at once; but in our days when they bake matzot with many helpers and also our ovens are very large and they knead and shape and bake with great zeal — one should not be stringent even to knead in a large measure at once, for certainly it will not come to chimutz; and the world practiced l'chatchila according to this view and one should not protest in their hands since they have upon whom to rely; nevertheless every God-fearing person should see to be stringent per the first view even today, and especially in small settlements that do not have many workers — certainly one should be careful not to knead more than the measure explained here. The poskim wrote that we are strict not to knead more than this measure when he kneads many doughs that can be combined for challah afterward, as below siman 457; but if he comes to knead only one dough one should not restrict so much lest there not be in it the measure and when he blesses it will be in vain.
