One must join. In the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rekanati: this secret was revealed to me in a dream on the night of the first Yom Tov of the festival of Sukkos. One Ashkenazic pious man was lodging with me, whose name was Rabbi Yitzchak. I saw in a dream that he was writing the Name with yud and hei, and he was distancing the final hei from the three letters. I said to him: what have you done? He replied: this is the practice in our place. I protested against him and wrote it complete. I was astonished at the vision and did not understand. The next day I saw that he shook only the lulav and its species without the esrog, and I understood the interpretation of my dream, etc., for all four species allude to Him, may He be blessed, as is stated in the Midrash, etc., end quote. The Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Orchos Chayim that if he took both of them in one hand, Rabbi Shlomo wrote that he has not fulfilled his obligation, as we say in the Gemara, that he does not bind the esrog with them because it is written "kapos" and not "ve-kapos," end quote. These words are not clear. On the contrary, it is proven there to the opposite, for it is stated on page 34: one might have thought that the esrog should be with them in one bundle; you say: was it said "and branches of palms," etc.? Why did it not say: one might have thought it should be with them in one hand, even if he did not bind it with them? Rather, certainly the Merciful One was particular specifically about the binding, that it should not be with them; but in one hand there is no issue. Furthermore, from the fact that Tosafos and the Rosh above mentioned several ways to make the blessing before the performance, and said that he should recite the blessing before taking the esrog, and they did not say that even if he takes it, he should only take it in one hand with the lulav wherever possible and he would not fulfill his obligation through this, it is clear that certainly if he took them in one hand he has fulfilled his obligation.
