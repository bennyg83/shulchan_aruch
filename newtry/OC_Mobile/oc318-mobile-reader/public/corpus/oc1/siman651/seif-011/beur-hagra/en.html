There are those who say, etc. Because the Ritva and the Ran disagree and wrote that with lulav we do not approach it because of interposition, for one does not need a verse to disqualify for interposition, but rather because of complete taking. Anything that is for beautifying it, or is subordinate to his hand, is nullified relative to the lulav and is considered complete taking; see there.
And they practiced, etc.; but, etc. This is according to the view of the Ran, that it is nullified to his hand, as above. But according to Tosafos, that the issue is interposition, there is no distinction, for we say at the end of Eruvin and in Zevachim that even a single hair interposes in the place of the garments. And likewise regarding the hand they said there: specifically not in the place of the service. And likewise these questions there: wind, dust, etc.
Se'if 8: he should shake, etc. As is written there regarding a minor who knows, etc., even though he does not know how to recite Hallel. And at the end of the fourth chapter of Berachos: one who rose early to go on the road, etc. And in the Yerushalmi: Rav Acha bar Yaakov would bring it to him and shake it when he recited the blessing over it, and he would say, etc.
And we shake, etc. See Tosafos there, for it is written: "Then they will sing," etc.
And there are those who say, etc. To double all the shakings, and there is a reason for the matter.
Se'if 9: he rustles the lulav, etc. For if not, why did they require that it extend one tefach beyond the hadas, etc.?
And so for every, etc. As it says there: for one who has four, etc.; for one, etc.
And the extending outward, etc. The one who marks the reference here as Tur in the name of a Gaon erred, for the Gaon wrote that one need not shake it, and he disagrees with the Ran mentioned above. And the view of the Rema here is unlike the Shulchan Aruch, who wrote: one outward movement and he shakes three times, and likewise one inward movement and he shakes three times. And in the Darkei Moshe, subparagraph 8, he wrote in the name of Mahara that one should extend it outward three times and bring it inward three times on each side, and with those outward and inward movements he should rustle the leaves.
And they incline, etc. For if not, toward every side in which he shakes it, it would not be in the manner of their growth. And furthermore, this is only the remnants of the mitzvah.
And there are meticulous people, etc. As with the two loaves, but it is not comparable to all the other shakings, since he is not truly turning it over.
Se'if 10: he should circle, etc. As is written: all turns, etc.
Se'if 11: one must join, etc. As is written in the Midrash: just as this esrog has taste and has fragrance, etc.; and they connected, etc. And even though it is not together with them in the bundle, it must be joined to them. And likewise in Menachos 27a: the four species of the lulav, two of them, etc.; and likewise the Jewish people, etc.
And all the shakings, etc. As it says there: this indicates that they are remnants of the mitzvah. And there: once he lifted it, etc.; and likewise in the first chapter of Pesachim, as above. And certainly in their order. This is what he wrote: "and all," etc., and "in which," etc.
