With his arm. See Baer Heitev. See Birkei Yosef in the name of Mahari Molcho, that if he has no right hand he should take both in the left, and if he has no left hand he should take them in the right, for that is preferable to taking this one after that one; see there. He wrote further that if he has no hands or arms, he should take with his teeth, since "hand" is not written, just as we maintain regarding chalitzah with a woman whose hand is cut off. In my humble opinion this requires analysis, whether this is called taking, for this is not the manner of taking at all. See Halachos Ketanos, siman 77, that one whose hand is cut off cannot take with his teeth. See the responsa cited by the Baer Heitev. It appears that in any event he should take it without a blessing. It is obvious that "he has no hand" or "hands" is not literal; rather, even if he has them but his hands are bound in such a way that because of some occurrence or cause he cannot take anything with them, this means that the law is the same. See Eliyah Rabbah subparagraph 13, who wrote in the name of the Kol Bo that one whose hand is cut off should take it with his arms, similar to a woman whose hand is cut off regarding chalitzah; this implies as I wrote, that he should not take it with his teeth. See Eliyah Rabbah, that when he has even a left hand, he should take the lulav in his hand and the esrog with his arm; see there. In my humble opinion, the words of Mahari Molcho appear correct, that he should take both in one hand. However, it may be possible to satisfy both: to take with the arm and afterward with one hand, or the reverse.
