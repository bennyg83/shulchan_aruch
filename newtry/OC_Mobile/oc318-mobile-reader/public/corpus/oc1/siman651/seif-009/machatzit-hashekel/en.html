(Subparagraph 9) And if he reversed them he fulfilled his obligation; this implies even one who is not left-handed, etc. This is not implied from the language of the Shulchan Aruch. On the contrary, the Shulchan Aruch somewhat implies the opposite, that it refers specifically to a left-handed person, since according to the view of the Rav Beis Yosef, with a left-handed person we follow the right hand of ordinary people. Therefore, even though the custom is that we follow his own right, nevertheless if he reversed them he has fulfilled his obligation, because we rely on the view of the Rav Beis Yosef. This is unlike one who is not left-handed and reversed them, who even after the fact has not fulfilled his obligation, for there is no one to rely upon. So too the Turei Zahav explained the view of the Rema. Rather, the intent of the Magen Avraham is that in the Kol Bo, brought in the Darkei Moshe, from which the law of reversal comes, it is explained that even one who is not left-handed, if he reversed them, has fulfilled his obligation.
They said: when he turned it upside down. This is regarding what we learned there: Rabbi Yosei says, if the first day of Yom Tov of the festival fell on Shabbos, and he forgot and carried the lulav out to the public domain, he is exempt because he erred in a matter of mitzvah, for he wanted to fulfill the mitzvah of lulav; and he holds that one who erred in a matter of mitzvah is exempt. The Talmud asks: but once he lifted it, he fulfilled the mitzvah of taking the lulav, and if so he is no longer called one who erred in a matter of mitzvah, so why is he exempt? Abaye answers that it is speaking where he turned it upside down, so that he still had not fulfilled the mitzvah. Rashi explained "turned it upside down" to mean that he turned the top downward and the place of the binding upward; since he took them not in the way they grow, he did not fulfill his obligation. But the Magen Avraham brought in the name of the Mishnah Berurah in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that he explained "turned it upside down" to mean that he took the lulav in the left hand, etc. If so, according to the approach of the Mishnah Berurah in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, if he reversed them and took the esrog in the right hand and the lulav in the left, he has not fulfilled his obligation. According to Rashi, although he explained "turned it upside down" in another manner, one may nevertheless say that as a matter of law he agrees with the view of the Mishnah Berurah in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel. Therefore, in any event, although the Kol Bo makes clear that he has fulfilled his obligation, as above, nevertheless in order to satisfy also the view of the Mishnah Berurah in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, he should take it again, with the lulav in the right hand, etc., without a blessing.
