(ב) They said: when they inverted. On that which we taught there: R' Yosei says — first day of Yom Tov that fell on Shabbat, and he forgot and carried out the lulav on Rosh Hashanah — he is exempt because he erred in a matter of mitzvah, for he intended to fulfill the mitzvah of lulav; and he holds one who erred in a matter of mitzvah is exempt. The Gemara challenges: but from when he raised it he fulfilled the mitzvah of taking the lulav, and therefore he is no longer called one who erred in a matter of mitzvah — why exempt? Abaye answers: the case is when they inverted, for he had not yet fulfilled the mitzvah. Rashi explains: they inverted — the top downward and the place of binding upward, and since he took them not in the manner of their growth he did not fulfill. Magen Avraham brought in the name of Mishna Berurah in the name of R' Chananel that he explains inverted: he took the lulav in the left, etc. — and therefore per Mishna Berurah in the name of R' Chananel, if he inverted and took the etrog in the right and lulav in the left he did not fulfill. Per Rashi, even though he explains inverted in another matter, nevertheless for the law he agrees with Mishna Berurah in the name of R' Chananel; therefore at least, even though in the Gemara it is clear he fulfilled as above, nevertheless to fulfill also per Mishna Berurah in the name of R' Chananel he should take again — lulav in the right, etc. — without a blessing.
