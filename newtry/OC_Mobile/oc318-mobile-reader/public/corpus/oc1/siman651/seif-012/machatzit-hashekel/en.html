(Subparagraph 12) Until, etc. As is written in siman 60 se'if 4: although we maintain that a Torah mitzvah requires intent, nevertheless there are authorities who hold that even a Torah mitzvah does not require intent; see there. And although it is still possible for him to intend explicitly not to fulfill his obligation, for in such a case according to everyone he has not fulfilled it, and so wrote the Turei Zahav; and what the Turei Zahav wrote, that since there is no act involved we are concerned that perhaps he will forget, see there. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham necessarily does not hold this way. For if so, he could have written briefly that one should also not rely on not intending to fulfill, because there is concern that perhaps he will forget and intend to fulfill. Although one can distinguish, that specifically this we are concerned about, that he will forget to intend not to fulfill and will take it without specification, but to be concerned that he will forget and intend to fulfill, this we are not concerned about. But it is more correct as the Bach wrote, that even in this there are differing opinions, brought in the Rav Beis Yosef at the end of siman 589, where the Rashba and the Ra'ah wrote that even if he stands and cries out that he does not want to fulfill, nevertheless he has fulfilled; see there. Know that from the first day onward, when lulav is rabbinic, the enactment of the Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah certainly does not help, for with rabbinic matters the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 60 in the name of the Radbaz that we maintain mitzvos do not require intent; see there. However, it appears to me that he should take, etc.; and it turns out that he satisfies both. That is to say, since the enactment of the Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah has already been rejected, for he does not fulfill according to everyone, since there is one who says mitzvos do not require intent.
And nothing remains except the enactment written in the Shulchan Aruch, either that he take the lulav alone and recite the blessing before taking the esrog, or that he also take the esrog but turn the esrog upside down. These are the words of the Rosh. The Bach challenged this: the Rosh wrote that the reason this is before the performance is because we maintain that the four species prevent fulfillment of one another. If so, if he recites the blessing before taking the esrog, he has not fulfilled; and likewise, if he turns the esrog upside down, it is not in the way it grows and he has not fulfilled the mitzvah of esrog. If so, he has also not fulfilled the mitzvah of lulav, for the four species prevent fulfillment of one another. About this the Bach asked: the Rosh holds that the species prevent fulfillment of one another specifically when one of the four species is missing. But when all four species are before him, even though he takes them one after another, he has fulfilled; and so we maintain in se'if 12. If so, what does it help that he first takes the lulav alone or turns the esrog upside down? In any event he has fulfilled the mitzvah of lulav, and the blessing over the lulav is not before its performance; see there. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote another enactment, namely that he should take the lulav and the esrog in one hand, for in such a case the Rav Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Orchos Chayim that he has not fulfilled his obligation, as the Magen Avraham wrote in subparagraph 10, though the Turei Zahav in subparagraph 14 disagreed with him; see there. But if he takes both in the right hand and recites the blessing, and afterward takes the esrog in the left, it turns out that he took the lulav first, whereas one must take the esrog first, as the Magen Avraham wrote in subparagraph 8. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote that he should take the lulav and esrog in the left hand and recite the blessing, and afterward take the lulav in the right hand. It turns out that he took the esrog first, and it is also before the performance according to everyone. Also, if he does it in this way when he takes it from his fellow, his fellow does not need first to place it on a vessel or on the ground, as written in subparagraph 8 in the name of the Shelah. Rather, his fellow first gives him the lulav according to its law, only he receives it in his left hand; afterward he also takes the esrog in his left hand.
And see the Shelah. This is what the Magen Avraham wrote in his name in subparagraph 8.
