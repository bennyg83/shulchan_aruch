(There, se'if 12) And if all four were present with him and he took them one by one, he has fulfilled his obligation. Note: I was uncertain in a case where he took them one by one and did not know that more were before him, but in truth it later became known to him that at the time he took it the other species were also before him. Should we say that he fulfilled his obligation, since in truth all of them were before him? Or perhaps, since mitzvos require intent, because he did not intend at the time of the act that all of them were before him, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Or perhaps the rule that mitzvos require intent applies only to the need that he intend for the sake of the mitzvah, so that if he did not intend at all for the sake of the mitzvah it does not help. But if he intended for the sake of the mitzvah, even though he did not know that all of them were before him, if the matter is revealed retroactively that all of them were before him, this helps even without his knowledge. This requires analysis now; examine it.
