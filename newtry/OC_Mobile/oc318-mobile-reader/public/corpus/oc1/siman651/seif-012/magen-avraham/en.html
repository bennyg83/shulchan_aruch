If he spoke. Hagahos Maimoniyos writes: Regarding this, Rabbeinu Simchah ruled that if one spoke between taking one species and the next, he must recite another blessing. This wording demonstrates that Rabbeinu Simchah follows his own view, for he also rules concerning shofar that if one spoke he must recite another blessing, as Hagahos Maimoniyos records there in his name. We, however, rule in siman 592 that he does not repeat the blessing because it is one mitzvah. The same should apply here, since the four species constitute one mitzvah, as stated in Menachos. Perhaps, because Raavad maintains that even without an interruption one recites a separate blessing over each species, Rama ruled that at least when one spoke between them he should recite another blessing. Orchos Chaim records an incident involving Maharam, who took the lulav and recited the blessing, and afterward discovered that it lacked a willow. He took it again with the willow and recited “concerning the taking of the willow” and Shehecheyanu because of the willow. Although strictly speaking he needed to take only the willow, it is the preferred manner of the mitzvah to take all four species together. Raavad writes likewise. These are the words cited by Beit Yosef. This requires study, because in siman 672 he writes in the name of Orchos Chaim that if on the fourth night one lit only three Chanukah lamps, he should return and light one more without a blessing, since the original blessing was recited over the obligation to light all the lamps. This is also implied by siman 206, seif 5, and siman 177, seif 4: even if the item was not before him when he recited the blessing, his blessing covers it when he had it in mind. See the gloss in siman 209, seif 2. It therefore appears to me that Orchos Chaim and Raavad follow their own view that one must recite a blessing over each species separately. According to our ruling, no additional blessing is needed unless he spoke in the interim. This is why this law was omitted from Shulchan Aruch. Abudraham likewise writes in Rambam’s name that if, when lighting the first lamp, he intended to light others, he need not recite another blessing. If he recited the blessing and lit, and only afterward additional lamps became available, he recites a blessing each time.
