(50) Prevent fulfillment of one another - For we require complete taking, and all of them are one mitzvah. He should not recite a blessing over the others, even if he knows clearly that all of them will ultimately come into his hand, since they are not present before him at the time of the blessing.
(51) Whether on the other days - The Maharshal wrote in a responsum that he instructed the prayer leader to take and shake it before Hallel and during Hallel, without a blessing, but that others should not take it at all, for we are concerned that in another year they will come to take it as well and recite a blessing. But the Eliyah Rabbah disagrees with this, and his view is that others may also take it and shake it like the prayer leader. On the contrary, if they do not take it at all and only the prayer leader does, a stumbling block may result the next year, for they will rely on the prayer leader and not take it at all. So too, from the plain wording of the Mechaber and the Rav it also implies that each individual may take it merely as a remembrance.
(52) And he took them one by one, he fulfilled his obligation - For the binding that we make with the lulav is only for the mitzvah, but not indispensable. This is what he taught us in se'if 1; and here he teaches us an even greater novelty, that even if the taking was not all at once but only one after another, he has also fulfilled his obligation.
(53) All of them before him - That is, near him, for he recites one blessing over all of them, as explained just ahead, and he will not need to interrupt between them.
(54) And he should take the lulav first and recite the blessing, etc. - For since it is mentioned in the blessing, it must be adjacent to the blessing.
(55) And his intent is also for the others - And through this he exempts all of them, even though he takes each one separately.
(56) Over each one by itself - That is, over the hadas he recites "on taking a leafy tree," and over the aravah, "on taking an aravah," and likewise over an esrog. Nevertheless, it is good that afterward he should also go back and take them all together. If he recited the blessing over the lulav, and after the blessing saw that it did not contain a hadas or an aravah, or that they were invalid or reversed, if he had the hadas or aravah in his house in such a way that he need not interrupt in the meantime to take them, he should take all four together and recite the blessing over that species that had been lacking. That is, over aravah he should recite "on taking an aravah," and over hadas, "on taking a leafy tree." If this was on the first day, he must recite Shehecheyanu another second time for the lacking item; but regarding the lulav, he has fulfilled his obligation, since all four were with him in his house. But if he did not have another aravah or another hadas in his house at the time when he recited the blessing, and afterward they brought one to him, he must recite the blessing over all four together a second time, "on taking the lulav," since they were not then present with him in the house. He also did not fulfill his obligation with the lulav, as the Mechaber ruled, and he must recite the blessing over it a second time. And if this was on the first day, he must also recite Shehecheyanu a second time.
