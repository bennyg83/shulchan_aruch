As a remembrance. Taz writes that it appears to him that waving is unnecessary, because the taking is only a remembrance. Maharshal writes in a responsum that he instructed the chazzan to wave it properly during Hallel, but not when taking it with the blessing. Responsa Maharil, siman 119, likewise states that he should wave it in the usual manner during Hallel, without a blessing, on all the days. Others, however, should not take it, because we are concerned that in another year they may also take it and recite a blessing. See there.
Itself. Taz writes that from here one may learn the following: if one recited the blessing over the lulav and afterward discovered that the myrtle was invalid because its leaves had fallen, and a valid myrtle was then brought to him, he must recite a blessing over taking the myrtle when attaching it to the lulav. Beit Yosef likewise cites Orchos Chaim concerning an incident involving Maharam, who took the lulav and recited the blessing, and afterward saw that it lacked a willow. He took it again with the willow and recited “concerning the taking of the willow” and Shehecheyanu for the willow. Although he need take only the willow itself, it is preferable to take all four species together. Magen Avraham challenges this and concludes that he need not recite another blessing unless he spoke in the interim; see there. Yad Aharon resolves Magen Avraham's difficulty; see there.
