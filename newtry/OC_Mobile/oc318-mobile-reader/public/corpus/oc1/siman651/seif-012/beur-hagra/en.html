Se'if 12: but he takes them, etc. From what is written, "He should not bring a quince," etc., it implies that he does take the lulav. And there is also no concern for destruction, for the four species are something about which even schoolchildren know: go read it in the schoolhouse.
Whether, etc. This is unlike the opinion of Hagahos Ashri there, s.v. "and specifically," etc., who said that we derive that a deficient one is disqualified from "and you shall take," meaning a complete taking; and likewise the four species, which prevent fulfillment of one another. And just as a deficient one is valid on the other days, so too they do not prevent fulfillment of one another. See Tosafos there at the beginning of the chapter, s.v. "we require beauty," etc.: "therefore it appears," etc.; and Tosafos in Menachos 27a, s.v. "and you shall take," etc.; and in Sukkah 34b, s.v. "that it should be," etc.
Provided, etc. The Rosh and the Tur explain: "but he has them" means that they are before him, unlike the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch.
And he should take the lulav, etc. Since it is mentioned in the blessing, it must be adjacent to the blessing, as I wrote regarding tefillin; so it appears to me. And his intent, etc. That it exempts all of them, even though he takes each one separately, like the tefillin of the hand, which exempts the tefillin of the head. This is what he wrote: "And if he interrupted with speech," etc., as it says there: if he interrupted with speech, he recites two blessings, etc., each authority according to his position.
