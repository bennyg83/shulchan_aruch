But he takes them merely as a remembrance - And one must be careful not to intend for the sake of the mitzvah, for there is in this a concern of bal tigra, as the Turei Zahav wrote in subparagraph 17. In truth, this is explicitly stated in the Sifrei, Re'eh; see there. However, the Turei Zahav wrote a novel point: even when he takes the lulav after he has already fulfilled the mitzvah, and only because of mere affection for the mitzvah, even then he should be careful to take specifically all four species, neither subtracting from them nor adding to them, so that he not violate bal tosif or bal tigra. All the later authorities challenged him on this, namely the Eliyah Rabbah, Chemed Moshe, Nahar Shalom, and Mateh Yehudah. Their view is that since he already fulfilled the mitzvah, bal tosif and bal tigra do not apply to this.
And if he spoke between them, he must recite a blessing, etc. - See the Magen Avraham, who wondered: according to what we maintain in siman 592, that if one spoke between the shofar blasts, he need not go back and recite a blessing, because all of it is one mitzvah, if so, here too in our matter it is one mitzvah, as is stated in Menachos. He was forced to answer this; see there. In my humble opinion it appears simply that the reason of the Hagahos Maimoniyos is that although we do not hold like the opinion of Rabbeinu Tam, that we require him to take them all in his hand at one time, for otherwise it is not called complete taking, nevertheless we require that they be taken one after another without interruption between them. For otherwise he holds that this certainly is not called complete taking. However, this is difficult for me: if so, he also has not fulfilled the mitzvah with the lulav, and he will need to go back and take it as well and recite the blessing over all of them, "on taking the lulav," and from the language of the Rema it does not imply this. I examined the Hagahos Maimoniyos on what the Rambam wrote, that if he did not bind them and took them one by one, he fulfilled his obligation. The Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote: and on this Rabbeinu Simcha wrote that if he spoke between one species and another, he goes back and recites a blessing, end quote. It is also possible that his intent is that he should go back and recite the blessing over all of them, "on taking the lulav." And so it seems in my humble opinion from the language of the Rosh, the Tur, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote: even if he did not take them together but one after another. This implies that we require at least that it be one after another, and not that he speak idle matters between them. Now afterward I saw in Birkei Yosef that he responded to the words of the Hagahos Maimoniyos from what the Ritva wrote in the name of his teachers to the contrary. In my humble opinion, because of this one should not reject the words of the Hagahos Maimoniyos, for it is known that the teachers of the Ritva are the Ramban, and this approach is also brought in the Ran in the name of the Ramban. It is known that the view of the Ramban is rejected in practical law because of the view of the Rambam and the Rosh, who do not hold this way; and likewise the Mechaber ruled anonymously not in accordance with him.
