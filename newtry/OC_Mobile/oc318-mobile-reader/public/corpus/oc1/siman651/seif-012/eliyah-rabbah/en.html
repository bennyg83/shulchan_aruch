[12] [Levush] And after the fact he fulfills, etc. The view of the Nachalas Tzvi is that specifically a left-handed person fulfills his obligation if he reversed them, but one who is not left-handed does not fulfill his obligation if he takes the lulav in his left hand. I already wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that he did not see the Darkei Moshe, who wrote in the name of the Kol Bo that even one who is not left-handed fulfills his obligation. I also saw this in Shibbolei HaLeket, siman 118, and Tanyei Maharil; and so I found in Sefer Imarkal in the name of Sefer Baruch. The proof that the Turei Zahav brought from the Talmud, page 43, where Abaye answers that he turned it upside down and did not say that he took the lulav in his left hand, see there. So too Chut HaShani, siman 28, wrote; this proof had already been brought in Sefer Shibbolei HaLeket. What Chut HaShani wrote according to the explanation of the Baal HaItur is forced. Rather, the primary explanation is as I explained in Eliyahu Zuta; see there. However, in Shibbolei HaLeket there, in the name of Rabbi Yaakov, it implies that he did not fulfill his obligation. Nevertheless, it is clear that Shibbolei HaLeket did not rule like him in this. The Magen Avraham wrote that Rabbeinu Chananel explained "when he turned it upside down" to mean that he takes the lulav in his left hand; therefore it is good to go back and take it without a blessing, end quote. In my humble opinion, one need not be concerned against the authorities mentioned above, and the Magen Avraham did not see the authorities mentioned above. Furthermore, I found that Imarkal brought this explanation and wrote about it: this is not clear, for it should have said "when he exchanged them" and not "when he turned them upside down," end quote. And likewise on page 36 the Talmud says: sometimes they become exchanged for him.
