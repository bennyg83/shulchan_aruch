He takes them merely as a remembrance. It appeared to me that he does not need to shake, since it is only a mere remembrance. But the Maharshal wrote in a responsum that he instructed that the prayer leader should shake according to its law at the time of Hallel, but not at the time of the blessing. So too it is written in Responsa Maharil, siman 119, that he should shake in his usual manner at the time of Hallel, without a blessing, all the days.
And if he spoke between them. From this one can learn that one who recited the blessing over the lulav and afterward saw that the hadas was invalid, because its leaves had fallen in the manner mentioned above for when it is invalid, and afterward they bring him another valid hadas, must recite the blessing "on taking the hadas." That is where he joins it to the lulav. Similar to this the Beis Yosef wrote above in this siman in the name of Orchos Chayim, in the name of Rabbi Meshullam, where he did not find an aravah, etc.
