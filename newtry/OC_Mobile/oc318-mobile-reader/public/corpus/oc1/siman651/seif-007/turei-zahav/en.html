And if he wrapped a cloth around it, etc. In Tosafos, page 37, in the dispute of Rabbah and Rava regarding taking by means of another item, they wrote that what we require here, complete taking, cannot be explained as meaning that there should be no interposition, as above. For above it is speaking when he binds the lulav, and here it is when he wraps his hand; and with something wrapped around his hand, interposition does not apply. They rejected this explanation, and one may say that it is speaking where he made from the cloth a kind of handle on the lulav, and interposition does not apply here, for the entire lulav is outside his hand and he holds the protruding handle made from the cloth; and Rava considers this complete taking. And the lulav that he carried out in a vessel at the end of the chapter is not where he held it by the sides of the vessel, but where he placed his hand under its bottom or held the handle of the vessel, end quote. The explanation of their words, in what they conclude, "and the lulav that he carried out," etc., appears as follows. What they wrote earlier, that it is speaking where he made the cloth like a handle, means that he benefits from the cloth in holding the lulav. That is, the lulav itself with the hadas and aravah is difficult for him to hold, as the Tur wrote at the end of siman 651, that they practiced taking 68 aravos. Therefore he wraps a cloth around the lulav and the end of that cloth is in his hand, and through this he holds the lulav like tongs, whether part of the lulav is inside the cloth at the place he holds, or even if all of it emerges from the place he holds. In any case, by holding the cloth he has assistance in holding the lulav, and it is permitted, for it is considered a handle. This is also explicit in their words at the end of chapter Makom Shenahagu, where they wrote: Rabbeinu Yitzchak answered that there it is speaking where he holds the lulav by means of the cloth like tongs, and he holds the cloth, for the cloth assists him in holding it, and in such a case it is not an interposition, etc. So too here, this is the intent of Tosafos. About this they said that it is complete taking according to Rava, and it is also not an interposition. About this they had difficulty: why do we say at the end of the chapter that if he carried it out in a vessel, it is not complete taking? How is the cloth here different from the vessel there? Granted, according to the first answer it is fine, for here it is speaking specifically where the hand is wrapped. But according to the concluding answer it is difficult. About this they wrote that if it were speaking there where he holds the sides of the vessel, meaning that the lulav is inside the hollow of the vessel and he holds it by the sides of the vessel from above, where this is useful for him in holding it, it would be like holding the lulav, since he benefits from this, and it would be a handle. In that case it would certainly be permitted, like here with the cloth. Rather, there it is speaking where he places his hand under its bottom, and in this he has no benefit from the vessel; and likewise if he holds the handle of the vessel, then if you say that the vessel is a handle, he holds a handle for a handle, and this does not look like tongs. In this case it certainly is not taking, and that is what is being discussed there. But if he held the sides of the vessel itself from above, it would be like tongs. It follows that there is no difference between a cloth and a vessel: wherever it is like a handle it is permitted, even with a vessel; and where it is not like a handle it is forbidden, even with a cloth. Therefore the Tur brought this, and this is the distinction in the Gemara, and the Tur copied it: taking by means of another item is called taking; therefore, even if he made a handle and placed the lulav in it and took it, it is permitted, even if the entire lulav emerges from beneath his hand, provided that it is in a respectful manner. But if it is not in a respectful manner, such as if he placed it in a vessel and took it, he has not fulfilled his obligation, end quote. The explanation of his words is that since we maintain generally that taking by means of another item is called taking, therefore if he took it by means of another item in a way that it is a handle, it is permitted, even though the whole lulav is outside his hand. One might have said that there is no taking here; nevertheless it is permitted. This is specifically in a respectful manner, meaning that it is useful for holding it, and he holds that it is a handle. But if it is not a handle, rather he simply placed it in a vessel and not for the benefit of holding the lulav, this is not respect and is forbidden. Therefore the Tur did not distinguish within the vessel itself, since he had already written that it is specifically in the manner of a handle. According to this, there is no permission in the world except where it is made like tongs. But if he wrapped his hand in a cloth and took the lulav, it is certainly forbidden because of interposition, and because of this Yissachar of Kefar Barkai was punished; it is not as Tosafos needed to press themselves according to the first explanation. So too they wrote explicitly in chapter Hotziu Lo and in chapter Makom Shenahagu, and there they distinguish between holding the rim of the vessel and holding its bottom. Their intent here is also the same. All this appears clear to me with the help of Heaven. But the Beis Yosef explains the words of Tosafos in another way. He wrote: it is not speaking where he holds the sides of the vessel, for if so the Gemara would exclude it because of interposition; rather, it is speaking where he places his hand under its bottom or holds the handle of the vessel, where there is no interposition, but only because it is not complete taking even according to Rava, because it is a disgraceful manner. Therefore with any vessel, whether he holds the sides of the vessel or places his hand under its bottom, he has not fulfilled his obligation: if he holds the sides of the vessel, also because of interposition; and if he places his hand under its bottom, because of complete taking. Therefore our master the Tur did not bring a distinction between holding the sides of the vessel and placing his hand under its bottom, end quote. With pardon for the honor of our master the Beis Yosef, he did not descend to the truth of the words of Tosafos in this. According to his explanation, why did Tosafos write that there it is not speaking of holding the sides of the vessel, because in that case it would be invalid not because of incomplete taking but because of interposition? If so, it will be difficult on the Talmud: who brought it into this matter, to establish Rava's statement as a case where he carried it out in its vessel, so that he did not fulfill his obligation, and on this it asks there that he should have fulfilled it, since taking by means of another item is called taking? It should have answered that Rava is speaking in every case where he carried it out in its vessel and did not fulfill his obligation, whether because it is an interposition or because it is not complete taking. Rava did not mention either the reason of interposition or the reason of incomplete taking. What forced the Talmud to establish Rava's reason specifically as incomplete taking, so that it is speaking only where he did not hold the sides of the vessel? Why not say that Rava is truly speaking in every case, and in every manner with a vessel he has not fulfilled his obligation? Furthermore, there is no place for his explanation, for why should there be more interposition when he holds the sides of the vessel than when he holds the handle of the vessel or its bottom? This is only astonishing. According to what I wrote, everything is fine. What it writes here, "and if he wrapped a cloth around it and took it," means not in the manner of a handle, and about this the view of Tosafos and the Tur is that he has not fulfilled his obligation. And what the Tur says, "even if the entire lulav is outside his hand," is a novelty regarding taking, that it is considered taking even in this case. But regarding interposition, there is more advantage if it is outside his hand than if it is partly in his hand. Therefore Tosafos wrote that there is no interposition here, for the entire lulav is not in his hand. Because of this it would seem that there is only a concern of taking, and they conclude that even so it is permitted because it is like a handle.
And the entire hand is not covered. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of the Agudah, chapter Makom Shenahagu. I do not know the source for this, that interposition with lulav is specifically with the whole hand. On the contrary, from the words of the Ran brought by the Beis Yosef, it implies that it is like immersion and tefillin. Furthermore, Tosafos wrote in chapter Lulav, page 36, as follows: and even though here too it interposes for the binding, and part of them is without interposition, yet Rabbah is particular, end quote. Even according to Rava, who disagrees with Rabbah, that is because anything done to beautify it is not an interposition. This implies that anything not done to beautify it is an interposition, as according to Rabbah. One should not object from the Mishnah where Rabbi Meir says one may bind the lulav with thread or cord, for there it is to beautify it, as the Tur wrote, and therefore it has no concern of interposition. And one should not object according to Rav, who holds that even to beautify it is an interposition, for one may say that according to Rabbah the bundle was outside the place where he held the lulav, as Rabbah said to those who bind the hoshanah: leave room for a handle, etc.
