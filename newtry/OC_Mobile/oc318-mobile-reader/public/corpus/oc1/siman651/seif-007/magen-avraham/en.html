A handle. For example, the entire lulav is outside his hand and he holds a protruding handle fashioned from the cloth. This is not an interposition, because he is not holding the lulav itself in his hand.
The lulav in a vessel. If he holds the walls of the vessel, it constitutes an interposition. If he places his hand beneath the bottom of the vessel or holds its handle, this is taking by means of another object in a degrading manner and is invalid. Beit Yosef’s explanation of Tosafos. The statement that holding the walls of the vessel is an interposition must refer to inserting the lulav into a hollow reed and holding the reed. See Bach, who explains it differently. Beit Yosef’s explanation appears correct to me, because the text says, “he held it by the walls of the vessel,” rather than, “he holds the walls of the vessel.”
Some say that he did not fulfill his obligation. This is presented as “some say” because Ran maintains that he fulfilled his obligation, since the material is subordinate to his hand. If, however, he wrapped a cloth around the lulav itself, even Ran agrees that he did not fulfill his obligation because of the interposition.
To remove the tefillin. Ran states that one should remove the tefillin before taking the lulav. He should first recite the blessing over the lulav in the sukkah and then perform the remaining wavings in the synagogue. Shelah in the name of the Arizal.
And the entire hand is not, etc. Bach asks that the Gemara considered saying that the binding of the lulav constitutes an interposition even though the entire hand is not covered. It appears to me that Agudah maintains that whenever the entire hand is not covered, the covering is subordinate to the hand, as stated above. The binding of the lulav is different.
