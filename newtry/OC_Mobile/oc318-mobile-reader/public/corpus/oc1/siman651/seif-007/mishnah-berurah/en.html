(30) A handle - For example, where the entire lulav is outside his hand and he holds a protruding handle made from the cloth. This is not an interposition, since he is not holding the lulav in his hand; Magen Avraham. The view of the Eliyah Rabbah is that a handle is for beautifying it, and therefore even if he holds the lulav in his hand through the handle, it is proper and is not an interposition, unless it was made in a way that is not beautiful. The Derishah agreed similarly, that it depends on how it was made; see there.
(31) In a vessel and he took it, etc. - Even if it is made of silver, for it is not a manner of honor when he holds it in a vessel, and it is not called taking. Several later authorities agreed that with a vessel one must be stringent in every case, whether he holds it by the walls of the vessel, or places his hands under its bottom, or holds the handle of the vessel.
(32) A cloth over it, etc. - Because of interposition. The view of the Ran is that it is because it is not complete taking.
(33) Or that he wrapped a cloth - And the same applies if he wore gloves on his hands. What the Mechaber wrote this law in the language of "there are those who say" is because of the view of the Ran, who holds that he has fulfilled his obligation because it is nullified to the hand. But if he wrapped a cloth around the lulav, even according to the Ran he has not fulfilled his obligation, because it is not complete taking; Magen Avraham. Therefore, if he took it, he should go back and take it without a blessing.
(34) And they practiced, etc. - In the name of the Arizal they wrote that one should first recite the blessing in the sukkah over the lulav, and afterward perform the rest of the shakings in the synagogue.
(35) To remove, etc. - This means that he should fold them behind his finger. And there are those who say that he should remove the tefillin entirely before taking the lulav. From the Magen Avraham.
(36) But by law there is no concern, etc. - Several later authorities wrote that even by law there is concern about this because of interposition, even though the entire hand is not covered by them. Therefore, if he took it while he had not removed the tefillin from his hands, or the rings, he should go back and take it without a blessing.
