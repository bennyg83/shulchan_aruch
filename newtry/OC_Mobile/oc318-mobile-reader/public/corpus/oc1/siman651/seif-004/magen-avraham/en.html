The lulav in his arm, etc. This resembles the law of one who lacks a thumb in Sanhedrin 45b, where the substance is placed upon the location of the missing thumb and he fulfills the requirement. This is preferable to taking the lulav and afterward the esrog, or applies where he has only a left hand or is left-handed. These are the words of Rokeach. This requires study. Since he has no hand at all, what does it mean that this is preferable to taking the lulav, etc.? In any event he would need to take it with his arm. Beit Yosef, citing Rokeach, emends the text to read: “An amputee who has only one hand,” etc. Beit Yosef understands that the word “only” is a scribal error, but in Rokeach the reading is: “An amputee who has only one hand,” etc. The explanation is that he takes the lulav in his hand and the esrog in his arm, and this is preferable, etc. Or, if he has only a left hand, he likewise takes the lulav in his left hand, which is now his right, and the esrog in his arm. If he is left-handed, he likewise takes the esrog in his own left hand, which is the right hand of ordinary people. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Orchos Chaim that if he took both species in one hand he did not fulfill his obligation, because the verse does not write “and branches of date palms”; see there. Nevertheless, Rokeach’s proof from Rabbi Eliezer requires study, because we rule like the first Tanna that one who lacks a thumb can never attain purification, as Rambam rules in chapter 5 of Hilchos Mechusrei Kapparah. Perhaps Rokeach follows the conclusion at the end of chapter 6 of Nazir that the positions are reversed; see Tosafos there, s.v. “as was taught.” He also follows Rabbeinu Tam that taking the species one after another does not fulfill the obligation, as is implied by his subsequent words. According to Rambam, however, it is preferable that he take them one after another in his hand, since this fulfills the obligation as stated in seif 12. Perhaps Rambam concedes here that taking it with the arm suffices, for does the verse state, “You shall take them in your hand”? A similar principle is stated regarding chalitzah in chapter 2 of Yevamos.
