And if. See Taz and Magen Avraham, who explain that if one reversed the hands he fulfilled the obligation even if he is not left-handed. In Darkei Moshe, in the name of Kol Bo, it implies that any person who reversed them has fulfilled the obligation. From the fact that in Shulchan Aruch the Rav did not gloss so in seif 2, it implies that this applies specifically to a left-handed person. The reason may be that since the Sages instituted the matter this way, he has not fulfilled his obligation D'Rabbanan in any event, or perhaps even according to the Torah; see Rabbeinu Yonah at the beginning of Berachos, Tosafot, Berachos 11a, and Sukkah regarding one whose head and majority, etc., where since he transgressed D'Rabbanan he has not fulfilled according to the Torah, for one may say this is included in 'do not turn aside,' etc. This is unlike a left-handed person, where there is a doubtful dispute, and however he acts he fulfills, for a D'Rabbanan doubt is ruled leniently, since this was their stipulation; see the first root in Megillas Esther. At first glance, a left-handed person, or one who reverses the species, should take them in both hands and thereby fulfill according to all possibilities. See note 14: if he took all four species in one hand, he has fulfilled after the fact. This means that he need not return and bless, even if he spoke. Nevertheless, there is no loss if he then takes them afterward in their proper manner, three in the right hand and the esrog in the left.
