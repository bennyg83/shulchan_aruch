(37) At the time when he recites the blessing - Even if he takes it not at the time of Hallel, he shakes it toward all directions, even though he will go back and take it at the time of Hallel and divide the shakings, some at "Give thanks to Hashem, for He is good," and some at "for His kindness endures forever," and likewise at "Please," but he should not shake when he says the Name. If so, at "Give thanks" he should make one shaking at each word, and at "Please" two shakings at each word. In all the shakings he should bring the end of the lulav opposite the chest; Magen Avraham in the name of the writings. In Eliyah Rabbah he brought in the name of Maharil that he would remain standing in his place, facing east for all the shakings, only turning his hands toward the directions; and toward the west he would raise the lulav on his shoulder behind him. This also seems implied by the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, which wrote, "and they incline the head of the lulav to every side toward which he shakes," implying that he stands in his place. Maamar Mordechai.
(38) And likewise he should shake at "Give thanks to Hashem," etc. - For it is written, "Then the trees of the forest will sing," meaning with the trees of the forest; and afterward it is written, "Give thanks to Hashem," etc.; and afterward it is written, "And say, save us." Therefore we also shake at "Please, Hashem, save now."
(39) One time - See the Beis Yosef, whose view is like the Ran, that we shake only at the first "Give thanks" that is said, but not at the "Give thanks" that the congregation says after the prayer leader says, "Let Yisrael now say," or "Let them now say," etc. But the Rema wrote: and they shake at every "Give thanks" that they say, like the view of the Rosh, and this is our custom. Darkei Moshe.
(40) And they shake at every "Give thanks" - That is to say, the congregation; but the prayer leader shakes only at the first "Give thanks."
(41) That the prayer leader, etc. - This means that the prayer leader shakes at "Give thanks" and at "Let Yisrael now say," but at "Let the house of Aharon now say" and "Let those who fear Hashem now say," he does not shake. For when he says "Let Yisrael now say," it is like a reminder to the congregation that they should say "Give thanks," and therefore he shakes with them, unlike "Let them now say," where he is not speaking about all of Yisrael. The congregation shakes each time they answer "Give thanks," making four times at "Give thanks." At "Please, Hashem, save now," the prayer leader and the congregation shake two times. At the "Give thanks" at the end as well, the congregation and the prayer leader shake two times. If one prays alone, he shakes only at "Give thanks" at the beginning of Hallel, and at the end of Hallel, and at "Please, Hashem, save now." If they did not bring him the lulav, and in the middle of Hallel they brought it to him, he may recite the blessing over it between the sections, namely between one psalm and another. Chayei Adam.
