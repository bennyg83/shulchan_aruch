[8] [Levush] These three, etc. They ask: let him derive it from the rule that anything over which one recites a blessing must be taken in the right hand. The Magen Avraham answered that this teaches us that even after the blessing, such as at the time of saying Hoshanah, he should take it in the right hand. To me it appears that if not for this reason, that these are three, in truth he would not have recited the blessing over the lulav. This is what the Levush wrote: and for this reason, etc. So too it implies in the Maharil, who asked why we do not recite the blessing over the esrog, since it is earlier in the verse, and answered because these are three. At first glance, there is difficulty with the Maharil, for in the Talmud, Sukkah 36, it gives a different reason, because it is higher among its species. One may say that nevertheless the reason that it is earlier in the verse is considered preferable, and therefore he gave another reason regarding lulav, that these three alone would also not suffice against the reason of precedence. But there is difficulty with the Levush, who also counts as an advantage that the esrog is a fruit, and this is preferable to the reason that the lulav is taller. If so, the difficulty remains that one should recite the blessing over the esrog, which is earlier. In Abudraham he wrote another reason, that the esrog resembles the heart, and the heart in the body inclines toward the left side. It is written in Sefer Imarkal in the name of Sefer HaItim that if one takes them one after another, he should also take the esrog in the right hand.
