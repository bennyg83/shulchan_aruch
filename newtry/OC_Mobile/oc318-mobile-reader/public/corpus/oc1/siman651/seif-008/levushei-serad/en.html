Concerning him he writes, “He takes the lulav and the esrog with his arm,” meaning that he takes the lulav in his hand and the esrog with his arm. This is preferable to taking both consecutively in his single hand, because they must be taken simultaneously, and it is impossible to take both simultaneously in one hand. As Magen Avraham later writes in the name of Beit Yosef citing Orchos Chaim, one would not fulfill the mitzvah in that manner. He therefore takes them simultaneously, the lulav in his right hand and the esrog with his left arm. Rokeach then adds a greater novelty: even if his left hand remains, he should proceed as before, taking the lulav in his hand and the esrog with his arm, because that hand is now his right hand and we follow his personal right side. He concludes, “or a left-handed person,” because in that case too we follow his personal right side, in accordance with Rama's ruling in seif 3.
