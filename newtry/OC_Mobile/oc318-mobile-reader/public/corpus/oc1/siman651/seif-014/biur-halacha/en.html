Because of bal tosif - There are different opinions among the early authorities. Some say that even though we say in the Gemara, Sanhedrin 88a, that according to what we maintain, that a lulav does not require binding, even if he bound the fifth species together with the four species, it is considered as though he did not bind it, and each one stands by itself, that is only regarding the fact that he does not thereby detract from the four species and he fulfills his obligation with them. Nevertheless, he at least violates the prohibition of bal tosif through this; see Tosafos, Sukkah 31b, s.v. "since." However, regarding this too there are opinions: from the Tur it implies that he does not violate it until he intends for the sake of the mitzvah of lulav; and in Eliyah Rabbah he proves from the Rosh that the Rosh holds like the Beis Yosef, and not like Rabbeinu Yerucham, that since it is in its time, he violates even without specification, unless he intends only for beauty. And some say that since each one stands by itself, he also does not violate bal tosif. Nevertheless, initially it is rabbinically forbidden according to everyone, because it appears like adding; see there in Sanhedrin, in Tosafos, and in the Maggid Mishneh and the Ran. The Gra mentioned this approach in his commentary. And even though regarding the binding we are lenient above in se'if 1, allowing one to bind it with another species even initially and we are not concerned about adding, binding is different, for he does not take it in the manner of its growth. Alternatively, there it is evident that he is not doing it for the mitzvah, but only for beauty, unlike in our matter. One who dyes the lulav green so that it should appear moist has no concern of bal tosif; Shevus Yaakov, volume 2, siman 108. In the book Eliyah Rabbah he expresses doubt about this; see there.
