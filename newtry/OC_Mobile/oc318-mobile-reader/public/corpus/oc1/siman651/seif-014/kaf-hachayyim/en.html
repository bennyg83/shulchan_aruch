14) Bind the willow on the left, myrtle on the right, and lulav in the center. This appears in Ibn Shuaib's derashos as a mystical practice. Shelah there; Taz 650; Magen Avraham here 4. Rabbi Yaakov Tzemach, however, writes: place one of the three myrtles right, one left, and one center, as in Zohar, Tetzaveh 186b. Hold the lulav with its spine facing you, so its right is at your right and its left at your left. Incline the central myrtle rightward. Lulav is Yesod, drawing from Daas through the spinal cord; the right branch is Chesed, left Gevurah, and center Tiferes, which inclines toward Chesed, so incline it rightward. The three myrtles correspond to Chesed, Gevurah, and Tiferes; the two willows to Netzach and Hod, one right and one left. He heard this from Rabbi Tzemach in the name of Rabbi Chaim Vital from the Arizal. Pri Etz Chaim, gate 29, end of chapter 1; Nagid U'Metzaveh; Magen Avraham there. Radbaz I:170 writes that this is his practice.
