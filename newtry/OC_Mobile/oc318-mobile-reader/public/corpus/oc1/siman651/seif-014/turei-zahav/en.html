He should not add, etc. In the Tur it is written: one should not subtract from them and should not add. The Beis Yosef wrote: our master's language is not precise; why did he need to write "one should not subtract," since he already wrote that the four species prevent fulfillment of one another? Granted, in the Gemara it says, "just as one may not subtract, so one may not add," and it teaches us a novelty regarding not adding, even without binding; but with not subtracting there is no novelty. It appears to me that he teaches us a novelty, that it is speaking even where one already took all four species and fulfilled his obligation with them at the time of taking, but afterward during the day, because of affection for the mitzvah, he wants to hold the lulav in his hand, as is stated below in siman 652 that there is affection for the mitzvah in this. About this he states that he should not take three species alone for the sake of the mitzvah, for he transgresses "do not subtract." In the Gemara another novelty was needed, and it did not state the novelty that we mentioned, for it says there, "just as one may not subtract, so one may not add," implying that with adding there is another novelty beyond what there is with subtracting. All this appears very correct to me. After I wrote this, I saw that the Beis Yosef challenged our master the Tur regarding what he wrote: if he holds it in his hand with them for the sake of the mitzvah of lulav, there is bal tosif. But we maintain at the end of chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din that to transgress in its time one does not need intent. That means that regarding transgressing bal tosif, one transgresses even if he did not intend for the sake of the mitzvah. Why, then, did the Tur write specifically that if he intended for the sake of the mitzvah of lulav he transgresses, since even without intent he transgresses? I said about this: rejoice, my innards, if this is so for your doubts, all the more so for certainty. For in what I wrote to settle the first difficulty of the Beis Yosef, which was from precision in the wording, this is now proven explicitly from the second difficulty from the legal side. In truth, both are answered with one matter: here too the Tur intended what I wrote, that it is speaking after the blessing, only because of affection for the mitzvah. Then certainly intent is required, as is stated at the end of chapter Ra'uhu Beis Din. And the reason our master the Tur mentioned that in this case there is bal tosif and did not mention bal tigra is because sometimes one takes even fewer than four species, namely when he does not have all of them, he takes what he has so that the Torah of lulav should not be forgotten, as is stated below. But with adding there is a prohibition if he intended for the sake of the mitzvah. And if he has all four species and takes fewer because of affection for the mitzvah, indeed he transgresses "do not subtract." The Tur took a definite case, namely adding. I saw that my father-in-law, of blessed memory, labored greatly to answer the Beis Yosef's difficulty, but this was unnecessary. What I wrote appears clear with Heaven's help. It appears to me that if he did not intend to add, meaning for the sake of the mitzvah, then he does not transgress. Nevertheless, he should be careful that even without intending for the sake of the mitzvah, he should not add, for in any event it appears like adding, and it resembles one who eats in the sukkah on the eighth day, as below in siman 666.
