Because of adding to the mitzvah. Tur writes that this applies specifically when he takes the additional species for the sake of the mitzvah. Beit Yosef asks that Rosh Hashanah 28b states that, during the proper time of a mitzvah, intent is not required in order to violate the prohibition against adding to it. Therefore, Shulchan Aruch writes without qualification that he violates the prohibition even if he did not take the additional species for the sake of the mitzvah. Beit Yosef did not see what Rabbeinu Yerucham writes in nesiv 3, part 3: that statement follows the opinion that mitzvos do not require intent. According to our ruling that mitzvos require intent, as stated in siman 60, seif 4, one does not violate the prohibition until he intends to add. The Gemara at the beginning of chapter 10 of Eruvin states the same. Maggid Mishneh and Ran maintain that one does not violate the prohibition against adding, because each species stands independently. Nevertheless, one should not initially do so, because it appears that he is adding to the mitzvah. Perhaps this is the position of Tur and Beit Yosef. When Tur states, “if he took it for the mitzvah of lulav,” his intention is to answer why binding the lulav with another species is permitted. There, the additional material is not used for the mitzvah but merely for beautification. Tosafos write that a binding is different because the additional material is not held in its manner of growth.
