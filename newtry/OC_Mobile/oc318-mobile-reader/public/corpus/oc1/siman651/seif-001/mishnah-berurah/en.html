(1) That he take - On the first day of Yom Tov it is better to go to a place that has a lulav than to go to a place where people pray, for lulav on the first day is a Torah obligation, while prayer is rabbinic. Even if it is uncertain whether he will obtain a lulav there, whereas he will certainly have prayer, on the first day he should go to the place that has a lulav. On the second day of Yom Tov he should go to the place where people certainly pray. Peri Megadim.
(2) Each person - And not that one person should take it on behalf of everyone, for it is written, "and you shall take," in the plural, and not "you shall take" in the singular, to teach that the obligation is upon the many. This is not only on the first day of Yom Tov; even on the other days, taking is required for each person. This is unlike what one person instructed in a case where there was only one esrog in the synagogue, that the prayer leader should take it on behalf of everyone and they should hear the blessing and answer amen. Responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim, siman 182.
(3) And two aravos - And if he took one branch of aravah, he must go back and take again and recite the blessing.
(4) And three hadasim - Even if all three of them are cut off, meaning their tops were cut off; and fewer than three does not help even if they were not cut off.
(5) And in pressing circumstances - Seemingly this means when he cannot obtain them from others. But if he can obtain them from others, he should take from others. In Bikkurei Yaakov he leans somewhat toward leniency in this, but it appears to me that one should be lenient in this only on the other days, and not on the first day.
(6) One that is not cut off suffices for him - See Shaarei Teshuvah in the name of Responsa Devar Shmuel, who leans toward requiring him to take it without a blessing. However, from Peri Megadim and Yeshuos Yaakov it implies that he may recite a blessing. Now the Magen Avraham brought in the name of the Ramban that his opinion is that even with one that is cut off he fulfills his obligation, and the Magen Avraham wrote that he is worthy to rely upon in pressing circumstances. In this case, however, he should certainly take it without a blessing, for in this matter even the view of Yeshuos Yaakov is not to recite a blessing.
(7) And it is a mitzvah to bind them, etc. - For even though we maintain that one is not obligated to bind them, nevertheless there is a mitzvah in this because of "this is my God and I will beautify Him."
(8) With a complete knot - And not with a bow, for this is not a knot that is forbidden on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and it is not called a binding. See Responsa Agurah BeOhalecha, who leans to say that what people commonly do, not making a knot but making from lulav leaves a kind of handle and inserting the lulav into it, is also included in a knot; see there.
(9) With another species - And there is no concern of bal tosif in this, because since there is no obligation to bind it, this one stands by itself and that one stands by itself. There is also no concern of interposition, even though the knot separates between his hand and the lulav, for anything done to beautify it does not interpose, even if the knot was made with something not of its own species.
(10) The string that is usually around the hadas - Meaning that people commonly buy the hadas branches from gentiles in bundles, and he says that one must remove the string from there, for this string is not of its own species and is not for beautifying it. But if he wrapped the hadas branches together with the lulav, it is permitted and is not an interposition, for this is called beautifying it. Levushei Serad. The Machatzis HaShekel wrote that the reason it is permitted is that all three species are considered as one.
(11) There are those who wrote, etc. - This refers to Yom Tov, for the Shulchan Aruch brought the permissibility of a bow, and the Rema, in the name of the Tur, brings the permissibility of wrapping. But what the Rema concludes, "and so is the practice," and the Magen Avraham explains that this applies even on a weekday, requires analysis. For although the Mordechai mentions the permissibility of wrapping even on a weekday, it is not in this manner, but rather that at first he ties once and afterward wraps, etc., and through this it is as though he ties two knots one upon the other. But without a tie it is no better than a bow. Afterward I found that the Gra also wonders about this. Therefore it appears, according to what is explained just ahead, that since the practice is to make three knots, the lower knot, namely the one that binds the three species together, should first be made with a tie.
(12) Higher - The reason is according to Kabbalah; see the Levush. The hadas must be tied on the right side of the lulav and the aravah on its left; Maggid Mishneh. And so is the practice. Regarding a left-handed person, whose own right is the left of ordinary people, the Peri Megadim leans to say that we do not follow his own side, but rather the ordinary side. See Bikkurei Yaakov.
(13) All three species in his hand - For even though taking through another item is called taking, as will be explained below in se'if 7, nevertheless, initially, where it is possible, we do not do so.
(14) Three knots - Corresponding to the three forefathers. It appears that the lower knots, meaning what he ties all three species together with for beauty, are counted as one, and he should make two more above if possible; Turei Zahav. But from Eliyah Rabbah it implies three knots on the lulav itself, apart from the fourth knot that binds the three species together. And if it was bound only with one binding, it is valid. The later authorities wrote that one must make it in such a way that he can rustle the leaves well, and therefore one tefach at the top must be open and not tied.
