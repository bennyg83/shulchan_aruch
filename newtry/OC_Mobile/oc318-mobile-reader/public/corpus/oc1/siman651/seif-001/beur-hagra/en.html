1 The mitzvah, etc. In accordance with Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Tarfon. And Rabbi Akiva is considered a lone opinion relative to them, and Shmuel ruled in accordance with Rabbi Tarfon.
And in a place, etc. From the fact that it says there, "Let him expound for them in accordance with Rabbi Akiva," it implies that we hold in accordance with Rabbi Akiva, but because of beautification of the mitzvah he did not want to permit it explicitly. And certainly, if he did not hold like Rabbi Tarfon and Rabbi Akiva, he would not have permitted it and caused them a loss, so that the students should not see. And Rabbi Yishmael also holds like him, as it says there: Rabbi Yishmael retracted. The explanation is that he retracted from what he said at first, that three hadasim are required, and instead one hadas that is not cut off suffices; and he mentioned two cut-off ones only for beautification of the mitzvah. This is unlike Rashi's explanation, according to which he retracted from the latter statement, and one would have to say that he did not say it and that it is not black. See the Ran.

There is one who, etc. So wrote the Mordechai. However, he did not write this except that one ties once and afterward wraps, etc., and through this it is as though there are two knots one upon the other; see there and in Hagahos Ashri. His reason is from what is stated in chapter 7 of Shabbos: "provided that he tied them." They asked: even without this, is he not liable because of tying? And they explained it as one knot together with two stitches, or as a case where he brought the two ends out to one side; one knot remains like two knots. This is also the view of Rashi in Shabbos 54a, s.v. "wrapping," etc. But the Rosh disagreed with him there and explains it as two knots. See the Mordechai there and the other authorities.
