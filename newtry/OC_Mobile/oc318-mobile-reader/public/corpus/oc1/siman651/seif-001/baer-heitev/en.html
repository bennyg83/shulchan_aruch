That is not severed. In pressing circumstances, even one myrtle branch suffices, and even if its tip is severed. Magen Avraham; see there.
The myrtle. Nevertheless, even if he bound the lulav with a material of a different species, it does not constitute an interposition, because anything added for adornment does not interpose. This is stated in the Gemara. The same applies if he bound the myrtle directly to the lulav, as is implied by Tosafos. Magen Avraham.
The custom is. Even on a weekday. Darkei Moshe; Magen Avraham.
To bind. The Arizal writes that one should bind the three myrtle branches as follows: one on the right side, one on the left side, and one in the middle. Hold the lulav with its spine facing you, its right side corresponding to your right and its left side to your left. Tilt the middle myrtle branch toward the right. Place the two willow branches, one on the right and one on the left. See Taz at the end of siman 650. Shelah writes in the name of Mateh Moshe that the willow should be on the left, the myrtle on the right, and the lulav in the middle; this is the custom.
Three knots. Corresponding to the three Patriarchs. Taz writes that the lower binding, by which all three species are tied together for adornment, is counted as one knot. He should make two additional knots higher up, if possible; see there. They must be bound in the direction in which they grow. Shelah.
