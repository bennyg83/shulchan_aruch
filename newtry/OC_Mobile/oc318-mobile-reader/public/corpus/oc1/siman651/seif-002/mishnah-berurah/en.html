(15) The right - Because these three are one mitzvah and the esrog is one. Even if he already recited the blessing over it and afterward takes it a second time, such as at the time of reciting hoshanos, he must take it in the right hand. And if he took both of them in one hand, the Orchos Chayim wrote that he has not fulfilled his obligation; but the view of the Turei Zahav is that he has fulfilled his obligation, since the esrog was not with them in one bundle. See Mateh Yehudah, who disagrees with him and whose view is like the Orchos Chayim. In practical law, one should be stringent with a Torah obligation and must go back and take it without a blessing.
(16) Their tops upward, etc. - For we require that one take them in the way they grow on the tree; and if he reversed them, even after the fact he has not fulfilled his obligation. For this reason the Maharil wrote that those who take dried hadasim that come from Italy should untie them and see that they are lying in the way they grow, for sometimes the top of one is placed next to the base of another. The Mateh Moshe wrote that even when one takes their tops from the side, it is considered not in the way they grow.
(17) And their bases downward - And even though when an esrog hangs, its stem is upward, nevertheless the way it grows is called with its stem downward, only the fruits weigh it down.
