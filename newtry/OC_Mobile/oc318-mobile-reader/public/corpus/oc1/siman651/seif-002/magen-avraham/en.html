In his right hand. Because these three species constitute one mitzvah unit, while the esrog is a single species. Gemara. Some ask why this reason is needed, since every object over which one recites a blessing should be held in the right hand, as stated in siman 206, seif 4, and in chapter 8 of Berachos concerning oil and a myrtle. One may answer that the meaning is: the lulav is held in the right hand and the esrog in the left. Why are they not both held in the right hand? The answer is that these three, etc. Meaning, the verse grouped these three species separately, for it states, “a branch of a thick-leaved tree and willows of the brook,” whereas it does not place the conjunction “and” before “branches of date palms.” See what I wrote in seif 4. Alternatively, this teaches that even if he already recited the blessing and afterward takes it a second time, he must take it in his right hand, such as during the recitation of Hoshanos. See Maharil for further discussion.
