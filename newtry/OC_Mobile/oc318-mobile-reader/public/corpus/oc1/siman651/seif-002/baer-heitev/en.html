The right hand. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Orchos Chaim that if one takes both in a single hand, he has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Taz, seif katan 14, disagrees and writes that it is obvious that if he took them in one hand, he has fulfilled the mitzvah; see there. See what Yad Aharon writes concerning this in siman 650.
Their tops. Even after the fact, he has not fulfilled the mitzvah if he inverted one of them. Taz.
In the left hand. He should first take the esrog in his left hand and afterward the lulav in his right. When setting them down or giving them to another person, he should give the lulav first, as with tefillin. In communities where the place beside the chazzan is sold, he must set down the lulav and then the esrog in some place. The person standing beside him should take the esrog from that place first, and afterward the lulav; see Shelah. If he receives them from another person, he should take the lulav in his left hand, then the esrog also in his left hand, and afterward transfer the lulav to his right hand. He should hold the lulav with its spine facing him, as written in seif katan 4 in the name of the Arizal. Shelah writes likewise. See Magen Avraham.
