Since it is not part of the essence of the mitzvah, it has no connection to the other three species of the mitzvah, and therefore there is no concern of bal tosif. Nevertheless, one might have thought that there is a concern of interposition, and if so the binding must be above the grip of the hand, and in the place where he holds the lulav in his hand there should be no binding; then there would be no interposition. Therefore the Magen Avraham teaches that even if the binding is within the hand, since the binding comes to beautify it, as above, it has no concern of interposition. So it is in the Gemara: Rabbah told those who tie and bind the lulav that they should leave the handle free and have the binding above the grip of the hand, so that there should be no interposition. Rabbah said there is no interposition in this, since the binding comes to beautify it. And we maintain like Rabbah.
And the same applies if he bound the hadas with the lulav, etc. This teaches us a novelty. Although the Magen Avraham already wrote that even if he bound the lulav with something not of its own species, it does not interpose because it is to beautify it, that is specifically when he bound the lulav, meaning that he bound all three species together, which is called beautifying it. But when he bound the hadas, this is not called beautifying it. There is proof for this from what the Rema wrote: therefore one should be careful to remove the string, etc. Therefore the Magen Avraham teaches that if he bound the hadas with the lulav, it is not an interposition, because all three species are like one. And although the Shulchan Aruch wrote that if some of the leaves fell within the bundle, etc., there is no concern, nevertheless the Magen Avraham needed to teach his point. One might have thought this applies specifically if lulav leaves fell, that they do not interpose between the lulav and the hadas and aravah, since they are lulav leaves; why should they interpose, for what difference is there whether they are attached to the lulav or fell from the lulav? But if he bound the hadas with the lulav and the aravah is adjacent to the hadas, and he did not do as the Magen Avraham wrote in subparagraph 4, which is only mere caution, one might have thought that the lulav interposes between the hadas and the aravah. Therefore he teaches that this is not considered interposition, for all three species are considered like one species.
So it appears in Tosafos, who wrote on page 37a, s.v. "so that it should not be an interposition," etc. Regarding what Rabbah said, as mentioned above, to those who bind the lulav, that they should leave the place of the hand's grip free without binding, so that the binding should not interpose between him and the hand, Tosafos wrote that Rabbah holds a species with its own kind interposes, etc. The Maharsha wrote as follows: from this matter there is no proof, etc., for one can say that it is speaking where they bound it with something not of its own species, etc.; rather, Tosafos relied on what is below regarding "do not press it," etc., where according to Rabbah it says explicitly that a species with its own kind interposes, etc., end quote. This appears forced to the Magen Avraham, for if so Tosafos should have based their words on the statement of "do not press it," and not on the statement here. Therefore the Magen Avraham explains the words of Tosafos that from the statement here too Tosafos precisely inferred that Rabbah holds a species with its own kind interposes. Since Rabbah stated plainly to those who bind the lulav that they should leave the place of the hand's grip free, he should have explained to them that only if they bind the lulav with something not of its own species do they need to leave it free; but with its own species there is nothing to it, because there is no interposition. Rather, it must be that Rabbah holds that even with its own species it interposes. The Magen Avraham still had difficulty: from where did Tosafos know this? Perhaps Rabbah always holds that a species with its own kind does not interpose, and nevertheless he warned them generally to leave the place of the handle free, even if they bind them with its own species, meaning, for example, that he binds the three species with the lulav; even so, it would be an interposition. Granted, on the side where the lulav is, the binding does not interpose between the hand and the lulav because it is a species with its own kind. But since the binding surrounds all three species, on the other side, where the aravah and hadas are, the lulav with which he bound it is a species not of their kind with the aravah and hadas, and it would be an interposition between the hadas and aravah and the hand. Rather, perforce Tosafos holds that all three species are considered like one species regarding interposition, and this is fine.
