Se'if 15: he should not take, etc. As it says there: let him take two or three, etc.; one fruit, etc. The same applies to the lulav. But regarding hadas we say there that Rabbi Yishmael retracted and validates even one, and he said three only for beautification of the mitzvah, as appears to me. It is therefore evident that one may add. And all the more so with aravah, for the Torah did not give a fixed measure, and it said "willows," only the minimum is two, and that is sufficient; but more is permitted. This is as we say regarding two witnesses: and from where do we know even one hundred? The verse states: "witnesses." See Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah 28b, s.v. "and from where," etc., and in Sukkah 37b.
And there is one who, etc. As he wrote, one may not add to them, and as explained above in se'if 14. And a wild hadas is another species, as is written in Ezra. And see below siman 664, that according to the Maggid Mishneh, anything intended for beauty is permitted even if it is another species. The Geonim, however, do not hold so, but rather follow the view of Tosafos and the Ritva in chapter 3 of Sukkah; and with another species it is initially forbidden, because he may come to be drawn after it. According to this, there is no proof from binding the lulav. See Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah 28b, s.v. "and from where," etc.
And there are those who permit. For it is not another species, since they grow in one bed and on one branch, only the Torah disqualified it; and it is no worse than fibers and the base of the palm, which are not bal tosif, as it says there, 36b. But according to the reason that he may come to be drawn after it, this requires analysis. The Ritva there also had this difficulty; see there.
