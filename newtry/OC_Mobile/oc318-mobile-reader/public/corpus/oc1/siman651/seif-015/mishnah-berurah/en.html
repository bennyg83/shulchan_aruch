(59) He should not take more than one lulav and one esrog - For it is written, "And you shall take for yourselves on the first day the fruit of a tree," etc., implying one and not two. The same applies to the lulav, for it is written "palm branches" without the letter vav, meaning one. And there are those who say that through this he also violates bal tosif. But with aravah, the Torah did not give it a fixed measure; and that which we maintain, that two aravos are required, is because it is written "willows of the brook," meaning that this suffices, for the minimum of "willows" is two. But more is also proper. The same applies with hadas, for it is written "a branch of a leafy tree," and one may also add as much as he wants.
(60) To place a wild hadas - Their reason is that it is another species and is forbidden, as above in se'if 14. Those who permit hold that it is not another species, for they grow in one bed and on one branch, only the Torah disqualified it. See above in siman 647 regarding what is called a wild hadas. It is proper initially to think that he is not binding them for the sake of the mitzvah, but only for beauty. And even though the first opinion holds that even for beauty it is initially forbidden to bind them, many authorities do not hold this way.
