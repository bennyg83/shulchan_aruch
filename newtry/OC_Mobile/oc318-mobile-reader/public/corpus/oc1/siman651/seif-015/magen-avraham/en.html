More than one lulav. Some maintain that if he takes two lulavim or two esrogim, he violates the prohibition against adding to a mitzvah; see Beit Yosef. Darkei Moshe writes that the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 states that once he placed the lulav upon the ground, it is forbidden to move it. This implies that after completing the mitzvah, if he placed it upon the ground, it may no longer be moved. This requires study, because we rule that an object does not become muktzeh for only part of Shabbos, and this lulav was fit for use during bein hashmashos. See what is written in siman 331, seif 6.
