(There, se'if 15) And if some of the leaves fell within the bundle in a way that separates, there is no concern, etc. Note: see Tosafos Yom Tov in Menachos, chapter 9, Mishnah 9, who brought in the name of Tosafos, Menachos 61, and the Rav Ovadiah of Bartenura there, that they asked why partners do not lean together because of interposition, and why the kohen places his hand beneath the hand of the owners and waves; see there. In my humble opinion it is fine. Granted, regarding partners it is considered an interposition, and we do not say that a species with its own kind does not interpose, because in any event this one is possible without that one, since it is possible to bring the offering without partnership. Therefore they are considered separate bodies and it is an interposition. But with the kohen and the owners, where the law is always this way and they need one another, and indeed even regarding a species with something not of its own kind, Tosafos wrote in Beitzah concerning water and salt in dough that since it is for improving it, it is like a species with its own kind. If so, all the more where they are inherently of the same kind and also need one another and one is impossible without the other, it is certainly considered truly a species with its own kind and does not interpose. Examine this. See Tosafos Yom Tov in Keilim, chapter 8, Mishnah 1, what he wrote in the name of Tosafos in Zevachim; from there it seemingly proves the opposite, that a species with its own kind interposes more, and is considered within its interior, while an earthenware vessel, which is not its own kind, was considered in the initial assumption not to interpose and to be only its interior. This requires analysis, for these are opposite lines of reasoning; examine it. Regarding the law whether it is permitted to purchase an esrog on Yom Tov, I wrote about this in my work some time ago. Now it appears that with this one can explain the piyyut for the second day of Sukkos, where it says: "Their taking with money is fitting for the sake of the Awesome One; they are fit to be taken on this first day." The intent is that, granted, if a borrowed one were permitted, then it would be forbidden to purchase them on Yom Tov. But since a borrowed one is invalid, and the mitzvah is specifically with his own, to buy with money, and consequently the more money he spends on them the greater the honor of the Creator, if so the acquisition itself is a mitzvah. Therefore they are fit to be taken even on Yom Tov itself. This is what it says: "their taking" "with money" is "fitting" "for the sake" of the "Awesome One"; therefore they are fit to be taken, meaning to be acquired from the seller, even "on this first day," meaning on Yom Tov itself. This is fine; examine it.
