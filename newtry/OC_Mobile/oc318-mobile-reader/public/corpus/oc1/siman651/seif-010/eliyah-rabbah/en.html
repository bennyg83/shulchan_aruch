[10] [Levush] There is one who says that he has not fulfilled his obligation, etc. So wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chayim. The Turei Zahav, subparagraph 15, challenged this from the Talmud, Sukkah 34, which says: one might have thought that the esrog should be with them in one bundle; and it did not say: one might have thought that it should be with them in one hand, end quote. In my humble opinion, one can say that this statement is according to the one who says lulav requires binding, whereas we maintain like the one who says it does not require binding. And if there is a difficulty, this is the difficulty: why did the Talmud on page 42 need to answer that it is when he turned it upside down? It should have said that it is when he took them in one hand. Rather, he has fulfilled his obligation. So too I found in Sefer Imarkal in the name of Sefer Baruch, that after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation.
