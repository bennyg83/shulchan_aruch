East, south, etc. In siman 667 I wrote what appears to me regarding Chanukah lights, in accordance with the view of the Levush in explaining "all turns that you turn," etc., except that the Levush made the matter depend on a person's natural tendency to turn, etc. Because of this he also wrote here that one should shake east, north, etc. In my humble opinion, there is no need to bring proof from nature in explaining "all turns," for if so the Sages would not have needed to mention this matter; they should have left it to human nature, for certainly he will act on his own according to his nature. Rather, the Sages warned us that you should always take the path that is to your right. In a place where right and left are before you, choose your right. Therefore, with a Chanukah lamp, you must begin immediately with what is more to your right, and afterward you are forced to turn left, because the lamps are placed that way. But here with lulav, where you immediately have right and left, we must first take the right, "the heart of the wise is to his right," and go by way of the right, which is south, because of the principle that in all turns you must turn first to the right. In the Levush he brought proof from the circling of the altar, where the ramp was in the south and the kohen would go around: southeast, then northeast, then northwest, etc.; so too should be the circling of the lulav. But he was not precise in this proof. On the contrary, from there there is proof to the opposite. There, when the kohen stood on the ramp, his face was to the north and his right was toward the southeast corner, as Rashi explained in Sukkah 48b. Therefore he took that corner first. This is unlike a person standing with the lulav facing east, where his right is toward the south; he must take what is on his right side. We do not take account here of a person's natural turning, for we choose the right side immediately. The Levush also brought from the cities of the Leviim, that they encircled east and south, etc.; and he wrote that this is not comparable to lulav, in the way of derash according to Kabbalah. We have no involvement with hidden matters, only with what is revealed to us and to our children, for this is fitting with lulav as well. Therefore there is no place to change the shaking from our custom, and one who changes has the lower hand. I later saw in Responsa Maharil, siman 40, that he also wrote as I wrote.
