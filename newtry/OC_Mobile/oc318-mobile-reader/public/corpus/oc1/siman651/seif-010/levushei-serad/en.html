Seif katan 12. Nevertheless, it appears to me. I examined every possible way to give these words a sound and acceptable explanation connected to the preceding discussion, but found none. It is clear to me that they were omitted from their proper place by mistake. Their correct location is in seif katan 8, where Shelah writes that one should therefore not hand it directly to his fellow, etc. Magen Avraham then writes, “Nevertheless, it appears to me,” meaning that he found a way for the recipient to take it directly from his fellow: he should take the lulav in his left hand. The giver has thereby released the lulav first, while the recipient has not yet received it in its proper hand, because the lulav belongs in the right hand and he took it in the left. He should then take the esrog in his left hand, its proper place. Thus, he has taken the esrog first. He then transfers the lulav to his right hand, thereby satisfying both the giver and the recipient. Magen Avraham concludes, “See Shelah in the name of Ibn Shuaib,” for it appears there that there is no concern unless he receives the lulav in his right hand. If he receives it in his left hand, as described, there is no concern. This appears correct. I did not record any of the explanations that occurred to me for leaving these words in their present location, because none seemed satisfactory.
