The hand. See Baer Heitev. Shevus Yaakov, volume 3, siman 46, wrote that one who is left-handed, doing all labors with his left but writing with his right, should apparently take the lulav in his left hand, which is his right. Even though he writes with the right hand of ordinary people, this matters only regarding tefillin, where there are opinions because it is written "and you shall bind" and "and you shall write," which implies that binding depends on writing. But regarding other matters, his right hand is the hand with which he performs all labors; what do we care about his writing? See there. It appears that if with regard to other labors he controls both hands, even though it is easier for him to do them with the left, and he writes with the right hand of ordinary people, he should take the lulav in the right hand with which he writes, since in any event he can perform all labors with that hand. See Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 128, who wrote that the words of the Rema emerge from the Baal HaItur; and in the Darkei Moshe he brings it from the Kol Bo, and it applies even to an ordinary person, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote; see there.
