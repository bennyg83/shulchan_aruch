(Subparagraph 10) Lulav, etc. He has no thumb, etc. Regarding a metzora, on the eighth day of his purification the Torah required him to bring a guilt-offering and a log of oil. It is written: "The kohen shall take from the blood of the guilt-offering, and the kohen shall place it on the ridge of the right ear of the one being purified, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot." Likewise, he must place from the log of oil on these three places. We say there in Sanhedrin that a metzora who has no thumb places it on its place, etc. In the Beis Yosef, in the name of the Rokeach, the version is: an amputee who has only a hand takes, etc.; so it should read. Therefore the Rav Beis Yosef holds that the word "only" is a scribal error, for you have no understanding of it. Based on this he wrote in the Shulchan Aruch: a person who has no hand, etc. This is according to the emendation of the Rav Beis Yosef, that the Rokeach is speaking where he has no hand at all. But in the Rokeach itself the version is: an amputee who has only one hand, etc. And the word "only" is fine, for it means that he has no left hand, only the right hand alone. He should take the lulav in the right hand, according to its law, and the esrog in the left arm. And so wrote the Beis Yosef in the name of Orchos Chayim, etc. That is to say, now that it is speaking where he has one hand, why was it permitted for him to take with the arm? Why should he not take both the lulav and the esrog in that hand that he has? Therefore he brought the words of the Orchos Chayim, who holds that in such a case he has not fulfilled his obligation.
Since it is not written "and branches," as I wrote above in subparagraph 6, that the version is reversed. That is to say, Rashi in Nazir brought the Mishnah mentioned above, about one who has no thumb, in reverse: that the first Tanna holds he places it on its place, and Rabbi Eliezer holds he has no purification. But in the Mishnah in chapter 14 of Negaim, the version is as brought in Sanhedrin; see Tosafos Yom Tov there. And he also holds like Rabbeinu Tam, etc. That is to say, now it is difficult: why did he write "it is better" than if he would take the lulav and afterward the esrog? Why is it better? On the contrary, seemingly it is more preferable if he first took the lulav and afterward the esrog, both in that one hand that he has. For now, when he takes it with the arm, granted the Rokeach holds that the version brought in Nazir is primary, that the first Tanna holds he places it on its place and we maintain like him; nevertheless the law depends on variant texts. According to the version in Sanhedrin, that the first Tanna holds he has no purification, so too here, if he took them with his arm, he has not fulfilled his obligation. If so, is it not more preferable to take them one after another in the hand he has, for in this way he fulfills his obligation according to everyone? About this he wrote that he holds like Rabbeinu Tam, etc.; and if so, taking this after that certainly does not fulfill the obligation. But when he takes with the arm, he fulfills according to the version in Nazir, which appears primary to the Rokeach as above.
But according to the Rambam, etc. For he ruled that one who has no thumb has no purification. He fulfills in this case because we do not maintain like Rabbeinu Tam.
And it is possible that here, etc. That is to say, granted, according to the words of the Rokeach, who compared the law here to one who has no thumb, it depends on whether we maintain there that he has purification or not. But it is possible to say that here is not comparable to one who has no thumb, and even the Rambam agrees that it is permitted to take it with the arm. For specifically regarding a metzora, where it is written "and he shall place it on the thumb," they disagree whether we require the verse specifically as it is written, namely on the thumb, as explained there in Sanhedrin. But here, where it is written "and you shall take," and it is not written "and you shall take in the hand," therefore the hand does not prevent fulfillment. If he has no hand, he should take with the arm.
Regarding chalitzah. For we say that a woman whose hand is cut off performs chalitzah with her teeth, and the reason is concluded: is it written "and she shall remove with her hand"? It is written simply "and she shall remove." Therefore even if she has no hand, she performs chalitzah with her teeth.
