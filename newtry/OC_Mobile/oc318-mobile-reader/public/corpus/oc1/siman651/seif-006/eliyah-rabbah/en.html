[6] On the lulav, three, etc. It implies that aside from the knot of the hadas and aravah with the lulav, one ties three knots above on the lulav. This is also reasonable, for below, the leaves do not need to be folded so much. This excludes the Turei Zahav, who wrote as follows: in the Mordechai, "kapos" is written missing a vav, meaning that for six days it is tied and resembles one palm, and on the seventh day they untie it, end quote. This is astonishing to me, for one must rustle the leaves, and this knot prevents the rustling. Therefore one must make it in a manner that he can rustle well. In any event, it appears that the knot that binds the three species together is counted as one, and he should make two more above. Until here are the words of the Turei Zahav. I also do not know what he wondered about the Mordechai, for one may say that the Mordechai holds that one need not rustle at all, as he wrote in se'if 9. He also overlooked what the Maharil wrote, that it should be open at the top only one tefach, and on Hoshana Rabbah he should untie it until halfway. Mahari Weil wrote: three bindings corresponding to the three forefathers; and lulav has the numerical value 68, corresponding to the seventy-one of the Sanhedrin. After the fact, one suffices.
