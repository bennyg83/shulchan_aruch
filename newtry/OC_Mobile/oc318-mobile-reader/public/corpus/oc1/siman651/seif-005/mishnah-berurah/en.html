(24) Before he takes the esrog - The reason is that one should not recite the blessing before he takes the lulav, for it is not reasonable to recite the blessing while the lulav is lying in a vessel. Likewise, one should not recite the blessing after he has taken all four species, for we require the blessing to be before the performance. Therefore he recites the blessing before taking the esrog, for this is before the performance, since they prevent fulfillment of one another; that is, if one of the species is lacking, according to everyone he has not fulfilled his obligation. Therefore it is called before the performance.
(25) Or he should reverse the esrog - With the stem upward, for he does not fulfill his obligation this way, since we require the way they grow, as above in se'if 2. After the blessing he should turn the stem downward, in the way it grows, and he fulfills his obligation this way. See the Beis Yosef, who gives another counsel so that it should be before the performance, namely that he should intend not to fulfill the mitzvah until he takes all of them in his hand. See the Biur HaGra, who wrote that this counsel is the choicest of all.
(26) Until he recites the blessing - And if he forgot to recite the blessing before taking it, he recites the blessing afterward, for the shaking is also part of the mitzvah.
(27) And he should recite the blessing while standing - And his taking should also be while standing. After the fact, if he recited the blessing and also performed the mitzvah while seated, he has fulfilled his obligation.
(28) Several times - For beautification of the mitzvah, like the custom of the people of Yerushalayim, as is stated in the Gemara, page 41b.
