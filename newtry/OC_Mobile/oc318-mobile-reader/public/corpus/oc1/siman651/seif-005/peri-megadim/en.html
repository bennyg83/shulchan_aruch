Before. See Taz and Tosafot, Sukkah 39a, s.v. over. The Levush takes a third approach, that one should intend not to fulfill, for in that case all agree that he has not fulfilled. The Taz answers that he may forget. The Magen Avraham, note 12, writes that this is a dispute in siman 60, seif 4. It is possible as the Bach wrote in the name of the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 589, in the name of the Raah and Rashba, that some say even if one stands and cries out that he does not want it, against his will he has fulfilled; see there. Although we do not rule this way, see Beit Yosef 589, which implies that we rule that intent is required as a stringency, and therefore he would not bless a second time. But if he explicitly protests, he certainly has not fulfilled and should bless a second time. Eating matzah is different, for in any event he derives benefit. Regarding D'Rabbanan matters, see Magen Avraham, siman 60, note 3, that D'Rabbanan mitzvos do not require intent; if so, on the second day this remedy would not help. One may say the opposite, that in D'Rabbanan matters even the Rashba and Raah agree that if he protests he has not fulfilled, for according to the Torah, fulfillment against his will is a decree of the King. I have written elsewhere regarding one who says, 'I will not take the lulav,' that he is beaten until his soul departs, whether this applies D'Rabbanan as well. In Sifsei Kohen, I believe under the rules of lashes for rebellious conduct, we wrote of this. This is not its place, and with G-d's help below in these laws something of this will be explained; see siman 656.
