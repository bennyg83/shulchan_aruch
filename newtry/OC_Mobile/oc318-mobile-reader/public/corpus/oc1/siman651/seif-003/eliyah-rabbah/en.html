[3] One should be careful to take the string, etc. This is the language of the Agudah: one should not wrap reed-grass around the hadas, for he violates bal tosif; but with a species of lulav leaves he may wrap it, end quote. This requires analysis, for we maintain like Rabbi Meir that binding is not required, and it is not bal tosif. This is why the Rema and the Levush wrote that the reason for removing the string is because of interposition, and did not write that it is because of bal tosif. So too it implies in the Talmud that anything done for beauty has no concern of bal tosif. According to the Agudah, it appears to me that one should protest against what some practice, to dye the white part of the lulav with the substance called petersil. Perhaps he holds that it has no substance upon which bal tosif can take effect, but this is forced. Also in siman 513 se'if 2, something done for appearance is not nullified; and see Tosafos, Sukkah 31, s.v. "since," etc.
