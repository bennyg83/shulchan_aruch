(18) For we follow the right and left of ordinary people, etc. - For specifically with tefillin, which is a Torah obligation, a left-handed person follows his own right. But with lulav, where taking it in the right hand is rabbinic, only because of its general importance, since it contains three mitzvos, a left-handed person is like every other person. The reason of the dissenting opinion is that it holds that we follow his own hand, as with tefillin.
(19) And if he reversed them, he has fulfilled his obligation - This means both a left-handed person and one who is not left-handed: if he reversed them and took the lulav in his left hand and the esrog in his right hand, he has fulfilled his obligation. There are those who are stringent in this, and therefore it is good to go back and take it without a blessing.
(20) And controls both hands - That is to say, he performs all labors with both of them equally. But if it is easier for him to do them with his left, even though he can also do them with his right, this is not called controlling both hands.
