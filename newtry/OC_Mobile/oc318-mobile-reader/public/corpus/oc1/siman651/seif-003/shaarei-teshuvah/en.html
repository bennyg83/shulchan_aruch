See there in the Mateh Moshe in the name of the Maharil, that during all six days the top of his lulav should be open only one tefach, and on the day of Hoshana Rabbah he should untie its binding until halfway. See Peri Megadim, who wrote: I was asked regarding the first day of Yom Tov, when there were no detached leaves from a lulav, whether it is permitted to detach from a lulav and make a binding. One may say that it is permitted, for anything detached has no prohibition; see Beitzah and siman 321, end quote. See siman 322 subparagraph 6, where from his words he appears somewhat uncertain about this, for he wrote that to cut a leaf from a lulav to bind the lulav on Yom Tov with a permitted knot has no proof from here, for it is fit to smell; see there. And the world practices stringency in this, not to detach on Yom Tov, and they prepare lulav leaves from before Yom Tov. Regarding matters that are permitted while others, etc., if he forgot to prepare and it is impossible for him to bind in another way, it seems to me that there are those who practice detaching with their teeth, for there is a change, and it is obvious that for the need of a mitzvah it is permitted in such a manner. Nevertheless, it is good to do it privately.
