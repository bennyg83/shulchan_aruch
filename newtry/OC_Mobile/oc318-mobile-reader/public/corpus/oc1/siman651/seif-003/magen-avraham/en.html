And one should take. He should first take the esrog, and when putting the species down he should first put down the lulav, similar to the order regarding tefillin. Mateh Moshe. Therefore, he should not hand the lulav directly into another person’s hand. Rather, he should put it down and then give the other person the esrog. Shelah. Abudraham writes in the name of Rav Saadia that the face of the lulav should be toward the person and its spine outward. Some hold it in the opposite manner, and the Gaon is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon. This is also ruled in Shiltei HaGibborim in the name of several great authorities. Shelah concludes that the spine should face the person; see there.
If he reversed them, he fulfilled his obligation. This implies that even a person who is not left-handed fulfills his obligation if he reversed the hands. In the Gemara, page 42, it states, “when he reversed it.” I found written in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that this means when he took the lulav in his left hand and the esrog in his right. His words imply that he did not fulfill his obligation. It is therefore proper to take them again without a blessing.
