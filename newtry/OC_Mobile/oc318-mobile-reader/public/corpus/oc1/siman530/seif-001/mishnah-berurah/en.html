(א) And permitted in some of them — the melachot permitted on Chol HaMoed are five: davar ha'aved if it will not be done now; needs of the moed [meaning for the need of ochel nefesh, which is permitted even ma'aseh uman as written at the beginning of siman 533, but other needs of the moed only ma'aseh hedyot as written in simanim 540 and 541]; for a worker who has nothing to eat; needs of the public; and ma'aseh hedyot even for an individual — each will be explained in its place. And behold regarding the fundamental prohibition of melacha on Chol HaMoed there are opinions among the Rishonim: some hold it is from the words of the Scribes because we find in the Torah they are called mikra kodesh, and it is a time of celebration in the Temple; therefore they forbade doing melacha so it should not be like other weekdays that have no sanctity at all; nevertheless they did not be stringent like actual Yom Tov and permitted in the five matters above. And many poskim hold it is from the Torah, and that which they were lenient in those matters is per the permission given to the Sages from the Torah in melacha of Chol HaMoed per the need of the matter to permit, as we expound a verse for this in Chagigah daf 18 — and this is what the Rama concluded "per the need of the matter," etc. And one must greatly beware not to be lenient more than what the Sages permitted, for they said one who treats the moed lightly [meaning who does melacha in them, as Rashi explains] is like one who worships idols. Also one must honor Chol HaMoed in food and drink and clean clothes so one should not conduct oneself on them with weekday conduct. Maharil wore the kiton of Shabbat. And see above siman 529 seif 2 and in Mishna Berurah there that "and you shall rejoice in your festival," etc. applies also to Chol HaMoed and it is explained there how to rejoice; and the Acharonim wrote that although it is clear in siman 188 seif 7 one is not obligated to eat bread specifically on Chol HaMoed and only that fasting is forbidden, nevertheless l'chatchila it is a mitzvah to establish a meal on bread once at night and once by day, since there is a mitzvah to honor it with eating and drinking and the essence of eating is bread.
