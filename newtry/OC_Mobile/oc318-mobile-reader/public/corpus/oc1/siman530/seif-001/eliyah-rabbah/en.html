[1] [Levush:] that they are permitted, etc. Nevertheless its punishment is great, for we say: one who desecrates the moadim is as if he worships idolatry. A person is obligated to honor Chol HaMoed with food and drink and clean clothing like other Yom Tov (Tanya, siman 51). The Maharil wore the kuta and matron of Shabbos. And in the Kol Bo, folio 66: and one is obligated to rejoice on it and to gladden his sons and the members of his household and all who accompany him, etc., as above in siman 529, se'if 2, regarding Yom Tov. Nevertheless eating bread on it is optional; and Magen Avraham wrote that l'chatchilah there is a mitzvah [to eat] bread. Yerushalmi: R. Abba bar Memel said: If there were someone to join with me, I would have permitted that they do melacha on Chol HaMoed; they forbade doing melacha only so that they eat and drink and rejoice and toil in Torah; and now they eat and drink and are lightheaded; thus far. And the Kol Bo wrote: it appears from this that the prohibition of jesting and lightheadedness is greater than [that of] melacha; and the intent of Hashem, blessed be He, in giving the moadim was so that one attach himself to His love and His fear and engage in His perfect Torah. And so wrote the Agudah at the end of the chapter "One who overturned": it is good to engage in Torah on Chol HaMoed. And in the Maharil: and for this reason they permitted a davar ha'aved, so that they not grieve over its loss and interrupt their learning. A sage who forbade even a davar ha'aved in a place where they did not know how to distinguish: another sage may not permit it for them (Maharashdam).
