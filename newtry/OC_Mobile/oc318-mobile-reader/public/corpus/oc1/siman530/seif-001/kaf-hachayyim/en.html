1) [Se'if 1] Certain melachos are forbidden on Chol HaMoed, etc. Tur holds that the prohibition against melachah on Chol HaMoed is rabbinic. Beis Yosef, however, cites several early authorities, some of whom hold it is rabbinic and some under Torah law. He writes: 'In my humble opinion, the prohibition against melachah on Chol HaMoed is a Torah law, as implied by Chagigah 18a, but the Torah entrusted the Sages to forbid those melachos they deemed appropriate.' See there. This also appears to be the view of Rama in the gloss, who records Beis Yosef's conclusion. Magen Avraham, subsection 1, and Shulchan Gavoah, subsection 1, write likewise. This implies that Maran holds the prohibition against melachah on Chol HaMoed is under Torah law. Shulchan Gavoah there likewise writes that below, siman 531, se'if 3, Shulchan Aruch rules according to the opinion that the prohibition is under Torah law and therefore treats a doubt stringently. See there. Bigdei Yesha writes likewise. Magen Avraham there, however, writes that siman 538 implies that Beis Yosef holds the melachos of Chol HaMoed are forbidden rabbinically. Erech HaShulchan writes likewise. See Sdei Chemed, section Chol HaMoed, subsection 17, who cites additional halachic authorities who disagree regarding whether Maran holds the prohibition is under Torah or rabbinic law. See there.
