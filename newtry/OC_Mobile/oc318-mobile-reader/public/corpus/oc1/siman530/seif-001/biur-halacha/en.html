And it is permitted in some of them — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote that some hold it is derabbanan; this is Rambam's view in chapter 7 of Hilchot Yom Tov and Rabbenu Tam and Rivba in Tosafot Chagigah 18, and Semag [and what Hagahot Maimoniyot and Shiltei Gibborim bring that Semag in the name of Behag also holds thus — I examined Behag and nothing is evident; on the contrary it implies slightly more that he follows other Rishonim who hold d'oraisa, only Scripture entrusted to the Sages to permit some as in the Gemara; and what Semag wrote derabbanan is his own words] and Riaz brought in Shaarei Halachah and Rosh and Mordechai [and what he wrote Raavan holds d'oraisa means Raavan in the name of his teachers — see there in Yereim siman 304 where he himself is uncertain and leans derabbanan; and in siman 318 he wrote there the matter is doubtful for him]. Tur and Rashbatz in Choshen Mishpat siman 211. However against this are many Rishonim who hold d'oraisa: Rif whom Tur brought in his name siman 536 that he holds d'oraisa [meaning from how he cited anonymously at the start of Moed Katan all the baraitot brought in Chagigah 18 that they learned from a verse melacha is forbidden from Torah and did not comment — see Beit Yosef who said he does not know Tur's source for this; perhaps even though he holds they are mere support he cited them; see Nehar Shalom and Nachal Eshkol who wrote Tur's words are primary per Rif]. So too from Sheiltot parashat Berachah and Eshkol and Ri tz Giat who all cited baraitot anonymously; Rashbam in chapter Erev Pesachim (Pesachim 118) who explained "one who treats the festivals lightly" refers to chol hamoed, from which Tosafot Chagigah 18 proved he holds melacha on chol hamoed is d'oraisa, and necessarily Rashi too holds d'oraisa since he explained like Rashbam in Makos 23 and Sanhedrin 99; so too Shulchan Aruch Lehem siman 223. Conclusion: Rif, Sheiltot, Eshkol, Ri tz Giat, Rashi, Rashbam, Raavan in his teachers' name, and Shulchan Aruch Lehem all hold melacha on chol hamoed is d'oraisa except what Chazal permitted; Ramban offered a compromise that every melacha not for festival need and not davar ha'aved is d'oraisa, and for festival need it is permitted even professional melacha despite extra effort; and every davar ha'aved even not for festival need is permitted despite extra effort; and Chazal from their words forbade some melachot — and the essence of chol hamoed is certainly Torah law; therefore Rashba agreed beginning of Avodah Zarah and Ritva in Mechilta, and Bach decided halachah follows this view; Magen Avraham cited likewise; and from Gra's Biur it also implies he leans this way; and in Rashba's responsum cited in Orchot Chayim Hilchot Tefillin he wrote so too is the view of French sages to forbid melacha on chol hamoed from Torah; She'agat Aryeh in Even HaEzer leans likewise; and Beit Yosef in this siman. Therefore per all these poskim, when any doubt arises in chol hamoed melacha [except matters Ramban and Rashba hold derabbanan] one must be stringent as safek d'oraisa; see below siman 537 in Biur Halacha that there are distinctions in this; and though Shulchan Aruch below siman 536 end rules like Rav Ami to be lenient against Rabbah, and siman 538 seif 1 and siman 539 seif 6 imply halachah follows the lenient view that the essence is derabbanan — perhaps he did not see all these Rishonim I brought; in any case one should not hurry to be lenient except for great need:
