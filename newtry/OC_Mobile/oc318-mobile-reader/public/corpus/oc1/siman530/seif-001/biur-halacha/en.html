And some of them are permitted. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that some hold it is rabbinic. This is the view of the Rambam in chapter 7 of Hilkhot Yom Tov, Rabbenu Tam and Riva in Tosafot Chagigah 18, the Semag, Riaz cited in Shiltei HaGibborim, the Rosh, Mordekhai, Tur, and Tashbetz part 2 siman 210. What Hagahot Maimoniyot and Shiltei HaGibborim cite, that the Semag in the name of Behag also holds this, is not evident from Behag; on the contrary, he seems somewhat more to follow the other Rishonim who hold it is Torah law, except that Scripture entrusted the Sages to permit some labors, as stated in the Gemara. The Semag's statement that it is rabbinic is his own. What is written that the Re'em holds it is Torah law means the Re'em in the name of his teachers; in Yereim siman 304 he himself is uncertain and inclines that it is rabbinic, and in siman 418 he writes that the matter is doubtful to him. Against these, many Rishonim hold it is Torah law: the Rif, as the Tur cites in his name in siman 536, because he copies at the beginning of Moed Katan all the baraitot in Chagigah 18 deriving from verses that melakhah is forbidden by Torah law and makes no comment; the She'iltot, the Eshkol, R. Yitzchak Giat, Rashbam in Arvei Pesachim, from whose explanation of "one who disgraces the festivals" Tosafot prove that melakhah on Chol HaMoed is Torah-forbidden, and by implication Rashi too, who explains like Rashbam in Makkot and Sanhedrin. So too writes Shibbolei HaLeket siman 223.
The conclusion is that the Rif, She'iltot, Eshkol, R. Yitzchak Giat, Rashi, Rashbam, the Re'em in the name of his teachers, and Shibbolei HaLeket all hold that melakhah on Chol HaMoed is forbidden by Torah law except for those labors Chazal permitted. The Ramban makes a compromise: any melakhah not needed for the moed and not a davar ha-aved is Torah-forbidden; melakhah needed for the moed is permitted even if it is skilled work and involves great exertion, and any davar ha-aved is permitted even if not needed for the moed and even with great exertion. The Sages rabbinically forbade some labors, but the essence of Chol HaMoed is certainly Torah law. The Rashba at the end of chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah and the Ritva in our tractate agree, and the Bach rules this way; Magen Avraham copies it. The Gra's Biur also seems to incline this way. In a responsum of the Rashba cited in Orchot Chayim, Hilkhot Tefillin, he writes that this is also the view of the sages of France. Sha'agat Aryeh in Avnei Miluim also inclines in practice that it is Torah-forbidden, and so in Beit Yosef in this siman. According to all these poskim, when a doubt arises in a Chol HaMoed labor, except matters that according to Ramban and Rashba are rabbinic, one must be stringent as a doubt in Torah law. See below in siman 537 in Biur Halacha, where there are distinctions. Although from Shulchan Aruch below in siman 536, and simanim 538 and 539, it sounds that he adopted the lenient view that the essence is rabbinic, perhaps he did not see all these Rishonim I cited. In any case, one should not rush to be lenient except for great need.
