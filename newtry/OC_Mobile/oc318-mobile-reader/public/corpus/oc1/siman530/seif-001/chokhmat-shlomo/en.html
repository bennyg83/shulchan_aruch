Se'if 1. Chol HaMoed is forbidden in some melachos, etc. Marginal note: Behold, regarding the law whether melacha of Chol HaMoed is according to the Torah or rabbinic, the poskim disagree about this; and the view of the Rambam is that it is rabbinic; and so is the view of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that Beis Yosef brought, who wrote that if it were according to the Torah, the Rabbis would not have been so lenient in it; see there. And in my humble opinion there appears a clear proof that it is according to the Torah, from what is written in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, folio 21b, regarding [the law that] a person should not rent a bathhouse to a non-Jew, because it is called by his [the Jew's] name and the non-Jew does melacha in it on Shabbosos and Yom Tov; and it objects: [does that imply] that to a Kuthi it is permitted? But a Kuthi also does melacha on Chol HaMoed. And [this] is difficult: what is the objection? For the cases are different: it makes sense regarding melacha of Shabbos and Yom Tov, which is according to the Torah, that they therefore decreed even against renting to a non-Jew; which is not so regarding melacha of Chol HaMoed, whose very prohibition is only rabbinic; if so, to decree further not to rent to a Kuthi would be a decree upon a decree. And especially since the Hagahos Maimoniyos himself wrote that if it were according to the Torah they would not have been so lenient, implying that he holds that since it is rabbinic they were very lenient; if so, one could say that in this too they were lenient, and why does it compare this to melacha of Shabbos and Yom Tov? Necessarily it is explicitly proven that melacha of Chol HaMoed is forbidden according to the Torah; and examine carefully. And afterward I saw in the book Yeshuos Yaakov on Orach Chaim, newly printed, that he also sensed this; see there. And with this it fits well what is difficult for us: why does it not object that the Kuthi does melacha in it on Yom Tov Sheni? And one cannot say that he holds that the Tanna is situated in Eretz Yisrael, where there is no second day of Yom Tov; for if so, what does it later object there: that his field to a non-Jew is permitted; for what reason? Because a tenant [does] his tenancy, etc.? And what difficulty is there? Perhaps the Tanna is situated in Eretz Yisrael, and therefore he could not mention his field, for the name of a field is in any case forbidden, as is explained there in the Mishnah; and therefore Rav mentioned a bathhouse, and it accords with R. Yose there that houses are permitted to rent. Necessarily the Talmud was not content to say so, for presumably the beraisa treats even outside of Eretz Yisrael. Or the difficulty for it is that it should have taught this law as well, that outside of Eretz Yisrael it is forbidden to rent him his field, etc.; and if so, it is likewise difficult regarding a Kuthi: why did it not teach us that regarding a Kuthi too it is forbidden outside of Eretz Yisrael, because of the second day of Yom Tov? Necessarily it is proven that melacha of Chol HaMoed is according to the Torah. And with this we understand what is difficult for me: [regarding] "what is he asking, what is he asking"; and would he not know that on Chol HaMoed it is permitted for a Jew to heat a bathhouse? But according to what I wrote it fits well: for behold, from this [sugya] there is a proof for the view of the Chok Yaakov in the laws of Pesach, siman 450, where the Magen Avraham wrote there that in any case on the four days that are Yom Tov it is forbidden to say "Take yours [and I take mine]" of Yom Tov, etc.; and the Chok Yaakov rejected his words, [holding] that we say so only regarding Shabbos, where kindling is forbidden for a Jew himself, and therefore they forbade renting because of a concern of mar'is ayin, that people would say he is the agent of the Jew; but on Yom Tov, where kindling for a need is permitted for a Jew himself, there is no concern that people would say he is the agent of the Jew; see there. And behold, the reason of the Magen Avraham is: granted that kindling is permitted for a Jew, that is only for the need of a Jew, but for the need of a non-Jew it is forbidden, for [the verse says] "for yourselves" and not for non-Jews; if so, if a Jew were doing [it] for the need of a non-Jew, he would be committing a prohibition; therefore one should forbid him even regarding rental to a non-Jew because of mar'is ayin. And the Chok Yaakov holds that they decreed only if there is no permission for a Jew in any way; but where there is any permission for a Jew for himself, they did not decree regarding this rental to a non-Jew; see there. And it appears that in this the questioner and the answerer would disagree: the questioner would hold that since melacha of Chol HaMoed is according to the Torah, and granted that the Rabbis permitted a bathhouse and the like, for the verse handed it to the Sages, that was only for a Jew, but for a non-Jew they did not permit; if so, for a Jew to heat a bathhouse for a non-Jew on Chol HaMoed is forbidden according to the Torah; if so, one should decree regarding rental to a Kuthi because of the aforementioned decree. And to this it answers: Chol HaMoed, since for us too it is permitted; if so, since this melacha is permitted for our own need, even though for a non-Jew it is forbidden, in any case they no longer decreed regarding this rental to a non-Jew, like the view of the Chok Yaakov regarding Yom Tov; and examine carefully well. However, according to this a new law would emerge for us: that on Chol HaMoed to heat the bathhouse for the need of a non-Jew is forbidden. However, perhaps in this very point the questioner and the answerer would disagree: the questioner would hold that for the need of a non-Jew even on Chol HaMoed it is forbidden; and the answerer answers that on Chol HaMoed it was permitted entirely, even for a non-Jew to heat the bathhouse for him; and it fits well. And in any case it appears from this a clear proof that melacha of Chol HaMoed is forbidden according to the Torah; and see in my novellae to the laws of Pesach, siman 450, what I wrote regarding these words of the Chok Yaakov; and in my novellae Avodas Avodah on tractate Avodah Zarah regarding what is written there; and examine carefully well.
