(seif 1) Chol HaMoed is forbidden in some melachot, etc. NB: Behold, whether melacha of Chol HaMoed is d'oraisa or d'rabbanan — the poskim dispute this; and the Rambam's view is that it is d'rabbanan, and so is the view of Hagahot Maimoni that the Beit Yosef brought, who wrote that if it were d'oraisa the Sages would not have been so lenient in it — see there. And in my humble opinion there appears a clear proof that it is from the Torah from what is written in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah daf 21b regarding one who may not rent a bathhouse to a gentile that is called for erev Shabbat and a gentile does melacha in it on Shabbatot and Yom Tov, and it challenges: but for a Samaritan it is permitted — a Samaritan also does melacha on Chol HaMoed. And it is difficult — why does it challenge? Behold this case is different and that case is different: granted, melacha of Shabbat and Yom Tov is from the Torah, therefore they decreed on it even to rent to a gentile; but melacha of Chol HaMoed whose prohibition itself is only d'rabbanan — if so, to decree not to rent further to a Samaritan would be a decree on a decree; and especially since Hagahot Maimoni himself wrote that if it were from the Torah they would not have been so lenient — it implies he holds that since it is d'rabbanan they were very lenient; if so, one could say they were lenient here too — and why compare this to melacha of Shabbat and Yom Tov? And necessarily it is proven explicitly that melacha of Chol HaMoed is forbidden from the Torah — requires study. And again I saw in Yeshuot Yaakov on Orach Chayim newly printed that he also sensed this — see there. And with this it fits well what was difficult to us — why did it not challenge that a Samaritan does melacha on Yom Tov, etc.; and one cannot say it holds the tanna speaks in Eretz Yisrael where there is no second day Yom Tov — for if so, why does it challenge there afterward "but his field to a gentile is permitted" — why does the tenant do his betrothal work, etc., and what is the difficulty — perhaps the tanna speaks in Eretz Yisrael; therefore it cannot cite his field, for there a field in any case is forbidden as explained there, in the mishnah, therefore Rav cites a bathhouse and it goes according to R' Yosi there that houses may be rented — and necessarily it is not satisfactory to the gemara to say so, for presumably the baraita deals even in chutz la'aretz. Or the difficulty is that it should have taught us this law also that in chutz la'aretz it is forbidden to rent him his field, etc. — and if so, it is also difficult regarding a Samaritan — why did it not teach us that for a Samaritan too it is forbidden in chutz la'aretz from the power of second day Yom Tov. And necessarily it is proven that melacha of Chol HaMoed is from the Torah. And behold from this we understand what was difficult to me that it cites to her — does it not know that on Chol HaMoed an Israelite may heat a bathhouse? But according to what I wrote it fits well: for from this is proof for the view of Chayei Adam in laws of Pesach siman 500 that Magen Avraham wrote there that in any case on the four days that are Yom Tov it is forbidden to say "take yours of Yom Tov," etc., and Chayei Adam rejected his words that we do not say so except on Shabbat, for an Israelite himself is forbidden kindling, therefore they forbade renting from the power of concern for marit ayin that they will say he is the agent of the Israelite; but on Yom Tov, where for the Israelite himself kindling is permitted for need, there is no concern they will say he is the agent of the Israelite — see there. And behold the reason of Magen Avraham is that since for an Israelite kindling is permitted only for the need of an Israelite but for the need of a gentile it is forbidden — "for you" and not for gentiles — if so, when an Israelite does for a gentile's need he does a prohibition; therefore one must forbid it even through renting to a gentile from the power of marit ayin; and Chayei Adam holds they did not decree except when there is no permission for an Israelite in any manner, but where there is any permission for an Israelite for himself they did not decree on renting to a gentile — see there. And it appears that on this the questioner and answerer will disagree: the questioner holds that since melacha of Chol HaMoed is from the Torah and since the Sages permitted a bathhouse and the like that Scripture entrusted to the Sages — that is only for an Israelite, but for a gentile they did not permit; and if so, for an Israelite to heat a bathhouse for a gentile on Chol HaMoed is forbidden from the Torah; if so, one should decree on renting to a Samaritan from the power of the above decree — therefore it answers Chol HaMoed, since for us too it is permitted; if so, since this melacha is permitted for our own need even though for a gentile it is forbidden, in any case they did not decree on renting to a gentile, as Chayei Adam's view on Yom Tov — requires study well. However, according to this a new law would be renewed for us that on Chol HaMoed to kindle for a gentile's bathhouse is forbidden. However, perhaps on this very point the questioner and answerer disagree: the questioner holds that for a gentile's need even on Chol HaMoed it is forbidden, and the answerer resolves that on Chol HaMoed it was permitted entirely even for a gentile to kindle the bathhouse for him — and it fits well. And in any case it appears from this a clear proof that melacha of Chol HaMoed is forbidden from the Torah; and see in my novellae on laws of Pesach siman 500 what I wrote on these words of Chayei Adam and in my novellae on Avodah Zarah on the tractate — see there — requires study well.
