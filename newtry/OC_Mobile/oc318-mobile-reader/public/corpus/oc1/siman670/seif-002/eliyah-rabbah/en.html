[2] And fasting, etc. The conclusion of the Bach in siman 686 is that the day before Chanukah is forbidden for fasting, and the day after it is permitted. The Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah concluded about this with this language: These are the words of the Raavach that I wrote in Kenesses HaGedolah. End quote. This is surprising, for in Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote as follows: To decree a fast on the twenty-fourth of Kislev is forbidden; Raavach, and this is obvious. The same law applies to the day after Chanukah. End quote. Thus he contradicts himself. If we say that in Kenesses HaGedolah he was speaking specifically of decreeing a public fast, that it is forbidden after Chanukah but an individual fast is permitted, then one can say that this is also his intent regarding the twenty-fourth of Kislev; how then did the Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah write that these are the words of the Raavach, etc., since he did not mention an individual fast on the twenty-third? Therefore it appears to me that in Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah he retracted what he had written in Kenesses HaGedolah. The Magen Avraham in siman 686 did not see the Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah. The Magen Avraham wrote: Some have the custom to fast before Chanukah as a replacement for erev Rosh Chodesh, and one should not change the custom; but ab initio one should not adopt this practice, and for a congregation it is obvious that they should not. See Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 47.
