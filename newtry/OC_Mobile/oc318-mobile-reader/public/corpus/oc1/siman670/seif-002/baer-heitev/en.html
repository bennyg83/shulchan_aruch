The women. Specifically women, because the miracle was performed through them; Mateh Moshe. In Maharil it is written: We have a tradition that a person should not do labor while the lamps are burning. In Mitzvas Chanukah he explained that this means as long as the synagogue lamps are burning, which is until midnight. In a place where they practiced a prohibition all day, one should not be lenient for them; Tur, Bach, and Mitzvas Chanukah. In Shiltei HaGibborim he wrote that their custom should be abolished, and it is enough on the first and eighth day. So too it is implied in Maharil; see Magen Avraham. See Taz, s.k. 2, and Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 89, who rejected his words; see there. The Bach wrote: On Chanukah it is forbidden to fast before it. As for the custom to fast on the twenty-fourth of Kislev as a replacement for erev Rosh Chodesh, one should not abolish their custom, for it is forbidden only to decree a public fast initially; but if they already practiced fasting, there is concern for the prohibition of a vow in the matter. See Devar Shmuel, siman 339. Even the day after it is forbidden; Kenesses HaGedolah.
