(1) We begin the eight days of Chanukah - For during the Second Temple, when the wicked kings ruled, they issued decrees against Yisrael, abolished their religion, did not allow them to engage in Torah and mitzvos, stretched out their hands against their money and their daughters, entered the Heichal, breached it, and made the pure things impure. Yisrael suffered greatly because of them, and they oppressed them with great oppression, until the God of our fathers had mercy on them, saved them from their hand, and rescued them. The sons of the Chashmona'im, the kohanim gedolim, prevailed, killed them, and saved Yisrael from their hand. Sovereignty returned to Yisrael for more than two hundred years, until the second destruction. When Yisrael prevailed over their enemies and destroyed them, it was on the twenty-fifth of Kislev. They entered the Heichal and found no pure oil in the Mikdash except one jug that was placed with the seal of the Kohen Gadol, and it had enough to light for only one day. A miracle was performed, and they lit the lamps of the arrangement from it for eight days, until they crushed olives and extracted pure oil. Because of this, the Sages of that generation enacted that these days, beginning on the twenty-fifth of Kislev, should be days of joy and Hallel, and that we light lamps on them in the evening at the entrances of the houses each and every night of the eight nights, to show and reveal the miracle. This is a mitzvah from the words of the scribes, like reading the Megillah. These days are called Chanukah, meaning: they rested on the twenty-fifth, for on the twenty-fifth they rested from their enemies. Because they are days of joy and Hallel, eulogizing and fasting are forbidden on them. The poor have the custom to go around to the doors on Chanukah, and there is a reason for this.
(2) And they are forbidden in eulogy and fasting - See below, siman 686, se'if 1, in Mishnah Berurah.
(3) The women - Specifically women, because the miracle was performed through them, as below in se'if 2 in the gloss. There are places where men too are stringent about this.
(4) While the lamps are burning - In his house, in order to recognize that it is forbidden to use its light, and this is about half an hour.
(5) One should not be lenient for them - For this is like things that are permitted but others practiced them as forbidden, which one may not permit in their presence. The Shiltei HaGibborim wrote, and Responsa Chacham Tzvi agreed, that in a place where they practiced a prohibition against doing labor all day, one should protest against them, because idleness is a transgression and brings a person to boredom.
