On the twenty-fifth of Kislev. The Avudraham asked in the name of the Baal HaIttur: why do we not make nine days of Chanukah, just as we make nine days for the festival of Sukkos, the ninth because of doubt that it is the eighth? He answered that the festival of Sukkos is according to the Torah, and our Rabbis were stringent regarding its doubt because we no longer establish the calendar by sighting, whereas Chanukah is of rabbinic origin; they said to make eight days of Chanukah, and they said the calculation of the molads. End quote. Maharer Eliyahu Mizrachi, in his novellae on the Semag, wrote as follows: One should wonder why nowadays the custom is not to light for nine days out of doubt, as on Yom Tov, for Megillah, although it is rabbinic, should have required reading on the fourteenth and fifteenth because of doubt, were it not that perhaps the fourteenth was established in its proper time and it is not on the fifteenth because of 'and it shall not pass,' as the Mordechai wrote in Megillah. End quote. Perhaps this is also the intent of our teacher, who wrote: on the first night one lights one, and from then onward he adds one each night, until on the last night there will be eight. One should be precise about the lengthy language; it was not enough for him to write, 'from then onward he adds one each night.' Rather, one can say that in this language he taught us that even nowadays one need light only on eight nights, and we do not say that nowadays it would be proper to light nine nights because of doubt, as on Yom Tov where we add one day because of doubt. Therefore he wrote, 'until on the last night there will be eight,' and not nine; Maharansh, of blessed memory. Chanukah is an acronym for eight lamps and the halachah follows Beis Hillel; Avudraham.
