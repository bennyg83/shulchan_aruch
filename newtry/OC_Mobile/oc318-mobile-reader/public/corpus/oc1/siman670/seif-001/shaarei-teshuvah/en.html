Chanukah. See Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 137, in resolving the words of the Bach, who wrote that the jug of oil they found was surrounded by a sealed cover. They challenged him, for it is proven that it was impure through moving. He, of blessed memory, answered that Rashi's view in Shabbos, folio 85, is like the Bach. See Tosafos there, who challenged Rashi, and see Tosafos there on folio 85. See Mor U'Ketziah, who also discusses this at length. See the words of my grandfather in Bechor Shor on Shabbos, what he wrote about this against the words of the Re'em. See Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 41, Chacham Tzvi, siman 87, and Pnei Aryeh, siman 40. See Eliyah Rabbah, who challenged what is explicit in Megillas Taanis, that they covered it with wood, meaning actual wood; this is not so, for it means tin, as Rashi wrote in Avodah Zarah, etc. Shaar Ephraim already preceded him in this; see there. In my commentary to Pesikta Rabbasi, in the passage of Chanukah, I wrote to resolve this according to what we hold, that coverings of vessels are pure, even gift coverings; on the contrary, the vessel is subordinate to it, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 4 of Hilchos Keilim. Although the Rambam speaks of a wooden vessel that has a receptacle, it appears that one should not distinguish between a wooden vessel that has a receptacle, which although it receives impurity becomes pure through the covering, and two types of metal. The general rule is that coverings of vessels are pure; see there, and see at the end of Chagigah regarding this.
The women; see Baer Heitev. See there in Chacham Tzvi, who holds that one should protest against them when they practice not doing labor, because idleness is a transgression, etc., and it is not comparable to Rosh Chodesh, which is called a moed and has a Mussaf offering; see there. What the Baer Heitev wrote in the name of the Bach regarding fasting on erev Chanukah, see below, siman 686, in the Magen Avraham there. See Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 43, who agreed in practice with the Bach, to forbid fasting before Chanukah. See Responsa Har HaCarmel, siman 10, regarding one who fasted for several years on erev Chanukah, that it is a mistaken vow, etc. In my humble opinion one should not be lenient in this, since release is possible through regret, that had he known there is a prohibition in this, he would not have fasted on this day. See Pri Yitzchak, who wrote that it is permitted to decree a public fast even in a place where the custom is not to fast on erev Chanukah, and there is one who disagrees; see there.
