1) [Seif 1] On the 25th of Kislev, the eight days of Chanukah begin, etc. What is Chanukah? For it was taught in a Baraisa, Shabbos 21b: On the 25th of Kislev, the days of Chanukah are eight; one may not eulogize on them, nor decree a fast on them. For when the non-Jews entered the Sanctuary, they defiled all the oils in the Sanctuary. When the Hasmonean dynasty prevailed and defeated them, they searched and found only one flask of oil that was sealed with the seal of the Kohen Gadol. It contained only enough to kindle for one day, but a miracle occurred with it and they kindled from it for eight days. The following year they established those eight days as festivals, with Hallel and thanksgiving. This means that Hallel is recited on them, and thanksgiving is expressed by saying Al HaNissim in the blessing of thanksgiving. Tur. One may ask: What difference did it make that it was sealed with the seal of the Kohen Gadol? Granted, even had they touched it externally it would not have become impure, because an earthenware vessel does not contract impurity from its exterior, only from its interior. Nevertheless, we should be concerned that they moved it, causing it to become impure. Tosafos there already answered that, assuming the decree that non-Jews have the status of zavim had already been enacted, one must say that it was set into the ground under a seal, so that they did not move the vessel. The Ran answered that had they moved it, they would have broken it to see whether it contained gold or pearls, since they saw that it was sealed with the seal of the Kohen Gadol. Rather, they certainly did not find it at all and did not see it. Beit Yosef. See the Bach, who wrote additional answers. Regarding the reason the flask was sealed with the seal of the Kohen Gadol, see Machazik Berachah, Kuntres Acharon, os 2, who wrote in the name of the work Kaf V'Naki that their practice was to place the oil in small flasks, each flask containing the amount needed for one night's kindling, and all of them were sealed with the seal of the Kohen Gadol; see there.
1) The reason they needed to kindle for eight days from that flask was that all the Jewish people were presumed to be impure through contact with the dead, and it was impossible to prepare pure oil until seven days had passed from the day of their impurity, plus one day to crush the olives and prepare them so as to extract pure oil. The Ran wrote that they had pure oil at a distance of four days' travel, and they therefore needed eight days for the journey there and back. One may ask why they established eight days, for since the oil in the flask was sufficient to kindle for one night, it follows that the miracle occurred only on seven nights. One can answer that they divided the oil in the flask into eight portions, and each night they placed one portion in the Menorah, yet it burned until morning. It follows that a miracle occurred on every night. One can further answer that after they placed oil in the lamps of the Menorah, the flask remained as full as it had been initially, and the miracle was thus recognizable even on the first night. Alternatively, on the first night they placed all the oil into the lamps and it burned throughout the night, and in the morning they found the lamps filled with oil; so too on each and every night. Beit Yosef. However, the final explanation may be challenged, because the Baraisa cited above implies that the miracle occurred in the flask and not in the lamps. Rather, one can say that when they placed oil in the lamps on the first night and some remained in the flask, it was blessed at the moment it was poured. It therefore follows that the miracle occurred on the first night as well. Likewise, on the final night, although the flask was not full, its contents sufficed, so the miracle also occurred on the final day. Yet one may ask: Since they entered the Sanctuary, we should be concerned that they touched the Menorah and rendered it impure, just as we were concerned regarding the oils that were there. If so, what benefit was there in pure oil, since when they placed it in the Menorah it became impure? However, see tractate Avodah Zarah 43a, where it is written that they made a Menorah of wood; see there. This implies that they made a new one. As for their touching the oil and kindling the Menorah, not all of them were impure through contact with the dead. Rather, for some reason they simply had no pure oil, as the Maharsha wrote in Chiddushei Aggados on the Gemara there.
