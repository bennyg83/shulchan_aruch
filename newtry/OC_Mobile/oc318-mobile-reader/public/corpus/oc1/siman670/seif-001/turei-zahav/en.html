Eight days of Chanukah. The Tur brought: the following year they established them as eight Yamim Tovim. The Beis Yosef asked why they established eight days, since the oil in the jug was enough to light the first night, so it turns out that the miracle was only for seven days. He answered with three answers; see there. All of them are difficult, for we have no hint that this happened, and if so our Rabbis should have mentioned this matter so that we would know the miracle that happened on the first night. It appears to me to answer that retroactively we see that on the first night too there was a miracle. We find in the Zohar on the verse, 'What do you have in the house,' that He, blessed be He, does not perform a miracle to give blessing except in something that already exists in the world; even if it is a small thing, then He, blessed be He, gives blessing to increase the little. But with an empty thing, blessing does not apply to create a new being. Therefore here it works well: if everything that existed had burned on the first night, there would have been no place for the miracle to take effect on anything. Rather, certainly even on the first night some of what was fit for it remained, and from it some remained; on that remainder the blessing came, and there a miracle occurred to increase it. If so, we see that on the first night too a miracle was performed.
That one should not be lenient for them. This is the view of the Tur, because things that are permitted but others practiced as forbidden may not be permitted in their presence. He wrote further: even if there are places where they practice a prohibition the whole day, one should not be lenient for them. The Beis Yosef wrote that this is not compelling, since there is no aspect of prohibition here. It appears to me to compare it to Rosh Chodesh at the beginning of siman 417, where by law labor is permitted, and nevertheless, since the women practice not doing labor, it is a good custom, and this is their reward for not giving their earrings for the calf, as the Tur wrote. We say about this: things that are permitted but others, etc., as the Beis Yosef wrote there. The same applies to Chanukah: since the miracle was performed through the women, it is proper for them when they practice not doing labor. But men certainly have no connection to this custom. In Maharil he wrote: We have a tradition that a person should not do labor while the lamps are burning, and some say that on the first day and on the last day the custom of our teachers is also to forbid labor.
