Se'if 1. On the twenty-fifth of Kislev we begin the eight days of Chanukah, etc. Note: Regarding what the Beis Yosef asked, why do I need eight days, since seven would suffice, and he answered that on the first day a miracle immediately happened, that the jug was refilled anew. The Pri Chadash objected: if so, why was the miracle at the end of the eighth day, since it had already happened the previous day? In my opinion one can say that the Beis Yosef's intent is this: immediately when they began to pour a little from the jug into the Menorah, it returned and was refilled at once. This happened each and every time; whenever they poured out a little, it returned and was refilled. If so, now we do not know whether what they lit on the first day was also from what remained by way of nature and that they left over what came by way of miracle, or the opposite, that they lit from what existed by way of miracle and left what existed by way of nature for the second day. This same doubt existed every day. Therefore one cannot say on the first day that a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently, and that it was lit by way of nature, because then on the eighth day we would say the opposite, that perhaps then it was lit from the miracle of what remained naturally, and the first day was by way of miracle. Since these are two rulings that contradict one another, we hold that we do not say in such a case that a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently. Therefore we light on both, and it works well; analyze carefully. Now the commentators asked: according to the one who says that impurity is permitted for a public offering, why was the miracle of Chanukah necessary, that the oil remained pure? It seems to me that I wrote about this somewhere, and now it appears to me as follows. One should be precise in the language of the Talmud in Yoma, folio 6, that according to the alternative version, which says that even where there are pure kohanim in that beis av they disagree, because all impurity was permitted by the Merciful One, why did the Talmud not say that even if there are pure kohanim in the congregation, the impure ones may perform the service? Perforce it is proven that even according to the alternative version, this is specifically if there are pure kohanim in that beis av but they are not now in the Azarah. But if they are now in the Azarah, it is certainly better that pure kohanim perform the service, and we do not leave the pure ones in order for impure ones to serve. Therefore here, since a pure kohen was found in the Azarah, it was certainly preferable to avoid impurity, and therefore this was the miracle of Chanukah. Furthermore, even without this it appears that even according to the one who says impurity is permitted for the public, that applies to the actual service, which we learn from 'a man' is deferred but the public is not deferred. But to inaugurate the Beis HaMikdash initially, certainly the initial inauguration must be with purity. Therefore then, since the service had first ceased and now the service began anew, the beginning of the inauguration certainly needed to be with purity. Therefore the miracle of Chanukah was needed so that the beginning of the inauguration would be in purity, as appears to me. See further what I wrote in resolving this difficulty in my work on Orach Chaim from the year 5612, in my responsum that acquired its place in the laws of nesias kapayim, siman 128; see there.
