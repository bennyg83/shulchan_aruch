That they did not. See the Taz. With the Megillah the miracle is publicized and recognizable to all on that night, with many miracles. See the Rama in Megillah, that it was not in any way according to nature at all. This is unlike the Chanukah lamp through Yehudis, who cut off the head of the enemy: although everything is from Him, blessed be He, it was not so publicized to all. See siman 219, Taz, letter 6, that a miracle is something evident and recognizable, that goes outside the pattern of the world, like 'place it on a pole,' etc. According to this, 'Who performed miracles' with the Chanukah lamp is only over the oil, not over the war. In Shabbos 23a, 'what does he omit? Time. Let him omit miracle, for there is a miracle every day.' From Rashi it implies that the one who lights recites all three, for with one who sees, the question of omitting from the larger number does not apply, only which blessing he recites; this is unlike one who lights. The questioner thought that the miracle of the war relates to their bodies, but the miracle of the oil relates to the soul, and one does not recite a blessing over it. The answerer replied that 'time' applies only to the oil. If so, the questioner thought the miracle occurred only once, that they divided the oil into eight days in eight vessels and all were refilled at once; see the Beis Yosef for something similar. The answerer replied that the miracle occurred every night, and this is what Rashi, of blessed memory, wrote, that all eight days they lit from the jug. Understand this. Regarding what the Taz wrote against the Levush, see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 14, that this is by consent, unlike one who causes him to sin under compulsion, etc.; see there.
