(11) And one does not fast - As above in se'if 1. If one transgressed and fasted on the day his father or mother died, or for a Torah scholar, he must sit a fast for his fast after Chanukah. See Derech HaChaim regarding what he wrote about this.
(12) See above in the laws of Rosh Chodesh, etc. - For there he wrote that one does not say Tziduk HaDin on a day when Tachanun is not said, and similarly below in siman 683; see there. But before and after them, it appears that one should not be stringent regarding Tziduk HaDin; see below, siman 686, for regarding eulogy itself many are lenient. Even if we wish to be stringent like some poskim who are stringent about eulogies before them, and consequently Tziduk HaDin also should not be said, in any event after them certainly one should not be stringent and may say Tziduk HaDin. So too wrote the Pri Megadim. Mourning applies on Chanukah according to everyone. An onen is exempt from the Chanukah lamp. The Eliyah Rabbah wrote that his wife should light. Whether he may answer amen is subject to differing views among the poskim; see Yoreh Deah, siman 341. It appears that if his wife is not with him and he is alone in the house, he should light the lamps for publicizing the miracle, without a blessing; Pri Megadim. A deaf person who cannot hear but can speak is obligated in the Chanukah lamp, for he is like a competent person in all respects; there.
