Seif 1: Even one who is not careful with gentile bread during the Ten Days of Repentance must be careful. In my opinion: Behold the Tur brought Yerushalmi Rosh Chodesh: the rabbi commands the rabbi — if you can eat all year in purity, eat; if not, eat seven days in the year; and Ibn Ezra wrote these seven days are between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur and calls them seven days because on Rosh Hashanah he need not be careful to eat in purity, for it is obvious he eats in purity, for a person is obligated to purify himself on the festival, etc. And I was asked by one sage that the Tur's words contradict each other, for below in laws of Yom Kippur the Tur wrote in siman 606, and if because R' Yitzchak said a person must purify himself on the festival — that would mean purify from all tumot including tumat met and sprinkle on him third and seventh — and nowadays we have no tumah and taharah, etc., end of his words — if so the Tur holds there is no obligation nowadays to purify on the festival; why in this siman did the Tur not challenge this? And it appears wondrous; to resolve this it appears: this obligation to purify on the festival the Sages learned from the verse "and in their carcass you shall not touch"; and according to this the negative includes two matters: one, if they were pure — not to make themselves tamei anew on the festival, as the simple meaning of the verse "and in their carcass you shall not touch" implies obligation not to make themselves tamei anew; and second, if they were already tamei they are obligated to purify on the festival, from the gemara's rule follow the reason — since the Torah was strict not to become tamei on the festival it proves He wanted all pure on the festival; if already tamei, fitting to purify on the festival. And therefore this obligation to purify on the festival from what was already tamei applies only if possible to purify completely; but if impossible to purify completely they are entirely exempt. Only to make oneself tamei anew on the festival — obviously even if already tamei one must be careful not to become tamei anew, similar to a kohen whom the Torah warned not to become tamei for a dead person — and if already tamei, is it permitted during his tumah days or on the day he became tamei to become tamei again for a dead person? Certainly not, and he is lashed for this. And it is true one may distinguish: a kohen is Scriptural decree without reason; but on the festival the Omnipresent wanted purity for eating kodshim — if tamei anyway not fit for kodshim, why not become tamei more? This answers those who hold the reason is kodshim; but for them Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are not festivals for this reason. And if so Ravyah, Ran, and Tur — it appears from their words that Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are included like festivals — necessarily the Rambam's reason from kodshim is not accepted; rather it is Scriptural decree; and if so it is truly like a kohen: even if tamei they are warned not to become more tamei, like cutting one hair after another and causing chametz after chametz where one is lashed — in cutting hair the reason is not to annul him from priesthood, etc., yet one is liable for hair after hair and chametz after chametz even if already leavened; so too becoming tamei after tamei on the festival violates "and in their carcass you shall not touch"; and this applies even nowadays. Therefore since one who eats tamei foods becomes tamei in his body, if he does not eat non-sacred food in purity the foods will return and make him tamei — even if rabbinic tumah, nevertheless on the festival one is warned as with all matters the Sages enacted like Torah enactment; therefore also rabbinic tumah one is warned not to make oneself tamei on the festival. Therefore in Rosh Hashanah laws dealing with eating non-sacred food in purity — meaning warned not to add tumah upon his tumah — this applies even nowadays per the Tur's view that the reason is not kodshim but Scriptural decree, and Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are included; but in Yom Kippur laws dealing with immersing from his old tumah, there the Tur properly wrote here that since he cannot purify from all tumat met he is not obligated to purify at all. So it appears to reconcile so the Tur's words do not contradict each other — examine and inquire. (there, in the gloss) And doubtful sin requires more repentance than certain sin, etc. In my opinion: see Magen Avraham note 1 what he wrote — one who sent a messenger and he was killed on the way must receive repentance, see there. It appears this is specifically if he began with the messenger to ask him to go on his mission; but if he did not intend this and the messenger asked him to send him, then he is exempt from repentance, for the source of this law is from the gemara that the Omnipresent said to David: through you Nov city of priests was killed — there David began the killing and caused it; but if the messenger began with him we have no proof from there, and therefore you have only its novelty. And so I answered in responsum to the community of Yagelnitza, see there, examine and inquire.
