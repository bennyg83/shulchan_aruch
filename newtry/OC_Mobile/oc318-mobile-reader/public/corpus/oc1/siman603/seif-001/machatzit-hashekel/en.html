The Magen Avraham wrote: And if, etc. It is enough to throw in a chip of wood. As written in Yoreh Deah, siman 112, seif 9, and the Rema wrote there that even if he blew with bellows or with his mouth, it helps. The Pri Chadash wrote there that even if the coals are glowing and the wood is burning, blowing helps, for through the blowing he adds heat and hastens the baking of the bread. Know that during the Ten Days of Repentance they practice prohibition even with bread of a non-Jewish baker. And they should be careful, etc., unless he sold them the flour. So too it is written in Teshuvas Masas Binyamin, siman 1. The reason is that the obligation of challah comes at the time the dough is kneaded, and then it was dough of a non-Jew and exempt from challah. Even if afterward he sold it to a Jew, nevertheless it remains exempt, since at the beginning of its obligation it was exempt. He brings proof from Mishnah, chapter 3 of Challah, which is in Yoreh Deah, siman 330, seif 4: a non-Jew who had dough and converted; if he kneaded it before he converted, it is exempt, etc. See what the Shach wrote about this at the beginning of siman 330 in the name of the Derishah, explained in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 112: namely, if Jewish bread can be found within four mil, one must wait for Jewish bread.
For they did not accept, etc. More than the rest of the year. That is to say, one who is not careful all year and relies on the opinion of the lenient authorities in Yoreh Deah did not accept upon himself, during the Ten Days of Repentance, to be more careful than one who is careful all year with non-Jewish bread. The Pri Chadash wrote there as follows: This applies specifically when he has begun traveling and from there until his place of encampment there are four mil. But if he is now encamped in one place, even though from this place to the first encampment where there is Jewish bread there are four mil, nevertheless he is not now traveling there and need not wait.
And the Shelah, etc. See siman 280. One who hired, etc. It is proper that he accept teshuvah, from what the Holy One, blessed is He, said to David: Through you, Nov, the city of kohanim, was killed. This shows that in such a case it was considered a sin for David; see there.
