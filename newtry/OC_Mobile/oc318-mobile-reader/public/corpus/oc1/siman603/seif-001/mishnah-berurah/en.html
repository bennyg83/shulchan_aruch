During the Ten Days of Repentance one must be careful. This means even with bread of a non-Jewish baker. If he cannot bake himself, he should make the oven in which the non-Jews bake acceptable by having a Jew throw one chip of wood into the oven. Even if he blew with bellows or with his mouth, it helps, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 112, seif 9. Nevertheless, one who is traveling, if there is no Jewish bread before him within more than four mil from the place where he stands and he wants to eat, may eat bread of a non-Jewish baker during the Ten Days of Repentance, just as it is permitted during the rest of the year, as written there in Yoreh Deah. For they did not accept upon themselves to be stringent during the Ten Days more than one who is careful all year, and even so it is permitted in this manner. They should be careful not to recite the blessing and separate challah from dough that initially belonged to a non-Jew, even if afterward he sold it to a Jew, for since at the time of kneading it belonged to a non-Jew, the obligation of challah was removed from it; only where the non-Jew sold the flour to the Jew is it obligated.
And every person should, etc. The Rosh wrote that during these days one should read the Iggeres HaTeshuvah of Rabbeinu Yonah. The Arizal wrote that there is an obligation to study mussar books the entire year, and so too the Gra wrote in Mishlei in several places and in Maaseh Rav. At least during these days, every person should act according to the view of the Zohar: before he lies down, he should repent, lament his sins, and examine his deeds. See Yaaros Devash, part 1, derush 1, that the seven days between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur correspond to the seven days of the week; on each day one should repent for that day. For example, on Sunday he should repent for whatever he sinned all his life on Sunday, and likewise on Monday, and so for all seven days.
To search and examine his deeds. See Chayei Adam, klal 143, where he expanded on this matter, and his words are healing for the eyes.

One who hired his fellow to travel and he was killed: it is proper that he accept teshuvah; Be'er Sheva, chapter Chelek, and see Teshuvos Maharam of Lublin, simanim 43, 44, 45; Givas Shaul, siman 48 and siman 82; and see Beis Yaakov, siman 10. Regarding a woman who caused a child to die indirectly, see Even Shoham, siman 40. See Teshuvos Chasam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 52, regarding a mohel, and regarding a woman who found a child dead; and in siman 184 regarding one who under compulsion ate forbidden foods and then emerged from the compulsion; in siman 173 regarding acts of teshuvah for one who cohabited with a niddah; siman 175 regarding one who was caught with a non-Jewish woman; and siman 177 regarding one who wanted to save someone and mistakenly killed his fellow. See Shaarei Teshuvah and Pischei Olam regarding these laws. See in the Rif the twenty-four things that impede teshuvah.
