For although the Tur gave a reason for Ashkenazi siddurim that have this blessing "afapeh chadashim lebokerim," etc., nevertheless since the blessing is not in the Gemara, therefore one should not say it — and so Maharshal in responsa siman 64. And behold in Semag he also counted this blessing, and Rama already testified to the custom among Ashkenazim that there is to say it, and nothing is after the custom. And proof for this that one may rely on custom: I find the Rosh's words contradictory — in the chapter after Bechoros and in the first chapter of Kiddushin he cited the blessing said on pidyon habechor "who sanctified the fetus from its mother's womb," etc., and concluded on this that in Spain and Ashkenaz they did not practice this blessing, for we find no blessing not mentioned in the Talmud, for after Rav Ashi's arrangement we find no new blessing instituted. And in the first chapter of Kesubos he brought the blessing of virgins "like a nut in its shell," etc., and wrote on this: it is possible the Geonim instituted this blessing, and it is reasonable to bless when he found virgins — end quote. Behold he contradicted himself, saying a blessing not mentioned in the Gemara cannot be — and it cannot be reconciled except thus: if there is already a custom to say that blessing, one should not abolish it even though not mentioned explicitly in the Gemara, for perhaps the Geonim who instituted it had support from the Talmud. Therefore the Rosh in Bechoros did not come to abolish that blessing where they practiced saying it, but gave a reason for Ashkenaz and France that it is impossible — unlike the blessing of virgins, whose custom is to say it; therefore he did not abolish it, as above, to reconcile the Rosh's words. And this is proof for what he wrote regarding the blessing "hanoten laya'ef koach" where there is custom. And one need not challenge: what help is custom, since there is concern of a blessing in vain? This is not so, for Rabbeinu Tam already proved, and the Rosh brought in the first chapter of Kiddushin, that there is no violation of "you shall not take [God's name in vain]," since he mentions the Name through a blessing even though not obligated — and he brings clear proofs on this. Therefore it appears the custom here suffices and one should not abolish the blessing; unlike the blessing "mageiv shefalim" that Beit Yosef mentioned, which is not in the Gemara — regarding this there is no custom at all to say it, although in Agudah he mentioned this blessing and wrote he saw it in some Talmud books: when one lifts his head he says "mageiv shefalim." And for this reason Machatzis HaShekel wrote one should not remove it from siddurim; unlike the blessing "somech noflim," which was not mentioned at all in any book — that one should remove from siddurim. And it appears that since there is no clear custom even in mageiv shefalim, one should not enter himself into doubt and not say them — and as written here in the following seif, so is the main point.
