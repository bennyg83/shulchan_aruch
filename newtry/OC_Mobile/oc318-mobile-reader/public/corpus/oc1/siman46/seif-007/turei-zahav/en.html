Their words do not appear correct. Tur explains that Ashkenazic siddurim include HaNosen LaYaef Koach based upon the verse, “New each morning.” Nevertheless, because the blessing does not appear in the Gemara, Beit Yosef rules not to say it. Maharshal, responsum 64, writes likewise. Semag also lists this blessing, and Rama testifies that Ashkenazic communities recite it. An established custom should not be disregarded. Rosh's rulings appear contradictory. In Bechoros and Kiddushin he records a blessing upon redeeming a firstborn and concludes that France and Germany did not recite it because no post-Talmudic blessing should be instituted. Yet at the beginning of Kesubos he records the blessing upon finding virginity and writes that the Geonim may have instituted it and that it is reasonable to recite it afterward. The resolution is that where an established custom already exists, the blessing should not be canceled even if it is not explicitly in the Gemara. The Geonim may have possessed a Talmudic basis for it. Rosh did not intend to cancel the firstborn blessing where it was customary, but merely explained why France and Germany did not say it. The virginity blessing already had an established custom, and he therefore did not cancel it. This supports reciting HaNosen LaYaef Koach where a custom exists. There is no concern of violating “You shall not take Hashem's Name in vain.” Rabbeinu Tam, cited by Rosh in Kiddushin, proves that mentioning Hashem's Name in a blessing does not violate that prohibition even where no obligation exists. The custom is therefore sufficient basis for this blessing. Magbiah Shefalim is different. Beit Yosef writes that some recite it although it is absent from the Gemara. Agudah reports that it appeared in some Talmudic texts, and Bach therefore writes that it should not be removed from siddurim. Since there is no clear custom, however, one should not enter the doubt and should omit it. Somech Noflim, which appears in no source, should certainly be removed. This is the primary ruling.
