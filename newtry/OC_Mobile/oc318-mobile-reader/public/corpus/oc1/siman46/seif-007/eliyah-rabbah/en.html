(7) The unlearned people, etc. This somewhat implies one who is not proficient. Tur likewise implies this. Bach writes: “They did not rely upon answering amen when there was a danger of death,” etc. Although Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah cites Bircas Avraham that they fulfill their obligation, and below, siman 58, seif 4, concerning Yotzer Or likewise implies this, one should nevertheless warn people in accordance with Bach. If he hears the blessings from another before reciting them himself, he should intend not to fulfill his obligation, but should still answer amen. Levush writes that although all blessings over mitzvos must be recited before performance, these are blessings of praise and may be recited afterward. This requires study. See below, siman 224, concerning the blessing upon seeing a king, which cannot be recited afterward even though it is a blessing of praise. Ran at the end of the first chapter of Pesachim explains that these blessings are different because they concern the world's regular order. Shelah writes that reason nevertheless dictates that one recite them as close to washing as possible and not interrupt unnecessarily between the washing and the blessing.
