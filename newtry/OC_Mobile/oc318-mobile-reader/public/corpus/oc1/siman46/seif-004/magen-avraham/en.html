Who did not make me a gentile. So is the correct text; the reading 'who made me a Jew' is a change from the printers. Bach wrote: some ask why one does not bless 'who made me an Israelite' like the other blessings one blesses on the good—'who opens the eyes of the blind' and the like. They answer: it is preferable for a person not to have been created than to have been created; therefore they say, 'If only I had not been created,' and now that I was created I bless that He did not make me a gentile, or a slave, or a woman. It appears to me that if he had blessed 'who made me an Israelite' he could not bless 'who made me a free man' and 'who made me a man,' for the latter is included in the former, and his intent is to bless over each matter separately, etc., end quote Bach. And it is implied from his words that if he erred and blessed 'who made me an Israelite' he should not again say that He did not make me a slave or a woman. It appears to me that even though when one says 'Israelite' or 'Jew' a woman is included—for the entire Torah is stated in masculine language and nevertheless a woman is included, as Tosafot wrote that only where it is written 'sons of Israel' do we say and not 'daughters of Israel'—nevertheless in Sanhedrin 84a it is implied that here the reason is because Scripture equated woman to man; see there. And in Bava Kamma 15a and Menachot 93a and the beginning of Eruvin, and as Beit Yosef wrote that one should precede slave to woman as stated in the Gemara that a slave goes more than a woman. And it is implied in Bach that according to what is written in siman 65 regarding 'who releases the bound,' the same applies here: if he blessed first that He did not make me a woman he should not again bless regarding gentile and slave; see there.

And even a convert. Meaning he should say 'who made me a convert,' for it is called 'making,' as written 'and the soul that they made in Charan' [Darkei Moshe]. Shlah and Bach wrote that it is not appropriate to say so, for what made him a convert was his choice that he chose the religion of Israel; therefore he will not bless this blessing except for the two—slave and woman, end quote. According to them, 'who did not make me a slave' refers to the beginning of his creation; and even though at the beginning of creation he was worse than a slave, nevertheless if he had been a slave he could not have converted unless his master freed him. And according to what the Kabbalists say—that these blessings are recited over the departure of his soul at night, that the soul of a gentile or slave did not attach to him—therefore a convert too may bless thus.
