She-lo asani goy. Many ask why they instituted this blessing in a negative formulation and not affirmatively — i.e., "she-asani Yisrael" — and likewise in the blessing she-lo asani ishah. They resolve: because Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel enumerated and concluded that it is good for a person that he was not created; therefore they did not institute to bless for the doing that man was made, for it is good for us that he was not made — but at least [they bless] for that he was not made a goy. Machatzis HaShekel of blessed memory explained: because if he blessed "she-asani Yisrael" it would imply a free man and not a woman, and it would not be relevant to bless afterward she-lo asani eved or ishah. And it is simple, for Tosafos wrote in Menachos and Yoma, chapter Ba Lo, that wherever one can exempt two matters in one blessing, it is forbidden to multiply blessings; if so, here too he should have blessed one blessing and included the other blessings. It appears they wanted to teach that a person should not, God forbid, attribute a deficiency in man's creation to the Creator of the goy, and likewise in the creation of a woman. And in truth there is need even in the creation of a goy, for converts come from them, as written in Bava Kamma, chapter HaParah: the Holy One spared two nations for the sake of two good fruits that would come from them — Ruth and Naamah. And with this one may resolve what is difficult in what we saw, that He blessed Abraham that through Hagar he would bear twelve princes — for what is this blessing, that many like them should come to the world? Is it not better that there be few wicked people? Rather, that from the multitude converts will increase, as our Sages said: the Holy One exiled Israel so that converts would be added to them, as it is written "and I will sow her to Me in the land." And this is a fortiori regarding a woman, in whom there is good in creation, for she too fulfills some mitzvot; therefore he said here that one gives thanks to Him that He did not make him a goy — teaching that there is need for creation even of a goy, and all the more so of a woman; but man merited not to be created in another manner in which creation was also necessary — namely she-lo asani goy — but for others He made him so, and likewise regarding a woman. And this would not have been hinted if he said "she-asani Yisrael," for they would say creation of one who is not Israel has no need at all; and regarding a woman it would be better resolved that one must mention there is virtue also in creating a woman, but that he did not need that virtue. And it appears to explain what women bless "she-asani kirtzono" — this blessing is not in the Gemara; and according to what was written it is satisfactory, for it is relevant even in a man's blessing that there is virtue in creating a woman; therefore she is properly obligated to bless for her virtue, as above — correct.
