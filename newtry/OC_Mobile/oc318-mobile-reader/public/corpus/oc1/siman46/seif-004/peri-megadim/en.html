"That He did not make me" — Taz; and in truth in some versions "she'asani Yisrael," and likewise in Menachos 43b and in the Tur. And what he wrote — why do we multiply blessings? One can say it is not one matter — one blesses and gives thanks that He lavished His blessed kindness upon us in three levels; and Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah prove in siman 46. And what he wrote "she'asani kirtzono" — Beit Yosef and Levush [hold] that it is like justifying the judgment upon her; but the Taz wrote that there is need for her health. And if not so, how does she bless a blessing not mentioned in the Talmud? Rather, since we see one blesses shelo asani isha, etc. — and see there letter 9 in Magen Avraham on this. And one should consider: a slave born a slave — why should he not bless she'asani kirtzono like a woman, whether according to Beit Yosef's explanation or the Taz's? Also a slave — apparently one should not say shelo asani isha, for a slave is worse, as concluded there in Gemara Menachos 43b; and see Magen Avraham letter 9. And one can say a slave is important regarding certain matters — a woman is endangered in childbirth, and a slave also has mitzvos, and the king's seal of circumcision is weighty, etc., and thirteen covenants were cut with him; also according to what Rambam wrote in Hilchos Akum that he is obligated in mitzvos of elders — see there. If so, in some matters he is preferable to a woman, as we wrote.
