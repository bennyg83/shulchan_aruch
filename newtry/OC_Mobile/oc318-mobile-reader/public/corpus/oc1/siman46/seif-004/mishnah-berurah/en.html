(15) Every day: Who did not make me, etc. He should take care not to say “Who made me an Israelite,” as appears in some siddurim because of a printing error. Some authorities hold that after saying this he can no longer recite “Who did not make me a slave” or “Who did not make me a woman.”
(16) A non-Jew. If he first recited “Who did not make me a woman,” some say he may no longer recite the blessings concerning a non-Jew and a slave. A woman has a higher status than both, so the broader gratitude includes the lesser cases. Likewise, if he recited “Who did not make me a slave,” he may no longer recite “Who did not make me a non-Jew,” because a slave is superior to a non-Jew by being obligated in some mitzvos. Many later authorities disagree with all this. Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChayim conclude that he may still recite the remaining blessings.
(17) A slave. Some say that a slave born into slavery likewise may not recite “Who did not make me a woman,” because a slave has a lower status than a woman. Others disagree. See Peri Megadim.
(18) He may recite the blessing. Meaning he should say, “Who made me a convert,” because conversion is called making, as in “the souls they made in Charan.” Others disagree because his conversion resulted from his own good choice to accept the true faith, rather than Hashem creating him as a convert. Everyone agrees that he may recite “Who did not make me a slave” and “Who did not make me a woman.” Although a non-Jew has a lower status than a slave, had he been born a slave his master might never have freed him, whereas his conversion depended upon his own will. A mamzer may recite all these blessings because he is fully Jewish. The same applies to a blind person, whom we rule is obligated in all mitzvos. A tumtum and androgynous person, who are obligated in mitzvos because of doubt, should not recite “Who did not make me a woman,” because their status is uncertain. Peri Megadim.
(19) He should not recite this formula. For the applicable ruling and its explanation, see the comments of Magen Avraham and Baer Heitev here.
