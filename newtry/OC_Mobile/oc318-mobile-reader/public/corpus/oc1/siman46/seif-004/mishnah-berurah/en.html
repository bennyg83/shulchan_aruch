(15) Every day, who did not make me, etc. - One should be careful not to bless "who made me a Jew," as appears in some siddurim through a printing error, for some say that in that case he can no longer bless "who did not make me a slave" and "who did not make me a woman."
(16) An idolater - If he first blessed "who did not make me a woman," some say he can no longer bless "who did not make me an idolater" or "who did not make me a slave," because a woman is superior to both, and the thanksgiving is included automatically, for two hundred includes one hundred. Likewise, if he blessed "who did not make me a slave," he can no longer bless "who did not make me a gentile," because a slave is superior to a gentile, since he is obligated in some mitzvot. But many Acharonim disagree with all this, and Eliyah Rabbah and Derekh HaChayim agreed that he may bless.
(17) A slave - Regarding a slave who was born a slave, some say he also cannot bless "who did not make me a woman," because a slave is more degraded than a woman. Some disagree; see Peri Megadim.
(18) Can bless - Meaning he should say "who made me a convert," for this is called making, as it is written, "and the souls that they made in Charan." Some disagree with this; their reason is that it is not relevant to say "who made me," since conversion came only from his good choice in choosing the true religion. According to everyone he may bless "who did not make me a slave" and "who did not make me a woman." Although a gentile is more degraded than a slave, nevertheless had he been born a slave, perhaps his master would not have freed him and he would have remained a slave, unlike now, when conversion depended on his own will. A mamzer may bless all these blessings, for he is a complete Jew; likewise a blind person, for we rule that he is obligated in all the mitzvot. A tumtum and androgynous, who are obligated out of doubt in all the mitzvot, should not say "who did not make me a woman," for they are a doubt, as Peri Megadim writes.
(19) He should not say - See Magen Avraham and Ba'er Heitev.
