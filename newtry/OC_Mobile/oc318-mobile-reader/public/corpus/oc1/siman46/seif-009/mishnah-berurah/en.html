(27) Selichos. According to what follows, that today the blessing over Torah is recited immediately after Asher Yatzar and no verses are recited beforehand, we hold that verses may not be recited even as supplication before the blessing over Torah. During Selichos, therefore, one should recite the blessing over Torah before Selichos and omit it afterward.
(28) And afterward they recite the blessings. This is the strict law, as Beit Yosef cites Maharil that such was the Ashkenazic custom. Rama's phrase “and they practiced arranging” means that nowadays the world follows Tur's stringent view and does not recite any verses before the blessing over Torah.
(29) Immediately after, etc. Some say Elokai Neshamah is adjacent to Asher Yatzar, and the blessing over Torah should therefore be recited after Elokai Neshamah. Peri Megadim writes that each community should follow its established practice.
(30) In the morning. Even those who rise very early and recite Krias Shema before dawn, when they certainly do not fulfill the obligation, customarily conclude it with Baruch Shem Kevod. The verse “And in Your Torah it is written, saying: Shema Yisrael,” recited before Yishtabach, should not be followed by Baruch Shem Kevod.
(31) Because sometimes. Since the congregation sometimes delays, Baruch Shem Kevod should always be said. Without it, the Krias Shema appears merely narrative. He should intend not to fulfill the obligation unless he fears that the congregation will miss the time for Krias Shema. If they will not miss the time, it is preferable to fulfill Krias Shema with the congregation, reciting all three paragraphs with their blessings as the Sages initially instituted. If he fears that the congregation will miss the time, he should concentrate as required for Krias Shema and intend to fulfill the positive commandment. Regarding a Torah mitzvah, we rule in siman 60, seif 4, that mitzvos require intention. Some say he should recite the entire first paragraph. It is preferable to recite all three paragraphs, as Peri Chadash writes, so that he mentions the Exodus during the time for Krias Shema. Chayei Adam agrees.
(32) Outside its time. Meaning after three hours, when the time for Krias Shema has passed, but before the time for its blessings ends after the fourth hour, as explained below in siman 58. If he fears that the congregation will also miss the time for the blessings of Krias Shema, he should not wait for them. He would lose both the blessings and the preferred time for prayer, which likewise extends only until four hours, as explained in siman 89. He should instead recite Krias Shema with its blessings at the proper time and pray alone.
(33) And he fulfills his obligation through this. Dagul MeRevavah and Rabbi Akiva Eiger write that on a weekday he should not intend to fulfill the obligation unless he first put on tefillin, because one who recites Krias Shema without tefillin is considered as though he bears false witness against himself. If for some reason he cannot put on tefillin now, he should not therefore neglect the positive commandment of Krias Shema. Tefillin and Krias Shema are two separate mitzvos and do not prevent one another. There is not even a prohibition in such a case. The statement concerning false testimony applies only to deliberate neglect, not to one prevented by circumstances, as Levush writes in siman 58. See siman 58, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 5. One should say, “You are He before the world was created,” and not, “before You created the world.” Later authorities.
