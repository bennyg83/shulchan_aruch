(27) Selichos — and according to what he wrote above, that today the custom is to bless birchas haTorah immediately after asher yatzar and not to say any verses at the beginning — if so, it seems to us that one should not say verses even as supplications before birchas haTorah; if so, one should likewise practice on Selichos days to say birchas haTorah before Selichos and skip it afterwards.

(28) And afterwards they bless — for all of this is from the essential law, and as the Beit Yosef brought in the name of Maharil that such is the custom in Ashkenaz; and the Rama concludes on this: And the custom is to arrange — meaning that today people have adopted stringency like the words of the Tur not to say any verses before birchas haTorah.

(29) Immediately after, etc. — And some say that the blessing of Elokai neshamah is adjacent to the blessing of asher yatzar, and therefore one should bless birchas haTorah after Elokai neshamah; and Peri Megadim wrote on this: river follows river, and that is its plain meaning.

(30) In the morning — And even those who rise early in the morning and say Kerias Shema before dawn — for there one certainly does not fulfill Kerias Shema with that — nevertheless the custom is to conclude after it Baruch shem, etc. But what they say before — Yishtabach and "in Your Torah it is written to say Shema Yisrael," etc. — one should not say after it the shemakmelv formula.

(31) Because sometimes — explanation: because sometimes the congregation waits, therefore they say each time the shemakmelv, for without Baruch shem the Kerias Shema appears only as narrative; but one should intend not to fulfill except if he sees the congregation will pass the time for Kerias Shema. But when they will not pass, it is better to fulfill Kerias Shema with the congregation, for with the congregation one reads all three paragraphs with their blessings as the Sages instituted lechatchilah. And where he sees the congregation will pass the time for Kerias Shema, he should intend in his heart per the law of Kerias Shema and intend to fulfill through this the positive commandment of Kerias Shema, for regarding a Torah commandment we hold (siman 60 seif daled) that mitzvos require intent. And some say he should read the entire first paragraph, and better still that he then read all three paragraphs as the Peri Chadash wrote, in order to mention yetzias Mitzrayim at the time of Kerias Shema; and Chayei Adam in klal 8 daf zayin agreed with the Peri Chadash.

(32) Not at its time — that is, after three hours when the time for Kerias Shema has passed and the time for blessings of Kerias Shema has not yet passed until after the fourth hour, as below in siman 58. But if he sees the congregation will also pass the time for blessings of Kerias Shema, he should not wait for them at all, for he will lose the blessings through them, and also the time for prayer which is likewise lechatchilah only until four hours, as below in siman 89 — rather he should recite it at its time with its blessings and pray individually.

(33) And one fulfills through this — see in Degel Machaneh Ephraim and likewise in the chiddushim of Rabbi Akiva Eiger who wrote that on a weekday one should not intend to fulfill except if he put on tefillin beforehand, for anyone who recites Kerias Shema without tefillin is as though he bears false testimony against himself. And it is obvious that if it is impossible for him to put on tefillin now, he should not refrain from this on account of the positive commandment of Kerias Shema, for tefillin and Kerias Shema are two mitzvos and do not hinder one another, and there is not even a prohibition, for they only said "whoever recites Kerias Shema..." regarding intentional violation, not when he has some constraint, as the Levush wrote in siman 58 — see there, and see in siman 58 in Mishna Berurah seif kaf heh where I copied the words of the Levush. One says "You are He" until "the world was not created," not "until You had not created" [Acharonim].
