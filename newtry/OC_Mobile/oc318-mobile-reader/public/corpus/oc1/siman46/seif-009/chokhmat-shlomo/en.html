(Seif 69 in the Hagahah) But the custom is per the latter view, etc. Note: See Taz what he wrote in the wording of the prayer "You are He until the world was not created," etc. And in truth the two versions do not contradict: in Midrash Rabbah, parashas Shemos, on the verse (Ex. 24:14) "Who is the Omnipresent that I should heed His voice" — there Moshe Rabbeinu told him: "Our the Omnipresent — His power and might fill the world; He was before the world was created and He will be at the end of the world" — see there. This is like our version. But in parashas Beshalach on the verse "Then Moshe sang," etc., the meaning of "Established is Your throne from of old," etc. — thus Israel said: "Before You created the world You are He, and from when You created the world You are He." If so, it is explicit like the version the Taz deleted; and on this the Taz wondered, for it is obvious that every maker precedes his making — it seems for this reason that the intent of this statement is not to say about the essence of His existence, blessed be He, that He is antecedent to the world; only the intent is to exclude the view of the heretics who say that the Holy One, blessed be He, needs man's assistance from below, like other heavenly ministers who receive change through what is offered to them below, as it says (Jer. 44:19), "From when we ceased offering to the work of heaven we have lacked everything" — but we believe that He, blessed be He, needs no assistance from any creature and accepts no change. Therefore if, the Omnipresent forbid, one would say that the Holy One, blessed be He, receives some change from the world, then He would not be considered on one level before creation as after creation, but would only have been made on another level; but we say "You are He before You created the world" — meaning on one level You are when You created the world, and after You created the world You are always on one level and no change is made in You. And likewise is the intent of the above midrash in parashas Beshalach: in all the descriptions the Holy One, blessed be He, accepted no change from before creation to after — only He is, so to speak, equal before creation and after creation; only this change alone was renewed — that initially He was called "standing" and afterward through Israel He was called "sitting." Therefore both versions are intended: this version "until the world was not created" intends about the essence of His existence, blessed be He, that He is antecedent to everything — with this it is fitting to say "until it was not created," as the Taz wrote; therefore in parashas Shemos, where Pharaoh denied His existence entirely and Moshe informed him of the essence of His existence, with this it is fitting to say "until the world was not created." But the version "until You did not create" intends only that He, blessed be He, stands equally between before creation and after and accepted no change — with this it is fitting to say "until You did not create," as at the sea when Israel's intent was not that He is antecedent — that is obvious — only to inform that He accepted no new change; only what is called "sitting" — with this it is fitting to say "until You did not create the world." And one should not disturb any version [and see in my work Ma'aseh Yedei Yotzer on the Haggadah, parashah as the statement "I am He and there is no other" — see there]. And in the body of the intent of the prayer it seems the intent is not to say that the Holy One, blessed be He, is antecedent to everything, only that He accepts no change between before creation and after. With this the language fits well of what concludes "Sanctify Your name upon those who sanctify Your name, and in Your salvation raise our horn" — the intent is that our intent in what we pray is not for what would continue for You, the Omnipresent forbid, some benefit — that is not Yours; there will be no change: "You are He from when the world was created, and You are He until the world was not created," and You accept no addition or diminution; but we ask "Sanctify Your name upon those who sanctify Your name" — that through those who sanctify Your name for our sake You sanctify Your name, etc.; and this is "and in Your salvation" — in what salvation will continue for us, through this raise our horn and good will continue for us, even though You have no gain in the matter. Therefore since the intent is thus, one need not disturb whoever says "until You did not create," as above; investigate.
