Se'if 9, in the gloss: But the practice follows the latter opinion, etc. Gloss: See what Turei Zahav writes concerning the prayer text, “You are He from before the world was created,” etc. In truth, neither of the two readings is mistaken. In Midrash Rabbah, parashat Shemot, on the verse “Who is the Omnipresent, that I should heed His voice?” Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, is described as telling Pharaoh: “Our God; His power and might fill the world. He existed before the world was created and He will exist at the world's end.” See there. This accords with our text. But in parashat Beshalach, on “Then Moshe sang,” etc., the Midrash applies the verse “Your throne was established from of old,” etc.: “Thus Israel said: Before You created the world, You are He; and after You created the world, You are He.” This explicitly follows the text that Turei Zahav deletes. Turei Zahav objects that it is obvious that every agent exists before the act he performs. The objection presents no difficulty. This statement does not intend to teach merely that the Omnipresent, blessed is He, existed before the world. Rather, it rejects the heretics' belief that the Holy One, blessed is He, requires assistance from human beings below, like other celestial officers whose condition is changed by incense offered to them below, as it is written, “From the time we ceased burning incense to the queen of heaven, we have lacked everything.” We believe, however, that He, blessed is He, needs no assistance from any creature and undergoes no change. If, Heaven forbid, the Holy One, blessed is He, received some change from the world, He would not remain on the same level before creation and after creation, but would enter a different state. We therefore say, “You are He from before You created the world”: before creating the world You existed in one unchanging state, and after creating the world You remain continually in that same state; no change occurred in You. This is likewise the intent of the aforementioned Midrash in parashat Beshalach. In all His attributes, the Holy One, blessed is He, underwent no change from before the world's creation to afterward. He is, so to speak, the same before and after creation. Only one designation was newly introduced: at first He was described as standing, and afterward, through Israel, He was described as sitting. The two readings are therefore both precise. The reading “from before the world was created” refers to His very existence, blessed is He, and teaches that He precedes everything. In that context the passive form “was created” is appropriate, as Turei Zahav writes. Thus, in parashat Shemot, where Pharaoh denied His existence entirely and Moshe informed him of the fundamental reality of His existence, it is appropriate to say, “before the world was created.” The reading “from before You created,” however, teaches only that He remains the same before creation and afterward and undergoes no change. The active form is therefore appropriate, as at the sea: Israel's intent there was not to teach that He precedes creation, which is obvious, but that He underwent no new change, except that He came to be described as sitting. It is therefore appropriate to say, “before You created the world.” Neither reading should be rejected. See my work Ma'aseh Yedei Yotzer on the Pesach Haggadah, concerning the phrase “I am He and no other”; see there. Regarding the prayer's essential intent, it appears that it is not merely to say that the Holy One, blessed is He, precedes everything, but that He undergoes no change from before creation to afterward. This also explains the concluding language, “Sanctify Your name upon those who sanctify Your name, and through Your salvation raise our horn.” We declare that our prayer is not intended to bring You, Heaven forbid, any benefit. That is impossible, because no change occurs in You: You are He after the world was created and You are He from before the world was created; You receive neither increase nor diminution. Rather, we ask: “Sanctify Your name upon those who sanctify Your name”; for the sake of those who sanctify Your name, for our sake, sanctify Your name, etc. “Through Your salvation” (through the salvation extended to us) “raise our horn,” so that benefit will come to us, even though You gain nothing from the matter. Since this is the intent, the reading “before You created” should not be rejected. So it appears to me; examine carefully.
