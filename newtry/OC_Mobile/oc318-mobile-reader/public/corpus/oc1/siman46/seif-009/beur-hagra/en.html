Seif 9: But the custom, etc. This was likewise the custom of the earlier authorities, as Tur writes and as explained below.
And they practiced arranging, etc. Tur writes that Ashkenazic siddurim place after Yehi Ratzon the passage Ribono Shel Olam. This is based upon Tanna DeVei Eliyahu: “The people faint from hunger at the head of every street.” Hunger means hunger for Torah, as the verse states, “Not a hunger for bread.” Concerning that generation it says, “A person should always be God-fearing in private,” etc. Shibolei HaLeket writes that the earlier authorities instituted reciting this passage every day before prayer. It was principally composed for a generation of persecution. This is why it says, “God-fearing in private.” It also says, “And they recite Shema Yisrael,” because the authorities had decreed that Krias Shema not be recited. For the same reason it includes, “You are He,” and, “Who sanctifies Your Name publicly.” These texts are mentioned in Tosafos, Berachos 46a, and Pesachim 104b. Tur writes that the passage was instituted based upon the Yerushalmi, beginning, “You are He,” as stated in the verse, “At that time,” etc. Afterward come the blessing over Torah and the passage of the daily offering. Tur writes: “This order was difficult for me, because it places verses before the blessing over Torah. I therefore personally began reciting the blessing over Torah immediately after Elokai Neshamah.” Darkei Moshe writes that the custom is to recite the blessing over Torah after Asher Yatzar. This follows Tur, although Tur himself placed it after Elokai Neshamah because he held one should not interrupt between Asher Yatzar and Elokai Neshamah. We hold that interruption is permitted, as explained above in siman 6. I have, however, seen in all early and later works that the ancestral text arranged the blessing over Torah after all the morning blessings, before the passage of the daily offering, while Elokai Neshamah immediately follows Asher Yatzar. Rabbeinu Yonah in Sefer HaYirah writes that Asher Yatzar is adjacent to Elokai Neshamah, and all the poskim write likewise. See Tosafos, Berachos 46a, s.v. “All”; Rosh, chapter 1, siman 13; his responsa, beginning of general rule 4; Rambam's order in chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillah; all the early siddurim; and Abudraham. Tur's difficulty applies only according to his first approach. According to the second approach, accepted by Rama, there is no concern. Even according to the first approach, one may recite “A person should always,” etc., before the arranged blessings. Rambam's order of prayer likewise places it there, as stated at the beginning of that order and in chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 11. The Geonim also write that one should not interrupt between Asher Yatzar and Elokai Neshamah, and that from Elokai Neshamah begins the continuous arrangement of blessings until HaGomel, etc.
And it is proper, etc. Without Baruch Shem Kevod, the recitation is merely a narrative statement.
And he fulfills his obligation through this. As stated at the beginning of chapter 2 of Berachos. Even according to the view that he must repeat and complete Krias Shema, a recitation outside its proper time still counts as reading Scripture. In my opinion, it is improper to fulfill the obligation in this manner without the blessings. Moreover, he will thereby fail to juxtapose redemption to prayer.
