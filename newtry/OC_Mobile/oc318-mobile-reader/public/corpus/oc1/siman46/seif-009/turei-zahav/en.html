For sometimes, etc. In the Tur it is written that R' Yehudah heChasid practiced thus, and Beit Yosef challenged this from the Yerushalmi that R' Acha sought to abolish those who practice thus. It appears R' Yehudah heChasid relied on our Gemara, which is primary: our Rabbi the Holy One would do so during his study with students and afterward read it with its blessings, as Beit Yosef wrote and elaborated. Maharshal wrote in responsa siman 64, and these are his words: when I reach the Krias Shema of R' Yehudah haNasi, who said "therefore I am accustomed to say the entire first section until u'veshareikha," and likewise R' Yehudah in his novellae on Berachos; and I also say the blessing after it, "mekadesh shimkha barabim," which is from the Yerushalmi and was instituted for the sanctification of the Name — it is properly relevant after it: just as He sanctifies His Name among the many, so we are obligated to sanctify His Name among the many, and this is included in "with all your heart," etc. — end quote. It is implied from his words that "mekadesh shimkha barabim" is as a blessing — i.e., with mention of the Name, saying "Blessed are You... who sanctifies..." And so in Beit Yosef, who wrote this blessing should be counted; and Semag counted it among the blessings; and so in the Tur; and so in Tosafos, chapter Eizehu Mekoman, page 104 — and it is astonishing that it was not mentioned in the Talmud. And what he brought from Modim derabanan that the Tur taught in siman 157 regarding the blessing "borei nefashot" — one does not conclude with the Name since it is not in our Gemara but in the Yerushalmi; also Rambam did not mention the Name in it — we have no authority to speak on this except as I remembered: since all already practiced saying it, "a custom is a custom." In the text of the Tur they say "You are He until the world was not created" — so in Tosafos, chapter Eizehu Mekoman, page 104, and chapter Sheloshah sheAchlu, and in Rambam, and in all old siddurim and in the Yerushalmi; and so in Yalkut, parashat Va'etchanan, siman 876, in the name of Eliyahu of blessed memory, that they say this text. But in Sephardic siddur texts there is "You are He until You did not create the world." No one should dispute old texts, except that recently one siddur was printed according to one punctuator who emended our text also like the Sephardic siddur — and certainly he did not act well. And what some say, that they challenged our text implying it was created, God forbid, by another — I say the opposite: on the text "You created," what innovation is it that He exists before and after the world's creation? If he meant to say that He, may He be blessed, is the Creator, he should have said only "You created the world" — and certainly He was before and after creation, for every agent exists before the action and after it. Rather it is clear he did not intend here to inform who the Creator is, but to testify to the eternity of the Holy One, blessed be He, that He is always in existence — both before the world and after it — as they instituted in Yigdal: "First of all that was created," exactly so here. And whoever changes — his hand is on the bottom, both here and in other places in prayers and praises; and so Machatzis HaShekel of blessed memory on this.
