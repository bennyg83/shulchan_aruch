He should not recite verses. And in Levush, siman 571: specifically Ashkenazim who practice saying the blessing on the Torah before the portion of the daily offering—therefore it appears permitted to say verses before the blessing on the Torah; but for us who say the blessing on the Torah immediately after 'who formed,' therefore it appears forbidden to say verses before the blessing on the Torah; therefore also on the days of Selichot one should say the blessing on the Torah before the Selichot and skip it afterwards, end quote. And so we practice, as written in siman 6, seif 2; and if there is one who did not say it, he goes down before the ark and says it afterwards.

For sometimes, etc. Meaning because sometimes the congregation waits; therefore they say it every time, but he should not intend to fulfill his obligation unless he sees that the congregation will pass the time of Keriat Shema; but when they will not pass it, it is better to fulfill Keriat Shema with the congregation and recite it in its proper order with its blessings and adjoin redemption to prayer; see siman 56. And with this the difficulty of Beit Yosef is resolved [Bach]. And even though he wrote not to say 'Blessed be the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever,' it appears there is no problem since he does not intend to fulfill his obligation thereby. And Maharshal in responsa wrote also the first paragraph of Shema; and it appears that specifically when he fears the congregation will be delayed, he should say 'You are He' until 'before the world was created' and not say 'before You created the world' (Shlah, Bach).
