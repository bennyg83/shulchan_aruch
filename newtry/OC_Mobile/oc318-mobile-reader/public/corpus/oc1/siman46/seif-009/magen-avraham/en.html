He should not recite verses. Levush writes in siman 581: The Ashkenazic communities recite the blessing over Torah before the daily-offering passage, showing that they permit verses before the blessing over Torah. We, however, recite the blessing over Torah immediately after Asher Yatzar, showing that we prohibit verses before it. Accordingly, during Selichos one should recite the blessing over Torah before Selichos and omit it afterward. This appears to be the correct practice, as written in siman 6, seif 2. If one participant has not yet recited it, he should lead the service and recite it afterward.
Because sometimes, etc. Since the congregation sometimes delays, this passage should always be recited. One should not intend to fulfill the obligation of Krias Shema unless he fears that the congregation will pass the proper time. If they will not, it is preferable to fulfill Krias Shema together with the congregation, with its blessings, and juxtapose redemption to prayer. See the end of siman 56. This resolves Beit Yosef's question. Although Bach writes that Baruch Shem Kevod should not be said, it appears to me that there is no concern, since he does not intend to fulfill his obligation through this recitation. Maharshal writes that the first paragraph of Shema should also be recited. It appears to me that this applies specifically where he fears the congregation will delay beyond the time. One should say, “You are He before the world was created,” and not, “before You created the world.” Shelah and Bach.
