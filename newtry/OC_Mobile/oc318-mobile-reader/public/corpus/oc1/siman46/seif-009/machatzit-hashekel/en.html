Seif katan 9: Who did not make me, etc. It appears to me that if, etc., everything is included. This resolves why one cannot say, "Who made me a Jew and a free man." Nevertheless, regarding the third blessing, one could have said, "Who made me a man." One must say that since these three blessings were instituted concerning the creation of man, the Sages established identical wording for all three. Since the first two must be phrased negatively, "Who did not make me, etc.," they established the same negative form for the third blessing as well.
In Sanhedrin 84b, Tosafos s.v. ish, etc. Scripture equates man and woman through the verse, "When a man or woman commits any of the sins of mankind." Therefore, specifically regarding Torah commandments, where no exclusion is written, even if the command is stated in masculine language, women are included by virtue of that comparison. Here, however, in the blessings, there is no such comparison, and the ordinary linguistic rule remains that masculine language does not include the feminine.
According to what he wrote in seif 5 regarding Magen Avraham, etc. According to the opinion that if one first recited "Who straightens the bent" before the blessing "Who releases the bound," he should not recite the latter blessing, the same would apply here if he already recited one of these broader blessings. But the Bach himself disagrees even concerning "Who straightens the bent," as Magen Avraham writes in his name in seif katan 11. The Taz, in seif katan 6, disagrees and holds that all the blessings are necessary. Even if one already recited "Who did not make me a woman," he may still recite "Who did not make me a non-Jew" and "Who did not make me a slave." In one respect a non-Jew or slave has an advantage over a woman, because a woman can never become obligated in all positive commandments, whereas a non-Jew may convert and a slave may be freed and become obligated in all the commandments. A non-Jew and a slave each also has an advantage over the other. A non-Jew has lineage and is attributed to his father, whereas a slave has no lineage. The same distinction could have been stated as Magen Avraham writes in the following seif katan, that a non-Jew has the ability to convert, while a slave does not have the ability to free himself. Conversely, a slave is at least obligated in the commandments that obligate a woman, while a non-Jew is not. See there.
