(Note 9) "who did not make me..."—and it seems to me that if... "that He made me"—everything is included; and with this it is resolved that it is impossible to say "who made me a Jew" and "a free man," but nevertheless the third blessing, "who made me a man," must be said; for since these three blessings were instituted on the creation of man, they fixed the wording equally in all three, and since in the first two blessings one must say "who did not make me..." they fixed likewise in this language also in the third blessing.

In Sanhedrin, page 84, in Tosafos s.v. ish... they equated the verse that writes "a man or a woman when they do any of the sins of man"—and if so, specifically in mitzvot of the Torah where it is not written in a limiting phrase, even though it is stated in masculine language a woman is included from a gezeirah shavah; which is not the case here in blessings where there is no gezeirah shavah, the external reasoning remains that masculine language does not include feminine.

According to what is stated in section 65 regarding Magen Avraham...—meaning that for one who says if one preceded and blessed "who straightens the bent" before "who did not make me a woman" one should not bless "who did not make me a woman"—the same applies here if he blessed "who did not make me..." But Bach himself disagrees also regarding "who straightens the bent," as Magen Avraham stated in his name, note 11; but Taz, note 6, disagrees, that all are needed; for even if he blessed "who did not make me a woman," nevertheless he can bless "who did not make me a gentile" and "a slave," for on one side there is superiority in gentiles and slaves over woman: for a woman cannot come into the category of obligation of all mitzvot, and in gentiles it is possible he will convert, and a slave it is possible he will be freed and become obligated in all mitzvot; and a gentile and a slave each also have a side of superiority, for a gentile can trace lineage and is attributed after his father, unlike a slave who has no lineage (and likewise regarding "who made me a man," as Magen Avraham stated note 17 afterward: gentiles have in their power to convert, but a slave does not have in his power to free himself); and a slave is in any case obligated in mitzvot that a woman is obligated in, unlike gentiles; see there.
