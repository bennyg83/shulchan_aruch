Regarding the text, 'You are, etc.,' it contains only God's Name in the conclusion, 'Blessed are You, Hashem, Who sanctifies, etc.,' but no mention of kingship. We rule that any blessing lacking God's Name and kingship is not a blessing, as explained in siman 214, and this is not a blessing adjacent to another blessing. See Levush's explanation that it does not open with 'Blessed' because it is a blessing of thanksgiving. Nevertheless, one can say that, like Elokai Neshamah, it does not require a mention of kingship. Beit Yosef writes this in siman 214: there is no greater expression of 'King of the universe' than that He created existence from nothing. According to this, the Sephardic wording at the beginning can be justified. Nevertheless, we do not alter our wording, and we say nivra.
