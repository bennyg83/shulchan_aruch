At the proper time, etc. This refers specifically to the end of his sleep period. Therefore, if he cannot remain awake until dawn, he should not recite Elokai Neshamah or HaMaavir Sheinah until morning. He should likewise not recite Asher Nasan LaSechvi until daytime. Some say that even one who awakens from daytime sleep should recite Elokai Neshamah, but this is not the accepted custom. One should pause slightly between the words Elokai and neshamah, so that it not sound as though the soul itself is his god, Heaven forbid. Abudraham.
When he places his hands upon his eyes. Meaning, through his garment if he has not yet washed his hands, because before washing, a hand that touches the eye deserves to be cut off. When he sits upon the bed and stretches his limbs, he recites Matir Asurim. One should be careful not to interlace the fingers of the right hand with those of the left. It is nevertheless proper to look at and contemplate the fingers from time to time. There are ten fingers upon the two hands, and one should contemplate that each finger alludes to the Divine Name Ekyeh in its expanded spelling with yods, whose numerical value equals that of the word etzba, “finger.” This is explained in the writings of the Arizal. Some say that one should first recite HaMeichin Mitzadei Gaver and afterward SheAsah Li Kol Tzorki. Rokeach, Maharash Damascene, Agur at the end of the chapter HaRoeh, and Semag, siman 114. My teacher Bach testified that this was written in all the siddurim.
