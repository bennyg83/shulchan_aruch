When he wakes, etc.; and this is specifically at the end of his sleep; therefore, if he cannot stand up until morning light, he should not recite E-lohai neshamah or Ha-ma'avir sheinah until morning. Also the blessing "who gives understanding to the rooster" he should not say except by day. And some say that even when one awakens from sleep during the day he must say E-lohai neshamah, but they did not practice so. One must pause slightly between "E-lohai" and "neshamah" so it is not heard that the soul is his God, heaven forbid (Sefer Abudraham).

When he places his hands over his eyes — meaning, through a garment if he has not yet washed his hands, for before washing hands, placing a hand to the eye is liable to be cut off. When he sits on the bed and stretches his limbs, then he blesses matir assurim. One must be careful not to interlace the fingers of the right hand with those of the left; nevertheless it is good to gaze and have kavanah at all times on one's fingers and to intend that the ten fingers on the two hands — one should intend that each finger is a secret of the name Ehiyeh spelled in full with yudin, which in gematria equals "finger," meaning alef-hei-yod-hei, which in gematria is 163 like the count of "finger"; and they are the secret of "fingers" (in Sefer HaKavanos of the Ari). And some say to bless first "who prepares the steps of man" and afterward "who has made for me all my needs" (Rokeach from Shas, Agur, parashat Ha-ro'eh, and Semag siman 114; and likewise my teacher Bach testified that it was written so in all siddurim).
