1) [Se'if 1] When he awakens from his sleep, etc. How good and pleasant it is if one can avoid speaking at all, so that his first speech upon rising from sleep begins with the mitzvah of blessing the Creator of his soul. This is exceedingly good. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2. See Moreh Be'Etzba, paragraph 63, and what we wrote above in siman 1, paragraph 40.
2) Tur writes: After putting on tefillin, he should arrange the blessings, etc. We already wrote above in siman 25, paragraph 26, that the Arizal would recite all Birchos HaShachar through the passage of the Akeidah, then wrap himself in a tallis, put on tefillin, and pray; see there.
3) There: When he awakens from his sleep, he should say Elokai Neshamah. This blessing does not begin with Baruch because it is a blessing of thanksgiving, and blessings of thanksgiving do not begin with Baruch, as Maran writes in siman 6, se'if 3. Nevertheless, it should be placed immediately after Asher Yatzar, as we wrote above in siman 6, paragraphs 3 and 5, in the name of the Arizal. See also below, paragraph 7.
4) There: He should say Elokai Neshamah. One should pause slightly between "Elokai" and "neshamah," lest it sound as though the soul were his god, Heaven forbid. Abudraham; Ateres Zekenim; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 45d; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 1; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Vayeishev, paragraph 2.
5) One should not say, "tehorah hi"; "it is pure," but only "tehorah; Atah verasah," etc., because in most cases a person has sinned and the soul is not pure. Kesher Gudal, siman 5, paragraph 8. Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 46a, writes likewise for other reasons; see there. Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 2; Chesed LaAlafim there; Ben Ish Chai there. See Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 4, who discusses this at length. The word "hi" likewise does not appear in Berachos 60b, as Shaliach Tzibbur notes. Although Shaar HaKavanos, folio 2a, reads "tehorah hi," this need not be exact. The custom is not to say "hi."
6) There: He should say Elokai Neshamah. This blessing should contain forty-seven words, corresponding to the combined numerical values of the Divine Names Havayah and Ehyeh, which represent Abba and Imma, who are never separated. See Nahar Shalom, folio 11c in the new edition, and what we wrote above in siman 6, paragraph 3. Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 46a, and Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 3, write likewise. This is the text:
6) My God, the soul You placed within me is pure. You created it; You formed it; You breathed it into me, and You preserve it within me. You will ultimately take it from me and restore it to me in the future. As long as the soul is within me, I thank You, Hashem my God and God of my ancestors, Master of all deeds, Lord of all souls. Blessed are You, Hashem, Who restores souls to lifeless bodies. Chesed LaAlafim there. This is unlike Ateres Zekenim to siman 6 and Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 3, which follows him and says that the text should be "You breathed it," omitting "into me." Shaliach Tzibbur there rejects Ateres Zekenim and writes that the common custom is correct. This is also the text in Berachos 60b and in Shaar HaKavanos, as stated in the following paragraph.
7) There: He should say Elokai Neshamah. The blessing "Elokai Neshamah shenasata bi," etc., is adjacent to the preceding blessing because it corresponds to Imma, which is always joined with Abba. Understand this. Although not every person merits the component called neshamah, he nevertheless possesses a share in the soul of Adam HaRishon, which encompassed all created beings, even if this individual has not yet personally attained it. "Tehorah" corresponds to the world of Atzilus, from which comes the neshamah of the neshamah. "You created it" corresponds to the component of neshamah that comes from Beriah. "You formed it" alludes to ruach, which comes from Yetzirah. "You breathed it into me" alludes to nefesh, which is in Asiyah. Thus, when saying "the soul You placed within me," he includes all four components and calls them all neshamah. "You will ultimately take it from me," etc., concerns the mystical elevation of feminine waters: in the future Malchus takes the souls of the righteous in the mystery of "new every morning," so that they become feminine waters within it; afterward, "You will ultimately restore it to me" in the morning, in the mystery of "new every morning." Shaar HaKavanos, folio 2a. See the discourses of the night, discourse 3, folio 53, and understand. See Shaliach Tzibbur, folios 45d and 46a, who gives another explanation. Siddur Beis Oved, Chesed LaAlafim, and other siddurim write that when saying Elokai Neshamah one should intend an interweaving of the letters of neshamah with the letters of his name. See Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 3. Since the Arizal did not write this and we do not practice it, we have written nothing further about it.
8) There: He should say Elokai Neshamah. If one recited Mechayeh HaMeisim in place of Elokai Neshamah, he has fulfilled his obligation. Pri Chadash; Yad Aharon, Hagahos on the Tur; Kesher Gudal, siman 5, paragraph 9; Shaliach Tzibbur, folio 46b; Siddur Beis Oved, laws of Birchos HaShachar, paragraph 2; Shesilei Zeitim, paragraph 1. Although Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, questions Pri Chadash, he concludes that one should take account of his view. Shaliach Tzibbur there cites this. In particular, doubts concerning blessings are ruled leniently, and the later authorities rule accordingly.
9) There. If one forgot to recite Birchos HaShachar before prayer, he should recite them afterward, except for HaMachazir Neshamos, because he has already been exempted through Mechayeh HaMeisim, and except for Birchos HaTorah, because Ahavas Olam has already exempted him. This will be explained, with Hashem's help, below in our discussion of siman 47, se'ifim 7 and 8, and Rema's words at the end of siman 52. If he did not recite them after prayer either, see our discussion below in siman 71, paragraph 4, regarding how long their time extends. Initially, however, it is obvious that one should not delay Birchos HaShachar until after prayer, because that reverses the order of the spiritual rectification they accomplish. See Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar HaBerachos, chapter 3, and Nahar Shalom, folio 11c in the new edition. See also below at the end of paragraph 32.
