Before coming to the synagogue, while he is still in the courtyard of the synagogue, he should say 'In the house of God we will walk with feeling.' And he should feel and tremble upon entering the synagogue from great awe, and wait and pause a little and say 'But I, through Your abundant kindness, will enter Your house; I will bow toward Your holy sanctuary in fear of You,' and then enter [Sha'arei Tefillah in the name of Pa'aneach Raza]. (1) When he wakes, etc.—All these blessings are because it is forbidden for a person to enjoy this world without a blessing, and whoever enjoys this world without a blessing is as if he misappropriated sacred property. They juxtaposed verses: it is written 'To Hashem is the earth and its fullness,' and it is written 'And the earth He gave to mankind.' They resolved the contradiction: here before a blessing, here after a blessing—meaning, before a blessing it belongs to Hashem and is forbidden to you as sacred property that belongs to Hashem; and after the blessing everything is permitted to mankind. If so, since before the blessing it belongs to Hashem, everything is holy to Hashem and there is misappropriation if one enjoys it, as with terumah which is holy; therefore our Sages, may their memory be blessed, instituted a blessing on every matter of worldly conduct from which a person enjoys.

(2) From his sleep—meaning specifically at the end of his sleep, and not every time he dozes off at whatever hour it may be.

(3) He should say Elohei neshamah—he must pause slightly between Elohei and neshamah so that it not be heard that the soul is his god, heaven forbid. And the laws of the blessing Elohei neshamah, see above in siman 6 in Mishna Berurah, note 12.

(4) To the heart— the heart is called sechvi in biblical language, as written 'Or who gave sechvi understanding'; and the heart is what understands; and through understanding a person distinguishes between day and night. And because the rooster also understands this, and also in hearing it there is benefit for a person who knows that day is near—in Arabic they call the rooster sechvi; therefore they instituted this blessing upon hearing the voice of the rooster. And see below in note 58.

(5) Who clothes the naked—do not say that one sleeps naked; rather, since a person puts on the upper garment that is upon him, he blesses this blessing.

(6) Over his eyes—meaning through the garment, for it is forbidden to place his hands over his eyes before washing, as written in siman 4.

(7) Who prepares— and in Agudah it is written 'who prepared,' and so is the practice.

(8) The sash— the sash here means trousers; and so the sash mentioned by the Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 91—for even though in biblical language 'avnet' means the belt, as written 'and with a linen avnet he shall gird,' nevertheless in rabbinic language they sometimes call trousers avnet; therefore he used the expression 'wears' and not 'girds' [Mateh Yehuda].

(9) Who crowns Israel— he mentioned Israel in this blessing, and so in 'who girds Israel,' unlike the others; the reason is that the rest are for worldly benefit in which all are equal, unlike these two which are unique to Israel because of modesty—for girding is so that his heart not see his nakedness, and the hat is for modesty, as stated in Shabbat 156b: 'Cover your head because of the fear of Heaven.'

(10) And may it be Your will, etc.—'that You accustom us in Your Torah' is plural language [Acharonim].
