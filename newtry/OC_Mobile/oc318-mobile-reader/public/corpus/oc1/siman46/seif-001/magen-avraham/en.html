Before entering the synagogue he should say, “But I, through Your abundant kindness,” etc. Upon entering he should say, “In the house of God we shall walk with the multitude.” Before Shacharis he should accept upon himself the positive commandment, “You shall love your fellow as yourself.” When he places his hands. Meaning, through a cloth, because he may not place his hands upon his eyes before washing, as explained in siman 4.
All my needs. Some say tzorkai in the plural. It appears to me that one should not change the established text, because Tur explains that the blessing concerns putting on shoes, which are a person's need. Bach writes that our Gemara has the reverse order: first HaMeichin Mitzadei Gaver and then SheAsah Li Kol Tzorki. Many poskim write likewise, and this was the text in all our siddurim. Recent printers changed the siddurim to follow Shulchan Aruch, based upon Rif, Rosh, and Rambam, and Bach writes that they will be held accountable. It appears to me that everything depends upon time and place. Nowadays everyone puts on shoes before walking.
Who prepares. Agudah has the text, “Who prepared,” and this is our custom.
May it be Your will. It contains fifty-eight words. Agudah. Tur's text reads, “That You accustom me to Your Torah.” Sefer Orach Chayim writes that it should be phrased in the plural, as in siman 110, seif 4. See Responsa Menahem Azariah, siman 25, and what is written there in the name of Rashba.
That it is one blessing. If these were two blessings, each would begin with Baruch, because they are not adjacent blessings. Each was instituted independently. Although a blessing's conclusion must resemble its opening, “Who bestows good kindnesses upon me” refers to the kindness Hashem performs by removing the bonds of sleep from one's eyes.
