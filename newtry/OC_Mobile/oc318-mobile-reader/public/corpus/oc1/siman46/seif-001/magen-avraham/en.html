Before coming to the synagogue one should say "Ve-ani be-rov chasdekha..." and upon entering he should say "Be-veit Elohim nehalekh be-ragesh" (scribed). Before Shacharit prayer one should accept upon himself the mitzvah of "ve-ahavta le-re'akha kamokha" [intentions]: **When he places his hands**—meaning by means of a cloth, for it is forbidden to place his hands on his eyes before netilat yadayim, as stated in siman 4.

**All my needs.** Some say "tzorki" is plural language; but it seems to me one should not change the formula, for it is stated in the Tur that it refers to putting on shoes, which are a person's need. In Bach, in our gemara it is written the opposite—"who prepares my steps" and afterward "who has made for me all my needs," etc.; and so wrote many poskim, and so was written in all our prayer books, and now the printers changed in the prayer books and wrote as stated in Shulchan Aruch according to Rif and Rosh and Rambam, and they will give judgment in the future, end quote; but it seems to me that everything is according to time and place, for now everyone puts on shoes before they go.

**Who prepares.** And in Agudah it is written "asher hechin" and so is the custom.

**And may it be Your will.** It has in it fifty-eight words [Agudah] "that you accustom me in Your Torah"—thus in the Tur; and in Orach Chayyim he wrote that it must be plural language, as stated in siman 110, section 4; and see responsa Me'ein Yaakov siman 29 [thus]; and see later in the name of Rashba.

**That all is one blessing.** For if they were two blessings they would open with Barukh, for they are not adjacent, since each was instituted independently; and even though we require a type of opening adjacent to the closing, one can say that "grant me good kindnesses" means the kindness that the Holy One, blessed be He, does, who removes sleep-cords from his eyes.
