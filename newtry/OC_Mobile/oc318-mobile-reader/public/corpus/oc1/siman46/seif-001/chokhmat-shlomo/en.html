Se'if 1. When one puts on his shoes, he should recite the blessing “Who has provided me with all my needs.” Gloss: The reason this blessing was instituted specifically when putting on shoes appears to follow the Sages' statement that a person should always sell everything he owns and purchase shoes for his feet. When he sees that he has shoes and therefore does not need to sell everything he owns, he blesses and thanks the Omnipresent for providing all his needs: he need not sell everything he owns, and he has shoes as well. This is well explained. One may further explain a great allusion in this practice. By reciting “Who has provided me with all my needs,” a person constantly fulfills the Sages' teaching that one must bless over misfortune just as he blesses over good. Every day he says that the Omnipresent provided all his needs, yet there are days on which, Heaven forbid, misfortune befalls a person. How can he then bless that all his needs were provided? Would this not be a blessing in vain? Necessarily, he declares that the apparent misfortune is also good and gives thanks for it. Misfortune is good because, as the Sages teach, suffering brings a person to the life of the World to Come. The Midrash states that Yitzchak requested suffering for this reason. A righteous person is called one who “walks,” because he proceeds from level to level, as the commentators of blessed memory explain. The blessing was therefore instituted when putting on shoes. Just as shoes are needed for walking (one remaining in one place does not need shoes, but one who walks does) so a person needs suffering, because through it he advances toward attachment to the Omnipresent, blessed is He, and observance of Torah and mitzvot. He therefore gives thanks even for misfortune. This is well explained, with the help of the Omnipresent; examine it carefully.
