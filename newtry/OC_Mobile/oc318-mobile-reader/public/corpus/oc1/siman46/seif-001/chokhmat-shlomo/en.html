Seif 1: When he puts on his shoes he blesses "Who has provided me all my needs." Note: The reason this blessing is specifically at the time of shoes seems according to their words that one should always sell everything one has and buy shoes for his feet — therefore when he sees he has shoes he need not sell everything, and he blesses and gives thanks "Who has provided me all my needs," and need not sell everything since he also has shoes — and this fits well. Another explanation of the matter: there is a great hint here — in "Who has provided for my needs" one constantly fulfills the saying of Chazal that a person must bless on the bad just as on the good, for he says every day "Who has provided me all my needs," yet there are days that are, God forbid, bad for a person — how can he bless "Who has provided me all my needs" when it is bad? How bless a blessing in vain? Perforce one says that even the bad is good and gives thanks for the bad too. That the bad is good is as Chazal said that suffering brings life in the World to Come; there is in midrash that Yitzchak requested suffering from the Holy One, blessed be He. A righteous person is called "walker," going from level to level, as the commentators explained. Therefore it was instituted in the blessing on shoes — as shoes are needed for walking: if one sits in one place he need not shoes; only to go does one need shoes — so too a person needs suffering, for thereby he will walk to cleave to Hashem and fulfill Torah and mitzvos; therefore he gives thanks for the bad too. This fits well, with God's help; investigate well.
