(Seif katan 1.) Soul. See Ba'er Heitev. Peri Toar brings proof for this from the wording of the Jerusalem Talmud. See what I wrote above in siman 46. See what Magen Avraham writes at the beginning of the siman: Before coming into the synagogue, one should say, 'But as for me, etc.' Pri Etz Chaim appears to say that one should first recite, 'In the house of God, etc.' He should sense and stir himself while preparing to enter the synagogue because of his great fear, and he should wait and pause briefly so that he does not enter suddenly. Before entering, he should say, 'But as for me, etc.' He should then enter, and upon entering the synagogue he should bow toward the ark and recite the psalm, 'God, etc.' It is possible, however, to explain that work as saying that he should not actually recite 'In the house of God' at all, but should only contemplate its meaning, as explained there. This is somewhat implied by the wording cited in Shalmei Tzibur. Mishnat Chasidim, however, writes that he should recite it and afterward say, 'But as for me.' Netiv HaChaim likewise emends Magen Avraham to read: Before coming to the synagogue he should say, 'In the house, etc.,' and upon entering he should say, 'But as for me, etc.' In truth, however, Pri Etz Chaim explicitly states that he must say the verse before entering and that after he says it he should enter. Mishnat Chasidim writes likewise. So too it is stated in the Zohar in Bereishit and Vayikra: A person should not enter a synagogue unless he first takes counsel, etc.; see there. See Shalmei Tzibur, who cites Mahar Chaim Vital's wording: 'After you have said this verse at the entrance to the synagogue, then enter the synagogue. Do not say this verse after you have entered, for those who customarily do so are mistaken.'
