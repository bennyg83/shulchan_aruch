One hundred blessings. See Taz and Menachos 43b, where Rabbi Meir teaches this obligation. Rashi explains the verse by reading mah, “what,” as meah, “one hundred.” See what I wrote in letter 1, and Peri Chadash's comments concerning Knesses HaGedolah. In simanim 193 and 213, a knowledgeable person may fulfill a blessing through another. Bach, however, implies that because of the danger associated with failing to recite one hundred blessings, a knowledgeable person should not rely upon another and merely answer amen. He should recite the blessings himself. See siman 6, seif 3. On a fast day and similar occasions, if he fears that he will fall short, he should recite forty-two blessings, hear fifteen blessings from others, and answer amen. In pressing circumstances this counts toward the total. With Hashem's help, I shall discuss this further in Magen Avraham.
