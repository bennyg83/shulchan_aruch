His eyes. See Ba'er Heitev. See Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Perishah, who writes that this refers to when one's eyes are still closed or when one wishes to sleep: It is customary to rub over the skin of the eyelid, thereby removing the bonds of sleep from one's eyes; see there. Eliyah Rabbah writes that this implies it is permitted to do so with the hand itself. See above, siman 4, where Machazik Berachah cites Mor Uketziyah as permitting contact from the outside. Machazik Berachah disputes this based on the later authorities here and writes that the Zohar, parashat Vayishlach, and Alfasi Zuta by Rema MiPano imply that even external contact is prohibited; see there. See below, siman 249, in Magen Avraham: If one reciting the morning blessings reached Pokeach Ivrim, mistakenly said Malbish Arumim, and corrected himself within the time required for a short utterance, it requires examination whether he fulfilled Pokeach Ivrim. If, however, he intended Malbish Arumim, he fulfilled Malbish Arumim. Responsa Panim Meirot, volume 1, siman 58, questions this, since the same law is stated explicitly in Shulchan Aruch regarding one who took a cup of beer, etc., where he fulfills his obligation. He therefore emends the text to read, 'It requires examination whether he fulfilled Malbish Arumim'; see there. In Yad Ephraim I wrote that no emendation is necessary and the words stand as written. Magen Avraham is uncertain here because the person is in fact obligated to recite Malbish Arumim, so the words Malbish Arumim cannot be treated as though they were not said. This differs from the case there, where the mistaken blessing has no connection to the present object and is considered as though it were not said. According to this, the statement 'If he intended Malbish Arumim' likewise means that he first erred and said Pokeach Ivrim and then returned and said Malbish Arumim. In that case he fulfilled his obligation, because the intervening words Pokeach Ivrim are considered as though they were not said, since he had already recited Pokeach Ivrim; see there.
