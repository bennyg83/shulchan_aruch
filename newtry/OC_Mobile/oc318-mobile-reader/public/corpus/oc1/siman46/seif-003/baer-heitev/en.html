Blessings. The reason is that one hundred Jewish souls were dying each day. David therefore instituted that one recite one hundred blessings every day. On Yom Kippur some poskim write that one should take myrtle or other spices and smell them from time to time, reciting a blessing. One cannot, however, recite a new blessing each time unless there was a lapse of attention. Other poskim write that it is improper to enjoy a pleasant fragrance on a day when the Torah commands a person to afflict himself. It is therefore preferable to rely upon the poskim who count answering amen to the chazzan's repetition and to the blessings of those called to the Torah and Haftarah toward the total of one hundred blessings. He will then not need to take spices or myrtle to smell. See Magen Avraham and Maharit, part 1, siman 104.
