(Seif 63) A person is obligated to bless one hundred blessings every day, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham s.k. 5 what he wrote — but one may not bless every time except with hesech hada'as. Here are the words of Shelah in the laws of Yom Kippur: he wondered at Seder haYom who wrote that on Yom Kippur one takes spices with him and blesses every time, for it is explicit that one who enters a perfume shop intending to return does not bless shehecheyanu; and after excusing their honor, their words are astonishing — for if so we do not say one must bless every time, only with hesech hada'as, meaning: if he did not expressly intend to exempt only this once, then he is exempted with one blessing for everything where there is no hesech hada'as. But if he expressly intends that this blessing does not exempt except this hour and not what he will do afterward — and likewise one who enters a perfume shop or sukkah even though he intends to return, if he expressly intends that this blessing exempts only this going and when he returns there he will return and bless — certainly he is obligated in a blessing, since he expressly intended he is not exempting further; only that lechatchilah one may not do so, because it is like causing an unnecessary blessing, since he could have exempted with one blessing. Not so if he intends to complete one hundred blessings — it is not called an unnecessary blessing, for he needs to complete one hundred blessings; and since they instituted thus, it is a necessity for him and is not considered an unnecessary blessing — therefore the words of Seder haYom fit well; so it seems to me; investigate. I was asked by a certain sage of the community of Bitchesh on the words of Rambam chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos halachah 2, who wrote that whoever washes his hands — whether for eating, Kerias Shema, or prayer — blesses al netilas yadayim, etc. If so, why did Rambam not count it in chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillah where he wrote that a person must bless one hundred blessings every day — why did he not count among them netilas yadayim for prayer each time, and why does he count netilas yadayim for eating but not netilas yadayim for prayer? This is a serious difficulty; and to strengthen the matter it seems to be resolved thus: this law that a person must bless one hundred blessings every day — why do I need a count? Without a count he bundles them anyway; perforce the intent of what he teaches is that it is fitting to bring oneself to this obligation and bless them specifically — and especially according to Bach who holds that hearing from others does not help, only that he bless himself; and likewise teaches the language of Shas and Rambam who says "a person must bless one hundred blessings," meaning he must bless specifically and not hear from others; in any case he must bring himself to obligation to bless them himself. Accordingly it fits well: certainly it is in his power to guard his hands from hesech hada'as and he will not need to wash his hands for prayer, or that immediately after eating he will stand to pray and not need washing — and it is true that also for netilas yadayim for eating one can be exempted as we hold that a person washes his hands and stipulates on them all day, etc., and in both one can hear the blessing from others. But even Rambam who holds that for prayer too one needs a blessing agrees that the obligation of netilas yadayim for eating is stronger than netilas yadayim for prayer — and know that for us, for prayer one does not need a blessing at all, and for netilas yadayim for eating there is no one who disagrees that one needs a blessing; and how many stringencies there are in netilas yadayim for prayer that are not in eating — therefore even for Rambam the obligation of netilas yadayim for eating is greater than for prayer. Therefore Rambam wrote that even though it is in his power to exempt himself from some blessings or hear from others, he must bring himself to obligation of blessing and bless them himself; therefore he teaches especially that it is preferable to bring oneself to obligation of blessing in netilas yadayim for eating than to bring oneself to obligation of blessing in netilas yadayim for prayer. And the essence of Rambam's count is: with which blessings should he be careful to bring himself to obligation of blessing by himself — therefore he counts those with which it is more fitting to strive to bring oneself to obligation of blessing and bless them oneself, for these blessings are more important and more necessary than others. But certainly if he blessed other blessings or was distracted and needed to wash his hands for prayer and blessed, this counts toward the one hundred blessings and he can reduce others in their place; only lechatchilah it is more fitting to bring oneself to obligation of blessing in those Rambam counted more than netilas yadayim for prayer, for these are more important and necessary blessings for a person — and especially since in truth it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary blessing, as written in Yoma (70a) and Magen Avraham (siman 215); only to fulfill the mitzvah of one hundred blessings we are not concerned for an unnecessary blessing, as Magen Avraham wrote there. If so, the surplus beyond one hundred blessings should be guarded from an unnecessary blessing. Therefore Rambam teaches as advice that regarding those blessings one should see to bring oneself to obligation in them and not be concerned for unnecessary blessing; but regarding the surplus one should be guarded not to come to causing an unnecessary blessing — and if he was not guarded, he is truly obligated to bless. As it seems to me this is correct; it fits well; investigate well.
