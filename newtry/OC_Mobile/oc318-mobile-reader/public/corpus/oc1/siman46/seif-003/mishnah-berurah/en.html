(14) Blessings. One hundred Jewish souls were dying each day, and David therefore instituted one hundred daily blessings. In fact, we recite more than one hundred each day. At night one recites HaMapil. In the morning he recites Al Netilas Yadayim, Asher Yatzar, sixteen morning blessings, and three blessings over Torah according to Rama below in siman 47, seif 6, totaling twenty-two. The blessings over tzitzis and tefillin add three according to our custom of reciting two blessings upon tefillin, totaling twenty-five. Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach add two. The blessings of Krias Shema at Shacharis and Maariv, together with Yir'u Eineinu, add eight, totaling thirty-five. The three Amidah prayers add fifty-seven, totaling ninety-two. Two meals, one at night and one in the morning, add sixteen, because each contains eight blessings: Al Netilas Yadayim, HaMotzi, the four blessings of Bircas HaMazon, and blessings before and after the cup. The total is one hundred and eight. Even on a fast day, when one meal is missing, the total remains one hundred. On Shabbos thirteen blessings are missing from the hundred, as Magen Avraham explains. One should complete them with fruit and delicacies. If he has none, in pressing circumstances he may intend to hear the blessings over the Torah and Haftarah and answer amen, as explained below in siman 284. Magen Avraham writes that one should not create a possible unnecessary blessing merely to complete one hundred blessings. On Yom Kippur he likewise relies upon listening to blessings as on Shabbos. After all calculations, three blessings still remain missing. Magen Avraham writes that he may complete them with blessings over fragrances. So long as he has not diverted his attention from smelling, however, he may not repeat the blessing, because that would be an unnecessary blessing. He may likewise complete the total with Asher Yatzar if the occasion arises. In pressing circumstances, perhaps intending to hear the chazzan's repetition also suffices.
