(Section 3) ha-Mekhin and in Agudah, etc. — and indeed the practice is as the gemara version "ha-Mekhin"; but in Rosh the version is "asher hechin." And the Levush challenged — it requires study what he wrote, that these are two blessings — namely asher hechin and she'asah li kol tzorki — unlike the other blessings, all of which were instituted in intermediate tense, and the two blessings mentioned above are in past tense; and he left it in doubt. Therefore in my humble opinion one should say "she'asah li kol tzorki," since the version is so in Shas one must say so, even though we do not know the reason why it was said in past tense. But "ha-Mekhin" — since the version in Shas is so, one should say "ha-Mekhin," since it is intermediate tense; and in a place where there is concern for dispute, God forbid, one should not change from the custom.
