One hundred blessings. Beit Yosef writes that we recite one hundred and five blessings each day. On a fast day eight are missing, leaving ninety-eight. This is a scribal error, because the total would be ninety-seven. According to our practice of reciting HaNosen LaYaef Koach and two blessings upon tefillin, the total is ninety-nine. Beit Yosef also did not count the blessing Yir'u Eineinu, so the total reaches one hundred even on a fast day. One who begins the fast before nightfall loses another eight blessings from one meal. Perhaps he can complete them by listening to the chazzan's repetition. On Shabbos, thirty-six blessings are missing from the three prayers, two tefillin blessings, and Yir'u Eineinu. Beit Yosef writes that we add the blessing MeEin Sheva. This requires study, because then every repetition of the Amidah should likewise be counted. One may answer that MeEin Sheva differs because the individual did not recite it, though this is forced because the individual still says the core themes of the blessing. Beit Yosef also counts Kiddush as one blessing, while one who uses wine actually gains three: two at night and one by day. It therefore appears that on Shabbos we count three Kiddush blessings, seven of Musaf, and sixteen from the two daytime meals. Since according to our calculation even a fast day contains one hundred blessings, these twenty-six leave a deficit of thirteen, which he completes with fruit as explained in siman 290. The statement in siman 284, seif 3, that listening to the Torah and Haftarah blessings suffices applies only to one who has no fruit. Otherwise, why would Rabbi Abbahu complete the total with spices and delicacies when Torah reading and Haftarah were certainly available? He must also intend to hear the entire blessing, as stated in siman 284. Since this resembles one personally obligated in the blessing, he must hear it, as explained in siman 124, unlike Beit Yosef here. I later found in Knesses HaGedolah, citing Maharit, part 1, simanim 117 and 180, that one fulfills the total through listening only where no fruit is available. Maharit nevertheless writes that on Yom Kippur it is preferable to rely upon answering amen rather than fragrance. On Yom Kippur, thirty-nine blessings are missing as on Shabbos, plus one meal, totaling forty-seven. Against these, there are Shehecheyanu, Musaf, and Neilah, totaling fifteen; seven people including the maftir at Shacharis and three at Minchah, totaling twenty blessings; and the Haftarah blessings at Shacharis and Minchah, totaling ten. This gives forty-five, leaving two. Knesses HaGedolah suggests reciting Mekadesh Shimcha BeRabim with Hashem's Name and sovereignty, and likewise Modim DeRabbanan with Hashem's Name and sovereignty. It appears to me that one should not enter a possible blessing in vain merely to complete one hundred blessings. His further suggestion to recite a separate blessing upon the tallis katan likewise appears improper, because one should not multiply blessings. See siman 215. According to our custom of omitting Al HaTorah at the Minchah Haftarah, another blessing is missing. If Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, seven people are called apart from the maftir, adding two blessings. Shelah writes not to use fragrance, while Seder HaYom permits it. Yoreh Deah, siman 265, explicitly indicates that fragrance is permitted on Yom Kippur, because it says that myrtle is not brought on Tisha B'Av, implying that it is brought on Yom Kippur. One cannot, however, recite a new blessing each time without a lapse of attention. See siman 217.
