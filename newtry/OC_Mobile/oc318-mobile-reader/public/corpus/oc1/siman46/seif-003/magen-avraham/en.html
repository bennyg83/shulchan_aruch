One hundred blessings. Rabbenu Yonah wrote: we bless every day 100 blessings; but on a fast day eight blessings are lacking, so there are 92 blessings, end quote. And it is a scribal error, for there are only 97. And for us, who also bless 'who gives strength to the weary' and two blessings on tefillin, there are 99; and also the blessing 'may our eyes behold' he did not count—if so there are 100 even on a fast day. And one who stops eating before the fast day lacks another eight blessings from one meal; it is possible he fulfills his obligation when he hears the repetition of the prayer from the prayer leader. And on Shabbat, from three prayers 36 blessings are lacking, and two blessings from tefillin and the blessing 'may our eyes behold.' And Rabbenu Yonah wrote that we add the blessing of Mein Sheva; and this requires further study—for if so, the entire repetition of the prayer leader of three prayers would be counted. And one can say that the blessing of Mein Sheva is different, for the individual did not say it; and it is strained, for in any case he says 'our God,' etc., 'Who has chosen,' which is the essence of the blessing. Furthermore, Rabbenu Yonah counted Kiddush as one, but in truth it is three for one who blesses on wine—two at night and one by day; therefore it appears that on Shabbat one counts three of Kiddush and seven of Musaf and 16 blessings at the two meals—for even on a fast day we have 100 blessings; for us, 26 are lacking, 13, and one fulfills them with fruits, as written in siman 208. And what is written in siman 284, seif 3, that one fulfills when he hears the blessing on the Torah and the haftarah—that is one who has no fruits; know that R. Abahu, who fulfilled them with spices and delicacies, was not in a town where they read the Torah and haftarah, but as we said; and one must also have intent and listen, as written in siman 284—for this is comparable to one obligated in a blessing who must listen, as written in siman 124; see there—not as Beit Yosef wrote here. Afterwards I found in Knesset HaGedolah in the name of Maharam of Trent, part 1, simanim 117 and 180, likewise that one does not fulfill through hearing the blessings except one who has no fruits. However, he wrote that on Yom Kippur it is better to fulfill by answering Amen than by smelling spices. If so, on Yom Kippur, where 39 blessings are lacking as on Shabbat, and also one meal—for 47 blessings—against this there is the blessing 'who has kept us alive,' Musaf, and Ne'ilah, 15; and at Shacharit they read seven with haftarah and at Minchah three, 20; and the haftarah blessing morning and afternoon, 10—for 45, two blessings are lacking: he should say the blessing 'who sanctifies His Name among the multitudes' with Name and kingship, and also the rabbinic blessing of Modim with Name and kingship, end quote. So too; and it appears to me one should not enter himself into doubt of a blessing in vain in order to complete 100 blessings. Also what is written to bless separately on a small tallit—it does not appear to me to multiply blessings; see siman 215. And for our custom, where there is no Torah reading at Minchah, another one is lacking; and on Shabbat, where they read seven without haftarah, two blessings are thereby added. And in Shlah it is written not to smell; but in Sedei Chemed it is written that one should smell; and in siman 265 it is stated explicitly that it is permitted to smell on Yom Kippur—from what is said 'and on the ninth of the month he shall not bring,' it is implied that on Yom Kippur it is permitted; but one cannot bless each time unless he was distracted; see siman 217.
