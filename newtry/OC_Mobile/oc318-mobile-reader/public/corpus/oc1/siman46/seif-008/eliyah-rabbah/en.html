[8] One hundred, etc. — there are commentators [who derive it] from the letters of a verse: "And now, Israel" — one hundred; and some say from the exchange of alef-tav, beit-shin, yud-tzadi — they equal one hundred in gematria; and likewise from "mimcha" one hundred. Rokeach wrote: one hundred blessings to protect against ninety-eight curses, and also every illness and every plague — they are one hundred; therefore the Temple was built one hundred cubits high, and corresponding to each blessing ten golden [vessels] — "the thousand is for you, Shlomo" — up to here. Shelah wrote page 256: a person should teach his household to say all blessings in a loud voice — whether mitzvah blessings or blessings of enjoyment — first because voice brings to intent, and also so listeners answer "Baruch Hu u'varuch Shemo" and Amen. R' David Abudraham wrote: if you say, what do we elevate to the Holy One, blessed be He, with our blessings? One should say: our blessings are only to merit His blessings, as they said, "Whoever blesses becomes blessed." Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Rashba at the end of the letters rochav basod keruv: rochav hints that He watches and saves, as the term rochav indicates, as Rashi explained on the verse "Rochev shamayim b'kerev" — that He has dominion and greatness over all; thus the term bechor. Keruv hints that He sits on the keruvim and "the Omnipresent is close to all who call Him" — see there. R' David Abudraham also wrote: that they instituted the blessings in second person and third person — because the Holy One, blessed be He, is revealed and hidden from the side of divinity; and the soul too appears and is hidden — one blesses in second and third person; and the blessing was in the mouth and thought in the heart, and it is fitting for him to cleave to his Maker and stand in His strength always from the side of his soul — only he cannot from the side of his blessings, second and third person. R' David Abudraham wrote page 13: if one can add beyond one hundred blessings, this is praiseworthy, as David said "and I will add to His praise." On Shabbat and Yom Tov when they lack in the Shemoneh Esreh — see below siman 290 that they complete with fruits; and on Yom Kippur some wrote that one should take a little hadas or spices and from time to time smell and bless; and some disagree that only when there is a bris, when it is customary to smell hadas and it is a mitzvah, is it permitted — but without reason, and many times merely to complete one hundred blessings when we can complete without smell, such as answering Amen in the repetition of prayers and birkas haTorah and the haftarah — it is not proper to enjoy and smell on a fast day by Torah law. Magen Avraham agreed with the first view siman 265 — see there; and it is no proof, for there it discusses a bris; and see end of siman 581.
