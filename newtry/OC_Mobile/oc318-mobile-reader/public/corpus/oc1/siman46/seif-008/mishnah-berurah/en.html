(24) That even. Eliyah Rabbah writes that Elokai Neshamah and HaMaavir Sheinah should not be recited by one who remained awake all night. Peri Megadim and Shaarei Teshuvah leave his ruling unresolved. Shaarei Teshuvah concludes that one should hear these two blessings from another and intend to fulfill the obligation. If he slept during the night for sixty breaths, everyone agrees that he recites them.
(25) He did not incur the obligation. Even one in the wilderness who did not hear a rooster recites the blessing. A blind person recites Pokeach Ivrim, and a deaf person may recite HaNosen LaSechvi even though he cannot hear a rooster. Derech HaChayim. Chayei Adam writes that a deaf person should delay this blessing until daylight.
(26) He recites them. See below in siman 47, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 31, and Biur Halachah there, where Peri Megadim's view is discussed.
