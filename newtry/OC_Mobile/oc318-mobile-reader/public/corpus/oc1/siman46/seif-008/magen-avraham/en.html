And there are those who say that even if he was not obligated, etc. And how is this in the Gemara? 'For he heard the voice of a rooster' comes to exclude for us in the wilderness where there is no rooster, neither for him nor for others; or alternatively to exclude a deaf person. And similarly a blind person should not bless 'who opens the eyes of the blind,' since the matter is lacking in his body [Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 7 on prayer in the name of Tosafot]. And the view of the poskim implies that even in the wilderness, and a blind person, and a deaf person bless; and this requires further study. Therefore it appears to me that a blind person should not bless 'who opens the eyes of the blind,' but a deaf person should bless 'who gives understanding to the heart,' etc.; see siman 59. And in Eitz Chaim he wrote that it is good that he bring himself to obligation to hear the voice of a rooster, and so for all of them.
