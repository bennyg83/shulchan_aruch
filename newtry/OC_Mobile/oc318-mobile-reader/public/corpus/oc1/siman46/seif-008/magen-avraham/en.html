And some say that even if he did not incur the obligation. The Gemara's phrase “when he hears the rooster” excludes one in the wilderness where neither he nor anyone else has a rooster, or perhaps excludes a deaf person. Likewise, a blind person should not recite Pokeach Ivrim because the deficiency exists in his body. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillah, in the name of Tosafos. The poskim appear to hold that even one in the wilderness, a blind person, and a deaf person recite these blessings. This requires study. It therefore appears to me that a blind person should not recite Pokeach Ivrim, but a deaf person should recite HaNosen LaSechvi. See the end of siman 69. Olas Tamid writes that it is proper for a person to bring himself into obligation by hearing a rooster, and similarly for the other blessings.
