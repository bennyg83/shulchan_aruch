(Seif katan 8) One hundred blessings. Beit Yosef's calculation of one hundred and five is as follows. At night one recites HaMapil. In the morning he recites Al Netilas Yadayim and Asher Yatzar, fifteen blessings from Elokai Neshamah through HaGomel Chasadim Tovim, and three blessings over Torah, because he regards VeHaarev Na as a separate blessing. This totals twenty-one. The blessings over tzitzis and tefillin add two, totaling twenty-three. Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach add two. The blessings of Krias Shema at Shacharis and Maariv add seven, totaling thirty-two. The three weekday Amidah prayers contain fifty-seven blessings, bringing the total to eighty-nine. Two meals, one at night and one in the morning, contain sixteen blessings, eight at each: Al Netilas Yadayim, HaMotzi, the four blessings of Bircas HaMazon, and blessings before and after drinking. Beit Yosef assumes Bircas HaMazon requires a cup even for an individual, and therefore counts the blessings before and after the cup. The total is one hundred and five. On a fast day eight are missing from the daytime meal, since the preceding night he eats. If he stops eating before nightfall, another eight are missing. Magen Avraham writes that “ninety-eight” is a scribal error and should read ninety-seven. This creates a difficulty with Beit Yosef's instruction to complete the total by putting on tzitzis and tefillin at Minchah. Beit Yosef counted only two blessings for both, since he holds that head and arm tefillin require only one blessing. Even after adding them, one blessing would still be missing. Perhaps Beit Yosef means that one blessing nevertheless remains missing, but he found no remedy for it, just as he later writes regarding Shabbos.
According to our practice of reciting HaNosen LaYaef Koach. Beit Yosef holds this blessing should not be recited, as stated in seif 6, and therefore counted only fifteen blessings from Elokai Neshamah through HaGomel.

Thirty-nine blessings are missing as on Shabbos. This calculation excludes the two meals that were previously used to complete the fast-day total. On Yom Kippur those daytime meals are also absent, leaving thirty-nine missing.
And also one meal. Meaning the meal on the night of Yom Kippur. The ordinary fast-day total includes eight blessings from the meal on the preceding night. On Yom Kippur the fast begins before nightfall, so those eight are also missing. The total deficit is therefore forty-seven.
Musaf and Neilah. Each contains seven blessings. At Shacharis seven people are called including the maftir. On Yom Kippur without Shabbos there are six regular readers plus the maftir. This creates twenty blessings, since ten people are called across Shacharis and Minchah and each recites one blessing before and one afterward. The Haftarah blessings at Shacharis and Minchah add ten. In addition to the two Torah blessings, the maftir recites one before the Haftarah and four afterward. Their custom was likewise to recite four after the Minchah Haftarah, including Al HaTorah and Al HaAvodah.
Mekadesh Shimcha BeRabim with Hashem's Name. Their custom followed Rambam, who holds that during the year this passage does not conclude with Hashem's Name and sovereignty, as Beit Yosef records. On Yom Kippur, however, to complete the number of blessings, they would conclude it with Hashem's Name and sovereignty.
Our custom, however, follows Tur and Shulchan Aruch and concludes it with Hashem's Name and sovereignty throughout the year. See Taz in this siman, seif katan 9.

It appears improper to multiply blessings. On Yom Kippur people rise for synagogue before daylight. As soon as the time arrives to recite the blessing over tzitzis, they put on the tallis katan and recite a blessing. That same blessing can exempt the tallis gadol. Only if an interruption occurred would each require its own blessing, as explained in siman 8.

On Tisha B'Av they did not bring myrtle. Their custom was to bring myrtle at a bris milah and recite a blessing over it. The cited source writes that on the other three fasts one recites a blessing over the cup and gives it to a woman who recently gave birth to taste. On Tisha B'Av, when even she fasts, one neither recites a blessing over the cup nor brings myrtle. Since it does not say this concerning Yom Kippur, it implies that myrtle is brought then. Shach disagrees even regarding Tisha B'Av and permits fragrance. Magen Avraham cites him below in siman 556.
