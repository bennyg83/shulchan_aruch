(Section 8) One hundred blessings, etc. — 105 blessings, and these are his words: At night when he goes to sleep he blesses ha-Mafill, and at dawn he blesses on netilas yadayim and Asher yatzar. And another fifteen blessings from Elokei neshamah until ha-Gomel chasadim tovim, and three blessings on the Torah (for he holds the essential part is as stated, and "Veha'arev na," etc., is a separate blessing, as explained in the dispute on this in siman 47 seif 6; therefore he counts three blessings on the Torah) — behold twenty-one. And the blessing of tzitzit and tefillin — behold two more, and there are twenty-three (and as discussed, he holds that on tefillin one blesses on that of the head and that of the hand as one blessing, as ruled above siman 25 seif 5). And Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach — behold two more. And the blessing of Kerias Shema, Shacharit and Arvit — seven; behold thirty-two. And fifty-seven blessings in the three prayers (for with the blessing against heretics there are nineteen blessings in each prayer) — behold eighty-nine. And in two meals that one eats, one in the evening and one in the morning, there are sixteen blessings: eight in each meal — netilas yadayim and ha-motzi and four blessings in Birkhat haMazon; and when he drinks he blesses before and after (meaning he holds Birkhat haMazon requires a cup even for an individual; if so, one must bless on a cup in Birkhat haMazon before and after) — behold 105, end quote — the Beit Yosef in Tosafos Bava Kamma. On a fast day eight blessings are missing — meaning from the daytime meal, for on the night before the fast one eats, unless one stops eating voluntarily. And Tosafos holds they are only ninety-seven. Rather, according to this reasoning one must understand what the Beit Yosef wrote on what is written that on a fast day eight blessings are missing, and these are his words: And he can complete it when he puts on tzitzit and tefillin at Minchah and blesses on them, end quote. But behold the Beit Yosef did not count tzitzit and tefillin as only two blessings — for he holds on head and hand one does not bless except one, as above. And it is impossible that there should be found ninety-seven blessings; if so, even if he puts on tzitzit and tefillin at Minchah, nevertheless he lacks one blessing. And it is possible this is the Beit Yosef's intention — that nevertheless he lacks one blessing, but he did not find a correction for that blessing. And as he also wrote afterward regarding Shabbat, see there.

And according to our custom, where we also bless Hanoten...—for the Beit Yosef there is no wording of the blessing Hanoten, as stated in seif 6; and therefore he did not count from Elohai neshamah until ha-gomel only fifteen blessings:

Still missing... from one meal—that is, the evening meal before the fast:

And on Shabbat... thirty-six blessings. For every prayer has nineteen blessings, and the Shabbat prayer has only seven blessings, for the individual did not say them. Unlike the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur—for the individual already prayed silently and we counted those blessings; therefore one should not count the blessings twice:

For that is the essence of the blessing—meaning that one should not count the blessing Me'ein Sheva either, for the individual already said the essence of the blessing as well.

For one who blesses on wine. For indeed one who sanctifies on bread does not add through kiddush more than one additional blessing at night; but the blessing ha-motzi of night and day is already counted among the eight blessings of meals. But one who sanctifies on wine has added to him another blessing on wine—one of kiddush at night and one of the day—and there are three.

In the two meals—meaning of the day—but the evening meal he does not count, for it comes to complete what was missing on Shabbat from the hundred blessings that one blesses even on the fast day, as stated before this; and on the fast day one counts the evening meals before the fast, as above.

"You should know..."—meaning that which is stated in siman 290 regarding filling with fruits is lekhatchilah for one who has fruits; and in siman 284 it deals with one who has no fruits, for he said the opposite, that it is preferable to rely on the blessing over the Torah. And that which is stated in siman 290 regarding filling with fruits deals with a road in a village where they do not read the Torah.

That is, R. Abahu, as stated in the chapter HaTekhelet—"with aspermaki" means with spices; and "magadi" means fruits.

In chever ir means the congregation.

For this is comparable to one who is obligated in a blessing..., siman 124 seif 8. As explained there, the Beit Yosef and Rama hold that specifically if he is not obligated in that blessing it is sufficient for him if he knows which blessing the blesser is on even though he did not hear the blessing and may answer amen; but if he wishes to fulfill his obligation through the blesser's blessing he must hear the blessing from the mouth of the blesser. And here, since he will gain answering amen in place of a blessing to complete the count of a hundred blessings thereby—if so, his law is as one who wishes to fulfill through the blesser's blessing, who must hear the blessing.

Not as the Beit Yosef wrote in his book on the Tur.

Afterward I found... this refers to the beginning of his words, that it is preferable to fill with fruits rather than rely on answering amen.

However, he wrote that on Yom Kippur... from smelling—for smell is pleasure and today is a day of affliction and pleasure is forbidden; and for this reason one does not bless on spices on Tisha B'Av that falls on Motzei Shabbat, as stated in siman 556.

That thirty-nine blessings are lacking as on Shabbat, were it not for the two meals that he counted above to make up against the fast day which has a hundred blessings. On Shabbat thirty-nine were lacking. And on Yom Kippur it does not have those meals during the day, and to complete them thirty-nine remained lacking.

And also one meal—meaning of the night of Yom Kippur, for even on the fast day they count the eight blessings of the evening meal before the fast within the hundred blessings as above; and on Yom Kippur, when one stops eating before midday, those eight blessings are lacking—behold forty-seven—that are lacking from the hundred of the fast day.

And Musaf and Ne'ilah, each of which has seven blessings; and at Shacharit they read seven with the maftir, for on Yom Kippur they do not read without a maftir but only six, as stated in siman 621. Behold twenty—for each of the readers blesses before and after. And behold ten readers. And the haftarah blessing, Shacharit and Minchah—behold ten; for the maftir blesses on the haftarah besides the two blessings that he blessed on the Torah, another one before the haftarah and four after it; and the custom is also at Minchah to bless after the haftarah four blessings—that is, also the blessing on the Torah and on avodah, etc.

Mekadesh shimkha be-rabim with shem... for they practice according to Rambam's view that throughout the year one does not conclude this blessing with shem u-malchut, as stated in the Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, on Yom Kippur, to complete the count of blessings, one concludes with shem u-malchut.

But in truth our custom follows the Tur and Shulchan Aruch to conclude it even all year with shem u-malchut; see Taz on this siman, note 9.

Also what Magen Avraham wrote to bless on a tallit katan independently—there is no siman 25, note 11; and in my humble opinion it does not refer to this, for without the blessing on tallit katan one can complete a hundred blessings as above; and it is possible Magen Avraham intended the Beit Yosef, who wrote that on Shabbat, if one fasts, he should bless on tallit katan on its own.

It does not seem to me to increase blessings. Since on Yom Kippur they rise early to the synagogue before daylight, and as soon as the time arrives when one may bless on tzitzit they put on tallit katan and bless on it—if so, is it not possible to exempt the tallit katan as well with this blessing? For only if one interrupted between them does one bless on each, as explained in siman 8.

See there siman 215 regarding causing blessings. And see there in Magen Avraham that even on Shabbat, if they brought him fruits during his meal, he is not permitted to set them aside until after the meal to complete a hundred blessings, for it constitutes causing a blessing; and if he instructs his household not to bring them until after the meal—there is a dispute whether he is permitted; see there.

And in our custom that one does not answer at Minchah on the Torah — meaning in the blessing of the maftir, as below siman 622 seif 2 in Hagah.

And on Shabbat — meaning when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbat.

And on Tisha B'Av one does not bring myrtle — meaning their custom was to bring myrtle at the time of circumcision to bless on it; and he wrote there that on the three fasts one blesses on the cup and tastes it for the woman in childbirth, but on Tisha B'Av, when the woman in childbirth also fasts, one does not bless on the cup, and one also does not bring myrtle, end quote. And since he did not write so regarding Yom Kippur, it implies that on Yom Kippur they bring it; and Shakh there disagrees also on Tisha B'Av, and ruled one may smell; Magen Avraham brought it later siman 556.
