Seif 2: Nowadays, etc. Two explanations are required. Although blessings of praise need not precede the act, just as in the blessings upon seeing phenomena in the chapter HaRoeh, they must nevertheless be recited close to the event, within the time of an ordinary utterance, as stated concerning lightning in siman 227, seif 3. All this follows Rambam, who holds that one recites a blessing only when he personally incurred its obligation. Ramban and other poskim, however, write that these blessings concern the ordinary functioning of the world, as stated in the gloss to seif 8. See Ran in the first chapter of Pesachim concerning Al Biur Chametz.
And also, etc. As stated in Rosh Hashanah 29a and in the Yerushalmi, chapter 3 of Berachos, cited by Ran at the end of the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah. This applies all the more where the listener is not proficient, as Ran writes there, and certainly where the person reciting the blessing is himself obligated. In all such cases he may discharge the other's obligation. This applies even to Bircas HaMazon where only two people are present, as stated at the beginning of chapter 7 of Berachos.
