(In Magen Avraham, note 14) Therefore it appears a blind person should not bless, etc. — see Peri Megadim there, who emends that a blind person blesses, etc. And this is because he benefits from light that others provide, as written on this in siman 69. And it does not appear to me that Magen Avraham would rule because of this contrary to Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Tosafos; and the distinction is explained: there one blesses on creating light from which benefit reaches him through others, unlike here — it is not reasonable he say pokeach ivrim, for the blessing is on opening eyes and his own eyes were not opened. And if we say: what of it — nevertheless he blesses on opening others' eyes — then a fortiori a deaf person should bless "HaNoten laSechvi," as Tosafos wrote: the essence of the blessing is discerning light, and a deaf person benefits from light and receives no benefit when others discern by the rooster even in a dark house, as the Rosh wrote, and they inform him too. What do I have regarding a blind person for opening eyes, and what do I have regarding a deaf person? Rather it is more reasonable there is no blessing on the essence of the thing he lacks, only on discerning the rooster — and why divide between them? And if you ask: it seems to him here from that he says "when he heard," etc. — one may punctuate: from that he says "when he opened his eyes," etc., a blind person should not bless; and even per the version "when he places his hands," one may punctuate thus: they were said only to one who has eyes on his head. Also what Magen Avraham wrote "therefore it appears," etc., is not precise. And if we explain his words without emendation, Magen Avraham must be stating his own reasoning: a blind person, since the matter he blesses on is lacking from him, should not bless even though he properly has need and benefit of pleasure through others — unlike a deaf person, who properly blesses "HaNoten laSechvi," for the essence of the blessing is that God created the needs of the world; and since he also benefits from giving understanding to the rooster through others, he properly blesses, as written in siman 69 regarding a blind person regarding yotzer or (and also the main explanation of "sechvi" is the heart in the language of the verse, as the Rosh wrote, except that they fixed this blessing "when he heard the rooster," because in the evening they call the rooster "heart"; also they benefit from discerning the rooster; therefore regarding a deaf person this blessing is properly relevant, for the heart understands discerning light). But were I not afraid to emend, I would say the word "cheresh" is a scribal error, and Magen Avraham's intent is thus: Hagahot Maimoniyot holds both deaf and blind bless, for he holds the blessing is on benefit; therefore even though he did not hear himself, he properly benefits through others who heard; and one sleeping in the desert who receives no benefit at all should not bless. And afterward he wrote "or also," to exclude a deaf person: since he cannot hear the rooster's discernment — neither now nor later — he should not bless on it. And on this Magen Avraham wrote: from the words of the poskim who wrote that the blessing "that God created the needs of the world" implies blessing in all formulations — and he left it in doubt; if so, it is a doubt in blessings and to be lenient. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote: therefore it appears a blind person should not bless pokeach ivrim, but should bless "HaNoten" — meaning, per the first view, a blind person should not bless "HaNoten" either, for the essence of the blessing is discerning the day, and so a blind person has no blessing like a deaf person; and therefore he said he properly blesses this blessing as explained in siman 69, and this is not like a deaf person who does not bless this blessing — for I distinguish: a deaf person who does not hear discernment does not bless on it, unlike a blind person who hears discernment and also has benefit from it through others; he properly blesses according to all — as it appears to me for judgment.
