(Section 2) "Who has provided all my needs" — meaning kamatz under the tzadei and chirik under the kaf, which is singular. For it is found in Tur that it refers... also Tur holds as the Beit Yosef stated seif 8, that in a blessing one is not obligated in, one does not bless; however he distinguished between them, and these are his words: And all blessings that are on the order of the world and its conduct, such as Elokei neshamah, ha-Noten laSechvi binah, rokea ha'aretz al hamayim, and ha-Mekhin mitzadei gaver — one must not omit them even if he did not hear a rooster's voice or did not walk, etc. But those that are for his benefit he does not bless if he did not benefit — such as one who lies on his bed and neither wears nor girds nor wraps, he has no obligation to bless, end quote. And since Tur did not include at the beginning among blessings on the order of the world also the blessing "she'asah li kol tzorki," it implies "she'asah li kol tzorki" refers to his benefit; though this proof can be rejected. But in Rosh it is explained explicitly; the Rav brought it, and these are his words: All those blessings that are not for his benefit, such as malbish arumim, oter Yisrael, and she'asah li kol tzorki — if he did not benefit, etc., one does not bless them, end quote. And it is explained explicitly that she'asah li kol tzorki refers to his benefit; if so, it is preferable to say it in singular.

Know that now everyone wears, etc. — and if so, it is fitting to say "she'asah li kol tzorki," for it refers first to putting on shoes.
