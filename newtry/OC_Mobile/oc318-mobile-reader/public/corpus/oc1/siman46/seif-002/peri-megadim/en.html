Girds — Taz, Avudraham, Prisha, and Levush: all that a Jew and a non-Jew are equal except that there is a difference between them — one mentions Yisrael in the blessing and in the belt, for this one's intent is toward Heaven and that one's is toward might, as it is written "a belt upon your loins," and therefore they say it in Gevurah — meaning, even though our intent is not for that (and the Torah also weakens strength) "You, Hashem, in kindness give us might." And likewise in the hat — this is because of heat, and we for the sake of Shabbos in glory, as it is written "and you shall be a crown of glory." And a hat — even though a non-Jew also [wears one], it is because of cleanliness, and we because of holiness. Since one says "Who has sanctified us," one need not mention Yisrael. And see siman 4 in Magen Avraham letter 13, that according to Rabbeinu Tam one needs a blessing only for kedushah like a kohen, etc. — and if so, the first paragraph suffices like a non-Jew; however if according to Rabbeinu Tam a blessing is required, they are not equal, for a non-Jew [says it] at one time and a Jew three times with interruption, as explained.

Pri Chadash wrote: one who rises before morning for tikun chatzos should leave Elokai neshamah until morning, and birchas haTorah and haGomel (which is haMa'avir sheinah) he says immediately and repeats and says again in the morning; and likewise in siman 47 seif 13, and concluding the rest of the blessings he leaves until morning. Behold what he wrote regarding Elokai neshamah from Rambam perek 8 of Hilchos Tefillah 13 — there is no proof, for when he is in bed and dozing with intent to sleep he does not bless; but when he stands for tikun chatzos, why should he not bless? Alternatively, it is possible he must also bless again when he awakens, with God's help. And what he wrote in siman 47 — all the blessings he leaves until morning — I do not know why, except for HaNoten laSechvi; and see Magen Avraham there letter 13 and Pri Chadash there that he was accustomed to say HaNoten laSechvi after chatzos; and see in Magen Avraham where it will be explained regarding the blessing of light whether sleep at night constitutes an interruption.
