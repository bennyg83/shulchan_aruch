He girds himself. See Taz, Bach, Perishah, and Levush. Whenever Jews and non-Jews perform the same act but differ in intention, the blessing mentions Israel's distinctive purpose. Regarding girding, the non-Jew acts for strength, while the Jew directs his intention Heavenward, as in the verse, “Gird your loins like a man.” We therefore say that Hashem girds Israel with strength. Although our intention is not physical strength, and Torah can even weaken bodily strength, Hashem in His kindness grants us strength. The same applies to a head covering. Others wear it because of the heat, while we wear it for the sake of Heaven and splendor, as in the verse, “You shall be a crown of splendor.” Regarding washing the hands, although non-Jews also wash for cleanliness, we wash for sanctity. Since the blessing already says that He sanctified us with His commandments, it need not explicitly mention Israel. See siman 4, Magen Avraham, letter 13. According to Rabbeinu Tam, washing once suffices merely for cleanliness, while ritual washing requires interruption and three washings. Thus, even the physical act differs between Jews and non-Jews.
Peri Chadash writes that one who rises before dawn for Tikkun Chatzos should postpone Elokai Neshamah until morning. He should recite the blessings over Torah and HaGomel, meaning HaMaavir Sheinah, immediately, and repeat them again in the morning. The endings of the other blessings should be postponed until morning. His proof concerning Elokai Neshamah from Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 3, is not decisive. Rambam speaks of one who awakens in bed and intends to sleep again. One who rises for Tikkun Chatzos should perhaps recite it immediately. Alternatively, perhaps he must recite it again when he awakens in the morning. What Peri Chadash writes in siman 47, that all the blessings should be postponed until dawn, is difficult. Only HaNosen LaSechvi should perhaps be delayed. See Magen Avraham there, letter 13, and Peri Chadash there, who would recite HaNosen LaSechvi after midnight. With Hashem's help, the law concerning the blessing over Torah after nighttime sleep will be explained in Magen Avraham.
