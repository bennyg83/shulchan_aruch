He girds. See Taz, Bach, Perishah, and Levush. Whenever Jews and non-Jews perform the same act but differ in purpose, Israel is mentioned in the blessing. Regarding a belt, one intends it for Heaven while the other intends physical strength, as stated, 'Gird your loins like a mighty man.' We therefore say 'with strength,' meaning: although this is not our purpose (and Torah study even weakens one's physical strength) You, Hashem, kindly grant us strength. The same applies to a head covering: the other wears it because of the heat, whereas we wear it for the sake of Heaven and for splendor, as stated, 'You shall be a crown of splendor.' Regarding washing the hands, although non-Jews likewise wash for cleanliness, we wash for sanctity. Since the blessing says, 'Who sanctified us with His commandments,' it need not mention Israel explicitly. See siman 4, Magen Avraham, ot 13: if no blessing is required for removing the ruach ra'ah and it is required only for sanctity, like a kohen's washing, then one washing suffices, just as for a non-Jew. If the blessing is required because of the ruach ra'ah, however, the acts are not the same: a non-Jew washes once, whereas a Jew washes three times alternately, as explained.
Peri Chadash writes that one who rises before dawn for Tikkun Chatzos should postpone Elokai Neshamah until morning. He should recite the blessings over Torah and HaGomel, meaning HaMaavir Sheinah, immediately, and repeat them again in the morning. The endings of the other blessings should be postponed until morning. His proof concerning Elokai Neshamah from Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 3, is not decisive. Rambam speaks of one who awakens in bed and intends to sleep again. One who rises for Tikkun Chatzos should perhaps recite it immediately. Alternatively, perhaps he must recite it again when he awakens in the morning. What Peri Chadash writes in siman 47, that all the blessings should be postponed until dawn, is difficult. Only HaNosen LaSechvi should perhaps be delayed. See Magen Avraham there, letter 13, and Peri Chadash there, who would recite HaNosen LaSechvi after midnight. With Hashem's help, the law concerning the blessing over Torah after nighttime sleep will be explained in Magen Avraham.
