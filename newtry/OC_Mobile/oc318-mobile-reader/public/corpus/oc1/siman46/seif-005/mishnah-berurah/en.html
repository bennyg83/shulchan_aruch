(20) He should not recite it. By thanking Hashem for fully straightening him, he has included Matir Asurim, whose obligation arose immediately upon sitting up. Some disagree. The later authorities conclude leniently. Peri Megadim writes that it is proper to hear the blessing afterward from another person and intend to fulfill the obligation, thereby satisfying both possibilities. If, when saying Hashem's Name, he intended to recite Zokef Kefufim, he should conclude Zokef Kefufim even though he will thereby be unable to recite Matir Asurim. All the more so, with the other blessings he should not be concerned about order, but should conclude according to his intention when mentioning Hashem's Name. Chayei Adam. If he reached Pokeach Ivrim, intended to conclude it, but misspoke and said Malbish Arumim, then remembered within the time of an ordinary utterance and added Pokeach Ivrim, some say he fulfilled Pokeach Ivrim because he canceled the first conclusion. Others remain uncertain. If, however, when he said “Blessed are You, Hashem, our God, King of the universe,” he intended Malbish Arumim, completed it, and then within an ordinary utterance added Pokeach Ivrim, everyone agrees he fulfilled Malbish Arumim and must recite Pokeach Ivrim again. The order does not prevent fulfillment. The same applies to all morning blessings except Matir Asurim and Zokef Kefufim. If he recited Zokef Kefufim first and within an ordinary utterance added Matir Asurim, he has fulfilled only the first blessing, Zokef Kefufim, but should not recite Matir Asurim again. Peri Megadim, siman 209.
