Ha-noten la-yaef koach — it is the common custom to recite it, and it is according to a midrash; and Maharshal, may he rest in Eden, would not recite it in Shas, siman 64. When he washes his face he blesses Ha-ma'avir sheinah mei-einai, etc., and "Yehi ratzon" and "guard me from entering into sin," and "do not bring me," and likewise one should not say "and subdue my inclination to be enslaved to You," for everything is in the hands of Heaven except fear of Heaven (Or Zarua). Here the rabbi expanded at length — the canvas is too short to contain it — and he proved here with proofs and lengthy responsa the general principle that emerges; therefore let one who girds himself not boast with the blessing Ozer Yisrael unless he removed the belt, and likewise Malbish arumim if he is not clothed, and Atar Yisrael if he did not spread his sudra on his head; and likewise, in my opinion, if he did not sleep the entire night he should not recite the blessing E-lohai neshamah shenatata bi or Ha-ma'avir sheinah mei-einai — and I proved this in a responsum and expanded on it, and my teacher and rabbi R. Yehoshua, may he rest in Eden, of Krakow agreed with me. But blessings that relate to the order of the world and its conduct, such as Hanoten la-sechvi binah, Rokea ha-aretz, and Ha-mechin mitzadei gever — one must recite them even if he did not hear the rooster or did not walk on the ground. The reason is that without these a person cannot exist in this world; whereas without clothing and without a belt one can live without them, as Adam before the sin, and those are only for human pleasure, from God's kindness to His creatures. Or one could say that these are different: even though he did not hear the rooster, nevertheless he blesses the Holy One, blessed be He, for giving him understanding to distinguish between day and night; and likewise even if he did not descend to the ground he blesses Rokea ha-aretz, for the blessing is not on the pleasure of going down to the earth — behold it is creation and stands as stated above.
