This is the view of R' Amram, which our Rabbi the Tur brought, and Beit Yosef explained: although one must bless these two blessings, one must bless first matir assurim, as in the Gemara — "when he stretches and sits" one says matir assurim; "when he stands" one says zokef kefufim — meaning, when he sits on the bed and stretches his limbs he blesses matir assurim because he moves his limbs that were all night like bound ones; and when he stands on his feet he blesses zokef kefufim because his stature was bent all night. And if he blessed zokef kefufim first, R' Amram said he should not bless afterward matir assurim, because included in straightening the stature is movement of the limbs — why return to bless a second time? — end quote. And the Tur disagrees with R' Amram and wrote: I do not know why, for the Gemara explains each one why; and one must understand his words, for R' Amram spoke well according to what Beit Yosef explained. It appears that certainly all agree that if we have before us two ways in blessings — i.e., it is possible to bless two blessings or one — one must bless one, for one may not multiply blessings, as written in siman 4 in the name of Tosafos. But here, although the blessing of malbish arumim was included in zokef kefufim, nevertheless in the Gemara when they instituted it, each had a special time: "when he stretches and sits" had no relevance to zokef kefufim but to malbish arumim; and afterward when he stood and it was a new act, the blessing of zokef kefufim was added. And R' Amram holds that although we say the blessings in the synagogue in order and we should have said zokef kefufim separately and it would have been included in malbish arumim, we do not change from what was at the time of the Talmud; but if it happened and he said zokef kefufim first, he is certainly exempt from malbish arumim, for even at the time of the Talmud the law was so: if he did not say malbish arumim when he stretched and sat, and afterward when he stood said zokef kefufim, he need not say malbish arumim. And the Tur holds: since nevertheless at the time of the Talmud each had a special time, likewise even at the time of the Talmud he was not exempt from malbish arumim when saying zokef kefufim, for the obligation of each blessing already rested upon him independently. He challenged: Machatzis HaShekel to R' Amram — he should have warned likewise that if he blessed she-lo asani ishah he should not again bless she-lo asani eved, and all the more she-lo asani goy, for the Gemara asks "eved is woman" and why need both, and answers "eved is worse" — if so, eved should be included in she-lo asani ishah; and likewise if he blessed she-lo asani goy he should not again bless she-lo asani goy — let it teach nothing. In my humble opinion, for this practical difference all are needed: woman and eved or goy — it is possible the eved will be freed or the goy will convert and become obligated in all mitzvot, unlike a woman who cannot become obligated in all mitzvot; therefore woman is not included in eved or goy, and goy and eved also need both — and this is explicit Talmud in the first chapter of Bava Kamma, page 15, regarding liability for damage payments, that it diminishes slaves and goyim; and both are needed: if it taught eved, because he has no pity, as it is written "with the donkey, with one like a donkey"; but a goy has pity, as written regarding Ben-Hadad — one might say no; and if it taught goy, because he is not relevant to mitzvot, but an eved who is relevant to mitzvot — one might say no — both are needed. Behold we see each has superiority over his fellow, and therefore one is not included in the other; therefore a person must give thanks that he was not made in any of these, but has superiority over all of them — unlike zokef kefufim, which is included in malbish arumim, as written. Therefore R' Amram's words are also correct, and well did Shulchan Aruch rule like him, that a doubt in blessings is to be lenient. And in Rokeach he lists in the order of blessings first zokef kefufim and afterward malbish arumim, and so is the text in old siddurim — and it requires study, for this is against the Gemara. It appears he explained what the Gemara says "when he stands" means he straightens his head alone while still sitting on the bed; and "when he stretches and sits" means afterward, when he awakens from his sleep and straightens on the bed his head, his limbs are still as bent until he moves his limbs and separates them from each other. And although in the Gemara they said first "when he stretches and sits," it is possible in Rokeach's version it was not so. And know that even in our version the blessings are not said in order, as written at the beginning of this siman regarding pokeach ivrim and placing hands on eyes; but certainly the main point is that zokef kefufim precedes malbish arumim.
