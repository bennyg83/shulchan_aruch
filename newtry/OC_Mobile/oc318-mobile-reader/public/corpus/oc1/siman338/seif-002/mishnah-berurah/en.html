(א) To say, etc. — for prohibition of making sound with musical instrument is only rabbinic decree lest he repair musical instrument; and speech to a gentile is also only rabbinic prohibition and it is shvut di-shvut; and in place of mitzva it applies, for joy of bridegroom and bride is only with musical instrument and permitted, as above siman 307 seif 5 see there; and there are places accustomed to be strict unless they prepared from erev Shabbat and he does not tell him on Shabbat anything, or he came on his own to play [so is the custom]

(ב) To tell gentile to repair — he holds joy of bridegroom and bride is more important than other mere matters of mitzva; therefore speech is permitted even for repairing musical instrument which is Torah melacha [and in Beur HaGra he wrote this view follows those who permit above siman 276 seif 2 all Torah melacha through gentile for mitzva need; if so for us who follow majority poskim who forbid also in this — forbidden]

(ג) But otherwise — meaning in other joy of mitzva such as at a circumcision meal or Simchat Torah — forbidden even to tell gentile to play unless they come on their own or he prepared them from erev Shabbat; see in responsa Mahari'a part 1 siman 59 who forbids playing instruments and organ during prayer through gentile; see in responsa Chavot Shaul part 6 siman 86 who brought proofs to forbid even on weekdays to play in synagogue during prayer, especially on Shabbat there is no permission

(ד) They were accustomed to be lenient — meaning the speech to play in other joy of mitzva; and Levush implies forbidden except joy of bridegroom and bride; and see there in Eshel Avraham

(ה) Regarding clapping and dancing — meaning since by law some say we should permit to play in our time with musical instruments since we are not expert in making them and there is nothing to decree lest he repair; therefore in any case speech to gentile to play is permitted; see in Magen Avraham who agreed that in any case forbidden to tell gentile to repair musical instrument, even for honor of bridegroom and bride
