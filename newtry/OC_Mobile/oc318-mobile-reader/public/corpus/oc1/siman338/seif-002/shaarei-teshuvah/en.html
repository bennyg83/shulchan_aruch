In Shulchan Aruch seif 3, clock pair that jingles. And Peri Megadim wrote in part 2 siman 122 that according to his view it is permitted to pull the chain in a clock on Shabbos, for it is better than a cup of parts on erev Shabbos; and see in She'elat Ya'avetz siman 41 that it is forbidden by custom, and so we practice; and so too in Peri Megadim that one may not permit except through a gentile, and for a sick person if impossible through a gentile one may permit through a Jew on erev Shabbos. And it appears that in such a case he should be swift to extend and pull the silver rope until its going that produces sound through rotation of the wheels does not stop — for in this, since the production of sound is from yesterday and he does not create anew in sound production but only pulling the chain is the law of garmi that it should not stop and not be annulled, and this is more lenient as explained in this siman; and also this is not applicable here that it is finishing a craft and fixing a vessel; but after the chain wrapped on the returning wheel finished and its going stopped, and through that he returns and pulls and extends it returns to go and jingle — it appears that there is to forbid because it is like finishing a vessel and fixing a utensil, as is written in responsa Mayim Rabim; and all the more so in a place where one needs to make movement in the iron wire that constantly moves when it goes called in foreign tongue etc.; I saw in responsa Har HaKarmel that he also wrote that it is permitted to extend it while it is going, and see there that there is some hesitation in his words, and his opinion is to permit on the second day of Yom Tov, and one who permits on the first day may not extend it on Yom Tov for his fellow; and he wrote not to rely on it in practice; and it appears that even one who is lenient for himself on the second day of Yom Tov, nevertheless he may not extend it only for the need of the day but not for the need of the day after, whether on weekday or on Shabbos; and in practice it appears to permit for the need of a sick person while it is going even through a Jew if with difficulty to find a gentile; and if he does through a gentile one may permit in every case; and so is the common practice; and see above beginning of siman 336:
