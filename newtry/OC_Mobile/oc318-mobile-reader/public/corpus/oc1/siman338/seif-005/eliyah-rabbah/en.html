[6] On a table, etc. This requires study, because at the end of siman 324 we decree regarding a feeding trough made of a utensil because of one made in the ground, as stated by the Magen Avraham. There is no difficulty. There, when done in the ground, the prohibited result is inevitable, as the Ran writes at the end of Perek Tolin, and Tosafot there writes that one ordinarily intends it. Rabbenu Yerucham writes that it is permitted on a mat, garment, or board. Regarding the game called tam and chaser, Shibolei HaLeket says it is called una muna, and some call it tachsu. The Magen Avraham explains it as grad or um grad in German. Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson prohibits playing chess on Shabbos, and likewise all forms of games, but in places where the custom is lenient one should not protest.
