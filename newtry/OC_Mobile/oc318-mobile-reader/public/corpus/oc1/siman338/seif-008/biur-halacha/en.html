Forbidden on account that one does not, etc. — behold the essence of the reason it is forbidden to place it under the drip is because one does not nullify a utensil from its primary use, that he should not be able afterward to move it from its place since there is muktzeh in it, as is in the Gemara; rather because if it were permitted to make graf shel re'i l'chatchila it would not be called nullifying a utensil from its primary use at all, since it is permitted to take afterward and pour out; as Mechaber concluded at the end; but since we rule one does not make graf shel re'i l'chatchila — therefore he must be prepared l'chatchila that it rest there permanently and now it is properly nullifying a utensil from its primary use [Magen Avraham in the name of Yeshuot Shmuel, and several Acharonim copied this]. Behold this law Mechaber copied from Rambam and Tur disagrees; see in Taz who expanded; and Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rambam. See in Chayei Adam who wrote that in great need one may rely on Tur who ruled that even when unfit it is permitted to place a utensil under it. Behold what we practice to take morning water in a vessel even though they are certainly unfit for washing and unfit for animal drinking, as explained above in siman 337; and likewise the last waters we take into a vessel — perforce because we hold like Tur; and it further appears to me this is not called unfit, for there is no prohibition on them except an evil spirit rests on them and it is not called unfit except if mixed with mud falling from the roof or there is a prohibition on them in any case rabbinically, like tevel rabbinically that the poskim mentioned
