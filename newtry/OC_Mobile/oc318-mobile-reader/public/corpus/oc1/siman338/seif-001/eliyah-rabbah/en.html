[1] To strike, etc. Certainly, if one does not need the sound, such as placing a hollow ring containing a stone that makes noise, it is permitted to put it in place. But if one places water in a perforated vessel so that it drips into a metal vessel and produces a pleasant humming sound, it is prohibited because this resembles a musical instrument. It is permitted for a sick person. If one strikes forcefully merely to wake a sleeper, it is permitted even for a healthy person because it does not resemble a musical instrument, as stated by the Tur and later authorities. Regarding bells on a Sefer Torah or parochet, the Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 282 and here prohibits them, while Nachalat Tzvi and Magen Avraham permit, as stated in siman 339, seif 3. According to this, one might say that people rely on the same reasoning to permit a bell fixed to a house door to make a sound when someone enters. However, below it will be explained that the leniency is specifically regarding clapping and dancing. Moreover, a parochet is used for a mitzvah. Yet according to the Magen Avraham's reason, that the one opening the parochet does not intend the sound, the same should apply to a doorbell. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient based on this reason alone. Rather, we say to leave those who are lenient as we do regarding dancing outside a mitzvah context.
