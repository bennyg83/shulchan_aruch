(א) With musical instrument — not specifically with musical instrument, but also with the hand, such as clapping hand to hand where it is in the manner of joy and song, as is written in siman 339 seif 3 lest he repair a musical instrument [and what they forbade in this seif clapping hand to hand when guarding fruits to drive away birds — even though it is not in the manner of song, the reason is lest he take a stone and throw for Rosh Hashana when he wishes to drive them, as explained there]; and if he wishes to clap hand to hand to gladden the child and quiet him — he should do so with the back of the hand, which is permitted, as explained there. And if they place water in a holed vessel to drip into a metal vessel to make pleasant sound through dripping — forbidden, for this is also like a musical instrument; and for a sick person to put him to sleep it is permitted; and if he drips forcefully to awaken the healthy from his sleep — permitted, for it is not like a musical instrument since it does not act pleasantly and gently — such a sound on the contrary puts to sleep more; rather he does it forcefully so he wakes through the sound — therefore permitted
(ב) But to knock, etc. — meaning even if his intent is to make sound so they open for him it is permitted, even if he knocks with a utensil, since it is not in the manner of song
(ג) Those who call their friends, etc. — meaning even if they sweeten the sound like song it is permitted, for they did not decree in this case when done with the mouth
(ד) Designated for that — meaning designated to make sound — we are concerned lest he intend for song; and all the more to make sound with a utensil [called in foreign tongue "gong"] — obviously forbidden, for it is a musical instrument and he also intends for song. And with iron that the door is locked and opened with it — it appears permitted to knock with it on the door so household members hear to open for him, for this iron is designated only to open the door or lock
(ה) Forbidden — see in Eshel Avraham and Peri Megadim that from this it is heard it is likewise forbidden to pull the doorbell cord on Shabbat so household members and courtyard hear and open for him, for this utensil is designated to make sound and his intent is also for this; see in Biur Halacha; and regarding whether it is permitted to open the door when he knows there is a bell — see below
(ו) And therefore forbidden for servant, etc. — Taz wrote here and in Yoreh De'ah siman 282 that from this it is proven forbidden to hang on Shabbat those curtains that have bells to make sound when opening the ark; and likewise to hang bells on the etz chayim of the Torah scroll; Shach there disagrees because we rule that for need of mitzva it is permitted as is written siman 339 — permitted to dance on Simchat Torah for honor of Torah; here too it is need of mitzva that people hear and stand; and it is not like calling in synagogue that is forbidden, for there it is possible another way; and furthermore that one who opens the curtain does not intend to make sound at all, only to take the Torah scroll [Magen Avraham]; see in Sha'arei Efrayim gate of Yoreh De'ah seif 3 that his view there is l'chatchila one should not do so on Shabbat; and in places where they practiced leniency per Magen Avraham one should not protest; and likewise regarding opening the door when he knows there is a bell — it depends on Taz and Magen Avraham cited above — for Taz forbidden and for Magen Avraham [here and siman 301 s.k. 35 see there] permitted, for when opening the door on Shabbat he does not intend to make sound; and from Gra's words there end of s.k. 23 it is proven he also holds like Taz, for since the bell is designated to make sound there is no difference that he does not intend; and in Eshel Avraham he sided one should not be lenient for this reason alone except regarding curtain which is a matter of mitzva; therefore certainly it is proper to remove erev Shabbat the bell from the door or plug it with wool or cotton so its sound is not heard; but in time of need [such as he forgot to remove erev Shabbat, etc.] it appears he can rely on the reasoning above to be lenient since he does not intend to make sound
(ז) By the designated utensil — and there are places where the servant takes another utensil to strike on Shabbat.
