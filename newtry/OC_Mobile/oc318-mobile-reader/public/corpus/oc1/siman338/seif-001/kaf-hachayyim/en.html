1) [Seif 1] Producing sound with a musical instrument is forbidden. This is a decree lest one repair a musical instrument. Levush.
2) There: With a musical instrument is forbidden. The same applies to anything performed in a musical manner, as stated in siman 339, seif 3. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1.
3) There: With a musical instrument is forbidden. This implies that specifically using an instrument is forbidden, but singing with the mouth alone is permitted. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraphs 1 and 2, writes the same. See below, paragraph 7.
4) There: When it is not musical, it is permitted. Certainly, it is permitted when one does not need the sound, such as wearing a hollow ring containing a stone that makes noise. Since one does not desire that sound, all opinions permit it, even the authority who rules that producing any sound is forbidden. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. Tur writes the same in the name of Maharam. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1. See above, siman 301, seif 23, and our words there in paragraph 133.
5) If water is placed in a perforated utensil and drips into a metal vessel to produce a pleasant humming sound, it is forbidden because it is like a musical instrument. It is permitted for an ill person to help him sleep. If the water drips forcefully to awaken a sleeping person, it is permitted even for a healthy person, because it does not resemble a musical instrument, since it does not produce a gentle, pleasant sound. Tur; Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1.
6) Leather boots worn during the rainy season, called gezmes, have iron nails called nalges fixed in their heels and make a loud sound while walking. Similarly, walking sticks used by the elderly have a large piece of iron fixed at their lower end that makes a sound when it strikes stones on the ground. All opinions permit these. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 2. See above, siman 301, seif 17, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
7) There, gloss: And one may whistle with the mouth, etc. This is the basis for the Ashkenazic and Polish practice of whistling and modulating the throat while singing, although it sounds exactly like playing a musical instrument. I have heard that holy sages and pious people also sang this way, and one hearing them would think he heard an actual musical instrument. The later authorities agree. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1. The renowned Rabbi Pinchas Anav, head of the court and rosh yeshivah of the holy community of Ferrara, however, inclines in Responsa Givas Shaul, manuscript, volume 1, siman 36, to forbid whistling like a bird or producing another melodic whistle on Shabbos. Birkei Yosef, Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1; Machazik Berachah there.
8) There, gloss: Like a bird. This is called pfeifen in the vernacular. Although this whistle is sometimes made melodically, when one intends to call a friend he does not produce a melody, and it is permitted. He also performs no instrumental act. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 4, in the name of Hagahos Alfasi. This implies that producing a melody is forbidden. Although singing with the mouth is permitted, this differs because it resembles a musical instrument. Nevertheless, it appears to me that it is permitted because no instrumental act is performed. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2.
