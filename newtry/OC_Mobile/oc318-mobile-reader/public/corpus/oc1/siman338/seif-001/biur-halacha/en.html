(א) But to knock on the door, etc. — and in Beur HaGra he wrote that the essence here is like the Yerushalmi who is strict here; behold his view is like R' Chaim brought in Rif, see there; but the world is accustomed to be lenient like Shulchan Aruch; yet this is difficult to me, for he wrote it is permitted only if he does not intend for sound at all, like the pit of the wheel and the like — and he wanted to resolve that there is no proof from Ameimar who permitted initially in Mehoza and was not concerned for making sound, and what will you answer the plain Gemara there that initially brought proof from the Mishnah of filling from the pit of the wheel — it should have said "according to you the Mishnah is not proof, for he did not intend for sound"; and perforce as Maggid Mishneh wrote there chapter 23, perhaps there is need for sound in this, see there; and perhaps this is only per the Gemara's view there, but for the Gemara's conclusion we no longer need all this; and the essence of the reason for all is only lest he fill his garden and ruin; nevertheless it appears to knock on the door with the back of the hand is permitted for all even if his intent is to make sound, for it is no better than clapping hand to hand where too his intent is to make sound and nevertheless Shulchan Aruch is lenient in siman 339 seif 3, see there

(ב) On the door, etc. — behold in Beit Yosef he brought this in the name of Agur and questioned there the reason; if he holds like Ula, even when not designated for that it is forbidden; and if like Rabba, even when designated, since it is not sound of song it is permitted; and he resolved the reason lest he intend for song; and it is difficult, for it is clear in Eruvin 104 that where he intends to wake his friend and knock, he does not strike pleasantly in the manner of song — such a strike on the contrary puts him to sleep; rather he strikes forcefully and thereby wakes and arouses him — why is there concern lest he intend for song; and if we say if we permit this he will come to strike pleasantly in the manner of song — if so why did the Gemara permit dripping for a healthy person with a strong thread to wake him; let us fear lest he drip another time pleasantly; and indeed one can say here, since it is a utensil designated to make sound, we are concerned for this, unlike there. But know that in Rambam's explanation of the Mishnah it is implied it is permitted to knock on the door even with the chain fixed there, since he does not act in the manner of song

(ג) Since the utensil is designated for that — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote in the name of Eshel Avraham and Peri Megadim; thereby Magen Avraham also agrees it is forbidden since his intent is to make sound. I saw in Sefer Kalkalas Shabbat who wrote that in a place of need when he cannot enter house or courtyard without this there is room to be lenient because we can rely on Rif's view that when it is not in the manner of song it is permitted — I do not know what he says, for per what Beit Yosef and Rama concluded, in a utensil designated for that it is different even per Rif who ruled like Rabba; perhaps he holds that since Mechaber wrote plainly knocking on the door is permitted and did not stipulate only a utensil not designated, he holds that per Rif in every case it is permitted; and Rama who brought Agur disagrees; and that we find elsewhere similarly is strained; but perhaps he holds Mechaber holds per Rif in every case permitted since it is not sound of song; and Rama who brought Agur disagrees; and what he brought from the plain Mishnah that kings' sons go out with bells — very difficult to rely on this, for Rama in siman 301 s.k. 23 ruled there plainly to forbid; and Gra there wrote the Mishnah deals with a plugged bell of a grape so the sound of the bell is not heard; even per Magen Avraham there s.k. 35 who permits even unplugged — his reason there is because he does not need sound; unlike here where he intends to make sound so they open for him — forbidden also for Magen Avraham; and as Peri Megadim wrote here in M.Z. in this siman; nevertheless one can say this is only per Rama's ruling that in a utensil designated to make sound it is forbidden even if not sound of song; therefore in time of need we can rely on what we brought above in Rambam's explanation of the Mishnah that he too holds this is permitted since it is not sound of song; and also in Hagahat Maimoniot there it is implied he is lenient with a bell since he does not need sound — he needs this only per R' Chaim; unlike Rif's ruling it would be permitted in every case since it is not sound of song; and one can say otherwise that Rama ruled in a utensil designated to make sound it is forbidden not because it is a complete shvut, rather because it resembles weekday activity; and so is somewhat implied in Peri Megadim s.k. 3 and in Sefer Tosafot Shabbat who permits knocking on the door with designated utensil only for Shabbat [see siman 327 seif 4 in Magen Avraham and Tur there]; if so per what is explained in siman 333 seif 1 in the gloss that shvut is permitted for need of mitzva — namely something that is only on account of weekday activity, as Peri Megadim wrote there [for a complete shvut certainly forbidden to do oneself] — therefore in our case when he cannot enter his house at night to sleep there is no greater Shabbat need than this [and do not refute this from Yerushalmi brought in Rosh and Beit Yosef that Ravin slept on the bolt because he did not want to knock on the door — there he held making sound is also forbidden, not specifically musical instrument, and it is complete shvut and we do not permit for Shabbat need; unlike for us we rule like Rabba who forbade only sound of song but making sound they did not forbid at all — we can say plainly what they forbade in designated utensil is only weekday activity; therefore for mitzva need it is permitted]; but all this we wrote to find a way in time of need; but l'chatchila he must urge himself not to come to this; and also b'dieved if he can pull the cord through some good change it is better, as explained in siman 339 seif 3 that for doubt one may use the back of the hand; and even though regarding jingling the clock pair it is implied there one should not be lenient, as explained there in Biur Halacha — nevertheless in time of need one should not be strict. The conclusion: to pull the bell-cord on Shabbat is explicit in several Acharonim [Eshel Avraham, Peri Megadim, and also from Gra it is clear] as forbidden; but when he cannot enter his courtyard to sleep without this we can rely to be lenient for honor of Shabbat; but to pull the bell cord made in his house to make sound for the maidservant — one should not be lenient at all, for he can do otherwise and there is degradation of Shabbat in this
