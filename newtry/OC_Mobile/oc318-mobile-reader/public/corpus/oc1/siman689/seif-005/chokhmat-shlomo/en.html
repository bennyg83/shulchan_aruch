Section 5. "And if all of them know, each reads for himself." Note: See Magen Avraham, seif katan 10, who brought opinions on whether one person discharges his fellow with megillah when there are fewer than ten; see there. At first glance it was difficult for me: what does the Gemara in Megillah challenge from the baraisa of the fifteenth that fell on Shabbos, regarding the statement that megillah in its proper time may be read individually, but not in its proper time requires ten? See there: "their times," does this not mean that not in their times is like in their times; just as in their times it may be read individually, so too not in their times it may be read individually. What is the challenge? Let us say that the baraisa speaks regarding whether an individual can read for himself; in this, not in their times is like in their times, and even not in their times he reads individually. Rav, however, speaks regarding whether an individual discharges his fellow, and in this he says: megillah in its proper time may be read individually, meaning that one is enough even to discharge his fellow, like the language of Rabbeinu Yerucham brought by Magen Avraham, that an individual discharges his fellow, and this language appears in several places in the poskim and in the laws of Rosh Hashanah; see there. Therefore he said, in its proper time, even individually. But not in its proper time, to discharge his fellow, we require ten. What then is the challenge? Perforce it would be proven that it is not logical to say that megillah requires ten in that sense. At first glance, one could reject this proof based on what appears to me: the disagreement of the poskim mentioned above depends on the Gemara's disagreement as to why Hallel is not recited on Purim. One opinion says: we are still servants of Achashverosh. The other says: its reading is its Hallel. According to the one who says that its reading is its Hallel, it is like prayer, and therefore it is proper to require ten. So too it is explicit in Magen Avraham in the laws of Rosh Chodesh, where he wrote in the name of Bedek HaBayis that the law of Hallel is like the law of prayer, and he brought proof for Hallel from a mishnah that speaks about the law of prayer; see there. But according to the one who does not say that its reading is its Hallel, it is not like prayer, and therefore we do not require ten. Accordingly, the one who holds that we do not require ten holds that the halachah follows the one who says we are servants of Hashem and not servants of Pharaoh, etc.; but the other, who holds that we require ten, holds that the halachah follows the one who says its reading is its Hallel, and therefore he holds that we require ten. According to this it is well understood: in its time, when the obligation is to read Hallel, this reading is in place of Hallel and therefore we require ten. But not in its time, there is no connection to Hallel, since feasting, joy, and all the laws of Purim apply only in its time, and the earlier reading is only for remembrance and publicizing. Therefore ten certainly does not apply to this. For that reason it is impossible to say that "not in its time requires ten" means that one person discharges his fellow only with ten; perforce the meaning is that he himself fulfills only with ten. But according to this, one can prove that the ten required are not satisfied by women and minors. Hagahos Ashiri is uncertain about this, and according to the above one can prove that they do not help. For if they did help, the Gemara's challenge is difficult: let us say the opposite, that what Rav said, not in its time requires ten, means that he himself does not fulfill with fewer than ten; and what the Tanna said regarding the eve of Shabbos, "their times," means the reverse: just as in their times one does not discharge his fellow except with ten, so too not in their times also requires ten. That is, although on weekdays it does not require ten because Hallel has no connection then, since it is not the day of the miracle and Hallel does not apply, therefore ten is not required; but on the eve of Shabbos, since in any case it is the day of the miracle, one does not discharge his fellow except with ten. What then is the challenge? In truth, however, the Gemara challenges properly, for if not in its time requires ten, then in any case ten are required even when he reads for himself, all the more so to discharge his fellow. For what then does it teach us that the eve of Shabbos is their time? But if we say that regarding those ten required not in its time, women and minors can join, while regarding discharging one another, if its law is like a matter of sanctity, we require ten adult males, then one might have thought that since not in its time Hallel does not apply, although ten are required, minors and women suffice. Therefore it teaches that the eve of Shabbos is their time, since it is the day of the miracle and to discharge his fellow ten are required, specifically adult males. Let us say that Rav holds like Rav Nachman, that its reading is its Hallel, and therefore the question from the eve of Shabbos as their time is not difficult for him. Since the Gemara did not answer this way, it is proven that women and minors do not help; rather, adult males are always required, and therefore it could not answer this way. Analyze this well.
