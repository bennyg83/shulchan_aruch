5) Ibid. And freed servants. The Tur wrote this without qualification. Beit Yosef wrote regarding his words that it refers to freed servants, as Rambam and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol wrote. As for servants who underwent circumcision and immersion for the sake of servitude, their obligation is derived from the law of women mentioned afterwards, for a servant is likewise obligated in every mitzvah in which a woman is obligated. However, he concluded that according to those who explain that 'they too were included in that miracle' means that the miracle was brought about through a woman, it is possible that servants cannot be derived from women, and since this is a time-bound positive commandment, servants are exempt. End quote. Here, in the Shulchan Aruch, he copied the wording of Rambam, which implies that his intent is as he wrote in the Beit Yosef, that servants are derived from women and are obligated, unlike the second explanation, which he presented only as a possibility. So too wrote Bach, that according to all opinions servants themselves, even if they are not freed, are obligated to read it, see there. This is likewise the opinion of Pri Chadash in seif 3, and it appears to be the opinion of Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. However, the opinion of Ateres Zekeinim is to exempt them, and there are additional poskim who hold likewise. Nevertheless, since several great authorities obligate them and this matter remains subject to dispute, a servant must fulfill the obligation according to all opinions. With regard to the blessing, however, he should not recite it, nor should others recite it for him, because of the principle that in cases of uncertainty concerning blessings we are lenient.
