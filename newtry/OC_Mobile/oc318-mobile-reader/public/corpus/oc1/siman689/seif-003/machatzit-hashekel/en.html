Seif katan 3: A deaf person, and the Bach ruled, etc. And so it appears in the Gemara. For we learned: all are fit to read the megillah except a deaf person, an imbecile, and a minor. It initially assumed that the mishnah follows Rabbi Yose. For we learned: one who recites Shema and did not make it heard to his own ears has fulfilled, and these are the words of Rabbi Yehudah; Rabbi Yose says he has not fulfilled. He holds that megillah and Shema have the same law, and the law of one who did not make it heard to his own ears is like the law of a deaf person. It initially assumed that what we learned, "except a deaf person, an imbecile, and a minor," means that even after the fact, one who heard from a deaf person, an imbecile, or a minor did not fulfill; that a deaf person is comparable to an imbecile and a minor, where the listener has not fulfilled even after the fact. It concludes that in truth it is Rabbi Yehudah, who said regarding Shema that he has fulfilled, but initially he agrees that we require that he make it heard to his own ears; and here, where it teaches "except a deaf person," it means initially not. Although an imbecile and a minor are invalid even after the fact, this one is as it is and that one is as it is. Since we hold regarding Shema like Rabbi Yehudah, that if he did not make it heard to his own ears he has fulfilled, if so the same law applies to megillah: one who hears the megillah from a deaf person has fulfilled. However, the Rav Beis Yosef in Shulchan Aruch copied the words of the Rambam: although regarding Shema he ruled that if he did not make it heard to his own ears he has fulfilled, nevertheless regarding megillah he ruled that one who hears from a deaf person has not fulfilled. The Rav Beis Yosef wrote that his reason is that Rebbe stated anonymously in Berachos regarding Shema that he has fulfilled without making it heard to his own ears, and in Megillah he stated anonymously, "except a deaf person, an imbecile, and a minor," and the plain meaning is that a deaf person is like an imbecile and a minor, that even after the fact one has not fulfilled. Perforce one must distinguish between Shema and megillah, because megillah is for publicizing the miracle and is more stringent. One must say that the words of the Gemara in Megillah, which compare Shema to megillah, do not stand as practical halachah and were said only for the give and take. But the plain implication of the Gemara's words is that megillah and Shema are equal, and just as with Shema, if he did not make it heard to his own ears he has fulfilled, so too one who hears the megillah from a deaf person has fulfilled, as the Bach says.
