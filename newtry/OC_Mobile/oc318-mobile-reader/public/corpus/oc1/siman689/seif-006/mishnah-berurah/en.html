16) It is a good custom to bring, etc. In order to educate them in the mitzvah of publicizing the miracle. For this reason the congregation is accustomed to read those verses aloud, as below in siman 690, section 17, for they are the essence of the beginning and end of the miracle, namely "There was a Jewish man," "And Mordechai went out," "For the Jews," and "For Mordechai." This is to awaken the children so they do not sleep and so they set their minds on the reading; they prompt them with those verses in order to educate them, Levush.
17) Male and female children. Provided that they do not bring very small children who confuse the listeners' attention, Magen Avraham and other authorities.
18) To hear, etc. Now, because of our many sins, the opposite occurs: not only do they not listen, but they confuse others so that the adults also cannot hear, and their entire coming is only to strike Haman. In this the father does not fulfill the mitzvah of education at all. In truth, because of the mitzvah of education, every father must keep his young children near him and watch over them so that they hear the reading. When the reader reaches the mention of Haman the Agagite, the child may strike him according to his custom, but this should not be the main reason for bringing the child to the study hall.
19) The reading of the megillah. Nowadays it is proper for each person to have a valid megillah and to read quietly, word by word, because it is impossible to hear from the prayer leader because of the noise and confusion caused by striking with wood and making sound. It is proper for all Israel who are able to conduct themselves this way, Peri Megadim. See below, siman 690, section 4.
