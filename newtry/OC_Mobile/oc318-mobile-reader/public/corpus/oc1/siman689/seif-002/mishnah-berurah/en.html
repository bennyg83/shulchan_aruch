4) He has fulfilled his obligation. The reader must intend to discharge, and the listener must intend to fulfill, like the law of other mitzvos where one person discharges another, and as below in siman 690, section 14.
5) A deaf person. This means one who speaks but does not hear at all. Although generally we hold that if he did not make it heard to his own ear he has fulfilled, as above in siman 62 and siman 185, here regarding megillah, which is because of publicizing the miracle, they were more stringent, and hearing it with his own ear is indispensable. Alternatively, a deaf person is worse, because he cannot make it heard to his own ear. See Shaarei Teshuvah, that this is specifically if he is completely deaf and does not hear at all, excluding one who hears when they speak to him in a loud voice. All this is according to the opinion of Shulchan Aruch, but several later authorities hold that even if he is completely deaf, one who hears from him has fulfilled. Nevertheless, initially everyone agrees that they should not appoint him to discharge the public from their obligation. Know that one whose ears are heavy, or who is far from the bimah and cannot hear the reader well, should be careful to read for himself from a valid megillah, or at least to hold a Chumash and immediately say from the Chumash the words that he misses.
6) Or a minor. Although a minor is also obligated at least rabbinically, as above in section 1, nevertheless he cannot discharge an adult, for regarding a minor it is two rabbinic levels, meaning that the reading of the megillah itself is only from the words of the Scribes, and a minor's obligation in all mitzvos is also only rabbinic, whereas an adult has one rabbinic level. Two rabbinic levels do not come and discharge one rabbinic level. Above in siman 675, section 3, the Mechaber brought that there is one who validates a minor who has reached the age of education, that he can discharge others with his lighting, and the same applies regarding reading the megillah. See Ikrei Dinim, who wrote in the name of Sama DeChayei that in pressing circumstances, when there are no men expert in reading the megillah, a minor who has reached the age of education can discharge them.
7) And some say that women do not discharge, etc. This is not comparable to Chanukah lights, for megillah is different, since it is like Torah reading, and she is invalid because of the honor of the congregation; therefore, even for an individual she does not discharge, because the Sages did not differentiate. Also, it is possible that a woman is not obligated in reading, only in hearing, as below, and she is called one who is not obligated in the matter relative to a man. But a woman discharges her female fellow.
8) If the woman read, etc. See Magen Avraham, who inclines that she should not read for herself at all, but should hear from the men. All this is where she has someone from whom to hear. But if she has no one to read before her, she should read for herself from a valid megillah and bless: Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to hear the reading of the megillah, Chayei Adam.
