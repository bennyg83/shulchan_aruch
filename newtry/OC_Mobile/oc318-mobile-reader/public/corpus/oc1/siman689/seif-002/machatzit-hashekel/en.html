Seif katan 2, "And slaves." The correct reading is: it is implied by Tur that even those who are not, etc. Tur wrote simply "slaves," whereas in Shulchan Aruch Beit Yosef wrote "freed slaves." Beit Yosef explains that this depends upon how one understands the statement of R' Yehoshua ben Levi that women are obligated in reading the Megillah because "they too were included in that miracle." According to the explanation that "they too were included in that miracle" means that the miracle itself came about through a woman, unfreed slaves are exempt. Reading the Megillah is a time-bound positive mitzvah, from which women would ordinarily be exempt; their obligation is exceptional because the miracle came through a woman. That rationale does not apply to slaves. According to the explanation that "they too were included in that miracle" means that women too were included in Haman's decree "to destroy, kill, and annihilate," then the same applies to unfreed slaves. Since they underwent brit milah and immersion and observe the Jewish religion like women, they too were certainly subject, Heaven forbid, to the cup of that decree. Accordingly, they too are obligated.
