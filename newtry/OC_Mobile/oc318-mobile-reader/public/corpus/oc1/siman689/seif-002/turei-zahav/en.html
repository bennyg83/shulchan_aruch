If the reader was deaf etc. — Mishnah: all are fit to read Megillah except deaf-mute etc.; R' Yehuda permits a minor; who taught deaf unfit even b'dieved? R' Yehuda, since we teach: one who reads Shema and did not make it heard to his ear etc. until now that we come to this etc.; see what Beit Yosef wondered — truly a great difficulty: whatever you answer to be strict on deaf in Megillah is difficult why establish Mishnah as R' Yosi when even R' Yosi agrees here deaf forbidden — it appears all is fine: certainly deaf is worse than one who can hear but didn't make heard to his ear — he is at least fit to hear unlike deaf; per Chazal "all fit for mixing" etc.; therefore at the beginning we explain R' Yehuda and R' Yosi — R' Yehuda even l'chatchila fit in Shema since he doesn't require "hear" at all as in chapter "he was reading" — R' Yehuda derives Shema in any language you hear; no lesson from lack of hearing — likewise deaf; therefore forced to establish Mishnah as R' Yosi who disqualifies even without making heard, kal vachomer deaf here; end we establish Mishnah as R' Yehuda per Raavad who said must make heard to his ear — difficult in his words since "must" implies l'chatchila as Rashi; then brings verse implying even b'dieved since verse didn't mention l'chatchila — verse excludes only totally unfit to hear, i.e. deaf not fit to hear; but one fit to hear, lack of making heard doesn't prevent — only l'chatchila warning; therefore our Mishnah disqualifying deaf for Tanna Kama even per R' Yehuda — deaf is different; gemara didn't initially distinguish deaf from not making heard because they thought R' Yehuda doesn't require derasha "make heard to your ear"; we needn't distinguish fit to hear or not; if we want to distinguish saying from reason there is inferiority in lack of hearing — cannot distinguish deaf from not making heard; therefore as challenged initially — perhaps R' Yehuda, l'chatchila forbidden — then wouldn't distinguish deaf from not making heard except both l'chatchila forbidden — then we'd know no verse; in conclusion they derive from verse inferiority of lack of hearing and verse certainly disqualifies even b'dieved — plain disqualification except Tanna said l'chatchila language but b'dieved not — therefore we must distinguish: verse speaks of deaf disqualified b'dieved since not fit to hear at all; Tanna's l'chatchila language speaks of not making heard — all fit for mixing etc.; therefore our Mishnah on deaf even b'dieved disqualifies even per R' Yehuda; though derasha in conclusion Mishnah also l'chatchila only, poskim don't accept — no l'chatchila distinction except not making heard; deaf disqualified even b'dieved — clear and bright without doubt.
