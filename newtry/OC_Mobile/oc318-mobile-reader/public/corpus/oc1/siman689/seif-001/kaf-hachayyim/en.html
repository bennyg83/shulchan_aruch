1) [Seif 1] Everyone is obligated in its reading, etc. Although the world contains 252 provinces, as stated in Midrash Rabbah on the Megillah, and Achashverosh ruled over only 127, according to this, what reason is there that those who were not under his authority and upon whom Haman's decree did not take effect are obligated in this reading? See at the end of Maaseh Rokeach on Rambam, vol. 3, 160d, where the author's grandson cited what R' Bigdei Aharon Peretz, of blessed memory, wrote on 51a in the name of the mekubalim: The 12 tribes correspond to the 12 permutations of the Name Havayah. If one tribe is lacking, the Name is incomplete and the world cannot endure, all the more so under Haman's decree, when there were 2 tribes, see there. This is why the Megillah begins with a vav in the word 'Vayehi' and concludes with a vav in the word 'zar'o,' for the numerical value of 2 vavs is 12, corresponding to the tribes of the sons of Yaakov. This alludes that because the 12 tribes correspond to the 12 permutations of the Name Havayah, therefore everyone, meaning all Jews in all their dwelling places, is obligated in its reading. Yafeh LaLev, vol. 3, letter 1. However, see Megillah 11a, where it is written that he ruled over the entire world. One can say that this applied only regarding certain matters in which they obeyed him, but not that he could destroy them, for they were not actually under his authority like the 127 provinces. Otherwise, why did it enumerate 127? It should have said that he ruled over the entire world. One can further say that although the world contains 252 provinces, the 127 constitute the majority, and we follow the majority. One can further say that although the world contains 252 provinces, at that time the Jewish people were dispersed only throughout the 127 provinces of Achashverosh. This is what Haman said to Achashverosh: 'Scattered and dispersed throughout all the provinces of your kingdom.' Analyze this carefully:
2) Ibid. And women. For they too were included in that miracle. Megillah 4a. Meaning, although reading the Megillah is a mitzvas aseh that comes from time to time, and women are exempt from a time-bound positive commandment, as stated in Kiddushin 29a, nevertheless, here they are obligated because they too were included in that miracle, as it is written in the verse, 'children and women, on one day.' However, there are those who explain that 'they too were included in that miracle' means that the miracle was brought about through a woman, as written in the Beit Yosef. See below, letter 5:
3) Ibid. And women. Therefore, one must read it in his home before the unmarried girls. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. So too is it stated in Tosafos, Arachin 3a, s.v. Pask: R' Yehoshua would gather all the members of his household and read before them. R' Yonah, the father of R' Menachem, would make a point of reading it before the women in his household, etc., see there. In some places, the custom is that they go to the women's section of the synagogue to hear the reading. Yet there they do not hear the Megillah at all, so how do they fulfill their obligation? Eshel Avraham, letter 1. One can say that not all places are alike. Indeed, if they did not hear it properly, they must hear it again at home from men. See below, letter 18:
4) Ibid. And converts. For it is written, 'They confirmed and accepted,' etc., 'and upon all those who joined them,' etc.:
5) Ibid. And freed servants. The Tur wrote this without qualification. Beit Yosef wrote regarding his words that it refers to freed servants, as Rambam and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol wrote. As for servants who underwent circumcision and immersion for the sake of servitude, their obligation is derived from the law of women mentioned afterwards, for a servant is likewise obligated in every mitzvah in which a woman is obligated. However, he concluded that according to those who explain that 'they too were included in that miracle' means that the miracle was brought about through a woman, it is possible that servants cannot be derived from women, and since this is a time-bound positive commandment, servants are exempt. End quote. Here, in the Shulchan Aruch, he copied the wording of Rambam, which implies that his intent is as he wrote in the Beit Yosef, that servants are derived from women and are obligated, unlike the second explanation, which he presented only as a possibility. So too wrote Bach, that according to all opinions servants themselves, even if they are not freed, are obligated to read it, see there. This is likewise the opinion of Pri Chadash in seif 3, and it appears to be the opinion of Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. However, the opinion of Ateres Zekeinim is to exempt them, and there are additional poskim who hold likewise. Nevertheless, since several great authorities obligate them and this matter remains subject to dispute, a servant must fulfill the obligation according to all opinions. With regard to the blessing, however, he should not recite it, nor should others recite it for him, because of the principle that in cases of uncertainty concerning blessings we are lenient. If possible, he should hear the blessing from others who are reciting it for themselves and intend to fulfill his obligation, or recite it without Hashem's Name and Kingship:
