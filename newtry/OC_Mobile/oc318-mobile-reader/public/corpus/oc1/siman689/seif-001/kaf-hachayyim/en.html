1) [Seif 1] Everyone is obligated in its reading, etc. Although the world contains 252 provinces, as stated in Midrash Rabbah on the Megillah, and Achashverosh ruled over only 127, according to this, what reason is there that those who were not under his authority and upon whom Haman's decree did not take effect are obligated in this reading? See at the end of Maaseh Rokeach on Rambam, vol. 3, 160d, where the author's grandson cited what R' Bigdei Aharon Peretz, of blessed memory, wrote on 51a in the name of the mekubalim: The 12 tribes correspond to the 12 permutations of the Name Havayah. If one tribe is lacking, the Name is incomplete and the world cannot endure, all the more so under Haman's decree, when there were 2 tribes, see there. This is why the Megillah begins with a vav in the word 'Vayehi' and concludes with a vav in the word 'zar'o,' for the numerical value of 2 vavs is 12, corresponding to the tribes of the sons of Yaakov. This alludes that because the 12 tribes correspond to the 12 permutations of the Name Havayah, therefore everyone, meaning all Jews in all their dwelling places, is obligated in its reading. Yafeh LaLev, vol. 3, letter 1. However, see Megillah 11a, where it is written that he ruled over the entire world. One can say that this applied only regarding certain matters in which they obeyed him, but not that he could destroy them, for they were not actually under his authority like the 127 provinces. Otherwise, why did it enumerate 127? It should have said that he ruled over the entire world. One can further say that although the world contains 252 provinces, the 127 constitute the majority, and we follow the majority. One can further say that although the world contains 252 provinces, at that time the Jewish people were dispersed only throughout the 127 provinces of Achashverosh. This is what Haman said to Achashverosh: 'Scattered and dispersed throughout all the provinces of your kingdom.' Analyze this carefully:
