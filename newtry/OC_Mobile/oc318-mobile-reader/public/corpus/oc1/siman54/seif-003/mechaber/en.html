One who speaks between Yishtabach and Yotzer—it is a transgression in his hand, and he returns from the ranks of the army. But there are those who say that for communal needs, or to distribute charity to one who comes to receive sustenance from charity, it is permitted to interrupt.From this spread the custom in many places to bless a sick person or to accept a claim in court between Yishtabach and Yotzer, for all this is called for the sake of a mitzvah. And afterward, when they return to pray, the prayer leader says some pesukei dezimra and says Kaddish on them, for one never says Kaddish without praise before it; therefore they begin Maariv without Kaddish (Kol Bo). Likewise one who did not have tzitzit or tefillin and they were] brought to him between Yishtabach and Kaddish may put them on and bless on them. But between Kaddish and Barkhu he may not interrupt for anything (Or Zarua, chapter Tefillat Hashachar), and all the more so he may not interrupt after the prayer leader said Barkhu before they begin the Yotzer blessing (Beit Yosef end of siman 57 in the name of the Maharil, and see there end of siman 56).]:
