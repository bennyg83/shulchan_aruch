(4) One who speaks—and in Ashkenazic communities they wrote to say on the Ten Days of Repentance the psalm "Out of the depths I have called You, Hashem" between Yishtabach and Yotzer—uncertain.

(5) From the ranks of the army—meaning as written in Scripture, "Who is the man who is fearful and soft of heart, let him go and return to his house," and our Sages expounded: fearful of transgressions in his hand; and this too is included as a transgression. The Arizal wrote in the name of Mateh Moshe that the kelipot prevent prayer from ascending, and through pesukei dezimra they are cut off; when one speaks they return because of this transgression—those troops that array war against us, Heaven forbid; therefore one ought not speak.

(6) To distribute charity—the same applies to every mitzvah matter; specifically between Yishtabach and Yotzer, which is a separate matter. But between Kaddish and Barkhu, or between the sections of Shema and its blessings, or of pesukei dezimra, it is forbidden to interrupt even for communal needs and a mitzvah matter, as above in siman 51, seif 4 (Rema and the Gra there, see there).

(7) The prayer leader—implies that even if he alone did not say them, only he said the verses, he may say Kaddish.

(8) Some pesukei dezimra—if he did not say verses he should not say Kaddish; he should only begin Barkhu without Kaddish—for one never finds Kaddish without verses before it, except the prayer leader after the repetition of the Amidah, or silently at Maariv when the Kaddish refers to the prayer. Magen Avraham wrote that therefore they are accustomed to say the Shema spread (Barkhu is called Shema spread, as below siman 69) after finishing the silent Shemoneh Esreh, so that Kaddish may be said on the Shemoneh Esreh; but afterward it is not appropriate to say Kaddish unless he first said some verses. Now newcomers have come who spread Shema after the final Kaddish and say Kaddish again—it is not proper; what reason is there to say two Kaddishim adjacent to one another? At minimum they should be taught to say some verses between the two Kaddishim.

(9) Without praise—or after prayer, as we wrote in the previous section. Likewise after one studied some matter of Oral Torah he may say Kaddish derabbanan; see Magen Avraham, who concludes that specifically if afterward he said at least some aggadic words, then he may say Kaddish derabbanan, for the essence of this Kaddish was instituted on aggadic matter; therefore the custom of all Israel is to say after Pirkei Avot and after "In what do they light" the statement of Rabbi Chanania ben Akashya, or "Rabbi Elazar said: Rabbi Chanina said: Torah scholars increase peace," etc. Therefore one should be careful when studying Mishnayot to say at the end of study Rabbi Chanania ben Akashya and the like, so that afterward they may say Kaddish derabbanan thereby. After Scripture they say full Kaddish, even after only three verses. One should not say Kaddish unless ten people were in the synagogue at the time of saying the verses and study, as will be explained in siman 55 in Mishnah Berurah seif 2. Even if only two or three are studying, they say Kaddish when ten are present; and even one who did not study may also say Kaddish, as in spreading Shema below siman 69.

(10) Maariv without Kaddish—see below siman 69 what we wrote there on this.

(11) And likewise one who did not have—for all this is a mitzvah need; therefore it is permitted to interrupt after finishing the blessing of Yishtabach.

(12) And they brought to him—whether for an individual or the prayer leader; and although for the congregation it is proper to wait, nevertheless since it is impossible otherwise it is permitted; and the prayer leader need not repeat and say pesukei dezimra, for the pause is brief (Darkhei Chaim). It appears this speaks of an individual in a case where he can finish donning tallit and tefillin before the prayer leader reaches Kaddish and Barkhu, or he already heard Kaddish and Barkhu—for otherwise we hold in siman 25 that it is forbidden to interrupt between donning the hand and the head, even to answer Kaddish and Barkhu; rather he is silent and listens and intends what is said—and it is not proper to do so ab initio to fulfill thereby, even according to our ruling "one who hears is as though he answered," as proven in Berakhot 21b in the passage "until" and in Rashi there. In this manner it is better that he wait from donning tefillin until the prayer leader finishes Barkhu, then don them; and regarding the blessings see the adjacent section.

(13) Between Kaddish—meaning from when Kaddish began; the Kaddish before Barkhu is also included. See the later authorities who wrote that between Kaddish and Barkhu its law is for all matters like between the chapters; and immediately after Barkhu, even though he has not yet begun the Yotzer blessing, it is like the middle of the chapter—for from there onward it is like the beginning of Yotzer, for the essence of what the prayer leader says "Bless Hashem" is so that they bless Him afterward in the Yotzer blessing. Therefore both individual and prayer leader: if a tallit did not become available until Kaddish began, he may wrap himself immediately but not bless until after prayer, then touch it and bless. Tefillin: if they became available before Barkhu he may bless on them, according to what the latter agreed in siman 66 that one may bless on tefillin between chapters; but if they did not become available until Barkhu began, he can no longer bless on them but puts them on without a blessing, and when he reaches before Ahavah Rabbah he touches them and blesses. See nearby.

(14) After he said, etc.—from this wording it is proven that even an individual may not interrupt; see the previous section. It appears all this is specifically when it speaks of facing the prayer, i.e., he already finished Yishtabach and stands to begin Yotzer Or; but if he is still engaged in pesukei dezimra, and all the more if he now came to pray and wants to pray in order and not skip pesukei dezimra and open from Yotzer and hear Kaddish and Barkhu—his law in this is not like the middle of the chapter; all agree he may don tallit and tefillin and bless on them; see in the glosses of the Gaon Rabbi Chaim of Sanz.
