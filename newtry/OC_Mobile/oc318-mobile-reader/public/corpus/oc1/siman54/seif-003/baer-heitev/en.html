For Yotzer. In writings it is written to say on the ten days of repentance the psalm Mimma'amakim karatikha the Omnipresent between Yishtabach and Yotzer—and it requires investigation. Magen Avraham:

Dezimra. And if he did not say verses he should not say Kaddish, but should begin Barekhu without Kaddish. And the one who repeats Shema after [the amidah] should not say Kaddish unless he says verses—for one never finds Kaddish without verses before it, except after the end of prayer; therefore the custom is to say Keriat Shema after the end of the Shemoneh Esreh silently, so that Kaddish may be said on the Shemoneh Esreh; but afterward Kaddish is not applicable. And now, recently, the custom spread to repeat Shema after Kaddish batra and say Kaddish a second time—and it is not correct: what reason is there to say two Kaddishim adjacent to each other? At minimum one should teach them to say some verses between the two Kaddishim. And one may not say Kaddish except after a matter of aggadah; and when one studies Mishnayot or any oral Torah one must say aggadah after it—that is, Rabbi Elazar, Rabbi Hanina, talmidei chakhamim increase peace, etc.—in order to say Kaddish on it. Magen Avraham there, and see above siman 55, seif katan 1, from Rashbash:

Barekhu. After the shaliach tzibbur has begun Kaddish he should not interrupt again for tallit, even between the chapters—see siman 66, seif 2. This is what Rama wrote here; but between Kaddish and Barekhu, etc., and all the more so that he should not interrupt, etc. Magen Avraham:
