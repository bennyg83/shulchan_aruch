To Yotzer. The writings state that during the Ten Days of Repentance one should recite the psalm 'From the depths I called You, Hashem' between Yishtabach and Yotzer. This requires study. Magen Avraham.
Of praise. If he did not recite verses, he should not say Kaddish, but should begin Barechu without Kaddish. One who conducts a shortened repetition of Krias Shema should not say Kaddish unless he first recites verses, for one never finds Kaddish without verses preceding it, except after completion of the prayer. Therefore, the custom developed to conduct the shortened repetition of Krias Shema after completion of the silent Amidah, so that Kaddish may refer back to the Amidah. Afterward, however, Kaddish has no basis. Recently, some have begun conducting the shortened repetition after the final Kaddish and then reciting Kaddish again. This is improper, for what purpose is served by saying two Kaddishes consecutively? At minimum, they should be taught to recite several verses between the two Kaddishes. Kaddish should be said only after aggadic teaching. When one studies Mishnayos or any subject of the Oral Torah, he should recite an aggadic passage afterward, such as 'Rabbi Elazar said in the name of Rabbi Chanina: Torah scholars increase peace,' so that Kaddish may be said upon it. Magen Avraham; see there. See also siman 55, seif katan 1, and what is written there.
Barechu. Once the chazzan has begun Kaddish, he may no longer interrupt to put on a tallis, even between sections. See siman 66, seif 2. This is what Rama means here by writing, 'But between Kaddish and Barechu,' and certainly he may not interrupt afterward. Magen Avraham.
