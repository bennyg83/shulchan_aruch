The one who speaks, etc. And in writings it is written to say on the ten days of repentance the psalm Mimma'amakim karatikha Hashem between Yishtabach and Yotzer—and it requires investigation:

Some verses. If he did not say verses he should not say Kaddish; he should only begin Barkhu without Kaddish. One who leads Ma'ariv and reads the Shema aloud should not say Kaddish unless he says verses—for one never finds Kaddish without verses before it, except after finishing the prayer (Darkhei Moshe, Kol Bo). It seems therefore they were accustomed to say the Shema spread after finishing the silent Shemoneh Esreh, so that Kaddish may be said on the Shemoneh Esreh; but afterward it is not appropriate to say Kaddish. Now newcomers have come who spread Shema after the final Kaddish and say Kaddish again—it is not proper; what reason is there to say two Kaddishim adjacent to one another? As is evident from the wording in siman 133, that he wrote Barkhu after the final Kaddish, implying they say only Barkhu without Kaddish; at minimum they should be taught to say some verses between the two Kaddishim. Beit Yosef wrote on page 65d regarding Kaddish derabbanan: Rambam at the end of Avot wrote one may not say it except on aggadah; therefore after the chapters they say "Rabbi Chanina said..." in order to say Kaddish on it. However in his Mishneh Torah he wrote to say it on all Oral Torah—end quote Beit Yosef. And this is Rambam, Laws of Prayer 1:10: All ten occupied in Oral Torah, even in midrash or aggadah, say Kaddish derabbanan. Tur siman 215 writes that schoolchildren, after learning, depart and say Kaddish. But in Lekuttei Pardes it is written: when the congregation says a verse or mishna there must be Kaddish; therefore they say Kaddish after pesukei dezimra and even after a mishna such as "In what do they light," and at their conclusion "Rabbi Elazar said, Rabbi Chanina said"—that is, like Torah reading, to sanctify, for one does not sanctify except at the end of a verse or exposition of a verse—end quote. If so, one may say that Rambam's intent in his Mishneh Torah is also to say aggadah after Oral Torah and then Kaddish, so his statements do not contradict one another; and thus is the custom of all Israel after chapters of Avot, "there is none like our God," and "In what do they light" the aggadah of Rabbi Elazar, etc. If so, when studying Mishnayot one may say aggadah after it in order to say Kaddish; likewise in Ma'aseh Tov siman 33, and Av Beit Din responsa siman 55.

He may put them on and bless. This is Darkhei Moshe citing Or Zarua, and in the responsa of the Geonim: "You asked regarding one to whom a tallit became available after he stood to pray Yotzer before he recited Shema—may he interrupt and bless? Thus we saw: he interrupts and blesses between Yishtabach and Kaddish, wraps himself, and says Kaddish; but if it became available to him after Kaddish began, he does not interrupt at all," etc. Therefore also by the books of Kabbalah one should not interrupt between Kaddish and Barkhu, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 53, seif 3, end quote Darkhei Moshe. It appears from this that he speaks of the prayer leader; nevertheless he is permitted to interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish (siman 53, seif 3). It also appears from the wording of Or Zarua that after Kaddish began he should not interrupt again for a tallit, even between the chapters (see siman 66, seif 2)—and this is what Rama wrote here: but between Kaddish, etc., and all the more so one should not interrupt, etc.
