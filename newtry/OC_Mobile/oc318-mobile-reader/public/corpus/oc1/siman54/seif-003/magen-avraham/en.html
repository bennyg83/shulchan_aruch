Several verses. If he did not recite verses, he should not say Kaddish, but should begin Barechu without Kaddish. One who conducts a shortened repetition of Krias Shema should not say Kaddish unless he first recites verses, for one never finds Kaddish without verses preceding it, except after completion of the prayer. Therefore, the custom developed to conduct the shortened repetition of Krias Shema after completion of the silent Amidah, so that Kaddish may refer back to the Amidah; afterward, however, Kaddish has no basis. Recently, some have begun conducting the shortened repetition after the final Kaddish and then reciting Kaddish again. This is improper, for what purpose is served by saying two Kaddishes consecutively? The wording in siman 133 likewise implies this, for it says 'Barechu after the final Kaddish,' indicating that only Barechu is said, without Kaddish. At minimum, they should be taught to recite several verses between the two Kaddishes. Beis Shmuel, page 65, column 4, writes regarding Kaddish D'Rabbanan that Rambam states at the end of Pirkei Avos that it may be said only upon aggadah, and therefore after the chapters one recites 'Rabbi Chananya, etc.' in order to say Kaddish upon it. In his code, however, Rambam writes that it may be said after any study of the Oral Torah. This is the wording of Rambam at the end of Sefer Ahavah: whenever ten people engage in study of the Oral Torah, even Midrash or aggadah, they recite Kaddish D'Rabbanan. Tur, siman 215, writes that when schoolchildren finish their study and depart, they recite Kaddish. Likkutei Pardes, however, states: when the congregation recites a verse or a Mishnah, Kaddish must be said. Therefore Kaddish is said after Pesukei DeZimrah, and also after a Mishnah such as Bameh Madlikin. At its conclusion they say 'Rabbi Elazar said in the name of Rabbi Chanina,' which is like a Scriptural exposition, in order to sanctify, for Kaddish is said only upon the conclusion of a verse or an exposition of a verse. If so, one may say that Rambam's intent in his code is likewise that an aggadic passage should be said after Oral Torah study, followed by Kaddish, so that his statements do not contradict one another. This is also the universal Jewish custom after Pirkei Avos, Ein K'Elokeinu, and Bameh Madlikin, to recite the aggadic teaching beginning 'Rabbi Elazar, etc.' Accordingly, when Mishnayos are studied, an aggadic passage should be recited afterward so that Kaddish may be said. This is also written in the work Maamar Mordechai, siman 33. See Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 55.
He may put them on and recite the blessing. Darchei Moshe writes: Or Zarua and a responsum of the Geonim state that they were asked about one to whom a tallis became available after he had begun Yotzer, before he recited Krias Shema, whether he may interrupt and recite the blessing. They answered that he interrupts and recites the blessing between Yishtabach and Kaddish, puts on the tallis, and then says Kaddish. But if it became available after he began Kaddish, he may not interrupt at all. Furthermore, according to the works of Kabbalah, one should not interrupt between Kaddish and Barechu, as stated at the end of Maareches Elokus. This is the wording of Darchei Moshe. It implies that the case concerns the chazzan, yet even he may interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish; see siman 53, seif 3. It also follows from Or Zarua's wording that once he has begun Kaddish he may no longer interrupt to put on a tallis, even between sections; see siman 66, end of seif 2. This is what Rama means here by writing, 'But between Kaddish, etc.,' and certainly he may not interrupt afterward. See citations 46, 50, 96, 100.
