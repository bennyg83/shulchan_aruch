Yishtabach does not open with Baruch, and one may not interrupt during Yishtabach—and it is a great danger (see Zohar, parashat Terumah). Yishtabach contains 52 words; and likewise 52 parshiyot in the Torah. Afterward they say half-Kaddish until yitbarakh—20 words corresponding to the first and last tablets; yitbarakh is 7 words corresponding to "its seven pillars are seven" (Prov. 9:1); and combined they are 26 words as the count of aleph-bet letters. Also in be'itbarakh are 22 words as the count of Torah letters excluding the final letters (mem, nun, tzadi, peh, khaf); and combined with everything it totals 49, as the count of faces by which Torah is expounded—and "His banner over me is love" (Song 2:4) equals the gematria of ahavah (love). The number 13 words—that is refined silver, smoothly hammered, purified sevenfold—7 times 7, which are 49 faces of Torah. "Yishtabach shimkha le'ad malkeinu" lacks a yud; also the acronym is Shlomo—therefore the custom is to stand when beginning Yishtabach. And they say Chai ha'olamim with tzere under the chet instead of patach, meaning that He is the Life of worlds, as R.
