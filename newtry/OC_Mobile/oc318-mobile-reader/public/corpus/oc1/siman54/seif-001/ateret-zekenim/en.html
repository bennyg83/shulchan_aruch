Yishtabach does not begin with 'Blessed,' and one must not interrupt during Yishtabach, for this entails great danger; see Zohar, Parashas Terumah. Yishtabach contains fifty-two words, corresponding to the fifty-two portions of the Torah. Thereafter, half Kaddish is recited. From the beginning of Kaddish through 'Yehei Shemei Rabbah' there are twenty words, corresponding to the first and last sets of the Ten Commandments. 'Yehei Shemei Rabbah' contains seven words, corresponding to 'She has hewn out her seven pillars.' Together they total twenty-six words, corresponding to the number of letters in the alphabet. Likewise, from 'Yisbarach' there are twenty-two words, corresponding to the letters of the Torah apart from the final letters. Together the total is forty-nine, corresponding to the forty-nine modes through which the Torah is interpreted, alluded to by 'and His banner over me was love,' since 'love' has the numerical value of thirteen words, and it is silver refined in a crucible, purified sevenfold, namely seven times seven, the forty-nine modes of Torah. In 'Yishtabach Shimcha la'ad Malkeinu,' the word 'Malkeinu' is written without a yod, and the initial letters spell Shlomo. Therefore, the custom is to stand when beginning Yishtabach. One says 'chei ha'olamim' with a tzerei beneath the ches, meaning that He is the life of the worlds. Rabbi Shabsai wrote this in his siddur. This is not the view of Maharal of Prague, who says it with a patach.
