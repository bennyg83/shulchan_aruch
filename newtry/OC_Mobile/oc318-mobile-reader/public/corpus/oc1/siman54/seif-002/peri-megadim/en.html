In siman 215 it is explained that our custom is not to answer amen after one's own blessing of Yishtabach, but only after Boneh Yerushalayim, because the latter follows two blessings and marks a division between Torah-level and Rabbinic obligations. Here, by contrast, the blessings of Krias Shema are also Rabbinic, even though Krias Shema itself is Torah-level. See Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav, citing Kol Bo. Orchos Chaim, however, writes that Krias Shema is Torah-level, while Kol Bo writes in the name of Rabbi Shemaya and Rabbi Yosef ibn Plat that the blessings of Krias Shema are Torah-level. I do not understand this, for we rule that blessings are Rabbinic. Likewise, even after Ga'al Yisrael, according to the view that one answers amen after the chazzan and after one's own blessing, this comparison does not follow. Although some hold that Emes V'Yatziv is Torah-level while prayer is Rabbinic, which would make it comparable to Boneh Yerushalayim, one may answer that there are not two blessings here as there are there, where all are Torah-level. Furthermore, once one has recited the passage of tzitzis, Emes V'Yatziv is Rabbinic. See Ateres Zekeinim, siman 67, letter 1. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further in siman 215.
In the text. 'Chei ha'olamim,' as in Tosafos Yom Tov at the end of Tamid and Eliyah Rabbah here. In the siddur of the great grammarian Rabbi Zalman Henna, it is written with a tzerei; see there. Some versions have it with a patach. In any event, one should say 'ha'olamim' with the definite article, even if 'chai' is pronounced with a patach, for its meaning is that He lives in both worlds and rules in this world and the World to Come. I have not presently found a clear resolution of this matter. With Hashem's help, it will be explained further.
