After (Turei Zahav)—according to our wording, not like the author's wording, and likewise Magen Avraham letter 1. In Tur it is explained that our custom is that one does not answer amen after Yishtabach after one's own blessing, only after Boneh Yerushalayim, which are two blessings; and the interruption between Biblical and rabbinic applies differently here, for the blessing of Shema is also rabbinic, even though Shema itself is Biblical—and Av Beit Yosef in the name of Mahariya in the name of Kol Bo. However in Avodat Hakodesh he wrote Shema is Biblical, and in Kol Bo he wrote in the name of Rabbi Shemaiah and Rabbi Yosef ibn Paltiel that the blessing of Shema is Biblical; and if we hold blessings are rabbinic, also after Go'el Yisrael for one who answers after the prayer leader after his own blessing—no, for although "true and established" is Biblical and prayer is rabbinic, if so it is like Boneh Yerushalayim; one could say there are not two blessings as there, where all are Biblical. Moreover, when he said the section of tzitzit, "true and established" is rabbinic (Turei Zahav siman 67 letter 1); and if so, in Tur it will be explained further on this.

In the wording "who lives forever"—Tosafot end of Tamid, and Rema here, and in the siddur of the great grammarian Rabbi Zalman Henna he wrote with tzere, see there, and letter 13 with patach; nevertheless one should say "the worlds" with a heh, even though "lives" is with patach, for its meaning is He lives in both worlds and rules in this world and the World to Come. I did not find anything clear on this now; and if so, it will be explained further.
