(s.k. 1) Because of the miracle — that each one took a lamb and tied it at the legs of their beds; and the Egyptians asked why, and they answered to slaughter by Heaven's command; and the Egyptians' teeth were blunt — for a detestable thing of Egypt was slaughtered before their eyes, and they were not permitted to speak a word; and even though perforce the miracle was all the days from the tenth of the month until erev Pesach when they slaughtered it — Rabbeinu Yosef wrote the essence of the miracle was at its beginning on the first day, and afterward since they were already accustomed. And Tosafot: the miracle in another matter — when Israel took on the tenth of the month a lamb for Pesach, the firstborn of the nations of the world gathered and asked them why; and Israel answered: it is a Pesach sacrifice to Heaven who will kill the firstborn of Egypt and our firstborn He will save; and they went to their fathers and to Pharaoh to request that Israel be sent to freedom; and they did not want, and the firstborn made war and killed many of them — this is what is written "who struck Egypt through their firstborn."

And in my humble opinion what was eaten in sixty — Kaf HaChayim has the reading "and in sixty" — one may say flour in flour is called liquid in liquid; nevertheless some say it is called dry in dry as Shach wrote; and in dry in dry there is lightness in siman 447; even though it was nullified before Pesach it returns and is revived on Pesach and forbids in any amount; and if so, if chametz was mixed in flour, one may say it returns and is revived on Pesach; and in order to satisfy this view he commanded to eat a little from it before Pesach, for one may rely that the prohibition was already eaten; even though if it were certainly a mixture, in my humble opinion what was eaten one before Pesach and returns and is revived on Pesach — nevertheless, since there is no presumption, fear lest it was mixed; and also since we hold the essence that flour in flour is liquid in liquid, they rely on what was eaten a little before Pesach.
