1) [Se'if 1] The Shabbos preceding Pesach is called Shabbos HaGadol, etc. The reason is that a great miracle occurred on that day. The offering in Egypt was selected on the tenth, as it is written, 'On the tenth of this month, they shall each take a lamb,' etc. The Pesach on which the Jewish people left Egypt occurred on Thursday, as stated in Seder Olam. This is a scribal error, for Seder Olam states that they left on Friday; the citation should instead be to Shabbos 87b. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3. It follows that the tenth of the month was Shabbos. Each person took a lamb for the Pesach offering and tied it to the legs of the bed. The Egyptians asked, 'Why do you need this?' They answered that they would slaughter it as a Pesach offering in fulfillment of Havayah's command. The Egyptians' teeth were set on edge because the Jewish people would slaughter their deity, but they were unable to say anything. Although the Jewish people slaughtered and ate rams and sheep every day, here they informed the Egyptians that through this act their celestial deity would also be slaughtered above. Bach. Because of that miracle, the day is called Shabbos HaGadol. Tur. According to this reason, all the days from the tenth of the month until the eve of Pesach seemingly should have been called great days. It may be answered that the primary miracle occurred at the beginning. Once the first day had passed and the Egyptians had become accustomed to it, the novelty was gone. Beis Yosef. Rabbi Moshe Alyash writes that the miracle consisted of their declaration that they intended to slaughter the Egyptians' deity. On Shabbos the Egyptian believed this declaration and assumed that they had not yet slaughtered only because it was Shabbos, unlike on the other days. Ba'er Heitev, subsection 1, cites his words. The miracle is attributed to Shabbos rather than to the tenth of the month because the Jewish people crossed up from the Yarden on the tenth of the month. Had the miracle been attributed to the tenth and that date been called a great day, people might mistakenly think it was called great because of the miracle of crossing the Yarden. It was therefore attributed to Shabbos, since it was known that the crossing of the Yarden did not occur on Shabbos. Bach; Taz, subsection 1.
1) Another reason it is called Shabbos HaGadol is that a great miracle occurred, as stated in the Midrash Rabbah on parashas Bo. When the Jewish people took their Pesach offerings on that Shabbos, the Egyptian firstborn gathered around them and asked why they were doing so. They answered, 'It is a Pesach offering to Havayah, Who will kill the Egyptian firstborn.' The firstborn went to their fathers and to Pharaoh and asked him to release the Jewish people, but they refused. The firstborn then waged war and killed many of them. This is the meaning of the verse, 'Who struck Egypt through its firstborn.' Tosafos on Shabbos 87b. Beis Yosef and Levush cite this. Kol Bo, siman 47, writes that when the Egyptians saw this, each girded his sword upon his thigh and they sought to destroy, kill, and annihilate all the Jewish people. Havayah, in His mercy, protected them, afflicted the Egyptians with unusual illnesses, and subjected them to severe suffering, so that they could not harm the Jewish people. Because great miracles occurred for the Jewish people on that Shabbos preceding Pesach, every Shabbos preceding Pesach is called Shabbos HaGadol. See above, siman 429, subsection 3, and the later authorities for additional reasons.
