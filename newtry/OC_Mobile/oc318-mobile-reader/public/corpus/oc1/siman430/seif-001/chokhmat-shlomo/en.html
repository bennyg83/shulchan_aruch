Seif 1 — the Shabbat before Pesach they call Shabbat HaGadol, etc. NB: Behold in the reason that one does not read haftarah Ve'aravah except when it falls on erev Pesach on Shabbat it appears thus: because in the first year the Mishkan was established, it was erev Pesach that fell on Shabbat, for then it was Rosh Chodesh Nisan on day one, for so we say that on that day he took ten crowns; and also the tribe of Ephraim offered on Shabbat as is written in the Midrash; and if so, since the first Pesach when the Mishkan was established fell on erev Pesach on Shabbat, therefore when it now falls on erev Pesach on Shabbat we request that there be an "aravah" before God — our Pesach like the days of old and former years, like the first Pesach that was in the Mishkan — and it comes out very well. But a new difficulty arose for me: why does the gemara not prove in the first chapter of Elu Devarim that Pesach overrides Shabbat from an explicit verse "and the children of Israel made the Pesach," and there it was Shabbat — and there is difficulty in this; again I saw a new light on Pesachim that challenged this difficulty, and it is possible to settle thus the gemara's language in chapter 6 of Pesachim "they forgot and did not know" — meaning they forgot what initially Pesach was on erev Shabbat, and also they did not know the law — examine. And behold what they call Shabbat HaGadol — one may give a new reason in my humble opinion: behold we hold Yom Tov and Shabbat are two sanctities, and the sanctity of Shabbat is more severe than the sanctity of Yom Tov; and since Nisan is Rosh Hashanah for festivals, and the Sages said in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah: a festival within it is Rosh Hashanah for festivals; therefore before the first festival began they called before it Shabbat HaGadol as a hint that it is greater than Yom Tov; and in particular since the Sages said in the chapter Kol Kelal gadol mikol kelal there is a small one, and Yom Tov is also called Shabbat; therefore they called the Shabbat which is Shabbat HaGadol, but Yom Tov is a small Shabbat. And behold with this is settled what Tur wrote another reason and not this reason: for one may say why they called it Shabbat HaGadol per my reason — there is a practical difference in law: behold there is a dispute whether needs of Shabbat may be done on Yom Tov or not; and see in the sugya "since this depends" whether it is one sanctity or not, for there is dispute of R. Eliezer and the rabbis in chapter 3 of Eruvin. And behold if we say it is one sanctity, then needs of Shabbat are done on Yom Tov; and if they are two sanctities, needs of Shabbat are not done on Yom Tov; and behold there is a practical difference in law: for per the view of the one who says needs of Shabbat are not done on Yom Tov, what one cooks from Yom Tov for Shabbat is only through "since" close to dark, for "since" does not apply — forbidden to cook, as Tosafot and Magen Avraham wrote siman 527; but per the view of the one who says needs of Shabbat are done on Yom Tov, it is permitted even close to dark. Therefore it teaches us to call Shabbat HaGadol as a hint: from the general rule that there is a small one it is proven they are two sanctities and needs of Shabbat are not done on Yom Tov except through "since," and forbidden close to dark. Therefore Tur and other poskim who wrote the reason from the miracle done on it — he holds the halachah is like the one who says needs of Shabbat are done on Yom Tov, and automatically there is no practical difference for us in law; therefore he was forced to give another reason from the miracle done on it. And see Magen Avraham siman 527 that there is dispute whom we follow; but for those who hold like the one who says needs of Shabbat are not done on Yom Tov, one may say the reason as above, and the matter will be understood more: for the matter is decided that this depends on this — behold the later authorities already wrote that Tur's reason depends on the one who holds on the fifth day Israel left Egypt and the tenth of Nisan was on Shabbat; and therefore there is in Yerushalmi chapter 8 of Pesachim [halachah 3], and these are its words: they disagreed — R. Yosi and R. Elazar: one says they consecrate and one says they do not consecrate, etc.; one tanna taught: he goes to sellers of lambs; one tanna taught: he goes to sellers of Pesachim; the one who says he goes to sellers of lambs — like the one who says they consecrate; the one who says he goes to sellers of Pesachim — like the one who says they do not consecrate; and see Ketzot HaChoshen who explained: per the view of the one who says sellers of Pesachim — meaning they are consecrated from erev [Pesach], therefore it is permitted to take Pesachim from them when it falls on erev Pesach on Shabbat; and the one who says sellers of lambs — that they are not yet consecrated, holds like the one who says they consecrate on Shabbat — see there. And at first glance it is difficult how one opinion holds they consecrate on Shabbat — behold an explicit mishnah in chapter 5 of Beitzah that one does not consecrate, etc.; and one must say the mishnah speaks for weekday need, but Yerushalmi speaks for the need of that day, for behold it falls on erev Pesach on Shabbat and it is the need of that day; therefore he holds they consecrate on Shabbat; and similarly Magen Avraham [siman 306] in the name of Yalkut that Pesach overrides Shabbat thus — see there. And at first glance it is difficult even for the one who says they do not consecrate — why does he not go to sellers of lambs and take and not consecrate them; but perforce it is proven that since there is a clear presumption that he buys it for Pesach and such is his intention, therefore since where there is clear presumption it is matters of the heart as matters, it is as if he consecrated with a full mouth; and therefore what the Holy One blessed be He said "take a lamb on the tenth of the month" when it was Shabbat — behold one does not consecrate on Shabbat; and behold Shabbat was also established for them by Moshe Rabbeinu while they were in Egypt, and from presumption he observed even rabbinic prohibitions as Abraham our father observed even the Torah; and also the Holy One blessed be He who knows all — how did He command them to take on the tenth of the month, and even if they do not consecrate with the mouth, behold there is clear presumption as is proven from the aforementioned Yerushalmi; and there it was not a need of Shabbat since its time is not that day; but perforce since it was a need of Yom Tov, since the Pesach is eaten on Yom Tov, it is as if it were a need of Shabbat; and as needs of Shabbat are done on Yom Tov — what is permitted on Yom Tov is permitted also for Shabbat — so too conversely: what is permitted on Shabbat for itself is permitted also for Yom Tov; and needs of Yom Tov are done on Shabbat — what is permitted for Shabbat itself; and since for a need of Shabbat they consecrate, so too for a need of Yom Tov; and therefore per the view of the one who holds on the fifth day they left and the tenth of the month was on Shabbat — it is proven that needs of Yom Tov are done on Shabbat and like the one who says they consecrate; and therefore since here "since" does not apply, because they are one sanctity — if so all the more needs of Shabbat are done like Yom Tov and "since" is not needed; therefore for all opinions one must give the reason to call it Shabbat HaGadol; also behold it is one sanctity; therefore he explains the reason from the miracle done on it — for all. But per the view of the one who holds on the sixth day they left Egypt — if so the tenth of the month was on a weekday and Tur's reason does not apply to Shabbat HaGadol; but if so there is no proof that needs of Shabbat are done on Yom Tov, and he holds they are two sanctities; and automatically it is called Shabbat HaGadol as a hint that it is greater than Yom Tov and they are two sanctities and needs of Shabbat are not done like Yom Tov except through "since," and close to dark where "since" does not apply it is forbidden; and there is practical difference in law — so it appears in my humble opinion by way of brief pilpul — examine well.
