The miracle. Each person took a lamb on the tenth of the month, and that year the tenth fell on Shabbos. The later authorities and Chok Yaakov ask why the commemoration was not fixed on the tenth of the month even when it falls on a weekday. It may be answered that the miracle was that the Israelites told the Egyptians they intended to slaughter the Egyptians’ deity. On Shabbos the Egyptian said that this was true, and that the only reason they had not yet slaughtered it was because it was Shabbos. This explanation does not apply to the other days. This is from Rabbi Moshe Rabbi Eliash. The haftarah of VeArvah is recited only when Shabbos HaGadol falls on Erev Pesach. This appears correct. Maharshal would teach that every person should eat a small amount before Pesach from the flour prepared for Pesach. Perhaps there was some concern of fermentation, and it could then be attributed to what he had eaten. Magen Avraham questions this, because that principle applies only to dry mixtures. See Yoreh Deah, siman 110. Flour mixed with flour is treated as a liquid mixture. Chok Yaakov writes that Maharshal stated the law in principle, although it is effective only where one of the items fell in inadvertently. See there. It is proper to give some of this flour to the poor. This is from Mateh Moshe and Shelah. The Haggadah. This applies even when Shabbos HaGadol falls on Erev Pesach.
