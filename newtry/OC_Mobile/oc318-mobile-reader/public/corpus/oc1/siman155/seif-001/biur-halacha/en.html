And he should establish, etc. Although a person who studies alone also fulfills the positive mitzvah of Torah study, ideally he should make every effort to study with a group, for this brings greater honor to Heaven. Chazal likewise said in many places, “In a multitude of people is the glory of the King.” Chazal further taught in Berachos 6a that there is a distinction between one who studies alone and one who does not study alone. Although the Holy One, blessed is He, assigns reward even to one who studies alone, his words are not recorded in the book of remembrance. By contrast, of those who study together the verse states, “Then those who fear Hashem spoke with one another, and Hashem listened and heard, and a book of remembrance was written before Him for those who fear Hashem and contemplate His name.” All the more so when ten study together, for then the Shechinah arrives first, as it is written, “God stands in the congregation of God.” Chazal also said that Torah is acquired only through fellowship. They further expounded in Berachos 63a on the verse, “Pay attention and hear, Israel,” that people should form groups and engage in Torah study. Through this, the Holy One, blessed is He, also shows favor to Israel, as stated in Tanna Devei Eliyahu, chapter 23. The Attribute of Judgment asked Him why He shows favor to Israel when it is written that “the great, mighty, and awesome God does not show favor.” The Holy One, blessed is He, replied: How could I not show them favor, and not only that, but they teach Torah, sit in groups, and engage in Torah study. See there. This is also the current custom in all Jewish communities, to establish communal study in the synagogue after prayer and then recite Kaddish D'Rabbanan. Kaddish D'Rabbanan is recited only after Oral Torah and not after verses. It is also recited after aggadic teachings. The later authorities write that for purposes of reciting Kaddish it is not necessary that ten people actually study. Even if one or two studied, Kaddish may be recited provided ten are present in the synagogue. Even another person who did not study may recite the Kaddish.
A time to study. Yoreh Deah 246:1 explains that a person must establish a fixed time for Torah study by day and by night. It is therefore proper that, in addition to the fixed study after Shacharis, he also establish a period between Minchah and Maariv, thereby fulfilling the obligation of Torah study at night as well. The concept of studying between Minchah and Maariv is also implied by Berachos 4b: A person comes from the field in the evening, goes to the synagogue, and if he knows Scripture he reads; if he knows Mishnah he studies; then he recites Krias Shema and prays, etc. See there, and see siman 1, where I cited the Shelah. Due to our many sins, many people entirely neglect Torah and do not even concern themselves with fixing a short daily period for study. The reason is that they do not recognize the magnitude of this obligation. Chazal already said that the Holy One, blessed is He, was willing to overlook the sins of idolatry, forbidden relations, and bloodshed, but did not overlook the sin of neglecting Torah study. Midrash Mishlei, chapter 10, states: Rabbi Yishmael said, come and see how severe the Day of Judgment will be when the Holy One, blessed is He, judges the entire world. One who knows Scripture but not Mishnah comes, and the Holy One, blessed is He, turns His face from him, while the torments of Gehinnom intensify against him. They seize him and cast him into Gehinnom. One who knows two or three orders of Mishnah comes, and the Holy One, blessed is He, says to him: My son, why did you not study all the halachos? One who knows halachos comes, and He says: My son, why did you not study Toras Kohanim, which contains, etc.? One who knows Toras Kohanim comes, and the Holy One, blessed is He, says: My son, why did you not study the Five Books of the Torah, which contain Krias Shema, tefillin, and mezuzah? One who knows the Five Books comes, and the Holy One, blessed is He, says: Why did you not study aggadah? One who knows aggadah comes, and the Holy One, blessed is He, says: My son, why did you not study Talmud? See there. A person who does not rouse himself to establish daily times for Torah will certainly, Heaven forbid, remain empty of all this. What will he answer on the Day of Judgment? The wise man has his eyes in his head.
