[1] He should go to the beis midrash, etc. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote as follows: The custom is to study in the synagogue after prayer and recite Kaddish D'Rabbanan. Even if only two study, they recite Kaddish when ten are present in the synagogue, just as with Poreis Al Shema, and even when children are the ones studying. Lechem Chamudos, folio 62. His words imply that even one who did not hear the study may recite Kaddish, similar to Poreis Al Shema, and apparently even one individual. Knesses HaGedolah writes that Kaddish D'Rabbanan is not recited after a verse, but only after Oral Torah. It appears to me that if Midrash or a passage of Gemara expounding that verse is recited, Kaddish D'Rabbanan is said. Thus far are my words in Eliyahu Zuta. It is called Kaddish D'Rabbanan because it is a supplication and request on behalf of the Rabbis, or because it was instituted only after Oral Torah, which is the study of the Rabbis. It appears to me that this fixed lesson should be studied in the synagogue while standing. Proof may be brought from the end of Sotah: When Rabban Gamliel died, the honor of Torah ceased. Rashi explains that weakness descended upon the world and people could no longer study while standing. Accordingly, for such a short period and in the synagogue it is proper to be careful. I further wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that one must be careful, since they begin Yisgadal, to clear his mind and not continue correcting or instructing others at that time, for the time for Torah and the time for Kaddish are separate. He commits a transgression if he nullifies the sanctification of Hashem because of his study. See above, siman 55, seif katan 3.
