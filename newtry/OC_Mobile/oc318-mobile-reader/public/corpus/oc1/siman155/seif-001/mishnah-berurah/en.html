(1) He should go to the beis midrash. The Gemara states that one who leaves the synagogue, enters the beis midrash, and engages in Torah merits to receive the Shechinah, as it is written, “They go from strength to strength; each appears before God in Zion.” In their times the synagogue was designated specifically for prayer and the beis midrash specifically for Torah, and their practice was to pray in the synagogue. Even in our times, when people pray in batei midrash, this teaching still applies: after prayer one should join those engaged in Torah, Mishnah, or the like, and the verse “They go from strength to strength,” etc., applies to him.
(2) And he should establish. The Gemara states that when a person is brought to judgment he is asked, “Did you establish fixed times for Torah?” It is proper to fix the time immediately after prayer, because of “They go from strength to strength,” as explained above, and also because if he first goes to his business affairs there is concern that he will be drawn after them and his fixed study will be canceled. Peri Megadim.
(3) To study. A person is obligated each day to study Written Torah, meaning Tanach, as well as Mishnah, Gemara, and the poskim. Working people who study only three or four hours a day should not study Gemara alone, for by this they do not fulfill their obligation. They must also study works of halachic rulings, each according to his level of understanding. Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, and Shach there.
(4) That he not let it pass. The primary mitzvah of Torah study has no fixed measure, and its obligation extends throughout the day whenever one has free time, as it is written, “This book of the Torah shall not depart from your mouth,” etc. When one has free time and voluntarily neglects Torah study, he approaches the category described by Chazal in Sanhedrin 99 on the verse, “Because he has despised the word of Hashem,” namely, one who is able to engage in Torah and does not do so. Chazal also taught in the Yerushalmi, at the end of chapter 9 of Berachos, in the name of Rabbi Chelkiyah quoting Rabbi Simon: One who makes his Torah only occasional, meaning that he studies only at special times even though he has leisure to study more, violates the covenant. They derive this from the verse, “It is time to act for Hashem; they have nullified Your Torah.” See the commentary of Pnei Moshe there. The meaning of establishing fixed times for Torah is therefore that a person must designate a fixed daily time that he never allows to pass. If unavoidable circumstances prevented him from completing his fixed study during the day, it becomes like a debt and he must make it up at night, as Eruvin 65 states concerning Rav Acha bar Yaakov, who borrowed by day and repaid at night. The later authorities write that before leaving the beis midrash after Shacharis, even if unavoidable circumstances will prevent him from carrying out his regular fixed study, he should at least study one verse or one halachah.
(5) To earn much. Such a person is among the people of faith, for he believes and trusts in Hashem that his sustenance will not be diminished because of this. The Yerushalmi in Sotah, chapter 9, asks: Who are “people of faith”? It gives the example of one whom customers called to come with his merchandise to sell, and he said, “I will not cancel my fixed time. Whatever is meant to come to me will come.” Korban HaEdah explains: He would say, I will not cancel the hour I fixed for Torah study in order to earn money. If profit is fitting for me, it will come from the Holy One, blessed is He, even after I finish my fixed study.
(6) He should go to the beis midrash. Meaning, to the place where people study. Even if he does not understand what they are learning, remaining in the beis midrash is itself a mitzvah. All the more so, if he can understand what they are studying in halachah or aggadah, he should establish himself there to listen, and it will count for him as study. Chazal said in Devarim Rabbah, Parashas Ki Savo: Rabbi Yehoshua said in the name of Rav Nachman that anyone who enters the synagogue and hears words of Torah merits to sit among the sages in the future, as it is written, “An ear that hears the rebuke of life will lodge among the wise.”
(7) Or he should establish a place for himself. This is a separate matter and refers back to the beginning of the seif. A person should establish his Torah study in the beis midrash and have a fixed place there for Torah. The correct reading is, “And he should establish a place for himself.”
(8) And he should study. According to the plain flow of the Rama’s wording, this is difficult, for at first he discusses a person who does not know how to study at all and receives only the reward for going. The truth is as we wrote initially, that the entire passage refers back to the beginning of the seif. It teaches that even one who knows how to study only a little should nevertheless establish a place for himself in the beis midrash and study what he knows. This is explicit in Rabbeinu Yonah, the source of this gloss, who writes: Even one who knows how to study only a little should establish himself in that place to study what he knows. If he does not know how to study at all, he should go to the batei midrash where people study, and the reward for going is his.
(9) A little of what he knows. When one studies only a little, it is proper that his primary study be halachos, so that he will know how to conduct himself in practice, as explained above. Chazal said on the verse, “Hashem loves the gates of Zion more than all the dwellings of Jacob,” that Hashem loves gates distinguished by halachah more than all synagogues, etc. They also said that whoever studies halachos every day is assured that he is destined for the World to Come. If he cannot understand halachos, he should study whatever he knows. In our times there are many books of teachings of Chazal translated into the vernacular, and every person can read and study them.
(10) And he should consider his affairs. Meaning, while he has some free time sitting in the beis midrash, he should examine whether his business dealings involve any aspect of transgression, such as theft, overcharging, interest, or the like, and separate himself from it.
