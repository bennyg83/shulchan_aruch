And after the reader finishes, etc. See Beit Yosef, who writes that even if the prompter says it aloud, this is permitted. At first glance, this refers to a case where the prompter also reads from the writing in the Sefer Torah. If he prompts from a Chumash, there is room for concern, based on what Beit Yosef cites below in siman 690 in the name of Orchos Chayim, that the custom is to rebuke one who assists the reader by heart, lest a listener direct his attention to the one reading by heart rather than to the chazzan’s reading. The same concern might apply here. This can be rejected, however, because since they do not read simultaneously there is no reason for concern. I later found that the later authorities discuss this matter.
