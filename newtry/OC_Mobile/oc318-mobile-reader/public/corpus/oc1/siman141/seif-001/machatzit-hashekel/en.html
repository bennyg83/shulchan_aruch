(s.k. 1) Standing. As Bach siman 26 and so Magen Avraham on Tur's words here — and these are his words: one must read standing, for we learned: one who reads the Megillah sitting has fulfilled; not so regarding Torah — end of his words. And from what is written in Megillah "has fulfilled" it implies specifically b'dieved; and so Tur explicitly in siman 690 that l'chatchila one does not read Megillah sitting either. And on this Tur wrote: not so regarding Torah — it implies that regarding Torah even b'dieved one has not fulfilled. And Magen Avraham holds that this is because he does not hold in Megillah like Tur, but rather that even l'chatchila one reads (individually) sitting, as written below siman 690. And therefore for us one may say what the Gemara said there in tractate Megillah — not so regarding Torah — that it is forbidden l'chatchila sitting but b'dieved one has fulfilled; nevertheless, since for us too there is no necessity that regarding Torah even b'dieved one has not fulfilled sitting, nevertheless one may say so from reasoning: for us in Megillah one reads sitting even l'chatchila, and regarding Torah even b'dieved one has not fulfilled sitting — in order not to increase dispute between us and Tur's view; and see Avraham Zuckerman. But from what Rabbeinu Bechaye wrote in the name of Rashi it appears — Rashi explained in the mishnah: one who reads the Megillah — if he wishes standing, if he wishes sitting — end of his words; behold Rashi permits sitting in Megillah even l'chatchila. And on what the Gemara taught "not so regarding Torah" — Rashi wrote: that one does not read Torah in congregation sitting — end of his words. The word "in congregation" that Rashi wrote seems superfluous, for where do we find Torah reading individually? It is obvious it refers to congregation; rather Rashi comes to hint the reason why they were strict in Torah — since it is in congregation, therefore for honor of the congregation one reads standing. And for this reason Rambam holds that also in Megillah when reading in congregation one must read standing, as written siman 690. And one must say perforce that which the baraita mentions Torah — even though also in Megillah in congregation one must stand — is that it mentions a matter that is definitive: in Torah you do not find sitting, for one does not read Torah except in congregation; not so Megillah — you find sitting when reading individually. And since for Rashi the reason is honor of the congregation, it is reasonable that one fulfills b'dieved sitting; and even though the Gemara derives from a verse in Torah that one must be standing — R' Abahu from "and you, stand here with me" — were it not so, etc., even the Holy One Blessed Be He would be standing — for Rashi the verse is an asmachta; also there honor of the congregation was not applicable.

And so from end of Perek 7 of Sotah daf 41, where they said the king reads, etc. — meaning the portion haMelech in Mishneh Torah: every outgoing of the first day of Yom Tov of Sukkot in the first year of the shemittah cycle, and he would read while seated in the Women's Courtyard on account of royal honor.

Regarding testimony, etc. — for the poskim inferred that although we hold witnesses must testify standing, as it is written "and two men shall stand," nevertheless b'dieved if they testified while seated their testimony is testimony; for they permitted a Torah scholar to testify l'chatchila while seated for the honor of his Torah; and it is impossible that even b'dieved testimony while seated would not be testimony — if so they would not have permitted for a Torah scholar.

And one should distinguish: the honor of a king is stringent, for he cannot waive his honor; and therefore although reading while seated for other people they would not fulfill even b'dieved, nevertheless it was permitted for the king even l'chatchila; unlike the honor of a Torah scholar, which is not so stringent, for he can waive his honor, for the Torah is his; and therefore if not that for other people their testimony while seated is testimony b'dieved, they would not have permitted l'chatchila for a Torah scholar; and see Shevuot daf 34b in Tosafot s.v. asah, etc., which implies according to one answer the opposite.
