[1] He must read, etc., while standing. Bach, siman 690, writes that even after the fact he has not fulfilled his obligation and must read again, and this is likewise implied by the Tur. Rashi, however, implies that after the fact he has fulfilled his obligation. Magen Avraham. In my humble opinion, even Bach wrote this only according to the opinion that one may not initially read the Megillah while seated. According to the opinion that even initially one may read the Megillah while seated, as Bach himself rules there, the Gemara means that Torah reading is different in that it may not initially be done while seated, but after the fact one has fulfilled his obligation, since the verse “Stand with Me” is merely an asmachta. Rashi’s commentary there makes clear that all this applies specifically to a public reading, and Rabbeinu Yerucham writes likewise. See the Yerushalmi. This requires study.
[2] And even to lean. Standing by means of leaning is not considered standing, as stated in chapter 2 of Zevachim. So writes the Tur. The passage there on 19a refers to one who would fall if the support were removed, as Rashi explains there. Accordingly, Levush’s conclusion, “Nevertheless, if he is corpulent,” etc., implies that such a person is permitted even in this case, unlike Magen Avraham. Nevertheless, initially, when one is not corpulent, even slight leaning is forbidden, so that he not treat the matter lightly, as Mateh Moshe, siman 236, writes. This resolves the question of all the later authorities from Choshen Mishpat, siman 17, where leaning is considered standing. Here they were stringent because of reverence and awe for the Torah.
