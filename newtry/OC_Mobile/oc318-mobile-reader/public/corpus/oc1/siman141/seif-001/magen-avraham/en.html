Standing. As Bach siman 429 — even b'dieved one did not fulfill and must return and read again; and so is the language of the Tur and Hagahot Maimoniyot; but from what Rav Yaakov Beirav wrote in the name of Rashi it implies that specifically l'chatchila we are strict but b'dieved one fulfilled; see there; and so from end of Perek 7 of Sotah, where they said the king reads while seated; and if you say that even b'dieved one did not fulfill — on account of the king's honor it is permitted l'chatchila; and similarly we infer in Choshen Mishpat siman 28 regarding testimony, and one should distinguish.

To lean — for one must stand in awe (Beit Yosef); and if so, even to lean slightly is forbidden; and see siman 585 that if he leaned so much that if that thing were removed he would fall, it is not standing; and if so, even a baal basar is forbidden; and all the more for those who say that even b'dieved one did not fulfill, as above — it is permitted to a baal basar only slight leaning, as written siman 63 regarding leaning while lying down; and it is difficult — in Choshen Mishpat siman 17 s.s. 1 Rama wrote that Rivash considers leaning standing, but here the Tur and Tosafot wrote that it is not considered standing — requires study; and it is possible that regarding testimony they were lenient, but it is not necessary in Rivash, for also regarding reading b'dieved one fulfills, and even so l'chatchila leaning is forbidden; and it appears Rama looked in Beit Yosef siman 28, who wrote in the name of Rivash that leaning is not like sitting, therefore Rama wrote that it is valid regarding testimony; but it appears to me it is a scribal error, and one must say that leaning is like sitting, and so is Shulchan Aruch there — see there; and in Tosafot Sotah daf 40 and Zevachim daf 19b Tosafot wrote: wherever requires sitting, leaning is not included in sitting — meaning specifically in the Courtyard, where sitting is forbidden, nevertheless it is permitted to lean, since standing is not written regarding them; if so, leaning is neither standing nor sitting; and see Shevuot daf 30b: sitting as one who removes his shoe — for a Torah scholar ordinary standing suffices.
