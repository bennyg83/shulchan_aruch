(1) One must read standing — the reading that is read in the congregation; and we derive this from what is written "and you, stand here," which implies that even the Holy One blessed be He, who read to Moses, was standing, as it were; and if so, all the more so that all Israel were standing before the awe of the Holy One blessed be He who gives it. And we also say: just as it was given in awe, so we must conduct ourselves with it in awe. Nevertheless, b'dieved if one read while seated one has fulfilled and need not return and read again.
(2) And even to lean oneself — meaning even slight leaning such that if that thing were removed he would not fall is also forbidden, for one must stand in awe.
(3) Or to stand — this is the reading table, and the like.
(4) Unless he is a baal basar — and the same applies to a sick person or a very old person for whom standing without support is difficult; but he should beware to lean slightly and not lean so much that if that thing were removed he would fall; and if he cannot lean except in this matter and he needs support, it is also permitted in this; but he should not lean on the cloth on the table, for it is a holy vessel, but on the table itself.
(5) And so the chazzan who calls up — and the same applies to the segan who stands at the Torah scroll [Shaarei Ephraim]. He also wrote there: sometimes the Torah scroll is long and large, and the reader must read from the script, and likewise the one called up must see in the Torah scroll what is read before him and read after him in a whisper, and they cannot see the top of the page unless they bow themselves near the Torah scroll, and thereby come to lean on the table — there is room to permit, for it is apparent to all that this leaning is not through pride, for that is when standing upright and leaning; but one who stands bent and bowed is standing in awe, and he may stand near [and likewise the segan, for most of his work is holding the silver pointer to indicate before the reader which line and word he is at, and sometimes he cannot except by bowing and leaning on the table — permitted]; but he should beware that after he reads the upper lines from the top of the page and reaches the middle of the page or near it, where he can see well without leaning, he should straighten from his bowing so he need not lean at all — until here is his opinion.
