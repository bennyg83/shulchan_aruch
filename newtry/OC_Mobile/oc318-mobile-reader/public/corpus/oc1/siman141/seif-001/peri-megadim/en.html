And even — Taz: see Choshen Mishpat in Semag 17 (1) and siman 28 at its end — there it is an asmachta and therefore they are lenient regarding leaning; and here too it is rabbinic and they are strict regarding leaning — one may say at the time of the Torah; but in Levush it appears the reason here is that leaning is not like standing — it is hard on him relative to himself in Choshen Mishpat there — and one may say. And see Rambam chapter 12 of Hilchot Tefillah 11 — he brought Yerushalmi: the translator may not lean to stand in awe and fear; and he omitted the law of the reader — all the more so he is like a translator, as Gemara Yevamot there 9 wrote; and therefore he omitted what is written in Megillah "not so regarding Torah — stand, stand" — it implies that in all cases sitting is forbidden because of awe and fear, and b'dieved one fulfills even actual sitting; for otherwise he would not have omitted sitting, for even b'dieved one does not fulfill; and likewise leaning — if he removes support he will fall, etc. And it is true: in chapter 2 of Megillah this is Rambam's view — that in congregation one does not read Megillah sitting; and necessarily "not so regarding Torah" means b'dieved also not — for otherwise "not so regarding Torah" is not applicable; and one may say honor of the congregation helps through their waiver, not so regarding Torah which is forbidden as it was given in awe and fear. One who examines Beit Yosef in siman 143 regarding chumashim — it appears that also for Rambam honor of the congregation does not help through waiver — somewhat difficult. And see in our chiddushim — one may say honor of the congregation supports from what is also permitted: in such a case there is no absence of honor of the congregation; and from this I say to her from what Magen Avraham 690:11 wrote from Tosafot Zevachim 19b — there is no sitting in the azarah — behold leaning is permitted; all the more so honor of the congregation which is permitted in leaning; and in Torah it is forbidden — and this is "not so regarding Torah"; and necessarily regarding sitting b'dieved one fulfills — for otherwise why did Rambam omit the law of sitting for the Torah reader? And it will be explained in Magen Avraham; and see in siman 690 from this; and here one need not elaborate.
