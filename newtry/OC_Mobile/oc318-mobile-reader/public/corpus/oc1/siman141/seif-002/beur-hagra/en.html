Seif 2: Rather, etc. This is the law of the Gemara.
Or, etc. This is the present enactment, so as not to embarrass one who does not know how to read, as stated in chapter 3 of Bikkurim; Tosafos in Megillah there; chapter 3 of Rosh Hashanah 27a; chapter 3 of Menachos 21a; and chapter 1 of Bava Basra. The Rosh gives another reason: because there are those who do not know how to read and wish to read by heart, and the congregation does not fulfill its obligation through their reading. See there.
Nevertheless, etc. The Rosh at the beginning of chapter 3 there, and as stated in siman 139, seif 2. The statement that two people should not read means aloud, as Tosafos in Bava Basra there, 46a, s.v. shemonah, explains. See there. Even in the era of the Gemara, the chazzan would assist the oleh quietly, as Rashi writes in Shabbos 12b, s.v. rashei, etc.
So that, etc. As stated in Zohar, volume 3, 22a: “If that prayer is audible to a person’s ears,” etc.; “therefore, when one reads from the Torah, one reads and the other remains silent,” etc. This implies that, as with prayer, he should not make it audible even to himself, following the Tosefta. Yet in siman 101 it was ruled that even in prayer one makes it audible to his own ears. This is the meaning of the gloss, “and even,” etc. There is no proof from the Zohar, because there it says only that it should not be audible to another person, as Magen Avraham writes there.
