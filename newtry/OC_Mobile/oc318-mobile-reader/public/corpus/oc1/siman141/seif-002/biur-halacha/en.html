In vain — see Mishna Berurah; and behold per what Rama ruled above siman 139 seif 3 that custom is to read for a blind person, therefore even if he cannot read with the chazan — here too he should have edited for us that even if not reading with the chazan it is fine; perhaps he relied on above; more plausible Rama did not rely on Maharil's words except for a blind person, therefore if they never call him there is great embarrassment and they come to resentment; but in our case where he is expert in reading he is certainly obligated to say with the chazan, for he already wrote in Darkei Moshe that Maharil's view does not appear correct to him, but the words of the Beit Yosef who brought in the name of several rishonim that if he cannot read with the chazan they cannot call him to the Torah; indeed the Taz brought support for Maharil from the Yerushalmi that ruled regarding Megillah that one reads and one blesses — hearing as answering; and Peri Chadash challenged the Shulchan Aruch from this Yerushalmi; in my humble opinion it is simple that the law is with the rishonim who were stringent in this, and the Yerushalmi case of Megillah is not at all comparable to Torah reading, for there with Megillah by right the entire congregation must read and bless each one separately, and therefore one blesses for all to exempt them — not so here where the congregation may not bless the Torah blessing on their hearing but the reader blesses; if he is not reading himself he is only like the rest of the congregation who hear — how then is he permitted to answer Amen to the blessing, etc.
