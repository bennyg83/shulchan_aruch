[s.k. 2] To lean — it must say in awe (Beit Yosef), and this is from the Yerushalmi: just as it was given in awe, so we must conduct ourselves with it in awe — until here.

And if so, even to lean — meaning: in a perfect case, if we were to say the reason that leaning is called sitting, it would be permitted to lean slightly, for in such a case it is certainly not sitting; but since the reason is that one must stand in awe, even to lean slightly is not the way of awe.

And see what is written siman 585, that l'chatchila one must blow the shofar standing; and he wrote that one may not lean so much that if he takes that thing he would fall; and there the reason of the Yerushalmi on account of awe does not apply; and therefore one must say that such leaning is called sitting.

And if so, even a baal basar, etc. — specifically what is forbidden because it is not the way of awe, namely to lean slightly — this was permitted for a baal basar, for since he is a baal basar it is recognizable that he is not leaning through pride; unlike complete leaning, whose prohibition is that it is not standing, and standing requires the verse "and you, stand here," as above, or that it is an asmakhta, as above — until here; in any case, since standing is required, even for a baal basar complete leaning was not permitted.

Regarding leaning while lying down — it is forbidden to read Keriat Shema except when he turns on his side; and Rashi wrote the reason: it appears that he accepts the yoke of the kingdom of heaven through pride; and for a baal basar it suffices when he places a bit on his side — behold, what is forbidden on account of doing the mitzvah through pride was permitted for a baal basar, and no more; but it is difficult in my humble opinion, for the permission of a baal basar Rav Yaakov Beirav wrote in the name of Mordekhai, first chapter of Berachot, and there it implies that even complete leaning is permitted for a baal basar; and these are his words: R' Yochanan came to a certain place, saw a man reading with leaning, said to him it is forbidden to you, as it is written "and you, stand here," etc.; he said I am a baal basar, and he permitted him; but another person is forbidden, and all the more so in prayer, as it is written to stand and to serve — Massekhet Rokeach, until here; and so in Rokeach siman 53; and this incident implies it was complete leaning, since he compared it to the verse "and you, stand here," etc.; for if it were slight leaning, namely to lean a bit, its reason would only be because just as it was given in awe, etc.; if so it was complete leaning, and nevertheless when he said I am a baal basar he permitted; and therefore one must say that also complete leaning, whose prohibition is from the verse that it is called sitting, its reason too is that we must conduct ourselves with it in awe; and for this reason the verse forbade sitting; and therefore even complete leaning is permitted for a baal basar; and it appears to me that nevertheless complete sitting was not permitted for a baal basar, since it is possible through leaning.

And it is difficult — in Choshen Mishpat, etc., he considers leaning standing; for he wrote that witnesses are permitted to testify standing by leaning, although they must testify standing; therefore one must say that this is called standing; and it appears to Magen Avraham that Tosafot deals with complete leaning and nevertheless it is called standing.

And here the Tur and Tosafot in siman 38, etc., wrote — and he brought proof from what we say in Zevachim daf 19b: How is sanctification of hands and feet in the Temple? He places his right hand on his right foot and his left hand on his left foot; R' Yosei bar R' Yehuda says: he places his two hands one on the other and his two feet one on the other and sanctifies; they said to him: you have divided — you cannot do so, lest he fall; R' Yosei his colleague supports him — and this is standing by leaning; and we say: in what do they disagree, and conclude: regarding standing by leaning — for the Tannaim Kama it is not standing, and for R' Yosei bar R' Yehuda it is standing; and we hold like the Tannaim Kama.

And one must say that regarding testimony they were lenient, but it is not necessary, etc. — for thus Rivash: from him flow the words of Rama in Choshen Mishpat; and the words of Rivash are brought in Magen Avraham below siman 422: if they accepted testimony standing (meaning the judges must sit at the time of accepting testimony), and if they were standing — what was done was done; and if they were leaning on the pillar, even l'chatchila it is permitted, for leaning is not like sitting — until here; and per Magen Avraham's understanding, what Rivash wrote "and if they were leaning," etc., refers to the beginning, meaning if the judges were leaning; and if so, one must say that what he concluded, that leaning is not like sitting, is an error, and one must say that leaning is like sitting, as Magen Avraham concludes afterward; and accordingly Rivash did not mention the law of witnesses at all; and if so, what Magen Avraham wrote that witnesses may testify l'chatchila standing by leaning is by reasoning one can say, although it is proven in Zevachim as above; and likewise from the responsum of Rivash, that it is called sitting — nevertheless regarding witnesses they were lenient and we call it standing; nevertheless, since Rama wrote thus in the name of the responsum of Rivash, and behold in Rivash the law of testimony was not mentioned at all — therefore one must say Rama derives the law of testimony from judges mentioned in Rivash; and thus he derives that Rama holds leaning is not complete sitting and not complete standing, and nevertheless sometimes we count it standing and sometimes sitting — namely regarding judges we count it sitting, since Rivash taught that judges stand by leaning l'chatchila, and although it is not complete sitting, nevertheless we are lenient regarding judges to call leaning sitting; from the leniency regarding judges we derive regarding witnesses the opposite — namely we require standing; nevertheless, since we hold that if they testified while seated their testimony is valid, as the poskim wrote as above — if so, if witnesses lean, even l'chatchila they may testify, for we count leaning as standing regarding witnesses, although it is not complete standing.

And on this Magen Avraham wrote that it is not necessary to say regarding a matter requiring standing that although b'dieved it helps if done while sitting, nevertheless it was not permitted l'chatchila by leaning.

For regarding reading, etc. — and it appears Rama looked in Beit Yosef, etc.; the responsum of Rivash is not available; and it is possible that in the responsum of Rivash it was printed in its corrected form that leaning is like sitting; and therefore Magen Avraham wrote that Rama looked only in Beit Yosef and not in the responsum itself; and therefore Rama wrote valid, etc. — meaning Rama understood: since Rivash concluded that leaning is not like sitting, therefore one must say it does not refer to the beginning that deals with judges (unlike what Magen Avraham wrote first, as above), but "and if they were leaning," etc., refers to the witnesses — if witnesses lean, even l'chatchila it is permitted; and on this he concluded well that leaning is not like sitting — meaning that regarding witnesses they are lenient, as above.

But it appears plainly a scribal error, etc. — meaning from the beginning that deals with judges, the end too "and if they were leaning" refers to the judges; and so Magen Avraham below siman 422; if so, scrutinize Rama.

Tosafot wrote that in every place, etc., on this that the gemara challenges there in Zevachim the dispute of the Tannaim Kama and R' Yosei bar R' Yehuda — he should sit to sanctify his hands and feet; and they answer: the verse said to stand and to serve — behold service requires standing, and sanctification is called service, as it is written "or when you approach the altar to serve"; and Tosafot wrote on the gemara's difficulty "he sat and sanctified" — these are his words: he did not mean literal sitting, for there is no sitting in the Courtyard except leaning — until here; meaning specifically in the Courtyard, etc.; meaning one must say how their difficulty is resolved in what is written "except leaning" — either way: if leaning is called standing, it is not difficult from "there is no sitting in the Courtyard"; if so, how does he answer that service requires standing, for leaning is called standing; and for this Magen Avraham wrote: specifically in the Courtyard, for there one must stand, but sitting is forbidden; therefore when not during service, leaning was permitted, for it is not called sitting; unlike regarding service, where it is written to stand and to serve — standing is required, and leaning is not called standing; and accordingly it is proven from Tosafot unlike Rivash, who taught regarding judges leaning, for judges must sit, and Tosafot proved leaning is not sitting; and regarding judges actual sitting is required, as they expounded in Shevuot daf 30b: "and Moses sat to judge"; and consequently for Rama too it is difficult regarding witnesses, who require standing, as Magen Avraham challenged above; and see Shevuot daf 30, etc., where they said: the wife of Rav Huna, after Rav Huna's death, came before R' Nachman for judgment; and R' Nachman said: I need to stand before her, for the wife of a colleague is like a colleague; but lest the claim of her litigant be undermined, that he see I honor her — therefore he told his attendant: a bird fly on me, son of a goose, and I will stand, and the litigant will not understand I stand before her, but will think on account of the son of a goose that flew on me; and the gemara challenges: at the time of concluding judgment, how did he act, for at concluding judgment judges must certainly sit, and he needed to stand for the wife of Rav Huna; and they answer: he was sitting as one who removes his shoe — meaning as one who removes his shoe, namely sitting by leaning; for regarding the wife of Rav Huna it is called standing, and for concluding judgment, which requires sitting, it is called sitting; and if so, this contradicts Zevachim, which proves leaning is not called standing, as above; and on this answer: for a Torah scholar ordinary standing suffices — meaning regarding service where standing is written, leaning is forbidden, for it is not standing, as written; and what works against the wife of Rav Huna, who has the status of a Torah scholar and leaning would be called standing, is because for a Torah scholar actual standing is not required, but honor — therefore leaning suffices; and still not resolved that in Shevuot it implies regarding judges it is called sitting, per Rivash; and in Zevachim Tosafot proved from challenging "let him sit and sanctify" although there is no sitting in the Courtyard — nevertheless by leaning it is permitted, for it is not called sitting, as above; and below siman 422 he wrote: it is different there (R' Nachman in the Courtyard), for only literal sitting is forbidden on account of honor, but in judges' sitting, leaning is called sitting — until here; and if so, do not say the words of the responsum of Rivash; but the words of Rama regarding witnesses are still not settled; and per Magen Avraham's approach one can say somewhat the difficulty of Bach and Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 17 s.k. 15 on Sma from the gemara in Shevuot mentioned — see there; and it requires study.
