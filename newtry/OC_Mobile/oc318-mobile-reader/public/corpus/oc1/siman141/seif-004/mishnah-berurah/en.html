(15) Through an intermediary — and this is Moses our Teacher, as it is written "I stand between the the Master and you."
(16) To conduct it through an intermediary — the practice is that the gabbai or the one who buys the mitzvah stands at the Torah scroll, and this is called the segan, so that there are three standing; meaning the segan is [as if] in the place of the Holy One blessed be He, who has a mitzvah to call up whoever he wishes, and the reader is the intermediary in place of Moses, and the one called up is the recipient in place of all Israel through their agency [Levush]. In truth, this is the custom of the ancients that three stand at the Torah scroll, as written in Massekhet Soferim chapter 14 halakhah 14 [and this is brought in Or Zaru'a, Laws of Shabbat]; only there a different reason is given: "It is not ideal that the chazzan stand alone before the ark, but that two stand with him, one to his right and one to his left, corresponding to the patriarchs." Shaarei Ephraim wrote: from this one learns that one should choose for segan a distinguished person and a man of deeds, and that people are comfortable with him, so they will not suspect that for his own pleasure or some reason he will support honor for one who is unworthy, or the reverse; and likewise it is fitting for the congregation not to harbor thoughts against the segan and judge him in every matter favorably, and what seems difficult to them about him they should attribute to error and mistake in judgment; and even if it happened that he called one not according to his honor and they suspect the segan acted intentionally to anger him — if fear of God touched his heart he should restrain himself and not speak to him, and bear it in his soul; if one is angered when touched in the tip of his honor as a hair's breadth, how much more must he be concerned for the honor of the Place blessed be He and for the honor of His holy Torah not to make a quarrel thereby [one who passes over his measures, they pass over all his transgressions for him]; and if one in pride of spirit made a quarrel with the segan, nevertheless the segan is obligated to be among the insulted and not leave his place; and if the segan set down the Torah scroll and left because this one angered him with words, he is worthy of punishment — for if a person sinned, why is the Torah scroll ashamed; and every clever person should act knowingly and spare the honor of the Place and the honor of the Torah, etc. — until here in brief.
