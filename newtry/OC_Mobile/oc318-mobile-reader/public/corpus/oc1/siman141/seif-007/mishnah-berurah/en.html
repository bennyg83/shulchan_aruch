(22) By the shorter path — on account of the honor of the congregation that they not wait for him long; and also on account of the honor of the Torah, to show that it is beloved to him and he hurries to read in it.

(23) Which is for him the longer path — so that it not appear on him as a burden.

(24) He ascends by the opening, etc. — as we say generally: all turns you make should be only to the right.

(25) And he descends by the opening opposite him — and they supported this from the verse in Ezekiel 46, as it is written: he shall not return by the gate through which he came in, for opposite it he shall go out. And the chazzan too, when he brings the Torah scroll to the reading desk, the same law applies: he ascends by the right path and descends from the desk by the opening opposite him. And it is possible that for the Torah scroll one always ascends by the right path even if it is the longer path; and in our places where the bimah always stands in the center facing the Holy Ark, as Rama wrote in siman 150 seif 5 in the gloss — if so, the two paths are equal, and certainly he ascends by the right path. The later authorities wrote: one whom they call to ascend to the Torah should go quickly immediately, and should not run, on account of the honor of the synagogue and the honor of the congregation.

(26) Until he has gone up, etc. — on account of the honor of the Torah scroll that he not leave it alone; and the custom is to wait until the second one begins the blessing; and the meticulous are concerned that on the way they will not be able to hear the reading properly; therefore they wait until the second finishes and descend between one reader and the next. And when descending from the bimah he should go gently and not run, so it not appear on him as a burden.
