The maftir, etc. This prohibition applies specifically where the maftir is a bar mitzvah and it is a Shabbos on which maftir is not read from a different Sefer Torah. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. The Rama and Levush write that he should not be called by name, which implies that if he is called simply as maftir it is permitted. It seems to me that this applies specifically in places where it is sometimes customary to call 'the maftir' without a name. Where that is not the custom, such a call is unusual, people will ask about it, and it may arouse even greater ayin hara. It is somewhat comparable to the case of a malformed firstborn animal in Yoreh Deah siman 14. This requires study. Emunat Shmuel, siman 47, writes that according to the Ashkenazic custom to call the seventh aliyah without specifying the person's name, two brothers may be called for the sixth and seventh aliyot.
Because of ayin hara, etc. The Beit Yosef's text says that they do not refrain because of ayin hara, and so is the text in Kol Bo. There must be a scribal error, with the word 'except' omitted. I also saw this in Tashbetz, siman 190. This requires study. Maharil seems to imply that even if a person says he is not concerned about ayin hara, one still should not do this. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that there is no distinction between brothers through the father or the mother, and the same applies to a father and son. If they were already called, they should not step down, since the entire concern is only ayin hara.
