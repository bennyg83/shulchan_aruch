The evil eye. The same applies to a father and son’s son. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah. Peri Chadash writes regarding this, ‘This is not clear to me.’ I was asked: Since the reason is concern for the evil eye, if he wishes to receive an aliyah, may he be allowed to do so? Yad Aharon concludes that if he has already gone up, he should not descend; all the more so, if he has already begun the blessing, he should not interrupt. If he merely wishes to go up, however, he should be prevented. Peri Chadash and Knesses HaGedolah write likewise; see there.
And one of them is the maftir. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes that this applies specifically to the maftir on Shabbos, when the maftir reads from the same Torah scroll as the seventh person. Since both read from one Torah scroll, there is concern for the evil eye. Regarding the maftir on Yom Tov and similar occasions, when two Torah scrolls are taken out, there is no objection.
