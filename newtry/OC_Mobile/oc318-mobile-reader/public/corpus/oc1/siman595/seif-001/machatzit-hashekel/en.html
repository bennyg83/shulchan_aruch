(s.k. 1) Doubt. Because shofar blasts are D'Oraisa. Even according to the poskim who hold that a Torah doubt is lenient according to Torah law, and that what we rule, that a Torah doubt is stringent, is rabbinic, nevertheless a doubtful shofar-blowing is preferable to definite blessings. Although both are rabbinic, nevertheless the essence of the shofar-blasts is D'Oraisa. The same applies where one knows only one, etc.; this too is included in doubt, for according to the Torah one of the orders is enough, but we are in doubt which one fulfills the obligation. Therefore, because of doubt, we do all of them, as I wrote above in siman 590. All the more so according to Rav Hai, who holds that one certainly fulfills the obligation with each one of the orders, as above. But the doubt is this: if it is clear to him that in one place they pray, and in another place they know how to blow but know no more than one order, and regarding that order that they know he is uncertain whether they will blow. According to Rav Hai, that with each of the orders one certainly fulfills the obligation, there is no doubt that he must go to the place of the blowers, for although there is a doubt that perhaps they will not blow, nevertheless it is a Torah doubt, as appears to me. But according to the other commentators, that what we blow, tashrat, tashat, tarat, is because of actual doubt, in such a case it is possible that it is preferable to go to the place where they pray, for in any event that is a definite rabbinic obligation. By contrast, the place of the blowers is a double doubt: first, perhaps they will not blow at all; and even if you say they will blow one order, perhaps with that order that they blow one does not fulfill the obligation. A double doubt is lenient even in a Torah matter.
