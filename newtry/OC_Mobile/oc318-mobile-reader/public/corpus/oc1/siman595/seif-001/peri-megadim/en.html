Even. See Turei Zahav, Rosh Hashanah 34b, and see Rashi, Beis Yosef, and Perishah. If it is doubtful whether he will find blowers, nevertheless a Torah doubt is preferable to a definite rabbinic obligation. This requires analysis regarding the second day, since we are expert in the fixing of the month. Also, if he blew one order in his house and no more, should he go to a doubtful place where they might blow? According to the Rambam, of blessed memory, every Torah doubt is lenient by Torah law and stringent rabbinically; one can say this does not apply here, for he is obligated to do what is incumbent upon him. All the more so, a doubt of the law, where he does not know how they blew, does not enter this category. In the Yerushalmi at the end of Rosh Hashanah, brought in part by the Beis Yosef, this is the language there: on a Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah that fell on Shabbos, one goes to the one who blesses, not to the one who blows. But for one who has no time to blow, you say he goes to the one who blesses? This says that they disagree. See the Gaon who explains there. It is clear that according to the one who says blowing is preferable, even on the second Yom Tov, where Shabbos and Rosh Hashanah are on the first day, he makes an eruv for both, as in siman 416; see there. Even so, blowers are preferable to those who recite the blessings. This still requires analysis in such a doubt.
