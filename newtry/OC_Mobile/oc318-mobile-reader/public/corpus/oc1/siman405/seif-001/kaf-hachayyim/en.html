1) [Se'if 1] One who went beyond the techum, etc. This means intentionally and knowingly, not for the purpose of a mitzvah. Rashi. Bach writes likewise. If he went out without awareness, see below, se'if 4 and subsection 3. If he went out unintentionally, see Eliyah Rabbah, siman 396, subsection 3, and in this siman, subsection 1, where he writes that an unintentional violation has the status of an intentional one. Se'if 5 below, however, proves that an unintentional violation has the status of one performed under coercion. See there. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2, writes likewise.
2) There. He may not reenter even if he went only one amah beyond it, etc. The Tur writes this, as does Rambam in chapter 27. Maggid Mishneh writes there that Rashba rules in accordance with Rabbi Shimon, who says that even if one went fifteen amos beyond the techum, one may reenter, because those who mark the measurements do not mark them precisely and leave fifteen amos beyond the marked location. See there. Beis Yosef cites this and writes that the ruling follows Rambam. This is also the opinion of the later authorities.
3) There. He has only four amos from the place where his feet stand and outward. Rambam writes this in chapter 27, halachah 11. This implies that he may not walk even one amah inward. Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam follows Rabbi Yehudah in the Mishnah there, who maintains that one has only four amos in one direction. Since this person is walking and wishes to proceed, he appears to select the direction before him, so he receives only four amos outward from where his feet stand. See there. However, see above, siman 396, se'if 1, in the gloss, which cites a dispute: some say that the person stands in the center of these four amos and has two amos in every direction, while others say that the person has four amos in every direction. We cited there the authorities who agree with the latter opinion. Regarding reentry and resuming the status of the city's residents, Magen Avraham, subsection 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, write that whenever one went out under coercion or for a mitzvah purpose and remains within four amos of the techum, one may reenter and regain the status of the city's residents. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 76, subsection 28; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 2.
