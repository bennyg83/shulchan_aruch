7) [Se'if 4] Without awareness. For example, his mind was preoccupied and he did not realize that he was leaving the techum, as explained in the Gemara. The same applies if he left under coercion or unintentionally, as explained in the following se'if. It likewise applies if he left for a mitzvah purpose, which is also considered comparable to coercion, as stated above in subsection 3.
8) There. It is permitted to make a partition for him, etc. The reason for this permission is that when people surround him to form partitions, the area resembles an enclosed karpeif, all of which is regarded as four amos. He can walk within the enclosure toward the city and thereby enter his techum permissibly. Once he enters his techum, it is as though he never left. Beis Yosef. This means that the entire city then again has the status of four amos for him, as initially, and he may walk two thousand amos beyond it in every direction, as stated in the following se'if.
9) There. It is permitted to make a partition for him, etc. The partition must not enclose more than two thousand amos. Rambam, chapter 27, halachah 14. Maggid Mishneh there, however, writes that Raavad and Rashba disagree and permit it no matter how large the enclosure is. See there. Beis Yosef cites their words. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, likewise writes that even if the partition extends more than two thousand amos, one may walk throughout it. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, write likewise.
