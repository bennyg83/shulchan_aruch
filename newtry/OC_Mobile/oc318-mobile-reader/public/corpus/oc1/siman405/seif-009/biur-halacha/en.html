(א) That he returned them for his need — the wording is imprecise: from "that he returned" it implies even the man who returned them is permitted; from "for his need" it implies it deals with another who took out and returned for his need — only in this they permit, since he himself did not do a prohibited act; but when he took out himself it is possible even for himself it is forbidden; Beit Yosef brought two views; Hagahot Ashiri is stringent — see Beit Yosef; you will see the Mechaber's wording is exactly Hagahot Ashiri's wording and the wording is truly ambiguous; in Or Zarua from which Hagahot Ashiri copied it says "they returned them" and not "he returned them" — referring to others who returned on his account, and only in this we permit; if so perhaps so is also the Mechaber's intent, as Levush and Pri Megadim explained the Mechaber's words; but Gra explains the one who took out himself is also permitted, per the plain sense of the wording to that Jew who returned them; for law one should certainly follow leniency per Gra's explanation, for Ritva in chiddushim also wrote so in his teacher's name, and so Ramban chiddushim and Rashba, and so Gra's responsum.

(ב) Why — they are under duress. See Magen Avraham in what he wrote to resolve why it is not difficult from that which we hold one who desecrates Shabbat intentionally is forbidden; and answer in the name of Poskim because there it is a Torah prohibition — see there what he expanded; behold in the Mechaber it is hard to say so, for he concludes this law also for Shabbat carrying whose prohibition is Torah unless we say it deals with karmelis; in truth there are more answers to Magen Avraham's question in the name of Poskim — this case is different since they were returned to their place and he does not benefit from a Shabbat act at all; see Ramban chiddushim who answered so; there are more answers — see Ritva there; so Acharonim Maamar Mordechai, Chemed Moshe, Acharonim, and Noda Biyehudah 5 Shabbat general 9 — see there who expanded.
