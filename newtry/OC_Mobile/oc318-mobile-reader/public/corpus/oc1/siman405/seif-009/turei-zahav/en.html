(א) If it is Yom Tov — meaning then it is permitted to carry from domain to domain, unlike Shabbat where permission is only in place.

(ב) And forbidden to carry them beyond four. In Tur it is written: if he took them out mistakenly they are permitted; intentionally forbidden to carry them beyond his four cubits. Beit Yosef wrote: certainly even if he took them out mistakenly they have only four cubits; and Tur when it wrote "intentionally" means because of eating prohibition. It appears to me to explain Tur's words: it deals with a walled city; thereby he holds like Rambam in seif 6 we mentioned there — one who left knowingly and entered pen or sahar — we do not say the whole city is like four cubits except when he left under duress; so too here: if mistakenly the whole city is considered his four cubits, but intentionally not; therefore Tur wrote "beyond his four cubits" with a vav to teach even in reshut hayachid or walled city he has only four cubits. In Rashi I saw in chapter Ein Tzoveh siman 9 he brought these words of Tur in what he wrote "and forbidden to carry beyond four cubits" and commented there: meaning in a city not enclosed with a wall, etc.; in my humble opinion as I wrote — even in walled city he forbids since there is no duress, as in the nearby case. I again saw to copy Rashi's words there and what appears to me there, thus: there Rif and Raosh wrote, etc.: "That which we say 'if there is of that species attached it is forbidden' — not only for eating that it is forbidden but even to carry; it is not fit for eating and is like muktzeh. But one who comes from outside the techum — although forbidden to eat, permitted to carry since permitted to another Jew, etc." Tur wrote at the end of siman 405 where an Israelite took fruit outside their place intentionally that they are forbidden to all for eating and forbidden to carry beyond four cubits — within four cubits permitted — and beyond, he did not descend to analyze; behold his father Raosh adopted Rif's words that whatever is forbidden to eat by force of the day's prohibition is forbidden to everyone also to carry; and at first glance so appears to rule — who can be lenient against them without proof? Yet when I descended to analyze the opening sugya of Ein Tzoveh I say the Rif's reasoning does not exist, though Raosh agreed — for it is Torah and I need to learn; since it is resolved there, in the mishnah of a non-Jew who brought fish to R' Gamliel who said they are permitted and concludes there R' Gamliel permitted only to receive, i.e. carrying, but for eating he agrees it is forbidden as doubtful trapping that day — why not forbid carrying too because forbidden to eat? And what of it that it is doubtful — still, since forbidden to eat it is muktzeh and forbidden to carry? Furthermore he challenges from a baraita: a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite — even split fish and same-day fruit are permitted; granted for one who says permitted to receive it works well; but for one who says permitted to eat — same-day fruit who permits eating? Thus he holds even same-day fruit forbidden to eat is permitted to carry — how could he challenge "they are permitted to receive" and why not forbid because of muktzeh? And even per the conclusion that answered him "per your reasoning same-day fruit who permits carrying" — implies all know same-day fruit is forbidden, namely because of muktzeh of attached as Rashi explained; for if per your view it should say "per your reasoning since forbidden to eat who permits carrying — it is muktzeh"; rather certainly this reasoning does not exist and Rashi's words are correct and primary. Now what Tur wrote that they are forbidden to carry beyond four cubits — within four cubits permitted is correct per Rashi and so Acharonim. I am astonished at this — he came to reject Rif and Raosh in Shulchan Aruch in the sugya they deal with — Heaven forbid to attribute this even to a minor author, all the more to great ones of Israel; the truth is as I shall explain. From where Rashi came on them — his proof is there in Ein Tzoveh in the mishnah: traps of beast, bird, and fish made on erev Yom Tov — one may not take from them on Yom Tov unless he knows they were trapped on Yom Tov; incident of a non-Jew who brought fish to R' Gamliel who said they are permitted but he does not wish to receive from him — incident to contradict missing material and it means: doubtful preparation is forbidden and R' Gamliel permits; incident too, etc.; Gemara: they are permitted; Rav said permitted to receive — Rashi: carrying, for the rabbis were not so strict on doubtful preparation to forbid carrying; but for eating R' Gamliel did not permit; Levi said permitted to eat; they challenged from a non-Jew who brought a gift to an Israelite — even split fish and same-day fruit are permitted; granted for one who says permitted to receive it works; but for one who says permitted to eat — same-day fruit who permits eating? And per your reasoning same-day fruit who permits carrying? Rather in red dye vats and salted fruits Tosafot explained there is doubt when they were detached — not like Rashi. Rif and Raosh explained Rav who said "they are permitted" that R' Gamliel said — certainly we do not rule like R' Gamliel but to explain R' Gamliel's words as Rashi also wrote; from R' Gamliel we learn Tanna Kamma who argues on R' Gamliel forbids; Rav holds R' Gamliel holds on doubtful preparation they did not forbid carrying but eating — thereby Tanna Kamma who forbids forbids even carrying; Levi permits even eating for R' Gamliel — thereby Tanna Kamma forbids only eating; they challenge from that case of fruit that is permitted; granted for one who says carrying it appears well that even attached and certainly no muktzeh here since we hold like R' Shimon there is no muktzeh except dried figs and raisins and this is attached better than dried figs and raisins — perhaps his mind was from yesterday it would be detached by a non-Jew; same deficient trapping; and although in truth it is attached like dried figs and raisins, nevertheless the challenger does not hold so and therefore challenges one who says permitted to eat; on this he said "per your reasoning who permits carrying" — he holds as is the truth that attached is like dried figs and raisins and forbidden; on this he answers it deals when there is doubt when detached or trapped; thereby R' Gamliel permits even eating for Levi and for Rav only carrying — thereby Tanna Kamma we rule like him forbids even carrying; and although no muktzeh here since doubtful, nevertheless since forbidden in doubt to eat today he designated it from yesterday from this and this — itself the reason Rif and Raosh ruled on attached even in doubt forbidden even to carry; Tur ruled here on fruit that left Shabbat and were not returned — permitted to carry within four cubits even intentionally since no muktzeh here for they were at twilight within techum; and what is forbidden to eat is rabbinic penalty for intentional violation as above — all clear; and Rashi incidentally did not examine this well.
