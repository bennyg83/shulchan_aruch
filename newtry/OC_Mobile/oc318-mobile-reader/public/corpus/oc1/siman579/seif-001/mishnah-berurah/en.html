The teivah. A Sefer Torah with the teivah.
To the town square. In order to be publicly humiliated; alternatively, on account of exile, for we have gone into exile from the synagogue, and exile atones for sin (Gemara). And according to this reason, if they cannot pray in the street because of the gentiles, they should pray in another house (Magen Avraham).
In sackcloth. To say: behold, we are regarded before You as an animal. And the Magen Avraham wrote that one is likened to an animal, for it is customary to cover an animal with a sack; therefore it appears that one should cover [himself] on top of the clothes.
Upon the teivah. Because of "I am with him in distress"; alternatively, "In all their affliction He was afflicted" (Gemara).
The people. And the reason they do not take [ash] themselves and place it on their heads is so that they will be ashamed before others, for through this their shame is greater.
And each one etc. on his head. Before the Shacharis prayer (Sefer HaEshkol).
Of stature. Rashi and Tosafos explained: men of stature, so that they will hear his words and accept them to soften the heart. And the Rosh explained that "a sage" means anyone whom the beis din appointed as a communal leader, and "a man of stature" means a Torah scholar who is not as learned as a sage; alternatively, one who is fit to be appointed as a communal leader.
And after he finishes. And the same applies on erev Rosh Chodesh, in a place where they say words of rebuke; they should say them before the prayer, so that afterward they pray with a broken heart (Magen Avraham).
Accustomed. So that he not err.
And burdened. With young children.
And he has none. For through this he will pray with a whole heart.
And acceptable to the people. So that they will assent to his prayer. And they required a pleasant [voice], that it draw the heart.
