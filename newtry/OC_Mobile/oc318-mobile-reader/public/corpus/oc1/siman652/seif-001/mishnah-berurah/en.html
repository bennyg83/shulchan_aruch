(1) By day and not by night - For it is written, 'and you shall take for yourselves on the day,' by day and not by night [Gemara].
(2) And the whole day is valid, etc. - If he did not take it until twilight on the first day, he is then obligated to take it, for it is a Torah doubt, but without a berachah, because berachos are rabbinic. The Acharonim agreed that on the other days, although the obligation is rabbinic, it is also proper to take it, since there is no exertion involved; he should only be careful not to recite the berachah then on the taking.
(3) From sunrise - If he took it from dawn, he fulfilled the obligation, for from dawn it is day. But because not everyone is expert in this, one must wait until sunrise. See above, siman 89 in Biur Halacha, that according to several Acharonim it is not called dawn until the eastern face has lit up.
(4) At the time of Hallel - In order to shake at Hodu and Hoshi'ah Na. Some of the meticulous have the custom to recite the berachah over the lulav at sunrise inside the sukkah and shake it, and afterward, at the time of Hallel, they shake it again.
(5) He takes it from when dawn has risen - Even though afterward he will recite Hallel without a lulav. He must shake it when he takes it.
(6) Also at the time of prayer - Although it is prohibited to hold any object at the time of prayer, here, since taking it is a mitzvah, he is not distracted by it. Now, although the Gemara states that the people of Yerushalayim would do this, that a person would leave his house with his lulav in his hand, read Shema and pray with his lulav in his hand, etc., nevertheless the Acharonim wrote that nowadays this appears like arrogance, except for one who is known to be meticulous in his deeds. However, this is practiced even today: one brings the lulav himself to the synagogue in the morning, and likewise brings it back home himself.
