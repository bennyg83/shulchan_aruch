1) [Seif 1] The mitzvah of lulav is during the day, etc. For it is written, ‘And you shall take for yourselves on the first day,’ meaning during the day and not at night. Megillah 20b. Beit Yosef. Levush:
2) Ibid. If he did not take it in the morning, he should take it afterward. See Beit Yosef, siman 665, in the name of the Ran. It implies that if he did not take it until bein hashmashos on the first day, he should take it during bein hashmashos, because a doubt concerning a Torah obligation is treated stringently. This is not so on the other days, when he is exempt; see there. Nevertheless, it appears to me that he should take it during bein hashmashos without a blessing. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. This means that even during bein hashmashos of the other days, he should take it without a blessing. So too wrote Levushei Serad, that during bein hashmashos of the first day he should take it without a blessing, and that during bein hashmashos of the other days he should likewise take it. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, sec. 1, that even during bein hashmashos of the first day he should take it without a blessing; see there. So too wrote Chayei Adam, klal 148, sec. 15. Regarding what is implied by Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1, that during bein hashmashos of the first day he should take it with a blessing, Erech HaShulchan, sec. 1, wrote that this is incorrect, for the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 7, and the Rivash, siman 221, wrote that one who is uncertain whether he took the lulav on the first day should not recite a blessing; see there.
