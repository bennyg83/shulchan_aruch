By day. See the Beis Yosef in siman 665, in the name of the Ran, where it implies that if he did not take it until twilight on the first day, he takes it at twilight, for a doubt concerning a Torah obligation is treated stringently. This is unlike the other days, when he is exempt; see there. Nevertheless, it appears to me that he should take it at twilight without a blessing. Magen Avraham.
The sun. And if he took it from when dawn arose, he has fulfilled his obligation. Magen Avraham.
The prayer. In the Tur it says, "one who is meticulous in his deeds." This means that, aside from this, he is meticulous in his other deeds; but if he is not known as such in his other deeds, it appears like arrogance if he wants to conduct himself in this more than the obligation requires. Turei Zahav. The Tur further wrote: when he reads from the Torah, he places it on the ground; see there. See Maharam ben Chaviv, who wrote that in practice he saw some great men who, when called to ascend to the Torah, would leave the lulav in its place and go up to read from the Torah without the lulav in their hands; and some great men would go up with the lulav in their hands, and at the time of the Torah blessing they would give it to the supporter who was at the reading platform, so that they could hold the column of the Torah at the time of the blessing. The first custom appears more correct, end quote. See Yad Aharon. And Mahara HaLevi, in the book Gan HaMelech, siman 139, wrote that specifically in the earlier time, when there was great exertion involved in opening and rolling the Torah scroll, therefore he would not bring his lulav up in his hand; however, as for merely holding the column at the time of the blessing, he can properly go up with the lulav in his hand; see there. In the Shelah it is written that he should shake the lulav in the sukkah and recite the blessing, and afterward the other shakings should be in the synagogue; see there. It is stated in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3: if he placed it on the ground, it is forbidden to move it, end quote. The Magen Avraham wrote: it implies that he holds that after the completion of the mitzvah of lulav, if he placed it on the ground, it is again forbidden to move it. This requires analysis, for we maintain that there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos; and see siman 331 se'if 6 and what the Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 651; see there. And see siman 308 se'if 3. But the esrog is permitted according to everyone.
