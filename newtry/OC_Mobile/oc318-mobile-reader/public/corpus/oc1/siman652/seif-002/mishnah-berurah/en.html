(7) It is prohibited to eat, etc. - Mere tasting is permitted by law; nevertheless, one should not be lenient in this unless for a great need. One who is traveling on Chol HaMoed and expects to reach the place of the lulav, or those who live in settlements and are being sent a lulav, should wait until midday and no longer, even on the first day, when the obligation is from the Torah, for it is prohibited to fast. Some say that if he expects that they will bring him a lulav, he need not wait at all, for he has someone who will remind him to take it, namely the one who will bring him the lulav. One whose heart is weak may certainly rely on this.
(8) On the first day, when it is from the Torah, he must interrupt - In the middle of his meal, like the law of any matter that is from the Torah, that if he began in prohibition he must interrupt, as above in siman 235:2. The second Yom Tov for us, which we observe because of the doubt of the day, has the same law as the first day.
(9) More than half an hour before the time of his obligation arrived - Meaning, more than half an hour before dawn; this is called beginning permissibly. See the Taz, who asked: in siman 89 the first opinion rules that where one began eating before dawn, nevertheless he must interrupt for prayer. If so, in any case he must interrupt because of prayer, and all the more so on Yom Tov, when he must make Kiddush. Therefore he disagrees and holds that he certainly must interrupt automatically because of lulav. Bikkurei Yaakov answered that it speaks where he began eating before dawn, prayed before reciting Bircas HaMazon, also made Kiddush, and afterward began to complete his meal and forgot to take the lulav; in that case he need not interrupt and may finish even on the first day, since he began permissibly. His answer is not clear as practical halachah, as I wrote in Biur Halacha.
