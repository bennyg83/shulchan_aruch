It is prohibited to eat before he takes it. The same applies to all the matters mentioned above in siman 232 before the Minchah prayer; see there, for the Gemara in Sukkah 38 compares them to one another; see there. So too the poskim in siman 235 learned regarding the evening Shema from this matter.
More than half an hour, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote in the name of Bikkurei Yaakov what he answered to the Taz's question. In my humble opinion, his answer is not clear, since he already interrupted for prayer, and by law, had he not forgotten, he would have been obligated to take the lulav before eating. Does the fact that he forgot to do so make it called beginning permissibly? Through forgetfulness it is certainly not called beginning permissibly, as is proven from the Shulchan Aruch. His whole reasoning is because he began eating before dawn, when he was not yet obligated to take the lulav and his eating was permissible. But since he was forced to interrupt because of prayer, his meal was interrupted, and he was obligated to take the lulav before completing his meal.
Where there is time in the day. This wording is not so precise, for if he began to eat before dawn, there will certainly be time to take it during the day after he finishes his meal. The truth is that the Ran is not referring only to this matter, but to every Torah matter, such as Shema: if he began permissibly and there is time in the day, he need not interrupt. Were it not for the Rema's words, it would appear to me to say that regarding lulav, where his permissible beginning is before dawn, even the Ran agrees that since he must interrupt because of prayer, he must automatically take the lulav too, like the Taz's opinion. Rather, the Ran refers only to other matters that are from the Torah, such as the evening Shema and the like, where if he began permissibly he need not interrupt when there is time.
