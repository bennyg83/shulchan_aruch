And from the first day onward. It appears that for us, the second day of Yom Tov, which is because of the doubt about the day, has the same law as the first day, even though we are expert in fixing the month, for one must not treat it lightly, as is stated regarding disqualifications above in siman 649. And the Gemara is not speaking about the doubt about the day, for that which we learned in the Mishnah, "one who was coming on the road," and from there they infer that it is speaking of the second day of Yom Tov, for if it were the first day, would it be permitted to travel on the road? From this it is clear that it is speaking of those who did not observe a second day of Yom Tov because of the doubt about the day.
And if he began to eat, etc. I am astonished at what the Rav wrote in this law, which is the view of the Ran in the Beis Yosef, for we certainly do not rule this way. First, even on a weekday he wrote above in siman 89 that if one began eating before dawn, he must interrupt according to the opinion of the Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, and in the Shulchan Aruch there he did not decide the matter; consequently we proceed stringently. All the more so on Yom Tov, when kiddush is required. Furthermore, the Rema wrote in siman 431 that even if one began learning while it was still day, he must interrupt for bedikas chametz, and that is only rabbinic; all the more so here, where on the first day lulav is from the Torah. And all the more so for eating, he certainly must interrupt even more than when the time for lulav has arrived; so it appears to me clearly.
