1) [Se'if 1] Every person has two thousand amos, etc. This is derived from the verse, 'Let no man go out from his place,' which refers to two thousand amos. Eruvin 51a. This prohibition is only Rabbinic, and the verse is merely a Scriptural support. Rif and Rosh write this at the end of chapter 1 of Eruvin, as does the Tur. Regarding traveling beyond twelve mil, however, Rif writes there that the Yerushalmi states that the prohibition is of Torah origin. See there. Rambam likewise writes this at the beginning of chapter 27. Maggid Mishneh writes there that this is the consensus of the Geonim. Rosh and Mordechai, however, write that according to the Babylonian Gemara, even traveling beyond twelve mil is prohibited only by Rabbinic law. Maggid Mishneh writes that Ramban and Rashba agree with this opinion. See there. Beis Yosef cites their words. Know that even according to the authorities who maintain that traveling beyond twelve mil is prohibited by Torah law, the violation does not incur kareis but only transgresses a negative commandment, since it is not counted among the thirty-nine melachos. Rambam there accordingly mentions only liability for lashes. Sefer HaChinuch and Sefer Mitzvos Hashem, parashas Beshalach, write likewise. See there. Mateh Yehudah challenges Levush's statement that one is liable to bring a sin offering, and Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, cites the challenge. See there.
