His intention is that there in note 7 it is explained that specifically an egg made for taste and appearance is not nullified, but water and salt in dough are nullified; according to this, this law contradicts the words of Beit Yosef there. Nevertheless, this law here cannot be rejected, for this is a statement of Rav Ashi in Beitzah that water and salt in dough follow the feet of both of them and are not nullified in dough because it is a davar sheyeish lo matirin. However, Tosafot there wrote two reasons: one, regarding techum, since it is because of monetary value it is treated strictly and is like a regular min b'mino; they also wrote another answer that water and salt that come to fix the dough are min b'mino. According to this, Magen Avraham should have written here the first answer of Tosafot. Nevertheless, Magen Avraham's intention appears to be that since it is a d'rabbanan prohibition, we are lenient everywhere; and here regarding techum prohibition, per the first answer of Tosafot the law would be so for every thing — if a woman took something for a dish on erev and another woman took from her, it would follow the feet of both, and we rely on the second answer that specifically water and salt are considered min b'mino. There in siman 513 regarding muktzeh prohibition which is not dependent on monetary value, we say the opposite — the first answer is primary, and therefore water and salt are nullified if they are muktzeh and became mixed in dough. In siman 318 he wrote an extended reason: since they were nullified before they became forbidden, even per the second answer of Tosafot it is permitted.
