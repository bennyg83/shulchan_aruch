"For the need of his fellow, they are as the feet of the one who drew the water." See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that some disagree. The Mechaber's position is based on Rabbeinu Tam, Rosh, and Ran. Ra'ah in his novellae to Beitzah also maintains Rabbeinu Tam's position, though he inclines to say that while the water is treated as the feet of the one who drew it, it must also be restricted by the techum of the person for whom it was drawn, so that the recipient may bring it only to a place permitted to both. His reasoning is understandable in light of Rashba's challenge to Rabbeinu Tam. In truth, however, most Rishonim disagree and hold that the water is as the feet of the person for whom it was drawn, because we rule throughout the Talmud that one who lifts a found object for his fellow effects acquisition for his fellow. This is the view of Rashi and Rashbam cited by Tosafos, and R' Chananel rules likewise. The wording cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of R' Chananel is difficult and may contain a scribal error. Baal HaMaor and Ra'avad likewise hold this view, as does Rashba in his novellae, where he discusses the matter at length and challenges Rabbeinu Tam. Meiri inclines likewise. Maggid Mishneh writes that this is also the view of Rambam and Rif, who omitted this particular ruling because they relied upon the established principle that one who lifts a found object for his fellow effects acquisition for his fellow. This explanation of their omission is more satisfactory than Ran's suggestion that they ruled like R' Shimon and relied upon the anonymous Mishnah concerning the well of the Babylonian pilgrims being as the feet of the one who draws from it, without distinguishing whether he draws for himself or for another. Riaz also follows this position, though he states it specifically after the water was given to the recipient and explains it through acquisition from hefker, following the wording of the sugya in Beitzah. Under the general rule that one who lifts a found object for his fellow effects acquisition, however, that further transfer is unnecessary. It is possible that Riaz agrees and merely copied the formulation of the sugya. The approach of Rivav on Rif likewise rules that the water is as the feet of the person for whom it was drawn. Eliyah Rabbah and Nahar Shalom discuss nearly all these points, though they frame the law after the water has already reached the recipient. From all these Rishonim, however, it appears that even while the water is still in the hand of the person who drew it, he may not carry it according to his own techum but only according to the techum of the person for whom he drew it, because it already belongs to that recipient. Avodat HaKodesh's wording that he "gave it to his fellow" merely follows the Gemara's formulation; the same law applies even before actual delivery. Accordingly, it is reasonable to rule not like the Mechaber but that the water follows the recipient's techum, particularly once it has already reached him, where Riaz is explicit and Bach also agrees. Indeed, there is even stronger reason to treat it as the recipient's feet once it is in his possession than while it remains with the drawer. See Rashba's novellae and his questions on Rabbeinu Tam.
