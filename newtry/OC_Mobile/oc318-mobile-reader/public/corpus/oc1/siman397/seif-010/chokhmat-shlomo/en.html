Seif 10. Two who acquired an animal in partnership. NB: Behold we hold that per the Torah there is no brir, and the later authorities dispute on this whether it is from force of certainty or from force of doubt; and we already wrote on this in several places with God's help; and now it appears further to bring proof that it is only doubt from what is stated in Menachot chapter 10 mishnah 4: "and the rest was redeemed and eaten by any person and is liable in challah and exempt from tithes" — R' Akiva obligates in challah and tithes — see in Ra'avad's explanation and Rashi and Tosafos; and it is difficult — for behold in any case there is no brir; and if so at the time of acquiring the consecration in grain and at harvest and grinding it is not known which of it will be for the offering — if so each one is in doubt of consecration since there is no brir — and why should he be liable in tithes? And one may say that R' Akiva holds like those who say there is brir — for behold at the end of Me'ilah it states — Rashi and Tosafos with one explanation that the Gemara wants to establish R' Akiva's view like those who say there is no brir — and it should have challenged from here how could R' Akiva hold there is no brir — if so why does he obligate here in tithes? And perforce it is proven that even for those who say there is no brir it is only doubt and not certainty — and therefore automatically R' Akiva obligates in tithes from force of doubt — to the contrary lest there is brir; but there, since R' Akiva obligates on doubt of offerable asham talui — if so, since in any case it is doubt lest there is no brir, he should have been liable there for asham talui; and it is proven from this that even for those who say there is no brir it is only doubt and not certainty — and it comes out well; requires study.
