If someone gave the wife a gift on condition that her husband have no rights in it and that she may do with it whatever she wishes, it has the techum of both spouses. It also appears obvious that if a person is in another city more than two thousand amos away while his produce is here, the produce may be conveyed toward his city. We are not concerned that he might have established an eruv two thousand amos away in the opposite direction, which would leave the produce only four amos, because we maintain the presumption that he did not establish an eruv. It further appears that if the owner was distant from here and it became known that he had already been moribund for three days, he is presumed dead and his utensils establish their Shabbos residence with his heirs, as stated in siman 329. See there, and Yoreh De'ah, siman 339, and Even HaEzer, siman 17. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3.
