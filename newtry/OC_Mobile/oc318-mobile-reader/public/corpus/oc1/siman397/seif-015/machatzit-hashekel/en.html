(א) (note 15) Corner, etc. — and so in several places in the Gemara, etc. — so it is in Beitzah 40: one who deposits fruits with his fellow — Rav said likewise it follows the feet of the depositor; Shmuel said it follows the feet of the one who deposited. We say: let us say Rav and Shmuel each per their reasoning, for we learned: one who brought his ox into his fellow's courtyard with the courtyard owner's permission and the ox was injured — the courtyard owner is liable, for since he gave him permission he certainly accepted guarding it upon himself; and Rabbi exempts for he did not accept guarding. Rav said the halachah follows the Sages; Shmuel said the halachah follows Rabbi. If so, Rav according to his reasoning that he accepted guarding its damages upon himself — therefore they are in the depositor's domain and likewise follow his feet; Shmuel who holds like Rabbi that he did not accept guarding upon himself — therefore it follows the feet of the one who deposited. It answers: Rav told him — here even Rabbi agrees, for specifically there Rabbi said that generally the courtyard owner did not accept its guarding upon himself, but here behold he accepted its guarding upon himself. Rashi explained here it deals with the depositor explicitly accepting guarding of the fruits. It asks on Rav from the mishnah that proves from it that likewise it follows the feet of the one who deposited; it answers: the mishnah deals with one who designated a corner for him. Rashi: he lent him his house — therefore they are in the domain of the one who deposited; but Rav deals with one who gave a guardian and accepted their guarding — likewise it follows his feet — end. Behold it distinguishes between the mishnah where he designated a corner and Rav whether he accepted them generally or explicitly accepted guarding upon himself; and when he designated a corner the reason is that he did not explicitly accept their guarding. So too Rosh wrote: specifically when he accepted guarding upon himself; but one may say regarding a corner it follows the feet of the one who deposited — end. Behold designating a corner is the opposite of explicitly accepting guarding, and so in the Gemara it says here behold he accepted, etc. — implying that specifically on this it depends whether he explicitly accepted guarding.
