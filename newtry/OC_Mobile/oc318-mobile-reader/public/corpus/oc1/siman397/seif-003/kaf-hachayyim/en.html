10) [Se'if 3] Just as a person is not permitted, etc. This is the wording of the Tur. Beis Yosef comments that this ruling and all the rulings from here to the end of the siman should have been written only in the laws of Yom Tov, for they do not pertain to Shabbos at all. The Tur writes them here only incidentally. Bach and Perishah, subsection 3, however, write that some of these laws properly pertain to Shabbos, while the others are included incidentally. See there.
11) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. An animal and utensils have the same techum as their owner. Beitzah 37a.
12) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. This discusses Yom Tov. On Shabbos, carrying them out is forbidden. Alternatively, it refers to clothing that one wears. Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 5; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3. See below, siman 403.
13) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. This excludes his Canaanite male and female servants, since they are obligated in mitzvos, as stated below in siman 414. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3. See our words there.
14) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. The same applies to his produce and anything else belonging to him. Beitzah 39b and Rashi there. Avodas HaKodesh, folio 35, writes: When does this apply? When the items are within his techum. Even if they are stored in warehouses outside the city, whenever they are within his techum they have the same techum as he does. If, however, they are situated in another city, then although the residents of that city established an eruv in his direction and can reach the place where he established his Shabbos residence, his produce has the status of that city and may not be moved from its place. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 5. See below, se'if 17.
15) There. So too his utensils and animal, etc. It appears that an animal and utensils belonging to nichsei melog have the same techum as the husband. If someone gave the wife a gift on condition that her husband have no rights in it and that she may do with it whatever she wishes, it has the techum of both spouses. It also appears obvious that if a person is in another city more than two thousand amos away while his produce is here, the produce may be conveyed toward his city. We are not concerned that he might have established an eruv two thousand amos away in the opposite direction, which would leave the produce only four amos, because we maintain the presumption that he did not establish an eruv. It further appears that if the owner was distant from here and it became known that he had already been moribund for three days, he is presumed dead and his utensils establish their Shabbos residence with his heirs, as stated in siman 329. See there, and Yoreh De'ah, siman 339, and Even HaEzer, siman 17. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3.
