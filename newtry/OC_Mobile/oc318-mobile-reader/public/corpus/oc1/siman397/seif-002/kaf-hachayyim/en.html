6) [Se'if 2] He walks two thousand steps. This is in addition to his four amos, as stated in the preceding se'if. See siman 399, se'if 10, where he is granted the diagonal measurement. This is also implied by Beis Yosef. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, writes likewise. See our words there, with the help of Heaven.
7) There. Medium steps. A person's ordinary stride is one amah. Rashi. This equals the length of two shoes: one shoe corresponds to the place occupied by the foot, and one shoe to the space between one foot and the other. Mahari Weil, siman 45; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Eliyah Rabbah, there; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2; Chayei Adam, rule 76, subsection 1. Ritva writes in his novellae that all this applies when one needs to walk on Shabbos for a mitzvah purpose. One may count the steps because this is comparable to measuring for a mitzvah purpose, which is permitted, as stated in siman 306, se'if 7. See there. Tosefes Shabbos, there.
8) There. Medium steps. If the person is tall and his medium stride exceeds one amah, he must estimate according to an average person. These two thousand amos are equal for every person, unlike the four amos in which one established Shabbos residence, which are measured according to the individual.
9) There. Medium steps. Their measure is one mil, which is eighteen minutes. Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah, siman 130. Shaarei Teshuvah writes likewise in the name of Chut HaShani, folio 109b. See there. See above, at the beginning of siman 261, in the gloss, and in the Shulchan Aruch there, se'if 2.
