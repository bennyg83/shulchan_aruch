(א) (note 13) The flowing ones, etc. — rain that fell on Yom Tov, etc. — so it is in Eruvin 45b: rain that fell from erev Yom Tov has two thousand cubits in each direction; rain that fell on Yom Tov likewise follows the feet of every person. It needs to establish it according to R' Yehoshua ben Nun, who holds that ownerless objects acquire residence, and therefore when they fell from erev Yom Tov one may walk two thousand in each direction for they acquired residence in their place. He rejects: perhaps ownerless objects do not acquire residence, and the first clause deals with rain that fell near the city, for the people of the city have their mind on it, and likewise it follows the feet of the people of the city. He asked: if so, why does it teach they have two thousand in each direction — implying from their place; it should have taught likewise it follows the feet of the people of the city. Or because he holds ownerless objects acquire residence, and it actually deals with rain that did not fall near the city; afterward it asks on this that it teaches rain that fell on Yom Tov likewise follows the feet of every person — why should they acquire residence in the ocean (from which clouds draw rain, per R' Eliezer, since he holds ownerless objects acquire residence)? R' Yehoshua ben Levi answered: the heavens were tied with clouds from erev Yom Tov (and when they fell on Yom Tov it is a sign they are those clouds tied from erev Yom Tov). It asks: let them acquire residence in the clouds; it concludes that neither in clouds nor in the ocean did they acquire residence, for they move constantly — end of Gemara. From this we come to Magen Avraham's words — that which he wrote that the people of the city have their mind on them is the Gemara's words according to its initial thought; even though it rejected that the baraita cannot be established this way, nevertheless the law remains, and the baraita deals with rain that fell far from the city. But what Magen Avraham wrote, rain falling on Yom Tov — it must be a textual error, for per the initial thought of the first clause, rain falling from erev Yom Tov one may walk two thousand, etc., deals with rain falling near the city that the people of the city have their mind on; nevertheless the latter clause teaches rain falling on Yom Tov likewise follows the feet of every person — therefore when rain falls on Yom Tov we do not need that the people of the city have their mind on it; therefore there must be a textual error in Magen Avraham, and it should read rain falling from erev Yom Tov. What Magen Avraham wrote afterward that they did not acquire residence in the ocean, etc. — should read and they did not acquire, etc., with a vav, and it is an independent law and does not refer to the above; meaning, in the ocean and in clouds they did not acquire residence. If so, the practical difference is if rain fell on Yom Tov likewise it follows the feet of every person — these are the words of the baraita above. Even though the reason he gave because they move is for us who hold ownerless objects do not acquire residence without need — otherwise when rain fell on Yom Tov, behold they were ownerless — the Gemara did not need this reason except per the one who holds ownerless objects acquire residence; one may say Magen Avraham cited the reason of movement, and there is a practical difference even for Raavad and his camp who hold ownerless objects acquire residence. One may also say there is a practical difference even for us: if they were tied in clouds from erev Yom Tov and fell on Yom Tov — even if you say that also in such a case we say the people of the city have their mind on them (though we do not find this explicitly) — nevertheless they did not acquire residence in clouds since they move, and it is like flowing rivers; even though one might say owners, nevertheless they did not acquire residence since they move; but according to this, that which he cited regarding the ocean the reason of movement is unnecessary.
