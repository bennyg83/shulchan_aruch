18) There. One who entrusts his animal to his son. Beis Yosef writes this in the name of Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Yom Tov, and rules accordingly in the Shulchan Aruch. Avodas HaKodesh, folio 35, however, disagrees with Rambam and rules that a son has the same status as another person. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 5 of Beitzah, likewise disagrees with Rambam. Rambam himself rules in his Commentary on the Mishnah that a son is like another person. This also appears from the Tur, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 33, who do not distinguish between a son and another person. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchos Yom Tov, also writes that Rashi and the other commentators rule that a son is like another person. It therefore appears clear to me that one should not rule in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch and Levush on this matter. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 6. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, however, rules in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch. This also appears to be the opinion of Magen Avraham and Tosefes Shabbos, cited in the preceding subsection, since they explain the Shulchan Aruch's words without disagreeing.
