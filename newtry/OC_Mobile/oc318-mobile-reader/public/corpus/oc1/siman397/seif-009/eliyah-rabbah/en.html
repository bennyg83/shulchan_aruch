Rather, the item has the same permitted range as the majority of the city’s residents. Avodas HaKodesh. Even if the shepherd made an eruv in another direction, this does not restrict the purchaser. If, however, someone came from another city by means of an eruv and purchased it, he may not bring it back to his own place. This appears from Rashi, Beitzah 38. Levush teaches that even if the shepherd made an eruv toward another city, and a purchaser from that city buys it, the purchaser may not remove it beyond its original permitted area. This appears correct to me. Nachalas Tzvi’s objection is unfounded. The same applies to the shepherd himself. He may not take it beyond the permitted boundary even though he made an eruv.
