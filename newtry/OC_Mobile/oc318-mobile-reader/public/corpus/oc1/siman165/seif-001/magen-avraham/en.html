He should wash twice. If he washed only once and first recited Asher Yatzar and afterward Al Netilas Yadayim, Asher Yatzar would interrupt between the washing and its blessing. If he first recited Al Netilas Yadayim and afterward Asher Yatzar, Asher Yatzar would interrupt between the blessing and HaMotzi. One who relieves himself in the middle of a meal, who need not recite HaMotzi again, as stated at the end of siman 178, washes once and recites Al Netilas Yadayim and afterward Asher Yatzar. Although this interrupts between washing and eating, we are not concerned, for even regarding an interruption between washing and HaMotzi there are authorities who disagree. See siman 170.
And over the second he recites the blessing. One may ask: in siman 158, seif 7, it is written that he should wash a second time without a blessing. The answer is that there the first washing was not intended for eating, so no blessing could apply to it. Here, however, he intended it for eating. Alternatively, during the first washing he does not wash properly. This is likewise implied by Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos, and this is the primary explanation. Shelah writes that he should touch an unclean place between the two washings. This does not appear correct to me, for since he washed properly, why should he return and deliberately render his hands unclean, wash again, and thereby cause a blessing? Rather, it must be as I wrote, that during the first washing he does not wash properly.
And he rubs. This refers to rubbing the hand itself, the fingers against one another. See siman 162, seif 4. Alternatively, it refers to a case where he poured a revi'is over each hand. Even so, the obligation to recite Al Netilas Yadayim does not yet take effect until drying. Bach. It appears to me, however, that the case is where he washed both hands together, as stated in siman 162, seif 5. According to this, the words of Mahari Abuhav are well resolved. See Beit Yosef and the end of siman 158. If he forgot to recite the blessing until after he had washed his hands, he should first recite Al Netilas Yadayim and afterward Asher Yatzar, as written in Hagahos Maimoniyos, because he also had purification of the hands in mind and this is not an interruption. Darchei Moshe. Maharshal writes in responsum 97 that one who ate something requiring Borei Nefashos as an after-blessing, forgot to recite it, and then urinated, should recite Asher Yatzar first, because it is more frequent. Furthermore, it has no fixed measure, as stated at the end of siman 7, unlike the after-blessing.
