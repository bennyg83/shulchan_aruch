(1) [Seif 1] One who relieves himself and wants to eat shall wash twice, etc. This is the view of R' Yaakov bar Yakar, as Rashi wrote in Sefer HaPardes; and similarly in Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 6 of Laws of Berachot; and similarly R' Yosef, as written in Beit Yosef. The reason that two netilot are required is: if he does only one netilah and blesses Asher Yatzar first and afterward on bread, the berachah of Asher Yatzar is an interruption between netilah and the berachah on bread; and if he blesses on bread first, the berachah of Asher Yatzar is an interruption between the berachah on bread and hamotzi—therefore two netilot are required, as Bach and Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, wrote. But Rashi holds that one netilah suffices for both, and Rashi means that there is no interruption at all, and whichever he wishes he may bless first; nevertheless Bach wrote that it is proper to precede Asher Yatzar first, since the obligation of this berachah comes to him first; see there. What is written afterward in Shulchan Aruch, and if he does not wish to wash but once, etc.—this is the Tur's ruling; and Bach wrote the reason: he holds that drying is part of the mitzvah, and anything not fully dried is considered before their performance, and there is no interruption here; see there. It turns out that one who relieves himself and wants to eat, l'chatchila, must do two netilot—on the first he blesses Asher Yatzar, and on the second he blesses on bread. If it is impossible for him, such as if he has water only for one netilah, or he has no leisure to delay to do two netilot, then he may do both berachot on one netilah—that is, he washes and rubs and blesses Asher Yatzar, and afterward blesses on bread, and afterward dries. Since there are divided opinions on this, as the Chafetz Chaim wrote in Kaf Hachayim siman 200, note 7, see there—therefore we write a matter common between the simple meaning and the sod. And know that we already wrote above in siman 4, notes 61 and 62, that one leaving the bathroom must pour on his hands three times in alternating fashion, since it is on account of ruach raah; but for netilah for a meal one does not need three times in alternating fashion, but only three times consecutively, one upon the other, as written in siman 162, note 2; see there. Therefore one who relieves himself and wants to eat should first do netilah three times in alternating fashion, and intend that this netilah is not on account of eating bread but only to remove the ruach raah that rests on the hands in the bathroom; and afterward he dries his hands, and afterward blesses Asher Yatzar, and afterward he does the order that we wrote above in siman 177, note 14—and this is also in order to make a separation between netilah and netilah. One may also touch covered places before the second netilah, and afterward wash, as written below, note 4; see there. If two netilot are not possible, such as if he has water only for one netilah, or he is hurried and cannot delay, then he does one netilah of three times in alternating fashion—for three times in alternating fashion is also effective for netilah for eating, as written above in siman 162, note 2: the intent of morning netilah and of eating are the same, except that morning netilah requires three times in alternating fashion on account of sleep, but for eating it is not required; but if he also did eating netilah in alternating fashion it does no harm, and he should intend in this netilah to remove bathroom ruach raah and also to purify his hands for eating bread. After he washed three times in alternating fashion, he then does rubbing also three times, as explained in siman 162, note 2; and afterward blesses Asher Yatzar; and afterward raises his hand against his head and blesses on bread; and afterward dries his hands and blesses hamotzi. See above siman 158, notes 82 and 83, and below, notes 6 and 7.
