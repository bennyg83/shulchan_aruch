1) [Seif 1] One who relieves himself and wishes to eat should wash twice, etc. This is the view of R' Yaakov ben R' Yakar, cited by Rashi, of blessed memory, in Sefer HaPardes. Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos, writes the same, as does Rabbeinu Yonah, as cited in the Beit Yosef. Two washings are required because if he performs only one washing and first recites Asher Yatzar and then the blessing over netilas yadayim, Asher Yatzar interrupts between washing and the blessing over netilas yadayim. If he first recites the blessing over netilas yadayim, Asher Yatzar interrupts between that blessing and HaMotzi. Therefore, he requires two washings, as the Bach and Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, write. Rashi, of blessed memory, however, holds that a single washing suffices for both purposes. His intent is that there is no interruption in either order, and one may recite whichever blessing he wishes first. Nevertheless, the Bach writes that it is proper to recite Asher Yatzar first because the obligation of that blessing arose first; see there. The Shulchan Aruch's subsequent statement, 'If he wishes to wash only once,' etc., is the Tur's resolution. The Bach explains that the Tur holds that drying is part of the mitzvah, and as long as one has not fully dried his hands, it is still considered before completion of the act, so there is no interruption; see there. Thus, one who relieves himself and wishes to eat should initially wash twice. Over the first washing he recites Asher Yatzar, and over the second he recites the blessing over netilas yadayim. If that is impossible, such as where he has water for only one washing or lacks time to perform two washings, he may recite both blessings over one washing. He should wash and rub his hands, recite Asher Yatzar, then recite the blessing over netilas yadayim, and afterward dry them. Since opinions differ about this, as R' Chaim Palagi writes in his sefer Kaf HaChaim, siman 22, paragraph 7, we will set forth a procedure that accords with both the straightforward halachah and Kabbalah. Know that we already wrote above in siman 4, paragraphs 61 and 62, that one who leaves the restroom must pour over his hands three times alternately, since this washing removes ruach ra'ah. Netilas yadayim for a meal, however, need not alternate. One pours three consecutive times, as written in siman 162, paragraph 2; see there. Therefore, one who relieves himself and wishes to eat should first wash three times alternately and intend that this washing is not for eating bread, but only to remove the ruach ra'ah that rests upon the hands in the restroom. He should then dry his hands and recite Asher Yatzar. Afterward, he should follow the order we wrote above in siman 157, paragraph 14, which also creates an interruption between the two washings. He should also touch a covered part of his body before the second washing, and then wash as written below in paragraph 4; see there. If two washings are impossible, because he has water for only one or is preoccupied and cannot delay, he should perform a single washing of three alternating pourings. Three alternating pourings are also effective for netilas yadayim before eating, as written above in siman 162, paragraph 2. The kavanos of the morning washing and the washing before eating are identical. The morning washing requires three alternating pourings because of Shibta, while washing before eating does not, but alternating the pourings for the meal causes no harm. He should intend with this washing both to remove the ruach ra'ah of the restroom and to purify his hands for eating bread. After pouring three times alternately, he should rub his hands three times as stated in siman 162, paragraph 2. He should then recite Asher Yatzar, raise his hands opposite his head, recite the blessing over netilas yadayim, dry his hands, and recite HaMotzi. See above, siman 158, paragraphs 82 and 83, and below, paragraph 6.
