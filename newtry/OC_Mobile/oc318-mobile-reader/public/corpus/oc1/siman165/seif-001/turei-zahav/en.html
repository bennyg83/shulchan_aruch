And he rubs—meaning: that he pours on both hands at once, then he is permitted afterward to rub them; but if he poured on each hand by itself, it is forbidden to rub one against the other, as written in siman 162, seif 6. And although I am not fit to rule, nevertheless it seems to me that it is more proper to practice like the first law—that is, he should wash twice—for regarding the second division there is concern, namely that Mahari Weil already questioned, which Beit Yosef brought: behold the first water purifies the hands, and if he wished to dry his hands in the first water he is already pure for eating; if so, he interrupts with Asher Yatzar for netilah, for what he does afterward is to purify the water on the hands, not the hands themselves; and he answered: since this requires second water because if he touches where he dried his hands from the first water his hands return to impurity—if so, all this comes to complete purification of hands for eating; end of his words. And accordingly, if he pours a revi'it on each hand, or on both hands together, he does not need second water at all, as above in siman 162; automatically there is interruption in the berachah of Asher Yatzar; and most times people are accustomed to give a revi'it at once, and so it is proper to practice, for thereby he removes all concerns, as above. And at the least there is doubt in this if he poured a revi'it; if so, there is concern of interruption here, for these matters were said only when taking less than a revi'it, and then second water is required and there is no interruption—which is not so when taking a revi'it at once, where there is interruption. And further, Beit Yosef brought in the name of responsa Rosh, kelal 4, and this is its language: after he poured on his hand once or twice, and his hands are already clean, he blesses Asher Yatzar and completes the netilah and blesses on bread; end of his words—it implies he does not deal with this case, for if so he would not need to complete netilah if he poured a revi'it at once; also the Tur's language does not imply so, for he wrote, and he seems to rule that after he poured once on his hands and rubs he blesses Asher Yatzar, and afterward at the time of drying on bread, etc.; from saying once it implies there is another; rather it should have said: and it appears to rule that he should pour a revi'it at once on his hand and say Asher Yatzar, etc. And further, if it deals with one who pours a revi'it at once, he does not need drying, as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 158, seif 13—except that above I wrote Rashal disagrees on this; but certainly it does not deal with one who pours a revi'it at once, and our difficulty returns to its place. And one could say: why did the Tur need to write here and he rubs—what does this rubbing want here? Rather, perforce we cannot say he says Asher Yatzar and on bread immediately one after the other, for then it would not be like R' Yosef bar Yakar whom the Tur brings first, who is of the first division that Shulchan Aruch mentions here, who does not hold one netilah counts for both; and we wish to fulfill all views—therefore we need a new act before he blesses on bread; therefore Rosh said he pours once—that is, if his hands are already clean from filth he does not need afterward but two pourings: then he pours once and blesses Asher Yatzar, and afterward does a new act with the second pouring and blesses on bread; and if his hands are not clean and he requires three times, then he pours first twice, and the Tur wrote the first division that twice suffices, and wrote that he rubs after the pouring to teach that then he fulfills netilah—for since he is permitted to rub them one against the other, automatically the second pouring is a new act for the need of netilah; and since this ruling is necessary when he does not pour a revi'it at once, as written, and sometimes he pours a revi'it once and there will be interruption—therefore it is good to act like the first division as above; correct.
