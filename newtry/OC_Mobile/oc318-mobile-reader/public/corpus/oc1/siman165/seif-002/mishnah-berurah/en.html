(4) The greatest person washes, etc. This is because it accords him honor.
(5) And the Rosh was accustomed, etc. He held that the Talmudic period was different, because each diner had his own table and ate immediately after washing. Nowadays, however, there is one table for everyone, and they begin eating together, meaning that after all have washed, the greatest person recites the blessing and discharges everyone through his blessing. It is therefore preferable for him to wash last, so that he not come to make an interruption or speak in the meantime. The Mechaber in siman 166 agrees that it is proper to be careful not to interrupt or speak, as explained there in Mishnah Berurah. The universal practice is to be stringent about this, even regarding a lengthy delay, following Rama there. Therefore, at large meals with many diners, if everyone waits until the greatest person washes, they will wait a very long time and may also come to speak in the meantime. It is therefore proper for the greatest person to wash first, as in the Talmudic period, and not discharge them with HaMotzi. Rather, he should recite the blessing for himself, and each diner should wash and recite the blessing for himself, so that no one needs to wait at all.
(6) Not to interrupt. Meaning, by a lengthy delay, as explained below in siman 166.
(7) And not to speak. Meaning, even brief conversation.
