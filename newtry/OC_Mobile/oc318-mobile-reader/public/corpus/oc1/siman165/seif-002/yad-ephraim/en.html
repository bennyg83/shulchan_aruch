There: when pouring a revi'it on each hand—meaning: per my view one could say that truly, where he pours a revi'it, there is interruption; if so, our difficulty returns to its place—meaning: how does he write the second division plainly, for with a revi'it there is concern of interruption, and most times people are accustomed to take a revi'it, etc., as above; and on this Taz wrote, and it appears one must be precise: why did the Tur write and he rubs, etc.—rather, perforce he holds Asher Yatzar and on bread are adjacent without the reason of interruption, only because netilah does not count for both; therefore a new act is required, etc. And the Tur, who wrote the first division that twice suffices—one could say the Tur deals with clean hands; nevertheless it deals with one who takes less than a revi'it the first time and takes twice. What the Tur wrote, that he takes once and rubs—does not mean he needs no more than this netilah, but means that in that he rubs one against the other he shows he fulfilled netilah for relieving himself; and afterward, when he pours the second time, it is a new act for netilah. What the Tur wrote, and afterward at the time of drying, etc.—means: after the second pouring he blesses on bread at the time of drying, as above, end of siman 158, that he blesses on bread at the time of drying. Accordingly we derive: whether he pours a revi'it or not, he always must pour twice—on the first he blesses Asher Yatzar, and on the second he blesses on bread; if he pours a revi'it, he must do so because of interruption, and if he does not pour a revi'it, he must also do so to fulfill R' Yosef bar Yakar, who holds one netilah does not count for both. With this the Tur ruled: although he does not need another netilah entirely and fulfills with that same netilah, nevertheless he blesses Asher Yatzar after the first pouring—for the rubbing shows it is a complete netilah (and although for relieving himself rubbing does not matter, for one may always rub, nevertheless this is only a mere indication that he already fulfilled netilat yadayim); and after the second pouring, which is a new act, he blesses on bread at the time of drying, as the law of netilot in general. This is what Taz wrote: since this ruling is necessary when he does not pour a revi'it at once—meaning, it is compelled from the Tur that even in such a case he must pour twice to fulfill R' Yosef bar Yakar; and sometimes he pours a revi'it and there is interruption—therefore it is good always to act like the first division. And it appears the wording in Taz was switched, and it should read: and our difficulty returns to its place; but certainly it does not deal with one who pours a revi'it at once—meaning, if it dealt with one who pours a revi'it at once, he would not need drying per Shulchan Aruch—except that above I wrote Rashal disagrees; but nevertheless Shulchan Aruch's view is not so, and also per Rashal drying is not required except for repulsiveness; nevertheless it does not purify the hands—if so, our difficulty returns, for immediately when he poured a revi'it the time for berachah on bread has arrived, for he does not need second water or drying; rather, perforce the Tur does not deal with pouring a revi'it. And again Taz wrote, and it should be that one could say why he wrote, etc.—and this is an abbreviation, and one could say there is proof why, etc.—meaning: there is also proof why the Tur wrote and he rubs, etc.—rather, perforce he means he truly must pour twice to fulfill R' Yosef bar Yakar; for this comes the rubbing, and rubbing shows the first pouring is for relieving himself, for he is permitted to rub them one against the other, whereas for eating rubbing is forbidden—and Taz does not now hold as he wrote initially that he washed on both as one who may rub; rather he holds he washed each by itself, and then rubbing shows he fulfilled netilah for relieving himself, and the second pouring is for netilah. And one could say: if so, behold the hands became impure through rubbing, and he must dry them first and then wash a second time for eating. One could say Taz holds that perforce they did not become impure through rubbing except if he washed for eating—then easily they accept impurity, for this was the enactment of the Sages that water on the hand that was washed becomes impure; unlike if he washed his hands from relieving himself, there is no impure water on them at all, as above, end of siman 158, regarding one who did not wash and touched water—the water did not become impure; and the same—the water on the hands did not become impure even though he rubbed; if so, the second pouring properly counts for netilah for eating. And although per this view there is no second water on them but only first water, nevertheless it suffices after he dries, as Mahari Weil wrote in his difficulty; if so, in any case it is proven from this that specifically with less than a revi'it, where he needs a second pouring and rubbing would have been forbidden if the first pouring were for eating—for this the Tur ruled to pour, etc.; and on this Taz wrote, and since this ruling is necessary when he does not pour a revi'it—meaning, it is proven that in this case the law deals. Or it is possible there is a printing error and it should read: he ruled—referring to the Tur's ruling; and it is possible the word dealing is missing, and it should read: the necessity is that it deals when he does not pour, etc.; and sometimes he pours a revi'it and there is interruption—therefore act like the first division, that he takes twice: once first, saying Asher Yatzar, and afterward takes twice for eating, with less than a revi'it, or once if taking a revi'it. And I saw in Mishbetzot Zahav that he explained what Taz wrote, and our difficulty returns to its place—referring to Rashal's view, with which Taz agreed that with a revi'it drying is also required; if so, what difference does it make whether second water is needed, for there is no interruption, or whether drying is needed—and on this Taz wrote that the reason is R' Yosef bar Yakar, therefore one must do a new act with the second pouring; unlike with a revi'it, where this does not apply. What Taz concluded, because of interruption, is not precise; one who examines Taz will see there is no place to explain thus; also his difficulty, explaining second water and drying—the main point is missing from the book, for Taz did not mention it at all. Also per my view there is no difficulty, as above: second water is different, for they are actual purification of hands, unlike drying.
