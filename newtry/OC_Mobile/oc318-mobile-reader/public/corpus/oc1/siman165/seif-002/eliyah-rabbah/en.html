[1] [Levush] And between HaMotzi, etc. Olas Tamid challenges Levush: in any event, he should first recite HaMotzi and afterward Asher Yatzar during the meal. See there. I say that Beit Yosef also implies as Levush does, and Bach and Nachalas Tzvi write likewise. It therefore appears to me that the matter can be resolved well. Certainly, Asher Yatzar should come first, because its obligation devolved upon him first, immediately when he relieved himself; only afterward did the obligation of HaMotzi for the meal devolve upon him. The reason Levush did not give this explanation regarding the blessing over washing is that Al Netilas Yadayim is not an interruption at all, since it pertains to the washing. Furthermore, many poskim hold above, siman 7, that whenever one relieves himself he also recites Al Netilas Yadayim, and this is also implied by Bach. See below. The explanation of Olas Tamid is not understood from the words of Beit Yosef. See there and examine carefully.
[2] [Levush] And some do, etc. Mateh Moshe concurs, and writes that Mahari Pollak practiced accordingly. However, Shelah, folio 81, Maharshal, responsum 40, and Taz agree with the first view, that one should wash twice. Responsa Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 188, and Maharil likewise rule this way. Shelah concludes that one should be careful to touch a concealed part of his body between the two washings, thereby obligating himself in a new washing. Otherwise, if his hands remain clean and he does not divert his attention from them, how can he afterward recite Al Netilas Yadayim? Magen Avraham writes that during the first washing he does not wash properly. According to what I wrote above, siman 158, seif katan 18, none of this caution is necessary, since he did not stipulate anything during the first washing. This is why none of the poskim warned about it, and this is likewise implied by Responsa Binyamin Ze'ev there. It further appears to me that here the matter is worse, because during the first washing he specifically intends that it not be for eating, unlike the earlier case, where he did not expressly intend that it not be for eating. Bach writes that one who is stringent to perform two washings, or to perform one washing, should follow that practice consistently and not sometimes do one and sometimes the other. Shelah writes that every person should examine himself and relieve himself before eating, and Sefer Seder HaYom likewise warns about this.
