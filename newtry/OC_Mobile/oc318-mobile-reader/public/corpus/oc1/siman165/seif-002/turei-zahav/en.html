And Rosh was accustomed, etc. And although in the baraita we say that the elder washes first—it appears he holds it is different in the time of the Talmud, when each one had a table by himself and the elder would eat immediately, as the gemara brings, which Beit Yosef brings; unlike now, where there is no honor for the elder to wash first, for he would need to sit and guard his hands, as Rav Sheshet questioned the Exilarch in the gemara. And Beit Yosef questioned this reasoning truly against the poskim who wrote the law of the Talmud. And Rashal elaborated in a responsum to disagree on this, and wrote that it is arrogance to practice so; rather, on the contrary, he should wash first and be careful not to speak, and others will learn from him—and his words there are not fit to reject Rosh's words. And further Rashal wrote there that words of Torah are not interruption—this too is not reasonable, for one should be concerned lest he forget the sugya and come to diversion of attention, as we find regarding bedikat chametz that one should not study at that hour; and similarly Maharshal.
