And he rubs—Taz, Magen Avraham (3), Derishah, and siman 162, seifim 5 and 7. The content of his intent: one could say two aspects in the Tur: (1) his ruling that even if he takes a revi'it on the first time, where there are no impure waters at all, nevertheless, since drying is required and anyone who eats without drying is like one who eats impure bread, the essence of netilah begins from drying and there is no interruption, and he blesses Asher Yatzar, and at the beginning of drying on bread; but for Mahari Weil it is clear specifically if he pours a revi'it in two times—then on bread begins from the second pouring, and there is no interruption (and he blesses on bread) before drying—the same before the second pouring is the essence, and there is no interruption; and similarly Levush in his gloss, except that the practice is before drying (as in siman 152, seif 1). If so, it is clear: if he poured a revi'it on each, there is an interruption, for the matter does not depend on drying at all; and further, from Rosh and Tur it is also proven that drying is not the matter, and the essence is the second pouring—from what Rosh wrote in responsa: after he poured once or twice (that is, on clean hands once, and on unclean hands twice, for three times are required as in siman 162, seif 2) and completes the netilah—it implies that he still requires a second pouring to purify the waters, etc.; and in the Tur: after he poured once on clean hands, and before the second pouring—then if he wishes he may bless on bread there, or before drying; in any case it was permitted that there not be an interruption, for the second pouring begins the start of on bread, etc.; for otherwise it should have said: after he poured on his hands, before drying, etc. And now there is a strong difficulty: why is the second pouring not an interruption? For in rubbing on a wall and the like, if he poured on each hand half a revi'it (as in siman 162 in Magen Avraham, note 16, and Taz 7), and the second water is only to purify the first so they not become impure if he touches afterward, as in siman 162, seif 8—they said that is the essence of netilah; the same applies to drying, and all the more so it is the essence of netilah; and even if he poured a revi'it on each hand—for those who require drying, and such is the main view as Taz wrote in 158, 13, like Rashal—if so, it is difficult (and this is what returns our difficulty to its place). And therefore one must answer that R' Yosef bar Yakar holds one netilah does not count for both even without the reason of interruption (see note (1), not as Derishah); and therefore specifically when doing a new act with the second pouring is his ruling proper, so that all views are satisfied; if so, when pouring a revi'it at once he does not fulfill R' Yosef bar Yakar's view, and presumably one pours a revi'it on each hand to remove the concerns, as in siman 162, seif 1; certainly one should take two netilot. What he wrote about pouring a revi'it on each hand being an interruption is as Mahari Weil wrote; nevertheless, the main point is to fulfill R' Yosef bar Yakar's view. And in siman 162, 7, it contradicts what is written here in the name of Mahari Weil that rubbing purifies and second water is not needed if he poured half a revi'it on each hand.
