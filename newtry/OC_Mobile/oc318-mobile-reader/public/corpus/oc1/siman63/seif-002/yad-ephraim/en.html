(In Magen Avraham, note 1) It appears he meant that seemingly from Rama it implies the exertion is because he lies prone. Therefore he said that in students of R. Yona it is explained the exertion is dressing. And what he wrote "and so it implies in the Gemara prone," etc. — that is, in the Talmud there it says one is called Keriat Shema "because inclined forbidden" — that is, he inclines on his side, and on this the students of R. Yona challenged from that which this one turns his face and reads; and they answered that there there is exertion to dress his garments; and Magen Avraham holds specifically exertion of dressing, but exertion of standing alone no — for this prone implies lying prone, that is, he is cast on his side, and nevertheless forbidden; but Beit Yosef holds in Rambam's method there is a distinction between lying actually on his side which is permitted, and this one who turns his face and reads — and we say "is called because inclined forbidden" — that is, he lies on his back and inclines a little on his side; and according to him there is no distinction whether he stripped his garments or not.
