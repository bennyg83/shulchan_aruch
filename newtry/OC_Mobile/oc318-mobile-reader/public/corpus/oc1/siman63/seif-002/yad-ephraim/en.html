In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1: It appears that he means as follows. At first glance, Rama implies that the exertion is due to the fact that he is already lying down. Magen Avraham therefore says that Rabbeinu Yonah explains that the exertion is that of putting on clothing. Regarding his words, “And so it is implied in the Gemara: one who lies supine,” etc., he means that the Talmud there says that reciting Keriat Shema while tilted is forbidden; that is, when he is tilted onto his side. Rabbeinu Yonah challenged this from the case of one who turns his face and recites, and answered that there the exertion of putting on his clothing is involved. Magen Avraham understands that specifically the exertion of dressing is a consideration, but the exertion of standing alone is not, for the case of one lying supine implies that he is already lying supine (that is, resting on his side) and it is nevertheless forbidden. Beit Yosef, however, understands Rambam's position to distinguish between one who is actually lying on his side, which is permitted and is the case of one who turns his face and recites, and the statement that reciting while tilted is forbidden, which refers to one lying on his back and tilting himself slightly onto his side. According to this view, there is no distinction between whether he removed his clothing or not.
