Taz — Tosafot Berakhot 13b in the words "al" — Beit Hillel: from Torah one may read while walking, and it is preferable from the elite to stand in the first verse even for Beit Hillel. (And if he read while walking one sees he need not return and read, for he can have intention.) And likewise one who walks — standing in verse one; and all the more a sitter is better. And it seems even in the evening one who walks may sit, for it does not appear he acts like Beit Shammai — specifically standing, not sitting; unlike walking — and so from Perishah. And what he wrote "riding," etc. — not like Bach; and Rambam chapter 2 halakha 3 Keriat Shema walking on his feet to exclude wagon, etc. (and Pri Chadash therefore changed the author and wrote "was walking on the way" so as not to err). And it implies from Taz that if another leads his animal he need not stand; and in a wagon even the driver must stand; and in Beit Yaakov 5 in name of Beit Yaakov — in a wagon the driver need not stand, and a rider even if his fellow leads the animal must stand; and it seems to decide regarding a rider — walking is like Beit Yosef, and so Pri Chadash; and in a wagon when driving he stands in verse one. And what he wrote "the whole paragraph he may stand" — from the rush of his words it implies in the evening when standing, if he wants to sit to have intention it is fine; and in Magen Avraham we wrote that in the Gemara it is not proved so — R. Yona and Ra'avad, see there. And what he wrote "he may stand because he has more intention" — this is true and sound: for if because he already stood in one verse the observer will be puzzled by his delay on the way and think he acts like Beit Shammai — for this he wrote that he should say he has intention since he already stood in verse one and has more intention for the rest of the paragraph too. And what he wrote proof from R. Yochanan Berakhot 13b "the whole paragraph in standing" — he said there according to his reason who holds the whole paragraph requires intention of the heart; except Taz holds Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel in the Mishnah (folio 9b) disagree — Beit Shammai read Shema paragraph in standing only in the morning, and Beit Hillel while walking even rabbinically (for it does not sound to him that Beit Hillel speak from Torah but even rabbinically, and read the paragraph while walking except verse one). If so, how did R. Yochanan rule like Beit Shammai that standing not walking — and whoever sees thinks he fixes the law like Beit Shammai not Beit Hillel (however R. Yochanan regarding sitting is fine, only in walking and standing it appears like Beit Shammai), and it is forbidden to do so; except whatever is because of intention — the same for us even though we do not require intention for the whole paragraph — whatever he does for intention there is no concern. And one may emend his words a bit, and still requires investigation.
