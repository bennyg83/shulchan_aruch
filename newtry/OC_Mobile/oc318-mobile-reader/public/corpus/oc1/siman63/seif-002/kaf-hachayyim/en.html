5) [Se'if 2] One who wishes to be stringent, etc. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 7, siman 41, that one who publicly acts stringently regarding a permitted matter when there is concern for ostentation is placed under a ban. If he is known to act for the sake of Heaven, he is not banned. If he acts stringently before his rav while his rav is lenient, he is banned even if he acts for the sake of Heaven. Even where the permission is not entirely obvious, he should not be stringent against his rav's ruling unless he has proof refuting it. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Soles Belulah, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2. See Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, and Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 2. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, cites Rabbi Menachem of Beis Meir, brought in Shitah Mekubetzes, Bava Kamma 81b: when a matter's permission is obvious, it is improper to be stringent in the presence of one greater in wisdom while that scholar follows the lenient ruling. If he does so, the scholar may place him under a ban. If the stringent person is recognized for piety, however, he may act stringently. See Responsa Maharadakh, end of responsum 26, and Ginnas Veradim, Yoreh Deah, rule 3, end of siman 6.
6) There: One who wishes to be stringent by standing when seated and reciting it standing is called a transgressor. Tur concludes in the name of Rav Amram that one should rebuke him, because the mitzvah is recited while seated. Beis Yosef explains this to mean that it may be recited even while seated. Prishah, paragraph 6, however, writes that it specifically should be recited while seated. Rema in Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, also appears to hold this. He comments upon Beis Yosef that Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 2, implies that sitting is better than standing, apparently disagreeing with Maran and requiring sitting. Pri Chadash, paragraph 2, similarly writes that Tur's statement in the name of Rav Amram means that the principal mitzvah is seated, so that one's mind will be more settled, like the conclusion at the end of the chapter Sheloshah She'achlu that in all those cases the halachah is to sit and recite the blessing. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, cites him and approves. This is also implied by Bereishis Rabbah, chapter 48, on "He was sitting at the entrance of the tent": Rabbi Berechyah in the name of Rabbi Levi says the word is written "sit." Avraham wished to stand, but the Holy One, blessed be He, told him, "Sit." So too your descendants will sit: when Israel enters synagogues and study halls and recites Krias Shema, they sit in My honor, while I stand over them, as it is stated, "God stands in the congregation of the Almighty." The same appears in Bamidbar Rabbah, Parashas Naso, chapter 11, and Midrash Chazis on "My Beloved resembles a gazelle." Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 2 concerning the repetition of the Amidah, folio 38d, and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, end of chapter 29, likewise imply that since Krias Shema corresponds to the world of Beriah it must be recited while seated. Although Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 6 concerning Krias Shema, folio 22c, writes that we are not obligated to recite it, etc., implying that standing would not matter, according to the mystical teaching sitting remains preferable, as implied by the cited discourse 2 and Pri Etz Chaim. See Ateres Zekenim, paragraph 2, and Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Va'eira, paragraph 16.
7) There: And recite it standing, etc. Sefer HaKanah, cited in the new Tzafnas Pane'ach, says that Krias Shema must be recited standing in Eretz Yisrael and seated outside Eretz Yisrael. This distinction appears not to be accepted by the poskim or kabbalists. Whether in Eretz Yisrael or outside it, all are equal: "the righteous sit." Kaf HaChaim by Rabbi Chaim Palagi, siman 14, paragraph 32. The same follows from Shaar HaKavanos and Pri Etz Chaim cited in the preceding paragraph: Krias Shema corresponds to the world of Beriah and must therefore be recited seated, with no distinction between Eretz Yisrael and elsewhere. Consider this carefully.
8) There: He is called a transgressor. This implies that he nevertheless fulfilled his obligation. Even according to Rav Yosef, who says that one who acted like Beis Shammai accomplished nothing, this means that he did not fulfill the mitzvah properly. The Gemara compares it to one whose head and most of his body were in a sukkah while his table was inside the house; after the fact he fulfilled the obligation, as Ran writes at the end of Arvei Pesachim regarding the Mishnah's statement that one who did not mention these three matters on Pesach did not fulfill his obligation. Ramban writes similarly in Milchamos at the beginning of Berachos, as does Tur at the end of siman 66. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1. See Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, for his discussion. Pischei HaDvir, paragraph 2, agrees with Erekh HaShulchan, and Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 2, appears to agree as well.
9) There: He is called a transgressor. If he stands in order to dispel sleep, it is permitted. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Va'eira, paragraph 15.
