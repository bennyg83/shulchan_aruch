(Note 2) One who wants, etc. — and if he is stringent before his rabbi — that is to say, in a matter whose permission has spread. And even if it has not spread so much to permit, but his rabbi is lenient in the matter — even though if the Talmud is stringent in such a case they do not put him under nidui, nevertheless it is not proper; and it implies there even not before his rabbi, and a simple matter to permit.
