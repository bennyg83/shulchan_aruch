One who wishes to be stringent. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 7, siman 41, writes: One who acts stringently in public regarding something permitted, where there is concern for ostentation, is placed under a ban. If it is known that he acts for the sake of Heaven, he is not placed under a ban. If he acts stringently in the presence of his rav while his rav is lenient, he is placed under a ban even if he acts for the sake of Heaven. Even where the permissive ruling is not entirely clear, he should not act stringently against his rav unless he has a proof refuting his words.
He is called a transgressor. What advantage is there in standing, since according to Beis Hillel the verse 'when you rise' does not refer to this at all, but rather to the time when people rise? By contrast, regarding Bircas HaMazon, Beis Hillel agrees that it is preferable for one to return to his place to recite the blessing, except that the Sages did not burden him to do so. See siman 184. This is from Tosafos Yom Tov to the first chapter of Shabbos in the name of the Rosh.
