(5) "One who wishes," etc. — for the halachah follows Beit Hillel, who hold that what is written "when you rise" does not mean standing as Beit Shammai hold, but rather at the time when people ordinarily rise from their beds; and it is forbidden for him to be stringent and act like Beit Shammai. And even if his intent in standing is not to act like Beit Shammai but only to sharpen his kavvanah and the like, even so it is forbidden, both by day and by night; and nevertheless in this regard he need not go back and recite again b'dieved [Peri Megadim — see there; and see in the book Matteh Yehudah].

(6) "To be stringent" — he wrote in Yeshuas Shlomo, chapter 7 of Bava Kamma, siman 41: One who is stringent before the public in a matter that is permitted, and there is reason to fear yehara, they place him under cherem. This is when that matter's permissibility has spread throughout all Israel [Peri Megadim]. And if it is known that he does it leshem shamayim, they do not place him under cherem. And if he is stringent before his rabbi while his rabbi is lenient, they place him under cherem even if he does it leshem shamayim. And even if it is not so widespread to permit, he should not be stringent against his rabbi's words unless he has proof to contradict his words — so he wrote.

(7) "To stand" — specifically in this case it is forbidden because it appears to everyone that he does so out of stringency; but if he is standing he may sit, for then it does not appear that he does so out of stringency but only because he needs to sit. And this applies specifically at Shacharit; but at Ma'ariv he should not lean and sit while standing, for it would appear he acts like Beit Shammai.

(8) "Called a rebel" — as we say in the chapter Keirah: one who transgresses against his rabbi's words, they may call him a rebel. Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Maharshal that on Yom Kippur everyone agrees it is permitted to stand, for he is not doing so out of yehara but rather his mitzvah is thus — meaning, to resemble then the angels who are called "standing"; and HaPeri Megadim forbids even this.
