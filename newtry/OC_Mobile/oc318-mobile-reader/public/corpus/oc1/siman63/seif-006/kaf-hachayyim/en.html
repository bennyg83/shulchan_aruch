6) There, "One who wishes to be stringent by standing when he had been sitting and reciting it standing is called a transgressor." Tur concludes in the name of Rav Amram that he should be rebuked, because its mitzvah is while seated. Beit Yosef explains that this means only that it may be recited even while seated. Perishah, letter 6, however, writes that it specifically should be recited seated. Rama in Darkei Moshe, letter 1, appears to agree, since he cites Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 2, that it is preferable to recite it seated rather than standing. Pri Chadash, letter 2, likewise explains Tur in the name of Rav Amram that the principal mitzvah is seated so that one's mind is especially settled, as we say at the end of Perek Sheloshah SheAchlu that in all those cases the halacha is to sit and recite the blessing. Maamar Mordechai, letter 2, cites him and writes that his words are correct. The same appears from Bereishit Rabbah, chapter 48, on "He was sitting at the entrance of the tent." R' Berechyah in the name of R' Levi says that "sit" is written. Avraham wished to stand, but Hashem told him, "Sit." So too your descendants sit when Israel enters synagogues and study halls and recites Kriat Shema; they sit in My honor while I stand over them, as it is written, "God stands in the congregation of God." The same is found in Bamidbar Rabbah, Parashat Naso, chapter 11, and Midrash Chazita on "My Beloved resembles a gazelle." Yafeh LaLev, letter 2. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 2 concerning the repetition of the Amidah, and Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Kriat Shema, end of chapter 29, likewise imply that because Kriat Shema corresponds to Beriah it should be recited seated. Although another passage in Shaar HaKavanos might imply that standing would not invalidate the recitation, according to the mystical structure sitting remains preferable. See Ateret Zekenim, letter 2, and Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Vaera, letter 16.
