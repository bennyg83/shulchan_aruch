(6) There — one who wants to be stringent to stand when he sits and read it standing is called a transgressor in Tur, at the end in the name of Rav Amram, and one must rebuke him that the mitzvah is while seated; and Beit Yosef wrote that it appears to him that this refers to the mitzvah even while seated — see there. But Perishah, letter vav, wrote that he speaks of the mitzvah while seated specifically — see there. And so is the opinion of our teacher in Darchei Moshe, letter aleph, who wrote on the words of Beit Yosef above: and Hagahot Maimoniot, chapter 2, implies that sitting is better than standing — until here; it implies that he comes to disagree with our teacher and to say that the mitzvah is specifically while seated. And so Peri Chadash, letter bet, [wrote] what Tur wrote in the name of Rav Amram — the main mitzvah is while seated so that one's mind be more settled upon him, as we say in the chapter "Three who ate" — the halakhah in all of them: seated and he blesses — until here. And they brought it from Eliyah Rabbah, letter bet, and wrote that his words are correct — see there. And so [appears] from what is written in the Midrash, Bereishit Rabbah, parashah 48: and he was sitting at the tent door — R. Berekhiah, in the name of R. Levi, said: "Sit" is written regarding the Shekhinah; he wished to stand. The Holy One, blessed be He, said to him: Sit, etc. — thus your children sit when Israel enter the synagogue and house of study and read Keriat Shema, and they sit for My honor, and I, as it is said: "God stands in the congregation of God." And so it is in Midrash Rabbah, book of Numbers, parashah 11, and in Midrash Chazit on the verse "my beloved is like a gazelle" — see there. Yefe Lalev, letter bet; and so [appears] from the words of Shaar HaKavanot, derush 2, on the return of standing according to Levush and Pele Yoetz, end of the chapter on Keriat Shema, folio 29 — that the intention of Keriat Shema is in the aspect of the world of Beriah, therefore it must be while seated — see there. And even though in Shaar HaKavanot, derush 6, on Keriat Shema, folio 23, he wrote that we are not obligated to say it, etc. — it implies that if one wishes to say it standing it does not matter; nevertheless per the secret of the matter, while seated is preferable, as appears in derush 2 on the return of standing and Pele Yoetz as mentioned; and see Taz, letter bet, Ben Ish Chai, parashat Vaera, letter tet-zayin.
