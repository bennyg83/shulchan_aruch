He should gesture. Yoma 19 somewhat implies that it is prohibited to gesture even for the purpose of a mitzvah. Magen Avraham.
First. Rambam did not write that the rule applies 'in the first paragraph,' because there is a prohibition in the second paragraph as well; the conduct is merely considered disgraceful only in the first paragraph. So writes Maharam; Magen Avraham. It appears to me that for the purpose of a mitzvah it is permitted to gesture in the second paragraph even according to Rambam. Otherwise, it is difficult to understand how Rav could have gestured with his hand to Ben Kabutal during the second paragraph, as the conclusion in Yoma 19 states, given that there is nevertheless a prohibition. Rather, it must be that it is permitted for a matter of mitzvah. This is readily understood. Accordingly, the question raised by the author of Yad Aharon against Rambam presents no difficulty; examine this carefully.
