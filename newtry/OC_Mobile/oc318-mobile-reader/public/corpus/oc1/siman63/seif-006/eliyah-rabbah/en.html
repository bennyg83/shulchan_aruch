(6) In the first verse, etc. And Bach disagrees on this and ruled in Knesset HaGedolah and Raavad and Rosh that until "upon your heart" one must stand, as we say. And so it appears to me as primary, for I saw that Rashba wrote thus in the chapter "He was reading" in the name of R. Yehudai Gaon, and so he ruled there; and so R. Yerucham and Avudraham, and so it appears to me. Bach wrote that it is forbidden to stand more [than required], lest it appear that one acts like Beit Shammai — "in the morning they stand," until here. But Taz and Magen Avraham wrote that it is permitted to stand, and so it appears to me from the language of Levush, who wrote "and from there onward one may read while walking" — it implies that if he wishes he may stand. And the reason appears to me that we say he does so in order to have intention, and so it is explained from the words of R. Yerucham, folio 23, who wrote thus: and Rosh wrote that according to this he should not stand until he reads all of Keriat Shema, for his mind is not settled upon him if he delays his way; and according to this everything depends on the way, place, and walking — until here. Behold explicitly that if his mind is settled upon him he may stand, and there is no prohibition at all in the matter; and so it appears to me in the Midrash at the beginning of Parashat Lekh Lekha.
