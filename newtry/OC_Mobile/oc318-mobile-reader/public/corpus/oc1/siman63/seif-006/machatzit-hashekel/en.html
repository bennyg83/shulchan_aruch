(Seif katan 6) And he returns, etc. That he reads it quietly. See the Taz, who wrote another remedy: one can say that he waits some time and afterward returns and reads, etc.; see Magen Avraham, siman 61, seif katan 7, who wrote in the name of the Shelah that those who say Shema in Selichos every time they say Vaya’avor are permitted, since they interrupt a great deal in between.
That he should read the whole parsha twice; see what is written in siman 61, seif katan 8.
And it implies that if he remembered, etc. Since he wrote in seif 8 that he should read the parsha twice, this implies that he had already read the whole parsha, and nevertheless he should again read the whole parsha. The reason is that if he would read only the first verse, it would be like reading out of order, for he first read from ‘And you shall love’ until the end of the parsha and afterward read Shema. Although he had also read Shema Yisrael at first, since he did not have intent when reading the verse Shema, that Krias Shema is considered as nonexistent, and he has read out of order. This is what Magen Avraham teaches, that this is called reading out of order.
And so it implies in siman 64. Meaning seif katan 1, in what he wrote in the name of the Bach; see there.
