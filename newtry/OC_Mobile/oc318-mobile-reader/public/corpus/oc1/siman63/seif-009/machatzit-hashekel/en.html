(Seif katan 9) One should not gesture, etc. ‘For the need of a mitzvah,’ etc. This is from what we learned there: on the night of Yom Kippur they would read before the Kohen Gadol matters that draw the heart, so that he would not come to sleep. It was taught: Rabbi Zecharyah ben Kevutal said, many times I read before him from Daniel. They said that Rabbi Chanan bar Rava taught Chiyya bar Rav before Rav: Rabbi Zecharyah ben Kefutal, with a peh, and Rav showed him with his hand, Kevutal, with a beis. The Gemara asks: let him say it to him explicitly, and why did he gesture with his hand? It answers that Rav was then reading Krias Shema and could not interrupt. It asks: if so, how could he gesture with his hand, since it is forbidden to gesture with his fingers and eyes, etc.? It answers that Rav was engaged in the second parsha of Krias Shema. What is the question? If it is permitted for a mitzvah matter, Rav gestured to him for a mitzvah matter, to save him from an error. Rather, certainly even for a mitzvah matter it is forbidden. However, this is not a complete proof, for one can say that there it was different, since it was not an actual mitzvah, for it was possible to wait until he finished Krias Shema and then tell him that he erred.
