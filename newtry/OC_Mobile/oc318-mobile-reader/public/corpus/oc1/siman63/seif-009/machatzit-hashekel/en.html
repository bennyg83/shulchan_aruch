(Note 9) One should not signal, etc. — for the sake of a mitzvah, etc. — this is from our Mishnah there: on the night of Yom Kippur they would read before the High Priest things that sustain the heart so that he not come to sleep. And we learned: R. Zechariah ben Kevutal says: I read before him four times in Daniel. And we say: R. Chanina bar Rava taught the baraita to Chiya bar Rav before Rav — R. Zechariah ben Kevutal (in the first chapter) and they showed Rav with his hand Kevutal (with a bet). And the Talmud challenged: let them say to him explicitly — why did he signal with his hand? They answered: Rav was then reading Keriat Shema and could not interrupt. They challenged: if so, how did he signal with his hand — behold it is forbidden to signal with one's fingers and eyes, etc. They answered: Rav was engaged in the second paragraph of Keriat Shema. And what is the difficulty — if for a matter of mitzvah it is permitted? Behold Rav signaled to him for a matter of mitzvah to save him from error — rather certainly even for a matter of mitzvah it is forbidden. However, there is no complete proof, for one may say it is different there, for it is not a true mitzvah — for it is possible to wait until he finishes Keriat Shema and then tell him that he erred.
