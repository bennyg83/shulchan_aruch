In the first verse. See seif katan 11, that the same applies to Baruch shem kevod malchuso le’olam va’ed. If he did not have intent in it as well, he must return and read it with intent.
He has not fulfilled. Even if before he began reading he intended to fulfill the positive commandment of Krias Shema through this reading, nevertheless if in the middle his heart turned to other matters, he did not accept the kingdom of Heaven upon himself with the agreement of the heart.
And he returns and reads. Quietly, so that it not appear as though he accepts two authorities, Heaven forbid. See above, siman 61, Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 22. Even if he already read all of the parsha of Shema and Vehayah im Shamoa, and afterward remembered that he did not read the first verse with intent, he must go back and read the entire parsha of Shema, for if he reads only the first verse, it is like reading out of order, through which one does not fulfill the obligation, as below in siman 64. If he remembered in the middle of Vehayah im Shamoa, he should finish until Vayomer, begin and read Shema until Vehayah im Shamoa, and then return to the parsha of Vayomer.
He agrees here. For although he does not intend to fulfill the mitzvah thereby, nevertheless he requires intent of the heart throughout the first verse in order to accept upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven.
