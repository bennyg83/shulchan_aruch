Magen Avraham, note 4: on siman 58, se'if 3. From siman 58 it is proven that even for us, who need not stand except for the first verse, nevertheless it is fitting that he stand at least until "upon your heart"; and if he goes on the way and cannot stand until "upon your heart," it is better that he read it while ascending on his roof at home, as Magen Avraham wrote, siman 24; and we see there is no prohibition when one stands more than required. And what he brought from siman 94, se'if 4, is proof for what Bach wrote — that a rider or one sitting in a wagon can have intention, for so it is there in siman 94 even regarding prayer; and even though some are stringent there in Avot, it is a general stringency for another reason, and specifically regarding prayer — see there.
