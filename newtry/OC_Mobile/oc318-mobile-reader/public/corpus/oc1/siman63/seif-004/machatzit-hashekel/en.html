(Note 4) In the verse, etc. — that it appears he acts like Beit Shammai, even though the prohibition is only when he sits and stood when he read — for in this it appears he acts like Beit Shammai; unlike when he stands before he began to read, that it is permitted to stand even during Keriat Shema — for in this it does not appear he acts like Beit Shammai. And here — behold, he already stood because of the first verse. Nevertheless, since he walks on the way and stands and reads, it appears he acts like Beit Shammai.

Or riding, etc. — Taz disagrees regarding a rider. But when sitting in a wagon he agrees — and this is specifically when others drive the wagon — see there.

If so, there is no prohibition, etc. — for it does not appear he is stringent like Beit Shammai but stringent like the opinion of those poskim.

Siman 58 — in Magen Avraham, note 4, that even in the author's view it suffices to stand in the first paragraph. Nevertheless, if he cannot stand on the way until "upon your heart," it is permitted for him to read Keriat Shema in his house from dawn so that he can read standing until "upon your heart" — behold it is explained, as Magen Avraham wrote, not like Bach.
