(Seif katan 4) In the verse, etc. For it appears that he acts like Beis Shammai. Although the prohibition is only when he is sitting and stands when he reads, for then it appears that he acts like Beis Shammai, whereas if he is standing before he begins reading it is permitted to stand during Krias Shema, since it does not appear that he acts like Beis Shammai, here he had already stood because of the first verse. Nevertheless, since he is walking on the road and stops and reads, it appears that he acts like Beis Shammai.
Or riding, etc. The Taz disagrees regarding riding. But when one sits in a wagon he agrees, and that is specifically when others are leading the wagon; see there.
If so, there is no prohibition, etc. For it does not appear that he is being stringent like Beis Shammai, but rather that he is being stringent like those poskim.
See siman 58 in Magen Avraham, seif katan 4, that even according to the Mechaber, for whom it suffices to stand for the first parsha. Nevertheless, if on the road he will not be able to stand until ‘upon your heart,’ he is permitted to read Krias Shema at home from the time alot hashachar has risen, so that he can read while standing until ‘upon your heart.’ This is explicit as Magen Avraham wrote, unlike the Bach.
