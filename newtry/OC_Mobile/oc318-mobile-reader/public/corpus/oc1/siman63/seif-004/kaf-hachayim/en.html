(4) There — and if he was corpulent, etc., or sick — inclines a little to his side and reads. R. Menachem wrote that one who is not corpulent or sick is forbidden to read except while settled like a person standing in awe and fear; and they brought Kesef Mishneh in chapter 2 of Keriat Shema, halakhah 2. Eliyah Rabbah, letter bet; and see Yad Eliyahu in Hagahot Tur who wondered at the words of Rambam above from the Gemara: the rabbis taught — in the army camp they stand and read, etc. — see there. And it appears that what Rambam wrote is for the optimal mitzvah, so that one can read in awe and fear properly and with more settled mind, as was written above, letter aleph, and below letter vav; and what he wrote "forbidden to read" is not forbidden by law but forbidden as optimal mitzvah, so that one's mind be more settled upon him and one can read in awe and fear properly. And see Knesset Yisrael and Yefe Lalev, letter aleph.
