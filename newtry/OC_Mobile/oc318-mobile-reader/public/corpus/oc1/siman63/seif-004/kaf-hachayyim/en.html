4) There: If he was corpulent, etc., or ill, he tilts slightly onto his side and recites. Rabbeinu Manoach writes that one who is neither corpulent nor ill may recite only while seated, like a person standing in awe and fear. Kesef Mishneh, Hilchos Krias Shema, chapter 2, halachah 2, cites him; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. See Yad Aharon, Hagahos on the Tur, who questions Rabbeinu Manoach from the baraisa in which Beis Hillel says one may stand and recite, etc. It appears that Rabbeinu Manoach means this as the preferred fulfillment of the mitzvah, so that one can recite with proper awe, fear, and greater composure, as we wrote above in paragraph 1 and below in paragraph 6. His phrase "may recite only" does not express a strict prohibition, but the requirement of preferred practice so that one's mind will be more settled and he can recite with proper awe and fear. See Kisei Eliyahu and Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1.
