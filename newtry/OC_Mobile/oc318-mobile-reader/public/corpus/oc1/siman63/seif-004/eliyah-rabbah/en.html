(4) A transgressor, etc. I found a writing, and this is its language: it implies specifically that if he sits and wants to stand it is forbidden because it appears to everyone that he does so out of stringency; but if he stands he may sit, for then it does not appear that he does so out of stringency but because he needs to sit — until here. And it appears that this applies also to Keriat Shema of the evening. But Bach wrote: in the evening, if he was erect it is forbidden for him to incline — until here is his language, and it needs examination; it is strained to distinguish between sitting and inclining. Rashal wrote in his glosses on Tur that on Yom Kippur everyone agrees it is permitted to stand, for he does not do so out of yohara but because the mitzvah is thus — and see Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 4 of Bava Kamma, siman 41: one who is stringent in a matter that is permitted — they put him under nidui if it is not known that he does so for the sake of Heaven.
