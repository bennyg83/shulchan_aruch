And he returns and reads, etc. Although we silence one who says ‘Shema, Shema,’ see above, siman 61, seif katan 13. The reason it did not also state that he must return and bless is, it seems to me, because he wrote below at the beginning of siman 101 that nowadays we do not repeat because of lack of intent. According to this, with respect to the first verse of Krias Shema, even nowadays we make him repeat. The reason, it seems to me, is to distinguish between Krias Shema, which is according to the Torah, and prayer, which is D’Rabbanan. Now it is understandable why the Shulchan Aruch and Levush did not write here the rule that nowadays we do not repeat; this is easy to understand. I likewise found this in Maadanei Melech, page 18. In Magen Avraham, siman 101, he wrote a strained reason, that one verse suffices, but the words of Maadanei Melech escaped him. It appears that even if he already read the whole parsha of Shema and Vehayah im Shamoa, and afterward remembered that he did not read with intent, he must return and read the entire parsha, for otherwise it is like reading out of order, through which one does not fulfill the obligation, as below in siman 64. See above, siman 51, seif 8.
He agrees here, etc. For although he does not intend to fulfill the obligation, he still needs intent of the heart to accept the kingdom of Heaven.
