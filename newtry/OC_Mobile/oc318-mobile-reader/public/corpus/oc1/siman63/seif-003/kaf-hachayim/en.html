(3) There — but he reads while lying on his side. Gloss: since he was already lying and there is exertion to stand. Explanation: the exertion is to return and dress his garments, as Beit Yosef wrote in the second explanation of R. Yona. Levush in the chapter "He was reading," letter tet-vav. Magen Avraham, note 1. It implies that even one lying in a shirt must stand; and so [appears] in the Gemara — prone, one does not read Keriat Shema — it implies that even if he already lies he should not read until he stands. However the opinion of Rabbeinu [Yosef Caro] in Beit Yosef is specifically when cast on his back and inclining a little to his side; but when lying actually on his side it is permitted lechat-chilah to read — therefore he omitted the opinion of the students of Rabbeinu Yona. Magen Avraham there. And so Chokhmat Shlomo, letter aleph. Eshel Torah, letter bet. And so Levush there — Shulchan Arukh, who copied the language of Rambam, formulated anonymously like his explanation in Beit Yosef in the first explanation; but Rama emended on it: since he was already lying, etc. — see there. And so Taz, note 1, wrote on the words of Rama above: it is astounding, for Rambam is the master of this law that Shulchan Arukh mentioned — he holds that even lechat-chilah it is permitted when he inclines completely, as Beit Yosef wrote, that Rambam does not hold that specifically where there is exertion — until here. And so Eshel Torah, letter gimel, challenged the words of our teacher above — see there. And so Peri Chadash ruled, letter aleph, and wrote: and not like our teacher, who followed Tur [R. Yehuda] who does not exist. And so R. Zalman ruled, letter heh.
