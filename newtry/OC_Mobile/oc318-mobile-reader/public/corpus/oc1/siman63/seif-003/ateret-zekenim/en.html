If one was walking, etc., one must stand for the first verse; and from verse one onward one reads in the manner of one's walking — for if one reads standing it appears as if one is acting like Beit Shammai. And specifically when walking on one's feet; but if riding and sitting in a carriage, one may direct oneself properly and need not stand (my teacher in Bach). And it appears that the first verse mentioned here also includes Barukh shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed, for it is also within accepting the yoke of the kingdom of heaven, and it has all the law of the first verse for this matter (Maharam of Italy).
