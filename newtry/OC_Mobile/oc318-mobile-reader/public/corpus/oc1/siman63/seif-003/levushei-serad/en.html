There. And if it would enter your mind that Rabbi Yehuda holds like Beis Shammai, there is some textual error here. The intent is: how could he stand for the entire parsha? If so, it would appear that he acts like Beis Shammai. Rather, one must say that when walking there is no concern, for everyone understands that he is doing it for intent. So too according to us, even though we do not hold like Rabbi Yehuda and it suffices to stand for the first verse, nevertheless if he stands for the entire parsha, we have no objection, for it does not appear at all that he acts like Beis Shammai, but rather that he acts for intent.
