3) There: He may recite while lying on his side. Gloss: Since he was already lying down and standing would entail exertion. This means that it is burdensome to dress again, as Beis Yosef explains in his second interpretation of Rabbeinu Yonah. Lechem Chamudos, chapter Hayah Korei, paragraph 15; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. This implies that even one lying in his robe must stand. The Gemara's wording, "One lying supine may not recite Krias Shema," likewise implies that even if he is already lying down he may not recite until he stands. The Rav Beis Yosef, however, holds that the restriction applies specifically when one lies on his back and tilts slightly onto his side; when one lies fully on his side, he may recite initially. This is why he omits the view of Rabbeinu Yonah's students. Magen Avraham there; Soles Belulah, paragraph 1; Olas Tamid, paragraph 2. Lechem Chamudos there likewise writes that Shulchan Aruch, which copies Rambam, states the law without qualification according to Beis Yosef's first interpretation, whereas Rema adds the gloss "since he was already lying," etc. Taz, paragraph 1, comments on Rema: This is astonishing, because Rambam, the source of the ruling cited by Shulchan Aruch, permits it initially when one fully tilts onto his side. Beis Yosef writes that Rambam does not require exertion to be involved. Olas Tamid, paragraph 3, also challenges Rema. Pri Chadash, paragraph 1, likewise rules against Rema, who followed Rabbeinu Yonah's students, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 5, concurs.
