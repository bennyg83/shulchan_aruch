If he was walking. Although above in seif 1 it implies that according to the law it is permitted to read while walking, nevertheless the preferred mitzvah is to stand at the time of the first verse, for it is the essence of Krias Shema, since he is not so settled and cannot have intent while walking as he can while sitting. After the fact, if he read while walking, he need not go back and read. Peri Megadim.
He must stand. Meaning, he must remain in one place. He may read it either standing or sitting, even the evening Krias Shema. Only when he changes from standing to sitting is it forbidden for the evening Krias Shema, because it appears that he is acting like Beis Shammai; but this does not apply when changing from walking to sitting. One who is sitting in a wagon or ship need not stand, for he can have proper intent; see Shaarei Teshuvah and Peri Megadim. Regarding one riding on an animal, there are differing opinions among the later authorities, and it is proper to be stringent.
In the first verse. Baruch shem kevod malchuso le’olam va’ed is included in the first verse. Some are stringent until ‘upon your heart.’ From there onward it is permitted to read while walking or standing.
