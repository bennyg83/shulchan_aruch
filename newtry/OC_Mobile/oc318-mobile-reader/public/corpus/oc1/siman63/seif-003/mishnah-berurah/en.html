(9) "If he was walking" — although above in se'if 1 it implies that by law it is permitted to recite while walking, nevertheless it is a mitzvah from the best option to stand at the time of the first verse, for that is the essence of Keriat Shema, because one is not as settled and cannot have kavvanah while walking as when sitting; and b'dieved if he recited while walking he need not go back and recite [Peri Megadim].

(10) "He must stand" — meaning, he must pause in one place; and it is permitted to recite it whether standing or sitting, even for the Keriat Shema of Ma'ariv — for only if he changes from standing to sitting is it forbidden for the Keriat Shema of Ma'ariv, because it appears he acts like Beit Shammai; but not so from walking to sitting. One sitting in a wagon or ship need not stand, for he can have kavvanah well — see in responsa and in Peri Megadim. And one riding on an animal — there are differing views among the later authorities, and it is proper to be stringent.

(11) "At the first verse" — and "Baruch shem" is like "malchuso" included in the first verse; and some are stringent until "upon your heart." From there onward it is permitted to recite while walking or standing.
