If he was walking, etc., he must stand, etc. Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter Hayah Koreh write that it is preferable to stand when one had been walking, because he is not sufficiently settled and cannot concentrate while walking as well as when standing. Accordingly, since the end of the first chapter of Kiddushin states that riding is considered like walking, the same should apply here: he should halt the animal while reciting the first verse, for one can likewise say that because he is occupied with directing the animal he cannot concentrate properly. If, however, he is sitting in a wagon, then although siman 2 states that this too is considered like walking with respect to not sitting frivolously, here the principal reason is that he cannot concentrate, and one sitting in a wagon can concentrate properly. The Rambam writes: 'One who is walking on foot stands for the first verse, and recites the rest while walking.' My father-in-law, of blessed memory, inferred from the words 'on foot' that one riding an animal is not included in the category of walking. In my humble opinion, however, the Rambam intended to exclude only one traveling in a ship or wagon, but not one riding an animal, for the reason I have written. Further, my father-in-law inferred from the Rambam's conclusion, 'and he recites the rest while walking,' which appears superfluous, that the Rambam teaches that it is forbidden to stand throughout the entire paragraph, since he would appear to be acting in accordance with Beis Shammai. In my humble opinion this is not correct at all. The concern applies only when one was sitting and then stands, for standing provides no advantage in concentration, since one can concentrate better while seated. It must therefore be because of a literal interpretation of the verse 'when you rise,' and he thus acts like Beis Shammai. By contrast, one who was walking and then stops does so because he can concentrate better, and Beis Hillel agrees that he acts properly. Concentration is required only for the first verse, and no more is necessary. If, however, he wishes to ensure concentration afterward as well, he is praiseworthy. This is proven by the Gemara's statement that Rabbi Yochanan requires the entire paragraph to be recited while standing. Were Rabbi Yochanan following Beis Shammai, Beis Shammai say regarding the paragraph of Shema that in the morning one must stand. It must therefore be that the concern of acting like Beis Shammai applies only when one was sitting and then stands, as explained. The Rambam states that he recites the remainder while walking merely to prevent one from thinking that, since he was required to stand for the first verse, he must stand throughout the paragraph. He therefore teaches that this is unnecessary. This appears completely clear to me.
