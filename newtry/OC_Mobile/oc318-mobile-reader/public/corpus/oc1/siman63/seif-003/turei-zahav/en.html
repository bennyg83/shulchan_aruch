He was walking, etc., must stand, etc. Tosafot beginning of chapter "He was reading" — preferable from the elite to stand when he is walking because he is not settled so much and cannot have intention when walking as when standing — until here. And accordingly it seems since it is brought in end of Kiddushin that a rider is like one walking — automatically likewise for this matter that he stand with the animal at time of reading the first verse — for here too one may say since he is busy leading the animal he cannot have intention well; but if sitting in a wagon — even though so too in siman 2 that also here it is like walking regarding not sitting with lightness of head — nevertheless here the main reason is because he cannot have intention, and one sitting in a wagon can have intention well. And Rambam wrote in this: one who walks on his feet stands in the first verse and the rest reads while walking — until here. Meiri learned from that he specified "on his feet" — we learn that if riding on an animal he is not in this category as walking; and in my humble opinion he did not come to exclude except one walking in a ship or wagon but not riding on an animal — for the reason I wrote. Further Meiri learned from that Rambam concludes "and the rest reads while walking" — it is superfluous language except to teach that it is forbidden to stand for the whole paragraph lest it appear he acts like Beit Shammai; and it seems in my humble opinion the general rule is there is no concern except in sitting and standing — for then there is no superiority in standing because of intention, for in sitting there is more intention; rather necessarily because of the decree of the verse "and in your rising" — therefore he is like Beit Shammai; unlike walking and standing who does so because he has more intention in this — Beit Hillel agree he acted well, only he need not intention except in the first verse and no more need; but if he wants to do that he have intention even afterward — very good. And this is proven — for R. Yochanan holds in the Gemara the whole paragraph entirely in standing; and if we thought R. Yochanan holds like Beit Shammai — behold in paragraph Shema they say Beit Shammai in the morning they stand — rather necessarily there is no concern in what he does like Beit Shammai except in sitting and standing as he explained. And Rambam who wrote "the rest reads while walking" — careful lest you say since he was compelled to stand in the first verse he stand until the whole paragraph — he teaches that he need not; as seems clear to me.
