First. And the same is true for a rider as for one walking; but if one is sitting in a wagon or on a ship, one may direct oneself properly — Taz and Pri Chadash. However, Bach wrote that we said only when walking on one's feet; but riding on an animal or sitting in a carriage one may direct oneself properly and need not stand, for regarding directing the first verse alone we did not say that a rider is like one walking — see there. And Bach further learned from the Rambam's conclusion, and the rest read when walking, that it is forbidden to stand for the whole paragraph and it appears as if one acts like Beit Shammai. And Taz disagrees with him and wrote that there is concern only for one sitting and standing; but for one walking and standing, in this Beit Hillel also agrees that one acts properly. And so Magen Avraham — there is no prohibition for one who stands for the whole paragraph; and it is simple that in 'and you shall bind them' it is included in the first verse regarding the garment. And Bach wrote that one must stand until 'upon your heart,' and see above siman 58, se'if 3.
