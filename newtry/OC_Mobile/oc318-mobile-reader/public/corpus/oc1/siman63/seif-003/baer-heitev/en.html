First. The same applies to one riding, since riding is considered like walking. If, however, he is sitting in a wagon or ship, he can concentrate properly, as stated by the Taz and Peri Chadash. The Bach, however, writes that this was said only of one walking on foot. One riding an animal or sitting in a carriage can concentrate properly and need not stand, since with respect to concentrating on the first verse alone we do not say that riding is considered like walking. See there. The Bach further derives from the Rambam's concluding words, 'and he recites the rest while walking,' that one may not stand for the entire paragraph, since he would appear to be acting in accordance with Beis Shammai. The Taz disagrees and writes that the concern applies only when one was sitting and then stands. If one was walking and then stops, Beis Hillel also agrees that he acts properly. The Magen Avraham likewise writes that there is no prohibition for one who stands throughout the entire paragraph. It is obvious that 'Blessed is the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever' is included in the law of the first verse, as stated by the Levush. The Bach writes that one must stand until 'upon your heart.' See above, siman 58, seif 3.
