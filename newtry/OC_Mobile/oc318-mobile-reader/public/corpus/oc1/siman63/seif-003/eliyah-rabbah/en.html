In the first verse, etc. The Bach disagrees with this, and ruled like Knesses HaGedolah, the Raavad, and the Rosh, that one must stand until ‘upon your heart,’ as we said. This also appears to me primary, for I saw that the Rashba wrote so in the chapter Hayah Korei in the name of Rav Yitzchak Giat, and he himself ruled so there; so too Rabbeinu Yerucham and Abudraham, and so it seems to me. The Bach wrote that it is forbidden to stand longer, for it appears as though one acts like Beis Shammai, that in the morning they should stand. But the Taz and Magen Avraham wrote that it is permitted to stand, and so it appears to me from the wording of the Levush, who wrote that from there onward he may read while walking, implying that if he wishes he may stand. The reason seems to me that we say he does so in order to have intent. This is also explicit from Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote that according to this one should not stand until he reads all of Krias Shema, because his mind is not settled if he delays his journey; according to this everything depends on the road, his place, and his walking. Thus it is explicit that if his mind is settled, he may stand and there is no prohibition at all. So too it seems to me from Tanchuma at the beginning of Parshas Lech Lecha.
