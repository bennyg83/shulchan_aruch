(3) And there is exertion, etc. And when he lies actually on his side it is not a way of pride (Levush). He combined two explanations and it is not correct: per the view of the one who says it is not called a way of pride, even without exertion it is permitted; and per the view of the one who says because of exertion, they only permitted it in a case where there is exertion — that he stripped his garments, for there is exertion to return and dress them. All this is explained from the words of Rabbeinu Yona and in Beit Yosef, and likewise the opinion of Rama and Lechem Chamudot, and so Lechem Chamudot explained the words of Rama and challenged Levush. In my view it appears that Levush learned from what Rama wrote — his words on the Shulchan Arukh are the words of Rambam, who holds the first view and did not write in the dispute; he holds that even per the first view, though there is no way of pride, nevertheless there is no way of honor and it is not permitted except where there is exertion. And there are those who, for the first view, [say that] inclining a little on one's side is forbidden even where there is exertion, since it is a way of pride — unless for a corpulent person, who is permitted even while lying in his garments; and R. Yehuda who reads and prays — that is when he lay in his garments. And with this the difficulty of Olat Tamid in the Gemara is resolved — see there; also even otherwise it is not difficult for one who examines. And per the second view, even inclining a little on one's side is permitted where there is exertion, even if he is not corpulent; and the first view is primary. One may also say that per the first view we still require exertion — that is, the exertion of standing, for he already lay down, even if he dressed in his garments; and so Rambam holds, and so Nachalat Tzvi ruled. And it appears that even if he can sit there is exertion, and since he already lies actually on his side it is permitted; and so it appears in Kesef Mishneh there, and so is primary.
