But not lying flat on his back or face - After the fact, he has fulfilled his obligation, as stated by Peri Megadim.
Since he is already lying down - See Mishnah Berurah. All this follows the view of the gloss, which rules generally in accordance with Rabbeinu Yonah. Beit Yosef, however, rules like the authorities who disagree with Rabbeinu Yonah and maintain that even initially one may lie on his side and recite, since lying fully on one's side is not considered an arrogant posture. As a matter of practical halachah, the words of Olas Tamid, Taz, and Magen Avraham imply that one should follow Beit Yosef, and Magen Gibborim rules likewise. However, Or Zarua rules like the Rama, Mishbetzos Zahav inclines to the view that the law accords with the Rama, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger also inclines to rule stringently like Rabbeinu Yonah. See there. One should therefore certainly be stringent initially.
He leans slightly onto his side and recites - See Peri Megadim, who writes that if sitting and standing are difficult for them, they may even initially go and lie while leaning somewhat onto the side and recite, if they are unable to lie fully on the side.
