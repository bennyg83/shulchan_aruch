In Turei Zahav, se'if katan 2: “And this is proven,” etc. It seems to me that words are missing here. His intent is that there it is said that Rabbi Yochanan follows his own reasoning, and he ruled in accordance with Rabbi Eliezer in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, who holds that once one directed his heart during the first verse, he no longer needs to do so. If you would think that there is also an objection when one who is walking stops, because it appears that he is acting in accordance with Beit Shammai, how could Rabbi Yochanan say that the entire paragraph is recited while standing? You cannot answer that Rabbi Yochanan follows the opinion that one who acts in accordance with Beit Shammai has fulfilled his obligation, for regarding the paragraph Vehayah Im Shamoa as well, Beit Shammai hold that in the morning one must stand. If so, to satisfy the opinion of Beit Shammai, one would also have to recite Vehayah Im Shamoa while standing. Yet Rabbi Yochanan said this only regarding the first paragraph, because it requires intention, as the Gemara says, “He follows his own reasoning,” etc. Moreover, Rabbi Yochanan implies that the same law applies even in the evening, whereas Beit Shammai hold that one stands specifically in the morning. Rather, it must be that the objection applies only when one who is sitting stands up, etc. I have expanded somewhat upon the meaning that he expressed in brief words.
