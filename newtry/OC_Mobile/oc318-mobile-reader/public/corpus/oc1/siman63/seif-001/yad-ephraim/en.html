In Taz, note 2 — and this is proven, etc. It seems to me there is here omission of language, and his intention is that there they say R. Yochanan followed his reason and said the law is like R. Eliezer because of R. Yochanan who holds once he had intention in his heart in the first verse he no longer needs; and if we thought that also in walking and standing there is concern that it appears he acts like Beit Shammai — if so how did R. Yochanan say the whole paragraph in standing? And one may say R. Yochanan holds — that is, he holds like the one who acts like Beit Shammai is proper. That is to say, behold also in paragraph "and it was if" Beit Shammai hold in the morning they stand; if so, in order to fulfill Beit Shammai's hand also "and it was if hear" must read in standing; and that is to say R. Yochanan does not hold except in the first paragraph and because of intention as he said, and R. Yochanan followed his reason, etc. And further — that it implies from R. Yochanan that even in the evening the law is so, and Beit Shammai hold only in the morning they stand; rather necessarily there is no concern except in sitting and standing, etc.; and his intention in short words I expanded their explanation somewhat.
