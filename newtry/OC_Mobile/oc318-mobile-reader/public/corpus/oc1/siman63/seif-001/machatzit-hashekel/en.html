(Seif katan 1) And there is, etc. ‘And so it implies in the Gemara, one lying face upward, etc.;’ meaning that even though he is already lying, etc. At first glance this is no proof, for the language of the Rama implies that two things are required: that he actually be lying on his side, and also that there be burden. If so, what proof does he bring from one lying face upward? There, the case is that he only leans somewhat on his side, and therefore it does not help even though there is burden in standing. However, the intent of Magen Avraham is only to clarify the words of Rabbeinu Yonah. The Gemara, page 13, states that Rav Yosef said: one lying face upward may not read Krias Shema. It asks: even without reading Krias Shema it is forbidden to lie face upward, for Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi cursed one who lies face upward. It answers: regarding lying, if he tilts somewhat, it is permitted; but for reading, even in such a case it is forbidden. It asks: but Rabbi Yehuda tilted and read. It answers: Rabbi Yehuda was different, for he was heavyset and there was burden. Rabbeinu Yonah asked from the baraisa on page 24a regarding two who were covered by one tallis while naked: one turns his face and reads Krias Shema, and the other turns, etc. How may he read while lying tilted on his side? Some answer that when they said reading is forbidden even if he tilts, this means a slight tilt, but when he is fully on his side it is permitted. The more correct answer is that since we see that where there is burden it is permitted, as with Rabbi Yehuda because he was heavyset, so too here they permitted him while tilted, because there is burden since he removed his clothing and would have to dress again. From Rabbeinu Yonah’s phrase ‘since he removed his clothing,’ Magen Avraham wrote above that if he lies in his shirt he must stand, for standing involves no burden. To leave no room for one to argue that standing itself is always called burden, and Rabbeinu Yonah mentioned removing clothing only as an added point because in fact the baraisa of two covered with one tallis speaks of naked people, Magen Avraham wrote that the Gemara implies otherwise. According to Rabbeinu Yonah, only dressing is called burden, not standing, for in the second answer of Rabbeinu Yonah they permitted even one who leans only somewhat on his side, as implied by their language. Furthermore, whichever way one understands it, how could Rabbeinu Yonah in the second answer require both that he actually lie on his side and that there be burden? If lying on one’s side is not a matter of arrogance, it should be permitted even without burden; and if even actual lying on the side involves arrogance but they permitted because of burden, then why not permit slight leaning because of burden, since both involve arrogance? Rather, Rabbeinu Yonah’s second answer permits even slight leaning where there is burden. The slight tilt needed is not because of Krias Shema, but because Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi cursed one who lies face upward. Therefore, since even slight leaning is permitted because of burden, standing cannot be called burden. This proves Magen Avraham’s question from ‘one lying face upward,’ for there too the Gemara must be speaking where he leans somewhat, and nevertheless it was not permitted although there is the burden of standing. Rather, standing is not called burden; only dressing after removing clothing is called burden. If Rama requires both actual lying on the side and burden, even though this is difficult according to Rabbeinu Yonah, one can say that Rama was uncertain which of Rabbeinu Yonah’s two answers is primary, and therefore adopted the stringency of both: actual lying on the side like the first answer, and burden like the second. Since he is stringent for the second answer, he must require that he be naked without a shirt, for according to that answer standing is not called burden, only dressing. Therefore Rama too means that he is lying naked, but abbreviated his wording, as Lechem Chamudos wrote.
However, the view of the Beis Yosef, etc. Meaning, do not say that both the Beis Yosef and the Rama hold like the second answer of Rabbeinu Yonah, that the permission is only because of burden, and if so even leaning only somewhat on his side is permitted; and that when the Beis Yosef wrote ‘and he is lying on his side,’ he means somewhat on his side, while the Rama came in the gloss to explain his words, ‘since,’ etc., and ‘there is burden,’ etc. Magen Avraham writes that this cannot be. If it were possible to explain that standing alone is called burden, one could explain that the Beis Yosef also holds like Rabbeinu Yonah’s second answer, even though the Beis Yosef did not mention burden, since in fact there is at least the burden of standing. But now that Magen Avraham wrote that the burden is dressing, and it was permitted only when he is lying naked, how can one say that the Beis Yosef permits even leaning somewhat on his side? The only basis for permission would be that he is naked, and he did not mention that at all. It is different with the Rama, where one can press his words ‘since he is already lying’ to allude to this, for perhaps ordinary lying is naked without clothing. In truth, Lechem Chamudos wrote that the Rama’s wording leaves room for error. In any case, since in his longer work he wrote that the words of the Rambam, Rashi, and Tur imply the first answer of Rabbeinu Yonah, presumably in the Shulchan Aruch too he ruled like the first answer, that one must actually lie on his side, and then it is permitted even without burden. The Rama, who wrote ‘since he already,’ etc., implies that even where he actually lies on his side, we require specifically that there be burden, for he comments on the Mechaber, who speaks of actually lying on his side. The reason is that he is concerned for the stringencies of both answers of Rabbeinu Yonah, as above, and so holds the Levush; see what Lechem Chamudos wrote against him. Although according to the Beis Yosef it is permitted even without burden, nevertheless Rama did not write ‘there are those who say,’ even though according to this Rama disagrees with the Beis Yosef, because it is possible to press the Beis Yosef’s words and say that he too meant specifically when he is lying naked. Although this is not the truth, he therefore did not write the language of ‘there are those who say.’ This is the way of the Rama in Shulchan Aruch, as is known.
And so implies the plain meaning of the Talmud, meaning like the first answer of Rabbeinu Yonah. If he is actually lying on his side, it is permitted even where there is no burden. For the Gemara answers that regarding lying, if he tilts it is permitted, but for reading, even if he tilts it is forbidden. This implies that only when he tilts, meaning he leans somewhat on his side, it is forbidden, but if he lies actually on his side, it is permitted. And the case there speaks of one wearing his clothing.
