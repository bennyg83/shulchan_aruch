For there it deals with one who does not incline except a little on his side, and therefore it does not help even though there is exertion of standing. However, the intention of Magen Avraham is only to clarify the words of the students of R. Yona, as follows: the Gemara, folio 13 — R. Yosef said: prone, one does not read Keriat Shema. They challenged: but even if he does not read Keriat Shema it is forbidden to lie prone, for R. Yehoshua ben Levi cursed one who sleeps prone. They answered: to lie down — if inclining (that is, leaning a little on his side) it is permitted, but to read — even so it is forbidden. They challenged: but R. Yehudah inclines and reads! They answered: R. Yehudah is different, for he is corpulent and there is exertion. The students of R. Yona challenged on this: as it is taught, folio 24a — two who were covered in one tallit (and they are naked) — this one turns his face and reads Keriat Shema (so that his heart not see his fellow's nakedness) and this one turns, etc. — and how is he permitted to read while he lies inclined on his side? And some say: when they said "to read" — even though inclining it is forbidden — this refers to inclining a little; but one who is inclined completely on his side is permitted. And it is more correct to resolve that since we see that where there is exertion it is permitted, as R. Yehudah said, etc., because he is corpulent — likewise they permitted him with inclining because he has exertion, since he stripped his garments to return and dress them — until here. And since the students of R. Yona wrote "since he stripped his garments," etc. — Magen Avraham wrote above that if he lies in his shirt he must stand, for in standing there is no exertion — so he holds. And so as not to give an opponent room to dispute: forever, even if he lies in his shirt, standing is called exertion; and that which the students of R. Yona wrote "since he stripped his garments" — they mentioned it as an advantage, since in truth that baraita of two who were covered in one tallit deals with them being naked, since the students of R. Yona do not intend to rule law but only to resolve their difficulty from that baraita — therefore they wrote the truth that there they are completely naked; even though this is strained, nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote that so it implies in the Gemara — that the students of R. Yona hold that only dressing is called exertion and not standing, for in the second answer of the students of R. Yona they permitted even if he inclines only a little on his side — as the language of the students of R. Yona implies, for they wrote "likewise" that they permitted him with inclining because he has exertion, etc. — as above; behold they mentioned inclining. And further, either way — how can he say that the students of R. Yona in the second answer require two things: lying actually on his side and also because there is exertion? If he holds that when lying on his side there is no concern of pride — then even without exertion it is permitted; and why did they permit in the first answer (for the prohibition of prone, even when inclining a little on his side, is because it is a way of pride) — for since there is no pride, what difference to me whether sitting, standing, or lying? And if he holds that even when lying actually on his side there is concern of pride, and nevertheless they permitted because of exertion — then why not permit one lying a little on his side because of exertion, since there is no difference between inclining a little on his side and actually on his side, for in both there is pride (and that Rama requires two things — as above — will be explained below, with God's help). Or that in truth the students of R. Yona, in the second answer that permits because of exertion, permit even if he inclines only a little on his side (and the slight inclining that we require is not because of Keriat Shema but because R. Yehoshua ben Levi cursed it, and there is another reason — see in the Gemara); and if so, since even with slight inclining it is permitted because of exertion, it is impossible that standing is called exertion — Magen Avraham's difficulty from what is said "prone," etc. — for there it must deal with one who inclines a little on his side, as the Talmud resolves, and nevertheless it was not permitted even though there is exertion of standing; rather standing is not called exertion except once he stripped his garments — dressing is called exertion; and it is impossible that Rama requires two things: lying actually on his side and also because of exertion, even though it is difficult either way, as written above, in the view of the students of R. Yona. Nevertheless, one may say that Rama was uncertain which of the two resolutions of the students of R. Yona is primary, and therefore he adopted the stringency of both resolutions: we require lying actually on his side — that is, like the first answer — and also because there is exertion — like the second answer. And since he is stringent also in the second answer of the students of R. Yona, therefore we require that he be naked without a shirt, for in the second answer standing is not called exertion but specifically dressing, as Magen Avraham proved. Therefore Rama also means when lying naked, and he abbreviated in language, as Lechem Chamudot wrote.
