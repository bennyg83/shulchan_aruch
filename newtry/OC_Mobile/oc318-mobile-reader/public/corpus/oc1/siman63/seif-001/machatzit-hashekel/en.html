(Note 1) And there is exertion, etc. And so it appears in the Gemara on prone — it implies that even though he already lies, etc. — at first glance this is not proof, for it implies from the language of Rama that we require two things: lying actually on his side and also because there is exertion; and if so, what proof does he bring from prone? For there it deals with one who does not incline except a little on his side, and therefore it does not help even though there is exertion of standing. However, the intention of Magen Avraham is only to clarify the words of the students of R. Yona, as follows: the Gemara, folio 13 — R. Yosef said: prone, one does not read Keriat Shema. They challenged: but even if he does not read Keriat Shema it is forbidden to lie prone, for R. Yehoshua ben Levi cursed one who sleeps prone. They answered: to lie down — if inclining (that is, leaning a little on his side) it is permitted, but to read — even so it is forbidden. They challenged: but R. Yehudah inclines and reads! They answered: R. Yehudah is different, for he is corpulent and there is exertion. The students of R. Yona challenged on this: as it is taught, folio 24a — two who were covered in one tallit (and they are naked) — this one turns his face and reads Keriat Shema (so that his heart not see his fellow's nakedness) and this one turns, etc. — and how is he permitted to read while he lies inclined on his side? And some say: when they said "to read" — even though inclining it is forbidden — this refers to inclining a little; but one who is inclined completely on his side is permitted. And it is more correct to resolve that since we see that where there is exertion it is permitted, as R. Yehudah said, etc., because he is corpulent — likewise they permitted him with inclining because he has exertion, since he stripped his garments to return and dress them — until here. And since the students of R. Yona wrote "since he stripped his garments," etc. — Magen Avraham wrote above that if he lies in his shirt he must stand, for in standing there is no exertion — so he holds. And so as not to give an opponent room to dispute: forever, even if he lies in his shirt, standing is called exertion; and that which the students of R. Yona wrote "since he stripped his garments" — they mentioned it as an advantage, since in truth that baraita of two who were covered in one tallit deals with them being naked, since the students of R. Yona do not intend to rule law but only to resolve their difficulty from that baraita — therefore they wrote the truth that there they are completely naked; even though this is strained, nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote that so it implies in the Gemara — that the students of R. Yona hold that only dressing is called exertion and not standing, for in the second answer of the students of R. Yona they permitted even if he inclines only a little on his side — as the language of the students of R. Yona implies, for they wrote "likewise" that they permitted him with inclining because he has exertion, etc. — as above; behold they mentioned inclining. And further, either way — how can he say that the students of R. Yona in the second answer require two things: lying actually on his side and also because there is exertion? If he holds that when lying on his side there is no concern of pride — then even without exertion it is permitted; and why did they permit in the first answer (for the prohibition of prone, even when inclining a little on his side, is because it is a way of pride) — for since there is no pride, what difference to me whether sitting, standing, or lying? And if he holds that even when lying actually on his side there is concern of pride, and nevertheless they permitted because of exertion — then why not permit one lying a little on his side because of exertion, since there is no difference between inclining a little on his side and actually on his side, for in both there is pride (and that Rama requires two things — as above — will be explained below, with God's help). Or that in truth the students of R. Yona, in the second answer that permits because of exertion, permit even if he inclines only a little on his side (and the slight inclining that we require is not because of Keriat Shema but because R. Yehoshua ben Levi cursed it, and there is another reason — see in the Gemara); and if so, since even with slight inclining it is permitted because of exertion, it is impossible that standing is called exertion — Magen Avraham's difficulty from what is said "prone," etc. — for there it must deal with one who inclines a little on his side, as the Talmud resolves, and nevertheless it was not permitted even though there is exertion of standing; rather standing is not called exertion except once he stripped his garments — dressing is called exertion; and it is impossible that Rama requires two things: lying actually on his side and also because of exertion, even though it is difficult either way, as written above, in the view of the students of R. Yona. Nevertheless, one may say that Rama was uncertain which of the two resolutions of the students of R. Yona is primary, and therefore he adopted the stringency of both resolutions: we require lying actually on his side — that is, like the first answer — and also because there is exertion — like the second answer. And since he is stringent also in the second answer of the students of R. Yona, therefore we require that he be naked without a shirt, for in the second answer standing is not called exertion but specifically dressing, as Magen Avraham proved. Therefore Rama also means when lying naked, and he abbreviated in language, as Lechem Chamudot wrote.

However, the opinion of Rabbeinu [Yosef Caro], etc. — that is to say, do not say that between Beit Yosef and Rama they hold like the second answer of the students of R. Yona, that the permission is because of exertion alone, and if so even if he inclines only a little on his side it is permitted; and the intention of Rabbeinu in what he wrote "and he lies on his side" means a little on his side. And Rama came in the gloss to explain his words "since he already lies," etc., and "there is exertion," etc. — and therefore Magen Avraham [says] that this is impossible: for granted, if it were possible to interpret that standing alone is called exertion, then it would be possible to interpret thus that Rabbeinu also holds like the second answer of the students of R. Yona mentioned, even though Rabbeinu is silent and did not write "because of exertion" — what of it, since in truth there is in any case exertion; unlike now, according to what Magen Avraham wrote, that the exertion is dressing and it is not permitted except while lying naked — if so, how can he say that Rabbeinu permits even when inclining a little on his side? And therefore the permission is only because he lies naked, and he did not mention this at all (granted, for Rama one must reject in what he wrote "since he already lies," etc. — it hints to this that perhaps ordinary lying is naked without clothing; and indeed Lechem Chamudot wrote on Rama that he gave room for error) (and in any case, since in his long work he wrote that from the words of Rambam and Rashi and Tur it implies the first answer of the students of R. Yona, presumably in Shulchan Arukh too he ruled like the first answer, that we require specifically lying actually on his side, and then even without exertion it is permitted). And Rama, who wrote "since he already," etc., implies that even if lying actually on his side, nevertheless he requires specifically where there is exertion, for he refers to the words of the author, who deals with lying actually on his side — the reason is because he is concerned for the stringency of both resolutions of the students of R. Yona, as above (and so holds Levush, and see what Lechem Chamudot wrote on him). And even though for Rabbeinu it is permitted even without exertion — nevertheless Rama did not write "and some say," for according to this Rama disagrees with Rabbeinu, since it is possible to strain that Rabbeinu too wanted to say specifically when lying naked; even though this is not the truth, nevertheless he did not write "and some say," for such is Rama's way in Shulchan Arukh, as is known.

And so the plain meaning of the Talmud wants to say, like the first resolution of the students of R. Yona: that if he lies actually on his side it is permitted even without exertion, for they answered "to lie down — if inclining it is permitted" and "to read — even inclining is forbidden" — it implies specifically inclining, that is, leaning a little on his side; but lying actually on his side is permitted. And there it deals with him in his garment.
