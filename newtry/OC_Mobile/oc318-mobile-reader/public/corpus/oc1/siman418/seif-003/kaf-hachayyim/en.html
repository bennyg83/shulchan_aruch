3) There. It is forbidden to fast on Rosh Chodesh. The end of chapter 3 of Eruvin implies that a partial-day fast is permitted on Rosh Chodesh. According to Rambam, however, it is forbidden because the prohibition is of Torah origin. Magen Avraham, there. This means that according to Rambam, who maintains that the prohibition against fasting on Rosh Chodesh is of Torah origin, as stated in se'if 4, even a partial-day fast is forbidden. See Eshel Avraham, subsection 1, however, who writes concerning these words of Magen Avraham that he did not find this distinction stated explicitly. Gaon Yaakov on Eruvin there likewise concludes as practical halachah that whenever fasting is forbidden, even a partial-day fast is forbidden. See there. The later authorities cite his words. This applies specifically if one formally accepted those hours as a fast. If one merely delayed eating, there is no concern.
