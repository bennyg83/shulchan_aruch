(11) Naphtha. The correct text is, 'a type of pitch whose odor is foul.' The reading 'white naphtha' is a scribal error, since white naphtha is not used even on a weekday because of its danger, as noted by Rabbi Akiva Eiger and Beis David.
(12) A regular servant. The Yerushalmi gives two possible reasons for leniency: either the servant's examination is brief, or he is familiar with the utensils and can recognize their cleanliness without close inspection. For practical law we do not rely on mere brevity, as seen from the prohibition against sorting vegetables. The Ran, Ramban, and Ritva imply that only close examination is forbidden. Shulchan Aruch HaRav is lenient even with close examination by a regular servant.
(13) Naphtha. Shulchan Atzei Shittim limits the prohibition to olive oil, reasoning that only there is concern he may take some fuel. This is difficult, for Beis Yosef and Tur imply that other oils are like olive oil; only naphtha is excluded because it is repulsive.
