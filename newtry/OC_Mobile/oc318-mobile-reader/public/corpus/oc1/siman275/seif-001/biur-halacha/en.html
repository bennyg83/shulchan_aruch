(ב) For the light of the candle — see Magen Avraham s.k. 3 regarding a wax candle; and behold today there is no custom in our countries to light a wax candle, only actual candles [called lamp]. And I have always wondered why most people today are lenient in this to read by their light — is this not relevant, lest he tilt as he does on a weekday? And at first I thought perhaps they rely on Raavad brought in Magen Avraham s.k. 3 who is lenient on a milk candle because it is repugnant, and certainly he holds it is similar to a naphtha candle, whom they are lenient on Shabbos daf 12b to inspect cups and bowls by its light — and if so, all the more in our case with [gas] lamps that are repugnant and stink more than milk, for certainly similar to naphtha candle at Gemara time that was permitted [on Shabbat there] per Tosafos and Rosh even by a sun that is not fixed, because since its smell is bad and its light is abundant one will not be satisfied from it and will not come to tilt. And even if we say our candles since they are sealed and no bad smell is heard from them are better than naphtha and milk candle of their time when the lamp was open above to air and smell wafted far — nevertheless its light is not abundant and this reason alone suffices that he will not come to tilt as implied in Beit Yosef; and also the lamp is repugnant in itself and one will not be satisfied from it. But afterward I saw it is not worthwhile to rely on this: one, Shulchan Aruch ruled below in seif 12 per Rambam that even for naphtha it is forbidden for a matter requiring much scrutiny, for we are concerned lest he tilt, and reading before a candle is considered by all to be a matter requiring much scrutiny; and even per the view of the early authorities who are lenient there, Ramban wrote explicitly in his chiddushim that only for inspecting cups they were lenient before naphtha because of cleanliness and not for other matters; and in truth Raavad's view is a lone view, for I found no posek who would hold it permitted to read even by naphtha candle, and all the more by milk candle. And I heard of craftsmen who write on a piece of paper in large letters "Today is Shabbat and forbidden to light" and attach the paper from before the day in the place [of the chimney] — and it appears that on this one may certainly rely to be lenient: one, Maharshal holds closed in a key is permitted, and the same if tied with an unusual knot as Machatzit HaShekel wrote — and so in our case where paper is tied on the candle at the place of tilting it is no worse; also since at every hour he sees before his eyes the prohibition of lighting on Shabbat it is somewhat similar to reading the chapter of "With what may we light" explained in siman 67 that it is permitted; and further it is similar to one who asks his friend to remind him, which was ruled permitted in s.k. 3 — and see what is written below at end of seif 3. However after writing all this I found in Masegeret HaShulchan who elaborated on studying before lamps and brought first the view of those who forbid as we wrote, but in conclusion sided in the name of several great authorities to permit even per Ramban above, because they are not concerned lest he tilt except in the wick and candle alone — for that is his way on a weekday: after lighting the head of the wick a bit it no longer burns as strongly as at first, therefore one must tilt oil toward the wick or draw the wick out somewhat; but our lamps burn from when one begins lighting until the end in one manner and the light does not diminish at all except at the end when little naphtha remains in the bottom — then its light diminishes and then turning the wick outward does not help because it immediately diminishes again and more so because it makes charcoal on the wick head; and before that there is no concern lest he turn to increase light, for presumably he prepared before Shabbat that it burn as needed, and if he wanted greater light he would have made it immediately greater, etc.; therefore where they put much naphtha in the lamp and make light only as they usually do on a weekday when wanting to study, again there is no concern lest he want to increase his light — end of his words briefly. And behold even though this permission is not clear, nevertheless one may rely to be lenient for Torah study so as not to cancel, and especially if he studies in the synagogue by a naphtha candle one may combine the view of Or Zarua who permits in public and holds that all in public is like two in one matter — and even though we do not rule thus, one may join his view with the above reasoning to permit.
