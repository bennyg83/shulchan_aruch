Seif 1: One may not search for lice and one may not read from a book by lamplight. NB: We read in Shabbat 11: they asked, what is it saying: one may not search for lice by lamplight and one may not read by lamplight, or perhaps one may not search for lice even by day lest he kill, and one may not read by lamplight, etc., until it brings: Come and hear, one may not search for lice by lamplight and one may not read by lamplight; all these are among the laws they said in the upper chamber of Chananyah ben Chizkiyah, etc. One should examine why it concluded, "these are among the laws they said, etc." The intent appears, with God's help, as follows. Seemingly, even from this the question is not resolved, for we hold that if one tells another to watch him it is permitted, as written later in this siman, seif 3, regarding lest he tilt the lamp; the same would apply if the concern is lest he kill, that it would help to tell another to watch him so he not kill. If so, one could well say that the Mishnah's statement, "one may not search his garments," applies even by day lest he kill, but one might have thought that the only issur is lest he kill, while the concern lest he tilt does not apply to this. Therefore even at night, if he tells another to watch him so he not kill, although he does not tell him to watch so he not tilt, it would be permitted. Thus the baraita teaches that one may not search for lice by lamplight even if he told his fellow to watch him so he not kill, because since he did not tell him also to watch so he not tilt, it remains assur. What proof, then, would there be? Therefore the Gemara concluded that all these are among the halachot stated in the upper chamber of Chananyah ben Chizkiyah ben Guryon; there they stated only eighteen matters. If we say that "one may not search" contains two decrees, by day lest he kill and by night lest he tilt, there would be nineteen decrees. It is therefore proven that "one may not search" is only one decree: if at night it is because lest he tilt, there is no daytime decree lest he kill. This resolves it, with God's help. Examine carefully.
Now regarding what is stated in Shulchan Aruch, "one may not search for lice and one may not read from a book by lamplight, etc." NB: So it appears in the Gemara, the first chapter of Shabbat 12b. There we read: "And he shall not read by lamplight. Rabbah said: even if it is ten stories high, etc. One person may not read, but two may. But was it not taught: not one and not two, etc.?" This is difficult to me. Why does the Gemara ask? Perhaps the meaning of "not one and not two" is below, where a person's hand can reach; there, not one and not two, for we decree one because of two. But above a person's height, one is forbidden because we decree above because of below. Yet when it is higher than a person's height, two would be permitted, because that is a decree upon a decree: above, even one would have been permitted except for a decree because of below, and likewise two, even below, would have been permitted except that we decree because of one. Therefore above, with two, there are several decrees and we do not decree. Thus when it says "he shall not read," implying one but that two are permitted, it states the categorical case: even ten stories high is forbidden, and in that categorical case only one is forbidden, not two. But when it teaches "not one and not two," it refers to below; there even two are forbidden. What, then, is the Gemara asking? This requires study. Examine carefully.
