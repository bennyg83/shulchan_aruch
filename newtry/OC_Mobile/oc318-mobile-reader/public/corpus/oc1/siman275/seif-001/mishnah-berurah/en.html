(1) "One may not read," etc. The same applies to examining one's tzitzit, or anything similar that requires close inspection, lest one tilt the lamp in order to draw the oil toward the wick so that it will burn better. (2) "In a glass enclosure." If it is closed with a key, some authorities permit reading by its light. Their reason is that when a lamp is merely 10 stories high, the Sages still prohibited reading because people sometimes arrange things in that manner in order to see something high up, and there is no unusual reminder. A locked enclosure is different. One does not ordinarily lock a lamp except because of the Shabbos prohibition, and the lock therefore serves as a reminder that prevents him from opening it and tilting the lamp. This resembles reading the chapter Bameh Madlikin. Others prohibit it in every case because the Sages made no distinction. Eliyah Rabbah inclines to permit when the key was given to another person, because this resembles saying to another, "Pay attention to me so that I do not tilt it," which is permitted in seif 3. It is forbidden to read by the light of the lamp even during the day when the lamp's light is actually needed for the reading, because the concern that he may tilt it remains. Where the room is sufficiently illuminated by daylight and the lamp contributes nothing to his reading, there is no reason for the decree.
