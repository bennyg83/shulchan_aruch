Because they did not distinguish, etc. Meaning Chazal standardized the decree within one category, but did not decree from one category to another. Therefore they did not prohibit an insignificant item because of a significant one, nor a naphtha lamp because of an oil lamp, as Ran writes in chapter HaShoel. Taz apparently overlooked this.
In a lantern, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot writes that Maharam Ash and the students of Maharam permitted reading when the lamp was locked with a key. Bach disagrees and prohibits, followed by Magen Avraham and the other later authorities. Bach's main question is why this differs from a fixed mirror, which is prohibited as stated at the end of siman 302, or from a mirror placed ten stories high, where Chazal still made no distinction. In my humble opinion, there is a distinction. A mirror is sometimes used in that very manner, and a high mirror may sometimes be needed to see something elevated. Locking a lamp, however, is never ordinarily done except because of the Shabbos prohibition, so there is no concern that he will unlock it to adjust the flame. It is comparable to reading Bameh Madlikin. Especially if he gives the key to another person, it is permitted, like telling another "watch me" in seif 3. If the lamp itself is fixed in the wall, however, they likely agree it remains prohibited, unlike Bach's implication.
[Levush] To prohibit by the lamp, etc. Taz here and in siman 278 argues at length for leniency with a wax candle because even if he comes to extinguish while adjusting it, the extinguishing is a melachah she'einah tzrichah legufah, which most poskim hold incurs no d'oraisa liability. Magen Avraham writes similarly but concludes that without need one should be stringent because some hold extinguishing here is d'oraisa. See the end of siman 334. This also requires study from the end of siman 514, where cutting the head of the wick is prohibited as repairing a utensil, and Taz there gives another prohibition. Malbushei Yom Tov writes to see Tur siman 680, which shows concern lest one adjust a wax candle. Yet Beit Yosef citing Hagahot Ashri and all later authorities write that adjustment does not apply to a wax candle. The Tur in siman 277 also says that if a wax candle was forgotten on a tray, one may shake the tray so it falls, apparently because adjustment is not relevant. In my humble opinion the Tur in siman 680 can be explained as wax placed where oil normally goes, as above siman 264 seif 3, whereas here the wax surrounds the wick. Still, Ramban in Milchamot at the beginning of Bameh Madlikin writes that wax candles need adjustment and trimming even more than oil lamps, so some degree of adjustment is relevant. Moreover, Ramban holds a melachah she'einah tzrichah legufah is exempt, yet nevertheless decrees because one may adjust, which challenges Taz and Magen Avraham's reasoning. Taz also inaccurately says Bach in the name of Maharshal permits reading by its light generally. Maharshal permitted only reading the Haggadah on Pesach night; Bach explicitly prohibits other reading.
[Levush] And he will cut, etc. I am surprised at Olat Tamid, who says the Levush fears he may cut the head of the wick with a utensil and asks that this should then also be prohibited on Yom Tov. The Levush never mentions a utensil. Perhaps Olat Tamid infers it from the word "cut," but even this is difficult because Beit Yosef uses the same wording. Moreover, the end of siman 514 shows that "cutting" can mean by hand. Shabbos 149 also implies we do not fear he will go fetch a utensil, because by the time he goes to obtain it he will remember Shabbos.
