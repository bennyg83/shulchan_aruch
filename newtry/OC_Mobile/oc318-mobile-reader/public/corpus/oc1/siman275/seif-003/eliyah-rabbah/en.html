Levush: To prohibit by candlelight, etc. Taz here and in siman 278 writes at length to permit this. Even if adjusting the lamp leads to extinguishing, the prohibition is not from the Torah because it is a labor not required for its own purpose, which most poskim permit. Magen Avraham writes similarly, but concludes that where there is no need one should be stringent, because some authorities hold that extinguishing is a Torah prohibition. See the end of siman 334. This also requires study from the end of siman 514, where cutting the top of the wick is forbidden because it repairs a utensil. Taz there gives another prohibition. See there. I also found in Malbushei Yom Tov: See Tur, siman 680, who writes that one must be concerned lest he tilt the lamp. In truth, this is surprising. Beit Yosef, in the name of Hagahos Ashri, and all the later authorities write that tilting is not relevant to a wax candle. It also appears surprising from Tur, siman 277, concerning a candle forgotten upon a board. He permits shaking the board so that the candle falls, provided it is a wax candle, implying that tilting is not relevant to a wax candle. In my humble opinion, Tur in siman 680 may be explained as referring to one who places wax where oil would ordinarily be placed, as above in siman 264, seif 3. Here, however, the wax is wrapped around the wick. Still, this requires study from Ramban in Milchamos at the beginning of the chapter Bameh Madlikin. He writes that wax candles require tilting and trimming more than oil lamps. Thus tilting is relevant to them. Maharshal, whom I cite in seif katan 11, likewise implies that some concern for tilting applies. There is a further difficulty. Ramban holds that one is exempt for a labor not required for its own purpose. According to the reasoning of Taz and Magen Avraham cited above, why then did Ramban decree lest one tilt the lamp? Furthermore, Taz writes that Bach, in the name of Maharshal, permits reading by its light. This is inaccurate. Bach permits it only for reading the Haggadah on Pesach night. Regarding other reading he explicitly prohibits it at the end of seif katan 5 and the end of seif katan 3.
