Utensils that resemble one another, etc. Maharshal's commentary to Semag says that nowadays one may not inspect tzitzit early in the synagogue by lamplight, because sometimes the strings are tangled and require careful examination to separate and inspect them. This is no better than similar-looking utensils, which a regular attendant may not inspect according to Rashi's version, and a non-regular attendant according to Rashbam's. He adds that he heard people nowadays are lenient in these matters when using a candle chandelier, where the concern of adjusting is less applicable than with oil. His words show that he understands the distinction between a regular attendant in the next seif similarly, and Rambam chapter 5 also implies this. At first glance this requires study, because the prohibition regarding similar utensils is stated by Shulchan Aruch without qualification and the poskim imply no distinction between regular and non-regular attendants; the distinction applies specifically to cups and bowls. See the next seif katan. Nevertheless, I learn from Maharshal that leniency with wax candles is only where there is already a dispute, but not in matters prohibited according to all opinions. Bach at the end of the siman also implies this, unlike Olat Tamid.
[Levush] And they need to distinguish, etc. Shulchan Aruch says that examination is needed, while Rambam chapter 5, halachah 16, says "much examination." This resolves why here it appears prohibited even for a regular attendant while in the following seif it is permitted: there little examination is needed, while here even a regular attendant requires much scrutiny. Ran, however, writes that here too little examination is needed and explains the later leniency as based on cleanliness. This requires study, especially since he does not mention Rambam. See the preceding seif katan.
