(יא) There. And so with a wax candle. Bach wrote in the name of Rashal that the custom is to be lenient only to examine vessels and tzitzit and to read the Haggadah by a wax candle, for there is no concern lest he tilt it; and they brought this — Magen Avraham, note 3. And so too the Taz in siman 278 permits reading by the light of a wax or milk candle, see there; and there Magen Avraham wrote: even though there is concern lest he trim or cut the head of the wick as the Bach wrote, one may say there is no Torah prohibition in this; nevertheless where it is not for a need one should be stringent, for some say extinguishing is a Torah melacha, as written in siman 334, see there. He also wrote there that the Raavad is lenient with a milk candle since it is repulsive. And Turei Zahav, letter 4, brought this; and Mateh Yehuda wrote that per the Rif and Rosh, who wrote two reasons for a naphtha candle — one because its light is great, and also because it is repulsive — it is proven not like the Raavad, see there. Thus a wax candle and milk candle — if permitted to read facing them is in a dispute between them; and Rashaz, letter 1, wrote that for present need one may rely on those who permit. And so in Netiv Chayim — we may be lenient to study by a wax candle, but other uses are forbidden, see there. And Ben Aharon, Parashat Noach, letter 18, in the name of the Acharonim — one should not die at the hands of the lenient regarding tallow wrapped around the wick, for they have upon what to rely, and therefore one may permit in such a case for the need of a mitzvah, namely to study Torah when he cannot leave a guard to watch it; but if he can leave a guard, one should not rely to permit even with a tallow or milk candle wrapped around the wick. He also wrote: know that there are milk candles wrapped around the wick that are not craftsman-made — these need constant trimming as long as they burn, as we see plainly — and behold with these one may not permit (and so wax candles not made well need trimming — with these one may not permit at all). And so glass candles where they place water and oil on the water and the wick burns in the middle of the candle — even though tilting does not apply, there is trimming, and therefore these too are forbidden. And even though we said one may rely on those who permit with tallow or milk wrapped on the wick where there is no way to trim them when he cannot leave a guard — nevertheless one should fear: if he can place the wax candle mentioned above inside a glass vessel with an opening and close the opening with an iron lock and give the key to another, or close the opening with an unusual knot — he should do so to fulfill the decree of the Sages without doubt, thus he concluded.
