Bach writes in the name of Maharshal that the custom is to be lenient and inspect utensils and tzitzis, and to read the Haggadah, by the light of a wax candle, because there is no concern that one might tilt it. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, cites this. Taz in siman 278 likewise permits reading by the light of wax and tallow candles; see there. Magen Avraham writes there that although there is concern that one might trim or cut the top of the wick, as Beis Yosef writes, one can answer that this entails no Torah prohibition. Nevertheless, one should be stringent when there is no need, because some say that extinguishing is labor forbidden by Torah law, as stated at the end of siman 334; see there. Magen Avraham further writes that Raavan is lenient regarding a tallow candle because it is repulsive. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, cites this. Machatzis HaShekel writes that Rif and Rosh give two reasons concerning a naphtha lamp, one that its light is bright and another that it is repulsive, demonstrating that they disagree with Raavan; see there. Thus, whether one may read by a wax or tallow candle is disputed. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1, writes that when necessary one may rely upon those who permit it. Nesiv Chaim similarly writes that it is sufficient for us to be lenient regarding Torah study by a wax candle, but other activities may not be performed by its light; see there. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Noach, paragraph 18, likewise writes in the name of the later authorities that one should not protest against those who are lenient regarding a wax candle wrapped around its wick, because they have authorities upon whom to rely. Therefore, such a lamp may be permitted for the sake of a mitzvah, namely Torah study, when he cannot appoint a person to watch him. If he can appoint a watcher, however, he should not rely upon the leniency even for a wax or tallow candle wrapped around its wick. He further writes: Know that some tallow candles wrapped around their wicks are not expertly made and require constant trimming while they burn, as we observe directly. These may not be permitted. The same applies to poorly made wax candles that require trimming; they may not be permitted at all. Similarly, glass lamps containing water, oil above the water, and a wick burning in the lamp's center may not involve tilting, but they do require trimming and are therefore forbidden. Although we stated that when one cannot appoint a watcher, he may rely upon those who permit a wax or tallow candle wrapped around its wick, which is not ordinarily trimmed, a person who fears Heaven should nevertheless do the following when possible: place that wax candle inside a glass vessel with an opening, close the opening before Shabbos with an iron lock and give the key to another person, or close it with an unusual knot. He should do so to fulfill the decree of our Sages without any doubt. These are his words.
