6) There: Even if it is enclosed in a lantern. If it is enclosed in a lantern locked with a key, there are grounds not to forbid it. Maharash similarly writes that he heard that Maharam's students would place a lamp in a lantern on Shabbos night, lock it with a key, and then read by its light. Rabbi Avraham Stein also acted this way. Bach, however, disagrees and writes that we adopt the stringent ruling, contrary to Maharam's students and Rabbi Avraham Stein; see there. Taz, paragraph 1, writes the same. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, similarly writes that it remains forbidden even if he closes the lantern with a lock and gives the key to another person who is not in the house, because the Sages made no distinction. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, however, writes that giving the key to another person is effective. Yad Yosef similarly permits it in accordance with Eliyah Rabbah, as does Responsa Nachalah LeYisrael, siman 18; see there. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 1, writes that one should be stringent even in this case. Shulchan Atzei Shittim, in the laws of extinguishing, siman 3, seif 2, similarly forbids it even if the lamp is locked with a key, and permits leniency only in a case of great need, because the Sages made no distinction; see there.
