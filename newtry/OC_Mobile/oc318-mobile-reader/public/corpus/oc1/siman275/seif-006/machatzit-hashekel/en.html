Paragraph 6: To recite piyyutim. The same applies to zemiros, etc. Tosefes Shabbos asks: Magen Avraham 3 cites Bach that Maharshal noted the custom to inspect tzitzis and utensils and read the Haggadah by wax candle, which must rely on authorities permitting wax. Certainly a tallow candle should also be permitted, since it shares only the concern of trimming and adds the leniency of being repulsive. Why then refrain from piyyutim, when synagogue candles are wax or tallow? I did not find Bach permitting Haggadah reading by wax. Bach says: Rashi forbids a regular servant to inspect utensils by olive oil but permits an irregular servant, though this is not publicly ruled; Rif, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Tam hold the reverse. Thus, with olive oil neither servant may be permitted amid a serious Shabbos dispute. The present custom of leniency follows great authorities who say a wax candle creates no concern of tilting. For this reason people inspect tzitzis at dawn by wax candle, as found in Maharshal's name. Bach mentions only inspecting tzitzis and a servant inspecting utensils, not Haggadah reading. Those two have independent grounds for leniency: utensil inspection is itself disputed between Rashi and Rif, Rosh, and Rabbeinu Tam, so the permissive wax opinion is combined with it. Tzitzis inspection was prohibited by Magen Avraham 1 in the name of Mateh Moshe and Maharshal; these three great authorities needed to state the prohibition because one might have thought it permitted, perhaps because it requires little examination, like the regular servant permitted in se'if 12.
To inspect, since it requires no close examination; the ruling teaches it is forbidden, for even distinguishing one's garments from another's is forbidden. Only utensil inspection was leniently permitted for cleanliness, as Magen Avraham writes there. Tzitzis inspection nevertheless has some lenient aspect because it requires little attention and lasts briefly, as Magen Avraham cites from the Yerushalmi in se'if 12. The lenient wax opinion was therefore combined to permit it. Haggadah reading has no independent leniency, and they did not rely on wax alone. But according to Magen Avraham's attribution to Bach permitting Haggadah reading too, Tosefes Shabbos's question concerning piyyutim remains.
