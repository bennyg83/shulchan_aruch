[Levush] That the ends of the logs are close, etc. It appears the Levush omitted Shulchan Aruch, Rashi, and Ran's reason that the readers sit far apart because he holds that when they sit far apart they are presumably reading from separate books, which is prohibited even by an ordinary lamp. Shulchan Aruch may follow the first rationale. It also appears that the Levush holds that even when they sit close and read from one book, a bonfire remains prohibited, and the later authorities agree. I found support in Knesset HaGedolah, who gives only the reason that the prohibited act is readily accessible. Perhaps Shulchan Aruch too agrees and gives the first reason as an additional stringency: even if their seats are far from the ends of the logs, it is prohibited when the people are far from one another. According to this, if they sit close together and are far from the log ends it would be permitted. More likely, however, once proximity to the log ends creates a prohibition, Chazal did not distinguish and it remains prohibited even farther away. Taz writes that although one may warm himself before a bonfire as stated at the end of siman 276, one should not sit close to the log ends because people commonly turn the ends of the burning wood to prevent them from falling. In my humble opinion we should not create this new stringency without mention in the Gemara or poskim. According to my explanation that a prohibition found nearby extends farther away, Taz's rule would prohibit all warming. Moreover, the laws of a bonfire above concern two people reading one subject in one book. A lone reader is certainly prohibited even at a distance according to all opinions. If Taz's concern were accepted, an individual could never warm himself at all. Yet Shabbos 21 says explicitly that materials prohibited for Shabbos lamps may nevertheless be made into a bonfire for warming, without distinguishing near from far, even though they do not burn steadily.
