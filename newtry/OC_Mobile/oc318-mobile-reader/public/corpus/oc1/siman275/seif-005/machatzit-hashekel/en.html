Paragraph 5: He will remind him, etc.; according to what I wrote in Bach's name, etc. This refers to the preceding paragraph. Tosefes Shabbos asks: why is this categorical? Riding may serve a mitzvah, such as bringing a lulav or shofar, while reading may be optional, such as reading a personal letter. In my humble opinion, a letter is not completely optional, since reading it is permitted lest it contain something needed for personal welfare, as below in siman 307.
Likewise, if it is written in Hebrew and he can learn the language from it, this too is not optional. The difficulty is easily answered. Most reading serves a mitzvah. Although some reading is optional and truly forbidden, we do not prohibit mitzvah reading because of that minority under a uniform decree. Riding is the reverse: most riding is optional and forbidden, so even the occasional mitzvah ride is forbidden under the uniform decree.
