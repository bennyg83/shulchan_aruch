(8) Wet goose—in Tur they soaked bran first in water; Mechaber ruled even dry bran only goose wet from water—novelty that even slight moisture on bird bran leavens and goose forbidden even if not rinsed afterward except fruit juice:

(9) Goose forbidden—because skin has many holes, bran remains; even if wants to rub and rinse afterward ineffective:

(10) And this is custom for chametz, etc.—meaning though for other prohibitions taste of holes ineffective to forbid unless meat hot or at least cooked or roasted and absorbed deep; here for chametz forbidden in smallest amount we concern even live if chametz remains in goose holes. Later authorities wrote even if wants to skin entirely—forbidden, bran enters through skin deep into flesh:

(11) That it is in smallest amount—since main reason is smallest-amount prohibition, from six hours until Passover nullified in sixty, may permit goose with good rubbing and rinsing in water provided eat before Passover lest something visible remains and revives on Passover; some lenient even eat during Passover where great loss:

(12) Not to singe, etc., wheat or leavened grain—meaning water fell after detachment and it leavened; later authorities agreed all this is mere l'chatchila caution; from basic law even if wheat there we do not presume leavened; even if known leavened, do not forbid since bird not touching grain holding it from above and flame rises below.
