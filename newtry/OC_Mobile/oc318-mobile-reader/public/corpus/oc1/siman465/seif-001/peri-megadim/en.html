Even. See Taz: the text was emended to "even if she rinses with cold water," meaning to say that cold water does not cause chimutz. And Eliyah Rabbah 3 challenged: for in the Rosh it is explicit that even with cold water it is forbidden to rinse afterward; and see siman 467. And there is a version: even if she does not rinse afterward with cold water it is forbidden (human sweat does not cause chimutz), because of a decree lest she not rinse well; so it appears. And know that this bran mentioned in the Mishnah: there is a dispute what it is; see siman 454 in Magen Avraham 1, whether the coarse husk or the fine, and both are of wheat; they are not of oats, for a stalk of oats certainly does not become chametz. And nevertheless regarding bran as well one must look into it, for I saw our great master in Penei Yehoshua, Pesachim 39b, that soaking bran is hard chametz (chametz nuksheh), for it is not fit for eating; see there. And in Magen Avraham siman 447, 5, that nuksheh is nullified in sixty: one can say that bran is nullified in sixty, in Peri Chadash. And from what the Rav wrote in the gloss of se'if 3 regarding bran in a goose, not in any amount: one can say that it is impossible without flour stuck to it, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 454, 1. And see in tractate Terumos, chapter 11, mishnah 5, the distinction between old and new; if so it implies that with old it is possible without flour; and in siman 454 we wrote of this. If so there is a great practical difference in this; also on the last day of Yom Tov of Pesach there is a practical difference; see siman 467.
