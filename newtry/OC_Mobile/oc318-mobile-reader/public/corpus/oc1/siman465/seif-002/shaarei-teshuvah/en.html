They scald. See Baer Heitev; and what he wrote "and see siman 328 in the Taz": that is to say, that there he wrote that one should not do it through a non-Jew for a sick person who is in danger; and Emunas Shmuel appears to hold like the Rama there, that if it is possible to do it through delay, they do it through a non-Jew. And nevertheless, what he wrote afterward, "and if it is impossible, it appears that he should boil etc.", implies that it also refers to one who is in danger, for Emunas Shmuel wrote, and this is his wording: that even though they practice to forbid regarding chalitah, nevertheless whenever we can do it in a permitted way, we do so, as the Rama wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 155 at its end; and there it is stated: and they do not permit any prohibited thing for a sick person if he can do the remedy in a permitted way as with a prohibited way, even though he must wait a bit before he finds the permitted [option], since there is no danger in the matter; end quote. Behold it is explicit that he deals with one who is not in danger. And on the contrary it appears that when there is danger one should not wait until it boils if it is possible to do it sooner, since there is danger in the matter. And it somewhat appears that there is a scribal error in what he wrote "rather, however, as written," and one must say in what he wrote, and that is to say, that it refers to what he wrote at first, namely that he deals with one who is not in danger. But from what he wrote "nevertheless whenever we can etc." it implies that he deals with one who is in danger; for if not so, since there is no non-Jew, if he will not do it through chalitah it is in truth forbidden, like that [ruling] that one should not chew. And in any event, for the practical law it appears to permit through scalding even for a sick person who is not in danger as well; for in siman 454 Magen Avraham is in doubt whether to forbid after the fact, and Chok Yaakov is lenient at least to leave it; and if so there is here no concern of Beis Yosef. And from the aspect of remedy, even so it is certainly permitted, since it is not in the normal manner of deriving benefit, and as explained in Emunas Shmuel there. And see below siman 466 s.k. 1. And these words of Baer Heitev in the name of Emunas Shmuel are copied from Leket HaKemach, and it implies that he deals with a case in which there is danger; and in my humble opinion, as I wrote, that even when there is no danger he permits in such a case.
