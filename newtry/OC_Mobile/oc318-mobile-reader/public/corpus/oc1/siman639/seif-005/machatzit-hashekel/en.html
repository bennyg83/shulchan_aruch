(Small paragraph 5) And wine, etc. As written in siman 174. Meaning: the Tur brought there the words of the Talmud, that wine before the meal exempts the wine after the meal from a blessing. We say there: this was taught only on Sabbaths and festivals, when a person establishes himself over wine; but on weekdays, when a person does not establish himself over wine, it does not exempt. Thus there is no fixedness for wine.
But according to what we rule there in Shulchan Aruch, siman 174, he did not distinguish between Sabbaths and festivals and weekdays; in every case it exempts, in accordance with the view of the Ra'avad that now, even on weekdays, people establish themselves over wine.
And in siman 213 the Shulchan Aruch wrote that one cannot discharge his fellow's obligation with the blessing over fruit, except with bread and wine if they reclined, for then it is called fixed. For us, sitting is considered like reclining in the days of the Talmudic sages and is considered fixed. Thus for us people establish themselves over wine.
And this is the language of the Bach: but in practice, etc. In order to remove himself from dispute, for the Bach first brought the words of the Hagahot Ashiri, who was uncertain whether drinking wine is considered fixedness.
And likewise regarding fixed eating of cheese and meat, for in this too the Tur brought a dispute among the decisors whether one is obligated in sukkah.
The same applies to fixed drinking of other beverages (and in my humble opinion, in our country the same applies to coffee). So wrote the Magen Avraham above, siman 213, small paragraph 6.
