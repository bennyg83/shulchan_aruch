See Ginat Veradim, rule 4, siman 10, etc., that pat haba'ah bekisnin should be considered actual bread for this matter: even if he eats it casually, since he ate the measure of more than an egg's bulk, he recites the blessing over the sukkah, etc. A cooked dish made from the five species should obligate him in sukkah even when eaten casually, but he should not recite the blessing over the sukkah unless he eats in a fixed manner. Meat, cheese, eggs, and the like also require a sukkah, but he should not recite the blessing, out of concern for the view of the Tur, of blessed memory. Fruit is exempt entirely, even if he eats it in a fixed manner; and one who wants to be stringent and eat it in the sukkah, even in a fixed manner, should not recite the blessing. In Machazik Berachah he wrote that he wrote in a responsum that according to those who disagree with the Rambam, one needs actual bread, more than an egg's bulk; but pat haba'ah bekisnin, even if he ate more than an egg's bulk, is like a temporary eating of bread until he establishes his meal on it. If so, it is better not to recite the blessing. But one should be concerned for the Rambam's view even with meat and cheese, to eat them in the sukkah without a blessing. So I saw great sages in the Land of Israel who did not recite the blessing over pat haba'ah bekisnin, even more than an egg's bulk; see there. In our country most people practice on Yom Tov and Shabbat to recite kiddush and eat after kiddush pat haba'ah bekisnin in place of a meal, and afterward, after kiddush, they go outside the sukkah and stroll to receive their teacher's presence and the like, and several hours pass until mealtime; and at the time of kiddush they recite the sukkah blessing. It appears that this is proper: although it is pat haba'ah bekisnin, nevertheless since he eats it as the meal required for kiddush, it is proper that he recite the sukkah blessing, for this intention makes it fixed. He should only be careful that it be more than an egg's bulk, and with this it is possible that even Machazik Berachah would agree regarding a meal. But on a weekday, or in the middle of the day on Shabbat and Yom Tov, when he eats pat haba'ah bekisnin without kiddush, I think the custom is also to recite the sukkah blessing over pat haba'ah bekisnin, and in my humble opinion one should be concerned for the view of Machazik Berachah. Nevertheless, it appears that if he does not intend to leave immediately, but to sit there for some time, it is proper to recite the blessing, for the blessing was also over the sitting; only the practice is to bless at the time of eating, and if so eating pat haba'ah bekisnin suffices. See there in Ginat Veradim at the end of that siman, where he wrote regarding eating fruit, that it is exempt even if fixed, distinguishing between entering to sleep or to sit there, where his entry to sit is like entering his house to sit, and entering for the sake of eating fruit, where people are not particular about eating fruit outside the house and his mind is not on sitting in the sukkah; see there. Therefore on a weekday, when he enters to eat pat haba'ah bekisnin, he should intend for both the eating and the sitting. See Magen Avraham regarding one who forgot to recite the blessing, that he recites it after eating because the sitting too is a mitzvah. But one who eats pat haba'ah bekisnin and leaves immediately should be concerned for what Machazik Berachah wrote, that one should not recite the blessing over pat haba'ah bekisnin.
