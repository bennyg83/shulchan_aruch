Seif 5: even, etc. As written in the commentary to the Mishnah. They wrote this with respect to a dish of grits, meaning ground beans, because the cooked dish made from them is quickly spoiled by water.
And even if there is no, etc. The Yerushalmi there: not only when it will become spoiled, but even when there is enough that it would become spoiled, etc.
And one who is not, etc. For the reason that one who is distressed is exempt, as Tosafot wrote there, 26a, s.v. "travelers," and likewise one who is distressed, etc.
And all this applies specifically, etc. From what is written: "if he wishes, he eats," but is it not obligatory to eat bread on Yom Tov, from what is written in Berachot 49b, "Sabbaths and festivals, where it does not suffice, etc."? Perforce it refers to bread, for fasting is forbidden even on Rosh Chodesh. R. Yitzchak answered that the practical difference is when rain fell, for on the first night, even if rain fell, there is an obligation. See the Rosh there, and so wrote the Semak. But in Tosafot it is written there: for example, if rain fell and he ate outside the sukkah, and then the rain stopped, he is obligated, as the Rashba wrote, for while rain is falling it does not have the status of a sukkah for him. The Shulchan Aruch omitted this because the Rashba, Semag, Or Zarua, and other decisors disagree with this and say that because of Yom Tov an olive's bulk suffices, and even with an egg's bulk of bread we do not require sukkah. But the view of Tosafot is primary.
And he should recite kiddush, etc. Meaning: although with respect to sukkah it would suffice for him to eat an olive's bulk at the end, as with matzah in chapter Arvei Pesachim, nevertheless this is so that he will not need to say Shehecheyanu another time over the sukkah; and as stated there, 46, "I saw Rav Kahana," and as written in chapter 4 of Yoma that one may not multiply blessings.
