(31) Rain fell, etc. - Just as one leaves because of rain, so too one leaves because of heat and mosquitoes. Even if the person himself is not distressed by this, but the food is spoiled by it, the same applies. Likewise, if the weather is cold and the fatty foods congeal before him, he may eat his entire meal in the house, and need not return until he finishes his meal, even if afterward he eats other foods that do not congeal, just as in seif 7, when he left because of rain, he need not return until he finishes his meal even if the rain stopped. The Bikkurei Yaakov wrote that if it is possible to fix the matter without great exertion so that the foods will not congeal, such as by placing them on a bowl containing hot water, he must fix it and is not exempt thereby from the sukkah.
(32) This is, etc. - If the wind blows into the sukkah and as a result splinters from the roofing fall into the food, if he is delicate and his mind is disturbed by this, he may leave the sukkah.
(33) Drops, etc. - Nevertheless, if they are not falling in the place where he is sitting, he must eat in the sukkah while they are still not falling upon him and upon his table. It is possible that he may even recite the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" [Bikkurei Yaakov; see there, where he brings some proof to this from Maharil]. It appears that if he is delicate and is distressed by this, he may leave, and in any event he should not recite the blessing.
(34) Of beans - For this type spoils quickly through a small amount of water.
(35) But the night, etc. - Although he is distressed because of the rain, and anyone distressed is exempt from the sukkah, the Rema holds according to the view of the decisors who maintain that on the first night even one who is distressed is obligated. Their reason is that since we derive "the fifteenth" from "the fifteenth" of the festival of matzah, we derive it completely: just as there he is obligated in all circumstances, so too on Sukkot. Know that there are other great early authorities who hold that this verbal analogy does not come for this, and one should not distinguish between the first night and the other days. Therefore several later authorities concluded that although he is obligated to eat in the sukkah, because of doubt concerning the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" he should not recite it, for in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently. They further wrote that it is proper to wait until midnight lest the rain pass; if it passes, he should enter the sukkah, recite "to sit in the sukkah," eat, and thereby fulfill the positive commandment according to all opinions. Some later authorities wrote that it is not worthwhile to wait so long, for if he waits with the eating of the adult and young members of his household, there is no greater prevention of Yom Tov joy than this [Sha'arei Teshuvah]. Likewise in Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim they did not mention the measure of midnight, but only about an hour or two; and if the rain has not stopped, he should then recite kiddush in the sukkah as the Rema wrote.
(36) And he should recite kiddush in the sukkah so that he says Shehecheyanu, etc. - Meaning: seemingly he should have done the reverse, reciting kiddush in the house and also saying there the blessing of Shehecheyanu for this time, eating his whole meal there, and only at the end of the meal entering the sukkah and eating an olive's bulk there. However, in that case he would need to recite another Shehecheyanu over the sukkah, and it is not proper to multiply blessings. Therefore it is better to recite kiddush first in the sukkah, so that the blessing of Shehecheyanu refers also to the sukkah. Even if we say that he is then exempt from sukkah, nevertheless he fulfills it thereby, for this is no worse than if he recited Shehecheyanu during the weekday at the time of making the sukkah, where he fulfills it. He should eat an olive's bulk in the sukkah, and the rest of his meal eat in his house. If the rain stops, even if he already recited Birkat HaMazon over the earlier eating, he must eat again the measure of an egg's bulk and a bit more in the sukkah and recite "to sit in the sukkah," in order to satisfy the view of the decisors who hold that he did not fulfill his obligation with what he ate while it was raining. This is when he has not yet lain down; but if he already lay down, he need not rise from bed, for if one who is distressed is obligated, he already fulfilled his obligation with his eating, and if one who is distressed is exempt, he need not rise from bed, as explained in seif 7. What the Rema wrote, "the first night," means that on the second night he recites kiddush in his house, recites Shehecheyanu over the Yom Tov, eats his meal, and at the end of the meal eats an olive's bulk in the sukkah, but does not recite "to sit in the sukkah" and does not recite Shehecheyanu over the sukkah; he relies on the Shehecheyanu that he recited on the first night. At the outset, it is good to wait a little on the second night too until the rain stops. If he ate in his house and only an olive's bulk at the end of the meal in the sukkah as we wrote, and afterward the rain stopped, he must enter the sukkah, eat the measure, namely more than an egg's bulk, and recite "to sit in the sukkah" [later authorities].
