A dish of beans. This dish spoils quickly with a small amount of rain.
Even if there is no cooked dish before him. So too the Tur wrote in the name of the Semag, and he wrote about it as follows: The Gemara does not imply this, for since it did not say "enough to spoil the congealed dish," but rather "from when the congealed dish is spoiled," it implies that it actually becomes spoiled. End quote. It seems to me that if it had said "enough to spoil," I would have explained that even one who sleeps in the sukkah has this measure, that he should not leave until the measure at which, if he were eating, the congealed dish would spoil; for the tanna said, "When rain fell, from when is it permitted to leave?" and the language also includes sleep. In truth, regarding sleep one does not need this measure, as the Rema writes nearby. Therefore it said "from when it is spoiled" to indicate that it speaks only of one who is eating, specifically, and not of sleeping. The Tur's view requires examination, for in the Gemara, in the baraita, we say that the tanna taught: from when a congealed dish of grits is spoiled. If it speaks of one who is eating a dish of grits, what novelty is there? It is obvious that if the food before him is spoiled, he is exempt. And what is the novelty of grits? Rather, perforce, even if he is not eating grits, we estimate as though he were eating grits; and nevertheless the tanna used the language "from when it is spoiled" and did not say "enough to spoil a dish of grits." We learn that this wording is not a precision at all. The Levush wrote that if the food spoils because of cold, he is exempt.
But the first night, etc. This implies not the second night. Its reason is as written in seif 3 in the name of Piskei Maharai, that we rely on the Semak and Or Zarua, who hold that even on the first night there is no obligation when rain fell. Therefore what is written here afterward, "and he should recite kiddush in the sukkah, etc.," requires examination, for it implies that he recites "to sit in the sukkah." This is difficult, for according to the Semak and Or Zarua it would be a blessing in vain, since there is no obligation at all when rain fell, even on the first night. Rather, it must be that it is clear to us that we hold like the Semag and Ashiri, that on the first night there is an obligation when rain fell, and there is no concern for a blessing in vain. If so, we should also be stringent on the second night, for one depends on the other. Therefore it appears that on the second night too there is an obligation to eat an olive's bulk when rain falls, but on the second night one should not recite "to sit in the sukkah" if rain fell. In Terumat HaDeshen, siman 95, he ruled that on the second night one need not wait except until the time of eating, and he should not recite kiddush in the sukkah, but rather recite kiddush at home and eat all he needs, and at the end eat an olive's bulk in the sukkah. It appears as I wrote, that he should not then recite the blessing over the sukkah, for perhaps he is entirely exempt when rain falls, as appears to my humble opinion.
