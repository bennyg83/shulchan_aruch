He reads and studies. Everything depends upon the circumstances. If he can study comfortably in the sukkah, he should study there. It requires study where he needs many sefarim and it is burdensome to bring them up to the sukkah. At first glance, the Gemara implies that we do not impose such a burden, as stated on page 26 regarding guards of gardens and orchards, and Ran there appears to agree. One may reject this proof, because there it would involve great effort to carry the materials out to the field, whereas bringing them from the house to the sukkah is not such a great burden. One should therefore be stringent. Levush in siman 640, seif 4, implies that he need not bring them there, just as he need not bring pillows. The Shulchan Aruch there likewise implies that for this reason he need not go to his fellow’s sukkah to eat.
He prays in the sukkah. This applies if he can pray there comfortably. If there is a synagogue in his city, he goes to the synagogue, as Bach writes in the name of Riaz.
