Even temporary sleep - Even if he places his head between his knees, where he certainly will not sink into sleep, it is also forbidden according to Rava's answer, since he is the later authority [Peri Megadim, and it is simple].
But it is permitted to eat a temporary eating - Even a Torah scholar, if he wishes not to be stringent upon himself in this, may do so; he is not considered one who is not meticulous in mitzvot [Ran, cited by the later authorities].
An egg's bulk of bread - But a little more is considered fixed [even if he ate it casually; Sha'arei Teshuvah]. So he ruled plainly above at the end of siman 232. This follows the view of Rashi, Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Ran, that the measure "like a student tasting and entering the study hall" is only the measure of an egg, for the throat cannot hold more than an egg's bulk; and not the view of the Rambam and R. Yitzchak ibn Giat, that even a little more than an egg's bulk is also included in temporary eating.
And wine - See Mishnah Berurah, that even if he established himself over it; so is proven in the Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe. Some disagree with this. I will briefly bring each view and its reason. The Gra brings a source for the view of the Shulchan Aruch from Berachot 36b, where it says explicitly regarding Birkat HaMazon that people do not establish a meal over wine; even if one did establish himself, his opinion is null in relation to everyone else. The same applies regarding sukkah. The proof brought by those who disagree from what is stated there on 42b, "they taught this only on Sabbaths and festivals, when it is common to establish a meal over wine," is no proof at all, for there the intent is at the end of the meal, and therefore it exempts wine after the food, as Rashi explains there; but not wine alone without eating, for there is no drinking without eating, as Hagahot Ashiri wrote. Likewise, the proof they bring from what is stated there on 43, that with bread and wine reclining helps to establish them together so that one may discharge his fellow's obligation, is also not such a proof, for one may say that because bread and wine are important, their reclining helps join them together, and through this one blesses for all; but that does not mean it is considered complete fixedness to obligate him in sukkah, for if so, let us say that members of a group who established themselves together to drink wine may thereby recite Birkat HaMazon. Now we will explain the view of those who disagree, and the proof they brought from the above. Although their proof can be rejected as above, nevertheless it seems to me that there is strong support for their view from what is stated in the Gemara, 28b: the Rabbis taught, "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live, etc.; one eats and drinks in the sukkah. So too Rashi explained at the beginning of chapter HaYashen that the essence of sitting in the sukkah is eating, drinking, and sleeping. In our sugya it implies that what Rabban Gamliel said regarding a bucket of water, "bring them up to the sukkah," was merely a stringency; perforce there it speaks regarding wine, which is important. So too it is proven from the Rambam's view, for he copied only that it is permitted to drink water outside the sukkah and did not mention wine at all; perforce, regarding wine one is obligated in sukkah. But one must say that just as with bread they obligated only fixed eating and not temporary eating, so too with wine in this manner. This is what is stated in the Mishnah: one may eat and drink temporarily outside the sukkah. This implies that fixed drinking is prohibited, and this means wine, for water is not important at all. This view does not accept the Ran's forced explanation that "temporary" refers only to eating, for that is a great forced reading. With God's help, I found a great tanna who says like my words, namely the Ritva in his novellae, who wrote explicitly that drinking a quarter-log of wine is considered fixed; see there. From Berachot 36 there is also no proof at all, for regarding sukkah, where we require "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live, the law is different, both leniently and stringently. That is, temporary eating, which is an egg's bulk, even according to those who hold generally that an egg's bulk obligates Birkat HaMazon by Torah law, is here exempt from sukkah, because in one's house too it is common to eat temporary eating outside his house, and the sages measured it as an egg's bulk. Likewise regarding drinking: although generally it is not considered fixedness to obligate him in Birkat HaMazon even if he drank several kav of wine, here, where we require the manner that one lives, and it is not the way to drink wine fixedly outside his house, he is therefore obligated in sukkah. So it seems correct to me, with God's help.
If he establishes himself over it, it is considered fixed - See Ma'amar Mordechai, who concludes that the matter depends on the individual: anything over which he establishes himself is considered fixed, even if it is not the measure over which people normally establish themselves. Although regarding pat haba'ah bekisnin it is explained above in siman 168 that we specifically require the measure over which people normally establish themselves, here, where we require "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live, whatever he establishes himself over is fixed enough to require a sukkah, and the matter depends on him; see there.
