Seif 2 It is permitted to eat a temporary eating. In Eliyah Rabbah, small paragraph 11, he cited in the name of the Tanya and Shibbolei HaLeket, who wrote in the name of R. Avigdor, that a temporary eating on Shabbat and Yom Tov is considered fixed. In Chiddushei Tzlach, Berachot 49a, in Tosafot s.v. "if he wishes," he wrote so by reasoning, that Tosafot's intent there is that since this olive's bulk is obligatory because of Yom Tov, it is not considered temporary, for its obligation makes it fixed; see there. However, according to this, after he has already fulfilled his obligation with the Yom Tov meal, whatever he eats afterward has the law of a temporary eating and does not require a sukkah unless it is more than an egg's bulk.
Magen Avraham, small paragraph 7 This is when he ate an egg's bulk, similar to temporary eating. It seems to me that his intent is that, according to his approach, since he proved unlike the Rosh, automatically with kinds of targima, when he eats more than an egg's bulk it is called fixed, and we do not require actual fixedness. By contrast, according to the Rosh the law is that we specifically require actual fixedness. This also appears from the Eliyah Rabbah's citation of the Magen Avraham's words. However, from the words of the Peri Megadim it seems that he understood the Magen Avraham's intent to prove that fixedness alone, if he eats less than an egg's bulk, does not require a sukkah; see there. In my humble opinion, the primary explanation is as I wrote.
