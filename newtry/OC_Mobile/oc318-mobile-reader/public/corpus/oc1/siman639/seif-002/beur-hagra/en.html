Seif 2: One may eat, etc. There.
And how much, etc. See Ba'al Halachot Gedolot: the measure of "a mouthful" is an egg's bulk, as written in the final chapter of Yoma. The proof is from what is stated there, 26a: "But is it not an egg's bulk, etc.? Let us say, etc."
And it is permitted to drink water. There: an incident that contradicts, etc.
And wine, and to eat, etc., and even, etc. Yoma 79b, because they are considered fruit, etc.; "Say that they ate them," etc.; "if you wish, say," etc. The reason is that a person does not establish himself over them, and their fixedness is not fixedness, as the Rosh wrote. And this is why it says "and wine," as written at the beginning of chapter 6 of Berachot: people do not establish themselves, etc.; if they did establish themselves, etc.
And a cooked dish, etc. From what is written in Sukkah and in Yoma there: if he completed with kinds of targima, he fulfilled his obligation; and in the Tosefta, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin: if kinds of targima were brought before him, he recites borei minei mezonot.
And it does not appear so, because, etc. As written in Arachin 3b; the Gemara obligated them only because it was not at the time of service. This is difficult from Rashi there, s.v. "and sons of service, etc.," which implies that he is obligated, for he wrote that although, etc. Rather, this means that what is stated there in the Gemara, "granted, they are exempt," etc., is an independent matter, and not because of "a man and his wife." See Tosafot, s.v. "it teaches us, etc." And this is the essential view.
