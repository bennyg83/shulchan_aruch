A temporary sleep. Because at times this amount of sleep suffices for a person, and it is therefore considered fixed.
And wine. Even establishing oneself to drink wine is not considered a fixed meal nowadays, as stated in siman 174, according to Darchei Moshe. According to our ruling there and in siman 213 that such a gathering is considered fixed, however, it requires a sukkah, and Levush rules likewise. Bach writes: “There is reason to say that an individual who sits to drink is not considered to have established a fixed gathering. A group that establishes itself to drink, however, constitutes a fully fixed gathering, especially in places where people customarily sit together for a fixed drinking session without any food. This is a fully fixed gathering, requires a sukkah, and requires the blessing ‘leishev basukkah.’ This appears correct as a matter of halachah. In practice, however, one should be stringent not to engage in fixed drinking except during a meal, thereby avoiding the dispute. The same applies to a fixed eating of meat, cheese, and similar foods.” The same applies to a fixed gathering for other beverages. It is explicit that one does not recite the blessing over drinking without a fixed gathering. Mateh Moshe, Mordechai, and Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Rosh rule likewise.
Even if he establishes himself to eat them. This is learned from Rosh, who writes: “It nevertheless appears evident there that even according to the opinion that fruit requires a sukkah, this applies when one establishes himself to eat fruit. The teaching is that a fixed meal of fruit is equivalent to a temporary eating of bread. No authority forbids a temporary eating of fruit.” Accordingly, the opinion that fruit does not require a sukkah means even where one establishes himself over it, and this is the accepted ruling. Rosh appears to maintain that even according to the opinion requiring a sukkah for fruit, one is not obligated upon eating an egg-volume alone, but only when he establishes his meal upon it. Otherwise fruit would have the same law as bread. He derives this from Yoma 79a, which states, “A temporary eating is permitted, but a fixed eating is not.” This requires study, for immediately beforehand the Gemara states, “If you would think that a large date is larger than an egg-volume,” which clearly implies that an egg-volume of fruit requires a sukkah according to the stringent opinion even without establishing a meal upon it. Otherwise the question proves nothing, for perhaps Rabban Gamliel ate them casually, like tasting a cooked dish. This requires study. In practice there is no consequence regarding fruit, because we rule that fruit does not require a sukkah even if one eats much and establishes himself over it, as also appears from the Gemara. There is nevertheless a consequence regarding cooked grain dishes, which require a sukkah when one establishes himself over them. This means that he ate an egg-volume, comparable to fruit according to the opinion that fruit requires a sukkah.
Because of the cold. Accordingly, one who lacks suitable pillows and blankets and is cold should not sleep there, as stated in the gloss to seif 7.
A man and his wife. This appears from the beginning of Arachin, where the Gemara says that kohanim are exempt while performing the service. This requires study, for Sukkah 28a initially suggests that “you shall dwell” means in the manner of ordinary residence: just as a home contains a man and his wife, so too a sukkah should contain a man and his wife. The Gemara rejects this because women are exempt. One may answer that the husband is nevertheless obligated in a sukkah only where he can reside there with his wife. Otherwise he is considered distressed and is exempt from sleeping there, as Shelah writes. This still requires study, because if he built the sukkah in a place where he cannot sleep, he has not fulfilled the mitzvah, as stated in the gloss to siman 640, seif 14. If he lacks a private sukkah, then, he should not fulfill the mitzvah at all. Furthermore, the Gemara says that all Israel could fit in a single sukkah. One must answer that because he could sleep there alone, the sukkah is valid as a residence. He is nevertheless personally exempt because he is considered distressed.
