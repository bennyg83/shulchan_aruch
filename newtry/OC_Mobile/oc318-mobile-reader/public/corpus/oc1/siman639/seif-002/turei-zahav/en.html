Even temporary sleep. For Rava said: there is no fixed measure for sleep, since sometimes one merely dozes a little and that suffices for him; therefore this is his sleep.
And it is permitted to drink water. In the Mishnah we say: there was an incident where they brought R. Yochanan ben Zakkai a cooked dish to taste, and Rabban Gamliel two dates and a bucket of water, and they said: bring them up to the sukkah. In the Gemara we say that this is what it means: if one comes to be stringent upon himself, he may be stringent, and it does not involve arrogance; and there was also an incident where they brought, etc.
And wine, etc. In the Rosh and Tur it is written: even if you say that fruit requires a sukkah, that is only one who establishes himself over them; but one who eats them casually certainly does not need a sukkah, and drinking wine is temporary. End quote. This implies that even if he established himself to drink wine, he is exempt. In Hagahot Ashiri, chapter 2 of Sukkah, it is written: However, drinking wine, honey-beverage, or beer together with fruit, or without fruit but only wine, and where he did not eat bread and relish, I cannot clarify whether it is permitted outside the sukkah or not, for the Merciful One said, "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live, and there is no drinking without eating; or perhaps sometimes people eat nuts and other fruit and establish themselves for drinking, and it is prohibited. End quote. The Levush wrote about drinking wine: this applies when he drinks to quench his thirst, but if he established himself to drink much, it is fixedness, as above in simans 174 and 212; therefore it seems that for sukkah too it is considered fixed and requires a sukkah. Regarding the blessing, it seems that one must bless over fixed drinking, since by law one should bless every time he enters, except that they fixed it over eating, as written nearby; if so, why should he not bless also over fixed drinking, just as one blesses over eating? End quote. This does not appear to me. One should not recite "to sit in the sukkah" at all over drinking, even fixed drinking, for even if you say that it requires a sukkah, nevertheless the blessing that he recites over eating exempts it entirely. The proof is from what Tosafot and the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Berachot: In a similar matter, R. Yitzchak asked Rabbeinu Tam whether one must bless over sleeping in a sukkah, for sleep is more stringent than eating, since it is permitted to eat a temporary eating outside the sukkah, but temporary sleep is forbidden outside the sukkah. R. Yitzchak would have wished to say that perhaps one cannot sleep, and when he falls asleep he cannot bless. But Rabbeinu Tam answered him that all mitzvot of sukkah that a person is obligated in from meal to meal, such as sleeping, strolling, and study, are exempted from blessing by the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" that he recited over the meal. End quote. You see that even sleep, which is more stringent than eating, is exempted by the eating blessing; all the more so drinking, whose obligation is not clear, does not need its own blessing, for the eating blessing exempts it. Even if he establishes himself to drink before eating, such as drinking spirits in these lands, nevertheless the blessing he will recite afterward at the time of eating, as written at the end of this siman, exempts it. Therefore one should bless only over eating, as written at the end of the siman.
And to eat fruit. The Rosh wrote that Rabbeinu Tam was stringent even with fruit, not to eat them outside the sukkah, because he held according to that version that fruit requires a sukkah, etc. I was astonished, for there are not two versions there in Yoma 79, but two answers to Rava's statement that fruit does not require a sukkah; and the one who does not answer there like Rava does not disagree with Rava about this. See there, and this requires examination. The Tur wrote in the name of R. Peretz that meat and cheese and the like require a sukkah, and the reason is from what the Gemara says, that according to R. Eliezer, if he completed his meal with kinds of targima, he fulfilled his obligation; this means meat and cheese. Afterward the Tur wrote in the conclusion: However, since it is not the way to establish oneself over them, even one who establishes his meal over them is not considered fixed, for it is no better than a temporary eating of bread, which does not require a sukkah. In some books the word "and water" appears instead of "however," and so is the text of the Beit Yosef, but it is a scribal error.
And it does not appear to me, because the mitzvah of sukkah, etc. This reason is difficult: if so, even eating would not be fulfilled unless he had a private sukkah, as the Mordechai wrote and the Rema brought in siman 640, that if one made a sukkah in a place where he is distressed in sleeping, he has not fulfilled even eating. It appears to my humble opinion to give a reason for the leniency regarding sleep: it is stated in the Gemara that agents of a mitzvah are exempt, meaning Rabbah bar Rav Huna and R. Chisda, who went to receive Rabban Gamliel's presence, slept outside the sukkah. So too one who is married to a woman and is obligated to gladden his wife on the festival, as above in siman 559, and women are exempt from sukkah: if he does not want to separate from his wife, it is called a mitzvah matter, and therefore he is exempt from the sukkah at night with respect to sleep. Even if his wife is not ritually pure, nevertheless there is joy when her husband is with her in the room, as stated in the Gemara that it is forbidden to sleep in a room where a man and his wife are sleeping; and Rav Yosef said: even with his menstruant wife, as it says, "the women of My people you drive out from their houses of delight." This appears to me to be the reason for the leniency for one who is lenient in this.
