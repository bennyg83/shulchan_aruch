A person who can reach a sukkah only by crossing a river in a boat on Yom Tov may not cross. If he crossed after the fact, where a mitzvah was involved and circumstances were unavoidable, in a pressing situation one may rely upon Rashba and Rabbeinu Yeshayah. See Responsa Maharam Yonah, siman 51, and what I wrote in siman 613, seif 6. Nevertheless, this requires study, for he is not obligated to go to his fellow’s sukkah, as stated in the gloss to siman 640, seif 4. But eating utensils. A pitcher used to draw water should remain outside the sukkah, as stated in the Gemara. Vessels in which flour is kept, kneading troughs, and spice utensils should also remain outside the sukkah, as stated by Mordechai, siman 730. If one brought eating utensils into the sukkah, the sukkah is not thereby invalidated, as Bach writes. See siman 666.

A degrading use. Such as washing pots and bowls, as Beit Yosef writes. This implies that there is no concern regarding cups. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies that pots and bowls have the same law: before eating they are permitted, while after eating they are forbidden because they are then repulsive. The common practice is to be careful not to bring in a pot even before eating, while people are not careful regarding a bowl even after eating. This practice is incorrect.
