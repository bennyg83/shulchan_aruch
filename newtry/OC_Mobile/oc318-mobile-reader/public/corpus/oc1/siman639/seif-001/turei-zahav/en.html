And sleeps and strolls. In Maharil it is written that a person who converses with his fellow should converse with him in the sukkah.
The lamp. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rokeach that one does not bring an earthenware lamp into the sukkah, but only an iron or copper lampstand, or a wax candle. End quote.
A small sukkah. Rashi explained: one of the exact minimum measure, seven handbreadths. This implies that the reason for the prohibition is that by this it reduces it from its measure, similar to the case of invalid roofing less than four handbreadths with open air next to it in siman 632, seif 3, which disqualifies a small sukkah. This is difficult for me: how is this different from the case in siman 630, seif 13, that if one supported the roofing on posts, etc., it is valid, since there are ten handbreadths from the ground to the roofing, and the bed does not reduce the measure since there is the measure if the bed were removed? All the more so here, if the lamp were removed. Further, if so, what is worse about a lamp than other utensils that may be brought into a sukkah, even a small one? It seems to me that it speaks of a lamp that is hanging and tied above in the roofing, like our lamp called a lampa; therefore the lamp reduces from the roofing by its own measure, and less than the measure remains. According to this, the prohibition is only when it is tied and the measure is exact. But Tosafot wrote on page 29 that the reason for a small sukkah is that we are concerned lest his sukkah burn. According to that, there is no distinction whether it is tied above or whether it is exactly seven handbreadths, except that it is proper to leave enough so that his sukkah not burn, as appears to my humble opinion.
Any degrading use. It appears that marital relations are not included in this, since they are the joy of a mitzvah. It is explicit in the Gemara, chapter HaYashen, that we do not make a wedding canopy in a sukkah because of the groom's distress, for he is embarrassed there to be playful with his bride, since it is a cramped place; this implies that it is permitted there.
