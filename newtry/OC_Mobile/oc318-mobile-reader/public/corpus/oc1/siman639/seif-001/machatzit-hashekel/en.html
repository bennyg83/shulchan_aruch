One who is not, etc. And if he transgressed after the fact, etc., there is basis to rely, etc. That is to say, since it is after the fact, and in a place of mitzvah and pressing need, one may rely on the decisors who permit it, and it is not called an act of prohibition. Therefore he may recite the blessing over the sukkah; for according to those who prohibit, one may not recite the blessing over the sukkah, for such a blessing is not blessing but blaspheming.
Rashbam and Rabbenu Yeshayah is how it should read, in my humble opinion. Their words were brought in the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 339, where he wrote in the name of Rashbam that it is permitted to enter a ship even on Shabbat, even if it afterward travels beyond the Shabbat boundary. And that which was taught, that one may not set sail on a ship less than three days before Shabbat, is Beit Shammai, and we do not rule according to that baraita. The Beit Yosef further brought there in the name of the Agur, who wrote in the name of Rabbenu Yeshayah, that he permitted and acted accordingly in Venice, traveling in a small ship on Shabbat. He stated the reason for the permission: the gentiles who steer the ship intend for themselves. End quote. All the decisors disagree with them; see there in his long work. Since the above case was already done, he holds that one may rely on them to be lenient.
Nevertheless, this requires examination, etc. As written in siman 640, etc., that where there is great exertion to enter his fellow's sukkah, they did not impose that exertion upon him. There is no greater exertion than having to cross by ship. If so, there are two difficulties: first, how did he write that in a place of mitzvah, etc., one may rely, etc.; what mitzvah is there, since in such a case he is exempt? Second, the practical difference is regarding the blessing, as above; but one who is exempt from the sukkah and sits in it is called a commoner, as written at the end of siman 639 in the gloss. If so, how may he recite the blessing here in this case?
