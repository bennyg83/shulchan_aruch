1) [Seif 1] What is the manner of the mitzvah of dwelling in the sukkah, etc. One who cannot reach a sukkah except by crossing a river in a boat on Yom Tov is forbidden to cross. If he crossed after the fact, however, in a situation involving a mitzvah, compulsion, and pressing need, one may rely upon the Rashbam and R' Yeshayah, of blessed memory. Responsa of Maharar Shmuel Yonah, siman 51; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, siman 640; Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. However, Chayei Adam, klal 146, sec. 48, permits this even ab initio when there is no other possibility, provided that a non-Jew transports the boat; see there. Nevertheless, the Beit Yosef, siman 339, cites the aforementioned words of the Rashbam and writes that the Rosh refuted them with several proofs, and Tosafos also refuted them. Likewise, the Beit Yosef there cites the aforementioned words of R' Yeshayah and writes that according to R' Yitzchak, Tosafos, and the Rosh it is certainly forbidden; see there. We have already written about this above, siman 613, seif 6; siman 586, sec. 144; and siman 339, sec. 74, citing the view of several poskim who forbid it. Take the ruling from there.
2) Ibid. What is the manner of the mitzvah of dwelling in the sukkah, etc. The reason we recite a blessing over the sukkah each time, whereas over matzah we recite a blessing only on the first night, is that through eating matzah one fulfills a positive commandment only on the first night, as it is written, 'In the evening you shall eat matzos.' Regarding sukkah, however, every moment that one dwells there he fulfills, 'You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days.' Derashos Maharil, Laws of Sukkah; Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, sec. 3; Magen Avraham at the end of the siman. See above, siman 475, secs. 70 and 71.
3) The reason we do not recite a blessing over sleeping, 'to dwell in the sukkah,' is that perhaps he will not fall asleep and the blessing will have been in vain. Rosh, final chapter of Sukkah and first chapter of Berachos. So too wrote the Tashbetz, siman 142. The Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that the blessing over the sukkah recited for eating exempts all other activities. Mateh Moshe, siman 914. Therefore, when one recites the blessing over the sukkah at the daytime meal, he should intend to exempt all dwelling, sleeping, casual eating, and drinking during the day from then until he recites the blessing at another meal. When he recites the blessing at night, he should intend to exempt the dwelling and sleeping of the night until he recites the blessing at the daytime meal.
4) Two brothers or partners who have no place to make a large sukkah and made a small sukkah that does not suffice for all of them: Regarding eating, each may eat alone, one after the other. Regarding sleeping, one sleeps one night and the other sleeps another night. If the mitzvah is dear to them, they are deserving of blessing if one sleeps part of the night and the other sleeps part of the night. If watchmen of gardens are exempt, all the more so one who has no place. Maharar Avraham Azulai in handwritten glosses, in the name of a responsum of Rav Hai Gaon; Birkei Yosef, sec. 1; Shaarei Teshuvah.
5) Ibid., Rama's gloss: And he sleeps and spends time there. One who wishes to discuss Torah teachings with his companions should go to the sukkah. Darkei Moshe, sec. 1, in the name of Maharil; Taz, s.k. 1. The Shelah wrote, folio 245a, that his teacher's custom was not to speak in the sukkah except words of Torah. Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 1. All the more so, one should not speak idle matters, for this is forbidden even outside the sukkah. Mishbetzos Zahav, sec. 1. Regarding playing with dice, see above, siman 338, sec. 39, where some wrote severe curses against one who plays any type of dice game, even on other days; see there.
6) It is fitting for every G-d-fearing person not to speak inside the sukkah during the days of the festival of Sukkos except matters of holiness, and not words of utter vanity, for the mitzvah of sukkah possesses great sanctity, as is known to all wise-hearted people. The proof is that sanctity rests even upon the wood of the sukkah, and it is forbidden throughout all eight days, as stated in the preceding siman. Kitzur Shelah, tractate Sukkah. See the Shach on the Torah, Parashas Emor, who wrote as follows: 'The seven righteous ones are alluded to as coming and dwelling in the sukkah. Therefore, one must sit in it with awe and fear, shame and humility. He should not be drawn after eating and drinking, lest he come to disgrace. He should not bring a non-Jew into it, for the sukkah is the shade of faith, and a non-Jew has no faith; then the holiness flees, and the seven righteous ones utter severe curses, etc.' See there. Beit David, siman 444, cited his words.
7) Ibid. And he dwells in the sukkah throughout all seven days, etc. For it is stated, 'You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days.' 'You shall dwell' means that you shall reside there, meaning that his primary dwelling should be in the sukkah and he should live there as a person lives in his home during the rest of the year. Levush.
8) The Zohar, Parashas Emor, 103b, writes as follows: 'Come and see. When a person dwells in this abode, the shade of faith, the Shechinah spreads Her wings over him from above, and Avraham, five other righteous ones, and King David establish their dwelling with him. This is what is written, 'You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days,' etc. A person must rejoice every day with a radiant countenance together with these guests who dwell with him, etc. So Rav Hamnuna Saba, when he entered the sukkah, would rejoice, stand at the entrance of the sukkah from within, and say, 'Let us invite the guests,' etc.' See there. Therefore, each night after lighting the lamps in the sukkah, one should stand at the entrance of the sukkah and say: 'For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah, etc., I hereby come to fulfill the positive commandment of dwelling in the sukkah, as it is written: 'You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days; every native in Israel shall dwell in sukkos, so that your generations shall know that I caused the Children of Israel to dwell in sukkos when I took them out of the land of Egypt; I am Hashem your God,' to rectify the root of this mitzvah and all the mitzvos included within it in the supernal realm. May the pleasantness, etc.'
8) Afterward he should say: 'Enter, fathers, exalted holy guests, Avraham, Yitzchak, Yaakov, etc.' On the first night he should say: 'Let Avraham, the exalted holy guest, enter, and with him let Yitzchak, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., enter.' On the second night, after saying, 'Enter, fathers, etc.,' as stated, he should again say: 'Let Yitzchak, the exalted holy guest, enter, and with him let Avraham, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., enter.' The same order is followed throughout all seven nights. This means to draw an illumination from Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferes, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and Malchus, which are designated by the names Avraham, Yitzchak, etc., into the aspect of the sukkah. Thus, the question will not trouble you as to how there can be many Avrahams, etc., who go to all the sukkos, as is known to those who know the hidden wisdom. I discussed this at length in a responsum. Afterward, he should say: 'Be seated, fathers, exalted holy guests, Avraham, Yitzchak, Yaakov, etc.' On the first night he should again say: 'Let Avraham, the exalted holy guest, be seated, and with him let Yitzchak, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., be seated.' On the second night he should say: 'Let Yitzchak be seated, etc., and with him let Avraham, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., be seated.' The same order is followed throughout all seven nights. Afterward, he should say: 'Fortunate is our portion, and fortunate is the portion of Israel, as it is written, 'For Hashem's portion is His people,' etc.,' as stated in the Zohar there.
8) Know that on the first night, when he says, 'Let Avraham enter,' he should intend to draw the encompassing light of Chesed within Chesed from the aspect of Binah to the aspect of Chesed of Malchus. On the second night, when he says, 'Let Yitzchak enter,' he should intend to draw the encompassing light of Chesed within Gevurah from Binah to Gevurah of Malchus. The same order applies: On the third night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Tiferes of Binah to Tiferes of Malchus; on the fourth night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Netzach; on the fifth night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Hod; on the sixth night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Yesod; and on the seventh night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Malchus of Binah to Malchus of Malchus. See above, siman 625, sec. 1, and analyze carefully. He should instruct that a chair be brought, with a splendid covering upon it, as a remembrance of the seven exalted holy Ushpizin, analogous to what is done on the day of a circumcision with a chair for Eliyahu HaNavi, may he be remembered for good. It is written in the holy Zohar, Parashas Lech Lecha, 93a, that if they do not prepare a chair for him, he does not come. This was the custom of those who tremble at the word of Hashem, to designate a chair for the seven exalted holy Ushpizin. Moed L'Chol Chai, sec. 289; Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 20, sec. 9. One should say, 'This is the chair in honor of the seven exalted holy Ushpizin,' just as one says concerning the chair of Eliyahu, as stated in the Zohar there. Although we wrote above that they allude to supernal lights, the chair is likewise symbolic.
