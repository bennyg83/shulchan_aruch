There in the Magen Avraham: Why do we not recite a blessing over matzah, etc., but rather because he does not eat chametz, etc. Meaning: the fact that he eats matzah is only because he cannot eat chametz, like one who eats the meat of a kosher animal because he cannot eat a non-kosher one, and he does not bless, "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to eat," for that eating is only for the needs of his body and to satisfy his hunger. But eating in the sukkah, although all seven days are optional, nevertheless, since the sitting is not for bodily need but for the purpose of a mitzvah, it is fitting to bless over it, "to sit in the sukkah." So wrote the Kol Bo in the name of the sage R. Shmuel Shekli, of blessed memory. It appears that this is also the intent of the Magen Avraham in the name of Maharil; see there. "But rather, because he does not eat chametz" means: because he does not eat chametz, which is forbidden to him, therefore he eats matzah for his bodily need. See Responsa Rashba, part 3, siman 286, at length, with reasoned explanations; see there.
