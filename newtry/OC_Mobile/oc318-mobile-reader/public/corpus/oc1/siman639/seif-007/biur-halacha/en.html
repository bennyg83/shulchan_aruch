And anyone exempt from the sukkah, etc. This rule applies specifically in a place where there is an element of prohibition, such as one who is distressed, for this is a desecration of Yom Tov, and even on Chol HaMoed one is obligated to honor it. But if he is not distressed, he may be stringent, like Rabban Gamliel, who was stringent upon himself with the bucket of water and said: bring them up to the sukkah [Pitchei Teshuvah in the name of Olat Shmuel, siman 98].
Commoners - See Mishnah Berurah. The book Bikkurei Yaakov wrote: It seems to me that what we say, that it is only an act of commoners and he receives no reward, applies specifically when he is exempt at the time he is sitting, such as when he is distressed and the like, especially in rain, since it is like a cup poured in his face, for his master showed him that he does not want his service, and he wants to force his master to be served by him; this is not proper conduct. But one who is exempt because of the burden of returning to the sukkah, such as when the rain stopped at night or in the middle of his meal, or one who would have to go to his fellow's sukkah, receives reward for doing so, for it is no worse than one who drinks water in the sukkah, about whom we say that he is praiseworthy, although he is exempt by law. All the more so here, where at the time of sitting he fulfills the mitzvah of sukkah properly, except that at the outset he was not obligated to go there. Therefore Hagahot Maimoni too wrote only: anyone exempt from the sukkah and does not leave, which implies that at the time of his sitting he is exempt. End quote.
