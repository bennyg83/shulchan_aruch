(39) He was sleeping and rain fell, etc. - The same applies when he was sleeping in his house at the beginning of the night because of rain, and it stopped [Chayei Adam].
(40) We do not burden him, etc. - This applies specifically when he has already lain down; but if he has not yet lain down, he should return to the sukkah [Magen Avraham]. In the book Bigdei Yesha, and likewise in Ma'amar Mordechai, they are uncertain about this after he already had the burden of bringing the pillows and blankets into the house; perhaps they did not burden him to return and bring them up.
(41) To return to the sukkah - The same applies to one who lies in a sukkah that has shutters, and during the rain he closed them, and afterward the rain stopped: it appears that he need not climb onto the roofing to open the shutters that he closed at the time of the rain. But if he can open them in the sukkah by means of cords, he must stand and open them, for this is only a small burden and is not called distress [Bikkurei Yaakov].
(42) He sleeps in his house until, etc. - Even if he wakes up, he need not go up to the sukkah during the night.
(43) And he awakens from his sleep - Meaning on his own; one need not wake him, and he is also not obligated to hand over his sleep to others so that they wake him when the day becomes light.
(44) And anyone who is exempt, etc. - Such as one who is distressed. The details of the laws of one who is distressed will be explained below in siman 640; see there.
(45) Commoners - All the more so this applies to one who wants to be stringent when rain is falling and go to the sukkah, recite hamotzi and the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" there, and then eat in the house; this is a blessing in vain, even if he eats a fixed eating there in the sukkah [later authorities].
