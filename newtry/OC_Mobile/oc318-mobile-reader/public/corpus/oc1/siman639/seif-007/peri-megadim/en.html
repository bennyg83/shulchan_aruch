And wine, etc. See Taz. In the Tur, one text has "and water," since it is not the way to establish oneself over water, even if he established himself it is temporary; and one text has "however" in place of "and water," since it is not the way to establish oneself, and this refers to meat and cheese; see well in the Bach what he wrote, and in the Beit Yosef about this. According to the Beit Yosef's text, "and water," it seems at first that with wine, when fixed, one requires a sukkah, for water is not commonly fixed, but wine, etc. One cannot say that wine fixedly drunk is like temporary bread and that one who wishes may be stringent, while water's fixedness is not even like temporary eating; for if so, how did he write "it is no better than temporary bread" (even if we say that Rabban Gamliel's stringency was because of the date; see Sukkah 27b). But according to the Bach's text one may say that wine, even fixedly drunk, is temporary. In practical halachah he concluded that one should not recite the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" over drinking, but should rely on the blessing he will recite with hamotzi. Nevertheless, a sukkah is required when one establishes himself to drink beer, mead, and the like, and it is forbidden to drink outside the sukkah. With God's help, in Magen Avraham letter 5 this will be explained further. In cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently; see Eliyah Rabbah 13, and see letter 8 further on this.
