8) The Zohar, Parashas Emor, 103b, writes as follows: 'Come and see. When a person dwells in this abode, the shade of faith, the Shechinah spreads Her wings over him from above, and Avraham, five other righteous ones, and King David establish their dwelling with him. This is what is written, 'You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days,' etc. A person must rejoice every day with a radiant countenance together with these guests who dwell with him, etc. So Rav Hamnuna Saba, when he entered the sukkah, would rejoice, stand at the entrance of the sukkah from within, and say, 'Let us invite the guests,' etc.' See there. Therefore, each night after lighting the lamps in the sukkah, one should stand at the entrance of the sukkah and say: 'For the sake of the unification of the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah, etc., I hereby come to fulfill the positive commandment of dwelling in the sukkah, as it is written: 'You shall dwell in sukkos for seven days; every native in Israel shall dwell in sukkos, so that your generations shall know that I caused the Children of Israel to dwell in sukkos when I took them out of the land of Egypt; I am Hashem your God,' to rectify the root of this mitzvah and all the mitzvos included within it in the supernal realm. May the pleasantness, etc.'
8) Afterward he should say: 'Enter, fathers, exalted holy guests, Avraham, Yitzchak, Yaakov, etc.' On the first night he should say: 'Let Avraham, the exalted holy guest, enter, and with him let Yitzchak, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., enter.' On the second night, after saying, 'Enter, fathers, etc.,' as stated, he should again say: 'Let Yitzchak, the exalted holy guest, enter, and with him let Avraham, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., enter.' The same order is followed throughout all seven nights. This means to draw an illumination from Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferes, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and Malchus, which are designated by the names Avraham, Yitzchak, etc., into the aspect of the sukkah. Thus, the question will not trouble you as to how there can be many Avrahams, etc., who go to all the sukkos, as is known to those who know the hidden wisdom. I discussed this at length in a responsum. Afterward, he should say: 'Be seated, fathers, exalted holy guests, Avraham, Yitzchak, Yaakov, etc.' On the first night he should again say: 'Let Avraham, the exalted holy guest, be seated, and with him let Yitzchak, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., be seated.' On the second night he should say: 'Let Yitzchak be seated, etc., and with him let Avraham, Yaakov, Moshe, etc., be seated.' The same order is followed throughout all seven nights. Afterward, he should say: 'Fortunate is our portion, and fortunate is the portion of Israel, as it is written, 'For Hashem's portion is His people,' etc.,' as stated in the Zohar there.
8) Know that on the first night, when he says, 'Let Avraham enter,' he should intend to draw the encompassing light of Chesed within Chesed from the aspect of Binah to the aspect of Chesed of Malchus. On the second night, when he says, 'Let Yitzchak enter,' he should intend to draw the encompassing light of Chesed within Gevurah from Binah to Gevurah of Malchus. The same order applies: On the third night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Tiferes of Binah to Tiferes of Malchus; on the fourth night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Netzach; on the fifth night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Hod; on the sixth night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Yesod; and on the seventh night, the encompassing light of Chesed within Malchus of Binah to Malchus of Malchus. See above, siman 625, sec. 1, and analyze carefully. He should instruct that a chair be brought, with a splendid covering upon it, as a remembrance of the seven exalted holy Ushpizin, analogous to what is done on the day of a circumcision with a chair for Eliyahu HaNavi, may he be remembered for good. It is written in the holy Zohar, Parashas Lech Lecha, 93a, that if they do not prepare a chair for him, he does not come. This was the custom of those who tremble at the word of Hashem, to designate a chair for the seven exalted holy Ushpizin. Moed L'Chol Chai, sec. 289; Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 20, sec. 9. One should say, 'This is the chair in honor of the seven exalted holy Ushpizin,' just as one says concerning the chair of Eliyahu, as stated in the Zohar there. Although we wrote above that they allude to supernal lights, the chair is likewise symbolic.
