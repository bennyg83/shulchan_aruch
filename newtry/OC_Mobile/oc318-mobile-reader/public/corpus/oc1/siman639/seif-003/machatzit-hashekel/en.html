(Small paragraph 3) Use, etc. It implies that cups are permitted; this is how it should read. Meaning: since he specifically mentioned pots and bowls, it implies that cups are permitted, because of "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live; and cups are commonly washed in a dwelling-house. There is no repulsiveness in them, for drinking utensils do not contain repulsive matters.
It implies, etc. And the common practice, etc., is not correct. Meaning: the fact that people are not careful with a bowl, even after eating, is not correct, for according to the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, bowls too are forbidden after eating. But regarding the fact that they are careful with pots even before eating, one may defend them, for they are stringent according to the view of the "some explain" cited by the Beit Yosef in the name of the Mordechai: the reason for the prohibition of a pot in the sukkah is that such things are not normally in a dwelling-house, and it is not because of repulsiveness but because of "you shall dwell" in the manner that you live. If so, even before eating it is forbidden. But after eating, although the Birkei Yosef cited in the name of the Ra'avyah that bowls are permitted, nevertheless we do not have license to be lenient against the Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch, who ruled not like him. In any case, whether before eating or after eating, whether a pot or a bowl, we have only the ruling of the Beit Yosef in Shulchan Aruch, and by its light we shall walk.
