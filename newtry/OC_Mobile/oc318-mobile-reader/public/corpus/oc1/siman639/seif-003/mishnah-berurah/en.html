(19) On the first night of Yom Tov it is obligatory - Meaning that on the first night of Yom Tov he is obligated to eat in the sukkah, even if he eats only an olive's bulk. From that night onward, however, even on Shabbat and Yom Tov, when he is obligated to eat bread, he is not obligated to eat in the sukkah unless he eats more than an egg's bulk, which is a fixed eating. The reason is that this night is more stringent, for we derive "the fifteenth" from "the fifteenth" of the festival of matzah: just as there only the first night is obligatory and from then onward optional, so too here regarding sukkah.
(20) An olive's bulk - Since we derive it from the festival of matzah, we derive it completely: just as there it is with an olive's bulk of grain, so too here with an olive's bulk of grain.
(21) Bread - And not kinds of targima, for this is a doubt in a Torah matter; and not pat haba'ah bekisnin, but actual bread [Sha'arei Teshuvah].
(22) He fulfilled his obligation - Although on the other days the measure of eating outside the sukkah is an egg's bulk, the first night is more obligatory; even if he wants to eat only an olive's bulk, it is forbidden to eat it outside the sukkah, and therefore he also fulfills his sukkah obligation with it and must recite "to sit in the sukkah" over it. This is when he has no more, but if he has more, it is certainly proper that he eat more than an egg's bulk, in order to satisfy also the view of those who hold that to fulfill the positive commandment one must eat the measure that obligates eating in the sukkah all seven days, namely more than an egg's bulk. The later authorities wrote that he must not delay in eating the olive's bulk longer than the time of eating a peras.
(23) All seven, etc. - This refers to Chol HaMoed, but on Shabbat and Yom Tov one must eat an olive's bulk of bread, though it does not require sukkah. Although in siman 291 the Magen Avraham wrote that for a Shabbat meal one also needs an egg's bulk and more so that it be a fixed meal, there that is only for the mitzvah at the outset, and after the fact an olive's bulk suffices [later authorities].
(24) Like the law of eating matzah on Pesach - I was uncertain, according to the well-known view of the Gra that there is a mitzvah to eat matzah all seven days of Pesach, according to the plain meaning of the verse, "seven days you shall eat matzot," except that the positive commandment is only in the evening, whereas afterward it is merely a mitzvah. Perhaps the same applies here: at the outset there is a mitzvah to eat bread all seven days and to recite "to sit in the sukkah."
(25) Definitely night - For eating in the sukkah on the first night is comparable to eating matzah on the night of Pesach, about which it is written, "in the evening you shall eat matzot," meaning after it becomes dark. If he recited the blessing during twilight and ate an olive's bulk, he must go back and eat an olive's bulk at definite night, but he should not recite the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" a second time, for in cases of doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently.
(26) Before midnight - Like matzah, where we require that he eat it before midnight, as is evident in siman 477, seif 1. After the fact, if he did not eat before midnight, he is obligated to eat after midnight, for this is also the law with matzah, as we wrote in siman 477. He may recite "to sit in the sukkah," except that then his law is like the other days of Sukkot, that one does not recite "to sit in the sukkah" until he eats more than an egg's bulk.
(27) From midday onward - The later authorities agreed in practice that it is forbidden only from the beginning of the fourth hour after midday, as the Mechaber ruled in siman 471; see there. All the details of the laws explained there regarding things that prevent one from eating at night with appetite apply here as well.
