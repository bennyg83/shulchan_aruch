On the first night of Yom Tov it is obligatory - See Mishnah Berurah. This is from the Ran, in order to resolve what all the decisors wondered: after all, on Shabbat and Yom Tov too there is an obligation to eat bread, and automatically there is a Torah mitzvah to eat in the sukkah, so what is the novelty of this night? In my humble opinion, I do not understand this question. There is a practical difference for what is stated above in siman 288: a person who is accustomed to fast every day, and for whom eating on Shabbat is distress, is exempt from eating, because Shabbat was given for delight and not for distress. This is not so on the first night of Sukkot, where the eating itself is a positive commandment like other positive commandments, and not because it was given for delight.
And he should not eat, etc. Kiddush could have been made a little before nightfall over wine, meaning that he would drink the whole cup in the place of a meal; this is only according to the Shulchan Aruch, that he may drink even a quarter-log of wine outside the sukkah and need not recite a blessing even when sitting in the sukkah. Hamotzi and the blessing "to sit in the sukkah" he would say at night. However, since he says the sukkah blessing and then Shehecheyanu, automatically he must make kiddush at night as well [Magen Avraham]. According to this, on the second night, according to those who hold that he may say Shehecheyanu and afterward the sukkah blessing, he may make kiddush a little before nightfall and also say Shehecheyanu, while hamotzi and "to sit in the sukkah" he says at night [Peri Megadim]. However, from the language of the Rema, who wrote "the first night," it implies that on the second night it is permitted to eat during twilight, because it is rabbinic, for we are expert in the fixing of the month, similar to counting during twilight, where several decisors are lenient for this reason. It appears that the Peri Megadim held that what he wrote, "the first night," means in the Land of Israel, and the same applies for us outside the Land on the second night, similar to siman 475, seif 7; see there. One may distinguish from counting, for there the essential counting nowadays is rabbinic, unlike the second day of Yom Tov.
