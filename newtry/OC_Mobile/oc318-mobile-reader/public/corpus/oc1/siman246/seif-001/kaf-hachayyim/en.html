1) [Seif 1] It is permitted to lend or rent out one's utensils, etc. According to Maran's unattributed opinion, there is no distinction: whether the utensils are used for labor or are not used for labor, it is permitted to lend or rent them out even on Friday. According to the opinion introduced as 'some say,' utensils used for labor may not be rented out on Friday, because the non-Jew appears to be acting as the Jewish person's agent, but utensils not used for labor are permitted. The requirement that rental payment be subsumed within payment for other days, however, applies even to utensils not used for labor, and all opinions agree to this, as Beis Yosef writes; see there. See below, paragraph 8.
2) There: Or rent out one's utensils, etc. If it is publicly known that the item belongs to a Jewish person and it is in a public place, it is forbidden to rent it out where sharecropping is not customary, as in siman 243. Magen Avraham and Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1.
3) There: Some say that utensils used for labor, etc. Some say that even utensils not used for labor, such as a robe or a tallis, may not be rented to a non-Jew on Friday, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Tosafos and Ran; see there. Although Maran and the halachic authorities permit this, when possible one should be stringent, for one who fears Heaven should satisfy all opinions.
4) There: Such as a plow, etc. This is not exclusive; the same applies to a vat used for making beer. Only a robe and a tallis are permitted. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1. See Shabbos 18b.
5) There: It is forbidden to rent them to a non-Jew on Friday. One may, however, rent even utensils used for labor to a Jewish person, even on Friday, provided the payment is subsumed within payment for other days. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. Although the Jewish person will certainly perform no labor with it on Shabbos, subsuming the payment is required because he will nevertheless use it for an activity that is not labor. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
