[1] Concerning the resting of, etc. Beit Yosef states this in the name of all the poskim, including the Rosh in the first chapter of Shabbos. This requires study, for at the end of the chapter Ein Bein HaMudar it appears that the Rosh holds that one is commanded concerning the resting of one's utensils. The same appears from the Rosh's rulings and his abridged rulings there. Bach likewise writes in the name of the Rokeach and concludes that every God-fearing person should be stringent for himself. This applies, however, only to utensils that perform an action, such as a mill or a trap whose flap closes and catches a bird, and similar cases. Bach's statement that even a non-Jew's mill is forbidden because of the sound it produces is contradicted by the Gemara's discussion, as is evident to one who examines it. His claim that Sefer HaTerumos says the same is also imprecise, for siman 220 explicitly states, “People will say that the mill belongs to so-and-so,” showing that it concerns a Jew's mill. This is also evident elsewhere there. Raavan, siman 339, writes that the command that utensils rest applies when a Jew causes work to be performed with the utensils, such as placing wheat in a mill before Shabbos so that it will grind on Shabbos. This resolves Tosafos' questions in Shabbos 18a, s.v. velo, and 19a, s.v. lo.
