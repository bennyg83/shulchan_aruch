We rely on the non-Jew's fear of being caught even regarding a biblical prohibition, as is clear at the beginning of Chullin concerning slaughter and similar matters, and not only regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine, which is rabbinic. Yet lending is prohibited even when he stipulates with the non-Jew, and even if he knows he could take the animal back, as the Bach explains in his first answer and Eliyah Rabbah writes. See siman 305:23. The Magen Avraham requires study: even if no additional rent is paid for Shabbat, and even in a loan, it should be prohibited because the Israelite is commanded regarding the animal's rest, and rental certainly does not transfer ownership biblically. There is therefore no double doubt. The Magen Avraham's statement that rental transfers ownership stringently is only rabbinic. This will be explained there; see Eliyah Rabbah. With a riding horse, however, there may be no prohibition of resting the animal because a living person carries himself; the concern is only that the non-Jew may take it beyond twelve mil. Since whether twelve mil is biblical is disputed, there may be a double doubt if he stipulates that the animal rest on Shabbat. But if the non-Jew later adds rent for Shabbat, accepting it is prohibited even by inclusion. This requires study and will be explained below. The source cited from siman 52:5 raises a difficulty from here; it will be explained there whether that case is biblical.
