A Jew's animal. If a Jew owns a horse and a ruler who knows him comes to borrow or rent it, and the Jew cannot refuse him because of possible hostility, he should sell it to him for a small sum. The ruler may then use it as he wishes. If afterward he returns the horse and also pays rent, these are merely gifts. Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 39; see there. The author of Yad Aharon disagrees and writes: It appears to me that if the non-Jew may sell it, then even though it was sold for a small sum it is permitted; but if he may not sell it, it is forbidden, since it remains regarded everywhere as a Jew's animal. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek likewise rules so; see there. The preferable course is to declare it ownerless.
