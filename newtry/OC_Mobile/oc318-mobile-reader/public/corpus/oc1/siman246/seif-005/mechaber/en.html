If a Jew and a non-Jew are partners in an animal, the non-Jew may perform melacha with it on Shabbos if, at the outset when they acquire it, the Jew stipulates with the non-Jew that the non-Jew will take its use on Shabbos and the Jew on a weekday. If they did not stipulate at the outset, it is forbidden even if they stipulate afterward. If the Jew lends the animal to the non-Jew as a complete loan, so that the non-Jew has the authority to remove it if he wishes without the Jew's permission, the price is charged to the non-Jew, and responsibility for the animals rests upon the non-Jew, it is permitted. Some permit it even where the non-Jew does not have authority to remove them, provided that the price is charged to the non-Jew as a debt and the non-Jew then designates them as an apotiki to the Jew, meaning that payment will be collected only from these animals, or pledges them to him as collateral, provided that he does not say that the transfer takes effect 'from now.' Some also permit it if the Jew warns the non-Jew not to perform melacha with the animal on Shabbos, and stipulates that if he transgresses and does so, responsibility for it, even for unavoidable accidents, will rest upon the non-Jew, and this is recorded in their courts. In that event, when the non-Jew performs melacha with it on Shabbos it is not regarded as the Jew's animal, because the non-Jew has acquired responsibility for its unavoidable loss. {Rama: All these avenues of permission are accepted halacha, and one may use whichever of them he wishes. Even if the animal belongs entirely to the Jew, its law is as though it were jointly owned with the non-Jew, provided that he publicizes that he arranged the matter in a permitted manner.}
