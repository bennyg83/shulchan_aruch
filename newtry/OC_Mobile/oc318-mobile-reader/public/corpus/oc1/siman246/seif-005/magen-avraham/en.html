By stipulating with the non-Jew. They may afterward divide equally, as stated in siman 245:2. Even when responsibility is shared equally throughout all the weekdays, it is permitted. [R. Levi ibn Habib.] If they did not stipulate initially, but only after becoming partners one says, “You take, etc.,” then although there is no prohibition of the animal’s rest because the non-Jew owns a share, it is nevertheless forbidden, because the Jew takes one weekday in exchange for Shabbat, constituting separate Shabbat payment. It would be more permissible not to say, “You take, etc.,” but simply to divide the proceeds of the entire week together. [Beit Yosef at the end of siman 245 in the name of Rivash; likewise R. Levi ibn Habib at the end of responsum 106.] Nevertheless, an unstipulated equal division is permitted only in a case of substantial loss. [Darkei Moshe.] See siman 245:1 in the gloss.
He should leave them with him as a pledge. Meaning: even if the animals remain in the Jew’s possession as collateral, and the non-Jew takes them and works with them, it is permitted. Since the Jew did not say “from now,” he did not acquire them, as stated at the end of siman 441; see there. This remains so even if he said, “If I do not pay you by a specified time, they shall be yours.”
And he should write this. This is in order to publicize the matter.
Even if the animal is entirely his. If the horses belong to the Jew and the non-Jew is also his employee and transports his merchandise, a sale provides no permission, because the Jew may not allow him to perform his work. The exception is when the non-Jew is hired only to travel with the horses; then it is permitted according to Rambam, as stated in siman 244:5. In any event, it is forbidden to tell him to go on Shabbat, as stated in siman 252. Beit Yosef holds that when the animal belongs entirely to the Jew, the non-Jew’s acceptance of responsibility is ineffective, as stated in seif 4, while Rama rules leniently.
