Seif katan 2: On Friday, because he appears to be the Jew's agent. Rabbeinu Yonah wrote this reason to exclude Tosafos' view. Tosafos write that even when the rent is included with payment for other days, since the rental begins close to Shabbos it appears to be payment for Shabbos. According to that reasoning, even utensils with which no labor is performed would be forbidden. Rabbeinu Yonah challenges this: what difference would there then be between renting to a non-Jew and renting to a Jew? According to that reason, even renting to a Jew on Friday would be forbidden despite inclusion with other days. Rabbeinu Yonah therefore explains that included payment does not constitute Shabbos payment, even when the rental begins on Friday. Rather, the prohibition applies when the non-Jew performs labor with it on Shabbos and the Jew receives rent from him, even as part of an inclusive payment, because it appears that the non-Jew is the Jew's agent to perform labor with it so that he will pay the Jew the rent, as will be explained. Renting to a Jew is permitted because the Jew will not perform labor with it on Shabbos. Likewise, renting to a non-Jew utensils with which no labor is performed is permitted, because he cannot be viewed as the Jew's agent. Lending it without payment is also permitted, because without payment he cannot be viewed as the Jew's agent. Renting it on Wednesday or Thursday is permitted because, since the rental did not begin close to Shabbos, he does not appear to be the Jew's agent. The Tur writes that this also explains why siman 252 rules in accordance with Beis Hillel that one may give hides to a tanner and clothing to a launderer close to nightfall on Friday, even if the non-Jew performs the work on Shabbos. There the Jew gains nothing from the non-Jew's working on Shabbos, so he does not appear to be his agent. Here, however, the Jew benefits from the work performed on Shabbos, because he receives rent for the Shabbos day as well. Although the payment is included with other days, it is nevertheless known that if the Jew did not permit the non-Jew to use it on Shabbos, the non-Jew would not pay as much rent as he pays when permitted to use it on Shabbos too.
