Because an observer will assume that the Israelite instructed him to carry it out. The Beit Yosef, citing Sefer HaTerumah, states that the concern is that he will carry it outside the house into the public domain, and observers will not know that the non-Jew is carrying it for his own need. This implies that where there is no public domain, there is no prohibition, as stated in siman 355 regarding a non-Jew who drew water for his animal, where a distinction is made between a public domain and a karmelit. If so, nowadays, when we do not have a true public domain, as the Tur writes in siman 355, there should be no prohibition against lending him a utensil with which no labor is performed, even on Shabbat. Although we rule that instructing a non-Jew is a rabbinic prohibition even regarding an act prohibited only rabbinically, as explained above in this siman, here there is merely a decree based on appearance, and perhaps the Sages did not decree in such a case. Yet since the authorities did not distinguish on this basis, it appears that the prohibition applies everywhere. Nevertheless, if the non-Jew lives within an area where carrying is permitted, it is certainly allowed.
