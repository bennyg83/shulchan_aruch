Seif katan 12. “Rather, it is prohibited rabbinically” means that an Israelite is prohibited to place a load upon the animal because this is a weekday activity. Consequently, when the non-Jew does so for his own purpose, there is no prohibition at all. Shibbolei HaLeket therefore writes that if the animal was transferred to the non-Jew before the festival, then even if he rides it and carries a load upon it, the Israelite need not protest. See the Beit Yosef, siman 305. There is no concern of resting one's animal, because the Israelite himself may carry on Yom Tov. He brings proof from Rashba's statement that within a courtyard there is no prohibition of resting one's animal. This shows that whenever the labor is not prohibited, its performance by one's animal is likewise permitted.
