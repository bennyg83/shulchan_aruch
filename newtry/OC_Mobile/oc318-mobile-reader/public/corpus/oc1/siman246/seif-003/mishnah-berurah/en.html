(10) It is forbidden to rent out, etc. - Even on the first day of the week, and even by inclusion.
(11) In order that it do, etc. - It should read: lest he do with it, as Gra writes, and so is proven from the Shulchan Aruch itself.
(12) Regarding the resting of his animal - For it is written, "so that your ox and your donkey may rest," etc. Although he rented the animal to a non-Jew, we hold that rental does not acquire, so it remains the animal of the Jew and he is commanded regarding its rest. Some say that stringently we say rental does acquire; therefore, if a Jew rented an animal from a non-Jew, he is commanded regarding its rest. It is proper to be stringent, since this is a Torah prohibition. If he rented it from a non-Jew and then rented it back to a non-Jew, it is permitted whichever way one looks at it. If he bought an animal from a non-Jew or sold it to a non-Jew with meshikhah alone or money alone, this is a doubtful law, and we follow stringency regarding the resting of an animal, as Tosefet Shabbat writes.
(13) The non-Jew is not believed - Nor is he afraid to do labor with it, since it is in his possession by borrowing or rental, and he will not be caught as a thief because of this. This is unlike the first case, where he stipulates that he return it before Shabbat, for the borrowing or rental has already ended before Shabbat. See below siman 305 seif 23, as the Acharonim write.
(14) He should declare it ownerless, etc. - This is specifically after the fact, where he already stipulated with him and the non-Jew detained it on Shabbat. But initially it is forbidden to rent it with the intention that when Shabbat arrives he will declare it ownerless; even if he declares it ownerless before three people, not everyone knows of the hefker, and it may lead to suspicion, for people will say that a Jew's animal is doing labor on Shabbat. Even with a sale, there is no permission except when he sells his animal to the non-Jew as a complete sale for all the days of the week, not when he sells it to him for Shabbat alone, because of suspicion, as above. Bach permits initially to rent it on the condition that he will declare it ownerless before three people, for since three know, the matter becomes public and will not lead to suspicion. In pressing circumstances perhaps one may rely on his words. At the end of the siman we will explain, God willing.
(15) Between himself and himself - Then he is not commanded regarding its rest, since it is not his, for he already declared it ownerless. Although hefker requires being before three people, that is rabbinic; but by Torah law even between himself and himself suffices. Here, where he already rented it and stipulated with him that he return it, and the non-Jew detained it, this is enough to be saved from a Torah prohibition. See Beit Yosef, for there are poskim who hold that even here, although it is after the fact, specifically three are required. Therefore it is good to be careful about this initially if possible. In any case, he should be careful to declare it ownerless before one person, for many of the great poskim hold that even by Torah law it is not hefker until he declares it ownerless at least before one person, and even one of his household suffices.
(16) His animal is acquired by the non-Jew - See Magen Avraham, whose view is that this is specifically if he says to him in his presence, "My animal is acquired by you," for then the non-Jew intends to acquire it and it is acquired to him by this statement, since it is in his possession and his courtyard acquires for him. But if he says this not in his presence, the non-Jew does not acquire it at all. Even if he transfers it to him through another Jew, this also does not help, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 243 seif 14. Peri Megadim and the other Acharonim agreed with Magen Avraham. It is obvious that even when he says, "My animal is acquired by you," there must also be a fixed price for it, for otherwise it is not a sale at all; so wrote Chayei Adam, klal 58.
(17) So that he be saved, etc. - Meaning, initially one should not rely on renting with the intention that he will declare it ownerless when Shabbat arrives, or that he will then transfer ownership to the non-Jew, for the hefker and transfer are not publicized, and there is concern for marit ayin, as above. See the end of the siman.
(18) For certainly his intention is not - Meaning, certainly he did not intend that it remain ownerless forever, but only for Shabbat, in order to remove from himself the Shabbat prohibition; after Shabbat it will return to his domain. But on Shabbat itself it is certainly complete hefker. This too is called hefker, as stated in the Gemara, that even hefker for one day is hefker. One need not be concerned that another will acquire it on Shabbat itself, for the non-Jew will certainly not allow that as long as it is in his hand for his labor.
(19) A person is not commanded, etc. - For the resting of one's animal is learned from the verse "so that your ox and your donkey may rest like you," and that verse is written regarding Shabbat. Nevertheless, he should rent them only by inclusion, so there not be payment for Yom Tov. Maharshal disagrees with this, and his view is that Yom Tov and Shabbat are equal in this matter, as in all labors. See Magen Avraham, who discusses this at length and brings that Beit Yosef in siman 495 cited the views of the Rishonim about this and inclines there to prohibit. Therefore one should be stringent. In Biur HaGra he also discusses this at length and concludes that the essential law is like Maharshal. Also below in siman 495 seif 3, Gra explains that what is written there in Shulchan Aruch, that one may not carry a load on an animal on Yom Tov, is because of the resting of one's animal.
