1) [Seif 1] One who has pain in his loins may not apply oil and vinegar. The Mishnah at the end of chapter 8 of Sheratzim reads, 'He may not apply wine and vinegar,' and this is the version in all the books available to us. Rif and Rambam, chapter 21, halachah 23, likewise have this reading. Tur, however, writes 'oil and vinegar.' It appears that this was the version before him, as Bach writes. Shulchan Aruch follows Tur's version. A practical difference is that according to the version 'wine and vinegar,' each substance is forbidden even by itself, as Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, writes. According to Tur and Shulchan Aruch's version, 'oil and vinegar,' oil is forbidden only when mixed with vinegar, but oil alone is permitted according to all opinions. It appears that even according to Tur's version, one may not apply wine, since wine resembles vinegar and is applied only for medicinal purposes. According to our version, it is obvious that oil mixed with vinegar or wine mixed with oil is forbidden. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, paragraph 1.
2) There: He may not apply oil and vinegar. These substances are forbidden because the Sages prohibited medical treatment on Shabbos as a decree lest one grind medicinal ingredients, as stated at the beginning of siman 328. It is forbidden even through a non-Jew when the person is able to go about like a healthy individual, as stated in siman 328, seif 1. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1.
3) There: He may not apply oil and vinegar. If he has no pain, it is permitted. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2. See below, siman 328, seif 37.
4) There: But he may apply oil alone. This is permitted even upon a wound, as stated below in siman 328, seif 22. This applies where people ordinarily apply oil for nonmedical purposes. In a place where people apply oil only medicinally, it is forbidden, as stated below in the gloss.
