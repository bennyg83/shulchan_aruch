The Levush wrote that some say one also interrupts for the Amen at the end of Shemoneh Esrei. The Malbushei Yom Tov wrote: Since we do not say it at the end, I do not know who they are. It seems to me that we rely on the Amen after Oseh Shalom. End quote. So too the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Lechem Chamudos. This does not appear correct, for after Oseh Shalom one says 'and say Amen' to others, not that he himself says it. Even when one prays alone, nevertheless one may say that he says it to the angels appointed to guard him, as written in Mateh Moshe. But the former custom was that one himself said Amen after Shemoneh Esrei, as by Birkas HaMazon. Therefore we take the position that one interrupts to answer Amen at the end of the last three blessings. The Rama wrote: for all these matters one interrupts all the more so while saying supplications. End quote. The Olas Tamid wrote that one may also learn all the more so that he may interrupt in Pesukei D'Zimrah. It appears that the Levush omitted it because he holds it obvious and unnecessary to state. It also seems that even for other Amens one interrupts during supplications, and likewise for 'yisbarach' and 'yishtabach,' etc., and Modim D'Rabbanan. Perhaps the same applies in Pesukei D'Zimrah, and it is somewhat implied in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 3. See above at the end of siman 65 and siman 51; this requires study.
