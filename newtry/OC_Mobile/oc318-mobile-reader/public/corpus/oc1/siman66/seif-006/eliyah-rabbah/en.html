[6] Modim alone, etc. — the explanation: modim anachnu lecha; and in barukh one says Barukh the Omnipresent haMevorakh le'olam va'ed. Levush wrote: some say one interrupts also for Amen at the end of the eighteen blessings. Malbushei Yom Tov wrote we do not say it at the end — I did not know who they are; and it seems to me we rely on Amen after oseh shalom — until here; and so wrote Magen Avraham in the name of Lechem Chamudot. And it is not clear: after oseh shalom one says ve'ameru amen for others and not what they said themselves; and even one praying alone — nevertheless one may say thus to the angels commanded to guard him, as he wrote in Mateh Moshe; but the custom that was earlier was to say Amen oneself after the eighteen blessings as in Birkat HaMazon; therefore we used the formulation that one may interrupt for answering Amen at the end of the last three. Rama wrote: and for all these matters we interrupt, all the more so when saying supplications — until here; and Olah Tamid wrote one should learn likewise in 'all the more so' that one may interrupt in pesukei dezimra — until here. And it seems Levush omitted this because it is plain and need not be said; also it seems even for other Amens one interrupts in supplications, and likewise yitbarakh, yishtabach, etc., and modim derabbanan; and it is possible the law is the same in pesukei dezimra; and somewhat so implies Terumat HaDeshen siman 3; and see above end of siman 65 and siman 51 — requires investigation; and see below siman 135 se'if 20.
