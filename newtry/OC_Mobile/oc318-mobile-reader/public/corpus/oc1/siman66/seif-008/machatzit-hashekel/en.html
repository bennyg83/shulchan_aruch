On the plain meaning of the Zohar. Meaning: in siman 141 Maharil wrote that from the words of the poskim it is clear that the one called to the Torah should also read with the prayer leader who reads — for if he does not read with him his blessing is in vain. But in the Zohar it is written that only one reads. If so, it implies the one called up does not read with the reader; and if so, even though everywhere that the Gemara disagrees with the Zohar we hold like our Gemara, nevertheless since this law is not explained in the Gemara but only in the words of poskim, one may rule like the Zohar that disagrees with them — and also because of [avoiding] a blessing in vain there is no need: one may say that since the one called up hears and directs his heart to the reader's words, it is as if he said them. Afterward Maharil wrote: since the Zohar compares Torah reading to prayer in a whisper, and in prayer the Zohar says one should pray in a whisper until his voice is not heard — but nevertheless speech is required in prayer — so too regarding one called to the Torah; certainly the Zohar was particular only that the voice of the reader alone be heard and not the voice of the one called up; but in a whisper the one called up also reads, as the poskim hold — only regarding whether he may make it audible to his own ears there is a dispute between Maharil and Rama below. In any case, the plain meaning of the Zohar is that the one called up does not read at all but only directs his heart to the reader's words; and thus was Maharil's initial view as above. If so, in this manner one may rely on this — not to read but only to direct his heart to the reader's words.
