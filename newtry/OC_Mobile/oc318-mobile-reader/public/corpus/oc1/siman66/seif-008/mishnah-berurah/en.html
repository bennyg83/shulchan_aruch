(37) One must adjoin [redemption to prayer] — see below siman 111.

(38) And one may not interrupt — even for a mere pause between them.

(39) That he said "Ga'al Yisrael" — and this is more severe than [interrupting in] the middle of a section, for one may not interrupt in it even out of fear, as in prayer, unless in a place where there is concern of danger.

(40) That he did not lay [tefillin] — and he could not wait in Keriat Shema until tefillin became available because he feared the time for Keriat Shema would pass; and likewise if he fears the time for prayer will pass — he prays without tefillin, and as soon as they come to his hand afterward, in the middle of the day or at Minchah, he lays them and says some psalm. There is a question: one who has no tefillin at the time the congregation prays — is it better that he pray with the congregation even though he is without tefillin, and afterward when tefillin reach his hand he fulfills only the mitzvah of tefillin; or is it better that he delay after the congregation's prayer in order to borrow tefillin from his fellow so that he read Keriat Shema and pray with tefillin? Magen Avraham ruled that it is better he delay after the congregation's prayer and borrow tefillin so that he read Keriat Shema and pray with tefillin.

(41) He lays then — for tefillin have greater connection to Keriat Shema and prayer than tzitzit.

(42) "On them" — and Pri Chadash disagrees and holds that since it is permitted to lay tefillin between redemption and prayer, one may also bless, and it is not called an interruption; and so ruled in Sefer Even Ha'ezer. However, Derekh HaChayim, Chayyei Adam, and many other poskim transmitted all the words of Shulchan Aruch as halakhah.

(43) "That he pray" — meaning he handles them and blesses on them.

(44) He does not lay then — for if he wishes to wrap in a tallit he would be compelled to stand in silence without beginning the Amidah, and that silence would be an interruption, and only tefillin were permitted.

(45) "He lays them" — for laying tefillin and donning a tallit are not [considered] interruption, for one can even read and engage in his work while wearing them; and as above in siman 63 se'if 1.

(46) "Before Ga'al Yisrael" — meaning before he said "Barukh Atah Hashem," for afterward it is certainly forbidden to interrupt for anything; rather he finishes "Ga'al Yisrael" and lays tefillin without a blessing; and see nearby what we wrote in the name of Ma'amar Mordekhai.

(47) "He blesses" etc. — it seems straightforward that here it is forbidden to say "Barukh shem k'vod malchuto l'olam va'ed," which they are accustomed to say after the blessing on mitzvot; also it is correct that he not bless on this with anything but one blessing for laying, for otherwise many poskim hold thus; and as above in siman 26, see there.

(48) And thus is the practice — and it is evident from the words of the Acharonim that thus is the halachah accepted.
