And he should not interrupt. Even by mere delay between them.
When he said Ga'al Yisrael. This is more severe than the middle of a section, for one may not interrupt there even because of fear, just as in prayer, except in a place where there is concern of danger.
Who had not put on. And he could not wait with Shema until tefillin became available because he feared that the time for Shema would pass. The same applies if he fears that the time for prayer will pass; he prays without tefillin, and as soon as they later come to his hand in the middle of the day or at Minchah, he puts them on and says some chapter of Tehillim. It requires study in the case of one who has no tefillin when the congregation is praying: is it better that he pray with the congregation even though he is without tefillin, and later, when the tefillin come to his hand, fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin alone, or is it better that he delay until after the congregation's prayer in order to borrow tefillin from his fellow, so that he can read Shema and pray with tefillin? The Magen Avraham ruled that it is better for him to delay until after the congregation's prayer and borrow tefillin, so that he reads Shema and prays with tefillin.
He puts them on then. For tefillin have a greater connection to Shema and prayer than tzitzis do.
On them. The Peri Chadash disagrees and holds that since it is permitted to put on tefillin between redemption and prayer, he may also recite the blessing, and it is not called an interruption. So too ruled the Even HaOzer. However, the Derech HaChaim, Chayei Adam, and many other poskim all cited the words of the Shulchan Aruch as the halacha.
That he should pray. For he touches them and recites the blessing on them.
He should not put on then. For if he wishes to wrap himself in a tallis, he will necessarily have to stand silently without beginning Shemoneh Esrei, and the silence is an interruption; this was permitted only for tefillin.
He puts them on. For putting on tefillin and wearing a tallis are not an interruption, since one may even read and engage in his work while reading, as above in siman 63, seif 1.
That precedes Ga'al Yisrael. Meaning before he said Baruch Atah Hashem. After that, it is certainly forbidden to interrupt for anything; rather, he should conclude Ga'al Yisrael and put on the tefillin without a blessing. See nearby what we wrote in the name of Ma'amar Mordechai.
He recites the blessing, etc. It appears obvious that here it is forbidden to say Baruch shem kevod, etc., which people are accustomed to say after the blessing of al mitzvas. It is also proper here to recite only one blessing, lehaniach, for in any case many poskim hold so, as above in siman 26; see there.
