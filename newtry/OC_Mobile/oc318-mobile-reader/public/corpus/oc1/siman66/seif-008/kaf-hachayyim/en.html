8) There: One whom he fears, such as his father or rav, etc. Chazal state at the beginning of the chapter Hayah Korei that in the middle one may inquire because of fear and respond because of honor, while between sections one may inquire because of honor and return a greeting to any person. The poskim dispute the definitions. Rashi explains "because of honor" as an honored person whom it is proper to greet first, and "because of fear" as one whom he fears may kill him. Rambam, Hilchos Krias Shema, chapter 2, halachos 15–16, similarly defines honor as one's father, rav, or a person greater in wisdom, and fear as a king or violent person. The Rosh, however, defines fear as fear of one's father or rav, since it is written, "Every person shall fear his mother and father," and the Mishnah says, "Fear of your rav should be like fear of Heaven." Tur cites him and writes that the Geonim explain likewise. Rashba agrees, as Beis Yosef records, and in Responsa Rashba, siman 321, he cites Rabbi Yitzchak ibn Ghayas. Abudraham and Agur likewise agree, as Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, records. Raavad and Nimukei Yosef in the chapter Yesh Nochalin also agree, as Divrei Menachem, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1, writes. Maran rules accordingly here in Shulchan Aruch, as do Levush, Pri Chadash, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, rule 20, paragraph 6; and Siddur Beis Oved, laws of Birchos Krias Shema, paragraph 9. Taz, paragraph 8, and Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, also appear to agree, since they resolve Bach's objections. This is unlike Bach, who writes that the Rosh and Rashba stand alone. The later authorities disagree, however, with Maran's inclusion of someone greater in wisdom among those feared; they classify him under honor. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Pri Chadash, Eliyah Rabbah, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, and Chayei Adam there. One should be stringent accordingly. A Torah scholar preeminent in his generation is nevertheless included under fear. Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, and Chayei Adam there.
