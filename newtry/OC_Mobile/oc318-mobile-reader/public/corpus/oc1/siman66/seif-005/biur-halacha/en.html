These are between the sections, etc. — see Mishnah Berurah in the name of Magen Avraham that between sections in Maariv we rule like Shacharit; and apparently they are like Shacharit, that wherever there is a pause it counts as between sections, and these are: between the first blessing and the second, between the second and Shema, between Shema and v'hayah, between v'hayah and vayomer, between vayomer and emet va'emunah, and also between Go'el Yisrael and Hashkiveinu — for although we say [Berakhot 4] that since the rabbis enacted Hashkiveinu like an extended redemption, nevertheless since two blessings occur it implies each is considered separately; also since we say blessings do not impede one another and he said all blessings — proof from page 9 that he said there, provided he does not say Hashkiveinu as above — plainly. But there is difficulty regarding parashat vayomer at night whether in its middle counts as middle of a section or not, since vayomer is practiced only by day; and in Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Keriat Shema it implies parashat vayomer at night equals the first two sections — and he follows his reasoning that he explained in the mishnah 'they mention Exodus' — the parashah of tzitzit that they enacted to say entirely because it contains a verse of Exodus; see Shnot Eliyahu chapter 1 of Berakhot on the mishnah 'they mention,' etc., and Berakhot 14b in the Gemara 'we begin,' etc., 'and he needs to mention,' etc. — difficulty. And after the blessing Shomer Amo Yisrael Le'ad it seems it also counts as between sections even for us who say Barukh Hashem le'olam amen va'amen — proof from siman 236 sk 4 and Beit Yosef there that it proves here the blessings conclude for all — see there. Written in the innovations of Re'em: one who lays tefillin of Rabbeinu Tam and reads Keriat Shema without blessings should not interrupt gratuitously, but they ask out of honor even in the middle of the section [Panim Me'urot part 1 siman 67]; and I examined there and it is proven from his proof that he brought there on Hallel on days when they do not finish it — it deals specifically with Shema that he read a second time as is practiced to read in RT tefillin, where one does not fulfill the Torah mitzvah of Shema and only reads in order to read Shema in tefillin; but if one reads Shema even without blessings to fulfill the reading because he fears time will pass — it is forbidden to interrupt in the middle of a section by law, as in other Keriat Shema. That which is stated at the end of siman 46 that it is good to say in the morning Shema Yisrael, etc. — there he speaks only for verbal amplification; but if he explicitly intends to fulfill with this, likewise it is forbidden to interrupt in the middle of a section; and similar wrote Pri Megadim at the end of this siman. But regarding between sections there is to examine this, for perhaps the blessing connects them together and without it is permitted in all cases; and from Rashba's words it seems ostensibly also here the law of between sections applies, for it is proven there that it applies regarding waiting in order to finish all — he returns to the head; see there.
