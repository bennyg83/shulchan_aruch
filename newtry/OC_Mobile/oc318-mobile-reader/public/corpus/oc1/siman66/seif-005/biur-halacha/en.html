These are the points between the sections, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah in the name of Magen Avraham, that the points between the sections at Maariv have the same law as those at Shacharis. Seemingly, they correspond to Shacharis, and every place where there is a break is considered between the sections. These are: between the first berachah and the second, between the second and Shema, between Shema and Vehayah Im Shamoa, between Vehayah Im Shamoa and Vayomer, between Vayomer and Emet V'Emunah, and also between Ga'al Yisrael and Hashkiveinu. Although the Gemara in Berachos 4b says that since the Sages instituted Hashkiveinu it is considered like one extended berachah of redemption, nevertheless, since they are called two berachos, each is evidently considered a separate unit. This also follows from the statement that the berachos do not prevent one another, which refers to all the berachos. There is also proof from page 9b, where it says, "provided that he does not say Hashkiveinu." This appears straightforward. However, the status of the paragraph of Vayomer at night requires study. If one is in its middle, is this considered the middle of a section or not, since Vayomer is obligatory only by day? Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Krias Shema, implies that Vayomer at night is equal to the first two paragraphs. He follows his interpretation of the Mishnah's statement, "The Exodus from Egypt is mentioned," as referring to the entire paragraph of tzitzis, which the Sages instituted to recite in full because it contains the verse concerning the Exodus. See Shenos Eliyahu at the end of the first chapter of Berachos on the Mishnah, "The Exodus is mentioned," and the Gemara in Berachos 14b, "We begin it, etc., but one must mention, etc." This requires study. After the berachah of Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad, it appears that it is again considered between the sections, even according to our custom of saying Baruch Hashem Le'olam, Amen V'Amen. The proof is from siman 236, seif 4, and Beis Yosef there, which demonstrates that according to all opinions the berachos conclude at this point. See there. Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes in his novellae that one who puts on Rabbeinu Tam tefillin and recites Krias Shema without its berachos should not interrupt without need, but may inquire after someone's welfare out of respect even in the middle of a section, citing Panim Meiros, volume 1, siman 67. I examined that responsum. From its proof from Hallel on days when the full Hallel is not recited, it is evident that it discusses specifically one who is reciting Krias Shema a second time, as is customary while wearing Rabbeinu Tam tefillin. He is not fulfilling the mitzvah of Krias Shema through this recitation, but recites it only in order to say Shema while wearing tefillin. If, however, a person recites Krias Shema even without its berachos in order to fulfill the obligation because he fears the time will pass, he is prohibited by law from interrupting in the middle of a section, as during any other Krias Shema. The statement at the end of siman 46 that it is proper to say "Shema Yisrael" in the morning refers to saying it merely as an added precaution. But if he expressly intends to fulfill his obligation through it, it is indeed prohibited to interrupt in the middle of a section. Peri Megadim writes similarly at the end of this siman. The law between the sections, however, requires examination. Perhaps the berachah is what binds the paragraphs together, and without it one may interrupt in all circumstances. Yet the beginning of the second chapter of the Rashba apparently implies that the law of between the sections applies even in this case, for it is evident there that the law that if he paused long enough to complete the entire recitation he must return to the beginning applies.
