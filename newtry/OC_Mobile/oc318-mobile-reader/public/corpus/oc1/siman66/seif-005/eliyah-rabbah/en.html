[5] And for Kedushah, etc. — Lechem Chamudot page 12 wrote: specifically for kadosh, etc., and for barukh — these two verses are what Isaiah and Ezekiel said they heard from the mouths of the angels and pertain to Kedushah; and even in those additions on Shabbat and festivals — 'then in a loud noise,' 'His glory fills,' etc. — one may not interrupt and say them. He further wrote one may interrupt and answer Amen after yitbarakh, etc., but not Amen after titkabbel, yehei shlamah, and oseh shalom, etc., for from titkabbel onward it is only custom, as Rambam wrote at the end of Sefer Ahavah. And on the blessing of Torah one answers Barukh the Omnipresent haMevorakh le'olam va'ed and also Amen; and see above siman 56 sk 5. Magen Avraham wrote in siman 124: in a place where one may not interrupt one should not say barukh hu uvarukh shemo. Magen Avraham: if he heard thunder he interrupts and blesses — until here; and it is not necessary; also somewhat difficult — one may compare it to blessing on tzitzit and tefillin; and see below end of siman 227 that it is passing — if one cannot bless afterward, unlike blessing on tzitzit and tefillin as above.
