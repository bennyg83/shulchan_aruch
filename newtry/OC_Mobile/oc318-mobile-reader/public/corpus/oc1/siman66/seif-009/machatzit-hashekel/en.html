(sk 9) Emet v'yatziv, etc. — one should not interrupt, etc. — Beit Yosef and Rosh. But Maharil wrote that Tur did not understand Rosh's view thus; rather he took "v'yatziv" not precisely — see there, for Rosh also used the words "emet v'yatziv."

But Shulchan Arukh's view is not so, for he concluded "rather he shall say 'I am the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent, emet,' and then he may interrupt" — behold he did not mention "v'yatziv."

On this matter. Meaning: also from Rama's words there it implies he did not [omit] "v'yatziv," for he wrote that one who is under constraint reads Keriat Shema until "emet," etc., and then says "v'yatziv," etc. — it implies that at first he did not say [both,] only "emet" and not "v'yatziv."

And nevertheless one may say in this [matter]. And accordingly, so that there will not be more than 248 words, he must intend that "v'yatziv" not be counted toward the 248 words. Or [he should intend] not to count the prayer leader's repetition of the three words "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet." For only the words "emet" — as above siman 61 sk 1, see there.
