(Seif katan 9) To Emes Veyatziv, etc. One should not interrupt, etc. Beis Yosef and the Rosh. But the Beis Yosef wrote that the Tur did not understand this to be the view of the Rosh; rather, he used 'Veyatziv' imprecisely. See there, for the Rosh also used the phrase 'Emes Veyatziv.'
But the view of the Shulchan Aruch is not so. For he concluded: rather, he should say 'Ani Hashem Elokeichem emes,' and then interrupt; thus he did not mention 'Veyatziv.'
See the end of this siman. Meaning, also from the Rama's words there it implies that he did not say 'Veyatziv,' for he wrote: one who is under compulsion, etc., should read Krias Shema until 'Emes,' etc., and when he prays, then he says 'Veyatziv,' etc. This implies that at first he said only 'Emes' and not 'Veyatziv.'
Nevertheless, one may say this. According to this, in order that there not be more than 248 words, he must intend that 'Veyatziv' not join the count of 248 words. Alternatively, he should not join from the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur the three words 'Hashem Elokeichem emes,' but only the word 'Emes,' as above in siman 61, seif katan 1; see there.
