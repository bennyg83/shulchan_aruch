He does not say amen. Whether after himself or after the shaliach tzibbur.
After Ga'al Yisrael. This is in the past tense because it refers to the redemption from Egypt. But in the Shemoneh Esrei he says Go'el Yisrael, because it is supplication and he is praying for the future. Regarding Maariv, where the custom is to say 'Baruch Atah Hashem, King, Rock of Israel and its Redeemer,' the Taz labored greatly to resolve it, and the Dagul MeRevavah answered his difficulty. Nevertheless, because of this question, men of deed are accustomed to say Ga'al Yisrael even at Maariv.
And some say. This 'some say' holds that even after his own blessing he answers, since it is the conclusion of the order of the blessings of Shema, and they do not consider it an interruption between redemption and prayer. Therefore the Rama says that, from one side, our custom is also like this opinion, that we do not consider it an interruption between redemption and prayer, and therefore we are accustomed to answer amen after the shaliach tzibbur. But if one prays alone, our custom is not to answer amen, as below in siman 215; see there.
To answer, etc. This is specifically if he has not yet begun the verse Hashem sefasei tiftach, for from that point onward it is like prayer. Some precise individuals wish to satisfy all opinions and wait at Tzur Yisrael or at Shirah Chadashah in order to answer amen after the shaliach tzibbur. All the later authorities wrote that they do not act properly, for he is in the middle of the section of Emes Veyatziv, and one may not answer any amen there except the two amens mentioned above in seif 3 in the gloss. Also, initially it is proper to begin Shemoneh Esrei together with the shaliach tzibbur and the congregation. Rather, there is another solution, and it is proper to do it in order to satisfy all opinions: he should intend to finish together with the shaliach tzibbur, and then he is not obligated to answer amen; or he should begin Shemoneh Esrei slightly before the shaliach tzibbur, namely the verse Hashem sefasei, etc., for this small amount is certainly not a concern, since his starting early is not noticeable at all.
After the shaliach tzibbur. Meaning, if it happened that he finished before the shaliach tzibbur and had not yet begun Shemoneh Esrei, and in the meantime the shaliach tzibbur finished. But according to all opinions he need not wait for this.
