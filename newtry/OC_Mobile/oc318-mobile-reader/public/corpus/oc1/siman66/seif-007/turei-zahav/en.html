After Ga'al Yisrael. In the Amidah, however, one says Go'el Yisrael, as stated below in siman 156. This is an explicit Gemara in the chapter Arvei Pesachim: In Krias Shema and Hallel one says Ga'al Yisrael, whereas in the Amidah one says Go'el Yisrael because it is a plea for mercy, meaning that he prays for the future. I remember that in my youth there was an elderly physician named Rabbi Shlomo, who toward the end of his life traveled to Eretz Yisrael. He asked the gaon Rabbi Meir of Lublin, of blessed memory, about the wording recited in Maariv, "Blessed are You, Hashem, King, Rock of Yisrael and its Redeemer," which appears contrary to the Gemara. Rabbi Meir had no answer at hand and said that when he returned home he would consider the matter. I later asked my father-in-law, of blessed memory, and many other rabbis, and none had an answer. The same question arises from the words of the Tur, who cites the Yerushalmi here: In Emet V'Yatziv one must mention the Exodus from Egypt, etc., and "the Rock of Yisrael and its Redeemer," etc. Yet in Emet V'Yatziv one says Ga'al, not Go'el. It appears to me that Go'el Yisrael is in fact appropriate even in Krias Shema, because it is present-tense language, and every present-tense expression may be used for both the past and the future, as Rashi explains at the beginning of Iyov on the verse, "Thus would Iyov do." This is also proven here. In the Amidah one must use language referring to the future, yet the term Go'el, which is present tense, is effective. If so, it is likewise effective regarding the past. The Gemara intended only to exclude saying Ga'al Yisrael in the Amidah. The reverse formulation, however, is also effective, and this is the correct explanation.
