Properly. But not that he did not fulfill Shema at all, for we rule that the blessings do not prevent fulfillment of Shema. Rather, through this his Shema is also not proper, and he should at least repeat it after prayer if he did not say it before prayer, as above in siman 60, seif 2. It is good for him to say Shema again, as stated by Birkei Yosef. The Tur wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that one must mention in Emes Veyatziv the exodus from Egypt, kingship, the splitting of the Reed Sea, and the plague of the firstborn; see there. It appears from the Rashba, Berachos 11, that this is indispensable even after the fact. This requires study from Berachos 14b in the Gemara, 'but he needs to mention,' etc., and 'and we sang to You,' meaning Mi Chamocha until the end of the blessing, as Rashi explains; according to this, the plague of the firstborn is missing. It is strained to distinguish between Shacharis and Maariv. Perhaps they had another text at the end that also contained the story of the plague of the firstborn. Also, from Yerushalmi Berachos, chapter 1, halacha 6, it seems somewhat that this is only initially; see there. This requires practical study. If he exchanged the morning blessing for the evening one or the reverse, if he has not yet said Hashem's Name at the end of the blessing, he returns to the beginning of the blessing; if he said the Name, he concludes and need not go back, as stated by the Chayei Adam in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach.
And he has no time. This requires study: who permitted him to interrupt because he has no time? Rather, one who has no time should read Shema alone without the blessings, and afterwards, when he has time, he should read Shema with its blessings and pray, as above in siman 60, seif 2, according to the Magen Avraham. In the work Nahar Shalom he wrote that nevertheless the Rama's law is true and correct in a case where he read Shema because he thought he would be able to finish the entire prayer, and then, after he read Shema, he was forced by some constraint to interrupt. In that case he says that it is better to interrupt after Hashem Elokeichem emes, and afterwards, when he comes to pray, he begins Veyatziv, etc. But where he knew from the outset that he would not have time to read Shema and pray, and he feared that the time for Shema might pass before he had time, he too agrees with the Magen Avraham that he should read Shema alone. The Peri Megadim similarly answers; see there.
