(53) "As enacted" — but [this means] not that he has not fulfilled Keriat Shema at all, for we hold that blessings do not impede Keriat Shema; rather through this his Keriat Shema is also not proper, and he should return in any case to say it after prayer if he did not say it before prayer, as above in siman 60 se'if 2. And it is good that he say Keriat Shema again [Birkhei Yosef]. Tur wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: one must mention truthfully in "v'yatziv" the Exodus from Egypt, monarchy, and splitting of the Sea of Reeds and the plague of the firstborn — see there. And it is implied in Rashba, Berakhot 11, that for fulfillment even b'dieved [it is required] [and there is difficulty from Berakhot 14b in the Gemara, etc., and we explained "mi khamokha" until the end of the blessing per Rashi, that accordingly the plague of the firstborn is missing, and it is strained to distinguish between morning and evening; perhaps they had a different text at the end that also included the account of the plague of the firstborn; and also from Yerushalmi Berakhot chapter 1 halakhah 6 it is somewhat implied that it is only lekhatchilah — see there; and for practice requires investigation]. If one exchanged the blessing of morning for evening or vice versa — if he has not yet said the Name at the end of the blessing, he returns to the beginning of the blessing; and if he said the Name, he finishes and need not return [Chayyei Adam in the name of R' Menachem].

(54) "And he has no leisure" — requires investigation: who permitted him to cut off [prayer] because he has no leisure? Only one who has no leisure reads Keriat Shema alone without the blessings, and afterward when he has leisure he reads Keriat Shema with its blessings and prays, as above siman 60 se'if 2 [Magen Avraham]. And in Sefer Nehar Shalom it is written that nevertheless Rama's law is true and correct where he read Keriat Shema because he thought he would be able to complete the entire prayer, and afterward after he read Keriat Shema he is compelled by some constraint to stop — in this [case] he says it is preferable that he stop after "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet" and afterward when he comes to pray begin "v'yatziv" etc.; but where he knew from the outset that he would have no leisure to read Keriat Shema and pray, and he fears lest the time for Keriat Shema pass before he has leisure — he too agrees with Magen Avraham that he reads Keriat Shema alone; and similarly explained Pri Megadim — see there.
