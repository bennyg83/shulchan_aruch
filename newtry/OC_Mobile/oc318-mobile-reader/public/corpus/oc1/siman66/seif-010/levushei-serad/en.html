Magen Avraham note 11—they wait. It is astonishing regarding them—for even though there is no law there between redemption and prayer, nevertheless it is in the middle of the section, where it is explained in siman 63 that one answers only two amens and not another amen; and likewise Eliyahu Rabbah posed a difficulty. And what is stated in seif 9 that one waits at shira chadasha—meaning for Kaddish and Kedushah, which are permitted in the middle of the section. And at first glance one can answer this, that what is explained in seif 3 that one answers only two amens—meaning of other blessings; but the amen of this blessing itself is not an interruption. However it is difficult—for the reason for this dispute, if one says amen after Goel Yisrael, also depends on this: those who forbid hold that even an amen belonging to this blessing itself is an interruption; and those who permit hold that regarding this blessing itself it is not considered an interruption. If so, these who are stringent, who do not rely on those who permit, hold that it is an interruption even that amen of that blessing; if so, before Tzur Yisrael it is also forbidden. And furthermore it appears that those who permit also did not say except after Goel Yisrael, for there is the place of answering amen, and this amen is as if it is from the wording of the blessing; but in the middle of the blessing this amen is like the amen of another blessing, since there is no place for this amen there—and thus it can be understood from what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 51, note 3, that in the middle of Baruch she'amar it is permitted to answer amen when the prayer leader finishes Baruch she'amar—because this blessing was not mentioned in the Talmud, see there; it implies that if it were mentioned it would be forbidden, even though it is the amen of that blessing. Therefore one should not act like these who are stringent.
