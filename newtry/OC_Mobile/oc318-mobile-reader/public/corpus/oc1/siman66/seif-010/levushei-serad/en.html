Magen Avraham, seif katan 11. They wait. This is surprising, for although there is no law of between redemption and prayer there, nevertheless it is the middle of a section, and it is explained in seif 3 that one answers only two Amens and not other Amens. So the Eliyah Rabbah also asked. What is written in seif 9, that he should wait at 'Shirah chadashah,' refers to Kaddish and Kedushah, which are permitted in the middle of a section. At first glance one could answer that what is explained in seif 3, that one answers only two Amens, refers to other blessings, whereas Amen of this blessing itself is not an interruption. But this is difficult, for the dispute whether one says Amen after 'Ga'al Yisrael' also depends on this: those who forbid hold that even Amen related to this blessing itself is an interruption, and those who permit hold that with its own blessing it is not considered an interruption. If so, these meticulous ones, who do not rely on those who permit, hold that it is an interruption even for that Amen of that very blessing; accordingly, before 'Tzur Yisrael' too it is forbidden. Furthermore, it appears that even those who permit said so only after 'Ga'al Yisrael,' for there is the place to answer Amen and this Amen is like part of the text of the blessing. But in the middle of the blessing, this Amen is like the Amen of another blessing, since that is not the place for this Amen. This can be inferred from the Magen Avraham in siman 51, seif katan 3, who wrote that in the middle of Baruch She'amar one may answer Amen when the shaliach tzibbur finishes Baruch She'amar because this blessing is not mentioned in the Talmud; see there. This implies that if it were mentioned, it would be forbidden, even though it is the Amen of that same blessing. Therefore one should not follow those meticulous ones.
