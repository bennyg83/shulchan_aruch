Interrupts. A Yisrael goes up in his place. The Levush wrote that the practice is to interrupt, and one should not change the custom because of dispute. Nevertheless he should not read with the chazzan at all. So too the Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that he should go up, and all the more so he should not interrupt to tell the chazzan to say a Mi Shebeirach. The Magen Avraham wrote that one must say that even if he delayed long enough to finish all of it, he need not return to the beginning, for this is not considered compulsion; see siman 65. The Bach and Lechem Chamudos likewise agreed that he should go up. The Taz wrote that during Krias Shema, which is according to the Torah, he should not interrupt, but during its blessings, which are D'Rabbanan, he should interrupt. It follows that during Pesukei D'Zimrah he should also interrupt. The Olas Tamid wrote that according to the view forbidding it during Krias Shema, the same applies in its blessings; only in Pesukei D'Zimrah is there room to be lenient. See siman 135, seif katan 6 in Magen Avraham. The Kenesses HaGedolah distinguishes: if they simply call 'Kohen,' he need not interrupt, but if they called him by name, even if there are other kohanim, he should interrupt; see there. In Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 24, he distinguishes that if there is no Kohen there except him, he should go up because of the honor of the public; see there. The Peri Chadash ruled like the Rashba, that he should not go up even if they called him by name and there is no other in the congregation; see there.
