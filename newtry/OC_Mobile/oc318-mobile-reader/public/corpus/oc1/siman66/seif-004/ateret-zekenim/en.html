A kohen who was reading Keriat Shema, etc. — and some say he does not interrupt, and the halachah is as his words. Rather they should call him only after, for this is not contempt for Torah when he informs that he is standing in Keriat Shema; therefore it is permitted to read even an Israelite in his place if there is no other kohen there (responsum of Rashba siman 365). Nevertheless, one who is concerned for the honor of Torah may interrupt (my teacher in Beit Chadash), and so is the custom to interrupt (Maharam of Padua). Nevertheless he should not read with the prayer leader at all, and should rely on the words of the Zohar parashat Vayakhel page 390 as above; and some wrote that also one may interrupt in the middle of Keriat Shema to answer 'Barukh Hashem haMevorakh' and likewise Amen after the reader in the Torah (Levush Chadash). But if he prayed alone, one does not answer Amen, for it is not the custom to answer Amen after the blessing 'Boneh Yerushalayim.'
