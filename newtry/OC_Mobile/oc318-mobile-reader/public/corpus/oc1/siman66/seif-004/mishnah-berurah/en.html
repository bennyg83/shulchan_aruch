(24) "Keriat Shema" — or he was engaged in its blessings, whether he is standing in the middle of a section or between the sections. And Eliyah Rabbah and Derekh HaChayim wrote that between redemption and prayer he does not go up even if they called him — and all the more so in the middle of Shulchan Arukh.

(25) "That he interrupts" — for out of honor of creatures and their fear one interrupts, and likewise all the more so out of honor of the Torah; and accordingly, even if they called a Yisrael to go up to the Torah, he also interrupts even in the middle of Keriat Shema. And Shulchan Arukh did not mention only a kohen because of the prominence of the second view that holds he does not interrupt even for a kohen; their reason is that this is not contempt for the Torah, since he does not interrupt because he is engaged in the mitzvah of Keriat Shema and its blessings. And even though he is a kohen, there is no concern for his honor if he does not go up, lest they speak libel that he is disqualified; nevertheless, since he does not go up because he stands in a place where it is forbidden to interrupt, there is no flaw in this, and everyone will know that for this reason he did not go up. And a Yisrael goes up in his place if there is no other kohen.

(26) "And the halachah is according to his words" — and the Acharonim wrote that today the world practices like the first view; and even if they called a Yisrael and he is standing in the middle of Keriat Shema — although we can attain another Yisrael — nevertheless the practice is to interrupt out of honor of the Torah alone. If he is in the verse Shema Yisrael or in Barukh shem, he should not interrupt in the middle of them but finish them and afterward go up. One should not change the custom because of the dispute. But if he can hurry and finish until the [end of the] section, he should hurry; and if he cannot finish until the section, he should finish at least where the matter concludes; but he should not delay on account of this. However, he should not read with the chazzan, but only incline his ear to hear from him; and all the more so he should not interrupt to tell the prayer leader to make Mi sheBeirakh. And it seems that if in the middle of Mi sheBeirakh the chazzan forgot his name and asks — according to all opinions it is permitted to answer him out of honor; and when he returns afterward to Keriat Shema he need not return except to the place where he stopped, even if it was a long section and he waited long enough to complete it all. And all this is b'dieved, after they already called him; but lekhatchilah, even if he stands in the blessing of Keriat Shema — and all the more so in Keriat Shema itself — they should not call him, even if there is no kohen but he alone, for there is no concern for flaw in this since he is engaged in Keriat Shema; only they should call a Yisrael in his place. And it is permitted to call him by name and say in place of kohen; and some disagree and say there is also concern for flaw for the kohen, for not everyone knows he is engaged in Keriat Shema; and it seems one may rely and be lenient to call him if he stands between the sections. And it is better if there is no kohen but he, that he leave the synagogue before they call the first one; and likewise the law for a Levite. And it seems straightforward that if the Torah scroll lies on the table and they have no one who can read in it except this one who stands in Keriat Shema and its blessings — according to all opinions he may interrupt out of honor of the Torah; but if it is easy for him to finish from the beginning until the [end of the] section, he should finish. Also he should then refrain from being the reader by the names of those called to go up to the Torah, because of the interruption.
