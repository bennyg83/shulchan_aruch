That he interrupts. For he interrupts because of human honor and awe, as above, and certainly because of the honor of the Torah. According to this, even if an Israelite was called to the Torah, he also interrupts, even in the middle of Shema. The Shulchan Aruch mentioned a kohen only to teach the novelty of the second opinion, which holds that he does not interrupt even for a kohen. Their reason is that this is not a disgrace to the Torah, since he does not interrupt because he is engaged in the mitzvah of Shema and its blessings. Also, although he is a kohen, there is no concern for discrediting him if he does not ascend, lest people speak ill of him as invalid, since his failure to ascend is because he is standing in a place where he is not permitted to interrupt; there is no discredit in this, and everyone will know that this is the reason. Therefore even an Israelite may ascend in his place if there is no other kohen present.
And the halacha follows his words. The later authorities wrote that today the common practice follows the first 'some say.' Even if an Israelite was called and he is standing in the middle of Shema, although another Israelite can be found, the practice is nevertheless to interrupt because of the honor of the Torah, except that if he is in the verse Shema Yisrael or Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed, he should not interrupt in the middle, but should finish them and then ascend. One should not change the custom because of dispute. If he can hurry and finish until the section, he should hurry; if he cannot finish until the section, he should at least finish until a place where the subject is completed, but he should not delay on account of this. However, he should not read together with the chazzan, but should incline his ear and listen to him. Certainly he should not interrupt to tell the shaliach tzibbur to make a Mi Shebeirach. It appears that if, in the middle of the Mi Shebeirach, the chazzan forgot his name and asks him, according to all opinions he may answer because of honor. When he then returns to Shema, he need return only to the place where he stopped, even if it was a long section and he paused long enough to complete all of it. All this is after the fact, when he was already called. But initially, even if he is standing in a blessing of Shema, and certainly in Shema itself, he should not be called, even if there is no kohen there except him, for there is no concern of discredit here since he is engaged in Shema. Rather, they should call an Israelite in his place, and it is permitted to call him by name and say 'in place of a kohen.' Some disagree and say that even here there is discredit to the kohen, for not everyone knows that he is engaged in Shema. It appears that one may rely on the lenient view and call him if he is standing between the sections. It is better, if there is no kohen there except him, that he leave the synagogue before they call the first person. The same law applies to a Levi. It appears obvious that if the Torah scroll is lying on the table and they have no one who can read from it except this person who is standing in Shema and its blessings, according to all opinions he may interrupt because of the honor of the Torah. But if he can easily first finish until the section, he should finish. He should also avoid being the one to call by name those who will ascend to the Torah, because of interruption.
