A kohen who was reading - See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote, that this seif applies even between the sections. This is from Peri Megadim, but I do not know his source at all. Responsa Rashba, siman 135, proves that the Rashba was asked about one who was in the middle of a section. One might suggest that Peri Megadim inferred it from Magen Avraham, siman 135, seif katan 6, who writes that during Pesukei DeZimrah it is permitted to call him, implying that between the sections of Krias Shema it is not. Yet there Magen Avraham discusses calling him initially; from where do we know the law after he has already been called? Perhaps Peri Megadim reasoned that since the Rashba holds that he does not interrupt, although ordinarily one responds out of respect, it must be that, Heaven forbid, this does not constitute a slight to the Torah because he is presently engaged in its mitzvah. If so, the same applies between the sections. Although ordinarily one may respond there to any person, that is when the other person greeted him first and he responds for the sake of peaceful relations, which does not apply here. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the words of Peri Megadim are correct only according to the Rama's ruling in seif 2, that one may not recite a berachah on a tallis even between the sections because the tallis is unrelated to Krias Shema. According to the Mechaber's ruling above, however, that between the sections one may recite a berachah even on a tallis, the Rashba certainly agrees that regarding Bircas HaTorah one may at least recite the berachah between the sections. According to the Mechaber, the Rashba discusses only the middle of a section. This is also evident from the Gra's explanation, who writes that the Rashba's law follows the opinion of Tosafos cited above, which distinguishes between tefillin and a tallis. It is well known that the Gra writes in seif 2 that, according to the Mechaber, this entire distinction applies only in the middle of a section, but not between the sections. See there. As a matter of practical halachah, however, since our initial practice follows the Rama's gloss above, Peri Megadim's words here are correct.
