For Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barekhu one interrupts, etc. — and some wrote that one may interrupt only for 'kadosh kadosh kadosh' and 'Barukh kevod the Omnipresent,' for these are two verses that Isaiah and Ezekiel said that they heard from the mouths of the angels and pertain to the matter of Kedushah (Beit Chadash). All the more so one does not interrupt for what they add on Shabbat and festivals — 'then in a loud noise,' etc., or 'His glory fills the whole world.'

But he should not say except the word 'modim' alone, etc. — meaning 'modim anachnu lecha' — to exclude the modim of the Rabbanan he should not say. (Beit Yosef chapter 5, Maharam of Padua, and so wrote Taz, and the rabbi in Taz intended his words.) And likewise Amen, yehei shemeih rabbah m'varakh — even though it is more than three words, nevertheless one may say them and interrupt in Keriat Shema — meaning because it is forbidden to interrupt in this even if engaged in ma'aseh merkavah, he stops; and likewise in the meaning of the Gemara in chapter 'one whose dead [relative] is before him.' But not more than Amen, yehi shemei rabba m'varakh; and some wrote likewise for every formula — only Yitbarakh and Yishtabach one may not answer from within Keriat Shema; but Amen after Yitbarakh one may answer (Beit Yosef in the name of Maharil). However, Amen after 'titkabbel' and after 'yehei shlamah rabba' and 'oseh shalom' one may not interrupt, because from 'titkabbel' onward it is only custom (see Rambam at the end of Sefer Ahavah).

And some say that Amen that one answers after the blessing 'HaKel haKadosh,' etc. — but in Amen that is at the end of the last three blessings one should not interrupt. And some say that also in Amen that one answers after the last three there is to interrupt — Maharam of Padua.
