(3) For Kaddish, etc. For it is not less than [interrupting] out of fear and honor; and furthermore, since they raised a question there in Kaddish chapter 2 whether to interrupt in prayer—from this it follows that in Keriat Shema one certainly interrupts.

And likewise for Modim, etc. As stated there—if he can [bow], etc.—clearly it is like Kedushah. And even though Rashi and Tosafot explained due to bowing, nevertheless in s.v. "ad," however lekhatchilah, etc., and he said "and some say" that amen, etc.

But not so, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. "ad," etc.—but it does not appear, etc., and he said in the gloss "and likewise," etc., also from this reason.

(ד) And some say, etc. Tosafot there, in the Yerushalmi there, etc., and Magen Avraham note 6. And what he wrote, "but in the Tosefta, first chapter," etc.—he erred in explaining the Tosefta, for there it refers to Kedusha deyotzer, as stated above in siman 59, note 13. And nevertheless, the words of Lechem Chamudot are primary; and also what he wrote, "and it appears," etc., is not primary, as stated above in siman 56, that in the Rambam, Tur, and all the posekim it is stated explicitly until forever.
