And behold in my responsum I wrote that this ruling accords with Magen Avraham. It may be said that Tosafot's words follow Rashba's view regarding "out of fear" — meaning fear lest they kill him, which is pikuach nefesh; therefore one may not compare answering Kaddish and Kedushah to "out of fear," and it is forbidden for Rabbi Meir. But according to what we hold in Shulchan Arukh that "out of fear" means one's father or teacher whom one is obligated to fear — a fortiori "out of honor" of the Divine Presence, which is called "out of fear"; if so, for practical law even in the middle one interrupts and blesses on thunder, and even though it is like greeting — nevertheless it is like greeting out of fear. And this is what Magen Avraham was precise about in his wording: that if he interrupts to greet out of honor, this means an honored person whom one must fear. However, in the essence of Magen Avraham's words, Eliyah Rabbah wrote it is not compelled; and see Tevu'ot Shor in the section on blessings — he elevated to the essence that one does not bless.

Se'if 3 in the Hagahah — and some say the Amen that they answer, meaning to interrupt in the middle. But between the sections it implies in Tur siman 61 that one interrupts for all Amens. And I have seen that Peri Megadim wrote so in Eshel Avraham siman 51 sk 3.
