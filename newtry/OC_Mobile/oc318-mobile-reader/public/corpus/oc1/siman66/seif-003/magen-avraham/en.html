"Interrupts." For if he stops to ask out of honor of flesh and blood — how much more so out of honor of the Holy One blessed be He. And what is the reason — in my humble opinion, if he heard the sound of thunder he should interrupt and bless.

"Modim alone." Meaning: "modim anachnu lecha" [Levush Chadash and Maharam in the introduction to Semak]. And in Barekhu one says "Barukh Hashem haMevorakh le'olam va'ed" and Amen yehei shemei rabba until "le'alamei almaya," and he should be silent and listen until Amen after Yitbarakh and answer Amen — for there is a dispute whether he is obligated to say the entire formula of Yitbarakh, as Beit Yosef wrote siman 56. And from Titkabbel onward one does not answer Amen, for it is only custom. And on the blessing of the Torah one answers "Barukh Hashem haMevorakh le'olam va'ed" and also Amen. And in Kedushah one says only "kadosh" and "barukh" and not "yimlokh" — all this in Levush Chadash. And in the Hagahah of Yad Nachman it implies they also answer "yimlokh," for this pertains to Kedushah; but in Tosefta chapter 1 of Berakhot it implies as Levush Chadash's words. And it seems to me that in Amen yehei shemei rabba one also says the word Yitbarakh, for Beit Yosef was very stringent regarding the penalty of one who interrupts between "le'alamei almaya" and Yitbarakh — see there.

Regarding the Amens that they answer, etc. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Maharil the reason: because it is the conclusion of the first three [blessings], and when one hears prayer it is the conclusion of the middle [blessings]. And that which he did not consider the Amen of the blessing "Shalom Rav" — because the individual also answers at the conclusion of the eighteen; therefore he has no need to interrupt on its account since he can say it as an individual — end quote. And Levush wrote: for us, who hold in siman 215 that the individual does not answer Amen after the conclusion of the eighteen, he must interrupt in Keriat Shema to answer Amen after "Shalom Rav." And Levush Chadash wrote he should not interrupt but rely on the Amen one said in "Oseh shalom" — and similarly, since Maharil wrote in the name of the poskim that even for these two Amens one does not interrupt — if so, do not add to this; and who knows for what reason the Yerushalmi linked these two Amens? Mateh Moshe wrote that even when praying alone one says after "Oseh shalom" "ve'ameru amen," for one says thus to the angels who guard him.
