Only Modim. Meaning, 'Modim anachnu lach.' Lechem Chamudos and Maharam in his introduction to Semak. For Barchu, he says, 'Baruch Hashem hamevorach le'olam va'ed.' For Amen, yehei shemei rabbah, he answers until 'le'olamaya,' and then is silent and listens until the Amen after 'yisbarach,' and answers Amen, because there is a dispute whether one is obligated to say the entire formula of 'yisbarach,' as written in Beit Yosef, siman 56. From 'tiskabel' and onward he should not answer Amen, for it is only a custom. For the blessing over the Torah, he answers, 'Baruch Hashem hamevorach le'olam va'ed,' and also Amen. In Kedushah he should say only 'Kadosh' and 'Baruch,' but not 'Yimloch.' All this is in Lechem Chamudos. In Hagahos Yesh Nochalin it implies that one also answers 'Yimloch,' for that belongs to Kedushah. But in the Tosefta, first chapter of Berachos, it implies like the words of Lechem Chamudos. It seems to me that in Amen, yehei shemei rabbah, he also says the word 'yisbarach,' for the Beit Yosef greatly emphasized the punishment for one who interrupts between 'le'olamaya' and 'yisbarach'; see there.
For the Amen that they answer, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahari Abuhav that the reason is because it is the conclusion of the first three blessings, and Shome'a Tefillah is the conclusion of the middle blessings. The reason he did not count the Amen of the blessing of Sim Shalom is that even an individual answers that at the conclusion of Shemoneh Esrei, and therefore one should not interrupt for it, since he can say it as an individual. End quote. The Levush wrote that according to us, who rule in siman 215 that an individual does not answer Amen after the conclusion of Shemoneh Esrei, one must interrupt during Krias Shema to answer Amen after Sim Shalom. But in Lechem Chamudos he wrote that one should not interrupt, but should rely on the Amen that he says at Oseh Shalom. This appears correct, since the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the poskim that even for these two Amens one should not interrupt; therefore, let us not add to it. Who knows for what reason the Yerushalmi singled out these two Amens. Mateh Moshe wrote that even when one prays alone, he should say after Oseh Shalom, 'and say Amen,' for he says this to the angels who guard him.
