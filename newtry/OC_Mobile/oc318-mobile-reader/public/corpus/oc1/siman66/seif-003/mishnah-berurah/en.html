For Kaddish. Meaning for Yehei shemei rabbah until almayya, but he should not say yisbarach. He answers amen after da'amiran be'alma, but for Tiskabbel, Yehei shelama, and Oseh shalom he does not answer amen, for these are only a custom. For Kedushah he says only Kadosh and Baruch, which are the essential Kedushah, but not Yimloch, and certainly not Nekadesh or the other additions said on Shabbos. All this he should not say even between the sections.
And for Barchu. He answers Baruch Hashem hamevorach le'olam va'ed. For Birkas HaTorah he answers Baruch Hashem hamevorach le'olam va'ed and also amen at the end of the blessing. Some question this amen, but between the sections one may be lenient.
He interrupts. For if he interrupts to ask after a mortal's honor, certainly he may do so for the honor of the Holy One, blessed is He. For this reason it appears that if he heard thunder, he should interrupt and recite the blessing, since it is a passing mitzvah. Others disagree, for since he is engaged in the praise of the Omnipresent, he should not interrupt for another praise; it is not comparable to all these cases, which are matters of sanctity, as stated by Tevuos Shor, Eliyah Rabbah, and other later authorities. It also appears from the Chayei Adam that if he heard them in the middle, he should recite the blessing only if he heard them between the sections.
The word Modim. Meaning 'Modim anachnu lach,' and not all of Modim D'Rabbanan, for that would be a long interruption. It appears that the same applies between the sections.
HaKadosh. Because it is the conclusion of the first three blessings, and Shome'a Tefillah is the conclusion of the middle blessings. But regarding amen after the conclusion of Sim Shalom, although it is the conclusion of all eighteen blessings, many later authorities agreed that he should not answer during Shema. See the Magen Avraham for the reason.
These words apply. At first glance this is not precise, for the same should apply to every amen, that one must interrupt when saying supplications. In truth, however, the Rama's words are very correct, for he is speaking about the supplications one customarily says at the end of the conclusion of the eighteen blessings; therefore only these amens apply. So I found explicitly in the responsum of the Rashba, from which this law derives.
Supplications. Certainly the same applies in Pesukei D'Zimrah. Regarding whether one may answer amen between the sections of Shema and its blessings, the Chayei Adam, klal 20, seif 4, wrote that one may answer only the amen of the blessing that he had just completed. But the Peri Megadim in siman 51 and Rabbi Akiva Eiger's chiddushim in this siman agreed that any amen may be answered between the sections. If he urinated, he should not recite Asher Yatzar then; rather, he should wash his hands and recite the blessing after prayer, as stated by Derech HaChaim.
