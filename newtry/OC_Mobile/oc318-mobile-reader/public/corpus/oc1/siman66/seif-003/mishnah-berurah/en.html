(17) "For Kaddish" — this refers to "yehei shmei rabba m'vorakh" etc., and one does not say "yitbarakh" and answer Amen after "be'almah"; but for "titkabbel tzeloteinu" which makes peace — one does not answer Amen, for it is only custom. And for Kedushah one says only "kadosh" and "barukh," for these are the essence of Kedushah, and not "yimlokh"; and all the more so one does not say "nekadesh" and other things they add on Shabbat. And all this — even between the sections one does not say.

(18) "And for Barekhu" — one answers "Barukh the Omnipresent haMevorakh le'olam va'ed." And in the blessing of the Torah one answers with "yehalelu" and also Amen at the end of the blessing. And some are uncertain about this Amen, and between the sections one may be lenient.

(19) "Interrupts" — for if he stops to greet out of honor of flesh and blood, how much more so out of honor of the Holy One blessed be He. And what is the reason — it seems that if he hears the sound of thunder he should interrupt and bless, for it is a passing mitzvah. And some disagree: since he is engaged in praise of the Omnipresent, he should not stop for another praise; and this is not comparable to all these, which are matters of sanctity [Tevu'ot Shor, Eliyah Rabbah, and other Acharonim]. Also from Magen Avraham it is implied that if he heard them in the middle he should not bless on them, but only when he heard them between the sections.

(20) "The word Modim" — meaning modim anachnu lecha, and not all the modim of the rabbis, for that would be a great interruption; and it seems the same applies between the sections.

(21) "HaKadosh" — because it is the conclusion of the first three blessings; and shome'a tefillah is the conclusion of the middle [blessings]. But the Amen after the conclusion of Shalom Rav — although it is the conclusion of all eighteen — many Acharonim agreed that one should not answer in the middle of Keriat Shema; see Magen Avraham for the reason.

(22) "These matters" — ostensibly not precise, for likewise for every Amen one must interrupt when saying supplications; but in truth Rama's words are very correct, for he deals with the supplications one is accustomed to say at the end of the conclusion of the eighteen blessings; therefore these Amens specifically. [Thus I found explicitly in the responsum of Rashba, from whom this law derives.]

(23) "Supplications" — and all the more so in pesukei dezimra. And regarding whether it is permitted to answer Amen between the sections of Keriat Shema and its blessings — Chayyei Adam in general wrote in se'if 4 that one may not answer except the Amen of the blessing itself that he concluded from the beginning; but Pri Megadim in siman 51 and likewise in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger in this siman agreed that every Amen may be answered between the sections. One who poured water should not bless Asher yatzar but only wash his hands, and after prayer he blesses [Derekh HaChayim].
