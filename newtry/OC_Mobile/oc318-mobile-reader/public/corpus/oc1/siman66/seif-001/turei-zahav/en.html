Like his father or his teacher. In the Mishnah they say simply out of fear. Rashi explained: out of fear of a man whom he fears lest he kill him. Rosh and Rashba challenged: it is obvious that nothing stands before saving a life — and say it refers to fear of one greater than him, as Shulchan Arukh wrote here. But it is difficult, for it is stated explicitly in the Gemara in chapter Eilu Devarim page 32 regarding prayer: R' Yosef said — it is nothing except regarding kings of Israel; but regarding kings of the nations he stops. And you will have difficulty there too — it is obvious — and one may say that there fear that he will kill him immediately was not mentioned; rather, on the contrary, an ordinary king does not kill immediately but rebukes in words as the law requires and says to him why he did so; and through this his words will be reconciled, and he will certainly be appeased out of fear that he did not act in a manner of rebellion against him but for the honor of Heaven — if so, one might say he should not stop on this; the verse teaches us that he may not rely on this. And this matter too contains fear, and this is the main reason the rabbis gave permission to interrupt if he wishes. And with this one may reconcile what was difficult there regarding the incident of a certain Hasid with the governor who challenged him: is it not written and you shall guard yourselves regarding your souls — and what did the Hasid answer him? And even if you do not say this Hasid relied on the merit of prayer, nevertheless what did he answer to the challenge? And according to what we wrote, it is satisfactory: from here the Hasid answered him wait so that he might appease him in words — for seemingly this was superfluous; it would have sufficed for him to say immediately if you were standing before king so-and-so, etc. Rather, with this he hinted to him the explanation: therefore you will wait for me and not kill me immediately, for this is not the law of royalty; rather first you will ask me why I acted thus, and through this he will appease him in words — it turns out he did not violate and you shall guard yourselves, etc. And even though Rambam cited king or distress, etc., and nevertheless Rosh challenged him — this means a king who is comparable to an distress, whom he cited afterward; and this is proven from that he said out of fear — it implies that fear of loss of life is already known in the case of an distress, unlike here. And if in the language of fear according to Rashi's explanation one is concerned that an enemy came upon him to kill him and he must save himself and therefore he will stop — this is obvious; therefore they explained it refers to fear of an important person. And with this it seems one may also reconcile Rashi's explanation: in truth he did not intend that an enemy came to kill him, but rather as if they do not stand came against him — some king was brought against him and he feared lest he kill him since he does not stop and will not accept the excuse from him. And according to Rosh's explanation — why is it difficult from what we find regarding Yaakov when Yosef came to him — he did not stop Keriat Shema, as Rashi wrote in the Chumash? And behold we find in another place that he gave him honor on account of kingship as written and Israel strengthened himself, and Rashi explained from here that they apportion honor to kings — if so, why did he not stop in Shema on his account? And one may say: in the first verse he was engaged, and in it one does not interrupt, as Shulchan Arukh wrote here.
