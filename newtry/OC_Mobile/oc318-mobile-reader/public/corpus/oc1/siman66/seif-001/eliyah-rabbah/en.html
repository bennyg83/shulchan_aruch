[1] Such as his father, etc. — since there is much to renew on this matter I must extend. Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter 'he was reading': in the middle he asks out of fear and responds out of honor; between sections he asks out of honor and responds peace to any person. Rashi explained: out of fear — a person he fears lest he kill him; out of honor — a distinguished person to whom it is fitting to greet first — until here. Rambam chapter 2 of Laws of Keriat Shema explained out of fear such as a king or coercer and the like; and in his commentary on the mishnah there he wrote thus: such as a coercer or informer to idolaters or idolaters; and out of honor he explained there such as one who owes him honor, such as his father or teacher or one greater than him in wisdom. And if so there is a dispute between Rashi and Rambam: for Rambam a distinguished person such as one great in years or wealth is not in the category of out of honor, and for Rashi he is in the category of out of honor and is compared to his father, teacher, and one great in wisdom. In Beit Yosef he did not comment on this; and for practical law the essence is as Rashi's words, as implied in Semag and Semak, even though they follow the method of Rashi and Rambam. Behold Rosh and Rashba wondered at Rashi and Rambam's explanation that they explained out of fear lest he kill him — plainly there is nothing that stands before pikuach nefesh, and therefore they explained out of fear, namely his father or teacher; and so ruled Shulchan Arukh. Bach achieved and wondered at them: if so what will they answer in the chapter 'one may not stand' that Rav Yosef said 'they taught only regarding kings of Israel,' but regarding kings of the nations of the world one rules — this matter is plain. Rather perforce there is a view to say doubt removes certainty, and danger from a king and coercer was doubt lest he not be particular, and here it is certainly prohibited — until here his words. In my humble opinion, also in doubtful danger it is plain we hold everywhere doubt regarding lives is lenient even regarding desecration of Shabbat; and that Rav Yosef needed to distinguish seems to me because in the mishnah it taught explicitly even a king who asks his peace — one does not respond; he needed to distinguish; and also to exclude the baraita the Gemara challenges from there: 'he saw a caravan or coercer coming toward him — he does not stop'; therefore Rav Yosef needed to distinguish so it can be resolved that it deals where one can shorten, as they answer there. Bach further challenged: what is the view of that pious man who did not respond to the governor when he gave him peace? It seems in my humble opinion he was stringent on himself; and in such a case Tosafot Eruvin 21, s.v. mutav, etc., regarding R' Akiva who said it is better he die his own death than eat without netilat yadayim — see there. I found in Sefer Chassidim siman 790 on the deed of the pious man mentioned above — thus his wording: behold a person who is stringent on himself in prayer even though he risks his life — he does well, as Daniel did — until here. And in Sefer Menorat HaMaor, third lamp chapter 18, it is also explained that a pious person who all his days is careful in a commandment is stringent even in complete danger and kindness surrounds him. Bach further wrote: Semag and Semak wrote like Rashi and Rambam, and Rosh and Rashba remain as an individual against many — until here. It is wondrous: for Rashba and Tur wrote that the Geonim also explained thus, and Rashba wrote in responsum siman 321 in the name of R' Yitzchak Gi'at, and so wrote Avudraham and Agur and later great ones; also in Chiddushei Rashba he proved this explanation with complete proofs. Therefore it seems to me the essence is as Shulchan Arukh's ruling and Levush; but what Shulchan Arukh wrote that one greater than him in wisdom is in the category of fear — in truth so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba; but a wondrous wonder to me: for he challenges in Chiddushei Rashba [Berakhot] 13 on Rashi and Rambam's explanation — thus his wording: 'We learned: he was reading Shema and encountered his father or teacher or one greater than him in wisdom — between sections he asks out of honor, and in the middle he asks out of fear' — it implies in these that are mentioned there is no out of honor but out of fear; and in all these, namely his father or teacher or the great one, etc., there is none who fears lest he kill him. Rather certainly out of honor, namely one greater than him in wisdom; and as we say later regarding Rav bar Shabba — his father and teacher are out of fear, that he is commanded in fear of him: regarding father it is written 'a man — his mother and father you shall fear,' and teacher — 'fear of your teacher like fear of Heaven' — until here his wording. Behold explicitly one great in wisdom is in the category of out of honor. (I again found in Magen Avraham that he challenged this on Beit Yosef.) And further it is hard for me: if one greater in wisdom is also in the category of fear, Rashba's difficulties still stand in their place, for it implies also out of honor applies there. It is possible Beit Yosef directed to Rashba's responsum siman 321, who resolved another way — thus his wording: because one's teacher has upon him two obligations — honor and reverence — he used both; and thus the mishnah teaches: he encountered his teacher or one greater in wisdom — if between sections he asks, and even out of honor; and even in the middle he asks out of fear — until here. And this is correct; but nevertheless in my humble opinion one should answer on this: although he resolves another matter, namely he wishes to establish the beginning and end regarding the teacher himself; but in truth it is possible he holds one great in wisdom is in the category of honor, and learn the unstated from the stated so Rashba's words not contradict. And further Beit Yosef should have brought Rashba's contradicting words and decided between them. And it is already known that the plain Rashba in Beit Yosef is Chiddushei Rashba even if they contradict. Therefore it seems to me the essence for practical law is as his innovations, for the answer in the responsum is very strained; and further Rosh, Tur, Rabbenu Yerucham, and Avudraham, who stand in Rashba's method, all mentioned only his father, mother, and teacher, who are in the category of fear, and not one of them omitted to mention also one great in wisdom; and further per Rashi and Rambam's method also his father and teacher are in the category of honor. Therefore it seems to me for practical law: one greater than him in wisdom is in the category of honor; and similarly one great in years or wealth; and his father, mother, and teacher in the category of fear; and likewise a Torah scholar distinguished in Torah as he wrote in Terumat HaDeshen siman 135. On this matter I have a great wonder on Ma'adanei Melekh page 11, who extended and wrote: whoever explains 'teacher' is in the category of fear does not read except 'one greater than him,' and they do not read 'in wisdom' — for if they read 'in wisdom' then the whole baraita deals only with out of fear alone — until here his words briefly. And one may wonder at him: in Chiddushei Rashba and in his responsa there it is explicit they read 'in wisdom'; he also resolves the baraita as dealing with both. And know there is no distinction between asking peace in the holy language or a foreign language. Magen Avraham wrote it implies like Rashba 14 that it is permitted lekhatchilah to ask peace of the reader even though he knows he will need to respond to him.
