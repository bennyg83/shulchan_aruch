(Regarding) "such as" — Taz: Rosh wrote only one's father and mother and teacher; and likewise Beit HaRav as Magen Avraham wrote in se'if 2. Nevertheless, since the Tur wrote simply "honored person," it implies on account of his wealth and the like, and it is not necessary that he be great in wisdom — necessarily so, for it is taught in a baraita (14a): "his father and his teacher and one greater than him" etc. — "fear" applies, and not as separate categories, as Tosafot HaRosh wrote: one who explains them as separate categories holds that a plain "honored person" is included in "any person." See Perush HaMishnayot of Rambam z"l and what Knesset HaGedolah wrote in chapter 2 of Mechaber Keriat Shema — Taz ruled in Shulchan Arukh unlike Rambam and Rashi, because Rashba and Rosh explained "fear" as father and teacher, and their reasoning is plausible due to the difficulty of the straightforward [reading] (and also why distinguish Keriat Shema from prayer). And "honor" is explained like the Tur, and it implies even any honored person whatsoever; and automatically in the baraita Berakhot 14a "his father and his teacher and one great in wisdom" — all are "fear," and for Rambam all are "honor," and for Beit HaRav as separate categories — and we explained in our chiddushim on this. And what he wrote that there is permission to interrupt implies it is not an obligation; see siman 156 and Magen Avraham 104:1 and Arukh on this that even doubtful pikuach nefesh [requires interruption when possible] — and I will explain below in 104 the matter of snake, that Nehushtan does not interrupt and okhel chullin interrupts — like Rashi and Rambam "fear" etc., and a king of Israel interrupts — and he challenged from Yerushalmi that one does not inquire after one's teacher's welfare; see Rambam siman 17 — the author ruled that one precedes peace in fear, see there; and on Rabeinu Bechor Shor there [he asks] why he was silent here.

(Regarding) "what" — that one interrupts between the second [blessing] and Keriat Shema: although it is forbidden to interrupt between a blessing and what they bless upon it, the blessings of Keriat Shema — since one cannot fulfill Keriat Shema without [first completing] its blessings, he may interrupt. So wrote chapter 2 Mechaber Keriat Shema in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, brought by Peri Chadash; see Magen Avraham se'if 2.
