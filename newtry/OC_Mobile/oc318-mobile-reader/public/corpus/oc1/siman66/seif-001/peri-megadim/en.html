Such as. See Taz. The Rosh wrote only father, mother, and rebbe, and also in Chidushei HaRashba, as the Magen Avraham wrote in letter 2. Nevertheless, since the Tur wrote simply, 'an honorable person,' this implies one honored because of wealth or the like, and not necessarily someone greater in wisdom. That which is taught in the baraisa, Berachos 14a, 'his father, his rebbe, and one greater than he,' appears to refer to fear, and is not divided into separate cases, as written in Terumas HaDeshen; for the one who explains it as separate cases holds that an ordinary honorable person is included in 'any person.' See the Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah and what the Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 2 of Hilchos Krias Shema. The Taz ruled in the Shulchan Aruch not like the Rambam and Rashi, because the Rashba and the Rosh explained fear as father and rebbe, and their reasoning is logical because of the difficulty, 'Is this not obvious?' And also, why specifically Krias Shema? The same should apply to prayer. The meaning of honor is as the Tur wrote, and implies even someone honored to any degree. Consequently, in the baraisa in Berachos 14a, father, rebbe, and one greater in wisdom are all cases of fear; according to the Rambam they are all cases of honor; and according to Chidushei HaRashba they are separate categories. See what I wrote in our novellae on this. What is written that one has permission to interrupt implies that it is not an obligation. See Yoreh Deah 156, Magen Avraham 104:1, and Eliyah Rabbah here, that with a doubtful danger to life he is also obligated, where possible. See Yoreh Deah 242:17, where the Mechaber rules that one greets in fear; see there. It is somewhat difficult why the Rav there was silent here.
What. That one interrupts between the second blessing and Krias Shema, even though it is forbidden to interrupt between a blessing and the matter over which one blesses. Regarding the blessings of Krias Shema, since one does not say, 'Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning Krias Shema,' he may interrupt.
