Everything that it is forbidden to speak, even in Lashon Hakodesh, is forbidden to speak. Even a single word is forbidden.
Between the sections. This applies specifically when they met one another incidentally. But to rise early and go to his doorway, or to go in the synagogue from his fixed place to his friend's place to greet him, even for his father or his rav, is forbidden, even before he began Baruch She'amar, since it is before prayer. See Peri Megadim in siman 89, and see there in Rashi. So too it is evident from the reason stated in the Gemara, 14, 'how did you regard this one,' etc.; see there in Rashi. See also Kiddushin 33b, that his honor should not be more severe than the honor of Heaven.
One may ask after his welfare. Even in a foreign language. This is specifically with new faces, one asks and responds, for if he does not respond it may lead to hatred. The Sefer HaChinuch also wrote that regarding one whom we have not seen to be particular with his fellow at all, one should not interrupt even between the sections. Therefore, according to our custom today, when we do not ask after another's welfare in the synagogue during prayer, Heaven forbid to ask or respond, even with words of Torah, not between the sections and not during Pesukei D'Zimrah. If one transgressed and interrupted during Shema even in a place where he was not permitted to interrupt, and he did not pause long enough to complete all of it, according to all opinions he returns only to that verse; and some say to that word from which he stopped, if it is a place where the subject is completed.
An honorable person. One for whom it is proper to greet first, such as an elder or Torah scholar, and likewise if he is wealthy and it is proper to honor him because of his wealth.
Any person. Who greeted him first. It appears from the chiddushim of the Rashba that initially one may greet a person who is reading Shema, even though he knows that the reader will need to respond to him. See above, seif katan 2.
His father or his rav. This means his primary rav, from whom he received the main part of his Torah, even if he is now greater in Torah than his rav. Awe applies to them, as it is written, 'A man shall fear his mother and father,' and we learned: the awe of your rav is like the awe of Heaven.
Greater than he. If they are equal, this is included in the category of honor. If the reader is greater than he, he does not interrupt at all, even for a Torah scholar.
In wisdom. The later authorities ruled that even if he is greater than he, this is only for honor, unless he is an outstanding Torah scholar of the generation; then he is included in the category of awe.
And certainly a king. Even a Jewish king is included in the category of awe, as it is written, 'You shall surely appoint a king over yourself,' meaning that his awe shall be upon you.
Or a violent man. Or an informer.
The verse. Some say that in the middle of a verse he should not interrupt unless it is at a place where the subject is completed, and if he did interrupt, he afterwards returns to the beginning of the verse. As a practical matter, when he interrupts to answer Kaddish or Kedushah, as will be explained later, if he cannot hurry to finish the subject, he may interrupt even in the middle of the subject and rely on the first opinion, which did not distinguish in this way for the need of such a great mitzvah and to answer with the congregation; afterwards he returns to the beginning of the verse.
Except for the verse. For nothing is greater than accepting the kingdom of Heaven. For this reason he should also not interrupt between Shema Yisrael and Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed, since it too is included in the unity of Hashem, as stated by the Beit Yosef.
In them at all. This implies that he does not even answer Kaddish, Kedushah, or Barchu; see Biur Halacha. It is possible that if the reading was after three hours, when he is merely reading from the Torah, he must interrupt.
If not, etc. Its law is like prayer below in siman 104.
