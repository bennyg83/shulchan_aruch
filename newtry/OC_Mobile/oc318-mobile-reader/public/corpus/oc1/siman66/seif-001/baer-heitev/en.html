With peace. Even in a foreign language. Wherever it is forbidden to speak, it is forbidden even in the holy tongue. Tosafot at the beginning of the second chapter of Berachos. Maharam Tiktin wrote in a gloss on the Rif in the name of the Nimukei Mahari: specifically with a new face does one ask and return a greeting, for if he does not ask he will come to hatred. But in a synagogue, where we do not greet one another, Heaven forbid to ask or return a greeting, neither between the sections nor in Pesukei D'Zimrah. End quote. In Sefer HaChinuch, parshas Vaeschanan, he wrote that one whom we have not seen to be at all particular with his fellow should not interrupt even between the sections. See Magen Avraham. In Eliyah Rabbah there is a deep analysis in the Gemara and poskim.
In wisdom. The Magen Avraham wrote that one who is greater than him in wisdom is included in honor. If the reader is greater than he, he does not interrupt even for a Torah scholar. It implies in the Rashba that it is permitted initially to greet the one reading, even though he knows that the reader will need to answer him. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 138, it is stated that an outstanding Torah scholar of the generation is included in fear; see siman 472, seif 5. Magen Avraham.
A king. Even a Jewish king is included among those whom one fears, as it is written, 'You shall surely place over yourself a king,' meaning that his fear should be upon you. Olas Tamid.
The verse. This is specifically where the matter has concluded. But in the middle of a matter, such as 'and you shall place these words of Mine upon your heart,' he should not interrupt; and if he interrupted, he returns to the beginning of the verse. Kesef Mishneh at the end of the second chapter; Magen Avraham. Rabbi Yehudah Chasid wrote that this applies specifically to saying peace, and no more; see Olas Tamid and seif katan 1.
