In peace. Even in a foreign language, and wherever it is forbidden to speak — even in the holy language it is forbidden. Tosafot chapter 2 of Berakhot; and Maharam of Tiktin wrote in the margin of the Rif in the name of Nimukei Maharash — specifically regarding new faces where one asks and responds, that if he does not ask hatred will result. And in Beit HaKnesset — that we do not ask peace, God forbid — to ask or respond, neither between the sections nor in pesukei dezimra — end quote. And in Sefer HaChinukh parashat Va'etchanan he wrote: one whose fellow we have not seen that he objects at all — he should not interrupt even between the sections — in my humble opinion. (And in Sefer Eliyahu Rabbah — deep pilpul in Shas and poskim.)

In wisdom. Magen Avraham wrote: one who is greater than him in wisdom — this is 'out of honor.' And if the reader is greater than him, he does not interrupt even for a Torah scholar. And it implies in Rashba that it is permitted lekhatchilah to ask peace of the reader even though he knows he will need to respond; and in Tashbetz siman 138 it states that a Torah scholar distinguished in his generation is in the category of 'fear' — see siman 472 se'if 5. Magen Avraham.

King. Even the king of Israel is among those one fears from him, as it is written: 'You shall surely set over you a king' — that his dread be upon you. Pri To'ar.

The verse. And specifically where the matter is concluded; but in the middle of a matter, such as 'and you shall place these words of mine upon your heart' — he should not interrupt; and if he interrupted, he returns to the beginning of the verse — as in chapter 2, Magen Avraham. And R' Yehudah haChasid wrote: specifically peace and nothing more — see Pri To'ar and in note 1.
