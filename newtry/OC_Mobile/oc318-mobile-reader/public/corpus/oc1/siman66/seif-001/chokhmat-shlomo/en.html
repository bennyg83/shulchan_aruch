Se'if 1: Between the sections, etc. — note: see Taz sk 1 what he challenged on Rashba and Rosh — why did they not challenge thus on the Gemara that Rav Yosef said 'they taught only regarding kings of Israel,' but regarding kings of the nations of the world one rules — they should have challenged thus as well; plainly it is strained there. And in my humble opinion it fits well: behold the Gemara deals explicitly with prayer, and in prayer we hold [siman 104] if he interrupted due to coercion and waited long enough to finish all of it, he returns to the head; and therefore one may say it teaches much: otherwise it would be a mistaken presumption that there too, for there is nothing that stands before pikuach nefesh, and certainly one may interrupt in any case since we hold pikuach nefesh is set aside and not permitted, as Rambam wrote in Laws of Shabbat that Shabbat is set aside for the ill, and from this it implies for all prohibitions; and if so, since it was only set aside, it would be a mistaken presumption that his law is only in another coercion, and if he waited long enough to finish all of it he is obligated to return to the head; and therefore it is that lekhatchilah one may certainly interrupt, for there is nothing that stands before pikuach nefesh in any case — if afterward he waited, he returns to the head. Therefore it teaches that here it is not set aside but permitted regarding prayer, for thus the rabbis enacted — they said and they said that in place of pikuach nefesh they permitted interrupting, and it was like permission and not coercion, and he is not obligated to return to the head — and a fine distinction applies here. But regarding Keriat Shema it fits well to challenge from both sides regarding what it teaches: if to permit interrupting lekhatchilah — plainly it is permitted, for there is nothing that stands before pikuach nefesh; and if to say that afterward he need not return to the head — this is unnecessary. To teach us that otherwise he need not return to the head in this — and this is clear and correct in my humble opinion, and it fits well; investigate.
