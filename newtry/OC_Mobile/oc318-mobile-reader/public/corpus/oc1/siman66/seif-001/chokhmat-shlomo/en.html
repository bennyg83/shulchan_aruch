Se'if 1: Between the sections, etc. See Taz, se'if katan 1, who asks why Rashba and Rosh did not challenge the Gemara's statement of Rav Yosef; 'They taught this only regarding Jewish kings, but for non-Jewish kings one interrupts'; as obvious. His resolution is strained. In my humble opinion the matter is clear. The Gemara explicitly discusses Shemoneh Esrei, where we rule in siman 104 that if one was compelled to interrupt and paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, he must return to the beginning. The Gemara therefore teaches a significant novelty. One might have thought that although nothing overrides pikuach nefesh and he certainly may interrupt, pikuach nefesh merely overrides prohibitions rather than rendering them permitted, as Rambam writes that Shabbos is overridden for a sick person; the same principle applies to all prohibitions. Thus, one might have treated this like any compelled interruption: He may interrupt initially, but if the interruption lasts long enough to complete the entire prayer, he must afterward return to the beginning. The Gemara teaches that regarding prayer, interruption for pikuach nefesh is treated as fully permitted rather than merely overridden. The Sages who instituted prayer also permitted its interruption for pikuach nefesh; it is therefore treated as voluntary cessation, not compulsion, and he need not return to the beginning. This is a genuine practical difference. Regarding Krias Shema, however, the question is compelling either way. If the teaching merely permits interruption initially, that is obvious because nothing overrides pikuach nefesh. If it teaches that he need not afterward return to the beginning, no teaching is needed, since in this case he would not need to return even otherwise. This is clear and correct in my humble opinion.
