Tosafot wrote at the beginning of chapter 2 of Berakhot: whatever is forbidden to speak — even in the holy language it is forbidden. "Asks peace" — even in a foreign language (Targum Yonatan). Maharam of Tiktin wrote in the margin of the Rif in the name of Nimukei Maharash — specifically regarding new faces one asks and responds, for if he does not ask hatred will result. And in synagogue — that we do not ask peace, God forbid, to ask or respond, even words of Torah, not between sections nor in pesukei dezimra — end quote. And in Sefer HaChinukh he wrote: one whose fellow we have not seen that he objects at all — he should not interrupt even between the sections — see siman 207 and siman 104 se'if 5.

"Greater than him in wisdom." Thus Maharil in the name of Chiddushei Rashba; and one who examines there will see it does not exist — only his intention is that one greater than him in wisdom is "out of honor," and so in the Gemara. And if the reader is greater than him he does not interrupt even for a Torah scholar. It implies there in Rashba page 19 that it is permitted lekhatchilah to ask peace of the reader even though he knows he will need to respond to him. And in Tashbetz siman 238 it states that a Torah scholar distinguished in his generation is in the category of "fear" — see siman 472 se'if 5.

In the middle of the verse. And some say specifically where the matter concludes; but in the middle of a matter, such as "and you shall place these words of mine upon your heart" — he should not interrupt; and if he interrupted he returns to the beginning of the verse — as in chapter 2 [Magen Avraham].
