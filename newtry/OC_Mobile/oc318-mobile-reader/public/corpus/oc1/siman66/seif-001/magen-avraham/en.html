Tosafot wrote at the beginning of the second chapter of Berachos: anything that it is forbidden to say, it is forbidden even in Lashon Hakodesh. He may inquire after his welfare. Even in a foreign language. Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah. Maharam Tiktin wrote in the margin of the Rif, in the name of Nimukei Mahara'sh, that specifically with a new face he may inquire and respond, for if he does not inquire it may lead to hatred. But in the synagogue, where we do not inquire after welfare, Heaven forbid that one should inquire or respond, even in words of Torah, neither between the sections nor in Pesukei D'Zimrah. End quote. In Sefer HaChinuch he wrote that with one whom we have not seen to be particular about his fellow at all, one should not interrupt even between the sections. See what I wrote in siman 207 and siman 104, seif 5.
Greater than him in wisdom. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of Chidushei HaRashba. One who examines it there will see that this is not so; rather, his intent is only that one who is greater than him in wisdom is considered a case of honor. So too in the Gemara. If the reader is greater than he, he does not interrupt even for a Torah scholar. It appears there in the Rashba, page 19, that one is initially permitted to inquire after the welfare of the reader, even though he knows that he will need to respond to him. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 135, it is stated that an outstanding Torah scholar of the generation is included in the category of fear. See siman 472, seif 5.
In the middle of the verse. Some say this is specifically where the subject has ended. But in the middle of a subject, such as, 'And you shall place these words of Mine upon your heart,' he should not interrupt. If he interrupted, he returns to the beginning of the verse. Kesef Mishneh, end of chapter 2.
