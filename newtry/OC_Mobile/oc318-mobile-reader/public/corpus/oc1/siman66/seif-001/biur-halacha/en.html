Between the sections, he may inquire after the welfare of a distinguished person - See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote, that this refers to an elderly person. This wording is taken from Olas Tamid, and it appears from his words there that if the person is not elderly, but is merely older than him, he is not included in the category of one whom he may greet out of respect. See Derech HaChaim and Peri Megadim.
To any person - See Eliyah Rabbah, whose citation from the Rambam proves that one may certainly respond to a non-Jew. If he is a violent person, he is included in the category of one whom he may answer out of fear. See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I quoted in the name of the Chiddushei HaRashba. It is taken from the wording of Magen Avraham. See Machatzis HaShekel, who struggled to identify its source. The source is straightforward, for the Rashba writes there concerning Rav bar Sheva that the reason he did not first inquire after Ravina's welfare was that it was not proper conduct. This implies that otherwise he would have been permitted initially to greet him in the middle of Hallel, even though this would require Ravina to respond to him. Nevertheless, the ruling of Machatzis HaShekel there is correct, for it is possible that one may greet him specifically between the sections, but not in the middle of a section.
Or a violent person - See Mishnah Berurah. I saw in the work Shalmei Simchah that he cites Maharshal as ruling that one may interrupt even to prevent a monetary loss.
And he responds, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah in the name of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid, that he responds with only one word. Magen Gibborim writes that the wording of Rabbeinu Yonah implies that he may give an actual greeting.
Even in the middle of a verse - See Mishnah Berurah regarding what I wrote, "As a matter of practical halachah, etc." This is also written in Avodas HaYom. Although Derech HaChaim rules oppositely, that the same applies to answering Kaddish and Kedushah, and that one may answer only if he happens to be at a point where the subject concludes, his proof is that answering Kaddish or Kedushah is derived from the law of responding to a greeting out of respect, and there Magen Avraham rules that this is permitted only where the subject concludes. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the ruling follows Avodas HaYom. This law of Magen Avraham is not universally accepted, for the Beis Yosef cites it only in the name of "some say," and Magen Avraham likewise mentions it only in the name of "some say." Furthermore, the wording of Shulchan Aruch here does not imply this. Otherwise, what is the novelty in ruling that one may not interrupt during "Shema Yisrael"? The entire verse is one subject, as is implied in siman 61, seif 14, in the gloss, which is derived from Sifrei. Although Magen Avraham is stringent to take this opinion into account regarding an ordinary interruption, it should not therefore prevent one from answering "Yehei Shemei Rabba," Kedushah, or Barechu.
That he should not interrupt in them at all - See Mishnah Berurah. Although the plain meaning implies that he should not answer, and Chayei Adam rules likewise, in my humble opinion this matter requires very great study. As we clarified above in siman 61 in Biur Halachah, the ruling follows the Bach and Shiltei HaGibborim that, after the fact, one who omitted "Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed" need not repeat Krias Shema. From where do we know that it is equivalent to the first verse to the extent that one may not interrupt during it to answer "Yehei Shemei Rabba," Kedushah, or Barechu? The proof that the Gra brings for the Beis Yosef's ruling, from Yaakov our forefather not interrupting for Yosef while he was reciting Shema, is no proof regarding "Baruch shem," for Yaakov did not say it then at all. He said it only before his death, as stated in Pesachim 57a. Even were we to assume that one may never interrupt in the middle of "Baruch shem," what is the law if the opportunity to answer "Yehei Shemei Rabba" arises between "Shema" and "Baruch shem"? Since the poskim did not distinguish, it appears that he should not answer, and this is also somewhat implied by the Tur's abridged rulings in this siman. See Yeshuos Yaakov. The practical halachah requires very great study.
