Between the sections one asks peace of a distinguished person — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote that 'elder' (zaken) is from the terminology of Olah Tamid, and it implies there that if he is not an elder but only greater than him in years, he is not in the category of 'out of honor'; see Dagul Meirevavah and Pri Megadim.

To any person — see Eliyah Rabbah in what he brought in the name of Rambam, which proves that to a non-Jew one may certainly respond. And if he is a notable person (gavra), he is in the category of 'out of fear.' See Mishnah Berurah in what I cited in the name of Chiddushei Rashba — it is from Magen Avraham's wording; and see Machatzit HaShekel, who strained at his source, and it is plain that it is from where Rashba wrote regarding Rav bar Shabba — therefore he did not ask on his own at first 'shalom' to Rabbina because it is not proper conduct, which implies that otherwise he would have been permitted to ask initially in the middle of hallel even though thereby Rabbina would need to respond to him; and nevertheless the ruling of Machatzit HaShekel that he wrote there is true — perhaps it is permitted specifically between sections to ask him, but not in the middle of a section.

Or coercion — see Mishnah Berurah. I saw in Sefer Shalmei Simchah that he brought in the name of Maharshak that even for monetary loss one may interrupt.

And responds, etc. — see Mishnah Berurah in the name of R' Yehudah HeChasid that specifically one word; and in Magen Giborim he wrote that from Targum Yonatan's wording it implies an actual greeting of peace.

And even in the middle of a verse — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote regarding ma'aseh, etc.; likewise wrote Sefer Avodat HaYom, and even though Beit Chadash wrote the opposite regarding answering Kaddish and Kedushah — only if he happened upon a place where the matter concludes — and his proof from answering Kaddish or Kedushah is learned from the law of responding to peace out of honor; there Magen Avraham did not decide specifically where the matter concludes; nevertheless it seems to me the law is with the Acharonim, for Magen Avraham's ruling there is not agreed upon, for there in all places it was not brought except in the name of 'some say' (yeish omrim), and also in Magen Avraham it was only mentioned in the name of yeish omrim, and also here from Shulchan Arukh's wording it does not imply thus, for otherwise what is the stringency of Shema Yisrael that one not interrupt — behold it is one matter, the whole verse, as implied in siman 61 se'if 14 in the Hagahah, and it derives from Sifri; and although Magen Avraham is stringent to be concerned for this yeish omrim view regarding interruption, not to cancel thereby Keriat Shema, Kedushah, and Barekhu.

He shall not interrupt in them at all — see Mishnah Berurah; and in my humble opinion, even though plainly it implies one should not answer, and so is in Chayyei Adam — for my humble opinion there is difficulty according to what we clarified above in siman 61 in Biur Halachah that the law is with Bach and Sha'agat HaGra that b'dieved if he did not say Shema with intention he need not return — whence do we compare it to the matter that it is forbidden to interrupt for Shema, Kedushah, and Barekhu; and the proof Gra brought for Bach's ruling from Yaakov, who did not interrupt for Yosef when he read Shema — no proof for barukh shem, for they did not say Yaakov then at all, only before his death, as stated Pesachim 57a. And even if we say in the middle of barukh shem he does not interrupt in any case — how is the law if he happened to answer Shema between Shema and barukh shem, and from that the poskim did not distinguish it implies one should not answer, and so is somewhat implied in Tur's hints in this siman; and see Sefer Yeshuot Yaakov; and there is difficulty lema'aseh.
