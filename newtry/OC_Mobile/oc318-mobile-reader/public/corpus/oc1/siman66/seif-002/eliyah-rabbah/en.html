[2] And he returns peace to every person, etc. I found in Sefer HaChinuch of the Ra'ah, parshas Vaeschanan, as follows: for some time we have not seen anyone be particular at all with his fellow if he does not interrupt for him, even between the sections. End quote. It is written in the glosses of Mordechai in the name of Rabbi Yehudah Chasid: he responds because of honor specifically with 'shalom' and no more; wherever it says he asks or responds, this is with a new face, where if he does not ask he will come to hatred. In a synagogue, where we do not greet, Heaven forbid to respond or ask, even words of Torah, neither between the sections, nor in the blessings, nor in Pesukei D'Zimrah. End quote. It implies that if he is not engaged in the blessings or Pesukei D'Zimrah, they do greet in a synagogue. So too it implies in Tashbetz, siman 246: the Maharam would greet every person before prayer, even in the synagogue.
