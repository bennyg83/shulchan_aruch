(Seif katan 2) Greater, etc. And so it is in the Gemara. Meaning, the Rashba rejected Rashi's explanation, who explained 'because of fear' as a king or violent person. This is his language: for we learned in a baraisa, 14a, if he was reading Shema and his father, teacher, or one greater than he encountered him, between the sections he asks because of honor, etc., and in the middle he asks because of fear, etc. This apparently implies that those mentioned in the baraisa have both honor and fear.
And among all these, etc. There is not one whom he fears will kill him. Rather, certainly 'because of honor' means one greater than he in wisdom, etc., while his father and his teacher are because of fear, for he is commanded in their fear. End quote of the Rashba.
And if the reader is greater, etc. So it is there regarding Rava bar Shaba; see there.
And it implies in the Rashba, etc. In my humble opinion I do not know the implication. Rather, it is from what the Rashba wrote, as follows: I wonder according to everyone why he may not respond, and even ask any person, between one blessing and another, since they do not prevent fulfillment.
And the men of the watch would read one and leave the second, even initially, etc. Perhaps one can say that although the blessings do not prevent fulfillment, nevertheless initially it is forbidden to interrupt. The men of the watch, since the time of Yotzer Or had not arrived, are like after the fact. However, this is satisfactory only according to the one who says the one blessing was Ahavah Rabbah, but according to the one who says it was Yotzer Or, it is difficult for me. End quote. Thus, by reasoning, even initially one may interrupt to greet any person; and according to the one who says that the men of the watch said Yotzer Or, it is even more difficult. Although regarding every person the Sages explicitly forbade, in any event in such a case it is permitted to greet the reader, even though he will need to answer him. We should not add a stringency. According to this, there is permission to greet the reader only when the reader stands between the sections; but when he stands in the middle of a section, there is no proof to permit asking if the reader will need to answer.
And in Terumas HaDeshen, etc. Outstanding, etc. For we rule in siman 472 that a student reclining before his teacher does not require reclining. Terumas HaDeshen wrote that the same applies when one reclines before an outstanding Torah scholar, that he does not require reclining, for he is called his teacher even if he did not study before him. He brought proof from Eli, who said to Shmuel, as stated in Berachos 31b, 'You have spoken well, but you are ruling halacha in the presence of your teacher.' Yet Shmuel had not yet studied before him, for that day his mother had brought him there. Rather, one must say that since Eli was the greatest of the generation, he has the law of his teacher. The Magen Avraham holds that the same applies regarding interruption.
