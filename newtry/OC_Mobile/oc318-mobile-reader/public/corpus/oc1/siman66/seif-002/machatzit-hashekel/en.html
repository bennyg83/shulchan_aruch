(sk 2) "Greater," etc. — and so in the Gemara. Meaning: Rashba rejected Rashi's explanation that "out of fear" means a king or oppressor. And thus: for behold we learned in a baraita (14a): he was reading Shema and encountered his father or his teacher or one greater than him (in our version it reads "or his father"). Between sections he greets out of honor, etc., and in the middle he greets out of fear, etc. — for ostensibly it implies that in those mentioned in the baraita there is both "out of honor" and "out of fear."

"And in all of these," etc. — there is not one who would kill him. Rather certainly "out of honor" means one greater than him in wisdom, etc., and "his father and teacher" means "out of fear," for he is commanded in fear of him — end quote Rashba.

And if the reader is greater than him — so too there regarding Rava bar Shabba, see there.

And it seems from Rashba, etc. — I do not know the meaning in my humble opinion. Rather from what Rashba wrote, and thus: "And I wonder at all of them — why does he not respond, and even if one greets any person between one blessing and another, since they do not impede."

"And the men of the watch would read one [section] and leave the second, even lekhatchilah," etc. — and perhaps it is said: although the blessings do not impede, nevertheless lekhatchilah it is forbidden to interrupt. And the men of the watch, since the time for Yotzer Or has not arrived, it is like b'dieved; but nevertheless it is not satisfactory except per the view that the first blessing is Ahavah Rabbah; but per the view that it is Yotzer Or it is difficult for me — end quote. Behold it is explained that even lekhatchilah one may interrupt to greet any person; and per the view that the men of the watch [say] Yotzer Or, they said it is more difficult, and it follows that for every person our sages forbade it in explanation — nevertheless in such a case it is permitted to greet the reader in peace even though he will need to respond to him; and do not add stringency. And accordingly there is no permission to greet the reader in peace except when the reader is standing between the sections; but when he is standing in the middle of a section there is no proof to permit asking if the reader will need to respond.

And in Terumat HaDeshen, etc. — a distinguished Torah scholar, etc., for we hold in siman 472 that a student with his teacher need not recline. And he wrote in Terumat HaDeshen that the same applies when reclining with a distinguished Torah scholar — he need not recline, for he is called his teacher even if he did not learn before him. And they brought proof from Eli, who said to Shmuel (as in Berakhot 31b): "You speak well; nevertheless you teach halachah in the presence of your teacher." And behold he had not yet learned before him, for that day his mother brought him there. If so, since Eli was the great one of the generation, he has the status of his teacher; and Magen Avraham holds the same regarding interruption.
