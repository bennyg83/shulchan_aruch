Magen Avraham sk 5 — he interrupts and blesses. In my humble opinion there is much to discuss — see Tosafot 14b who wrote, etc.: "One greets out of fear" — Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah; the halachah is like Rabbi Yehudah that even in the middle one responds out of honor; if so it seems permitted to answer Kaddish and Kedushah in the middle of Keriat Shema, for you have no greater "out of honor" than this — end quote. It is evident from their words that for Rabbi Meir, who does not respond out of honor, it is forbidden to interrupt and answer Kedushah — only since the halachah is like Rabbi Yehudah who responds out of honor, therefore it is permitted to answer Kaddish and Kedushah. And even though he is not greeting out of honor, it must be that Tosafot hold: since the entire congregation is engaged in holiness in the recitation of Kedushah, it is like responding. And therefore they were precise in their language "you have no greater out of honor than this" — it implies they compared this to responding, which is permitted out of honor. And if so, all this applies to Kaddish and Kedushah where the congregation answers — one may answer with them, for it is like responding. But for hearing the sound of thunder, when the entire congregation is engaged in Keriat Shema — perhaps they may not interrupt, for it is not like responding. And if so it would seem to distinguish: if he heard the sound of thunder between the sections, where one greets out of honor — a fortiori he should interrupt to bless on the thunder; but in the middle, where he does not greet out of honor, he should not bless.
