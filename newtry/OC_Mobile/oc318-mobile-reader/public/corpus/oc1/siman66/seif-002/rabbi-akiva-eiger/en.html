Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. He should interrupt and recite the blessing. In my humble opinion, this requires considerable discussion. See Tosafot on page 13, who write: 'One initiates a greeting out of fear. Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehuda disagree, and the halakhah follows Rabbi Yehuda, who permits one even in the middle of a section to respond out of respect. It therefore appears that one may answer Kaddish and Kedushah in the middle of Keriat Shema, for there is no greater matter of respect than this.' Their words demonstrate that according to Rabbi Meir, who does not permit responding out of respect, one may not interrupt to answer Kedushah. It is only because the halakhah follows Rabbi Yehuda, who permits responding out of respect, that answering Kaddish and Kedushah is permitted. Although one may not initiate a greeting out of respect, Tosafot must maintain that because the entire congregation is engaged in Kedushah by reciting it, joining them is considered like responding. This is why they carefully write, 'There is no greater matter of respect than this,' implying that they compare it to responding, which is permitted out of respect. Accordingly, all this applies to Kaddish and Kedushah, which the congregation answers: one may answer together with them because this is like responding. But if he hears thunder while the entire congregation is engaged in Keriat Shema, perhaps he may not interrupt, because this is not like responding. It would therefore appear proper to distinguish: If he hears thunder between the sections, where he may initiate a greeting out of respect, then all the more so may he interrupt to recite the blessing over thunder. In the middle of a section, however, where he may not initiate a greeting out of respect, he should not recite the blessing.
