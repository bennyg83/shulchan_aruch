We kindle the Chanukah lamp, etc. In Beis Yosef he brought this in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, siman 60, and afterward he brought in the name of Rabbi David Abudarham that there are those who kindle them after reciting Havdalah over the cup. Here in Shulchan Aruch he ruled like Terumas HaDeshen. He wrote his words after it was found in the customs that this is what they do, in order to delay the departure of the Shabbos day. I heard that the Rabbi Maharal of Prague issued a practical ruling according to the second view, to recite Havdalah first, and so he, of blessed memory, wrote in his sefer Or Chadash. I set my heart to this, according to my humble opinion, and this is what I concluded with the help of Heaven: it is proper to act this way and to put Havdalah before the Chanukah lamp. For it is stated in the Mishnah in chapter Kol Hatadir that what is frequent comes before what is not frequent, and it derives this from the verse, besides the continual burnt offering. This is clear: whenever we have no clear proof from the Talmud to override the frequent because of some reason, we say to that reason, you have done nothing, for from where do you have license to uproot the rule? And here there is no reason to override the frequent. I will lay out before you the relevant places where we find a superiority in delaying the departure of Shabbos. In chapter Arvei Pesachim, page 105, it is stated: one who enters his home on Motzaei Shabbos blesses over wine, over the light, over spices, and afterward Havdalah over the cup. If he has only one cup, he leaves it until after the meal and arranges all of them after it. We do not say that a mitzvah is beloved in its time, meaning that he should recite Havdalah before the meal, which is the time of Havdalah. Rashi explains there that the reason is that we delay the departure of the day through Havdalah, so that it should not appear as a burden upon him. Further, there on page 103, regarding the order of wine, kiddush, lamp, Havdalah, and time, we arrange kiddush before Havdalah because of the reason explained by Rashbam, that if he recites Havdalah first and afterward kiddush, the sanctity of Shabbos appears to him as a burden. Further, in Berachos, chapter Elu Devarim, page 53, the Rabbis taught: Beis Shammai say he blesses over the day and afterward blesses over the wine, because the day causes the wine to come. Beis Hillel say he blesses over the wine and afterward over the day, because the wine causes kiddush, for if there is no wine they do not recite kiddush; and one who recites kiddush over bread also has it in place of wine, and the blessing over bread precedes. Another reason: the blessing over wine is frequent, and the blessing over the day is not frequent, etc. What is the meaning of another reason? We answer: lest you say that there it has two reasons and here only one, here too there are two reasons for wine. And we ask: do Beis Shammai hold that the blessing over the day is preferable? But it was taught: one who enters his home on Motzaei Shabbos blesses over the wine, over the light, over the spices, and afterward Havdalah. If he has only one cup, he arranges all of them after it. And this follows Beis Shammai, etc. The Gemara answers: Beis Shammai hold that the entry of the day is different from the departure of the day. Regarding the entry of the day, meaning kiddush, the more one advances it, the better, etc. Further, regarding counting the Omer, Terumas HaDeshen, siman 60, wrote to bless before Havdalah because of delaying the departure of Shabbos. Therefore, also regarding Chanukah, one would have thought we should advance the Chanukah lamp and delay Havdalah because of the departure of the day. About this I say to you that it is not so. First, let us bring proof from that passage of Elu Devarim to the opposite. For perforce, according to Beis Hillel, who hold that he blesses over wine first, they hold that frequency overrides the superiority of the entry of the day. If so here, regarding Chanukah, the superiority of frequency should override the superiority of delaying the day, and all the more so, for in several respects the prohibition of Shabbos is more stringent at the entry of the day than at the departure of the day. Do not say that there is different, because wine has one additional superiority, namely that wine causes the day, as stated. There is support for this from what is stated in chapter Hechalil: Rav said the blessing of sukkah and afterward the blessing of time. Rashi explains: one who did not recite the blessing of time, namely Shehecheyanu, at the time of making it, etc., says the blessing of sukkah first. Rabbah bar bar Chanah said: time and afterward sukkah, because when there is something frequent and something not frequent, the frequent comes first. Let us say that Rav and Rabbah bar bar Chanah disagree in the dispute of Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel above: let us say that Rav says like Beis Shammai, and Rabbah holds like Beis Hillel. He said to him: Rav can say that I speak even according to Beis Hillel. Beis Hillel said their ruling only there, because wine causes kiddush to be said. But here, regarding sukkah, without the blessing of time, would we not still say the blessing of sukkah? Rashi explains that because of the reason of frequency alone, they would not have advanced wine, because the obligation of the day is preferable. About this I say the opposite: from there the opposite is proven. For one must examine: what was the question, let us say that Rav speaks like Beis Shammai? Did the questioner not see what Beis Hillel explicitly said, another reason: wine causes kiddush? Rather it seems clear that at first glance sukkah and time are equal to wine and day. That is, regarding sukkah and time, each has one superiority: sukkah has the obligation of the day, and time has frequency. Regarding wine and day, each has two superiorities: wine has frequency and causes kiddush, and the day also has two superiorities, for it has already entered before the wine, and the day causes the wine to come. Therefore the questioner thought that Rav follows Beis Shammai. The Gemara answers that this is not so. Rather, the obligation of the day of sukkah is preferable to the superiority of the time blessing, because sukkah is not dependent on the time blessing at all, for we say the blessing of sukkah even without the time blessing, such as at Shacharis; but the time blessing without sukkah we do not say on the fifth day. Therefore sukkah overrides the superiority of frequency of the time blessing. This is not so regarding the day and wine of Beis Hillel, where each has two superiorities. We say that the superiority of the day causing and the superiority of the wine causing are exactly equal, for there is no kiddush without the day and there is no kiddush without wine. It follows that wine remains with the superiority of frequency, and the day remains with the superiority that it already entered, and we say that frequency overrides that superiority. So too, the superiority of frequency of Havdalah overrides the superiority of delaying the day that Chanukah would have to come first. Further, it seems that perforce this superiority, that Shabbos has already entered, includes within it the superiority of the entry of the day. For if not, what is this that they said in chapter Elu Devarim, here too there are two? If you would think that the superiority of the entry of the day is one superiority by itself, and the superiority that it already entered is another superiority by itself, it is difficult, for then there would be three superiorities in the day, while wine has only two. Rather, perforce they are one and the same: the entry of the day and the fact that it already entered are one. If so, since the frequency of wine overrides the entry of the day, certainly the frequency of Havdalah overrides the departure of the day. The order of wine, kiddush, lamp, Havdalah, and time that we mentioned is no proof that there is a superiority in the departure of the day, because there is no opposing superiority of frequency there, for kiddush and Havdalah are equal in frequency. All this I wrote to give the matter breadth, if we say that there is here a superiority of the departure of the day. But it appears that in our case of Chanukah and Shabbos there is no superiority at all because of the departure of the day. For one cannot say here that Shabbos should not be a burden upon him, since when he blesses to kindle the Chanukah lamp, he thereby removes from himself the yoke of Shabbos through this blessing. How can you say that he is still in the sanctity of Shabbos? This is unlike wine and day, where the sanctity of Yom Tov can exist together with Shabbos, and one does not contradict the other. Likewise in the order of wine, kiddush, lamp, Havdalah, and time, the sanctity of Yom Tov that he puts first does not contradict the sanctity of Shabbos, for it is possible for the sanctity of both to be upon him. This is not so regarding Chanukah and Shabbos, where he has already blessed explicitly upon the removal of Shabbos. With this, everything written regarding the superiority of the departure of the day is removed. Only from counting the Omer there is still proof, for there too they contradict each other, since he explicitly counts the day of tomorrow. But one may say that counting is different, for it begins at the very start of the night immediately, because we require complete weeks, while Havdalah begins after some time of the night, because one must add from the weekday to the holy. If so, certainly a mitzvah is beloved in its time. This is not so regarding the Chanukah lamp. Although its mitzvah is until feet cease from the marketplace, nevertheless on Shabbos, when it is impossible to do so except by ending Shabbos, then the mitzvah of Havdalah takes effect, and it is frequent; if so, it certainly precedes the Chanukah lamp. There is further proof that even in a place where there is the superiority of delaying the day, it does not override what is frequent, and it is not comparable to the obligation of the day. For in chapter Kol Hatadir the Gemara asks, when one matter is frequent and another is more sanctified, which of them comes first, and it remains in doubt. Why did it not resolve the question by saying that just as regarding the obligation of the day of sukkah, frequency is overridden, so too regarding the superiority of sanctity? Rather, perforce, specifically the obligation of the day, which is the very great superiority of sukkah, do we say that sukkah is recited without time, and this is preferable to the frequency of the time blessing. This is not so regarding another superiority, such as sanctity or another superiority like the publicizing of the Chanukah miracle, which does not override what is frequent. Although the question remains in doubt, it implies that we should rule that what is frequent is preferable, since it is a rule in the Torah and we have no clear matter to remove it from the rule, especially since we have proofs as we have explained. Nor should one say that the superiority of publicizing the miracle should decide the matter and put the Chanukah lamp first, as stated above regarding the Chanukah lamp and kiddush of the day, where the Chanukah lamp is preferable because of publicizing the miracle. This is not so, for regarding precedence this is not effective, as Tosafos wrote there in chapter Bameh Madlikin regarding the Chanukah lamp and the Shabbos lamp, as follows: the reason we first read the Rosh Chodesh portion before that of Chanukah is that in Torah reading one can do both, frequency and publicizing the miracle; we do both. But where it is impossible to do both, publicizing the miracle is preferable. Until here. So too wrote Rosh. If so, the same applies regarding the Chanukah lamp and Havdalah: we have a clear proof to put the frequent first, since it is possible to fulfill both. Do not object from what Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Megillah, brought by Beis Yosef at the beginning of the laws of Megillah, that Megillah reading comes before the Temple service, even though it is possible for the kohanim to read the Megillah after the service; nevertheless there is greater publicizing of the miracle when they read with the community. This teaches that because of publicizing the miracle we put it first. This is not comparable, for there certainly is a distinction between the kohanim reading alone, apart from the congregation, and their reading with the congregation. This is not so here, where the entire congregation reads the Chanukah portion after the Rosh Chodesh reading, and likewise the kindling of the Chanukah lamp after Havdalah is equal for all. We do not care about publicizing the miracle here with respect to precedence. One also should not object to our words from the fact that our practice regarding Megillah reading on Motzaei Shabbos is to delay Havdalah, as Rema wrote in siman 693. This is not difficult, for here too Megillah reading does not contradict the sanctity of Shabbos, as I distinguished above. Further, regarding Megillah too it is like what I wrote above concerning counting, for Megillah too has its time immediately at the start of the night, while Havdalah comes after the addition from weekday to holy. Afterward I saw a gloss in Alfasi as follows: there are those who kindle before Havdalah after the order of Kedushah, and Raavad wrote that it is not proper to do so; first, because of the blessing of the lights of fire, and afterward they kindle. Thus even regarding the blessing over fire, he did not consider it proper to put the kindling of Chanukah first, for there is no logic that one should first bless over a mitzvah dependent on fire and afterward bless over the original creation of fire. Although we recite Havdalah after they have already kindled lamps for the Megillah, nevertheless they did not bless over them, unlike the Chanukah lamp. This is similar to the reasoning that I wrote concerning the blessing of Havdalah: how can he recite Havdalah after he has already blessed over the lamp and interrupted Shabbos through his blessing? Raavad mentioned a greater novelty. Therefore it appears to me that he should first bless over Havdalah and afterward over the Chanukah lamp. In Mordechai, chapter Bameh Madlikin, he wrote as follows: although what is frequent comes first, and he should have put that of Rosh Chodesh first, nevertheless publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. Until here. This matter does not have a broad explanation except with difficulty, and it has already been written there in the margin that this text is not in two versions of Mordechai. Therefore nothing should be learned from these words. What appears to me as practical halachah I have written. Although the custom has already spread in synagogues to kindle the Chanukah lamp first, nevertheless one who grasps the matter according to halachah should first recite Havdalah in his home and afterward kindle the Chanukah lamp.
