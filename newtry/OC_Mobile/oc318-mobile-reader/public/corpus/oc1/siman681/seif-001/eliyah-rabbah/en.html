Levush: In order to delay, etc. So wrote Terumas HaDeshen, and his proof is from the whole order of Havdalah, where we delay the blessing of Havdalah and the other blessings, and from the order of wine, kiddush, lamp, Havdalah, and time. Rabbi David Abudarham wrote that one recites Havdalah and afterward kindles. Our teacher Rabbi Loew, of blessed memory, instituted this here in Prague, and he wrote in the Ner Mitzvah that he authored that kindling is different, for it is impossible unless he recited Havdalah in prayer, and sometimes he did not recite Havdalah and will commit a transgression; and the words seem correct. This is from Malbushei Yom Tov. But from the day I began to understand, I have seen no custom here in Prague other than that they kindle and afterward recite Havdalah. Therefore I took to heart that the aforementioned reason may be rejected, for the whole world is already accustomed throughout the year to kindle the lamps given to them by the shamash after the order of Kedushah. And if one forgot Atah Chonantanu, he says Blessed is the One Who separates, etc. If so, Motzaei Shabbos Chanukah is no worse, especially in the synagogue, where the chazan kindles the Chanukah lamp, and the chazan is more careful in his prayer not to forget Atah Chonantanu. Magen Avraham wrote that one who forgot Atah should recite Havdalah and afterward kindle, but this is not clear. He also did not see in Ner Mitzvah there, where it wrote about this that we have not found that one should sometimes act this way. Now Turei Zahav wrote at great length with a new reason to put Havdalah first, from what Tosafos wrote in chapter Bameh Madlikin, as follows: the reason we first read the Rosh Chodesh portion before that of Chanukah is that in Torah reading one can do both, what is frequent and publicizing the miracle. Until here. If so, the same applies regarding the Chanukah lamp and Havdalah: we have clear proof to put the frequent first, since it is possible to fulfill both. Until here are his words. I am astonished at him, for Tosafos there wrote two further answers: one, that in Torah reading the publicizing of the miracle is not recognizable; second, in order to read the haftarah from the subject with which he concluded, the lamps of Zechariah. If so, according to these two answers the opposite is explicit, that publicizing the miracle comes before what is frequent, even when it is possible to fulfill both. Thus Terumas HaDeshen and the authorities who say that the Chanukah lamp precedes Havdalah hold like the last two answers of Tosafos. Turei Zahav further wrote as follows: Mordechai in chapter Bameh Madlikin wrote, Although what is frequent comes first and he should have put that of Rosh Chodesh first, nevertheless publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. Until here. This matter does not have a broad explanation, and it has already been written there in the margin that this text is not in two versions of Mordechai, and therefore nothing should be learned from these words. Until here are the words of Turei Zahav. With pardon to his honor, he was not precise at all, for immediately before this it is also explicitly explained in Mordechai that publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. He wrote as follows: and we read in the Torah that of Rosh Chodesh first and afterward that of Chanukah, so that the last reader will read the haftarah from the lamps of Zechariah, in the subject with which he concluded. Until here. This implies that if not for the reason of the haftarah, Chanukah would come first because publicizing the miracle is preferable to what is frequent, even though both are fulfilled. With this Hashem illuminated my eyes in the words of the aforementioned Mordechai, which Turei Zahav and Rabbi Menachem of Tiktin deleted. Also in Responsa Rema, siman 38, paragraph 2, he wrote at length in explaining it, and in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 684, he wondered about him and rejected his explanation. But in my humble opinion it has a broad explanation: what Mordechai wrote, Although what is frequent comes first and he should have put that of Rosh Chodesh first, means that he is asking himself: why do I need the reason of the haftarah? Let it derive from the rule of frequency, like the first answer of Tosafos. He answered: nevertheless, publicizing the miracle is preferable even for precedence. This is against the aforementioned first answer of Tosafos, and if so it would have been proper to read Chanukah first, because it publicizes the miracle; therefore the reason of the haftarah was needed. The rule that emerges is that there is clear proof from Mordechai to put the Chanukah lamp first. Turei Zahav further wrote: I saw in Shiltei Giborim that some kindle before Havdalah, and Raavad wrote that it is not proper to do so because of the blessing of the lights of fire. Thus even the blessing over fire did not seem proper to him to precede, for there is no logic that one should first recite a blessing over a mitzvah dependent on fire and afterward bless over its original creation; all the more so regarding the blessing of Havdalah, how can he recite Havdalah when he has already interrupted Shabbos with his blessing? Raavad mentioned a greater novelty, that even though we kindle lamps for the Megillah and afterward recite Havdalah, nevertheless they did not bless over them. Until here. Now a great error fell into the understanding of Raavad's words, as if the problem were because he blessed over the Chanukah lamp. For I found in Kol Bo, page 44, as follows: Raavad wrote that it is not proper to do so, and likewise regarding the Megillah one should not read until after Havdalah, for it is not proper to bless over light after one has filled his eyes with it. Until here. It is thus explicit that he holds Megillah and the Chanukah lamp to be equal. What he wrote because of the blessing of the lights of fire means that one should not bless over fire after he has filled his eyes with it. According to this, since we hold regarding the Megillah that it is read before Havdalah, the same applies to the Chanukah lamp. According to this, it seems to me that there is no proof either from Rabbi David Abudarham, cited by Malbushei Yom Tov and Mateh Moshe, for he may likewise hold regarding the Megillah that Havdalah is recited first. Mateh Moshe wondered: how can we delay Havdalah because of the departure of the day, when by kindling the Chanukah lamp one shows that it is weekday? It seems to me that this is not difficult at all, for every Motzaei Shabbos we recite Borei Me'orei Ha'eish over fire that was lit before Havdalah, and the practice is always to kindle lamps before Havdalah. Rather, certainly, nevertheless, there is still a trace of the sanctity of the Shabbos soul until Havdalah over the cup. Now in Eliyahu Zuta I brought my view to recite Havdalah at home first, because I saw the wonder of Ner Mitzvah, Mateh Moshe, and Malbushei Yom Tov. Nevertheless, I myself did not practice this way, and I wrote only that one should not protest against those who practice so. Now, thank God, I have settled everything; therefore I say that one should not change the custom at all, even at home.
