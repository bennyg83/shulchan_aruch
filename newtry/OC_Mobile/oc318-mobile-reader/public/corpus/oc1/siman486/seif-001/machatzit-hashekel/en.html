In Kerisos folio 14a. [Regarding] "a gerogeret is a third of an egg": for he wrote there that the measure of eiruvei chatzeros for eighteen people or more is eighteen gerogerot, which is about six eggs; and according to the other opinion, that is to say according to the other opinion in siman 368, that eighteen gerogerot are eight eggs. And we say in chapter HaMatznia at the beginning of folio 91. And elsewhere we say etc. in tractate Yoma folio 80a; and see Tosafos in the aforementioned places.
And it appears to me that this is what it means: three olives, etc. That is to say: in Kerisos it refers to one who reckons, there is one who eats one eating and is liable upon it for five chatas offerings and one asham: a tamei person who ate chelev the size of a koseves (which is two olives), and it is nosar from the kodashim on Yom Kippur. And [the Gemara] asks: Let him teach six chatas offerings, for example where he added in that same eating and ate with it a kezayis of blood. And it answers: it deals with one eating; it does not deal with two eatings; and the Sages estimated that the throat does not hold more than two eggs; thus far the words of the Gemara. If so, the intent is that it does not hold three olives; and if he ate a kezayis of blood together with a koseves of chelev, that would be three olives; and see Rashi there. However, according to the Rambam, that is to say he holds that a gerogeret is a third of an egg as above: if so, three gerogerot are a kebeitzah; all the more so an olive, which is smaller than a gerogeret, so that three olives are smaller than a kebeitzah: the difficulties of the Gemara of Kerisos [remain] difficult. And in the days of my youth, the late rabbi Moreinu HaRav R' Yisrael H.P., of blessed memory, posed to me the difficulty of that Gemara of Kerisos from the words of the Mordechai that Magen Avraham cited above in siman 475, s.k. 4: when it is mashed it holds three olives, leaving the Gemara's difficulty in Kerisos remaining; and I answered him at length; and see there in Tosafos Yeshanim.
