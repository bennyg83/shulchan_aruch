(א) In Keritot daf 14a — a dried fig is a third of an egg, as he wrote there that the measure of courtyard eruvin for eighteen people or more is eighteen dried figs, which is like six eggs; and for a first view, see siman 468 that eighteen dried figs are eight eggs. And we say in the chapter Hametzui at the beginning of daf 91, and everywhere we say etc. in tractate Yoma daf 80a — and see Tosafot in those places.

(ב) And it appears to me that this is what "three olives" etc. means — that in Keritot it deals with one who considers there is one eating and he is liable for five sin-offerings and one guilt-offering: a tamei who ate chelev like a kotevet (which is two olives) and it is leftover of consecrated things on Yom Kippur, and it challenges why not six sin-offerings as if he added in that eating and ate with it a kezayit of blood. And it answers: it deals with one eating; it does not deal with two eatings. And the Sages measured: the swallowing organ does not hold more than two eggs — end quote of the Gemara; if so the intent is it does not hold three olives, and if one ate a kezayit of blood with a kotevet of chelev it is three olives — see Rashi there. However for Rambam it means he holds a dried fig is a third of an egg as explained; if so three dried figs are like an egg; if so all the more an olive which is smaller than a dried fig — that three olives are smaller than an egg — the difficulties of the Gemara in Keritot are difficult. And in winter days the late R' Israel Heller z"l challenged me on that Gemara in Keritot from the words of Mordechai who brought Magen Avraham above siman 475 s.k. 4 that when crushed it holds three olives — the difficulty of the Gemara in Keritot remains; and I answered him at length — see there in old Tosafot.
