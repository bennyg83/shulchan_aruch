In Tur it is written, etc. See Taz. The principal laws of the Seder may be summarized as follows. See Levush. A cup used for a blessing should be rinsed on the outside and washed on the inside. See simanim 271 and 183, Rambam, Hilchos Shabbos, chapter 29, halachah 7, and Berachos 51a. Bach writes in siman 479 that on this night one should be meticulous to rinse the cup even when it appears clean. Cups two and four are likewise cups of blessing and require rinsing. If a new cup is brought for either of them, the same laws apply. On Pesach night, if one has only a single glass vessel that has not been immersed, it may perhaps be immersed in foul water for the sake of the mitzvah of the four cups. If no water at all is available, perhaps one may use it without immersion, because the D’Rabbanan positive mitzvah of the four cups may override the D’Rabbanan immersion requirement for glass. This would not apply to a metal vessel, whose immersion is a Torah obligation. See Yoreh Deah, siman 120, seif 16. It is also possible that the mitzvah and the prohibited act do not occur simultaneously, and the matter requires study. Our custom is to bring the Seder plate and everything needed before Kiddush. While holding the cup for Kiddush in the right hand, one covers the matzah; afterward one uncovers it for the Haggadah, which is recited over the bread of affliction. One takes a roasted foreleg from a male lamb, where possible, as a remembrance of the Korban Pesach, together with a second cooked item as a remembrance of the festival offering. Even when the first night falls on Motzaei Shabbos, two cooked items are taken; only the wording concerning the Korban Pesach and the festival offering changes in some customs. There is no need to remove the egg and meat while saying “This is the bread of affliction,” contrary to what is printed in some siddurim. When reciting the blessing over eating matzah, one should intend also for korech and the afikoman. One should therefore avoid unrelated conversation until after the afikoman. Shelah writes that, ideally, the entire meal should be eaten while reclining, apart from the Haggadah and Hallel. Bircas HaMazon should be recited sitting with awe. After the fact, it is sufficient to recline for the required kezayis of matzah and for the cups. See Magen Avraham, siman 472, seif katan 7. When reciting Hamotzi, one holds all three matzos. He then releases the bottom matzah and recites the blessing over eating matzah while holding the upper whole matzah and the broken middle matzah. He breaks a kezayis from each and eats them together. See siman 475. A kezayis of maror is required, because less than a kezayis is not considered eating. If one cannot eat that amount, he certainly may not recite the blessing “concerning eating maror.” For korech as well, one should take a kezayis where possible. If this is very difficult, the first kezayis of maror is the essential requirement. At the first dipping, he should eat less than a kezayis and intend that the blessing cover the later maror. If he possesses only maror, see siman 475, seif 2. If he has another vegetable whose blessing is Shehakol, it may be used after the fact for the first dipping, with intent that its blessing cover the maror. Kiddush should not be recited until definite nightfall, meaning after the appearance of the stars, because one should not initially rely on a D’Rabbanan uncertainty. See siman 489. One should hasten to complete the Seder before midnight. After the fact, matzah eaten after midnight should be eaten without the blessing. Every mitzvah practiced on the first night is practiced on the second night as well. A woman should not discharge men in the Haggadah even on the second night, lest the second night come to be treated lightly in comparison with the first. Tur implies that one may not drink wine after the afikoman. See siman 478 and Magen Avraham. Likewise, between the third and fourth cups one should not drink lest he become intoxicated and fail to recite Hallel properly. Some authorities permit wine within the meal when it will not intoxicate. See siman 479, Taz, seif katan 2.
