Seif 1: the measure of a kezayit — some say it is like half an egg. Note: I saw in Rashi on Horiyot 7b s.v. hu bechalav hamkaseh et hakarban, who wrote: perhaps they erred regarding chelev on the offering, whether with a kezayit or less than a kezayit, for less than a kezayit is not written explicitly, and it is a matter the Sadducees do not concede; and he wrote similarly on "vehen badam" — see there. At first glance his words are not understood — how is error applicable here? On the contrary, if they erred that even less than a kezayit is forbidden, all the more they should not have eaten — in what did they err? One cannot say they held that eating is not considered until one eats a great amount to satiation: first, Rashi's language that he wrote "between kezayit and less than kezayit" does not imply that; second, why did he write that the Sadducees do not concede — if they lack halacha leMoshe miSinai, on the contrary it would be eating in any case, and we do not require eating that includes satiation except where it is written "and you shall eat and be satisfied," but not in plain "eating." Therefore Rashi's intent appears per what is written in Yoma 80a — R' Elazar: one who eats chelev in our time must write the measure, lest a court come and dispute measures — see there — that they erred between a large kezayit and a medium kezayit. I wrote in this siman in our chiddushim earlier that we do not hold like R' Elazar; therefore this will be Rashi's intent: they erred between kezayit and less than kezayit — meaning that what is truly a kezayit they thought was less than a kezayit, because they erred that we require a large kezayit; and what he wrote that the Sadducees do not concede — the main reason is that if they erred in what the Sadducees concede, that error is close to intentional and would be negligence, not true inadvertence; but in what the Sadducees do not concede it is complete inadvertence. If so here too, since the Sadducees have no measure at all, and for us we require a measure, the matter is not revealed how much a kezayit is; therefore if they erred between large and medium kezayit it is considered complete error and he is liable for a korban — as I developed per his intent; it fits well — investigate. We learn in the Talmud in Berachot, Eruvin, and Sukkah that R' Anan said: this entire verse was stated for measures, etc.; and the Gemara challenges in Sukkah and Eruvin: behold R' Yochanan said measures are halacha leMoshe miSinai — why does it challenge? Behold we say in tractate Yoma cited above that the Rabbis were uncertain whether we require a large kezayit or a medium kezayit, and similarly there is a question whether a large date with its pit or like it without its pit — and if so why challenge? Say that for this it would be halacha: if we require like it with its pit, one could say from Scripture it implies it is possible without its pit, and halacha teaches with its pit; and if halacha were without pit, meaning from Scripture it implies with pit, therefore halacha is the reverse; and similarly for kezayit — if halacha were large kezayit, one could say from Scripture it implies medium, or the reverse — this requires study. And in the omissions to Hilchot Niddah it is still in manuscript in Hilchot Mikvaot we discussed there why it does not answer regarding the measure of eruv of mikvaot — see there and investigate. Magen Avraham wrote here: it states in Keritot etc. that the swallowing organ does not hold more than two olives, etc. — end quote of Magen Avraham. From this one can say the words of Rambam in Hilchot Nezirut regarding the law of a nazir who drank wine all day — he is liable only once; they said to him do not drink, do not drink — he is liable for each one. In his commentary to the Mishnayot he explained, and Ra'avad and Tosafot Yoma brought in ch. 8 of Makkot mishnah 3 and 6 regarding do not drink do not drink — similarly if it were in one vessel several measures and they warned him do not drink, for he would become liable for several lashings even though it is one warning — see there. Tosafot Yoma wondered: since this law is from Yerushalmi and Rambam wrote it in his commentary to the Mishnayot, why did he not write it in his rulings — see there. In my opinion one can answer: behold even when they said to him do not drink do not drink, we require that he drink kedei hasra'ah for his warning, as Ra'avad and Tosafot wrote; therefore if they warned him once regarding much, how is it possible that drinking a second revi'it would be kedei hasra'ah for the first warning — behold certainly the measure of drinking a revi'it is more than kedei she'elah of the Talmud to Rav. Therefore it appears Yerushalmi holds the swallowing organ holds two revi'it — then one finds a case that he drank two revi'it together; or Yerushalmi holds the measure of drinking is also a kezayit, and since the swallowing organ holds at least two olives, one finds that he drank kedei hasra'ah for the warning and receives two lashings, as proven in the Talmud in the first chapter of Sukkah regarding a vine of revi'it wine for a nazir — if so it is proven from this that we require a revi'it for a nazir and not a kezayit. Also proven in Keritot that the swallowing organ does not hold more than two olives — if so one does not find that he would be liable for a second lashes to be kedei hasra'ah for the first warning; therefore Rambam omitted it — and it fits well — investigate; and see in Mei Niddah 72b what he wrote to discuss the matter of measures there regarding revi'it wine for a nazir, and what he wrote there to challenge the Talmud in Eruvin and Sukkah why it challenges that measures are d'oraisa — see there and investigate.
