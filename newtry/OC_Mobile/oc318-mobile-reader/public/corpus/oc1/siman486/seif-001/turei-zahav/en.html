In Tur he wrote in this siman that one drinks a revi'it or its majority — this requires study: why did Tur write in siman 472 he need not drink all of it, only the majority, and here wrote revi'it or majority? And further, what clarity does he give us here — if majority suffices, why mention revi'it? Maharshal z"l explained that Tur here means a revi'it l'chatchila and b'dieved majority suffices; and in the name of Rashal he brought that there is a distinction between cups when one needs a revi'it and when majority suffices, and Rashal elaborated — all this does not settle for me that Tur would speak by hint and not explain in commentary, especially what the essence of the law depends on; and further the change from siman 472 is not settled. But per what is written siman 497, by law majority suffices even l'chatchila, only one who wishes to fulfill the mitzvah optimally drinks a full revi'it because of Birkat HaMazon — then Tur's words here, revi'it or majority, are fit to teach that by law they are equal, only on the side of better practice; and one cannot here divide between b'dieved and l'chatchila; and even optimal practice we do not require more there, only here he spoke since here he mentioned the law of Birkat HaMazon and thereby it is fit to do optimally, unlike the first cups where even optimally no more than majority is required; and although in siman 489 Tur mentioned the law of Birkat HaMazon, nevertheless he did not mention there the measure of drinking at all, only here in the order of the Seder where he mentioned the measure and remembers the law of Birkat HaMazon — therefore he mentioned here also optimal practice; and this explanation is pleasant and accepted in Tur's language, and his intent b'siyata diShmaya; and from this is great support for my explanation in siman 497 — see there. Further, Tur wrote: they bring before him a bowl containing three matzot, as in the mishnah: they said bring before him — he dips in lettuce etc.; bring before him matzah and lettuce etc.; and Rashi and Rashbam explained that bringing matzah is after the first dipping; and here Tur did not order it so, because in the mishnah it deals with bringing to him one after another, then he does in that order; but Tur mentioned that they bring everything together. Further, Tur wrote: he concludes "the King who is praised in praises." Beit Yosef challenged: since he concludes in Yishtabach, he should conclude in "He who chooses song" which is the conclusion of Yishtabach; and above siman 481 he wrote: but now disciples came etc., he concludes on the fourth cup Yehalelucha and concludes "the King who is praised in praises," and on the fifth cup concludes in Nishmat "He who chooses" etc. — it implies here he mentioned only "the King who is praised in praises," implying he concludes only in "the King who is praised in praises"; and he brought the view of Maharam of Padua that one should wait in Yehalelucha until reaching the conclusion of Yishtabach and there say — (here missing) — Yishtabach gadol batashbachot is in Tur's nusach "the King who is praised in praises," as explained siman 54 that one does not answer amen after the conclusion of Yishtabach when saying "the King who is praised in praises," but waits until "He who lives forever"; and this is his intent here too when he says and concludes "the King who is praised in praises," meaning until in song etc. "He who lives forever," only he cited the head of the conclusion; and above siman 481 where he cited the conclusion of Yishtabach until "He who chooses" — he had to give this siman, since there he said before that after Yehalelucha he concludes "the King who is praised in praises," which is the conclusion of Yehalelucha that he concludes only in this alone; therefore he had to say here another siman for the conclusion of [Yishtabach], not in "the King who is praised in praises," so one should not err that it is like the conclusion of Yehalelucha — and this appears straightforward per Tur's view, and aligns with what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of hagahot Semak that the conclusion of Nishmat is until "He who lives forever," and all ascends on one reed.
