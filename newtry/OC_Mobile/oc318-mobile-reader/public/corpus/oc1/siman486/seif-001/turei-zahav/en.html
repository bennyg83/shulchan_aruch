In the Tur in this siman he wrote: and he should drink a reviit or most of it. This requires study: why in siman 472 did the Tur write that one need not drink all of it, only most of it, while here he wrote a reviit or most of it? Also, what choice is he giving us: if most suffices, why mention a reviit? My father-in-law z"l explained that the Tur here means a reviit initially, while after the fact most suffices. In the name of Maharshal he brought that there is a distinction among the cups, when a reviit is required and when most suffices, and Maharshal discussed this at length. None of this settles for me that the Tur would state his words by hint without explaining them explicitly, especially when the core law depends on it. Nor is the change from what he wrote in siman 472 settled. According to what I wrote in siman 190, that by law most suffices even initially, but one who wants to follow the better counsel should drink a full reviit because of the final blessing, it is clear why the Tur wrote here "a reviit or most of it": to teach that by law they are equivalent, only that one is the better counsel. There is no distinction here between after the fact and initially. Even the better counsel is not needed there more than here, but here they spoke because here he mentioned the law of the final blessing, and in that respect it is proper to follow the better counsel. This is unlike the first cups, where there is not even better counsel beyond most of it. Although in siman 480 the Tur mentioned the law of a final blessing, nevertheless he did not mention there the required amount of drinking at all; only here, in the order where he mentioned the measure and mentioned the law of a final blessing, he also mentioned the better counsel. This explanation is fine and acceptable in the language of the Tur and his intent, with God's help, and from this there is a great proof to my explanation in siman 190; see there.
Further the Tur wrote: they should bring before him a plate containing three matzot, etc. In the Mishnah they said: they brought before him, he dips in chazeret, etc.; they brought before him matzah and chazeret, etc. Rashi and Rashbam explained that bringing matzah means after the first dipping. Here the Tur did not arrange it that way, because the Mishnah speaks where they bring them one after another, in which case he acts in that order; but the Tur mentions that they bring everything together.
Further the Tur wrote: and he concludes "King praised with praises." The Beit Yosef asked: since he concludes with Yishtabach, he should conclude "Who chooses songs of praise," which is the conclusion of Yishtabach. Above in siman 481 he wrote: "but now the students came," etc., he concludes over the fourth cup with "Yehalelukha" and "King praised with praises," and over the fifth cup with Nishmat, "Who chooses songs," etc. This implies that here, since he mentioned only "King praised with praises," he concludes only with "King praised with praises." He brought the opinion of Mahar"r Meir Arama that one should wait with Yehalelukha until reaching the conclusion of Yishtabach and say it there. Here something is missing. "Yishtabach, great in praises" in the Tur's versions is "King praised with praises," as explained in siman 54, where he wrote that one should not answer Amen after the conclusion of Yishtabach when he says "King praised with praises," but should wait until "Life of the worlds." This is his intent here too: he says "and concludes King praised with praises," meaning until "with songs of praise, etc., Life of the worlds," but he stated the beginning of the conclusion. Above in siman 481, where he identified the conclusion of Yishtabach as until "Who chooses songs," he had to give that sign because earlier he had said that after Yehalelukha he concludes "King praised with praises," namely the conclusion of Yehalelukha, which concludes only with that. Therefore he needed here another sign for the conclusion of Yishtabach, not "King praised with praises," so that one should not err and think it is like the conclusion of Yehalelukha. This appears simple to me in the Tur's view, and accords with what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot Semak, that the conclusion of Nishmat is until "Life of the worlds," and everything fits together.
