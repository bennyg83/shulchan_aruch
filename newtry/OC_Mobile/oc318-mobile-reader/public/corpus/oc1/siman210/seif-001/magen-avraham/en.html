One who eats. It appears to me that all foods combine for a kezayit — if he ate half a kezayit from the seven species and half a kezayit of another, he blesses after them Borei Nefashot Rabot (so it is). And it appears to me that likewise bread and another thing; and all beverages combine for a revi'it — but food and drink do not combine; see siman 212. And they said in Yoma: brine on top of a vegetable combines, and we do not say it is a beverage, for all things that prepare food (a beverage that comes to sweeten the food) are food — and likewise bread soaked in a beverage or in wine, as is clear in Tosafot Zevachim daf 109a; see above siman 205 seif 2. And requires further study regarding meat gravy whether it is called food; and in Chullin daf 120 Rashi explained: brine on top of the vegetable is beverage that comes out from the vegetable — or that they would dip their food in brine; and even though it is a beverage, and we learned "the eater and the drinker do not combine" — that is when he ate and drank, for it is clear to the Rabbis that his mind is not settled thus; but brine on top of a vegetable — it is clear to the Rabbis that his mind is settled, end of his words. If so, specifically brine on top of a vegetable; and so too Rambam: things that prepare food that are mixed with food are like food, end of his words — implying that gravy does not combine; and so too from what is written siman 158 seif 5. And if he ate a little and returned and ate a little — if he waited more than kedei achilat pras, it appears to me they do not combine, as written siman 212; and so too in Yoma daf 80 that his mind is not settled thus; and so too from Rashi's explanation that he explained the reason it requires a kezayit for the after-blessing is because it is written "eating," and eating is with a kezayit — and if so, in chelev and blood and in all prohibitions too it is written "eating," and if he waited beyond kedei achilat pras it is not called eating; and likewise regarding blessing; and so too from what is written siman 208 seif 9 and siman 475 seif 6. And regarding drinking — see siman 212 regarding the measure of combination; and in Be'er Heitev siman 207 he wrote in the name of Knesset HaGedolah to distinguish between Yom Kippur and Birkat HaMazon, and he holds that for Birkat HaMazon even if he waited more than kedei achilat pras it combines; and in my humble opinion, as I wrote, for all prohibitions in the Torah are equal to Yom Kippur in this. And it appears to me that if he ate less than the measure and went outside and returned to his place, he must bless again initially — even for bread, for it does not require an after-blessing in its place and its law is like other things; therefore requires further study if he returned and ate less than the measure whether they combine, for it is like distraction of the mind; and it appears to me they combine — see above siman 177 and so too siman 168.

A revi'it. One fills a wine vessel and places into it an egg and a half, and what comes out from it is a revi'it — and specifically with wine that is heaped on top of the vessel and does not overflow so much; but with water it overflows more — therefore one should do as written in siman 456. And in Shlah he wrote that it is nearly full of two shells of a chicken egg; and all things are measured with medium eggs, as Kelaim states and Berachot daf 39.

Beriah. It implies in Chullin daf 119 that one lentil is called a beriah even though many grow on one stalk — letter 1.

He removed the pit. And if he ate what is inside the pit, it is called a beriah — for behold he ate what is fit to eat from it; it is not reasonable to say that R' Yitzchak ate with the shell of the pit — rather certainly he ate its inside and threw away its shell. And even if he ate the pit, it is not called a beriah in something that is not customary to eat, such as an olive; but in grapes and pomegranates it is customary to eat the pit (so it is in R' Yitzchak). But for Rashba it is always a beriah; and for Rosh and Tosafot — if it was brought before him whole, even if he did not eat the pit, it is a beriah. And it appears to me per Maharar'i that R' Yitzchak did not eat an olive but one other beriah that is as large as a kezayit; and here in Shulchan Aruch it deals with grapes and pomegranates — if so, Rama's gloss is according to all, that even Rosh agrees that if he did not eat the pit in something whose custom is to eat it, it is not a beriah. And b'dieved siman 101 in Beit Yosef in the name of Maharar'i — that if a little fell from it through cooking, as it is accustomed to crumble, it is called a beriah — see there; and he holds like Rosh.

Or a revi'it. Meaning an egg and a half, and this is simple, peshat al pi peshat — and meaning because satiation of drinking is a revi'it, as written siman 212; and the Mechaber erred in this. And if he drank a kezayit and does not have a revi'it, he should not drink until a kebeitza, because some say that on a kebeitza one blesses the after-blessing.
