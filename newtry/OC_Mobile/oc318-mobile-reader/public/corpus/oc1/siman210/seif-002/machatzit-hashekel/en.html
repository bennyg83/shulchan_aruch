(s.k. 2) From a revi'it one fills a wine vessel, etc. — and specifically with wine that is heaped; therefore one should do as written in siman 456. And it is written in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah, and these are his words: From where do we know this — that in Eruvin and Shlah it is written explicitly in the name of Maharil that one fills with water; and so too Tur below siman 456, etc.; and so too Beit Yosef in Yoreh De'ah siman 98 in the name of Mordechai regarding orlah, etc., end of his words. And according to what one understood in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah — Magen Avraham's intent is specifically that one fill wine that is heaped and not water, for the measure of a revi'it is slightly less than an egg and a half; therefore when one fills wine that is heaped as when one places into it an egg and a half (for at first when one puts wine into the vessel there is no way to heap it, since one cannot compress the heap; and when he heaps it, if he puts a little more than the measure before it is heaped it spills outside — therefore the way is to fill the vessel from the line and not heaped, unlike when one places into it an egg and a half, the wine is heaped by itself from its own side) the heap lacks from the measure of an egg and a half — unlike if one fills with water, where there is no heap, it comes out as the measure of an egg and a half. According to this, what Magen Avraham wrote is difficult: "but in water," etc., "therefore one should do as written in siman 456" — and what does this help? Even if one does as written in siman 456, nevertheless since one fills with water it comes out more than a revi'it, i.e., as the measure of an egg and a half compressed, which is more than a revi'it per the understanding of the author of Eliyah Rabbah — the words of Magen Avraham. But in my humble opinion this is not Magen Avraham's intent; certainly the measure of an egg and a half compressed is the measure of a revi'it — only that Tur wrote in siman 456 regarding measuring the measure of challah, which is 43 eggs and a fifth of an egg: how should he do it? He fills a vessel with water and places that vessel into an empty vessel and inserts into it 43 eggs and a fifth of an egg one after another gently, and the water that overflows — the vessel that holds them is the measure. And afterward he wrote that in such a manner one cannot aim precisely in placing the eggs into it when it is full — meaning, perhaps he will not be so careful to place them gently; and also by the weight of the eggs in their falling into it it comes out more than 43 eggs and a fifth of an egg — therefore it is better that he fill a vessel with water and pour the water from it into another vessel, and afterward place in the empty vessel 43 eggs and a fifth, and return into it the water he poured from it, and the water that remains is the measure, end of Tur's words. And since Magen Avraham wrote here to measure per the first method that Tur wrote — that one fill the vessel and place into it an egg and a half — and in such a manner there is concern that one cannot aim precisely, as Tur wrote, and it comes out more than an egg and a half — therefore Magen Avraham holds that when one fills wine that is heaped, the measure of the heap rises nearly to the measure of what one cannot aim precisely, etc., and it turns out more than the measure of an egg and a half (nevertheless the matter is difficult, that the heap should be compressed per the measure that comes out more because one cannot aim precisely). Unlike with water, where there is concern for Tur's concern that one cannot aim precisely, etc. — and therefore if he has no wine to fill and must fill with water, he should do as written in siman 456; and meaning: the Mechaber in Shulchan Aruch siman 456 did not bring only the second method that Tur wrote — and likewise here one should do so: fill a vessel with water and pour the water from it into another vessel, and afterward place in the empty vessel an egg and a half, and afterward pour upon it the water, and the water that overflows is the measure of an egg and a half compressed — for in such a manner one can aim precisely, as Tur wrote.

Kelaim chapter 17 mishnah 6 — the measure of an egg whose measure the Sages established is a medium one; and in Berachot daf 38 regarding an olive — they measure with it a medium olive; and likewise here.
