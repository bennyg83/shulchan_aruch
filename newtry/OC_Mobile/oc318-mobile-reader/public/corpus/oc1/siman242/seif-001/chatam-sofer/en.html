Regarding Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, “and proof from the Mishnah.” Note: The case under discussion is not comparable to the proof. There, they did not hold that the remaining offerings were prohibited; rather, the rest were no longer obligatory upon him. Nevertheless, if he wished, he could bring the remaining offerings as well, and presumably they did so after the prices of the bird offerings declined. Here, however, if Shabbos passes without fish, he cannot remedy it afterward. Furthermore, there the enactment was intended to save the poor person from a prohibition punishable by kareis, whereas here a poor person who cannot afford it is exempt in any event. Nevertheless, it is proper to institute measures to break the power of those who inflate prices. To derive from that case a general rule for other contexts, however, requires study. See Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah at the end of chapter 4 of Gittin, and Tosafos Yom Tov there. See also Magen Avraham below, at the beginning of siman 656.
