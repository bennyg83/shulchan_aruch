And regarding what is stated in the Midrash, brought in Pri Chadash, that the Shechinah never departed from Israel, even on a weekday Shabbat, see what I wrote, with God's help, in my work on the Torah from the year 5615; see there and examine carefully. Now see the Magen Avraham here, who brought in the name of Olat Shabbat that on Shabbat we require "entirely for you." They wondered at him, for in Pesachim we say only that all agree that on Shabbat we also require "for you." The later authorities were pressed to say that the Olat Shabbat had a different text. In my humble opinion, his words appear correct and he had no other text at all. He proved this from the sugya here, where Rava said that Rabbi Eliezer agrees that we also require "for you" because oneg is written concerning it. This implies that Shabbat is superior to Yom Tov because oneg is written concerning it. This is well according to Rabbi Eliezer, for on Yom Tov one may say it is entirely for God, and Shabbat has the added quality that it is also for you. But according to Rabbi Yehoshua, who holds that on Yom Tov we also require "for you," in what way is Shabbat superior, such that oneg is written concerning it? Why is oneg written for Shabbat and not for Yom Tov? Perforce, on Shabbat it is entirely for you. And from Rabbi Eliezer we learn for Rabbi Yehoshua: just as according to Rabbi Eliezer Shabbat is superior to Yom Tov in one respect, so too according to Rabbi Yehoshua this must be so. Therefore, for us, we require entirely for you. The words of the Olat Shabbat are correct, as appears to me in my humble opinion, with God's help; examine carefully.
There, even one who needs others, etc. NB: See Magen Avraham s.k. 2, who wrote that one may borrow with interest for the needs of the Shabbat meal. See Peri Megadim, who explains that this means in a permitted manner. This does not appear correct, for if so it is obvious, and what does it teach us? And if he is poor and falls under "make your Shabbat like a weekday," why should he be obligated to borrow? See what I wrote on this in my Torah novellae from the year 5589, in Parashat Behar, that one may say borrowing with interest is permitted for the need of a mitzvah, as is implied by the words of the Rama in Yoreh De'ah siman 160. One may also say that it refers only to rabbinic interest; see there, and what I explained there about the wording of the Yerushalmi, from which this law originates. Later the book Pnei Moshe on the Yerushalmi, order Nezikin, came to my hand, and in chapter 8 of Sanhedrin, where this law originates, the Pnei Moshe explained in Mareh Panim that it refers to rabbinic interest. I rejoiced that I had arrived at his opinion, thank God. See there, and see what I wrote on this in my novellae to Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah siman 160, and examine carefully.
