Niddon: see in Taz seif kaf alef — he brought here a statement of chazal "whoever guards Shabbat" etc., "and your sin is removed" etc., and he explained the intent; and see in his explanation there's help in his writings on the Torah, year 5599, in parashat Ki Teitzei on the verse "if there is in a man a sin deserving death" on the scribes there, see there; and in parashat Vayelech in his writings of year 5600, see there and examine carefully. And what the Taz wrote — what Rashi explained in the Chumash on the verse "remember the day of Shabbat to sanctify it" like Beit Shammai, and the Taz questioned why he did not rule like Beit Hillel and was strained in this — and see in his novellae on tractate Niddah daf 38a what he wrote in settling the matter properly regarding the early pious ones who did not use their beds on Shabbat, see there and examine carefully. And behold, what is stated in the midrash brought in Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer — "the Divine Presence never departed from Israel, even on an ordinary Shabbat" — see in his writings on the Torah, year 5676, what he wrote there's help, see there and examine carefully. And behold, see in Magen Avraham here who brought in the name of see there that on Shabbat we require "it is entirely for you," and they wondered at him, for in Pesachim we say only that all agree that on Shabbat we require also "for you" on erev Shabbos; and the later authorities were strained to say that there was another version for see there; and in my humble opinion it appears like his words and he had no other version at all, and he proved thus from the Talmud here — Rava said that according to R' Eliezer he agrees that we require also "for you" from the fact that it is written in it "enjoyment," and if so it implies that Shabbat is preferable to Yom Tov, for it is written in it "enjoyment" — if so, it works out for R' Eliezer that on Yom Tov one may say "it is entirely for Heaven," on Shabbat there is an advantage that it is also "for you"; but for R' Yehuda, who on Yom Tov we also require "for you" — if so, in what is Shabbat preferable, that it is written in it "enjoyment," and why is enjoyment written on Shabbat and not on Yom Tov; and therefore it must be that on Shabbat it is "it is entirely for you," and from R' Eliezer we hear for R' Yehuda as well, just as for R' Eliezer Shabbat is preferable on one side to Yom Tov, so too it is compelled for R' Yehuda as well; and therefore for us we require "it is entirely for you," and the words of Olas Shabbos are correct, as it appears to my humble opinion there's help, and examine carefully.
