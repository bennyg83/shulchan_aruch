(א) Must — Bach and see there and Turei Zahav wrote that they should enjoy as is fitting, and so it is proven in Beit Yosef that such is his opinion, and so I concluded in Mishna Berurah like them; and the Taz wrote that it suffices with a plate of harsana alone, and the greatness of the Shulchan Aruch is that we should not say that he is like one who has nothing, that two meals suffice for him on erev Shabbos; but according to what we wrote, that even one who has nothing must be given three meals and a plate of harsana — if so, there is no greatness in this that he has a little of his own for this matter; and it is possible that we should not say that on a weekday he suffices from others who give him and on Shabbat he makes from the little he has, and even though from this he reaches only to buy two meals — it may be said that he should make his Shabbat ordinary since on Shabbat it is possible for him to restrict so that he need not people at all, and therefore this is not comparable to one who has nothing of his own; and see in Beur HaGra who also explains like the Taz; but according to what he wrote in siman 263 that the honor of the day equals four cups for the matter of a small thing — that is, a plate of harsana — if so, all the more so the third meal itself; and therefore according to this we are compelled to explain that what the Mechaber wrote at the beginning — one must honor Shabbat — meaning to honor it as is most fitting, more than a plate of harsana; for regarding a plate of harsana, even one whose time is very pressed is obligated, and so too see there; and because the opinion of the Gra is not clear to us whether as he explains here or as he wrote later — therefore I concluded like most poskim and not like the Taz, who is alone in this:

(ב) To honor etc. — and if he has only a little money and a hired worker comes to claim payment for his work that he finished for him today [for if he finished it from yesterday, upon him is only the prohibition of "do not withhold," as is explained in Choshen Mishpat, and he is like other creditors in the world; but regarding a Torah scholar there is to examine — see Yoma chapter 7 halacha 4, Choshen Mishpat, etc.] — it appears that he must give them to the worker in order to fulfill what is written "on his day you shall give his wages and the sun shall not set upon it," even though thereby nothing will remain for him with which to enjoy Shabbat, or that he appease him that he agree to wait until after Shabbat; for the obligation of paying a worker on time is d'oraisa, and this is only from words of Kabbalah. And even for those poskim that this too is d'oraisa, as we wrote in Mishna Berurah there — it is a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and the positive commandment does not come and push aside the negative and positive commandment [Yevamos 21], and this is not called "he claims and he has not" — for he does not transgress "do not let it remain overnight." And our words are specifically if he already brought the work to the employer's home and received from him; for if not, the employer has another counsel not to receive from him, and then he does not transgress even if the worker said to him "come and take the object from me and I do not wish to withhold it in place of the wages," as is written in Shtah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia daf 112 in the name of R' Yehonatan, see there:

(ג) And they did not say etc. — that is, initially it was thought that what they said "make your Shabbat ordinary" deals with people who are not so pressed, and nevertheless they warned not to spend on enjoyment of Shabbat so that he not come thereby in the end to need people; and if so, here too in our matter it is better that he restrict and not spend on enjoyment of Shabbat from the little he has so that he not become very impoverished; and for this he explained and said that there it deals with one whose time is very pressed — that is, that from his money reaches him only two meals for Shabbat — and for this they said that better he make his Shabbat ordinary so that he not need people; but in our matter, where he has a little of his own to spend on Shabbat, he must enjoy Shabbat with what he has and trust in Heaven that He will give him others for this, for Shabbat and Yom Tov expenses are outside the sustenance fixed for a person, as chazal said:

(ד) In order to honor Shabbat — see in Magen Avraham who wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that one borrows with interest for the need of a Shabbat meal, and we copied it in Mishna Berurah:

(ה) And it is honor of Shabbat etc. — and there is a hint for this in Scripture "and it was on the sixth day, and they prepared what they would bring, what you will bake, bake" etc. — it implies that one should bake on erev Shabbos to prepare for Shabbat; also in the time of the Gemara there was a fixed custom for this, as Magen Avraham brought; and in our time, alas, some women began to abolish that custom and buy from the baker, and they do not act properly, for they diminish thereby the honor of Shabbat:

(ו) In remembrance of the manna that was covered — at first glance this custom is puzzling to many — what did they see to do on Shabbat a remembrance of the manna, which did not descend on it at all; and Tosafos in the chapter "All writings" wrote that because the manna did not descend, one should make a remembrance of it, and this is strained; also according to this they should have made pashtida also on Yom Tov, and the Rama speaks regarding loaves on Shabbat and Yom Tov, and regarding this he speaks only on Shabbat night — it implies specifically; and how well-spoken are the words of Torat Chaim who wrote a proper reason for this — namely, that Shabbat Bereishis is a model of the Shabbat of the world to come, which is a day entirely Shabbat; and therefore we do many things on Shabbat as its model — that is, to eat meat and fish corresponding to the meal of the wild ox and leviathan, and we recite kiddush on wine corresponding to the wine guarded in its grapes for the righteous in the world to come; and chazal already said that for this reason the heavens are called "shechakim," in which they laugh for the righteous in the world to come; and therefore it is proper to make on Shabbat a remembrance of that manna [Tosafos Shabbat]:
