(ב) If the pairs become expensive etc., and proof from the mishnah at the end of chapter 1 of Kerisos — for we learned: a woman who has upon her five births and she needs to bring a sacrifice for each birth — that is, a wealthy woman brings a lamb for an olah and a dove or a turtledove for a chatas; and a poor woman — two doves or two turtledoves, one for an olah and one for a chatas. And all this although she did not bring her sacrifice even though she immersed at the end of forty days for a male and at the end of eighty days for a female and is permitted to her husband — nevertheless she is called lacking atonement and is forbidden to eat kodshim until she brings her sacrifice; the same law for kodshim of impurity. And if she is obligated for five births she brings one sacrifice and eats kodshim, for the sacrifice is for her for purity to kodshim like the mikveh to purify the impure — for even though he became impure many times, with one immersion he is pure; so too she is pure for kodshim through one sacrifice; nevertheless the rest of the sacrifices are still obligatory upon her to bring afterward. And we learned there — an incident where pairs stood in Jerusalem at golden dinars; Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said — this store (meaning he swore in the Temple) — not these tonight until they be at silver dinars. He entered the study hall and taught — a woman who has upon her five births brings one sacrifice and eats kodshim, and the rest is not obligatory upon her; and pairs stood that day at a quarter silver dinar. And Rashi wrote — even though he was lenient on Torah law, "it is a time to act for Heaven" — for if not they would cease to bring even one; and they would eat kodshim in bodily impurity. And see in tractate Bava Batra daf 166a in Tosafos s.v. "this entered" etc.; and if so, the same may be said for fish; and even though there is to distinguish — for regarding buying fish they will not cease to buy even if they are expensive, for all his sustenance is fixed for him from Rosh Hashana except for Shabbat and Yom Tov expenses, and one who adds, they add for him etc. — nevertheless behold Tosafos wrote in Beitza daf 15b and likewise in Pesachim daf 113 — that which we say "borrow on My account and I will pay" is specifically when he has with what to repay, but if he has no means to repay — behold R' Akiva said "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc.; if so, one whose time is very pressed should refrain from buying fish when they are expensive, at this time Tzemach Tzedek. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah and Sefer Teshuvah they brought in the name of Bach that nevertheless if they become expensive up to a third, they must buy, for we rule hiddur mitzvah up to a third; and they disagree — for what of it; nevertheless there is concern that they will refrain entirely from buying, and it is preferable to diminish one honor of Shabbat and two in order to honor many Shabbats. See there wrote — it appears from the sugya of Pesachim daf 68b — one verse says "a festival for the Heaven your Heaven" and one verse says "a festival shall be for you" — how so? R' Eliezer says — either entirely for Heaven or entirely for you. R' Yehuda says — half for Heaven and half for you. Rava said — on Shabbat all agree that it requires "for you" (so too Magen Avraham — such was the version of see there), for it is written "and you shall call Shabbat enjoyment"; and perhaps there is a difficulty for the author of see there — since we rule like R' Yehuda, if so the intent of Rava that on Shabbat all hold that it requires also "for you" — that is, half for Heaven and half for you — if so, the main statement of Rava comes to teach according to R' Eliezer, and this is not the halacha (and see Bach); and therefore he would explain that on Shabbat R' Yehuda agrees that it is entirely "for you":
