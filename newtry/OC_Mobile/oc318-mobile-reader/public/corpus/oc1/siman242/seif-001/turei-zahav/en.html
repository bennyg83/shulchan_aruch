In the Tur he brought the Gemara of "All writings" — "whoever guards Shabbat according to its law, even an idolater like a mortal man, they forgive him" — there is to examine: if he did not repent, what does Shabbat avail; and if he repented, why need Shabbat. And it appears that it deals with one who repented, but repentance does not avail that he be forgiven as is in the chapter of Yom Kippur — one who transgressed kares and court-imposed death — repentance suspends and death purges; it teaches us here that if while doing repentance he guards Shabbat according to its law, repentance avails that he also be forgiven. (1) Even one who needs others. In the Tur he wrote as follows — and that which R' Akiva said "make your Shabbat ordinary and do not need people" is when it is not possible for him; but when it is possible for him, he must honor it according to his ability etc.; and that which R' Chiya said at the beginning — even a small thing that he made for the honor of Shabbat is enjoyment; and Rava said — even a plate of harsana — meaning for one whose time is very pressed; and several times I asked and gave a matter before my teacher the Rosh like today when I have a little of my own and it does not suffice for me and I need others — am I in the category of "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc. or not, and he did not answer me a clear word; afterward I found in Pirkei Avos to Rashi who explained on that mishnah of R' Yehuda ben Teima etc. — he wrote as follows: immediately after that mishnah R' Akiva says "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc. — that statement of ben Teima is to elucidate R' Akiva — he said only for one whose time is very pressed; but a person must exert himself like a leopard and eagle to honor Shabbats very much, on erev Shabbos. Therefore every person must restrict on other days in order to honor Shabbat, and he should not say "how shall I lack my sustenance," for on the contrary, if he adds, they add for him, as the tanna taught etc.; it appears in clarity of his words that initially it was difficult for him on what was said previously that there is an obligation to enjoy Shabbat, and there is a difficulty from what R' Akiva said "make your Shabbat ordinary" — at first glance the explanation that he warned not to spend money for eating and drinking, as they said in the chapter "All writings" — "eat onion and sit in the shade and do not eat geese and chickens" — for this reason that even on Shabbat he should restrict his sustenance so that he not be poor; and if so, enjoyment of Shabbat is nullified; for this the explanation is not so in the words of R' Akiva, but it deals with one who is unable to spend for enjoyment of Shabbat; and if so, for one who is able he must spend according to his wealth; and if so, there is a difficulty from what is said "even a plate of harsana" — for this the explanation is that the time is very pressed for him; and our rabbi the Tur was uncertain what is called "the time is very pressed for him" — the relevance for him, for he had a little of his own and needed others — that is, presumably they gave him support for the whole week as is the way they give to students in holy communities; and you should know that this statement of R' Akiva who said "make your Shabbat ordinary" is that they do not give him from charity on Shabbat except for two meals, as is in the chapter "All writings" (Shabbat daf 118); and if so, there was a doubt for our rabbi whether he is in this category since he needs support from others, and this is charity automatically — he was not obligated on Shabbat except for two meals, and he is also not in the category of obligation of enjoyment at all; or one may say that "the time is very pressed" is that he has nothing at all of his own and is entirely dependent on charity — and this was resolved for him from the words of Rashi who expounded adjacent mishnayos; and had it not been for the words of Rashi I would have explained that ben Teima does not deal at all with Shabbat but from the path of ethics on a person's traits, as our rabbi wrote at the beginning of Orach Chayyim; but from the words of Rashi we learn that this refers to Shabbat and he divides that that statement of R' Akiva deals with one whose time is very pressed — that is, who has nothing of his own but is entirely dependent on charity; but ben Teima said that a person should be bold like a leopard to strive that he have a little of his own, and then he is not dependent etc. on charity automatically he is in the category of obligation of enjoyment; but one cannot say that ben Teima deals with one whose hand reaches, for in this there would not have been need for "be bold like a leopard"; it turns out there are three divisions — if he is a measured man and it is fitting for him to restrict in sustenance so that he not be poor — nevertheless on Shabbat this was not said, and he should honor according to his ability; and if he is a man who has a little of his own and needs others, he is in the category of obligation of enjoyment — and this is that which they said "even in a small thing for the honor of Shabbat," such as a plate of harsana, and all the more he is obligated in three meals; but if the time is very pressed for him — that is, who has nothing at all of his own and is entirely dependent on charity — then he is not in the category of obligation of enjoyment and is obligated only in two meals, and this is R' Akiva "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc.; and therefore the Tur concluded "therefore every person must restrict on other days" etc. — meaning that it is fitting for him to bring himself to obligation of enjoyment — his obligation if he has a little of his own, and this he can do after he restricts on weekdays — automatically he will have a little of his own etc., as it appears to my humble opinion clearly and correctly; and I saw an explanation of "very pressed" in another matter, and as it appears to my humble opinion I wrote correctly. And Beit Yosef wrote that in the chapter "All writings" the tanna of the school of Eliyahu — although R' Akiva said "make your Shabbat ordinary," nevertheless one does a small thing, R' Eliezer ben Yaakov, such as a plate of harsana, as we learned R' Yehuda ben Teima says "be bold like a leopard" etc., and Rashi explained "be bold" — strengthen yourself in the mitzvah more than your ability; and it appears that Rashi did not have this language in the Gemara and as we learned, for behold Rashi in tractate Avos derives this matter from the power of adjacent mishnayos that I mentioned; and in my humble opinion that Rashi read this language properly as we learned, for if not for adjacency one would say that R' Yehuda ben Teima does not deal with the matter of Shabbat; and from the power of adjacency we derive it; and Rashi came in tractate Avos so that you not question from where for the Talmud — in truth ben Teima deals with Shabbat; for this Rashi revealed there in Avos that from adjacency we derive it, and it turns out everything is resolved there's help. A short path in explanation of the words of the Tur — for there is to challenge two conclusions against each other — that is, that which they said one must enjoy Shabbat specifically, and it is stated "make your Shabbat ordinary" — it implies one need not enjoy at all; and therefore one must say there is a division between a very pressed time or not; and it is further stated that even a plate of harsana suffices; and here there is a doubt whether there are three divisions here or there are only two, and that of a plate of harsana is included in "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc.; and the relevance from this for the law of our rabbi the Tur, who had a little of his own and needed others. If we say there are only two divisions here and one who has a little of his own is in the category of "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc., or there are three divisions here — that is, one who has money enough for his need is in the category of enjoyment of Shabbat according to ability, and one who has a little of his own is in the category of "even a plate of harsana," and one who has nothing at all of his own is in the category of "make your Shabbat ordinary" — and this was resolved for him from that which Rashi explained that "be bold like a leopard" is regarding Shabbat; and this necessarily does not deal with a man who has all his need, for he needs no urging, for there is no lack of money here, and it is obvious that he is obligated in enjoyment of Shabbat; and also it does not deal with a complete poor person, for even a little he has not — for urging would not avail him; but certainly it deals with one who has a little of his own, who needs urging that he enjoy, even with a plate of harsana it suffices for him; and therefore the Tur concluded "very pressed" to indicate the third division; and our rabbi concluded that one must restrict etc. — meaning that every person, even if he has no little, should see that he be at least from the middle category — that is, at least in enjoyment of Shabbat and not from the third category; and this is the intent of the Gemara — the tanna of the school of Eliyahu — although R' Akiva said "make your Shabbat ordinary" etc., and even a plate of harsana — meaning although there are three divisions and the third division is completely exempt — nevertheless, since there is a middle division emerging even with a plate of harsana, every person should see that he be in the middle category, per the explanation of the words of the Tur.
