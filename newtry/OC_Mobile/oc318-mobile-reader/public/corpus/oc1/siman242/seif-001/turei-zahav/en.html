The Tur brings the Gemara in Kol Kitvei: whoever observes Shabbat according to its law, even if he worshipped idolatry like Enosh, is forgiven. One may ask: if he did not repent, what does Shabbat help; and if he did repent, why is Shabbat needed? It appears that it speaks where he did repent, but repentance alone would not be effective for him to be forgiven, as stated in Perek Yom HaKippurim: if one transgressed prohibitions punishable by karet or court-imposed death, repentance suspends and death completes atonement. Here it teaches that if, while repenting, he observes Shabbat according to its law, repentance is effective so that he is forgiven.
(1) Even one who needs others. The Tur wrote: That which R. Akiva said, "make your Shabbat like a weekday and do not need people," applies where it is impossible for him; but where it is possible, he must honor it according to his ability. That which R. Chiyya bar Ashi said, that even a small thing made in honor of Shabbat is oneg, and Rava said, even small fried fish, applies to one whose circumstances are strained. I discussed this several times before my father and teacher the Rosh, for someone like me today, who has a little of his own but not enough and needs others: am I included in "make your Shabbat like a weekday," etc., or not? He did not give me a clear answer. Afterward I found in Rashi on Pirkei Avot, on the Mishnah of R. Yehudah ben Teima, "be bold as a leopard," that he wrote: immediately after that Mishnah, R. Akiva says "make your Shabbat," etc.; the teaching of ben Teima was placed there to teach that R. Akiva spoke only of one whose circumstances are extremely strained, but a person must strengthen himself like a leopard and eagle to honor Shabbat greatly. Therefore every person must economize on the other days in order to honor Shabbat, and should not say, "How can I diminish my livelihood?" for on the contrary, if he adds, they add for him, as taught, etc. The clarification of his words appears as follows. The Tur was first troubled by what he wrote earlier, that there is an obligation of oneg Shabbat, because R. Akiva's statement "make your Shabbat like a weekday" appears to warn a person not to spend money on food and drink, like the statement in Perek Arvei Pesachim, "eat onions and sit in the shade, and do not eat geese and chickens," meaning that even on Shabbat he should restrict his food so as not to become poor, which would nullify oneg Shabbat. He answered that this is not the meaning of R. Akiva; rather, he speaks of one who cannot spend for oneg Shabbat. Where he can, he must spend according to his wealth. Then it is difficult: why did they say even small fried fish? He answered that this is where his circumstances are strained. The Tur was uncertain what is called "strained circumstances," which mattered for his own case, where he had a little of his own but needed others, presumably meaning that they gave him weekly support as communities give to Torah scholars. Know that R. Akiva's statement means that charity collectors give him on Shabbat only two meals, as stated in Perek Kol Kitvei, Shabbat 118. Therefore the Tur was uncertain whether he is included in that category, since he needs others for support and that is charity, so on Shabbat he would be obligated only in two meals and not included in oneg at all; or perhaps "strained circumstances" means one who has nothing of his own and is entirely dependent on charity. This was resolved for him from Rashi's words, who expounded adjacent Mishnayot. Without Rashi's words I would have explained that ben Teima does not speak of Shabbat at all but of ethical traits, as our master wrote at the beginning of Orach Chaim. From Rashi he learned that it refers to Shabbat, distinguishing that R. Akiva speaks of extremely strained circumstances, meaning one who has nothing of his own and is entirely dependent on charity. But ben Teima says that a person should be bold as a leopard to strive to have a little of his own, and then he is not entirely dependent on charity and is included in the obligation of oneg. Ben Teima cannot be speaking of one with means, for then it would not need to say "be bold as a leopard." Thus there are three categories: one of means, who though it is proper to economize in food so as not to become poor, on Shabbat is not told this and should honor it according to his ability; one who has a little of his own and needs others, who is included in oneg, namely the statement that even a small thing for Shabbat, such as small fried fish, suffices, and all the more so he is obligated in three meals; and one whose circumstances are extremely strained, meaning he has nothing of his own and is entirely dependent on charity, who is not included in oneg and is obligated only in two meals, namely R. Akiva's "make your Shabbat like a weekday." Therefore the Tur concluded that every person must economize on other days, meaning it is proper for him to bring himself into the obligation of oneg, whose obligation applies if he has a little of his own; by economizing during the week he will have a little of his own. This appears clear and correct to my humble mind. I have seen forced explanations in Darkei Moshe, and I have written what appears correct. The Beit Yosef wrote that in Perek Arvei Pesachim it says in Tanna DeVei Eliyahu: although R. Akiva said "make your Shabbat like a weekday," one should make a small thing. Rav Pappa said: such as small fried fish, as we learned, R. Yehudah ben Teima says, "be bold as a leopard," etc. Rashi explained: be bold, strengthen yourself in the mitzvah beyond your ability. It appears that Rashi did not have the Gemara's language "as we learned," for in Avot Rashi derives this from the adjacency of the Mishnayot. Yet to my humble mind Rashi did have that wording, for without the adjacency one might say R. Yehudah ben Teima does not speak of Shabbat; from the adjacency we expound it. Rashi in Avot came to answer how the Talmud knew ben Teima speaks of Shabbat, revealing that we learn it from the adjacency. Thus everything is settled with heavenly help. In brief: the Tur's words rest on two apparently conflicting Gemarot: one says there is a specific obligation of oneg Shabbat, while "make your Shabbat like a weekday" implies no oneg is required at all. Perforce there is a distinction between strained circumstances and not. A further statement says even small fried fish suffices. The doubt is whether there are three categories or only two, and whether small fried fish is itself included in "make your Shabbat like a weekday." The practical difference was the Tur's own case, where he had a little of his own and needed others. He resolved it from Rashi, who interpreted "be bold as a leopard" regarding Shabbat. This cannot refer to one who has all he needs, for he needs no strengthening and is plainly obligated in oneg; nor to a completely poor person, for strengthening does not help him. It must refer to one who has a little of his own, who needs strengthening to make oneg, and even small fried fish suffices. Therefore the Tur ended "extremely strained" to indicate the third category, and concluded that one should economize, meaning every person, even one with nothing, should see to it that he at least enters the middle category, with some oneg Shabbat, and not the third category. This is the intent of Tanna DeVei Eliyahu. In the Gemara Beitzah chapter 2 they said of Shammai the Elder that all his days he ate in honor of Shabbat, etc. Rashi explained the verse "Remember the Shabbat day" according to Beit Shammai, while Ramban wrote there that the halakhah follows Beit Hillel. My teacher and father-in-law z"l wrote: I found in the name of Or Zarua that Hillel agrees that Beit Shammai's practice is preferable, and there is further proof from the incident of the butcher in Perek Kol Kitvei, who praised himself for acting like Beit Shammai.
