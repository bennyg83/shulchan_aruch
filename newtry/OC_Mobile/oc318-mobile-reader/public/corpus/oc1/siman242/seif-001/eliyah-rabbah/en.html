(1) [Levush] whoever enjoys etc. In Sefer Chasidim — a good meal of vegetables on Shabbat and love with his wife and household, and honor it that he honor Shabbat — that he not quarrel with his wife and household; it is written in the Zohar — one who becomes angry on Shabbat is as though he kindles the fire of Gehennom. Further in the Zohar, parashat Tzav — Torah scholars are called Shabbat, and they are obligated to enjoy for them all kinds of food and drink and fine garments like the manner of Shabbat; and one who disgraces a Torah scholar is as though he disgraces Shabbat, which is as though he worships idolatry. Tzemach Tzedek wrote — if fish sellers raise the price because they buy fish for Shabbat, it is proper to enact that they not buy fish on some Shabbats; and in the gloss of Sefer Beit Hillel to Yoreh De'ah siman 218 he wrote — specifically if it rose more than a third than it was before, as in hiddur mitzvah up to a third — see there at length. And in my humble opinion the reason is explained in the responsa there — it states that the poor should not buy ever because of the expense; if so, there is no distinction. And it appears to me that one who has fish from before the enactment is permitted to eat them unless they stipulated in the enactment explicitly, and not as the implication in Sefer Beit Hillel there, for the intent of the enactment is because of expense and this he already has; and examine carefully.
