[Levush] Whoever delights in, etc. Sefer Chasidim says that on Shabbos it is better to have a simple vegetable meal with love among one's wife and household. "And you shall honor it" means that one should honor Shabbos by not quarreling with his wife and family. The Zohar says that one who becomes angry on Shabbos is as though he kindled the fire of Gehinnom. The Zohar in Parashat Tzav further says that Torah scholars are called Shabbos and one must delight them with all kinds of food, drink, and fine clothing like Shabbos itself; one who disgraces a Torah scholar is as though he disgraced Shabbos and is compared to an idolater. Tzemach Tzedek writes that if fish sellers raise prices because people buy fish for Shabbos, it is proper to institute that fish not be purchased for several Shabbatot. The gloss to Beit Hillel on Yoreh Deah siman 218 limits this to a price increase of more than one-third, comparing it to the rule of spending up to one-third extra for hiddur mitzvah. In my humble opinion, however, the reason stated in the responsum is that poor people will otherwise never buy fish because of the price, so no such distinction applies. It appears to me that one who already owns fish from before the enactment may eat them unless the enactment explicitly included such fish, unlike what Beit Hillel seems to imply, because the enactment was aimed at market prices and his fish were already acquired. Emek Berachah writes that it is good to learn Torah on Shabbos before eating.
