Question. Is a double doubt effective in a matter of danger or not? According to what I wrote at the end of Yoreh Deah, siman 110, that we do not prohibit where there is a presumption, Pnei Yehoshua discusses this in his addenda to Kesubos in the sugya of an open entrance. A double doubt is weaker than a presumption, and danger is more stringent than prohibition, as shown by the rule that where an animal may have been attacked by a wolf, etc., and in cases of uncovered liquids, we do not rely on a presumption. This indicates that a presumption is not relied upon in matters of danger, and the same should be true of a double doubt. According to the view that a double doubt is like a majority, or stronger than one, perhaps it is effective even in a matter of danger, just as in matters of prohibition. A single doubt, however, is certainly prohibited. This requires study.
