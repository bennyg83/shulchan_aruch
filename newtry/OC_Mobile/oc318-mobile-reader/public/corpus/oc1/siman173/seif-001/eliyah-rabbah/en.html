[1] Levush: It is not obligatory, etc. Rama, however, in Yoreh Deah, siman 89, seif 3, writes that some require washing even between a dairy dish and a meat dish, or vice versa. He writes likewise in Toras Chatas, principle 76, and states that this is the accepted custom. Mateh Moshe, siman 293, likewise rules that it is obligatory and not optional.
[2] Levush: Barley flour, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov disagrees with Levush and prohibits every type of flour. Shach likewise rules this way in Yoreh Deah, siman 89. In Eliyah Rabbah I cited the wording of Bedek HaBayis by the Raah, page 83: except for flour, dates, vegetables, and similar foods. End quote.
[Levush] The custom is to permit, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov and Lechem Chamudot, folio 218, question the Levush, because in Yoreh Deah the Rama, and the Levush himself, rule that even poultry fat is prohibited. It seems to me that the Levush relies on what he writes at the end in the name of the Zohar, that one should be stringent even with poultry. The same would apply when one first ate poultry fat. In my sefer I wrote that meat broth has the status of a cooked meat dish. If it is thick, such as vegetables cooked in it, it has the status of meat itself. I further proved in Api Ravrevei, folio 40, from Torat Chatat that the Rama's ruling there, that the custom is not to eat cheese after a meat dish just as after actual meat, applies specifically to cheese. A dairy dish may be eaten after a meat dish if one cleans and rinses the mouth. Regarding eating meat after a dish containing cheese or milk, Torat Chatat, klal 76, implies that hand washing and cleaning the mouth are required. Eating a meat dish after a dairy dish, however, appears there not to require cleaning and rinsing. I questioned this in my sefer, because the Rama derived the law from Hagahot Shaarei Dura, where the opposite is explicit: even before a meat dish one requires cleaning and rinsing. I found that Rabbi Natan Shapira and Mahari of Kremnitz also ruled this way.
