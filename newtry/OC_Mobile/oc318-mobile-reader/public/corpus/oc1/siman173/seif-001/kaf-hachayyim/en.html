1) [Seif 1] The middle waters are optional; this applies between one cooked dish and another, etc. This means that between one cooked dish and another, even if the first was a meat dish and the second a cheese dish, one need not wash, since the meat is not physically present and only its taste remains, while the second likewise contains only the taste of cheese. If, however, after the meat dish he ate actual cheese, washing is obligatory. Tur. Maran, of blessed memory, rules accordingly in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 89, seif 3, although there are other explanations, as stated in Beis Yosef; see there. See, however, Rema there, who writes that meat fat has the same law as meat itself. He also writes that the present custom is to act stringently and not eat cheese after a meat dish, just as after meat itself, etc.; see there. This is now the universal practice throughout the Jewish diaspora, to rule stringently in accordance with Rema, of blessed memory. It is therefore obvious that one may not deviate, since they accepted the stringent opinion upon themselves.
