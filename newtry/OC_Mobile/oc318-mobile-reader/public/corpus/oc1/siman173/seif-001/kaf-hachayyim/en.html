1) [Seif 1] The middle waters are optional; this applies between one cooked dish and another, etc. This means that between one cooked dish and another, even if the first was a meat dish and the second a cheese dish, one need not wash, since the meat is not physically present and only its taste remains, while the second likewise contains only the taste of cheese. If, however, after the meat dish he ate actual cheese, washing is obligatory. Tur. Maran, of blessed memory, rules accordingly in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 89, seif 3, although there are other explanations, as stated in Beis Yosef; see there. See, however, Rema there, who writes that meat fat has the same law as meat itself. He also writes that the present custom is to act stringently and not eat cheese after a meat dish, just as after meat itself, etc.; see there. This is now the universal practice throughout the Jewish diaspora, to rule stringently in accordance with Rema, of blessed memory. It is therefore obvious that one may not deviate, since they accepted the stringent opinion upon themselves.
2) Regarding eating meat after cheese, Beis Yosef writes that some act stringently not to eat meat after cheese during the same meal, because the Zohar, parashas Mishpatim, 125a, states: We have found that regarding anyone who eats these foods joined together, within the same hour, or at the same meal, for forty days a roasted kid appears in its skin before those above; an impure company draws near him, and he causes unholy judgments to be aroused in the world. If he fathers a son during those days, they lend the child a soul from the Other Side that is unfit for him. Beis Yosef writes that it is proper to rule stringently even regarding poultry; see there. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Mishpatim, writes that the Arizal's practice was that on a day when he ate cheese he would not eat meat until night; see there. The words of the Zohar cited above imply that there is no distinction between hard or soft cheese, or milk. In every case, one must not eat dairy and meat foods within the same hour or at the same meal, even after an hour. Therefore, every G-d-fearing person should act stringently for himself and for those who heed him, not to eat meat after cheese, even soft cheese, or after dairy foods until one hour has passed and at a different meal. This is so although according to the strict law it is permitted after rinsing and cleansing the mouth, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 89, seif 2; see there. One who follows the Arizal's stringency not to eat meat for that entire day shall be called holy. Regarding hard cheese, see Rema there in the gloss to seif 2, who writes that some practice not to eat even poultry after it, just as cheese is not eaten after meat, while others are lenient; he writes that it is proper to act stringently; see there. Thus, every place should follow its custom. Even in a place that is lenient, however, one should take the Zohar's words into account and eat meat only after one hour and at a different meal, as stated. See Birkei Yosef there, Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 13, and understand this.
