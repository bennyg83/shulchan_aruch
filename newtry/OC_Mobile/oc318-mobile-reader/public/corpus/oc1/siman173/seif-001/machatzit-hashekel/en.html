Magen Avraham: The laws, etc. “However, I saw,” etc. His words are: It implies, etc. Since the Tur writes that two people who do not know one another may eat at one table, one meat and the other cheese, without any distinguishing sign, but two people who know one another must make a distinguishing sign between them, such as placing a pitcher between them, or each eating on his own tablecloth, this implies that where they do not know one another and no distinguishing sign is required, they may eat on one tablecloth, one meat and the other cheese.
He further writes that when one places a hot meat bowl, etc., it absorbs, etc. Although the lower item is cold, and we rule that the lower item dominates, it nevertheless absorbs to the depth of a peel.
For there is no, etc. From one vessel to another. This is comparable to two pots, one containing meat and one milk, that touch one another. See Taz there.
Seif katan 1: It is difficult, etc. See Yoreh Deah, siman 316. Although according to the Gemara, a cow that gives milk is assumed already to have given birth, and a calf born afterward is therefore exempt from the laws of a firstborn, nowadays this is no indication. Even a cow giving birth for the first time may produce milk before giving birth, because natural conditions have changed.
And in Even HaEzer. Rama writes that although the Gemara concludes that a woman who gives birth after nine months does not give birth before the months are complete, nowadays conditions have changed and she may give birth before the months are complete.
