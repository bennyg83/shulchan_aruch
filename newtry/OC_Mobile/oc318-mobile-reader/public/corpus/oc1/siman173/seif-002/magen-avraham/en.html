The laws of meat and cheese are explained in Yoreh Deah, where they belong. I did, however, see that Bach writes here as follows: It implies that when the two people do not know one another they may eat on one tablecloth. This is difficult, etc., because meat may adhere to the place on the tablecloth where cheese was lying, etc. In my view, the case concerns a new tablecloth or one that was thoroughly washed, and one person eats at one end and the other at the opposite end, so they do not touch one another. This is obvious. He further writes that when one places a meat bowl on the table and afterward places a milk pot in that place, the pot absorbs the taste of meat absorbed in the table, etc., and is prohibited to the depth of a peel. End quote. We do not rule this way, as written in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 92, because absorbed taste does not pass from one vessel to another. Some are accustomed to wash between cheese and fish, based on Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 87, which states that eating cheese and fish is dangerous. This is how it is printed. Darchei Moshe already writes that this is a scribal error in Beit Yosef and should read fish with meat. See there, and so write the Acharonim. For it causes another ailment. It is possible that nowadays the danger is not as great. We see that many matters described in the Gemara as dangerous because of an evil spirit or for other reasons do not cause harm nowadays, because natural conditions have changed. It also depends upon the nature of each country. See Yoreh Deah, siman 316, seif 3, and Even HaEzer, siman 156. Bach writes similarly in the name of Rambam.
