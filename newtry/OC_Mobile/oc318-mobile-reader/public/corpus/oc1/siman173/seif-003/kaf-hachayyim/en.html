3) [Seif 2] Between meat and fish, washing is obligatory, etc. Some practice washing between cheese and fish. Knesses HaGedolah writes in Hagahos Beis Yosef that this practice arose from Beis Yosef's statement in Yoreh Deah, siman 87, that it is forbidden to eat fish with milk because of danger; the hands must likewise be washed, just as between fish and meat; see there. Magen Avraham writes at the beginning of this siman that Darkei Moshe there already identified this as a scribal error in Beis Yosef and that the text should read fish with meat; see there. Knesses HaGedolah already resolves this in Hagahos Beis Yosef there, paragraph 19, and in Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah on this siman, paragraph 2; see there. Knesses HaGedolah writes that physicians say fish with milk or cheese poses a danger of tzaraas, and therefore the practice is also to wash the hands between cheese and fish; see there. Beis Lechem Yehudah there, paragraph 3, writes the same, but says that there is no danger with butter or the fat that rises upon soured milk; see there. Mishbetzos Zahav there, paragraph 3, and Eshel Avraham at the beginning of this siman write the same; Be'er HaMayim, siman 1; Chukei Chaim, siman 61. Responsa Adnei Paz, siman 42, similarly writes in the name of Rabbeinu Bachya, parashas Mishpatim, that fish with cheese or milk poses absolute danger, but butter is permitted; see there. Pischei Teshuvah there, paragraph 9, cites this; see there. See, however, Taz there, paragraph 3, and Sifsei Kohen there, paragraph 5, who permit even milk; see there. Chasam Sofer, siman 101, holds likewise, and Pischei Teshuvah there writes that nowadays it is permitted because the public is accustomed to it; see there. It appears that since this depends upon medical conditions and the climates of different locations are known to differ, it may be harmful in one place but not in another. Therefore, in a place where the public is not accustomed to eating fish with milk or cheese, one should act stringently, because danger is treated more stringently than a prohibition. Washing the hands between them should also be required, as Knesses HaGedolah writes, as well as rinsing and cleansing the mouth, like one who eats meat after milk, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 89, seif 2; see there.
