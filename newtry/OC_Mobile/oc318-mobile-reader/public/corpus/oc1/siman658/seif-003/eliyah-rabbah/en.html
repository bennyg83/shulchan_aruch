[3] Because he creates a fragrance, etc. This is difficult, for the prohibition applies only when one places a cup there in order to perfume it. Where one does not intend to perfume it, it should be permitted, because the principle of an inevitable consequence applies only to a full melachah, whereas perfuming is not itself a melachah; only extinguishing and kindling are melachos (Yam Shel Shlomo, Beitzah, chapter 2). His meaning is that when the Gemara, Beitzah 23, forbids placing spices even upon a shard because this creates fragrance in the shard, the prohibition is due to the fact that kindling is involved, which is a full melachah. Creating fragrance without intent is permitted. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham concludes that the Rama is correct, because there the kindling is for food preparation and is permitted, yet creating fragrance in the shard remains forbidden. I find this explanation difficult. Yam Shel Shlomo explicitly mentions both extinguishing and kindling, while upon a shard there is no extinguishing at all, as stated there in Beitzah. Rashi also explains that kindling upon a shard is not a full melachah, since it is done unusually and constitutes an indirect manner of performance. Yam Shel Shlomo therefore plainly refers to placing spices upon coals, where both extinguishing and kindling are present. The Magen Avraham's statement that there is no prohibition of kindling because it is for food preparation is likewise difficult, for the opinion that forbids using a shard maintains that this is an excessive luxury not needed by everyone, as explained by the Gemara and Rashi. According to the opinion that it is a food need, even placing the spices upon coals is permitted despite the extinguishing, kindling, and creation of fragrance, as Rashi explains. There is also an apparent contradiction, for in siman 511, seif katan 10, Magen Avraham writes that creating fragrance without intent is permitted, and Beit Yosef cites Ran likewise. The Gemara cited above would then also be difficult according to Ran. It therefore appears clear that Rav, who permits even upon coals, disagrees entirely with Rabbah, who forbids upon a shard because it creates fragrance. Rav maintains that creating fragrance without intent is permitted, and the halachah follows Rav, as stated at the end of siman 511. Yam Shel Shlomo undoubtedly relied upon Ran and therefore disagreed with the Rama. In my humble opinion, however, Rokeach and Maharil, from whom the Rama derived this ruling, did not interpret Ran this way and hold that even an unintended creation of fragrance is forbidden. The permission to roast meat upon coals applies because it is actual food preparation, as is also evident from Rashi. It is also possible that even according to Ran, permission applies there only because smoking fruit has some element of food preparation, although the fruit could be eaten without it and the act merely improves it. One should therefore be stringent in accordance with Rama and Levush. Although the Taz at the end of siman 511 agrees with Yam Shel Shlomo, it appears that he did not see the Rama's ruling here when he wrote there, nor did he address all the considerations stated above. One should not treat lightly the ruling of Rokeach and Maharil in the name of Pardes.
