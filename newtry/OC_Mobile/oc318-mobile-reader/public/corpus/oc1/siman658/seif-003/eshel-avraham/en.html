For we require "for you." In responsa Maharam Mintz 2 they asked about betrothal with a borrowed ring: Rosh holds that since he said "lend me to betroth with it," we are witnesses he resolved to give it in effective language, and presumably they borrowed on condition of return — see Eshel Avraham 28. So too for lulav: he borrowed in a way one fulfills with it; we do not reduce "borrowed" except when unspecified, like borrowing a ring unspecified — "I will betroth with it" — so too "I will fulfill with it." The rabbi answered: certainly not to say that — one who borrows to betroth fully acquires, for if he became unable to pay this is not so — see Rosh. Rather we say: since he said to betroth with it, he betrothed with something worth a perutah as a complete gift or to return; but he did not remove its name from him — at least she is betrothed, unlike lulav where we require entirely his — see there.
