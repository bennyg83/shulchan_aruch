For we require 'yours.' In Responsa Mishnas Moshe, siman 2, he was asked why this is different from one who betroths a woman with a borrowed ring, where she is betrothed. He answered according to the view of the Rosh, of blessed memory, that since he said, 'lend it to me to betroth with it,' we are witnesses that he fully intended to give it to him in effective language, and presumably he borrowed it as a complete gift on condition to return. See Even HaEzer siman 28. If so, regarding lulav too, this should be so, for he lent it in a way that he could fulfill the obligation with it; and the borrowed case is excluded only where it was unspecified, similar to one who borrows a ring without specifying, where he did not say, 'and I will betroth with it.' Here too, he did not say, 'and I will fulfill the obligation with it.' The above Rav answered that it is not comparable. Certainly this does not mean that when he borrowed it to betroth, he transferred it to him entirely, for if so, if an unavoidable accident occurred, he would not be liable to pay, and that is not so; see the Rosh. Rather, we say that since he said 'to betroth with it,' he transferred to him through it the value of a perutah as a complete gift, or to return. But he did not remove its name from himself from now on. In any event, she is betrothed. This is not so with lulav, where we require that all of it be his; see there.
