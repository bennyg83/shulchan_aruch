For it is like borrowed. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote the reason: since it is only for a time, etc. Something similar is explained in the Ran, Nedarim 29, that anything that has no permanent acquisition is only an acquisition of produce. See Ketzos HaChoshen in Choshen Mishpat siman 241, who discusses at length the poskim who disagree with this; according to them, whenever the giver says explicitly that he transfers the body of the object to the recipient, even if he said for a fixed time, it is also a gift. Regarding esrog, even if he did not say so explicitly, he also acquires the body of the object for the time that he told him, because if only for its produce, what did he give him? This is like the reasoning of the Talmud in Bava Basra 137. See also Avnei Miluim, siman 28, seif katan 53, where he also strengthened his words. That which says that a person should not transfer ownership of a lulav to a child, from which the Rosh supports himself, he resolves there in Ketzos HaChoshen and Avnei Miluim in two ways. However, in Nesivos there he agreed with the Mechaber; see there at length.
