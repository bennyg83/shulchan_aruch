(8) A person does not fulfill, etc. - If the man is not at home and the woman gave the lulav to one of his friends or relatives as a gift, he has not fulfilled the obligation, for perhaps he is pleased only with a sale, unless he is an important person, for whom selling it is degrading; then it is permitted, for we assess her mind that he is pleased [Magen Avraham]. The Bikkurei Yaakov challenged him: even without this, we hold that a person is pleased that a mitzvah be performed with his property, and because of this it is permitted to take another's lulav to fulfill the obligation with it even without his knowledge. Nevertheless, this reasoning helps only for the other days, when it is as though he borrowed it himself, but not for the first day, when we require that it be 'yours,' as stated above in siman 649:5. If so, what does it help to say that he is pleased? In any case it is not actually his, and even when she conducts business in the house, she has no authority to give from his property. Therefore he concludes there that even if her husband heard afterward and was pleased, it does not help, for at the time of taking it was not his, and he must take it again.
(9) With his fellow's lulav that he lent him, etc. - The Magen Avraham proved from the case of one who borrows an item from his fellow in order to betroth a woman with it, where we say that she is betrothed [Even HaEzer siman 28:19], because we say that presumably he gave it to him as a gift on condition to return, so that he would be able to betroth with it. If so, the same applies to an esrog: if he lent it to him to fulfill the obligation with it, it is as though he gave it to him as a gift, and he fulfilled the obligation. So too the Beis Shmuel wrote there in Even HaEzer. But the Taz above in siman 14 and Responsa Beis Yaakov rule that he has not fulfilled the obligation; therefore he must take it again, without a berachah.
(10) For we require 'yours,' from your own - Some say that those who buy an esrog for the mitzvah and pay after the festival do not act properly, for as long as he has not given the money, he acquires only rabbinically, and we require 'yours' from the Torah, unless he brought it into his domain and acquired it through the law of courtyard. Ab initio, it is proper to be careful about this and pay before the festival.
(11) For it is like borrowed - For this is not the language of a gift, since he specified that it is only for a time.
