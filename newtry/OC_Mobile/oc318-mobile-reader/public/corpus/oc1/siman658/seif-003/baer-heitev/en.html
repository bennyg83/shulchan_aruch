From your own. If the man is at home and the woman gave the lulav to one of his friends or relatives as a gift, it appears to me that he has not fulfilled the obligation, for perhaps he would have preferred to sell it on the first day. To sell it, however, appears to me permitted, for one may acquire benefit for a person not in his presence. However, if he is an important person, for whom it would be degrading to sell the esrog, it is permitted, for we assess his mind that he is pleased with this. Magen Avraham; see there. One who borrows a lulav from his fellow, and the fellow gave it to him without specifying, we say that presumably he gave it to him as a gift, and he fulfills his obligation with it; Magen Avraham 3, and in siman 14, seif katan 8; see there. But the Taz there, seif katan 5, wrote that he has not fulfilled the obligation, because we require that he say explicitly that it is as a gift and not as a loan; see there. So too R. Manoach wrote that since a borrowed lulav is invalid on the first day, it is not proper to say, 'lend me your lulav,' for he took it in the form of a loan and does not fulfill the obligation with it. Rather, he must say, 'give me your lulav.' End quote.
