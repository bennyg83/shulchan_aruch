He should not give it, etc. The first opinion is the Rambam's language. See Bedek HaBayis, who wrote that since he wrote it without qualification, it implies that even if the child has reached the age of pe'utos, one should also not give it to him. The reason is that the Rambam follows his own approach in chapter 29 of Hilchos Mechirah, that where another person's intent transfers ownership to him, the child acquires from the Torah. If so, even if he reached the age of pe'utos, for whom the Sages enacted that his gift is a gift, nevertheless from the Torah he cannot transfer ownership. This is what he wrote: 'and he does not transfer ownership to others from the Torah,' and it is not 'yours' from the Torah. The opinion of the 'some say' is that even when another person's intent transfers ownership to him, his acquisition is only rabbinic. Therefore, when he reaches the age of pe'utos, just as he acquires rabbinically, so too he transfers ownership rabbinically [Lechem Mishneh]. It is obvious that this law applies to all four species. It is also obvious that ab initio one should certainly be stringent like the first opinion, which is stated without qualification, not to give it as a gift to any minor, even as a gift on condition to return, as explained in the poskim. Know that according to the first opinion, one is called a minor until he is thirteen years and one day old. See Peri Megadim, who wrote that even if he is thirteen years old, as long as we do not know that he has brought two hairs, one should not give it to him, for a Torah doubt is treated stringently. However, in Responsa Kesav Sofer, siman 129, his opinion is that it is permitted ab initio because of Rava's presumption, and there is also a double doubt: perhaps even with pe'utos it is permitted, like the view of the 'some say.' Further there, in siman 128, regarding a minor who trades esrogim, buying and transferring ownership, he concludes that ab initio one should refrain from buying; but after the fact, if he fulfilled the obligation with it, he does not return and recite the berachah, because of concern for a berachah in vain; see there.
Before he fulfilled the obligation with it. Some Acharonim hold that even after he fulfilled the obligation with it, it is not worthwhile to give it to a minor as a gift, lest someone come who asks him to give it to him to fulfill the obligation with it. See Eliyah Rabbah.
It is not returned. Meaning, and he himself cannot fulfill the obligation with it. The poskim wrote that even if he gave it to him as a gift on condition to return, he also does not fulfill the obligation afterward, because the minor's return is not a return. Seemingly, he gave it to him on condition to return; if the minor cannot transfer ownership back to him afterward, his gift should be void. But in truth this is not so, for we hold that any condition that cannot ultimately be fulfilled, when he stipulated it at the outset, has its condition void. It follows that the minor acquired it even though he cannot return it [Ran].
