[6] Even if an accident occurred, etc. If it was given without qualification and was lost through circumstances beyond his control, he pays its value and fulfills the mitzvah (Rabbeinu Yerucham). This is also found in Shibbolei HaLeket, though later authorities did not cite it, and Ran and Tur do not appear to agree. Nevertheless, it seems proper to be lenient, because Shibbolei HaLeket and Kol Bo cite authorities who rule that even when one said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' the recipient fulfills the mitzvah if it was lost through unavoidable circumstances and he pays its value. Their intent is that the giver required the lulav itself to be returned only if it remained intact. Although the halachah does not follow them in that case, when it was given without qualification we may rely upon this view. It also appears that on Chol HaMoed Sukkos one fulfills the obligation even if the giver said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' since many authorities cited in siman 649 maintain that one fulfills the obligation with a stolen lulav on those days.
