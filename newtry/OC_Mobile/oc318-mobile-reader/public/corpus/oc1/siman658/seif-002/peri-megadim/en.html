On. Shabbos. See the Taz, Sukkah 43a. The same applies lest he carry it from a private domain to a public domain; and even according to the one who says that nowadays we have no public domain, the decree remains in its place. See siman 588, Taz letter 5 and Magen Avraham letter 4. See Tosafos 42b, s.v. 'tiltul,' and from Rashi and Chiddushei HaRan there it implies that the lulav is muktzeh, even though it is fit for a mitzvah, because it is not a utensil, like the arrangement of the reeds, which does not override Shabbos when Shabbos is not the first day. Therefore, on the other days outside the Mikdash, which are rabbinic, one may say they decreed because of muktzeh on Shabbos. But in the Mikdash, or on the first day of Yom Tov, why? See there. According to this, what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 651:28 from the Yerushalmi, that it is muktzeh after he fulfilled the obligation, is seemingly understandable, for the rule that there is no muktzeh for half of Shabbos does not apply, since it is always muktzeh, only that they decreed also outside the Mikdash on the other days as a remembrance of the Mikdash. Nevertheless, after he fulfilled the obligation it is muktzeh. Branches of a tree that he designated to wave and the like are considered a utensil, unlike a lulav, which is prohibited for benefit. See Shabbos 123. This requires some investigation.
