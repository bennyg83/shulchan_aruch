There, but here we require "for you" — yours. In kiddushin it helps when not theft; therefore they say: if he knew the law that one does not betroth with borrowing, he would give in language of gift on condition of return — therefore not theft though the lender only said borrow. But for lulav, as written "for you" — yours — this reasoning does not apply: even if he knew, in kiddushin it helps that in Beit Hillel it is not theft; nevertheless we cannot say it is called "for you" since he still does not know it is not his — this is Magen Avraham's intent. In one manuscript: "we require" — should read kapei yadi (abbreviation); it is an acronym: kollei alma yad'i — all know — see there; requires study. In my humble opinion this is not Magen Avraham's intent; his distinction is as I wrote. Nevertheless the law for kiddushin requires study — see there, in the Gemara regarding Mari bar Isak and Even HaEzer there whether remission helps retroactively; I expanded there in kiddushin.
