Because he creates a fragrance. Yam Shel Shlomo, Beitzah, chapter 2, siman 34, writes that this is difficult, because it is forbidden only to place the cup there in order to perfume it, as stated at the end of siman 511. This is why the Gemara speaks of silks, which are ordinarily perfumed. Where one does not intend to perfume the item, it is permitted, because the rule of an inevitable consequence applies only to a full melachah. Perfuming is not itself a melachah; only extinguishing is. His meaning is that when the Gemara, Beitzah 23, says that even placing spices upon a shard is forbidden because it creates fragrance in the shard, this is forbidden despite the lack of intent because kindling is inevitably involved and is a full melachah. Creating fragrance without intent, however, is permitted. Nevertheless, the rulings of Maharil and Rokeach appear primary, because there is no prohibition of kindling there, since the act is performed for food preparation, yet it is still forbidden because it creates fragrance in the shard. Their ruling is therefore primary. If the fragrance was already on the garment before Yom Tov, it is permitted to place the fragrant item upon it on Yom Tov. See siman 322:5.
