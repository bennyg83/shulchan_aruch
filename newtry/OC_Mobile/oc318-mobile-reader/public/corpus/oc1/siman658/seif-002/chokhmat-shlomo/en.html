Seif 2. On Shabbos he does not take it, even if it is the first day. Note: In the Talmud the reason is explained: lest he carry it four amos in the public domain. Tosafos asked why it does not say: lest he take it out from a private domain to a public domain. Now, according to what I wrote in my work on Orach Chayim, Mishnas Tadar, siman 631, in a responsum to the holy community of Krakow, in the sugya of 'since it is a wall,' etc., there in my work, page 61, where I concluded that although Yom Tov and Shabbos involve a positive commandment and a negative commandment, nevertheless regarding carrying, which is a lesser melachah, it is not included in actual melachah and does not emerge from the general expression 'Shabbason'; it has only a negative commandment and not a positive commandment; see there. If so, this is understandable. One may say that in truth, even if he carries it out, what of it? A positive commandment overrides a negative commandment. Although it is not at the same time, one may say first that it is a public mitzvah. See Magen Avraham siman 446, that there is room to say that even a mitzvah where each individual performs the mitzvah separately is considered a public mitzvah, and we do not require that it be at the same time. One may also say according to the view of those who hold that preparations for a mitzvah are considered at the same time; if so, here too, since he will go to an expert, it is included in 'at the same time.' If so, it would be fitting to say that a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment. Therefore it took the case of lest he carry it four amos, and that is well, for that involves a positive and negative commandment. This is a clear proof to our words above, that carrying has only a negative commandment. Examine this carefully. Now, we learn in the Talmud in Sukkah: R. Yose says, if the first Yom Tov of the festival occurred on Shabbos, and he forgot and took the lulav out to the public domain, he is exempt. The Talmud asks: but from the time he lifted it, he fulfilled his obligation with it. It answers: when he took it out in a vessel. Now, I saw in Tosafos Yom Tov that he brought in the name of the Ran, who asked that he should be liable for the vessel, and answered that it is subordinate to the lulav; see there. In my humble opinion, his words are astonishing. If it is subordinate to it, then it is again not called taking by means of another object. Something like this is implied in Yoma 58 regarding placing one bowl inside another, etc., where it says there: a foot is different, for he does not subordinate it; see there. If so, here too, if it is subordinate to the lulav, it is not considered taking by means of another object. His words are even more astonishing because, since he is exempt for the lulav because he erred in a matter of mitzvah and is considered coerced, he should be exempt for the vessel too for the same reason, since he was occupied with a mitzvah and forgot that it was Shabbos; therefore he took it out in a vessel. What difference is there between the vessel and the lulav, since he was occupied with it and is like one coerced? He is exempt also for the vessel, for R. Yose holds that when one erred in a matter of mitzvah, even if he did not perform a mitzvah, he is also exempt. If so, what distinction is there between the lulav and the vessel in which the lulav is? The Ran's words require investigation. Examine this carefully.
