(Seif katan 2) For he creates, etc. To place the cup, which is perfumed and gives off fragrance, in order to perfume, means that he intends that the garments be perfumed and scented through it. He took the case of silk garments. Meaning, in the Gemara in tractate Beitzah, daf 23, they said: turning a cup over onto silk garments is prohibited because it creates fragrance. 'It is not a melachah, except for extinguishing and kindling.' So it is there in Yam Shel Shlomo. Explanation: what do extinguishing and kindling have to do here? Therefore he wrote that Maharshal came to resolve what was difficult to him.
For that which we say on daf 23, etc. His words are very brief, and I must explain at length according to my humble understanding, and clarify what Maharshal and the Magen Avraham dispute. R. Yehudah said: smoking fruits, meaning placing spices on burning coals while fruits are suspended above the coal so that they absorb the taste and smell of the spices, is prohibited on Yom Tov. At the beginning, by placing the spices on the coal, he extinguishes, and afterward he kindles. Although this is for food preparation, nevertheless, since it is not equal for every person but only for delicate people, it is prohibited. But on a heated earthenware shard, it is permitted to place spices and smoke the fruits. This is the wording: there is no extinguishing, and the kindling is also through an unusual manner, backhanded, and there is no Torah prohibition. Rabbah said: even on an earthenware shard it is also prohibited, because he creates fragrance. Rashi wrote as follows: it enters the shard, which had no fragrance, and it is rabbinically prohibited, etc. End quote. Rabbah and R. Yosef both said: turning a cup over onto silk garments on Yom Tov is prohibited because it creates fragrance, etc. Rava said: even on coals it is permitted, just as with meat on coals, where there is also extinguishing, kindling, and creating fragrance in the coals, and nevertheless it is permitted [see above siman 511 in Magen Avraham, what he wrote]. This is the sugya of the Talmud. Maharshal wanted to resolve what is difficult regarding his words, on what he wrote that if one does not intend to create fragrance, as with an esrog, even though it is an inevitable consequence, it is nevertheless permitted. But Rabbah prohibits even on an earthenware shard because he creates fragrance in the shard, although he certainly does not intend to create fragrance in the shard, and nevertheless it is prohibited. Although we do not rule like him, that is because we rule like Rava, that even on a coal it is permitted because it is for food preparation, similar to meat on coals. But in a place where there is no need of food preparation, even Rava agrees with Rabbah that it is prohibited. Regarding this Maharshal wrote to resolve that there, in any event, there is kindling, etc. Meaning, he found it difficult why Rabbah prohibited on an earthenware shard because of creating fragrance, although creating fragrance is only a rabbinic prohibition, as Rashi wrote and as above. One must say that he holds that since it is only for delicate people, even a rabbinic prohibition was not permitted. If so, why mention a new prohibition, namely creating fragrance? Let him derive it from the prohibition of kindling. Although it is through an unusual manner and there is no Torah prohibition, nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition, as Rashi wrote. Rather, one must say that Rabbah holds that one rabbinic prohibition is permitted for food preparation even though it is only for delicate people, but two rabbinic prohibitions are not permitted. According to this, since there is also creating fragrance, for there is the prohibition of kindling in an unusual manner and also the prohibition of creating fragrance, therefore it is prohibited. According to this, Maharshal holds by reasoning that since the prohibition of creating fragrance is a light prohibition, when he does not intend to create fragrance, even though it is an inevitable consequence, it is permitted even where there is no need of food preparation. But on an earthenware shard, since even without this there is a complete prohibition, namely kindling in an unusual manner, although for that rabbinic prohibition alone we would permit it for food preparation that is only for delicate people, when another prohibition of creating fragrance joins it, even though it is a light prohibition and he does not intend it, nevertheless it is prohibited. But where there is no kindling, only the prohibition of creating fragrance, and he does not intend this, even if it is not for food preparation at all, it is permitted. This is Maharshal's view. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote: nevertheless, it appears to me, etc., for there the prohibition is not because of kindling, etc. Meaning, the Magen Avraham holds that it is impossible to say like Maharshal that Rabbah prohibits because there are two things, kindling in an unusual manner and creating fragrance; for if so, Rabbah should have mentioned the prohibition of kindling, which according to Maharshal is the main point, as above, and why did he mention only the prohibition of creating fragrance? Rather, it must be that Rabbah holds that although kindling, even in an unusual manner, is nevertheless rabbinically prohibited, we would permit it because it is for food preparation.
This is not so with creating fragrance, where there is no food preparation need, for he creates fragrance only in the shard and not in the fruits; therefore it is prohibited because of creating fragrance alone. If so, with an esrog too, where there is creating fragrance and it is not for food preparation, even though he does not intend it, since it is an inevitable consequence, it is prohibited. Know that although the Magen Avraham concludes in siman 314 not like the Terumas HaDeshen, but that even with a rabbinic prohibition an inevitable consequence is prohibited, nevertheless he needed to bring proof that it is prohibited to create fragrance when one does not intend it but it is an inevitable consequence. This is because it is implied from Maharshal's words that the prohibition of creating fragrance is a light prohibition, and weaker than other rabbinic prohibitions.
And if it was on the garment from yesterday, it is permitted, etc. See siman 322. Meaning, we say there that it is permitted to crumble fragrant woods to add fragrance, since there is already fragrance. But with an esrog on a garment, where the garment has no fragrance at all, he newly creates fragrance through the esrog, and this is prohibited, as it is in the Gemara. If so, the same applies to an esrog: if it was on the garment from yesterday, and the garment already absorbed fragrance yesterday, it is permitted, etc., for this too is only an addition of fragrance, which is permitted. Although the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 511 that regarding a garment that already has fragrance, it is nevertheless prohibited to place spices on it because he adds a new fragrance, see there what the Magen Avraham wrote to distinguish between the spices there and the esrog here, and see what I wrote there.
Know what the Taz wrote in seif katan 3, that a lulav may be moved for use of its body or place, similar to shofar. The book Chemdas Moshe challenged him, because specifically a shofar has the status of a utensil whose work is prohibited, and therefore it is permitted to move it for use of its body or place, as stated in siman 308. But a lulav does not have the status of a utensil, and even for use of its body or place it is prohibited, as the Rema wrote in siman 308:7 in the gloss; see there. In my humble opinion, he spoke well. Although seemingly it is implied that a lulav, by being designated for a mitzvah, is called a utensil, and if so on Shabbos it would be a utensil whose work is prohibited, for the Talmud in Sukkah 33b discusses whether one may reduce the berries of the myrtle on Yom Tov, and asks: is he not repairing a utensil? So too on daf 42a, Rashi wrote that the reason it is prohibited to add water to the lulav on Yom Tov is that he exerts himself to repair a utensil. Nevertheless it appears to me that the term 'repairing a utensil' is necessarily not precise, for something designated for a mitzvah does not have the status of a utensil. So too it is implied at the beginning of chapter Lulav VaAravah, from what Tosafos wrote on the Talmud's question, 'but it is mere moving,' that it does not have the status of a utensil.
