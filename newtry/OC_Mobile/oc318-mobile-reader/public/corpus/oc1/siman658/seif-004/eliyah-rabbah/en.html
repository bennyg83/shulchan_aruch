[4] A person does not fulfill his obligation, etc. If one already began using it for the mitzvah, Magen Avraham considers this equivalent to having given it as a gift, as with a ring used for kiddushin in Even HaEzer, siman 28, and as he writes in siman 14. This requires study, for in siman 649, seif katan 9, he writes that one does not fulfill the obligation; perhaps there is a scribal error there. The Taz there and Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 114, imply that one does not fulfill the obligation. If the owner is not at home and his wife gives the lulav to one of his friends, the recipient does not fulfill the obligation, because the owner may have preferred to sell its use for one day, unless he is a distinguished person for whom such a sale would be demeaning (Magen Avraham). It appears to me, however, that the husband does not object to this, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 248, particularly where she conducts the household's business affairs.
