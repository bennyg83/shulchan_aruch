(12) Its name is a gift - For when he says, 'this object is given to you,' all his power over it is removed, and what he said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' is only a condition, like other conditions that a person may make in his sale or gift.
(13) And if he did not return it - The same applies if it became invalid in his hand and he returned it.
(14) Even if he gave him its value - We do not say in this case, 'what difference is there to me between it and its value,' since he needs it in order to fulfill the obligation; he was certainly particular about the lulav itself.
(15) And even if it was taken from him by unavoidable accident - Although according to the Mechaber in Choshen Mishpat siman 241, with a gift on condition to return, if it was taken by unavoidable accident he is exempt from payment, that is because he does not have the law of a guardian, since it came into his hand as a gift. Nevertheless, the condition was not fulfilled, and it is only like a deposit in his hand; therefore he did not fulfill the obligation in our case. It is implied from most poskim that even when it was taken by unavoidable accident, returning money does not help. See Chayei Adam, who is uncertain what the law is if the giver said, 'it is as though I received it.' In the handwritten Meiri that is under my hand, his opinion is explicit that the giver may waive his condition and say, 'it is as though I received it,' and this person has fulfilled his obligation, even though he did not return it.
(16) After the time of its mitzvah - Meaning, even on that same day, if it is an hour when one can no longer fulfill the obligation with it, namely in the evening. If, at the time he gave it to him, he had already recited the berachah over it, he must nevertheless return it to him the next day at the time of its mitzvah. Although he did not explicitly specify when to return it to him, it is an evident assessment that his intent was that he return it to him at a time when he can fulfill the obligation with it. The poskim wrote that all this is when he said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' which implies that it should be fit for the thing for which it stands. But if he says, 'on condition that you return it,' and did not say 'to me,' then even if he returned it after the festival, when it is worth nothing, he has still fulfilled his condition and fulfilled the obligation. See Biur Halacha.
(17) And it is permitted, etc., that he not consecrate it - Meaning, if he stipulated in his gift that the recipient would not be able to consecrate it, nevertheless it is a gift and one can fulfill the obligation with it. As he concludes, it is no worse than one who said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' where he also cannot consecrate it, and nevertheless it is a gift. See Biur Halacha.
