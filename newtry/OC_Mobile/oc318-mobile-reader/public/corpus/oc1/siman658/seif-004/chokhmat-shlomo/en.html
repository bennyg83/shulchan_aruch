(There, seif 6.) He should not give it on the first day to a minor before he has fulfilled the obligation with it, etc. Note: Regarding the practice of giving it first to a minor and afterward the adults recite the berachah, it appears to me to defend this based on the view of the Chacham Tzvi in a responsum, that if one does not have valid species on the first Yom Tov, it is permitted to take the valid species on the second, for the first Yom Tov is no worse than the second Yom Tov, and there is a rabbinic obligation on it, no worse than the second; see there. If so, one may say the same here regarding a minor: one does not transfer ownership to him, but he recites the berachah over it as borrowed, just like on the second Yom Tov. Since a minor is not obligated from the Torah, but only rabbinically because of education, and rabbinically a borrowed item is enough, therefore one does not transfer it to him completely. Specifically with an adult, who is obligated from the Torah, and by Torah law we require 'yours' on the first Yom Tov, we are witnesses that their intent is in an effective manner, and he intends a gift on condition to return. But for a minor, for the law of education, where a borrowed item suffices, there is no presumed assessment of intent, and we say that it was given to him as borrowed. That which we say in the Talmud, that a person should not transfer ownership, etc., teaches that if he wants to transfer ownership to him he is not permitted; but in truth, acquisition is not required for a minor. Through this our custom is properly resolved, with Hashem's help. Examine this carefully.
