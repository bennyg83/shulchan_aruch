[7] He gave it to him without qualification, etc.; presumably, etc. Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 114, asks that the Shulchan Aruch appears to contradict Choshen Mishpat 241:6, where it rules that if one said, 'on condition that you return it,' without saying 'to me,' the recipient may return it whenever he wishes. One cannot answer that an unspecified gift is stronger than an explicit condition lacking the words 'to me,' because Beit Yosef writes that an unspecified gift is no stronger than an expressly stated gift whose condition was not doubled. He answers that Choshen Mishpat speaks where the giver has another lulav with which to fulfill the mitzvah. If he said, 'on condition that you return it to me,' however, it must be returned immediately, because he wishes to perform the mitzvah with his own property. This answer is strained. He further answers that Choshen Mishpat concerns monetary enforcement, so the court cannot compel immediate return, but the recipient has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Magen Avraham, however, writes that he has fulfilled it, and Responsa Rashba, siman 1000, likewise implies this. I would have said that since the Shulchan Aruch does not write here, 'and likewise, if he returned it after the time of the mitzvah,' as it writes in the preceding seif, an unspecified gift is not equivalent to saying, 'on condition that you return it to me,' except insofar as it must eventually be returned, not that it must be returned immediately. This resembles Rabbeinu Yerucham's distinction above regarding unavoidable loss. Yet Levush, who places the statement 'that which we said, etc.' after the law of an unspecified gift, clearly indicates that the same immediate-return rule applies. Ran also implies this. Nevertheless, on Chol HaMoed Sukkos it appears that one fulfills the obligation even if he did not return it immediately when it was given without qualification, as stated above.
