(18) They gave it to him without specification, etc., since he needs, etc. - Meaning, in a case where he has only this etrog: even if they give it to him without specification, it is likewise a clear presumption that they did not intend to give it to him permanently, but rather on condition that he return it. And even if on that day he had already fulfilled the mitzvah himself, and he needs it only for the remaining days of the festival, and on the remaining days he can fulfill it with a borrowed [etrog] - nevertheless we assess his intent: a person prefers more to fulfill with what is his own, and he intended only on condition that it be returned to him so that it be his as at first.
(19) And he must go back and give it, etc. - This refers to all of the above, including seif 4 as well. And the reason is that whoever was given it, it is entirely his, and 'on condition to return' that he said is like saying 'on condition that you go back and give it to me as a gift.'
(20) So that it be the owners' and they may fulfill with it - The language is awkward, for it implies that if he does not go back and give it to the owners as a complete gift, the owners will not be able to fulfill their obligation with the etrog; and seemingly that is not so, for the owners can in any case fulfill, since they gave it only on condition that it be returned to them as a gift as required, and if it was not returned to them in that manner the gift is void retroactively and it is as if they never gave it; and this requires further study.
(21) But only to another, etc. - Meaning that he too gave the etrog to another person as a gift on condition to return, and that one likewise gave it to a third to return to the original owners - he has fulfilled, and we do not say: he stipulated with him 'on condition that you return it to me,' and he himself did not return it to the original owners. For presumably the owners' intent and insistence was only that the object be returned to them, whether through the first or through his agents. However, it apparently follows from this law that one who receives a gift on condition to return has permission to go back and give it to whomever he wishes, provided his condition is fulfilled. However, all this is when unspecified; but if he explicitly states in his gift that he has no permission to give it to another, he cannot confer ownership on another so that he may fulfill with it, and if he gives it the gift is void and he himself also has not fulfilled with it.
(22) And another to another - And it implies even to many, provided it is returned to the first without a disqualification. And it appears that where there is a presumption that the giver is particular that many not take it, lest it be ruined for him, he is not permitted to give it. And even aside from this, there are Acharonim who are stringent about the Mechaber's essential ruling and hold that a lulav received as a gift on condition to return one should not give to another person without the knowledge and permission of the giver.
