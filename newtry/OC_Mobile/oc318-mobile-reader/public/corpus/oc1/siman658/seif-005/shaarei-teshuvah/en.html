From your own. See Baer Heitev. See what I wrote in the Kuntres Acharon to the new responsa of the Bach, siman 61, regarding a gift on condition to return, and what I wrote in Yad Ephraim on the words of the Magen Avraham in this matter. See Machaneh Ephraim, Hilchos Kinyan Meshichah, who wrote that according to the poskim who hold that a rabbinic acquisition does not help to fulfill a Torah obligation, those who buy an esrog for the mitzvah and pay for it after the festival do not act properly, for as long as they have not given the money, they have in it only the acquisition of meshichah, which is rabbinic, and we require 'yours' from the Torah. See there in the gloss of his son, Mahari Gaon, of blessed memory, who wrote that according to what the Ramban wrote, brought in the new responsa of the Bach, that even a gift of movable property is acquired only through exchange, courtyard, or along with land, if so there is concern even with a gift whenever he did not bring it into his domain. According to this, one must explain that which is stated in Sukkah 41, 'R. Yehoshua took it and fulfilled the mitzvah with it,' etc., to mean that he took it into his domain, etc. In Birkei Yosef he wrote about this that it is difficult from the Mishnah in chapter Lulav VaAravah, that they would bring their lulavim to Har HaBayis and instruct, etc., 'let it be his as a gift.' According to what he wrote at the end of the book in the omissions, that whenever he took it into his hand and intended to acquire it, according to everyone he acquired it from the Torah, this is understandable; but from the words of his father, the master, it does not imply so, etc.; see there. See what I wrote in my responsa Beis Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 8 and siman 9, clarifying the matters of rabbinic acquisition with respect to a Torah obligation. See also Responsa Masas Moshe, siman 4, and Birkei Yosef, seif 10, and Choshen Mishpat siman 8 at length. See Get Pashut, siman 120, and the book Toras Gittin regarding acquiring parchment for a get when he did not give money. One who is meticulous in his deeds should be stringent in this too. See Magen Avraham above, siman 306, that the reason one gives the esrog as a gift on Yom Tov is that it is not a complete gift, but only on condition to return after he fulfills the mitzvah with it, etc.; also, it is permitted for the need of a mitzvah. See the book HaMakneh on Kiddushin 6b, resolving what the Beis Yosef wrote against the Semag, who wrote that one must double the condition in order that there be a mitzvah also in the return; see there. See Birkei Yosef in the name of Mutzal MeEsh, who was uncertain about one who said in the language of admission, 'this lulav belongs to Reuven,' whether he fulfilled his obligation; and the Birkei Yosef wrote that he did not fulfill it; see there. See Ketzos HaChoshen 241 and Mekor Chayim, siman 448, regarding this.
