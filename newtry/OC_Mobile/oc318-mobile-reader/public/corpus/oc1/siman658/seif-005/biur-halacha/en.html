Nevertheless, where it was taken by unavoidable accident in his hand, I found that Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that the implication of the Baal HaItur is that he fulfills the obligation by returning money, for in any event he did not say explicitly, 'return it to me' [Eliyah Rabbah].
However, etc., but only afterward. See Yad Ephraim, who wrote that this is not implied from the words of Tosafos and the Rosh. Bikkurei Yaakov further challenged that according to Rashi's explanation in the Gemara, that Rabban Gamliel gave it to them on condition that they return it, we have no source for this leniency of the Mechaber, that the recipient himself may give it to another. He remained in difficulty regarding the Mechaber, who ruled simply like the Rashba, Tur, Ran, and Ritva, when according to Tosafos, the Rosh, and Rashi one should be stringent in this. However, in truth there are several other Rishonim who rule simply to be lenient, like the Shulchan Aruch: Shibolei HaLeket brought in Magen Avraham, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Kol Bo, and Meiri. Although in other matters the Meiri does not decide, regarding esrog his opinion is to be lenient, because it is for a mitzvah; presumably the giver's intent is not to be particular. Also according to Rashi's explanation there is no proof to be stringent in practice. Although according to his explanation the incident there was that he gave it to all of them on condition to return, nevertheless, as a matter of law perhaps even if he gives it to one person, since it is his he may also give it to another, provided that in the end the condition to return it to the owners at the time of its mitzvah is fulfilled. Know that the Ran also copied Rashi's wording, and nevertheless in the end he copied this law leniently.
And the last one returns it to the owner. Know that I am uncertain: perhaps this is specifically when at least the agents of the recipient returned it; however, if the recipient did not return it at all until the owners themselves were troubled to come to him and take the esrog, even though he said to him that he is returning it to him as a gift, perhaps this is not considered a return. It appears that this depends on the nature of the giver and how particular he is. Know further that it is obvious that if he returns it to the owner by means of an agent, it is certainly considered a return, all the more so from the law of this seif. Seemingly it appears that the law of agency is not required for this: even if he returns it to him through a non-Jew or a minor, who are not capable of agency or of transferring ownership, he also fulfills his condition through this, since his intent is to give it to him as a gift and the other acquires it, while the non-Jew or minor is only like a mere physical instrument. This is all the more so according to what the Rosh wrote in the name of the Itur, that even if he returns it to him without specifying, we assess his intent that presumably he gives it to him as a gift. Nevertheless, this is not so clear.
