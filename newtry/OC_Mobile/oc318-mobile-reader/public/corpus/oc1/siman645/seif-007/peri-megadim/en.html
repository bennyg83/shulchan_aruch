But. See Turei Zahav at the end of siman 649. The Tur means that the Rambam intended specifically dry, but not other invalidations; and likewise the other species, even esrog, are invalid, for only a lulav is called hadar even when dry, not the other species. About this the Tur wrote that it is not clear, for what difference is there between this and that? If so, one can say that the Rambam's reading is not specifically a dry lulav; the same applies to all invalidations and all species, that in pressing circumstances one fulfills with them. The Maggid Mishneh indeed wrote so in the name of the Geonim in chapter 8 of Hilchos Lulav, halachah 1. If so, the words of the Beis Yosef require analysis, when he wrote that most poskim hold that after the fact it is invalid, for many hold that with all invalidations one fulfills in pressing circumstances. The Rema here speaks of pressing circumstances. In siman 649, he validated a dry lulav even if others have one, as long as he does not; he recites a berachah over it. In pressing circumstances, where there is no other at all, one can say that all invalidations due to blemish, aside from stolen, etc., one recites a berachah over them. However, the Turei Zahav there, letter 12, wrote that in pressing circumstances one should not recite a berachah except for dry; with Hashem's help this will be explained there. What is printed in the Tur, that he holds like the Rambam, appears to be a scribal error; it should say, 'in the view of the Rambam.' See Bayis Chadash at the end of siman 649 and Eliyah Rabbah here. From what was written it seems that the Rema validates even where the upper leaves were cut in pressing circumstances, as one who examines Darchei Moshe, Eliyah Rabbah, and Or Zarua will see. In siman 649 this will be explained further, with Hashem's help.
