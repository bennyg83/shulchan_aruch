4) Ibid. And they did not become detached, etc. This means that they were not uprooted, as occurs with palm branches whose leaves have been uprooted. Taz, s.k. 1. That is to say, although they separated, nevertheless the place where they are attached did not harden, and they can still be bound; this is called not uprooted. Levushei Serad on Taz, s.k. 2. This is also compelled by the words of Taz, s.k. 2. What he wrote, that where one can bind them it is valid, means that after binding, the leaves will lie properly upon the spine and be bound together, for otherwise even palm branches can be bound. So too is implied by what was written in the preceding paragraph.
