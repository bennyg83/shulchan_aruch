(3) Separated. The Turei Zahav concluded that the Maggid Mishneh's stringency for the choicest mitzvah applies specifically where all its leaves hang downward but can still be raised together with the lulav. The case of 'its leaves were split apart,' which is invalid, is where its leaves were completely detached from the lulav, or where they hang downward and also do not rise with the lulav, or where they became loose from their place of attachment in the spine and remain attached only somewhat. But if its leaves do not hang, and merely lean somewhat to the sides, there is not even a concern of choicest mitzvah. However, the Maamar Mordechai and Bikkurei Yaakov question the words of the Turei Zahav. Their view follows the plain wording of the Rema, that for the choicest mitzvah one should be careful initially not to take a lulav unless its leaves lie one upon another and not to the sides.
