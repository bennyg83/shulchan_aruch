Se'if 5: A lulav that dried, etc. So wrote the Rashba: dry means that most of its leaves dried; even if its spine is moist, it is not hadar. The same applies in reverse. Some wrote the opposite, and some wrote that both must have dried. The first view appears correct. It is not common that the spine is dry while the leaves are moist. So wrote the Ran as well. The second 'some explain' is the view of the Ra'avad brought by the Rosh, according to the reading in the Geonic text: what is the law if the leaves dried, etc. It appears that the question there was resolved leniently. His view is that all invalidations, such as its top was cut off, are in the body of the lulav, meaning in its spine, just as with a myrtle. The Rashba, Rosh, and Ran disagreed with him from what is said there, 32b: it said, etc., and a lulav four, etc., the spine of a lulav, etc. This proves that the body of the lulav is not its spine and is not comparable to myrtle. One must read there in the Yerushalmi: its blossom dried, etc., meaning the upper leaf, which is the upper tiyomes according to Rashi; and what difference is there between this and cut off? See se'if 6. The Rosh wrote: since if most of its leaves were split apart it is invalid, all the more so if most of its leaves dried. It appears that the primary dry case of the Mishnah refers to the spine. This is what the Tur wrote: a lulav whose spine dried or, etc., and here in the gloss. But the Shulchan Aruch takes the Rashba's explanation, that all the invalidations in the Mishnah concern the leaves, and that is called the lulav, as it appears to me. Also, since a dry spine without dry leaves is uncommon, he omitted it.
And the measure, etc.; and some say, etc. As is written regarding the ear of a firstborn animal at the beginning of chapter 6 of Bechoros. The first view challenged them: the Rabbis disagree regarding the ear of a firstborn animal, and specifically regarding a lung they hold that its measure is when it can be crumbled with the fingernail, because air does not affect it, as written in chapter 3 of Chullin. There is proof from the Yerushalmi, which said, 'the dead do not praise Hashem.' Even though the reason is hadar, as written in the Gemara, the Yerushalmi merely took a sign. The Ra'avad, Rashba, Rosh, Ran, Maggid Mishneh, and all the poskim agreed, and see Turei Zahav. This is also implied by what he said: that it does not, etc.
