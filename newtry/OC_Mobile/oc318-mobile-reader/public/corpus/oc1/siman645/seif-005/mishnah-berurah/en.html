(4) Meaning, etc. This means that this too is included in 'hanging loosely' along the length of the spine, even though they have not yet been uprooted from their attachment. The same applies if they were uprooted from their place of attachment in the spine and remain attached somewhat, even if they do not hang downward. If the spine itself broke and hangs downward, meaning it remains attached somewhat, it appears to me that it is valid. Specifically where most leaves are broken it is invalid, for it resembles an animal whose majority of ribs were broken, which is a tereifah, whereas if the spine was broken it is valid. However, one should bind it there so that it does not fall downward; so wrote the Magen Avraham. But many Acharonim disagree with this, because there the case is different: the spinal cord remains intact, and its validity depends on that, for if the cord was severed, even if the spine remains intact, it is a tereifah, as explained there. This is unlike our case. In Bigdei Yesha he wrote that the Magen Avraham did not mean that it actually broke, for if so it would be no better than its top being cut off, which is invalid. Rather, he means that it droops downward; if so, it is properly comparable to a broken spine where the cord was not severed. This requires analysis.
