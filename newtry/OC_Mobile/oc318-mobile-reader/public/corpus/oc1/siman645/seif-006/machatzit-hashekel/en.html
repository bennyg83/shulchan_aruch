(Subparagraph 6) One recites a berachah, etc. For the Mordechai wrote this regarding a myrtle. This means that the Rema's gloss derives from the words of the Mordechai. But in the Mordechai we find that he wrote this only regarding a myrtle. Regarding a lulav, the Mordechai did not speak, and the Rema transferred it to a lulav. From where did the Rema know this?
And so it appears from the Gemara; Bayis Chadash. Meaning, regarding a cut myrtle, one should recite a berachah in pressing circumstances, even though we learned in the Mishnah that a myrtle whose top was cut off is invalid. Nevertheless, since we conclude that the halachah follows Rabbi Tarfon, that even if all three of them were cut off they are valid, why should we say that the Mishnah is not in accordance with halachah? Perforce, the word 'invalid' taught in the Mishnah means initially, where it is possible to use a whole one that is not cut. He brought proof that we sometimes find the Mishnah teaching 'invalid' when it must mean only initially. This is specifically regarding myrtle: even though it teaches 'invalid,' we must say that this is not exact and that it means only initially, because of the necessity mentioned above. But regarding a lulav whose top was cut off, where the Mishnah teaches that it is invalid, why should we remove the word 'invalid' from its plain meaning, which implies even after the fact, and say that it means only initially, without any necessity? Rather, it is completely invalid, even where no other is available. However, the Turei Zahav in this siman and at the beginning of siman 646 also understood that the Mordechai's words apply to lulav as well. He explains the Mordechai's words: although Sefer Yere'im wrote this only regarding a myrtle, nevertheless the Mordechai equates lulav to myrtle, since in both cases the Mishnah teaches 'invalid.' Since regarding myrtle we must say that 'invalid' means only initially, the same applies to lulav.
Regarding the measure of cutting, the Turei Zahav in subparagraph 7 brought the words of the Ran, that its measure is even the smallest amount. In my humble opinion, this 'smallest amount' is precise. Although the Turei Zahav wrote in subparagraph 4 regarding a split tiyomes that the Ran wrote that even a minority is invalid, and the Turei Zahav proved that perforce the smallest amount is valid, there he means that 'minority' refers to a tefach or an olive-volume. It is not comparable to here, for although the expression 'even a minority' can be interpreted as an olive-volume or a tefach, the expression 'the smallest amount' can only be interpreted as the smallest amount literally. If, according to this, the difficulty of the Turei Zahav in subparagraph 4 is raised, that if so the Gemara in Chullin 17 should have counted cutting by the smallest amount, it truly requires resolution. Aside from this, one should analyze the Turei Zahav in subparagraph 4, for it remains difficult: the Gemara in Chullin should also have counted the invalidation of an esrog at its nose, which certainly invalidates by the smallest amount.
