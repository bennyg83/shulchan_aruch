And if the upper leaf was cut off, etc. He was not precise in his wording, for he should have written, 'and some say that if it was cut off, etc.,' since this disagrees with what the Shulchan Aruch wrote about most of the leaves, etc. According to that view, the middle leaf has no greater stringency in this matter than the other leaves.
But if no other is available, etc. In the Beis Yosef he wrote in the name of the Mordechai, in the name of Re'em, that the invalidation taught regarding a cut-off top means only initially, as we find elsewhere that the Mishnah teaches 'invalid' and it means initially; if no other is available, one may recite a berachah over it. The Beis Yosef wrote about this: I do not know what forced Re'em to explain this way, and this is not implied by the words of all the poskim. These are his words. I wondered about this, for the Rambam validates a dry lulav in pressing circumstances, with proof from Rabbi Yehudah's statement: there was an incident with the people of Karkom who would bequeath their lulavim to their descendants. They answered him: pressing circumstances are no proof. The Maggid Mishneh wrote: I saw it written that the Geonim relied on this to say that with a dry lulav, a cut-off top, and all invalid items, one fulfills the obligation in pressing circumstances even on the first day. These are his words. Although the Ra'avad disagrees with allowing dry ones in pressing circumstances and says that they bequeathed them only as a remembrance, not to recite a berachah over them, the Maggid Mishneh wrote there that the Ramban rejected this and holds like the Rambam, and this is reasonable. Thus the Rambam, Ramban, and Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Geonim wrote to permit in pressing circumstances. It is only that the Tur holds, according to his understanding of the Rambam, that pressing circumstances help only with dry, not with other invalidations, as he wrote at the end of siman 649, and he wrote there: this does not appear to me, and this is the view of the Rosh. How then did the Beis Yosef write that it is against all the poskim? Therefore the Rema ruled well here. See siman 646 regarding the words of the Mordechai that we mentioned about this, and the end of siman 649. In the Minhagim it is written: if its top was cut off, it is valid, and some say it is invalid; if no other is available, one recites a berachah over it. These words cannot be upheld as written, for the explicit Mishnah says that a cut-off top is invalid. And if he is speaking of pressing circumstances, why does he then conclude by deciding what to do when no other is available? The one who invalidates invalidates even in pressing circumstances. It appears to me that there is a scribal error there, and it should read: if its top was cut off, it is invalid, and some say that if no other is available one recites a berachah over it. This is the dispute of the Rambam and Ra'avad that we mentioned. Regarding the invalidation of a cut-off top, the Ran wrote on the Mishnah that 'its top' means any amount. So too what the Gemara says, that cut, split, and bent are invalid, refers to this middle leaf, because they made the top of the lulav like the nose of an esrog, where even the smallest amount invalidates. These are his words. It appears to me that he concluded this comparison to an esrog only regarding cut off, for regarding split we say that as long as it did not split like a hemnek it is valid, even if you explain, as the forced explanations do, that this refers to across its width. In any event, it is certainly not invalid in every way that an esrog is invalid. Rather, the Ran said this only to teach that this also refers to the middle leaf, but not to compare it to an esrog even in this respect. Even if you say that the Ran holds regarding split as well that any amount invalidates, as the Beis Yosef understood from him at the end of this siman regarding bent, it will be difficult, for regarding bent we certainly do not rule like him to forbid any bend, but like the Rosh, who disagrees and forbids only when it is bent in its body, not at its top, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote at the end of this siman. There is no choicest-mitzvah reason there to invalidate any bend at its top; on the contrary, it is more valid. Why then should a split of any amount invalidate? They were all stated together. Rather, certainly what I wrote is primary, and this appears to me clear.
