With the fingernail. See Be'er Heitev. See my Responsa Beis Ephraim, siman 61, where I explained the views of the poskim on this and wrote to wonder about the Mishnah Berurah who cited the Magen Avraham in siman 646. I wrote that crumbling with the fingernail alone is invalid according to the view of Tosafos and the Ra'avad, who also brought that we follow the appearance, so even if it does not crumble with the fingernail it is invalid, etc., when its green appearance has entirely disappeared; see there. See Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 28, who fully permitted those who dye lulavim they own with a green leaf so that it appears new, since this beautifies the mitzvah and there is no prohibition of bal tosif in this coloring; see there. Be'er Heitev brings this below in siman 651. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 651, subparagraph 3, who wrote that according to the Agudah, who says not to wrap reeds around the myrtle because of bal tosif, it appears to me that one should protest the practice of some who dye the whiteness of the lulav with a type called painter's oil. Perhaps they hold that it has no substance upon which bal tosif can take effect, but this is forced. Also in siman 532 we say that something done for appearance is not nullified. See Tosafos, Sukkah 31, s.v. ho'il. These are his words. However, the Shevus Yaakov's view is like what the Minchas Yaakov wrote in rule 74, that appearance is not a legally significant thing; and even if you say it is significant, since it is not called by the name of the dye but only a green lulav like new ones, there is no concern of bal tosif, for anything done to beautify it, etc. See siman 33 regarding the inner side of tefillin straps and tzitzis, where any color is permitted; see there. So it also appears to me. There is no proof at all from siman 513 to here. It only appears to me that one who sells lulavim is not permitted to do this, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 228, and all the more so where genuinely green ones are available to buy, for through this people will refrain from seeking an actually new one. By buying it he misleads the buyer in this, and he should be penalized by people not buying from him. However, anyone who has a white lulav may dye it for himself or give it to a dyer. If it is known that this seller has white lulavim and dyed them, it is permitted. See there in Sema, subparagraph 16, regarding soaking meat in water.
However, the Shevut Yaakov's view is as Minchat Yaakov wrote in klal 74, that appearance is not halachically substantive. And even if one says it is substantive, since it is not called by the name of the dye but rather a green lulav, in the manner of new ones, there is no concern of bal tosif, for anything done for beautification, etc. See siman 33 regarding the inner side of tefillin straps and tzitzit, where it is permitted to use any color; see there. So it appears to my humble mind, that there is no proof at all from siman 513 to here. However, it appears to me that one who sells lulavim may not do this, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 228, and all the more so in a place where genuinely green lulavim are available for purchase, for by doing so he causes people to refrain from seeking a truly new lulav. Through this he misleads the buyer, and he should be penalized so that people do not buy from him. But one who has a white lulav may color it for himself or give it to a dyer. If it is known that this seller has white lulavim and colored them, it is permitted. See there in Sema, seif katan 16, regarding soaking meat in water; see there.
