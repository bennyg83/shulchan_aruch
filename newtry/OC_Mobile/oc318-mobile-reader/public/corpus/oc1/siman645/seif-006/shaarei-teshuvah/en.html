With the fingernail. See Baer Heitev. See my Responsa Beit Ephraim, siman 61, where I explained the methods of the poskim in this matter and wrote to question the Mishnat Binyamin cited by the Magen Avraham in siman 646. I wrote that being crumbly by the fingernail alone renders it invalid according to Tosafot and the Raavad, whom they also cited, for we follow appearance, and even if it is not crumbly by the fingernail it is invalid, etc., once its green appearance has been lost; see there. See Shevut Yaakov, vol. 2, siman 28, who completely permitted those who dye their lulavim with a green leaf so that they appear new; this is an enhancement of the mitzvah and there is no issue of bal tosif in this coloring; see there. Baer Heitev brings this below siman 651. See Eliyah Rabbah, siman 651, seif katan 3, who wrote that according to the Agudah, who says not to bind a reed around the hadas because of bal tosif, it appears to me that one should protest against the practice of coloring the whiteness of a lulav with a substance called feiter zeil. Perhaps they hold that it has no substance on which bal tosif could take effect, but this is difficult. Also in siman 532 we say that something done for appearance is not nullified. See Tosafot, Sukkah 31, s.v. hoil; end quote.
However, the Shevut Yaakov's view is as Minchat Yaakov wrote in klal 74, that appearance is not halachically substantive. And even if one says it is substantive, since it is not called by the name of the dye but rather a green lulav, in the manner of new ones, there is no concern of bal tosif, for anything done for beautification, etc. See siman 33 regarding the inner side of tefillin straps and tzitzit, where it is permitted to use any color; see there. So it appears to my humble mind, that there is no proof at all from siman 513 to here. However, it appears to me that one who sells lulavim may not do this, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 228, and all the more so in a place where genuinely green lulavim are available for purchase, for by doing so he causes people to refrain from seeking a truly new lulav. Through this he misleads the buyer, and he should be penalized so that people do not buy from him. But one who has a white lulav may color it for himself or give it to a dyer. If it is known that this seller has white lulavim and colored them, it is permitted. See there in Sema, seif katan 16, regarding soaking meat in water; see there.
