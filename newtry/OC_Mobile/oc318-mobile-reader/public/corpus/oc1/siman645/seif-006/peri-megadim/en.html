And if. See Turei Zahav. According to the Mechaber, the cutting of the tiyoma alone does not invalidate unless most of the upper leaves were cut. If we say that 'most of the upper leaves' means that if the lulav ends in three leaves and two were cut it is invalid, then where it ends in one leaf and the like, it is invalid because of that leaf alone, and the Rema does not disagree with the Mechaber. But if we explain 'most of the upper leaves' as the upper leaves that rise above the spine, one can say that the Rema disagrees. See Beis Yosef and Bayis Chadash. Even according to this, one can say that it means the majority of the number, etc.; nevertheless one can say that they do not disagree. As to how much must be cut, this requires analysis. One can say it is the majority of each of the upper leaves, and in the middle leaf the majority, not any amount; or one can say even any amount. See the Ran. What the Rema wrote, 'if there is no other valid one,' in Darchei Moshe it implies that the cut-off top in the Mishnah refers even to the majority of the upper leaves being cut, and so it appears in the Levush and Or Zarua; see there. In Eliyah Rabbah, letter 7, he wrote that the Rema agrees regarding the cut-off tops of the leaves; this requires analysis. The Levush wrote that if the majority of the heads of the upper leaves were cut by any amount, it is invalid, and likewise the upper leaf. This shows that he explains that they do not disagree, as I wrote. What he wrote, 'any amount,' requires analysis. One must be very careful about this with the middle leaf, to inspect it and see whether the leaf was cut even slightly; one can recognize this by examining it. This requires analysis.
