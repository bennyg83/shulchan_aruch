Se'if 6: Its top was cut off, meaning, etc.; and if, etc. Tosafos and the Rosh wrote that at first glance this seems to refer to the two upper leaves. They asked: if so, why was it necessary to say that if the tiyomes was removed it is invalid? They then explained that it refers to the majority of the upper leaves. They wrote two further answers to uphold the first explanation: one can say that cut off is worse, because it is not hadar; and further, cut off requires specifically that both leaves be cut. The Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh wrote that it refers to the upper middle leaf that was cut. The Beis Yosef wrote that perhaps they do not disagree: Tosafos and the Rosh speak of lulavim that end in two leaves, while some lulavim end in three leaves, and the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh speak of the middle one among them. Since the Beis Yosef viewed the latter answers of Tosafos and the Rosh as forced, namely that cut off should be worse than removed, and likewise the second answer that it is only if both were cut, for that is not so, since some are invalidated by one leaf as mentioned above, he therefore wrote in the Shulchan Aruch according to the first answer. But the Rosh wrote that the first answer is forced. The Rashba and the Ran also wrote both explanations and agreed to this explanation, for they wrote that since it does not say 'its leaves were cut off,' as it says 'were split apart,' but says 'its top,' it implies the top of the lulav, the upper leaf. The Maggid Mishneh mentioned only this explanation, writing that there are many interpretations, and the correct one is that the top of the upper leaf, where the lulav ends, was cut off. They wrote that a cut of the smallest amount invalidates it. Some say this is only when most of the upper leaf was cut, as with other invalidations, but they agreed that when it is cut, even the smallest amount invalidates, as with the nose of an esrog.
