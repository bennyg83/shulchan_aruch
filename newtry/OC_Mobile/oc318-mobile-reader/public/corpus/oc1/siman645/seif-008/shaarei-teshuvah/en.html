(Se'if 9 in Shulchan Aruch) But its leaves are bent at the top, etc. The Bayis Chadash wrote that it is proper to be careful like the Ran even when its leaves are bent at their tops. The She'eilas Yaavetz also wrote that although the Rosh wrote that he liked to fulfill the mitzvah with it, according to other great authorities not only is it not the choicest, but one does not fulfill the obligation with it. Eliyah Rabbah also brought the words of the Bayis Chadash and wrote that Baal Halachos Gedolos wrote similarly in the name of the Radbaz. However, it appears that the Ran did not mean literally any amount, but a large section bent, as written in subparagraph 4. This means that in subparagraph 4, regarding split, he wrote in the name of the Turei Zahav that 'part of it split' means at least a tefach or an olive-volume; see there. If so, the same would apply here. In truth, a bend of a tefach is not common at all. It also appears that this is not comparable to a split, which the Turei Zahav compares to a blemish of the altar; see there. Here, by contrast, it appears that the Ran invalidates in every case, because he holds that it is a change from its manner of formation, even though such growth is sometimes found. See Turei Zahav: at first he used the measure of a tefach, and afterward he wrote a tefach or an olive-volume. It appears that the first formulation is primary, and the latter formulation was stated because of the dispute of Rabbi Shimon and Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov regarding a blemish of the altar. The Rambam there ruled like Rabbi Shimon, that its measure is a tefach. See Chacham Tzvi, siman 10, who brings proof for the Rambam from Sukkah, where we say, 'and on that day the horn of the altar was blemished,' etc. See the Levush, brought by the Magen Avraham, that if most of the leaves, or all of them, are bent, it is invalid. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that the Turei Zahav disagrees and validates even when most are bent. In the Turei Zahav it implies that even when all are bent, his view is to validate; see there. It appears that in this case, at least, it is proper to be careful when another lulav is available not to fulfill the mitzvah with one whose leaf-tips are all or mostly bent.
