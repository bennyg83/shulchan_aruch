If. See Turei Zahav, Gemara 32a, and Bayis Chadash. At first glance the words of the Turei Zahav and Bayis Chadash are one; see there. To add some explanation: since Rav Papa did not say that the baraisa is different because it was made from the beginning of its formation like a hemnek, and that Rav Huna speaks of a case that split afterward and is valid, you learn that he means that even a split has a distinction between being like a hemnek or not, etc. 'Split' certainly implies afterward. Therefore the Tur distinguishes, even when it split afterward, between a split at its head into two heads, which is invalid, and a split in the middle while its head is split, where even if it widened in the middle of the spine, it is valid. The Mechaber uses the Rambam's language, chapter 8, halachah 3: if it split, if the parts became separated and appear like two distant from each other, like a hemnek, which is called a circle with a center, see the Maggid Mishneh there, it is invalid. But if they did not become distant and are truly adjacent, they are valid. See Magen Avraham, letter 7, for at first glance there is a difficulty for the Rema in se'if 3: how can this be found without the tiyoma splitting? Granted, according to the Rambam and Mechaber, it is specifically the majority of leaves, etc. Therefore the Magen Avraham says that it speaks of a split from the side while the upper leaf remains, and the spine split near the upper leaf. However, if it was born from the beginning of its formation with two spines, one can say it is invalid even if they did not become distant from each other, because it is a change. Likewise, if it was born without any upper leaf, or with two leaves and with the tiyoma already divided from birth, one can say it is valid, for only if the tiyoma split or was removed is it invalid, whereas if it was born that way, lacking or split, it is valid. See Yoreh De'ah regarding tereifos; this requires analysis. See Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. See Rashi there, who says 'but it is made like a hemnek' to exclude the questioner's assumption that the baraisa's split case also means that it split afterward like a hemnek from birth. According to the Rambam and Mechaber, when it is like a hemnek, meaning the parts spread apart, even if it split afterward, it is invalid. See letter 9.
