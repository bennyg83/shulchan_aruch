And similarly. See Turei Zahav, Sukkah 32a: charus is invalid, similar to charus is valid, according to Rashi. See letters 1 and 2. It appears to me, the lowly one, that the Turei Zahav has a special intention here, as I will explain. In the Mishnah, 'separated' is valid and 'split apart' is invalid. Some explain simply: separated means somewhat distant from the spine; split apart means hanging downward like leaves of charayos, but not hardened, and one can force and bind them, and nevertheless it is invalid. This is the Rambam's language there, using the comparison 'like the leaves of charayos.' The baraisa says: charus is invalid, similar to charus is valid, meaning anything fit for mixing; and the Mishnah rules that a similar case is invalid. This is what the Rema wrote, 'and all the more so if they were detached.' Although the phrase 'all the more so' would mean like Rashi, that they were uprooted at their attachment, even though they remain attached in a minority and can be bound, they are invalid. The same applies to leaves of charayos: even though he can force them upward with binding, they are invalid, for we do not multiply disputes between them. In the Maggid Mishneh and Ran one can say that Rashi and the Rambam are saying one thing. Understand that the invalidation is because they do not rise with the spine. If they rise but are separated and hardened so that they cannot be forced upward, that too is invalid. For the choicest mitzvah they should not be fully separated, as the Rema says in se'if 1; but one can say that 'similar to charus' means it began to harden, as the Turei Zahav wrote. Understand this.
