3) Ibid. A lulav whose leaves have separated, etc. This means that they are like the branches of a rod, which stand apart but can be bound together. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. At first glance, his meaning is that they are like the branches of a rod, widely separated and far from the spine, yet they rise together with the spine at a distance and do not hang downward. Such a lulav is valid, provided that they can be bound together. If the leaves hardened in this manner, however, it is invalid, as the Rama wrote in seif 2. This is what the Mechaber means by saying, 'even though he did not bind them,' namely that in such a case they must at least be fit to be bound. But where they hang downward like the leaves of palm branches, even if they can be bound, it is an abnormal change, is not beautiful, and is invalid. Eshel Avraham, sec. 1.
