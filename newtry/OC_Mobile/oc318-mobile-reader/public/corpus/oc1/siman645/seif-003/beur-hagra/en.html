Se'if 3: The formation, etc.; if it split, etc. So the Rif and Rambam explain what is meant by 'the tiyomes split,' and that it is in the majority of the leaves, as the Rosh and the gloss wrote. The Yerushalmi there supports them: Rabbi Maloch said in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, if the matched leaves split, it is like the leaves being split apart. This shows that it speaks of all the leaves, and of a majority, like 'split apart.' This is what Baal Halachos Gedolos wrote, brought by the Rosh: Rav Papa asked, if the tiyomes split, that back of the leaf, etc., that matched part, etc., is it like its leaves were split apart, or etc. So too the Rif wrote that it is as if the leaves were split apart, and it is invalid. This is the language of the Yerushalmi. It also appears so from the Gemara, since it placed this after split apart and separated; just as split apart is in the majority, so too split tiyomes, for its invalidation is because of split leaves, as it appears to me. This accords with the Rashba, that all invalidations in leaves are by their majority, for we judge them as if split apart, as written regarding willow: if it was split, etc., but if some of its leaves fell it is valid. The same applies to all four species, for they are all equal in their invalidations, except that each has an added defect that does not apply to the other, such as chazazis on an esrog, and similarly all of them. The Mishnah mentions some leaves falling regarding willow because it is more common there, and likewise withered, which it teaches there; but the baraisa teaches these laws about all four species.
If its leaves were, etc. This is the Rambam's explanation of what is said there: this lulav that rises with one leaf, etc.
Or if all, etc. This is Rashi's explanation of the statement mentioned above.
And some explain, etc. This is Rashi's view, brought in the glosses to Semak, though it is not in the Rashi text before us. This is the language of Semak there: 'the tiyomes split, etc.' Rashi explained: the two upper leaves, where the spine ends. The gloss there wrote: the middle leaves, even though each leaf is whole by itself like the other leaves of the lulav, each of which is doubled into two, etc. Therefore most lulavim found among us are invalid, for most do not have the two middle leaves joined together. That is the explanation before us. He further wrote: the early authorities explained another explanation of 'the tiyomes split,' namely that the middle leaf, which is normally doubled like the other leaves, split into two. According to this, most lulavim found among us are valid, since the middle leaf is whole. However, some have the middle leaf split at its top, and nevertheless they should be validated according to the explanation of the early authorities, who explain that it is only when it split down below the leaves, as explained in Tosafos Rabbi Yehudah. These are his words. This is as Rashi before us wrote on the first explanation, that they split from each other, etc., and below them; see there, s.v. 'split.' Terumas HaDeshen wrote in a responsum: the matter of a split tiyomes has many interpretations; how should we practice? Answer: one should practice according to the other explanation in Rashi, as written in the glosses to Semak, that the middle leaf, etc., split down below the leaves. The Or Zarua added to be lenient, requiring even the spine to split down to the leaves below it. The Or Zarua wrote: I rely on Rashi's explanation for practical halachah. So it is in our Rashi, according to the first explanation, that the spine split until, etc. In the gloss here, the Rema copied only what is written in the glosses to Semak.
However, etc.; because there are, etc. This refers to the Rashba, the Ran, and the Maggid Mishneh, who also explained according to the second language in Rashi. The Rashba asked: but we say it is invalid only when it is made like a hemnek. One can answer that there it split only a minority, namely at its top, while here most of it split. Another answer is that even a minority is invalid, similar to where its top was cut off, and when they said there that if it split it is valid unless it is made like a hemnek, that refers to where it split across its width, where it is valid if only a minority split until it is made like a hemnek. According to this, one should be concerned for a lengthwise split even in a minority, because the split widens through shaking. Men of piety are concerned for this, and so wrote the Ran. As the principal law, however, they hold that it is only when most of it split, as the Ran wrote regarding the second answer; and this is not the plain implication, only that it is proper to be concerned and stringent. Nevertheless he also wrote like the first answer. There is one who explained that 'the tiyomes split' means the middle leaf, the uppermost of all, etc., and it is invalid only when most of the leaf split, not a minority. But the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh hold in practice like the Geonim and the Shulchan Aruch, that it concerns all the leaves, as it appears to me. Therefore the gloss omitted this opinion regarding most of the leaf, because in their view, although they accept Rashi's explanation regarding the majority, they do not agree with his whole approach. According to Semak and Or Zarua, it is invalid only when it split entirely; the Rema mentioned the view of those who are stringent only as a stringency. But as a matter of law, one should be stringent according to the Rashba's explanation, that most is required, for with every invalidation a majority is like the whole. This is obvious according to the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh. Regarding a small minority, one should not be stringent at all, as the Ran wrote that this is not implied; he explains 'split like a hemnek' as across its width, for it is not usual to become like a hemnek across its width, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote. This appears to me.
And if that, etc. So wrote the Kol Bo regarding what is said, 'this lulav that rises, etc.,' as the Rambam explained the tiyomes according to his own view.
