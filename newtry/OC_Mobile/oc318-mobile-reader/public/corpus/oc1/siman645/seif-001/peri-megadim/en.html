And not. See Turei Zahav. The Mechaber used the Rambam's language in chapter 8, halachah 4, of Hilchos Sukkah and Lulav. The term 'leaves of charayos' comes from the baraisa on 32a: a charus is invalid. According to Rashi, this means a palm branch whose leaves fall away and whose spine hardens and becomes wood. The Beis Yosef explained that the leaves do not actually fall; rather, the leaves at their points of attachment to the spine harden and cannot be bound and tied to the spine; see there. The Maggid Mishneh there explains the Rambam that whenever its leaves hang downward like the leaves of charayos, it is invalid. This is the explanation of the baraisa, 'charus is invalid,' meaning that although it has not hardened and one can force the leaves upward, nevertheless, since they hang downward and do not rise at all on their own with the spine, it is invalid even according to the first tanna. But if they rise somewhat on their own with the spine, although their tips hang and bend somewhat downward, it is valid; initially it is a mitzvah to bind them in that case, as I wrote in letter 2, with Hashem's help.
