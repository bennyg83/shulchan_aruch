Meaning. See Turei Zahav and the Maggid Mishneh there, chapter 8 of Hilchos Lulav, halachah 3. In letter 1 I wanted to explain that there is a distinction whether the tips of the leaves are bent downward or not. Now I see that the correct explanation is this: charayos are leaves hanging downward, and at their points of attachment to the spine they hardened and cannot be forced upward, as if they had fallen and become loose, as the Beis Yosef wrote. This is what the Rambam wrote, that they became loose like the leaves of charayos. It implies that if they did not harden like wood, but merely hang downward in this way and do not rise by themselves to the spine, except through tying, they are valid. One can also say that even in such a case they are invalid whenever they do not rise on their own; therefore it is a choicest mitzvah to take a lulav whose leaves do not hang downward, although one can force and bind them, because one can say that such a case is invalid. As a matter of law, however, anything fit for mixing, etc., is valid, and only something like charus, which hardened and cannot be forced upward, is invalid. But whenever the leaves rise on their own with the spine, even though somewhat separated from the spine, there is not even a choicest-mitzvah concern. This is what he asked on the Rema in se'if 1, who wrote that they should not be fully separated, for in that case there is no mitzvah concern; the concern is only when they hang downward like the leaves of charayos, except that they have not hardened and can be forced upward. In that case some say it is invalid, and therefore it is a mitzvah to satisfy all opinions. According to this, what the Rema wrote, 'if the leaves hardened and he cannot join them to the spine,' I do not know what it means, for this is included in charayos. One can say he means that the leaves hardened above, even though they rise along the sides with the spine; it is invalid, because it is not fit for binding, and we require bound. See the Ran. All this requires analysis, and the Maggid Mishneh's explanation also requires study. What the Turei Zahav wrote, 'that is, etc.,' is because it was difficult to him: at first glance the Rambam explains 'became loose' like Rashi, that they were uprooted from their place of attachment in the spine and remain attached in a minority, while the explanation 'hanging downward' is another explanation that disagrees. How then could the Rema write 'that is'? This is not so, for 'became loose' can also mean that they hang downward and became loose along the length of the spine, even though they were not uprooted from their attachment. Understand this, and there is no need to elaborate. See letter 3.
