9) [Seif 2] Its leaves were torn, meaning that they became detached, etc. The Gemara, 32a, explains that this means it was made like a broom. Rashi, in his commentary to the Mishnah on 29b, explains: Its leaves were torn from the spine and are attached only by a binding, like a broom called ashkiva; this is not beautiful. The Rif and Rambam explain that 'its leaves were torn' means that they became detached from the spine of the lulav like the leaves of palm branches, and it is invalid. Tosafos and the Rosh questioned Rashi's explanation, and they explain that a broom means that each leaf was split into two at the place where it is doubled along its back. This is the meaning of 'its leaves were torn' here, that the tip of each leaf was split along its back in the majority of the leaves of the lulav, as all this is written in the Beit Yosef; see there. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch states here without qualification according to the Rambam, while the law of a split leaf is written in the following seif.
