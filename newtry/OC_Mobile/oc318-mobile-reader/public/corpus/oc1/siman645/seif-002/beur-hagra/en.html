Se'if 2: If they were split apart, etc. This is from the Rif and the Rambam.
And all the more so if, etc. This follows Rashi's explanation of the Mishnah.
And similarly if, etc. So the Tur explains what is said there, that a charus is invalid. See Beis Yosef.
And all this is when the majority, etc. The Tosefta, chapter 2, teaches: a lulav made like a charus, or one whose majority of leaves were split apart, is invalid. This also supports the Tur's explanation of charus and the Rif's explanation of 'split apart,' for the Tosefta compares them. This is why the Rif, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch wrote 'like the leaves of charayos,' meaning like a charus.
And the lulav still remains, etc. For otherwise, even if only a minority did so, it is invalid, as written regarding palms of Har HaBarzel: this was taught only, etc. Anything not covered is a mere stick. 'And the lulav still, etc.' means the required measure of the lulav. See the Rashba and Ran, who wrote that likewise with a myrtle, we require its entire required measure to be avos, and if some of its leaves fell it is invalid, for its name is no longer upon it and it is not called avos. The same law applies to a lulav: its name is no longer upon it and it is called a mere stick, as stated in the Gemara, as it appears to me. They wrote that one should not learn these laws from willow.
