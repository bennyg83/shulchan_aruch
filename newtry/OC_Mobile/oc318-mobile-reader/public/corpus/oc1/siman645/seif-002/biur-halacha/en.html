And the lulav still remains covered with leaves. See Biur HaGra, who wrote that this means the required measure of the lulav. See the Rashba and Ran, who wrote that similarly regarding a myrtle, we require its entire required measure to be avos, and if some leaves fell it is invalid, for its name is no longer upon it and it is not called avos. The same is true of a lulav: its name is no longer upon it and it is called a mere stick, as written in the Gemara, as it appears to me. They wrote that one should not learn these matters from willow. These are his words. It appears plainly from the Gra that our case, where the spine remains exposed, is exactly comparable to myrtle. According to this, since below in siman 646, se'if 5, regarding myrtle, we rule that only the majority of the myrtle's length must be avos indispensably, and not all of it, because we derive from willow, as the Gra explained there, then in our case as well the indispensable requirement should be only that the majority of the spine's required length be covered, not all of it. Also, the Ran's statement regarding myrtle, that it is invalid if some of its leaves fell, is only the view of the 'some say' that he brought under the heading 'it is found with an Egyptian myrtle.' But the Ra'ah's view there is not so; see there. The Ran concluded that one should not be lenient that way, showing that he was uncertain. In the Rashba, 32b, s.v. the Rabbis taught, if most of its leaves fell, he also brings the Ra'ah's view and writes that it is primary. Therefore perhaps one should not be stringent in our case either. What the Rema wrote, 'and the lulav still remains covered with leaves,' means the majority of the spine. Afterward I found in Shulchan Aruch HaRav that he also wrote that we require covering for the majority of the spine. It is possible, however, that the Gra's reasoning is that in our matter it simply says that the spine of the lulav is covered with leaves, which implies that all of it must be covered. All this requires analysis.
