Levush: It is a mitzvah, etc. This applies specifically when the leaves hang downward, even if they rise together with the lulav. If they do not hang downward, but merely incline somewhat to the sides, there is not even a preferred manner of performing the mitzvah. Taz. If the spine itself broke and hangs downward, it appears to me that it remains valid. The disqualification applies specifically when most of the leaves are broken, resembling an animal whose majority of ribs were broken, which is treifah, whereas an animal with a broken spine is valid. Nevertheless, it should be tied at that point so that it does not fall downward. Magen Avraham. This requires study, because there the cord remains intact, and the cases are also not comparable. Regarding a dried lulav, Bach writes that even according to the authority who validates one whose leaves dried, if the spine dried it is invalid.
