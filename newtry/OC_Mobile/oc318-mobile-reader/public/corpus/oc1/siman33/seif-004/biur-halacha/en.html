**However bediavad, etc.** — and see Pri Megadim, who brought in the name of Bach that one should be strict if he has other tefillin; and it seems plain that for batim one may certainly rely and be lenient if an Israelite returns and blackens them lishmah; and as Magen Avraham wrote, in any case there are many doubts here; and as Dagul Merevavah wrote; and in Levushei Srad — see there.

**But the retzuot** — the reason Sefer HaTerumah wrote: since the essence of blackening is halachah leMoshe miSinai, for lishmah we also require it, as we require lishmah when spinning tzitzit and dyeing tekhelet. Accordingly it appears that the essence of making the retzua — i.e. cutting it from the hide — also requires lishmah; and I found Emek Halachah wrote thus.

**Even bediavad, etc.** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; and all the more so, etc. See below in siman 39, seif 2, that all these included in making the body of tefillin are included in writing tefillin — see there in Magen Avraham. If so, plain they are no worse than blackening retzuot, which requires lishmah. And I saw in Sefer Magen HaElef that Rambam therefore does not hold the law of Sefer HaTerumah — for he wrote in chapter 3, Laws of Tefillin, 61: tefillin are made only by Israel, etc.; therefore if a gentile covered or sewed them they are invalid, etc. And it cannot be because lishmah is required — derive that it was not lishmah, for Rambam holds that in everything requiring lishmah, a gentile does not help even if an Israelite supervises. And according to what Gra wrote in his chiddushim on siman 39 on Magen Avraham's difficulty like this — see there — his view agreed; and especially since Pri Megadim sides in siman 32, seif katan 32, that writing lishmah is rabbinic — practical difference for several matters; see Pri Megadim's introduction to Laws of Keriat Shema. Yet in practice one should not be lenient against Rama's ruling. And perhaps that scribes are not careful in all this to intend lishmah is because stam is for its sake, since already processed lishmah — but this too is not so: for regarding hanging tzitzit on a garment it states above in siman 14 that if Israel attached tzitzit to a garment without intent, one does not bless on it — even though the threads were already spun for tzitzit — nonetheless we do not say stam is lishmah. So too regarding writing a Torah scroll — even bediavad stam does not help, as proven in siman 32, seif 9 — see there. And what is written in seif 8, "good to remove," etc. — means bediavad thought suffices, but not stam. And perhaps since Baal HaTerumot himself was not clear on this law, as explained in Beit Yosef — whether we learn it from tzitzit or not — if we are strict bediavad regarding gentile making and an Israelite who intended not lishmah, but not stam; and it requires study. And even though it is not clear to us to invalidate bediavad for this, nonetheless lekhatchilah one must do all making of batim, Divine Name, their sewing, making retzuot, and blackening them explicitly lishmah. And know what we wrote regarding blackening retzuot — permitted through a minor when a gentile follows — even though Pri Megadim at the beginning of the introduction to 32 wanted to be strict, indeed in his place here Machatzit Hashekel, Orach Chaim 5, and in 39, Eshel Avraham, Orach Chaim 6, he retracted and permitted; and I found explicitly in Azah Yosef in the responsum of Maharam Lublin, siman 68, that blackening retzuot is not in the category of repair of making. And what he wrote that through a gentile when an Israelite follows it is permitted — though one may doubt this — see above in siman 11 what Dagul Merevavah wondered on Shulchan Aruch, who permits spinning for Rosh through a gentile; if so, here too. Nonetheless in my humble opinion one should not be strict bediavad, after Sefer HaTerumah itself was not decisive in his law so much.

**Invalid** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote: acharonim challenged Magen Avraham's advice; nonetheless it seems to me that if an Israelite blackened the retzuot the first time and did not intend lishmah, only stam — in this one may rely on Magen Avraham that they blacken a second time explicitly lishmah; and ink is like two [layers]. And apparently I would have advised otherwise that would help for all — even if a gentile blackened the first time: their entire difficulty on Magen Avraham is that it would be ink on ink; now paint it first with black appearance in red lead or green and white, etc., and this appearance comes and nullifies the black, as we say in Gitin 19 — red lead on ink erases it; then paint from above with black appearance lishmah — ink on red lead, all require it. And even though we say there "because we compare," etc. — here is different, for it is again like two layers, since Baal HaTerumah himself was not decisive, as stated. Yet it requires study: perhaps he wrote specifically ink on red lead writing — the upper nullifies the lower, for ink writing is superior for all, not so black dye on red lead dye and the like — perhaps the lower dye is superior; one who does so on Shabbat is not liable for dyeing. In our case too the upper black dye is not considered to fully nullify the dye that was underneath from the start, that only the name black remain on it — as we say there, red lead on ink — see there in Gemara Gitin 19; and it requires study.
