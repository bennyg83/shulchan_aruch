It is proper to blacken them. This implies that he holds they remain valid after the fact even if they were not blackened for the sake of the mitzvah. If so, Rama in his gloss disagrees with him and should have introduced the ruling with “some say.” Nevertheless, we find several similar cases, as Sema writes.
The leather of the bayis. For even if it was not blackened at all, it is valid. Tur, siman 32; Darkei Moshe.
Invalid. It appears to me that if a Jew subsequently blackened them for the sake of the mitzvah, they are valid. Gittin 20a states that if one wrote the Divine Name without sanctifying it, he may trace over it with a pen and thereby sanctify it. Even the Sages disagree only because it then appears mottled. Were it not for that concern, they would permit it. Here, therefore, everyone would agree that tracing over the blackening is effective. See Even HaEzer, siman 131.
