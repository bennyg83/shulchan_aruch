(22) Good, etc. — but b'dieved it is valid per him even through a gentile, for he holds that even for retzuot we do not require blackening at all leshman; and Rama in his hagahah disputes and holds that for retzuot, where blackening is halachah leMoshe miSinai, blackening is required leshman; and a fortiori a gentile, who does not do leshmah, is invalid — and its law is as above in siman 32 regarding parchment, see there. But for the hide of the batim, most poskim hold its blackening is for the mitzvah in general and not me'akev, as above in siman 32 s"m; therefore we do not require blackening leshman b'dieved either. Rama should have written his hagahah in the language of "some say," but we find this formulation several times.

(23) But the retzuot, etc. — if a gentile made them, or even an Israelite but not leshman; and the reason is as above in s.k. 22. And all the more so for anything required in the tefillin themselves me'akev — such as making the bayit and its shin in all its details that it be according to law, or its sewing, or making its knot — all these are halachah leMoshe miSinai; if an Israelite made them not leshman they are invalid. Pri Megadim wrote one should prevent doing any of this through a minor, even if an adult stands over him and commands him to do leshmah; and likewise not through a woman; and all the disqualifications explained below in siman 39, see there in seifim 1 and 2. Blackening the retzuot — since this is not in the body of the tefillin — is permitted through a woman, for she knows to do leshmah like a man; but through a gentile and minor it is not permitted until another stands over them, as above regarding parchment in siman 32, see there s.t.

(24) Invalid — Magen Avraham wrote that if an Israelite returned and blackened them leshman they are valid, and brought proof; Pri Megadim and other acharonim challenged his proof and remained with this law in safek. Pri Megadim gave another advice: blacken the other side of the retzuot leshman — for even if blackened on both sides it is valid, as explained in s.g. — and turn the side blackened leshman upward; and all this if he has no other retzuot, as we explained in Biur Halacha.
