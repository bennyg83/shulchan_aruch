(22) It is proper, etc. According to the Mechaber they are valid after the fact even if a non-Jew blackened them, because he maintains that even the blackening of the straps need not be performed for the sake of the mitzvah. Rama disagrees. Since the blackness of the straps is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, it must be done for the sake of the mitzvah. A non-Jew, who cannot act for that purpose, invalidates them, as with parchment above in siman 32. Regarding the leather of the batim, however, most poskim hold that blackness is merely for preferred fulfillment and not indispensable. Therefore, after the fact their blackening need not be for the sake of the mitzvah. Rama should formally have introduced his gloss as “some say,” but similar formulations occur elsewhere.
(23) But the straps, etc. If a non-Jew made them, or even a Jew made them without intent for the sake of the mitzvah, they are invalid, for the reason just stated. All the more so, every indispensable component of the tefillin themselves, such as forming the bayis and its shin in all their details, stitching it, and making its knot, must be done properly and for the sake of the mitzvah, since all these are halachos transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. Peri Megadim writes that one should not have these acts performed by a minor even while an adult supervises, nor by a woman or any of those disqualified below in siman 39. Blackening the straps, which is not part of the physical body of the tefillin, may be performed by a woman, who can act for the sake of the mitzvah like a man. A non-Jew or minor may do it only while another person supervises and directs them, as with parchment above in siman 32.
(24) Invalid. Magen Avraham writes that if a Jew subsequently blackened them for the sake of the mitzvah, they are valid. Peri Megadim and other later authorities reject his proof and leave the law unresolved. Peri Megadim therefore proposes another remedy: blacken the reverse side for the sake of the mitzvah, turn that side outward, and use it as the exterior. Although the straps are then black on both sides, they are valid, as stated in seif 3. This applies only where no other straps are available, as explained in Biur Halachah.
