(25) Some permit. Even within the required length encircling the head or upper arm.
(26) To sew. Specifically sewing is permitted according to this view. Tying within the required length is invalid according to all opinions by Torah law, for the verse says, “You shall bind them,” which the Sages interpret as requiring a complete binding: the strap used for binding must be whole and not itself tied. Sewing must likewise be done specifically with sinews, not ordinary thread. Responsa Devar Shmuel writes that if the ma'abarta tore, it may be sewn.
(27) On the inner side. So that it is entirely unnoticeable from the outside. All the more so if the repaired place is hidden within the ma'abarta, it is permitted according to this view.
(28) The finger. Meaning, the strap should be stretched straight without windings. This is the required length of the arm-tefillin strap, as explained above in siman 27. See Darkei Moshe for why greater leniency is shown regarding the head tefillin.
(29) The lenient authorities. They permit sewing with sinews even within the section encircling the head or upper arm. Beyond that section, even tying is permitted, and certainly sewing with ordinary thread. Nevertheless, he may not recite a blessing over such tefillin, because the primary halachah follows the stringent opinion and a doubt concerning a Torah law is treated stringently. To avoid entirely neglecting the mitzvah, he should put them on until he obtains others. Taz rules that even for the head tefillin one may not recite a blessing unless the right strap remains whole to the navel and the left to the chest, without knot or stitching. Eliyah Rabbah, however, concludes that for both head and arm tefillin, the section beyond that which encircles the head or upper arm may be sewn even outside pressing circumstances, and one may recite a blessing, provided it is sewn with sinews on the inner side so that the repair is not visible. Ordinary thread or tying is forbidden even on the head tefillin. Yeshuos Yaakov and Derech HaChayim rule likewise. Still, where a complete strap is available it is certainly proper initially to follow Taz, for many later authorities cite his view. In pressing circumstances one may rely upon the lenient authorities and recite a blessing. This is especially so where, beyond the section encircling the head, two handbreadths remain hanging, for there is strong basis to permit sewing. See above, siman 27. If the strap tore across its width and less than a barleycorn's width remains, Peri Megadim leaves unresolved whether it may be sewn. See above, siman 27.
