If it was severed, etc., and for the tefillin of the arm enough to encircle, etc. Machatzis HaShekel writes that after sewing, one must first stretch the repaired strap straight from the upper arm to the finger, and only afterward return with the strap from below and wind it around the arm according to custom. In his view, the length reaching the finger must be free of any knot or repair. In Shenos Chayim, Rav Shlomo Kluger writes that the world does not follow this practice and is correct not to do so; see there. Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, later authorities, siman 4, likewise disputes Machatzis HaShekel. The main concern is only that a sufficient total length remain for stretching and winding. Although after winding around the arm the repaired point remains above the finger, we are not concerned. He is nevertheless uncertain whether the repair itself may lie directly upon the finger, since binding applies there as well. See his lengthy discussion. In my humble opinion, initially it is good to sew that place with sinews and on the inner side so that it is not noticeable. Even if we take Machatzis HaShekel's stringency into account and regard that place as within the required length, it appears clear that we may rely here upon Rabbeinu Tam and the other early authorities who permit sewing. Know that when some people reconnect the strap to itself without actual stitching, this is no better than tying and may perhaps be worse.
The sewing and tying do not help, etc. Nevertheless, there is a practical difference between the tefillin of the arm and those of the head. Regarding the tefillin of the head, if the strap remains severed and he does not reconnect it by tying or sewing, he certainly may not recite a blessing over the tefillin. We cannot validate it on the basis of a remaining remnant, meaning the second strap, as we do for tzitzis above in siman 12, because Menachos 35b concludes that remnants of tefillin straps are invalid. Nevertheless, he is obligated to put on the tefillin if the strap was lost and he has no alternative, for perhaps the halachah follows those who maintain that the phrase “and how much is their measure?” refers only to the tefillin of the arm. See Beit Yosef above in siman 27. In truth, he has another remedy. He may untie the knot and draw the remaining strap equally to both sides. In pressing circumstances he may fulfill the mitzvah if two handbreadths remain on each side. See siman 27 and Mishnah Berurah there. The practical difference would therefore be where both straps of the head tefillin were severed on both sides and only the portion encircling the head remains. Regarding the tefillin of the arm, even if he does not reconnect the strap at all, he may still recite the blessing over the tefillin, as explained above in siman 27, seif 8. Accordingly, sewing with ordinary thread and on the outer side, or even tying, certainly helps. All this is where he does not make the seven windings. According to our custom to make those windings, we have already written that it is preferable to sew the strap with sinews and on the inner side so as to satisfy Machatzis HaShekel's concern.
