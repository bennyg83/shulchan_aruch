(1) Became damaged. Meaning, it split and a tear developed.
(2) Of the compartments of the head tefillin. If a tear developed in the bayis of the arm tefillin, it is invalid according to all opinions in every case, even if the titura remains intact. It is unlike the head tefillin, where at least some complete compartments remain.
(3) Adjacent to one another. Meaning, the compartments are next to one another. This applies even if the tears themselves are not adjacent, such as where one tear is in the right wall of the second compartment and the other is in either wall of the third compartment. It also applies if one tear is above and the other below, and all the more so if they are in the same space opposite one another.
(4) Old tefillin are invalid. For they have deteriorated through age. The dissenting opinion maintains the reverse: with new tefillin, the fact that they tore after a short time reveals that the leather was defective. Even where these reasons do not apply, such as where the tear was caused by a knife or the like, one should be stringent. Nevertheless, Pischei Teshuvah tends to permit gluing where we know the leather is not defective, even according to those who require the tefillin to be made from one piece of leather.
(5) New tefillin are valid. Even if the tears are in two compartments and in two of their walls.
(6) Remains intact. Meaning, the titura. Beit Yosef, however, explains that the tefillin must remain whole on every outer side, and only a tear on the inside between one compartment and another is tolerated. This is also Rama's view. His statement that the batim must remain intact except that they tore somewhat means that the tear is between the compartments, while the exterior sides remain whole. Even where only one compartment tore on the outer side, one should be stringent.
(7) To invalidate both. Accordingly, one must invalidate the practice of some scribes who, when forming the four compartments and placing them in a mold, cut the leather between one compartment and another so that it not wrinkle and protrude, and then cover the cut from the inside so that from the outside it appears whole.
(8) The bayis spreads. This applies to those who make the ma'abarta from the same leather as the batim. Under our practice, where the ma'abarta is a separate piece of leather, this issue does not arise.
(9) The first and third. This is considered nonadjacent. Even if the third compartment tore on two of its walls, it is valid because one complete compartment separates the tears. If, however, the second and third compartments tore, then even where one tear is on the right side of one compartment and the other on the left side of the other, they are considered adjacent because the two compartments themselves are next to one another.
(10) Three compartments. Even if only one wall of each compartment tore.
(11) In every case. This includes even new tefillin. Although above two adjacent tears were permitted in new tefillin according to one view, three torn compartments are invalid.
