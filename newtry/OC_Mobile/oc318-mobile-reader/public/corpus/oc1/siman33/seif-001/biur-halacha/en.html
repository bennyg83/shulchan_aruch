**This next to this** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; and all the more so if both are in one "air," etc. See Beit Yosef; and it requires study in what he brought there — that Nekudot Yosef also holds like the first wording of Rashi, that one looks inside, at s.v. "shtayim" and s.v. "aval." But it appears explicitly that Nekudot Yosef holds it depends only on "airs," and according to him, if the tear is in one air — even on two partitions — it is valid; and vice versa, if they were in two airs — e.g. the tear was in the first bayit on his left and in the third bayit on his right — it is invalid, for this is called according to him "this opposite that"; and all the more so if the third bayit was torn on both its partitions — this is called according to him three airs — which Rashi does not hold for any of this, neither in the first wording nor the latter. And perhaps Beit Yosef brings only proof for what Rashi holds in his first wording — that if a tear was in the first bayit on his left and in the second bayit also on his left, it is invalid — Nekudot Yosef also holds here like him, for behold there are two airs, and not like the latter wording; and it requires study.

**First and third** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; and even if the tear was on both its partitions — so wrote Pri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, in Magen Avraham's view that he holds thus, and he also proved from Shulchan Aruch, since at the end he states "if three batim were damaged" — implying batim, but not three partitions — see there; and we do not rule like Rashi's latter wording, who is strict here; also from the Gra in his commentary it appears that Shulchan Aruch's view is only Rashi's first wording, and he wrote that so is the view of all poskim; and so I saw in other acharonim — therefore I concluded thus, and not like Machatzit Hashekel, who sides to be strict here.
