[A] It's at each other's, etc. The interpretation of even the tear of one stanza below next to the Titorah and of your Idach above is even the most incorrect:
[2] The batim must also remain intact, etc. Meaning, they tore somewhat between one compartment and another. The exterior sides, however, must remain completely intact. Beit Yosef likewise writes that the batim must remain whole on all their other sides, for we validate only a tear between one compartment and another. Lechem Chamudos, Bach, and the later authorities rule this way.
[3] For example, the first and third, etc. This implies that if two adjacent compartments tore, they are invalid even when the tears themselves are not adjacent, but one tear is on one side and the other on a different side.
[4] They are valid, etc. Levush writes, “They are valid according to all opinions.” The phrase “according to all opinions” is not appropriate, because no dispute was mentioned. He should have written, “They are valid in every case,” meaning whether they are old or new. It is more likely that a scribal omission occurred: the two words “in every case” were omitted, while “according to all opinions” also belongs there, meaning that here everyone agrees there is no distinction between new and old. Malbushei Yom Tov writes similarly. It remains uncertain whether the base of the batim must likewise remain intact. The flow of the sugya somewhat implies that it must, for Rava's statement follows Rav Chisda, and Rav Chisda follows Rav Huna, who said: so long as the face of the tablet remains intact.
