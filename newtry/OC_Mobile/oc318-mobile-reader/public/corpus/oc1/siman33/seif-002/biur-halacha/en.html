**For Rambam, etc.** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote; but Rashba, etc. See above at the end of siman 32 in Beit Yosef there, s.v. "ve'im nifsakah hatefira," etc., at the end of his words, where he wrote: regarding practical halachah where it is impossible, one may rely on the lenient view so as not to nullify the mitzvah of tefillin. From this it appears that his view is that all those poskim he brought initially — Rosh, Sefer HaTerumah, and Semag — all are lenient and disagree with Rambam, and hold that if two stitches were severed it is permitted in every case [and in truth I did not know — perhaps they too are strict, and what they cited "three" is because with three, even repairing the stitch in that place does not help and one must sew anew. Later I found in Maharshal's commentary on Semag that he indeed wrote this is their view; and it is possible Beit Yosef's view is that if so, they hold like Rambam — they should have mentioned some legal distinction between new and old and between "this opposite that" and "not opposite." Moreover, after Rosh, Aruch, and their supporters interpret the statement of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda in Menachot not regarding stitches — whence do they derive being strict about two stitches?] And so Avodat HaKodesh understood Beit Yosef's view here; and in truth sometimes Rambam is more lenient than they — e.g. if three stitches were severed in new [tefillin], Rambam is lenient even without any repair at all, whereas according to them re-stitching is required, in new and old alike. And it is plain that all this is Beit Yosef's rule there, in what he wrote that one may rely where impossible on the lenient view. And what Rama wrote afterward on this — "and some say that in new ones they are invalid," etc., and Rashi, Tur, and Rosh are cited on this — it is not that they hold thus, for according to them there is no distinction at all in stitching between new and old; and as stated, the Gemara does not discuss stitching — only that according to their view above, who have in the Gemara a reading opposite Rambam's words regarding new and old, it would follow according to his interpretation, who explains the statement of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda regarding stitches, that the law is reversed from what he wrote; and I found this in acharonim — and it is plain.

**To be concerned** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote: one may rely, etc. — this is from Avodat HaKodesh in the name of the aforementioned Beit Yosef, which they copied into halachah; and it is plain that for three stitches too one may rely in pressing need, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, in new and old alike — since in any case one would not bless on them, why not fulfill the mitzvah of tefillin out of doubt per the view that validates them? Later I found in Darkhei Chaim that he too seems lenient in every case in pressing need; but he wrote one should not bless on them. And it seems to me that for two stitches one may rely in pressing need — many poskim disagree with Rambam and are lenient here; and accordingly I wrote my words inside. However, regarding the essence of the law of severed stitches, in my humble opinion there is much to consider: apparently, according to what we rule, stitching with gidin is halachah leMoshe miSinai; and from Rambam it appears the square of the stitching is also halachah leMoshe miSinai; and some are even stricter about this than the square of the batim — see above in siman 32, seif 40, in the Gra's commentary there. If so, according to what Shulchan Aruch ruled above in siman 32 — if one made them square and afterward their squareness was ruined, they require repair and are invalid if not repaired, as implied there in Sha'ar HaTziyun on Taz — why is Rambam lenient here when three stitches were severed in new ones? For automatically the squareness was ruined, for according to most there are stitches on every side — unless we say we discuss specifically where the three stitches were not on one side, and he holds this is still called square in its stitching. But if the squareness of the stitching was ruined by severing the gidin — e.g. a corner of the stitching was severed — I could tell you that even by fewer than three it becomes invalid. Yet if so, Rambam and the poskim should have distinguished here; and since they mentioned no hint of distinction in all this, it implies valid in every case — thus it appears they do not hold the above, that if one made them square and afterward their squareness was ruined. And perhaps one could say this is also Rambam's intent in what he wrote, "the faces of the tablet exist" — a tablet is a square tablet, as Aruch explained, brought by Rosh in Hilchot Ketanot; and the intent would be that the square of the stitching exists, as we wrote initially. And apparently this interpretation fits better than what they explained — that the intent is the titura exists; for what relevance has the titura's existence to ruining the stitching? And it requires study in all this.
