According to Rambam, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote, “But the other poskim, etc.” See above, at the end of siman 32, in Beit Yosef, s.v. “And if the stitching was severed, etc.” At the end of his words he writes: “As a matter of practical halachah, where no alternative is available, one may rely upon the lenient opinion so as not to be deprived of the mitzvah of tefillin.” This implies that in his view all the poskim he cited initially, namely Rosh, Sefer HaTerumah, and Semag, are lenient and disagree with Rambam, maintaining that where two stitches were severed the tefillin are valid in every case. In truth, I did not initially understand this. Perhaps they too are stringent, and they mentioned three stitches because when three were severed, even repairing the stitching in that place does not help, and the section must be sewn anew. I later found in Maharshal's commentary to Semag that he indeed writes that their view is lenient. It is possible that Beit Yosef reasoned that if they agreed with Rambam they should have mentioned some practical distinction between new and old tefillin, and between corresponding and noncorresponding tears. Furthermore, since Rosh, Aruch, and those who follow them explain the statements of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda in Menachos as not referring to stitching at all, from where would they derive stringency regarding two stitches? Eliyah Rabbah likewise understands Beit Yosef this way. At times Rambam is actually more lenient than they are. If three stitches were severed in new tefillin, Rambam validates them even without repair, whereas according to them they require restitching, whether new or old. It is clear that all of this is included in Beit Yosef's statement that where no alternative is available one may rely upon the lenient view. Rama then writes: “Some say that with new tefillin they are invalid, etc.,” and the references cite Rashi, Tur, and Rosh. This does not mean that they themselves hold this view, for according to them there is no distinction in stitching between new and old tefillin, as explained above, since the Gemara's discussion did not concern stitching. Rather, Rama means that according to their earlier reading of the Gemara, which reverses Rambam's distinction between new and old, if one nevertheless interprets the statements of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda as referring to stitching, the practical ruling would be the reverse of Rambam's. I found this explanation in the later authorities, and it is straightforward.
To be concerned. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that one may rely upon the lenient view, etc. This follows Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Beit Yosef, cited above, who records it as practical halachah. It is clear that even where three stitches were severed one may rely upon the lenient view in pressing circumstances, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah, whether the tefillin are new or old. Since in any event no blessing will be recited over them, why should he entirely forgo the mitzvah of tefillin when there is a valid opinion that permits them? I later found that Derech HaChayim likewise appears lenient in every case in pressing circumstances, although he writes that no blessing should be recited. It appears to me that where only two stitches were severed, one may rely in pressing circumstances upon the majority of poskim who disagree with Rambam and are lenient. On this basis I wrote the ruling inside. However, the basic law concerning severed stitching requires great study. We rule that stitching with sinews is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai. Rambam also implies that the squareness of the stitching is a halachah transmitted to Moshe at Sinai, and some are even more stringent concerning this than concerning the squareness of the batim. See above, siman 32, seif 40, and Biur HaGra there. Accordingly, since Shulchan Aruch rules above in siman 32 that if the tefillin were originally square and their squareness later became damaged they must be repaired and remain invalid until repaired, as implied there in seif 39 and Taz, why does Rambam validate new tefillin when three stitches were severed? Ordinarily, severing stitches also damages the squareness, since there are generally stitches on every side. Perhaps the case is specifically where the three severed stitches were not all on one side, and Rambam maintains that the stitching is still considered square. If, however, the squareness of the stitching itself was damaged through severed sinews, such as where a corner of the stitching broke, perhaps even fewer than three severed stitches would invalidate it. Yet if so, Rambam and the poskim should have distinguished this explicitly. Since they give no indication of such a distinction, it appears that they validate it in every case. This would imply that they do not accept the ruling above that tefillin whose squareness became damaged after being made square are invalid. Perhaps this is also Rambam's intent when he writes, “So long as the face of the tablet remains intact.” The “tablet” means a square tablet, as Aruch explains and Rosh cites in Hilchos Tefillin Ketanos. The meaning would then be: so long as the squareness of the stitching remains intact, as we first suggested. This explanation appears more precise than the interpretation that the phrase means only that the titura remains intact. What connection does the existence of the titura have to damage in the stitching? The entire matter requires study.
