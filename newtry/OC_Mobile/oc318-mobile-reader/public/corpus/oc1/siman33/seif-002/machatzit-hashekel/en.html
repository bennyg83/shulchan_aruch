(Seif katan 2) That tore somewhat, meaning, etc. The sides must remain whole, and likewise the upper portion must remain whole. Beit Yosef cites Mordechai as follows: “All the more so, one must invalidate the practice of some tefillin writers who, when forming the four compartments and placing them in a mold, cut the leather between one compartment and another so that it not become wrinkled and protruding, and then cover the cut from the inside so that from the outside it appears whole.” Thus far. Although the reasons written by Taz in seif katan 1 do not apply there, see there, I do not know upon what basis scribes rely when, by stretching the grooves between one compartment and another in the mold by means of a thread, the sides of the bayis are drawn lower than the center. They then cut the lower portions of the sides of the bayis until they are even, leaving the compartments connected together only in the middle. I do not understand the distinction between cutting the upper sides of the bayis, which Mordechai invalidates, and cutting below at the bottom of the bayis. One cannot defend the practice by saying that only the sides of the bayis are critical, because without complete walls it is not called a bayis, whereas at the bottom, even if it were entirely open it would still be called a bayis, except that it must be made from one piece of leather. Since the compartments remain connected in the middle even below, and are cut only on both sides, it would still be one piece of leather. This defense is not satisfactory.
