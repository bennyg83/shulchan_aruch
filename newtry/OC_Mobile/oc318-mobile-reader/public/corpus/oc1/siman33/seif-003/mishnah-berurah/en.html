(16) From kosher animals. Only material from a kosher species is fit for sacred use. Gemara.
(17) And it must. Every requirement stated here is indispensable even after the fact.
(18) For its sake. Even Rambam, who is lenient regarding the leather of the batim as explained above in siman 32, agrees regarding the straps. See Levush and Magen Avraham for the reason. The blackening must likewise be performed for the sake of the mitzvah. If the leather was not processed for its sake, later blackening it for the sake of the mitzvah does not help. Responsa Devar Shmuel rules this way, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger agrees and strongly protests those who are lenient. Be'er Eisek, however, maintains that blackening for the sake of the mitzvah does help.
(19) Black. Baruch She'amar implies that it is a mitzvah to blacken them until they are black like a raven. If age or wear causes the blackness to fade, they must be blackened again, as above in siman 32. The blackness commonly wears away at the place where the knot is tightened, and one must be especially careful about this.
(20) On the outside. Meaning, on the hair side, which is the smooth side. If they were blackened on the inside, that does not help, and they must be blackened again on the outside. See Biur Halachah.
(21) He may make. Meaning, he may color the inner side if he wishes, but he is not obligated to color it at all. Although Rambam requires the inner side to be black like the batim, we do not follow Rambam in this matter, as explained by Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe.
