Seif 3: The leather must be, etc. This is Rambam's view. See Magen Avraham, whose words are difficult to understand. Rambam writes in chapter 3: “The leather of the straps requires processing for the sake of the mitzvah. The leather with which the batim are covered, however, does not require processing at all; even if made from matzah leather it is valid.” His reason is from Shabbos 78b and 79a, where leather sufficient to make an amulet is discussed, and from the statement there concerning covering, as the Mishnah says “leather.” This shows that amulet leather is unprocessed leather, even matzah leather, as the Gemara explains there regarding boiling. Eruvin 96b states, “Would a person trouble himself, etc.?” This shows that the leather of tefillin is comparable to the leather of an amulet. Thus the sugya follows the first Tanna in Menachos 42b. Although with tzitzis we rule like Shmuel, because the sugya in the first chapter of Sukkah follows him, as explained above in siman 11, the reason here is that since no processing is required, certainly processing for the sake of the mitzvah is unnecessary. This differs from spinning tzitzis, where spinning itself is required, as explained in seif 4 in the name of Sefer HaTerumah concerning blackening. The straps are different because it is a received tradition that they must be black, and they are processed leather. This is Magen Avraham's intent when he writes, “It appears to me, etc.” One might have explained similarly the ruling in Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 301, seif 42, that straps are recognizable as not being amulets; see there. Rambam, however, follows Rif and Rosh there, who require a recognizable knot as well. Nimukei Yosef at the end of chapter 6 of Sanhedrin maintains the opposite; see there. As explained at the end of siman 42, he follows Tosafos in Menachos 42b that Rava rules like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Yet processing is effective because it is performed upon the object itself, as explained at the end of siman 42 in Rama's gloss, that in such a case designation certainly has halachic force. This raises a difficulty: why should weaving a garment for a deceased person not likewise be comparable to processing? Baal HaMaor answers in the first chapter of Sukkah that weaving is different because the woven garment is merely an accessory to the primary object. He nevertheless writes that the strap is itself an object of sanctity. Rambam's words initially require study. He writes that if a non-Jew formed or sewed the batim they are invalid, because their making is like their writing due to the shin. He follows his own view that processing is not required for every sacred writing. He does not require processing for a mezuzah, but only for a Sefer Torah and tefillin, and according to his explanation he does not require processing even for other sacred books. Tosafos in Menachos 42b, s.v. “Tefillin,” explain Rambam this way; see there, where they raise a difficulty and answer it.
