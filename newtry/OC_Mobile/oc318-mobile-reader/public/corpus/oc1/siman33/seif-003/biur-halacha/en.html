**The hide of the retzuot, etc., that are pure** — see Mishnah Berurah; and Pri Megadim wrote in his introduction: I do not know if this is from the Torah or rabbinic; and there is practical difference to be lenient in their doubt — if one does not know whether it is pure or impure; also regarding processing lishmah, if there is doubt it seems one should be lenient, for it is rabbinic and its doubt is lenient. See there further regarding covering the hand tefillin hide; and see Rambam, chapter 3 of Laws of Tefillin, halachah 15, who included covering and retzua in one category; and see above in siman 32 in Taz, seif katan 27, and in Pri Megadim there.

**And it must be processed for its sake** — see Mishnah Berurah in what he wrote: if one did not process them lishmah, blackening them afterward lishmah does not help — not like Be'er Eshek. And I saw in Sefer Damasek Eliezer, seif katan 11, that he meant the Gra holds like Be'er Eshek — see there. But in truth it seems plain this is not so; rather the Gra's intent is: since we see the Torah was particular that the hide be fully prepared — even in blackening, all the more so in the essential repair of the hide, i.e. processing that must be in it. And this is not a halachic a fortiori — it is only revealing a matter; and this is very precise in the Gra's language afterward. And this is what Magen Avraham wrote "and it seems to me," etc. — his intent is to resolve what Pri Megadim challenged on Magen Avraham — see there; for according to Damasek Eliezer, Magen Avraham's language would be very difficult, where he wrote processing and blackening, that blackening alone suffices. And the reasoning I wrote I learned from Sefer HaTerumah brought in Beit Yosef regarding blackening lishmah, where he wrote blackening is like other inferior hide repairs after processing — see there. And know further, in my humble opinion, that according to Rambam, processing lishmah is only rabbinic; even if one also holds like Sefer HaTerumah that blackening requires lishmah, nonetheless it is only rabbinic — for Sefer HaTerumah's reason is: since the essence of blackening is halachah leMoshe miSinai, therefore it requires lishmah, as explained in Beit Yosef. If so, processing too — is not the essence of processing halachah leMoshe miSinai, as Pri Megadim explained in Eshel Avraham at the beginning of siman 32? Yet processing lishmah is only rabbinic, as Pri Megadim extended there in siman 32 — so in my humble opinion.

**Retzuot from hide, etc., are valid** — and klaf processed for a Torah scroll — it requires study whether one may make a retzua from it [Pri Megadim in the introduction to siman 32, see there].

**Halachah leMoshe miSinai, etc.** — and for invalidation it applies even bediavad — Rambam, chapter 3, halachah 11; and he needed to explain this, for there is a halachah that is only a mitzvah in general, as in Shabbat 79b, see there.

**The retzuot are black** — whether of the head or hand tefillin — Rambam there; and I am uncertain whether from the Torah it suffices in each [retzua] up to its required measure and the rest is for mitzvah and adornment in general, or perhaps since it is connected as one whole it must all be black. Some evidence for this from the Gemara's difficulty in Menachot 35a — "nonetheless, between green," etc. — that it does not distinguish in permitting regarding the measure, and it would not have asked further whether from inside, etc. And one can reject: the Gemara holds the baraita would not permit even rabbinically to dye two shades on one side because it appears speckled — see Magen Avraham, seif katan 7; and it requires study. Also I do not know whether the halachah leMoshe miSinai applies also to the piece of retzua entering the ma'avarta; and lekhatchilah I am not uncertain that one must blacken — perhaps the bayit turns over from that place — see Gemara there; but bediavad perhaps it is like what is inside; and see Rambam, chapter 3, Laws of Tefillin, 14. Regarding the measure of blackening, see Baruch She'amar, who wrote they blacken once, and after it dries somewhat they blacken a second time, and a third, until the hide is black for every need, literally like a raven; and it seems this is only for mitzvah in general, but by law whatever has the name black applied to it suffices. Accordingly, even if its appearance resembles blue [so called in foreign tongue] it is valid; proof from Niddah 19a regarding blood appearances — if black as ink it is impure; if paler than that, even if black like blue it is pure, as Rashi explained there — thus blue appearance is included in black. So too in Chullin 47a regarding lung — if its blackness resembles blue; and as stated in Rambam, chapter 7 of Laws of Shechitah, halachah 19 — black like blue — thus blue appearance too is included in black. Nonetheless lekhatchilah it is surely a mitzvah to blacken so it is truly black, as Beit Shmuel wrote. See what he wrote above in siman 32, seif 3, regarding ink blackness, where they require true black — see there. And know that what we wrote there removes one difficulty I saw a certain gaon raised on Rambam: why was the halachah not stated simply that they write with black matter? According to our words it is satisfactory — "black" simply would mean even if only as black as blue; examine this.

**From outside** — see Mishnah Berurah: so is proven from Rashi 35b, s.v. "venoyeihen" — if not so, one need only dye the place one wishes to wear on the outside side; and this is the Gemara's intent in what it answers here "from outside" versus what is explicit to Rashi — he dyes that smooth place so it may be called "for this and its adornment." Rashi could have explained simply that place which is smooth — adornment he wears above, and automatically one must dye that place black because of the halachah leMoshe miSinai; and so in Tur, as stated. And I saw in Pri Megadim that if a gentile blackened the retzua and he has no other retzua, he may blacken the other side — for although tanned black, it is not specifically from the hair side; his intent: if he turns the retzua and wears the black to the outside side; and Darkhei Chaim explained his words. Accordingly, what the Gemara answers there on 35a — "here from outside" — depends only on the place worn on the outside; and what they said in the Gemara "and their adornment outside, black" — the smooth part is only a preferred mitzvah; and he brought proof from this Rashi, s.v. "venoyeihen." But in truth it seems the opposite; and even if you reject our words, in any case there is no proof from Rashi for his words; but from Rambam, Laws of Tefillin, 14, it seems somewhat like his words. Nonetheless lekhatchilah he certainly agrees, as he wrote himself. And know: according to Pri Megadim, sometimes a great stringency arises — if the retzua is placed at the knot-wrap with the black side below, one does not fulfill the mitzvah and it is a blessing in vain. And it appears this matter is in dispute — see above in siman 27 in Avodat HaKodesh, who wrote so in the name of Gilyon Mordechai; and in my humble opinion Beit Yosef disagrees and holds he violated only "and their adornment outside"; therefore even if turned afterward it is forbidden — see there in Avodat HaKodesh; examine this.
