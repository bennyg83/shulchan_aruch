Magen Avraham, seif katan 3: "and one bayit remains" — he means thereby that one should not err to explain that the matter depends on partitions, that we require two partitions torn one next to the other, and not as the Taz wrote in seif katan 3; but automatically what the author wrote here, "first and third," refers also to partitions, and even though a whole bayit does not intervene between them it is valid; therefore Magen Avraham wrote that the whole matter depends on batim, and like the Taz that whenever two consecutive batim are torn it is invalid even though the partitions are not adjacent, and we require a whole bayit between them.
