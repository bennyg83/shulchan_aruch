First — Taz. Here the examiner in Beit Yosef is thus: in the Gemara Menachot 35a there are variant readings — for Rashi and Rosh old is preferable, and for Rif and Rambam new is preferable. And indeed in explaining the sugya, Rashi, Rosh, and Rif agree that it discusses torn hide of batim; Rambam ch. 3 of Hilchot Tefillin interprets it regarding stitches (as the author wrote in seif 2), and the author ruled the version like Rif and Rambam against Rosh (as his rule), and in his exposition like Rif and Rosh. In this exposition, "this opposite this" versus "not opposite this" — there are two interpretations. Interpretation one in Rashi: two stringencies — (1) whenever two batim next to each other are torn they are invalid, and only the first and third are validated (and the wording "this opposite this" means in the air of one); and a second stringency that three tears even in all cases are invalid, whether new or old — for otherwise only one tear for Rashi and Rosh, and old for Rif and Rambam — why mention three; necessarily two batim next to each other are invalid (as Bach's difficulty, see there). In the second interpretation, two leniencies — (1) whenever there are not two tears in one air it is valid; (2) one could say in old ones three are valid (for Rashi and Rif in reverse) and in new ones he speaks — and Bach's questions are resolved. But the second leniency is unnecessary, for in any case it is proven in the Gemara: "new too we did not say" — the word "too" shows Rav Chisda also refers to old ones (for Rashi and Rif in reverse, and Bach, Derishah — understand). Nonetheless, there is a stringency in the second wording of three sides: even one whole bayit is invalid; and in the first wording, whenever one bayit is whole and the first partition inside is torn and the third has two sides torn, it is still valid. If so, it is proper for us to worry for the stringency of both interpretations: three sides invalid per the second interpretation; and two consecutive batim with two sides torn invalid — this is what the author wrote first "this next to this," and afterward "not opposite" first and third; he omitted the words "such as in hide" (Bach in grammar of the Tur on the word "next to" and "opposite"). One cannot say he captured the leniency of both interpretations but the stringency of both — that whenever two batim are torn they are invalid, and also for first and third that are valid one must worry for the second interpretation that three sides are also invalid — "not opposite this," understand. And this is what Taz wrote: it seems, etc., that not first and third — even three sides are valid; and so I wrote.
