The first. See Taz. The matter in Beit Yosef is as follows. Menachos 35a contains variant readings. According to Rashi and Rosh, old tefillin are preferable; according to Rif and Rambam, new tefillin are preferable. Regarding interpretation of the passage, Rashi, Rosh, and Rif all understand it as referring to torn leather of the batim, while Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchos Tefillin, interprets it as referring to the stitching, as the Mechaber records in seif 2. The Mechaber follows Rif and Rambam's reading, in accordance with his general rule, but follows Rif and Rosh's interpretation that the passage concerns the leather of the batim. There are two explanations of “opposite one another” and “not opposite one another.” According to Rashi's first explanation, there are two stringencies. First, whenever two adjacent compartments are torn, the tefillin are invalid; only the first and third may be valid. Second, three torn compartments are invalid in every case, whether new or old. Otherwise, if the law concerned only new tefillin according to Rashi and Rosh, or only old tefillin according to Rif and Rambam, why mention three? Two adjacent compartments would already be invalid. According to the second explanation, there are two possible leniencies. First, whenever the two tears are not in the same open space they are valid. Second, one might say that three tears are valid in old tefillin according to Rashi, or in new tefillin according to Rif. The second leniency is not accepted, because the Gemara's wording itself indicates that Rav Chisda's qualification applies also to the earlier case. Nevertheless, the second explanation carries a stringency: three torn walls invalidate even where one entire compartment remains between them. According to the first explanation, if one complete compartment remains, the tefillin may be valid even where the first compartment tore on an inner wall and the third on two sides. We should therefore take into account the stringencies of both explanations: three torn walls invalidate, and two adjacent compartments with torn walls invalidate. This is why the Mechaber first writes “adjacent to one another,” then explains “not opposite one another” as the first and third compartments. He deliberately omits the word “for example.” He did not adopt the leniencies of both explanations, but their stringencies. Thus, whenever two adjacent compartments tear they are invalid, and even the first and third are valid only where three walls did not tear. This is Taz's point when he says “this implies,” lest one think the first and third are valid even with three torn walls.
