(1) And he stipulates concerning them: He should articulate it verbally, or at least intend in his heart that this washing should serve for all the eating occasions of that entire day. He must ensure that the washing meets all the requirements for washing before eating, such as using an intact vessel, water poured by human force, water whose appearance has not changed, and water with which no work was performed, as explained in the preceding simanim. Although regarding morning washing one is not as particular about all these matters, as explained in siman 4, this case is different because the washing is intended for eating.
(2) Even when there is no pressing circumstance: Some poskim write that one may be lenient only in a pressing circumstance, such as when traveling and fearing that he will not find water. Likewise, when he is sitting in a wagon with non-Jews who will not wait while he descends and searches for water, this is considered a pressing circumstance, even if he knows that water is available along that route. It is then effective for him to wash in the morning and stipulate. See below, at the end of seif katan 4, regarding this.
(3) He should not divert his attention: Meaning, from guarding his hands so they do not touch a filthy place. This is what Rama means by ‘and he should not soil them’; it explains the Mechaber’s words, and the word ‘and not’ means ‘that he should not.’ Even so, although his hands remain clean, he must also stipulate beforehand that the washing should serve for eating, because washing before eating requires intent. According to the poskim who hold that washing does not require intent, as explained in siman 159, seif 12, one may explain that the stipulation is necessary because it is intended for the whole day and we do not rely upon his guarding his hands, lest he forget without noticing, unless he stipulated. By stipulating, he becomes more mindful to take greater care.
(4) And he should not soil them: The type of soiling that invalidates the washing is only excrement or any amount of perspiration, even if he merely touched covered parts of his body, as explained below in seif 2. Ordinary dirt or mud does not invalidate the washing. The poskim write that if he placed his hands in gloves immediately after washing and did not remove them, the stipulation is effective, because there is then no concern that he diverted his attention, since his hands were certainly guarded. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Eliyah Rabbah write that nowadays the universal custom is not to make such a stipulation, because people are unable to take care not to divert their attention from guarding their hands, and therefore they abandoned the stipulation entirely. Derech HaChayim also records this. Nevertheless, it appears that everything depends upon the degree of hardship. In a severe pressing circumstance, one may rely on the stipulation even nowadays, provided that he exercises extra care and diligence in guarding his hands. He should therefore place his hands in gloves. Even so, if water later becomes available, he should wash a second time, as explained below. Chayei Adam writes similarly.
(5) And if water is available: The reason is that some poskim hold that even if we are lenient and allow washing in the morning when there is no complete pressing circumstance, this applies only when water is not available nearby and he would have to trouble himself to obtain it. If it is available nearby, we are not lenient at all. The Mechaber initially takes this opinion into account and therefore writes that it is proper to wash again.
(6) That is not needed for eating: It is not only morning washing upon arising that may be accompanied by such a stipulation. The same applies to any washing that is not for the purpose of eating, even during the day, such as when he washed upon leaving the privy or washed for food dipped in liquid and stipulated that the washing should serve him for eating later. Since he washed for another purpose and not at the time of eating, the fact that the meal does not immediately follow the washing was not treated as a concern.
(7) A stipulation is ineffective: Meaning, if he washes his hands in order to eat and stipulates that he will perform another activity before eating that very meal, this is ineffective, because at least initially the meal must immediately follow the washing, in accordance with the Mechaber’s conclusion in siman 166. However, if he washed for one eating occasion and stipulated that it should also serve for a second eating occasion later that day, and he also guarded his hands, it is effective. This is no worse than washing for another purpose, since the time for the second eating occasion has not yet arrived.
