Even—Taz, Chullin 107a, and Avnei Yosef and Bach 13: he washes and blesses. This is the Bach's girsa, and I copied the language of Semak brought in Beit Yosef: a condition—one opinion, only in duress, and if water becomes available he washes and blesses; and one opinion, not in duress, and travelers are like duress, and if water becomes available he does not bless. Understand the Bach: for those who say only in duress does he wash and bless when water becomes available—for the other opinion, even not in duress, no berachah is needed. And he asked: if so, the letters are reversed; therefore he emended at the end, also he washes and blesses, for those who say duress specifically. But the Taz's girsa is: at first he blesses, and at the end he does not bless, like the Beit Yosef girsa. And the explanation: one opinion, in duress—and if so, when water becomes available he blesses; one opinion, even not in duress. And he ruled halacha: specifically duress, and travelers are considered like duress; nevertheless he does not bless, lest since initially it was duress, even when water becomes available the condition stands, and therefore he does not bless—unlike the view without duress, for which there is no reason to wash at all. Therefore the Tur, like Semag, rules duress specifically; but we, although we rule not in duress, nevertheless wash and do not bless, as a general stringency. This is what the Mechaber said; and Taz, etc. See note (3), end of his words.
