(א) Even not at the time of duress. In the gemara there are two versions; one version holds only in duress. And the Tur wrote that from the words of Semag it implies like those who say only in duress. And Beit Yosef wrote that they learned from needing to give travelers a reason, because it is like duress. And it seems that he also learned thus from that it is written: when water becomes available to him he does not bless—which implies that in any case he requires netilah; and if one holds even not at the time of duress, why need netilah here at all? Granted, if one holds only in duress, one could say: since there is a distinction whether netilah is required—that is, not in a place of duress—and here too it was duress, since he is not accustomed for water to be there in his view, but water became available to him, one could doubt whether this is considered duress, since he is not accustomed for water to be there except that now water became available to him from what was not in his mind; or one could say: nevertheless, water is before him, so he must wash without a berachah. With this is resolved what Maharshal questioned on this girsa, and he emends a different girsa: he washes and blesses; and so in some Semag books. And it is written in the gloss on Semag that in most books it is not so, and Rabbenu Tur also does not have it.

(ב) And he shall not soil them. In the Tur it is written, and similarly R' Peretz: specifically at dawn, specifically where water is not common, and specifically that he not soil them; but Rosh permits even not through duress. However, Rashba explains that he should be careful not to contaminate them and not to soil them, etc. This and specifically, etc., is R' Peretz's language; from this the Tur learned that R' Peretz ruled stringently, since he was stringent on these three conditions—for if the lenient version, that even not at the time of duress it is permitted in all ways, this is Rosh's view that he brings afterward. And although Tosafot in the name of Rabbenu Tam brought them, Beit Yosef wrote that even for the version that even not at the time of duress one must say specifically dawn—as the second answer in Pesachim: specifically if there is no water nearby, etc.—for otherwise it is difficult from that which is in the chapter These Things. One could say Rosh holds like R' Yosef whom Beit Yosef brings, who answers with another answer; and with this is settled what the Tur wrote. However, Rashba explains, etc., which is difficult—for Semak also says so. One could say: even though Rosh holds even not at the time of duress, automatically there is no need to distinguish and say specifically dawn and water not common; nevertheless, the third distinction—namely, that he not soil them—is for all. And even though this is not necessary from the side of the difficulty, it is reasonable.

(ג) And if water is available to him—meaning: although we rule like Rosh that a condition is effective even not at the time of duress, and therefore it is permitted in all ways, as written shortly; nevertheless Shulchan Aruch is doubtful lest we hold like Rabbenu Tam's second answer, that even for the lenient version one requires specifically that water not be somewhat common nearby. And one may not be lenient with available water; therefore he wrote that one should wash without a berachah.

(ד) That it is not for the need of eating, etc. Beit Yosef brought the answer of R' Yosef, and this is its language: that which we learned, they pour and afterward they wash—there, he had not conditioned on morning netilah and was obligated to wash at the time of eating; on that they said, immediately to netilah of a meal—for after he washes his hands to eat, he may eat immediately. But here, where he is not obligated to wash from the time of eating, since he guarded them from the morning and conditioned upon them—for this they did not say, immediately to netilah of a meal. And Beit Yosef wrote on this: and it is possible that this is Rabbenu Tam's view, who wrote specifically when conditioning at dawn—for all conditioning not at the time of eating is called dawn conditioning; otherwise there is no reason to distinguish between a dawn condition and a condition for the rest of the day; end of his words. Accordingly Rama ruled here that all conditioning not at the time of eating is effective. And Maharshal questioned: for we hold that one who washes not for eating and changed his mind to eat must return and wash, as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 158 seif 7; therefore he disagreed with this ruling and said: only dawn, which they enacted for bread, because he blesses thus at the time of eating, as Beit Yosef wrote above siman 4 in the name of Rashba—therefore the condition is effective, etc. And it is astounding—for certainly also at dawn netilah it is not effective if he changed his mind and came to eat afterward; rather, it is only because of the condition that it is effective, and the same applies for the rest of the day. And there is no difficulty here: if he comes to wash in order to eat and not for another reason, then he must eat immediately and the condition does not help—for on that they said, immediately to netilah of a meal. But if he washes for another reason, such as at dawn or during the rest of the day when leaving the bathroom, then the condition is effective if he wishes to eat afterward; and although when leaving the bathroom he will not bless on bread, and at the time of eating he also will not bless on bread for what he already washed—for this there is no matter, since he is exempt from netilah, since his hands are guarded. Therefore this ruling is correct: the condition is effective when he washes not for the need of eating, but for another reason that requires netilah, and he conditions that it should count for him when he comes to eat afterward—since immediately to netilah of a meal is not relevant here. And this immediately to netilah of a meal is in R' Peretz, These Things.
