1) [Seif 1] A person may wash his hands in the morning and stipulate, etc. This applies even when there are no pressing circumstances. See the Beit Yosef, who cites a dispute. Some say that a stipulation is effective only in pressing circumstances, while others say that it is effective even without pressing circumstances. Maran, of blessed memory, rules like the latter view in the Beit Yosef, and these are his words here in the Shulchan Aruch. Maharshal, however, in responsum 94, rules like the authorities who say that a stipulation is effective only in pressing circumstances. He also writes that the statement in Pesachim, chapter Eilu Devarim, that one must travel four mil for prayer and washing, must concern one who washed his hands in the morning and stipulated concerning them. Therefore, within four mil is not considered pressing circumstances and the stipulation is ineffective, while more than four mil is considered pressing circumstances and the stipulation is effective. He writes that this applies specifically to travelers on foot in an all-Jewish group, who can stop wherever they wish. Nowadays, however, one who rides in a wagon among non-Jews traveling as a group, fears traveling alone, and must stop wherever they stop is considered to be in pressing circumstances even within four mil, and his stipulation is effective. One who is stringent and does not eat until he reaches water, washes, and eats properly on the wagon will be blessed; see there. The Maharshal's explanation that the four-mil rule concerns one who washed in the morning and stipulated is also written by the Beit Yosef in the name of Sefer Mitzvos Katan; see there. The other poskim, however, apply the four-mil rule even to one who did not stipulate when washing in the morning. This is the view of Maran, of blessed memory, and the later authorities, for they require him to wrap his hands in a cloth, as written in the preceding siman. If he had stipulated, why would he require a cloth? See there. The statement that a stipulation is effective only when water is more than four mil away applies only according to the view that permits a stipulation solely in pressing circumstances. According to the view that permits stipulating without pressing circumstances, one may stipulate in every situation. As a practical ruling, however, it would have been proper to be stringent and stipulate only in pressing circumstances. This also appears to be the view of Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, rules accordingly. Regarding the law in practice, however, Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, end of paragraph 3, writes that nowadays the common practice is never to stipulate, even with the morning washing. There is no doubt that people rely upon the poskim who hold that a stipulation is effective only in pressing circumstances. Even in pressing circumstances people cannot reliably guard their hands from becoming dirty, so they abandoned the stipulation entirely. Eliyah Rabbah there cites him and writes that he found in the Agur as well that the common custom is not to stipulate. Beis Menuchah, paragraph 3, and Derech HaChaim, paragraph 3, write the same.
