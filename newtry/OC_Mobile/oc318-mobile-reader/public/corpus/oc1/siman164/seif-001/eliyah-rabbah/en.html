[1] [Levush:] And he wishes to stipulate, etc. Damesek Eliezer, page 325, writes that this stipulation does not mean that he must articulate it with his mouth and lips. Rather, it means that he should take care not to soil or defile his hands. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah objects that, if so, why did the poskim also write, ‘provided that he does not divert his attention,’ etc.? These are the same requirement. In my humble opinion, Damesek Eliezer can be defended. A stipulation is indeed required, but he holds that it need not be verbalized. Rather, it is sufficient that he intends to eat and, because of that intention, takes care not to soil his hands. I found similarly in Bach at the end of siman 158, who is uncertain whether a stipulation made mentally is effective. See there. However, I saw in Shibolei HaLeket and Sefer HaTanya, siman 22, the following wording: ‘He should say: With this washing I shall be permitted to eat throughout the entire day, and he must guard his hands from anything impure or repulsive.’ Nevertheless, it is possible that ‘he should say’ means in his heart, as Levush writes below in siman 436 concerning the words ‘he should say: All chametz.’ It also appears to me that there is proof from Toras HaBayis HaAruch, page 160. It asks why a stipulation is necessary if we already require him not to divert his attention, and answers that intent is also required even for non-sacred food. See there. Certainly, this intent need not be verbal. The same is implied by Bedek HaBayis there, Toras HaBayis HaKatzar, and Bircas Avraham, page 185. See there and examine carefully. Kol Bo writes that if he placed his hands in gloves after washing, the stipulation is effective. This implies that it is effective even though he then diverts his attention, and so is implied by Bach there, unlike what Magen Avraham seems to imply.
