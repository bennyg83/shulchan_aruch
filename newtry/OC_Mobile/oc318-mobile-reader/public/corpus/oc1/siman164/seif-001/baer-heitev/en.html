A pressing circumstance. Maharshal was stringent that a stipulation is effective only in a pressing circumstance. Nevertheless, one who is sitting in a wagon with non-Jews who will not wait for him is considered to be in a pressing circumstance.
He should not soil them. For example, he places them in gloves. Knesses HaGedolah writes that nowadays the universal custom is not to make such a stipulation, because people are unable to take care not to soil their hands. See there.
Eating. For example, if he left the privy or washed for food dipped in liquid, and stipulated that this washing should also serve for eating, this constitutes proper intent. What is written in siman 158, seif 7, concerns a case in which he made no stipulation. Magen Avraham.
A stipulation. This is because the meal must follow immediately after washing. But if he washed for one eating occasion and stipulated that it should also serve for another eating occasion, the stipulation is effective. Indeed, no stipulation is required if he guarded his hands. Magen Avraham.
