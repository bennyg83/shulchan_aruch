Even when there is no pressing circumstance. Maharshal was stringent that a stipulation is effective only in a pressing circumstance. Nevertheless, one who is sitting in a wagon with non-Jews who will not wait for him is considered to be in a pressing circumstance.
He should not divert his attention. This is difficult, for if so, why is it necessary to stipulate? It must be that intent for washing is required, and therefore he must stipulate. See siman 159, seif 13. Rashba.
And he should not soil them. For example, he places them in gloves.
Are available to him. Meaning, they are ready before him and he need not trouble himself to obtain them.
That is not needed for eating. For example, he left the privy or washed for food dipped in liquid, and stipulated that this washing should serve him for eating. This constitutes proper intent. What is written in siman 158, seif 7, concerns a case in which he did not stipulate. See there.
A stipulation is ineffective. This is because the meal must follow immediately after washing. See siman 167. But when he has already washed for another purpose, he is not obligated to wash again at the time of eating, since he guarded his hands beforehand and stipulated concerning them. This, in my view, is plainly the opinion of Rabbeinu Yonah. See there regarding his question from Kiddush, unlike Bach. Lechem Chamudos labors to be stringent and writes that if one washed for one eating occasion and stipulated that it should also serve for another, it is ineffective because of a decree. See there. In my view, it is plainly effective, and indeed no stipulation is required if he guarded his hands. See what I wrote in siman 179, end of seif 1.
