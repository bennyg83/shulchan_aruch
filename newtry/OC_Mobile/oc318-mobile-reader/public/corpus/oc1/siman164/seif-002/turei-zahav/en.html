And he should recite the blessing over washing the hands. The Beit Yosef cites this in the name of a responsum of the Ramban, and writes that for this reason on the night of Pesach we wash and recite a blessing for the first dipping, and then wash again and recite a blessing for the second dipping, etc. This is difficult to me. Since we do not recite a blessing over the washing for the first dipping on the night of Pesach, as stated below in siman 473, why did the Shulchan Aruch rule here that one should recite a blessing? There is a further difficulty. If a blessing is required because he touched covered parts of the body, then all the more so it should be required if he relieves himself during the meal. Yet this is not so, for in siman 7 the Shulchan Aruch rules that one does not recite the blessing over washing the hands even if he intends to pray. I later saw that the Maharshal writes in chapter Kol HaBasar that one should not recite a blessing over this washing, because we do not find that the Sages instituted a blessing in the middle of a meal, etc. See there, where he discusses this at length. This appears to me to be the primary ruling. It is also implied by the words of the Rama at the end of siman 178, who writes that relieving oneself does not constitute an interruption in the meal and does not mention that one should recite a blessing over washing the hands.
