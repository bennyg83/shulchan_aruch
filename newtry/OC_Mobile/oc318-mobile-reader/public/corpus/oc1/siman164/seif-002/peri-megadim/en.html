He shall not soil them—Taz, in my simple opinion: since our master ruled with a special intention, as I will say, end of his words—the Tur in siman 161 rules specifically through duress, and similarly R' Peretz; and specifically where there is no water, and specifically at dawn, and specifically that he not soil them; but Rosh permits even not through duress. However, Rashba explains that he should be careful not to contaminate them and not to soil them; and Beit Yosef questioned this. However, Rashba explains—behold, R' Peretz also holds thus. And one could further say: for what purpose did he bring altogether this and specifically at dawn and he shall not soil them—for they are not relevant to duress? And the Taz's answer is that Tosafot in Chullin 106b, s.v. notel, questioned against Rav from that which one who washes shall not sanctify, and also immediately to netilah of a meal, and one does not interrupt all day. And Rabbenu Tam has two answers: (1) dawn specifically—for the rest of the day it is forbidden to interrupt at all; (2) dawn is not specifically for the law, but rather Rav—even though he does not require great duress (that is, either beyond four mil, or even within four mil whenever it is very far, it is great duress)—nevertheless, that which a condition is effective is only when it is not before him, but through some bother; when it is truly available before him, a condition is not effective. Therefore he said at dawn: one netilah suffices, also for eating—for during the rest of the day, if he has water for the meal, why should he wash much beforehand and condition? It would not be effective. And accordingly, from that which R' Peretz wrote that water is not common, one could say like Tosafot's second answer; and still one could say even without great duress. And from where do we know that R' Peretz holds like the language of specifically great duress? For this he brought specifically dawn, and necessarily his reasoning is specifically great duress—for otherwise, like Rav, from where does Rav know that Rav deals with some duress? He states it plainly. And R' Chananel's difficulty is from the mouth, and kiddush for the evening meal—since specifically dawn, we learn that we require great duress, as the language that said so; and dawn implies or proves from R' Peretz, and understand this. And Rosh is completely lenient—even when it is truly available before him, a condition is effective; and he also disagrees and says there is no distinction of dawn. And Tosafot's difficulty there (for now there are not two answers) he resolves like R' Yosef: it is different at the time of a meal, for it is forbidden to interrupt because of it; therefore he said: although Rosh disagrees with R' Peretz on two divisions, regarding soiling them he agrees with him. And Rashba similarly says it is reasonable. Taz, end of his words, in note (3) from this.
