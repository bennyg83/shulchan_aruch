(8) And he remembered that he touched: The same law applies if he diverted his attention from guarding his hands; he must wash, as stated in siman 170, seif 1. See the Mishnah Berurah there regarding this. If he touched while the piece of bread was still in his mouth, he may not swallow it until he washes his hands. Magen Avraham.
(9) And covered places: Meaning, the other covered places of his body contain perspiration. This excludes exposed places, such as his face and the exposed portion of his arms, where touching is not a concern, as explained in siman 4, seif 21. See the Mishnah Berurah there for laws relevant to this matter.
(10) Or he scratched his head: If he did not scratch but merely touched his hair, he need not wash. This applies even if he wiped his hands on his hair, as stated above in siman 162, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 58.
(11) And in filthy places: The correct text should read ‘in places,’ without the connecting vav. The phrase ‘which contain’ gives the reason for all the preceding cases.
(12) He must wash again: All the more so if he touched actual filth, relieved himself, or even urinated and rubbed with his hands, as explained below in siman 170.
(13) And he should recite the blessing over washing the hands: Maharshal disagrees and holds that touching perspiration during the meal does not require a new blessing. He likewise holds that even if one relieved himself during the meal, he need not recite a blessing unless he went away and was absent for a substantial interval. The later authorities express many different views as to whether his opinion should be followed in practice. See Biur Halachah, where we conclude that if one relieved himself, touched actual filth, or went away and was absent for a substantial interval, he must wash with a blessing. If he merely touched an ordinarily covered place, or urinated even if he rubbed with his hands, he must wash according to law but should not recite a blessing. See there.
