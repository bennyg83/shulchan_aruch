[2] Not in a pressing circumstance, etc. Maharshal, in responsum 94 and Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 22, disagrees and rules that a stipulation is effective only when water is farther away than the measure explained in the preceding siman. This appears to me to be the primary ruling, for I have seen that Semak, Hagahos Semak, Hagahos Maimoniyos, Mordechai, Kol Bo, and Tashbetz all rule this way. The Tur likewise cites the words of Semak. Beit Yosef understood them to be the words of Semag, but this is incorrect, as Bach writes. They also overlooked Agur, siman 204, who copies the Tur’s words in the name of Semak. The same is implied by Tzeidah LaDerech. Nevertheless, Maharshal writes that travelers accompanying non-Jews, who must stop wherever the non-Jews stop even within four mil, are considered to be in a pressing circumstance, and the stipulation is effective. One who is stringent is deserving of blessing. I further saw that Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah writes as follows: ‘Nowadays the universal custom is not to make a stipulation. There is no doubt that they relied upon the poskim who hold that a stipulation is effective only in a pressing circumstance. Even in a pressing circumstance, people are unable to take care not to soil their hands, and therefore they entirely abandoned the stipulation.’ I found similarly in Agur there: ‘The universal custom is not to make a stipulation.’ Examine carefully.
