(12) Several cups. Whether he drinks between the first and the second or after the second cup, and all the more so during eating; but between the third and the fourth it is forbidden, as below in siman 479.
(13) Permission is in his hand. According to what the Rav wrote below in siman 474 in the gloss, that our custom is to recite a first blessing over every cup: that which is permitted to drink between the cups is specifically if at the time of the blessing his intention was to exempt all that they would afterwards bring him to drink [or the wine was before him, for then automatically he is exempt so long as he did not think explicitly not to drink]; for if his intention was not to drink between the cups and afterwards he changed his mind to drink, if so he would need to recite a blessing over them, and this is forbidden, for it appears like adding to the cups since he blesses over this cup as over the other four cups, as we wrote above in siman 472, s.k. 21 in the Mishnah Berurah.
(14) It is proper to be careful. And so is our custom (Darkei Moshe).
(15) Not to drink between the first and the second. To be concerned for the one who says (and it is brought in Beis Yosef) that that which we permit to drink between the cups is [only] between the second cup and the third cup, which is adjacent to eating, or during eating, for then it does not intoxicate; but not between the first and the second, so that he not become intoxicated, and just as it is forbidden between the third and the fourth.
(16) So that he not become intoxicated. It implies specifically wine or other beverages of this kind that intoxicate; but a beverage that does not intoxicate, it is permitted to drink between the cups.
