(s.k. 3) And not, etc. It implies that he holds it is forbidden, etc., from what he wrote "but all year it appears," etc.: from that he needed to give a reason if one washes before Kiddush, it implies that it is preferable if one washes after Kiddush; and in siman 271 there is a dispute of the poskim about this.
And the reason is explained in the Gemara, etc. He abbreviated greatly here the reason of Beis Shammai, who hold it is forbidden to use a vessel whose outside is impure even though its inside is pure, for the reason clarified there. Therefore they hold one must wash before mixing: for if he mixes first, perhaps droplets will fall at the time of mixing onto the outside of the cup, and those liquids will receive impurity from the hands that are impure before washing, and they are secondaries, and the liquids will become firsts; for whatever disqualifies terumah, namely a second, makes liquids impure to be a first, and afterwards the liquids will make impure the outside of a vessel; even though a vessel receives impurity only from a primary source of impurity, nevertheless by rabbinic law a vessel receives impurity from impure liquids even though the liquids are only firsts. Therefore he should wash his hands first and they will be pure. And Beis Hillel hold it is permitted to use a vessel whose outside is impure, and therefore one must mix first: for if he washes his hands first, perhaps at the time of mixing his hands will not be dry until there is upon them water that is moist enough to moisten [another thing], and perhaps the vessel's outside will be impure, and the liquids on his hands will receive impurity from the outside of the vessel and return and make the hands impure. And further, even according to the words of Beis Shammai, nevertheless Beis Hillel hold that even if it is forbidden to use a vessel whose outside is impure, it is preferable to mix first so that the mixing not be an interruption between washing and HaMotzi; thus far the substance of the Gemara's words.
Therefore it appears to me, etc. That is to say, Maharil's intent is not as Darkei Moshe understood; for this is Maharil's language brought in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah: if he washes before Kiddush, then it appears that the halacha is like Beis Shammai, who wash their hands and afterwards make Kiddush; end quote. If so, Maharil's intent is not that Beis Shammai hold one must wash before Kiddush; rather that Beis Shammai necessarily hold one need not be concerned about interrupting between washing and HaMotzi, for if not so he would concede to Beis Hillel that one mix first, for Beis Shammai's concern because of impurity of the outside of a vessel is only a mere concern, and the interruption is certain; rather, necessarily Beis Shammai hold that even if one interrupts between washing and HaMotzi, they have no concern about it. But we hold like Beis Hillel that it is forbidden to interrupt between washing and HaMotzi; and even so, all year one who washes before Kiddush has no [problem] of interruption, for Kiddush is not an interruption; specifically mixing is an interruption, as Tosafos wrote, brought by the Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 166, that they would mix with hot water, and one needs careful precision not to diminish or add water; see there. But on Pesach, where there is a major interruption between washing and HaMotzi in saying the Haggadah, it is certainly an interruption like mixing. And even though immediately after Kiddush one needs washing for dipping, and the washing before Kiddush would count for the need of dipping, nevertheless people will not enter their minds that he is washing for the need of dipping, since all year they are not accustomed to wash for something dipped in liquid, and they will err that he is washing for the need of HaMotzi, and this is an interruption like mixing, and people will think he holds like Beis Shammai that we are not concerned about interruption; such is Maharil's intent according to the Magen Avraham. And what the Magen Avraham concluded, "and all agree about this," namely even according to Beis Shammai who hold that mixing is not an interruption, nevertheless he concedes that Haggadah and Hallel are an interruption. And what Maharil wrote, "then it appears that the halacha is like Beis Shammai," even though in such a case also according to Beis Shammai it is an interruption, one can say that also in this people will err that the interruption of the Haggadah is only like the interruption of mixing, and just as mixing is not an interruption according to Beis Shammai, so too the Haggadah.
And Maharshal, etc. Before Kiddush, so as not to change from all year.
And the Bach wrote that nevertheless he should wash a second time for dipping, for he holds that Kiddush is an interruption: granted, between washing and HaMotzi Kiddush is not an interruption, since Kiddush is needed for the meal, for there is no Kiddush except in the place of a meal, [so] they are considered as one; which is not so regarding dipping, in which there is no need for Kiddush, [so] Kiddush is an interruption.
And if so it appears as contradicting; therefore if he washes before Kiddush it is not recognizable that he is washing for the need of dipping, but only as he does all year that he washes before Kiddush; even though afterwards one sees that he washes a second time for HaMotzi, nevertheless it is not so explicitly apparent; and see the Taz, s.k. 6, and Chok Yaakov.
