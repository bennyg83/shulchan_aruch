(א) (s"k 3) And one should not, etc. It implies he holds it forbidden, etc.; since he wrote "but all year it appears," etc., he had to give a reason if one washes before kiddush — it implies preferable if one washes after kiddush; in siman 471 there is dispute among poskim on this.

(ב) And the reason the Gemara explains, etc. He abbreviated greatly the reason of Beit Shammai, who hold it forbidden to use a vessel whose back is impure though its inside is pure, for the reason explained there; therefore Beit Shammai hold one must wash before pouring — if he pours first, perhaps sparks at pouring will fall on the back of the cup and receive impurity from hands that are impure before washing — they are second [degree], and the liquids become first [degree] and then defile the back of the vessel; though a vessel does not receive impurity except from a father of impurity, nevertheless rabbinically a vessel receives impurity from impure liquids even if the liquids are only first — therefore he washes hands first so they be pure. Beit Hillel hold it is permitted to use a vessel whose back is impure; therefore one must pour first — if he washes first, perhaps at pouring his hands are not dry and water is "touching in order to touch"; perhaps the vessel's back is impure and liquids on his hands receive impurity from the back and return to defile the hands. And further, even for Beit Shammai, Beit Hillel hold even if forbidden to use a vessel whose back is impure, preferable to pour first so pouring not be an interruption between washing and ha-motzi — thus far the Gemara.

(ג) Therefore it appears to me, etc. Meaning: Maharil's intent is not as Darchei Moshe understood — Maharil brought in Sefer Avraham Rokeach: if one washes before kiddush it appears the law is like Beit Shammai who washes and then sanctifies — end of his words. If so, Maharil does not mean Beit Shammai require washing before kiddush; rather Beit Shammai must hold one need not be concerned for interruption between washing and ha-motzi — otherwise he would agree with Beit Hillel to pour first, for Beit Shammai's concern of back-impurity is only a slight concern and interruption is certain; or Beit Shammai hold even if interrupting between washing and ha-motzi it does not matter; but we hold like Beit Hillel that interruption is forbidden — nevertheless all year one who washes before kiddush has no interruption concern, for kiddush is not an interruption — only pouring is interruption, as Tosafot brought Magen Avraham in siman 166 that they poured in hot water; care needed not to omit or add water — see there; but on Pesach there is great interruption between washing and ha-motzi in Hagadah — certainly an interruption like pouring; and although immediately after kiddush he needs washing for dipping, and washing before kiddush could rise for dipping — nevertheless people will not understand he washes for dipping, since all year they do not wash for things dipped in liquid; they will err that he washes for ha-motzi and this is interruption like pouring; they will think the law is like Beit Shammai who are not concerned for interruption — such is Maharil's intent per Magen Avraham; and what Magen Avraham concluded "and all agree on this" — even Beit Shammai who hold pouring is not interruption nevertheless agree Hagadah and Hallel are interruption; and what Maharil wrote "then it appears like Beit Shammai" — even if for Beit Shammai it is interruption, one may say people will err that Hagadah interruption is only like pouring interruption, and as pouring is not interruption for Beit Shammai, so Hagadah.

(ד) And Maharshal, etc. before kiddush so as not to change from all year.

(ה) Bach wrote: nevertheless wash again for dipping, for he holds kiddush is an interruption in the complete sense between washing and ha-motzi — not that kiddush is interruption, since kiddush requires a meal — there is no kiddush except where there is a meal — they are considered one; unlike dipping, which has no need for kiddush — kiddush is the interruption.

(ו) If so it appears contradictory; therefore if he washes before kiddush it is not evident he washes for dipping, only as all year when one washes before kiddush even though afterward he washes again for ha-motzi — nevertheless it is not so evident; see Taz s"k 6 and Kaf HaChayim.
