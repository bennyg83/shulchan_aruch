He washes his hands. Taz writes that this is an open rebuke to those who are not careful throughout the year to wash their hands for foods dipped in liquid. Even if one wishes to purify himself more on this night, he should at least exercise the same care during the Ten Days of Repentance. Yet there is only a lack of care here, and people are not exacting to make their practices consistent. Lechem Chamudos, chapter Kol HaBasar, writes that the common custom is not to wash and that there is authority for this, because Tosafot hold this way. This is the opinion of Maharam of Rothenburg cited by Tur here, and Knesses HaGedolah's challenge to Maharam is therefore unwarranted. Magen Avraham writes likewise in siman 158. The reason we wash on this night is as one of the other unusual practices performed so that the children will ask.
And he does not recite an after-blessing. This applies even if he ate a kezayis. Bach writes that one who wishes to satisfy all the Geonim should eat a full kezayis at this first dipping and intend when reciting Borei Pri HaAdamah not to exempt the maror with that blessing. He will then be obligated, according to all opinions, to recite Borei Nefashos after the first dipping. When he later recites HaMotzi, he should likewise intend not to exempt the maror with HaMotzi, and should again recite Borei Pri HaAdamah over the eating of maror and Borei Nefashos afterward. In this manner he satisfies all opinions, and there is no issue of an unnecessary blessing because he acts to remove himself from a dispute. Nevertheless, he has still not satisfied the opinion of Semag, siman 220, who holds that no blessing is recited over maror because it is not eaten for enjoyment but only as a remembrance. It therefore appears to me that the primary ruling follows the agreement of most early authorities, Maharil, siman 59, and Magen Avraham, that no after-blessing is recited at all.
And he should give half. The later authorities write that this means most of the matzah.
And he should lift the ke'arah. He lifts it together with everything upon it. Some have the custom to place the afikoman, wrapped in a cloth, upon their shoulders in remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt. Pesach Me'ubin, siman 245, writes: “It is proper to lift the ke'arah in one hand and the matzah in the other, and recite Ha Lachma Anya.”
Ha Lachma. Some say Ha KeLachma. See Magen Avraham. Maharshal, siman 88, writes that it should be recited aloud.
He should instruct that they be removed. Magen Avraham writes that nowadays the custom is not to remove them. Some have the custom to say that the ke'arah should be removed even though they do not actually remove it. Pesach Me'ubin, siman 249, writes that people of pious conduct open the door when saying Kol Dichfin, for if the door is closed, how will people enter? Raavan writes that the invitation refers to the members of one's household, not to outsiders who may come.
