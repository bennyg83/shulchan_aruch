He washes his hands for the need of the first dipping. For anything whose dipping is in liquid requires netilas yadayim. From here there is open rebuke to those who are not careful all year to wash before they eat something dipped in liquid: what is different about this night from all the days of the year? And if he comes to purify himself more on this night, he ought at the very least to be careful during the Ten Days of Repentance, when all are careful to eat specifically bread of a Jew; rather, certainly there is here only a lack of care, and they are not precise to make their measures equal.
And he should not recite a blessing over the washing. In the Tur he wrote: and the Ra'avam wrote that nowadays one need not wash for something dipped in liquid, and therefore he would not recite Al Netilas Yadayim over the first dipping; and so wrote the Baal HaItur; and it does not appear so to me; and so wrote the Geonim that one must recite a blessing; and so wrote the Rosh in the name of Rashi; and one who wishes to fulfill the doubt should bring himself to an obligation of washing from another side, for example by casting water and rubbing. And the Ra'avam's reason is that the washing is not because of resemblance to terumah as with bread, but because of cleanliness, and now we are not careful about cleanliness, as Tosafos wrote in the chapter "Arvei Pesachim." And Beis Yosef wrote that what he wrote "and it does not appear so to me," his words are not correct, for since it is a dispute whether washing is required, it is better not to recite a blessing; end quote. He further challenged what the Tur wrote, "he should cast water and rub," that this matter does not bring him to the blessing of Al Netilas Yadayim according to the Rosh's view in siman 7. And it appears to me that the Tur did not write "and it does not appear so to me" regarding the blessing, but regarding what he wrote that nowadays there is no obligation of washing; also what he wrote "one who wishes to fulfill the doubt" refers to the washing, not to the blessing: for if we say that washing is not needed for this, it turns out that there is a prohibition in washing, for it is like the arrogant, like one who washes his hands for fruit, which is from the arrogant because washing is not needed there; so too here. Therefore he wrote that he should bring himself to washing by means of rubbing, and automatically he is obligated in the blessing of Al Netilas Yadayim, since something dipped in liquid has a resemblance to bread; automatically, one who cast water and rubbed and comes to eat this thing, all agree that there is here an obligation of washing as with bread without rubbing; and in this all agree, since there are two advantages for the obligation of washing, and it is like one who wishes to study in siman 7; nevertheless Beis Yosef and the Shulchan Aruch correctly ruled not to recite a blessing in this and not to heed that this blessing is written in all the siddurim; and so wrote Tosafos in the chapter "Arvei Pesachim," folio 115, that one who recites a blessing recites a blessing in vain, and in all the siddurim it is written to recite a blessing over the washing, and it does not appear [correct]; end quote. And it is very startling that he wrote that on this night, because of the obligation of the festival, one should recite a blessing, and all year he himself wrote in siman 158 that one should not recite a blessing; and this matter is not correct at all, to obligate a blessing at one time more than its fellow; therefore one should not recite a blessing at all, as all year. And the reason for this dipping, Rashi and Rashbam explained, is for recognition, because people are not accustomed to eat a vegetable before the meal, and the children will ask about this; and it appears the answer for them will be that if he dipped only the second [dipping] of maror, it would not be recognizable that it was because of a mitzvah, but rather as people customarily dip during the meal; therefore they enacted to dip before the meal, for by this they hint that the dipping does not come on account of the meal, and it is a sign for the second dipping which is also not on account of the meal but a mitzvah of the Sages; so it appears to me according to the view of Rashi and the Rambam.
Less than a kezayit. Because if he eats a kezayit he will be obligated to recite Borei Nefashos, and there is a dispute about this whether he is obligated to recite it over the first dipping; therefore it is good that he eat in such a way that there be no obligation at all. And the dispute is between Rabbeinu Yitzchak and Rashbam: for Rabbeinu Yitzchak holds that the Haggadah and Hallel are an interruption between the dipping and the meal, as is written regarding washing, that one must wash a second time for the meal, as is written in se'if 1; so too regarding an after-blessing over the dipping, and Birkas HaMazon of the meal does not exempt it. But Rashbam holds, as the Rosh explained, that specifically regarding washing is the Haggadah considered an interruption, lest he touched an unclean place in between; but regarding an after-blessing it is not so: just as one need not recite Borei Pri HaAdamah over the maror, for the reason that it is exempted by this blessing that he made at the first dipping, so too regarding the after-blessing of the first dipping he is exempt from it, for it belongs to the meal and it is as if he ate it during the meal. And Rabbeinu Yitzchak holds that it is not for this reason that he is exempt from Borei Pri HaAdamah and from an after-blessing for maror; rather, that the maror itself belongs to the meal and is exempted by HaMotzi and by Birkas HaMazon; but the first dipping has no connection to the meal because of the interruption of Haggadah and Hallel. And it appears that to this dispute another dispute pertains regarding the after-blessing of the first cups: according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak one must recite a blessing, and according to Rashbam one need not say [it].
