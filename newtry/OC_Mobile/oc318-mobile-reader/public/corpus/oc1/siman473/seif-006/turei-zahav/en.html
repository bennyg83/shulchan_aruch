(א) He washes hands for the need of the first dipping. For everything whose dipping is in liquid requires netilah. From here there is open rebuke to those who are not careful all year to wash before eating something whose dipping is in liquid — how is this night different from all the days of the year? And if he comes to purify himself more on this night, he should be careful at least on the ten days of repentance when everyone is careful to eat bread of a Jew specifically; but certainly here there is only lack of carefulness, and they do not scrutinize to equalize their measures:

(ב) And he should not bless on the washing. Tur wrote, and the Rambam wrote that nowadays one need not wash for something whose dipping is in liquid; therefore he would not bless al netilat yadayim on the first dipping — and likewise the author of Ittur, and I disagree; and likewise the Geonim that one must bless; and likewise Rosh in the name of Rashi; and one who wishes to fulfill doubt should bring himself to obligation of washing from another side, such as pouring water and rubbing. And the Rambam's reason: because washing is not on account of residue of terumah as with bread, but on account of cleanliness; and now we are not careful about cleanliness, as Tosafot wrote in the chapter of Arvei Pesachim; and Beit Yosef wrote that that which I disagree, his words are not correct, for since it is a dispute whether washing is required, it is better not to bless, end of his words. He further challenged that which Tur wrote "pour water and rub" — this thing does not bring him to berachah of al netilat yadayim per the Rosh in siman 7; and I disagree: Tur did not write "I disagree" on the berachah but on that which he wrote there is no obligation of washing nowadays; also in that which he wrote "one who wishes to fulfill doubt" — it refers to washing, not to the berachah; for if we say washing is not required for this, there is prohibition to wash, for it is arrogance, like one who washes hands for fruits — he is among the arrogant because washing is not needed there, the same here; therefore he wrote that he should bring himself to washing through rubbing, and by necessity he is obligated in berachah of al netilat yadayim, since something whose dipping is in liquid has similarity to bread; thus one who poured water and rubbed and comes to eat this thing — all agree there is obligation of washing as with bread without rubbing; and in this all agree, since there are two factors elevating obligation of washing; and it is as one who wishes to learn in siman 7 — a pity, nevertheless Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch ruled well not to bless on this and not to heed that this berachah is written in all siddurim; and likewise Tosafot in the chapter of Arvei Pesachim page 115: one who blesses blesses a berachah in vain; and in all siddurim it is written to bless on washing — and it does not appear, end of his words. And one should greatly wonder that he wrote on this night because of the obligation of the festival one should bless, and all year he himself wrote in siman 158 that one should not bless — and this thing is not correct at all to obligate berachah at one time more than another; therefore one should not bless at all as all year. And the reason for this dipping — Rashi and Rashbam [explain] on account of recognition, because people are not accustomed to eat vegetable before the meal, and the children will ask about it; and the apparent answer will be for them: if he had dipped only the second of maror, it would not have been recognizable that it was on account of a mitzvah, but in the manner of people to dip during the meal; therefore they enacted to dip before the meal, for thereby they hint that the dipping does not come on account of the meal, and it is a sign for the second dipping, which is also not on account of the meal but a rabbinic mitzvah, as above according to Rashi and Rambam:

(ג) Less than a k'zayit. Because if he eats a k'zayit he will be obligated to bless Borei nafashot, and there is a dispute whether he is obligated to bless it on the first dipping; therefore it is better he eat in a manner where there is no obligation at all. And the dispute is between R' Yitzchak and Rashbam: R' Yitzchak holds that the Haggadah and Hallel are an interruption between the dipping and the meal, as is written regarding washing that one must wash again for the meal, as written in seif 1 — the same for blessing acharonah on the dipping, and the Grace after Meals of the meal does not exempt it; but Rashbam holds as the Rosh explained: only for washing is the Haggadah considered an interruption, lest he touched an unclean place in between; but for blessing acharonah, no — just as one need not bless Borei peri ha'adamah on maror because he is exempted by the berachah he made at the first dipping, so too for blessing acharonah of the first dipping he is exempt, for it is related to the meal and as if he ate during the meal. And R' Yitzchak holds: not on account of this reason is he exempt from Borei peri ha'adamah and blessing acharonah on maror, but maror itself is related to the meal and exempted by HaMotzi and Grace after Meals; but the first dipping has no relation to the meal because of the interruption of Haggadah and Hallel. And it appears that in this dispute there is also a dispute regarding blessing acharonah of the first cups: for R' Yitzchak one must bless, and for Rashbam not:
