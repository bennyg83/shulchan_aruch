Seif 6, "he washes his hands." Pesachim 115a-b. "And he does not recite a blessing." See Tosafos there, beginning "Kol," etc., and what I wrote in siman 158, seif 4, that the primary halacha is that one recites al netilat yadayim for food dipped in liquid. See there. "And he takes." The Mishnah states that one dips with chazeret, as explained above. "Less than a kezayit." Since he takes it only as a recognition for the children, and one recites the first blessing even over less than a kezayit, as Tosafos writes in the second chapter of Sukkah in the name of the Yerushalmi and as explained at the beginning of the sixth chapter of Berachot. He should not take a full kezayit. See Taz. "And he recites a blessing." Gemara there. "And he does not recite an after-blessing." Meaning even if he ate a kezayit, as explained above. This follows from the Gemara, 115a, which indicates that the first blessing ultimately exempts it. See Rashbam 114b, beginning "Peshita," and Tosafos 115a, beginning "Vehadar," who dispute Rashbam and R' Yosef Tov Elem. Rosh, however, explains like Rashbam. Tosafos' question from 115b, that Haggadah and Hallel constitute an interruption, applies specifically to handwashing, not to the blessing, because even companions who changed place do not create an interruption where some companions remained. See Tosafos 102a, beginning "Ve'akru." Seder HaPesach writes that a practical difference between these explanations concerns the after-blessing. See also Tosafos 100b, at the end of "Yedei Yayin," and Rosh there. "And he takes the matzah." As explained above. The middle matzah is specified in accordance with the law of placing a broken piece together with a whole loaf. "And he places half." So that both matzah-eatings performed for the mitzvah come from the broken piece. "And he raises." As the Gemara states that matzah requires raising. See Hagahot Maimoniyot. "And he says in the language," etc. As Scripture states, "And you shall tell your son," etc. "And afterward he instructs," etc. The Gemara there discusses why the table or seder plate is removed, and Tosafos explains the reason.
