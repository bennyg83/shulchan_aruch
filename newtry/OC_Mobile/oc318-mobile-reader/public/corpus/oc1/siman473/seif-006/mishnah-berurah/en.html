(א) For the need of the first dipping — as he explains and continues. And the reason is that everything whose dipping is in liquid requires netilah, as above in siman 158:

(ב) And he should not bless, etc. — as the Mechaber ruled above in siman 158 seif 1; and see there in Mishna Berurah the opinion of the Gra on this matter:

(ג) Less than a k'zayit — because with a k'zayit there is doubt in the blessing acharonah whether he should bless it or not; therefore it is better that he eat less than a k'zayit so there should be no obligation at all according to all:

(ד) In vinegar — or in wine or in salt water, and this comes only to exclude that he should not dip in charoset, for charoset is only for the second dipping when one dips the maror in charoset [Beit Yosef]:

(ה) And he blesses Borei peri ha'adamah — and he should intend to exempt through this berachah also the maror that he will eat afterward [Acharonim]:

(ו) And he does not bless after it — even if he ate a k'zayit, because the first berachah stands also for the maror, as above; and therefore the Grace after Meals that exempts the maror that he eats during the meal stands also for the karpas that he ate earlier — and see in Biur Halachah what he wrote in the name of the Gra on this:

(ז) The middle one and he breaks it into two — in order to fulfill what the verse called "bread of affliction," and the way of a poor person is in a piece; and he said "the middle one" because the berachah of eating matzah primarily applies to it, and the berachah of HaMotzi that he blesses at the beginning applies to the upper one placed before him:

(ח) And he gives half, etc. — so that the two eatings that he eats for the sake of matzah should be in a piece; and it is good that that portion should be larger [Acharonim]:

(ט) Under the cloth — a remembrance of what is said "their leftovers tied in their garments"; and some place it on their shoulders as a remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt. And they should be careful to wrap the afikoman in a cloth that was not laundered with k'rachma'al, for cloths are generally laundered with k'rachma'al [Chayei Adam]:

(י) And he raises the plate — with everything that is on it, and he need not remove the cooked dishes:

(כ) Ha lachma anya — and some say k'ha lachma anya. And he must say it in a loud voice:

(ל) And he says in the language, etc. — meaning from Ha lachma anya onward, which is the beginning of the Haggadah:

(מ) In a language that they understand, etc. — and he fulfills therewith what the verse said "and you shall tell your son," etc.:

(50) (Sed) So that the women understand - after all, women are also obligated to recite the night mitzvah and to recite the Haggadah, etc., in the sign of Ta'ab Sidd, and therefore the obligation is also on the maid to sit at the table and listen to the entire Haggadah, and if they have to go outside to cook, she is obligated to hear the Kiddush, and when he comes to the rabbi, he says whatever he did not say, etc. Ten plagues that the Omnipresent brought upon Egypt to tell them how many miracles the Omnipresent had done for Israel [8]:

(ס) And to place them at the end of the table — and if the table is small, remove them entirely from the table, for otherwise there is no change for the child [Eliyah Rabbah]:
