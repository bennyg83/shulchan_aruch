(51) For the need of the first dipping. As he explains going forward. And the reason is that anything whose dipping is in liquid requires netilas yadayim, as above in siman 158.
(52) And he should not recite a blessing, etc. And as the Mechaber ruled above in siman 158, se'if 1; and see there in the Mishnah Berurah the Gra's view on this matter.
(53) Less than a kezayit. Because with a kezayit there is a doubt regarding the after-blessing whether he should recite it or not; therefore it is better that he eat less than a kezayit so that there be no obligation at all according to all opinions.
(54) In vinegar. Or in wine or in salt water; and this comes only to exclude that he should not dip in charoses, for charoses is only for the second dipping when one dips the maror in charoses (Beis Yosef).
(55) And he recites Borei Pri HaAdamah. And he should intend to exempt through this blessing also the maror that he will eat afterwards (Acharonim).
(56) And he does not recite a blessing after it. Even if he ate a kezayit, because the first blessing also stands for the maror, as above; and therefore Birkas HaMazon that exempts the maror that he eats during the meal also stands for the karpas that he ate beforehand; and see in Biur Halacha what is written in the name of the Gra on this.
(57) The middle one, and he breaks it into two. In order to fulfill what Scripture called it bread of poverty, and a poor man's way is with a broken piece; and he said "the middle one" because the blessing of eating matzah primarily stands upon it, and the blessing of HaMotzi that he recites first stands upon the upper one placed before him.
(58) And he gives half of it, etc. So that the two eatings that he eats for the sake of matzah be with a broken piece; and it is good that that portion be larger (Acharonim).
(59) Under the cloth. A remembrance of what it says "their kneading-troughs bound up in their garments"; and there are those who place it on their shoulders as a remembrance of the Exodus from Egypt. And they should be careful to wrap the afikoman in a cloth that was not washed with starch, for the cushions are presumably washed with starch (Chayei Adam).
(60) And he raises the plate. With everything that is upon it, and one need not remove the cooked dishes.
(61) Ha lachma. And some say "Keha lachma." And one must say it in a loud voice.
(62) And he says in the language, etc. That is to say, from "Ha lachma" and onward, which is the beginning of the Haggadah.
(63) In a language that they understand, etc. And he fulfills therewith what Scripture said "and you shall tell your son," etc.
(64) So that the women understand. For behold women too are obligated in the mitzvos of the night and in saying the Haggadah, as above in siman 472, se'if 14; and therefore the obligation is also upon the maidservant that she sit by the table and hear the entire Haggadah. And if they need to go outside to cook, at the very least she is obligated to hear Kiddush; and when he reaches Rabban Gamliel who says "whoever has not said," etc., she should enter and hear until after drinking the second cup, for behold one who has not said these three things has not fulfilled; and they customarily also call them so that they hear the order of the ten plagues that the Holy One, Blessed be He, brought upon Egypt, in order to tell them how many miracles the Holy One, Blessed be He, performed for Israel (Chayei Adam).
(65) And to place them at the end of the table. And if the table is small, remove them entirely from the table, for otherwise there is no change for the child (Olat Shabbos).
(66) And they will ask. [About] the matzos prepared for eating: why do they remove them? And he should tell them that they are not permitted to eat until he tells of the Exodus from Egypt. Maharil wrote that one should not place any matzah on the table until the time of the meal.
