(1) Less than a kezayit. From here it is implied that something dipped in liquid requires netilas yadayim even for less than a kezayit; yet regarding even plain bread the Mechaber wrote in siman 158, se'if 3, in the name of some authorities that one need not wash for less than a kezayit, and this is not as we wrote there in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 20; and I already noted this in the second printing in the margin. However, I have now found a Tanna who supports my words, namely the Rashbatz in Sefer Yavin Shemuah, folio 35, who wrote regarding karpas that the Rambam's view is that we require a kezayit, and he wrote that this is reasonable, for since netilas yadayim is required, certainly a kezayit is required, as with one who eats plain bread, for it is reasonable that one is not obligated in netilas yadayim [for less], for they certainly did not decree in such a case on account of resemblance to terumah; and likewise regarding fruits for something dipped in liquid; and Mateh Yehudah also cited this. Behold, this is exactly like our words; and for the practical law this requires further study.
(2) And he does not recite a blessing after it. See in the Mishnah Berurah; and the Gra wrote in his commentary that all this is according to the Mechaber's view in siman 474, that one recites only one blessing over the first two cups, because Hallel and the Haggadah are not an interruption, and therefore one does not recite an after-blessing, for one relies on the blessing of karpas and will eat the maror without a blessing; but according to the view of the gloss and according to our custom, that one recites a blessing over every cup, for one cannot rely on the blessing of the first cup because one interrupted with Hallel and the Haggadah, and if so one cannot say that for eating maror we rely on the blessing of karpas, since one interrupted with the Haggadah and Hallel (rather, the exemption of maror from a blessing is because it is like things that come during the meal, which are exempted by the blessing of HaMotzi), and if so one is obligated to recite an after-blessing after eating karpas, since it is not connected to the meal that comes afterwards; this is the substance of the Gra's words. And in truth the Magen Avraham was careful about this and wrote in siman 474 that every cup requires a blessing according to all opinions, even according to the one who holds that the Haggadah and Hallel are not an interruption, because each one is a mitzvah on its own, therefore one must recite a blessing over each one (as the Rif wrote at the beginning of his words in the name of the authorities); and according to the Magen Avraham the Gra's words are rejected; and like the Magen Avraham many later authorities wrote above in siman 178, se'if 6, aside from the Taz; see there. And one must say that here too one can say this reasoning, that karpas and maror are two mitzvos, and therefore one does not exempt its fellow; and this requires further study.
