In Mechaber seif 5 tamcha — n.b. notarikon: always tell the glory of the Almighty; and see responsa Orach Chayim siman 132 [there it is written in this language about the mitzvot of matzah and maror on Pesach — truly correct is what Chacham Tzvi wrote (and it is in siman 119), and the content of his words is that the chazeret mentioned in our Mishnah and in the language of the Sages is chasa, which is the head, and the first head mentioned in the Mishnah; and the mitzvah to return to it is the vegetable called in our country (salad), and so all my rabbis of blessed memory practiced and we practice after them; but I am accustomed to preach on Shabbat HaGadol: one who lacks designated people of fear who inspect and clean it of swarms of tiny worms found very much in Pesach days and not visible to weak eyes — therefore one who lacks in his house someone to inspect it properly should dip and take tamcha called horseradish; and if it is the third in the Mishnah and chasa is the first, nevertheless it is preferable from anywhere — Heaven forbid to stumble in a prohibition or many prohibitions even in safeik because of fulfilling a positive rabbinic mitzvah of maror in our time, which is rabbinic, especially since both can be fulfilled through tamcha] and we fulfill with stalks even dry and also with leaves — the view of Peri Chadash is that one fulfills with dried, and it is known by sense that dried and withered do not retain their taste in it and its smell is bitter much more than pickled in water from time to time, which Magen Avraham forbids below sk 14 and Kaf HaChayyim disagrees that water does not nullify taste, only pickling in vinegar; nevertheless it is also clear that honey is worse than pickling in vinegar so that it stands on the fire and begins to boil; and therefore it must be that there it is different, for bitter taste emerges through another power mixed in, namely the power of vinegar or even only water, unlike as long as the vegetable is by itself without another power it does not lose as long as some of its own bitterness is tasted — unlike dried leaves for those who forbid; and this is the reason they are accustomed to salt tamcha to nullify its bitterness somewhat so they can eat a kezayit of it; and so when we reclined at the table our master the pious one of priestly lineage, the honor of our teacher R. Nathan Adler, in the year 5545 in Vienna, no chasa was found for dipping without worms, and he commanded to salt tamcha and we ate it; and I expanded on this unlike the words of the Gaon in the Haggadah miracle story at first that the Gemara Berachot 39a forbids prima prima zita to sweeten taste — it is a fortiori from boiling in water see there; and per my view his a fortiori does not apply. And further it appears to me that if possible to eat Pesach and matzah with wormwood or even tamcha, which is not the normal way of eating, as Rambam wrote that mixing a bitter thing is not the normal way of eating — rather they nullify taste through prima zuta; and see below siman 475 in Magen Avraham sk 10; and in truth crushed tamcha dipped in vinegar is eaten thus, and it must be if one soaks it in vinegar again he should not bless on eating maror anyway after he filled his belly he should not bless again on eating maror:
