[1] [Levush] And one need not wash, etc. So wrote Rama, and Taz agreed, and so is widespread custom not to wash; and if hands are not clean he washes a little but does not recite on washing; for first dipping wash without berakha, for second dipping wash with berakha. What Magen Avraham challenged — that all year one does not wash hands for dipping — is no difficulty, as Levush wrote seif 6; and this is Maharil's wording: if one washes before kiddush it appears the law is like Beit Shammai who washes hands and then sanctifies — until here. So he explained what Darkei Moshe wrote — that Beit Shammai's view is kiddush requires washing, meaning they wash before pouring the cup, i.e., kiddush; investigate, for I abbreviated; Magen Avraham wondered in vain; Ma'agalei Tzedek wrote the reason: Hagadah recitation is an interruption between washing and eating.
