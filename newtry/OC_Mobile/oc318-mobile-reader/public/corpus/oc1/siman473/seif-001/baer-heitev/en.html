Shehecheyanu. And if he did not say it on the first day, he recites the blessing on the second day or on one of the other days when he remembers; and if he has no cup of wine at the time of Kiddush, he recites Shehecheyanu without a cup. And it appears to me that even if he recited [it] on the first [day] and did not recite it on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora, he recites [it] all seven days (Magen Avraham). And Chok Yaakov wrote: and it appears that the same applies on Shavuos, even though it is only two days; nevertheless he has a make-up all seven days. And further it appears to me that if he forgot to say Shehecheyanu on the first night and on the second night said Shehecheyanu, he need not again say Shehecheyanu on account of the first night for make-up: for if the first day is holy, then he completed Shehecheyanu on the second day; and if the second day is holy, behold he already said Shehecheyanu (Chok Yaakov); see there. And if he forgot to say Kiddush at night, its law is as above in siman 271, se'if 8; and so wrote Elya Rabbah at the end of siman 485. And if he forgot to say the Haggadah, he has no make-up at all, for it is written "for the sake of this," at a time when matzah and maror are set before you; and so wrote the Magen Avraham below in siman 485; see there. And one does not say "Who performed miracles," because he is going to say it in the Haggadah (Tur and Chok Yaakov).
On Shabbos. And it is permitted to say the Haggadah from a book even though he is alone, and we are not concerned that perhaps he will tilt [the lamp], as is written in siman 275, se'if 9 (Chok Yaakov).
He should make Havdalah. And if he remembered in the middle of the meal that he did not make Havdalah, he makes Havdalah in the middle of his meal over the cup; and if his intention was to drink wine during the meal, he does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen; and if that was not his intention, he must recite a blessing. And he should not wait to make Havdalah until after the meal, for if so it would be like adding to the cups; and one should not say to him that he say Havdalah and Hallel over one cup, for one cannot [place] two sanctities on one cup; specifically Kiddush and Havdalah are permitted, for both are one matter. And if he forgot to make Havdalah until after Birkas HaMazon, he says Hallel and Havdalah over one cup, for there is no other way (Magen Avraham); and see above, siman 296.
Kiddush. See the later authorities on this. And the main ruling is like the Rama's ruling; so is the consensus of the Taz: that one who sees him washing his hands thinks he will also recite Al Netilas Yadayim as during the rest of the year, and in truth it is forbidden to recite the blessing, since this washing is not for eating matzah, for [there] is a major interruption; and so is the custom.
