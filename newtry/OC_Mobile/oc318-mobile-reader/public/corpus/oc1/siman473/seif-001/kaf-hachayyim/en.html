1) [Se'if 1] The first cup is poured for the leader, etc. The cups require rinsing inside and washing outside because they are cups of blessing. Derashos Maharil, order of the Haggadah; Rokeach, siman 283. Mahari Weil writes likewise in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, as cited by Darkei Moshe, siman 486, subsection 2. Chayei Adam, rule 130, subsection 10, writes likewise. Chayei Adam writes there that if the cups are clean, they need not be rinsed and washed between cups; rinsing the first cup is sufficient. Nevertheless, see above, siman 183, se'if 1, and our words there in subsection 4. We wrote that according to the sacred Zohar and Tikkunei Zohar, the cup used for Birkas HaMazon requires rinsing and washing even when clean. See there. It is therefore proper to keep a vessel filled with water near the table so that the cups can be rinsed and washed between cups. Siddur HaMinhagim; Tosafos Chaim on Chayei Adam, there, subsection 34. See also below, subsection 142, and siman 479, subsection 1.
2) There. The first cup is poured for the leader, etc. See above, siman 472, subsection 70, and below, siman 474, subsection 2.
3) There. And one recites Kiddush over it, etc. Before Kiddush, one should say this formula: For the sake of unifying the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah, through the interwoven Name of Havayah and Adnus, with fear and love, through the corresponding interwoven Name, and with love and fear, through the corresponding reverse interwoven Name, to unite the yud-hei with the vav-hei in complete unity, Havayah, in the name of all the Jewish people and in the name of every nefesh, ruach, neshamah, chayah, and yechidah associated with the roots of our nefesh, ruach, neshamah, chayah, and yechidah, their garments, those close to them, and those joined to them, from the totality of Atzilus, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah, and from every particular aspect of Atzilus, Beriah, Yetzirah, and Asiyah within every partzuf and sefirah. We now come, with the help of Heaven, to fulfill all the mitzvos observed on this night, the night of Pesach, the first night of the Festival of Matzos. They are: the mitzvah of sanctifying the day over the first cup of wine; the first washing of the hands because of dipping karpas; eating karpas; the positive commandment to recount the Exodus from Egypt, as the verse states, 'You shall tell your child on that day,' and so forth; reciting the blessing of redemption over the second cup of wine; the second washing of the hands for the meal; the blessing over washing the hands; the blessing of HaMotzi and eating matzah; the positive commandment to eat matzah, as the verse states, 'In the evening you shall eat matzos'; eating maror to remember that the Egyptians embittered the lives of our ancestors in Egypt, and dipping it into charoses in remembrance of the mortar; wrapping matzah and maror together according to the opinion of Hillel the Elder; eating the afikoman in remembrance of the korban Pesach; washing the hands with mayim acharonim; the positive commandment of Birkas HaMazon and reciting it over the third cup of wine; reciting Hallel; and drinking the fourth cup of wine. May all these mitzvos rectify their roots in the supernal realm through that Hidden and Concealed One. May the pleasantness, and so forth.
4) There. And one recites Kiddush over it. See above, siman 472, subsection 51, where we wrote that the first cup corresponds to chochmah within binah. See there. With the first cup, one should intend that the cup itself has the numerical value of Elokim, but one should contemplate its letter substitution, the Name Akdatam. The letters lamed, hei, and yud of Elokim are exchanged for the preceding letters, kaf, dalet, and tes, while alef and mem are not exchanged. The five fingers of one's right hand correspond to the five letters of Akdatam. The wine in the cup also alludes to the Name Akdatam, because wine has the numerical value seventy. Together with the five fingers holding it, the total is seventy-five, equal to Akdatam when its collective unit is included. When reciting Havayah in the blessing of Kiddush, one should intend the Name of seventy-two, corresponding to chochmah.
4) The second cup corresponds to binah within binah. One should intend the Name Akda'am as divided into two groups, alef-mem and gimel-lamed, because kaf-dalet-ayin has the numerical value of gimel-lamed. When reciting Havayah in the blessing Asher Ge'alanu, one should intend the Name of sixty-three, corresponding to binah.
4) The final two cups correspond to chesed and gevurah within daas. Therefore, one should not drink between the third and fourth cups, so as not to separate them. The third cup corresponds to the chasadim within daas. One should intend the Name Akda'am in its numerical calculation, which is seventy-four, and with the collective unit, seventy-five. This is three times the unification Yabok, meaning three combinations of Havayah and Elokim, each having the numerical value Yabok. One should also intend the Name of forty-five, corresponding to the chasadim within daas. The final cup corresponds to the gevuros within daas. One should intend the Name Akda'am in its numerical calculation, which is seventy-four, and the Name of fifty-two, corresponding to the gevuros within daas. Nevertheless, one should combine the two sets of intentions together for the first cup and then repeat both for the second cup, as explained in Sha'ar HaKavanos and Eitz Chaim, chapters 2 and 3. See there.
5) There. And one recites Kiddush over it. One should recite Kiddush while standing. Seder HaYom. This is not our custom unless Pesach falls on Shabbos, because of Vayechulu. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef. He writes likewise in Pesach Me'ubin, subsection 208. According to the Arizal, however, every Kiddush, whether for Shabbos or Yom Tov, should be recited while standing, as stated above in siman 271, subsection 62. See there. Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, subsection 1, writes likewise. He writes that when Pesach falls on a weekday one begins, 'These are the appointed festivals of Havayah,' and so forth. See there. When it falls on Shabbos, one first says, 'The sixth day,' and Vayechulu, then, 'These are the appointed festivals of Havayah,' then Savri Maranan, the blessing over wine, the blessing sanctifying the day, and Shehecheyanu. Before everything, one should recite the chapter of Tehillim beginning, 'A psalm of David: Havayah is my Shepherd,' as stated above in siman 271, subsection 63. See there.
5) Know that Kiddush on Yom Tov is not required under Torah law, as stated above in siman 271, subsection 6. See there. See also above, siman 472, subsection 83.
6) There. And one recites Shehecheyanu, etc. This exempts the matzah, over which Shehecheyanu is not separately recited. Rabbi Yosef Molcho in a manuscript responsum; Birkei Yosef, subsection 1. Therefore, when reciting Shehecheyanu during Kiddush, one should intend to include the mitzvah of eating matzah. See below, siman 643, se'if 1.
7) The reason one does not recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv on the festival is that this benefit is not shared with another person. It resembles receiving rain when one does not jointly own a field, in which case the established law in siman 221 is that no blessing is recited. Rabbi Yosef Molcho in manuscript responsum, siman 101; Birkei Yosef, subsection 2. Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 58, writes that instead of HaTov VeHaMeitiv, the Sages instituted the festival prayers and the words in Kiddush, 'You have chosen us,' and so forth. No benefit is greater than this. See there.
