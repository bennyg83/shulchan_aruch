And he recites Shehecheyanu. If he did not recite it on the first day, he recites it on the second day, or on one of the other days when he remembers. If he does not have a cup of wine at Kiddush, he recites Shehecheyanu without a cup. Riaz in Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter Bameh Me'arvin, and Magen Avraham write likewise. This is also explicit in the Gemara, chapter Lulav VaAravah, and a similar ruling is explained in the laws of Chanukah, siman 677; see there. Magen Avraham further writes: “It appears to me that the same applies if he forgot to recite Shehecheyanu on the second night, for he may recite it throughout all seven days.” It appears that the same applies to Shavuos. Although it lasts only two days, its offerings may be made up throughout all seven days. It also appears obvious that if he forgot to recite Shehecheyanu on the first night and recited it on the second night, he need not recite it again as compensation for the first night. If the first day was sacred, he made up the blessing on the second day; and if the second day was sacred, he recited it at its proper time. If he forgot Kiddush at night, the law is as stated above in siman 271, seif 8. Olas Shabbos writes likewise at the end of siman 485. And if he forgot to recite the Haggadah, it is obvious that there is no possibility of making it up, for the verse states, “Because of this,” meaning at the time when matzah and maror are placed before you. Magen Avraham writes likewise below in siman 485 and challenges Olas Shabbos, who understood his words as referring to the Haggadah. This is incorrect, for Olas Shabbos wrote only the law of one who forgot Kiddush, as stated.
Shehecheyanu. We do not recite Sheasah Nissim, because we are destined to mention the miracle in the Haggadah. Thus writes the Tur. Maharil gives another reason; see there. Rokeach, Agudah, and Kol Bo write that we do not recite a blessing commanding us to drink four cups because each cup is a separate mitzvah and there is an interruption between them.
And if it falls on Shabbos. It is permitted to recite the Haggadah on Pesach even when one is alone, and we are not concerned that he may tilt the lamp, as explained above in siman 271, seif 9. This is unlike the opinion of Mahari Weil, siman 193, who forbids it.
He recites Yaknehaz. We do not recite a blessing over spices when Motzaei Shabbos falls on Yom Tov. Many reasons have been given; see Tosafot and Rosh in chapter Arvei Pesachim. And Magen Avraham writes that Havdalah is recited while seated, like Kiddush, and the custom is indeed to sit. It appears to me that the reason is as explained above in siman 422: although Hallel is generally recited specifically while standing, on the night of Pesach it is recited while seated, in a manner of reclining and freedom. The same therefore applies to Kiddush and Havdalah. This is unlike Knesses HaGedolah, who writes that one should stand when the first night falls on Motzaei Shabbos.
He should complete the Haggadah. Levush writes, “He should not interrupt the Haggadah,” implying that the reason is that one may not interrupt during the Haggadah. I have not found this in any posek, however. Why should he not interrupt the telling of the Haggadah for the mitzvah of Havdalah, especially between sections? See above, siman 472, seif katan 2. Rather, the reason appears simple. Havdalah requires a cup. If he were to recite Havdalah during the Haggadah, he would afterward interrupt between Havdalah and the blessing over the cup by continuing the Haggadah. Nor can one say that he should recite the blessing over that cup and then pour another cup for the Haggadah, because this would appear as though he were adding to the prescribed cups, since he took a designated cup for Havdalah. Since the matter can be arranged otherwise, we do so. See seif 3.
And afterward he should recite Havdalah. Magen Avraham writes that if he remembers in the middle of the meal that he did not recite Havdalah, he should immediately recite Havdalah over a cup. He should recite Borei Pri HaGafen if he had not intended to drink wine during the meal. He should not wait until after the meal, because that would appear as though he were adding to the cups, since Havdalah and Hallel should not be recited over one cup. If he did not remember until after Birkas HaMazon, he should recite Hallel and Havdalah over one cup, because there is no other possible arrangement. Thus far the words of Magen Avraham. See above, siman 296.
And he should not wash his hands at all before Kiddush. The later authorities express many opinions on this matter. In my humble opinion, the primary ruling follows the Rama, and Taz agrees. One who sees him washing his hands will assume that he also recited the blessing Al Netilas Yadayim, as during the rest of the year. In truth, he may not recite the blessing, because this washing is not for eating matzah, from which there will be a substantial interruption.
He should not pour it himself. This is also the Mechaber's intent in writing, “Others pour for him.”
