(1) And he makes Kiddush over it. Before he makes Kiddush he should intend that he wishes to fulfill the mitzvah of Kiddush and also the mitzvah of the four cups, for the cup of Kiddush is one of the four cups; and there are those who customarily say "Behold I am ready to make Kiddush and to fulfill the mitzvah of the four cups," only that they must be careful not to say this after Kiddush, for he already recited Borei Pri HaGafen over the cup; rather specifically before Kiddush. And before the Haggadah he should intend or say [that he wishes] to fulfill the mitzvah of telling the Exodus from Egypt (Chayei Adam). And he recites Shehecheyanu: before drinking; and if he did not recite before drinking, he recites afterwards whenever he remembers on the first day, and even in the middle of the marketplace, for Shehecheyanu applies to the festival; and if he remembered on the second night after the day was sanctified, he exempts himself with the Shehecheyanu that he will recite over the cup after Kiddush; and if he forgot to recite Shehecheyanu in Kiddush of the second night, even if he already recited on the first night, he is obligated to recite whenever he remembers throughout the festival, that is, until the end of the last Yom Tov of the Diaspora; and likewise the same applies on other Yamim Tovim: he is obligated to recite until the end of Yom Tov.
(2) On Shabbos. And it is permitted to say the Haggadah from a book even though he is alone, and we are not concerned that perhaps he will tilt [the lamp], as is clarified above in siman 275, se'if 9; see there.
(3) He says Yaknehaz. That is: first the blessing of the wine and Kiddush and lamp, namely the blessing Borei Me'orei HaEsh, and Havdalah and Shehecheyanu. And one does not recite a blessing over spices on any Motzaei Shabbos leading into Yom Tov. And they say this Havdalah while seated, like Kiddush.
(4) He should complete, etc. For to make Havdalah immediately when he remembered he cannot, for Havdalah requires a cup and the one who blesses must taste, and in the middle of the Haggadah it is forbidden to interrupt with drinking. And from the Mechaber's language who wrote "until he began the Haggadah," it is implied that if he remembered before he began the Haggadah he need not wait, but immediately mixes a cup and says over it the blessing of Havdalah and drinks it; however, this is only according to the Mechaber's view that one does not recite a blessing over every cup; but according to the Rama's view in siman 474 in the gloss, and according to our custom, that one recites a blessing over every cup and he would need to recite Borei Pri HaGafen over it, if so it appears like adding to the cups, which is forbidden, as above in siman 472, se'if 7; if so, perhaps even if he remembered before he began the Haggadah there is also no repair until he completes the Haggadah, unless at the time of the blessing of the first cup he intended that he would drink between the cups, as above in siman 472, se'if 7; and see Biur Halacha. If he forgot to say the Haggadah at night, he has no make-up at all, for it is written "for the sake of this," at a time when matzah and maror are set before you. And if he forgot to say Kiddush at night, its law is as above in siman 271, se'if 8.
(5) And afterwards he should make Havdalah. That is, he recites Borei Pri HaGafen over the second cup and also the blessing of the lamp and the Havdalah of Yom Tov and drinks it. And if he remembered in the middle of his meal that he did not make Havdalah, he is obligated to interrupt immediately from his meal and make Havdalah, and he should not recite Borei Pri HaGafen over it, for the blessing of the second cup that he drank before eating exempts him, unless at that time he did not intend to drink during the eating, for in such a case he must also recite Borei Pri HaGafen over it. And if he remembered during Birkas HaMazon that he did not make Havdalah, he recites Birkas HaMazon and Havdalah over one cup; and likewise if he did not remember until after he drank the cup of Birkas HaMazon, he should wait until after completing Hallel and the Haggadah, and then make Havdalah over the fourth cup; and if he did not remember until after he drank the fourth cup, he should make Havdalah over a fifth cup, and he must also recite Borei Pri HaGafen over it, for he already diverted his attention from drinking (Acharonim).
(6) And one should not wash his hands, etc. That is to say, even according to those who all year wash their hands for the meal before Kiddush, and for them Kiddush is not considered an interruption between washing and the meal since it is of the needs of the meal, as is clarified in siman 271: nevertheless on Pesach night, when they interrupt a great deal after Kiddush with saying the Haggadah and in the meantime he diverts his attention from guarding his hands, according to all one should not do so; and even to wash his hands so that he not need to return and wash his hands after Kiddush for eating karpas is also not correct according to so many later authorities.
(7) He should wash a little. He wrote "a little" in order to heed the reason that Darkei Moshe brought, that one should not wash hands before Kiddush lest it appear that he holds Kiddush requires washing; and therefore he should not perform a complete washing.
(8) But one should not recite a blessing, etc. That is to say, even if he performs a complete washing, he should at the very least be careful not to recite Al Netilas Yadayim over it, for this washing does not count for him for the need of eating, as above, and it turns out that it is a blessing in vain.
(9) Only another should pour for him. If it is possible for him.
