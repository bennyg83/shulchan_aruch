(Magen Avraham seif kaf 13) It implies that one does not fulfill with wilted ones; Mishna Berurah: so wrote Riaz in the name of the Yerushalmi chapter 2 halachah 5 that leaves are invalid — and this is a very great stringency, for how will he act properly? If in water we are concerned for pickled; if he leaves it without water we are concerned for wilted — go out and see what the public does, for they are accustomed to fulfill with wilted ones. And in order to reconcile the custom I searched the source of the law in the Yerushalmi, and this is its language: "between wilted" (meaning it refers to the mishnah that reads "between wilted") — there is a baraita: two, but not wilted. Rav Chisda said: the one who says wilted [applies] to the stalk, and the one who says not wilted [applies] to the leaves, end of his words. And from this he ruled that one does not fulfill with wilted leaves. And behold, this is per the text of the Yerushalmi that reads in the mishnah "between wilted," which requires resolving the baraita so it should not contradict the mishnah; but according to our text that reads "between dry ones," one can say the baraita comes according to one opinion and we rule like R' Meir and his colleagues and like Ra'avad, and so I found in Ra'avan page 73 that one fulfills with wilted ones. And further, since R' Eliezer said only in the stalk, but in leaves moist ones — not dry ones — from that R' Eliezer said "dry" and did not say "wilted," it implies wilted ones are valid; the truth was to explain his words as Rashbam wrote in Pesachim page 101 s.v. lekiv'a kama, etc.; and that which he said "moist" is not precise, as the Rosh wrote in the chapter of stolen lulav siman 10 see there — and therefore the poskim omitted this law, for they did not need it: individual and many — the halachah is like the many, and examine:
