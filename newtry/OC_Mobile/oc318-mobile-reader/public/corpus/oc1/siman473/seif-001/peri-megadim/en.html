And there is no Taz: Beit Yosef understood that Mordechai refers to the first dipping — that if he washes hands before Kiddush and blesses al netilat yadayim, he will need at the first dipping of vegetables in salt water to wash again, and the berachah of al netilat yadayim of the first [washing] will be in vain because of the interruption; and therefore Beit Yosef wondered, for Kiddush alone is not an interruption as on other days of the year. And Taz explained that Mordechai refers to the second dipping — maror in charoset — and it is not specifically the second dipping but rather it refers to HaMotzi, for he needed to wash again and bless al netilat yadayim for HaMotzi, for the Haggadah is an interruption and the first berachah is in vain; and the language of the Gemara Pesachim 116b holds as Tosafot wrote there s.v. tzarich. And that which Mordechai wrote "because he needs," etc. — emend and it is fitting that it should read: because for this reason he needs to wash hands at the second dipping even though he washed at the first dipping, for the Haggadah is an interruption; all the more so if he washes before Kiddush it is obvious there is interruption and he will need to wash again for the second dipping and for HaMotzi. Or one must emend and the reading is: even though if he washes before Kiddush he will still need to wash for dipping 2 and for HaMotzi, etc.; and see N'tziv who explained like Taz. And that which N'tziv wrote there that Mordechai holds for dipping in liquid one need not bless al netilat yadayim — one can explain likewise, but since Kiddush is an interruption on Pesach according to Beit Yosef's explanation in Mordechai, therefore berachah 1 of al netilat yadayim was in vain and by necessity he must afterward bless a second time al netilat yadayim; however that which Taz wrote plainly that the Haggadah is an interruption — one can explain: behold in siman 475 Beit Yosef at the beginning of the siman brought in the name of Mordechai that if from the outset he intended for this and did not divert his attention, he is permitted; Magen Avraham brought it there sign 1 and explains that which is written in the Gemara "perhaps diverting attention" means without intention, but if he intended for this and did not divert attention it is fine; if so, why is it different on Pesach from all days of the year — behold there is a remedy as written, and Eliyah Rabbah 475 and see there it will be explained. And see Levush in seif 1: he wrote if he washes before Kiddush he will need at the first dipping to wash and bless, and the second berachah will be in vain, see there; and he follows his view in seif 6 that even on other days of the year one does not bless al netilat yadayim for dipping in liquid; on Pesach one blesses al netilat yadayim for dipping in liquid (and the custom is not so even on Pesach, see below); and if so one should explain Mordechai thus, and N'tziv's question is not difficult either; and for the halachah: if he washed hands before Kiddush and blessed al netilat yadayim, it is obvious — whoever did not divert his attention during the Haggadah and did not touch an unclean place, it is forbidden to bless again al netilat yadayim for HaMotzi; and as a mere stringency he should wash hands for HaMotzi without berachah al netilat yadayim, for in the Gemara it says that according to one opinion "diverting," etc.; and see siman 158 seif 7 in the Hagah and Taz there sign 11, that even when he washed hands for dipping in liquid and did not bless al netilat yadayim and did not intend that this washing should count for HaMotzi, nevertheless he should not bless afterward al netilat yadayim for HaMotzi as long as he did not divert his attention; and if so it is fitting for everyone when he washes hands for dipping 1 that he should intend explicitly not to fulfill with this washing for HaMotzi; and proof from what Rashal wrote Yoreh De'ah siman 3: even though non-sacred meat does not require intention, nevertheless if he intended to gouge and uproot the opposite of the intention, it is invalid — the same here; and see 198 siman 61 in niddah who went down to inspect: it appears at first glance that even intention not for immersion but only to inspect, immersion applies to her; and if so it is better that before HaMotzi he bring himself to obligation of washing by pouring on his feet or foot-water and rubbing, and bless al netilat yadayim, for whoever acts out of doubt does not bless a berachah that is not needed, and Eliyah Rabbah;
