And one should not wash his hands before Kiddush. Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Mordechai that it is permitted to wash one's hands before Kiddush, for it is not a diversion of attention; but on Pesach, where there is a major interruption in between, the head of the household should not wash his hands before Kiddush, because he must wash his hands another time for doing the dipping, and it turns out that the first blessing was in vain, etc. And Beis Yosef wondered at this: what interruption is there more on Pesach than on Shabbos and Yom Tov? And it appears that there is no difficulty, for it is stated in the chapter "Arvei Pesachim": if he washed his hands for the first dipping, he should wash his hands for the second dipping. And they explain the reason: since he must say the Haggadah and Hallel, his mind was diverted and he touched an unclean place. And Tosafos explained that this "second dipping" is not literal, for it is after HaMotzi; rather it refers to the washing for the meal: that you should not say that since he washed for the first dipping it also counts for him for the bread, for ordinary food does not require intent; it teaches us [otherwise]. And one needs to emend a bit in the Mordechai in what he wrote "because he must," etc., and one should say "therefore he must"; and this is what he means: one should not wash before Kiddush for the sake of the meal on Pesach, and he brings a proof: just as we say that one must wash a second time for the bread because he interrupted between the first washing with the Haggadah and Hallel, so too if he washes his hands before Kiddush, all the more so are the Haggadah and Hallel considered an interruption before the eating of matzah. And even without the emendation one can explain it so: because in any case he must wash his hands for the bread, it is clear that there is an interruption here; and that he mentioned "to do the dipping" follows the language of the Gemara, and in the Gemara that [language] is not literal, as is written in the name of Tosafos. And Maharshal wrote in his Seder that one should wash his hands beforehand so as not to change from other Kiddushes during the year; only that nevertheless he should bring himself to an obligation of washing by covering his feet and rubbing, so that he be obligated to wash before Kiddush, and he should not recite Al Netilas Yadayim but rather Asher Yatzar; and if so, automatically for the dipping he does not need washing, for this dipping is no better than actual eating during the whole year, where Kiddush is not an interruption and he eats by virtue of the washing before Kiddush, all the more so dipping; thus far. And my master and father-in-law of blessed memory required washing a second time for the first dipping; and I do not know a repair in any way for this extra washing before Kiddush on Pesach: for if [it is] so as not to change from the rest of the year, behold it is necessarily a change, for now he does not recite Al Netilas Yadayim and all year he does recite; and further, this repair is a ruin, for when another sees that this one washes, he thinks he also recites a blessing over it as during the rest of the year, and in truth it is not fitting for him to recite a blessing now on Pesach because of the interruption, as above. Therefore what is most correct is as the Rama wrote according to the Mordechai, and it is a widespread custom that they do not wash until the first dipping. And as to what Maharshal wrote that one should not change from all year, I say: would that they would do so all year, to wash after Kiddush, as is written in siman 271, se'if 9.
