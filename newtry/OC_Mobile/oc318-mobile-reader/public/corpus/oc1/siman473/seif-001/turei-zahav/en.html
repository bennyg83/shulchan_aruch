One should not wash hands before Kiddush. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mordechai: it is permitted to wash hands before Kiddush, for it is not diversion of attention; but on Pesach, where there is a great interruption in between, Beit Yosef should not wash hands before Kiddush, because he needs to wash hands another time to perform the dipping, and the first berachah will be in vain, etc.; and Beit Yosef wondered on this: what interruption is there more on Pesach than Shabbat and Yom Tov? And it appears there is a dispute, for it is stated in the chapter of Arvei Pesachim: if he washed hands at the first dipping, he washes hands at the second dipping. And they explain the reason: since he needs to say the Haggadah and Hallel, he diverted his attention and touched an unclean place. And Tosafot explained: this second dipping is not precise, for it is after HaMotzi; rather it refers to washing for the meal — for non-sacred [meat] does not require intention, it teaches us; and one must emend somewhat in Mordechai in that which he wrote "because he needs," etc.; and it should read "therefore he needs," and the reading is: one should not wash before Kiddush on account of the meal on Pesach; and he brings proof as they say that one must wash again for bread because he interrupted between the first washing with the Haggadah and Hallel; and if so, if he washes hands before Kiddush, all the more so the Haggadah and Hallel will be considered an interruption before the eating of matzah; and even without the Hagah one can explain thus, because in any case he needs to wash hands for bread — thus there is interruption here; and that which he mentioned to perform the dipping follows the language of the Gemara, and in the Gemara it is not precise as written in the name of Tosafot. And Rashal wrote in his order that one should wash hands before, so as not to change from other Kiddushim of the year; but nevertheless he should bring himself to obligation of washing by pouring on his feet and rubbing, so that he should be obligated to wash from before Kiddush and not bless al netilat yadayim but rather Asher Yatzar; and if so, for dipping he does not need washing, for this dipping is not better than actual eating — for all year Kiddush is not an interruption and he eats through washing before Kiddush, all the more so dipping; and Maharach z"l required washing again for the first dipping, and I do not know the remedy in any matter for this extra washing before Kiddush on Pesach; for if on account of not changing from the rest of the year — behold he changes anyway, for now he does not bless al netilat yadayim and all year he blesses; and further, this remedy spoiled it, for when another sees this one washing he thinks he also blesses on it as the rest of the year, and in truth it is not fitting for him to bless now on Pesach because of the interruption as above; therefore the more correct [view] is as Rama wrote according to Mordechai, and it is the simple custom that they wash only until the first dipping. And that which Rashal wrote that one should not change from all year — I say: would that they would do so all year, to wash after Kiddush as written siman 471 seif 9:
