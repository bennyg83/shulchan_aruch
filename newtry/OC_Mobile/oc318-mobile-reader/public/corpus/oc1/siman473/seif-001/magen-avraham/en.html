Shehecheyanu. And if he did not recite the blessing on the first night, he recites it all seven days; and the same law applies on every Yom Tov (Eruvin folio 40). And it appears to me that even if he recited [it] on the first [day] and did not recite it on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora, he recites [it] all seven days.
And afterwards he should make Havdalah. And if he remembered in the middle of his meal that he did not make Havdalah, he makes Havdalah in the middle of his meal over the cup; and if his intention was to drink wine during the meal, he does not recite Borei Pri HaGafen, since there is a dispute, and a doubt regarding blessings is resolved leniently; see siman 299, se'if 3. And if that was not his intention, he must recite a blessing. And he should not wait until after the meal, for if so it would be like adding to the cups; and one should not say that he say Havdalah and Hallel over one cup, for one cannot [place] two sanctities on one cup; specifically Kiddush and Havdalah are permitted, for both are one matter, as is stated in Pesachim, for also regarding Havdalah we say "Who distinguishes between holy and holy." And if he forgot to make Havdalah until after Birkas HaMazon, he says Hallel and Havdalah over one cup, for there is no other way, as is written in siman 294, se'if 3.
And one should not wash his hands, etc. For if they washed before Kiddush, it would imply that they act like Beis Shammai, who hold that Kiddush requires washing; but all year it appears that he is washing for the meal; and even though here too the dipping after Kiddush requires washing, nevertheless it would not enter people's minds that he is washing for the dipping, and they would think he is washing for Kiddush; end quote (Darkei Moshe in the name of Maharil). It is implied that he holds it is forbidden to wash himself before Kiddush (and see siman 271, se'if 12). And nevertheless I do not know where we find that Beis Shammai hold that Kiddush requires washing; only in Berachos chapter 8 they disagree: Beis Shammai hold one washes the hands and afterwards they mix the cup, and Beis Hillel hold they mix the cup and afterwards wash the hands; and the reason is explained in the Gemara: because of impurity and purity according to Beis Hillel, because of an interruption between washing and HaMotzi. Therefore it appears to me that he holds like Beis Hillel, and specifically mixing is an interruption, as Tosafos wrote in the chapter "Arvei Pesachim," but Kiddush is not an interruption; but on Pesach, where there is a major interruption, therefore they wash adjacent to the meal; and all agree about this. And Maharshal wrote that one should take water before Kiddush and wash his hands, and not wash afterwards for the dipping; and the Bach wrote that nevertheless he should wash a second time for the dipping; and it appears to me as Maharshal wrote, for all year we do not wash hands for dipping, so [doing both] appears as contradicting one another; and see Beis Yosef, siman 158.
