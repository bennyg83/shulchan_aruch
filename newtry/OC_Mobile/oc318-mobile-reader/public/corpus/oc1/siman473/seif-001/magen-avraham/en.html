(א) Sheheheyanu. And if he did not bless on the first night he blesses all seven; and the same for every Yom Tov (Eruvin daf 40); and it appears to me that even if he blessed on the first and did not bless on the second day of Yom Tov of the diaspora, he blesses all seven.

(ב) And afterward he makes havdalah. If he remembered in the middle of his meal that he had not made havdalah, he makes havdalah in the middle of his meal on the cup; and if he had in mind to drink wine during the meal he does not recite borei peri hagafen, since there is a dispute — doubtful berakhot to be lenient — see siman 499 s"k 3; if he did not have in mind he must bless and should not wait until after the meal, for then it is like adding to the cups; and one should not say havdalah and Hallel on one cup, for two sanctifications on one cup are impossible — only kiddush and havdalah are permitted, since both are one matter, as Pesachim says: also in havdalah we say "who distinguishes between holy and holy"; if he forgot havdalah until after Birkat HaMazon he says Hallel and havdalah on one cup, for it is impossible otherwise, as siman 494 s"k 3.

(ג) And one should not wash the hands, etc. For if they wash before kiddush it implies they act like Beit Shammai, who hold kiddush requires washing; but all year it appears he washes for the meal, and although here too dipping after kiddush requires washing, nevertheless it should not enter people's minds that he washes for dipping and they think he washes for kiddush — end of Darchei Moshe in Maharil's name, who implies it is forbidden to wash oneself before kiddush (and see Orach Chayim 12); nevertheless I do not know where we find Beit Shammai hold kiddush requires washing — only in Berakhot chapter 8 they dispute: Beit Shammai: wash hands then pour the cup; Beit Hillel: pour then wash; the Gemara explains because of impurity and purity for Beit Hillel, because of interruption between washing and ha-motzi — therefore it appears we hold like Beit Hillel, and only pouring is interruption as Tosafot Arvei Pesachim, but kiddush is not interruption; on Pesach, where there is great interruption, they wash close to the meal — and all agree on this; Maharshal: take water before kiddush and wash hands, and not wash again for dipping; Bach: nevertheless wash again for dipping; I am not persuaded — as Maharshal, for all year we do not wash for dipping, so it appears contradictory — Abudraham siman 188.
