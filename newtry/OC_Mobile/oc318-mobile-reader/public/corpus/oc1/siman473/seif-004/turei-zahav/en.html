And they customarily [use] meat and an egg. For meat they customarily take a zeroa on account of "an outstretched arm," and it is a remembrance of the Pesach offering that was roasted on a spit; therefore they roast it on coals; and an egg is a remembrance of the Chagigah that they would offer on the fourteenth and that was eaten with the Pesach. And "egg" (beitzah) is the Aramaic language "baya," that is, "Hashem seeks (ba'ei) to redeem us." And Maharshal wrote: the Ashkenazic custom is that they roast the meat and the egg, namely because they do not eat them and they keep it for the second night; and this custom is against the custom of the Talmud; see the chapter "Arvei Pesachim"; and it is proper to distribute to each one, and they should be cooked, for one cannot [serve] roast; and I have not seen in the Talmud from this that one must eat them; rather, it somewhat appears the opposite, from what Rava said: meat need not be raised, and not only that, but it appears like one eating sacred food outside; Rashbam explained [regarding] the two cooked dishes: when he says "the Pesach that our fathers ate," he should not raise the meat that is a remembrance of the Pesach; behold it is forbidden to do raising with this meat for the reason that it appears as if he consecrated it while alive; and even though he says that our fathers ate, which implies of itself that we do not have a Pesach, even so it is forbidden to raise; if so, when he eats them and distributes them and says "a remembrance of the Pesach," there is certainly more reason to be concerned about this. And further, Rashbam and Tosafos wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that the meat should be roasted, which implies of itself that they do not eat [it]; also from what the Rama wrote that regarding the egg they customarily cook it, it appears the reason is for this intent: that the meat which they set as a remembrance they do not eat then, for it is only as a remembrance of the Pesach; but the egg has a further reason because of mourning for the Beis HaMikdash, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Kol Bo, and if so they eat the egg in the manner of mourners (Beis Meir). And they customarily cook many eggs on that night for this reason, as the Rama wrote in siman 477; and therefore the egg should specifically be cooked, because they do not eat roast on Pesach nights, as Maharshal wrote. As above, the custom of our fathers is correct, that they do not eat the meat, and they eat the egg, for even without [its being] a remembrance of the Chagigah it has a reason because of mourning, as is written; and in siman 477 it will be clarified that there is no prohibition of roast regarding an egg nor regarding fish; see there.
