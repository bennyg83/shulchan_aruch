(ב) And they are accustomed with meat and egg. For meat they are accustomed to take a foreleg on account of "outstretched arm," and it is a remembrance of the Pesach that was roasted on a spit; therefore they roast on coals. And an egg is a remembrance of the festival offering that they offered on the 14th and ate with the Pesach. And egg is the language of beya in Aramaic — meaning, the Merciful One seeks to redeem us. And Rashal wrote: the Ashkenaz custom to roast the meat and egg is because they do not eat them and keep them for the second night; and this custom is against the Talmud's custom — see chapter of Arvei Pesachim; and it is fitting to divide for each and they should be cooked, for it is impossible roasted; and I did not see in the Talmud from this that one must eat them; rather somewhat the opposite appears, for Rava said meat need not be raised; and not only that, but it appears as eating consecrated [meat] outside [the Temple]. Rashi explained: of the two cooked dishes — when he says "the Pesach that our forefathers ate," he should not raise the meat that is a remembrance of the Pesach; behold it is forbidden to make raising with this meat, lest it appear as if he consecrated it while alive; and even though he says that our forefathers ate, it implies we have no Pesach — nevertheless it is forbidden to raise; if so, when he eats them and divides them and says remembrance of the Pesach, there is certainly more reason to be concerned for this; and further Rashbam and Tosafot wrote in the name of R' Chananel that the meat should be roasted — thus they do not eat; also from what Rama wrote that the egg they are accustomed to cook, the reason appears for this intention: the meat that they leave for remembrance they do not eat then, for it is only on account of remembrance of the Pesach; but the egg has another reason on account of mourning for the Temple, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Kol Bo; and if so they eat the egg in the manner of mourners in Bava Metzia. And they are accustomed to cook many eggs on that night for this reason, as Rama wrote siman 477; and therefore the egg should specifically be cooked, because they do not eat roasted on Pesach nights, as Rashal wrote as above — the custom of our forefathers is correct that they do not eat the meat, and the egg they eat; for without remembrance of the festival offering it has a reason on account of mourning, as written; and in siman 477 it will be explained that there is no prohibition of roasted on egg or fish — see there:
