(17) They bring before the head of the household. But before the other members of his household one need not set [things] in this order; rather all take from the head of the household's [portion]. And this applies even in places where there is a small table before each and every one, and all the more so in our place where all sit at one table.
(18) That it has three matzos. Two for lechem mishneh as on other Yamim Tovim, and one in order to break it into two: half of it to fulfill with it the mitzvah of eating matzah, for the Merciful One called it bread of poverty, and what is a poor man's way? With a broken piece; and the second half for the afikoman, as is clarified below in se'if 6.
(19) And charoses. In order to dip the maror in it, as below in siman 475. And karpas is a species of vegetable that they call karpas, and they chose l'chatchila this species because it is a notarikon of "samech perekh," that is, six hundred thousand did hard labor (avodat perekh).
(20) Or another vegetable. Of whichever species it is; but l'chatchila it is good that he take from that species over which one recites that blessing that one recites over maror, so that he exempt through this the maror that he eats during the meal; but he should not take for this one of the five species of maror, for after he has filled his belly with maror, how shall he afterwards recite a blessing over eating maror? And see below in siman 475, se'if 2.
(21) Or salt water. To dip in them the karpas and the vegetable. If it falls on Shabbos he should make the salt water before Shabbos and not make it on Shabbos, as is written in siman 321, se'if 1; and if he did not make it before Shabbos and he has no vinegar to dip in them, he should make a little salt water, as is written there. And behold, the matter of dipping the vegetable in liquid is of the enactments of the Sages in order to astonish the children, so that they see a change: that they eat the vegetables by dipping, in a manner that is not the way to eat them before the meal on all days of the year, and they will ask about this change; for the mitzvah of saying the Haggadah is to say it by way of an answer to questions that they asked him, as it is said "when your son asks you," etc., "and you shall say to your son: We were slaves," etc.
(22) A remembrance of the Chagigah. And if erev Pesach falls on Shabbos, some say one need take only one cooked dish corresponding to the Pesach, for the Chagigah does not come then, since it does not override Shabbos; and some say that even so one must make two cooked dishes as in other years, and the Sages made no distinction in this since it is only a mere remembrance; and so is the custom.
(23) And they customarily [use] meat and an egg. And all the more so if he wishes to make two kinds of meat, one roast and one cooked, as a remembrance of the Pesach and the Chagigah, for that is fine; only that even with an egg he has fulfilled this. And there are those who wrote that what they chose with "beitzah" means "Hashem seeks (ba'ei) to redeem us." And there are those who wrote [it is] because we make a remembrance of mourning for the Beis HaMikdash, that we cannot offer the Pesach sacrifice.
(24) In a manner that he does not need, etc. That is to say, that he not need to skip over them.
(25) Above, etc. Meaning adjacent to it.
(26) And they should be closer, etc. And there are those who wrote that one need not be particular about meat and egg if he will need to skip over them, since they are not a mitzvah but a mere remembrance; and also regarding maror and charoses it is possible that "one does not pass over [mitzvos]" does not apply; and see in the later authorities the order of the plate of the Ari of blessed memory.
(27) Zeroa. On account of the outstretched arm that the Holy One, Blessed be He, showed in Egypt. And one who does not have a zeroa should take other meat even without a bone (Peri Megadim). The poskim wrote that we require that there be a little meat on the zeroa, for it is a remembrance of the meat of the Pesach sacrifice.
(28) It should be roasted. And not cooked, as a remembrance of the Pesach sacrifice.
(29) On the coals. And even though the Pesach was ideally roasted on a spit, nevertheless since it is roasted only on a spit of pomegranate, which requires trouble to seek after it, therefore it is enough for us with roasting on coals, for according to the main law one also fulfills with this for the Pesach sacrifice (Magen Avraham).
(30) And the egg should be, etc. For it is a remembrance of the Chagigah, which does not require roasting.
(31) And the same applies if roasted. For the Chagigah comes whether roasted or cooked.
(32) And so is the custom in our city. To roast the egg. And it is forbidden to eat the zeroa at night, for one does not eat roast at night; but the egg, even though it is roasted, is permitted, for there is no prohibition of roast regarding an egg. And according to this it is forbidden to roast the zeroa at night, but rather from while it is still day, unless his intention is to eat it tomorrow on that day; and therefore if he forgot and roasted it at night, he should be careful to eat it tomorrow in the morning; so wrote the Magen Avraham and other later authorities. And if his intention is not to eat the egg until the second night, the egg too is forbidden to roast at night, but rather from while it is still day. Chayei Adam wrote: the practice that they throw away the zeroa is bad in my eyes, for it is contempt of a mitzvah; and it is a mitzvah to place it on the second Yom Tov in the morning into the roast that they roast for Yom Tov and eat it.
