(א) They bring before the master of the house — but before the other members of his household they need not arrange in this order; rather all take from the master of the house's [portion]. This applies even in places where there is a small table before each one, and all the more so in our place where all sit on one table:

(ב) That it has three matzos — two for lechem mishneh as on other Yom Tov, and one in order to break it into two: half of it to fulfill therewith the mitzvah of eating matzah, for the Merciful One calls it "bread of affliction," and the way of a poor person is in a piece and a half; the second for afikoman, as explained below in seif 6:

(ג) And charoset — in order to dip the maror therein, as below in siman 475. And karpas is a type of vegetable called karpas, and they chose l'chatchila this species because it is a notarikon: sixty [times ten thousand], meaning six hundred thousand performed hard labor:

(ד) Or another vegetable — of any species; but l'chatchila it is good to take from that species over which one recites that berachah that one recites over maror, so that he should exempt through this the maror that he eats during the meal; but he should not take for this one of the five species of maror, for after he has filled his belly with maror how can he afterward bless on eating maror — and see below in siman 475 seif 2:

(ה) Or salt water — to dip therein the karpas and the vegetable. If it falls on Shabbat he should make the salt water before Shabbat and not make it on Shabbat, as written in siman 321 seif 1; and if he did not make it before Shabbat and he has no vinegar to dip therein, salt water should be made in small quantity, as written there. And behold, the matter of dipping the vegetable in liquid is a rabbinic enactment, in order to astonish the children so they see a change — that they eat vegetables by dipping, which is not their manner to eat them before the meal throughout all the days of the year — and they will ask about this change, for the obligation of reciting the Haggadah is to say it as an answer to questions that were asked him, as it is said: "when your son asks you," etc., "and you shall say to your son: we were slaves," etc.:

(ו) A remembrance of the festival offering — and if [the first day of Pesach] falls on Shabbat, some say one need take only one cooked dish corresponding to the Pesach, for the festival offering does not come then since it does not override Shabbat; and some say that nevertheless one must make two cooked dishes as in other years, and the Sages did not distinguish in this, since it is only a mere remembrance — and such is the custom:

(ז) And they are accustomed with meat and egg — and all the more so if he wishes to make two types of meat, one roasted and one cooked, as a remembrance of the Pesach and the festival offering, for that is fine; rather that he also fulfills through the egg. And some wrote that what they chose an egg — meaning, the Merciful One seeks to redeem us. And some wrote because we make a remembrance of the mourning for the Temple, for we are unable to offer the Pesach sacrifice:

(ח) In a manner that he does not need, etc. — meaning that he should not need to skip them:

(ט) Above, etc. — meaning next to it:

(י) And they should be closer, etc. — and some wrote that one need not be strict about the meat and egg if he will need to skip them, since they are not a mitzvah but a mere remembrance; and also regarding maror and charoset it is possible that "one does not pass over" does not apply — and see in the Acharonim the order of the seder plate of the Ari z"l:

(כ) Foreleg — on account of "and I shall pass by/outstretched arm" that Hashem showed in Egypt. And one who has no foreleg should take other meat, even without a bone [Peri Megadim]; the poskim wrote that we require there be a little meat on the foreleg, which is a remembrance of the meat of the Pesach sacrifice:

(ל) It should be roasted — and not cooked, as a remembrance of the Pesach sacrifice:

(מ) On the coals — and even though the Pesach was ideally roasted on a spit, nevertheless since it was only roasted on a pomegranate-wood spit, which requires trouble to turn it around, therefore it suffices for us with roasting on coals, which fulfills from the essence of the law also for the Pesach sacrifice [Magen Avraham]:

(נ) And the egg should be, etc. — since it is a remembrance of the festival offering, which does not require roasting:

(ס) And the same applies if roasted — for the festival offering comes whether roasted or cooked:

(ע) And thus is our custom in our city — to roast the egg. And it is forbidden to eat the [meat of the] foreleg at night, for one does not eat roasted [meat] at night; but the egg, even though it is roasted, is permitted, for there is no prohibition of roasted [meat] on an egg; and therefore it is forbidden to roast the foreleg at night except m'b'eod yom (before nightfall), unless his intention is to eat it tomorrow on that day; and therefore if he forgot and roasted it at night, he should be careful to eat it tomorrow morning — so wrote Magen Avraham and other Acharonim; and if his intention is not to eat the egg until the second night, the egg too is forbidden to roast at night except m'b'eod yom. Chayei Adam wrote: evil is this practice that they throw away the foreleg, and it is disgrace to a mitzvah. And it is a mitzvah to leave [it] on the second Yom Tov morning into the roast that they roast for Yom Tov, and they eat it:
