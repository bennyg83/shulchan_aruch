(א) Afterward. It is good to have live and cooked [vegetables] over which one blesses borei peri ha'adamah, as written siman 203; and custom is to take what they call parsley. And it is good to take karpas, which is a notarikon: 60 (sixty) ten thousands worked hard labor, and they call it in foreign tongue epich; and so Acharonim, and see Magen Avraham:

(ב) Salt water. And if [Pesach] falls on Shabbat he should make the salt water from erev Shabbat; and if he did not make it from erev Shabbat and has no vinegar he should make a little salt water, as written siman 321, Taz. And Magen Avraham wrote, and it appears to me that after he ate the vegetable it is permitted to remove the vegetable and salt water from the bowl:

(ג) For the chagigah. Even if it falls on erev Pesach on Shabbat. And see in Rosh what he wrote in the name of Mahari of Orleans and Mahari of Corbeil that their words are the opposite of the Gemara, and see Yad Efraim on this:

(ד) And he arranges. The order of the bowl of the Ari is copied from Etz Chayim, and thus: the order of the guarded [matzot] shall be in this order — one takes three guarded matzot and places Kohen on top, then Levi, and below him Yisrael, corresponding to the three mochin of Abba Ilaa; afterward one takes maror, karpas, charoset, and two kinds of cooked dish — a roasted lamb shank and a boiled egg — and places these five things above the three matzot: namely the shank, which was kindness at your right, and the egg, which is gevurah at your left; maror, which alludes to tiferet, in the middle between the shank and the egg, for tiferet mediates between kindness and gevurah; afterward charoset on the right line under the shank because it alluded to netzach; afterward karpas, which alludes to hod, place under the egg on your left line; afterward take chazeret and place below the maror on the middle line corresponding to yesod, in order to make afterward a korech with chazeret. And the bowl itself that includes them all is malchut — behold the ten sefirot of chochmah; and one should not change this order, and fortunate is one who intends for the above — end quote:

(ה) Shank. And there should be a little meat on it. Darkei Moshe:

(ו) Roasted. They eat it because it was a remembrance of mourning, but roasted meat they do not eat, and such is custom; and see Taz. And if he forgot to roast the meat or the egg on erev Yom Tov he should not roast at night unless his intention is to eat that night and day. Maharil and see Magen Avraham:
