Afterward. For it is good [to have] both raw and cooked [vegetables] over which one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah, as is written in siman 205; and the custom is to take what they call parsley. And it is good to take karpas, which is a notarikon of "samech perekh," that is, six hundred thousand did hard labor (avodat perekh); and in Ashkenazic they call it "Ipakh"; and so wrote the later authorities; and see the Magen Avraham.
Salt water. And if it falls on Shabbos, he should make the salt water from erev Shabbos; and if he did not make it from erev Shabbos and he has no vinegar, he should make a little salt water, as is written in siman 321 (Taz). And the Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that after he ate the vegetable it is permitted to remove the vegetable and the salt water from the plate.
For the Chagigah. Even if erev Pesach falls on Shabbos. And see in the Rosh what he wrote in the name of Mahari of Orleans and Mahari of Corbeil, that their words are the opposite of the Gemara; and see Yad Aharon on this.
And he should arrange. The order of the Ari's plate is copied from Sefer Etz Chaim, and this is its language: the order of the plate of the shemurah [matzos] shall be in this order: that you take three shemurah matzos and place the Kohen above, and after it Levi, and below it Yisrael, which correspond to the three brains of the Supernal Father; and afterwards take maror and karpas and charoses and two kinds of cooked food, a roasted shank of a lamb and a cooked egg, and place these five things above upon the three matzos: namely, the zeroa, which is Chesed, at your right; and the egg, which is Gevurah, at your left; maror, which alludes to Tiferes, in the middle between the zeroa and the egg, for Tiferes decides between Chesed and Gevurah; and afterwards the charoses on the right line under the zeroa, because it alludes to Netzach; and afterwards the karpas, which alludes to Hod, place under the egg on your left line; and afterwards take chazeres and place it below the maror on the middle line, which corresponds to Yesod, and it is in order afterwards to make a wrap with chazeres. And the plate itself that includes all of them is Malchus; behold, the ten sefiros of Chochmah; and one should not change this order; and fortunate is he who intends according to what is mentioned above; end quote.
Zeroa. And there should be a little meat on it (Darkei Moshe).
Roasted. And they eat it for the reason that it was a remembrance of mourning, but roasted meat they do not eat, and so is the custom; and see the Taz. And if he forgot to roast the meat or the egg from erev Yom Tov, one should not roast at night unless his intention is to eat that night and day (Mahari Weil); and see the Magen Avraham.
