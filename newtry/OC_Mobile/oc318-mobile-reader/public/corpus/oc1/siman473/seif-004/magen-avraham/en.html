(א) And karpas. Maharil wrote: custom to take karpas, notarikon: sixty ten thousands did arduous labor; his father was unsure whether permitted to take parsley because it is better cooked than raw — then no borei peri ha-adamah, as siman 200 — end; I wonder why our countries are accustomed to take parsley; perhaps they hold through meat they improve, as written; and per what is written in the name of Tur — only when not eaten at all raw does one recite she-hakol; but when eaten raw, even if better cooked, nevertheless one recites when raw borei peri ha-adamah — if so, no difficulty; still my view wavers why they chose something disputed — he should take another vegetable; perhaps other vegetables are not so available on Pesach, or because in the Gemara vegetables are mentioned and other things are called lefet, and our vegetable is not eaten raw unless pickled, unavailable on Pesach; Tur and Gemara say one also recites on maror borei peri ha-adamah — maror there must have been fit to eat raw like latukh — see there.

(ב) Salt water. Hagahot Maimoniot: if Pesach is Shabbat make salt water on erev Shabbat; but siman 321 s"k 2: whatever is needed for that meal may be done on Shabbat; it appears after eating the vegetable it is permitted to remove vegetable and salt water from the plate.

(ג) Above everything. Meaning: close to it.

(ד) Forearm (zeroa). Ran wrote: bone without meat is not called a cooked dish; therefore a little meat should be attached (Darchei Moshe).

(ה) On the coals. For the Pesach was roasted only on a pomegranate skewer and trouble to return to it (Beit Yosef, Hagahot Maimoniot); forbidden to eat, for one does not eat roasted on Pesach nights; therefore forbidden to roast it on Yom Tov, for Yom Tov does not prepare for another day; it appears if he forgot to roast until night he roasts at night and eats it morning of the first Yom Tov; Maharshal wrote to cook a piece of meat and divide among those reclining; so Bach and Shlah; if he does not wish to eat the egg until the second night, likewise forbidden to roast it the first night (Darchei Moshe, Mahari"v) — see 503.
