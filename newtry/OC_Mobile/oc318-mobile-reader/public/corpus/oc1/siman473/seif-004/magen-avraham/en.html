And karpas. Maharil wrote: they customarily take karpas, which is a notarikon of "samech perekh," that is, six hundred thousand did hard labor (avodat perekh); and his father was in doubt whether it is permitted to take parsley, because it is better cooked than raw, and if so one does not recite Borei Pri HaAdamah over it, as is written in siman 205; thus far. And I am astonished why in our country they are accustomed to take parsley; and perhaps they hold that by means of the meat they improve, as is written there; and according to what is written there in the name of Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah, specifically when they are not eaten raw at all does one recite Shehakol, but when they are eaten raw, even though they are better cooked, nevertheless when they are raw one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah; if so it is fine. And nevertheless my mind hesitates about this: why did they choose something about which there is a dispute? They ought to take another vegetable; and perhaps other vegetables are not so common in the days of Pesach; or alternatively, because in the Gemara a vegetable is mentioned, and other things are called "lefet," and our vegetable is not eaten as it is raw unless pickled, and this is not common on Pesach. And in the Tur and Gemara it is stated that also over maror one recites Borei Pri HaAdamah; and one must say that their maror was fit to eat raw, like lettuce; see se'if 5.
Salt water. It is written in Hagahos Maimoniyos that if it falls on Shabbos he should make the salt water from erev Shabbos; but in siman 321, se'if 2, it is stated that whatever is needed for that meal it is permitted to make on Shabbos. And it appears to me that after he ate the vegetable it is permitted to remove the vegetable and the salt water from the plate.
Above everything. Meaning adjacent to it.
Zeroa. The Ran wrote that a bone without meat is not called a cooked dish; therefore it appears that there should be a little meat attached upon it (Darkei Moshe).
On the coals. For the Pesach was roasted only on a spit of pomegranate, and there is trouble to seek after it (Beis Yosef; Hagahos Maimoniyos); and it is forbidden to eat it, for one does not eat roast on Pesach nights; and therefore it is forbidden to roast it on Yom Tov, for one Yom Tov does not prepare for another. And it appears to me that if he forgot to roast it until night, he should roast it at night and eat it in the morning on the first Yom Tov. And Maharshal wrote to practice that one cook a piece of meat and distribute it to those reclining; and so wrote the Bach and the Shelah. And if he does not wish to eat the egg until the second night, it is also forbidden to roast it on the first night (Darkei Moshe from Mahari Weil); see siman 503.
