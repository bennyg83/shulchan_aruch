(א) For his obligation — meaning for the obligation of the mitzvah of maror, to fulfill what is written: "upon matzos and bitter herbs shall they eat it"; and now that we have no Pesach, matzah and maror are only d'rabbanan. Just as one does not fulfill with stolen matzah, as above in siman 454, so one does not fulfill with stolen maror; and therefore one should be careful that a Jew should not uproot the maror himself from a gentile's land, even if he gives him permission, for generally gentiles are land-thieves; and abandonment does not apply to land, as below in siman 649 seif 1 in the Hagah — rather the gentile should uproot it himself, and then abandonment applies there, and afterward the Jew acquires it from him, for there is through this abandonment and change of ownership:

(ב) Meaning: types of bitter herbs — all of them are referred to; and because we do not know clearly which they are in our language, therefore he wrote in general "all of them," that they are bitter species; and the Acharonim wrote that tamcha is chrain in our language, and on chazeret wrote Chayei Adam, and likewise Chafetz Chaim which is what we call salatin:

(ג) Maror — it is also a species of bitter vegetable known to them by the name maror, on account of that it is very bitter:

(ד) But not in the root — meaning: small roots that branch to here and to there at its base or at its sides; but the large root in which the vegetable stands is the stalk. And behold, in our countries the other species are not found, and therefore the maror customary in our country to eat is tamcha (and it is chrain); and one should be careful not to eat it when it is whole, for it is nearly a danger and there is no mitzvah in it, for because of its sharpness it is greatly harmful; therefore one must grate it on a grater and leave it thus exposed, and then it mitigates its sharpness. And the Gra instituted not to grate it before his coming from the synagogue, so that he should not mitigate the taste, for then one does not fulfill with it; rather he should grate it after his arrival and cover it until the beginning of the seder [and when Pesach falls on Shabbat he should grate it m'b'eod yom and place it in a vessel and cover it until the beginning of the seder] and then he should scatter it on the plate and thereby mitigate its sharpness; and then he should crush it and estimate that there should be a k'zayit, and he can eat it properly:

(ה) Unless they are moist — dried ones have no taste of maror and behold they are as mere dust; not so in the stalk, for since it is thick, even if it is dry it does not lose its taste. And wilted leaves — some say one fulfills with them, and some are strict even with wilted ones:

(ו) But not pickled — that is, if he soaked them in water from the outset; and some say specifically if he pickled them in vinegar; and therefore l'chatchila one should be careful not to soak the chrain in water from the outset, and b'dieved one may rely to be lenient when he has no others, because in chrain we see that there is still in it taste of maror. And it appears to me that with leaves one should not be lenient, for possibly through pickling from the outset in water the taste of maror emerges in them. And know that one fulfills with leaves even l'chatchila. And behold, I have seen many weak people who press themselves to fulfill the mitzvah of maror with stalks, and I do not know why they do not take the leaves for the mitzvah — perhaps because dry and wilted ones are commonly found in them. However, if they are moist, one should not be strict in this at all:

(ז) And not boiled/softened — greatly boiled and boiled in the manner of cooking — all because they have no taste of maror through this:

(ח) And all of them, etc. — meaning all five species mentioned; and we do not say that since every vegetable certainly has a bitter taste by itself they do not combine, for the taste of one is nullified in its fellow — it teaches us that since in any case all of them have a taste of bitterness, they combine [Ran]:

(ט) To a k'zayit — it is the measure of half an egg (and see below siman 486); and if he fulfills the mitzvah with leaves, he should see to press them together, for the space between the leaves does not combine to a k'zayit; and one should be careful in this, for otherwise it is a berachah in vain, since he blesses on eating and eating implies a k'zayit; and also he does not fulfill therewith the mitzvah of maror:

(10) (MB) in hazrat - which is a reminder of the slavery of Egypt which had a soft beginning and at the end was hard, and yes, he created a horseradish that started sweet and ended bitter, and his last writings are even more precious than the rest of the bitter ones. (Shakorin Kharayan) even though it is third per the order that they are different because the Omnipresent forbid it fails because of the existence of a Durbanan, and in particular it is possible to have both of them by Timka:

(כ) He should seek first first — and one who is ill or delicate is permitted to take whichever species is more pleasant to him; and he may also eat the k'zayit little by little within the measure of k'vei'achalat peras, for per the essence of the law one fulfills thus, as below siman 475 seif 6; and if even so it is impossible for him because of his health, at minimum he should eat a little or chew in his mouth to remember the taste of bitterness, but he should not bless on this:

(ל) One of these vegetables — meaning the five mentioned:

(מ) He should take wormwood — see in Biur Halachah:

(נ) Or other bitter vegetable — those that have the signs mentioned in the Gemara regarding maror, that it should be fit for eating, and that it has sap (meaning: when they cut it, at the place of the cut white sap comes out like milk), and its leaves are pale (meaning its leaf is not very green like the leaves of onions and other vegetables, but leans somewhat to whiteness); nevertheless regarding berachah one should not bless on them, because we are not so expert in these signs:

(ס) He should make it thick — and make it m'eve Yom Tov; and if he forgot, it is permitted to make it on Yom Tov:

(ע) A little vinegar — of wine or red wine, in order to soften it and it shall be a remembrance of blood. If it falls on Shabbat he should put the liquid in it from erev Shabbat; and if he forgot, see above in siman 321 seif 16 and in Mishna Berurah there seif kaf 68:

(80) (Matt) such as apples - according to what is written under the apple, you have been awakened, as well as the rest of the fruits, all of which are written in the Song of Songs and almonds in the name that the Almighty the Omnipresent chose to make:

(צ) Resembling straw — that they are not ground well and they are long:
