Chazeres. It is lettuce; olshin is endives ("Korvel"); tamcha is horseradish ("Krin"). And the custom spread to take horseradish for maror and to recite the blessing over the leaves, and the stalk they take for korech. And in Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 119, he wrote that the chazeres taught in our Mishnah is the vegetable called in Ashkenazic "Salt" and in Spanish "Salata," and it has the signs stated in the Gemara; and because in the lands of Ashkenaz it is not common at the time of Pesach, they did not become accustomed to take it for maror, and they take horseradish, which is tamcha, and from this comes a ruin, that they cannot eat even half a kezayit because of its sharpness, and they nullify the mitzvah of maror; end quote; see there. And so I heard from a foreigner, that in foreign lands whose countries are warm, chazeres is found among them in the days of Nisan, and it is doubled and thick like what we call in these countries "Hayf Saltin," and they eat that species for maror; and see Chok Yaakov.
In the root. Meaning small roots that split here and there, but the large root on which the vegetable stands is included in the stalk (Mahari Weil); and see the Taz.
For a kezayit. For it is like half a chicken egg, and one needs this measure in full apart from the space between the leaves, as is written in siman 486; and one should be careful about this, for if not so the blessing is in vain, since he blesses over his eating, and eating implies a kezayit. And one who is ill or delicate is permitted to take for himself whichever species of maror is most agreeable to him; and if it is impossible for him at all, at the very least he should eat a little or chew in his mouth as a remembrance of the taste of bitterness without a blessing; and it is proper to push himself with all his ability even if it pains him, to fulfill the mitzvah of the Sages, as above in siman 472, se'if 10.
A bitter vegetable. Even though one cannot [do otherwise], in any case one should recite a blessing only over these species that are listed in the Mishnah; and so is the consensus of the Shach and the Magen Avraham (Chok Yaakov).
Vinegar. And when Pesach falls on Shabbos, one must put the liquid into it from erev Shabbos; and if he forgot, one should do it by means of a shinui, namely that he put the liquid first into the vessel and afterwards place the charoses into it, as above in siman 321, se'if 16; and so wrote the Magen Avraham; and see there in the Taz, s.k. 10, who forbids putting ground baked matzah to mix it with wine or mead on Shabbos even by means of a shinui; and his words are not compelling, and the world also is not careful about this (Chok Yaakov).
For straw. And one should not crush it well, so that [the pieces] be long (Beis Yosef).
