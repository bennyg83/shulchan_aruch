(א) Chazeret. It is lettuce; olshin is coriander; tamcha is horseradish. Custom spread to take horseradish for maror and bless on the leaves; and the stalk one takes for korech. In responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 119 he wrote: the chazeret mentioned in our Mishnah is the vegetable called in foreign tongue salad and in Spanish salad, and it has the signs mentioned in the Gemara; and because in Ashkenaz lands it is not found at Pesach time they did not become accustomed to take it for maror and take horseradish, which is tamcha, and one fulfills with it — though they cannot eat even half a kezayit because of its sharpness — and they nullify the mitzvah of maror — end quote, see there. And I also heard from one foreigner that in a hot country lettuce is found in Nissan days and is double and thick like what we call in these lands head lettuce, and they eat that species for maror; and see Kaf HaChayyim:

(ב) In the root. Meaning small roots that split here and there, but the large root on which the vegetable stands is included in the stalk. Maharil, and see Taz:

(ג) For a kezayit. For it is like half a chicken egg, and this measure is required in full apart from the space between the leaves, as written siman 486; and one should be careful about this, for otherwise it is a berachah lebatalah, since he blesses on eating it and eating implies a kezayit. One who is ill or fastidious may take for himself whichever kind of maror is most palatable to him; and if it is impossible for him altogether, in any case he should eat a little or chew in his mouth for remembrance of bitterness of taste without a berachah, and it is fit to push himself with all his ability even if it pains him to fulfill a mitzvah of the Sages, as written above siman 474 seif 10:

(ד) Bitter vegetable. Even though one cannot [bless] on another matter, in any case one should bless only on these species enumerated in the Mishnah, and so agreed Shach and Magen Avraham. Kaf HaChayyim:

(ה) Vinegar. And Pesach that falls on Shabbat — one must put the liquid in it from erev Shabbat; and if he forgot, one may do so through shinui, namely that he put the liquid first in the vessel and afterward put charoset in it, as written siman 321 seif 16, and so Magen Avraham; and see there in Taz sk 10 that he forbids putting ground baked matzah to mix with wine or honey water on Shabbat even through shinui, and his words are not compelling; also the world is not careful about this.
