Tamcha. Horseradish ("Krin"), and in Ashkenazic "M'retich"; and Maharil was in doubt whether "Korvel" is maror; and in Loaz "Marubya"; charchavina: palm shoots.
Not from the root. This is the language of Mahari Weil: if he takes horseradish ("M'retik"), he should take what is above the ground, such as the leaves and what is adjacent to it, but not what is below the ground, for that is root; end quote. And so ruled Mateh Moshe and the Shelah. However, before this Mahari Weil wrote, and this is his language: in the first chapter of Sukkah we say hyssop [has] three stalks and on them three stems, and Rashi explained: stalks [means] roots; and for this reason it appears that what Rabbeinu Tam wrote "and not roots" means small roots that split here and there, but the large root on which the vegetable stands is included in the stalk; end quote. And so wrote Hagahos Mordechai, and this is his language: I heard that it is called stalk down to below its tail, and small spikes come out from them; end quote; and as above is the main [ruling]. And the world customarily recites the blessing over the leaves, and for korech they take the stalk; and it appears to me the reason is from what it says "and one fulfills with their stalk," which implies that leaves are preferable; therefore they take the leaves for the blessing. However, one must take a kezayit, as is written in siman 486; and because there are not so many leaves, therefore they take the stalk for korech; however, if he wishes to take the leaves for korech, the choice is in his hand.

Not pickled. Rashi explained: in vinegar; and it appears to me that he goes according to his view, as Beis Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 105; but we hold that something soaked in water overnight is pickled and is like cooked; if so, if he soaked the maror in water overnight, one does not fulfill with it, for its bitterness was nullified. And see in Beis Yosef, siman 527: Mahari Weil wrote, and this is his language: Mahari of Rothenburg was in doubt: since maror is compared to matzah, and stolen [produce] is invalid, if so a Jew should not uproot the maror from the ground, as is written in siman 649; and it appears to me that it is not comparable, for there lettuce survives in the ground, therefore it is like ground; end quote. It appears to me that this is the meaning: since maror does not survive in the ground, it is like movables, and the gentile's acquisition [works] by despair and change of domain, and there is a change of domain in the Jew's hand. And nevertheless this requires further study, for we hold that whatever is attached to the ground is like ground, as is written in siman 637, se'if 3, and all the more so when it is rooted in the ground, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 6, se'if 2; and see Choshen Mishpat, siman 95, and the end of siman 193. And perhaps it refers to where the maror has already fully ripened and stands ready to be pulled, and he holds that whatever stands ready to be pulled is like detached.
Or another bitter vegetable. Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote, and this is its language: in the Gemara it is evident that one fulfills only with those enumerated in the Mishnah; and what the Agur wrote that one should take wormwood, it appears that he explains that "maror" is wormwood; and according to this, with another bitter vegetable one eats without a blessing; and the Rav's words require further study; end quote. And it appears to me that he hit upon the truth in the Agur's intent, for the Agur wrote so in the name of Maharil, and this is Maharil's language in a responsum: one who does not have lettuce, some say that what is earlier in the Mishnah is earlier for the mitzvah, and there the four species are mentioned in the gloss in Ashkenazic (as above); and Tashbetz wrote to take "Wirmot" in Ashkenazic, which is the maror of the Mishnah; and the reason is that there is one who seeks after bitterness, therefore if there is no chazeres, according to all bitterness is preferable; and Agudah wrote to take "M'retich," which is tamcha; and perhaps because it was not clarified that olshin is "Kirvel," for in the siddurim it is written to take "Korvel" for the first dipping, which implies that one does not fulfill with it the obligation of maror; thus far Maharil's language. And Agudah also wrote that maror is "Wirmot," and it is known that wormwood is "Wirmot" in the Ashkenazic language; and in the customs of Maharac he wrote: a place where there is no lettuce, they take wormwood that is called "Yerimda," or another thing that is bitter (Tashbetz); end quote. If so, he holds that the maror of the Mishnah includes all bitter vegetables; and so Rashi wrote on Chumash: every bitter herb is called maror. But in the Gemara it does not imply so; and also in Sukkah folio 13 it is stated that even the bitterness of a marsh one does not fulfill with, because it has an accompanying name, for it must be that its name is maror without an accompanying name. And also in Rashi's intent it appears to say that everything is called maror, but nevertheless one fulfills only with those that at the time of the Giving of the Torah were called maror; therefore one should not recite a blessing over other vegetables.
