(א) Chazeret. Latukh; Levush wrote it is called in Polish zarzich.

(ב) Endives (olashin). Korvil; krishfla in Arabic; andibi in Spanish (Hagahot Maimoniot).

(ג) Tamcha. Krien; in foreign tongue maertikh; Maharil was unsure whether korvil is maror; in foreign tongue marubya; charchevina: palm shoots.

(ד) Not from the root. Mahari"v: if taking maertikh, take what is above ground, leaves and adjacent parts, not below ground, which is root — end; so Mishna Berurah and Shlah; but earlier Mahari"v wrote in first chapter of Sukkah: they said hyssop three stalks with three pods — Rashi: stalks are roots; from this it appears what Rabbenu Tam wrote "not roots" means small roots branching, but the large root the vegetable stands in is included in stalk — end; Hagahot Maimoniot: I heard stalk is called until below its tail and small spikes emerge — end; and so is essential: the world recites on leaves and for korekh takes the stalk; it appears the reason: since it says one fulfills with its stalk, leaves are preferable — therefore leaves for berakha; nevertheless need a kezayit, siman 486; because leaves are not so plentiful, take stalk for korekh; but if he wishes leaves for korekh, permission is his.

(ה) Whether moist. It implies one does not fulfill with wilted; so Tur and Rambam — see Gemara.

(ו) Not pickled. Rashi: in vinegar; it appears he follows his view as Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah 105; but we hold something in water from the eve is pickled like cooked — if maror soaked from the eve one does not fulfill, bitterness nullified; see Beit Yosef siman 527 — Mahari"v: Maharar"a was unsure — since maror is compared to matzah and stolen is invalid, perhaps a Jew should not uproot maror from the ground, siman 649; it appears not comparable — there lettuce is sustained in earth therefore like earth — end; it appears since maror is not sustained in earth it is like movable property, gentile acquisition through despair and change of domain, change of domain in Israel's hand; still difficult, for we hold whatever is attached to ground is like ground, siman 637 s"k 3, especially rooted in ground Yoreh De'ah 6:2 and Choshen Mishpat 95 and 193; perhaps when maror was already fully cooked and stands to be uprooted, he holds whatever stands to be uprooted is considered uprooted.

(ז) Or another bitter vegetable. Shibolei HaLeket: Gemara proves one fulfills only with those enumerated; what Agur wrote to take wormwood — he explains maror is wormwood; if so, other bitter vegetables he eats without berakha — the Mechaber's words require study — end; it appears the truth is Agur's intent, for Agur wrote so in Maharil's name: one without latugi — some say the earlier in the mishnah is earlier for the mitzvah; there the four species are mentioned in foreign tongue (as above); Tashbetz: take vermet in foreign tongue, maror of the mishnah; reason: some reverse the order — therefore if no chazeret, for all maror is preferable; Agudah: take maertikh, tamcha; perhaps because olashin/korvil was not clarified — siddurim say korvil for first dipping, implying not maror — thus Maharil and Agudah; also maror is vermet; known wormwood is vermet in Ashkenaz; in Minhagei Maharik where no latugi they take wormwood called verimda or other bitter thing — Tashbetz — end; if so, maror of the mishnah includes all bitter vegetables; so Rashi in Chumash: any bitter herb is maror; but Gemara does not imply so; also Sukkah 13: even field bitterness one does not fulfill because it has a qualifying name — must be called maror without qualifier; also Rashi's intent seems all are called maror, nevertheless one fulfills only those called maror at matan Torah; therefore no berakha on other vegetables.

(ח) A little vinegar. If Shabbat, put liquid from erev Shabbat (Agudah) — see 321:16: through change permitted to put liquid on Shabbat — put liquid first then charoset.

(ט) Similar to straw. Not ground well and they are long (Beit Yosef, Rokeach); Maharil: do not grind them — they should be long.
