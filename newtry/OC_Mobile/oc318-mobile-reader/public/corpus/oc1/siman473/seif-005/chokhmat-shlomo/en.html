Se'if 5. These are the vegetables with which one fulfills one's obligation, etc. Note: we learn in tractate Pesachim, folio 39b: Rav Menashya bar Tachlifa asked, what is the law regarding fulfilling [the obligation] with second-tithe maror? etc., until Rava said, it is reasonable [that] matzah and maror [are alike]; and see what I wrote explaining this sugya in my work on Orach Chaim from the year 5612, in the laws of Birkas HaMazon, siman 184, and it is in my composition there, folio 131a; see there and examine carefully.
(There, se'if 5, in the gloss.) And if one does not have one of these vegetables, one should take wormwood or another bitter vegetable, etc. Note: it appears clear in my humble opinion that it is permitted to take a radish that is called "Retich" in our country for maror, for it is explicit in the Magen Avraham and in Baer Heitev that "tamcha" is what they call "M'retich"; and even though in Ashkenaz they call horseradish "Retich," nevertheless they also call radish "Retish," and if so both are called thus; only, so that the difference between them be recognizable, they call this one "M'retich" and that one "Retich," but the root of both is one, and reason supports this, for in radish too there is the sharpness like horseradish, and its beginning is soft and its end is hard, and it is a sharp thing like horseradish. And behold, the Rama's view is that even without this one fulfills with any bitter vegetable, and likewise is the view of Rashi on Chumash, as the Magen Avraham wrote; and this does not contradict the words of the Mishnah, for one can say that regarding those species for eating matzah, concerning which it is explicit in the Torah with respect to the distinguished [produce] of the Land of Israel that specifically with the five species it is called bread and the others are not bread, and they also do not come to leavening, and for matzah we require that it be able to come to leavening, therefore one fulfills only with those enumerated in the Mishnah; but maror, where the Torah did not specify what it is, therefore Rashi correctly explained that according to the Torah every bitter vegetable is called maror, only that the Sages listed these that are common in their place, whose beginning is soft and whose end is hard, and therefore they found these and listed them. And similarly, in Megillah folio 10: why did the Sages list these? etc., rather, they found these and listed them; and not only these, but anything for which you have a tradition from your fathers, etc. So even though the Sages listed these, there is no proof that specifically these [alone qualify], for one can say they found these and listed them. So too regarding maror: they found these and listed them, and likewise if another species like them is found, it too is maror. And therefore Rashi explained that since the Torah did not specify the species, whatever is a bitter vegetable is called maror; which is not so regarding matzah, where the Torah specified that we require that it be able to come to leavening and that it be called bread, and the Torah specified that bread is only wheat and barley; therefore there we require specifically these, which is not so regarding maror. And therefore, at the very least regarding "Retich," where it is a doubt to us whether it is included in "tamcha," there is a reasoned possibility that perhaps it is included in "tamcha"; and even if you say it is not included in "tamcha," perhaps [one can rely] like Rashi and like the Rama that one fulfills with anything. And the main thing seems to me that both horseradish and "Retich" are included in "tamcha" and they are one; so it appears correct in my humble opinion; and likewise several elders told me that they saw people fulfill the obligation of maror with "Retich"; and examine carefully.
