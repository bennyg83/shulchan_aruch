Seif 5. "These are the vegetables with which one fulfills his obligation, etc." NB: We read in Pesachim 39b: Rami bar Chama asked, what is the law about fulfilling the obligation of maror with maaser sheni, etc., until Rava said, "it is reasonable regarding matzah and maror." See what I wrote in explanation of this sugya in my book on Orach Chayim from the year 5612, in Hilchot Birkat HaMazon, siman 184, in my work there fol. 131a; see there and examine carefully.
There, seif 5, in the gloss. "And if he does not have one of these vegetables, he should take wormwood or another bitter vegetable, etc." NB: It appears clear to me that one may take the radish called rettich in our country for maror. It is explicit in the Magen Avraham and Be'er Heitev that tamcha is what is called marrettich. Although in Ashkenaz they call horseradish rettich, nevertheless the radish too is called rettich; both are called by that name, only to distinguish between them they call one marrettich and the other rettich. But the root of both is one, and reason supports this, for radish also has hardness like horseradish, begins soft and ends hard, and is sharp like horseradish. Moreover, the Rama's view is that in any event one fulfills the obligation with any bitter vegetable, and so too is Rashi's view in Chumash, as the Magen Avraham wrote. This does not contradict the Mishnah. Regarding the species for eating matzah, the Torah explicitly indicates, with regard to the important produce of Eretz Yisrael, that only the five species are considered bread and the others are not bread; they also do not come to chametz, and matzah requires something that can become chametz. Therefore one fulfills the obligation only with those listed in the Mishnah. But regarding maror, the Torah did not specify what it is; therefore Rashi properly explained that by Torah law every bitter vegetable is called maror. The Sages merely listed those that were common in their place, which begin soft and end hard, and they found these and listed them. A similar point appears in Megillah 10: why did the Sages list these? Rather, they found these and listed them, and not these alone; anything for which you have a tradition from your fathers, etc. Thus even though the Sages listed these, there is no proof that only these qualify; one may say they found these and listed them. So too with maror, they found these and listed them, and the same applies to another species like them. Therefore Rashi explained that since the Torah did not specify the species, anything that is a bitter vegetable is called maror, unlike matzah, where the Torah specified that it must be something that can become chametz and is called bread, and the Torah specified that bread is only wheat and barley and the other grains. Therefore, at least regarding rettich, where we are uncertain whether it is included in tamcha, there is a double consideration: perhaps it is included in tamcha, and if not, perhaps the halacha follows Rashi and the Rama that one fulfills the obligation with any bitter thing. The main point appears to me that both horseradish and rettich are included in tamcha and are one. This appears correct to me, and several elders told me that they saw people fulfill the obligation of maror with rettich. Examine carefully.
