He takes wormwood — see in Mishna Berurah that we explained in the words of the gloss that wormwood is not among the five species of maror, and so is proven in the long Darkei Moshe see there in his explanation of the words of the Agur. However Magen Avraham and Shach disagreed with him and proved that the Agur's intent is that wormwood is one of the five species of maror and is the maror mentioned last among the five species; and in my humble opinion it requires further study, for as it appears wormwood is not fit for eating, as implied from several verses that describe every evil thing with wormwood, as written "a root bearing gall and wormwood" or "its end bitter as wormwood," and we require something fit for eating by a person in any case, as explained in the words of Riaz and Ritva and as also proven from the Gemara that we require something purchasable with ma'aser money, unless impossible — then it is fit in any case somewhat for eating. Afterward I found in Rashbatz in Sefer Yavin Shemuah that he wrote explicitly one does not fulfill with wormwood because it is a type of tree and we require from types of vegetables, as we learned explicitly "these vegetables," etc., and so explained in the Gemara that it challenges "perhaps bitter caper," etc., and this is not like the view of Magen Avraham and not like the view of the gloss, for even if we say it has sap and one's face is ashamed, as we explained in Mishna Berurah, nevertheless these signs help only for a vegetable. Unless Rama did not write to fulfill with it as law but only as a general remembrance when one lacks the five species, and as the Gra also wrote; and the law requires further study. And know further that the sign that it has sap and one's face is ashamed — even though Rif and Rambam and Rosh omitted it, nevertheless it is mentioned in several poskim; see Riaz and Rashbatz and Maharam Chalavah who brought this sign for law; and in Beit Meir he wrote perhaps even Rif and those with him omitted it only because we are not expert in these signs well; therefore though it is difficult to bless based on these signs, nevertheless if one lacks the five species he should certainly seek any bitter species of vegetables that has the sign of sap and face ashamed, provided it is fit for eating by a person. And behold per what I wrote above on maror, if we require it be fit for eating, I now found in Peri Megadim he is somewhat doubtful whether one fulfills with maror even if not fit for eating, and he did not see the words of the aforementioned Rishonim; also the simple sense of the Gemara that we require it be purchasable with ma'aser money, as above (and see Rashi there); and what was difficult for him on the Gemara's challenge "perhaps bitter caper" — one may say this is the second answer of the Gemara even per the view that we do not require purchasability with ma'aser money; but nevertheless it is not at all produce of the ground; but for the conclusion in truth we do not need the Gemara's change of "produce of the ground," and such is the way of sugyot in many places:
