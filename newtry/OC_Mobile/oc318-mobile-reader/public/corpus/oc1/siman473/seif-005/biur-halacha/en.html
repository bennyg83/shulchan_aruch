He should take wormwood. See in the Mishnah Berurah, where we explained in the words of the gloss that wormwood is not from the five species of maror, and so is evident in the long Darkei Moshe; see there in his explanation of the words of the Agur. However, the Magen Avraham and other later authorities disagreed with him and proved that the Agur's intent is that wormwood is one of the five species of maror, and it is the "maror" mentioned last among the five species; and in my humble opinion this requires further study, for as it appears, wormwood is not fit for eating, as is implied from several verses that describe every bad thing as wormwood, as it is written "a root bearing gall and wormwood," or "its end is bitter as wormwood," and we require something fit for human consumption at least, as is clarified in the words of the Riaz and the Ritva, and as is also evident from the Gemara that we require something that may be purchased with ma'aser money, unless it is possible that it is fit for eating at least somewhat.
Afterwards I found that the Rashbatz in Sefer Yavin Shemuah wrote explicitly that one does not fulfill with wormwood because it is a species of tree, and we require from the species of vegetables, as the Mishnah teaches explicitly, "These are the vegetables," etc.; and so is clarified in the Gemara, which asks, "And say haridofni," etc. And this is not according to the view of the Magen Avraham, and not according to the view of the gloss; for even if we say it has sap and its face is pale, as we explained in the Mishnah Berurah, nevertheless these signs help only regarding a vegetable; unless the Rama did not write to fulfill with this according to the strict law, but only as a remembrance when one does not have from the five species, in the manner that the Shulchan Aruch HaRav also wrote; and this requires further study.
Know further that the sign that it has sap and its face is pale, even though the Rif, the Rambam, and the Rosh omitted it, nevertheless it is mentioned in several poskim; see the Riaz, the Rashbatz, and Maharam Chalavah, who copied this sign as practical law; and Beis Meir wrote that it is possible that even the Rif and those of his view who omitted it did so only because we are not well expert in these signs; and therefore, granted that it is hard to recite a blessing on the basis of these signs, nevertheless if one does not have the five species, one should certainly seek out every bitter species among vegetables that has the sign of sap and a pale face, provided that it be fit for human consumption. And regarding what I wrote above about maror, whether we require that it be fit for eating, I have now found in the Peri Megadim that he is somewhat in doubt about this, that perhaps one fulfills with maror even if it is not fit for eating, and he did not see the words of the early authorities mentioned above, and also the plain sense of the Gemara that we require that it be purchasable with ma'aser money, as above (and see Rashi there); and as for what was difficult for him regarding the Gemara's challenge, "Say the bitterness of a palm-heart," one can say that this is [only] a reply of the Gemara even according to the view that we do not require that it be purchasable with ma'aser money, but nevertheless it is not at all a product of the ground; but according to the conclusion, we in truth do not need the reply about products of the ground; and such is the way of the Gemara's sugya in several places.
