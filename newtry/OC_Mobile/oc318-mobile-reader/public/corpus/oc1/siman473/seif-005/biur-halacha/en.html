He should take wormwood. See Mishnah Berurah, where we explained the Rama's words that wormwood is not one of the five species of maror; this is also evident in the long Darkhei Moshe, in his explanation of the Agur. However, the Magen Avraham and other authorities disagree and prove that the Agur meant that wormwood is one of the five species of maror, namely the maror listed last among the five. In my humble opinion this requires study, for apparently wormwood is not fit for eating, as implied by several verses that describe every evil thing as wormwood, such as "a root bearing gall and wormwood" and "her end is bitter as wormwood." We require at least something fit for human consumption, as explained by the Riaz and Ritva, and as is evident from the Gemara, which requires something purchasable with ma'aser money, unless perhaps it is at least somewhat edible.
I later found in the Tashbetz, in Yavin Shemu'ah, that he writes explicitly that one does not fulfill with wormwood because it is a tree species, and we require vegetable species, as the Mishnah explicitly says, "these are the vegetables." So too the Gemara asks, "perhaps oleander," etc. This is unlike the Magen Avraham and unlike the gloss. Even if we say it has sap and a pale face, as we explained in Mishnah Berurah, these signs help only for a vegetable. Unless the Rama did not write to fulfill the law with it, but only as a remembrance when one lacks the five species, similar to what the Gra of Liadi wrote. This requires study.
Know further that the sign that it has sap and its face pales, although omitted by the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, is mentioned by several poskim; see Riaz, Tashbetz, and Maharam Chalavah, who copy this sign as law. Beit Meir writes that perhaps the Rif and those with him omitted it only because we are not expert in these signs. Therefore, although it is difficult to bless on the basis of these signs, if one lacks the five species he should certainly seek any bitter vegetable that has the sign of sap and a pale face, provided it is fit for human consumption. Regarding what I wrote above about whether maror must be fit to eat, I now found that Peri Megadim is somewhat uncertain, perhaps one fulfills with maror even if it is not fit to eat. He did not see the words of the Rishonim cited above, nor the plain Gemara requiring that it be purchasable with ma'aser money. What troubled him from the Gemara's question "perhaps marirta de-kufya" can be answered: the Gemara gives that answer even according to the assumption that purchasability with ma'aser money is not required; in any case it is not produce of the ground. But according to the conclusion, we truly no longer need the answer of produce of the ground. This is the Gemara's way in many places.
