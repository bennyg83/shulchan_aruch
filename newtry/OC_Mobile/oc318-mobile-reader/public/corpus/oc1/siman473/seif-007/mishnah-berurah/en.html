(א) Immediately — meaning that he should not wait in mixing the cup until he reaches "therefore," for then he needs to hold the cup in his hand; rather immediately when he removes the plate from the table he mixes:

(ב) Second cup — and he need not rinse and wash, for they already rinsed for Kiddush:

(ג) So that they will ask, etc. — meaning and thereby he will be aroused to ask further questions and wonders that he sees on that night:

(ד) And when the son or the woman asks — and the same applies if one Torah scholar asks his fellow; the one asked need not say "why is this night different":

(ה) And he reads the entire Haggadah — and one should not say it reclining, only in awe and fear [Shelah]. When he says "and we shall say before Him song," it must be "v'ne'emar" with segol vowels under the alef, and "ne'emar" is past tense, for it refers to the redemption from Egypt [Acharonim]:

(ו) And likewise when he reaches "this maror" — but when he reaches "the Pesach that our forefathers," etc., he should not hold in his hand the meat that is a remembrance of the Pesach, for it is as raising consecrated [meat] outside [the Temple precincts], for it appears from this that he consecrated it for the Pesach. Mordechai wrote that one must say "and we shall eat there of the sacrifices and of the Pesach offerings," for they eat first of the festival offering, which is the sacrifice; but the Pesach is eaten on the full [stomach]:

(7) (Ag) And when he comes to the conclusion of Magbia, etc. - and in the Omnipresent's time he wrote a model when the Piscah is said and she is the one who stood for our ancestors and for us until the Almighty saves us from them, he will hold the cup in his hand and then the mouth will be covered when he holds the cup in his hand:

(8) (at) with his finger - it is customary to throw with the finger because the finger of the Omnipresent is weak as a reference to the custom of throwing a small finger:

(9) (Eh) 16 times - against the 16th letters of the name of the Omnipresent who struck Pharaoh:

(י) And the bread should be uncovered, etc. — for therefore matzah is called "bread of affliction," that one answers upon it matters:

(כ) That he holds the cup in his hand — in order to say song over the wine:

(ל) And then he covers the bread — so that his nakedness should not be seen, as is written siman 471 seif 9:
