Why is this night different. Tur wrote that one should not say "we are not obligated to dip," etc., for it implies that on this night we are obligated to dip — and this is not so, for one is not obligated to dip except on account of recognition for children; rather he should say "we do not dip," etc. And in our Gemara it states "obligated to dip" — and it requires study, for it is an error: It is written in Maharil that the large piece should be taken for afikoman and not less than half — meaning the small portion; and a reason can be given for this, because that half of afikoman is the essence of the mitzvah that he eats at the end: Mordechai wrote that one must say "and we shall eat there of the sacrifices and of the Pesach offerings," for they eat first of the festival offering, which is the sacrifice; but the Pesach is eaten on the full [stomach]. And Mahariu wrote that if Pesach falls on Shabbat one must say that night "of the Pesach offerings and of the sacrifices," for the festival offering is not offered on Shabbat. Tur wrote that one should say "and we shall thank You, a new song," and not say "a new shirah," since he speaks of the future redemption which is masculine language, as stated in the midrash, end of his words. And in the text in our siddurim it is written "and we shall say before Him a new shirah, Halleluyah" — this too is not correct, since he says "v'ne'emar," it refers by necessity to the future redemption. And in some books they did not write at all this "new shirah," and that is correct; for in truth it refers to the redemption that passed, for they recite the Egyptian Hallel. And in Mordechai of the elders of Mahari bar Betzalel z"l it was vocalized "v'ne'emar" with segol, and this is more correct; and it is fitting to say "a new shirah," for when "ne'emar" is with segol it is past tense:
