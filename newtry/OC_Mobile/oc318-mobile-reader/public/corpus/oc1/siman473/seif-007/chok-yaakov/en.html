Immediately, the second cup. Maharshal writes in responsum 88 that it does not require rinsing and washing, and this is the custom. One does not hold the cup until reaching Lefichach, as the Rama writes below. Shelah writes that when reciting the paragraph Vehi Sheamdah, from “It is this that stood for our fathers and for us” until “and HaKadosh Baruch Hu saves us from their hand,” one should also hold the cup. At that time the matzah should be covered while the cup is held.
Mah Nishtanah. Maharil writes that it should be recited in a pleasant melody, in praise of Hashem. If Erev Pesach falls on Shabbos, one should proceed quickly in all matters so as to eat the afikoman before midnight and so that the children do not fall asleep, since one must remain awake into the night before beginning Kiddush. Shelah writes that the Haggadah should not be recited while reclining, but with awe and fear. When saying “Venomar lefanav shirah chadashah,” the word venomar should be vocalized with a segol, indicating the past tense. Regarding the future redemption one may not say shirah, a feminine term, as stated in the Midrash. This is the consensus of the later authorities.
And likewise when he reaches maror. By contrast, when he reaches “the Pesach offering that our fathers ate,” he should not hold it in his hand, for that would be like raising consecrated offerings outside the Temple. Nevertheless, he should look at it. Mahari Weil, siman 193.
With his finger. Hagahos Minhagim writes that one should cast the drops with the smallest finger, meaning the little finger. Magen Avraham writes that one should use the ring finger, which is next to the little finger, as stated in Yalkut that the plagues were inflicted with the ring finger. Darkei Moshe writes that one should specifically use the index finger, in remembrance of the verse, “It is the finger of God,” and this is the primary practice. Pesach Me'ubin, siman 261, writes that some pour out the wine, rinse the cup after casting the drops with the finger, and wash their hands, because they do not wish to drink wine over which the plagues were mentioned, and because the finger was dipped into it and made it repulsive. In these countries this is not the custom, and one should not change unless he is especially delicate. There is also a concern of wasting food; see above, siman 171. This custom is not mentioned by any early posek.
While reciting the Haggadah. Maharil writes that the matzos should remain covered until beginning Avadim Hayinu. At that point they are uncovered slightly from above, fulfilling the description of lechem oni, bread over which matters are answered and discussed. No matzah other than the three matzos should be placed on the table until after completion of the Seder, for otherwise how could removal of the ke'arah be regarded as taking the place of removal of the table? The phrase “until completion of the Seder” cannot be exact, however, for when Avadim Hayinu begins, the ke'arah is returned to its place, and other matzos may then also be placed upon the table.
Who recites the Haggadah. See Beit Yosef, who rules that a blind person is also obligated to recite the Haggadah, unlike Rabbeinu Yerucham and Responsa Radbaz, siman 59. This is also the conclusion of the Gemara in Pesachim 115, that he is obligated in reciting the Haggadah and may discharge the obligation of others. See Shaar Ephraim, siman 27, who reconciles the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham but agrees in practice with Beit Yosef's conclusion. Nevertheless, it appears to me that when he reaches “this matzah” and “this maror,” he should omit the word “this.” The Gemara there says that “this” excludes the blind, because the word implies that the object is recognized by sight, as Rashbam explains. Although the Gemara's conclusion may permit a distinction, nothing is lost by saying the shorter formula, “The matzah that we eat,” and so forth. See Bach in siman 460, who writes in the name of Maharshal concerning one who was imprisoned and did not know how to recite the Haggadah but possessed a Chumash, that he recited all the passages concerning the Exodus from Egypt.
The cup in his hand. Regarding the text of the blessing Asher Gealanu, Mahari Weil writes that when Pesach falls on Motzaei Shabbos, one should reverse the order and first say “from the Pesach offerings” and afterward “from the festival offerings,” while on the second night one should reverse it. Pesach Me'ubin, siman 261, writes that one should use a single text throughout, “from the festival offerings and from the Pesach offerings.” The later authorities agree with Mahari Weil.
