The Magen Avraham wrote: in the Minhagim, etc. This is also implied by siman 651, se'if 5, where he wrote as follows: one recites the berachah of al netilas lulav and Shehecheyanu before taking the esrog, etc. These are his words. Thus he wrote to recite Shehecheyanu as well even before taking it, all the more so before the shakings.
It is like reciting it at the time of making, etc. For the Rabbis taught in Sukkah 47: one who makes a lulav for himself says Shehecheyanu, etc. The Rosh wrote: nowadays the custom is not to recite Shehecheyanu except at the time of taking, just as we say regarding Shehecheyanu for sukkah, that Rav Kahana would arrange the berachah of Shehecheyanu over the cup of Kiddush. The same applies to lulav: one recites it at the time of taking. These are his words. The reason is explained in the Beis Yosef, siman 641, in the name of the Mordechai: nowadays, when one person makes a sukkah for several people, it does not seem proper that the one who makes the sukkos should recite many berachos while those who sit in the sukkah do not recite; therefore we rely on Rav Kahana. This reason applies to lulav as well. Nevertheless, if one acted according to the law of the Gemara and recited the berachah at the time of making, he certainly fulfilled his obligation. If so, he fulfilled it through the berachah he recited even if you say the first day was a weekday, for when one recites the berachah at the time of making, he recites it during the weekday. Although, if the first day was a weekday, it was neither the time of making nor the time of Kiddush, this does not matter; see the Tur.
Which is not so regarding shofar. For it is explained in siman 600 that we say Shehecheyanu for shofar also on the second day. Even according to the one who disagrees there and holds that we do not say it, that is because the two days of Rosh Hashanah are one sanctity and are considered one long day. Were it not for this, all would agree that one says Shehecheyanu on the second day and does not fulfill it with the Shehecheyanu said on the first day, because perhaps the first day was a weekday. Regarding shofar, one does not recite a berachah at the time of making according to the law of the Gemara, and the reason is explained in Magen Avraham, siman 641; see there. Likewise regarding sukkah, we say Shehecheyanu on the second day, even though he already said Shehecheyanu on the first day, because of the doubt that perhaps the first day was a weekday. Since regarding sukkah one must say Shehecheyanu because of making the sukkah and because of the festival, if we knew that the first day was certainly Yom Tov, then he would fulfill through the Shehecheyanu he said on the first day both because of sukkah and because of the festival, and it would exempt both at once. But because of the doubt that perhaps the first day was a weekday, although he nevertheless fulfilled through the Shehecheyanu of the first day with respect to sukkah, for it is no worse than reciting it at the time of making, he did not fulfill the Shehecheyanu that comes because of the festival, since perhaps the first day was a weekday and the festival had not yet begun. How could it exempt the Shehecheyanu that comes because of the festival? Therefore Shehecheyanu must be said also on the second day.
We do not say El Na. The Levush gave a rule for this: we say El Na only in a place where the poet established the phrase 'Living and enduring.'
Know that what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, that we complete Hallel. Although on Pesach we read it with skipping except for the first two days, the Gemara explains that Sukkos is different, since the days differ from one another in their korbanos, unlike Pesach. See above, siman 490, Turei Zahav subparagraph 3, who wrote another reason, and so too in the Minhagim; these are words of Midrash. See Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 225. What is written in the Shulchan Aruch, that one recites the berachah 'to complete,' we do not rule so, as the Rema wrote above in siman 428, se'if 1; rather, we recite 'to read,' lest one skip a word. Nevertheless, if one recited 'to complete,' even on days when Hallel is read with skipping, he need not repeat, as the Rema wrote above in siman 422, se'if 2, because 'to complete' can also be interpreted like 'to read,' as we say in Berachos: the vatikin would complete it, meaning the morning Shema, at sunrise. What the Shulchan Aruch wrote, 'if he interrupted and delayed, etc., he need only return to the place where he interrupted,' see Magen Avraham above, siman 422, subparagraph 9, that if he delayed long enough to read all of it because of compulsion, one should be stringent to avoid the dispute and return and read it again without a berachah.
