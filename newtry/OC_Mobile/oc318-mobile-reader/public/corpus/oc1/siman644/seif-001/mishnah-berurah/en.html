(1) In the morning, etc. In the Tur it says that we come early, because the zealous perform mitzvos early, in order to fulfill the mitzvah of lulav. This is from the Bayis Chadash.
(2) The prayer. We do not say El Na on the first day of Sukkos, because in its place he says 'then was the encampment of His sukkah,' which is also song and praise like El Na. In places where they reverse the order when the first day falls on Shabbos, it is proper to say El Na on the first day and not on the second day. The world did not practice so because they did not know the reason for the matter.
(3) Al netilas lulav and Shehecheyanu. Afterward he shakes, and so it is implied in siman 651, se'if 5. This Shehecheyanu is for fulfilling the mitzvah. Therefore, although the previous night he recited Shehecheyanu, there it was for the mitzvah of sukkah and for Yom Tov, while this one is for the mitzvah of lulav. If he took the lulav on the first day and did not recite Shehecheyanu, he may recite it all seven days whenever he remembers, but specifically at the time of taking. If he recited it on the first day, he does not recite Shehecheyanu again on the second day.
(4) And likewise all eight days of the festival. This is not like Chol HaMoed Pesach, when we skip, because on Sukkos each day is considered its own Yom Tov, since its korbanos differ from the previous day.
(5) And we recite a berachah 'to complete.' See above, siman 488, se'if 1, in the gloss there, that in our countries the custom is to recite the berachah 'to read Hallel.' See Mishnah Berurah there.
(6) Whether the congregation or an individual. Meaning, this is not like Rosh Chodesh or Chol HaMoed Pesach, where according to several poskim one does not recite a berachah on Hallel even for the congregation, and especially for an individual; see above in siman 488, se'if 2, and siman 490, se'if 4. There, Hallel is only a custom of all Israel. But here, since it is required by law, everyone agrees that both the congregation and an individual recite a berachah.
(7) And one does not interrupt during it, etc. The Chayei Adam, rule 147, wrote that if a lulav was brought to him in the middle of Hallel, he may interrupt between sections, meaning between one chapter of Tehillim and another, in order to recite the berachah.
(8) Only in the manner they said regarding Shema, etc. All this is explained above in siman 488 regarding Hallel on the first two days of Pesach, when we complete Hallel. It was repeated here only to teach that on Sukkos all eight days are equal in this law. For us outside Eretz Yisrael, Shemini Atzeres/Simchas Torah is also included, for on it too we complete Hallel, since it is possibly the eighth day.
(9) In the middle he may ask, etc. The explanation of all these matters until the end of the siman is explained in siman 488 in the Mishnah Berurah there. See references 100, 160, 249.
