Seif katan 1: They are exempt, etc. Prayer specifically requires standing, unlike Krias Shema, for which it is sufficient to stand for the first verse, as explained above in siman 63, seif 3.
Just as one prays while traveling. Such prayer is preferable to not praying at all. Had one who prays while walking not fulfilled his obligation at all, there would be no difficulty. But since one traveling does pray while walking and thereby fulfills his obligation, the question remains. It may be answered that while they are walking they are occupied with a mitzvah and therefore exempt from prayer. Nevertheless, they remain obligated in Krias Shema because it can be recited easily. See siman 38, seif 8, in the gloss: although one occupied with a mitzvah is exempt from another mitzvah, this applies where both cannot be performed without exertion, such as mitzvah agents having to exert themselves to sleep in a sukkah. If both can be fulfilled easily, he must fulfill both. Prayer, which requires extensive concentration, is considered burdensome, so they need not pray while walking because they are occupied with a mitzvah. Krias Shema, by contrast, can be recited easily, because concentration is required only for the first verse. Know that Beit Yosef writes that the wording used by Shulchan Aruch, which is Rambam’s wording, implies that even those accompanying the bier are exempt. This differs from the implication of the Tur, according to which only the bearers of the bier are exempt, even when the bier has no need of them, whereas those merely accompanying it are obligated.
