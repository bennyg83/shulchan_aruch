(Note 1) Exempt, etc. — specifically while standing, unlike Keriat Shema where it suffices that he stand for one verse, as above siman 63 seif 3.

Comparable to one who prays on the road. And it is better than not praying at all — in a case where he did not go out at all; for one who prays while walking is in doubt. But since on the road he prays while walking, therefore that he went out is difficult. And one can say at the time, etc. — for Keriat Shema they are obligated, for easily he can engage; Levush siman 38 seif 8 in the gloss explained that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt from the mitzvah — that is, when it is impossible to fulfill both except through exertion, such as agents of a mitzvah who must exert themselves to sleep in a sukkah. But if one can fulfill both easily, he must fulfill both; and therefore in tefillah, which requires much kavanah, it is called exertion — therefore he need not pray while walking, for he is engaged in a mitzvah; unlike Keriat Shema, for easily he can recite, since he needs kavanah only for one verse. And know that the rabbi Beit Yosef wrote that this wording that is written in Shulchan Aruch, that it is the Rambam's language, implies that even the escorts are exempt — unlike what seems from the Tur's words, that specifically the bier-bearers are exempt even when the bier does not need them, but the escorts are obligated.
