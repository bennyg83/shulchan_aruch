So too the Rambam, for he holds that tefillah is a positive commandment by Torah law, as it is written "and to serve Him with all your heart," etc.; but by Torah law it suffices once a day and in whatever wording one wishes; therefore the custom arose that most women do not pray at length, because they say immediately in the morning near netilat yadayim some request, and by Torah law that suffices; and it is possible that the Sages too did not obligate them more. And Ramban holds that tefillah is rabbinic, and so rule most poskim; and see Peri Chadash and Sefer Megillas Esther, and Sefer Lev Sameach; and in Semak he wrote that it is a mitzvah to pray at a time of distress. And see Tosafos in Berachos 20a.
