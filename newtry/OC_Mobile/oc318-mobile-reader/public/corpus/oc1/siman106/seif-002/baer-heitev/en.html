Time-bound. Rambam writes this because he holds that prayer is a Torah-level positive commandment, as it is written, 'and to serve Him with all your heart,' etc. By Torah law, however, once a day is sufficient, in whatever wording one wishes. Therefore the custom developed that most women do not pray regularly, because immediately in the morning, near the time of washing their hands, they recite some request, and by Torah law that is sufficient. It is possible that the Sages likewise did not obligate them beyond this. Ramban holds that prayer is rabbinic, and this is also the view of most authorities. See Pri Chadash, Sefer Megillat Esther, and Sefer Lev Sameach. Semak writes that there is a mitzvah to pray in a time of distress. See Tosafos, Berachos 20b. To educate them. Nevertheless, one may give them food before prayer. Magen Avraham.
