(4) For it is a positive commandment, etc.: All this follows Rambam, who maintains that only the fixed times of prayer are Rabbinic, while the essential mitzvah of prayer is from the Torah, as it is stated, “To serve Him with all your heart.” What service is performed in the heart? Prayer. The Torah does not prescribe a fixed text, and one may pray in any wording and at any time. Once one has prayed once by day or night, he has fulfilled his Torah obligation. Magen Avraham writes that according to this reasoning most women have customarily not prayed the Amidah regularly both morning and evening, because immediately in the morning, close to washing their hands, they recite some request, thereby fulfilling the Torah obligation. Perhaps the Sages did not obligate them further. Ramban, however, maintains that the essential mitzvah of prayer is Rabbinic, instituted by the Men of the Great Assembly as eighteen blessings in order, obligatory at Shacharis and Minchah and optional at Maariv. Although it is a Rabbinic time-bound positive commandment, and women are exempt from every time-bound positive commandment even Rabbinically, such as Kiddush Levanah, the Sages nevertheless obligated them in Shacharis and Minchah like men, since prayer is a request for mercy. This is the primary ruling, as it is the view of most authorities and was adopted by Shaagas Aryeh. Women should therefore be instructed to pray the Amidah. It is also proper that they accept the yoke of Heaven by at least reciting “Shema Yisrael,” as stated in siman 70, and recite the blessing Emes Veyatziv in order to connect redemption to prayer, as Magen Avraham writes there. All this concerns Shacharis and Minchah. Maariv is optional, and although all Israel has now accepted it as obligatory, women did not accept it, and most do not pray Maariv. Regarding Mussaf, Tzelach writes that women are exempt, while Magen Giborim rules that they are obligated. See there.
(5) To educate them: To pray the Amidah morning and evening. Nevertheless, one may give them food before Shacharis, and it is forbidden to cause them discomfort, as written in simanim 269 and 343 regarding feeding them before Kiddush.
