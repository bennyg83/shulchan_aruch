(4) That it is a positive mitzvah, etc. — all this is per the Rambam, that only the times of prayer are rabbinic, but the essence of the mitzvah of prayer is from the Torah, as it says, and to serve Him with all your hearts — what service is in the heart? Say: this is prayer — except that it has no fixed text known from the Torah, and one can pray in any wording one wishes and at any time one wishes. And once one prayed once a day or once at night, one fulfills the eighteen from the Torah. Magen Avraham wrote that per this reasoning most women who do not pray the eighteen regularly morning and evening — since they say immediately in the morning near netilat yadayim some request — fulfill by Torah law through this; and it is possible that the sages also did not obligate them more. But the view of the Ramban is that the essence of the mitzvah of prayer is rabbinic, like Hallel and the like, that they instituted eighteen blessings in order to pray them — shacharit and mincha obligatory, and maariv optional. And although it is a positive mitzvah rabbinic that is time-limited, women are exempt from every positive mitzvah that is time-limited, even rabbinic, such as kiddush levanah — nevertheless they obligated them in shacharit and mincha prayer like men, since prayer is a request for mercy. And this is the main ruling, for so is the view of most poskim, and so ruled Shaagas Aryeh. Therefore one should warn women to pray the eighteen; and it is also proper that they accept upon themselves the yoke of the kingdom of heaven — meaning that they should at least say Shema Yisrael, as stated in siman 70 — and they should also say the blessing Emet Veyatziv, in order to adjoin redemption to prayer, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 70. All this is regarding shacharit and mincha; but maariv prayer, which is optional — although now all Israel have already accepted it upon themselves as obligatory — nevertheless women did not accept it upon themselves, and most of them do not pray maariv. Regarding musaf prayer, Tzelach wrote that women are exempt; but in Magen Giborim he ruled that they are obligated; see there.

(5) To educate them — to pray the eighteen evening and morning. Nevertheless one may give them to eat before shacharit prayer; and it is forbidden to answer amen for them, as written in siman 269 and 343 regarding feeding them before Kiddush.
