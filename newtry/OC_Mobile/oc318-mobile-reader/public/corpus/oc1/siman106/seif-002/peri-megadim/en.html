Not [time-limited]. The citation burdened the Mechaber, who holds prayer is rabbinic — unlike the Rambam — and one can call a rabbinic matter also a positive mitzvah, like Hallel and the like, which is a positive mitzvah rabbinic. Certainly for those who hold it is rabbinic: from rabbinic law it becomes time-limited because the times are fixed — nevertheless it remains not time-limited, as Rabbi Yochanan said, would that one pray all day, and it also has compensation; see Levush from this, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger note 2 in the name of Mateh Yehuda. And this is the view of Pri Megadim note 23 — prayer is rabbinic, and nevertheless it is a rabbinic positive mitzvah not limited by time; see there. Nevertheless, what he wrote from Tosafos Berachos 20b, s.v. u'vitefillah — there they wrote that one can call it a positive mitzvah not limited by time; see there. But the Rambam holds prayer is by Torah law, and therefore women are obligated, for it is a positive mitzvah not limited by time, as written in chapter 1 of the Laws of Prayer 1:2. What Rashi has as the textual reading mercy — Tosafos also have that reading, and the Rambam does not have it. The Mechaber and Rif also do not have mercy, only because of would that one pray all day; see Rashba on the Rif, and see what Magen Avraham wrote in note 2 from this.

If one did not pray from the outset and remembered at night — seemingly this is in a dispute cited, similar to lulav on the first and second days, etc., that mincha at night too, as in siman 233 note 1, and once suffices; and understand Magen Avraham there, note 18, on this.
