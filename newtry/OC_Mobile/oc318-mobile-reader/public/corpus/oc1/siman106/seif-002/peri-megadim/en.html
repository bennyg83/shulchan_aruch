Which is not. See Taz, who reads into the Mechaber that prayer is Rabbinic, unlike Rambam. A Rabbinic obligation can also be called a positive commandment, such as Hallel and similar mitzvos, which are Rabbinic positive commandments. Certainly, according to the view that prayer is Rabbinic, it has fixed Rabbinic times and would be time-bound. Nevertheless, it is considered not time-bound because Rabbi Yochanan said, “Would that a person pray the entire day,” and because prayer has compensation. See Levush. See Eliyah Rabbah, section 2, in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov. This is also the view of Olas Tamid, sections 2 and 3, that prayer is Rabbinic and nevertheless is regarded Rabbinically as not time-bound. See there. Tosafos in Berachos 20b, s.v. “and prayer,” write that it can be called a positive commandment that is not time-bound. See there. Rambam, however, maintains that prayer is D’Oraisa, and therefore women are obligated because on a Torah level it is not time-bound, as he writes in chapter 1 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachos 1 and 2. Rashi’s reading is “because it is a request for mercy,” and Tosafos likewise have that reading. Rambam does not. According to the Mechaber and Rif, the reading is also not “because it is a request for mercy,” but the reason is that one may pray the entire day. See Rabbeinu Yonah on Rif, and see what I wrote regarding Magen Avraham, section 2.

This is not so, because Minchah may also be prayed at twilight, as stated in siman 233, seif 1, and one prayer suffices. Understand this. See Magen Avraham, God willing, regarding this.
