(2) That the time did not cause, etc. — for the entire day is its time itself or make-up prayers (Levush); and Malbushei Yom Tov wrote it is not precise that make-up prayers too have a time, but the reason is as Rabbeinu Yonah wrote that they said "Would that a person might pray all day long," until here. And I say that it is clear in the Talmud and Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter Tefillas HaShachar that the reason there are make-up prayers for tefillah is because of "Would that a person might pray all day," and it is easy to understand; and regarding Rabbeinu Yonah's reason cited above, Olas Tamid wrote thus: however, in any case it is not difficult as Bach wrote in the name of the Rambam that by Torah law it has no fixed time but only rabbinically, until here. And he was not precise, for Bach wrote thus according to what seems correct to him for the Rambam that tefillah is by Torah law; but according to one who says tefillah is rabbinic — if so, even though there is a fixed time rabbinically, nevertheless it is a time-caused obligation; therefore the reason "Would that he might pray," etc. was needed. And I have another reason to settle what Bach and Olas Tamid challenged on the Talmud, which states the reason of the Sages is because they are supplications and does not state the reason Rif wrote that the time did not cause; and in this manner they challenge Rif z"l that he wished to say the Talmud's intent is "they are supplications" and hints that the time did not cause, for "Would that he might pray," etc. is the reason they are supplications, as implied in Tosafos at the beginning of Tefillas HaShachar there — and see above siman 70. Magen Avraham wrote that one may give minors to eat before tefillah, as he wrote in siman 269, until here. And it is not clear, for regarding Kiddush the reason we say is that even an adult may drink according to some — and what he wrote in the name of Rashba and Ran requires study for me in Beit Yosef.
