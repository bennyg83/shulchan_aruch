[2] Which is not time-bound, etc. For the entire day is its time, either for the prayer itself or as compensation, as stated by the Levush. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that this is imprecise, because compensation also has a fixed time. Rather, the reason is as Rabbeinu Yonah writes, that the Sages said, “Would that a person pray the entire day.” I say that the Gemara and Tosafos at the beginning of the fourth chapter of Berachos explain that the reason prayer has compensation is precisely because of the principle, “Would that a person pray the entire day,” and this is readily understood. Regarding Rabbeinu Yonah’s reason, Olas Tamid writes that in any event there is no difficulty, because Bach writes in Rambam’s name that on a Torah level prayer has no fixed time, only Rabbinically. This is imprecise, because Bach wrote this only according to Rambam, who maintains that prayer is D’Oraisa. According to the view that prayer is Rabbinic, since it has a fixed Rabbinic time it would still be time-bound. Therefore, the reason of “Would that a person pray,” etc., is necessary. I have a further explanation of the question raised by Bach and Olas Tamid against the Gemara, which gives the reason that women are obligated because prayer is a request for mercy, rather than the Rif’s reason that it is not time-bound. Likewise, those who question the Rif can be answered by saying that the Gemara’s statement that prayer is a request for mercy alludes to its not being time-bound, for the principle that one may pray the entire day is itself rooted in prayer’s nature as a request for mercy, as implied by Tosafos at the beginning of the fourth chapter of Berachos. See above, siman 70. Magen Avraham writes that one may give children food before prayer, as written in siman 269. This does not appear correct, because regarding Kiddush the reason is that some authorities permit even an adult to drink. What he cites there in the name of Rashba and Ran requires study in Beit Yosef.
