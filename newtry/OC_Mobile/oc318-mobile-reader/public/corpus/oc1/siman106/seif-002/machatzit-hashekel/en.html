Seif katan 2: A positive commandment, etc. “To serve Him with all your heart.” The Sages expounded: What service is performed with the heart? This is prayer, whose essence is in the heart. See also Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 101. Rambam maintains that this is a full exposition, while Ramban holds that it is merely an asmachta.
See Tosafos, Berachos 20b, regarding the Mishnah there that women are obligated in prayer. The Gemara asks: Is this not obvious, since it is a positive commandment that is not time-bound? It answers that because it is written, “Evening, morning, and afternoon I speak and moan,” it resembles a time-bound positive commandment; therefore, it teaches that they are obligated because prayer is a request for mercy. Rashi deleted that reading and read only: women are obligated in prayer because it is a request for mercy. Tosafos explain that he deleted it because prayer is Rabbinic, so how can it be called a positive commandment? Tosafos add that it can nevertheless be reconciled, since Hallel is Rabbinic and women are exempt from it because it is time-bound. Thus, Rashi and Tosafos clearly regard prayer as Rabbinic.
