If he teaches others, he does not interrupt; even for Krias Shema he does not interrupt. This applies specifically if they would cease their learning when he interrupts. If they would not cease, he must interrupt. See Beit Yosef, siman 70. The proper place for this discussion is siman 61, but because I did not wish to leave the page blank, I recorded what I found in the aforementioned Beit Yosef in Orach Chayim, siman 61. It is written in Sefer HaPelia concerning the words, “I am Hashem your God, truth,” that in Egypt there was someone who followed this practice, and all the great scholars of the generation rebuked him. In truth, Maharshal wrote in a responsum that when he prayed on a yahrzeit he repeated “I am Hashem your God,” and not the word “truth,” because “truth” is not part of Krias Shema, thereby completing the number of words in Krias Shema. Mateh Moshe agreed with Maharshal. Although Beit Yosef cited three passages from the Zohar that say to recite “Hashem your God, truth,” this is the view of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai’s circle, whereas Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai himself states in the Zohar that one should say, “I am Hashem your God, truth.” The Zohar explains that corresponding to the seventy-two words of the heavenly tribunal, Israel below completes the count with the seventy-two words of the passage of tzitzis, and repeats three words: “I” corresponding to Avraham, “Hashem” corresponding to Yitzchak, and “your God” corresponding to Yaakov. His circle said: “Hashem your God, truth,” with “Hashem” corresponding to Avraham, “your God” to Yitzchak, and “truth” to Yaakov. See there. However, I found that David, son of Rabbi Yosef Tzimlish of Krakow, wrote that in Abudraham, Tzeror HaMor, Tzioni, Sefer HaMussar, Tolaas Yaakov, Taamei HaMitzvos, and Recanati, all read “Hashem your God, truth.” As for completing the count with “truth,” although it is not part of Krias Shema, Maharam Alashkar and Maharl Chaviv explained that since one may not interrupt between the word “your God” and the word “truth,” it may be counted. Thus far Mateh Moshe. I found all of this in the writing of the aforementioned David.
