For Krias Shema. He must recite the blessings before and after it, as stated by Rambam in chapter 2. Although they interrupted for Krias Shema, they return to their learning. They may not pray, since in any event they do not interrupt their learning even for a moment. This is also implied by Tosafos on Shabbos 11a, s.v. “such as Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai,” for otherwise the Gemara’s question would have no force, since perhaps he merely wished to be stringent upon himself. This is clear.
He does not interrupt. Nevertheless, he should study a halachah that mentions the Exodus from Egypt, in order to mention it at its proper time, and after his learning he should complete all of Krias Shema. See Gemara Berachos 13.
He does not interrupt at all. This applies even if he studies alone, as stated by the Levush and Olas Tamid. Although siman 89 rules that it is forbidden to study once the time for prayer has arrived, nevertheless, if he began, he does not interrupt, as stated by Magen Avraham. Or Zaru’a writes that if his learning would not cease when he interrupts, he must interrupt.
