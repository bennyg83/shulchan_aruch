One whose Torah is his occupation, etc., but we stop — see Eliyah Rabbah siman 70, who elaborated to challenge the Levush in what he ruled that if he has leisure to recite after finishing his study, he need not stop even when he studies alone; and Eliyah Rabbah brought several proofs for this from the words of Rambam, Ran, Ra'avad, and the words of Rama in the gloss where he explained in siman 106 that it deals specifically when he studies with others. However, per what Peri Chadash wrote in siman 70 end of letter 3 — see there — he well rejected all his proofs from Ran and Ra'avad; and Peri Chadash wrote there that the essential point is that when there is leisure one need not stop from Torah study even for Keriat Shema. And from the words of the Gra at the end of this siman, per what Demsek Eliezer explained his words, it also implies that he agrees even regarding Keriat Shema with Rama — even if he studies alone [even though from the beginning of the Gra's words, what he wrote that certainly he is not lesser than a meal seems at first glance the opposite; nevertheless it can be settled that this is his intent in what he added to bring proof from the Yerushalmi to prove that it is even more preferable than a meal, and therefore the Sages do not disagree with R' Shimon except where through his study Keriat Shema would be entirely nullified; for if you say the Sages are stringent even when there is leisure, perhaps they disagree with R' Shimon only for this reason and the Yerushalmi would not challenge at all — see there]. Also what Eliyah Rabbah further wrote to question whether not to permit even if he began during the permitted time — Shulchan Aruch siman 235 seif 2 and siman 252, and Magen Avraham at end of siman 70, and several other achronim upheld this distinction regarding other matters; therefore it seems to me that needless to say if he began to study before the time of Keriat Shema arrived, he certainly need not stop if he knows the time will not pass thereby; and even if he began to study alone in his house after the time of Keriat Shema arrived, nevertheless if when he does not study now his study would be nullified and he would not study at all, it is possible to be lenient and rely on the words of Levush and Peri Chadash cited above; and it is possible that it is better in this that he stop and recite Keriat Shema and return to his study in order to fulfill the view of the stringent ones, even though he is without tefillin — and this requires further study. And know that in a beit midrash one need not be stringent at all in this, and even l'chatchila it is permitted to begin and study even alone before tefillah when the time of Keriat Shema will not pass thereby, for in a public beit midrash the concern "perhaps he will be distracted from his learning" does not apply, as above in siman 89 seif 7 in Mishna Berurah.

And if he teaches others, etc. — see in Sefer Maamar Mordekhai who found great difficulty with this gloss, for per what the achronim explained the intent is that he does not stop even if the time will pass thereby; and from what Rama brought who wrote as explained above siman 89 — one who examines there will see well in the words of Rabbeinu Yonah brought in Beit Yosef, from whom is the source of the ruling there in Shulchan Aruch seif 6, and likewise in Levush there it appears the reverse; and perhaps here it deals when he began to teach others before the time of Keriat Shema arrived — therefore we are lenient not to stop even if the time will pass. And see Berachos 13b in Rashi s.v. lo chazina, etc., that he would recite to his disciples before the time of Keriat Shema, etc. — it implies from Rashi that after the time of Keriat Shema arrived it is forbidden for him to begin to study even with others if he estimates the time will pass, but he must recite it beforehand.
