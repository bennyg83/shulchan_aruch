One whose Torah study is his occupation, etc., but we interrupt: See Eliyah Rabbah in siman 70, who argues at length against the Levush’s ruling that if one will still have time to recite Krias Shema after completing his learning, he need not interrupt even when studying alone. Eliyah Rabbah brings several proofs from Rambam, Ran, and Baal HaMaor, and explains that Rama’s gloss here, in light of siman 106, applies specifically when one studies with others. However, according to Peri Chadash at the end of siman 70, section 3, see there carefully, all his proofs from Ran and Baal HaMaor are rejected. Peri Chadash rules that where time remains, one need not interrupt Torah study even for Krias Shema. The words of the Gra at the end of this siman, as explained by Damesek Eliezer, likewise imply that he agrees with Rama even regarding Krias Shema and even when one studies alone. Although the beginning of the Gra’s words, that Torah study is certainly no worse than a meal, appears at first to imply the opposite, it can be explained that his purpose in adding the proof from the Yerushalmi is to establish that Torah study is even more weighty than a meal. The Sages disagree with Rabbi Shimon only where his learning will cause him to miss Krias Shema entirely, for were the Sages stringent even where time remains, perhaps Rabbi Shimon disagreed only about that, and the Yerushalmi’s question would prove nothing. See there. Eliyah Rabbah also questioned whether one may be lenient even when he began at a permitted time. Yet Shulchan Aruch in siman 235, seif 2, and siman 652, together with Magen Avraham at the end of siman 70 and several other Acharonim, uphold this distinction regarding other matters. Therefore, it appears to me that if he began learning before the time for Krias Shema arrived, he certainly need not interrupt if he knows that this will not cause the time to pass. Even if he began studying alone at home after the time arrived, if failure to study now would cause him to lose the learning entirely and not study at all, perhaps one may be lenient and rely on the Levush and Peri Chadash. It may be preferable that he interrupt, recite Krias Shema, and return to his learning, thereby satisfying the stringent views, even though he is without tefillin. This requires study. In a public beis midrash there is no need for stringency at all, and one may even initially begin studying alone before prayer, provided this will not cause the time for Krias Shema to pass, since in a public beis midrash there is no concern that he will become engrossed in his learning, as explained above in siman 89, seif 7, in the Mishnah Berurah.
If he teaches others, etc.: See Maamar Mordechai, who found this gloss very difficult. According to the Acharonim, its meaning is that he does not interrupt even if the time will pass as a result. Yet from Rama’s reference to what was explained above in siman 89, one who carefully examines Rabbeinu Yonah’s words cited by Beit Yosef, which are the source of the ruling there in Shulchan Aruch, seif 6, and the Levush there, will see the opposite. Perhaps here the case is that he began teaching others before the time for Krias Shema arrived, and therefore we are lenient that he need not interrupt even if the time will pass. See Berachos 13b, Rashi s.v. “I did not see,” etc., which states that he was teaching his students before the time for Krias Shema. Rashi implies that after the time has arrived one may not begin learning even with others if he estimates that the time will pass, but must first recite Krias Shema.
