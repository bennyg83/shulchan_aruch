(6) Such as Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai — for they were not engaged at all in their work, and they do not interrupt their study even for a moment. But we, who interrupt words of Torah for our work, all the more so should we interrupt for prayer.

(7) Kriat Shema — because it is by Torah law, unlike prayer, which is rabbinic. And even if we hold it is by Torah law, nevertheless by Torah law it has no fixed time, and once a day suffices, as above. And when he reads Kriat Shema he should read it with its blessings, and afterward return to his study and not pray. And even though one must cancel and interrupt one's study for the performance of all mitzvot, even rabbinic ones — "one who learns and does not fulfill, better for him that he had not been born" — nevertheless prayer, since it is only a request for mercy, is lighter than other mitzvot for people such as these, who do not interrupt their study even for a moment.

(8) He does not interrupt — even if through this the time will pass; and specifically in a case where if they do not study now they will be cancelled and will not be able to gather afterward to study. Otherwise he interrupts if the time for Kriat Shema or prayer will pass. From Pri Chadash it is implied that in every case he interrupts if the time for prayer will pass. One who cancels prayer because of study — even if he studies with others the whole day — is as if he did not study [Rokeach]. And in Beit Avraham he also wrote in the name of Peskei Tosafos to be strict about this. See Biur Halachah, where we explained that for all opinions, ab initio it is forbidden for him to begin studying, even with others, after the time for Kriat Shema has already arrived, if he assumes that through his study the time will pass; rather he is obligated to read Kriat Shema first.

(9) The first verse — in order to accept the kingdom of heaven at the time of Kriat Shema; and he should return afterward to a halachah that contains the exodus from Egypt, in order to mention the exodus from Egypt at the time of Kriat Shema; and after his study it is good that he complete the entire Kriat Shema, even though its time has passed.

(10) He does not interrupt at all — meaning even for the first verse, and even if he studies alone. The later authorities wrote that even if he began to study in a forbidden manner — meaning after the time for Kriat Shema and prayer has already arrived, when it is forbidden for him to begin studying until he prays first, if he studies in his house and is not accustomed to go to the synagogue to pray, as written in siman 89 — nevertheless, if he already began, he need not interrupt and may study as long as time still remains. Eliyah Rabbah ruled that regarding Kriat Shema, which is by Torah law, even if time will remain afterward to read it, nevertheless he must interrupt immediately and read all of it if he studies alone; see there in siman 70 and in this siman. All this is if he studies in his house. But if he studies in the beit midrash, or even in his house yet he began to study permissibly — meaning before the time for Kriat Shema arrived — certainly one relies on the ruling of the Rama not to interrupt even afterward, as long as time remains. See Biur Halachah.
