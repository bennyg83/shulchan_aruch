(6) Such as Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai: They did not engage in work at all and did not interrupt their learning even for a moment. We, who interrupt Torah study for our work, must certainly interrupt for prayer.
(7) For Krias Shema: Because it is D’Oraisa, unlike prayer, which is Rabbinic. Even if prayer is D’Oraisa, on a Torah level it has no fixed time, and once a day is sufficient, as explained above. When reciting Krias Shema, he recites it with its blessings, then returns to his learning and does not pray. Although one must interrupt Torah study to perform all mitzvos, even Rabbinic mitzvos, for one who learns but does not fulfill is better off not having been created, prayer is merely a request for mercy and is therefore more lenient than other mitzvos for people such as these, who do not interrupt their learning even for a moment.
(8) He does not interrupt: Even if the time will pass as a result. This applies specifically where, if they do not learn now, their learning will be canceled and they will be unable to gather later. Otherwise he interrupts if the time for Krias Shema or prayer will pass. Chayei Adam implies that in every case he interrupts if the time for prayer will pass. One who neglects prayer because of study, even if he studies with others all day, is regarded as though he did not study, as stated by Rokeach. Eliyah Rabbah likewise cites Piskei Tosafos stringently. See Biur Halachah, where we explained that all agree one may not initially begin studying, even with others, after the time for Krias Shema has arrived if he estimates that the time will pass because of his learning. He must first recite Krias Shema.
(9) The first verse: To accept the yoke of Heaven during the time for Krias Shema. He should then review a halachah mentioning the Exodus from Egypt, in order to mention the Exodus during the time for Krias Shema. After learning, it is proper that he complete all of Krias Shema even though its time has passed.
(10) He does not interrupt at all: Meaning, not even for the first verse, and even when studying alone. The Acharonim write that this applies even if he began learning improperly, after the time for Krias Shema and prayer had already arrived, when one may not begin learning before praying if he studies at home and is not accustomed to go to the synagogue, as written in siman 89. Nevertheless, once he began he need not interrupt and may continue learning as long as sufficient time remains. Eliyah Rabbah rules that regarding Krias Shema, which is D’Oraisa, even if time will remain later, one who studies alone must interrupt immediately and recite all of it. See there in siman 70 and here. All this applies when he studies at home. If he studies in the beis midrash, or even at home but began permissibly before the time for Krias Shema arrived, one may certainly rely upon Rama and not interrupt as long as sufficient time remains. See Biur Halachah.
