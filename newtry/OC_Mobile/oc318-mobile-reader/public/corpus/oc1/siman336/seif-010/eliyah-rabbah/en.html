[11] It is permitted, etc. It appears that the later authorities agree with this ruling, as stated by Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. He overlooked the Maharshal in Perek Ein Tzadin, siman 10, who prohibits it. See the end of siman 515. Nevertheless, as practical law the primary ruling is to permit, because all the halachic authorities I have seen permit it. Only the Ran in Perek Ein Tzadin, in the name of the Geonim, is a lone dissenting view, and the Ran himself in Perek Lulav HaGazul permits it.
[12] It is prohibited, etc. If when he enters the garden he smells the fragrance from a distance, he recites the blessing then. But once he reaches the fragrant object itself, he should not recite the blessing, as stated by Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. See siman 217, seif katan 8.
[13] Lest he cut it, etc. If you ask that he could bite it from the attached plant with his mouth, Rashi answers that there is no greater act of detaching than this. Nevertheless, perhaps he would not be liable because this is not the usual manner of detaching, as stated by the Magen Avraham.
