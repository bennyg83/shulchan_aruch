1) [Seif 1] One may not climb a tree. Although one may walk upon grass, as stated in seif 3, there the principal concern is that the grass may be detached by his stepping. We rule like Rabbi Shimon that an unintended result is permitted. Regarding a tree, however, the reason is a decree lest one detach something, and Rabbi Shimon agrees that this is forbidden. Beis Yosef writes this, and Taz, paragraph 1, cites him; see there.
2) There: Whether moist or dry. The Sages decreed against a dry tree because of a moist one. Although Tur writes that a dry tree may be used during the summer when it has neither fruit nor branches that could be removed, Beis Yosef writes that we follow Rif and Rambam, who make no distinction between moist and dry; see there. He therefore omits Tur's words from Shulchan Aruch. This is also Levush's view. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1; Birkei Yosef, Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 12, paragraph 5. This is the view of the later authorities. See below, paragraph 80.
3) There: One may not hang from it. One may not lean upon it either. Eruvin 100a; Rambam, chapter 21, halachah 6. The prohibition against leaning applies when the tree is thin and is moved by his weight. If it is thick, leaning is permitted. Chayei Adam there, paragraph 6. See below, paragraph 90.
4) There: One may not hang from it. One may not use it in any manner, such as placing an object upon it, taking an object from it, tying an animal to it, or anything similar. All this is a decree lest one climb and detach fruit, leaves, or branches. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
5) Regarding those who tie ropes to a tall tree on Shabbos and seat children upon them to swing back and forth, see Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, who writes that this involves several prohibitions, including tying and using a tree. He concludes that the communal leaders are responsible to correct this improper practice; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah cites him.
6) There: One may not use something attached to the ground, etc. This refers to a tree or something resembling it, such as species of ground produce whose stalks are hard and tall like a tree. This is precisely implied by the wording, 'as a decree lest one climb and detach.' One may use a vegetable, provided he does not move it, as stated above in siman 312, seif 6; see there. See below, paragraph 15.
7) There: One may not use something attached to the ground, etc. This applies when it is more than three tefachim above the ground, as stated below in seif 2.
8) There: If he climbed inadvertently, he may descend. Although descending also uses the tree, it need not be forbidden for this reason, since remaining seated there likewise uses the tree. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2.
9) There: If he climbed deliberately, he may not descend. He is penalized to remain there throughout Shabbos. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
10) There: In every case he may descend, etc. This applies even if he climbed intending to descend after nightfall. Levush. It applies even if he climbed deliberately. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
11) There: Some say, etc., and this is primary. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, however, writes that in a rabbinic matter the law follows the lenient view. We likewise wrote above in siman 13, paragraph 7, that whenever Shulchan Aruch gives an unqualified ruling and afterward cites 'some say,' his intent is to rule like the unqualified view; see there. The later authorities agree.
12) There, gloss: Specifically a person, etc. This means that only a person was permitted to descend in these cases, because remaining there itself constitutes a prohibition and he must descend. If one placed an object upon the tree, however, it may not be taken down under any circumstances. Taz, paragraph 3; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
13) There, gloss: It is forbidden to take it, etc. Taking the object from the tree itself uses something attached to the ground. For this reason, one may not place a lamp upon a tree, as stated in siman 277, seif 4; see there. Although one may tie a rope to a cow's mouth, as stated in siman 317, seif 7, a tree differs because this is its ordinary manner of use and is properly called using something attached to the ground. An animal is not ordinarily used this way. Rosh, however, implies that even with a tree, the prohibition does not arise from taking the object but from the concern that one may climb and detach something. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. See below, siman 514, seif 6, and examine carefully.
