(א) One does not climb a tree — even though regarding grass it is permitted to walk on them as in seif 3, there the reason is because the main concern is that they be uprooted by his treading; and here we hold like R' Shimon that davar she'eino mitkaven is permitted; but on a tree the reason is lest he detach — and in this R' Shimon agrees it is forbidden, as Beit Yosef wrote; and the Rosh wrote it appears Rif does not distinguish between tree and grass, and therefore he did not bring that law "one does not climb a tree" etc.; rather certainly Rif holds everything is forbidden even dry, not as the custom. Maharshal challenged Beit Yosef, for Rif also distinguishes between moist and dry — otherwise Rif should have written like the Rosh.

(ב) And one does not use anything attached to the ground — from this language it appears even regarding a vegetable; and see afterward.

(ג) And specifically a person who climbed there — meaning specifically regarding a person they permitted these because he does a prohibition by standing there and must descend; but if he left an object — forbidden to remove it in any manner.

(ד) But soft reeds, etc. — this requires study. First clarify: this is the gloss that Beit Yosef brings; his reason is that it was only regarding a vegetable, and regarding a vegetable there is no shevus at all; and he wrote they learned this from "R' Nachman said go flatten" etc.; Beit Yosef concluded and the poskim who did not mention this possibly relied on what is written "forbidden to use a tree," implying specifically a tree but a vegetable is permitted — otherwise it should write "forbidden to use attached." If so, since Shulchan Aruch already wrote at the start of the seif "one does not use attached," it implies even a vegetable; and how did Rama write soft reeds are permitted to use? Beit Yosef challenged how the poskim ruled not like the plain Gemara that distinguishes between soft and hard. It appears another path: the sugya in Eruvin 34 — before this, if one placed eruv on a tree it was eruv though he cannot take the eruv because one does not use a tree; they establish it like R' who holds all shevus they did not decree in Beit HaMikdash; eruv acquisition is Beit HaMikdash. Then the Mishnah: if placed on top of a reed while detached and inserted — eruv; they say the latter part is not R', for otherwise what difference from a tree that was eruv at first? Ravina said the whole thing is R' and the latter part is a decree lest he cut — Rashi: because the reed is soft there is concern lest he cut when he takes it; but in the first case on a hard tree between twilight lest he climb and detach they are not concerned; but for cutting a reed it is certainly so since it is soft it will be cut — not shevus but melacha of reaping. That army came to Neharde'a; R' Nachman said go make a flattening in the marsh and tomorrow we will sit on them. Rami bar Chama challenged R' Nachman: detached and inserted — yes; not detached and inserted — no. Rashi: reeds are like a tree and forbidden to use. He said: there regarding ozradin that were already hardened like a tree; but regarding ozradin while still soft it is like a vegetable and they did not mention shevus for a vegetable. We see a dispute: for Ravina it is stricter when soft like a vegetable; for R' Nachman wherever soft like a vegetable there is no shevus; where hard like a tree there is shevus; we need not decree lest he cut. The gloss follows R' Nachman and is lenient on soft; but Ashiri rules like Ravina — soft is stricter lest he cut; all the more so a vegetable that is softer — concern lest he cut and forbidden to use. And what he wrote afterward permitting walking on grass — that case is different because he does not use it and his hands are far; unlike one who uses it and there is concern lest he cut; use of a tree is also from this lest he detach since he is near his hand; concern lest he cut applies even to a vegetable; therefore Rambam wrote one does not use attached to include also a vegetable after mentioning tree law — like Ravina; and also from muktzeh one may not use a vegetable. They gave one reason for all even for those who do not hold muktzeh and to distinguish tree and reeds for all; we do not hold this gloss to permit soft reeds and vegetable; Rama's words are astonishing after seeing Shulchan Aruch per Rambam.
