(1) A Sefer Torah is placed upon the bimah and they circle it, etc. The bimah remains for us in place of the altar, as stated in the Gemara, Megillah 31, that Hashem answered Avraham Avinu that whenever his children read before Him the order of the offerings, He considers it as though they had offered the sacrifices before Him.
(2) They would circle the altar. They would likewise circle it once each day, and seven times on the seventh day.
(3) Toward the right. Since the Sefer Torah is upon the bimah, members of the congregation who stand on the eastern side must turn their faces toward the Sefer Torah before beginning to circle. North then becomes their right side. Their order of circling is therefore north, west, south, and east. The chazzan, who stands facing the Aron, should not initially turn in a way that places his back toward the Aron. Nevertheless, he too must circle northward together with the congregation, even though for him that is toward the left.
(4) On Shabbos they do not circle. This is because in the Beis HaMikdash they did not take the aravah on Shabbos. All the more so according to our custom, when we circle with the lulav, since the lulav is not taken on Shabbos.
(5) They do not take out a Sefer Torah. Since no circling is performed, as stated by Darkei Moshe.
(6) Upon the bimah. Rather, they merely open the Aron Kodesh and recite the Hoshanos, as explained below by the acharonim.
