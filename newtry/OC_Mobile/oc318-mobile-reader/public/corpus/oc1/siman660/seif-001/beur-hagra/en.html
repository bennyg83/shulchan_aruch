Seif 1: The custom is, etc. This appears in the Aggadah and is cited in Yalkut Tehillim at the end of remez 703. See there. The passage nevertheless requires study, because it describes the order of the circling as including all Israel, adults, etc. In the Beis HaMikdash, however, only the kohanim circled, since a non-kohen may not enter between the Ulam and the altar, as stated explicitly in the fourth chapter of Sukkah.
As a remembrance, etc. This follows the fourth chapter of Sukkah. We circle the Sefer Torah, which remains for us in place of the altar, as stated at the end of Megillah: This is understandable while the Beis HaMikdash exists, etc.
And they take out seven, etc. The Jerusalem Talmud states that the circlings commemorate Yericho. On the seventh day they circled seven times, while on each of the first six days they circled once. We similarly commemorate the seven shofars. Although at Yericho the shofars were sounded each day, the principal mitzvah of the aravah is only on the seventh day. According to the final opinion, on the seventh day everyone sounded the shofar.
And the circlings, etc. As stated in the sixth chapter of Zevachim.
And when it falls on Shabbos, etc. See Magen Avraham and Sukkah 43b. This is certainly true according to our calendar, in which the seventh day does not fall on Shabbos, and all the more so according to the opinion concerning lulav that the lulav is not taken on Shabbos.
And they do not take out, etc. Since they do not circle. This is from Darkei Moshe.
