Once. See Baer Heitev. See Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote that therefore a mourner who has a lulav should honor one who does not have one by letting him circle, for perhaps the halachah is like the one who says that a mourner should circle, and when he does not circle he does something wrong. In Eshel Avraham he wrote in the name of Beis Yehudah that this means that he is not obligated to circle, but this is incorrect. It appears that the reference was marked by mistake, and should be marked on the beginning of the gloss, that one who has no lulav should not circle; regarding this he wrote that he is not obligated to circle. So too the Bach wrote in the name of Rashal. I examined the responsa Beis Yehudah and found in siman 18 there as I wrote, and there he brought from Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, who brought from the Bach in the name of Rashal; see there. Regarding the order of the piyyutim said each day and on Hoshana Rabbah, see the Levush. He wrote that the custom is that when they say Hoshana, they shake the lulav; see there: 'then the trees of the forest will sing,' etc. See there regarding the recitation of 'Ani vaho' mentioned in the Mishnah, which Rashi explained are two Names from the seventy-two-letter Name. See the Levush for the explanation of the matter. In Eliyah Rabbah he brought what the Beis Yosef, Levush, Abudraham, and Tosafos Yom Tov wrote in the name of Ani, that it is the alef of Yisrael; but Rashi in Sukkah wrote that it is the alef of 'achareihem.' See there. See Birkei Yosef, who brought from the Yerushalmi. See in my responsa Beis Ephraim regarding the explanation of the piyyut 'kehosha'ta elim,' etc., in the stanza 'kehosha'ta goy veElokim derushim leyesha Elokim,' based on the Yerushalmi in Sukkah, which expounds 'that You redeemed from Egypt, a nation and its God' to mean: as it were, You redeemed Yourself; see there. I also wrote regarding the stanza 'chanun beyadam maavirim' that in my humble opinion it is a scribal error that entered the old books. Now I have seen in a siddur written on old parchment that it is also written according to the version before us. It appears to me that it should be 'chinam beyadam maavidim'; see there, with the reason for the matter.
