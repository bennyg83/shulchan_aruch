Yet the practice is to circle even the mourner himself — end of his words. We practice not to circle, and the Magen Avraham brought proof not to circle from what is said in the chapter Lulav and Aravah 44: R' Levi holds that blemished kohanim enter between the Ulam and the altar to go out with the aravah, and R' Yochanan said: who says blemished kohanim enter? perhaps only unblemished ones. Tosafot wrote: those with uncovered heads are judged like blemished kohanim; if so, according to R' Yochanan it is forbidden for one with uncovered head, and a mourner has uncovered head. This is not correct — if so, anyone who is not a kohen should not circle now, for in the Temple only kohanim circled around the altar and a non-kohen was forbidden to enter there; even blemished kohanim would not circle now. Rather we certainly do not compare this to the Temple but we do it as a remembrance of the Temple. It seems there is some reason for our practice, like the custom that a mourner does not lead prayer on days without tachanun, as Rama wrote in a responsum — the reason is that from the outset it is harsh against the mourner; therefore it is not proper to be prayer leader even though this reason is weak. In that responsum it is explained at length.
