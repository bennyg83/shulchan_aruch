2) Ibid.: The custom is to bring a Torah scroll, etc. However, Rambam, Hilchos Lulav, chapter 7, halacha 23, did not mention this custom of bringing a Torah scroll onto the tevah, but only of setting down a tevah; see there. Likewise, Shaar HaKavanos, 105c, did not mention that one must bring a Torah scroll onto the tevah and circle it. He wrote as follows: "Afterward, they circle the altar or the tevah once each day with the lulav and its species. The intention is to draw the aforementioned chasadim from the aspect of surrounding light to Malchus, which is called altar or tevah, etc." See there. So too wrote Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Haazinu, os 15, that regarding the circuits with the lulav on all the days of the festival, one may circle the tevah in the synagogue even though there is no Torah scroll upon it. Placing a Torah scroll on the tevah is not indispensable for the circuits, because according to the mystical meaning, the essential element is the tevah. However, those people who do not go to the synagogue because of circumstances beyond their control, and place a chair in the house and circle it, accomplish nothing, because it is not considered a tevah. Nevertheless, it appears to me that one who is ill and cannot circle the tevah in the synagogue should place a chair in his house, place a Tanach upon it, and circle it, so as not to forgo the mitzvah of the circuit, etc. End quote. This implies that if he places a table, it is certainly comparable to a tevah, and for some reason he may circle the table, such as when they pray in a house for some reason, or in order to perform the circuits in the sukkah, and the like. Without a reason, however, one should not annul the custom to place a Torah scroll upon the tevah and circle it.
