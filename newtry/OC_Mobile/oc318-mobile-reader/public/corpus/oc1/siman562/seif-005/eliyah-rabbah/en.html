[5] [Levush] To pray Aneinu, etc. This implies that nevertheless he must fast, and not like Magen Avraham, siman 563. Also according to what I wrote above, that the Rama required praying Aneinu even without ever having accepted: all the more so in this case where he contemplated; and even according to the Levush's view, about which I wrote above that whenever he accepted, even not at the time of Minchah, he prays: if so, the same law applies here regarding hirhur when he accepted. And even though the Taz wrote that a person's way is to reconsider in his thought, [and] perforce the hirhur is not complete until its fixed time at Minchah according to everyone: this is not clear; rather the Tur and Shulchan Aruch follow their rationale that even acceptance does not help not at the time of Minchah; but according to us, since [acceptance] helps, hirhur also helps. And so it is proven from Tosafos and Semag and Kol Bo and Agur, siman 869, and in the Minhagim of between Pesach and Shavuos: that even hirhur not at the time of Minchah requires fasting, and he prays Aneinu as an individual. And incidentally I will explain the Rosh's words in the first chapter of Taanis, where he asked against Rabbeinu Tam who holds that hirhur helps; this is his language: Rashi's wording does not appear so, for he says "any fast that he did not accept while it was still day is not called a fast," implying that there is no designation of a fast at all for praying Aneinu and for fulfilling his vow; until here. And the Bach wrote: I do not understand, for according to Rabbeinu Tam, when he resolved in his heart it is as if he brought it out of his mouth and accepted it explicitly while it was still day; until here. And in my humble opinion I understood: for the Rosh himself afterward wrote that hirhur is like accepting; rather it appears to me that the difficulty is not about hirhur, but about what Rabbeinu Tam wrote that even before the Minchah prayer it is acceptance after the fact; about this he asked that it appears in the Gemara that it is not a fast at all, for the Gemara explained "while it was still day" as meaning at Minchah. Further the Bach wrote: a great wonder upon the Rosh regarding what he wrote that he need not fulfill his vow; until here; and he was not precise, for the Rosh said only regarding praying Aneinu and not fulfilling a vow that he already vowed; but he certainly must complete since it is like accepting. Further the Bach wondered with a standing wonder upon the Rosh and Tur and Shulchan Aruch, who ruled so that hirhur is like accepting, while at the beginning of Shevuos and the Tur in Yoreh De'ah, siman 210, he ruled that even regarding a neder it is not like accepting; and therefore he ruled in practice not to say Aneinu when he contemplated at Minchah; until here. And in my humble opinion there is no difficulty at all: for here regarding a fast it is different, for it is like tzedakah, for which hirhur helps; also "the reward of a fast is tzedakah." And what he further wondered from the Tur in Choshen Mishpat, siman 212, who ruled like the Rosh that hirhur does not help regarding tzedakah, and also wrote that such is the view of most poskim; until here: I wonder, for Maharik's responsum, root 161, escaped his notice, which asks this and concludes that the primary [view] is like the Rosh's words here, and that such is the view of most poskim. And further, in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 258 and Choshen Mishpat, siman 212, the Shulchan Aruch brings two views, and the Rama concludes that hirhur is like speech. Also it appears to me to resolve the Rosh's and Tur's words: that a fast is better than tzedakah, and is like actual hekdesh, for he offers his fat to the Most High, as is written in siman 568 at the end of se'if 2; and so it appears in the Taz.
