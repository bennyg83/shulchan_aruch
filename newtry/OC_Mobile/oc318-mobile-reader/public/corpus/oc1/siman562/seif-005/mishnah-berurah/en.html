(21) That the individual did not accept upon himself, etc.: to exclude a communal fast, which needs no acceptance, as explained in se'if 12.
(22) While it was still day is not a fast: this implies that even though he afterward accepted it at night, it does not help. And that which is explained below in se'if 10 according to the first view, that acceptance helps regarding a fast of hours to pray Aneinu: here, since he began to fast for the sake of an obligation and afterward accepted it, it is not acceptance at all [Magen Avraham in s.k. 15]; and even if they completed until tzeit hakochavim it is also not considered a fast, as it is said "sanctify a fast," implying that we require prior designation [Rif].
(23) He has not fulfilled his vow: and nevertheless, if he began to fast on this day he must pay his vow with what he now accepted, even though he did not accept it while it was still day; and it is also an atonement for sin, for it is no less than one who afflicts himself by abstaining from meat and wine, as below in se'if 11; so wrote Peri Megadim and the Gra in explaining the Rama's words. And Yad Ephraim, and likewise Shiyurei Leket, dispute this: for since he intended to fulfill with this the vow that he vowed previously, if so the acceptance was in error and he may retract and eat that very day.
(24) However, some say that he prays Aneinu: their reason is that what they said in the Gemara, "any fast, etc., is not a fast," means only regarding not having fulfilled a vow by which he was previously obligated; but the designation of a fast is upon it and he may pray Aneinu, even if they did not accept it at all.
(25) Regarding an individual's fast: because an individual says Aneinu in Shomea Tefillah and does not fix a blessing for himself, as above in se'if 1 in the gloss. However, it is possible that this is specifically with one deficiency, namely that they did not accept it while it was still day, but they completed until evening; but if he also does not intend to complete it, it is possible that it is not considered a fast even regarding Aneinu; and it is proper that in such a manner he skip the words "the fast of our affliction" and say "Answer us, Hashem, answer us on this day, for we are in great trouble," etc.
(26) He prays Aneinu even though, etc.: and in this, according to everyone, he may properly say regarding it "the fast of our affliction" even though they did not accept it from yesterday at all, since Heaven imposed it upon him, and that stands in place of accepting it himself.
