8) The Mishbetzos Zahav wrote in os 1 that it is implied by the aforementioned words of the Taz that even if one did not accept the fast at Minchah, and even for a partial-day fast, he should recite Aneinu while omitting the two words. It is implied that it is preferable to recite it after the prayer, in Elokai Netzor, rather than interrupt Shomea Tefillah. He further wrote that during the Ten Days of Repentance, when people fast a partial-day fast until after Minchah Gedolah or the like, it appears that one may not eat until he first prays Minchah, just as with a bridegroom in seif 2. However, this would seemingly apply according to the view that he says "the fast of our fasting," so that he not be speaking falsely in the prayer after he has already eaten. If, however, he omits those two words, perhaps he may eat after midday without first praying Minchah, and afterwards, when he prays Minchah with the congregation, he may say Aneinu "on a day of distress," even though he has eaten, since he does not mention "the fast of our fasting," provided that he stipulated that he would eat after midday or that this was his intention. He left this unresolved; see there. Therefore, it appears that whether he stipulated that he would not complete his fast, or he did not accept the fast on the preceding day, which according to the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch is likewise not considered a fast, as written in seif 5, in all these cases it is preferable to recite Aneinu after completing the Minchah prayer, in Elokai Netzor, meaning before saying the final "May the words of my mouth, etc." after Elokai Netzor. It is also preferable not to say the two words "the fast of our fasting," thereby removing oneself from the dispute.
