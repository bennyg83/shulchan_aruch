At first glance, one could say that the halachah follows Rosh in his responsum, that a firm mental resolve is ineffective regarding charity, and that fasting is different because it closely resembles an offering. If so, Rama’s statement in his gloss in Choshen Mishpat and in Yoreh Deah 258:13, that the primary view is the first opinion because Rosh ruled this way regarding a fast, is not a proof, as explained. It would follow that if one merely contemplated fasting for several hours as a measure of discomfort, so as not to separate from the congregation, as discussed in paragraph 3, perhaps this does not resemble an offering and thought is not equivalent to speech. This too requires study. Maharik, however, maintains that Rosh’s responsum, which rules that thought is not equivalent to speech regarding charity, disagrees with what Rosh wrote in Taanis in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam. Therefore, although Rosh rules in the responsum that thought is not equivalent to speech for charity, the principal ruling follows what he wrote in his halachic rulings regarding a fast, and the same applies to charity: thought is equivalent to speech, because his rulings take precedence over his responsum where they conflict. Mordechai also cites Rashi that, regarding charity, thought is equivalent to speech. End quote from Maharik. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and Yoreh Deah, siman 18, that even regarding a vow, thought is not equivalent to speech and one’s mouth and heart must agree.
