(s.k. 2) Even though, etc. That is, if he spelled out, etc.; but, etc., he must complete: not because of saying Aneinu, but in order to fulfill his acceptance; for a plain fast means [intent] to complete.
See se'if 2 and the end of siman 249. Meaning, that from these two places it appears so. For in se'if 2 he wrote that specifically during the Ten Days of Repentance he need not complete, for the reason explained there. And likewise even a bridegroom need not complete even though he does not stipulate; nevertheless certainly his intent is according to the custom, and the custom is to hold the chuppah by day, and the bridegroom must taste from the cup of blessing; if so it is as if he stipulated that he would not complete. But those who do not fast all Ten Days of Repentance, who need acceptance, it is explained in se'if 2 that they must complete. And likewise at the end of siman 249 it is explained explicitly that accepting a plain fast requires completion until tzeit hakochavim, unless he spelled out explicitly at the time of acceptance that he would not complete. And so it is in this siman, se'if 3.
And if, etc. Siman 568, se'if 1.
And if, etc.: he does not pray Aneinu. And therefore the Rama wrote in se'if 2 that the bridegroom should pray Aneinu before he enters the chuppah.
