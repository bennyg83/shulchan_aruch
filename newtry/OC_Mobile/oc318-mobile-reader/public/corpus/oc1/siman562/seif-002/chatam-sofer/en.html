There in Magen Avraham, seif katan 16: "It is strained to distinguish." In my humble opinion, although regarding charity the Mechaber rules like Maharam cited by Mordechai, nevertheless regarding a fast Maharam must agree that the law is different, whether one derives this from reasoning or from the Gemara. By reasoning: Mordechai's rationale is that a verbal declaration to Heaven is like delivery to an ordinary person, and an asmachta can likewise take effect in certain dealings between people; see Sema, siman 207, seif katan 56. This explains a vow to give an object to sacred use, because there exists a corresponding form of acquisition that can take effect between ordinary people. A vow to fast, however, has no comparable acquisition between ordinary people. In such a matter there is no basis to say that an asmachta takes effect merely because the declaration was directed to Heaven. The Gemara likewise supports this. In the relevant passages concerning vows and conditional undertakings, a distinction emerges between a commitment involving property that can be transferred or consecrated and a personal undertaking such as fasting. A property commitment can take effect through the special force of a declaration to Heaven, whereas a conditional personal undertaking remains subject to the ordinary law of asmachta. Accordingly, the Mechaber's conclusion concerning one who says, "If God saves me from this distress," need not follow Mordechai's principle. Mordechai in Bava Kamma brings proof from Yaakov's statement, "If God will be with me," as cited in Shulchan Aruch, Choshen Mishpat. If Maharam held that every vow undertaken in a time of distress becomes binding because of the rationale recorded by Magen Avraham, there would be no proof from Yaakov that asmachta generally takes effect. The same is true of the verse, "If You will indeed deliver this people into my hand, I will devote their cities": there the commitment concerned devotion of property to sacred use, where the declaration itself has the force of transfer. It must therefore be that Maharam does not accept the broad rationale stated by Magen Avraham here. Once we have established that asmachta applies fully to a vow to fast, even a vow made in a time of distress does not automatically become binding according to Mordechai. Beit Yosef records here a stringent view of R' Yehonatan, but Mordechai's own principle does not prove such a stringency for fasting. Yaakov's vow, "If God will be with me," concerned making the stone a house of God and tithing everything God would give him. Those are property commitments to Heaven and therefore take effect by the special rule of a declaration to Heaven. Likewise, "If You will indeed deliver this people" concerns devoting the cities, again a commitment of property to sacred use. There is therefore no proof from either verse concerning a conditional fast. With this, the difficulty of Taz, seif katan 17, is resolved. See Mordechai, chapter Shor Shenagach 4 and 5, siman 44; Nekudat HaKesef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 258; and Nimukei Yosef, chapter Mi SheMet, on the Mishnah concerning one who says that if his wife bears a male child he shall receive 1 maneh. Examine all this carefully.
