NB: In my humble opinion, although for charity the Mechaber ruled like R' Meir in Mordechai, nevertheless for a fast he concedes to Maharam — if you wish, say reason; if you wish, say Gemara. From reason: Mordechai's reason is because we say speech to the Most High is like handing over to an ordinary person that takes effect, and asmakhta is also found at least for an ordinary person in a case of uncertainty; and see in Shulchan Aruch Choshen Mishpat siman 207 s.k. 56. And if so it works out for a vow to give something to the Most High, for we find acquisition that takes effect for an ordinary person in such a case; but to fast — asmakhta is not relevant for an ordinary person in this manner: there is no law and no judge that asmakhta applies here. And with this the proof of R' Yehonatan is rejected, from what R' Tarfon said: nezirut was given only for astonishment in Sanhedrin 25a — implying asmakhta also applies to vows, and there is no difficulty, for nezirus is similar to a fast and Maharam concedes. And if you wish, say Gemara: this responsum of Maharam also brings in responsa of Maimonides to Sefer haPela'ah, responsum from the beginning of the responsum of Rav Natronai bar Hilai etc. — see there at length; and at the end he brings proof from the mishnah of Nazir 10a: a cow said etc., R' Yehuda etc., when one says this cow is upon me as an offering etc. — we learn it becomes an offering through asmakhta — see there. And it is difficult: why does he bring from offering of R' Yehuda in the seifa and not from nezirus of the first tanna in the beginning of the chapter; and further difficult: contradiction from this mishnah that there is no asmakhta in nezirus to R' Tarfon in chapter Zeh Borer mentioned above. Rather it must be said that the first tanna of this "a cow said" is the first tanna who also argues on R' Tarfon mentioned above in Nazir 32b; and we hold like R' Tarfon, and therefore he brings from the seifa of R' Yehuda, who there said in the name of R' Tarfon: nezirut was given only for astonishment, and there is asmakhta; and nevertheless he holds the cow became an offering; and therefore the reason is because for an offering asmakhta applies like handing over that takes effect for an ordinary person, not so for a nazir, who is only refraining from drinking wine — it is like a fast, and asmakhta applies. And this is clear in my humble opinion. And according to this, that which he concludes in Shulchan Aruch "if God saves us from distress" is not like Mordechai, for behold he brings in Bava Kamma proof from "if God will be with me" of Jacob, as Shulchan Aruch Choshen Mishpat wrote above; and if it were that he holds one who vows at a time of distress — I am from the reason Magen Avraham s.k. 17 wrote and so in Levush — then there is no proof from Jacob that asmakhta helps; and likewise the verse "if You will surely give this people into his hand" and "I will devote their cities" — that case is different, for there he concluded and acquired; rather it must be that Maharam does not hold the view of Magen Avraham s.k. 16 mentioned above. And if so, according to what we proved that for a fast in all respects asmakhta applies — if so, even one who vows at a time of distress does not help for Mordechai, for it is proven he has no view of concluding and acquiring; rather Beit Yosef recorded here a stringency of R' Yehonatan; but for Mordechai it is not so, that even at a time of distress asmakhta does not help for a fast. And the verse "if God will be with me" — there he vowed for the pillar to be a house of God and to tithe all that He gives him; and in such a case it helps like handing over that takes effect for an ordinary person, and there is no proof from this to a fast. And likewise "if You will surely give" and "I will devote their cities" — devoting the city is My devotion to the Most High. And the difficulty of Taz s.k. 17 is removed; examine well; and see inside Mordechai chapter Shor sheNagach 4 and 5 siman 44; and see Nekudat haKesef in Yoreh Deah siman 258; and see well in Nemukei Yosef chapter Mi sheMet, mishnah: if his wife bore him a male he takes a maneh etc. — see there.
