(In Taz s.k. 8) But he himself does not hold so. Note: I say that what the Rosh wrote, that Rabbeinu Tam's proof was not [based on] his own view, but Rabbeinu Tam would say that even if he did not resolve in his heart until nightfall he can fast and pray Aneinu and fulfill his vow: so wrote Tosafos in his name in Avodah Zarah, folio 34. And if so, his words are difficult from [the law of] tzedakah. Therefore the correct answer is as the Drishah wrote there, s.k. 12: that what the Rosh wrote "a proof from tzedakah" means the tzedakah of the verse, which deals with tzedakah of bedek habayis, similar to "what goes out of your lips you shall keep," which we expound in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah as referring to korbanos; and to this he likewise compares a fast, for it resembles a korban, in that through it his fat and blood are diminished, and the wording is "May it be [Your] will that my fat and blood that were diminished today be in place of a korban." And on this it works well what the Rosh wrote in the chapter "Shevuos Shtayim," siman 14: Shmuel said, if he resolved in his heart, he must bring it out with his lips, as it is said "to utter with the lips," for it is not a shevuah until he brings his intent out with his lips; and the same applies to a neder; and the Bach left this as requiring further study. For there [the derivation] is expounded regarding tzedakah nowadays. And what the Rosh wrote regarding a fast, "it is logical that all matters of neder are learned one from another," means nedarim of fasting, which are like a neder of a korban. And it appears even more preferable to emend there the word "and if," and it should read: and the same applies to a neder if he resolved in his heart to bring out wheat bread and brought out barley bread; for that matter applies even regarding a korban, as is taught in the second chapter of Terumos: if he intended to say "olah" and said "shelamim," he has said nothing. And that which is written "nediv lev" is where speech does not contradict his intent of heart. And so wrote the Rash and R' Ovadiah of Bartenura there, mishnah 8; see there.
