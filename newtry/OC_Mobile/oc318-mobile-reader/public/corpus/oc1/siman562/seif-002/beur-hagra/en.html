Se'if 2: Some say that one who, etc. As I wrote above in se'if 1 from what is written there "there, to afflict, etc."; and specifically in such a case where there are two [factors]: mere affliction because of teshuvah, and he does not accept; for if he accepted without specification it is a complete fast, as the Rosh wrote at the end of the second chapter there and at the end of the third chapter of Eruvin, and as is written in the gloss: "But one who, etc., and likewise, etc."; and as is written in the Gemara there, etc., which implies that it is not a fast at all; and since he accepts a fast, he necessarily must complete.
Since there is no, etc. For he fasts on account of the neder, as is written at the end of Nedarim, and he needs no acceptance; and see se'if 12.
And the individual, etc. As above in se'if 1.
And likewise a bridegroom, etc. For he likewise fasts only as affliction because of teshuvah, to atone for his sins, as is written at the end of Bikkurim in the Yerushalmi: It was taught: a scholar, a bridegroom, a nasi: greatness atones; a scholar because of old age, etc.; "And when a stranger sojourns," etc.: just as a convert has all his sins forgiven him, so a scholar who is appointed has all his sins forgiven him. "And Esav went to Yishmael and took Machalas the daughter of Yishmael": and was her name Machalas? But was her name not Basmas? Rather, that all his sins were forgiven him. "Saul was one year old when he began to reign": and was he one year old, etc.?
He should pray, etc., and then, etc. Which is not so afterward: for they said that he eats and drinks and prays the fast prayer only on the night before the fast, but not in such a case.
And it appears to me, etc. As is written in the Yerushalmi, and the Rosh brought it there, and Tosafos in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah regarding R' Yochanan's statement "they fast for hours": for R' Yochanan said, "Behold I am fasting until we finish our chapter"; and the Rosh explained regarding this that it is called a neder and he must fulfill his vow, even though it is not a fast for praying Aneinu, as I wrote above in se'if 1; and not like Rashi, s.v. "to slip away," etc.; for Rashi follows his own view, explaining that all that is written there is not a fast, for if he wishes to eat he has license, and he need not fulfill his vow; and so he explained what is written there, etc.
