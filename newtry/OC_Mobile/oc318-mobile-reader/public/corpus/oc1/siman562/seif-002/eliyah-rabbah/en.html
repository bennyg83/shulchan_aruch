[2] [Levush] However, some say that in this, etc. And the Rama wrote without qualification: "However, some say that he prays Aneinu," and "so it appears to me to practice," implying even without a prior neder; and so wrote Darkhei Moshe. But in the Levush it appears specifically in this case where he already vowed; but without a prior neder, according to everyone they do not say Aneinu. And this requires further study: for these "some say" are the Mordechai in the name of R' Yehudah, and there he deals even without a neder; however, one may say that he deals where he did not vow while it was still day but [only] after dark; but without accepting at all, one may say they do not say [Aneinu], and so it appears in the Bach. And the Bach wrote that if he skips the two words "the fast of our affliction," even if he did not fast at all he may say so. And the Taz wrote: in a place where the Rama decided that an individual should say Aneinu, he should skip so; until here; and this requires further study. And it is obvious that even though we hold that he prays Aneinu, nevertheless he has not fulfilled his vow that he already vowed to fast one day, and he must fast another day; and see Magen Avraham s.k. 9, which is astonishing.
