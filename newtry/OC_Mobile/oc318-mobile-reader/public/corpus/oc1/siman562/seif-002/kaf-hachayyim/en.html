10) [Seif 2] Some say that one who is accustomed, etc. This was written in the name of "some say" because according to this opinion, although he does not complete his fast, he recites Aneinu, as written in the Beit Yosef, Bach, and Darkei Moshe, os 6, and in the Rama's gloss in this seif. According, however, to those who explain that any fast upon which the sun did not set is not a fast for the purpose of reciting Aneinu, as the Shulchan Aruch ruled above in seif 1, the same applies here. Since he does not complete the fast until sunset, he does not recite Aneinu. So too wrote Darkei Moshe there:
11) Ibid. Since it is not customary to accept them, etc. They stand accepted by force of their established custom. Even those who fast on all of them except one day, so that the practice should not take effect upon them as a vow, require neither acceptance nor completion. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 146. Accordingly, those who always fast on the first day of Selichos, as written in our words below in siman 581, seif 2, require neither acceptance nor completion, since it stands accepted by force of their custom. Nevertheless, the common practice is to complete it, and perhaps it is forbidden to release them from this practice, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 214. It is also possible that the practice spread due to an error, because people saw that the Shulchan Aruch, in the Rama's gloss, wrote that one who does not fast all of the Ten Days, etc., requires acceptance and completion. They erred in this, for it refers to one who is not accustomed to fast every year. Such a person must accept the fast and must therefore complete it. One who fasts every year, however, even for only one day, requires neither acceptance nor completion, and certainly this is so for one who stipulates at the time of acceptance that he need not complete it. Magen Avraham, s.k. 4. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 581, os 20. One can further say that the common practice to complete it is so that it should be considered a fast and one may recite Aneinu according to all opinions. See below, os 21, and siman 581, os 63:
12) Ibid. He need not fast until the emergence of the stars, etc., because he accepted upon himself merely an affliction. Tur. This applies specifically in such a case, where both factors are present: it is merely for affliction as part of repentance, and he did not formally accept it. If he accepted it without qualification, it is a complete fast and he must complete it. Biur HaGra. Mishna Berurah in Biur Halacha. This follows from what is written below in seif 3, that if he accepted a fast without qualification and did not specify otherwise, he must fast until the emergence of the stars; see there. Only here, where the practice is not to complete it and he fasted in accordance with the custom, it is as though he explicitly stated that he would not complete it. If, however, he accepted a fast without qualification, he must complete it. Nevertheless, it is preferable to stipulate if he intends not to complete it, especially now that some do complete it, as written in the preceding os:
13) Ibid. Rather, until he leaves the synagogue after Maariv. Hagahos Ashri. This implies specifically after the Maariv prayer, which is after 10¾ hours, as written in the Beit Yosef, siman 249, in the name of Mordechai, because this is called completion after the following day has begun. So too is implied by seif 3, seif 11, and siman 559, seif 9. However, it appears to me that whenever he prayed Minchah at the aforementioned time, meaning after 10¾ hours, that suffices, because this is the beginning of sunset and is called completion of the fast. Magen Avraham, s.k. 5. Regarding his citation of proof from siman 559, however, see our words there in os 74, where several later authorities challenged the words of the Magen Avraham there and hold that even after Minchah Gedolah it is permitted to break the fast; see there. So too wrote Machatzis HaShekel, siman 581, s.k. 10, that nowadays many are accustomed to fast only until the time of Minchah Gedolah and first pray Minchah Gedolah; see there. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, os 5. The Mishna Berurah, os 10, cited their words and wrote that a weak person may certainly rely upon the lenient view, fasting only until the time of Minchah Gedolah, praying Minchah and reciting Aneinu, and then eating. End quote. It appears preferable to stipulate while it is still day that he is fasting the following day only until Minchah Gedolah.
