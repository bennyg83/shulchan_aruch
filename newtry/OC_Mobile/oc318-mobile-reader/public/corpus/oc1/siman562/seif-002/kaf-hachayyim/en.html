Although it is evident from the Gemara that bein hashmashos begins at the end of sunset, which is before the emergence of the stars, nevertheless, because people are not expert in determining the end of sunset, they practiced waiting until the emergence of the stars. Although it is evident from the Gemara that, were it not for the stringency of Tishah B'Av, its bein hashmashos would be permitted, implying that the doubtful period of other fasts is permitted, one can say that beginning a day is different from ending a day. At the beginning of the day, meaning entering the fast, its bein hashmashos is permitted, such as on erev Tishah B'Av. But when ending the day, meaning at the conclusion of the fast, one does not leave the fast because of a doubt until three stars emerge, at which point it is certainly night. Beit Yosef in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos. However, Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh, at the end of the second chapter of Shabbos, wrote that bein hashmashos is permitted on a private fast even when ending the day; see there. Their words are cited in Biur HaGra. So too, we wrote above in siman 342, os 8, regarding Shabbos, that several poskim disagree with this and hold that there is no distinction between beginning the day and ending the day; see there. Accordingly, one must say that the primary reason we are stringent even regarding a private fast is that people are not expert in determining the end of sunset, as stated.
