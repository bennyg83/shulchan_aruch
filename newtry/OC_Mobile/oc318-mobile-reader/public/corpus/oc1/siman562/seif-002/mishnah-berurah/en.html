(9) That it is not customary to accept them in prayer: for they stand already accepted by force of their custom, and also the fast is only mere affliction because of teshuvah so that his sins be atoned. And according to this, those who always customarily fast on the first day of Selichos and on erev Rosh Hashanah need neither acceptance nor completion, since they are accepted by force of their custom.
(10) Until he leaves the synagogue: Ma'ariv; and even if they prayed while it was still day, that is, from plag haminchah and upward, which is eleven hours less a quarter. And the Ma'ariv prayer does not hold this up; rather once this time has arrived it suffices, provided he prays Minchah first [Magen Avraham]. And see Machatzit HaShekel in siman 581, who wrote that nowadays many are accustomed not to fast except until the time of Minchah Gedolah, and they pray Minchah first; and so wrote Peri Megadim. And at any rate it appears that a weak person may certainly rely to be lenient to fast only until the time of Minchah Gedolah and to pray Minchah and the Aneinu prayer and eat.
(11) And likewise a bridegroom, etc.: for he likewise fasts only as affliction because of teshuvah to atone for his sins, and it is not customary to accept it at the Minchah before it.
(12) And then he may drink, etc.: that is, likewise even if he did not stipulate at first that he would not complete, even so he may drink, as above; and what is written "and then" means that he should not drink before Minchah, for then he cannot say Aneinu. And nevertheless it is better that the bridegroom stipulate explicitly that he would not complete [later authorities].
(13) To complete: the reason is that they fast for the past, for the churban, and we do not say he intends only to afflict himself; which is not so during the Ten Days of Repentance, when they fast for the future, that their decree be torn up, [and] we say that they accepted only to afflict themselves. And this is the reason for those who fast the forty days before Yom Kippur: that they need not complete, for they likewise fast because of teshuvah.
(14) Even though he needs no acceptance: for he stands already accepted because he is accustomed to this every year.
(15) Only fasts a day or two: deals with one who is not accustomed to fast every year, and if so he must accept the fast, therefore he must complete; but one who fasts every year, even if he fixed only one day for this, needs neither acceptance nor completion; and all the more so one who stipulates at the time of acceptance, who certainly needs no completion.
(16) He stipulated: at the time of accepting the fast; and it is possible that the same applies if he stipulated beforehand: we say that the acceptance he afterward made is according to the earlier condition.
