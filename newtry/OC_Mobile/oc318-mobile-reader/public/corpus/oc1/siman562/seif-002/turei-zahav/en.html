That it is not customary to accept them. So wrote the Tur and Mordechai; and Terumat HaDeshen explains that they stand already accepted by force of their custom; but one who fasts only two or three days alone needs to accept, as the Rama wrote nearby. And it appears to my humble opinion: even though nowadays they do not accept those who fast in that way, nevertheless whoever fasts some days has his intent accordingly, for such is the custom, and it is as if he spelled out that he does not wish to complete. And it is difficult: if they stand already accepted, he has truly accepted upon himself, as is written in se'if 12; rather it appears that this "that it is not customary to accept" means that intentionally they do not maintain this as a fast, but only mere affliction, as he wrote afterward, even according to the view of Terumat HaDeshen.
From the 17th of Tammuz, etc. Terumat HaDeshen wrote that these resemble the four fasts of the churban, and are not comparable to the Ten Days of Repentance, where the fast is mere affliction for the future so that the prayer be accepted and their decree be torn up. And one cannot challenge the author of Terumat HaDeshen from what the Gemara asks against Rav Chisda, who required a fast for the entire day: Come and hear, for R' Elazar ben Tzadok said, Once Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbos, etc., and we did not complete because it was our Yom Tov; and it answers, There too he intends to afflict himself; and behold R' Elazar ben Tzadok did not pray for the future: there is no difficulty, for what they were lenient about because of accepting affliction alone is that they did not intend at all that this be a fast, but only affliction and mere remembrance; but in Terumat HaDeshen he is dealing with a fast, and with this he distinguishes between past and future. And with this is resolved what Tosafos ask there on what is said there "to afflict, etc."; and even though we say that since the Beis HaMikdash was destroyed, Megillas Taanis was annulled, etc., this tanna holds that it was not annulled; and according to our words it works well: a fast that is in the category of a fast annulled the prohibition that they decreed in Megillas Taanis not to fast on Yom Tov; but accepting mere affliction was never forbidden, and therefore was not included in the annulment; as it appears to my humble opinion.
