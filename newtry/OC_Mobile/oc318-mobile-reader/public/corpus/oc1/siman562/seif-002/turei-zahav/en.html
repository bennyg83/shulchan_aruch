From the seventeenth of Tammuz etc. Tosafot haDaf wrote: these are comparable to the four fasts of the destruction; and I say they are days of repentance, that the fast is pain in general over the future in order to receive prayer and tear up the decree; and one may say on Tosafot haDaf: from what the Gemara challenges on Rav Chisda, who required fasting the whole day — come and hear from R' Elazar ben Tzaddok: once Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbat etc., and they did not complete because our Yom Tov was; and it answers there too: he intends to pain himself; and behold R' Elazar ben Tzaddok did not pray for the future — there is a distinction: what they were lenient for because of accepting pain alone was that they did not intend at all that this should be a fast, but pain and remembrance alone; but in Tosafot haDaf it deals with a fast; and with this he distinguishes between past and future; and with this what Tosafot challenged there on what was said there "to pain" etc. is resolved; and although we say since the Temple was destroyed Megillat Taanit was annulled etc. — this tanna holds it was not annulled; and according to this it is satisfactory: a fast that is in the category of fast annulled the prohibition they decreed in Megillat Taanit not to fast on Yom Tov; but accepting pain in general was never forbidden, and therefore was not included in the annulment — as it appears to me.
