That it is not customary to accept them. For they stand already accepted by force of their custom; and even those who fast all of them except one day so that it not be upon him like a neder need no acceptance and no completion (Terumat HaDeshen). And according to this, those who always fast on the first day of Selichos need no acceptance and no completion, since they are accepted by force of their custom; however, the world is accustomed to complete [the fast], and it is possible that it is forbidden to permit it for them; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 214. And it is possible that the custom spread from a mistake: they saw in the Shulchan Aruch that he wrote, "one who does not fast all his days, etc., needs acceptance and completion," and they erred in this, for it deals with one who does not customarily fast every year, and if so he must accept the fast, therefore he must complete it; but one who fasts every year, even one day, needs no acceptance and no completion, and all the more so one who stipulates at the time of acceptance that he need not complete.
Until he leaves the synagogue. Ma'ariv [Hagahos Asheri]: it implies specifically after Ma'ariv, which is after eleven hours less a quarter, as Beis Yosef wrote in siman 249 in the name of the Mordechai, for that is called completion after the next day has entered; and so it appears from se'if 3 and se'if 1, and siman 559, se'if 9. However, it appears to me that whenever he prayed at the aforementioned time it suffices, since it is the beginning of shkiyah and is called completion of a fast; and see siman 552, se'if 11; and so wrote the Bach regarding a bridegroom; and so it appears in Terumat HaDeshen.
He must complete. For they fast for the past, for the churban; we do not say he intends only to afflict himself; which is not so during the Days of Repentance, when they fast for the future, that their decree be torn up, [and] we say that they accepted only to afflict themselves (Terumat HaDeshen). And if so, the same applies to those who fast the forty days before Yom Kippur: they need not complete, for they fast because of teshuvah; and the reason they fast those days is because they are days of favor, like the Ten Days of Repentance. The Bach asked: but R' Elazar ben Tzadok fasted and did not complete on a Tisha B'Av that was deferred, even though he fasted for the past, for the churban. And it appears to me that there is no difficulty: for R' Elazar ben Tzadok need not have fasted at all, since it was his Yom Tov; rather he accepted upon himself to afflict himself with the congregation; see there; and so wrote Rashi. But an individual who accepts upon himself a fast for a trouble that has already passed must complete.
