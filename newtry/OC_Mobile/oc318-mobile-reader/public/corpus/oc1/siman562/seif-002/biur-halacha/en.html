Some say that one who, etc., and the individual, etc. - This view is the view of some of the great authorities brought by Rama in seif 1, that if at the time of accepting the fast he specified that he would not complete it, and it is only for mere affliction, he recites Aneinu even though he did not complete it. Only when he accepted a fast without specification, which means specifically a full day, then if he did not complete it he does not recite Aneinu. Based on this, they concluded that regarding the Ten Days of Repentance, where the practice is not to accept them and the fast is only for affliction, one does not complete them and nevertheless recites Aneinu, because we consider them as though he specified that he would not complete. According to this view, the shaliach tzibbur also says Aneinu as a separate berachah between Goel and Rofeh. But since the first view in seif 1 does not hold so, but rather that in every fast that he did not complete until evening he does not say Aneinu, and so the Mechaber wrote plainly in seif 11, and that is the view of most poskim, as written in Darkei Moshe, therefore Rama concluded that only the individual says Aneinu, like his ruling in seif 1.
Who is accustomed to fast in the Ten Days of Repentance, etc. - At first glance, even if he is not accustomed to fast, for this is only the first time for him, but in that city many are accustomed to fast and he also wants to fast like the other townspeople, presumably his intent too is according to the custom, and he does not need acceptance or completion. So too is proven from the end of Rama's words, where he wrote, "and likewise one who," etc., "only fasts one or two days." Necessarily it speaks where that day was not always fixed for him, for if it was fixed he would not need acceptance, as the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 4. It follows that if he fasts all ten days of repentance, in every case he needs neither acceptance nor completion. It is seemingly reasonable that the same applies to the fasts of Monday-Thursday-Monday after Pesach and Sukkot, that if they are accustomed to fast them, one no longer needs acceptance or completion, and the individual prays Aneinu as in the Ten Days of Repentance. However, this is seemingly difficult, for according to this the Mordechai contradicts himself: this law in seif 2 regarding the Ten Days of Repentance is the law of the Mordechai in the name of Avi HaEzri, and he himself wrote there immediately afterward that one fulfills acceptance even if he accepted long before Minchah, and his proof is from the fact that the shaliach tzibbur blesses on Shabbat those who will accept upon themselves to fast Monday-Thursday-Monday. In their time, almost the entire community practiced fasting Monday-Thursday-Monday, as the Taz wrote at the beginning of siman 566; according to our words, no acceptance is needed at all. Perhaps he speaks not of Ashkenaz and France, where most of the community does not practice fasting, but this is forced. It is also possible to say that regarding the fasts of Monday-Thursday-Monday, even in a place where they are accustomed, acceptance is needed, for that is how they established the custom from the beginning, that each one accept it, like the wording of the one blessing, who says, "may He bless whoever accepts upon himself," etc. In our time, when people are not accustomed to fast Monday-Thursday-Monday, it is certainly reasonable that explicit acceptance is required, or at least that he answer amen to the shaliach tzibbur who blesses, as the poskim wrote in siman 566.
Now if he explicitly accepted, he certainly must complete the fast (unless he stipulated not to complete it, as the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 2). If he merely answered amen, although that is considered acceptance regarding Aneinu, nevertheless it requires study whether he must complete it, since it is not a full acceptance, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 566. This doubt also applies regarding Aneinu, for if we say he must complete it, then if he did not complete it he cannot say Aneinu. Nevertheless, regarding an individual in Shomea Tefillah, it appears that he may say Aneinu even if he did not complete, since he answered amen. Regarding the fast of Erev Rosh Chodesh, since there is no fixed custom for this except for some people, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 417, it is reasonable that acceptance from the previous day is required; if so, he must complete it unless he explicitly stipulated not to complete it. Regarding the fast of Erev Rosh Hashanah, see below in siman 581 in the gloss.
Since there is no regularity, etc. - For if he accepted without specification, he must afterward fast a complete fast [Gra; so too Peri Megadim].
Until he leaves the synagogue - See Magen Avraham, that the intent is until plag haminchah. As a matter of law, this requires much examination, for the source of this law is that we say this fast is only mere affliction for repentance, similar to what the Gemara answers regarding Tisha B'Av that was postponed in the incident of Rabbi Elazar bar Tzadok, where he said that they fasted on it and did not complete it, as the Gra wrote. There, the intent is only until after Minchah Gedolah, as stated above in siman 559 seif 9; see there in Mishnah Berurah. Now the Magen Avraham follows his own approach, for there too his view inclines to eleven hours, and therefore he noted, "see the end of siman 559." But in truth, Eliyah Rabbah and the other Acharonim already challenged him there, and their view is that it is only until after Minchah Gedolah. If so, the same applies in our matter: wherever it is mere affliction, he may eat after Minchah Gedolah. Afterward I found that Machatzit HaShekel also noted this. It is surprising that Chayei Adam there copied that until Minchah Gedolah is sufficient, unlike Magen Avraham, while here he copied like the Magen Avraham. Perhaps the language of the Shulchan Aruch here, "until he leaves the synagogue," pressed him, implying that where one fasts to afflict himself so his sins be atoned, it is not enough to wait until after Minchah Gedolah. But this too is no proof, for perhaps the Shulchan Aruch spoke according to their custom, that then this was the custom, but not that this is the law; the practical difference is in a place where the custom is otherwise. Now the Peri Megadim wrote on the words of the Magen Avraham as follows: however, the world practices according to their custom, after midday, and it is as though he specified from the beginning that he accepts no more; end quote. Meaning, he may also say Aneinu. So too it is implied in the siddur of Derech HaChayim. Thus it is as we said, that this truly depends on each place according to its custom. Especially for a weak person, it appears that certainly one may rely on this leniently, and he should not be stringent upon himself, so that he have strength to fast on Yom Kippur, which is the main thing. It is better that he stipulate from the beginning that he is not accepting upon himself to fast like the rest of the people, but only until after Minchah Gedolah, and he may also say Aneinu, as above. Afterward I found in Machatzit HaShekel siman 581 that he wrote explicitly that now many practice to fast only until the time of Minchah Gedolah, and they pray Minchah first. After writing all this, I found in Pitchei Teshuvah in siman 581 that he wrote in the name of Responsa She'ilat Yaavetz that his view is that one who fasts only until midday cannot pray Aneinu. Nevertheless, it appears that one who wishes to rely on the words of the Peri Megadim and Machatzit HaShekel above to say Aneinu certainly also acts properly.
And the individual prays Aneinu - Meaning specifically an individual, like his ruling above in seif 1 [Gra].
