Some say that one who, etc., and the individual, etc. Behold, this view is the view of some of the authorities that the Rama brought in se'if 1: that if he spelled out at the time of his acceptance that he would not complete, for it is only mere affliction, he prays Aneinu even without completing; for specifically when he accepted upon himself a fast without specification, which is a full day, only then if he did not complete he does not pray Aneinu. And on this basis it emerged for them that during the Ten Days of Repentance, when it is not customary to accept them and the fast is only mere affliction, they do not complete, and even so he prays Aneinu, for we consider them like one who spelled out that he would not complete. And according to this view the chazzan too says Aneinu as a separate blessing between Go'el and Rofei; except that since the first view in se'if 1 does not hold so, but rather that on every fast that he did not complete until evening one does not say Aneinu, and so the Mechaber stated without qualification in se'if 1 that such is the view of most poskim, as Darkhei Moshe wrote, therefore the Rama concluded that only the individual says Aneinu, according to his decision in se'if 1.
Who is accustomed to fast during the Ten Days of Repentance, etc. At first glance, even if he is not accustomed to fast, for it is only the first time for him, but in that city many are accustomed to fast and he likewise wishes to fast like the other people of the city, presumably his intent is also according to the custom, and he needs neither acceptance nor completion. And so it is proven from the end of the Rama's words, where he wrote "and likewise one who, etc., only fasts a day or two": necessarily he is dealing where that day was not fixed for him always; for if it were fixed, he needs no acceptance, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 4; which implies that if he fasts all Ten Days of Repentance, in every case he needs neither acceptance nor completion. And it is logical at first glance that the same applies regarding the Bahab fasts after Pesach and Sukkos: that if they are accustomed to fast on them, then again he needs neither acceptance nor completion, and the individual prays Aneinu as during the Ten Days of Repentance. But at first glance it is difficult according to this that the Mordechai contradicts himself: for this ruling of se'if 2 regarding the Ten Days of Repentance is the Mordechai's ruling in the name of Avi HaEzri, and he himself wrote there immediately afterward that one fulfills acceptance even if he accepted a long time before Minchah, and a proof from that the chazzan blesses on Shabbos "whoever will accept upon himself to fast Bahab"; and in their time almost the entire community was accustomed to fast Bahab, as the Taz wrote at the beginning of siman 566; and according to our words no acceptance is needed at all. And it is possible that he is dealing not in Ashkenaz and France, where most of the community are not accustomed to fast there; and it is forced. And one may further say that regarding a Bahab fast, even in a place where they are accustomed, acceptance is needed, for so they fixed the custom from the outset that each one should accept, as in the wording of the one who blesses, who says "May He bless whoever will accept upon himself, etc." And in our time, when people are not accustomed to fast Bahab, it is certainly logical that explicit acceptance is needed, or at least that he answer Amen to the chazzan who blesses, as the poskim wrote in siman 566.
And behold, if he accepted explicitly, he is certainly obligated to complete (unless he stipulated that he would not complete, as Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 2). And if he only answered Amen, even though it is considered acceptance regarding Aneinu, nevertheless this requires further study whether he is obligated to complete, since it is not complete acceptance, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 566. And this doubt applies also regarding Aneinu: for if we say that he is obligated to complete, if he did not complete he cannot say Aneinu. And nevertheless regarding an individual in Shomea Tefillah it appears that he may say Aneinu even if he did not complete, since he answered Amen. And regarding a Rosh Chodesh eve fast, since there is no fixed custom for this except for some people, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 417, it is logical that he needs acceptance while it is still day, and if so he must complete unless he stipulated explicitly that he would not complete. And regarding a Rosh Hashanah eve fast, see below in siman 581 in the gloss.
Since it is not customary, etc. For if he accepted without specification, he must afterward fast a complete fast [Gra; and so wrote Peri Megadim].
Until he leaves the synagogue. See Magen Avraham, that the intent is until plag haminchah; and for the practical law one must examine this greatly, for the source of this ruling is that we say that this fast is only mere affliction because of teshuvah, similar to what the Gemara answers regarding a deferred Tisha B'Av in the incident of R' Elazar ben Tzadok, who said that they fasted on it and did not complete it, as the Gra wrote; and there the intent is only until after Minchah Gedolah, as is stated above in siman 559, se'if 9; see there in Mishnah Berurah. And behold Magen Avraham follows his own view, that there too his opinion leans toward eleven hours, and for this he cited "see the end of siman 559"; but in truth Eliyah Rabbah and the other later authorities already disputed him there, and their view is that it is only until after Minchah Gedolah; and if so, the same applies in our matter: wherever it is mere affliction he may eat after Minchah Gedolah. Afterwards I found in Machatzit HaShekel that he too remarked on this. [And it is astonishing regarding Chayei Adam, who there copied that it suffices until Minchah Gedolah, unlike Magen Avraham, and here copied like Magen Avraham; and it is possible that the wording of the Shulchan Aruch, who wrote in our matter "until he leaves the synagogue," pressed him, implying that where he fasts to afflict himself so that his sins be atoned it is not enough that he wait until after Minchah Gedolah; and even this is not a proof, for it is possible that the Shulchan Aruch stated according to their custom, for then such was the custom, but not that the law is so; and there is a practical difference in a place where the custom is otherwise.] And behold Peri Megadim wrote on Magen Avraham's words, and this is his language: "However, the world is accustomed [to eat] after midday according to their custom, and it is as if he spelled out from the outset that he does not accept more"; end of his words. Meaning, and he may also say Aneinu; and so it appears in the Siddur of Derech HaChayim: as our words, that this truly depends in every place according to its custom; and especially regarding a weak person it appears that one may certainly rely on this to be lenient, and he should not be stringent upon himself so that he have strength to fast on Yom Kippur, for that is the main [thing]. And it is better that he stipulate from the outset that he does not accept upon himself to fast like the other people except until after Minchah Gedolah, and he may also say Aneinu, as above. Afterwards I found in Machatzit HaShekel, siman 581, who wrote explicitly that nowadays many are accustomed to fast only until the time of Minchah Gedolah, and they pray Minchah first. After I wrote all this I found in Pitchei Teshuvah in siman 581, who wrote in the name of a responsum of She'elas Yaavetz that his view is that one who fasts only until midday cannot pray Aneinu. And nevertheless it appears that one who wishes to rely on the words of Peri Megadim and Machatzit HaShekel aforementioned to say Aneinu certainly also does well.
And the individual prays Aneinu. Meaning, specifically an individual, according to his decision above in se'if 1 [Gra].
