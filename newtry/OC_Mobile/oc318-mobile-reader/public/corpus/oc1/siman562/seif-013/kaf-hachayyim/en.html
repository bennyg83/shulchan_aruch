This implies specifically after the Maariv prayer, which is after 10¾ hours, as written in the Beit Yosef, siman 249, in the name of Mordechai, because this is called completion after the following day has begun. So too is implied by seif 3, seif 11, and siman 559, seif 9. However, it appears to me that whenever he prayed Minchah at the aforementioned time, meaning after 10¾ hours, that suffices, because this is the beginning of sunset and is called completion of the fast. Regarding his citation of proof from siman 559, however, see our words there in os 74, where several later authorities challenged the words of the Magen Avraham there and hold that even after Minchah Gedolah it is permitted to break the fast; see there. So too wrote Machatzis HaShekel, siman 581, s.k. 10, that nowadays many are accustomed to fast only until the time of Minchah Gedolah and first pray Minchah Gedolah; see there. So too wrote Eshel Avraham, os 5. The Mishna Berurah, os 10, cited their words and wrote that a weak person may certainly rely upon the lenient view, fasting only until the time of Minchah Gedolah, praying Minchah and reciting Aneinu, and then eating.
