Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. The wording implies specifically this case. Meaning, even in those cases where we are lenient and regard the observance as a fast although it was not completed, it must nevertheless be a case that bears some resemblance to completion. By contrast, those who fast only until after midday may not recite Aneinu. This follows the alternative opinion cited by Magen Avraham at the end of siman 559, seif katan 13, and not the Tashbetz there, who is lenient even until Minchah Gedolah. This is also implied here in seif 3, which states, ‘until the congregation completes its prayer.’ It implies that anything less is ineffective even if he expressly stipulated it, and is not considered a fast. The same is evident from seif 11, which states, ‘until midday,’ implying that he intends to eat after midday. Although after midday it is already the time of Minchah Gedolah according to Torah law, and in any event half an hour after midday is the time of Minchah Gedolah according to rabbinic law, as explained above in siman 293, it is nevertheless not considered a fast for purposes of Aneinu. Otherwise, the text should have stated that if he fasts an additional half hour after midday, he recites Aneinu.
