Se'if 1: that is, that he did not, etc. Meaning the end of the second shkiyah; and see Tosafos at the end of the second chapter of Shabbos, and as is written there: "and that is provided he tasted nothing until evening"; and further, from what it asks there: They objected: the men of the mishmar, etc.; Come and hear, etc.; hence "the sun set" means for completion. And we say at the end of the third chapter of Eruvin: a student of the yeshiva, etc.; it was taught, etc.; and if it were the first shkiyah he would not say "so that he not enter Shabbos," etc. And see what I wrote above in siman 261, se'if 2. And even the beginning of the second shkiyah: behold Rabbi Yosi holds that bein hashemashot is like the blink of an eye; he completes Rabbi Yehudah's bein hashemashot and then begins Rabbi Yosi's bein hashemashot, and before that it is day according to Rabbi Yosi, as is written at the end of the second chapter of Shabbos; and see Tosafos there, s.v. "If you are not," etc.
Until tzeit hakochavim. So wrote the Rosh there, and so wrote the Mordechai there; except that in the Gemara, chapter 4 of Pesachim, they disagreed regarding Tisha B'Av whether its bein hashemashot is forbidden, implying that on other fasts it is certainly permitted; except that the Mordechai at the end of the first chapter of Taanis, siman 975, wrote in the name of R' Elchanan that logic is [to wait] until tzeit hakochavim. And if you say: but even regarding Tisha B'Av they disagreed, etc.; one may answer that entering the day differs from exiting the day, etc.; and so wrote the Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 4 of the laws of fasts. But Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rosh at the end of the second chapter of Shabbos wrote that bein hashemashot on an individual's fast is permitted even at the exit of the day; and so appears the plain sense of the Gemara, which says "its doubt," etc. And see Tosafos of Avodah Zarah, folio 34a, s.v. "They fast."
That is, that they see, etc. Gemara, end of the second chapter of Shabbos.
Or that the moon, etc. Yerushalmi, first chapter of Berachos: It was taught: as long as the face of the sun is reddening, that is day; when they pale, that is bein hashemashot; when they blacken and the upper becomes equal to the lower, that is night. Rebbi says: the moon in its tekufah: when the solar disk begins to set and the lunar disk begins to rise, that is bein hashemashot. Said R' Chanina: [when] the end of the solar disk [begins] to set and the lunar disk begins to rise. Meaning, that R' Chanina disagrees and says that the moon does not begin to rise until the end of the sun's setting; and he brings a support there, and Shmuel taught similarly: the moon does not shine at the hour the sun sets, and does not set at the hour the sun rises. Meaning, that it does not shine at the time of [the sun's] setting, but only after its setting is finished. And even though Tosafos HaRosh explained the Yerushalmi regarding the two shkiyos, that the first Tanna holds that the first shkiyah is bein hashemashot and R' Chanina said the second shkiyah, his interpretation is not aligned, for he said "as long as the face, etc.," and that is after the second shkiyah, as is written at the end of the second chapter of Shabbos; rather everything refers to the second shkiyah, for there is no view that during the first shkiyah the measure of four mil is all bein hashemashot; rather the first Tanna holds that the beginning of the lunar disk rising is bein hashemashot, which is the beginning of shkiyah, and R' Chanina said that it is night, which is the end of shkiyah; and "to rise" means to shine, for it rises even by day.
And if his intent, etc. As is written there: they fast for hours and pray, etc.; said Rav Chisda: that which you said, etc.; and the version of Rabbeinu Chananel: for Rav Chisda said, Any fast, etc.
And so is the view of some of the authorities. So wrote the Mordechai and Sefer Ravyah: "Any fast upon which [the sun] did not set, etc."; it appears that this means the fasts of the 17th of Tammuz, etc., and likewise one who accepted the fast explicitly, for a plain fast is a full day; but during the Ten Days of Repentance it is not customary to accept them, and he accepted upon himself mere affliction, as is written there: They objected: the men of the mishmar, etc.; there, to afflict, etc.; Come and hear, etc. Meaning, that if he accepted mere affliction, even if he did not complete, it is a fast regarding Aneinu; for whenever he accepted upon himself ideally not to complete, it is a fast for this purpose that he prays Aneinu, for it is mere affliction; for specifically if he accepted upon himself a full day and did not complete, so that it is not a fast at all, then he does not pray Aneinu. And so is the view of the Hagahos Asheri.
