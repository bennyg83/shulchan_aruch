And so is the view of some of the authorities. This is the view of the Rambam, who wrote that even if he ate and drank and afterward began to fast, it is called a fast for hours, and that is according to the Yerushalmi, and against our Talmud, where Rav Chisda said: and that is provided he did not eat that entire day. And they have already written in the name of the Rashba that he heard that the Rambam retracted and required specifically that he not taste all day. And therefore what the Rama wrote, that an individual should say Aneinu, is astonishing: how can he say "on this day of the fast of our affliction" when his fast is not a fast? And my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that nevertheless one has license to say Aneinu, and he wrote that the Rambam, Ramban, Razah, and Ran hold that accepting a fast before the hours of the fast helps, etc. And in truth they do not hold so except if in any case he fasted the entire day, only that part of the day was without acceptance of a fast, but nevertheless he did not eat all day, as the Tur wrote, and as is mentioned below in se'if 10; only that the Rambam held so, that we do not require a fast for the entire day, and Ravyah sought to resolve him; and what do we need his resolution, since he himself retracted, as the Rashba wrote? Only that the Mordechai in the name of Ra'avan, and the Hagahos Asheri, hold that even so he prays Aneinu. And it appears to my humble opinion that certainly custom overrides nothing; nevertheless, one who does not pray Aneinu on a fast that he does not complete gains [avoidance of] a doubtful blessing according to most poskim; and even though for this there is no separate blessing for an individual, but rather he includes it in Shomea Tefillah, nevertheless the wording "the fast of our affliction" is not fitting according to them, for a plain "fast" means a full day; and furthermore, since according to the poskim who say not to say Aneinu it is an interruption in the blessing of Shomea Tefillah, as the Tur wrote in siman 563 regarding accepting a fast in Shomea Tefillah, it is good not to interrupt in the prayer; and one who then skips these two words, "the fast of our affliction," does well, in my humble opinion.
