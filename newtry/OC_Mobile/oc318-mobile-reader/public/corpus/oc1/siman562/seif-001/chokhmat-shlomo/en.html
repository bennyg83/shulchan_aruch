Se'if 1. Any fast upon which the sun did not set, etc. Note: See Magen Avraham s.k. 1, what he wrote: "the Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote, etc." And see above in the glosses on the laws of Tisha B'Av, siman 553, what I wrote: that this ruling is not unanimous, and it depends on the law of a rabbinic doubt where issur has taken hold, whether it is to be lenient or stringent; and see there what I asked about this. And now I find a further difficulty from what the Gemara asks in Pesachim, folio 54b: Rava expounded, etc., and "during bein hashemashot his [Tisha B'Av] is forbidden," and so they said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan; and did Rabbi Yochanan say so? But Rabbi Yochanan said that Tisha B'Av is not like a communal fast; is that not regarding bein hashemashot? See there. And it is difficult: what is the question? Let us say that what Rava and Rabbi Yochanan said, that during bein hashemashot his [day] is forbidden like Yom Kippur, is at its end, because it is the outgoing of the day; and what Rabbi Yochanan said, that Tisha B'Av is not like a communal fast regarding bein hashemashot, is at the incoming of the day; for at the incoming of the day as well a communal fast is forbidden, for they were very stringent about it, and on Tisha B'Av they were not stringent in this, and they left it standing on its [ordinary] law that a rabbinic doubt is [resolved] leniently; but at its end they were stringent even on Tisha B'Av: so what is the question? And from this it appears, at first glance, to prove like the view of the Hagahos Maimoniyos: that on every fast, even if it is not a communal fast, it is forbidden at its end; therefore one cannot interpret the words of Rava and Rabbi Yochanan as concerning its end, for if so, why specifically Tisha B'Av? Even any fast that is not a communal fast would likewise be so. Perforce he is dealing with its beginning, and the question of Rabbi Yochanan against Rabbi Yochanan is well asked. And from this it appears, at first glance, that according to everyone the law is so, even according to those who hold that a rabbinic doubt where issur has taken hold is permitted: it is forbidden on every fast at the outgoing of the day; for if we say that this depends on a rabbinic doubt where issur has taken hold, granted that the Hagahos Maimoniyos hold like those who forbid, nevertheless what is the question of Rabbi Yochanan against Rabbi Yochanan? Let us say that Rabbi Yochanan holds like the lenient view in Eruvin regarding a rabbinic doubt where issur has taken hold, and therefore on other fasts it is permitted even at the end, and regarding Tisha B'Av he holds that it is severe and they were stringent at its end but not at its beginning, and on a true communal fast they were stringent even at its beginning: and from this it appears, at first glance, that the Gemara holds that at the outgoing of the day, according to everyone, it is forbidden on every fast, and there is no rationale to permit at all in this, and it proves like the words of the Hagahos Maimoniyos. But in truth the reason of the matter is not known to me: if a rabbinic doubt where issur has taken hold is permitted, why should we be stringent in this? What of it that it is the outgoing of the day? It is no better in this than all other chezakos; for regarding one who is tamei who went down to immerse, if it is a light tumah he is tahor in a case of doubt, and there too he was certainly tamei and it is doubtful whether the tumah departed, and even so with a light tumah he is tahor; and what distinguishes the outgoing of the day from the outgoing of tumah? And at first glance one could distinguish between one chezakah and two: for with tumah there is only one chezakah, that he is presumed tamei; but with a fast there are two chezakos: the person has a chezakah of issur, that until now he was forbidden to eat, and also since the doubt concerns the day, whether it is day or night, and thus the day too was until now [day], and it is doubtful whether it has become night, and thus there is a chezakah of the day [remaining] day and a chezakah of issur for the person, and therefore they are two chezakos; and against two chezakos, according to everyone we do not say regarding a rabbinic doubt where issur has taken hold [that we are] lenient. And this is a novel ruling, and it is a clear proof from the above; and see similarly in tractate Eruvin, folio 36, where Rava answers there: here there are two chezakos, here there is one chezakah; see there; and the same may be said regarding a rabbinic doubt; and examine carefully.
