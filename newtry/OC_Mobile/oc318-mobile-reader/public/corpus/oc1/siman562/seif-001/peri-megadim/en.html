He concludes that one should omit the two words ‘the fast of my fasting’ or ‘the fast of our fasting,’ because according to most poskim it is not considered a fast, and he would consequently speak falsely in his prayer. He writes the same in paragraph 5. This implies that even without accepting it at Minchah, and even for a partial-day fast, he should recite Aneinu while omitting those two words. It also appears preferable to recite it after the prayer in Elokai Netzor rather than interrupt in Shomea Tefillah, because according to most poskim it would be an unnecessary interruption. This can be answered. During the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, when people observe a partial-day fast until after Minchah Gedolah or the like, it appears that they may not eat until they first pray Minchah, like a chassan in seif 2; see Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, citing Terumas HaDeshen, siman 157. At first glance, however, this applies only according to the opinion that he should say ‘the fast of our fasting,’ so that he not speak falsely in his prayer after he has already eaten. If he omits those two words, perhaps he may eat after midday without first praying Minchah, and afterward, when he prays Minchah with the congregation, he may say, ‘Answer us on the day of distress,’ even though he ate, since he does not mention ‘the fast of our fasting,’ provided that he stipulated that he would eat after midday and this was his intention. This presently requires study. See Terumas HaDeshen, siman 157. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham. See also Taz, seif katan 2, on this matter.
