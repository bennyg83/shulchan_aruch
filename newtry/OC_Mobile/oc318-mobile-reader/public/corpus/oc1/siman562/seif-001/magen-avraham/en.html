The Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote: even though in the Gemara it is proven that if not for the stringency of Tisha B'Av, its bein hashemashot would be permitted, implying that on other fasts their doubt is permitted, one may say that entering the day differs from exiting the day: to enter a fast, its bein hashemashot is permitted, for example erev Tisha B'Av; but at the end of the fast, from a doubt its sanctity does not depart until tzeit hakochavim, for then it is certainly night; end of his words (Beis Yosef in his name); and it is in the Gemara, chapter 4 of Pesachim [and see siman 386, se'if 9, what is written]. And it is stated there that on a communal fast, when they cease [eating] while it is still day, its bein hashemashot is forbidden; but there is no communal fast in Bavel; see siman 575, se'if 10; and if so, an individual who fasts night and day need not cease [eating] during bein hashemashot; but if he accepted upon himself a communal fast, he must conduct himself with the stringencies of every communal fast [Beis Yosef, siman 563]; and see siman 249; and nevertheless it is good not to enter a matter of doubt, and he should cease [eating] while it is still day; see what I wrote in siman 299, end of se'if 6. Intermediate [people]. But (small ones) [great ones] do not help [so one should emend; and it is possible that its meaning is very small ones, as is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 263, se'if 5, and in the Shach there].
Even though they do not complete. That is, if he spelled out at the time of acceptance that he would not complete [the fast], even if he fasted until Minchah Gedolah he prays Aneinu; but if he accepted a fast without specification, he must complete it [see se'if 2 and the end of siman 249]. And if he did not complete it and ate, see siman 568; and if he ate before he prayed, he does not pray Aneinu [Terumat HaDeshen, siman 157].
The chazzan should not say. For the main ruling we hold is that it is not called a fast without completion, as is written in se'if 1 [Darkhei Moshe].
