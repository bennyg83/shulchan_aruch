(1) Any fast, etc.: that is, whether the four fasts or any individual's fast.
(2) Three stars: for two are still bein hashemashot.
(3) Intermediate ones: but great ones do not help, for they are visible even by day. And behold in siman 293 regarding Motza'ei Shabbos the Shulchan Aruch ruled that one should not do melachah until three small consecutive stars are seen, and that is because of the stringency of Shabbos; and the same applies regarding Motza'ei Yom Kippur; but in our matter intermediate ones suffice, and even scattered ones [later authorities].
(4) It is not a fast: both regarding not having fulfilled with this the obligation of his fast, and regarding not praying Aneinu.
(5) If his intent is to eat, etc.: for example, he stipulated at the time of his acceptance that he would not complete the fast.
(6) Even though they do not complete: that is, if he spelled out at the time of acceptance that he would not complete, even if he fasted only until Minchah Gedolah he prays Aneinu; but if he accepted a fast without specification he must complete until tzeit hakochavim, as below in se'if 3 [and see Beur HaGra, from which it appears that in this case if he did not complete, even according to the view of some of the authorities they do not pray Aneinu]. And if he did not complete and ate, see below siman 568, se'if 1, and in the gloss there. And that the Rama stated without qualification to be lenient when he spelled out at the time of acceptance is specifically when he prays Minchah before his eating; but when he ate first, in every case he does not pray Aneinu.
(7) And so is the view of some of the authorities: their reason is that they hold that what the Gemara said, "any fast upon which the sun did not set is not a fast," and that is even regarding Aneinu, is only if he accepted upon himself to fast a full day and did not complete, so that it is not a fast, then he does not pray Aneinu; but if he accepted upon himself only until Minchah, for it is mere affliction, it is a fast for this purpose that he prays Aneinu within his prayer even though he does not complete. But most poskim disagree with this; therefore the Rama decided that we should not rely on them except regarding an individual, that he say it in Shomea Tefillah and conclude "for You hear," etc., and not say "Blessed are You, Hashem, Who answers in time of trouble." And see the Taz, who leans toward the view that even in this he should skip the words "the fast of our affliction," so that he not appear as one who speaks lies since it is not a fast; and in Sefer Bigdei Yesha he rules that he need not skip, since he fasts until after midday, for then his eating is like throwing a stone into a sack and he does not appear as a liar; see there; and so leans Sefer Machatzit HaShekel; see there.
(8) But the chazzan, etc.: that is, in the chazzan's repetition, where he adds a special blessing for this between Go'el and Rofei; but in the silent prayer he too should say Aneinu before "for You hear"; and see Biur Halacha.
