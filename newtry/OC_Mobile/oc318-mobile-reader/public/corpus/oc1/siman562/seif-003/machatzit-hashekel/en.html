(s.k. 3) The chazzan, etc. For the main ruling we hold, etc., as is written in se'if 11: that what we say in the Gemara "they fast for hours" is specifically when he did not eat all day, and in those manners explained in se'if 10 and 11; but if he ate before nightfall it is not called a fast regarding saying Aneinu. Only regarding an individual's fast do we rely on the view of the Rambam, who holds that a fast of an hour is called [a fast] even if he did not complete the fast and ate after midday; and since anyway an individual does not fix for Aneinu a blessing by itself but includes it in Shomea Tefillah, if so even according to those who disagree with the Rambam there is nothing wrong in this, for in Shomea Tefillah he may add whatever he wishes, as above in siman 119. And what the Taz wrote in s.k. 1 to skip the two words "the fast of our affliction": and if they skip, they do well. But what the Taz wrote to cast doubt on the Rama's ruling for two reasons: first, since the primary [view] is like those who disagree with the Rambam, if so when he says "the fast of our affliction" he is speaking lies, since according to those who disagree with the Rambam there is no designation of a fast upon him; of what avail is it that he may add in Shomea Tefillah? Nevertheless he is bringing a lie out of his mouth. And further, according to those who disagree it is an interruption in the middle of a blessing; and he brought a proof from the Tur's words, who wrote in siman 563 that because of a doubt of the law one should not accept a fast in Shomea Tefillah because of interruption; his words are brought in the Taz in this siman, s.k. 7; see there. In my humble opinion one may dispute his words. What he wrote, that if he says "the fast of our affliction" he is speaking lies: one may say, granted that according to those who disagree with the Rambam it is not called a fast, that is regarding saying Aneinu and fixing it as a blessing by itself, for whenever he does not complete the fast the Sages did not enact fixing it as a blessing by itself; but nevertheless it is called a fast, for the root of the name "fast" is affliction, and he in any case afflicted and pained himself. And a proof for this is from what is stated in Taanis, folio 12a, regarding that which Rav Chisda said: any fast upon which the sun did not set (that is, tzeit hakochavim) is not called a fast.
For it asks: But we learned: the men of the mishmar fast and do not complete; and Rashi explained, and this is his language: the men of the mishmar are kohanim and Levi'im who serve, etc., and they do not complete because they are occupied with the avodah, etc.; hence even though he does not complete, it calls him one who fasts; end of his words. And it answers: there it is mere affliction of himself. And Rashi explained, and this is his language: to afflict themselves with the congregation, but it is not a fast for praying the Aneinu prayer, nor for fixing it upon him as an obligation, and whenever he wishes to eat he eats; end of his words.
And one must say that in any case what it asked was not answered, for we see that even if he does not complete the Mishnah called it a fast. And one must say that in this the questioner and the answerer disagreed: for the questioner had assumed that what Rav Chisda said, "any fast upon which the sun did not set is not called a fast," means that it is not called a fast; and for this he asked from the Mishnah that called it a fast even if he does not complete. But the answerer answers him that certainly the designation of a fast is called upon it; and what Rav Chisda said "it is not called a fast" means regarding the Aneinu prayer and fixing them upon him as an obligation; and regarding the men of the mishmar it was in truth only mere affliction, and they would not pray the Aneinu prayer, and it was not fixed upon them as an obligation. According to this it is proven that even if he does not complete, even so it is called a fast, for the Mishnah regarding the men of the mishmar called it a fast (and against the Rambam the poskim in truth asked from this Gemara); and if so, even if he says "the fast of our affliction" he is not speaking lies, for it is possible that just as it is called a fast, so too it is called a "tzom."
And what he wrote that it is an interruption, and he brought a proof from the Tur's words: one may say that the Taz himself wrote in s.k. 7 to resolve the Tur's words: but in Shomea Tefillah one may add whatever he wishes; and so the Beis Yosef asked; and the Taz answered that acceptance of a fast is different, for when he says "I will be fasting" it is not a matter of prayer, and therefore it is an interruption, for he may add only matters of prayer; see there. If so, saying Aneinu, which is prayer, even according to those who disagree with the Rambam, nevertheless is not an interruption.
