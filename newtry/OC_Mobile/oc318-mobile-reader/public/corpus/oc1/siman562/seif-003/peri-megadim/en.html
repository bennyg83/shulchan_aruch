From the seventeenth of Tammuz — Eshel Avraham in Taanit haDaf siman 146 wrote that we require two reasons on the fasts that they do not complete: (1) that they are not accustomed to accept them but stand accepted from custom; and (2) one may say they intend to pain; and therefore one who fasts on the second or third must accept, for it is not custom, and therefore must complete; and likewise the seventeenth of Tammuz — although they are not accustomed to accept them, nevertheless there is no intent to pain over the past, and therefore they complete the seventeenth of Tammuz. And on this he challenged the fast of the twelfth of Av; and Bach challenged on this that for the destruction pain over the past is not relevant; but Taz hinted another difficulty: certainly Tisha B'Av deferred to Sunday does not need acceptance of a fast at mincha, and matters stand accepted; and see letter 2: since he need not accept, if so it is only the reason of pain alone — and how were they lenient there because their Yom Tov was? Behold we hold Megillat Taanit was annulled and it is permitted to fast on those days; and if they intend to pain, pain alone does not help — therefore he answered there are two types of pain: there they did not intend at all that it should be considered a fast on Tisha B'Av on Sunday, but only a remembrance and not for the sake of fasting at all; not so when he intends it should be a fast and to pain himself — then one may distinguish between past and future. And with this what Tosafot challenged in Taanit there, s.v. there, is resolved: they challenged on what was said there "to pain" etc., and although we say since the Temple was destroyed Megillat Taanit was annulled etc. — this tanna holds it was not annulled; and according to this it is satisfactory that a fast that is in the category of fast annulled the prohibition they decreed in Megillat Taanit not to fast on Yom Tov; but accepting pain in general was never forbidden from the outset, and therefore it was not included in the annulment — as it appears to me. And Tosafot cited there "to pain" and did not cite "because our Yom Tov" — one may explain this a bit; and see Magen Avraham 559 letter 11 and 572 seif 2, and Eshel Avraham there will be explained; and see Magen Avraham 6.
