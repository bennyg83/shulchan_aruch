That he did not accept it upon himself while it was still day. This implies that even though he afterward accepted it at night, it does not help. And if you say: but it is written in se'if 10 that if he accepted upon himself a fast of hours before the hours of the fast, it is called acceptance; one may answer that if so, you would have a difficulty from what is written in se'if 5: any fast that he did not accept while it was still day is not called a fast, which implies that even if he accepted it after dark, it is nothing. And if so, it is likewise difficult: why should it be inferior to a fast of hours? Rather, one must say as the Mordechai wrote: that we always require acceptance before he begins to fast for the sake of a fast; for a fast of hours, before he begins to fast those hours; and for a full day, before dark. And so it appears from the Ran. And if so, even though he accepted it at night, it does not count for him as a full-day fast; nevertheless he prays Aneinu, since he accepted it before he began to fast for the sake of an obligation. But if he began to fast for the sake of an obligation and afterward accepted it, it is not acceptance at all; and according to the other view in se'if 10, he does not pray Aneinu at all.
