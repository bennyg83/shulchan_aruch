Nevertheless, the practice is to recite Aneinu even though one does not complete the fast, etc. This means that if he explicitly stated when accepting the fast that he would not complete it, then even if he fasted only until Minchah Gedolah, he recites Aneinu. If, however, he accepted a fast without qualification, he must complete it. If he did not complete it and ate, see siman 568, seif 1. If he ate before praying, meaning where he had explicitly stipulated, he does not recite Aneinu. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 157. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. Eliyah Rabbah, siman 564, os 2. The Taz, s.k. 1, wrote that although one does not question an established practice, nevertheless, one who does not recite Aneinu on a fast that he does not complete avoids a possible blessing according to the majority of poskim. Although for an individual Aneinu is not an independent blessing, but is included within Shomea Tefillah, nevertheless, according to them the wording "the fast of our fasting" is inappropriate, since an unspecified fast means a complete day. Furthermore, according to the poskim who hold that Aneinu should not be recited, it constitutes an interruption in the blessing of Shomea Tefillah, as the Tur wrote in siman 563 regarding accepting a fast in Shomea Tefillah. It is therefore proper not to interrupt the prayer, and one who omits these two words, "the fast of our fasting," acts properly; see there. Bigdei Yesha wrote that even if he says "the fast of our fasting," he is not speaking falsely, for he did fast. End quote; see there.
