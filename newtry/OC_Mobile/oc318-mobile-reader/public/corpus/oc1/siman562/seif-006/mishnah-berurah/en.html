(27) During the Minchah prayer: for we require that the acceptance be adjacent to the time the fast begins. And it appears that ideally it is better that it be at Minchah Ketanah, which is at nine and a half hours, and not earlier; and after the fact it helps even at Minchah Gedolah. And the later authorities wrote that after the fact, if he did not accept during the Minchah prayer, he may accept it afterward the entire time that it is day.
(28) In Shomea Tefillah: for it is permitted to ask for one's needs in Shomea Tefillah; and since he concludes "May it be [Your] will," etc., as below, it is not an interruption, for it is included in asking for one's needs.
(29) Behold I am on an individual's fast: to exclude lest they err that he is accepting upon himself to fast with the stringency of a communal fast; and nevertheless after the fact, even if he said without specification "Behold I am fasting tomorrow," its law is also like an individual's fast [Beis Yosef].
(30) If he contemplated in his heart that he is accepting, etc.: and specifically that he contemplated in this language that he is accepting; but that it was in his mind to fast is not called acceptance and he may retract; but if he began to fast it is forbidden to eat without a release.
(31) It is acceptance: for even though regarding a shevuah and neder, when he forbids some thing upon himself it does not help until he brings it out with his lips, a fast resembles a neder of tzedakah, for which it helps when he resolved in his heart for this. And the Taz wrote that since through the fast his fat and blood are diminished, it is considered like a korban, and regarding a korban it is written "every nediv lev for olos."
(32) At the time of the Minchah prayer: and not beforehand, for hirhur is no better than bringing it out with the mouth, which does not help if he brought it out with his mouth before the Minchah prayer to fast tomorrow; and the details of these laws: see below in siman 563.
(33) And it is better, etc.: this refers to the beginning of the se'if, where he wrote "he says in Shomea Tefillah or afterward," etc.; and upon this he says that it is better to accept it after the prayer, that is, before Yehiu Leratzon, or afterward before he uproots his feet.
(34) So as not to interrupt: for the essence of the acceptance, that he says "Behold I am fasting," is not related to prayer. And the later authorities wrote that it is good that also in Shomea Tefillah he contemplate that he is accepting upon himself the fast for tomorrow.
