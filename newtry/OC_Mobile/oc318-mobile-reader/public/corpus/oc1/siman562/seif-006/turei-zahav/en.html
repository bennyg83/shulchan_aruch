When does he accept it? In the Gemara they disagree: Rav holds [it is] at Minchah, that is, when the time of Minchah arrives, and the Rosh holds that before that, even if he utters it with his mouth, it does not help; and Ravyah holds that all the more so acceptance before Minchah helps; and Shmuel holds [it is] during the Minchah prayer. Rashi explained: that he should say in Shomea Tefillah, "Behold I am on an individual's fast tomorrow; may it be [Your] will that You accept me with love"; end of the Rif's words. And the Tur wrote: and since there is a dispute among the authorities, the Rif rules like Shmuel and the Ra'avad like Rav, it is good not to interrupt in the prayer; meaning, since there is reason to rule like Rav or like Shmuel, we should choose like Rav not to interrupt. And Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Maggid that in some texts of the Rambam the wording is: when he prays Minchah or after Minchah; and so he copied here; and it is obvious that it is better to say it after the prayer, that is, before Elokai Netzor, and not to interrupt in the prayer itself in Shomea Tefillah. And one cannot say that we hold a person may ask for all his needs in Shomea Tefillah, for here it is different: he says "I will be fasting," and that is a matter of neder, not a matter of prayer, and it is an interruption to say it before Netzor; and so wrote the Semak.
If he contemplated in his heart. This is the language of the Rosh, first chapter of Taanis: R' Yehudah wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: if it was in his mind to fast from yesterday and he did not accept it upon himself at Minchah, but in his heart he was [resolved] to fast from yesterday, only that he did not bring it out of his mouth, it is properly called a fast, for it is included in "nediv lev," as is stated in the third chapter of Shevuos: that if he resolved in his heart, even though he did not bring it out with his lips, it helps; and even though it is said there that regarding a shevuah, resolving does not help until he brings it out with his lips, as it is written "to utter with the lips," it is logical that all matters of neder are learned one from another; for regarding tzedakah we expound "what goes out of your lips you shall keep," and resolving in the heart helps; and that we require acceptance in prayer is ideally required. And the Rosh concluded on this: and it appears to me that Rabbeinu Tam's proof was only for one who contemplated acceptance of a fast during Minchah and did not bring it out, from his bringing a proof from "nediv lev" and from tzedakah, that intent of the heart is as if he brought it out with his mouth; but hirhur of the entire day is not better than bringing it out with his mouth; end of his words. And the Tur rules so here; and it is difficult, for in siman 212 in Choshen Mishpat the Tur brings a responsum of the Rosh regarding one who buys land with the intent to make it hekdesh: if he brought nothing out of his mouth, they are unspoken matters of the heart; and Shmuel said, if he resolved in his heart he must bring it out with his lips; even though it is written "every nediv lev for olos," we say we do not learn chullin from kodashim; and nowadays every hekdesh has the law of chullin, for there is no hekdesh for bedek habayis now, and it is only for tzedakah; therefore he must bring it out with his lips, etc. If so, he does not hold like Rabbeinu Tam, and the words of the Rosh and the Tur contradict each other. And Beis Yosef in Yoreh De'ah, siman 228, brought in the name of Maharik, root 161, that the Rosh in this responsum does not take hold like Rabbeinu Tam, for in the first chapter of Taanis he wrote that regarding tzedakah resolving in the heart helps, and therefore he ruled that regarding tzedakah resolving in the heart does not help; end of his words. And also the Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Basra, wrote in the name of a responsum of Rashi that tzedakah is like nedarim and nedavos; therefore one who resolved in his heart to give tzedakah is obligated as if he brought it out of his mouth; end of his words. And it appears to my humble opinion that the Rosh and Tur agreed to Rabbeinu Tam's ruling regarding a fast; but nevertheless regarding the plain meaning of the text they did not agree. For it is stated in the third chapter of Shevuos: Shmuel said, if he resolved in his heart he must bring it out with his lips, as it is said "for whatever a person will utter in a shevuah"; they objected: "what goes out of your lips you shall keep and do": I have only [the case] where he brought it out with his lips; from where [do we know] if he resolved in his heart? The verse states "every nediv lev." That case is different, for it is written "every nediv lev"; and shall we learn from it? And they conclude: we do not learn chullin from kodashim. And one may ask against Rabbeinu Tam, who distinguishes shevuah from neder: what does the Gemara ask from a neder, where "what goes out of your lips" refers to a neder, for it is written regarding a neder, and Shmuel deals with a shevuah? And one cannot say that it asks we should learn shevuah from neder, and with this it answers that we do not learn chullin from kodashim; for if so, Rabbeinu Tam should not have distinguished between shevuah and neder, but between chullin and kodashim, whether in a shevuah or in a neder. And it appears to me that Rabbeinu Tam holds that certainly regarding a shevuah it never helps in any place, since we do not learn from kodashim; but regarding a neder we learn every neder from this verse, and we need no derivation for this, but rather everything was included in this verse, since the verse revealed to us regarding a plain neder, and we find regarding another neder that hirhur helps, namely regarding kodashim, and presumably everything is included, even chullin; which is not so of a shevuah, which was not included in this verse, but rather one now comes to learn from this verse: regarding that we say we do not learn chullin from kodashim. So it appears to me is Rabbeinu Tam's view. But the Rosh does not hold this rationale, that every neder is included in this verse; rather the verse is written specifically regarding kodashim, and you come to learn from it other nedarim; therefore he said we do not learn chullin from kodashim even regarding a neder itself; except that here regarding a fast it is considered truly like kodashim, for a fast stands in place of a korban, in that he offers his body to Heaven, better than tzedakah which is for the needs of people. And a proof for this is from the words of the Rosh, first chapter of Taanis, where he brought there the words of the Ra'avad who wrote that acceptance of a fast is only like tzedakah, as if he volunteers of his fat and blood to the Most High, etc.; and the Rosh there disputed him on what he wrote that it is like tzedakah, namely that it has a redemption, but rather he pays specifically with his body, for so he vowed his body; hence at any rate a fast stands in place of a korban; therefore he properly decided here that regarding a fast we learn from a neder of kodashim; for this reason hirhur helps here; which is not so in Choshen Mishpat, siman 212, regarding tzedakah. And furthermore the Rosh disagrees with Rabbeinu Tam: for Rabbeinu Tam interprets that verse "what goes out of your lips" and the verse of "nediv lev" regarding tzedakah, and holds as written nearby in the name of the Mordechai [citing] a responsum of Rashi, that tzedakah is like nedarim and nedavos, namely that everything is included in the neder of the verse; and the Rosh interprets it as referring specifically to true nedarim and nedavos, and automatically the same applies to a fast, but not to tzedakah. And even though the Rosh wrote here "from his bringing a proof from 'nediv lev' and from tzedakah, that intent of the heart, etc.," that is according to Rabbeinu Tam's view, who equates tzedakah to nedarim; but he himself does not hold so regarding tzedakah. And if so, everything is resolved, with Heaven's help.
At the time of the Minchah prayer. But before that, hirhur does not help according to the Rosh: it is obvious that even if he brought it out with his mouth it does not help; and even according to Ravyah, who holds that when he brings it out with his mouth before Minchah, all the more so it helps, nevertheless it appears that with hirhur it helps only at the time of Minchah, since a person's way is to reconsider in his thought; therefore the hirhur is not complete until its fixed time according to Rav or according to Shmuel, for then he resolves in his heart in such a way that retracting from the hirhur will not help him; as it appears to my humble opinion.
And it is better to accept it, etc. The better side appears to be that at the time of the prayer he should accept the fast in his heart, and after the prayer before Elokai Netzor he should accept it with his mouth, and he fulfills everyone's [view]; so it appears to me; and I saw so in the name of Maharshal.
