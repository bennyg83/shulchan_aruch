When does he accept it? During the Minchah prayer. Behold, from Rashi's language it appears that the reason is that we require that the acceptance be adjacent to the time of the fast; and according to this, if he prayed Minchah at the beginning of the time of Minchah Ketanah, that is, nine and a half hours, he may at first glance ideally wait after his acceptance until a later time adjacent to sunset so that it be closer to the time of the fast; but from the words of the poskim it does not appear so, for they fixed it specifically during the Minchah prayer so that he request in prayer that his fast be accepted; see the Eshkol and Chiddushei HaRa'ah on Taanis. And in truth one may also align Rashi's view with this, for he wrote s.v. "during the Minchah prayer": at its end, an addition of appeasement and tachanunim; and it is proven all the more explicitly in Chiddushei Ritva, who wrote that they fixed this acceptance in prayer similar to what we say regarding Kiddush and Havdalah, that at first they fixed them in prayer; see there. And nevertheless one may say that all this is ideally, but after the fact it helps, as the Bach and Magen Avraham agreed: that the entire time that it is day he may accept the fast upon himself. But regarding the main ruling I am much in doubt until when he may accept it upon himself after the fact; for behold, according to what is explained in the poskim, that the acceptance must be adjacent to the time of the fast, and it is known that for all fasts except Tisha B'Av and communal fasts, their bein hashemashot at the entrance is permitted, for the obligation of the fasting is not yet upon him, and even an individual who accepted upon himself to fast night and day, as explained in Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman: if so, even if the acceptance was after sunset it is also fine; and especially according to R' Yosi it is still day; and perhaps it is proper to rely on him in this; and this requires further study.
And that is provided he contemplates so at the time of the Minchah prayer. That is, and not beforehand, for it is no better than bringing it out with his mouth before the prayer, which does not help to say Aneinu. And even though there are poskim [Ravyah, Or Zarua, Hagahos Maimoniyos, and Mordechai] who hold that Shmuel agrees with Rav that even beforehand it helps, the Mechaber stated without qualification for the practical law like the view of Rashi and the Rosh and those who support them, that beforehand it does not help. And nevertheless it appears that if he delayed praying Minchah until near sunset, such that he finished Minchah after sunset, it is good that he accept the fast upon himself before Minchah and again accept also in his prayer, to fulfill everyone's [view]; for even though regarding Minchah he relies on the poskim that the time of prayer has not yet passed, as explained above in siman 233 in the Rama, nevertheless regarding acceptance of the fast he can fix [it] by accepting also beforehand, for that is complete acceptance according to those other aforementioned poskim.
