And Tosafot haRosh disagree specifically regarding Yom Kippur, for their intent in their fast is for repentance, that they wish to return to God — in this we say he accepted to pain himself, and it is comparable to the men of the watch, who also fasted for the future that their offering would be accepted. But those who fast from the seventeenth of Tammuz until the ninth of Av, who fast over the past — are comparable to the fast of the seventeenth of Tammuz and the ninth of Av; we do not say they intend only to pain, but for the sake of fasting, and require completion. And on this Bach challenged: for afterward the Gemara challenges R' Chanina from R' Avraham bar Tzaddok, who said: I was among the sons of Sena'ah ben Binyamin, who were wealthy; their father's Yom Tov was when they brought the wood offering while the Temple stood on the tenth of Av; and once Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbat and was deferred until Sunday, which is the eleventh of Av; and since it was the Yom Tov of the family members, they fasted on it and did not complete it; and it answers there too: it is only to pain in general — behold R' Avraham bar Tzaddok fasted on Tisha B'Av that was deferred, which was in the past, and nevertheless did not complete because it is only to pain in general; and it appears to me there is a distinction etc.; but an individual etc. — meaning in Tosafot haRosh it is explained that the reason one who fasts from the seventeenth of Tammuz until the ninth of Av must complete is as follows: there they did not need to accept pain in general upon themselves, for on the contrary they are comparable to the four fasts of the seventeenth of Tammuz and the ninth of Av and the three of Tishrei and the tenth of Tevet that they fast on the destruction; and these three weeks are also hinted in Daniel to fast on them for the destruction etc. — end of his words. It appears his intent: presumably the intent of one who fasts in the three weeks, since they are for the destruction, is to fast comparable to the fasts the sages established for the destruction — namely to complete; not so for R' Elazar ben Tzaddok, since it was his Yom Tov he was not obligated to fast at all, and what he fasted part of the day was not because of the destruction but to share in the congregation's pain who fast — if so, R' Avraham bar Tzaddok's fast was not over the past, i.e. the destruction, but like now to share the congregation's distress; and similar to this Taz explained.
