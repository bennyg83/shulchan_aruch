(s.k. 11) It is, etc. See Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 258, where the Rama brought a dispute whether if he contemplated in his heart to give tzedakah he is obligated to give; and he agreed with the view that he is obligated, and that is Rabbeinu Tam's view: that regarding tzedakah hirhur is like speech; and he also holds that a fast has the law of tzedakah in this. But if it was only in his mind, etc.: for sometimes a person regrets his intention; therefore we require complete acceptance. And see the Taz.
And so it appears in Yoreh De'ah, siman 214, where he wrote: one who is accustomed to fast the fasts before Rosh Hashanah, etc., and wishes to retract, etc., needs three who will release him: if at the time he began to practice it was his intent to practice so forever, and he practiced so even once, etc.; until here his language. Hence that it was merely his intent to practice so forever does not help; rather [it helps] in combination with his having practiced so once. And one must say: even where it is complete acceptance that helps, nevertheless it does not help that he be obligated so forever; for regarding future years it is not called complete acceptance unless he practiced so once.
And so he wrote in siman 553, se'if 1 in the gloss, as it should read: meaning, what he wrote: if he ceased eating on erev Tisha B'Av and accepted in his heart not to eat more, it is not acceptance; rather he must bring it out with his lips; until here his language. But here he concludes that acceptance in the heart is acceptance. Necessarily, above in siman 553 he is dealing where it was not complete acceptance. And what the Rama concluded there "unless he brought it out with his lips" is not precise; rather the same applies where it is complete acceptance in the heart; but he stated a fixed rule. But in Magen Avraham's words above in siman 553, s.k. 2, it does not appear so, for he wrote, and this is his language: and the Bach wrote that it is acceptance; therefore it is good to stipulate, etc.; end of his words. And one cannot say that above he is dealing where it was not complete acceptance, for the Bach too agrees that [mere intent] is not acceptance.
