4) [Seif 2] Wine covers, etc. The reason is that other beverages are subordinate to it. Olas Tamid, paragraph 2. Taz, paragraph 2, writes that Mordechai in the chapter Keitzad Mevarchin explains that wine covers other beverages specifically when they were before him as he recited the blessing over the wine. Rema, of blessed memory, however, cites these words of Mordechai in Darkei Moshe, paragraph 6, and concludes that the reason is that nowadays we do not establish a fixed drinking session over wine. In the name of R' Avraham of Prague, he writes that according to the Tur's statement that our sitting is equivalent to their reclining, there is no distinction between the present era and theirs. He concludes by referring to siman 213 for the practical ruling. He means that there the Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule that our sitting constitutes an established session like their reclining with respect to one person discharging another. It therefore likewise constitutes an established session for covering other beverages, even if they were not before him when he recited the blessing over the wine, contrary to Mordechai. See there and understand this. Dagul MeRevavah similarly writes regarding the cited words of Taz that he was imprecise and that we do not rule like Mordechai; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 1, cites this. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8, and Nehar Shalom, paragraph 2, write the same. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2, likewise writes that according to the Tur's statement in siman 213 that even nowadays drinking can constitute an established session, it is like a meal. Thus, even if the beverages were not before him when he recited the blessing over the wine, they require neither an initial nor a concluding blessing, even outside a meal. If possible, however, one should satisfy all opinions by placing the beverages before him when he recites the blessing, by intending with the blessing over the wine to cover them, or by subsequently reciting Shehakol over another item and intending to cover them. If this is impossible, such as when he did not intend to drink other beverages when he recited the blessing and thus neither placed them before him nor intended to cover them, and he also cannot cover them afterward with another item whose blessing is Shehakol, he may rely upon the Shulchan Aruch and the authorities who follow it.
5) There: Wine covers, etc. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, end of paragraph 10, writes that wine covers all other beverages specifically when one drinks it as part of an established session. If he drinks a small amount of wine casually, it covers them only if the beverages were before him when he recited the blessing; see there. Maran's words, of blessed memory, however, imply no distinction between established and casual drinking. Rather, whenever wine is before him and he intends to drink more, if other beverages are brought before him he does not recite an initial blessing over them. Only if he had already removed from his mind the intent to drink more wine, even though he had not yet recited its concluding blessing, and beverages were then brought to him, does he recite an initial blessing over them. He does not recite a concluding blessing over them, however, because they are covered by the concluding blessing over the wine. See Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8, and our words below concerning siman 208, seif 16, and understand this.
6) There: Wine covers, etc. See Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 47, who inclines to say that even something subordinate to a beverage requires no blessing. For example, if after drinking wine one drinks coffee and wishes to eat a little sugar to sweeten it, he need not recite a blessing even over the sugar; see there. This does not appear correct to me. The principal law is itself disputed, for according to Mordechai, nowadays wine does not cover other beverages unless they were before him when he recited the blessing, as stated in the preceding paragraph. Although we follow the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, it is sufficient that wine covers other beverages, and not also what is subordinate to those beverages. The best course is that when eating the sugar, he should intend to eat something sweet and not merely to sweeten the coffee, and then recite a blessing over it. Alternatively, he should place it into the coffee until it dissolves and is no longer discernible, and not recite a blessing.
7) There, gloss: Even from an initial blessing. The Shulchan Aruch below, siman 208, seif 16, rules likewise; see there and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
