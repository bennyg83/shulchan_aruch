(2) Wine exempts, etc. This is because wine is the foremost of all beverages, and all other beverages are subordinate to it. Tosafos.
(3) All kinds of beverages. This applies provided that they were before him on the table when he recited the blessing over the wine. If they were not before him at the time of the blessing, then even if they were brought while the wine was still before him, this does not suffice and he must bless over them. Some are lenient even when they were not before him, provided that he had them in mind. All this applies when he did not establish himself to drink wine, but intended to drink only this one cup. If he established himself to drink wine, several later authorities agree that the wine exempts even beverages that were not before him when he blessed, provided that they were brought while the wine was still before him. If they were brought only after he finished drinking the wine, he must bless over them, as Maharam Banet writes in his commentary, unless he had them in mind before completing his drinking. Ideally, it is always preferable that the other beverages be before him when he blesses over the wine. Know also that if he recited Kiddush over wine and discharged others with his blessing, but they did not taste from the Kiddush cup and now wish to drink other beverages, they must bless over those beverages even if they were before them when he blessed over the wine. The reason wine exempts other beverages is that they are subordinate to it, and this rationale does not apply to one who does not drink the wine. See Biur Halachah for further discussion.
(4) Even from the first blessing. See below, siman 208, seif 16, and the Mishnah Berurah there, regarding the after-blessing.
