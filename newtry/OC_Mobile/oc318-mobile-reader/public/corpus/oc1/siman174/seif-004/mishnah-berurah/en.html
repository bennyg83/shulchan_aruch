(6) If he established, etc. The later authorities write that establishing is not literal. The same law applies even if he drank only one cup of wine before the meal: it exempts the wine drunk during the meal, provided that he intended to drink wine during the meal as well.
(7) During the meal. The same wine also exempts wine drunk after the meal but before Bircas HaMazon, in a place where people customarily drink wine after finishing their food, or when he explicitly intended to do so. If, however, he had no wine before the meal and blessed over wine during the meal, he must bless again over wine drunk after the meal. Wine during the meal comes only to settle the food in his stomach and is not sufficiently significant to exempt wine after the meal, which comes for drinking and enjoyment. Beit Yosef writes that if he is thirsty because of his eating, then even if much time has elapsed after the meal, so long as he has not recited Bircas HaMazon the wine is considered wine during the meal, since it comes to settle the food. Rashba also somewhat implies that if he drinks immediately after eating, it is presumed to come for that purpose. In practice, this law is uncommon among us, because we do not withdraw from bread until Bircas HaMazon, and everything is considered part of the meal. Consequently, wine during the meal exempts wine afterward, since both serve to settle the food.
(8) And likewise the wine of Kiddush. This teaches that one should not think that only ordinary wine before the meal, which comes to open the digestive organs and for drinking, exempts wine during the meal, whereas Kiddush wine, whose primary purpose is a mitzvah, does not. Rather, it too exempts. It appears obvious to me that in our countries, where we are not accustomed to drink wine during the meal even on Shabbos, Kiddush wine exempts it only if he had this in mind initially, as stated above.
(9) During the meal. The same applies to wine drunk after the meal. Later authorities.
(10) And likewise one who recites Havdalah, etc. Although Havdalah is not connected to the meal at all in the way Kiddush is, nevertheless, since he recited Havdalah at the table, meaning that he had prepared himself for the meal, it is connected to the meal. This refers to the case described above, where he intended to drink wine during the meal as well.
(11) Because the blessing, etc. The reason is as stated above: Havdalah is not connected to the meal as Kiddush is, since Kiddush may be recited only in the place of the meal. Therefore, according to this opinion, he must at least wash his hands for the meal first, for this is certainly considered the beginning of the meal.
(12) Havdalah wine. Magen Avraham writes that this applies specifically to Havdalah wine, which does not come for the needs of the meal. Ordinary wine before the meal, however, comes for the meal by opening the digestive organs, and therefore exempts wine during the meal even if drunk before washing. For this reason the Mechaber states the rule at the beginning of the seif without limiting it to wine drunk after washing.
(13) He washed his hands. This is not literal. The same applies if he established himself for the meal at the table. Magen Avraham. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees, and this also appears from Biur HaGra.
(14) Before Havdalah. One might ask how it is permitted to interrupt with Havdalah between washing and Hamotzi. The answer is that this does not constitute a diversion of attention, since he intends to eat and may not eat without first reciting Havdalah. It is, however, forbidden to interrupt between washing and Hamotzi by drinking voluntarily.
(15) He should intend not to discharge. He should then recite the after-blessing over this cup before the meal.
