If he established. The same applies if he recited a blessing over wine before the meal. He need not recite another blessing over wine after the meal but before Bircas HaMazon. If, however, he had no wine before the meal and recited a blessing over wine during the meal, he must recite another blessing over wine after the meal, because wine during the meal comes to soak the food and is not significant enough to exempt wine that comes for drinking. See the end of siman 177, seif 2, which explains that this case is not common. See there.
Kiddush wine. It also exempts wine after the meal. Bach; Rashba. This is the principal ruling.
Havdalah wine. This implies specifically Havdalah wine, since it comes only for the sake of the mitzvah and is not needed for the meal. So it appears from Tosafos. But when one drinks to quench his thirst, wine before the meal exempts wine during the meal. Terumas HaDeshen likewise writes this. Although some disagree even in this case, Beit Yosef ruled not in accordance with them. So it appears from seif 7 and from siman 474.
He washed his hands. This is not precise, for the same applies if he established himself for the meal. So it appears from Tosafos, s.v. “wine was brought to them.” See there. Although they washed only one hand and needed to wash again, the earlier wine nevertheless exempted the later wine. See also there regarding those who recited Kiddush in the synagogue, etc. The same appears from Bach. If you ask how it is permitted to interrupt with Havdalah between washing and HaMotzi, the answer is that this is not a diversion of attention, since he intends to eat.
