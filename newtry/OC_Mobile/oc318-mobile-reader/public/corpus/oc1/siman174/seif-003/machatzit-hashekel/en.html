Seif katan 3: If he established, and the same applies if he recited a blessing, etc. Know that regarding the words “if he established,” the Taz writes in seif katan 4 that this is not precise, and it comes only to exclude a case where he later changed his mind.
After the meal, when he has removed his mind from eating any more.
And it is not considered significant enough to exempt wine that comes for drinking. Meaning, wine before the meal comes for drinking, and also to open and stimulate the appetite, as explained below. Wine after the meal likewise comes for drinking. Wine during the meal, however, comes to soak the food he has eaten. Therefore, wine before the meal, which is significant, exempts the wine during the meal, which is less significant, and also wine after the meal, which is of equal significance. By contrast, wine during the meal, which is less significant, does not exempt wine after the meal, which is significant because it comes for drinking.
See siman 177. Meaning, the reason Beit Yosef omitted this law is that siman 177 explains that the case of wine after the meal, namely, after one has removed his mind from eating but has not yet recited Bircas HaMazon, is not common among us, because we do not remove our minds from eating until after Bircas HaMazon. Beit Yosef therefore omitted it.
