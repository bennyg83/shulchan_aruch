Bach writes that the Mishnah teaches a novelty according to Beis Shammai: even when the time for Bircas HaMazon has arrived, one nevertheless first recites the blessing over the wine. Rashi, however, disagrees with what he writes concerning the Gemara's case that the wine did not arrive during the meal, which implies that if it did arrive during the meal, even Beis Hillel would agree that one blesses over it and need not leave it until after the meal. Taz writes that according to Rashi, Beis Hillel likewise holds this way. It is specifically according to Beis Shammai, who holds that Bircas HaMazon does not strictly require a cup but that using one is merely a mitzvah, that if wine arrives during the meal he blesses and drinks it, whereas if it arrives after the meal he blesses, tastes from it, and leaves it for Bircas HaMazon, since the time for Bircas HaMazon has arrived. This is unlike Beis Hillel, according to whom the same law applies even if it arrives during the meal. See the Gemara there and understand. See what I will write, with Hashem's help, on Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, concerning this. On further consideration, it may be said that when Rashi writes, “and it did not arrive during the meal,” he does not mean that it did not arrive at all during the meal. Rather, his meaning is that had it arrived during the meal and they possessed it then, even Beis Shammai would agree that they should recite Bircas HaMazon over it. Only where they had no wine during the meal does Beis Shammai hold that “and you shall be satisfied” refers to drinking, as Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, writes in the name of Rashba. The case of wine arriving after the meal is therefore a novelty according to Beis Shammai, as Bach explains, even according to Rashi. Yet Rashi's words, “if he wishes,” do not imply this. The Gemara did not state that they dispute whether “and you shall be satisfied” means drinking because one must in any event wait approximately six hours, lest a cup for Bircas HaMazon become available.
