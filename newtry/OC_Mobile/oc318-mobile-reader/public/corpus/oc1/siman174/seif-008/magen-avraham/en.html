Every place. This applies even at a wedding under the chuppah, for the blessings of erusin and nisuin. Bach in the name of Maharam Schor. Nowadays, however, this is not the custom. It appears to me that only in a setting where permission is formally requested does one say “savri” over wine in place of “birshus.” At a wedding or bris, where no permission is requested, one should not say “savri” at all. The Tur writes: The one who recited the blessing over the wine after the meal recites the blessing over the incense brought after the meal. Although incense was ordinarily brought after Bircas HaMazon, if it was brought before Bircas HaMazon, the one who recited the blessing over the wine acquires the right to bless over it even if another person present is greater, because once he began the concluding blessings he completes them. Rashi and Rabbeinu Yonah. The Geonim explain that the one who recited Hamotzi recites the blessing over incense brought during the meal. Although it was ordinarily brought after eating but before Bircas HaMazon, at which point another person could have recited the blessing because it is like a separate meal, nevertheless, since it was brought during the meal, the one who was honored with Hamotzi is also honored until the completion of the meal. It is possible that he likewise recites the blessing over wine drunk during the meal. Although the listeners cannot answer amen, since they smell immediately it is impossible to wait until each person recites his own blessing, for meanwhile they would smell without a blessing. It is therefore preferable that one person recite the blessing for all, even though they do not answer amen. Rabbeinu Yonah. It appears to me that this is also the view of the Raavad, unlike the strained interpretation in Kesef Mishneh; see there. Regarding incense brought after Bircas HaMazon, the most distinguished person recites the blessing, even if he had authorized another person to lead Bircas HaMazon, for that authorization has ended and the privilege returns to the most distinguished person. So write the Raavad. Baal HaMaor writes that the one who led Bircas HaMazon recites the blessing over it. Similar cases may be derived from this. See also what I wrote in siman 213.
