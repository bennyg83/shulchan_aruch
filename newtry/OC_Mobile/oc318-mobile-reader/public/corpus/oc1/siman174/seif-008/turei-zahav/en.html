Because they cannot answer amen. This explains the Gemara's statement that the throat is not free. The alternative opinion cited by Rama understands “the throat is not free” to mean that they do not hear the blessing because they are eating. Therefore, saying “Attention, my masters” is effective, since it causes them to pay attention and listen. It appears to me that even according to this interpretation, its effectiveness depends upon their stopping eating while he recites the blessing, for they must at least be concerned that the food might enter the windpipe before the esophagus. According to the first opinion, even this does not help, because we are concerned that they may fail to stop eating. So it appears to me. In siman 213 it is written that regarding wine consumed outside a meal, one person recites the blessing for everyone.
