[Levush] Because of its importance, etc. This means because Kiddush, Havdalah, and other blessings are recited over wine. Some explain that unlike all other items, wine is uniquely identified by the name of its source, the vine, in its blessing.
He established himself, etc. This means casually. The wording 'established' comes only to exclude a case where he had no intention to drink wine during the meal and later changed his mind, which is treated as a new decision. Taz. The Rosh in Arvei Pesachim, folio 131, explains this.
[Levush] Because it is important, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that this is not the reason, but rather because the wine also comes for drinking. In my humble opinion that is precisely the Levush's intent when he says it is more important, namely because it also comes for drinking, as he wrote immediately before that it is more significant than ordinary wine. One might have thought wine of Kiddush is less significant because it comes for a mitzvah; the Levush teaches that since it is also drunk, as implied by the Rosh, it is more significant.
[4] Levush: That we do not, etc. This is not the real reason. Even in their time, when they would withdraw their hands from bread, wine drunk before the meal exempted wine drunk after the meal. His words are also difficult in light of siman 177, seif 2, where according to the Tur even nowadays people withdraw from bread at large meals. The Tur is also difficult, for he omitted what the Rosh wrote, that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not exclusive and that the matter depends on a person’s intention. Lechem Chamudos, folio 52, likewise expresses surprise at the Tur. In my humble opinion, however, there is no difficulty. This is included in what the Tur cites from the Raavad, that in places where wine is common there is no distinction between Shabbos, Yom Tov, and weekdays. It therefore follows that everything depends on the person’s intention. This is explicit in Pesachim, chapter Arvei Pesachim, and in Rabbeinu Yerucham, folio 142. After they write that Shabbos and Yom Tov are not exclusive, they cite the Raavad in the same manner. The Levush may be reconciled as follows. His reason, that we do not withdraw from bread, was written regarding the latter clause, that wine drunk during the meal exempts wine drunk after the meal. In truth, at large meals where people withdraw from bread and drink wine, the wine drunk during the meal does not exempt the later wine; only wine drunk before the meal does so, because both are drunk for the sake of drinking. The Tur’s wording is: “Wine drunk before the meal exempts wine drunk after the meal.” This means a person who regularly establishes a session to drink wine after finishing his meal but before Bircas HaMazon. It appears to me that this necessarily refers to one who has withdrawn from bread and drinks wine, for if he had not withdrawn from bread, even wine drunk during the meal would exempt it. Rather, the case is one who withdrew from bread, and the Tur teaches that he must regularly establish such a drinking session after finishing his meal. If he does not regularly do so, but it happened on one occasion that he withdrew from bread and drank wine, the earlier wine certainly does not exempt it unless, when drinking before the meal, he intended to withdraw from bread later and drink wine, as explained above. This excludes Beit Yosef’s strained interpretation of the Tur, which does not withstand examination.
