And even wine should not have required, etc. This is the Gemara's question: bread should exempt wine as well. It answers that since wine has a blessing established for itself because of its importance, as written in seif 1, bread does not exempt it. The second opinion holds that the Gemara's question concerned wine specifically, that bread should exempt it because wine provides nourishment. Water, however, provides no nourishment, so there is no reason that bread should exempt it. It appears to me that our beer, which is made from grain, requires no blessing during the meal even according to the second opinion. The Rosh writes in the chapter Eilu Ovrin, cited by Beit Yosef below in siman 472, that the beer made in Ashkenaz contains far more than the measure of achilas pras. It therefore certainly provides nourishment, and bread exempts it.
He should sit before washing, etc. One might ask that he should be required to recite an after-blessing after drinking a revi'is before HaMotzi, since Bircas HaMazon does not exempt Borei Nefashos or the one blessing that is an abridgment of three. This is evident from siman 177, concerning foods eaten after the meal that require blessings both before and after them, and Bircas HaMazon does not exempt them. Beit Yosef likewise writes in siman 197, s.v. kasav achi, and in siman 473 in the name of a responsum of Rashba, that Bircas HaMazon does not exempt what one ate before the meal if it does not facilitate the meal. There the discussion concerns Borei Nefashos. If so, what he drank before the meal should not combine with what he drinks during the meal. It may be answered that what he drank before eating was consumed for the purpose of what he would eat afterward. As written in seif 6, Bircas HaMazon exempts wine consumed before the meal because it opens the intestines and stimulates the appetite, as the Rosh writes in the chapter Arvei Pesachim. The same applies to other beverages. He therefore need not recite an after-blessing before eating. Certainly, however, one who was already obligated to recite Borei Nefashos or Me'ein Shalosh is not exempted by Bircas HaMazon. I found the following in Sefer Kavanos HaArizal: “Before eating, one should drink a little water, less than a revi'is, recite an initial and final blessing over it, and intend to exempt the water he will drink during the meal. Once I drank more than a revi'is, and they showed me on my forehead that there had been a blessing in vain.” It appears that this passage does not accept the reasoning I cited from the Rosh, that wine consumed before the meal is exempt from an after-blessing. Since an after-blessing would then be required, it would interrupt between the blessing recited over the wine before the meal and the wine consumed during the meal, and therefore it instructed him to drink less than a revi'is. This is difficult. Why was he shown that there had been a blessing in vain? The sin in such a case would be omission of a required blessing. Either he recited Borei Nefashos before the meal, in which case, according to this second opinion, he should afterward have recited Shehakol over the water consumed during the meal, and by failing to do so he omitted a blessing during the meal; or he did not recite Borei Nefashos, in which case he sinned by omitting that blessing. Furthermore, the statement that he recited both an initial and final blessing over less than a revi'is is difficult, since one does not recite an after-blessing over less than a revi'is. It appears to me that these statements are unreliable and did not originate from the holy Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, of blessed memory.
And the custom follows the first opinion. From this I learned that one who drinks liquor during a meal must recite Shehakol. The two opinions dispute only other beverages, based on the reasoning that eating is impossible without drinking. Regarding liquor, however, everyone agrees that a blessing is required. A beverage called mead, by contrast, requires no blessing, because it too is included among beverages consumed with food and has the same law as beer. Liquor is different, since it is not consumed as an ordinary drink. Levush rules likewise in siman 177, because liquor is not included among items that come because of the meal.
