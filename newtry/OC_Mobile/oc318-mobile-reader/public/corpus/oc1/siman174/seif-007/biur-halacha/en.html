And bread exempts them. Know that there is a distinction in this matter. If one is establishing a meal on bread, at least to some degree, he recites HaMotzi over the bread and the beverage is subordinate to the bread. If, however, he eats only a small amount of bread so that the beverages will not harm him on an empty stomach, the beverages are primary and the bread is subordinate. Strictly speaking, the bread should then be entirely exempted by the blessing over the beverages. Since, however, he eats the subordinate bread first, before the primary item, he recites Shehakol over the subordinate bread, and that blessing applies both to the bread and to the beverages. This appears to be the intent of Magen Avraham, seif katan 12, and it is likewise explained in Shulchan Aruch HaRav, unlike Machatzis HaShekel. Even HaOlas Tamid linked this statement of Magen Avraham to his words in siman 177, seif katan 1. In my humble opinion, this is incorrect. Here the bread is entirely subordinate, since its sole purpose is to prevent the water from harming him. There, however, although the food is not primary so as to exempt the other foods, it is nevertheless not subordinate such that it should lose its proper blessing and receive Shehakol instead. For this reason Magen Avraham does not entertain such a doubt there; see there.
And he should recite the blessing with the intention of drinking, etc. This applies specifically when he derives at least some benefit from the water he drinks. If he derives no benefit at all, it is not proper to recite a blessing over it. See siman 204, seif 7, and what we wrote there.
And the custom, etc. If he has wine, it is preferable that he exempt those beverages by reciting the blessing over wine during the meal, for wine exempts all beverages. Eliyah Rabbah.
