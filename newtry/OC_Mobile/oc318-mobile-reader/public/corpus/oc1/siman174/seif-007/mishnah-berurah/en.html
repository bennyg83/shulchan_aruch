(28) If he has no wine, etc. This refers back to seif 1. One recites a first blessing over wine during the meal because of its importance, but not over other beverages, which are subordinate to the meal.
(29) As items that come because of the meal. Meaning, during the meal. Siman 177 explains that no blessing is recited over them because Hamotzi exempts them.
(30) They require no blessing. Neither before nor afterward. This applies even if they are brought after the meal has ended, according to our practice of not withdrawing from bread, so that everything is considered part of the meal.
(31) The water. Drunk on an empty stomach. The same applies to other beverages.
(32) Some say that one should bless. Meaning, a first blessing. They hold that such beverages are like fruit eaten during the meal, which requires a blessing beforehand. All agree, however, that Bircas HaMazon exempts the after-blessing.
(33) The water. The same applies to all other beverages. Although wine is considered to come because of the meal, and requires a blessing only because of its importance, wine is different because it satisfies the heart more and also stimulates the appetite, unlike other beverages.
(34) Each time, because presumably, etc. Even according to this opinion, only water is presumed to involve a new decision, since a person drinks water only because of thirst. Beer, mead, and similar beverages are not ordinarily considered a new decision, and one blessing suffices according to all opinions. Lemon drinks and similar beverages, however, which are drunk only for thirst, have the same law as water according to this view. Magen Avraham.
(35) Before washing. After washing, one should not interrupt by drinking voluntary beverages. Havdalah is different, for one may have thought it permitted because eating is forbidden before Havdalah.
(36) In the place of his meal. He should not change his location, because a change of place requires a new blessing when he drinks afterward, as explained in siman 178. If he changes places, he has accomplished nothing.
(37) He should bless, etc. The later authorities write that he should be careful to drink less than a reviis. If he drinks a reviis, his solution accomplishes nothing, for he enters a doubt regarding the after-blessing over these beverages. Perhaps Bircas HaMazon does not exempt them, since they are not connected to the meal, having been drunk before washing. If, however, he has a cup of beer over which to recite Bircas HaMazon, he need not be careful about this, because the Borei Nefashos recited over that cup will also exempt the beverages he drank before Bircas HaMazon.
(38) With the intention, etc. Alternatively, he may ask another person who is not eating to bless over the beverage and intend to discharge him.
(39) The custom follows the first opinion. The reason is as stated above: eating generally requires drinking, so beverages are regarded as items that come because of the meal. Regarding liquor, some later authorities write that it requires a blessing during the meal because it is not drunk on account of thirst caused by the food, unlike other beverages. Magen Avraham, however, writes that during the meal no blessing is required, because it then comes to stimulate the appetite and is therefore also considered to come because of the meal. The same applies to fruits used to stimulate the appetite, such as salted lemons, salted olives, salted cucumbers, and the like. They differ from ordinary fruit eaten during the meal, which requires a blessing. Several later authorities adopt his view. In truth, it would have been proper to follow the Mechaber’s advice for other beverages, namely to bless over a small amount of liquor before washing with intent to exempt what he drinks during the meal, because there are differing opinions. The world, however, is accustomed to be lenient, and there is basis for this practice. All this applies during the meal. If he drinks liquor after the meal, when his sole intention is certainly to digest the food, all agree that it requires a blessing. Later authorities. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if he ate a fatty food and takes a little liquor to remove the greasiness from his mouth, and this is his regular practice, the liquor is subordinate to the food. Therefore, even after completing the meal, he need not bless. Chayei Adam writes that coffee drunk after the meal is only for digestion and differs from other beverages, so it requires a first blessing. This is not conclusive, and it is proper to bless first over a small amount of sugar in order to exempt the coffee. All this applies only on weekdays. On Shabbos and Yom Tov morning, the issue does not arise, for he certainly already recited Kiddush over a cup. If he recited Kiddush over wine, the wine exempts all beverages, under the conditions explained above in seif katan 3. If he recited Kiddush over beer or the like, he recited Shehakol, which automatically exempts the coffee as well.
