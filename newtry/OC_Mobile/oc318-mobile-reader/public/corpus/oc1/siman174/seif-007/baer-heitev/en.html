Drinking. This implies that spirits, which are not ordinarily drunk continuously, are not considered items that come because of the meal and require their own blessing. Even one who is accustomed always to drink them is disregarded relative to ordinary people. Mateh Moshe, Maharshal, Lechem Chamudos, and Taz; see Magen Avraham. Ateres Zekeinim in siman 177 writes in the name of the Gaon Rabbi Feivish that one should place the spirits on the table when reciting HaMotzi, in which case HaMotzi exempts them. If he did not do so, he should dip a little bread into them; see there. On Shabbos, however, none of this is necessary. Either he recited Kiddush over wine and was thereby exempt, as stated in seif 2, or he recited Kiddush over beer and was exempt through the blessing of Shehakol. See Magen Avraham. A beverage called mead requires no separate blessing, for it too is included among drinks that are subordinate to eating, and its law is like beer. Taz.
His meal. Nevertheless, it requires study whether, if he drank a reviis, he must recite an after-blessing. He should therefore drink less than a reviis before the meal and thereby avoid the doubt. This is also stated in the intentions of the Arizal. Alternatively, he should have another person who is not eating recite the blessing and intend to exempt him. If he drank a reviis, as commonly occurs at large meals where people drink before the food, he must be careful not to go outside before the meal, for that would constitute an interruption. If he remains, Bircas HaMazon exempts everything and he need not recite an after-blessing. See Magen Avraham, Taz, and Yad Aharon. Regarding the custom at large meals that after Bircas HaMazon is recited over a cup of wine they bring another beverage called coffee to settle a person after his food, Responsa Ginas Veradim, principle 1, simanim 1, 2, and 3, rules that no blessing should be recited over that coffee; see there. Shiltei HaGiborim writes that anise seeds eaten after the meal to sweeten the drink are like radish and olives, and the blessing over the wine exempts them. Magen Avraham writes that regarding other beverages drunk during the meal, the blessing over the bread exempts them, because the beverage is subordinate to the bread, while the anise seeds are subordinate to the beverage.
