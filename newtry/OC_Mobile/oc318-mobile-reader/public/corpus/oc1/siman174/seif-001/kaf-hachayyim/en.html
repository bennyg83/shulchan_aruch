1) [Seif 1] Wine consumed during the meal, etc., is not covered by the bread. This is because wine is significant, for in many situations a blessing is recited over it even though it is not needed for drinking, such as Kiddush and Havdalah. Taz, paragraph 1. Some explain that it generates a blessing for itself because it covers other beverages. Others explain that it generates a blessing for itself because with all other things the blessing does not mention the tree that produced them, whereas over wine one mentions the vine from which the wine emerged. Others explain that it generates a blessing for itself because one recites Borei Pri HaGafen over wine, whereas over grapes one recites Borei Pri HaEtz. Mateh Moshe, siman 298; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 1.
2) There: Wine consumed during the meal, etc. If his hands are soiled with food, he should wipe them, then recite the blessing over the wine and drink, as it is stated, 'Raise your hands in holiness,' etc. Sefer Chassidim, end of siman 28; Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1. See above, siman 158, paragraph 53.
3) There: The bread does not cover it. This implies that even if he had the wine in mind when reciting the blessing over the bread, it is not covered, and he must recite a blessing over it. Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 1, writes the same. See Tzelach on Berachos 41b, who writes that according to Baal HaMaor, if the wine was before him when he recited HaMotzi, he need not recite a blessing over that wine during the meal; see there. Pischei Olam, paragraph 2, cites this. We have only the plain meaning of the Gemara, the Shulchan Aruch, and the authorities who follow them, which explicitly implies that even if he had the wine in mind when reciting the blessing over the bread, it is not covered. The universal practice is likewise to recite a blessing over wine during the meal even if the wine was on the table when the blessing over the bread was recited. We have already written above in siman 17, paragraph 2, that where there is an established custom we are not concerned about a doubtful blessing; see there.
