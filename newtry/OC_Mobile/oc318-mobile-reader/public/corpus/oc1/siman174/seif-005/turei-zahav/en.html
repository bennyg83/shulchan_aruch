He did not intend to drink, etc. It is obvious that the same law applies to eating. For example, if one bought a single loaf for himself with the intention of eating it all and recited the blessing with that in mind, and afterward desired to eat more and sent someone to buy another loaf, he must recite HaMotzi again. If, however, he has bread in his house and cuts himself a piece to eat, and afterward wishes to cut another piece, this is not considered a change of mind, because this is the ordinary manner of eating. So it appears to me.
And they hand him cups, etc. It appears to me that nowadays, when the custom is that if someone joins people who are already drinking, many cups are offered to him, his intent when reciting the blessing over the first cup presumably includes everything they will subsequently give him. This resembles the law in siman 169, seif 3, that if an important person is present at the meal, etc. Here too, he knows they will give him more, and therefore he need not recite another blessing over a later cup.
