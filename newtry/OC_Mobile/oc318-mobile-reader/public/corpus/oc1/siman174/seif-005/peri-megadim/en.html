Of Kiddush. See Taz. Since Kiddush is valid only in the place of the meal, it pertains to the meal and exempts wine consumed during the meal. Havdalah, although it does not pertain to the meal, likewise exempts it. See Beit Yosef and the poskim. The novelty is that one should not think only ordinary wine consumed before the meal, which is significant because it opens the intestines and is consumed as a drink, exempts wine during and after the meal, whereas Kiddush wine, which is not consumed for the sake of drinking, does not. The ruling teaches the opposite: wine consumed for a mitzvah has even greater significance. This is the intent of Taz. Regarding an after-blessing, see siman 299. According to the opinion that wine consumed before washing does not exempt wine during the meal, one should recite an after-blessing. According to our practice, however, a doubt regarding blessings is resolved leniently, and one does not recite an after-blessing over Havdalah wine; it exempts the wine consumed during the meal.
