Seif katan 5: Wine, etc. This implies specifically Havdalah wine. Meaning, there is an opinion that it does not exempt unless he has already washed his hands.
For the sake of a mitzvah, and it is not needed for the meal. Meaning, although Kiddush too is only for the sake of a mitzvah, nevertheless it is connected to the meal, for we rule that Kiddush is valid only in the place of the meal. Havdalah, by contrast, is needed only for the mitzvah and is not needed for the meal.
And so it appears from Tosafos, but, etc. The words “and so it appears from Tosafos” are certainly a printing error, for on the contrary, Tosafos disagree, as Magen Avraham writes in the following seif katan.
But, etc. Wine before the meal. Meaning, even before washing. Although some disagree, these are Tosafos, as Magen Avraham writes in the following seif katan.
And so it appears from seif 7. This is the correct text, for it states that one who wishes to avoid the uncertainty should sit before washing, etc. Thus, even before washing is effective for beverages drunk before washing, and the same applies to wine. This is not an absolute proof, however, according to Magen Avraham’s statement in seif katan 6 that “he washed” is not precise, for the same applies if he established himself for the meal, and in that case he had established himself.
And so it appears from siman 474. Meaning, from the Tur there, who challenges the Rif. The Rif writes that on the first two nights of Pesach one recites a concluding blessing after the first two cups, which are before the meal. The Tur and Rosh challenge that these cups exempt the wine during the meal from an initial blessing, so how can one recite a concluding blessing after them? If even wine before the meal exempts only when drunk after washing, what difficulty is there with the Rif? The two cups are drunk before washing and would not exempt the wine during the meal at all, and Kiddush wine is certainly no better than ordinary wine before the meal. The Rif’s ruling that one should recite a concluding blessing after them would then be readily understood. Bach in fact defends the Rif’s view in this siman and below in this manner. Although the Tur holds that no authority maintains that wine before the meal, when drunk before washing, fails to exempt wine during the meal, and although Tosafos do hold this, he did not consider it reasonable to attribute this view to the Rif.
