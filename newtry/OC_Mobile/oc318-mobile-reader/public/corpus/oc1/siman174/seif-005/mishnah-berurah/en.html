(16) During the meal. Meaning, even when the cups were not drunk consecutively.
(17) With one blessing. Presumably, when he recited Borei Peri HaGafen over the first cup, he also intended all that he would drink during the meal.
(18) He did not intend, etc. Meaning, he intended to drink only that cup and no more, but afterward reconsidered and wished to drink more. Taz writes that the same applies to eating. For example, if he bought one loaf for himself intending to eat it all, blessed on that basis, and afterward desired more and sent someone to buy another loaf, he must recite Hamotzi again. His actions prove that he initially intended to eat only the loaf he purchased, for otherwise he would have bought more, and only afterward did he desire additional food. If, however, he has bread at home, cuts a piece to eat, and afterward wishes to cut another piece, this is not considered a new decision. People commonly think at the time of the blessing that one piece will suffice and then take more when they see that they are not satisfied.
(19) Who came to the meal. Meaning, he was not among the invited guests but came there incidentally. We therefore regard him as having made a new decision, since at the time he blessed he did not know whether they would give him more. See below, siman 179, seif 6, and the Mishnah Berurah there.
(20) And they hand him. Meaning, not all at once, but one after another.
(21) Over the cup of the wedding blessings. This refers to a setting with many participants, and similar cases.
(22) Each person must bless. Presumably, he does not intend to fulfill his obligation through the blessing of the one reciting it, since he does not know whether the cup will reach him. See below, siman 190, Magen Avraham seif katan 7, and the Mishnah Berurah there.
