And it likewise exempts wine consumed before the meal. This ruling is not clear. See Ramban in Milchamos, chapter Arvei Pesachim, and Ran, who hold that both Kiddush wine and other wine consumed before the meal require an after-blessing. Tosafos, Pesachim 101b, s.v. yedei yayin, also holds this view; see there. Accordingly, it appears proper with respect to Kiddush to drink only a cheekful. I later found that Chayei Adam, rule 41, seif 3, writes likewise. If one drank the entire cup, he should have in mind during Bircas HaMazon to exempt the wine he drank beforehand, and after the fact this is effective, as explained below in siman 208, seif 17. However, according to what the poskim explain, see siman 190, there are opinions that for beverages one becomes obligated in an after-blessing even for a kezayis, and certainly for most of a revi'is. It is therefore proper always to have in mind during Bircas HaMazon to exempt the Kiddush wine.
And even if he did not have, etc. See Mishnah Berurah regarding other beverages consumed before the meal, where we rule in the name of several later authorities that one should recite an after-blessing. This is the view of Magen Avraham, Birkei Yosef, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, Chayei Adam, and Magen Giborim. Although Eliyah Rabbah inclines to rule like Olas Tamid and Taz, that other beverages have the same law as wine because they too stimulate the appetite, except for water, regarding which Eliyah Rabbah agrees with the later authorities cited above, we did not see fit to be concerned for their view. Even regarding wine itself, where the Mechaber rules without qualification that Bircas HaMazon exempts it in accordance with the Rosh, the matter is not clear, as we wrote above. This is especially so in light of what I have now found in the Kuntres Acharon of Shulchan Aruch HaRav, who writes that most poskim disagree with the Rosh on this matter. I found a similar statement in Nishmas Adam, rule 41. The gloss of Rabbi Chaim Tzanzer, printed in the Zhitomir edition of Shulchan Aruch, likewise challenges the Rosh's view from the Tosefta cited in siman 177 by Magen Avraham, seif katan 6; see there. Consequently, he rules like the poskim who disagree with the Rosh, according to whom even wine consumed before the meal requires an after-blessing. We therefore have sufficient basis to exempt only wine, since wine unquestionably stimulates the appetite, as explained by the Rosh, but not other beverages. Even if one did not recite the after-blessing until after Bircas HaMazon, he should recite it afterward, as ruled by Chayei Adam. Nevertheless, liquor, which unquestionably stimulates the appetite, has the same law as wine in this regard. Therefore, if one drinks liquor before washing his hands in order to stimulate his appetite, he does not recite an after-blessing even if he drank a revi'is, because Bircas HaMazon exempts it. Chayei Adam writes that even regarding water, if one is very thirsty and has no appetite because of his thirst, and he drinks water before washing so that he will afterward desire to eat, he likewise need not recite an after-blessing, because the drinking pertains to the meal. Know that everything written above, that one must recite an after-blessing over beverages consumed before the meal, applies only when he does not drink beverages during the meal. If he also drinks during the meal, Magen Avraham, seif katan 13, remains uncertain whether an after-blessing is required; see there. He therefore writes that in such a case it is preferable not to drink a revi'is before washing; see there.
