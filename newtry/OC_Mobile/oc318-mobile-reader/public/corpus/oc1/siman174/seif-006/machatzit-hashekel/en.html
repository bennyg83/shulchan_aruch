Seif katan 6: He washed his hands is not precise, etc. And so it appears from Tosafos, s.v. “wine was brought to them,” etc. Meaning, Berachos 43a states: What is the order of reclining? Guests enter and sit on benches until all have arrived. Water is brought, and each washes one hand, although we do not follow this practice, as explained above in siman 158, seif 6, and see Beit Yosef there for the reason. Wine is brought, and each recites his own blessing. They ascend and recline, and each returns and washes both hands. Wine is then brought, and one person recites the blessing for all. Tosafos write there: Although wine before the meal exempts wine after the meal, all the more so it should exempt this wine before the meal. Here it is different, because when they ascended to recline there was a change of place and a new blessing is required. Thus, absent a change of place, the first wine would have exempted the later wine, even though it was drunk before washing, for they had washed only one hand and now needed to wash both hands. Necessarily, “he washed his hands” is not precise, and it is sufficient that he established himself for the meal, as they had done there. One might object that perhaps this proves nothing, for actual washing is never required for Havdalah wine, and Tosafos there discuss wine before the meal, which according to Beit Yosef exempts even when drunk before washing, as Magen Avraham writes in seif katan 5. To this Magen Avraham writes: See there regarding those who recited Kiddush in the synagogue. Meaning, from there it is evident that Tosafos hold that even wine before the meal does not exempt when drunk before washing. Tosafos write on Berachos 42b, s.v. “and Rav Sheshes,” that wine drunk before the meal plainly exempts wine used to soak the food, namely wine during the meal. They prove this from Pesachim regarding people who recited Kiddush in the synagogue, where Rabbi Yochanan said that they had also fulfilled their obligation regarding wine, meaning that not only had they fulfilled Kiddush, for he holds that Kiddush may be recited outside the place of the meal, but they also need not recite another blessing over wine during the meal. Although Rabbi Yochanan was refuted, he was refuted only regarding a change of place. If there was no change of place, we rule like him that wine before the meal exempts wine during the meal. Tosafos continue that the same should apply to Havdalah, for there is no reason to distinguish between Havdalah and Kiddush, contrary to those who say that Kiddush alone exempts because Kiddush is valid only in the place of the meal, whereas Havdalah wine does not exempt even if Havdalah was recited after washing. Tosafos explain that the ruling that wine before the meal exempts wine during the meal refers to a case where one already washed, as implied by the Baraisa concerning the order of reclining. Nevertheless, once he has washed, even Havdalah wine exempts, as they prove from the Pesachim passage. Since Rabbi Yochanan holds that Kiddush is valid outside the place of the meal, Kiddush according to him has the same status that Havdalah has for us, and yet he holds that it exempts wine during the meal. He was refuted only because of the change of place. Tosafos thus explicitly write that wine before the meal exempts only after washing. Yet they ask on 43a why the blessing recited when they washed one hand should not exempt the wine drunk after washing both hands. Necessarily, washing is not precise, and establishing oneself for the meal is sufficient. Their proof from Rabbi Yochanan in Pesachim also shows that “he washed” is not precise, for presumably those who heard Kiddush in the synagogue had not yet washed their hands, since they would eat at home, and nevertheless according to Rabbi Yochanan that wine exempted the wine during the meal. Necessarily, establishing oneself for the meal is sufficient.
And if you ask, how is it permitted to interrupt, etc. Although Magen Avraham writes that “he washed” is not precise, the Baraisa nevertheless states that he washed, so such a case certainly exists even after washing.
See siman 166 regarding what Magen Avraham writes. Meaning, he writes there in seif katan 1 in the name of the Agudah that Kiddush and Havdalah are not considered an interruption between washing and HaMotzi.
