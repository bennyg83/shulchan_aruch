[6] Wine of Havdalah, etc. Bach writes that according to Tosafos and Mordechai, even one who drinks wine before washing does not thereby exempt later wine. Perishah, however, writes that they refer specifically to Havdalah, which is not for the sake of the meal. Regarding ordinary wine drinking, although their practice was to drink only after washing, if one happened to drink before washing, it does exempt the later wine. Magen Avraham rules this way, and Beit Yosef also implies it. It appears to me that there is proof from the Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter Eilu Devarim: Beis Hillel say that one first mixes the cup and then washes his hands. Rashi explains that this means the wine served before the meal is mixed and drunk, and only afterward one washes his hands. This appears to refer to the Mishnah that wine drunk before the meal exempts wine drunk after the meal. The Mishnah is thus dealing with precisely this case. However, Tosafos on folio 42, s.v. veRav Sheshes, write that the wine of Havdalah is no worse than wine drunk before the meal, which exempts later wine. This supports Bach’s reading. Perishah further writes that Tosafos’ statement, that nowadays people drink the wine before the meal after washing, is not exact; it means after Hamotzi, since one may not interrupt with wine drinking between washing and Hamotzi. Even according to the Geonim cited above in siman 166, who permit an interruption by speaking, this refers only to speech, not an act of drinking. Magen Avraham, however, cites Hagahos Mordechai to Arvei Pesachim: “One might ask how it is permitted to interrupt with Havdalah between washing and Hamotzi. The answer is that it is not a diversion of attention, since he intends to eat.” According to this, even ordinary wine drinking may be discussed as occurring between washing and Hamotzi. Beit Yosef likewise writes this in the name of Maharam, and Tosafos there also imply that Havdalah and ordinary wine drinking are equivalent in this respect. Nevertheless, Bach writes in seif katan 8 that it is permitted to interrupt and recite a blessing over a drink between washing and Hamotzi because he does so for the sake of a mitzvah, to avoid the doubt discussed below in seif 4. This implies that otherwise one may not interrupt. It appears that all agree that if he recited the blessing after Hamotzi, it exempts the later wine. For this reason, it appears to me that at large meals the practice is to recite the blessing over wine immediately after Hamotzi, before eating, in order to exempt the wine drunk after the meal, as I wrote in seif katan 4. I also see support for Perishah from the Gemara on folio 52, where Beis Hillel challenges Beis Shammai, who say that one first washes and then mixes the cup: Is it not necessary to proceed directly from washing to the meal? See there. However, according to Tosafos in Pesachim 106b, who explain that the case concerns mixing with hot water and requires care not to diminish the quantity, there is no proof. See Birchas Avraham, folio 201, whose words are difficult in light of the Tosafos cited above. Examine carefully, for I have been brief. See also below, at the end of siman 211 and siman 231.
