1) [Seif 1] There are four domains regarding Shabbos, etc. Carrying out and bringing in from one domain to another, meaning from a public domain to a private domain or the reverse, is a primary labor. Although this and all fundamentals of Torah were transmitted orally to Moshe at Sinai, the Torah states, 'Let neither man nor woman perform any more labor for the sacred donation,' and 'the people were restrained from bringing.' This teaches that bringing is called labor. The oral tradition likewise teaches that one who carries an object four amos in a public domain is considered to have transferred it from one domain to another and is liable. Rambam, chapter 12, halachah 8. Shulchan Aruch explains below at the beginning of siman 346 which domains are defined by Torah law and which rabbinically; see there. According to the mystical meaning, one who carries from a private domain to a public domain causes sparks of holiness already refined to be drawn into the forces of impurity, which are the public domain. One who brings from a public domain into a private domain figuratively causes the Shechinah to descend on Shabbos into the place of the forces of impurity, the public domain, to refine sparks from them and raise them above into the private domain. This is a great blemish, as explained in Eitz Chaim, Gate 11, at the end of chapter 7; see there.
2) There: There are four domains regarding Shabbos, etc. Why does the Mishnah not list five domains? A karpef larger than beis sasayim that was not enclosed for residence is a full private domain, so one who throws from a public domain into it or the reverse is liable, yet one may carry within it only four amos. One can answer that it is a full private domain, and only regarding the restriction against carrying more than four amos did the Sages treat it like a karmelis. Since the Mishnah already lists a private domain and karmelis, it need not list this case. For the same reason, it does not list a courtyard whose residents did not establish an eruv. Tosafos, Shabbos 6a.
3) There: And a karmelis. Yerushalmi at the beginning of Shabbos cites Rabbi Chiya: karmel means tender and full, neither moist nor dry but intermediate. A karmelis likewise is neither a private nor public domain. Tosafos there. Rashi, Shabbos 3b, explains karmelis from the expression 'its forest and its fruitful field,' referring to a place that is neither constantly traversed by the public nor used as a private domain. Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah, chapter 1 of Shabbos, compares a karmelis to a widow, who is neither a virgin nor married, and explains that the accurate form of the term is ke'armelis. Ran there writes the same.
