Behold Beit Yosef equated Hagahot Maimoni's view to Ran's view; and likewise my friend the Gaon R' Moshe Ny in Beit Meir raised to infer from Hagahot Maimoni from comparing to a reed in reshut ha-yachid — he holds standing in karmelit ten high and not four wide is not makom patur; and he challenged Beit Yosef above — behold from Ran's words explaining that karmelit above ten applies also to a defined post in karmelit — above ten is makom patur unlike Hagahot Maimoni; and I answered that in my humble opinion one cannot derive thus from Hagahot Maimoni — behold initially when he came to say dung in karmelit is not makom patur he learned from Rav in the first chapter of Eruvin "the kind found its kind and was aroused"; and afterward he wrote latrines between two houses, etc.; and even if you say dung in reshut ha-rabbim would be makom patur — here d'rabbanan we do not find; and he learned this from a reed in reshut ha-rabbim; and it appears he already learned from the first chapter of Eruvin; and therefore it appears clear that what he said in domains d'rabbanan means domains of courtyards and houses that did not eruv together which is complete reshut ha-yachid — in truth even ten high is not makom patur; and therefore he brought new proof from a reed because in such a case even ten high likewise; or he came to say the first proof — even if we say in karmelit it is also makom patur, nevertheless in such a case of two houses which is reshut ha-yachid for all it is not makom patur as explained in Shulchan Aruch gloss siman 355 — in karmelit there are two views and in two houses all agree it is not makom patur; if so the main proof of Hagahot Maimoni from the reed is for the latter law of latrines between two houses — in this too even ten high is not makom patur; but in karmelit one can say he also holds above ten and not four wide is makom patur — and Beit Yosef's words are clear:
