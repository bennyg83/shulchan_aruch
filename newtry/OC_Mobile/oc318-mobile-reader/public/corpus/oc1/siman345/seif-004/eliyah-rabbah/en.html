[8] Holes, etc. This applies even when they are below ten handbreadths. Tosafot's statement that below ten they belong to the public domain was only according to an initial assumption, unlike Olat Shabbos, as stated by the Magen Avraham. I found the Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh explicitly writing that even below ten and without four by four they have this law. I later found Rabbenu Yerucham, page 71, citing Tosafot that if the holes pass entirely through the wall, opening both toward a private domain and a public domain, then below ten they are treated as holes of the public domain, while above ten they are like the private domain. The Magen Avraham and Olat Shabbos overlooked this. See siman 353, seif katan 5.
[9] [Levush] And they do not face inward, etc. This implies that if they face inward as well, they are treated as part of the private domain even though they also face outward. See what I wrote in the previous seif katan. This requires study.
