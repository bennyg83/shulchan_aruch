16) [Seif 4] Holes in the walls of a private domain, etc. Even if they pass completely through toward the public domain, their law is that of the private domain. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 4; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 7; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 8; Chayei Adam, klal 47, paragraph 7. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, writes that even below ten tefachim they have the status of a private domain. The statement in Shabbos 7b that below ten they belong to the public domain was made only in the initial assumption, contrary to Olas Shabbos. Tosefes Shabbos and Shulchan Aruch HaRav cite him. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8, writes the same in the name of Avodas HaKodesh by Rashba. In the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, however, he writes that if the holes pass through, opening both toward the private and public domains, those below ten tefachim have the law of holes in a public domain, while those above ten are a private domain. He writes that Magen Avraham and Olas Shabbos overlooked this; see there. Nesiv Chaim likewise challenges Magen Avraham and writes that below ten is not a private domain; see there. Biurei HaGra and Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah write the same. The latter adds that Eliyah Rabbah's claim that Rashba disagrees is incorrect, because Rashba discusses holes opening only toward the private domain; see there. See the following paragraph.
17) There, gloss: If they face outward, etc., they are judged by their height and width. If they measure four by four tefachim and are ten tefachim high, they are a private domain. If they measure four by four but are less than ten high, they are a karmelis. If they do not measure four by four, they are an exempt place even if ten tefachim high, provided they are at least three tefachim above the ground. If lower than three, they are treated as joined to the ground and are part of the public domain. Tur; Levush, seif 13; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraphs 8 and 26. See below, seif 13.
18) There, gloss: If they face outward, etc. If these holes are between two courtyards, they are in airspace controlled by both domains. Neither courtyard may use them unless the residents established an eruv. Pis'chei Olam, paragraph 6.
19) If a tree is ten tefachim high and four wide, and a basket hangs from it level with the tree, the basket is considered a hole of the private domain. Even the bottom of the basket, which extends more than three tefachim downward, is a private domain. Although animals pass beneath the basket, regarding holes of a private domain it is immaterial that animals pass beneath. Eruvin 33a and Tosafos there; Rabbi Akiva Eiger; Pis'chei Olam there. See above, siman 336, seif 13, and our words there in paragraph 88.
