[2] Enclosed by partitions, etc. This applies even if the fourth side has neither a lechi nor a beam. On a Torah level, even two partitions and a lechi create a private domain. See siman 313, seif 11. If one makes four corner posts at the four corners, like the boards around wells, it is a private domain d'oraisa.
[3] And it contains, etc. See seif 19, which states that there must be four handbreadths between the partitions, unlike Olat Shabbos, as stated by the Magen Avraham. However, according to the Taz's explanation of the Rambam, that 'between them' means the total span including the walls, perhaps the Shulchan Aruch also holds the thickness of the partitions joins to make four. The Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh, page 9, rules so, but distinguishes that this applies only to sturdy walls that could bear a covering suitable for use. Weak walls unable to support such a covering do not join. According to this, it appears that the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch in seif 19 discuss detached walls. This is evident because the Rashba on page 10 likewise writes that if the interior space is not four wide it is a makom petur, so he must be discussing detached walls there. This also answers the Taz's questions from Perek HaZorek, Shabbos 99, and resolves Rashi's words. Examine carefully.
[4] Four handbreadths wide, etc. I do not know why the Levush omitted the Rama's statement that some require four by four together with its diagonal, as in siman 349, according to Malbushei Yom Tov. In my humble opinion, the Levush correctly omitted it. Rashi in Eruvin 51 makes clear that the requirement of the square and its diagonal applies specifically in siman 349 regarding carrying in a public domain, where there are no fixed corners. Here, four by four suffices. So too it appears from Avodat HaKodesh, page 14b, Rabbenu Yerucham, page 71, the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 14, halachah 6, the Rambam in chapter 12, the Tur, and the unqualified rulings of the authorities.
[5] [Levush] If it is enclosed for residential use, etc. This refers to permission to carry within it. On a Torah level it is still a public domain even if not enclosed for residential use. See siman 346.
[6] [Levush] Locked, etc. This means when all the conditions of a public domain listed in seif 7 are present, and specifically when the two gates are directly opposite each other. If not, the Beit Yosef writes that even if they remain open at night the area is a private domain.
[7] And likewise a mound, etc. A sloped mound that rises ten handbreadths within four amot of horizontal distance is a private domain. If it rises ten within five amot, it is a public domain. If one plants a pole ten handbreadths high in a public domain and places atop it a basket four handbreadths wide, we do not extend the basket's walls downward because goats can pass beneath them. But if the basket itself is ten high and four wide, the space beneath it is also a private domain. A pillar ten high and four wide at the top but not four wide at its base does not render the area beneath a private domain even where goats cannot pass; only its upper surface is a private domain, as stated by the Magen Avraham. Avodat HaKodesh, page 9, writes that if its narrow base is three handbreadths wide, it is treated as a private domain, because when the narrow section has this thickness we extend the walls downward. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 14, writes likewise.
