[14] And some say, etc. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, Yam Shel Shlomo, and Masat Binyamin responsum 92 agree with the first view that an area is a public domain even when six hundred thousand people do not pass through it. The Magen Avraham and Taz are lenient, and responsum 251 of Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon rules likewise. However, where there is an additional basis for leniency, it appears that even the authorities mentioned above permit relying on the latter view. Regarding the Taz's unresolved question from siman 334 in the Beit Yosef, there is no difficulty: the Beit Yosef wrote that there as the first reason because of fear to carry out. I have abbreviated. The Bach distinguishes regarding fields and forests: if roads pass openly through them, they are a public domain; otherwise they are a karmelit.
