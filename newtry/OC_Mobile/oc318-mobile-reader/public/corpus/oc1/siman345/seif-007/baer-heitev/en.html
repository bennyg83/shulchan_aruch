(א) Streets. Roads along which people pass from city to city are reshut ha-rabbim. Magen Avraham:

(ב) Open. Meaning the gates face each other and that road aligned from gate to gate has all the law of reshut ha-rabbim; and one should examine whether there is a distinction between a city of an individual and of the public. Maharival — Magen Avraham; see siman 392:

(ג) Locked. It appears that if one door is locked it also nullifies reshut ha-rabbim since it is not open — Taz. Chakham Tzvi responsum siman 37 wrote: I found Rashba in Sefer Avodat HaKodesh — a great novelty that a platya even if the city gates are locked at night has Torah law of reshut ha-rabbim; it appears his view is specifically in open alleys designated for the alley's residents and not everyone's equal access — but in a platya where everyone's access is equal, even locking a door does not help; I wonder that none of the poskim brought this — end of his words; see there; and see Yad Aharon:

(ד) Sixty. Mishna Berurah siman 392 and Rashal in Beitza siman 8 ruled stringently; however the view of most poskim is to be lenient, and so too in Shulchan Aruch siman 303 seif 18, and likewise Rama in siman 346 seif 3 that all our domains are karmelit — implies like the latter view; and among the public it is customary now that we have no reshut ha-rabbim, which is like the view of "some say" and they are many — therefore one who is stringent should be stringent for himself and cannot protest what is practiced now like those many who are lenient — Taz, Magen Avraham:
