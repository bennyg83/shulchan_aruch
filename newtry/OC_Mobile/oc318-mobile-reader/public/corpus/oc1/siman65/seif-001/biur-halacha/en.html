One who recited it out of order, etc. — know that the author's view [which is Rif and Rambam's view] applies also to shofar blasts in siman 588 seif 2, and to Birkat hamazon in siman 183 seif 6; and likewise to Hallel in siman 422 seif 5, and to Megillah in siman 692 seif 5; and the same applies to all other blessings except prayer: if he tarried in the middle the time to finish it all, he does not return to the beginning, even if the pause was because of compulsion. But in prayer, if he tarried the time to finish it all, even if the pause was not because of compulsion, he returns to the beginning. And the view of "there are those who say" is Tosafot and Rosh's view, that one should not distinguish between prayer and other matters; rather in everything, if he tarried because of compulsion he returns to the beginning [except Birkat hamazon, which does not appear explicitly in their words]. And Darkei Moshe's ruling in siman 422 is that for a rabbinic matter alone, apart from prayer, one may be lenient and rule like Rif not to return to the beginning even if he tarried because of compulsion; but Levush, Beit Chadash, and Eliyahu Rabbah disagree there — see there. And see Magen Avraham in siman 422, who requires returning and reading Hallel without a blessing because of Darkei Moshe's concern; if so, the same applies to Megillah — he should return to read it without a blessing. And in Pri Megadim siman 692 letter 7 he is uncertain there regarding the blessing; it implies that for practical law it is not clear to him like Magen Avraham. If so, in every rabbinic matter, if there is concern of a blessing in vain, one should consider: if he tarried therein the time to finish it all, even under full compulsion, whether he returns to the beginning. And one should consider similarly regarding the blessing of Keriat Shema before or after it, which is also rabbinic, since he already mentioned the Exodus from Egypt in the section of tzitzit. And afterward I found in Nishmat Adam, general rule 5, that he is uncertain regarding all blessings; if so, the same applies to the blessing of Keriat Shema. But from Darkhei Chaim's words in what he wrote regarding Keriat Shema, law 8, it implies that in the blessing of Keriat Shema too the law is like Keriat Shema itself, even though he himself ruled in the laws of Hallel like Magen Avraham that one returns to read it without a blessing; and perhaps he holds that in the blessing of Keriat Shema the Sages equated it to Keriat Shema. And see Magen Avraham siman 183 letter 11, who is uncertain regarding Birkat hamazon: if he tarried therein the time to finish it all because of compulsion, whether he returns to the beginning, because they were lenient in it also regarding what is explained in siman 185 seif 5. However, per the above Darkhei Chaim, that even in the blessing of Keriat Shema, which is rabbinic — and even there, after the fact, if he found excrement opposite him he need not return and bless, as explained in Chayei Adam, general rule 3, law 33 — see there; and nevertheless he returns to the beginning — if so, all the more so in Birkat hamazon, which is from the Torah; and thus ruled Nishmat Adam, general rule 5, law 13, that when the person or place is not fit he returns to the beginning — see there. [Gloss: And indeed Magen Avraham's words are not understood at all, for what Tosafot and Rosh were uncertain about — one who prayed and found excrement opposite him — is not specifically regarding Birkat hamazon; the same applies to all matters apart from Keriat Shema and prayer, as explained in the poskim's words. And nevertheless, Tosafot and Rosh's method regarding tarrying the time to finish it all is known: they do not hold like Rif that this law is only in prayer alone; rather the same applies to Hallel, Megillah, and shofar blasts — if he tarried the time to finish it all because of compulsion he returns; if so, automatically the same applies in other matters. And let the examiner not be troubled: how can compulsion be conceived where the place is not fit in every matter apart from prayer? Per what Shulchan Arukh is uncertain — when he blessed and there was excrement opposite him — and their reasoning that they said this only for prayer; but this is a blessing in God's name: after he blessed he afterward found excrement in his place — on this they are uncertain that perhaps this law was said only regarding prayer, for they decreed and said his prayer is an abomination; and thus it appears in Levush and Shlah. But according to all opinions, every holy matter is forbidden to say in a place that is not clean, even only from urine, and all the more from excrement — end gloss.] And know further: in Birkat hamazon, where they are strict to return to the beginning, this applies even if he tarried between blessing and blessing — for Magen Avraham deals in this manner there. And although regarding the blessing of Keriat Shema they are lenient in this in Mishnah Berurah there — for the blessings do not impede one another, unlike Birkat hamazon, where all three blessings are important as one, as explained in Tur siman 188 and Shulchan Arukh end of seif 66 — see there in Magen Avraham — it seems simple to me that the same applies to Birkat Kohanim: all this law of tarrying the time to finish it all applies; and if the tarrying was because of compulsion he returns to the beginning of Birkat Kohanim. And the measure of "to finish it all" means the essence of the three blessings from beginning to end, without saying Ribono shel olam and the long Yehi ratzon that the congregation says; and it is possible that in places where the kohanim chant while the congregation speaks, this too should be counted within the measure — and this requires study.

Whether through speech — see in Mishnah Berurah what he wrote: even if he chatted intentionally. See Magen Avraham siman 104 note 6, who at first was uncertain in this regarding Keriat Shema or its blessings that perhaps he returns to the beginning, even if he did not tarry the time to finish it all; however in his conclusion he leans there that after the fact he should not return to the beginning, for we judge them as unwitting, since most of the world is not careful — it implies that in places where people are careful, or if he himself said he knew the law so that it is again fully intentional, he returns to the beginning as in prayer according to his view. And thus he ruled in truth in siman 183 note 11 regarding Birkat hamazon — see there. And from the Gra's words in the name of Rashba siman 104 cited above, notes 5 and 6, it implies he returns to the place where he stopped, even when he stopped in the middle of a blessing; and automatically the same applies regarding Birkat hamazon — for although it is from the Torah, how is it stricter than Keriat Shema? And likewise in Eliyahu Rabbah siman 183 regarding Birkat hamazon he wrote that this law of Magen Avraham, who was strict regarding chatting intentionally to return to the beginning, requires study; therefore in my humble opinion it is very difficult to act on this as Magen Avraham's words. And further, in prayer itself there are many views among early and later authorities regarding chatting intentionally; and from the Torah this extends to Birkat hamazon and Keriat Shema, as explained in Pri Megadim — therefore I concluded that after the fact he should not return. Nevertheless, it is proper that regarding the sections of Keriat Shema he repeat them again; but in Birkat hamazon, and all the more in other blessings, he should not return after the fact.

Returns to the beginning — see Mishnah Berurah note 5. And the reason for all these matters is that apparently one should be uncertain in three details. (1) Whether the law that if he tarried the time to finish it all he returns to the beginning applies specifically when he tarried in the middle of a section, or even between section and section. (2) Whether the law of tarrying the time to finish it all applies specifically to Keriat Shema, or even if he tarried in the blessing of Keriat Shema. (3) The law of returning to the beginning — whether he returns also to the blessings, or perhaps since we hold blessings do not impede Keriat Shema and they are a mitzvah in themselves, and there is proof that we bless them even after the time of Keriat Shema has passed — why should he return to the blessings? And the same when he interrupted in the blessing of Keriat Shema, such as in emet ve-yatziv — must he also return to Keriat Shema and the other blessings? Or perhaps blessings with Keriat Shema are two separate matters, for generally they do not impede one another; and the same: if he tarried the time to finish it all in ahavah rabbah, he need not therefore return except to the beginning of this blessing and not to yotzer or, since we hold blessings do not impede one another as stated in Berakhot 12a — if so, apparently each blessing is a matter in itself and is not comparable to prayer, Hallel, and Megillah — why should he return to yotzer or? Behold, the first law I found, with God's help, in Rashba at the beginning of chapter 2 of Berakhot [his words are brought below siman 104 in the Gra's commentary], that it is proven tarrying applies even between section and section. And the second law I found in Darkhei Chaim, who wrote that tarrying applies even in the blessing of Keriat Shema [and there it implies from him that even if he tarried only the time to finish that blessing alone, it is called tarrying the time to finish it all — and we will speak of this below]. And only the third question remains for us; and it seems to me it depends on what is stated in Yerushalmi chapter 2 of Berakhot [brought in the novellae of Rabbenu Asher and other later authorities], and thus: Rabbi Bun bar Chiyya asked: for example, Keriat Shema and its blessings, or Keriat Shema without its blessings, or its blessings without it? And Penei Moshe explained, and thus: that which we say, if one interrupted and tarried the time to finish it all he has not fulfilled and must return to the beginning — what is "it all" that he says? For example, Keriat Shema with its blessings, or it without its blessings, or even the time to finish its blessings without it — end quote. And one depends on the other: if we say one must tarry until the time of Keriat Shema and its blessings specifically, because they are connected to one another — the same applies when he returns to the beginning, he returns also with its blessings. And if we say "to finish it all" means Keriat Shema itself — the same applies when he returns: he need return only to Keriat Shema. And it seems to me: although it is proven from this Yerushalmi that the law of tarrying the time to finish it all is only rabbinic, since it was asked whether with the blessings or not — nevertheless regarding Keriat Shema itself it is proper to be strict, because it is from the Torah and there is no concern of a blessing in vain; unlike regarding the blessings. Therefore, if he tarried in Keriat Shema, even only the time to finish the sections, he must return — and only Keriat Shema alone. But the blessings — even if he tarried in Keriat Shema the time to finish the sections and also the blessings — he need not return, for we may go to leniency and say the blessings are not connected to Keriat Shema. And if he tarried in the blessing of Keriat Shema, he need not return to the beginning unless he tarried the time to finish from the beginning of yotzer or through ga'al Yisrael; for this Yerushalmi asks whether we say "its blessings without it" or not. If so, we may go to leniency that we do not say "its blessings without it," but rather blessings with Keriat Shema are one matter for this — and thus too Chayei Adam, unlike the above Darkhei Chaim. And to which blessing does he return? If he interrupted in ahavah rabbah, he need not return to yotzer or in any case, for perhaps we hold "its blessings without it"; and automatically the same: blessings too have no connection one with another, as we hold generally that they do not impede one another — and he need return only to the beginning of this blessing itself. Only if he stopped in the middle of emet ve-yatziv it appears he should return to Keriat Shema for the above reason; and automatically, if he stopped between blessing and blessing in any manner, he has no return at all for the above reason — as is my humble opinion, with God's help; and accordingly I wrote all these matters in Mishnah Berurah.
