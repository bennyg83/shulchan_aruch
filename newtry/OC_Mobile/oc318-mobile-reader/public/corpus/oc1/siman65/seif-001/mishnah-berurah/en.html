(1) Whether by speech. Even if he spoke intentionally, although he thereby committed a prohibition, he nevertheless need return only to the place where he stopped, even if he stopped in the middle of a blessing. The same applies if he spoke in the middle of Hallel, the Megillah, Bircas HaMazon, or another lengthy blessing. This applies specifically when he recites it himself. If he listens to another person who is discharging his obligation and he speaks in the interim, he consequently does not hear the recitation, so how can he fulfill his obligation? With Hashem's help, this will be explained below in each appropriate place. See there. See Biur Halachah, where we wrote that regarding Krias Shema it is proper to repeat the sections when he spoke intentionally, because some authorities maintain that he must return to the beginning.
(2) Some say that if, etc. Their reasoning is that when he was not compelled, since he could have continued reciting had he wished, the silence, and even speech, does not constitute an interruption. When he was compelled, however, the pause is considered an interruption because he could not recite at that time. See Magen Avraham, who concludes that this applies specifically when the compulsion arose because the person was unfit, such as when he needed to relieve himself, or because the place was unfit, such as when excrement or urine was found there and he had to wait until it was removed. Another form of compulsion, such as robbers or the like, is treated like a voluntary silence and is not considered an interruption according to this view. This applies specifically to Krias Shema, its blessings, and similar recitations. Regarding prayer, however, he returns to the beginning even for another form of compulsion, as will be explained below in siman 104. See there in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 16.
(3) Long enough to complete. This means from beginning to end, even if he is presently near the end.
(4) He returns. All this concerns the law after the fact. Initially, however, one should not interrupt by pausing long enough to complete the entire recitation, even without compulsion, even in complete silence, and even in a matter that is D'Rabbanan, such as the blessings of Krias Shema, Hallel, the Megillah, and the like. Certainly, one must be especially careful regarding prayer, since some authorities are stringent regarding prayer even after the fact. It appears from Magen Avraham that initially it is proper to ensure that the chazzan does not prolong his melody during a blessing of Krias Shema to the extent that a single pause would be long enough to complete the entire recitation, because the congregation would thereby be forced to remain silent for a long time. It appears to me that one must certainly be careful during Bircas Kohanim, which is D'Oraisa, that the congregation not prolong the supplications recited during the blessing, thereby causing the kohanim to remain silent for a long time in the middle of the blessing. It is therefore preferable to follow what I saw written in the name of the Gra, which is also implied by the gloss in siman 128, seif 45, that on the third occasion they should again recite Ribbono Shel Olam, and not the lengthy Yehi Ratzon. It frequently occurs that the kohanim do not sing at all, but merely stand and wait until the congregation finishes its recitation, and the resulting pause can sometimes be long enough to complete the entire blessing. To answer Kaddish or Kedushah, one may pause even long enough to complete the entire recitation. Shulchan Shlomo implies that this applies specifically if he is near the end of the blessing Emes V'Yatziv, namely at Shirah Chadashah, as explained below at the end of siman 66. If he has not yet reached Shirah Chadashah, it is better that he pause intermittently several times in the middle of the blessing, each pause being shorter than the time required to complete the entire recitation.
(5) To the beginning. This means that if he interrupted during Krias Shema, then even if the interruption was only long enough to complete Krias Shema, he returns to the beginning of Krias Shema. During the blessings of Krias Shema, he does not return to the beginning of the blessing in which he stopped unless he paused long enough to recite from the beginning of the blessing Yotzer Or until Ga'al Yisrael. See Biur Halachah, where we explained that if he interrupted between one blessing of Krias Shema and another, he need not repeat the blessing after the fact under any circumstances. Regarding Krias Shema, however, if he interrupted between one section and another because of compulsion, and the pause was long enough to complete all the sections, he returns to the beginning.
(6) This is the accepted practice. The later authorities rule likewise. The same applies to prayer, Hallel, the Megillah, and Bircas Kohanim, as we explained in Biur Halachah. See there.
(7) According to the reader. This applies both leniently and stringently.
