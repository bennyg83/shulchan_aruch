(1) Whether through speaking — even if he chatted intentionally, even though he transgressed a prohibition thereby, nevertheless he need not return except to the place where he stopped, even when he stopped in the middle of a blessing. And the same applies when he chatted in the middle of Hallel, Megillah, Birkat ha-Mazon, or another long blessing — and specifically when he recites them himself; for if he listens to another who fulfills his obligation and he chats in the meantime, he is automatically not listening — how does he fulfill thereby? This will be explained later in each place in its context; see there. And see in Biur Halakha that we wrote that in Keriat Shema it is proper that he repeat the sections again when he chatted intentionally, for there are those who say he must return to the beginning.

(2) "And some say that if..." — Their reason: granted, if he was not compelled, that if he had wanted he could have recited — the silence and even the speech would not be considered an interruption; but in ones, the waiting is considered an interruption since he could not then recite. And see in the Magen Avraham, who concludes that specifically if the ones is because the person is not fit — such as he needed his bodily needs, or the place is not fit — such as he found excrement or urine there and had to wait until they remove it; but another ones, such as on account of bandits and the like, is like one who was silent willingly — it is not considered an interruption according to this view. And specifically in Keriat Shema and its blessings and the like; but in prayer one returns to the beginning even in another ones, as will be explained later in siman 104; see there in Mishnah Berurah note 16.

(3) Long enough to complete — meaning from beginning to end, even if he stands at the end.

(4) He returns — all this is ex post facto; but ab initio one should not interrupt to wait long enough to complete the entire [recitation], even if not due to ones, and even through mere silence, and even in a rabbinic matter such as the blessing of Keriat Shema, Hallel, Megillah, and the like — and all the more so in prayer one must be very careful in this, for some are strict in prayer even ex post facto. And it is implied from the Magen Avraham's words that ab initio it is proper to be careful that the shaliach tzibbur not chant extensively in the blessing of Keriat Shema so that there be one interruption long enough to complete the entire [Shema], for then the congregation will need to interrupt extensively in silence on his account [and in my view, all the more one must be careful in Birkat Kohanim, which is from the Torah, that they not then prolong the congregation's recitation of supplications during Birkat Kohanim, whereby the kohanim are silent a long time in the middle of the blessing — and for this reason it is good to do as I saw written in the name of the Gra (and similarly implied in siman 128, se'if 45 in the gloss) that they also say on the third time Ribono shel olam and not the long Yehi ratzon, for many times it happens that the kohanim do not chant at all but only stand and wait until the congregation finishes their recitation — and thereby there is sometimes time long enough to complete the entire [Shema]]; and to answer Kaddish or Kedushah it is permitted to wait even long enough to complete the entire [recitation]. And it is implied in Sefer Shulchan Shelomo that specifically if he stands at the end of the blessing emet ve-yatziv, i.e., at Shirah Chadashah, as below at the end of siman 66; but if he has not yet reached Shirah Chadashah, it is better that he wait intermittently in the middle of the blessing several times for less than long enough to complete the entire [recitation].

(5) To the beginning — meaning: if he interrupted in Keriat Shema, even if the interruption was only long enough to complete the Keriat Shema, he returns to the beginning of Keriat Shema; and in the blessing of Keriat Shema he does not return to the beginning of the blessing in which he stopped unless he paused long enough to complete from the beginning of the blessing yotzer or through ga'al Yisrael. And see in Biur Halakha that we explained: if he interrupted in the blessing of Keriat Shema between blessing and blessing in any manner, he need not return and bless ex post facto; but in Keriat Shema, if he interrupted between section and section due to ones and paused therein long enough to complete all the sections, he returns to the beginning.

(6) And such is the custom — and thus ruled the later authorities; and the same applies in prayer, Hallel, Megillah, and Birkat Kohanim, as we explained in Biur Halakha; see there.

(7) Per the reader — whether to be lenient or to be strict.
