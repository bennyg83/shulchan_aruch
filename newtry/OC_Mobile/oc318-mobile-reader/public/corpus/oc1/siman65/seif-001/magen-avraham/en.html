"Even if he paused..." — Tosafot and the Mordekhai at the end of Rosh Hashanah wrote: the matter is very difficult, that the congregation waits and is silent until the shaliach tzibbur finishes — except that if they do not wait long enough to complete the entire [recitation] from beginning to end, there is no concern, and they do not calculate from where he stands to the end — end quote. It implies that he holds that even if not due to ones, it is forbidden to interrupt ab initio, even in the blessings of Keriat Shema; see above siman 53. However, to answer Kaddish and Kedushah it is permitted to wait even long enough to complete the entire [recitation]; see siman 66, se'if 9.

"And some say that if..." — It implies from the Tur's and Tosafot's and Rosh's words that even in ones of bandits or coercion one returns to the beginning; and similarly in the responsum of the Rema mi-Fano siman 85, who wrote regarding two people who quarreled together and interrupted — that it is not a devar hadachui, for at any time they could have reconciled — end quote. It implies that if the ones came on account of another matter, such as on account of bandits, one returns to the beginning. But the Bach wrote there that the Tur's view is specifically when the person or place is not fit to recite in it — that is ones; but in another ones one need not return to the beginning. And in Beit Yosef siman 104 he wrote that per the Ravad, specifically where he is a devar hadachui, such as water flowing on his knees — for initially he was not fit to pray since he needed his bodily needs — therefore he returns to the beginning; but otherwise not; and similarly the Maggid Mishneh; and see in the Milchamot there. Afterward I saw in the Rashba's novellae that he explains that a devar hadachui means at the hour when water is flowing on his knees he was not fit to pray — and if so, he holds like Tosafot's explanation; and see siman 99. And in my view, in prayer, if one paused long enough to complete the entire [prayer], even in another ones one returns to the beginning, since the Rif and his supporters hold that one returns even without ones; and moreover, would that a person pray all day long! But in Keriat Shema one does not return unless the person or place is not fit.
