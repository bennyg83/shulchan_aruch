Paused. Initially, it is forbidden to interrupt by pausing, even during the blessings of Krias Shema. See above, beginning of siman 53. Nevertheless, in order to answer Kaddish or Kedushah, one may pause even long enough to complete the entire recitation. See siman 66, seif 9. Magen Avraham.
Compelled. This implies that even if the compulsion was caused by robbers or a coercive official, he returns to the beginning. So too writes Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 85. Bach, however, writes that it is only when the person or the place was unfit for recitation that this is considered compulsion, but in another form of compulsion he need not return to the beginning. Magen Avraham writes: It appears to me that regarding prayer, if he paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, he returns to the beginning even in another form of compulsion, since one may ideally pray all day. But regarding Krias Shema, he should not return unless the person or the place was unfit. See there.
