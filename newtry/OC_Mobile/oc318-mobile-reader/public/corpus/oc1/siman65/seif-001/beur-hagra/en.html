Seif 1: If he recited it, etc. Megillah 18b. See Tosafos there, s.v. nekot, etc.; see also Rosh Hashanah 34b and Berachos 23a, chapter 3, where all agree that if, etc. Rif writes there that this applies specifically to prayer, but regarding Krias Shema, Hallel, the Megillah, and shofar blasts, he returns to the place where he stopped.
Whether by speech, etc. Hagahos Maimoniyos explains that speech does not constitute an interruption, for one may ask and respond to greetings. Were he required to return, such speech would be forbidden, as stated regarding tefillin.
Even if he was, etc. This follows from the challenge raised in Rosh Hashanah there: But Rabbi Abahu, etc.
Some say, etc. They found it difficult to distinguish between all these cases and prayer, and therefore maintain that all are alike. They answer that the case of prayer there concerns an interruption caused by compulsion. They were also troubled by the statement in Rosh Hashanah there, "According to my view," etc., since it is not customary for a teacher to answer a student in that manner when the student asks him a question. Furthermore, how is it known that Rabbi Elazar holds this view? Rather, this is what he said: According to my view, one should not interrupt at all, as stated in Berachos 24b, and therefore I need not return. But you, who did interrupt, according to your own view, since the interruption was caused by compulsion, must return to the beginning. See Tosafos there, 22b, s.v. ela, etc. Ri presents another approach there in Tosafos, that even regarding prayer one need not return, unlike Rif, and even when the interruption was caused by compulsion. This is also the view of Tosafos in Megillah cited above, who state the rule without any distinction. Tosafos in Rosh Hashanah there, s.v. ledidi, present yet another approach, that in all cases one must return even without compulsion. According to all three of their approaches, they do not accept Rif's distinction. See Milchamos Hashem.
We measure, etc. Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 2: Do we measure according to the particular reader, or according to an average person? Rabbi Masnah said: It is logical that we measure according to the reader.
