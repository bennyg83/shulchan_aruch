(M.H. 1) "Even if..." — It implies that he holds that even if not due to ones, etc., even in the blessings of Keriat Shema, etc., for there are two leniencies: the leniency of the Rema, since it was not due to ones; and the leniency of the Rav Beit Yosef, since it is in the blessings of Keriat Shema and not in prayer — nevertheless ab initio it is forbidden to interrupt. However, his words need softening, for in Berakhot 22a it states: The Sages taught: If one was standing in prayer and urine was flowing on his knees, he stops until the water finishes, and returns and prays — to where does he return? And Rashi concludes that all agree: if he paused long enough to complete the entire [prayer], he returns to the beginning. And the Rif wrote: for R. Yochanan said in Rosh Hashanah that one who heard nine tekiot in nine hours of the day has fulfilled — thus he holds that even if one paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation], one does not return to the beginning; and the same applies to Keriat Shema. And specifically in prayer, which is stricter, we say: if he paused long enough to complete the entire [prayer], he returns to the beginning. But Tosafot wrote to distinguish: certainly Keriat Shema, prayer, and tekiah are equal; certainly where one interrupted not due to ones — even if he paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation], he does not return to the beginning but only to the place where he stopped. But if he stopped due to ones — such as water flowing on his knees, which is a devar hadachui who could not pray — then in such a case, if he paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation], he returns to the beginning. And Rav Ovadiah Yosef ruled like the Rif. And the Rema brought the view of Tosafot and the Rosh — except that Tosafot on Rosh Hashanah 34b, s.v. le-didi, hold that these sugyot are divided and one does not distinguish between ones and will, nor between Keriat Shema, tekiot, and prayer. And they wrote initially that it appeared the halakha follows R. Yochanan, who holds regarding tekiah — and similarly regarding prayer — that even if he paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation], he does not return to the beginning. And they concluded: however, since R. Abahu disagrees, it is somewhat plausible to rule like him (that if he paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation], he returns to the beginning), for he is later. And afterward they wrote: "And the matter is very difficult — the congregation waits..." — and therefore one must ask: what proof does the Magen Avraham bring that ab initio it is forbidden even not due to ones? And regarding the blessings of Keriat Shema, from the words of Tosafot — behold, Tosafot on Rosh Hashanah, per their view there, do not distinguish between Keriat Shema and prayer nor between ones and will — and we must say: certainly, if it were clear to Tosafot on Rosh Hashanah that the halakha follows R. Eliezer that if one paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation] he returns to the beginning, they would have properly written "the matter is very difficult..."; but since Tosafot's hands weakened — on the contrary, they first wrote that it appears the halakha follows R. Yochanan that even if he paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation] he does not return to the beginning — only that afterward they wrote that since R. Eliezer disagrees, it is somewhat plausible to rule like him; on this basis one should not infer that the "very difficult" difficulty falls away. On the contrary — how can this difficulty prove from custom that we rule like R. Yochanan as they said initially, that even if he paused long enough to complete the entire [recitation] he does not return to the beginning? One must rather say that they hold that even if we rule that he does not return to the beginning, nevertheless ab initio it is forbidden. From this the Magen Avraham infers for us, who rule like the Rif in Keriat Shema that one does not return to the beginning — and for Tosafot, Rosh, and Rema: if he paused not due to ones, it is ex post facto that he does not return to the beginning — nevertheless ab initio it is forbidden.

"From where he stands..." — meaning: even if one stands in the middle of a blessing, nevertheless they calculate from the beginning of the blessing to the end.
