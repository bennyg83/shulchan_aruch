[1] Some say, etc. Beit Yosef writes that Rashbam wrote this in the name of Raavad. This concludes his words. It appears that the text should read Rashba, for I saw this in Chiddushei Rashba, chapter Mi Shemeso, in the name of Raavad. I am surprised, however, because in Sefer Tamim De'im, siman 240, Raavad cites this view in the name of Baal HaMaor, disputes it, and agrees with Rif, which is the first view. Tosafos write that it is very difficult for the congregation to wait silently until the chazzan finishes. Nevertheless, if they do not pause long enough to complete the entire recitation from beginning to end, there is no concern, and we do not measure from the point where he is standing until the end. This concludes their words. Magen Avraham writes that even when the pause was not caused by compulsion, it is initially forbidden to interrupt, even during a blessing of Krias Shema. Nevertheless, to answer Kaddish or Kedushah, one may pause even long enough to complete the entire recitation.
