Shulchan Arukh seif 1 — because of compulsion. This is the view of Rif: there is no distinction between compulsion or not; rather there is a distinction between prayer and Keriat Shema, Hallel, Megillah, and shofar blasts — in prayer one returns to the beginning and not in all the others. And Tosafot and Rosh, whom Rama cites, hold that prayer and all the others are equal: if he tarried the time to finish it all. The source of that law is in Tosafot Berakhot page 13, and thus: "Rabbenu Tam would wait during a wonder-worker in order to answer Kaddish, and he would hold that even if he tarried to finish it all he does not return to the beginning" — end quote. It implies that Rabbenu Tam holds like the first view here; but per the second view he returns to the beginning — and this is unlike Magen Avraham. And apparently one could say Magen Avraham does not explain Tosafot's words as I explained, because he follows his reasoning in siman 53 note 5 that the dispute here whether one returns to the beginning is only in Keriat Shema, but in the blessing of Keriat Shema all agree one does not return; if so, what is this that they wrote "and he would hold," etc.? Therefore explain Magen Avraham: Tosafot speak regarding ab initio — Rabbenu Tam would wait for Kaddish and would hold it is permitted to wait ab initio even to finish it all; but what Tosafot concluded, "he does not return to the beginning," does not imply that. And further, Magen Avraham in siman 53 speaks only that in rabbinic matters we are not stringent like Ri and Rosh, as Darkei Moshe wrote siman 422, brought in Magen Avraham there note 5; but Tosafot and Rosh themselves are stringent even in rabbinic matters — and this requires study. Nevertheless, for practical law he wrote well: because one who tarried for Kaddish is not considered compulsion according to all of Magen Avraham's rulings, note 2 — examine.
