What the author wrote in se'if 1, and the Mishnah Berurah in the gloss that in ones one returns to the beginning — requires study according to what is written in Turei Zahav in the Magen Avraham: that in a rabbinic matter, even through ones one does not return to the beginning. If so, if we say Keriat Shema is two sections of Torah — behold, the section of tzitzit is not Torah, as the Peri Chadash wrote in siman 67 (and especially since they do not say Shirat ha-Yam) — if so, if he finished "that they multiply" and paused in the section of tzitzit long enough to complete the entire [Shema] from its beginning, he has already finished the Torah sections; and it is possible that through ones one does not return to the beginning; and it is possible not — and it requires study.

"Such as" — Taz: his words need explanation. What is written similarly in Turei David: one who examines Turei David siman 3 will see that from Barukh she-amar until Yishtabach one interrupts, and in the blessings of Keriat Shema one does not interrupt — and similarly the Rambam; except that Turei David there wrote that from what we say it is forbidden to chat from Barukh she-amar until Yishtabach, it implies that mundane speech is permitted for the sake of a mitzvah; and we rule in siman 53 and 54 that between Yishtabach and yotzer mundane speech is forbidden, but to recite charity is permitted — yet from Barukh she-amar until Yishtabach even for a mitzvah matter it is forbidden. And if so, the author ruled from his own reasoning that from Barukh she-amar until Yishtabach one does not interrupt for Shema Yisrael; and therefore there are three laws: if he sits idle, he must not appear as one who does not wish to accept the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven; if he says supplications, it is optional; and from Barukh she-amar, and all the more in the blessings of Keriat Shema, it is forbidden to interrupt. And what he wrote "and the same law" is not entirely equal, for one engaged in supplications has permission and not obligation, since he is engaged in praise of the Omnipresent. And what he wrote from the Gemara Berakhot 20b that Turei David brings proof specifically for one sitting idle — for whoever is engaged in songs and praises has no prohibition; and Taz challenged: if so, that which is asked "let us sing in another chapter" and nevertheless answers "in a matter the congregation is engaged in" — apparently another matter does not help; and one should not say "in another chapter" must mean proper conduct as stated there 22a, that R. Yehuda says one with keri repeats in tractate Derekh Eretz — for otherwise why ask? It is obvious Keriat Shema is preferable; rather he asks why in the public domain it does not appear as accepting the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven — so too let us sing with another's mouth, and answer: a matter the congregation is engaged in. If so, one can say proper conduct — no; service is fine — if so, still why ponder Keriat Shema? Let him ponder other songs and praises. And I did not merit to understand this, for certainly wherever one can only ponder, obviously Keriat Shema is preferable — and that is when one sits completely idle; but if engaged in service, he need not interrupt for Keriat Shema, as Turei David holds who considers "sitting completely idle" — understand.

However, the Bach, may his memory be blessed, inferred from that sugya the opposite of Turei David's words, and I will explain his words according to my ability, with God's help: certainly per the one who says thought is like speech, thought too is forbidden, and Keriat Shema of Torah has no alternative and they permitted thought (see Rashi and Tosafot there, and you will understand that there is apparently a distinction between them); and he asks "let us sing in another chapter" — proper conduct that is permitted on folio 22a — and why did they permit thought? And answers: a matter the congregation is engaged in — thus it is proven not like Turei David; and therefore one can say that "another" is different. However, the plain sense implies that per the one who says thought is not like speech he asks; and behold there are two difficulties: (1) R. Eliezer, who said that one should not sit idle — how does the mishnah state plainly that one with keri must ponder Keriat Shema in his heart? Are we not dealing with one who ponders Torah and praises, who does not need Turei David? And furthermore, why ask "let us sing in another chapter" — proper conduct (Avodat ha-Kodesh) — since thought is not like speech, it is obvious it is preferable to engage in what the congregation engages in; or R. Eliezer too relies on Rav Ada bar Ahavah, that even if pondering other Torah he must ponder Keriat Shema — a matter the congregation engages in; and "sitting idle" means from a matter the congregation engages in, and R. Eliezer understood from "sitting idle" that when he is completely idle he must ponder Keriat Shema — but if engaged in other service he need not ask "let us sing in another chapter," meaning: and what is the conclusion? Are we not dealing with one who reviews through thought another chapter — why ponder Keriat Shema (and according to Maharai's view)? And Rav Ada bar Ahavah answers that even if engaged in service he must interrupt and ponder Keriat Shema specifically — and if so, the conclusion is not like Maharai; this is, in my view, the Bach's intent, see there; and for halakha the Taz and Peri Chadash and Rambam agreed that in the blessings of Keriat Shema one does not interrupt. And what Taz wrote that one fulfills with the first verse — one can say he should intend not to fulfill; and see siman 66, se'if 9 in the gloss regarding the first verse in the morning before Barukh she-amar — and one should not elaborate.
