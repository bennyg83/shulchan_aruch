(There) he wrote in the name of the Rashba that per the Ravad, etc., as will be stated; and his intent is that Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba on the Ravad that in prayer too one does not return to the beginning except in a devar hadachui, like the case of water flowing on his knees. If so, when a scorpion wrapped around him and he stopped — since he is not a devar hadachui, he does not return to the beginning even in the middle of the blessing of Keriat Shema, etc.; see there. And the Magen Avraham explains the Ravad's intent: specifically in that case of water flowing does the Ravad hold it is called a devar hadachui, since initially the person was not fit; but otherwise he does not return to the beginning. But according to what Tosafot wrote, that from their words it appears a devar hadachui means at the hour when it flows he is not fit — if so, one can say that even in other ones one returns to the beginning according to Tosafot's explanation. And again the Magen Avraham wrote that afterward he saw in the Rashba's novellae that he explains it means at the hour when it flows, etc. (see the Rashba's novellae there, where it somewhat appears he holds primarily like this explanation) — and if so, he holds like Tosafot's explanation; and if so, what did the Rashba write according to what the Ravad said, that in prayer too specifically in a devar hadachui, etc. — and that is even according to this explanation of Tosafot, whether the person is fit to recite or not; and if so, in other ones one does not return to the beginning even according to Tosafot and the Rosh, as the Bach wrote per the Tur's view.
