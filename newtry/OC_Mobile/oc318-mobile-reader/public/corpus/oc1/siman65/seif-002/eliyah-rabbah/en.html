[1] Some say, etc. Beit Yosef writes that Rashbam wrote this in the name of Raavad. This concludes his words. It appears that the text should read Rashba, for I saw this in Chiddushei Rashba, chapter Mi Shemeso, in the name of Raavad. I am surprised, however, because in Sefer Tamim De'im, siman 240, Raavad cites this view in the name of Baal HaMaor, disputes it, and agrees with Rif, which is the first view. Tosafos write that it is very difficult for the congregation to wait silently until the chazzan finishes. Nevertheless, if they do not pause long enough to complete the entire recitation from beginning to end, there is no concern, and we do not measure from the point where he is standing until the end. This concludes their words. Magen Avraham writes that even when the pause was not caused by compulsion, it is initially forbidden to interrupt, even during a blessing of Krias Shema. Nevertheless, to answer Kaddish or Kedushah, one may pause even long enough to complete the entire recitation.
[2] The first verse, etc. He should also recite, "Blessed is the Name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever," as stated above in siman 63. Lechem Chamudos writes likewise, and this is also explicit in Tashbetz, siman 241. Levush writes that the choicest fulfillment of the mitzvah is to recite all of Krias Shema. Sefer Tanya writes that the same applies to any other passage the congregation recites, such as Ashrei, and it is proper to recite it with them.
[3] For example, from Baruch She'amar, etc. This requires study, for Beit Yosef derived this from Terumas HaDeshen, siman 63, where it implies that only during Krias Shema and its blessings should one not interrupt, but from Baruch She'amar until Yishtabach one should interrupt. This also appears from Lechem Chamudos, folio 20. Bach is even more lenient and entirely disagrees with Terumas HaDeshen, concluding that even during the blessings before Krias Shema, even in the middle of the blessing, one must interrupt and recite the verse, and he writes, "This is my practice." Taz disagrees with him regarding the blessings of Krias Shema. See there. This also appears to me to be the practical halachah.
