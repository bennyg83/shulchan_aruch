(8) He recited Keriat Shema — and all the more if he has not yet recited, that he must recite the first verse; but he should not intend to fulfill thereby the mitzvah of Keriat Shema, since he has no tefillin on his head and he will also be without blessings, as above at the end of siman 46 in Mishnah Berurah; see there. And in my view, even if he is before Barukh she-amar, since he does not say the verse for the sake of study — only that in this he should be careful not to say more than the first verse.

(9) That they recite Keriat Shema — and the same applies to anything else the congregation says, such as Tehillah le-David or Aleinu: one recites with them, for such is proper conduct.

(10) The first verse — and also in Barukh she-amar; and the Gra's view in his commentary is that one must recite with them the entire Keriat Shema. However, in the verses of song it appears one need not be stricter than the first verse, for otherwise the author's view is to be strict in the verses of song, as below.

(11) He should not interrupt — and the later authorities wrote that in the blessing Barukh she-amar and Yishtabach and in the verses of song one may interrupt to accept the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven with the congregation; but in the blessings of Keriat Shema, and likewise between the sections, one should not interrupt at all but should say those words to the congregation's melody as the congregation says Keriat Shema.
