Such as Maharshal, etc. — so too Tehillah L'David siman 3; and his reason: for we say in chapter "one who reads" that a baal keri thinks Keriat Shema in his heart — Ra'ah said: this teaches thought is not like speech; for if like speech he would utter with his lips. But what — not like speech? Why think? R' Elazar said: so that the whole world should not be engaged with him while he sits idle. From this Tehillah L'David learned: since this one is not sitting idle — behold he is engaged in verses of song — he need not read the first section. And there is prohibition since one may not interrupt there; and in the Gemara there afterward it seems not so, for they challenge "let us study in another chapter" and answer R' Ada bar Ahavah: in a matter the congregation is engaged in — it seems there is a side of superiority since the congregation is engaged in it, and that is necessarily in accepting the yoke of Heaven; otherwise why mention Keriat Shema here? Rather necessarily Keriat Shema has greater superiority; if so, even when engaged in verses of song it is appropriate to interrupt for a matter the congregation is engaged in. Maharshal also wrote proof that one interrupts for verses of song; but he also wrote that one interrupts even in the blessings before Keriat Shema with the congregation — and this does not seem right to me, for we say in the chapter "one who reads" that the first verse is Keriat Shema of R' Yehuda HaNasi; and since he fulfills Keriat Shema through this — how can he interrupt in blessings that belong before Keriat Shema, as we learned "he blesses two before it"? For here in the middle of the blessing he fulfilled Keriat Shema — why should he return and read again? Therefore it appears one may interrupt in verses of song but in the blessings before Keriat Shema one may not interrupt at all for the first verse of Keriat Shema; rather then he says those words in the congregation's melody of Keriat Shema as above.
