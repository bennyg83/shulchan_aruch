"Such as from Barukh she-amar..." — similarly Turei David siman 3; and his reason: for we say in chapter Mi she-meito that one with keri ponders Keriat Shema in his heart; R. Chiyya said: this teaches that thought is not like speech — for if like speech, let him utter with his lips; but if not like speech, why ponder? R. Eliezer said: so that not all the world be engaged in it while he sits idle — from this Turei David learned that since this one is not sitting idle, for he is engaged in the verses of song, he need not recite the first section; and there is a prohibition since one may not interrupt there. And in the Gemara there afterward it appears this is not so, for we ask "let us sing in another chapter" and answer: Rav Ada bar Ahavah — in a matter the congregation is engaged in — it implies there is a side of superiority in this since the congregation is engaged in it; and that is necessarily in accepting the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven — for otherwise why mention Keriat Shema here? Rather necessarily Keriat Shema has greater superiority in it; and if so, even when engaged in the verses of song it is proper to interrupt for a matter the congregation is engaged in. Also Maharach wrote proof that one interrupts in the verses of song — except that he also wrote that even in the blessings before Keriat Shema one interrupts with the congregation for this — and this is not plausible to me, for we say in the Gemara, beginning of chapter Hayah Korei, that the first verse is the Keriat Shema of R. Yehuda the Nasi; and since one fulfills Keriat Shema thereby, how can one interrupt in blessings that belong before Keriat Shema? As we learned: one blesses two before it — for it is not "before it" here, since in the middle of the blessing he fulfilled Keriat Shema — why return and read again afterward? Therefore it appears that in the verses of song one may interrupt, but in the blessings before Keriat Shema one may not interrupt at all for the first verse of Keriat Shema; rather then one says those words to the congregation's melody of Keriat Shema, as above.
