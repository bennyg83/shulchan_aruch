He recited Krias Shema, etc., or interrupted during its recitation.
Regarding the gloss, which states, "Some say that if he was compelled," etc., the gloss upon this explains: But if he was not compelled, then even if he paused long enough to complete the entire recitation, he does not return to the beginning. If he did not pause long enough to complete the entire recitation, all agree that he does not return to the beginning. The same law applies to Hallel and the Megillah. With regard to prayer, however, even if there was no compulsion, if he paused long enough to complete the entire prayer, he returns to the beginning.
For example, from Baruch She'amar onward he should not interrupt, but should say, etc. The gloss upon this explains: Some say that even if he is engaged in the zemiros from Baruch She'amar onward, or even if he is standing in the blessings preceding Krias Shema, even in the middle of a blessing, he must interrupt and recite one verse of Krias Shema with the congregation. This is the ruling of my teacher in Bach. But wherever Chazal prohibited interruption, even if he wishes to speak in Lashon HaKodesh, it is forbidden.
