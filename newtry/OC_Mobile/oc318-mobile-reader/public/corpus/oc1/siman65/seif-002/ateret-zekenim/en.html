One who recited Keriat Shema, etc., or who interrupted during the time of its recitation — in the gloss: and there are those who say that if he was under compulsion, etc. Gloss on this: but if he was not under compulsion, even if he tarried the time to finish it all he does not return to the beginning; and without tarrying the time to finish it all, according to all opinions he does not return to the beginning. The same is the law for Hallel and the Megillah; but regarding prayer, even without compulsion, if he tarried the time to finish it all he returns to the beginning.

For example, from Barukh she-amar and onward he should not interrupt but should say, etc. Gloss on this: and there are those who say that even if he is engaged in psalms from Barukh she-amar and onward — or even if he is standing in the blessings before Keriat Shema, even in the middle of a blessing — he is obligated to interrupt and recite Keriat Shema with the congregation, one verse (my teacher in Beit Chadash). But if he wishes to interrupt in every place where the Sages forbade interrupting, even if he wishes to speak in the holy tongue, it is forbidden.
