(comment 2) And some say if, etc. — it seems, etc., that even in coercion of bandits, etc., since they sealed and wrote simply "coercion."
And in Beit Yosef siman 104 he wrote for Ra'avad: regarding where it says there in prayer one stops because of a scorpion or ox and the like; and when he returns Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in the name of Rashba who wrote according to Rif's view that in prayer if he waited long enough to finish the entire thing he returns to the beginning — the same here if he waited long enough to finish the entire thing he returns to the beginning; but according to Ra'avad's view that in prayer too he does not return to the beginning except in a coerced person like urine dripping on his knees — the same here since he is not a coerced person he does not return but to the place he stopped, etc., end quote. And it is difficult for Magen Avraham: why did he write in siman 104 it is not a coerced person — behold he was repelled because of scorpion or the like; therefore Magen Avraham held Rashba and Ra'avad explain specifically in urine dripping on his knees it is called a coerced person — it is negligence that he should have relieved himself before prayer; and likewise if the place is not clean he should have checked — and this does not apply in this siman 104; therefore he wrote in siman 104 it is not a coerced person and he does not return to the beginning. And if so, coercion of bandits is also not coercion since he was not negligent — he is not a coerced person; and this is Bach's view. But according to Tosafot there a coerced person means he cannot pray while the water drips and not because he was negligent — if so the same for coercion of bandits: he also cannot pray because of bandits; and what difference whether the hindrance is from dripping urine or from bandits — this is Rema's view. Afterward I saw in Rashba's novelae (page 26) who explains (in the name of Ra'avad) a coerced person, etc. — if so he holds like Tosafot's view of coerced person even if he was not negligent it is called coerced person; nevertheless as Ra'avad wrote regarding scorpion and ox and the like it is not called coerced person; therefore one must say he holds: although negligence is not required, nevertheless it is called coerced person only when the coercion comes because of the reader — like this case of dripping urine; not so this case of scorpion where the coercion does not come because of the reader — it is not called coerced person; and if so even according to Tosafot's view who hold even without negligence it is called coerced person — nevertheless one may say they agree with Rashba and Bach that bandits are not called coerced person since the coercion is not because of the reader; and if so he need not return to the beginning. And if so Rema has no support from the early poskim who explicitly hold like him; since one may say Tosafot also hold like Bach; therefore Magen Avraham was in doubt for practice; and through this he wrote "and it appears to me in prayer," etc.
