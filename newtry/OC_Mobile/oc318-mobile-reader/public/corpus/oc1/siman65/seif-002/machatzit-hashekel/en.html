(M.H. 2) "And some say that if..." — It implies etc. that even in ones of bandits, etc., since they concluded and wrote "ones" without qualification.

And in Beit Yosef siman 104 he wrote that per the Ravad, etc., regarding that which is stated there that in prayer one stops on account of a scorpion or ox and the like. And when he returns, the Rav Ovadiah Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba that he wrote per the Rif's view that in prayer, if one paused long enough to complete the entire [prayer], one returns to the beginning — the same applies here: if one paused long enough to complete the entire [Shema], one returns to the beginning. But per the Ravad's view that in prayer too one does not return to the beginning except in the case of a devar hadachui, like the case of water flowing on his knees — so too here, since he is not a devar hadachui, he does not return to the beginning but only to the place where he stopped, etc., end quote. And this is difficult for the Magen Avraham: why did he write that in siman 104 it is not a devar hadachui — behold, he was repelled on account of a scorpion or the like; therefore the Magen Avraham held that the Rashba and Ravad explain specifically in the case of water flowing on his knees it is called a devar hadachui because it is negligence — he should have relieved himself before prayer; and similarly if the place is not clean he should have checked — and this does not apply in this case of siman 104. Therefore he wrote that in siman 104 it is not a devar hadachui and one does not return to the beginning. And if so, the same applies: ones of bandits is not ones, since he did not act negligently it is not a devar hadachui — and this is the Bach's view. But according to Tosafot there, a devar hadachui means that he cannot pray while water is flowing on his knees, not because of negligence — if so, the same applies to ones of bandits, for on account of bandits too he cannot pray; and what difference is there whether the hindrance is from urination or from bandits? — this is the Rema mi-Fano's view. Afterward I saw in the Rashba's novellae (on folio 26) that he explains (in the name of the Ravad) a devar hadachui, etc. — and if so, he holds like Tosafot's explanation of a devar hadachui: even if he did not act negligently it is called a devar hadachui. Nevertheless he wrote in the name of the Ravad that for a scorpion, ox, and the like it is not called a devar hadachui — one must therefore say that he holds: although negligence is not required, nevertheless it is not called a devar hadachui except when the ones comes on account of the body, like this case of urination. Unlike this case of a scorpion, where the ones does not come on account of the body — it is not called a devar hadachui. And if so, even according to Tosafot, who hold that even without negligence it is called a devar hadachui — nevertheless one can say that they agree with the Rashba and Bach that bandits are not called a devar hadachui, since the ones is not on account of the body — and if so, one need not return to the beginning. And if so, the Rema mi-Fano has no early authority who holds explicitly like him, since one can say that Tosafot too hold like the Bach; therefore the Magen Avraham was uncertain in practice and for this reason wrote "and in my view" regarding prayer, etc.
