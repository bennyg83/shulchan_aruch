And what is called enclosed for dwelling? One who built a dwelling house in it, or opened for it a door from his house and afterward enclosed it; and if it was enclosed not for dwelling, he need not breach all of it but should make in it a breach more than ten [amot], and the house will be found open in it without an enclosure, and he returns and closes the entire breach, or one amah more than ten amot — the ten amot are the door and it is open, and in the end it was enclosed; and if he breached one amah and fenced it and returned and breached one amah adjacent to what he fenced and continued thus until he completed ten, it is permitted: (see Tosafos Shabbos):(And if it is difficult for him to breach the wall, some say he may place earth alongside the wall from both sides until the wall is diminished from a height of ten, and the width of the earth should be wider than ten amot in the length of the wall and four in width; and if the height of the earth is not ten, then it is as if he breached the wall there, for there does not remain there ten high above the earth, for it is like the ground of the karpef; and even if he returns afterward and takes the earth from there, since it was nullified there one Shabbat; and some disagree that earth is not nullification unless he will never clear it (Terumat HaDeshen siman 75, Rosh first chapter of Sukkah):.
