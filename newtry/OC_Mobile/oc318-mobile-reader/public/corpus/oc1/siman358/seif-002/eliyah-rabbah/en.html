[7] [Levush] Although it is not a dwelling house, etc. It is also not considered residential merely for that reason, because an area the size of the Mishkan courtyard is permitted without a dwelling house, and some amoraim even permit where the enclosure preceded the dwelling. Rather, this appears to be only a logical distinction, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov.
[8] [Levush] It should be wide, etc. The required breach is four handbreadths along more than ten amot of the wall's length, as written in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 75. The Rama's text should be emended accordingly.
[9] [Levush] And we extend the wall upward, etc. This concept is unnecessary here, because any partition ten handbreadths high is not considered a breach, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov. See seif 6.
[10] Even if he returns, etc. The Rama's wording is similar, but it is incorrect. He must actually remove the material so that the area becomes enclosed for dwelling, as Terumat HaDeshen writes, and only afterward may he return it. Malbushei Yom Tov emends the text to read, 'even though he returns it.'
[11] And some disagree, etc. The Beit Yosef cites Tosafot for this view, and the Bach rules accordingly. I have already written that the Agudah rules this way, and so is the implication of the authorities. Olat Shabbos argues at length that Tosafot wrote this only according to Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava, while Rabbi Akiva would permit, and the Magen Avraham likewise writes that it is permitted. This is incorrect. Tosafot proves that Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava and Rabbi Akiva do not disagree at all regarding an area larger than two seah, and according to both the arrangement does not help. Chiddushei Halachot makes this explicit. Moreover, the Agudah rules so in the name of Tosafot. Olat Shabbos's statement that Chochmat Shlomo emends Tosafot differently does not appear relevant to this point. Hagahot Ashri writes that regarding a large orchard not enclosed for dwelling adjacent to a house, if one builds a dwelling in it he should make a path with reeds spaced less than three handbreadths apart from the doorway of his old house to the doorway of the new house, and may carry through that path. This requires study, because Hagahot Ashri above permits when the enclosure is adjacent to a house. Yet the fact that he built a new house does not itself create a difficulty compared with a guardhouse, because a guardhouse was present before the enclosure, as the Magen Avraham writes. Perhaps the present case is where he explicitly intended not to use the enclosure, or where the adjacent house too was built only after the enclosure.
[12] [Levush] Adjacent to his house, etc. Rabbi Yonatan writes that if an area of two seah contains something like a permanent shepherds' hut or a guardhouse for fields and vineyards used day and night throughout the year, or a dwelling house for the owner even if used only occasionally, or if it is near the city within two thousand amot so that the owner can walk there for recreation, Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava permits it. The gloss rules that each of these conditions permits even more than two seah. The Rama and Levush mention only adjacency to one's house, apparently giving merely one example, as Malbushei Yom Tov implies. The Magen Avraham writes that they omitted a guardhouse and dwelling because everyone agrees those permit, and that the dissenters disagree only regarding adjacency to one's house. He was imprecise. Tosafot, s.v. u'vilvad, in Eruvin 23 makes clear that even a guardhouse and dwelling do not help for more than two seah. The Beit Yosef and Bach write likewise, and I found this explicit in the Agudah there and implied in Sefer Zikaron of the Gaon.
[13] Unless he does not intend, etc. According to this view, the repair using earth can never work. As practical law, however, Terumat HaDeshen appears to permit like the first view, and so too it appears from the Beit Yosef. Moreover, below in siman 633, seif 4, the ruling is stated without qualification accordingly.
[14] [Levush] Except within four amot, etc. Until he reduces the enclosure to the permitted size, as stated by Avodat HaKodesh.
