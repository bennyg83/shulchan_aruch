For dwelling — see Mishna Berurah and Shaarei Teshuvah. In the Mishnah at the chapter's start several matters are explained as enclosed for dwelling: dir, sahir, muktzeh, courtyard, and posts for wells — and in all these, even more than beit se'asayim, it is permitted to move objects; and all are not roofed, yet they are like a dwelling house. Rashi explained the reason: posts for wells, since their water is fit for a person to drink, are superior dwelling use; and dir and sahir, etc., because it is airspace whose use is human dwelling for constant entry and exit. The matter needs explanation in part: in a courtyard the reason fits, for many things needed constantly are placed in a courtyard, therefore it is called dwelling; likewise muktzeh, a wide place behind houses where dwelling needs are also placed, but use is not as constant as a courtyard, as Ritva wrote in the last chapter in Rashi's name: a wide place behind houses is called muktzeh because not used constantly, and wood for storage is placed there. But dir and sahir — what is the reason? It should be said: even though not related to his house, since one enters and exits constantly to inspect manure and incidentally to milk and shear them (see Sheviit chapter 3 in Rashi), it is like his dwelling place. Rashi should say wide place is unlike karpef, for though karpef too stores wood, when karpef is outside the city, when placing wood there his mind is not immediately to use them; conversely behind houses he presumably placed wood intending constant use and enters anytime — therefore dwelling needs. [Yet it is astonishing: wide place behind houses in the Gemara 24 — forbidden except four amot; must be Mishnah deals open then enclosed as conclusion there; otherwise what advantage muktzeh over karpef — karpef too if open then enclosed helps; must be this karpef outside city has no opening, therefore karpef not enclosed for dwelling, wide place enclosed for dwelling because majority opening faces where needed for house — but truly Gemara language "wide place forbidden" and Rabbi Nachman "if opened," etc., implies opposite: majority no opening to wide place, therefore forbidden — requires study on Rashi; see Peri Chadash different explanation in muktzeh.] Know Rabeinu Yonatan wrote: dir with partitions and a house inside for their shepherd — implying dir's reason is human dwelling there, not from Rashi who suffices constant entry to tend them; Ritva: since shepherd stands with them constantly, considered enclosed for dwelling — also as we explained constant tending, not separate dwelling; see Ritva's opening. Know Rashi's view on posts for wells — water fit for drinking is superior dwelling use — not agreed per Tosafos 18 s.v. even for final answer that in such case not enclosed for dwelling — they challenged from ropes and posts lenient only for mitzvah; Ritva 22 agreed; Rashba inclines temporary drinking or watering animals is not dwelling. Main question from Rashi: only human dwelling counts, not animal dwelling — whence? Why not say dir and sahir are designated for animals coming from field and night lodging — not karmelit and reshut ha-rabbim to decree; Rambam chapter 15 mentions nothing requiring human dwelling; Rashba brought Rashi: posts too enclosed for dwelling because animals stand there — though our Rashi stresses water for human drinking; before Rashba this version existed — animal standing counts as dwelling; this view needs no stretch that tending counts as human dwelling — all know not for human dwelling, enclosure for animals; when animals present man must tend — still enclosure not for human dwelling; even Rabeinu Yonatan's shepherd dwelling — why prefer over field-watch dwelling that does not help commentators — we rule below siman 362: what difference field watch and animal watch? Rather for Rabeinu Yonatan also field watch counts as dwelling if watchman sits constantly day and night, as last chapter Mishnah tree canopy — not enclosed for dwelling meaning dwell day and night (Tosafos Shabbos); here watchman sits constantly — all strained since dir and sahir move daily as Rashi in Mishnah — strained to say fixed day-night dwelling for watchman; yet this view fits. Later Noda B'Yehuda OC 47 brought Or Chadash: animal dwelling also counts for more than beit se'ah; Noda Biyehuda debated and questioned our Rabeinu Yonatan point; also wrote no poskim say dir and sahir permitted because shepherd dwells — see there; Noda Biyehuda did not directly attack, only doubted proofs; practical ruling see there; requires study.
