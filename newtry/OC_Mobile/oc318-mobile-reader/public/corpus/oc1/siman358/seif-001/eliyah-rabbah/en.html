[1] [Levush] But they always require, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov criticizes the Levush for not keeping his promise in his second introduction to search the Gemara, for had he done so he would have seen that the Sages did not take a small difference into account in the measurement. He similarly criticizes the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 16 for saying the remainder is five sevenths. But in the Rambam's commentary to Mishnah 2:5 it is explained that five sevenths is according to Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava, while according to Rabbi Akiva the remainder is two thirds, a slightly smaller difference, and the Gemara rules like Rabbi Akiva. Although it explicitly rules against Rabbi Akiva regarding whether guarding is required, if the halachah also rejected him regarding the measurement the Gemara should have said the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava on that point too. End of his words. I am greatly astonished that Malbushei Yom Tov did not see the end of the Rambam's commentary there, where the Rambam explicitly says the halachah follows Rabbi Akiva only in saying that any one of these features suffices, but not in his statement about the exact seventy and remainder. Thus the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava regarding the small added amount, exactly as the Levush and Maggid Mishneh say. The Bartenura rules this explicitly. The Gemara in Eruvin 23 also supports this. When it says that had it taught only that the halachah follows Rabbi Yose we might have thought guarding was still required, it could instead have said we might think the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava regarding the measure, unless in truth it does. Rashi, the Ran, and Tosafot, s.v. Rabbi Akiva, likewise imply that all the tannaim allow a small additional amount. This also explains why the Gemara did not need to state separately that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah ben Bava on this point. So too the Gemara's phrase that both rulings are leniencies proves it; if Rabbi Akiva's measurement also prevailed, that would create a stringency by prohibiting carrying within that small extra area. This is unlike Olat Shabbos.
[2] [Levush] Together it will be 149 amot and two handbreadths, etc. Sixteen times nine amot equals 144 amot. Sixteen times two handbreadths equals thirty-two handbreadths, which are five amot and two handbreadths.
[3] [Levush] Similar to a karmelit, etc. This concerns carrying within it. It is nevertheless permitted to transfer from a private domain into it and vice versa, according to the Mabit. See siman 343.
[4] Enclosed for dwelling, etc. Torat Chaim explains this as requiring explicit intent when enclosing it that one will regularly enter, leave, and use it. The halachic authorities do not appear to require that degree of explicit intent, but rather the conditions explained in seif 3.
[5] More than two, etc. This means specifically a full amah, as stated by the Shulchan Aruch. So too it appears from the Rosh in Perek Osin Pasin, page 23. Nachalat Tzvi overlooked this. There is no need to elaborate.
[6] And he built a dwelling house, etc. The same applies if he enclosed the area with the intention of making within it a house suitable for dwelling, as stated by the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 15. See the Bach and Torat Chaim. The Magen Avraham cites Mabit, seif 48, that if one side of the enclosure consists of houses, their walls count as an enclosure for dwelling even though the houses were built for their interiors, just as the walls of an alleyway count as an enclosure for dwelling. He adds that this is specifically where the opening was made before the enclosure, but if the outer wall of the house fell and only its inner wall remained, that does not help. At first glance this is difficult because the Mabit does not mention an opening. I examined the Mabit itself and saw that he discusses windows from houses opening into the enclosure, after previously discussing doorways as well. If the enclosure was made after those openings, it is certainly permitted. Yet perhaps his intent is even that no opening was made into it, since being enclosed by house walls itself counts as enclosed for dwelling, similar to a city wall which counts as a residential enclosure for a garden. This requires study. Tosafot on page 104 appears to imply that a tzurat hapetach works like a partition.
