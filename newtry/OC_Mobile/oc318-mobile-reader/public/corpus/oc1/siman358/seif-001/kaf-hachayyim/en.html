1) [Se'if 1] Any enclosure that was not enclosed for residential use. This means for the residential use of a person, with continual entry and departure. Rashi on Eruvin 22a, beginning with the words 'Any open area.' Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
2) There. Such as gardens and orchards. They are enclosed to protect the fruit and seeds within them, not for people to reside in them. Rabbeinu Yehonasan writes that the same applies to naturally occurring partitions, such as clefts between rocks and the like, which are not considered enclosed for residential use. Olas Shabbos, subsection 2, cites his words. See below, at the beginning of siman 362.
3) There. Such as gardens and orchards, etc. The same applies to an open area behind houses and to a karpeif, which is a large enclosure into which wood is brought for storage. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there.
4) There. And watchmen's huts. This means a hut used by watchmen of fields. Rashi on Eruvin 15a. Although Rashi explains in Sukkah 8b that it refers to watchmen of the city, here he must explain it as referring to watchmen of fields, as is evident from the Gemara in Eruvin 22a. See there.
5) There. And watchmen's huts. Although the watchman resides within it, it was nevertheless made not for residence but for guarding. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. If the watchman lives in it to guard by day and night, the Bach implies that it is considered a residential building. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1, writes likewise, and this is also implied by the Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 362. See below, subsection 26.
6) There. Which are made only for guarding, etc. Although it is covered by a roof, it is not considered a residence. Rashi on Eruvin 22a, beginning with the words 'And every residence.' See there. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2, writes likewise. However, if a karpeif is covered by a roof, it is permitted to carry throughout it, as stated above in siman 346, subsection 25. See there.
7) There. The Sages prohibited, etc. Under Torah law, these are complete private domains, as stated in Eruvin 67b: Regarding a karpeif larger than beis sasayim that was not enclosed for residential use, even if it measures a kor or even two kor, one who throws an object into it from a public domain is liable. The Tur and Beis Yosef cite this. This applies when the partitions are ten or more handbreadths high. Rambam, beginning of chapter 16. See above, siman 346, subsection 24.
8) There. The Sages prohibited carrying, etc. Know that in a karpeif not enclosed for residential use that is larger than beis sasayim, the prohibition against carrying more than four amos applies in every circumstance, even if it belongs to one person. It certainly applies if two people are partners in a garden and established an eruv, for an eruv does not suffice to render it a private domain. Mateh Yehudah, siman 336, subsection 2. See below, subsection 89.
9) There. If it is larger than beis sasayim. Ritva writes in his novellae to Eruvin 16b that just as it is stated there regarding a vertical partition without horizontal members that if there are three people, who are deemed a caravan, they are granted all the space they require, even more than six se'ah, the same applies to a karpeif that was not enclosed for residential use. The restriction applies specifically when it belongs to one person, but a caravan is granted all the space it requires, even more than beis sasayim. See there. Shulchan Shlomo cites his words. However, see below, siman 380, subsection 13, where we cite a dispute regarding this matter. See there.
10) There. If it is larger than beis sasayim, in which case it is called a karmelis. If it is exactly beis sasayim, however, it is called a karpeif. It constitutes a single domain with the courtyard regarding utensils that began Shabbos within them, but not regarding utensils that began Shabbos within the house, as stated in siman 372. Taz, subsection 1. This applies specifically to carrying from it to a courtyard, but carrying from it to a karmelis is forbidden, as stated above in siman 346, subsection 26. See there, and see below, subsection 13.
11) There. But if it is beis sasayim, etc. Even if there are no watchmen in it and it never contained a dwelling, it is permitted, like the courtyard of the Mishkan, which measured beis sasayim and contained no dwelling but was merely enclosed by curtains. Tur.
12) There. Which is a measure of seventy amos and four handbreadths, etc. There is also an additional minute amount, equal to the width of a finger. The Sages did not take care to mention it because it is small, but it is always needed to complete the measure. Levush. This is also stated by Rambam in his Commentary on the Mishnah, chapter 2 of Eruvin. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, writes in the name of several authorities that this is the halachah and that one may include this minute amount in the calculation. See there. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 2, writes likewise. Consequently, wherever the enclosure does not exceed seventy amos, four handbreadths, and that minute amount, it is permitted to carry throughout it.
13) There. Which is a measure of seventy amos, etc. If it exceeds beis sasayim by even the slightest amount, it is forbidden to carry within it except within four amos. It is likewise forbidden to carry from a courtyard into it or from it into a courtyard. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3. Although the calculation is not exact, the slight discrepancy was disregarded in the direction of stringency. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. See above, siman 346, subsection 26.
14) There. Or long and narrow. For example, it is one hundred amos long and fifty amos wide, which is equivalent in area to a square measuring seventy amos and four handbreadths on each side. Magen Avraham, subsection 2.
