(S.k. 8) Blood, etc. The Lechem Chamudos wrote, etc., that Tosafot hold so. This is because we say in Pesachim, folio 115: Rabbi Elazar said in the name of Rabbi Oshaya: Anything dipped in liquid requires netilas yadayim. Rashi explained the reason as the Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 6. According to his words, they would require a blessing just like bread. We then say afterward: Rav Pappa said: Learn from this that one must dip the entire maror in charoses; for if not so, why must they wash their hands before eating the maror, since he does not touch the liquids? It rejects this: We are concerned lest they touch. Tosafot challenged Rashi's explanation: If the law of something dipped in liquid has the law of bread, what was Rav Pappa's initial thought, that he did not conclude that we decree lest one touch? For with bread we rule below, siman 163, seif 2, that if his fellow feeds him and the eater does not touch the food, nevertheless the eater requires washing, lest he touch. Therefore they explained that certainly the washing for something dipped in liquid is not because of sanctity as with bread, for there it is because of the trace of terumah, unlike something dipped in liquid. And what difference does it make if the liquids and the food become impure? Rather, the reason is that since the liquids on the food are impure and he will eat them, they will make his body impure, for this is among the eighteen decrees: one who eats impure foods or drinks impure liquids, his body becomes impure. They were careful regarding purity. According to this, one does not recite a blessing on the washing. This also works well, for Rav Pappa initially thought that we do not decree regarding them lest he touch, since the washing is not because of sanctity. They concluded with these words: All the more so for us, who are not careful to avoid making ourselves impure or eating impure foods, we do not need that washing, so one should not recite the blessing of netilas yadayim. End quote. Thus they wrote explicitly that we do not need that washing.
