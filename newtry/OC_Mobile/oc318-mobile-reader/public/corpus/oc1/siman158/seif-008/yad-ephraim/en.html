(Magen Avraham, s.k. 6 and 15) Such as one who exits the bathroom, etc. Then we are not particular that it be before performing the act, since the person is not yet fit. See above, siman 8, Taz, s.k. 7. Because of this, the practice in all washings is to recite the blessing before he takes second waters, not before drying, and this is also called before performing the act. Although when he pours a revi'is he does not require drying and also does not require second waters, as stated in siman 162, nevertheless they did not distinguish, as the Tur wrote. In truth, this requires study: how does it apply here to say that they did not distinguish, thereby explaining why the practice is lenient not to bless before performing the act? According to what the Tur wrote, that sometimes his hands are not clean, they instituted that he always bless afterward, and it works well, since the enactment of the Sages was so from the beginning. But according to what the Beis Yosef wrote there, that certainly according to the law he blesses beforehand, only that for this reason the world practiced this way, and based on this he wrote here in the Shulchan Aruch, it requires study. This is the intent of the Taz in s.k. 12, who wrote that according to the first reason written by the Tur, that his hands are not clean, it is satisfactory. That is to say, what the Tur wrote, that for this reason they instituted it so, works well. But according to what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, that they practiced so for this reason, if so one must say that even after washing it is called before performing the act because it is before the second waters or before drying. For if not, how could they have practiced against the law? If so, in those washings that do not require drying, he should have to recite the blessing before washing, and it does not apply to say that they did not distinguish in order to be lenient.
