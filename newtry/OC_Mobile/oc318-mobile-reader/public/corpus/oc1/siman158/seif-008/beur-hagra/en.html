Seif 8, one who was, etc. He derived this from the end of the first chapter of Eruvin, regarding four matters, etc. But it is not compelling, as Rabbeinu Yehonasan wrote there: this applies specifically to a camp going out to war, even for an optional war, but not to a caravan traveling from place to place, for the law of a caravan regarding these four matters is like the law of an individual standing in his house, end quote. This is as written in the Gemara there, and as written in the Tosefta at the beginning of the first chapter of Eruvin: tents in a caravan are obligated in eruv, while in a camp they are exempt from eruv. This means a camp going out to war, as the Maggid Mishneh wrote in the first chapter there. Even according to the Ramban and Rashba, who did not write that it refers to war, nevertheless they wrote that it applies specifically with ten, as written in the Yerushalmi. Nevertheless, the words of Rabbeinu Yehonasan are primary. Even according to the Rambam, who wrote the opposite, that in a camp they are obligated and in a caravan they are exempt, like the version of the Yerushalmi, nevertheless he maintains that travelers are obligated, and they exempted only in war, as he wrote in Hilchos Melachim. So too regarding netilas yadayim, as written in the third chapter of Pesachim, and derive from there regarding netilas yadayim, four mil, etc.
