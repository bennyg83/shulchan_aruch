8) [Seif 2] Some say that if he eats less than an egg's volume, etc. The Beit Yosef explains the reason: since less than an egg's volume does not contract food impurity, one can say that the Sages did not require washing for it. Alternatively, one can say that they required washing without qualification for anyone who eats bread and did not distinguish between less than an egg's volume and more than an egg's volume. Therefore, he should wash because of the doubt but should not recite a blessing. The Taz, paragraph 3, and Olas Tamid, paragraph 3, cite him. For this reason, the Shulchan Aruch presents this law as 'some say,' since either possibility can be maintained, and rules that one should not recite a blessing because a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently. Since the reason that less than an egg's volume does not require washing is that it does not contract food impurity, it follows that the egg's volume that requires washing is an egg's volume without its shell, for the minimum measure for food impurity is an egg's volume without its shell, as the Rambam, of blessed memory, writes in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tumas Ochlin; see there. Dagul Mervavah writes the same; see his Sefer Noda B'Yehuda, Orach Chayyim, siman 38. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, and Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 9, write the same. Chok Yaakov in siman 472, paragraph 18, writes in the name of Maharil that two eggshells hold the volume of one and one-half eggs with their shells. It follows that the volume of one egg without its shell equals three-quarters of an egg with its shell. Noda B'Yehuda there writes the same. According to what we wrote above in siman 81, paragraph 3, in the name of the Rambam, that the volume of an egg with its shell is eighteen dirhams of water, the volume of an egg without its shell that requires netilas yadayim is thirteen and one-half dirhams. Therefore, when possible, one should eat this amount or more so that he may recite the blessing over washing according to all opinions and avoid a doubt concerning blessings.
