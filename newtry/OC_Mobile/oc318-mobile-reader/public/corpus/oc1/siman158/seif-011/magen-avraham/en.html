Because sometimes there is no, etc. For example, one who has just left the privy. For this reason the custom is to recite the blessing after every washing. Although when one pours a revi'is at once drying is unnecessary, the Sages did not distinguish between cases, as the Tur writes.
He recites the blessing afterward. This differs from other blessings that may not be recited afterward, because there we follow the rule that once something was fit and then rejected, it does not become fit again, as written in siman 167, seif 8. Here, however, he was initially unfit to recite the blessing, so he may recite it afterward. See what I wrote in siman 432, seif 1. Yam Shel Shlomo to Chullin writes that if he already recited Hamotzi, he no longer recites Al Netilas Yadayim. Tanya states: If one washed his hands and does not know whether he recited the blessing, he does not recite it again. I found in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon that if one erred, failed to recite Al Netilas Yadayim, and already ate bread, he should not recite the blessing afterward.
