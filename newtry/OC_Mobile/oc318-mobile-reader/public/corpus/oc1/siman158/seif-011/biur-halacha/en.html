He recites the blessing before washing, etc. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies that one who wishes to recite the blessing before washing may do so if his hands are clean. Beit Yosef likewise rules this way, unlike the implication of the Tur that one should not deviate from the usual order. Peri Megadim writes that blessing before the washing may perhaps be considered even more fully prior to performing the mitzvah. However, Or Zarua cites Sefer HaMiktzo’os in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, and likewise the Geonim who were his teachers, that one recites the blessing and afterward washes his hands. He then cites Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that the blessing is recited only afterward. The view of Sefer HaMiktzo’os also appears to be Rambam’s view, for in chapter 8, halachah 1, he lists the order as: he recites the blessing over washing the hands and washes them. Likewise in halachah 6 he writes that he recites the blessing over washing the hands and washes them a second time. His plain wording indicates that he recites the blessing first and then washes. It is therefore reasonable that the law follows Beit Yosef, and one should not protest against a person who wishes to recite the blessing before washing when he knows that his hands are clean. He must only ensure that the place is clean and fit for reciting a blessing.
He recites the blessing afterward. See Mishnah Berurah, where I cited the poskim that after one has recited HaMotzi he no longer recites the blessing over washing. They explain that the primary purpose of washing is the meal, and once he has begun eating, the blessing over washing is no longer applicable. Shulchan Aruch HaRav implies that this is so even if he has not yet actually eaten. It appears, however, that if he forgot and recited HaMotzi before drying his hands, he may still recite the blessing over washing afterward, since the washing has not yet been fully completed. One who eats without drying is regarded as though he eats impure bread. This is also evident below, siman 167, seif 37. Although that case concerns one who heard HaMotzi from another person, we maintain that hearing is equivalent to reciting.
