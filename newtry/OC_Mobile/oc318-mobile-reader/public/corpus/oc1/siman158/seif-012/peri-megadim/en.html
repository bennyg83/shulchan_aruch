That also. See Taz. Magen Avraham, letter 15, also gives the answer that no distinction was made; see there. Taz was not satisfied with this. According to the first reason, the custom to bless after the first washing but before the second water is indeed still prior to completion of the act. According to the second reason, however, one blesses after the second water but before drying. In a case of immersion, or when pouring a revi’is all at once, the act has already been completely performed. It is therefore difficult to say that one should regularly bless afterward merely because no distinction was made. Although one who forgot may still bless afterward, this does not justify establishing such a practice initially. As for the Tur’s statement about blessing after drying, one may say that the Tur speaks only of the preferred practice, which should be prior to completion, while after the fact the blessing may still be recited, as Magen Avraham writes. Taz’s difficulty from siman 168 is therefore not compelling, for there the opportunity appeared and was then rejected, and so forth. Hagahos Ashri in the first chapter of Berachos, cited here by Darchei Moshe, distinguishes between mitzvos, for which one may bless afterward, and blessings over benefit, for which one may not. Shach cites this in Yoreh Deah 19:3. Accordingly, there is no difficulty from siman 168. Nevertheless, Magen Avraham here and Shach there conclude that even for mitzvos one does not bless afterward because in a doubt regarding blessings we rule leniently. There is, however, a remedy: he may relieve himself or touch a normally covered part of his body, then wash and bless. See siman 164, Taz and Magen Avraham, letters 5 and 7. This creates a combination of doubts and permits the blessing even before eating. It may be that Maharshal there also agrees in this case. I did not understand the statement concerning hands that remained clean without touching anything. There, regarding one who washes in the morning with a stipulation and keeps his hands covered, it is considered as though he did not divert his attention. Nevertheless, according to our ruling that eating with a cloth is forbidden, as in siman 163, he should bless after washing and before eating. The phrase “over washing the hands” can at times also refer to an act already performed. This requires study.
