For drying as well, etc. According to this reason, there is a difficulty, for there are many washings that do not require drying, as stated in the following seif. According to the first reason cited by the Tur, however, namely that his hands are not clean, the matter is understandable. Rama writes that if one forgot to recite the blessing until after drying, he may recite it afterward. This implies that he may not recite it after the blessing of Hamotzi. Maharshal writes in chapter Kol HaBasar: “If he forgot to recite the blessing until after drying, I wrote in the first chapter of Pesachim, siman 2, that he may recite it before Hamotzi, but not afterward.” I do not know his proof. In my humble opinion, however, it appears far more likely that once he has completed fulfillment of the mitzvah, namely the drying, he should no longer recite the blessing. All mitzvos require the blessing to be recited before their performance. In a mitzvah whose fulfillment extends over a period of time, if one forgot to recite the blessing before beginning, there remains time to recite it while he is still engaged in the mitzvah. Once the period of fulfillment has ended, however, he may no longer recite the blessing. For this reason, the Tur wrote that he should recite the blessing before drying, and did not write that he may do so even afterward. Proof may be brought from siman 167, seif 8, which states that if one did not remember until after completing his meal, he does not recite Hamotzi. One cannot answer that here is different because he has not yet eaten, for eating may be done with clean hands without touching the food, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 164 in the name of Kol Bo, that if one placed coverings over his hands he need not wash afterward. Moreover, if the blessing could still be recited because of the eating, its text should have been, “Who commanded us to eat with clean hands,” since the mitzvah of washing has already passed. Nor can proof be brought from Rama in siman 172, who rules that one expels the liquids from his mouth and recites the blessing. That case is different because he remembered while the liquids were still in his mouth, as the Rosh writes there and Beit Yosef cites. Indeed, that passage itself provides proof, for the Gemara says there, “Once it was deferred, it was deferred.” The same applies here: once the blessing was deferred from before drying, it is entirely deferred.
