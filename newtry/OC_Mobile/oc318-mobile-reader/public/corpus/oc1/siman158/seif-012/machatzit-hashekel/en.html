(S.k. 12) There is one, etc. Who washed one hand for drinking, for their practice was that when they brought them a cup of wine to drink before eating, they would wash one hand because of cleanliness, as is stated in Berachos, folio 43. When he later washes his hands for eating, he must wash both hands.
And the Nachalas Tzvi did not understand, etc. The language of the Darchei Moshe misled him, etc., for it implies that the Beis Yosef's proof that washing not done for the sake of sanctity does not count is from another place in Tosafot there. Namely, we say there that although he washed his hands on Pesach night for the first dipping, he must again wash his hands for the second dipping of maror. It asks: Why? Since he already washed his hands for the first dipping. It answers: Since he must say the Haggadah and Hallel, perhaps he became distracted and touched a filthy place. Tosafot wrote, s.v. hesach hadaas, as follows: Why is this reason needed? Let him say that the first washing was not for the sake of sanctity, but only so that he should not render the liquids impure. For they follow their view regarding the reason for washing for something dipped in liquid, unlike Rashi, as I wrote above in s.k. 8. They also follow their view that less than an egg-bulk receives impurity according to the Torah and requires washing. Since one must eat matzah before maror, he requires washing because of the bread, which is because of sanctity, and the first washing does not count. They answered: One can say that the initial assumption was that since he washed, it should help even for bread, for chullin does not require intention. Therefore this reason was necessary. End quote. According to the Beis Yosef, who holds that the first washing does not count for bread, it is difficult: Although the questioner initially assumed that since chullin does not require intention, etc., nevertheless the answerer should have answered that the first washing does not count. One must say that the answerer answered even according to the questioner's view, that chullin does not require intention, and even so washing is required because perhaps he became distracted. Although this is not a difficulty against the Beis Yosef, who holds that the first washing does not count for bread, nevertheless there is no proof here. On the contrary, the simple implication is that since he needed to answer perhaps he became distracted, the questioner's assumption remains, that chullin does not require intention and the first washing would have counted were it not for the concern of distraction. To this the Magen Avraham wrote that the Beis Yosef's proof is from what Tosafot wrote, s.v. kol shetibulo, etc., as the Magen Avraham wrote. Accordingly, one must say that what it answered, that we are concerned that through Haggadah and Hallel his mind was diverted, was answered according to the words of the questioner. See Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 85.
