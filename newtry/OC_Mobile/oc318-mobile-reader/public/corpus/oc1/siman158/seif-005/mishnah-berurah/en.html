(22) This is among the arrogant. He presents himself as exceedingly meticulous in a matter that does not require it, for the Sages did not enact washing for fruit at all, as explained in seif katan 2; see there. Know that the prohibition against treating it as an obligation applies even if he does not recite a blessing over this washing, since he washes for the sake of the mitzvah of washing.
(23) His hands were not clean. See the Acharonim, who agree that even if he knows of no dirt or tumah on them but wishes to be stringent and wash out of respect for the blessing, this is permitted. Rama specifies that his hands were not clean to teach that even where he is certainly required by law to wash them for the blessing, he should not wash in the manner of obligatory netilah, but merely as ordinary cleansing.
(24) There is an opinion that it appears, etc. The Acharonim write that all agree that liquid exuding from meat is not considered a beverage. If, however, water with which the meat was rinsed before roasting remains moist upon it, it is certainly considered liquid. Ordinarily roasted meat is not wet from that water, because it dries during roasting. The Gra holds that if it was rinsed with water before roasting, even the liquid later exuding from the meat has the status of a beverage.
(25) And they are dry. Meaning that the sauce upon them has dried. If they remain moist from the sauce, they are included in the law of food dipped in liquid and require washing.
(26) Its law is like fruit. The Acharonim agree that cooked foods, which are not normally touched by hand but eaten with a spoon, do not require washing. Even if one happens to touch the food while it is in the spoon, washing is still unnecessary, and this is the practice. Something normally touched by hand, however, requires washing even if one eats it with a utensil. The Acharonim dispute whether fruit cooked in water requires washing. Therefore, if liquid remains wet upon it, it is proper to be stringent and eat it with a spoon. Regarding pickled foods, such as cucumbers and the like, it is certainly proper to be stringent and wash unless the water upon them has dried. Derech HaChayim writes that one who eats lekach to temper a drink and dips the lekach in spirits is not considered to be eating food dipped in liquid, because the spirits are vapor derived from grain and are treated as fruit juice. Even if the spirits are diluted with water, the water is a minority and washing is unnecessary. This implies that if water constitutes the majority, according to the proof level, it is included among the liquids. If the lekach is less than an olive-sized amount, it appears that one may be lenient, as written above in Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 10.
