For fruit. Terumah of fruit is D'Rabbanan, and therefore the Sages did not decree washing for ordinary fruit. Although a blessing must be recited, hands are presumed fit for a blessing unless one knows that he touched a filthy place, as written at the end of siman 4. Rashba, siman 153. See siman 92, seif 5.
When the moisture is wet enough to moisten. It appears to me that all agree in this case. Tosafos to Chullin 33 write that roasted meat may be eaten with unclean hands because it does not require rinsing. This implies that its own moisture does not render it susceptible, because it is not called a liquid. Moreover, the Gemara plainly states that only blood of slaughter renders food susceptible. If actual water remains wet upon it, it is obviously considered liquid, but roasted meat is not ordinarily wet with water. Maharshal writes that even when liquid remains upon a cooked dish, washing is unnecessary, because the decree applies only to food that must be dipped in liquid, not to its own moisture. Rabbi Yom Tov, part 2, siman 14, writes similarly in the name of his teacher, since after cooking it is considered food, though he expresses doubt. Rashi to Berachos 52a writes that the liquid of a cooked dish has the law of food. It appears to me that this applies specifically to the dish's own moisture, but actual gravy is considered liquid. This also appears from the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 3 of Taharos; see the comments of Rash and Rabbi Yehudah. Rashba writes in Toras HaBayis that only dry fruits, meat, fish, and similar items do not require washing, but anything containing liquid or dipped in liquid does require washing. In the abridged work he similarly writes: or items soaked in water and still moist.
