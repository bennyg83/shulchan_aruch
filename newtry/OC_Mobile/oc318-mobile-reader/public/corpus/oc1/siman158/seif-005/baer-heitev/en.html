Of wheat. The Maharshal wrote that in all these cases, even if liquid is moist upon them, it is not called dipping in liquid. They decreed only regarding something that must be dipped, but not regarding its own moisture. The Taz disagrees and wrote that even because of its own moisture it requires netilas yadayim, meaning because of water or another liquid from those seven that is on the fruits; but moisture from their own juice does not help, for they are not of the seven liquids. Based on this, it appears to me that cooked wheat also speaks specifically when it is dried from the water in which it was cooked. But regarding roasting, it appears to me that even if the meat was rinsed in water before roasting, nevertheless at the time of roasting that moisture dries, and juice comes out of the meat; it is from the moisture of the meat and is not called a liquid. He further wrote: It appears to me that when types of dough are cooked in water, even if there is broth there, the dough does not require washing. This is similar to what the Beis Yosef wrote regarding drinking, that one does not require washing because it is not usual for one to touch the liquids inside the cup, etc. So too here, it is not usual for one to touch that dough with his hand, but rather he eats with a spoon from the bowl. However, if it is dough fried in fat, it appears to me that one must distinguish: if it is fried in butter, it requires washing, for butter is included in milk and is one of the seven liquids; but if it is fried in goose fat or meat fat, it does not require washing, for it is not one of the seven liquids.
