[5] A thing whose dipping, etc. — but a thing that is not its way to be dipped or to have liquid upon it — even if he dipped it, his mind is nullified regarding everyone; and likewise when he inserts his finger into oil or honey and sucks — he does not require washing, Radbaz siman 78. And the Taz wrote: one who eats honey requires washing, for he acts so that his hand touches the honey. The Shlah wrote (daf 81): and for a confection made from honey — washing is required; and in Berachot HaZevach daf 31 in Menachot it is written that one need not wash once it has hardened; and likewise Radbaz wrote siman 101 — even if it hardened through cold; but if they put a little in it and they still flow, it is a liquid. And Magen Avraham wrote permission, since it stands for eating; and the Taz rejected this, since once it is made into honey it becomes an act of liquids — thought of eating does not remove it from being an act. And in Kenesset HaGedolah and She'eirei Kenesset HaGedolah they brought poskim that a thing dipped in cooked liquid does not require washing unless the liquid was not upon it alive but only that after cooking it came out of it; and he concluded that he would tell questioners regarding cooked food: I cannot decide — do as your heart desires. And Olat Tamid wrote that pickled is like cooked also in this. And behold it is clear that what comes out of it after cooking — for everyone it is a liquid; and this is not like Rashal, whom the Taz and Magen Avraham brought in s.k. 10; however specifically if what comes out is one of the seven liquids. And in Derishah he wrote that it is implied it is not yet in water but still touches from the liquid of the cooked food — netilat yadayim is required; and according to this he should wash for okra or for salad even if they are not in water and vinegar; but for plums and fruit cooking it appears that even if they are still in their broth, washing is not required at all, for we bless on the broth "Who creates fruit of the tree" — it is found there is no name of liquid on the broth; and likewise on types of confections fried in honey — we say the honey is nullified regarding the confection and we bless on them "Who creates fruit of the ground," just as the thing is nullified regarding berachah — possibly it is also nullified regarding washing, unless the confection is steeped in honey and one takes the confection from within the honey with the hands; but when taking with a knife or vessel one need not be stringent — until here. And She'eirei Kenesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Radbaz: a thing dipped in liquid — even if he ate it by means of a utensil, washing is required; however in cooked liquid, even for one who is stringent, there is room to be lenient in this, and this agrees somewhat with Derishah. Magen Avraham wrote that it is implied in Kesef Mishnah ch. 1 of Hilchot Tum'at Ochlin that also oil and milk and fat that hardened are not liquids even if they returned and melted, except wine — until here; and likewise it is implied at the beginning of ch. 3 of Taharot; but in Rambam and in Rif there it is clear that also oil when melted is a liquid; and likewise Rambam wrote ch. 9 of Hilchot Tum'at Ochlin. And see in Berachot HaZevach Menachot there: he wrote that Rambam ch. 1 — snow is neither food nor liquid; he considered it a liquid to become impure with impurity of liquids.
