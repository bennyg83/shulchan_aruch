Seif 5, one who washes, etc. This follows Rav Nachman, for the sugya in the second chapter of Chagigah is in accordance with him.
And specifically, etc. Tosafot in Chagigah there, s.v. one who washes, etc.
And meat. Etc. See Tosafot to Chullin 107b, s.v. all of them, etc. So too the Rashba wrote, and he did not establish it as a case where brine is moist upon it. And there, 33a, s.v. and they are eaten, etc. It is obvious that brine is not considered a liquid, for even blood of extraction does not render food susceptible to tumah, only blood of slaughter, as written in the first chapter of Pesachim, the second chapter of Chullin, and the sixth chapter of Machshirin.
And a cooked dish, etc. That is to say, this does not refer specifically to fruits, but to anything that is not bread, as written in seif 1. And they are dry. This is specifically so. See Rabbeinu Shimshon, seventh chapter of Mikvaos, Mishnah 2, in the Yerushalmi, and see Beis Yosef. He maintains that gravy is considered a liquid, as written at the beginning of the third chapter of Taharos. Even though in Yoma, chapter 8, we say regarding brine that is upon, etc., according to the one who says, etc., behold, in Chullin 120a we say: there, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. and he answers, etc.: but here, etc. Rashi needed this, even though it comes out of the meat, as he wrote there, s.v. fat, etc.; nevertheless, since it comes out through cooking in water, it is a liquid, like sweat at the beginning of the second chapter of Machshirin. This is unlike the Taz and Magen Avraham, who wrote that liquid that is moist from itself is not considered liquid. The roasted item mentioned above is because it does not require rinsing at all. The words of Rashi in Berachos 52a, end of s.v. there is no, require study, even according to the Magen Avraham's explanation; for according to him, is there no concern for gravy?
