And a cooked dish. See Taz and Magen Avraham, letter 10. One may say that in a cooked dish the liquid is considered part of the food. This will be explained there, with Hashem’s help. What he writes regarding food fried in butter means that even though congealed milk is considered food when one designates it as such, when it melts it has the status of liquid. See Rambam, chapter 8, halachah 2, and chapter 9, halachah 2, of Hilchos Tumas Ochlin. Accordingly, one who eats a dough dish with solid butter would not require washing. Magen Avraham, letter 7, however, implies that milk which later melts still does not acquire the status of liquid. If so, perhaps the same applies to butter that was coagulated through a deliberate manufacturing process. This will be explained there, with Hashem’s help.
