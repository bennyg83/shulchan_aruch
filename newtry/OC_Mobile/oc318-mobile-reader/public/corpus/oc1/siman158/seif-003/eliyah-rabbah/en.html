[3] He shall wash his hands, etc. — out of doubt; thus the Rokeach wrote in siman 328, for it is implied thus in Chullin daf 107. In Nachalat Tzvi he wondered about the Rokeach, for it is stated in the Gemara: what is the law of eating with a cloth? Come and hear: when they gave R. Tzadok food less than an egg-bulk, he took a cloth and ate outside the sukkah — is it not that an egg-bulk requires washing? Perhaps an egg-bulk requires a sukkah. Behold it is clear even for that which he wished to reject "perhaps," etc., that it does not require washing; and if because he ate by means of a cloth one could say that washing is required — if so, it should be difficult for him why he wrote in siman 483 (and so in our text) [and this is his language] that for less than an egg-bulk one does not require washing, as we say in Sukkah — until here. There too the Gemara brought proof — this of R. Tzadok who took a cloth; and without this his words contradict one another, for in siman 483 he ruled that washing is not required and in siman 328 he ruled that washing is required out of doubt — until here his words briefly. To my humble opinion, to resolve everything well: the Rokeach analyzed finely that for that which he rejected in Chullin — if so, even for more than an egg-bulk a cloth suffices, for a cloth is like washing; for if not, what does our Mishnah teach us that he took a cloth — did it come to teach us that R. Tzadok practiced cleanliness? Rather it teaches us incidentally in what it taught "and he took a cloth" that for less than an egg-bulk washing is required, for cloth and washing are one. But in Sukkah daf 27, which says that perhaps less than an egg-bulk does not require washing, etc. — if so, a cloth does not help in place of washing, as Tosafot explained there s.v. ha, etc.; if so, we say that our Mishnah teaches that for less than an egg-bulk washing is not required, since he took a cloth — examine this. Also what he wrote in Nachalat Tzvi and the words of the Rokeach contradict — he overlooked the words of Lechem Chamudot in Chullin daf 222, for in siman 483 the Rokeach wrote regarding obligation and that one blesses on netilat yadayim. However it is difficult: for the Rokeach wrote there, this is his language — in Chullin it is implied that one who eats with a cloth requires netilat yadayim — until here; and if so, from that R. Tzadok took a cloth for less than an egg-bulk, apparently he did not require netilat yadayim. And it is possible the Rokeach speaks out of doubt that washing is required with a cloth, and here stringently; but it is difficult: if so, he should have ruled not to bless; and also from the conclusion of Chullin it is implied that certainly washing is required, as is implied later siman 163 in Beit Yosef, see there. And it appears to me that it is possible: even if for less than an egg-bulk we require netilat yadayim, nevertheless one can say that R. Tzadok's taking a cloth was because he was a priest and they permitted a cloth for those who eat terumah, as Tosafot wrote there; and that which it mentions less than an egg-bulk was because of sukkah and berachah; and even though I saw in Tosafot Yoma daf 79 s.v. ha, etc., that they permitted only for terumah and not for non-sacred food — it appears to me that is for that which the Gemara in Sukkah was compelled to say to Abaye and R. Yosef that less than an egg-bulk refers to washing; but if it is possible to say it does not refer to washing, it is preferable to say they permitted a cloth for priests even for non-sacred food.
