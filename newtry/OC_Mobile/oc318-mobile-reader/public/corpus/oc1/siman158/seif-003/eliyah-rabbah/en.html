Nachalas Tzvi questions Rokeach from the Gemara's inquiry whether one may eat using a cloth. The Gemara brings proof from the incident in which Rabbi Tzadok was given less than an egg's volume of food, wrapped it in a cloth, and ate it outside the sukkah. The Gemara asks whether this implies that an egg's volume requires washing, and rejects the proof by saying that perhaps an egg's volume requires a sukkah. Even according to this rejection, less than an egg's volume would not require washing. If the fact that he ate through a cloth itself implied that washing was required, Rokeach's ruling in siman 283 would be difficult, for there he writes that less than an egg's volume does not require washing, as stated in Sukkah. Yet there too the Gemara cites Rabbi Tzadok's use of a cloth. Moreover, Rokeach appears self-contradictory, ruling in siman 283 that washing is unnecessary and in siman 328 that one should wash because of doubt. In my humble opinion, all of this may be resolved. Rokeach precisely inferred that according to the rejection in Chullin, even for more than an egg's volume a cloth would suffice, because using a cloth is equivalent to washing. Otherwise, what does the Mishnah teach by saying that Rabbi Tzadok used a cloth? Surely it did not merely teach that he acted cleanly. Rather, by recording the cloth it teaches incidentally that even less than an egg's volume requires washing, since a cloth and washing serve the same function. In Sukkah 27, however, where the Gemara says that perhaps less than an egg's volume does not require washing, the use of a cloth cannot substitute for washing, as Tosafos explain there, s.v. ha. The Mishnah therefore teaches that less than an egg's volume does not require washing, as shown by his use of a cloth. Examine this carefully. Nachalas Tzvi's claim that Rokeach contradicts himself also overlooks Lechem Chamudos to Chullin, page 222, who explains that in siman 283 Rokeach discusses whether there is an obligation carrying a blessing of Al Netilas Yadayim. There remains a difficulty, however, because Rokeach writes there that Chullin implies that even one eating with a cloth requires washing. If so, Rabbi Tzadok's use of a cloth for less than an egg's volume would indicate that washing itself was unnecessary. Perhaps Rokeach means that because of doubt one must wash even when using a cloth, and here he rules stringently. Yet if so, he should have ruled that no blessing be recited. Moreover, the conclusion of Chullin implies that washing is certainly required, as appears below in siman 163 in Beit Yosef. It may be that even if less than an egg's volume requires washing, Rabbi Tzadok used a cloth because he was a kohen, and the Sages permitted a cloth for those eating terumah, as Tosafos write there. The mention of less than an egg's volume would then relate only to sukkah and the blessing. Although Tosafos in Yoma 79, s.v. ha, write that the allowance applies only to terumah and not to ordinary food, that is according to the Gemara's necessity in Sukkah, in the discussion of Abaye and Rav Yosef, to interpret less than an egg's volume as referring to washing. Where it is possible to say that the passage does not refer to washing, it is preferable to say that kohanim were permitted to use a cloth even for ordinary food. This also resolves the apparent contradiction between Tosafos in Chullin, s.v. mai, and Tosafos in Yoma. Examine this carefully.
