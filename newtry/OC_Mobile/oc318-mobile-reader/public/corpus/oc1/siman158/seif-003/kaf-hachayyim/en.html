10) [Seif 3] If he eats less than an olive's volume, etc. The Shulchan Aruch writes this based on the Beit Yosef's citation of the Rokeach, siman 283, that less than an olive's volume does not require washing. Lechem Chamudos on Chullin, chapter Kol HaBasar, paragraph 72, however, writes that an error entered the Beit Yosef's transcription and, based upon that error, the Shulchan Aruch wrote this as halachah. Rather, the text should say that less than an egg's volume does not require washing. He writes that this is the text in the Rokeach and concludes that one should therefore wash without a blessing for any amount of bread, whether little or much, until an egg's volume, at which point one must also recite the blessing over netilas yadayim; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, write the same. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 3, however, reconciles the words of the Shulchan Aruch. He concludes that although Lechem Chamudos and Magen Avraham disagree with the Shulchan Aruch regarding less than an olive's volume and maintain that its law is like less than an egg's volume, requiring washing without a blessing, we have only the practical ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. This also appears to be the view of the Bach, of blessed memory, and one who wishes to be stringent will be blessed. The Levush similarly writes that according to all opinions, less than an olive's volume does not require washing. So too the Taz, paragraph 3; Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef; and Birkei Yosef, paragraph 4. Birkei Yosef writes that this is the ruling we accept. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, however, writes that one should be stringent and wash without a blessing. This also appears to be the view of the Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 6; Nehar Shalom, paragraph 2; and Derech HaChaim, paragraph 2. Beis Menuchah, laws of netilas yadayim, paragraph 1, rules like Birkei Yosef and writes that this is the common custom, unlike Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim; only one who wishes to be stringent will be blessed. Since this law is disputed, although Beis Menuchah writes that the common custom is not to wash for less than an olive's volume, it is proper to be stringent and wash in order to satisfy all opinions. In any event, some are careful not to eat less than the minimum measure, so that they do not eat without a concluding blessing. This is all the more appropriate regarding bread, upon which several kavanos apply, as written in the preceding siman. Who, then, would presume to eat less than the minimum measure without netilas yadayim and Birkas HaMazon? See below, siman 168, paragraph 75.
11) There: Less than an olive's volume, etc. If he eats more than an olive's volume, or exactly an olive's volume, he should wash his hands because of the doubt but should not recite a blessing. Levush, seif 2. This follows the view of Maran, of blessed memory, and those who agree with him. We already wrote in the preceding paragraph that the later authorities are stringent even for less than an olive's volume; see there.
12) There: Less than an olive's volume. The volume of an olive is one-half of an egg's volume, as written below in siman 486. It is the volume of a vessel that holds nine dirhams of water, as written above in siman 81, paragraph 3; see there. Whether the measure is determined by volume or by weight will be explained below, with Heaven's help, in siman 168, paragraph 46.
