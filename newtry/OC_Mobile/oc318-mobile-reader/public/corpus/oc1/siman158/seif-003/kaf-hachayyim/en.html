3) There: He should wash his hands, etc. One must wash the right hand first, so that the left serves the right. Recanati, parashas Eikev. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. Shaar HaMitzvos, parashas Eikev, writes the same and gives a kabbalistic reason; see there. This implies that there is no difference whether one washes his own hands or another person pours for him. Darkei Moshe in Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 2, writes that at a meal one should wash the left hand first, so that the right hand of the attendant who pours will face the left of the person washing, and this was attributed to the Arizal, of blessed memory. This does not appear correct from the aforementioned Shaar HaMitzvos. We have only what R' Chaim Vital, of blessed memory, wrote in Shaar HaMitzvos in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, and nothing else. This is the common practice. One should also first take the vessel of water in his right hand, then give it to his left hand and pour from his left onto his right hand first, and afterward from his right onto his left, as written above in siman 4, seif 10. Machatzis HaShekel writes the same. See what we wrote on siman 4, paragraphs 34 through 37.
