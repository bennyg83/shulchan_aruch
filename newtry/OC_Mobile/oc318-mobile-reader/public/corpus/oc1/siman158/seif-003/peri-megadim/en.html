Levush likewise writes that for less than a kezayis no washing is required at all, because it is not a significant measure in any prohibition. See Magen Avraham, letter 4, who disagrees and holds that even for less than a kezayis one must wash without a blessing. See Beit Yosef’s citation of Rokeach at the beginning of chapter 3: “They wash their hands for the second dipping and recite the blessing over washing, but for eating the matzah, for which one fulfills the mitzvah with a kezayis, washing is unnecessary, since less than a kezayis does not require washing, as stated in Sukkah and Berachos.” This may be understood as follows: only for the second dipping does one recite the blessing, but for the kezayis of matzah one does not bless, as written in siman 328. One should not say that this is an astonishing statement because even less than a kebeitzah does not require a blessing. Rather, he writes that less than a kezayis certainly requires no washing at all, as is clear from Sukkah 26, where casual eating is permitted outside the sukkah, and less than a kezayis is certainly casual eating. The same applies to washing the hands: the enactment was made only for a fixed meal, not for casual eating. See what I wrote in the introduction, that according to the view that terumah of fruit is D’Oraisa, washing was required only for bread because bread is the basis of a meal. Pas haba’ah bekisnin is regarded as casual eating like fruit, as Beis Yosef writes in Bedek HaBayis to Toras HaBayis, 158a. According to this, the wording is precise. Otherwise it would be repetitive and should simply have said that for a kezayis no washing is required. This indicates two distinct rulings: for a kezayis, washing is required but without a blessing; for less than a kezayis, no washing is required at all. Although this reading is somewhat strained, it appears to me to correct the words of Chazal as cited by Beit Yosef and Or Zarua. In my humble opinion, the word “but” refers to the phrase “for less than a kezayis.” For less than a kebeitzah, Bircas HaMazon is nevertheless recited, as ruled in siman 184, seif 6, since there is at least Rabbinic satisfaction. The distinction regarding interruption is that this amount does not contract impurity. Understand this.
