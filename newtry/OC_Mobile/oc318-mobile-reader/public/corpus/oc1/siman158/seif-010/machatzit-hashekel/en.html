(S.k. 10) That the juice, etc. It appears to me that all agree, etc. That is to say, in the Shulchan Aruch he wrote, there is one whose words appear to indicate, implying that the permission is not clear. Therefore the Magen Avraham wrote that all agree and that the permission is clear, for even Tosafot in Chullin hold so. The Beis Yosef proved this only from a precision in the words of the Rashbi, who wrote regarding what is stated in Chullin, folio 107b, that Rav Hamnuna ate roasted meat and required washing, that perforce he ate bread with it, for roasted meat alone does not require washing. On the contrary, we rule that one who washes his hands for fruit is among the arrogant. The Beis Yosef wrote that one must say that he holds that the juice on roasted meat is not considered a liquid, for one cannot say that it speaks of dry roasted meat, since it would still be difficult: from where did he know that it speaks of one who ate bread with it? He should have explained it as moist roasted meat. These are the words of the Beis Yosef.
And they may be eaten with impure hands, etc. This refers to what the Mishnah teaches: If one slaughtered a domesticated animal, wild animal, or bird and blood did not come out of them, they are valid and may be eaten with impure hands, because they were not rendered susceptible through blood. Tosafot asked: But it requires rinsing and salting, so it was rendered susceptible through water. Tosafot answered that it speaks of roasting, etc.
Blood of slaughter renders susceptible, and that juice is not called blood at all. Rather, it is not the way of roasted meat, etc. This does not refer to the opinion of Tosafot above, for Tosafot were speaking with respect to susceptibility to impurity, and perforce water never came upon it, for if so it was already rendered susceptible, even though now it is dry. Rather, it refers to the opinion of the Rashba above. There is still room to ask: how does he know that Rav Hamnuna ate bread? Perhaps it speaks of roasted meat moist because of water. To this he wrote that it is not the way of roasted meat, etc. The Maharshal wrote that even moist liquids, etc., meaning even water.
And in Rashi in Berachos, etc. It is stated there that Beis Hillel holds that they mix the first cup, at the beginning of the meal, before he washes his hands for eating, because he holds that it is permitted to use a vessel whose outside is impure. We are concerned that if he first washes his hands and afterward mixes the cup, perhaps his hands will still be moist from the washing when he touches the outside of the cup with his hand, and the liquids on his hands will become impure from the outside of the cup and will make his hands impure. Therefore he should first mix the cup and afterward wash his hands. During the meal, however, if he takes a cup in his hand, we are not concerned, for before eating he must dry his hands well, as written at the end of the siman. Rashi wrote as follows: And if you will say that sometimes gravy liquids are moist on the hands, they do not have the status of liquids, but the status of food. End quote.
