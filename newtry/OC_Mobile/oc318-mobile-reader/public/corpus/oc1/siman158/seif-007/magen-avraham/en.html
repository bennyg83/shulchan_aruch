There is an opinion that appears. This is the view of Tosafos to Pesachim 115, s.v. kol shetibulo. They prove it from Berachos 43, which states that although one washed one hand for drinking, he must wash both hands again, since the first washing was not performed for sanctity. Nachalas Tzvi did not understand Beit Yosef and wrote improper criticism of him. The wording of Darchei Moshe misled him. See there, and see what I wrote in siman 159, seif katan 13.
Not intending to eat. For example, his hands were dirty and he washed them without intending to eat, but afterward decided to eat and did not divert his attention in the meantime. The first washing does not count because it was not performed for sanctity at all. Rama rules that he should wash again without a blessing. According to those who hold that intention is unnecessary, the first washing already counted, and no blessing may be recited over it because he had no intention for ritual washing at all. This resembles a niddah who fell into water, or one who threw a knife to lodge it in a wall and it proceeded to slaughter an animal, where no blessing is appropriate. Even if he originally washed for food dipped in liquid, according to Tosafos no washing is required at all, as explained in seif katan 4, and therefore no blessing should be recited. He likewise does not bless over the present washing, since perhaps the first washing counted. I needed to elaborate because Bach offered an explanation that appears strange to me. I do not know what compelled him, for the matter is straightforward as I have written.
