7) There: And he does not establish his meal upon them, etc. This implies that if he does establish his meal upon them, so that he recites HaMotzi over them as written below in siman 168, seif 6, he must wash his hands and recite the blessing over netilas yadayim. This also appears from the Beit Yosef, who, after considering the matter and presenting arguments in both directions, concludes that the Rashba's words imply that netilas yadayim is required; see there. The Levush, however, writes that if one establishes his meal upon them, he should wash his hands but not recite the blessing, because of the doubt. It is preferable for him first to eat an egg's volume of regular bread, so that he may wash his hands and recite the blessing over netilas yadayim, and then eat these foods; see there. The Shelah writes the same. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, cites the words of the Shelah and Levush and writes: 'I do not know who disagrees about this. Although the Beit Yosef initially questions the matter, he afterward concludes in the name of the Rashba that netilas yadayim is required, which implies that the matter became clear to him after he had questioned it. The Rambam likewise writes at the beginning of chapter 6 that anyone who eats bread over which HaMotzi is recited must wash, etc. The Bedek HaBayis and Mishmeres HaBayis explicitly write that washing is required when one establishes his meal upon it; see there. Therefore, he should recite the blessing over netilas yadayim.' The Taz, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; and Mateh Yosef, volume 2, siman 18, paragraph 5, write the same. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3, writes that this is the primary halachic ruling; see there. Zechor L'Avraham, paragraph 50, cites him. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 2, writes the same and rejects the words of Atzei Almogim, Nesiv Chaim, and Mor U'Ketziah, who hold that one should not recite the blessing; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; the Gra, paragraph 5; Chayei Adam, klal 36, paragraph 2; and Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2, also rule this way. Chesed LaAlafim adds that it is preferable also to eat an egg's volume of bread in order to satisfy all opinions, but if he has none or cannot eat bread, he should wash and recite the blessing; see there. Beis Menuchah, laws of netilas yadayim, paragraph 3; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 48, paragraph 1; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 1; Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 8. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 7, however, writes that one who wishes to be stringent by not reciting the blessing or by eating bread together with these foods acts more properly; see there. Thus, this matter remains disputed. According to the rule that a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently, even against the majority and even against Maran, of blessed memory, as we wrote above in siman 18, paragraph 7, it would have been proper to rule that one should wash without a blessing. Nevertheless, the common custom is also to recite the blessing, following Maran, of blessed memory, and the authorities who agree with him. We already wrote above in siman 7, paragraph 2, and siman 17, paragraph 2, that in a place with an established custom we do not apply the rule that a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently, and they may recite the blessing; see there. The practical difference is that if a place is found where the custom is not to recite the blessing, one should not protest, for one can say that they follow the rule that a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently. The quantity that constitutes establishing a meal upon pas haba b'kisnin will be explained below in siman 168, seif 6, with Heaven's help.
