Only. See Taz. This is not actually the Gemara’s conclusion, but merely a rejected answer. See siman 475, seif 1, and Eliyah Rabbah here, letter 10. One may also say that there the concern is that he might immerse the entire item so that the harmful substance dies, whereas generally there is no such concern. Regarding preserves made with bee honey, he writes that the honey has the status of liquid because it is produced through an act of crushing the combs. Once an act has established it as liquid, a later act cannot turn it into food, for thought can negate thought, but an act does not negate an act in matters of food. Magen Avraham, letter 7, writes that it is food. The citation there contains a scribal error: it should read chapter 1 of Hilchos Tumas Ochlin, halachah 18. This is what he means by asking why the Tanna counted it. I do not know the difficulty, for the Tanna in chapter 4 of Machshirin, mishnah 6, counted bee honey independently as a liquid. His question is valid, however, against the Shulchan Aruch: why does it state food dipped in a liquid such as honey? Specifically bee honey is included, whereas honey from wasps or hornets is pure and permitted for consumption, as stated in Machshirin, chapter 6, mishnah 4.
