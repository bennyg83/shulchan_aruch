Seif 7, he washed, etc. This is from Tosafot cited above, s.v. all, etc., from the passage in Berachos as above. In Darkei Moshe he challenged it from Arvei Pesachim and from what Tosafot wrote there, s.v. distraction, etc.; and one can answer, etc., although his words in Darkei Moshe are not understandable. This is what is written in the gloss: and if he did not distract his mind, etc. He disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch, but was concerned for his words and ruled that he washes his hands without a blessing. Even according to Tosafot cited above, that is according to their view, for they maintain that netilas yadayim for something dipped in liquid is not because of sanctity, and from this they proved their position, as above. So too the Rashba wrote. But according to what I wrote in seif 4, not like Tosafot, he should not have followed Tosafot, even though he was concerned for the doubt and wrote without a blessing. If so, here too he should have said the same. Nevertheless, the second law remains in force when he washed his hands not in order to eat. At first glance, the words of Tosafot contradict one another, as Darkei Moshe asked, but the words of Tosafot are correct. For in s.v. distraction, etc., when they say: one can answer that it was initially assumed, etc., they mean that it was initially assumed that chullin does not require intent; but the Gemara in Berachos holds that it does require intent.
And if he touched, etc. As written in Arvei Pesachim there: perhaps, etc., and he touched. See Rashi there, s.v. he became distracted, etc., and Shabbos 14a, s.v. busy, etc.
