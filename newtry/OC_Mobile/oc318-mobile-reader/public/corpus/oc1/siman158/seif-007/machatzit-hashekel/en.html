So too in Shabbos, folio 19, it is stated there: Who is this Tanna who holds that anything that comes on its own is permitted? That is to say, although we forbid on Shabbos liquids that flowed out on Shabbos as a decree lest one squeeze, nevertheless, if one loaded beams and presses upon grapes and they were crushed before Shabbos, and through this the liquids come out on their own on Shabbos, they are permitted on Shabbos, for there is no concern lest he squeeze, since even if he squeezed them by hand there would be no liability for a sin-offering, for they were crushed before Shabbos and would in any case come out on their own. Rabbi Elazar said: It is Rabbi Eliezer, as we learned: Honeycombs that one crushed before Shabbos, and the honey came out on Shabbos by itself, Rabbi Eliezer permits. Rabbi Yose bar Chanina rejected this there: With honey, at first it is food and afterward it is food; but here, at first it is food and now it is liquid. That is to say, regarding honey, even after it flows from the combs it is still food, and there is no concern lest he squeeze, for squeezing does not apply to it. But regarding olives and grapes, which are now liquid, one can say there is a concern lest he squeeze. End quote of Rashi. Thus they called it food.
See Bava Basra, folio 80a, and Tosafot there; so the text should read. It is stated there: Rav Kahana said that honey in the hive never leaves the status of food. Tosafot wrote: The Ri finds this difficult, for in tractate Machshirin honey is counted among the seven liquids that render food susceptible to impurity. The Ri answered that this is after it was separated from the wax, but while it is in its hive, together with the wax, it has the status of food. End quote. The Magen Avraham holds that the reason that with the wax it is called food is that there it is congealed.
And also oil and honey, etc.; so the text should read.
See there in the name of Tosafot in Pesachim, where Tosafot asked that in several places it implies that oil is called a liquid. In Taharos we learned that Rabbi Shimon Shezuri disagrees with Rabbi Meir and the Sages, and holds that honey and oil are not called liquids, but wine is called a liquid. In Menachos, the Talmud rules that the halacha follows Rabbi Shimon Shezuri. Tosafot answered that what is said that honey and oil are not called liquids refers to when they congealed, even though they later melted again; but wine, even if it congealed, if it later melted it is a liquid. According to this, we have two grounds of permission for preserves: one, because it stands for eating, and second, if it was once congealed, even though now it has melted. But the Taz, s.k. 7, disagreed with the first permission. The Tosefta teaches that thought for eating helps only where it flowed out on its own; but if he removed it by hand, since he performed an act of removing it for the sake of liquid, the later thought by which he intends it for eating does not remove it from the first act. It appears that he also disagrees with the second law, for he wrote that dough fried in goose fat does not require washing because it is not one of the seven liquids, but if it was fried in butter, since butter is included in milk, it requires washing. Yet before he fried the dough in it, the butter was congealed, and even so he requires washing. However, the Magen Avraham brought proof for the latter law from Tosafot in Pesachim, unlike the Taz.
