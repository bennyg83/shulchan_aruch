(There) And one should not say that here is different, since he has not yet eaten. This is not so, for eating can be with pure hands even without washing, etc.; so the text should read. That is to say, at first glance one might say that since he has not eaten, the mitzvah has not ended, for the mitzvah is upon him to eat with pure hands, and therefore he properly recites the blessing. To this he said that nevertheless it is not relevant to the blessing of washing, for eating with pure hands can occur even without washing, as written in siman 164 in the name of the Kol Bo, that if he placed his hands in gloves, he does not afterward require washing. The intent of the Taz is not to bring proof from there that he does not require washing, for that is not so, since there it speaks when he made a stipulation upon them in the morning, as explained there. Rather, his intent is to bring from there that we find pure hands even though he does not wash them now. If so, eating with pure hands does not necessarily require washing, such as if his hands were purified through morning washing without any stipulation at all, and he placed them in gloves, they remain in their purity even though he did not wash them. Therefore, because he eats with pure hands, he has no basis to recite a blessing for this upon washing. It is possible that what the Taz wrote, without touching, means without touching filthy places after he already washed in the morning. Nevertheless, it appears as I emended. For you were not both taken in one net. The Taz further asked that if it is so, that the main blessing is upon fulfilling the mitzvah of eating with pure hands, then he should have recited, Who commanded us to eat, etc., for in any event the mitzvah of washing is gone. Initially he had difficulty that he should not recite a blessing at all, and afterward he asked that in any event he should have recited a different formula.
