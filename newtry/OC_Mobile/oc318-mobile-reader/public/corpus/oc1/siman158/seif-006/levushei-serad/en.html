Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. “For matzah.” Beit Yosef first cited in the name of the Rokeach the law in seif 2, and afterward cited in his name the law in seif 3, in the following language: “In the Seder of Pesach, he should wash his hands for the second dipping, for because of the matzah he would not need to wash, since an olive-bulk of matzah suffices, and one who eats less than an olive-bulk need not wash his hands.” Magen Avraham objects that the text of the Rokeach must read “less than an egg-bulk,” for matzah itself requires an olive-bulk. Although the Rokeach ruled as in seif 2 that one must wash for less than an egg-bulk, his meaning must be that this washing does not prove that less than an egg-bulk requires washing, since it may have been performed for the dipping. Thus, as a matter of law, for every amount less than an egg-bulk there is no distinction between an olive-bulk and less than an olive-bulk. One washes without a blessing, and all of seif 3 is unnecessary. This appears to me to be Magen Avraham’s view.
