Regarding the statement that immersion requires no drying, this is taught in a Baraisa and in chapter 2 of Yadayim, and Rabbi Elazar discusses it in Sotah 4a. See Magen Avraham, letter 18. Levush writes that according to the view that drying is required because of disgust, the same applies to immersion, and Rabbi Elazar disagrees with the Baraisa; see there. Know that Levush offers another resolution: the Baraisa that says immersion does not require drying means that no towel is needed, but drying in the sun or in another manner is still required. When one pours in two stages, so that the first water becomes impure and the second purifies it, drying specifically with a towel is necessary. This distinction has practical consequences. Regarding food dipped in liquid, one may say that drying is unnecessary because the concern that the bread becomes impure applies specifically to bread, which becomes repulsive when held with wet hands, but not to other foods. If one poured a revi’is in two stages, however, the water certainly becomes impure through contact with liquid, and drying is required.
In Samuel. I Samuel 14 states, “He dipped the staff,” and so forth. One might infer that because of the liquid, washing of the hands was required. This is incorrect. See Magen Avraham, letter 5, Birkei Yosef, letter 6, and Ya’aros Devash, part 2; see there and understand.
