(S.k. 4) Less than an olive-bulk is an erroneous text, etc. For this seif has its source in the Rokeach cited by the Beis Yosef. This is his language: They wash their hands on Pesach night after the order of the Haggadah, before eating, and recite the blessing of netilas yadayim, because one must dip a second dipping with maror, for he holds like the view of Rashi below, that one must recite the blessing of netilas yadayim for something dipped in liquid. But for eating matzah, with which he fulfills the obligation with an olive-bulk, he does not require washing, for less than an olive-bulk does not require washing, as we say in Sukkah and Berachos. End quote. This was the version before the Beis Yosef, and based on this he wrote in the Shulchan Aruch that for less than an olive-bulk one does not require washing. Although in the preceding seif he wrote that for less than an egg-bulk one should wash his hands and not recite a blessing, implying that less than an olive-bulk is more lenient than less than an egg-bulk, the Magen Avraham wrote that it is a scribal error in the Rokeach, and the text must read: for less than an egg-bulk one does not require washing. For so it is in Sukkah, folio 26b, in the Mishnah: When they gave Rabbi Tzadok food less than an egg-bulk, he took it in a cloth and ate it outside the sukkah, etc. Rashi explained: He took it in a cloth, meaning he did not wash his hands, but wrapped his hands in a cloth for cleanliness. End quote. Rashi is among those who hold that less than an egg-bulk does not receive impurity according to the Torah, and therefore it does not require washing, as written in the previous seif. Tosafot disagree and hold that less than an egg-bulk receives impurity and requires washing, except that Rabbi Tzadok was a kohen and it was permitted for him to eat without netilas yadayim by means of wrapping in a cloth, so that he did not touch the food, as we say in Chullin, folio 107b, because they are zealous, even though for a Yisrael it was not permitted to eat by means of a cloth, as below in siman 163. The Rokeach holds like Rashi, and it is proven there that less than an egg-bulk does not require washing. But that less than an olive-bulk does not require washing has no proof from Sukkah, for one can distinguish between less than an egg-bulk and less than an olive-bulk.
And so it is implied from within it, etc. That is to say, from the words of the Rokeach it is proven that the text must read less than an egg-bulk. For if the text reads less than an olive-bulk, but an olive-bulk, he holds, does require washing, how did the Rokeach write: but for eating matzah, which is an olive-bulk, he does not require washing? If so, there is no distinction between less than an olive-bulk and less than an egg-bulk, and in both of them washing is required without a blessing, as written in seif 2.
