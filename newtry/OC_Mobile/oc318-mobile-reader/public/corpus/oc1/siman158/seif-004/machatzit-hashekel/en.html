(א) (s.k. 4) "Less than a kezayit — mistaken text," etc. — for this seif has its source in the Rokeach, whom Beit Yosef brought; and this is his language: and they wash their hands (on the night of Pesach after the order of the Haggadah, before eating) and bless al netilat yadayim because one must dip the second dipping in maror (for he holds like Rashi later that one must bless al netilat yadayim for the need of a thing dipped in liquid); but for eating matzah, where one fulfills with a kezayit, washing is not required, for for less than a kezayit washing is not required, as we say in Sukkah and Berachot, end of his words. Thus was the text before Beit Yosef; accordingly Shulchan Aruch wrote that for less than a kezayit one does not require washing, even though in the previous seif it wrote that for less than a kebeitzah one washes and does not bless — it is implied that less than a kezayit is more lenient than less than a kebeitzah. And Magen Avraham wrote that it is a mistake in the Rokeach, and it must be said: for less than a kebeitzah washing is not required, for thus it says in Sukkah daf 26b in the Mishnah: and when they gave R. Tzadok food less than an egg-bulk, they took a cloth for him and he ate outside the sukkah, etc.; and Rashi explained: they took a cloth for him — he did not wash his hands, but wrapped his hands in a cloth because of cleanliness, end of his words — for Rashi is among those who hold that less than an egg-bulk does not receive impurity by Torah law, and therefore washing is not required, as written in the previous seif; and Tosafot disagree and hold that less than an egg-bulk receives impurity and requires washing, but R. Tzadok was a priest and it was permitted for him to eat without washing by wrapping a cloth, for he did not touch the food, as they say in Chullin daf 107b, because priests are scrupulous — even though for an Israelite it was not permitted to eat by means of a cloth, etc.**** END BLOCK ****
