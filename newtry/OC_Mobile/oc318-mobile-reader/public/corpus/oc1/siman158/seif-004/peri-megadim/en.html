In one. See Taz, who cites Rashi in Pesachim 116a. One who examines Levush will see that he presents four explanations. First, according to Rashi, wine and oil are liquids of terumah D’Oraisa, and because one touches the dipped food with his hand, unlike drinking, the Sages extended the requirement from terumah to the other five liquids. The reason they did not similarly decree regarding fruit may be that liquids become a first degree of impurity. This is Rashi’s phrase, that they render liquids first degree. Hagahos Ashri cites the view of Rabbeinu Yonah that since liquids are especially susceptible, receive impurity without prior preparation, and become first degree, washing was required. Rabbeinu Yerucham holds that the reason is cleanliness, since touching liquid with one’s hands is especially repulsive. Tosafos holds that the concern is lest he subsequently render his body unfit through impurity. Beit Yosef, however, implies that Rabbeinu Yonah and Rashi express one view. Apparently the meaning is that with fruit or another food dipped in liquid, the Sages were stringent because of the association with terumah: since liquid is also present, they treated such fruit like bread. This appears from Tosafos’ explanation of Rashi. There is therefore no need to say that the decree was specifically because of wine and oil. This is what Magen Avraham writes in letter 5 against Levush; see there, and see Eliyah Rabbah, letter 9. According to Rashi, a blessing is required because the enactment is associated with terumah. Rashi mentions vegetables and terumah, although terumah of vegetables may be only Rabbinic, and one may even be eating food that is not subject to terumah or maaser at all. Nevertheless, anything dipped in liquid requires washing. According to Tosafos, since the reason is only that one’s body should not become unfit, no blessing is recited, for this is not an enactment of sanctity or cleanliness. It resembles removing prohibited fat or the like, upon which no blessing is recited. Moreover, today we are all impure through contact with the dead, so that rationale no longer applies. Rabbeinu Yerucham’s reason of cleanliness also does not warrant a blessing. He disagrees with Tosafos, for Tosafos elsewhere holds that a mitzvah instituted for cleanliness does require a blessing. According to Rabbeinu Yerucham, one might seemingly be stringent even regarding liquids outside the seven enumerated liquids, since the concern is cleanliness. Nevertheless, we follow Rashi and Tosafos that only the seven liquids require washing. See what I will write, with Hashem’s help, in letters 5 and 6 concerning this.
