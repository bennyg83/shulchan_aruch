[4] He does not need, etc. According to all opinions, he does not need to wash. Levush. So writes Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah. Lechem Chamudos writes there that a copying error in Beit Yosef caused this error in the Shulchan Aruch. The correct wording of Rokeach, siman 283, is that less than an egg's volume does not require washing. According to this, less than an olive's volume has the same law as less than an egg's volume: he should wash without a blessing. So write Nachalas Tzvi and Magen Avraham. In my humble opinion, the Shulchan Aruch did not intend this at all. Rather, he inferred from the earlier ruling of Rokeach, that for less than an egg's volume one washes without a blessing, that the novelty regarding less than an olive's volume is not merely that one washes without a blessing, but that no washing is required at all. This also appears from Bach, who evidently understood the Shulchan Aruch this way. Nevertheless, this is not a decisive proof for practical halachah, because had Rokeach taught the law only for less than an olive's volume, one might have thought that less than an egg's volume requires a blessing. I saw that Taz writes that Sukkah explicitly states that washing is unnecessary. This is astonishing, for it is not stated there, and all the great authorities cited above make clear that this is not explicit in the Gemara. There is also some proof from the fact that Rambam and the poskim link the law of washing to the law of the blessing Hamotzi, and below in siman 168 we rule that Hamotzi is recited even on less than an olive's volume. One should therefore be stringent and wash without a blessing. This also provides some support for Rama's responsum, siman 8, ruling that one who eats bread merely to sweeten a drink need not wash, since he does not recite Hamotzi, as explained below in siman 212. Mateh Moshe, siman 327, and Shelah, however, rule that washing is required, and this also appears to be the view of Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah.
