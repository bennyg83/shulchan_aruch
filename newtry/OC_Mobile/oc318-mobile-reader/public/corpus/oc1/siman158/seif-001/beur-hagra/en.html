Seif 1, when he comes, etc. Mishnah in Chagigah and the Gemara, folio 11b: here it is referring to eating bread, etc.
That one recites a blessing, etc. As written in the Yerushalmi at the end of the sixth chapter of Berachos: It teaches the order of a meal. Guests enter and sit on benches and chairs until all have gathered. Wine was brought to them, each one recites a blessing for himself. Water was brought to them for the hands, each one washes one hand. This is a textual error, and one must read: before wine was brought to them, etc.; and so too in the latter clause. So it is also written in the Tosefta, and so it appears in our Gemara there. Refreshments were brought to them, each one recites a blessing for himself. They ascended and reclined. Wine was brought to them, and even though he recited a blessing over the first wine, he must recite a blessing over the second, and one recites the blessing on behalf of all of them. Water was brought to them for the hands, and even though he washed one hand, he must wash both hands, etc. Thus, he does not need to wash for refreshments, and these are types of mezonos, as Tosafot wrote on folio 42a, s.v. he recited a blessing, etc. This is what he wrote: but for bread, etc. This is unlike what is written in Terumas HaDeshen, that he washes one hand, and the Raah already challenged him; see there. For what relevance does all this have here? There, he washes only because of the wine.
Even, etc. As written in chapter 25, because of the concern for terumah. With respect to terumah, ordinary hands disqualify it, as written in the first chapter of Shabbos. So too the Mishnah in the second chapter of Chagigah says: one washes the hands, etc., and it is speaking of ordinary hands, as is evident from the challenge there: and raise a contradiction, etc. With terumah, even ordinary hands apply, as written in the first chapter of Shabbos. This is what the Mishnah states: and for chatas, if his hands became tamei, etc. There is no difficulty: with ordinary hands, and with chatas he immerses his body. See Rashi to Chullin 106b, s.v. and he stipulates, etc.; nevertheless, this applies specifically with a condition.
And he recites the blessing, etc. As in the morning, and as written in the second chapter of Shabbos, that all mitzvos, even D'Rabbanan, require a blessing.
And he does not, etc. But if he establishes it as a meal, so that he recites Hamotzi, he requires netilas yadayim. This is from the Rashba and the Raah, and so it also appears from the Yerushalmi cited above, which concludes: he must wash both hands. Refreshments were brought to them, and one recites the blessing on behalf of all of them. A guest may not enter after three servings of refreshments. This implies that these refreshments were also before the meal, yet he washes both hands, because here it is in the setting of reclining and establishing a meal; therefore one recites the blessing for all of them.
