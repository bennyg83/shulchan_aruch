[1] [Levush] And they supported it on this verse, etc. If you ask: at the end of Elu Devarim we derive it from "and you shall sanctify yourselves and be holy"; one may answer that that is for the one reciting ha-motzi and because of the honor of the berachah, but here it refers to the other diners (Torat HaBayit HaAroch, p. 156 [68a]). To me it appears that there it refers to when it is known that his hands are dirty, but here to ordinary hands; and so it is explained in Ba'al Halachot Gedolot, p. 9. So too it is implied in Chiddushei Rashba, p. [106] in Chullin, and in Torat HaBayit HaAroch, p. 161 [73b]. And this requires study in Mishmeret HaBayit, p. 156, where he wrote the opposite, that the verse speaks of ordinary hands.
