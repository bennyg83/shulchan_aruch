[Levush] And they attached it to this verse, etc. One might ask that at the end of Elu Devarim the law is derived from 'You shall sanctify yourselves and be holy.' The answer is that there it concerns the person reciting HaMotzi, because of the honor of the blessing, whereas here the law concerns the other diners. Torat HaBayit HaAruch, folio 156. It seems to me, however, that there the case is one where his hands are known to be dirty, whereas here it concerns ordinary hands. This is explicit in Baal Halachot Gedolot, folio 9, and is also implied by the Rashba's novellae to Chullin and by Torat HaBayit HaAruch, folio 161. This requires study in light of Mishmeret HaBayit, folio 156, which writes the opposite, that the verse concerns ordinary hands.
[Levush] There are those who require, etc. This is imprecise, because the Beit Yosef records no dispute on the matter. Rather, the Beit Yosef himself was uncertain based on his own reasoning. Moreover, the Beit Yosef's concluding words, 'but I found that the Rashba, etc.,' show that he ultimately rules that washing is required; his uncertainty existed only before he found that statement in Torat HaBayit HaAruch. The Shulchan Aruch likewise makes this clear. I am further surprised that the Beit Yosef learned this only from an implication in Torat HaBayit HaAruch, because I found that the Rashba explicitly writes in Mishmeret HaBayit, folio 158b, that if one establishes a meal upon them, he washes both hands. Therefore I say there is no room for doubt: netilat yadayim with a blessing is required. After Magen Avraham and Taz were printed, I found that they too ruled in accordance with my view.
[3] He should wash his hands, etc. This is because of doubt. So writes Rokeach, siman 328, since this appears to follow from Chullin 107. Nachalas Tzvi questions Rokeach from the Gemara's inquiry whether one may eat using a cloth. The Gemara brings proof from the incident in which Rabbi Tzadok was given less than an egg's volume of food, wrapped it in a cloth, and ate it outside the sukkah. The Gemara asks whether this implies that an egg's volume requires washing, and rejects the proof by saying that perhaps an egg's volume requires a sukkah. Even according to this rejection, less than an egg's volume would not require washing. If the fact that he ate through a cloth itself implied that washing was required, Rokeach's ruling in siman 283 would be difficult, for there he writes that less than an egg's volume does not require washing, as stated in Sukkah. Yet there too the Gemara cites Rabbi Tzadok's use of a cloth. Moreover, Rokeach appears self-contradictory, ruling in siman 283 that washing is unnecessary and in siman 328 that one should wash because of doubt. In my humble opinion, all of this may be resolved. Rokeach precisely inferred that according to the rejection in Chullin, even for more than an egg's volume a cloth would suffice, because using a cloth is equivalent to washing. Otherwise, what does the Mishnah teach by saying that Rabbi Tzadok used a cloth? Surely it did not merely teach that he acted cleanly. Rather, by recording the cloth it teaches incidentally that even less than an egg's volume requires washing, since a cloth and washing serve the same function. In Sukkah 27, however, where the Gemara says that perhaps less than an egg's volume does not require washing, the use of a cloth cannot substitute for washing, as Tosafos explain there, s.v. ha. The Mishnah therefore teaches that less than an egg's volume does not require washing, as shown by his use of a cloth. Examine this carefully. Nachalas Tzvi's claim that Rokeach contradicts himself also overlooks Lechem Chamudos to Chullin, page 222, who explains that in siman 283 Rokeach discusses whether there is an obligation carrying a blessing of Al Netilas Yadayim. There remains a difficulty, however, because Rokeach writes there that Chullin implies that even one eating with a cloth requires washing. If so, Rabbi Tzadok's use of a cloth for less than an egg's volume would indicate that washing itself was unnecessary. Perhaps Rokeach means that because of doubt one must wash even when using a cloth, and here he rules stringently. Yet if so, he should have ruled that no blessing be recited. Moreover, the conclusion of Chullin implies that washing is certainly required, as appears below in siman 163 in Beit Yosef. It may be that even if less than an egg's volume requires washing, Rabbi Tzadok used a cloth because he was a kohen, and the Sages permitted a cloth for those eating terumah, as Tosafos write there. The mention of less than an egg's volume would then relate only to sukkah and the blessing. Although Tosafos in Yoma 79, s.v. ha, write that the allowance applies only to terumah and not to ordinary food, that is according to the Gemara's necessity in Sukkah, in the discussion of Abaye and Rav Yosef, to interpret less than an egg's volume as referring to washing. Where it is possible to say that the passage does not refer to washing, it is preferable to say that kohanim were permitted to use a cloth even for ordinary food. This also resolves the apparent contradiction between Tosafos in Chullin, s.v. mai, and Tosafos in Yoma. Examine this carefully.
[4] He does not need, etc. According to all opinions, he does not need to wash. Levush. So writes Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah. Lechem Chamudos writes there that a copying error in Beit Yosef caused this error in the Shulchan Aruch. The correct wording of Rokeach, siman 283, is that less than an egg's volume does not require washing. According to this, less than an olive's volume has the same law as less than an egg's volume: he should wash without a blessing. So write Nachalas Tzvi and Magen Avraham. In my humble opinion, the Shulchan Aruch did not intend this at all. Rather, he inferred from the earlier ruling of Rokeach, that for less than an egg's volume one washes without a blessing, that the novelty regarding less than an olive's volume is not merely that one washes without a blessing, but that no washing is required at all. This also appears from Bach, who evidently understood the Shulchan Aruch this way. Nevertheless, this is not a decisive proof for practical halachah, because had Rokeach taught the law only for less than an olive's volume, one might have thought that less than an egg's volume requires a blessing. I saw that Taz writes that Sukkah explicitly states that washing is unnecessary. This is astonishing, for it is not stated there, and all the great authorities cited above make clear that this is not explicit in the Gemara. There is also some proof from the fact that Rambam and the poskim link the law of washing to the law of the blessing Hamotzi, and below in siman 168 we rule that Hamotzi is recited even on less than an olive's volume. One should therefore be stringent and wash without a blessing. This also provides some support for Rama's responsum, siman 8, ruling that one who eats bread merely to sweeten a drink need not wash, since he does not recite Hamotzi, as explained below in siman 212. Mateh Moshe, siman 327, and Shelah, however, rule that washing is required, and this also appears to be the view of Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah.
