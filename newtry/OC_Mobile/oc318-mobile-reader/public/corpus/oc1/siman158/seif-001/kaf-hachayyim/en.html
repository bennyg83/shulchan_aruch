1) [Seif 1] When one comes to eat bread, etc., he should wash his hands, etc. Washing the hands for non-sacred food was instituted because of the concern arising from terumah, and also because it is a mitzvah to obey the words of the Sages. Chullin 106a. Rashi, of blessed memory, explains: it was necessary because of the concern arising from terumah, since hands have secondary impurity and disqualify terumah. Secondary impurity does not affect non-sacred food. Nevertheless, because of the concern arising from terumah, so that those who eat terumah will become accustomed to washing their hands, the practice applies to non-sacred food. The Taz, paragraph 1, cites him. Tosafos there explain that the reason it is a mitzvah to obey the words of the Sages is that they instituted washing for cleanliness; see there. One recites the blessing over netilas yadayim even though the obligation is only rabbinic, for one may say 'and commanded us' regarding a rabbinic requirement, since the Sages supported it with the prohibition, 'You shall not deviate.' Tur. The Beit Yosef writes that since the prohibition of 'You shall not deviate' commands us to obey the Sages, when we fulfill their words we fulfill Hashem's mitzvah. This is the reason for all the blessings that we recite over rabbinic mitzvos. See Halachos Ketanos, volume 1, siman 275, who writes that the Sages decreed impurity even upon the hands of a one-day-old infant; see there. We cite this below in siman 162, paragraph 31; see there.
