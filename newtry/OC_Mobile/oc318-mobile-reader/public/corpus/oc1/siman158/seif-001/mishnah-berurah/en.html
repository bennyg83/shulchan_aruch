(א) When he comes, etc. — the reason for the enactment of washing is from two matters: one, because of proximity to terumah — meaning, since hands are busy and touch everything, and in the time when impurity and purity were practiced and the priests ate terumah, they had to wash their hands by rabbinic law before eating terumah so they would not defile it by their touch; and so that priests would be accustomed to this, they also decreed upon every Israelite who eats bread that it is forbidden to eat until he washes his hands. And also now, when priests do not eat terumah because of impurity, this enactment was not annulled, so that Israelites would be accustomed when the Temple is rebuilt to eat in purity. And another reason for the enactment of washing is because of cleanliness and holiness; and they relied in the Gemara [Berachot 51] on the verse "and you shall sanctify yourselves and you shall be holy" [Semag].

(ב) To eat bread, etc. — excluding fruit, as below in seif 5. And the reason: because most terumah is only from types of bread, as it is written "the first of your grain," and the way to eat types of grain is only after making bread from them — they did not decree washing except in this manner [Levush].

(ג) That we bless upon it Hamotzi, etc. — for they enacted it only in a matter upon which one establishes a meal.

(ד) He shall wash his hands — both hands and in a vessel; and even if he eats only with one, we are concerned lest he also touch with the other [Acharonim]. And when he washes his hands, it is good that he wash the right first so the left serves the right.

(ה) Even if he does not know, etc. — and also they are not soiled.

(ו) And he shall bless al netilat yadayim — meaning "Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us regarding washing of hands"; and it is fit to say "and He commanded us" even though it is from the words of the Sages, for we are commanded to heed them, as written "upon the Torah that they teach you, you shall not turn aside," etc. [Gemara].
