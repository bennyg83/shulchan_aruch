(1) When one comes, etc. The enactment of washing has two reasons. First, it was instituted because of terumah. Since hands are active and touch everything, when the laws of tumah and taharah were observed and kohanim ate terumah, they were required by Rabbinic law to wash their hands before eating terumah so that they would not render it tamei through contact. To accustom the kohanim to this, the Sages also decreed that every Jew who eats bread may not eat until he washes his hands. Even now, when kohanim do not eat terumah because of tumah, this enactment was not annulled, so that the Jewish people will be accustomed to eating in a state of taharah when the Beis HaMikdash is rebuilt, speedily in our days. A second reason for the enactment is cleanliness and sanctity. The Gemara in Berachos 51 derives support from the verse, “You shall sanctify yourselves and be holy.” Semag.
(2) To eat bread, etc. This excludes fruit, as explained below in seif 5. The reason is that most terumah consists of grain products, as it is written, “the first of your grain,” and grain is ordinarily eaten only after it is made into bread. The Sages therefore enacted washing only in this manner. Levush.
(3) Over which one recites HaMotzi, etc. The enactment applies only to something upon which one establishes a meal.
(4) He should wash his hands. He must wash both hands and use a vessel. Even if he eats with only one hand, we are concerned that he may also touch the food with the other. Acharonim. When washing, it is preferable to wash the right hand first, so that the left serves the right.
(5) Even if he does not know, etc. This applies even when they are not soiled.
(6) And recite the blessing Al Netilas Yadayim. Namely: “Who sanctified us with His mitzvos and commanded us concerning washing the hands.” It is appropriate to say “and commanded us” even though this is a Rabbinic enactment, because we were commanded to heed the Sages, as it is written, “According to the Torah that they instruct you, you shall not deviate,” etc. Gemara.
(7) Thin rolls, etc. This will be explained below in siman 168, seif 6; see there. The ruling teaches that although these are made from the five species of grain, since he does not establish a meal upon them, washing is unnecessary. All the more so, bread made from other ingredients that are not among the five species never requires washing.
(8) And he does not establish a meal. If he establishes a meal upon them, however, he must wash and recite the blessing just as for bread, because he must also recite HaMotzi and Bircas HaMazon over them when establishing a meal, as explained below in siman 168, seif 8. The amount that constitutes establishing a meal will also be explained there, with Hashem’s help. If, when he began eating, he intended to eat only a small amount but later decided to eat the amount constituting a meal, he must wash his hands and recite HaMotzi, as will be explained there below in seif 6; see there.
