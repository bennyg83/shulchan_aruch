(1) [Seif 1] When he comes to eat bread, etc., he shall wash his hands, etc. Netilat yadayim — from Chullin because of proximity to terumah, and also because of the mitzvah to heed the words of the Sages. Chullin 106a. And Rashi explained: because of proximity to terumah it was required, for hands are second-degree and disqualify terumah, but for non-sacred food they do not have effect as second-degree; and because of proximity to terumah, that those who eat terumah would grow accustomed to wash their hands — it applies to non-sacred food, end of his words; and the Taz brought this in s.k. 1. And because of the mitzvah to heed the words of the Sages — Tosafot explained there that they enacted it because of cleanliness, see there. And one blesses on netilat yadayim even though it is only rabbinic, for it is fit to say "and He commanded us" regarding something rabbinic, and they relied it on the negative commandment "you shall not turn aside." Tur. And Beit Yosef wrote: meaning that since they filled in "you shall not turn aside," we are commanded to do the words of the Sages; when we fulfill the words of the Sages, behold we fulfill the mitzvot of Heaven — and this is the reason for all berachot we say on rabbinic mitzvot, end of his words. And see Leket Yosher ch. 1 siman 475, who wrote that even a one-day-old infant — they decreed impurity on his hands, see there; and Bahag later siman 162 s.k. 31, see there.**** END BLOCK ****
