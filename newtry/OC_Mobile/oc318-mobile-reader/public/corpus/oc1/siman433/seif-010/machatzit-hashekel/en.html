(א) (s.k. 10) But, etc. — and even if chametz remains there he does not transgress, for he declared it ownerless before its prohibition time; nevertheless if he finds chametz there after its prohibition time he must destroy it, as below siman 445. Because the essence of destroying chametz is burning or casting to the wind; when it lies in a courtyard dogs and animals will eat it. It is explained below siman 448 seif 6 in the name of Yerushalmi that after its prohibition time it is forbidden to give even to other animals or as ownerless; here although he does not give to them but they eat on their own, nevertheless l'chatchila it is good to destroy them, for doubt does not remove certainty — meaning: doubt "they ate it" does not remove certainty of chametz we know is there; as written at the beginning of siman 438: one saw a mouse enter with a loaf in its mouth and another enter after it and found crumbs even the size of the whole loaf — we do not say these crumbs are from this loaf; and since he found them again, no checking is required, for a mouse is not accustomed to crumble; therefore these crumbs are not from this loaf but from elsewhere, and the mouse entered with the loaf into the house — therefore checking is required. He infers from the unspecified wording that even a small loaf — and why not? when the loaf is large one could say "I ate it" — nevertheless for a small loaf one could say "I ate it"; or because the chametz is definite and "I ate it" is doubt, and doubt does not remove certainty. So too in the Gemara daf 9 on what we taught there: we are not concerned lest a weasel dragged from place to place — as Rashba wrote siman 434; and the Gemara inferred the reason: we do not see it take, but if we see it take, checking is required even if it says "I ate it" — because doubt "I ate it" does not remove certainty of chametz.

(ב) And it is difficult: if so what does it ask, etc. — daf 8 as above in s.k. 3: since Rava speaks specifically of possible chametz, perhaps the baraita that teaches one does not check by sunlight refers to a courtyard — his courtyard with definite chametz, and Rava agrees checking is required; on this the baraita said one does not check by sunlight; and although in the baraita it teaches one does not check with definite chametz, the language of checking is not appropriate but language of destroying — as Ran brought, Beit Yosef siman 436. Nevertheless in truth it is not now definite chametz, for perhaps they ate it, but we do not rely on this since we know definite chametz was there; if so the language of checking fits well; and although it is still difficult why one does not check by sunlight, for it is not worse than a portico — as regarding skylight, if alongside the skylight, etc. — this is another question; but the Gemara's question that asks "but Rava said middle of courtyard need not checking" remains.

(ג) Therefore even definite chametz need not checking, as stated in the Gemara, etc. — meaning: although doubt does not remove certainty, here there is certainly that they dragged it — this is the latter answer in the Gemara daf 9b; and although in the first answer there they do not say "certainly they dragged it," Rambam chapter 9 of Hilchot Tefillin ruled like the first answer — perhaps the Gemara and Semak do not hold like Rambam, and poskim hold like the latter answer that they say "certainly they dragged it." Further, Rambam did not rule like the first answer except because of doubt we go stringently, as Kessef Mishneh wrote there in chapter 9 of Hilchot Tefillin; but here regarding bedikat chametz which is d'rabbanan — d'oraisa nullification suffices — perhaps Rambam also agrees we go leniently and say "certainly they dragged it," for according to one answer Tosafot daf 9a in Pesachim s.v. so that it be, etc.: if he nullified, which is d'rabbanan, we say doubt comes and removes certainty of d'rabbanan.

(ד) If so, in the mouse case it is well — meaning in siman 438 and Gemara daf 9 we well say doubt "it ate it" does not remove certainty, although certainly it dragged it — what of it? it nevertheless remained in his house, for mice drag into their holes.

(ה) But ravens, etc. — meaning here in a courtyard we rely on ravens; and perhaps what Semak wrote is Shulchan Aruch's words "but definite chametz, no" — its source is not Semak; it appears to Magen Avraham strained to explain Semak speaks of birds found in a courtyard, for it is not a poskim's way to be cryptic — therefore he explained Semak really speaks in every case. What he wrote "but definite chametz, no" — meaning we do not say "they ate it"; here we speak where we say ravens ate it, and nevertheless we say they dragged it; on this he said nevertheless there is a difference if birds are found in the courtyard — we do not say "they dragged it." Chayei Adam asked: if so Magen Avraham's question returns on Bach in its place — what does it ask on Rava, perhaps the baraita speaks of definite chametz and birds found in the courtyard. In my humble opinion it is resolved: one — per what Chayei Adam himself wrote to settle what Magen Avraham wrote on Rambam who reads middle of courtyard need not checking — implying the sides require checking; if so what does it ask on Rava — it should have said the baraita speaks of the sides of the courtyard. Chayei Adam answered per Ran on Rambam's view: Ran — a courtyard need not checking; middle of courtyard — but holes require checking; so too Rambam — end; and since Rambam only exempts courtyard holes, the question no longer applies — it should have said the baraita speaks of courtyard holes, because there is no sunlight and the language "one does not check by sunlight" is not relevant — end Chayei Adam; with this Magen Avraham's words are also resolved — now the Gemara need not establish the baraita as definite chametz and birds found in the courtyard, for then the language "one does not check by sunlight" is irrelevant — necessarily if they ate it, what will he check, and if they did not eat it, certainly they dragged to their holes, as Rashi wrote daf 9b, and there is no sunlight, as explained. Also per Bach's words to settle Rambam who said middle of courtyard, etc. — Magen Avraham's words can be explained there. However, per Chayei Adam's understanding of Magen Avraham's words it remains difficult: "still it should be difficult — perhaps it speaks of definite chametz in a place where ravens are not found, only birds" — implying Magen Avraham agrees where both birds and ravens are found, no checking is required; therefore the baraita must be established where ravens are not found, only birds, and the law with him that where birds and ravens are found no checking is required because it is a double doubt: perhaps they ate it, and if you say they did not eat, nevertheless they dragged it — and whence that the birds in the courtyard dragged it, perhaps ravens dragged elsewhere. Accordingly it is difficult: the baraita should not speak where there are no ravens, only birds — for then how do we hang this question on Magen Avraham's words, for in any case it is difficult, for Rava gives the reason that a courtyard need not checking because ravens are found there — therefore the baraita should be established where ravens are not found; but one may say: do you take ravens from elsewhere? Even where ravens are not found, other birds wander after their food and one may rely on them; Rava mentioned ravens because in the amoraim's locale ravens were found, as they say regarding meat hidden from sight that perhaps ravens exchanged it — and there too ravens are not precise. If so, if we establish the baraita where there are no ravens or other birds coming from elsewhere, we find this only in a covered courtyard where a bird from elsewhere cannot enter; with this the language "one does not check by sunlight" is irrelevant, since when covered there is no sun, and even by daylight one cannot check. Also from what Bach wrote regarding a place where they regularly eat, etc. — this difference applies also to Magen Avraham's words, for Magen Avraham did not disagree with him except regarding ravens that we say "certainly dragged elsewhere," unlike here where it speaks of a house where ravens are not relevant but only birds in the house that drag to their holes, and one can only rely that they ate, and it is doubt that does not remove certainty. So too seif 8, etc. — "this is in general," etc. — behold although definite chametz requires a hoe and shovel, nevertheless in general nullification suffices; therefore although we know chametz was used there, nevertheless it is not called definite chametz, as Magen Avraham wrote, for there is much doubt.

(ו) And what Bach wrote in the name of Rambam is not precise, etc. — meaning Bach came to support his answer that with definite chametz there is a difference: in his own courtyard forbidden, in partners' courtyard permitted, as explained, and brought proof from Rambam: Bach — it appears to me that for this Rambam was precise and wrote regarding a cattle shed and coops and the like that definite chametz requires checking, and did not write so regarding a courtyard — because the matter is not clear regarding a courtyard, since when not designated to one person, no checking is required — end; Magen Avraham wrote this refers to storehouses: Rambam chapter 2 law 16 — holes of the house, etc., roof, cattle shed, coops, hayloft, wine and oil storehouses from which one does not take mid-meal, large fish houses — no checking unless chametz was brought in — end; this "unless" when he adjoined storehouses, etc., means there no checking is not because we rely birds ate it, but because one does not take mid-meal — therefore we say he did not bring chametz there; on this he said specifically from the general case, but if he knows he brought chametz there, although one does not take mid-meal, checking is required; but regarding a shed, etc., where animals ate it, Rambam did not speak at all whether there is a difference between possible and definite chametz.

(ז) And perhaps all thirty days, etc. — accordingly what he wrote in s.k. 6 before this, and where they eat all year and there are no chickens there — one must check under benches — implying if there are chickens, no checking and sweeping suffices, as he wrote before in the name of Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, Ri'az, Rashba, and Kol Bo; therefore it must mean within thirty days they did not eat there, or he was careful chametz did not fall under benches — otherwise even if there are chickens, checking is required, as he wrote here that doubt "they ate it" does not remove certainty. And I am dealing with siman 465: how should one conduct if he feeds his animal barley on Pesach.
