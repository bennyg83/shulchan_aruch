Where a person's hand cannot reach. The Tur wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that this means if it is ten handbreadths high, and the Tur objected that, on the contrary, the main use is at a height of ten handbreadths. See the later authorities, who labored to resolve the Yerushalmi. It appears to me that the Yerushalmi speaks only of something ten handbreadths above a person's stature, so that a person's hand cannot reach there. This is also implied there in the Yerushalmi, similar to the pit and cistern taught there, which are below a person's stature. See below, siman 438, regarding a loaf on a beam and a loaf in a pit.
They do not require checking. But if children are commonly present, even low places require checking. Likewise, if we know that chametz was used in them, they require checking. So write the later authorities.
