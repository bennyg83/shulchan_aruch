(There, seif katan 10.) What does the Gemara ask, "but," etc.? In Pesachim 8 we say: Rava said, what is this case of sunlight? If we say in a courtyard, but he said a courtyard does not require checking, etc., and therefore it is permitted. This requires study: what proof is brought from Rava? Nothing is proven from Rava, for perhaps he speaks of doubtful chametz, and therefore we rely on the possibility that ravens will come there and eat it; but with definite chametz, doubt does not remove certainty, and perhaps ravens will not come there at all. Even if one says ravens are ordinarily found in a courtyard, the assumption that ravens are certainly found there and certainly dragged it away is not proven at all from Rava. At present this requires study.
What the Magen Avraham wrote, "and perhaps what the Semak wrote," etc., appears difficult, for according to this the question returns to its place: what does the Gemara ask from Rava? Perhaps here it is definite chametz and ravens do not come from elsewhere to here. The later authorities asked this as well. It appears to my humble mind that this interpretation applies specifically to the Semak's case. But there, where it teaches "one does not check by sunlight," it must be speaking of a courtyard exposed to the open air, where ravens presumably come from elsewhere. Yet in my humble opinion, the Semak's intent is that definite chametz means where actual chametz certainly lies before us, so that only the language of removal applies; in such a case one cannot rely on ravens and the like. But in the latter clause, where the language is checking, as in "one does not check by sunlight," it cannot speak of such a case. Rather, the chametz is not before us, but we know that chametz certainly fell there because the place was used. In this case we say the middle of a courtyard needs no checking, meaning he need not search after it, because presumably it was already eaten or dragged away. The Magen Avraham asks properly according to the Bach's view, who holds that it is called definite chametz where we know the place was used.
