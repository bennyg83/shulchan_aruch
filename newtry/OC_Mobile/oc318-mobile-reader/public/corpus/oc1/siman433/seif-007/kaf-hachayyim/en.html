This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 2, halachah 4, and implies that sunlight suffices only after the fact, whereas initially the portico must be searched by candlelight at night. Kesef Mishneh there writes likewise. Bach writes that there are three opinions. According to Rashi and Rif, even on the thirteenth one may initially search a portico by sunlight. According to Rambam, even on the fourteenth one should initially search only by candlelight, as is also clear from Sefer Mitzvos Gadol and Sefer Mitzvos Katan. According to Rabbeinu Yerucham, and apparently Rosh, one may not search it on the thirteenth, but on the fourteenth one may initially search by sunlight. This implies that according to Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham, if one searched it by sunlight on the thirteenth, one must search it again at night. Bach concludes that we adopt the stringent position: only if one searched it after the fact by sunlight on the fourteenth is that sufficient. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 3, however, explains the Shulchan Aruch to mean that initially one must search by candlelight on the night of the fourteenth, but if one failed to do so, one may initially search the portico by sunlight on the fourteenth. He writes that this is contrary to Bach. See there. Levush likewise appears to hold that on the fourteenth one may initially search it by sunlight. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 2; Peri Chadash; Chok Yaakov, subsection 3; Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 3; Mekor Chaim, novellae, subsection 2, write likewise. Birkei Yosef, subsection 2, Chukas HaPesach, subsection 2, and Kisei Eliyahu, subsection 1, however, explain the Shulchan Aruch to mean that even on the fourteenth one should initially search it by candlelight. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, rules according to the first opinion, that one may initially search it without a candle. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Tzav, subsection 3, rules likewise, and this appears to be the opinion of the later authorities. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there that even on a cloudy day one may search a portico without candlelight.
