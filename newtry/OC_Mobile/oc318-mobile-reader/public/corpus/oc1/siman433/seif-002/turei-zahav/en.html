One does not check by torchlight. See Tur for the reason — it appears here it is forbidden l'chatchila but not to invalidate b'dieved, unlike sunlight and moonlight which are not called checking at all and do not help even b'dieved; and so Tur's language implies at the beginning: for sunlight and moonlight one must use candlelight, which teaches even b'dieved; but for a torch he wrote "one does not check," implying l'chatchila not, but b'dieved it is also called checking. Therefore what he wrote afterward — "therefore Ashkenazim practice only with a wax candle" — implies custom; and since he spoke regarding torch law, it follows they are comparable: although the baraita states both in one clause "one does not check by sunlight nor by torch because candlelight is good for checking," nevertheless from "because candlelight is good" it implies it is good but not to invalidate; and automatically for what the baraita first said "one does not check by sunlight" it refers even b'dieved; and what he said in another clause "nor by torch" means l'chatchila, and on that alone he said at the end "because candlelight is good for checking" — from this language it is proven l'chatchila but not to invalidate, as I wrote.
