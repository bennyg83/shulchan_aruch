1) [Se'if 1] The search must be performed by candlelight, etc. The Sages supported this requirement with a verse, as stated above in siman 431, subsection 6.
2) There. It must be performed by candlelight, etc. The search must be performed on the night of the fourteenth. Even if one searched by candlelight during the day of the thirteenth, one must search again on the night of the fourteenth in accordance with the enactment of our Sages, because candlelight is effective for searching then and people are present in their homes, as explained above in siman 431, subsection 4. This is the consensus of the later authorities. If, however, one searched on the night of the thirteenth and was then meticulously careful not to bring in any more chametz, no additional search is necessary. Levush, se'if 11, and Bach nevertheless write that even if one searched on the night of the thirteenth, one must search again. Their conclusion is not compelling. If one wishes to be stringent, one should at least not recite another blessing and thereby enter a possible doubt concerning a blessing. One may, however, resume using chametz in the area and then recite a blessing and search again. Chok Yaakov, subsection 1; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Mekor Chaim, novellae, subsection 1. This will be explained further below in se'if 11. See there.
3) There. If one transgressed, etc. Initially, it is forbidden to search during the day, even by candlelight in a dark house. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Peri Chadash.
4) There. When one searches during the day of the fourteenth, etc. One should search in the morning, for those who are diligent perform mitzvos promptly. Chok Yaakov, subsection 3; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2; Mekor Chaim, novellae, subsection 2. One should not eat until after searching. Eliyah Rabbah, there. See above, siman 431, se'if 2, and our words there in subsection 12.
5) There. Only by candlelight. This is necessary because of holes and cracks. Levush.
6) There. And a portico, etc. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, explains that this means a structure built upon columns without a wall, namely, the Roman portico mentioned in the Gemara. An ordinary portico, however, has three walls, as explained in Bava Basra 11b, Tosafos there beginning with the word 'Here,' Menachos 33 in the chapter HaKometz, and Yoreh De'ah, siman 286. Since the Shulchan Aruch here refers simply to a portico, it likewise discusses one with three walls. It nevertheless has abundant light because its fourth side is completely open. Ri'az in Shiltei HaGibborim explicitly gives this explanation of a portico. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. Birkei Yosef, subsection 3, agrees and answers Chok Yosef's objection to Chok Yaakov, writing that Ri'az, Tashbetz, and Orchos Chaim explicitly state that a three-walled portico may be searched by its natural light. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, rules likewise.
7) There. If one searched it by sunlight, that is sufficient. This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 2, halachah 4, and implies that sunlight suffices only after the fact, whereas initially the portico must be searched by candlelight at night. Kesef Mishneh there writes likewise. Bach writes that there are three opinions. According to Rashi and Rif, even on the thirteenth one may initially search a portico by sunlight. According to Rambam, even on the fourteenth one should initially search only by candlelight, as is also clear from Sefer Mitzvos Gadol and Sefer Mitzvos Katan. According to Rabbeinu Yerucham, and apparently Rosh, one may not search it on the thirteenth, but on the fourteenth one may initially search by sunlight. This implies that according to Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham, if one searched it by sunlight on the thirteenth, one must search it again at night. Bach concludes that we adopt the stringent position: only if one searched it after the fact by sunlight on the fourteenth is that sufficient. See there. Magen Avraham, subsection 3, however, explains the Shulchan Aruch to mean that initially one must search by candlelight on the night of the fourteenth, but if one failed to do so, one may initially search the portico by sunlight on the fourteenth. He writes that this is contrary to Bach. See there. Levush likewise appears to hold that on the fourteenth one may initially search it by sunlight. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef, subsection 2; Peri Chadash; Chok Yaakov, subsection 3; Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 3; Mekor Chaim, novellae, subsection 2, write likewise. Birkei Yosef, subsection 2, Chukas HaPesach, subsection 2, and Kisei Eliyahu, subsection 1, however, explain the Shulchan Aruch to mean that even on the fourteenth one should initially search it by candlelight. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, rules according to the first opinion, that one may initially search it without a candle. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Tzav, subsection 3, rules likewise, and this appears to be the opinion of the later authorities. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes there that even on a cloudy day one may search a portico without candlelight.
8) There. If one searched it by sunlight, that is sufficient. This applies specifically to a portico. Another house that is not a portico must be searched by candlelight even if it has abundant light. Bigdei Yesha on Mordechai, chapter 1 of Pesachim, subsection 28, in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. See there, and see Beis Yosef. Bach, however, writes that the same law applies to any place having abundant light. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 37, likewise writes that other houses and rooms with many windows and abundant light have the law of a portico.
8) One who has only a portico to search, which does not require candlelight, nevertheless recites the blessing over eliminating chametz. Similarly, if one is in a place where no candle is available and must search the house during the day, one recites the blessing. Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 194. This applies when the house has abundant light like a portico, enabling one to see into the holes and cracks, as explained above. Otherwise, one has not fulfilled the mitzvah in its prescribed manner and cannot recite a blessing. See below, subsection 82.
9) There. The same applies directly beneath a skylight in a room. This means a window in the roof, as stated in the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 10 of Ohalos and at the beginning of chapter 5 of Beitzah. Rabbeinu Yerucham therefore writes that one may not search directly opposite an ordinary window, since a window is in the wall. The Tur, however, maintains that one may search even opposite a window in the wall, and this also appears from Rambam. Magen Avraham, subsection 4. Levush likewise writes that no candle is required directly opposite a window. Chok Yaakov, subsection 4; Mekor Chaim, novellae, subsection 3; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, agree. If the window contains a glass pane, all opinions agree that one may not search opposite it without a candle. Magen Avraham, there; Chok Yaakov, there; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Mekor Chaim, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. Therefore, one must open the window when searching opposite it without a candle.
10) There. The same applies directly beneath a skylight, etc. At the sides, however, a candle is required. Tur; Levush; Chemed Moshe, there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3. Any area not directly opposite the window opening is included among the sides and requires a candle. This is also explicit in Rashi's explanation. Bach; Zechor L'Avraham. This also appears from Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4.
