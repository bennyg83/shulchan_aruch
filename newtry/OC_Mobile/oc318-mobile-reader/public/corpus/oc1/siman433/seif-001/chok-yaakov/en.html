It must be by the light of a lamp. And this means on the night of the fourteenth. Even if he checked on the day of the thirteenth by the light of a lamp, he must go back and check on the night of the fourteenth, in accordance with the enactment of the Sages, for lamplight is effective for checking and people are found in their homes, as is explained in the Beit Yosef in the name of the early poskim and in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 133, and this is also the consensus of the later authorities. However, if he checked on the night of the thirteenth and was very careful not to bring in any more chametz, he is not one who is diligent to his own loss, and he need not check again. This is clear from the implication of the early poskim, from the Magen Avraham at the end of this siman, and from the Olas Shabbos at the beginning of siman 431 and in this siman. But the Levush and the Bach went too far, saying that even if he checked on the night of the thirteenth he must go back and check. He derived this from the Mordechai in the first chapter of Pesachim in the name of the Yerushalmi. In truth, his words are not compelling, for the Yerushalmi is speaking of the night of the thirteenth; on the contrary, the Olas Shabbos at the beginning of siman 431 proves the opposite from the Yerushalmi. Furthermore, the Mordechai himself concludes, however, in our Talmud, etc.; thus the Mordechai himself explicitly wrote that our Talmud disagrees with the Yerushalmi, and we have a rule everywhere that the halacha follows our Talmud against the Yerushalmi. Although the Bach presses to reconcile our Talmud with the Yerushalmi, nevertheless it appears to me proven from several passages that checking on the night of the thirteenth is effective. First, from what the Gemara says on 4b: they asked, if one rents a house to his fellow with the presumption that it has been checked. Perforce this must be speaking on the thirteenth, for on the fourteenth the obligation to check is on the landlord. If so, why say with the presumption that it has been checked? Tosafot there explicitly wrote this, as did the other commentators, though see the Ran, who wrote that there is another explanation of this. Now, if you say that one who checked on the night of the thirteenth must check again, what was the question whether this is a mistaken transaction? In any event he must check again. Further, from what the Gemara says there on 9b: Abaye said, there is no difficulty, this is on the fourteenth and this is on the thirteenth. If so, according to the Yerushalmi's view that one does not distinguish between one checking and another, checking on the thirteenth would not be effective. There is certainly no basis to say that it speaks specifically of one leaving on a journey, who is permitted to check earlier. Rather, certainly, where he checked properly by lamplight on the night of the thirteenth, he has fulfilled the obligation and need not check again. If he wishes to be stringent upon himself, he should in any case not recite a blessing, so as not to enter a concern of a doubtful blessing. Rather, he should again use chametz inside it, and then he can go back, recite a blessing, and check. So it appears to me.
And an achsadra. The Magen Avraham wrote that this means one built upon pillars and having no wall, end quote. That is the Roman achsadra mentioned in the Talmud. But an ordinary achsadra has three walls, as is explained in the Gemara in Bava Basra 11b, in Tosafot there, s.v. hacha, in the chapter HaKometz in Menachos 33, and in Yoreh De'ah, Hilchos Mezuzah, siman 286. If so, here, where it mentions an achsadra without qualification, it is also speaking of such a case. It too has much light, since the fourth side is entirely open. I do not know from where he derived another explanation of the achsadra here. So too it is explicit in Riaz in Shiltei HaGibborim, in the explanation of achsadra, as I wrote.
If he checked it by sunlight, it is sufficient. Even initially, if he did not check on the night of the fourteenth, he checks an achsadra by daylight. Nevertheless, initially one should be careful to check on the night of the fourteenth. So write the later authorities. It is explained in the Gemara there that one who did not check on the night of the fourteenth and checks by day should check in the morning, because the diligent hasten to mitzvos.
The same applies opposite a skylight in a room. A skylight refers to an opening in the middle of the roof. Regarding opposite a window in the wall, the Magen Avraham cited two opinions whether one may check opposite it by its light or not. It appears to me that the primary view is that one may check by its light, as by a skylight. There is some proof to this from the Gemara's discussion of what the Mishnah taught, not by sunlight, where it asks, what is the case? It does not simply establish it as speaking opposite a window in a room. Rather, certainly, it was checked by its light, as by a skylight. The Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that if the window has glass partitions, all agree that it is forbidden to check opposite it. End quote. That the Gemara did not establish it this way must be because this is obvious, and what novelty would it be teaching us?
