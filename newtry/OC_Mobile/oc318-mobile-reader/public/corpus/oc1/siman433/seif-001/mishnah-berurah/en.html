(א) By candlelight — on the night of the 14th, when people are in their homes and candlelight is then good for checking, unlike by day when light does not shine as at night; the Sages fixed checking specifically by candlelight so one can check also in holes and cracks. If he checked early on the 13th by candlelight, he must return and check on the night of the 14th per the Sages' enactment; nevertheless it appears one should not bless then on bedikat chametz, for some hold b'dieved he fulfilled with the first checking. However, if he checked on the night of the 13th by candlelight — at night candlelight is good for checking — and was very careful not to bring chametz, he need not check again — most Acharonim agree; one who wishes to be stringent and check again should at least not bless then.

(ב) And if he passed — but l'chatchila it is forbidden to postpone checking to the day of the 14th, as above.

(ג) But by candlelight — through it he can search well in hiding places, holes, and cracks.

(ד) And a portico — a place with three walls and a roof, with great light through a fourth side completely open.

(ה) If he checked by sunlight — this too refers when he passed and did not check on the night of the 14th and checks on the 14th; for a portico whose light is great he need not use a candle, sunlight suffices. Shulchan Aruch's b'dieved language is because l'chatchila even a portico requires checking on the night of the 14th. It is explained in Shas that one who did not check at night and checks by day checks in the morning because the diligent precede mitzvot, and he should not eat before checking (Acharonim).

(ו) It suffices — regarding whether one may check on the 13th by its light: l'chatchila forbidden, he must wait for the night of the 14th; b'dieved it appears he need not check again — see Magen Avraham end of siman who wrote nevertheless one should return and check at least one room at night — see there.

(ז) Opposite the skylight in a room — skylight means what is in the middle of the roof; likewise opposite windows in walls; specifically opposite skylight and window itself where light is great, unlike from the sides where there is also light but not like a portico; even opposite the window itself — only when there is no glass; with glass, its law is like the sides.
