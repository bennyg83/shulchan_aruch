6) There. And a portico, etc. Magen Avraham, subsection 2, explains that this means a structure built upon columns without a wall, namely, the Roman portico mentioned in the Gemara. An ordinary portico, however, has three walls, as explained in Bava Basra 11b, Tosafos there beginning with the word 'Here,' Menachos 33 in the chapter HaKometz, and Yoreh De'ah, siman 286. Since the Shulchan Aruch here refers simply to a portico, it likewise discusses one with three walls. It nevertheless has abundant light because its fourth side is completely open. Ri'az in Shiltei HaGibborim explicitly gives this explanation of a portico. Chok Yaakov, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1. Birkei Yosef, subsection 3, agrees and answers Chok Yosef's objection to Chok Yaakov, writing that Ri'az, Tashbetz, and Orchos Chaim explicitly state that a three-walled portico may be searched by its natural light. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, rules likewise.
