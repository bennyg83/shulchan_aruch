(ב) And this is from doubt. Since he said no checking is needed — from doubt; and Tosafot there s.v. here, etc.; and there 9b — the reason we do not see, etc. — what is this, etc.; as explained in Yerushalmi I will write below.

(ג) But below, etc. Although Acharonim resolved, nevertheless they appear as divided views; as Rama wrote: Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 3 s.k. 20 — see Hagahot Gra chapter 2 s.k. 3 s.v. Ribak was accustomed to forbid throwing chametz in a completely ownerless place, for we see in Temurah that all items forbidden in benefit require burial — meaning even for those who hold like the rabbis, nevertheless burial is required, or anything that destroys it from the world; he brings proof from Yerushalmi: one may not feed chametz even to dogs — how are we situated? If to his dog, that is benefit; rather even to other dogs; and what we taught "he crumbles" is because they dilute it with water before it reaches prohibited eating. However, before prohibition time one may throw it anywhere ravens are found, since it is not yet forbidden in benefit — meaning both for Rabbenu Yehudah and the rabbis, all is after it became forbidden in benefit, and that is what is discussed that a courtyard need not checking; and if chametz is found in a courtyard after its prohibition time, it is forbidden to leave it so ravens will take it, for the essence of destroying chametz is burning — meaning for Rabbenu Yehudah and the rabbis, nevertheless burial is required, as explained; therefore Shulchan Aruch wrote siman 445: although he holds like the rabbis, he permits even in a courtyard from what he said "and in this is discussed courtyard," etc.; and he learned from possible chametz to definite; so too "and if chametz is found in a courtyard," etc., unlike what Taz wrote there; one cannot say Semak and Shulchan Aruch here speak after prohibition time, for here it discusses the night of the 14th; but Semak's and Shulchan Aruch's words here are primary, as explained; Hagahot Maimoniyot specifically in a completely ownerless place on Rosh Hashanah — his words are correct; nevertheless if found after prohibition time, one need not destroy — so too Yerushalmi chapter 2 halacha 2: there is a tannaitic version for you — you do not see; you see — you see in the plaza; there is a tannaitic version for you — even in the plaza; one version: you do not see; you see — you see in the plaza — when he declared ownerless before destroying it; one version: even in the plaza — when he declared ownerless after destroying it; one version: even on Pesach one need not destroy when he declared ownerless before prohibition time, and even for eating it is permitted after Pesach, for they forbade in Yerushalmi only one who declares ownerless within his house because of trickery; so Shulchan Aruch wrote siman 441 s.k. 5 in a Jewish house; Rosh learned from ownerlessness — see there. All this on Rosh Hashanah; but in a courtyard, even before prohibition, it is forbidden.
