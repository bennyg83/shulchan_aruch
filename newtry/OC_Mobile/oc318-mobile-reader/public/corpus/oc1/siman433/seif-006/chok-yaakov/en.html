And likewise a chicken coop. The same applies in a house: if chickens are commonly present there, it does not require checking. One should sweep beneath the benches and beneath the beds. So the later authorities wrote, and so too my brother-in-law the Rav wrote in his work Eliyah Zuta according to the words of Mahari Weil, unlike the Olas Shabbos. Such is the custom.
And likewise the middle of a courtyard. This is the language of the Rambam. The Ran wrote that this comes to exclude holes in the courtyard. One must say that it does not come to exclude the sides, for if so there would be a difficulty on the sugya at the beginning of Pesachim, which says: not by sunlight, what is the case? If you say in a courtyard, but Rava said that a courtyard does not require checking. Why did it not answer that it is speaking of a courtyard and at the sides? Rather, certainly, even the Rambam agrees that the sides do not require checking. It only comes to exclude holes in the courtyard, which require checking. The reason the Gemara did not answer this way is that there sunlight is not present. All this appears to me simple and clear in the words of the Ran as the intent of the Rambam, unlike the Bach, who explained that the Rambam's view is against the implication of the Gemara and the other poskim.
And this applies only to doubtful chametz, but not to definite chametz. So the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Semak, but I labored and did not find this in his words. Only in the Agudah, in the first chapter of Pesachim, is it written in this language: that a courtyard does not require checking means regarding doubtful chametz, but definite chametz must be removed. End quote. So too Mahari Weil wrote in siman 192. In Hagahos Maimoniyos it is written: Before the time of its prohibition, one may throw it wherever ravens are commonly found, since it is not yet forbidden in benefit, and this is the case in which the courtyard does not require checking. But if one finds chametz after the time of its prohibition, he may not leave it there so that the ravens will take it, for the essential removal of chametz is burning. End quote. At first glance there seem to be two contradictory opinions here, for from Hagahos Maimoniyos it is clear that even definite chametz does not require checking, and one may throw it there even initially, unlike the view of the Agudah. The Rama therefore noticed in his glosses on the Beit Yosef that the Beit Yosef's rulings here and in siman 445 contradict one another. The later authorities labored greatly to resolve the Beit Yosef's view and entered into great difficulties because of this question. It is even more astonishing regarding the Beit Yosef, for after citing in this siman the language of the Semak, he wrote the language of Hagahos Maimoniyos and did not notice any contradiction between them. Furthermore, according to the Agudah there is a difficulty: what did the Gemara ask, what is the case? If you say in a courtyard, but Rava said that a courtyard does not require checking. Perhaps it is indeed a courtyard and definite chametz. The Magen Avraham noticed this question and pressed to resolve it according to the Agudah, but he did not resolve it at all, for it would still be difficult: perhaps it speaks of definite chametz in a place where ravens are not commonly found, only birds. Examine what the Magen Avraham wrote. However, when one examines well the language of the Agudah and Mahari Weil, which is like the language of the Semak cited by the Beit Yosef, one sees that there is no difficulty at all and no contradiction whatever in the words of the early authorities. Their words must be read precisely: at first they wrote that chametz does not require checking, and at the end they wrote that it must be removed. Rather, the explanation is this: that a courtyard does not require checking refers to doubtful chametz, for we assume that the ravens and birds will eat it. But if there is definite chametz, and the time of removal has arrived, we do not say that they need not remove it at the time of removal and may rely on the birds eating it. Rather, they must remove it, exactly like the view of Hagahos Maimoniyos. But before the time of removal, it is certainly permitted to leave chametz there relying that they will eat it, as in the language of Hagahos Maimoniyos which he rules below in siman 445. The reason they mention doubtful chametz at the beginning is that the term checking applies only to a doubt, for with definite chametz, what language of checking applies? So too the Beit Yosef wrote below in siman 438; rather, there is only preparation to remove it the next day. This is also necessary and explicit in what Mahari Weil wrote in siman 153: the middle of the courtyard does not require checking, but if there is definite chametz there it must be removed, as stated in Hagahos Maimoniyos. Thus he explicitly combined the two opinions together, as I wrote. The later authorities erred in this because the Beit Yosef abbreviated the citation of the Semak, so they understood that they were divided opinions and challenged the Beit Yosef. In truth, it is all one opinion, joined together without any separation at all. Blessed is Hashem, Who enlightened my eyes in this to understand the words of the early authorities; they left me room to distinguish myself in this matter, and it is simple.
