(א) Chicken coop. Likewise the floor of the house where chickens are found — no checking (Beit Yosef, Ri'az, Rashba); and Mahari Weil wrote one must sweep under the beds because sometimes chametz is dragged there in chickens' mouths or another reason — end; it appears his intent: a small piece chickens eat, but we fear a large piece remains that chickens cannot eat — therefore sweeping suffices without checking. And where they eat all year and there are no chickens, check under benches — impossible chametz did not fall — it is like clear chametz in a pit (Mahari Weil).

(ב) But below. If so, here in Shulchan Aruch it also refers after prohibition time; Bach wrote below: in a courtyard where many participate it is like an ownerless courtyard — even if chametz remains he does not transgress; but in his own courtyard, if there was definite chametz, checking is required, for doubt does not remove certainty, as siman 438; so in the Gemara daf 100, etc.; for this reason checking is required; in a room where they regularly eat, even if chickens are there, it is definite chametz and "perhaps they ate it" — doubt does not remove certainty — end. Difficulty: if so, what does the Gemara ask from Rava "middle of courtyard need not check" — perhaps here definite chametz; rather, even definite chametz need not check, as the Gemara: regarding eating there is doubt perhaps they did not eat, but regarding dragging we say "certainly they dragged it." If so, in the mouse case, even if it dragged, it remained in the house; but ravens come from elsewhere and go elsewhere and drag elsewhere — therefore permitted. Perhaps Semak's words "but definite chametz, no" mean we do not rely on "perhaps they ate"; if birds are found in the courtyard, checking is required; but if ravens come from elsewhere here, we say "certainly dragged elsewhere." Also Bach's words regarding a place where they regularly eat — definite chametz — it appears only if they ate close to Pesach; but if they ate long before Pesach and close to Pesach it was guarded that chametz not fall there, no checking, for it is unreasonable that if we saw a mouse drag chametz to a place where chametz was not brought long before Pesach, checking would be required — then all holes where mice are found, even high and low, would require checking, impossible that mice did not drag chametz there; rather there are many such doubts — perhaps dragged elsewhere, perhaps ate, perhaps it became repulsive and unfit for a dog, perhaps was swept, etc. siman 438 — this is not called definite chametz, as written: a wall used with chametz, etc. — these matters are in general, etc. Bach in the name of Rambam and Semag was not precise in their language — it refers to storehouses, not cattle sheds and coops; perhaps all thirty days are called close to Pesach — therefore Maharil wrote not to throw grain to chickens in a damp place thirty days before Pesach — see siman 465.
