(א) Chicken coop — likewise floor of house where chickens are found — no checking; nevertheless sweep under beds, for sometimes a large piece of chametz is dragged there by chickens that they cannot eat, or another reason. Where they eat all year and no chickens — all agree check under benches and tables where chametz commonly falls; some say even with chickens, if they ate chametz close to Pesach it is like definite chametz — impossible chametz did not fall on the ground, and chickens' eating does not remove certainty — checking required as law.

(ב) Middle of courtyard — not specifically middle — same law for sides of courtyard; it comes to exclude only holes in sides of courtyard that require checking.

(ג) But definite chametz — for definite chametz we do not rely on this — checking required, for doubt does not remove certainty; all the more where they place geese in their coop and put outside a vessel with oat and barley stalks — kernels fall outside and they cannot eat there — definitely requires checking, for this is real chametz when kernels are soaked in water (Chayei Adam).

(ד) No — see Magen Avraham: his view is chametz brought long before Pesach, guarded close to Pesach that chametz not fall — no checking; perhaps all thirty days are called close to Pesach; Mekor Chayim holds only what was on the night of the 14th after checking time arrived — then we do not rely birds will eat; but what was definitely before that is not called definite chametz — no checking.

(ה) But below — Acharonim elaborated to resolve the contradiction; for halacha several agree: in one's courtyard or partners' courtyard it is forbidden to throw chametz relying ravens will eat; b'dieved if thrown, must check and destroy; but ownerless place for many, like a street — permitted to throw chametz and declare it ownerless before prohibition time; if ravens did not eat and it remains after prohibition time, we are not concerned since it left his possession — like selling to a non-Jew before prohibition time.
