(א) Used with chametz — meaning regularly used, not known that chametz is there now.
(ב) Even if not three handbreadths for a dog to dig — meaning still within dog-digging height, smell reaches three handbreadths; we might fear a dog will dig during Pesach and reveal it — nevertheless no checking required, etc.
(ג) Need not check — nevertheless must nullify in heart lest chametz be there and he transgress; if he did not nullify until after prohibition time, world practice on Shabbat is he must search with hoe and shovel — see Beit Meir: Rashi and Ran agree; Tosafot are doubtful — see there.
(ד) Scorpion danger — specifically where scorpions are found; perhaps where snakes are found one must check, for harm is not so common (Magen Avraham and Eshel Avraham); see siman 104 what we wrote regarding snakes that kill — law like scorpions.
(ה) After he finishes his checking — during checking, even searching for a needle, he is an agent of a mitzvah and not harmed, like one who says "this rock for charity that my sons live" — completely righteous even if he also intends his benefit.
(ו) Must remove it — if he did not and Pesach passed, forbidden even though he nullified, like all chametz he transgressed on Pesach — siman 448 seif 5.
(ז) He nullifies in his heart — nullification is d'rabbanan lest he open the mound on the festival and transgress "it shall not be seen"; d'oraisa he does not transgress at all — only hiding with hands is d'oraisa forbidden; but here since the mound fell on him automatically he does not transgress. Some say d'oraisa he must nullify, otherwise even without intent to open on the festival he transgresses "it shall not be found" like hiding l'chatchila; per their view if he did not nullify until after prohibition time he must open the mound to destroy since not in his power to nullify; but if a large mound fell impossible to open and lost from him and everyone — all permit, for we do not call it "yours."
(ח) And it suffices — if after Pesach the mound was opened and chametz revealed, it is permitted even to eat, for chametz he transgressed on Pesach is not forbidden except rabbinic penalty for transgressing "it shall not be seen" and "it shall not be found" d'oraisa or d'rabbanan as explained siman 448; here he did not transgress at all — not fit to penalize.
