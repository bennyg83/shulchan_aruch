He need not check beneath it. Since it did not say here as well, he nullifies it in his heart, as it says at the end regarding known chametz, it implies that here even nullification is not required. This is true according to Rashi, who explains that the reason known chametz requires nullification is lest the mound be opened during the festival and he be found transgressing bal yera'eh. That applies specifically to definite chametz, but here there are two doubts: perhaps there is no chametz here, and even if there is, perhaps the mound will not be opened. Even according to the Semak's reason that one must nullify it because of the prohibition not to hide, since he does not know whether chametz is there, the prohibition not to hide does not apply. See what I wrote in seif katan 13. All this appears to me simple and clear from the implication of the Gemara and the poskim, unlike some of the later authorities. In Olas Shabbos he brings a proof from the sugya of the Gemara that even this requires nullification, but his words are not compelling or clear at all. Although we rule that everyone who checks must nullify, as explained in siman 434, nevertheless the practical difference in those cases where it teaches that he nullifies in his heart is that if the time of prohibition passed and he did not nullify, so that he can no longer nullify it, he must make every possible effort and search for the known chametz in order to remove it. This is unlike here, as it appears to me. See below, seif katan 19.
That it contains danger from a scorpion. The Magen Avraham wrote that this is specifically in a place where scorpions are commonly found. This is unlike snakes, where harm is not so certain, and one must check. See above, siman 104.
For he is not engaged in a mitzvah. This implies that while he is engaged in the mitzvah, we are not concerned that he will come into danger, because agents of a mitzvah are not harmed. Why do we not say, as regarding a hole between a Jew and gentiles, that where harm is common it is different? See above, seif katan 15. The Olas Shabbos answered that there it is different, for there is danger from a person, who has free choice. End quote. The sugya in Kiddushin escaped him, for even regarding danger from a shaky ladder and the like, which is not from one with free choice, we also say that where the harm is established or common, even an agent of a mitzvah can be harmed, just as with one who has free choice. This is learned from Shmuel, who said: How can I go, when Shaul will hear? See there. So too it is explained above in siman 104 that during prayer one interrupts for a scorpion that is prone to harm. Perhaps for this reason there it is not called common harm, since no scorpion is before us. There are many doubts and concerns: perhaps there is a scorpion here, perhaps it will harm him, perhaps he will not see it during the checking to protect himself from it, and perhaps it cannot harm because it is in the heap. Therefore, because of these concerns it is not called common harm so as to cancel a mitzvah. But when he is not engaged in a mitzvah we are concerned for danger, for danger is treated more stringently than prohibition.
He nullifies it in his heart. The later authorities wrote that there is a dispute whether this nullification is according to the Torah or D'Rabbanan. The practical difference is if he did not nullify and it is after the time of its prohibition, whether he must clear the collapsed heap in order to remove the chametz or not. According to what I wrote at the end of seif katan 16, it appears to me that according to all opinions he must clear it, since he did not act properly and did not nullify. It is for this purpose that the Gemara and the poskim state that he must nullify, for otherwise what would it be teaching? Every person must nullify all his chametz according to the enactment of the Sages, with the formula of nullification. What is different that regarding a collapsed heap it says that he must nullify? Rather, certainly, it comes to say that if he did not nullify, he is obligated to clear it.
And it is sufficient. The later authorities wrote in the name of Riaz that he may eat it after Pesach, since a collapsed heap fell upon it and there is no concern for trickery. End quote. The Bach left this law requiring study. The Taz also distinguishes in this law. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rashba that Rav Hai forbids it. In my work Toras HaShelamim, question 6, I discussed this at length and brought support for this law from the Yerushalmi, the Rosh, and the Rashba in a responsum. Also, although the Rashba in siman 70 wrote in the name of Rav Hai that he forbids, he concludes there in the responsum that even Rav Hai agrees regarding a collapsed heap. See below, simanim 436 and 448. The Magen Avraham wrote that if a large mound fell upon it, so that it is not in his power to open it and it is lost from him and from every person, it is permitted even if he did not nullify it, for it is not called yours.
