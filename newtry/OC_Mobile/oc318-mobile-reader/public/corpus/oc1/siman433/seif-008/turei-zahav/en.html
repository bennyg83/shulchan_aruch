(א) He may search for a needle. Rishon L'Tzion wrote: Rashi explained "that it was lost to him" before the checking — it appears his intent is not to say a needle was lost during checking when his mind was diverted by checking and that caused harm — for then the difficulty returns to its place: agents of a mitzvah are not harmed, and how can one say harm came from checking; rather it refers to one already lost before checking, but after checking he will immediately search for the needle while the checking candle is still in his hand — although here too one could say agents of a mitzvah, nevertheless since the loss already preceded him and checking did not cause the main harm, since he could search whenever he wished — with this the difficulties of Beit Yosef on the poskim who omitted the distinction between general and known cases are resolved, as that distinction is necessary per the first resolution in the Gemara regarding scorpion danger; because Tosafot's difficulty — that regarding a mound it implies if a dog can dig after him it is not as concealed and requires saying it refers to definite chametz — but in truth Ramban does not hold the first resolution regarding scorpion danger, because it was difficult for him: even if checking caused searching for a needle even if already lost beforehand, nevertheless he is within agents of a mitzvah, etc. Therefore he resolves regarding non-Jewish danger where harm is common — therefore Tosafot's difficulty has no place; and one can say even for possible chametz one may check with scorpion danger, and here it refers to non-Jewish danger; and poskim who omitted the distinction hold like Ramban, as I wrote.

(ב) He nullifies in his heart and it suffices. So in the Gemara daf 30 in the mishnah: chametz that fell on him from a collapse — R' Chisda says he must nullify it; Rashi explained lest he open the mound on the festival and be found transgressing; Semak wrote he must nullify because of "you shall not hide" — implying even where he will not open the mound on the festival he must nullify, otherwise he transgresses "you shall not hide," i.e. the prohibition applies even if he hides until after Pesach; Rashi does not hold so, since he does not hide with his hands he does not transgress. It is simple to me that Shulchan Aruch's words that one need not check because of scorpion danger is also in this way that he must nullify in his heart if there is chametz there for this reason; and from this the baraita is explained that they do not obligate putting hands into holes because of danger — meaning with hands there is no obligation, but nullification in the heart is required, as I wrote. Hagahot Alfasi in the name of Riaz: if it has height of three handbreadths, since he did not transgress "it shall not be seen," it is permitted in eating after Pesach with no concern for trickery since it fell from a collapse — end. It appears there is a distinction: if there was first a wall there and it fell, certainly he will clear the collapse to repair the wall, somewhat like hiding in pits; but if a collapse of stones fell on him in a way with no expectation of clearing, then after Pesach it is permitted if he nullified. On what is written regarding less than three handbreadths that he must remove — one could ask why not say a dog will eat it as written regarding ravens, that regarding ravens too it is forbidden when in his possession — see siman 445 seif 3.
