(א) (s.k. 3) If he checked — and in the Gemara it states, etc. — since these words of Shas are needed in their places in this siman, for many laws flow from them, I will copy: Our Rabbis taught: one does not check by sunlight, nor by moonlight, nor by torchlight, but by candlelight, because candlelight is good for checking, etc. And we asked: this sunlight — how so? If we say in a courtyard — but Rava said the middle of a courtyard (so Rambam's text) need not be checked because ravens are found there; rather in a portico. And Rava said a portico is checked by its light. And it answers: it is not necessary except for a skylight in a room; and how so — if alongside the skylight, that is like a portico; rather from the sides — end of Shas daf 8. From this it implies a portico is checked even l'chatchila by its light: one, the language "is checked by its light" implies l'chatchila; and further, if so what does it ask from the baraita — the baraita teaches how one checks by sunlight, i.e. l'chatchila, and with this Rava agrees; it cannot be that Rava who said "is checked" means b'dieved. Bach wished to settle the questioner's difficulty: it would seem sunlight is like moonlight and torchlight that even b'dieved do not help — see there; but this is somewhat strained, since the baraita speaks only of l'chatchila law and not b'dieved — how is it relevant to say "like" regarding something the baraita does not discuss. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote to settle what implies in the Gemara that even l'chatchila is permitted: when he checked by day — meaning he forgot to check at night by candlelight and comes to check by day; although everywhere even when checking by day one needs candlelight, a portico he checks even l'chatchila without a candle but only by its light. What implies in Shulchan Aruch is Rambam's language specifically b'dieved, because l'chatchila he must check on the night of the 14th and not rely to check by day by its light. Now it is resolved what the Gemara asks on Rava from the baraita that states one does not check l'chatchila: it means l'chatchila one must check on the night of the 14th; now it is well said it is like moonlight — meaning even when the moon shines, i.e. at night, one does not check by it; and likewise sunlight when the sun shines, i.e. by day, and nevertheless it teaches one does not check l'chatchila. If so there is no dispute here, unlike Bach who wrote three disputes: Rashi's view that even l'chatchila they check on the 13th by its light — he wrote: "is checked by its light and need not bring candlelight at night, rather checks by day" — end; Bach wrote so is Rif's view; Ri'az and as appears Rosh's view: on the 13th one does not check, but on the 14th one may check l'chatchila by sunlight; Rambam's view, which is Shulchan Aruch's language, that even on the 14th l'chatchila he does not check except by candlelight — end. Per Magen Avraham's words there is no dispute, for certainly Rambam and Rosh are clear from Magen Avraham's words that they do not disagree, as explained; but nevertheless it appears Rashi disagrees, as explained. However, after investigation it comes out well that in this law Magen Avraham discusses there is no dispute and Rashi also agrees. For on the Sages' enactment that checking time is the night of the 14th and not the morning of the 14th, Rabbenu Nissim Beit Yosef daf 4 gave two reasons: when people are in their homes, and further because candlelight is good for checking (and although in the baraita above that one does not check by sunlight, etc., only one reason was mentioned — because candlelight is good for checking — one may say he cited one of them; also we do not find anyone who disagrees with Rabbenu Nissim Beit Yosef on this). If so, in a portico its light is great and equal to candlelight, and the reason "candlelight is good for checking" is not relevant there; nevertheless other reasons remain — "when people are in their homes, where will he go" — and so Kessef Mishneh explicitly in chapter 2 law 4: why one does not check a portico l'chatchila except at night — because people are present, etc. Therefore what Rashi wrote that they check it by its light l'chatchila on the 13th must mean he holds this reason "when people are in their homes" applies only to why we do not check on the morning of the 14th — because then most people have gone to work and perhaps one will forget and not come home until night, when Pesach has already entered and he transgresses "it shall not be seen"; unlike when he wishes to check on the 13th.

(ב) He will come home only at night — then Pesach has already entered and he transgresses "it shall not be seen"; unlike when he wishes to check on the 13th — it appears forbidden, because then people are not in their homes and perhaps he will forget until night; and what of it if he forgets until night — then too people are not in their homes, but on the night of the 14th is the main time of checking. Rather on the 13th it is forbidden because candlelight is good for checking; and since a portico has great light, it is permitted to check it l'chatchila on the 13th and he need not wait until the night of the 14th. However there is another reason not to check on the 13th, as Mordechai brought in Magen Avraham s.k. 20: not to divide checking from checking.

(ג) But it is possible Rashi disagrees with Mordechai on this; but for us who rule like Mordechai, one does not check a portico on the 13th, and in this we disagree with Rashi. From this law Magen Avraham did not speak here; but if he did not check the portico on the 13th and the night of the 14th arrived, in this all agree, also Rashi, that l'chatchila he must check at night and not wait until tomorrow on the 14th — meaning for Rashi too, even though one need not divide checking from checking, nevertheless it is forbidden to wait until the 14th because people are present, etc. If so Magen Avraham's words are resolved, and there is no dispute — meaning in this law Magen Avraham discusses: when standing on the night of the 14th l'chatchila, all require checking at night; b'dieved if he waited until day, all agree checking by its light suffices. Chayei Adam wrote: when he forgot to check at night and checks by day — whether a portico by its light or other places by candlelight — he should check in the morning because the diligent precede mitzvot, as stated daf 4a.
