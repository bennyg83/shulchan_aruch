And it is a kind of, etc. Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchot Berachot, writes that one blesses over lightning and thunder, over a booming sound heard in the earth like great millstones, and over lights in the sky that appear like falling stars running from place to place, or stars with a tail.
That blew violently, etc. This is the wording of the Tur, Rambam, and Shulchan Aruch. Olat Tamid explains that this excludes winds that blow gently, over which no blessing is recited. Tosafot, however, cite the Yerushalmi that over gentle winds one recites Oseh Maaseh Bereishit. He concludes that practical law requires study. Nachalat Tzvi rules plainly like Tosafot: over violent wind one says "Whose strength and might fill the world," and over gentle wind Oseh Maaseh Bereishit. Rokeach, Mordechai, and Agudah rule likewise. It also appears from Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah, chapter HaRoeh, where he defines the winds as great storms that are not ordinarily common. This implies that any wind sufficiently uncommon is what the Gemara calls violent.
And if he wishes, etc. So writes the Tur. I am therefore surprised by Shiltei Giborim, who says Tosafot, Rambam, Semag, and Tur agree that it is preferable to say "Whose strength, etc." The Tur implies the opposite, that Oseh Maaseh Bereishit is preferable. Taz likewise requires examination on this point. The other poskim cited above, however, clearly support Shiltei Giborim, as do Agudah, Rokeach, Recanati siman 78, and Rabbi David Abudarham.
