1) [Seif 1] Over meteors, which are like a star, etc. We have not seen people recite a blessing over a star that races from place to place. Since this is explained by the halachic authorities, however, one must recite the blessing, and absence of observation is not proof. R' Moshe ibn Chaviv, author of Get Pashut, handwritten responsum, siman 3; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, the universal practice is to recite the blessings over everything listed in this siman and the following siman without the Name and kingship. It is possible that this custom spread based upon what Rema, of blessed memory, writes in the gloss above to siman 223, seif 1; see there.
2) Likewise, over a star with a tail and a radiant rod, which is the second explanation of the Rambam, Rav Hai Gaon, and Aruch, one must recite the blessing. The might of the Creator, may He be exalted, is even more evident in it because it is wondrous and occurs only at rare intervals. R' Moshe ibn Chaviv there; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2; Shaarei Teshuvah there; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 24; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 60, paragraph 2.
3) Regarding meteors: over a racing star, one recites the blessing only once during the entire night. Even if he sees a different star race, he does not recite another blessing that night. Regarding a star with a tail, if he subsequently sees no different star but sees that same one each night, he need not recite the blessing again if he sees it within thirty days. Regarding earthquakes, if enough time passed between one tremor and another for him to have diverted his attention, he must recite the blessing again even within thirty days. Otherwise, he need not recite it again. The same applies to winds. R' Moshe ibn Chaviv there; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 3; Shaarei Teshuvah there; Chesed LaAlafim there; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there.
