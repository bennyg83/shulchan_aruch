(2) That blew in wrath etc. Thus is the language of Tur and Rambam and Shulchan Aruch; and Olas Tamid wrote the explanation is to exclude if they come calmly that one does not bless; but Tosafos wrote in Yerushalmi that in calm one blesses Blessed are You Heaven Who makes the work of Creation, and in practice requires study, until here his words. But Nachalas Tzvi ruled in general like Tosafos that in wrath Blessed are You Heaven our Heaven King of the universe Whose strength and power etc., and in calm Blessed are You Heaven Who makes etc., and so ruled Rokeach and Mordechai and Agudah. And further it appears to me from Rambam in his commentary on the Mishnah chapter HaRo'eh who wrote and this is his language: and winds means the great storms that blow that are not always present, until here. It implies that whatever is not always present one blesses on it and this is called 'in wrath'.
