1) [Seif 1] After the reading of the Megillah, etc. After the Amidah, the shaliach tzibbur recites Kaddish through 'l'eila,' but does not say Tiskabel, because the order of Kedushah must be recited after the reading of the Megillah, and he then says Tiskabel. Abudraham. So too wrote Kol Bo, siman 45, and the Levush; such is also the view of the Taz, s.k. 1. However, Minhagim states that a complete Kaddish is recited after the Amidah, before the reading of the Megillah. So too wrote Maharil in Responsa, siman 56, in the name of the Rokeach; so too wrote Tosafos, folio 4, and Hagahos Maimoniyos. The Magen Avraham cited their words at the beginning of the siman and wrote that in these countries, where the custom is to recite a complete Kaddish then, after the reading of the Megillah they should recite a complete Kaddish without Tiskabel; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, os 5, wrote that after the reading of the Megillah they again recite a complete Kaddish with Tiskabel, and that Tiskabel refers back to the prayer in the order of Kedushah; see there. Eshel Avraham, at the beginning of the siman, wrote that in some places the custom follows the words of the Magen Avraham, to recite a complete Kaddish with Tiskabel after the Amidah of Maariv, and after the Megillah and 'But You are holy,' a complete Kaddish without Tiskabel; see there. So too wrote Bigdei Yesha. Accordingly, since there is a dispute in this matter and it depends only upon custom, each person should maintain his custom, for every river follows its own course.
2) In the morning, after the Amidah he should recite half Kaddish, as after every Torah reading on a day without Mussaf. After the Megillah and the order of Kedushah, he should recite a complete Kaddish with Tiskabel. Eshel Avraham there; Derech HaChaim, os 2; Mishna Berurah, os 1.
3) Ibid. He says, 'But You are holy.' Rav Amram wrote that on Purim night, after the reading of the Megillah, one recites 'A redeemer shall come to Tzion.' Some say that it is not recited, because the main redemption was not at night; rather, one begins with 'But You are holy.' Tur. This is what the Shulchan Aruch means here by saying, 'He says, But You are holy,' meaning that he does not say, 'A redeemer shall come to Tzion,' in accordance with the second opinion cited by the Tur. So too wrote Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, os 1, in the name of several poskim, that it is not recited. He wrote that such is the custom, not to recite it except during the day.
4) Ibid. He says, 'But You are holy.' Kol Bo, siman 45, wrote the reason: The obligation to read the Megillah by day and by night is derived from the verse, 'I call by day, but You do not answer; and at night, but there is no silence for me.' That verse appears in the psalm, 'My God, my God, why have You forsaken me,' which was said regarding Esther. Therefore, the custom is to begin after the reading with the verse, 'But You are holy,' which is in that same psalm. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, os 2.
