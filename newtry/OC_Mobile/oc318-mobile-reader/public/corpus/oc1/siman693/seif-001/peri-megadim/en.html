They say — Taz emends: after the Megillah one says "Asher he'eini," "Yehi noam," "Ata Kadosh," and "Veyiten lecha," unlike Kol Bo who omits "Veyiten lecha." R' Yosef Karo records Chatzi Kaddish after Mincha, then the Megillah, then havdalah (against Kol Bo who makes havdalah first). Be'er HaGolah's text: Megillah reading, then havdalah; the Taz adds Chatzi Kaddish before the Megillah and Shema in the sidra. Ateret Zekenim and Darkei Moshe (Minhagim) describe "Yehi noam," havdalah, then "Veyiten lecha." On Purim we likewise make havdalah after "Veyiten lecha," unlike weekdays when havdalah precedes it (Eshel Avraham 695:6). Abudraham (Kol Bo) requires "Borei me'orei ha'eish" before the Megillah; others rely on "Yotzer hame'orot" (Peri Megadim/R' Shimshon Halevi): the fire blessing commemorates Adam's stones, not a general benefit prohibition (Taz 298:1; Tosafot Pesachim 53).
