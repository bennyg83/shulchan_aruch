And they say. See the Taz, who emends the text, and this is how it should read: “And they say VeYiten Lecha,” meaning that after Megillah reading they say Asher Heini, Vihi Noam, VeAtah Kadosh, and VeYiten Lecha. This excludes the Kol Bo cited in the Darkei Moshe, who holds that VeYiten Lecha is not said. Afterward the Rav wrote, “and they say half Kaddish,” meaning after Shemoneh Esrei, and they read the Megillah, and afterward they make Havdalah. This excludes the Kol Bo brought there by the Darkei Moshe, who holds that Havdalah is made first, for the reason that will be explained nearby, while the Rav rules that Havdalah is made afterward. But according to this, this is not the place to mention half Kaddish. To say that the Taz deletes “and afterward they make Havdalah” is forced. The Be'er HaGolah appears to have emended the text to read: “and they read the Megillah, and afterward they make Havdalah.” In the Taz, the text was only “and they read the Megillah,” and he emended it to say that they say half Kaddish and read the Megillah, meaning that after Megillah reading and Kedushah DeSidra they say Kaddish Shalem. The Be'er HaGolah emended: “afterward they make Havdalah.” What the Be'er HaGolah wrote, that this teaches that we delay the departure of the day, I do not know why he wrote “and they say VeYiten Lecha,” except to exclude the Kol Bo, who holds that VeYiten Lecha is not said. See Ateres Zekenim: they say Vihi Noam and VeAtah Kadosh, make Havdalah, and say VeYiten Lecha; see there. In truth, this is also how it appears in Darkei Moshe, letter 2, in the name of Minhagim. See siman 295 in Eliyah Rabbah, letter 6, that Minhagim holds that throughout the rest of the year as well, Havdalah is made before VeYiten Lecha, but we make Havdalah before Aleinu, after VeYiten Lecha; see there. If so, the same should apply to Purim on Motzaei Shabbos as well, that Havdalah is made after VeYiten Lecha, for what is the difference? See the Beis Yosef in the name of the Kol Bo, that one says Borei Meorei HaEish before the Megillah, while some say he is exempted with Yotzer HaMeoros. See the Pri Chadash in the name of Rav Shmuel HaLevi, who explains that the blessing Meorei HaEish is not because it is forbidden to benefit without a blessing, since in the morning one says Yotzer HaMeoros, which includes this as well. Rather, because Adam HaRishon brought two stones on Motzaei Shabbos and produced fire from them, and so on, therefore we bless after the Megillah. So is the custom, after the Megillah. See the Taz, siman 298, letter 1, in the name of Tosafos, Pesachim 53, and Eliyah Rabbah there, letter 3.
