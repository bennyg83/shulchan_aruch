And if he did not say it, and so on. In Mordechai, siman 240, he wrote as follows: Days that do not have, and so on; he made it obligatory upon himself, and so on. This is the end of his words. His words contradict one another regarding Chanukah and Purim, for at first he says that we do not make him repeat, and at the end he writes that we do make him repeat, apart from the fact that this is against the explicit Talmud, which says that we do not make him repeat. Many great authorities delete the words “on Chanukah and Purim” that he wrote at the end. The Maharshal in responsum, siman 48, wrote that one of the great men of his generation, Maharai Pollak, deleted the word “Chanukah” at the end. From the fact that he emended it this way, it follows that both are true: on Purim at Maariv and Shacharis we do not make him repeat, for it is no better than davening; but the Purim meal, which the prophets and sages imposed on all Yisrael as days of feasting and joy more than Shabbosos and Yamim Tovim, and so on, is not less than the Shabbos meal, and so on. This is the end of his words. It emerges that his conclusion is that on Purim there is a distinction: we make him repeat only at the Purim meal, but not at Maariv, Shacharis, or Minchah, neither in davening nor in Birkas HaMazon, except at the time of the meal alone. This emendation is difficult to me from what the Mordechai wrote there in the name of the Raavyah: even though he concludes here that mentioning Chanukah is optional in Birkas HaMazon, and so on. This implies that the Yerushalmi had the word Chanukah. All the more so is it difficult according to the first emendation I mentioned, which deletes entirely the words Chanukah and Purim at the end. It appears to my humble opinion that the words in the Mordechai regarding Chanukah have been reversed, and this is how it should read: “This was said regarding Rosh Chodesh and Chanukah, but not regarding Shabbos, Yom Tov, and Purim, when it is impossible for him without eating,” and so on. Purim has an obligation like Shabbos and Yom Tov, for it is written “days of feasting and joy,” and Rava said that one who ate the Purim meal at night did not fulfill his obligation. This shows that the meal is obligatory, and if so it is certainly impossible that he not eat. It appears to me that the Tur's view is also so, for in siman 695 he wrote that one says Al HaNissim in Modim, and did not mention there that if he did not say it, we do not make him repeat, as he wrote in the laws of Chanukah, siman 682. Rather, it is certainly as I explained. So it appears to me as halachah, as the Maharshal wrote in the name of the great Maharai Pollak, of blessed memory, that in Birkas HaMazon of the meal one must repeat because of Al HaNissim on Purim. However, it appears to me that this is not specifically at the meal made after Minchah, but at the first meal one eats on Purim. The main reason is that there is an obligation of a meal on that day, for it is written “days of feasting and joy,” and it is impossible without eating. If so, the first meal he eats that day is the one in which he fulfills the Purim meal. We need not make the law depend on what we call the meal after Minchah the Purim meal, for that is an addition of joy, and regarding that it is not true that it is impossible not to eat. So it appears to me as practical halachah.
