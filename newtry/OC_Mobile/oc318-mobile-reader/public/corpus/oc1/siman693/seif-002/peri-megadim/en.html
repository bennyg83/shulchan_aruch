And if. See the Taz. I will copy the words of the Mordechai in chapter Bameh Madlikin regarding what is written in Shabbos 24a about days that have no Mussaf. The Sefer Mitzvos Gadol ruled: if one did not say Al HaNissim on Chanukah or Purim, we do not make him repeat; and so it is in the Tosefta and Yerushalmi. On days that have no Mussaf, such as Chanukah and Purim, if he did not say it, we do not make him repeat. We further read there: if he erred and did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Birkas HaMazon, we do not make him repeat, because it is possible without eating bread. This was said regarding Rosh Chodesh, but not Shabbos, Yom Tov, Chanukah, and Purim, when it is impossible without eating bread, so he repeats. It appears to the Raavyah that even though it mentions here that mentioning Chanukah in Birkas HaMazon is optional, since everyone has the custom to mention it, and he recited the blessing intending to mention it, they made it obligatory to mention. This is the end of the Mordechai in the Krakow edition. These words appear contradictory. First he wrote that on Chanukah and Purim we do not make him repeat, and afterward he wrote that we do make him repeat. Some delete at the end the words Chanukah and Purim, leaving only Shabbos and Yom Tov. Maharai Pollak, of blessed memory, deleted the word Chanukah, but not Purim: in davening on Purim we do not make him repeat, but in the meal we do make him repeat. The Taz challenged this from the wording “It appears to the Raavyah that even though he mentions Chanukah,” for according to Maharai Pollak, who deletes the word Chanukah, where is Chanukah mentioned at all? And if it refers to the beginning of the Yerushalmi's words, there it mentions Chanukah and Purim, and necessarily it refers to davening, not Birkas HaMazon. In Sefer Chemed Moshe he had difficulty with this, and according to what I wrote it is resolved. All the more so according to the first emendation, which deletes Chanukah and Purim, and even Purim does not require repetition in Birkas HaMazon, there are two difficulties: first, where is Chanukah mentioned in Birkas HaMazon as a case where one does not repeat? Second, why did he leave Purim in Birkas HaMazon and say that they made it obligatory? Therefore the Taz wrote that an error fell in the printing, with the lines reversed, and this is how it should read: We further read there, and so on, “This was said regarding Rosh Chodesh and Chanukah, but not Shabbos, Yom Tov, and Purim.” Then the words of the Raavyah are clear, that he mentions Chanukah in Birkas HaMazon as a case where one does not repeat. The conclusion of the Taz is as follows: at the first meal, on the morning of the fourteenth, we make him repeat Birkas HaMazon, but on the night of the fourteenth after the Megillah, or at a second meal on the thirteenth day, we do not make him repeat. What Sefer Chemed Moshe wrote, that the Tur in siman 187 wrote that Chanukah and Purim do not require repetition, can be answered that there it is speaking of the second meal and of after the Megillah at night. In siman 682 regarding Chanukah, he wrote explicitly that in davening and in Birkas HaMazon we do not make him repeat. Here in siman 693, the Tur wrote that on Purim in davening we do not make him repeat, but regarding Birkas HaMazon he did not say this. This shows that at the meal through which he fulfills his obligation, repetition is required. This is what is meant in siman 695, that we make him repeat, because bread is required. See Magen Avraham 695, letter 9, who holds that we do not make him repeat. If so, according to the Taz there is a new dispute: at the meal through which one fulfills the obligation, bread is required, while according to the Magen Avraham one fulfills it with fruits and meat. This requires analysis, and with Hashem's help it will be explained further in siman 695.
