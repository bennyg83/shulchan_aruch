And if I copy Taz — I will copy Mordekhai chapter Bameh Madlikin — see Shabbat 24a: days without Musaf — Semag ruled: if he did not say on miracles in Chanukah and Purim they do not return him; and so in Tosefta and Yerushalmi; and days without Musaf such as Chanukah and Purim — if they did not say they do not return him; and further our version there: he erred and did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Birkat Hamazon — they do not return him since possible without bread; this would say Rosh Chodesh; but not Shabbat and Yom Tov and Chanukah and Purim which cannot be without bread — they return; and it appears to Rabbenu Yonah: even though he mentions here Chanukah mention is optional in Birkat Hamazon — since all are accustomed to mention and he blessed intending to mention — we treat it obligatory — end Mordekhai Krakow edition. And it appears his words contradict: first he wrote on Chanukah and Purim they do not return, then he wrote they return; and some erase at the end Chanukah and Purim only Shabbat and Yom Tov; and Mahari Pollak erased the word Chanukah but Purim in prayer they do not return and in the meal they return. Taz challenged from "and it appears to Rabbenu Yonah" etc. — requires study; Taz's conclusion: on the first morning meal of 14 they return in Birkat Hamazon; but night of 14 after megillah or second meal on day 13 they do not return. Chemed Moshe wrote Tur in 687 wrote Chanukah and Purim need not return — perhaps there second meal and after megillah at night; and in 682 Chanukah he wrote explicitly prayer and Birkat Hamazon they do not return; here 693 Tur wrote Purim in prayer they do not return and in Birkat Hamazon — unclear whether meal requiring bread; and here 695 they return because bread is required; Magen Avraham 695 letter 9 they do not return. If so Taz has a new dispute: meal requiring bread — Magen Avraham fulfills with fruit and meat — requires study; will explain further in 695.
