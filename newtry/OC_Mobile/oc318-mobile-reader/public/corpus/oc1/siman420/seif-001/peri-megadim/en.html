Likewise. See Taz. Levush in siman 670, cited here by Magen Avraham, writes that although one delivers a eulogy for a Torah scholar in his presence, Tziduk HaDin is nevertheless not recited. It may, however, be recited on the fifteenth of Av, the fifteenth of Shevat, and Lag BaOmer for a distinguished person. See Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 2, who writes that the restriction during the days of Nisan is only customary, as is the restriction between Yom Kippur and Sukkos and from the beginning of Sivan until Shavuos. On Shavuos itself, one may say that the restriction is a matter of law, while on Isru Chag of Pesach and Sukkos it is only customary. See siman 429, Magen Avraham, seif katan 8, and siman 494. On erev Shabbos and erev Yom Tov after midday Tziduk HaDin is not recited, while on erev Rosh Chodesh and erev Chanukah after midday it is recited. See there. It would appear that the same is certainly true on the eve of the fifteenth of Shevat and Lag BaOmer. However, if Maariv was prayed while it was still daytime, one may say that it is no longer recited. Regarding comforting a mourner on Rosh Chodesh, see Levush and Yoreh Deah, siman 401.
