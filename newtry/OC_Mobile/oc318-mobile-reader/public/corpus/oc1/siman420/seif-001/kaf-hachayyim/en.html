1) [Se'if 1] But they may not chant dirges, etc. This applies to an ordinary person. For a Torah scholar, however, whether on Chol HaMoed or on Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, or Purim, the women may respond in lamentation and chant dirges as they ordinarily do on a weekday. This applies only in the scholar's presence, not in his absence. The day on which news of the death is received, even distant news, is treated like the person's presence. Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De'ah, siman 401, se'if 5. Chochmas Adam, rule 169, subsection 25, rules likewise. Chayei Adam, rule 118, subsection 7, writes that lamentations and eulogies should not be recited even for a sage in his presence, because nowadays we do not have the status of a Torah scholar. See there. It is possible that he reconsidered this ruling in Chochmas Adam. This subject will be explained further below at the end of siman 547, with the help of Heaven.
