The custom is not, etc. Even for a Torah scholar, in his presence, Tziduk HaDin is not recited, because it is a supplication. If he is worthy of it, however, a eulogy may be delivered, and Kaddish is recited after the eulogy. Levush, simanim 670 and 696, as cited there by Magen Avraham. If the deceased was not a Torah scholar, it is forbidden to eulogize him on those days. Magen Avraham writes even more strongly at the end of siman 547, that nowadays no one has the legal status of a Torah scholar. In my opinion, Levush holds that for this matter there is still a category of Torah scholar nowadays, as he writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 243, at the end, and as Sema writes at the end of siman 262. It is possible that Magen Avraham was stringent only on Chol HaMoed, since he wrote this nowhere else. This requires study. Levush and Shach, Yoreh Deah, siman 401, write that Tziduk HaDin is not recited on Erev Shabbos or Erev Yom Tov after midday. It is recited on Erev Rosh Chodesh and Erev Chanukah. It appears that the same law applies on Erev Purim. Here in Prague, the custom is not to recite it even on Erev Rosh Chodesh after midday. The first view is primary, because some poskim permit it even on Yom Tov. See Yoreh Deah, siman 401. Taz writes that Rama died on Lag BaOmer and Tziduk HaDin was recited for him. The same applies on the fifteenth of Av and the fifteenth of Shevat, but not on Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, or during Nisan, because on those days there is a prohibition based upon Talmudic law. This is incorrect. Nisan is only a matter of custom, as are the days between Yom Kippur and Sukkos and from Rosh Chodesh Sivan until Shavuos. Isru Chag, the fifteenth of Av, and the fifteenth of Shevat, however, are mentioned in the Gemara. See siman 573. Even on Lag BaOmer, it appears to me that one should rely upon this only for the leading Torah authority of the generation, Heaven forbid, as in the case of Rama. Magen Avraham’s words are also astonishing. In siman 577 he writes that Isru Chag is based upon Talmudic law, while in siman 288, seif katan 3, he writes that it is only a custom. Perhaps one may distinguish between Isru Chag of Shavuos, which has a basis in law, and Isru Chag of Pesach and Sukkos, which are matters of custom. His wording, however, implies that he refers to every Isru Chag.
