2) [Se'if 2] Tzidduk HaDin is recited, etc. The Tur cites a dispute. The sages of Worms recite it while walking, whereas the sages of Mainz recite it only for a great person. Rabbi Yitzchak ibn Ghayyat writes that the custom dating from the earliest elders was not to recite Tzidduk HaDin on Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, or Purim. Rashi's students write in his name that Tzidduk HaDin and Kaddish are recited even on Chol HaMoed. See there. The Shulchan Aruch rules in accordance with Rashi's students because Rambam likewise holds this opinion, as stated in Beis Yosef. Rosh explains that according to Rashi's students, Tzidduk HaDin and Kaddish do not constitute a eulogy, but rather an expression of thanksgiving and acceptance of the judgment of Heaven. See there. Beis Yosef and Bach cite his words. Since the matter is disputed, every community should follow its established custom. Where no known custom exists, refraining is preferable.
