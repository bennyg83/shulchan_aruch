(א) Any animal. And in Maharil one forbids even fowl, anything of its species that requires shechita; and it is a stringency that he was stringent, for by law there is no prohibition except in something offered upon the altar.

(ב) That one consecrated it while alive. His words contradict one another: according to this reason it is forbidden only in a kid and lamb that are fit for Passover, as Tosafos and Rosh wrote; but according to Rashi, who forbids all animals, then the reason is that they will say one consecrated it for the monetary value of the Paschal offering. And it appears that the Tur was asked: why did the Tur write that according to Rashi it is forbidden in all animals — he should have said that in all matters it is forbidden, for everything is fit for the monetary value of Passover? Rather, one must say that even if they will think one consecrated it for the monetary value of Passover, there is no prohibition in eating it — they will say one redeemed it, for monetary consecration has redemption; but when one consecrates an animal, they say in Shevuos daf 11: if one consecrated a male for its monetary value, it is holy — bodily holiness — and it has no redemption; and if so, they will say one eats consecrated items outside. And regarding wheat too, the viewer thinks they are holy — bodily holiness for meal-offerings — even though in truth it is not so, as is proven there, in the Gemara; nevertheless there is concern because of viewers; therefore one had to say that in wheat they know one means "I am guarding it for Passover." The Bach wrote: from that which the Gemara challenges from a kid mekulas, see there — and what is the challenge? Perhaps a kid mekulas is forbidden even b'dieved; rather, we learn that if one said "this meat for Passover," even b'dieved it is forbidden to eat it, and see Yoreh De'ah siman 5. And Taz wrote that it is permitted b'dieved, and so too Shach; and "this requires further study," for the Bach brings proof from the Gemara to which there is no answer; therefore it appears to me that in a kid and lamb one should be stringent b'dieved, and in other meat one should not be stringent. And it appears to me that in Ashkenaz language, where they are accustomed to say "this meat for Passover," there is no concern; for if it were that one consecrated it for the Paschal offering, one should have said "this meat for Passover."
