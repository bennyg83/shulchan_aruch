It is forbidden to say, “This meat is for Pesach,” etc. The implication is that there is only a prohibition by reason of a decree. If one transgressed and said it, however, the meat is not forbidden to be eaten b'dieved. Although a roasted kid prepared whole is forbidden to be eaten even without any declaration, there the principal prohibition takes effect upon the meat because of the eating, since nothing was said. By contrast, when one says, “This meat is for Pesach,” the prohibition takes effect through his declaration, but not upon the eating itself. We therefore attribute his intention to mean that this meat is designated for the Yom Tov of Pesach. With regard to a roasted kid prepared whole, there is also a prohibition against preparing it, as indicated by the Tur's wording, “It is forbidden to prepare it,” which implies that this applies even if he will not eat it. My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, went too far in requiring the meat to be buried if one declared it “for Pesach.” This is not the implication of the halacha.
It is permitted to say, “These wheat kernels,” etc. Rashi explains that this bears no resemblance to consecrated property. When one says, “These wheat kernels are for Pesach,” he means that he is safeguarding them for Pesach, that is, setting them aside to eat on Pesach. We do not say that it appears as though he means to sell them and purchase a Pesach offering with their proceeds. End quote. This implies that we are concerned that something appear to be the monetary value of a Pesach offering only when it bears some resemblance to the Pesach offering itself. The Maharil writes that it is forbidden to say this even regarding veal or poultry. The reason is that since they are species that require shechita, they bear some resemblance to the meat of a kid or lamb. The same rule appears below regarding eating roasted meat on Pesach night, in siman 476. One might ask that wheat too can be consecrated, since fine flour may be made from it for a meal offering. One can answer that, if this were his meaning, he would not mention Pesach, but rather a meal offering, since such an offering is no more associated with Pesach than with other occasions. It is clear that fish have the same status as wheat, and his declaration means that he is setting them aside to eat on Pesach.
