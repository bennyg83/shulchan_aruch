(א) Forbidden to say meat, etc. — it implies a general prohibition because of a decree; but if one transgressed and said, b'dieved there is no prohibition of eating, although in a kid mekulas it is forbidden to eat it even from the outset — there the essence of the prohibition applies to the meat because of eating, for he said nothing; which is not so in declaring "this meat for Passover," where the prohibition applies to it by his declaration but not on eating — we rely on saying one's intention was for the Festival of Passover that this meat shall be. And in a kid mekulas there is also prohibition in making it, as the language of Tur "forbidden to make it" — it implies even if he will not eat it; and Maharam of Lublin exaggerated the measure to mislead him to buried meat if he said on it "for Passover" — and that is not the implication of the halacha.

(ב) One may say wheat, etc. — Rashi explained that it is not at all for consecrated items, for when one says "this wheat for Passover," one means "I am guarding it for Passover" — to set it aside for Passover to eat them; and we do not say it appears as one who says to sell them to buy a Paschal offering with their value — end of his words; it implies we are not concerned for monetary value of Passover except where there is some resemblance to Passover. And in Maharil it is written: even in meat of a calf and fowl it is forbidden to say thus; and the reason is that since it is something requiring shechita, it has resemblance to meat of a kid and lamb; and so too below regarding eating roasted on Passover night in siman 476. And it is difficult: behold wheat too is relevant to consecrated items to make from it flour for a meal-offering; and one may say that otherwise he would not have mentioned the name Passover but meal-offering, which is not designated for Passover from other meal-offerings as stated above. And it is simple that fish — our law is as wheat, and "to set aside to eat on Passover" one means.
