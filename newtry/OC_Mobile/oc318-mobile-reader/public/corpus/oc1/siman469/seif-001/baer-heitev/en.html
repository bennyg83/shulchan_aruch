Animal. And Maharil forbids even fowl, anything of a species that requires shechita. And it is an extra stringency that he was stringent, for by law there is no prohibition except in something offered upon the altar (Bach). And Magen Avraham and Or Zarua wrote that even in fish and the like it is forbidden, because it is possible that one consecrated them for their monetary value since one said "for Passover," which is not so regarding wheat that require guarding — we say that one means "I am guarding it for Passover," see there. And b'dieved, if one said "this meat is for Passover," the Bach wrote that it is forbidden even for benefit, see there; and in truth his words contradict what he wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 5, see there. And Eliyah Rabbah and Taz permit even in eating, and so too Magen Avraham in the name of Shach — but he concluded "this requires further study," for the Bach brings proof from the Gemara to which there is no answer; therefore it appears to me that in a kid and lamb one should be stringent b'dieved, and in other meat one should not be stringent — end of his words Magen Avraham. And Kaf HaChayyim wrote: it appears to me as the agreement of other later authorities that one does not forbid at all b'dieved (for anyone who did not vow in the manner of those who vow, his words are nothing), see there; and see in Peri Chadash and in responsa Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 14. And Magen Avraham wrote: what people are accustomed to say thus in a foreign language — "this meat for Passover" — there is no concern; for if it were that he consecrated it, he should have said "this meat for Passover." And so is the custom to say thus, and people are not particular. And the Rokeach wrote siman 283: one should not say "how much trouble is Passover." In the Yerushalmi: the wicked one — what does he say? "What is this work for you?" "How much trouble is this" — end of his words. And Kaf HaChayyim reconciles the custom that people are not careful about this, see there.
