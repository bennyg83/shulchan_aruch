An animal, see Baer Heitev; and see what my grandfather wrote in Bechor Shor in the chiddushim on Pesachim, folio 13, where he wrote to resolve the Bach's difficulty; and see that he wrote that regarding a kid roasted whole there is no prohibition except to eat it on the nights of Pesach, for it appears that he is eating it for the sake of the Pesach offering; and if he leaves it for the next day and for another day, it is permitted (and that is to say, even though he roasted it on the night of Pesach while it was whole-roasted, there is no concern except regarding the eating; see there). And see in the Shach, siman 5, that even if he says at the time of shechitah "for the sake of Pesach," it is permitted according to the Torah; only by rabbinic law it is forbidden; see there; and in Toras Shabbat, s.k. 12. And see in the Responsa Chinuch Beis Yosef, siman 17, regarding one who saw by the butcher a slaughtered kid and said to the butcher, "For how much are you giving this Pesach offering?": one who is lenient has not lost out. And see in Kenesses Yechezkel, siman 18, who elaborated to prove that one who says "this meat for Pesach" is permitted after the fact regarding eating, like the view of the Taz and the Shach; see there.
