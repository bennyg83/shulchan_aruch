It is forbidden to say regarding any animal. And Maharil forbids even a bird, for any species that requires shechitah; and it is an excessive stringency that the Bach and the Magen Avraham were so stringent. And see in the Rokeach, siman 274, who also wrote like the view of Maharil; see there; and below, siman 476. And my brother-in-law the Rav in his work Ateres Zekenim wrote that even regarding fish and the like it is forbidden, for it is possible that he consecrated them for their monetary value, since he said "for Pesach"; which is not so regarding wheat, which require guarding, where we say that he meant "guarded for Pesach." And his words are correct for one to be careful ideally. And after the fact, if he said "this meat for Pesach," the Bach wrote that it is forbidden even for benefit (and in truth his own words contradict what he wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 5; and I elaborated on this in my commentary on Tur Yoreh De'ah; and now that the sefer Pri Chadash has been printed, I have seen that he also noted this difficulty; see there). And Olas Shabbos and the Taz permit even for eating; and so wrote the Magen Avraham in the name of the Shach, but he concluded that this requires further study, for the Bach brings a proof from the Gemara to which there is no answer; therefore it appears to him that regarding a kid and a lamb one should be stringent after the fact, and regarding other meat one should not be stringent; end quote of the Magen Avraham. And in my humble opinion, like the consensus of the other later authorities, that one should not forbid at all after the fact. And that which the Bach brought a proof from the Talmudic sugya that it is forbidden even after the fact: if it is from that alone, it is not a decisive proof, for the Talmud concludes there on folio 33 that according to R' Shimon, who holds that anything that was not donated in the manner that people donate, his words are of no effect, there is no prohibition if he said "this meat for Pesach" (and see there in Tosafos, s.v. "because it appears"). And if so, since we hold in this that the halacha follows R' Shimon, and as the Rambam ruled in chapter 16 of the Laws of the Procedure of the Sacrifices, therefore one should not forbid at all after the fact; only as a mere stringency they are stringent ideally. And the later authorities rightly challenged the Bach on this; examine carefully.
This meat for Pesach. And the Magen Avraham wrote: that which they practice to say in the vernacular "this meat for Pesach," there is no concern, for if it were that he consecrated it, he would have had to say "this meat to Pesach"; end quote. And so is the custom to say thus, and they are not particular.
This for Pesach. And the Rokeach, siman 283, wrote: one should not say "how much trouble is Pesach," for in the Yerushalmi [it says of] a wicked person, what does he say: "What is this work to you?" and "what is this trouble?"; end quote. And all the poskim did not mention this warning; also I have not seen people practicing to be careful about this. And it appears to me that they explain the Yerushalmi as applying specifically to the Pesach offering, that it is forbidden to say "What is this work to you?," for it appears that a mitzvah according to the Torah is a burden upon him. Which is not so nowadays, when he is speaking only of the great care and excessive stringencies, "how much trouble": there is no concern; so it appears to me.
