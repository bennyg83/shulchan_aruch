Magen Avraham, seif katan 2. “People will say that he consecrated it for the monetary value of a Korban Pesach.” According to Rashi, the concern is not that people will assume he had already consecrated it, but that it appears as though he is consecrating it now. With slaughtered meat, however, one cannot say that he is now consecrating it as a Korban Pesach. One must therefore explain that it appears as though he is consecrating it for the monetary value of a Korban Pesach. According to that reasoning, meat from every type of animal should be forbidden. Magen Avraham therefore writes in response to Beit Yosef’s difficulty, etc. In my humble opinion, his words require study. Regarding a living animal, the concern is understandable, since if one assumes that he consecrated it for the monetary value of a Korban Pesach, it cannot simply be redeemed in the ordinary manner. Slaughtered meat, however, is comparable to other property possessing monetary sanctity and can be redeemed. Although Shulchan Aruch speaks of one who consecrated it while alive, according to Rashi why should we assume that? Since he is eating it, presumably he would not commit a prohibition. We could instead say that he consecrated it only now, that it possessed merely monetary sanctity, and that he redeemed it. It appears to me that Mechaber adopted the stringencies of both Rashi and Tosafos. We are concerned that an observer will say that it was consecrated while alive for the monetary value of a Korban Pesach. Therefore, even slaughtered meat from another animal is forbidden. Nevertheless, this requires study. What is the source for this assumption? Perhaps Tosafos hold that it appears to have been consecrated while alive only because they do not accept Rashi’s explanation that it was consecrated merely for its value. Since the wording sounds like consecration, observers will assume it was consecrated while alive. But where we must already say that it was consecrated only for the monetary value of a Korban Pesach, why should they assume that this occurred while the animal was alive? They could just as readily assume that it was consecrated now for that monetary value and that he may eat it after redemption. The matter requires study.
