In Magen Avraham note 2: that they will say one consecrated it for the monetary value of Passover — meaning, according to Rashi the reason is not because we say one already consecrated it, but that we are concerned it will appear as though he is consecrating it now; and if so, in slaughtered meat there is no room to say one consecrates it now for Passover, and one must say it appears as consecrating it for the monetary value of Passover; and according to this reason all animals are forbidden; and on this Magen Avraham wrote to answer what was asked of the Beit Yosef, etc. And his words "this requires further study" — in my humble opinion they are reconciled: regarding a living animal it fits well that there is concern, since if we say one consecrated it for the monetary value of Passover, it has no redemption — which is not so in slaughtered meat, which is comparable to other matters of monetary holiness and has redemption. And although in Shulchan Aruch he cited that one consecrated it while alive, nevertheless according to Rashi from the Torah one should be concerned for this; in truth it is said: since he eats it, presumably he did not make a prohibition, but he consecrated it now and it is only monetary holiness and he redeemed it. And it appears to me the Mechaber cited the stringency of both — Rashi and Tosafos — and we are concerned the viewer will say one consecrated it while alive for the monetary value of Passover; and therefore even slaughtered meat from other animals is forbidden. And nevertheless "this requires further study": from where do we know this reasoning? Perhaps the Tosafos do not hold it appears as though one consecrated it while alive, but because the reasoning of Rashi — that one consecrated it for its monetary value — is not reasonable to them; and since this appears from things one consecrated, they will think one consecrated it while alive; but anything we need to say one consecrated for the monetary value of Passover — from the Torah they will rely to say one consecrated it now for the monetary value of Passover, and he may well eat it through redemption; and "this requires further study."
