Section 1: Forbidden to say regarding any animal, whether living or slaughtered, "this meat for Passover." It appears to me: I saw in the Yerushalmi chapter "How do we roast," halacha 1: they bring in a calf mekulas on the first night of Yom Tov of Passover, but not a kid mekulas — end of his words. And the Korban HaEdah there wondered, for in the chapter "In a place where they practiced" it is explicit that even calf meat is forbidden, and so ruled all the decisors, and he left it "this requires further study." And in truth it is difficult for me, for it is in the manner of what is stated in Shas in Chullin that fowl goes up on the table with cheese and is not eaten, and Beit Shammai say it does not go up and is not eaten; and it is explicit there in Shas that they disagree whether we decree a decree upon a decree or not. And accordingly, here too the intention is thus: certainly to eat — even calf meat is forbidden, for we decree on account of a kid, since its prohibition is weighty; if, since its commandment is that day — so when the Temple stood one should decree more, and therefore they decreed even a decree upon a decree. But to bring in and not to eat — if a kid mekulas, we decree bringing on account of eating, and in this we decree a decree upon a decree; but a calf to bring in, which is three decrees — we do not decree; therefore to bring in alone, a calf mekulas is permitted. And what it cited at the beginning the language "they bring a kid" on the night of Yom Tov — that is because of the seifa that specifically it is bringing and not eating; it also cited the beginning "they bring." And in the chapter "In a place where they practiced," which deals with eating — here even a calf mekulas is forbidden; and this resolves it, and it is clear and correct; understand well.
