Seif 1. “It is forbidden to say regarding any animal, whether alive or slaughtered, ‘This meat is for Pesach.’” Note: I saw in the Yerushalmi, chapter Keitzad Tzolin, halachah 1: “One may bring in a calf roasted whole on the first night of Pesach, but not a kid roasted whole.” Korban HaEdah, in Shiyarei Korban there, wonders that the chapter Makom Shenahagu explicitly prohibits even calf meat, and all the authorities rule accordingly. He leaves the matter unresolved. In truth there is no difficulty. This resembles the dispute in Chullin whether poultry may be placed on the table with cheese although it may not be eaten with it. Beit Shammai say it may neither be placed on the table nor eaten, and the Gemara explains that they dispute whether one decrees one safeguard upon another. Here too, eating even calf meat is certainly prohibited as a safeguard against eating a kid. Since the underlying prohibition is severe, and the Korban Pesach was the mitzvah of that very day when the Beis HaMikdash stood, the Sages imposed even one safeguard upon another. Merely bringing it in without eating it is different. Regarding a kid roasted whole, they prohibited bringing it in lest one eat it, which is one safeguard upon another. Regarding a calf, however, prohibiting its entry would require three successive safeguards, and such a decree was not imposed. Therefore, one may bring in a calf roasted whole, although one may not eat it. The first clause uses the expression “bring in” because the latter clause specifically concerns bringing in rather than eating. The chapter Makom Shenahagu discusses eating, and there even a calf roasted whole is prohibited. This is simple, clear, and correct. Examine carefully.
