Forbidden — Magen Avraham permitted b'dieved, not as Bach; and so agreed Eliyah Rabbah; and Magen Avraham 2 brings proof from Pesachim 53b, that a kid mekulas is forbidden in eating to eat thus; but if one roasted it thus mekulas and afterward cut it into limbs — if so, that also in mekulas it is permitted and it is not forbidden because one roasted it all as one; and the same is true when one says "this meat for Passover" — it is not forbidden b'dieved by declaration for eating. And what is stated at the beginning of siman 476, and in many places, that it is forbidden to eat what is roasted all as one — even in a place where one eats roasted meat, it is forbidden to place it on the table whole because of viewers; rather one cuts into limbs before placing on the table. And in Rambam's Laws of the Paschal Offering chapter 10 it implies eating the Paschal offering while it is still whole on the table, and one cuts the meat from above and afterward, if he wishes, cuts the bones between the joints; and see Olas Shabbat, who explicitly permitted a kid mekulas if one roasted it as one to cut into limbs and it is permitted to eat. And that which is stated in Pesachim 4:1: what is a kid mekulas that is forbidden to eat in this time — that is, to eat whole; but if one cuts into limbs, it is permitted — somewhat strained. And the language of Taz in 476 may be explained simply: if one roasted as one, it is forbidden to eat afterward when one cuts into limbs before placing on the table. And what Taz wrote: although in mekulas it is forbidden in eating — there, because of eating it will appear as though one consecrated it, that is, if one eats at the time of eating whole — it is obvious that this is its prohibition; but if one roasted as one and cut it into limbs, even mekulas is permitted in eating in a place where they eat roasted meat. And what is stated: and in a kid mekulas too there is prohibition in making it — apparently he brought proof and evidence that mekulas in making is forbidden, as the language of Tur and Rambam; and if so, in 476 why did they write forbidden to eat roasted as one — also cutting it is forbidden? We learn: because of making there is no prohibition; likewise declaration — there is no prohibition in eating. And one may say: below, forbidden to eat roasted as one — even if one cuts afterward before placing on the table into limbs — except his apparent intention is that mekulas, eating too is forbidden; how did Rambam write "a kid mekulas is forbidden to make it" — it implies it is comparable to saying "this meat for Passover"; but it is not so according to this explanation, that in mekulas there is also prohibition in making — strained. And one may say simply: a kid mekulas has two prohibitions: (1) to make it whole with its head and innards — forbidden in making; (2) forbidden to eat whole — and proof from Beitzah 22b: "and they make a kid mekulas, and the Sages forbid" — it implies making too is forbidden (and according to Beit Yosef it implies from Pesachim 52, and "this requires further study"). And accordingly, it is difficult what Rambam wrote, as below 476 — there he speaks of eating; and according to this explanation — no. Eating is forbidden from the time of making, and "this requires further study." And it implies from Taz: even a live sheep fit for Passover, and one said "this for Passover" — anything one did not explicitly consecrate is permitted b'dieved, and "this requires further study"; and see Yoreh De'ah siman 5 on this.
