Regarding the Mechaber’s words, “concerning any animal,” see Responsa Chatam Sofer, Orach Chayim 139. A youth owned a suckling lamb and said to his uncle, “This is my Pesach offering.” After an uproar arose, the youth explained that he knew nothing about consecration and had spoken only in jest, as youthful banter. Chatam Sofer first analyzes Pesachim 53b and writes that, on that basis, saying “This lamb shall be a Pesach offering” would consecrate it according to all opinions. He then writes that even if the youth is believed that he spoke in jest, it is difficult to accept this as a valid defense, as explained there. He should therefore seek annulment of his vow on Pesach through regret. Yet it is difficult to permit the severe prohibition of consecrated property through the petition and opening presented by an unlearned person today, when people are lenient regarding annulment of vows and do not wish to understand the regret explained to them. Chatam Sofer therefore proposes an additional remedy combined with annulment. After the vow has been annulled, the youth should instruct a minor gentile, who will not be confused with an adult Jew, to inflict a blemish upon the lamb of the sort that permits a firstborn animal through the examination of three members of the congregation. The animal should then be brought before a beit din, which will assess its value. It should be redeemed for that amount, and the redemption money should be cast into the river. A kohen is not required, as stated in Sanhedrin 15a, although as an added propriety there is no disadvantage in having a kohen perform the assessment. See the entire responsum carefully.
