(1) It is forbidden to say, etc. This prohibition applies l'chatchilah, but b'dieved the meat is not forbidden for consumption.
(2) Concerning any animal, etc. This applies even if it is young and is not a kid or lamb fit for the Pesach offering. It is nevertheless forbidden, because people may suspect that he consecrated it while alive in order to purchase a Pesach offering with its value. When he eats it, he then appears to be eating consecrated meat outside the Temple precincts. Therefore, one should not say to another person, “Roast a foreleg for me for Pesach.” The wording of the Mechaber implies that this applies specifically to an animal of a species offered upon the altar. Some poskim are stringent even regarding poultry and fish, since his statement “for Pesach” may mean that he consecrated them for their monetary value. Although many are lenient and follow the Mechaber, it is nevertheless proper l'chatchilah to avoid saying “for Pesach.” The acharonim write that the common vernacular expression “this meat for use on Pesach” is not problematic, even when said about a kid or lamb. Had he intended to consecrate it, he would have said, “This meat is for the Pesach offering.”
(3) “This meat is for the Pesach offering.” Likewise, one may not say, “Take this money and buy me meat for the Pesach offering.” If he says, “Buy me meat with this money for use on Pesach,” it is permitted, as explained above.
(4) Rather, he should say, etc. Meaning, when purchasing or salting the meat.
(5) Meaning, whole, etc. This means roasting it whole, including its head and the other parts, on erev Pesach after midday or on Pesach night. Siman 476 explains the prohibition against eating it, even if it was roasted several days earlier. Here the Mechaber teaches that preparing it during the Pesach period is itself forbidden, since it is prepared in the same manner as the actual Pesach offering.
(6) It is permitted to say, “This wheat is for Pesach.” Since wheat does not resemble consecrated offerings at all, we do not say that he is consecrating it in order to sell it and purchase a Pesach offering with its value. Rather, he means that he will set it aside and guard it from becoming chametz so that it may be eaten on Pesach.
