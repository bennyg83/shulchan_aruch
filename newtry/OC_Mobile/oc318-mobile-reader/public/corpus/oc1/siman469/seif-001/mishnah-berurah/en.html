(א) Forbidden to say, etc. — that is, l'chatchila; but b'dieved one does not forbid the meat in eating.

(ב) Regarding any animal, etc. — that is, even if it is small and also is not a kid or lamb that is not fit for Passover — it is also forbidden, lest they suspect one consecrated it while alive to buy from its value a Paschal offering, and when one eats it, it appears as eating consecrated items outside; and therefore one should not say to his fellow "roast for me a foreleg for Passover." And from the language of the Mechaber it is implied that specifically an animal that is a species offered upon the altar; and there are decisors who are stringent also on fowl and fish, because it is possible one consecrated them for their value since one said "for Passover." And although many are lenient in this and hold as the Mechaber's opinion, nevertheless l'chatchila it is good to be careful not to say "for Passover." And the later authorities wrote: what people are accustomed to say in a foreign language "this meat for Passover" — even if one said so regarding a kid and lamb, there is no concern; for if it were that one consecrated it, one should have said "this meat for Passover."

(ג) "This meat for Passover" — and the same law, that it is forbidden for a person to say "take this money and buy me meat for Passover with it." And if he said "buy me meat with it for Passover," it is permitted, as above.

(ד) Rather one should say, etc. — that is, when buying or when salting.

(ה) Meaning "whole," etc. — meaning to roast it whole — its head, etc., verbally after midnight or on Passover night. And below in siman 476 the prohibition on eating it is explained, even if one roasted it several days beforehand; and here he explained that also making it at Passover time is forbidden, since one made it like the roasting of the Paschal offering itself.

(ו) One may say "this wheat is for Passover" — for since it is not at all comparable to consecrated items, we do not say one consecrates them to sell them and buy a Paschal offering with their value; rather, his intention is to say he will set them aside and guard them from leavening to eat them on Passover.
