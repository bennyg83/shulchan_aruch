A sukkah that does not have seven, etc. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote in the name of the Magen Avraham that if in one place there is a corner, etc. Now, in Bigdei Yesha and Bikkurei Yaakov they strongly questioned his proof from the small chambers, for there it is different, since a small chamber is a room by itself and the partition separates it, except that it opens into a large sukkah. Here, by contrast, where there is no division at all, perhaps it is nullified to the large sukkah. [Now, regarding what Bikkurei Yaakov brought, that if so, according to the Magen Avraham, when we validate in this siman a round sukkah that has enough space to square within it seven by seven, one would have to be careful to sit specifically in its exact center, in that place where there is enough to square seven by seven, for at the side, where it narrows progressively, there is not seven by seven and it should have the law of the corner according to the Magen Avraham. See what the Derech HaChayim wrote, and I will quote his words below. In my humble opinion the two cases are not comparable, for there the entire circle proceeds inside as one surface, and one cannot divide one part from another; all of it is valid, since it is not at all too narrow for him to sit there, for it is one surface with the square. Therefore none of the later authorities failed to distinguish this. But in our case, even though no partition separates it, nevertheless it is recognizable that this corner is divided from the large sukkah, and it is too narrow for him to sit in that corner; therefore the Magen Avraham is stringent in this.] Now, I saw in Derech HaChayim that he also copied the words of the Magen Avraham, and he concluded about this: specifically when the corner is also not seven wide; but when the corner is seven wide, only it is not seven long, it is valid, since it is joined to the large sukkah. Know this from the fact that with a circle that has enough to square seven by seven, one may sit near the wall, even though in a round sukkah near the wall there is not seven by seven. Rather, perforce, since it is joined to the large one, it is valid. For granted, when the corner is also not seven wide, one can say that it is considered like a chicken coop, as Rashi and Tosafos wrote, and is invalid; but when it is seven wide, only not seven long, it is certainly valid, for it is joined to the large sukkah, just as in the case where his head and most of him are in a small sukkah and his table is in a large sukkah. These are his words. Now, we have already rejected their proof from a round sukkah. And what he wrote, "just as in the case where his head and most of him are in a small sukkah, etc.," is very astonishing, for that is valid only according to the Rif's approach, that a sukkah which holds only his head and most of him in the sukkah while his table is inside the house is invalid only rabbinically, because of the decree lest he be drawn after his table; and when his table is inside the large sukkah there is no concern for such a decree at all [but not because the small sukkah joins to the large one, as he reasoned]. But according to the Ran and those who support him, that a small sukkah which holds only his head and most of him [and it is six by six] has an intrinsic disqualification, for it is not a dwelling at all and is invalid by Torah law, one certainly has not fulfilled his obligation when eating in it, even when his table is inside the large one, as the Tosafos Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote. And all the Magen Avraham's words are only according to the Ran. In my humble opinion the law of the Magen Avraham is comparable to what we say in Shabbos 7, that a corner adjacent to the public domain, since its use is inconvenient, does not join with the public domain, even though there is no separation between them, and it is like a karmelis. So too here, since it is too narrow for him to sit there. The Magen Avraham's words also imply unlike him, for he wrote "which does not have seven by seven," and it continues in one statement with the preceding matter, implying that its law is like any other sukkah that does not have seven in length and seven in width, which is invalid. Therefore, in my humble opinion one should not move from the words of the Magen Avraham. However, in one particular it seems that one may be lenient, as Bikkurei Yaakov wrote: "Although my heart has not emboldened me to be lenient to sit there against the view of the Magen Avraham, nevertheless it appears to me that one need not be careful not to place the table in that corner, and we are not concerned here lest he be drawn after his table, since many poskim hold that even when his table is inside the house we do not decree so; they are worthy to rely upon in this matter." Know further that the requirement of the measure of seven by seven applies throughout a height of ten handbreadths, as above in siman 631:10 in the gloss. See Pri Megadim, that everything must be squared, and sukkah decorations that reduce the space from the side also do so up to a height of ten handbreadths. See below in se'if 3 what I wrote there.
Is invalid. See the Rosh for the reason: for the head and most of a person an amah is needed, since a person sits within an amah, and his table occupies one handbreadth. So wrote the Ritva as well. Now, the dispute among the early authorities is known. According to the Rif, who ruled like Beis Shammai in both matters because one reason applies to both, the reason we prohibit a sukkah that holds his head and most of him while his table is inside the house is also only because of the decree lest he be drawn after his table, as the Ran explained his view. According to this, by Torah law his head and most of him alone is enough, and that is a measure of six by six; the Sages enacted seven by seven so that he can place the table inside the sukkah and not be drawn after it. But according to the other poskim, whenever it does not have seven by seven, so that his head and most of him can be there, it does not have the status of a valid sukkah by Torah law, for it is not a dwelling at all, as the Ran wrote. I have some difficulty with the words of Pri Megadim, who wrote that a sukkah which does not have seven by seven is invalid by Torah law, and that when his head and most of him are in a large sukkah while his table is inside the house, it is prohibited only because of the decree lest he be drawn after his table. In my humble opinion he has mounted two steeds at once, for the one who holds that when it holds only his head and most of him its invalidity is by Torah law does not hold of the decree at all when one sits in a large sukkah and his table is inside the house, as is explicit in the Ran and the other poskim; and the one who holds of this decree holds that a sukkah that holds only his head and most of him while his table is inside the house is invalid only rabbinically, as above. [Also what he wrote, that anything without seven by seven is invalid by Torah law because it is a law given to Moshe at Sinai, is also not clear, for the Rambam's statement is known that there is no dispute about a law given to Moshe at Sinai, whereas here Beis Shammai and Beis Hillel disagree, as stated in the Gemara.] Perhaps according to the Ramban, whom I brought in Sha'ar HaTziyun, it is somewhat implied that he held that even according to the Rif it is Torah law. More likely, he relied in this on the language of the Rambam, chapter 4 of Hilchos Sukkah, halachah 1, who wrote: if it was lower than ten, or less than seven by seven, or higher than twenty amos, etc.; thus he included this with all disqualifications that are by Torah law. Nevertheless, in chapter 7, halachah 8, his view is that we decree even in a large sukkah when his table is inside the house.
