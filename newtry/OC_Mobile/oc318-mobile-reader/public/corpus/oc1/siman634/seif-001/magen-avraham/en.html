We require seven by seven. Because their manner of eating was while reclining, and they did not eat upright and seated as we do, therefore seven by seven is required, so that it contains his head, most of him, and his table, excluding a small one into which his head and table enter but not most of him, or his head and most of him but not his table. These are Rashi's words. It is explicit that if it is not wide, even though it is very long, it is invalid, for he cannot sit in it with his head, most of him, and his table. So it is also implied from all the poskim, and so it is implied in se'if 2 regarding a round sukkah. The Bach pressed himself and wrote that the Tur's view is that if it is long and narrow it is valid, and Tosafos likewise wrote on page 3, s.v. lo nitzrechah, that a long sukkah that is not seven wide is valid. These are his words. But he was not precise, for it is obvious that there is a scribal error in Tosafos, and it must read: "and it was only seven wide," for it is referring to the words of Rashi and Rabbi Yehudah; see there, as they are discussing one that is seven wide. See there. On the contrary, it is proven from there that it is called a small sukkah. The main law is that a round sukkah must have a circumference of twenty-nine handbreadths and two fifths. As for what the Bach wrote, "why is it different from mezuzah, where even though it is long and narrow it is obligated, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 286:13," this is not difficult at all, for here we require that he sit in it with his head, most of him, and his table, as written above, and therefore we require a square. It appears to me that even for us, who sit upright and for whom six by six would suffice, nevertheless we require seven by seven, for less than that is not a dwelling at all, as the Ran wrote. It appears to me further that even if the sukkah is very large, but in one place there is a corner drawn inward that does not have seven by seven, it is forbidden to sit there, since the place is too narrow for him to sit there. So it is implied in the Gemara, page 3; see there, regarding the small chamber.
