1) [Seif 1] A sukkah that does not measure seven by seven tefachim is invalid. For so it is stated in the Gemara, Sukkah 3a: The halachah requires that it contain his head, the majority of his body, and his table. The Rosh writes that this means seven tefachim by seven tefachim, because a person sits within one amah and his table occupies one tefach, as stated in the Yerushalmi: How much space is needed for a table? One tefach. His words were cited by the Beit Yosef, who writes that this is agreed upon by all the poskim; see there. So too wrote the Bach.
2) Ibid. A sukkah that does not measure seven by seven tefachim is invalid. This applies specifically to this exact form, namely, a square measuring seven by seven tefachim. If it is not square, but is less than seven tefachim wide and more than seven tefachim long, it is invalid. Taz, s.k. 1; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 142; Responsa Divrei Shmuel, siman 203; Maamar Mordechai, os 1; Beit Me'ir. See Beit HaShoevah, os 6, who discusses this at length and also cites the words of Birkei Yosef, os 1, who writes that Tosafos to Sukkah came into his possession, etc. Beit HaShoevah challenges him and concludes that the Bach's ruling, which validates such a sukkah as a matter of halachah but is concerned to be stringent in practice, is incorrect. Rather, even as a matter of halachah, whenever it does not contain a square of seven by seven tefachim, it is invalid even after the fact according to all opinions, including Tosafos; see there.
3) Even according to our practice of sitting upright, for which six by six tefachim would seemingly suffice, seven by seven tefachim are nevertheless required, because anything smaller is not considered a dwelling at all, as the Ran writes. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Beit HaShoevah, os 4.
4) It appears that even if the sukkah is very large, but in one place there is a projecting corner that does not measure seven by seven tefachim, it is forbidden to sit there, because that place is too narrow for him to sit in. Magen Avraham, ibid.; Eliyah Rabbah, ibid.; Beit HaShoevah, os 7; Mishna Berurah, os 1. See there in Biur Halachah, where he challenges those who question the aforementioned words of Magen Avraham. He therefore writes that one should not depart from Magen Avraham's ruling. In one particular respect, however, one may be lenient: One need not be careful not to place the table in that projecting corner. We are not concerned that he may be drawn after his table, because many poskim hold that even when one's table is inside the house we do not issue this decree. They are sufficient authorities upon whom to rely in this respect; see there.
