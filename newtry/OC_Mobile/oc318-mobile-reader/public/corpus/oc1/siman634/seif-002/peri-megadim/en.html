See Turei Zahav, who discussed this at length, and the content of his intent is as I wrote in letter 1: that in a sukkah we require an actual seven by seven handbreadths according to Rava, not through combining parts. That is, the square that emerges from within the circle must be seven by seven handbreadths, and what the circle has beyond the square does not combine at all. Just as according to Rabbi Yochanan in Sukkah 8a such a circle requires four amos by four amos, the same applies for us; see the Bach, of blessed memory. In Yoreh De'ah 286:13, and see Shach, letter 23, the Mechaber there ruled that what is at the sides in a circle does combine properly, and if among all of it there is enough there to square four amos, it is obligated in mezuzah even though there is not a square emerging from within the circle, like the Rambam there, unlike the Rosh, who held that there too we require an actual square emerging from within the circle. See the Magen Avraham here, letter 1, so that the words of the Shulchan Aruch should not be difficult from one place to another: here, regarding sukkah, we require that he sit with his head, most of him, and his table, and this is impossible without an actual square; by contrast, regarding sukkah of four amos by four amos, according to the Rambam of blessed memory, as understood by the Rosh and Beis Yosef, the total area through combination suffices. See there in the printed Magen Avraham: where it says "Rashi" it should read "the Bach"; and that is according to what the Rosh understood in the Rambam. It is not difficult why sukkah differs from mezuzah, as stated. See siman 631 in the Magen Avraham, letter 10, that seven by seven handbreadths is required at the height of ten handbreadths; this implies that everything must be squared, and sukkah decorations that reduce the space from the side likewise reduce seven by seven handbreadths from the side throughout the entire height of ten handbreadths. All these cases require analysis whether, by Torah law, a sukkah in such a case is invalid, or whether it is rabbinic. See siman 629 in the Magen Avraham, letter 22. See Gemara Sukkah 10b: "we are agents of a mitzvah and exempt from sukkah," and see Tosafos and Chiddushei HaRan there. It appears that according to the one who says that sukkah decorations that are distanced four handbreadths invalidate rabbinically, that is why they considered it a pressing circumstance and as if they had no other sukkah; therefore they sat there, and they also recited a blessing, according to what is written in Chiddushei HaRan regarding the statement that a time of danger is not a proof. But if it was invalid by Torah law, they would not have slept there, for what difference would there be in it? Alternatively, since it is a dispute, they were at least stringent upon themselves. Understand this, for there is a practical legal difference in this, as we have said.
