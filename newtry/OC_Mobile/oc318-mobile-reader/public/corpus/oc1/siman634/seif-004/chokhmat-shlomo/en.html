There, se'if 4. One whose head and most of him were in the sukkah and his table was outside the sukkah, etc. Note: From the precision of his wording, that he wrote "it is as if he did not eat in the sukkah" and did not write "he has not fulfilled his obligation," it implies that he teaches us according to the view of Tosafos in Sukkah 3, who wrote that "you have never fulfilled the mitzvah of sukkah" implies by Torah law, meaning that even by Torah law he did not fulfill the mitzvah of sukkah properly, unlike the view of the Ran on this Mishnah, that it is only rabbinic and by Torah law he fulfilled the mitzvah. See Tosafos Yom Tov on the Mishnah at the source of this law, who brought these two views. From the Shulchan Aruch it appears like Tosafos, that he holds like Tosafos. Now, in my opinion this requires analysis, both regarding the Shulchan Aruch, who ruled so, and regarding Tosafos themselves, who wrote as follows: It was necessary there to bring a case where Beis Hillel are stringent rabbinically and Beis Shammai establish it as Torah law and did not decree; if they acted according to Beis Shammai he has not fulfilled his obligation even by Torah law, as we find according to Beis Shammai where they sat at the mouth of the sukkah and decreed lest one be drawn after his table, and it says: you have never fulfilled the mitzvah of sukkah, meaning that even by Torah law he did not fulfill it; and from Beis Shammai we learn to Beis Hillel. This is difficult for me. What proof is there from there? Logically, it is certainly impossible to say that something valid by Torah law but invalid only rabbinically should not be effective even so that he has not fulfilled the mitzvah by Torah law; the intellect certainly does not allow this. Therefore it would seem that this applies only to sukkah, and according to Beis Shammai. For Rabbi Eliezer holds that a borrowed sukkah is invalid, and since Rabbi Eliezer was a Shamuti, which the commentators explained means that he was among the students of Beis Shammai, if so presumably Beis Shammai also hold this way. And even if it is not necessary that all of them hold so, nevertheless one can say that they hold like Rabbi Eliezer in this matter. In particular, necessity points that way, for otherwise how do the Sages have the power to nullify the Torah mitzvah so that one has not fulfilled his obligation by Torah law? Rather, perforce they hold that a borrowed sukkah is invalid. Therefore, according to them, since we require the sukkah to be truly his, the Sages indeed have power to make their enactment equivalent to Torah law, for they declared his property ownerless, and this is within their power, for property declared ownerless by Beis Din is ownerless. If so, the sukkah is not his. And even though it is not stolen but only borrowed, that too does not help according to him. But regarding other mitzvos, where the device of ownerless property by Beis Din is not relevant, or even regarding sukkah, although we rule in this matter like Beis Shammai that it does not help when his head and most of him are in the sukkah and his table is inside the house, nevertheless since we rule that a borrowed sukkah is valid, even if they declared his property ownerless, in any case it is no worse than borrowed. If so, from where do we know that he has not fulfilled the mitzvah by Torah law? How do the Sages have the power to make their words Torah law where no monetary matter is involved? And how could Tosafos write, "from Beis Shammai we learn to Beis Hillel"? What proof is this? One can say that there Beis Shammai hold that a borrowed sukkah is invalid, and they touched the matter through ownerless property by Beis Din, but in general it is certainly considered a fulfillment by Torah law. All the more so regarding the Shulchan Aruch, from whose wording it appears that he holds this way; this requires much analysis for now. Examine this carefully. See further what I wrote regarding the reasoning of the Shulchan Aruch and its explanation in my work on Orach Chayim, in the year 609, page 46b, in a responsum to the holy community of Mikolayev, which established its place in the laws of Pesach, siman 445, where I wrote to explain this correctly, with Hashem's help. Examine this carefully.
