There, s.v. 'until all of them break.' This is imprecise. If enough thread remains to tie a bow, it is valid even when all the threads broke. If that amount does not remain, then even when a single thread broke completely (meaning its two ends broke, one on each side) it is invalid even according to Rosh, as explained in siman 12. Perhaps this is a scribal error and the text should read: 'He holds that they are not invalid even if all of them break.' This means that it remains valid when enough remains to tie a bow, and we are not concerned that it may break further and no such amount will remain. According to our practice, however, if both ends of a thread were severed, it is invalid even if enough remains to tie a bow, because we are stringent in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam's view. It is therefore necessary to be concerned and require inspection. See the Rosh's wording cited by Beit Yosef: 'Moreover, tzitzis do not break easily, and people customarily repair them even before they reach the point of invalidity, etc.' See there.
