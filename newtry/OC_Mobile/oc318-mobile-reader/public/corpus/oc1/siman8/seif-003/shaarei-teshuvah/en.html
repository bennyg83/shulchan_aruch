Biur HaTaz — and see Mas'as Binyamin siman 48: wayfarers who place the tallis folded and hang it around their necks on their shoulders such that one end when folded with the tzitzis comes down before him from the right and the other end with two tzitzis comes down from the left — do not fulfill tzitzis in this manner, and one rebukes them, for in this case all agree it is not atifah, etc. And Dvar Shmuel siman 123 wrote: wrapping the tallis is a mitzvah that the tallis be on the head and not on their shoulders alone; and where the majority are accustomed not to cover their heads and are strict about the minority who cover their heads — they must follow the custom of the majority, etc. And it appears this is after the blessing when he is wrapped in it to pray; but when he blesses on it and wraps himself immediately — then at that time he should cover his head with the tallis and not rely on throwing the tallis over his shoulders alone; and thereby we need not heed the custom of the majority, which is a mistaken custom that they did not fulfill the mitzvah of blessing, as written in the name of Mishna Berurah; and likewise Radbaz ch. 2 siman 242: those who do not cover the head act improperly, and thus practiced — the custom of our fathers is Torah, etc.; and in Dvar Shmuel there he cited from this responsum of Radbaz, etc.: (3) In Shulchan Aruch tallitos. And Rashbatz wrote ch. 2 siman 40: one should always bless "to wrap oneself in tzitzis," whether on a large or small tallis, whether by way of wrapping or by way of wearing; and there are two hundred customs in total; and likewise if one wished to bless to wear while holding it in his hand but it is not evident from his blessings that he is a Torah scholar — we are accustomed to bless "to wrap oneself" even when wearing as clothing, etc.; and see below seif katan 7 that Rama's opinion is to bless "on the mitzvah," and it appears that if one wears a tallis katan by way of wrapping he may properly bless on it "to wrap oneself":
