Responsa Masas Binyamin, siman 48, discusses travelers who place the tallis around their shoulders while it is folded, with one folded end and its tzitzis hanging down in front on the right and the other end with two tzitzis hanging down on the left. In this manner they do not fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis, and they should be rebuked, because according to all opinions this is not considered wrapping. See there. Dvar Shmuel, siman 123, writes that the mitzvah of wrapping in a tallis requires that the tallis be upon the head, rather than merely upon the shoulders. In a place where the majority customarily do not cover their heads and they object to the minority who do, those who cover should follow the majority custom. See there. It appears that this applies after the blessing, while one remains wrapped in the tallis during prayer. At the moment when he blesses and immediately wraps himself, however, he should cover his head with the tallis and should not rely merely upon casting it over his shoulders. In this matter we should pay no attention to the majority practice, because it is an erroneous custom under which they do not fulfill the requirement connected with the blessing, as stated in the name of Masas Binyamin. Radbaz, part 2, siman 342, likewise writes that those who do not cover their heads act improperly, notwithstanding their claim that this is their established ancestral custom. Dvar Shmuel there also cites this responsum of Radbaz.
In Shulchan Aruch: tallisos. Tashbetz, part 2, siman 40, writes that one should always recite the blessing “to wrap oneself in tzitzis,” whether upon a large or small tallis, whether it is worn by wrapping or as an ordinary garment. The broader formulation includes the narrower one. If he wishes to recite “to wear tzitzis,” he is permitted to do so, but his blessing will not reveal that he is a Torah scholar. Our custom is to recite “to wrap oneself” even when wearing it as a garment. See below, seif katan 7, where Rama’s view is to recite “concerning the mitzvah of tzitzis.” It appears that if one wears a tallis katan by wrapping himself in it, he may properly recite “to wrap oneself in tzitzis.”
