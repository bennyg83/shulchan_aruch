13) [Se'if 3] Small talleisim, etc. Ateres Zekeinim writes: The shoulder sections should be somewhat wide, and the garment somewhat large, so that it can cover one's head and the majority of his body from behind; otherwise it is not called a garment. Terumas HaDeshen, cited by Beis Yosef, writes: One who wears a tallis with a neck opening should make it large enough to cover his head and the majority of his body from behind. At the time of the berachah, he should wrap it over his head, cast it behind himself, and remain wrapped this way for at least the time required to walk four amos. He should then draw it forward over his head so that it hangs from his neck in the tallis's neck opening. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 32. See siman 16 and our comments there, with G-d's help.
14) There: It is proper to place it upon his head. See Shalmei Tzibbur, page 32c, for his explanation of these words of Maran. It is a sound interpretation, for this is also implied by Maran's words, 'and wrap himself in it': he means that one should cast it behind himself so that it also covers the body, and remain wrapped this way, etc., as stated above in the name of Terumas HaDeshen. Levush states this explicitly. Yafeh LaLev 8.
15) There: It is proper to place it upon his head with its width along his height. In every context, the long side is called the length and the short side the width. Machatzis HaShekel 4. See Magen Avraham 11:16. If he placed its length along his height, it is also valid. Beis Yosef; Bach; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur 2; Eliyah Rabbah 5; and Shesilei Zeisim 7.
16) There: He should remain wrapped this way, etc. This applies specifically to a garment made as a single square piece with the neck opening in the center. When he wraps its length around his head and its width around his body, two tzitzis are on one side and two on the other. If the garment is sewn, however, then when he wraps its length around his head and its width around his body, all four tzitzis will come to one side, which is improper. He should therefore put it on in its ordinary manner, not wrap it over his head at all, and not recite 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis,' but rather 'concerning the mitzvah of tzitzis.' Shulchan Aruch HaRav 7.
17) There: Afterward he should draw it forward over his head and wear it. The custom developed to make an atarah from a piece of silk as a marker, so that the tzitzis that were in front remain forever in front, just as a board that merited being placed on the north side of the Mishkan remained forever on the north. Magen Avraham 6 in the name of Shelah. In the name of Shaar HaKavanos, however, he writes that the Arizal was not particular always to place the same side upon his head, as many people practice. This appears in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, at the end of derush 2. See also Matzas Shimurim, page 24a; Nagid U'Metzaveh, laws of tzitzis; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 9; and Ben Ish Chai, Bereishis 8.
