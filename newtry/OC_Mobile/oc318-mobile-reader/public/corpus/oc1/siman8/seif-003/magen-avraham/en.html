“He should stand.” Some say one needs actual wrapping, and “wrapping” everywhere implies head wrapping (Taz). It appears to me this applies only to a garment made like one square piece with the neck opening in the middle—then when he wraps along its length, as Beis Yosef cites Ri, only that is called wrapping, so he would have two tzitzis on each side; but if the garment is sewn so all the tzitzis come from one side—see siman 10:6. Still there is not so much stringency, for most poskim hold wrapping is not required—and all the more for us who bless “lemitzvas tzitzis,” obviously no wrapping is needed—so too Magen Mishpat siman 110.
“And afterward.” For the main stringency is at the moment of donning, for if he wished he could remove it afterward—therefore standing is required only for donning (Taz).
“And put it on.” The custom is to make an atarah from a piece of silk so the tzitzis that are “before him” remain always before him, as it says of the plank that merited to be placed in the north—it is always in the north (Shelah). Writings state the Arizal was not particular to always place the tallis on one side as many do; in one version of Kavvanos the Arizal was not particular to always place one side on the head as many do—end quote. Some place the right side on the left shoulder so they cross—see siman 41:36.
