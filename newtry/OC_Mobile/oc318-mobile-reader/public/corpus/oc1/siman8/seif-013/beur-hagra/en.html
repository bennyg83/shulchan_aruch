Seif 13, "one who wears," etc. So wrote the Rosh at the end of Hilchos Tzitzis. The Beis Yosef explained that walking is an interruption, as he wrote. This is not so; how is it different from moving from corner to corner in a large hall, as written at the beginning of siman 273? See below siman 104, seif 2 and 3, and Magen Avraham there, small paragraph 3. Rather, it is because of change of place, as Magen Avraham wrote. This too is not so, as written below siman 217, seif 1, "specifically," etc., and as Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote in Sukkah chapter 6, halachah 12: it seems to me that if he went out, etc.; and as written in seif 14. But the Rosh's view is that he interrupts with tefillin and other matters. So too in the Tur: if he interrupts, etc., therefore, etc.
"With speech." His view is that speech is an interruption, like the Behag, Raavad, and Rosh in Chullin chapter 6 in the name of Rabbenu Tam regarding between one slaughter and another. As written there, specifically shofar blasts and Hallel reading are different, since they are in the middle of the mitzvah and he must complete them, so speech is not an interruption. But there, since if he wishes he need not slaughter, there is completion of the mitzvah and interruption. So it appears from the Rif at the end of Rosh Hashanah regarding the blasts, and so wrote Yoreh Deah siman 265, seif 5. Tosafos in Chullin there were uncertain about this. The Ramban in Milchamos, Arvei Pesachim, wrote that only where the two mitzvos are obligatory, like tefillin, does speech interrupt; but where one is not obligatory for the other, speech does not interrupt, except a matter that is obligatory, such as the Haggadah between cups and covering the blood between slaughterings in Chullin there. But where they are not obligatory, it does not; therefore blessing on wine before the meal exempts, etc.; and similarly prayer in the middle of a meal in Arvei Pesachim; and in one mitzvah, such as greeting and responding in Shema, Hallel, and Megillah. See there, and Ran there and in Chullin chapter 6, and Yoreh Deah siman 19.
