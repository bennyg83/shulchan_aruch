Walking constitutes an interruption. The measure of walking that is considered an interruption is twenty-two amot; this is from the words of Tosafos in the chapter “Elu ne'emarin,” and the Rama brought it below in siman 166. See the responsum of Beit Yaakov, siman 16, and Taz, seif kaf-yud. And in the responsum of Chacham Tzvi, siman 158, what he wondered about this; and see in Sefer Bnei Chayya what he answered on this; and see Chavot Yair 227. And in Magen Avraham, siman 166, seif kaf-gimel: Magen Avraham wrote, it seems to me that if both are in one house, going from room to room is not considered an interruption, see above. The Taz ruled that even walking from his house to the beit haknesset is not an interruption unless he interrupted with other matters, or spoke, or put on tefillin in the meantime—in which case the blessing on tefillin is an interruption between the tallit katan and the tallit gadol; but walking alone is not an interruption, see above. And see in Shiurei Knesset HaGedolah, who elaborated.
