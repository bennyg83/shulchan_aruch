Is considered an interruption. The measure of walking that is considered an interruption is twenty-two amos. This is from the words of Tosafos in chapter Eilu Ne’emarin, and the Rama brings it below in siman 166. See Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 16, the Taz, s.k. 10, Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 158, who wondered about this, Bnei Chiya and what he answered regarding it, Chavos Yair, siman 227, and Magen Avraham, siman 166, s.k. 3. The Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that if both are in one house, going from room to room is not considered an interruption; see there. The Taz ruled that even walking from one’s house to the synagogue is not an interruption unless he interrupted with other matters, spoke, or put on tefillin in the meantime. If so, the blessing over tefillin is an interruption between the tallis katan and tallis gadol, but walking alone is not an interruption; see there. See Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, who discusses this at length.
