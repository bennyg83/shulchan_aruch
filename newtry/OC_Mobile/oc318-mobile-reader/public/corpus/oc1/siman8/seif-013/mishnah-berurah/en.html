(32) To bless over it. Even if he immediately went to the synagogue and did not divert his mind at all in the meantime.
(33) For walking. The later authorities agreed that walking itself is not considered an interruption. Even so, they wrote that one must bless over the tallis gadol because of the change of place. According to this, the same applies if when he left his house he immediately returned to his house and put on the tallis gadol there: he must also bless over it, even though he had the tallis gadol in mind at the time of the blessing, for it is like matters that do not require a blessing afterward, where a change of place is an interruption, as written in siman 178. (34) Is considered an interruption. See Chayei Adam, who holds that one must bless, and so is written in Sefer Ketzos HaShulchan. But other later authorities disagree and hold that since he had it in mind, a change of place is not considered an interruption in this matter, and one should not bless again because of a doubtful blessing. If he spoke in the meantime regarding matters not needed for wearing, all opinions agree that he must bless again. If he had no intent at all at the time of the blessing to exempt the tallis gadol, he must certainly bless over it.
(35) Over the tallis katan. If he wore a tallis katan under his clothing and blessed over it, and afterward he goes to the synagogue and wraps himself in the tallis gadol, he should bless over the tallis gadol, for this is another mitzvah act and the custom is to bless over it. However, if he explicitly intended at the time of the first blessing to exempt it, there are opinions to be lenient, and one should be careful from the outset to intend properly according to the custom.
