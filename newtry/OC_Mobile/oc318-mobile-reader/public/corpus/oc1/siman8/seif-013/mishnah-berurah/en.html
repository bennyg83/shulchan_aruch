(32) To bless on it—even if he immediately went to the synagogue and did not distract his attention in the meantime at all.

(33) For walking—and the later authorities agreed that walking alone is not considered an interruption. Even so, they wrote that one must bless on the large tallis because of change of place. And according to this, the same applies if when he left his house he immediately returned to his house and donned the large tallis there—he must likewise bless on it, even if his mind was also on the large tallis at the time of the blessing; for it is like things that do not require a blessing afterward, where change of place is an interruption, as written in siman 178. And see the note after this.

(34) It counts as an interruption—and Chayei Adam was concerned for the view of those who hold that change of place is not an interruption in this; therefore he was stringent not to bless unless the synagogue was far from his house, or he did not have it in mind at the time of the blessing, or he distracted his attention between them.

(35) Inside his house—even if he wore the small tallis in this room and the large tallis in another room, change of place from room to room is not an interruption, since he had this in mind; as below in siman 273 at the end of seif 1. And what he wrote regarding speech—even one word. And see above in note 28 what we wrote in the name of the later authorities for practical halachah.
