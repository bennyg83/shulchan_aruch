Diversion of attention is an interruption — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote: and Chayei Adam, etc. Know that from the Taz's words it also seems he holds that change of place is not an interruption here; and the Gra in his commentaries wrote like the Taz. But according to his words it would seem the law applies to Kiddush as well — his intent helps even from house to house. The Shulchan Aruch did not write thus below in siman 273, seif 1, even according to those who say [otherwise]. Neshmas Adam reconciles with difficulty and distinguishes between them — this requires study.

If his intent was — it would seem that even if his intent was not expressly for this but only implicitly, since he is accustomed to wear it every day it is as if his intent was for this, as above. Now, per the Rama who ruled above that on a tallit katan one blesses "al mitzvas tzitzis," it fits well that implicitly one does not intend in this blessing also for the tallit gadol, as Peri Megadim wrote and we copied in the Mishna Berurah. But per the Mechaber's ruling above in seif 5 that one may bless "lehisateif," then automatically the blessing applies also to the tallit gadol — as with slaughtering in Yoreh Deah siman 19, when several birds were before him: even if he blessed on one they took in his hand, implicitly the blessing covers all of them. Therefore we must say that here the tallit gadol was not before him at the time of the blessing. It is surprising that the Shulchan Aruch commentators did not explain all this.
