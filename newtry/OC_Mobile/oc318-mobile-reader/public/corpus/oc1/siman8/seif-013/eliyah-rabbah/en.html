He must bless, etc. The Bach’s view is that even if other garments were before him and he had in mind at the time of the blessing to wear them, he must bless, since his mind was not on this one that was brought to him. This is not comparable to fruits in siman 206 or slaughter in Yoreh Deah siman 19, for eating and slaughter have a fixed framework, unlike dressing, which has no fixed framework. But the Taz and Olas Tamid challenged him, saying that slaughter and dressing are the same, and he need not bless. This is difficult, for if so, even where it was not before him, if they bring him more of the same kind he should also not need to bless, as the Rama ruled in Yoreh Deah there. Although the Taz wrote there about the Rama that the Beis Yosef wrote that the Ran, Raavad, and Rambam hold like the Rosh, so that the Rama’s words are a lone view against them, end quote, I already wrote in Eliyah Rabbah that with respect, he erred in this, for the Beis Yosef did not write so. Rather, in slaughter there is a law of changed intention, as he brought proof there that it is not comparable to withdrawal from a meal, where one does not bless again; see there. But regarding the law of siman 1 he did not write at all. See there, where I concluded in accordance with the Rama. Even aside from this it is difficult, for the Rama should have ruled so here as well. Rather, the main ruling is like the Bach, and his reason may be that in slaughter, as long as something is before him, he fixes his intention that more may be brought for him to slaughter; this is not so by tzitzis, where with one garment he has fulfilled the mitzvah. This is further implied by the Bach and Perishah to Yoreh Deah siman 13. Sefer Magen Avraham wrote that lechatchilah one should not interrupt, for it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary blessing.
