And some say. See Taz. He copied the words of the Beis Yosef, who brought the words of the Agur, and the Beis Yosef explained that it speaks where his intent was to wear it immediately; otherwise, what is Rabbeinu Chaim’s reason for distinguishing whether a tallis katan remained on him? This is unlike the Tur, whose view inclined that if he removed the tallis intending to wear it immediately, he need not bless again even if no tallis katan remained on him. In Shulchan Aruch, the Beis Yosef ruled according to his explanation and according to his lengthy view, that he must bless again even if a tallis katan remained on him. Perhaps he rejected Maharam’s distinction, for what reason is there to distinguish when a tallis katan remains on him, as the Levush wrote. However, it appears to me that the Mechaber omitted Maharam’s distinction for this reason: the Maharam mentioned in Agur, and Rabbeinu Chaim Or Zarua, follow their own views in what the Beis Yosef brought above in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, who copied a responsum that Maharam holds that walking from his house to the synagogue is not considered an interruption, and he did not go to the synagogue in a tallis katan. There is a difficulty from siman 178, seif 2, in the gloss, from the Gemara that by things that do not require a blessing afterward in their place, going from room to room is an interruption. One must answer as Magen Avraham wrote, letter 17, that when a tallis katan is upon him the mitzvah has not yet left. Therefore, in the case about which the Agur was asked, where one left the synagogue, removed the tallis, and later came to the synagogue and wore it, Rabbeinu Chaim Or Zarua properly distinguished according to Maharam’s own view: when no tallis katan is on him and he returns to the synagogue and wears it, the walking from room to room is an interruption and he blesses again; but when the tallis katan is on him, he does not bless, for according to Maharam, walking is not considered an interruption. He holds like the Tur, that when his intent is to wear it again he does not bless, and we come to require a blessing only because of moving from room to room. This is unlike the Mechaber, who in seif 13 decided that walking is considered an interruption and the law of seif 14 is in the same room, not like the Tur, with proof from Rava by tefillin, and he does not accept the distinction of the Darchei Moshe. If so, what reason is there to distinguish whether a tallis katan remained on him or not?
And the Taz. The Taz, of blessed memory, explained Rabbeinu Chaim’s view as being like siman 206 regarding fruits: if some are still before him, it helps, and the same applies here. He follows his approach in letter 10, where we wrote that some of the fruits must still remain. Even according to the Mechaber’s ruling in seif 12 that wearing is not compared to fruit, as the Taz wrote there in letter 10, here there is no new act. The Mechaber was not satisfied with this, and therefore omitted it. The Taz challenged the Rama with the difficulty of Lechem Chamudos and Magen Avraham, letter 19: if the ‘some say’ require both conditions, this is difficult from siman 25, seif 12, for there no tefillin remained, etc., and nevertheless he need not go back and bless. If one says that this ‘some say’ applies even where he had no intent, the Beis Yosef’s difficulty returns. See Magen Avraham on this and letter 12. The words of the Levush at first glance seem very difficult, for here he ruled like the Mechaber that one must bless, and in siman 25, seif 12, he ruled that one need not bless when they shifted if his intent was to return them to their place. I saw that Eliyah Rabbah, letter 16, noticed this. One may say that he distinguishes between removing them entirely and mere shifting. This also answers the apparent contradiction in the Rama, for he too distinguishes between removing them entirely and shifting. The Levush’s words are clear as a garment. However, in Eliyah Rabbah 25, letter 20, he wrote that the same applies where he removed them. One must align the Levush’s words, but I do not know how, as I wrote. See what is written in Magen Avraham, letter 18, about this.
