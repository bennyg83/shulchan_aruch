And there are those who say — Taz: He copied the words of Beit Yosef, who brought the words of the Agur and Beit Yosef explained that it deals with intent to wear it immediately — for if not, what is R' Chaim's reason who distinguished between when a small tallit remains on him and this is not like Tur, who inclined that if he removed the tallit with intent to wear it immediately he need not bless again, even without a small tallit remaining on him. And in Shulchan Aruch the author ruled like Beit Yosef per his explanation and like his view in the Tur that one must bless again even if the small one is on him; and it is possible he rejected the distinction of Maharam — what reason when a small tallit remains on him, as Levush wrote. And it appears to me to say that therefore the author omitted Maharam's distinction — namely the Maharam mentioned above in the Agur, and R' Chaim here follow their methods in what Beit Yosef brought above in the name of Terumat HaDeshen, copying a responsum that Maharam holds walking from his house to the synagogue is not considered an interruption, and he did not go with a small tallit to the synagogue. And it is difficult from 178:2 in Hagahah from the Gemara that things that do not require a blessing afterward — walking from room to room is an interruption; and one must say as Magen Avraham letter 17 wrote, that with a small tallit on him the mitzvah had not yet departed. And therefore in that case asked of the Agur — he left the synagogue and removed the tallit and afterward came to the synagogue and wore it — R' Chaim distinguished well here according to Maharam himself, who explained thus: that he blesses again when there is no small tallit on him, and walking such as from room to room is an interruption when he returned to the synagogue and wore it; not so when the small one is on him — he does not bless, for according to Maharam walking is not considered an interruption, and he holds like Tur that with intent to wear immediately he does not bless — only from the reason of room to room do we come upon him. Not so the author in seif 13, who ruled that walking is considered an interruption, and the law of seif 14 is in that same room and not like Tur; and proof from Rava regarding tefillin (and he does not hold the distinction of Darkei Moshe, Taz 12) — if so, what reason between whether a small one remained or not?

And Taz, of blessed memory, explained R' Chaim's view as in Berachos regarding fruits — that if some still remain before him it helps here too (and this follows his method in letter 10 — see there — that we require some of the fruit still remain). And even for the author's ruling in seif 12 that one does not compare to fruits regarding wearing, as Taz wrote there letter 10 — here there is no new act. And the author was not satisfied with this and therefore omitted it. And he challenged Rama in the question of 38 and Magen Avraham letter 19 — if there are two, both are required — difficult from siman 25 seif 12, for there tefillin did not remain, etc., and nevertheless one need not return and bless; and if one says these even without intent — difficult why, as Beit Yosef's question and Magen Avraham on this — and see letter 12. And here the words of Levush seem to graze the scalp, for here he ruled like the author that one must bless, and in siman 25 seif 12 he ruled one need not bless when they moved it with intent to return to its place; and I saw in Eliyah Rabbah letter 16 he sensed this, and one can say it is different for him between complete removal and shifting; and through this Rama's contradiction is settled, for he too makes a difference between complete removal and shifting, and Levush's words are clear as a garment. And indeed in Eliyah Rabbah 25 letter 20 he wrote likewise regarding removal, and one should align Levush's words — and I do not know how, as I wrote. See in Magen Avraham letter 18 from this.
