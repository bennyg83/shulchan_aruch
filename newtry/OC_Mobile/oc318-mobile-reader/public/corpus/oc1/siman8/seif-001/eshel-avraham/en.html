And he should recite the blessing. Regarding why blessings are not recited over all mitzvot, Responsa Ramban, siman 189, answers as follows. No blessing is recited over a mitzvah that entails no act, such as releasing debts in the shemittah year, or over judicial matters entrusted to a court. The same applies to matters dependent upon another person's decision, such as charity and the like; to a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, such as returning stolen property or interest; and to gifts to the kohanim, because the recipients acquire them from the table of the Most High. This differs from redemption of the firstborn son and separating terumah: the blessing there is not recited over giving the item but over separating it. Likewise, no blessing is recited over a mitzvah that is not the primary mitzvah, such as chalitzah and yibbum. The primary mitzvah is procreation, and these mitzvot branch from it. No blessing is recited over court-imposed executions either, because the Holy One, blessed is He, does not desire the death of the wicked. For the same reason, no blessing is recited over mayim acharonim, which are required because of Sodomite salt; one does not recite a blessing over calamity. See there at length. In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4: The citation attached to the word “and he should stand” is a scribal error and should be placed upon the words “over his head.” Regarding his words, “If so, when he wraps himself, we explained,” etc., one may say that he lets most of the tallit hang along the length of his body, for otherwise the wrapping would be lengthwise.
