1) [Se'if 1] He should wrap himself in tzitzis, etc. This should be done immediately after netilas yadayim. Tur; Magen Avraham 1; Chayei Adam 11:1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 1. Machatzis HaShekel explains that the reason is so that he immediately wraps himself in a mitzvah. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham, writes that it is so he does not walk four amos without tzitzis; Chayei Adam there writes likewise. One should not, however, don tzitzis before netilas yadayim, because it is proper to recite the berachah immediately before performing the mitzvah; see se'if 10. According to our practice of reciting 'to wrap oneself' over the tallis gadol with intent to exempt the tallis katan, this is not necessary, and he could first don it before washing his hands. Yet Magen Avraham, siman 4:1, writes in the name of Seder HaYom that one should not touch his garments before netilas yadayim. Accordingly, he should not don the tallis until after washing. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 1. See Levushei Serad and our comments above in siman 4:6. Even if one rises before daylight, he should wrap himself immediately; when daylight comes, he should touch the tzitzis and recite the berachah, as stated in se'if 16. Machatzis HaShekel. According to our practice, when reciting 'to wrap oneself' over the tallis gadol, he should intend to exempt this one, as will be explained there with G-d's help. Although our custom, following the Arizal, is to sleep in a tallis katan, as will be explained in siman 21:3 with G-d's help, this ruling remains relevant if one happened not to sleep in his tallis, or in places where people cannot sleep in their clothing because of fleas or the like. See Shaarei Teshuvah 1.
2) There: And he should recite the berachah, etc. Responsa Ramban 189 explains why a berachah is not recited over every mitzvah. No berachah is recited over a mitzvah that involves no action, such as release of debts, or over matters entrusted to beis din. The same applies to matters dependent upon another person's decision, such as tzedakah; to a mitzvah occasioned by a transgression, such as returning stolen property or interest; and to gifts to kohanim, because they receive them from the table of the Most High. Pidyon HaBen and separating terumah differ: the berachah is not recited over giving them, but over the act of separation. Nor is a berachah recited over a mitzvah that is not itself the primary mitzvah, such as chalitzah and yibbum, for the primary mitzvah is procreation and these mitzvos branch from it. No berachah is recited over judicial executions either, because the Holy One, blessed is He, does not desire the death of the wicked. For this reason, no berachah is recited over mayim acharonim, which are required because of Sodomite salt; one does not recite a berachah over calamity. See the responsum at length. Eshel Avraham 1; Gra's commentary 2; Kerem Shlomo.
3) There: He should wrap himself in tzitzis and recite the berachah, etc. This implies that one does not recite the berachah before beginning to wrap himself, but while wrapping, before he is fully wrapped, as with tefillin. Eliyah Rabbah 2 writes that Responsa Beis Yaakov 88 implies this; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, writes that after placing the tallis upon himself, but before taking the tzitzis in his hand (having merely thrown them behind him) he may recite the berachah; see there. Chida, Machazik Berachah 1, however, writes in the name of Shaagas Aryeh 32 that it is proper to recite the berachah before wrapping, when one holds the tallis and is about to wrap himself, because this is immediately before performance. He writes that this is the custom, unlike Responsa Beis Yaakov. Kesher Gudal 2:5; Maamar Mordechai 6; Ikrei HaDat 2:34 in the name of Shaagas Aryeh; and Tefillah LeDavid, page 5b, rule likewise, unlike Beis Yaakov. See also Zechirah LeChaim, Hilchos Berachos 9; Beis Yaakov 21 in the names of Shaagas Aryeh and Machazik Berachah, noting that Rokeach at the beginning of siman 361 agrees; Kerem Shlomo in the name of Shaagas Aryeh; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 9:8; and Chayei Olam on Shulchan Aruch, se'if 1, who writes that this is implied by Magen Avraham. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 2, likewise writes that Magen Avraham implies this. See Pnei Yitzchak, maareches tes, paragraph 118, and Yafeh LaLev 1:3, who agrees and cites several additional later authorities. Shalmei Tzibbur discusses this at length and writes on page 32a that there is a straight path by which one can satisfy Taz and Beis Yaakov even while reciting the berachah beforehand with the tallis still in his hands: immediately after the berachah, he should place the entire tallis backward over his head and body at once. The berachah then unquestionably precedes performance. After putting it on this way, he should raise the corners over his neck and wrap himself in the manner of the Yishmaelim for the time required to walk four amos. He concludes: 'I have seen people of exemplary conduct among the prominent residents of Yerushalayim, may it be rebuilt and established, follow this practice.' Ben Ish Chai, first year, Bereishis 5, however, writes: 'After completing the berachah, wrap only your head in it, unlike those who consider themselves clever and first wrap their entire body. We have only the Arizal's words.' Nevertheless, the chassidim of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, who follow the Arizal, practice as Shalmei Tzibbur writes, because this matter is not stated explicitly in the Arizal's words; see Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis. I, the servant, provided support for their practice in Responsa Be'er Mayim Chaim; see there.
4) There: And he should recite the berachah while standing. This means that both the berachah and the wrapping should be performed while standing. Olas Tamid 1; Magen Avraham 2; Eliyah Rabbah 2; Shulchan Gavohah 1; Makor Chochmah 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 3-4; Kesher Gudal 2:6; Siddur Beis Yaakov 31; and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, ibid.
5) There: And he should recite the berachah while standing. Shemen HaMaor writes: Shulchan Aruch did not explain the after-the-fact law; if one wrapped himself while seated, must he wrap himself again while standing? Magen Avraham 2 merely refers to what he wrote in siman 585. Pri Megadim explains that there, se'if 1, regarding shofar, Shulchan Aruch rules that one who sounded it while seated fulfilled his obligation; the same applies here, and one who wrapped himself while seated has fulfilled his obligation. This is correct. Chayei Adam 12:3 likewise writes that one who recited the berachah while seated fulfilled his obligation. See also Amudei HaShulchan on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch 9:13 and Tefillah LeDavid 18, who writes that one who wrapped himself and recited the berachah while seated fulfilled his obligation. Regarding Eliyah Rabbah 2, see Shemen HaMaor there, who rejects his words.
6) Before the berachah and wrapping, it is proper to recite the verses of Barchi Nafshi through 'Who stretches out the heavens like a curtain.' Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, in the name of Mishnas Chassidim; Siddur Beis Yaakov. Those accustomed to say these verses should not recite them before Birchos HaTorah, as Chida writes in Tziporen Shamir 2:6 and Kesher Gudal 5:31, and as Ruach Chaim writes in siman 5:2.
