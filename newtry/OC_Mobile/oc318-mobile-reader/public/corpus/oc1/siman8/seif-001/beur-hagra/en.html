Seif 1, "he should wrap." Meaning that the wrapping too is done standing, as written in Semak, siman 92, that shofar and tzitzis are derived by the verbal analogy "for you" and "for you" from the Omer, where it is written "standing grain," and we say: read not "standing grain" but "standing," etc., as written in the Rosh at the end of Arvei Pesachim; and the mnemonic is: for counsel, always stand.
"And recite the blessing." It seems proper to give the reason why no blessing is recited on some mitzvos: 1. any mitzvah that has no act, such as remission of debts; 2. one not entirely dependent on the doer, such as charity, lest the recipient not accept it, and judgment, lest the litigants not accept, and the like; 3. a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, such as returning stolen property, for we were not commanded to steal in order to return it; and similarly court-imposed death penalties, for God has pity on His creatures and does not desire, etc., as they said: one does not bless over misfortunes. For this reason one does not bless over mayim acharonim or priestly gifts, for he is not the giver; they receive from the table of the Most High. But on challah and the like one blesses over its separation. What I wrote above, "lest they not accept," includes many mitzvos, such as honoring father and mother, for if they waive it, it is waived and uprooted, as stated in Kesubos 40a: where do we say, etc.; and likewise a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as stated at the beginning of Bava Kamma chapter 9. See Abudarham, Laws of Blessings, and the responsum of the Ramban, siman 189, in the name of the Raavad and RIF.
"Standing." Orchos Chaim wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: all blessings are recited standing. He explained that this refers to blessings over mitzvos, for it cannot refer to blessings over enjoyment, since we say: if they sat, each blesses for himself; if they reclined, etc., and likewise: what is the order of reclining, etc.; and likewise Grace after Meals and many similar cases. See Magen Avraham, who objected from the Mishnah in Challah: a woman who is sitting, etc.; there it is because of the blessing. See Rosh, Sukkah chapter 4, siman 3. But Rabbenu Yonah wrote only that the wrapping is standing; earlier, regarding the blessing, he did not write "standing," and neither did the Tur. The Magen Avraham's statement that challah is not a mitzvah has no basis, for it is forbidden to make one's dough in small kav measures, and we say in Pesachim chapter 3: what are you thinking, to be stringent? It is a stringency that leads to a leniency, etc.
