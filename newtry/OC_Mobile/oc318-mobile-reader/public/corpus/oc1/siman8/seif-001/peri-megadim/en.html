Standing. See Taz. The Beis Yosef, in the name of Semak, derives it from the Omer, for it is written, ‘from when the sickle begins upon the standing grain you shall begin to count,’ etc. See what is written in Magen Avraham, letter 1, and in Yoreh Deah siman 265 in the Tur, that one blesses standing, and the Rav in the gloss brought it. I do not know why he omitted the main law, that one should perform the circumcision standing, since this is according to the Torah by the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem,’ and mentioned the blessing, which is D’Rabbanan. See Beis Yosef, Bach, and Magen Avraham, what is written about this. If you say the reading is not ‘bakomah’ but ‘bekomah,’ I did not find this in Menachos 66, only in the Rosh at the end of Pesachim, and the Riatz ben Giat brought it. The Kesef Mishneh, chapter 7 of Temidin UMusafin, halachah 23, wrote that it is a tradition; see there, and he brought the Zohar, parashas Tetzaveh. What the Bach wrote about milah and lulav, in Hagahos Semak, siman 93, letter 2, he brought only the mnemonic Omer, tzitzis, shofar, and did not mention there milah and lulav at all; this requires study. What was marked as Semak 90 is a scribal error and siman 93 is missing; see there, folio 26a, in Hagahos Semak, where this is discussed. What he wrote that standing is required means at the beginning of the wrapping, for certainly the Torah did not obligate standing the whole day, since ‘with which you cover yourself’ is in the manner of people who work while sitting, etc. Later they showed me Semak, siman 145, Hagahos letter 2, in the name of the Rambam, of blessed memory, that any mitzvah in which ‘lachem’ is stated requires standing, with the Omer as the prototype. The mnemonic is Omer, milah, lulav, tzitzis. This accords with the Bach here, and he did not count tekiah here. It is on folio 47a, page 4.
