Standing Taz, Beit Yosef in the name of Semag, and he derives from omer as it is written (Devarim 15:9): "When the sickle begins in the standing grain, you shall begin to count," etc. — see Magen Avraham note 1; and see Tur siman 265: "and he shall bless standing," and the Rabbi in the gloss brought this; and I am perplexed — why did he omit the essential law that one should circumcise standing, for this is Torah from a verbal analogy of "for you"; and the blessing, which is rabbinic, is done standing — Bach and Magen Avraham wrote of this; and you shall ask: "in standing grain" — but "in stature" (bekomah) — I did not find it in Menachos 66, only Rosh at the end of Pesachim and R' Yitzchak ben Gayyat brought it — Kesef Mishnah chapter 7 from Mishneh Torah chapter 23 wrote it is a received tradition, see there; and he brought Zohar, parashas Tetzaveh. And what Bach wrote — milah and lulav in the glosses of Semag siman 93, letter beis — he did not bring only atifah, omer, tzitzis, tekiyah; and he did not mention milah and lulav there at all — difficult (what is inscribed in Semag 99 and lacks 93, see there on page 26a in the gloss of Semag, Torah in this). And what he wrote that standing is required — this refers to the beginning of wrapping, for certainly the Torah did not obligate all day, for "that you cover" is in the manner people engage in their work while sitting, etc. Again they showed me in Semag siman 145 in the glosses, letter beis, in the name of Rambam z"l: every mitzvah in which it is said "for you" requires standing, and the father of them all is omer; and the mnemonic is OMeTZaL — omer, milah, lulav, tzitzis; and Bach relied on this here and did not mention tekiyah here — and it is on page 47a, column 4.
