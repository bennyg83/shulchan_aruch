Seif 14, "if he removed," etc. Like the conclusion of Sukkah chapter 4 regarding sukkah and tefillin; and so they said in Menachos chapter 4 regarding tzitzis, about Rav Yehudah who acted like Rabbi, etc.

"And some say," etc. They explain that case there as where he did not have intention to return. So wrote the Beis Yosef in siman 639 in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, and so wrote Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Raavyah, Sukkah chapter 6, halachah 12. So it appears from the above Tosefta: if he was doing one mitzvah all day, etc., and as written above at the beginning of siman 217. It has already been explained that removal is not an interruption. At first glance, Rava's case with tefillin in Sukkah there supports the Shulchan Aruch's ruling; but the Darkei Moshe already answered that tefillin in the privy is different, since he must remove them against his will, and that is an interruption. Furthermore, there he actually interrupts, and only when he returns immediately and also intended to return immediately, as written in siman 273. But the main law is like the Shulchan Aruch.

"And some say," etc. So wrote the Agur in the name of Mahari Chaim; but Mahari Chaim speaks of an unspecified case, as written in Yoreh Deah siman 19, seif 7. See below siman 206, seif 5, and Magen Avraham there.
