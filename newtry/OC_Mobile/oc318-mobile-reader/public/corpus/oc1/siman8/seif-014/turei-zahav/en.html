“One must bless,” etc. He wrote this according to his approach in Beit Yosef explaining the Tur — our Tur doubted this halacha whether it resembles tefillin “whenever they moved from their place one must bless again,” or perhaps even for tefillin if they moved one need bless only if one does not know what moved them, but if one knows — no need to bless; and Beit Yosef resolves this doubt from lulav and willow — Rava blessed on tefillin after exiting the privy — presumably they were on him before too. I say: to my mind our Tur did not intend Beit Yosef’s understanding that even for tefillin there is doubt whether he removed before going to synagogue — if so he should have raised the doubt in one clause for tzitzis and tefillin — rather it was clear to Tur on tefillin that one removes to enter privy, then must bless afterward like Rava’s case, because one may not enter privy with tefillin — hence full interruption — unlike tzitzis, which one may wear in privy — here alone Tur questioned whether similar to tefillin moved deliberately where no prohibition — and concludes even for tefillin no blessing when deliberate in permitted place — like tzitzis going to synagogue — in both no blessing; I found this distinction in Darkhei Moshe and proof from Gemara “One whose dead lies” on interrupting prayer whether return to start — see there — words are clear: one does not bless on tzitzis after exiting privy when wishing to wear again — so it appears to me; see nearby following.
“Some say specifically when he remained,” etc. In Agur: Mahari”l was asked about one leaving synagogue and removing his tallis and returning — must he bless? He answered Maharam requires blessing and Maharach distinguishes: if tallis katan remained on him, when he returns he does not bless. Beit Yosef sided: when he intends to wear it later — and Maharam argues on Tur: if no intent, why Maharach hold no blessing? If he wore tallis in morning, removed, wore again at Mincha — would he not bless if tallis katan remained? What difference if he removed intending not to put back immediately? In Levush he too wondered why the ruling depends on tallis katan remaining — what does it help? It appears to me to justify Maharach: this halacha resembles blessing on fruits and eating from them while some remain before him — they bring more — no second blessing since still engaged with the first — similarly whenever tallis katan remains and he removed tallis gadol intending to return it — no second blessing — even though usually if one garment with tzitzis is on him and he adds another he blesses on the addition — not comparable to fruits as above — there he does a new act; here he removed intending to return — this distinction explains this view. One could ask on Rema who rules we need tallis katan “without intent to return” — he first cites “some say” no blessing at all if had intent — then cites latter view specifically when katan remained — means they do not distinguish intent vs no intent but whether katan remained — what superiority has katan? Same Beit Yosef’s question. One could answer: the latter view holds both are needed — intent to return and tallis katan — for Darkhei Moshe wrote explicitly this latter view is Maharach even without intent katan helps; further siman 25 s. 12 — if moved tefillin intending to return — no blessing — explained above siman 8 — yet here writes even with intent to return one needs katan — answer: there since tefillin still on head even if moved from place it is like katan here — not so when tefillin not in place — like laid on table. For practice it appears to me: doubtful blessings lenient — do not bless again with intent to wrap even without katan — like Tur and Rema siman 25 on tefillin — so here too — especially since opinions against Tur are unclear in wording.
