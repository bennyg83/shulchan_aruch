“He must bless.” This requires study: Magen Mishpat in siman 639 cites that if one left the sukkah intending to return immediately he need not bless—why here must he, for the Gemara learns sukkah from tefillin in this regard? In truth later authorities follow Rema: if he changed place it is not an interruption since he donned the same tallis again—as siman 206 and Darkei Moshe, not like Eliyah Rabbah—see siman 178. Still it appears to me: if he did not intend to put it on immediately he must bless—as regarding sukkah—and Tur here, not like Eliyah Rabbah gloss 23.
“A small tallis.” Rema’s wording implies even if he intended to return and wrap again it does not help unless a small tallis remained on him. Taz challenged: siman 25:12 ruled no berachah is needed, and answered “what is the difference here”—the practice follows the first Beis Yosef, but the wording does not read that way. Bach wrote Maharam Chananel speaks when he removed it casually; therefore if a small tallis remained he has not removed his mind from the mitzvah—so Darkei Moshe. R. Yerucham end Berachos: tefillin and tzitzis, when removed, their mitzvah is finished—therefore when he puts them on again it is another mitzvah and he must bless; but Torah reading once begun is not yet finished, so when he reads again for his mitzvah he continues and need not bless—end quote; this is also Maharam Chananel’s intent—but Rema’s wording in Shulchan Aruch is hard to reconcile except with difficulty; for practical halachah Bach’s words are primary—so Darkei Moshe.
