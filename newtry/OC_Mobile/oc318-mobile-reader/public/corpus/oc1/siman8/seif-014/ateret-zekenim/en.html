If he removed his tallit, etc.—that is, to enter the beit haknesset; and the same applies if he removed it in order to walk among gentiles. Some say one does not bless [again], and that is the essential ruling, because it is not comparable to tefillin, for which one must return and bless again—for tefillin are different, since it is forbidden to go with them to the bathroom; therefore it constitutes an interruption. Therefore, according to this, if he removed his tefillin to walk in a place where it is permitted to walk with tefillin, he need not bless again if he had in mind to put them on immediately, as stated above.

{Rama: Shulchan Aruch} And some say specifically when a tallit katan remains on him—and so is the custom. {Rama: On this}—that is, when he removed his tallit without specification; therefore, when a tallit katan remains on him he has not distracted his mind from the mitzvah of tzitzit, and he need not bless when he returns and dons it again. But when no tallit katan remains on him, he has presumably distracted his mind from the mitzvah of tzitzit entirely; therefore he must return and bless. However, if he explicitly distracted his mind, he must bless even when a tallit katan remains on him (my teacher in the Bach). And some disagree—even if he did not distract his mind, and even if a tallit katan remains on him, nevertheless he must return and bless (Maharam Isserles); but his words are very confused and do not appear correct at all. On the contrary, Maharam Isserles’s words contradict one another: above, in siman 12, he ruled that there are those who disagree even though he removed the first before donning the second—in which he revealed explicitly that he does not wish to be clothed in any tallit at all—and nevertheless he ruled that he need not return and bless; yet here, regarding this law, if a tallit katan remains on him, he ruled that he must return and bless. Therefore, in conclusion, he need not return and bless when a tallit katan remains on him; and when in doubt about blessings, we rule leniently.
