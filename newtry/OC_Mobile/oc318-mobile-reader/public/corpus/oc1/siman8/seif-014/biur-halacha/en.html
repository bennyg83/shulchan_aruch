If he removed his tallit, etc. he must bless, etc. — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote: "And see above in comment 31" — that is, it is proven there from the Magen Avraham that if at the time of the blessing his intent was that he would remove it and put it on again, per all views he need not repeat the blessing. This, it seems to me, is the proper advice so that all views may be satisfied: he should intend at the time of the blessing that if he must remove it he will put it on again. See in Shaarei Teshuvah.

And some say one does not bless, etc. — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote, that if he did not put it on immediately then he must bless according to all views in every case. See in the book Arzei HaChaim in the name of the responsum Kol Eliyahu, that according to this one who leaves the bathhouse needs a second blessing on the tallit katan, for there is a great interruption. He brought this in the name of the elders of Jerusalem, end quote. People are not accustomed to be careful about this. Perhaps their reason is that diversion of attention does not apply here, since he must return and put on his garments. It would seem that this does not help even if he intends in the morning at the time of the blessing that he will remove it and put it on again, since we derive the law from the reason of interruption. One may say that when he intends this at the time of the morning blessing, the interruption between removal and donning does not apply, for the blessing refers to the second donning. Know that it seems from the Magen Avraham that this advice helps even for the Beit Yosef, who holds that as soon as he removed it the mitzvah departed and his prior intent to return and wear it does not help — perforce because the blessing refers to the second donning. One may reject this: he holds that through the intention at the time of blessing, the closeness of removal to donning afterwards helps — this requires study. From the Bach's words in this siman it seems that the bathhouse is not considered an interruption regarding the tallit katan. Perhaps one may distinguish whether he stayed a long time or not; the Bach deals with one who did not stay long. It is better that he intend in the morning at the time of the blessing that this blessing not exempt the tallit katan, but only until he removes it in the bathhouse — and then he may bless afterwards according to all. See in Shaarei Teshuvah.

If his intent was, etc. — see in the Mishna Berurah the conclusion of the later authorities on this. Therefore a sandek who holds the child for circumcision and wraps himself in tzitzis — if the tallit is borrowed, he should not bless on it (and as below in siman 14 in the Taz there; see in the Mishna Berurah there). If the tallit is his own: if after prayer, at the time of removal, his intent was to wear it and he did not stay long in the interim, he need not repeat the blessing even if there was a change of place — e.g. the circumcision was at home. If his intent was not to wear it, it does not help even if the circumcision was in the place of prayer. If they removed it without specific intent, it depends: if a tallit katan remains on him he need not repeat and bless on the tallit gadol; if not, he repeats and blesses in every case. If they folded it and placed it in his pocket, it is as if he removed it expressly with intent not to return and wear it, and he must afterwards repeat and bless in every case.
