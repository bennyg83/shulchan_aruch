And he goes back. See Taz. The Beis Yosef wrote that the Tur holds that he goes back and blesses, as by tefillin. The Taz asked: if so, then even if most of it slipped it should be like tefillin. The proof from siman 97, where the Tur wrote as though there is a difference, is that interruption in prayer is different; most of it is not an interruption, and he will bless afterward, although lechatchilah a blessing must precede the performance, as in siman 25, seif 8, regarding a continuing mitzvah, but here it is impossible. However, the Beis Yosef brought only that just as we find a distinction regarding prayer, so one can say there is a distinction regarding the blessing. What he wrote regarding tefillin and most of them, I do not know; indeed, according to the Beis Yosef one need not bless. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 18. However, for us, below in siman 25, seif 12, we do not bless even if all of them slipped; see Magen Avraham there, letter 21. Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah that if he blessed and his entire tallis immediately fell from his hand, he need not bless again. The ruling is obvious from siman 206, seif 6, specifically where the item was lost or became repulsive. Above we wrote that if its tzitzis snapped, it is comparable to that case.
