He must go back, etc. Some disagree with this, and that is reasonable if at the time of the blessing his intent was also on this one and it was lying before him at the time of the blessing, as the Levush wrote. In Sefer Migdal David he wrote at great length to challenge the Levush, asking how he ruled against Terumas HaDeshen, siman 45, who ruled that one must bless, end quote. I am astonished, for on the contrary, one who examines Terumas HaDeshen there will see that he rules like the Levush; he only wrote that by reasoning it would seem that one blesses, but since we find that the Or Zarua ruled that one does not bless, we hold that one does not bless. He also wrote at length to wonder that the Levush contradicts himself, for he ruled in seif 14 that if one wore a tallis and removed it, even if he did not divert his mind, he must bless, end quote. Nachalas Tzvi likewise compares the two cases. In my humble opinion there is room to distinguish: there he already fulfilled the mitzvah with this tallis, and once he removed it, the mitzvah left him. Here, however, his intent at the time of the blessing was also on this one, and he has not yet worn it and has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Even though he removed one tallis, nevertheless with this second one his intention on which he blessed has not yet been fulfilled. Furthermore, it is explained in the Levush in seif 14, when he writes, ‘all the more so we should say that by removing this very garment its mitzvah has left him, etc.,’ that he holds there is a distinction, and removing the same garment is a greater diversion of mind than diversion from another garment. Olas Tamid, s.k. 16, who wrote the reverse, was not precise in the Levush; see there, for I have been brief. In Eliyahu Zuta I brought further proofs for the Levush that he does not go back and bless again, and so ruled Sefer Magen Avraham.
