And so is the practice, etc. The Levush wrote: I am astonished, for nearby he wrote that if one removed the first garment before he wore the second, he must go back and bless; this shows that by removing the garment the mitzvah leaves him, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that this is not difficult at all, for there is different because no tallis katan remained upon him and the mitzvah left him entirely. Here, however, since something of the mitzvah still remains upon him, he does not go back and bless, end quote. In my humble opinion this is rejected by what the Levush wrote: if so, also when he blessed on one, etc.; it is easy to understand. But there is a difficulty on the Levush, why he omitted the ‘some say’ brought by the Rama, that one does not bless at all even if he has no tallis katan on him. It is even more surprising that below in siman 25, seif 12, by tefillin, he ruled that one need not go back and bless. A similar difficulty applies to the Rama, who distinguishes here between whether he has a tallis katan on him or not, while below he ruled that he need not bless. Because of this difficulty the Taz ruled that one should not bless at all, even if he has no tallis katan on him. Lechem Chamudos likewise emends the Rama’s words, that the phrase ‘and so is the practice’ should refer to not blessing at all. But Nachalas Tzvi and Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah ruled like the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, that one always goes back and blesses. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote that it appears that if his intent is to return and wear it immediately, he need not bless even if he has no tallis katan on him; but if he does not intend to return and wear it, he always must bless. When he removed it without specification, he holds that there is a distinction between whether he has a tallis katan on him or not. This also appears to be the view of the Bach and is implied by Magen Avraham. The same law applies to tefillin.
