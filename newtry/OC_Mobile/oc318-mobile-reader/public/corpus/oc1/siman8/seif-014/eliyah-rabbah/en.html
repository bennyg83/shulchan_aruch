[14] He must repeat, etc. Some disagree on this; it also seems correct if at the time of the blessing his intent was also for this and it lay before him at the time of the blessing (Levush). In Magdelei David he greatly challenged the Levush — how did he rule against Terumat HaDeshen, siman 45, who ruled he must bless, until here. I wonder: on the contrary, one who examines Terumat HaDeshen there will see he rules like the Levush, but wrote it would seem he blesses — yet since we find Or Zarua ruled one does not bless, we hold one does not bless. Also what he extended wondering that the Levush contradicts himself in what he ruled in seif 14 — if he wore a tallit and removed it, even without diverting attention he must bless, until here. So too in Nachalas Tzvi he compares them to each other. It seems to me one may distinguish: there the mitzvah was already fulfilled with this tallit, and when he removed it the mitzvah departed; but here his intent at the time of the blessing was also for this and he had not yet worn it and had not fulfilled the mitzvah — even though he removed one tallit, his intention for which he blessed has not yet been fulfilled. Also it is explained in the Levush, seif 14, in what he wrote "all the more so," that removing this garment itself departs his mitzvah, etc. — he holds removing the garment itself is greater diversion than diverting to another garment. Olas Tamid, comment 16, wrote the opposite — he did not analyze the Levush; see there, for I abbreviated. In Eliyahu Zuta I brought more proofs for the Levush that one should not repeat and bless again; so ruled in Magen Avraham.
