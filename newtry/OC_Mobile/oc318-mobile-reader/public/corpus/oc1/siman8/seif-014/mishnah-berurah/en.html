(36) He must bless—even if a small tallis remains on him, and even if he immediately wrapped himself in it and did not change his place at all in the meantime—for removing itself is an interruption in this. And see above in this siman, note 31, what we wrote there.

(37) That one does not bless—even if at the time of removing no small tallis remained on him; and the later authorities ruled thus. And the reason of those who say is: since at the time of removing his mind was immediately to wear it and he did so, removing is not an interruption. And even if he changed his place in the meantime—such as if he removed it to enter the bathroom and all similar cases—change of place in this is not an interruption, since he returned and wore that same tallis. And it is not similar to what the poskim agreed in siman 25 [in seif 12, and see there in Mishna Berurah] that if he removed tefillin with intent to enter the bathroom, it is an interruption through this—for it is different there, since the bathroom is forbidden for tefillin, it is a complete interruption. What is not so with a tallis—for by law it is not forbidden, only it is not proper to do so; therefore it is not an interruption through this. And know: if at the time of removing he did not have in mind to wear it immediately, but only after some time—even if afterward he returned and wrapped himself in it immediately—for all he must return and bless, even if a small tallis remained on him; for as soon as he removed it, the mitzvah departed from him. And the same applies in the reverse: if at the time of removing his mind was to wear it immediately, and afterward he delayed some time and distracted his attention—even in this, for all he must return and bless, even if a small tallis remains on him.

(38) Specifically when it remains—the later authorities ruled like this view of those who say; but they conclude that this view of those who say applies only when he removed the tallis without specification; therefore we say that if a small tallis remains on him—he has still not distracted his attention from the mitzvah, and if not—presumably he distracted his attention. But if his mind was explicitly to return and wear it immediately, he need not return and bless when he wears it immediately—even if no small tallis remains on him. And likewise in the reverse, the small tallis does not help, as we wrote above. And it appears that if he removed it in the middle of prayer—even without specification it is as if he removed it with intent to return. And likewise if he folded it and placed it in his pocket—it is as if he removed it explicitly with intent not to return.
