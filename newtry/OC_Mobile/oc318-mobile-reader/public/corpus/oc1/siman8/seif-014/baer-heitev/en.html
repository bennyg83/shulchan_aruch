Tallit katan. The Taz wrote that for practical application one certainly has a doubt about blessings, and we rule leniently; one should not bless again if he had in mind to return and wrap himself in it, even if no tallit katan remained on him—as the Rama ruled in siman 25 regarding tefillin, that if he had it in mind he need not return and bless, see above. Magen Avraham wrote that the words of the Bach are essential, for he wrote that Maharach was dealing with one who removed it without specification; therefore, when a tallit katan remains on him he has still not distracted his mind from the mitzvah, see above. It is also implied that if he intends to return and wrap himself, even if no tallit katan remains on him, he need not bless; and from this, if he did not have in mind to don it immediately, it does not help if a tallit katan remains on him, and he must bless—and then he need not check the tzitziyot when he returns and dons it (Magen Avraham, siman 13, seif heh; and see Magen Avraham in this siman). And according to all, if he removed his tallit in the morning and a tallit katan remains on him, and he returns and dons his tallit in the afternoon—even though when he removed it at dawn he had in mind to return and don it in the afternoon—nevertheless he must bless, because so much time elapsed. Peri Megadim; and likewise in Knesset HaGedolah in the gloss of the Beit Yosef, seif heh, see above, who elaborated.
