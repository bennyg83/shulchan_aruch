Tallis katan. The Taz wrote in practice that certainly, in a doubt about blessings, we are lenient, and one should not bless a second time if his intent was to return and wrap himself in it, even if no tallis katan remained upon him, just as the Rama ruled in siman 25 regarding tefillin, that if his mind was upon them he need not recite the blessing again, end quote. The Magen Avraham wrote that the words of the Bach are primary, who wrote that Maharam Chalawah was speaking where he removed it without specifying intent. Therefore, when a tallis katan remains upon him, he has still not diverted his mind from the mitzvah; see there. It also implies that if his intent was to return and wrap himself, even if no tallis katan remained upon him, he need not bless. From this follows that if he did not intend to put it on immediately, it does not help that a tallis katan remained upon him, and he must bless. Then, when he returns and puts it on, he need not check the tzitzis. Magen Avraham, siman 13, s.k. 5, and see Magen Avraham in this siman. According to all opinions, if he removed his tallis in the morning and a tallis katan remained upon him, and he returns to wear his tallis at Minchah, even if when he removed it in the morning he intended to wear it again at Minchah, he must nevertheless bless, because he delayed so long. Olas Tamid, and so wrote Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah in the glosses to Beis Yosef, seif 5; see there, where he discusses it at length.
