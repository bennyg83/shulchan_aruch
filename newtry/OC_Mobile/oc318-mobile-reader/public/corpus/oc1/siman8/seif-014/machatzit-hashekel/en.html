(Seif katan 14) He must, etc. Meaning, if he spoke between them, as written in seif 13; this is how the text should read. For the Rosh follows his own view, since the Rosh is a party to this matter.
It is considered an interruption even if he had it in mind at the outset.
Two mitzvos, for we find regarding a meal, which is only optional, that after he tasted from HaMotzi he is permitted to speak, even though the blessing of HaMotzi applies to the entire meal. And regarding a mitzvah, we find by shofar that if he heard nine blasts, which are according to the Torah, over nine hours of the day, he has fulfilled the obligation even though he interrupted between them. But tefillin of the hand and of the head are two mitzvos, and we rule that if he spoke between the shel yad and the shel rosh, he must bless again, for speech is an interruption. The reason is that with shofar, all the blasts are one mitzvah and are connected to one another, so light speech does not interrupt them. This is not so with tefillin, which are two mitzvos and are not connected together. Therefore even light speech interrupts between them, and it does not help that he had the second in mind at the time of the blessing.
Two obligatory mitzvos; and since he has begun them, it is not proper by law that he interrupt and occupy himself with conversation. This is the language of the Ran there.
Therefore each person should be careful, etc. Since there is a dispute among the poskim whether he should bless, he should therefore not speak and should not enter himself into a dispute.
In between, as written there in Yoreh Deah; this is how the text should read. The later authorities there decided that one should bless, as the Shach wrote there in the name of the later authorities, but the Taz agreed there that one should not bless. And if, etc.
But initially, etc. Meaning, even though in such a case everyone agrees that he should bless, and one might think that he may make a major interruption and then bless, since by doing so he is not entering himself into a dispute, it teaches us that nevertheless it is forbidden, for it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary blessing. And as written at the end of siman 215; this is how the text should read.
