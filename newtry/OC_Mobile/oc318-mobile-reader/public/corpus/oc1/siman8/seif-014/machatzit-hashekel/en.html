(Seif kama 14) One must, etc. Explanation: if he spoke between them, as stated in seif 13—so too. For Rosh, according to his view—Rosh is of this opinion.

It constitutes an interruption—even if his mind was on it initially.

Two mitzvos that we find at a meal, which is only optional for another—for the reason the one who recites ha-motzi may speak even though the berachah of ha-motzi applies to the entire meal. And also regarding a mitzvah we find with the shofar: one heard nine blasts (which are biblical) at nine hours of the day and fulfilled his obligation even though he interrupted between them. And tefillin of the arm and of the head are two mitzvos—we hold that if one spoke between the arm tefillin and the head tefillin he must return and bless, for speech constitutes an interruption. And the reason is that regarding the shofar, all the blasts are one mitzvah and have connection to one another—light speech does not interrupt it; unlike tefillin, which are two mitzvos and have no connection together—therefore even light speech interrupts between them, and it does not help that his mind was initially at the time of the berachah on the second.

Two obligatory mitzvos—and since one begins with them, it is not proper that he interrupt and engage in speech—such is the language of Ran there.

Therefore let each one be careful, etc. Since there is dispute among the decisors whether to bless, therefore he should not speak and should not place himself in dispute.

In the meantime, as stated in Yoreh De'ah there—so too—the later authorities there ruled that one should bless; likewise Shach there cites the later authorities. But the Taz agreed there not to bless—and if so, etc.

But lekhatchilah, etc. That is, even though in such a case everyone agrees he blesses, and one might think he is permitted to make a great interruption and bless—for in this he does not place himself in dispute—he teaches us that nevertheless it is forbidden, for it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary berachah, as stated at the end of siman 215—so too here.
