(20) He should examine — it implies from the words of Bach and Eliyah Rabbah that on Shabbos as well one must examine the tallit. And see in siman 13 in Biur Halacha[3].

(21) In the tzitzis threads — one must also examine them above, at the place of the tallit's hole up to the tying, for there it is invalid even if one thread is broken. And see Biur Halacha[4].

(22) Regarding a berachah l'vatalah — although even without this there is concern of prohibition, that he wears a four-cornered garment without tzitzis, nevertheless we rely on its presumption of kashrus; only because of the severity of "do not take [Heaven's name] in vain" that applies to a berachah l'vatalah, they were stringent to check it. According to this, one who wears several tzitzis garments need check only one of them, since one blessing [exempts] all. But the later authorities wrote that one must check all: first, because we do not rely on presumption where it is possible to clarify; and also because the presumption is weak, for the threads are likely to break. Therefore one must check every day in the morning when wearing them; and then, even if he removed them and was distracted from them, although he requires a blessing when he puts them on again, nevertheless he need not check again, for in such a short time they are not likely to break and we rely on presumption. And even in the morning, if through checking he would miss prayer with the congregation, or he was called to ascend to the Torah, he may wear his tallit without checking — provided he knows the threads were intact yesterday. Nevertheless it is proper to look briefly as much as possible. And if he borrowed a tallit from another to ascend to the Torah — for our custom is not to bless on it — he need not check it at all [and see Biur Halacha[5]]. And if he checked the tallit when he removed it from upon himself and placed it in his bag, he need not check it again tomorrow when he wraps in it.
