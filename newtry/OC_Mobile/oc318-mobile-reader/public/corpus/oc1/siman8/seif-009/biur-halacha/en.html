Before he blesses — it is simple that whether it is his own tallit or a borrowed tallit, since they asked him to pray in it — and if so he must bless on it as though it were his own — he must therefore check it, unless he takes it immediately from the lender after his prayer, as below in siman 13 in the Magen Avraham, comment 5. See in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote: "And if he borrowed a tallit from another" — so is proven in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger that for a borrowed tallit, if he intends not to acquire it — and if so it is not his and he is not fit to bless on it — he need not check it. The Bach and Eliyah Rabbah already preceded this. If so, in our case, according to what Darkei Chayim ruled and decided not to bless on a tallit borrowed to go up to the Torah, it is simple that he need not check it. But one who took the congregation's tallit to go up to the Torah, or to preach in it before bein hashemashos — it would seem simple that he must check it, for the decisors ruled that it is like a partners' tallit, as below in siman 14. Even per the Taz in siman 13, who wishes to be lenient and rely on presumption because of communal burden — perhaps that is only for one's own tallit, which has a superior presumption that he checked it himself; whereas a congregation's tallit, which very often has invalid tzitzis and corners torn from their place with no one watching over it, is not fit to bless on without checking. And even if he wishes to wear it without a blessing — that too is not proper, for it is like a partners' tallit, and also out of concern for nullifying the positive commandment of tzitzis; therefore one must in any case check the tzitzis strings at least as much as possible — so it seems to me [and see in siman 14 in Biur Halacha, gloss "she'alah"].

He should look, etc. — see in the Mishna Berurah in comment 21 that he must, etc. It seems to me that one may be lenient if he wears several garments with tzitzis, so that he need not [check] also at the place of the tying hole but only the one on which he blesses; the rest suffice with checking the strings only, for the Magen Avraham disagrees with the Taz on this — see in Mishbetzos Zahav. If so, one need not check the tallit katan but only the strings, since the blessing is on the tallit gadol. Nevertheless it is proper to look a little at the corners, for they are accustomed to become defective there too, especially for those who sleep in the tallit katan.
