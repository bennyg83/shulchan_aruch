9) Regarding whether one must cover his head with a tallit—customs differ, and even in one congregation some cover their heads and some do not, and they do not protest one another. But in a place where the majority would object to the minority's change, a person should not deviate from the majority's custom—responsa Divrei Shalom, siman 123. And they brought Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 3, and wrote: this is specifically after the blessing while he is wrapped in it to pray; but when he blesses on it and wraps immediately, then at that time he should cover his head with the tallit and not rely on throwing the tallit on his shoulders alone—and in this we need not heed the majority's custom, which is a mistaken custom lest they not fulfill the eighteen blessings—see there.

9) And whose language is Matzmiach Yeshuah, 23:4: those who wrap in a tallit gadol only their body and not their head—the blessing is in vain, for "to wrap in tzitzit" primarily means the head, and they wrap only the body; and so the Zohar in Bamidbar, as explained by Parsa on it: it is a mitzvah—until here. And Hebad—Shaarei Berachah, letter 6; and Shach, 34:3.

9) And those people who place the tallit while folded and drape it around their necks on their shoulders such that one end, when folded with two tzitzis, comes down before him on the right and the other end with two tzitzis before him on the left—they do not fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzit in such a case, and one rebukes them, for such is not wrapping according to all—Masat Binyamin, siman 48; and Hebad from Magen Avraham, seif kama 2; Eliyah Rabbah, seif kama 3; Shaarei Teshuvah, letter 3; and Chesed LeAlafim, letter 4.

9) And those who do not cover themselves with their tallit except the head and around the neck alone, and do not take care to cover the body—one must protest to them, for the main wrapping is on the body according to all; they differ only whether one must also cover the head, but covering the head alone is not called covering at all according to all—Razah, letter 8; and Beit Yosef Yashan, letter 27. And see the Chida in Machazik Berachah, letter 5: after he brought the words of Divrei Shalom he wrote whose language is: there are other customs in this—each river and its channel—and for all the mitzvos and all the utterances at the time he blesses it is proper to wrap for the measure of walking four amos in Ishmaelite fashion, as the Arizal wrote, and afterward he practices according to his custom—until here. And so in his sefer Kitzur Shulchan Gavoah, siman 2, letter 8; and Shaarei Tziyon there; Beit Yosef Yashan, letter 26. And for the law, to remove concern of a blessing in vain, after the blessing he should wrap in Ishmaelite fashion as above, and afterward drape the tallit on his body and remain with head and body covered at once for the measure of four amos, and afterward practice according to his custom.
