“He should inspect.” Rosh wrote the custom is not to check them, leaving them in their presumed validity, but one anxious for God’s word should check. Bach wrote we do not rely on chazakah when the chazakah is not natural but from a person who tied the tzitzis—and “majority is stronger than chazakah,” as said in the last chapter of Gittin and Bekhoros’s conclusion: a majority that depends on human action is not considered a majority—end quote; I do not understand his proof—there we discuss whether a man visited her and whether she conceived, unlike here where the tzitzis were properly tied, so leave them in chazakah like a mikveh into which forty seah were drawn that need not be checked every immersion. Still it appears to me one must check because we do not rely on chazakah where one can verify, as Yoreh Deah siman 1; therefore if checking would make him miss the minyan he may wear without checking. Yet it appears to me another answer: it is not a chazakah that they cannot snap at any hour, as Yoreh Deah 201:65 cites Ran responsum—hence they did not check the strings above the gedil resting on the garment because they are not prone to snap. Even though Beis Yosef siman 13 cites Rosh that we are not so concerned—even if they snap they are not immediately invalid, for he holds they are invalid only when all snap—but we are concerned, see siman 12; still one may rely in pressing need if he knows all strings were whole yesterday—so Bach here; siman 17 writes if they checked when storing they need not check at the berachah—as Yoreh Deah 18—see there.
