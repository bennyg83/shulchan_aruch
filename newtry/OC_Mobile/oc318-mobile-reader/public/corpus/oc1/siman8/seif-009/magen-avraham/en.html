“He should inspect.” Rosh wrote the custom is not to check them, leaving them in their presumed validity, but one who is anxious over the word of God should check. Bach wrote we do not rely on chazakah when the chazakah is not natural but from a person who tied the tzitzis — and “majority is stronger than chazakah,” as said in the last chapter of Gittin and at the end of Bechoros: a majority that depends on human action is not considered a majority — end quote; I do not understand what he says — there we discuss whether a man visited her and whether she conceived, unlike here where the tzitzis were properly tied, so leave them in chazakah like a mikveh into which forty seah were drawn that need not be checked every time one immerses in it. Still it appears to me one must check them, because we do not rely on chazakah where one can verify, as Yoreh Deah siman 1; and according to this reason, if while he occupies himself with checking he will be unable to pray with the congregation, he may wear it without checking. Yet it appears to me another answer: it is not called chazakah, for they are apt to snap at any hour, as Yoreh Deah 201:65 in the name of Ran’s responsum — therefore they did not check the strings above the gedil resting on the garment, because they are not apt to snap. Even though Beis Yosef siman 13 cites Rosh that we are not so concerned — even if they snap they are not immediately invalid, for he holds they are invalid only when all snap — but we are concerned; see siman 12; nevertheless one may rely on this in a pressing situation if he knows all the strings were whole yesterday — so Bach here; siman 17 writes that if they checked when they stored it they need not check at the berachah — as Yoreh Deah 18 — see there.
