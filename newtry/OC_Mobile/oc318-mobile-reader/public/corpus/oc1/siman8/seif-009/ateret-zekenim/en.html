Before he blesses he should examine the tzitzit strings, etc., so that his heart trembles at the word of God. Some say one may not rely on presumption and must examine—not out of piety alone. However, if he checked his tallit and placed it in a known place and found it as he had left it, he need not check it again, for it is certainly presumed to have been checked. It is proper that those tzitziyot that come first to the side of the head should always remain on the side of the head, because we ascend in holiness and do not descend; therefore the custom is to make an atarah on the tallit to recognize which part is for the head, so that one does not switch the upper part to the bottom and the lower to the top. Some wrote not to make an atarah on a tallit, lest it be called a head garment exempt from tzitzit; so they practice in the land of Turkey to this day. It is likewise proper to mark the sukkah partitions A, B, and C in order to see their order of placement, so as not to change it from year to year.
