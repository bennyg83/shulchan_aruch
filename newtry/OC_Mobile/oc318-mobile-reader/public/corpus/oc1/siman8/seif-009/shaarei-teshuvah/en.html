In vain. Biur HaTaz in the name of Taz — to check the corner hole; and Taz wrote the reason that it is also accustomed to become invalid; and I do not know why he omitted Magen Avraham's words, who wrote the opposite — because they are not made to break, etc.; and it is clear from this that in the hole until the tying, if it broke it is invalid; and likewise in She'eilot Ya'avetz siman 33, that even if only one thread broke in the corner hole such that through this two half-threads were made and the rest whole — it is invalid; he explained the reason that the thread that broke there — the threads are not hanging on the garment, etc.; and Magen Avraham wrote that in a time of need one may rely on the presumption if he knows the threads were whole yesterday; and it appears he means a time of need when he cannot delay himself to check — such as Taz wrote: they called him to go up to the Torah and he stands with his tallis in hand to wrap and has no leisure to check because of human dignity, or similar; but what Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of Beit Yaakov — one traveling on the road who fears he will not have another tallis when this becomes invalid may rely on presumption that by law he need not check — this is not clear; for what difference whether on the road or at home? The check comes to save him from the prohibition of wearing a tallis without tzitzis and a blessing in vain; and since he could clarify from the outset he accepted this upon himself and made it an obligation — what of it if when this is invalid he will not find another tallis? In any case, since he is on the road there is no great concern for human dignity that the observer will say it was lost or invalidated from road jostling, and because he is on the road it is impossible to find another; and the time of need I mentioned is only where he could have clarified easily at the outset; and also what Beit Yaakov wrote there — if he did not check and found it had become invalid while meanwhile it was in a guarded place, so certainly the invalidation was prior — is considered inadvertent, not unavoidable, etc.; in my humble opinion this too does not apply: if we were to say it is permitted to bless and wrap without checking since he acted permissibly even though it was found invalid he was unavoidable — and what I imagined from Tosafos Beitzah has no proof at all, for he wrote the reason that he had no reason to hurry so much, and that does not apply here:
