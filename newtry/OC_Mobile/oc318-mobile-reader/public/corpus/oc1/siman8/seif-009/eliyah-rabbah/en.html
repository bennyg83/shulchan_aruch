He should examine the strings, etc. One must be careful to check also above at the corner, where it is invalid even if one string snapped, and likewise in the braided section. So wrote the Taz from the Shelah, unlike Magen Avraham. The Bach wrote: if one checked his tallis, left it in a known place, and found it as he left it, he need not check it, as the Rosh ruled regarding a slaughtering knife in Yoreh Deah siman 18, end quote. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote about this that he saw meticulous people who, even though they place the tallis in their pouch, check it every time they put it on, perhaps because some incident occurred and it was taken from the pouch without their knowledge, end quote. This reason is remote. It appears to me rather that one can reject the Bach’s proof, for a knife is checked after slaughter, and therefore we rely on it when he left it in a known place. Furthermore, the Rama rules there in seif 14 that even by a knife, lechatchilah it is forbidden without checking. However, in my Eliyah Rabbah I raised difficulty against the Rama. It is also possible to distinguish. Nevertheless, one may rely on this when checking it would prevent him from praying with the congregation. One may also say that one who left the synagogue, removed his tallis, and returns and puts it on with a blessing need not check it, since he already checked it that day and left it with the status of checked; this is also implied in Magen Avraham, siman 13. The Taz wrote in siman 13 that if one was called to the Torah and wants to put on a tallis, one should not check at that time because of burdening the congregation, as we find in Yoma that he reads by heart because of the honor of the congregation, end quote. But I saw in Mateh Moshe, siman 16, that he wrote that he saw Maharash check each time he was called to the Torah before wrapping himself. His proof from Yoma can also be rejected, for this does not burden the congregation as much as rolling the scroll in public, and the Beis Yosef wrote similarly in siman 144, especially according to the Bach, who wrote that nowadays in any case the congregation is delayed. The Taz further wrote in this siman that they were stringent to check specifically because of a blessing in vain, so that he not transgress ‘You shall not bear,’ but not because he is wearing it without tzitzis. According to this, one who wears many tzitzis garments need check only the first one over which he blesses. But from the Bach it is clear that the reason is also because he wears it without tzitzis, and this is likewise implied in the Levush, siman 13, seif 2. There is a little proof from Maharash, for one may say that Mateh Moshe testified that he checked for the Torah reading every time, even though Maharash holds that one need not bless, as I will write below in siman 14 regarding a borrowed tallis. It is strained to say that it speaks where the tallis was not borrowed, for from his plain language and the phrase ‘every time’ it implies that he always checked. Furthermore, he was presumably always wearing tzitzis. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 84, wrote that if one is on the road and has no other tallis, he may avert his eyes and bless without checking.
