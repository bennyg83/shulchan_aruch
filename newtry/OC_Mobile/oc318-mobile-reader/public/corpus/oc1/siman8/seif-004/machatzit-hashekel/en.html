(section 4) “And he shall stand,” etc. — specifically wrapping. For we bless “to wrap oneself,” and for a mourner who requires wrapping the Gemara concludes: like the wrapping of Ishmaelites — that is, down below the mouth. However, Beis Yosef wrote that specifically for a mourner we learn from Yechezkel, where the Holy One, blessed be He, said to him when his wife died: do not wrap over the mouth; and we learn from this that another mourner requires mouth-wrapping. But regarding tzitzis — since we bless “to wrap oneself,” the intention is not Ishmaelite wrapping; for how can we say “and He commanded us to wrap ourselves” — where were we commanded about wrapping? For it is only written, “with which you shall cover yourself.” Therefore what we bless as “to wrap oneself” means to cover oneself. And in order to satisfy all views, one should stand while wrapped for the distance of four amot, and that suffices — as stated in section 5.

“In a garment that they make,” etc. — meaning what we call “four corners.”

“In its length” — the long side is called length everywhere. And the short side is called width. But if the garment is sewn — meaning what we call a ready-made shirt (leibel). See siman 10, seif 16. Even so, for a ready-made shirt one still requires that most of it be open.

“But there is no stringency.” — meaning that even for a ready-made shirt, although wrapping does not apply to it, nevertheless one fulfills the mitzvah with it.
