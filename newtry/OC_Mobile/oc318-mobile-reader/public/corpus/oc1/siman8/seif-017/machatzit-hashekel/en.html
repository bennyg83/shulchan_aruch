(Seif katan 17) It is considered, etc. It seems to me that if both of them, etc. For the reason is not that walking is called an interruption, as the Taz initially thought, and therefore he challenged from the words of Tosafot and from later, siman 104, that if a snake is wrapped around his heel while he is in the middle of prayer, he should walk and throw it off him, for walking is not an interruption. The Magen Avraham also alluded to this at the end of this seif katan by writing, see siman 104. Rather, the reason is that a change of place is an interruption, as we rule in Perek Arvei Pesachim, page 101. Therefore he wrote that from one room to another is not an interruption, because a change of place such as this is not considered a change, as later in siman 178.
For if he went out, etc. That it is among matters, etc. Although there is a dispute between the Beit Yosef and the Rama in siman 178, and the Rama's opinion is that with matters that require an after-blessing in their place, such as bread, a change of place is not an interruption, nevertheless he agrees regarding matters that do not require an after-blessing in their place that a change of place is an interruption. All the more so here, where no after-blessing is required at all, a change of place is an interruption.
And the view of the Maharam mentioned above, who did not wear the small tallis before entering the synagogue, was because he was uncertain lest the blessing he would recite over the small tallis also exempt the large tallis with which he would wrap himself in the synagogue, even though there was a change of place through his walking to the synagogue. Likewise, when he returned home, he did not put on the small tallis until he needed to speak about other matters, so that he would be permitted to bless over the small tallis, even though there was a change of place, and we rule that it is an interruption.
And although the Terumas HaDeshen wrote, etc. Meaning, granted, this explains why he did not wear the small tallis before entering the synagogue. Nevertheless, when he left, since he removed the large tallis, as Maharch wrote in the responsum, why did he still need to wait until he needed to interrupt with other matters? Let him say that he removed the large tallis and that the change of place is an interruption.
That is after he came, etc. Meaning, certainly if he removed the large tallis in the synagogue, it would certainly be a change of place and an interruption, and in this Maharam agrees, and he would not need to wait until he needed to speak. But in truth, his practice was to remove it at home after leaving the synagogue; if so, he had not changed, etc.
For the blessing of Shema. For it is stated later in siman 85 that if he was reading Shema and entered filthy alleyways, he must pause; and when he leaves the alleyways, he finishes from the place where he stopped onward and need not return to the beginning. Thus, neither walking nor change of place is an interruption.
In the middle of one mitzvah, interruption does not apply, as when he heard nine blasts, etc., as above.
