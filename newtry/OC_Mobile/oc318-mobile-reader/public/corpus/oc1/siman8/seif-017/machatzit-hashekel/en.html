(note 17) It counts, etc. It appears to me that if both, etc.—for the reason that walking is called an interruption is not so, as Taz holds; and therefore he challenged from the words of Tosafos and from below, siman 104: if a snake is wrapped on his heel and he is in the middle of prayer, he goes and throws it off him—for walking is not an interruption; and Magen Avraham likewise hinted to this in this note in what he wrote in siman 104. Rather, the reason is that change of place is an interruption, as we rule in the chapter “Erev Pesachim,” folio 101. And therefore he wrote that from room to room is not an interruption—for some hold that change of place in such a case is not so, as below in siman 178.

For if he went out, etc. For it is from the category of things, etc.—for even though there is a dispute between Rabbeinu Yerucham and Rama in siman 178, and Rama’s view is that in things that require a blessing afterward in their place—such as bread—change of place is not an interruption, nevertheless he admits regarding things that do not require a blessing afterward in their place that change of place is an interruption; all the more so here, where no blessing is needed afterward at all, change of place is an interruption.

And the view of the aforementioned Maharam, who did not wear a small tallis before entering the synagogue—because he was in doubt: perhaps a blessing he would recite on the small tallis would also exempt the large tallis with which he would wrap himself in the synagogue, even though there is change of place in his walking to the synagogue; and likewise when he returned home he did not wear the small tallis until he needed to distract his attention with other matters, so that he would be permitted to bless on the small tallis, even though there is change of place—and we rule that this is an interruption.

And even though Terumat HaDeshen wrote, etc. Meaning: it was pleasing that he did not wear a small tallis before entering the synagogue; nevertheless, upon his leaving, when he removed the large tallis—as Maharach wrote in his responsum—even so he had to wait until he needed to interrupt with other matters; one must say that he removed the large tallis and change of place is an interruption.

That is, after his coming, etc. Meaning: certainly, if he would remove the large tallis in the synagogue, certainly change of place would be an interruption—and in this Maharam agrees; and he would not have needed to wait until he needed to distract his attention. But in truth his custom was to remove it in his home after leaving the synagogue; if so, he did not change place, etc.

For the blessing of Krias Shema. For it is stated below, siman 85: if he was reciting Krias Shema and entered filthy alleyways, he must interrupt; and when he exits from the alleyways he completes from the place where he stopped onward, and he need not return to the beginning—thus neither walking nor change of place is an interruption.

In the middle of one mitzvah it is not relevant to call it an interruption—like hearing nine tekios, etc., as above.
