Biur HaTaz — and see what he wrote on Taz's words, who brought proof from Tosafos Yad Ephraim; and what Taz wrote that the tefillin blessing is an interruption between the blessing he recited on the small tallis and the large — in my humble opinion it requires study: in siman 25 Magen Avraham wrote regarding one who removes tefillin and enters the synagogue — if it is not far he need not return and bless, even though he interrupts in the middle with "Who formed," etc.; and see Ba'er Heitev below siman 53 in the name of Ginat Veradim: when entering the synagogue in the middle of the verses of song he washes hands, blesses, dons tzitzis and tefillin and blesses; and Ba'er Heitev wrote there that in Magen Avraham siman 25 it does not imply thus; and according to what I wrote one could say the reason of the author of Ginat Veradim: since he interrupted with saying "Who formed," he holds it is proper to connect saying "Who formed" with doing one's needs and netilas yadayim and not interrupt in between; and Magen Avraham holds that although certainly lekatchilah it is proper to do so, nevertheless in this case if he does so he will cause an unnecessary blessing — better after saying "Who formed" until he dons tefillin and tzitzis without blessing, for the delay is not considered interruption, and afterward he says "Who has redeemed." And see below siman 227: if he is in synagogue and hears thunder he goes out and blesses; Magen Avraham wrote he should wash hands — and therefore he must delay after "Who has redeemed" until after he blesses on thunder; and one could say there it is a passing blessing; and likewise what he wrote in seif 7 in the name of Rashal regarding a final blessing one may precede — there too because the obligation already applied beforehand; and nevertheless one could say that here too if he delays "Who has redeemed" he will not need to multiply blessings of tzitzis and tefillin — better to do so. And see Eshel Avraham what he wrote as the reason one may delay "Who has redeemed" — requires study. But without this one could say according to Magen Avraham's view that perhaps he too holds one should bless "Who formed" first and it is not considered interruption since he returns to don those same tefillin — unlike one who interrupts with another blessing between donning two garments of tzitzis, which is properly considered interruption; and see Magen Avraham seif katan 18, who wrote this distinction regarding moving one's place in between, etc.; and see below siman 53 seif 66 what he wrote on this; and nevertheless it appears that in this case of interrupting with the tefillin blessing and the like it is no worse than speech; and according to what I explained, Turei Shlah's view that there are opinions one need not bless, since every doubtful blessing is lenient — likewise regarding interruption of blessing the law is the same that one need not bless; and to escape the dispute one should act as I wrote above from Turei Shlah's words regarding speech between slaughter and slaughter:
