Here — Taz lengthened — Eshel Avraham — and they are like his words. And here what he wrote "obligation of mina" means there is no obligation to walk in tzitzis, only one who wears four corners is obligated to make tzitzis (but not actual obligation of mina, for we establish a box vessel is exempt; and if one were obligated to walk in tzitzis, night would well interrupt it). And the Gemara Menachos 42a and Rashi there s.v. "if so" — only once when it was new, and from then on not; thus it is clear he certainly removed it sometimes — only for Rabbanan of Rabbi, even if he removed it and diverted his attention for a while, it is not diversion of attention, and words of Torah one blesses when wrapping. One who examines in Sukkah 46a regarding sukkah concludes: it is a dispute — Rabbi blesses tefillin every time he puts them on, and Rabbanan bless only in the morning; and Rashi explained similarly sukkah for Rabbanan — one day he blesses and no more, for nights do not interrupt. Thus it is clear all seven days he was not enclosed in the sukkah and went out to the market and diverted attention for a long time — nevertheless for Rabbanan, since there is no exempt time that interrupts, one blessing suffices; likewise here the questioner holds he removed it at night, and nevertheless for Rabbanan one blessing once and no more. And the answer: like Rabbi — whenever he puts them on, even by day many times he blesses each time. And he challenges: if so, by day too if like Rabbi — not so for Rabbanan, fine. And the answer: he was a modest person and did not permit his cloak all day; and Rashi: and always like Rabbi and not like Rabbanan. And he challenges further what he wrote from morning; and Rashi: to bless at dawn. And the answer: because he changed from night garment to day; and Rashi: he had two tallits; and Rosh proved from this in Hilchot Ketanot 5 Tzitzis that if one wore a tallit and wears another afterward he must bless again — Beit Yosef brought it; thus like Rashi he holds. But in responsum he explained in the sugya another explanation: the questioner would have held the halachah is certainly like Rabbi as concluded in Sukkah 46, and we practice like Rabbi — see there — if so, what was difficult for Rav Yehuda? Rather he held he was not negligent at night regarding the tallit (since night is time for tzitzis, presumably also at night, etc.). And the answer: like Rabbi — that is, like Rabbi he would remove tefillin several times a day and put them on and bless; likewise Rav Yehuda would remove it at night — and this is the main answer (in Beit Yosef it is written in the Berlin edition "Rabbi" and it should read "like Rabbi" or "Rabbi's words" — meaning he would remove them at night, etc., and the intent is Rav Yehuda, as if he said "removes" and the tallit is referred to). And he challenges: if so, all day too when he removes; and the answer: he was a modest person and did not remove all day — infer that for the one who says night is not time for tzitzis, even if he sleeps in his tallit he does not bless, for the questioner challenged that night is time for tzitzis, and on this the answerers do not disagree. And Taz raised Rashi's essence: the questioner too holds he removed at night, only he held like Rabbi Rabbanan of Rabbi, and according to them even long diversion like sukkah — only one day he blesses, since there is no exempt time that interrupts; and therefore he challenges specifically if it is time for tzitzis — behold if not time for tzitzis, since he removed, certainly he blesses; not so if he does not remove, even though night is time for tzitzis; and also for us, in day garment he is obligated at night — per Rosh one does not bless in the morning. And you see from "he did not permit all day" — it implies he did permit a moment — he blesses; and this agrees with the author seif 14, if not all day but only a long time. And from Sukkah 46 that Rava said not as you heard that he enters the bathroom and places tefillin, etc. — no difficulty, for perhaps the bathroom is different, for it is forbidden to enter there with tefillin, as Darkei Moshe divided in seif 14 and in Darkei Moshe letter 7; one can say for Rabbanan, since there is not much time and diversion of attention does not interrupt — all the more so tefillin when entering the synagogue. Understand this, and see letter 16; and see Sukkah 42 — Rav Yehuda said he forgives and he does not hold so; nevertheless Rashi's explanation is more plausible.
