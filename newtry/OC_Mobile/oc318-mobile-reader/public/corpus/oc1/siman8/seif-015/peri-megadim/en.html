One who sleeps. See Taz, who wrote at length, and see the Bach, whose words are similar. What he wrote, ‘the obligation of the person,’ means that there is no obligation to go dressed in tzitzis; rather, one who wears a four-cornered garment is obligated to make tzitzis. It does not mean an actual obligation of the garment, for we hold that garments stored in a box are exempt. If one were obligated to go in tzitzis, one could well say that night interrupts it. The Gemara in Menachos 42a and Rashi there, s.v. im kein, say only once when it was new, and from then onward not; it is clear that he certainly removed it at times. Yet according to the Sages who disagree with Rabbi, even if he removed it and diverted his mind for a time, it is not considered a diversion, and according to the words of the Torah he blesses when he wraps. One who examines Sukkah 46a regarding sukkah will see that it concludes this is a dispute of Tannaim: Rabbi blesses on tefillin every time he places them, while the Sages bless only in the morning. Rashi explains that the same applies to sukkah, that according to the Sages one blesses on the first day and no more, for the nights do not interrupt. It is clear that certainly he was not locked in the sukkah all seven days, but went out to the marketplace and diverted his mind for a long time, and nevertheless according to the Sages, since no exempt time intervenes, one blessing suffices. The same applies here. The questioner also thought he removed it at night, and even so according to the Sages one blessing suffices. The answer is that it follows Rabbi, that every time one places them, even many times in the day, he blesses each time. The Gemara asks: if so, then by day also, if it follows Rabbi, unlike according to the Sages where it works well. It answers that he was a modest person and did not remove his cloak all day. Rashi explains that indeed it follows Rabbi and not the Sages. It then asks further: what is different in the morning? Rashi explains: why bless at dawn? It answers: because he changes from night garments to day garments. Rashi explains that he had two talleisim. The Rosh proved from here in Hilchos Ketanos, Hilchos Tzitzis, brought by the Beis Yosef, that if one is wearing one tallis and then wears another, he must bless again; clearly he follows Rashi’s explanation. But in a responsum he explained our sugya differently: the questioner assumed the halachah is certainly like Rabbi, as concluded in Sukkah 46, and we practice like Rabbi; if so, what was difficult about Rav Yehudah? Rather, he thought he did not remove the tallis at night, since night is a time for tzitzis. It answers, ‘like Rabbi,’ meaning just as Rabbi would remove tefillin several times by day and put them on and bless, so too Rav Yehudah removed it at night. This is the main answer. The Gemara asks, if so, by day also when he removes it; and it answers that he was modest and did not remove it the entire day. We learn that according to the one who says night is not a time for tzitzis, even if he slept in his tallis he does not bless, for the questioner asked on the assumption that night is a time for tzitzis, and the answerer did not dispute that. The Taz concludes that Rashi’s explanation is primary, that the questioner also thought he removed it at night but thought like the Sages of Rabbi, and according to them even if he diverted his mind for a long time, as by sukkah, one blesses only once for the day, since no exempt time interrupts. Therefore he asks specifically if night is a time for tzitzis; but if it is not a time for tzitzis, since he certainly removed it he blesses. This is not so if he did not remove it...
