(Seif 15) If his tallit fell, etc. In my view: if a little fell from it and he put it on, and afterwards a little more fell and he put it on, and so on until combined it would be considered that all fell — there is room to question whether it is considered that all fell and he must bless, or not. It seems simple from what is stated in Eruvin [24a]: "He breached an amah and fenced an amah," etc., until "you said to us: new aspects have come here; so too new aspects have come here," in his name in the sugya, and Rashi, and we rule thus below [siman 258], seif 2. If so, here too: certainly if he wore a tallit without a blessing he must bless as long as it is still on him, as written above in the Shulchan Aruch; if so here too, even if a little fell and he put it on — since combined it is as if all fell — it is considered new aspects have come here, and it is like wearing a tallit without a blessing and he must bless. So too per the Taz [siman 16] in a tallit split in two and sewn back — one part of the tzitzis is nullified in any case and he must make new tzitzis, Beit Yosef. If so, if a little was split and sewn, and again a little split and sewn until combined the whole is split and there is new sewing after making the tzitzis — new aspects have come here and he must make tzitzis anew on one side a second time. So too one may learn from this to what is written in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah [siman 296], one who vowed from this house and it fell and he rebuilt — it is permitted to dwell in it. And in Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer (siman 95) regarding a widow's residence — if it fell it departed from him; if so, it seems the same if he rebuilt a little anew and again a little — even though initially most stood and it was considered the first house, nevertheless if combined little by little most became new, new aspects have come here and it is permitted regarding the vow prohibition; and regarding a widow's residence the heirs are exempt from giving her this residence — so it seems to my humble opinion, clear and correct. From this one may learn for every similar case; it fits well; examine carefully.
