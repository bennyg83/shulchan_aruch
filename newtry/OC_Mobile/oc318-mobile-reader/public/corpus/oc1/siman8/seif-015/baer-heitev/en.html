If his tallit fell off. In this all agree, for it fell off without intent and he diverted himself from its mitzvah; even if a tallit katan remained on him it does not help, as stated in the previous seif kaf-tet. And if it fell from his entire body—even though it remained in his hand—he must bless, for he diverted himself from its mitzvah of wrapping the body. Magen Avraham.

If most of it fell off. The Taz disagrees—even if most of it fell off, one must return and bless, analogous to tefillin when most of them slipped from their place, for which one must return and bless, see above. One who took the tallit to wrap with both hands, and when he raised his hands to wrap the tallit remained in one hand—he need not bless again (Shiurei Knesset HaGedolah in the gloss of the Beit Yosef, seif tet). One who took a tallit to put it on and blessed on it, and before he finished the blessing it was lost from his hand—he should draw out the blessing and go to the place of the tallit and finish the blessing (Hilchos Ketanot, part 2, siman 153). One who was praying the Shemoneh Esreh and his tallit fell from upon him, and while he was still praying others placed it on him—when he finishes his prayer he should handle the tzitziyot and bless (Shiurei Knesset HaGedolah, siman 10). In a place where they are accustomed to throw a tallit over the bridegrooms and brides at their wedding, the groom must bless on the tallit the blessing of tzitzit. Peri Megadim; and likewise Knesset HaGedolah, seif heh—and this follows the sides of the laws explained in this siman, as explained there, see above. And in the responsum of Ginat Veradim, part 18, clause 1, siman 25, he disagreed and wrote that since the tallit they throw on the bridegrooms is only to protect and not to honor and elevate, one need not bless, see above; and the author Yad Aharon contradicts the words of the author of Ginat Veradim and ruled that one must bless. And likewise the author of Hilchos Ketanot, part 1, siman 22, ruled that one must bless; and likewise in the responsum of Shevut Yaakov, part 2, siman 23—and he concludes there that to satisfy all opinions he should take a borrowed tallit, see above.
