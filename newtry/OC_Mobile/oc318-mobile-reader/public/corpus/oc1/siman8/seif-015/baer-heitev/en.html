If his tallis fell. In this all agree, for it fell without his intent and its mitzvah departed from him. Even if a tallis katan remained upon him, it does not help, as written in the preceding s.k. If it fell from his whole body, even though it remained in his hand, he must bless, for its mitzvah departed, since the mitzvah of wrapping is on the body. Magen Avraham.
That most of it fell. The Taz disagrees: even if most of it fell, he must recite the blessing again, comparable to tefillin, where if most of them slipped from their place, he must recite the blessing again; see there. One who took the tallis in both hands to wrap himself, and when he raised his hands to wrap, the tallis remained in one hand, need not bless a second time. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah in the glosses to Beis Yosef, seif 9. One who took a tallis to put it on and blessed over it, and before finishing the blessing it was lost from his hand, should draw out the blessing, go to the place of the tallis, and finish the blessing. Halachos Ketanos, part 2, siman 153. One who was praying Shemoneh Esrei and his tallis fell from upon him, and while he was praying others placed it upon him, when he finishes his prayer he should handle the tzitzis and bless. Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, siman 10. In a place where the custom is to throw a tallis over the groom and bride at their wedding, the groom must recite the blessing over tzitzis on the tallis. Olas Tamid, and so wrote Knesses HaGedolah, seif 5, according to the legal distinctions explained in this siman, as explained there; see there. In Responsa Ginas Veradim, Orach Chaim, klal 1, siman 25, he disagreed and wrote that since the tallis thrown over the groom is only for protection and not for honor and elevation, he need not bless; see there. The author of Yad Aharon refutes the words of Ginas Veradim and ruled that he must bless. Likewise, the author of Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 22, also ruled that he must bless, and so wrote Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 23, concluding there that to satisfy all opinions he should take a borrowed tallis; see there.
