Ibid. To bless. Therefore one should be careful, for if he speaks he has entered himself into a doubtful dispute. Nevertheless, if he transgressed and spoke, he should bless, for so the later authorities ruled. Regarding this they wrote that with a long interruption all agree that he must bless. If so, at first glance a long interruption should be permitted initially, for in this way he has not entered himself into doubt. Therefore he wrote that even so it is forbidden for another reason, for he causes an unnecessary blessing. This is the flow of his words. It is somewhat difficult: when he interrupted a little, why should he bless according to the ruling of the later authorities against those who disagree? It would be better for him to interrupt more and fulfill all opinions. Granted, initially it is forbidden to make a long interruption because he causes a blessing; but after he already interrupted a little, let him interrupt more. This can be resolved.
