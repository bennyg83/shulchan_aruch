{Rama: Ibid} To bless—therefore he should be careful: if he speaks, he has placed himself in doubt of a dispute; nevertheless if he already spoke he blesses, for so the later authorities ruled. And on this they wrote that for a great interruption, according to all, one must bless; if so, apparently a great interruption is permitted initially—for in this he did not place himself in doubt; therefore he wrote that nevertheless it is forbidden for another reason, that it causes an unnecessary blessing. This is the continuation of his words. And it is difficult: when he interrupted a little, why should he bless per the later authorities' ruling against the dissenters—it is better for him to say he will interrupt more and satisfy all views; for granted, initially it is forbidden to interrupt much because it causes a blessing, unlike when he interrupted a little—he should interrupt more, and this can be resolved.
