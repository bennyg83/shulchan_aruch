[15] Walking from his house, etc. So Beit Yosef explained the Tur's words: one accustomed to wrap in a large tallit in the synagogue must bless on it even though he already blessed on the tallit katan he wore, until here. One may say walking from his house to the synagogue is considered an interruption. Here the Taz extended to disagree with this ruling, and so Olas Tamid; it seems to me the Shulchan Aruch and Levush are primary, therefore I must extend somewhat. What the Taz challenged on Beit Yosef — the Tur's reason for needing to bless is because he holds, as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 25, seif 2, that one puts tefillin between the tallit katan and gadol, and that is the interruption, not the walking, until here — it is not clear: if so the Tur should have distinguished Shabbat and Yom Tov when tefillin are not worn, from weekdays when they are worn; yet he wrote unconditionally that one always must bless. Also from the Tur's language at the beginning of siman 25 — "and after he wraps in tzitzis he puts on tefillin immediately so they be available at Shema and prayer," until here — it implies he holds even the tallit gadol is wrapped before putting on tefillin; otherwise he should have been concise and explained. So too from the reason he wrote "so they be available," etc.; also the language "wraps" implies the tallit gadol. So too in the Rosh's language Beit Yosef brought, siman 28: "A person wearing a tallit katan who when he prays wraps himself must bless," until here — the language "wraps" refers to the tallit gadol. Also what the Taz and Olas Tamid wrote that Terumat HaDeshen, siman 105, explicitly says walking is not an interruption but only conversation about another matter, until here — and he extended in Beit Yaakov responsum, siman 16. It seems to me one may reject: in Terumat HaDeshen it does not mention one accustomed to wrap in the synagogue, but one who wraps at home; perhaps the same applies when he leaves the synagogue — see there and examine carefully. It also seems to me proof from what we say below, siman 178, that change of place requires blessing according to all in matters that do not require blessing in their place — and the law of tallit is comparable. I also found Berachas Avraham ruled explicitly in ch. 4 that walking to the synagogue is an interruption; it seems there the reason of walking and change of domain is an interruption. With this is resolved what the Taz challenged from siman 104, seif 13, that walking is not an interruption because there is no change of place. Also what he challenged from Tosafos Sotah 39 — they derive the measure of "immediately" for washing hands and blessing from "kippah," comparing to slaughter: the leper could not enter Nikanor's gate and from there to the altar was 22 amos — would prove waiting is an interruption, perhaps walking is — one may say it refers to one who says partial entry is not called entry and he puts his hands inside the gate and leans; from gate to altar is not change of domain, even if now the place of the hand where the animal is may be less than 22 amos, for from the gate they begin counting 22 amos — one may say he was not precise in such a small matter; see below. With this I am satisfied what Magen Avraham challenged in siman 166 on Tosafos cited above in Zevachim 33 — it proves change of place is an interruption even for slight walking, until here — there is no difficulty, for there it is change of place; but Tosafos deals with walking without change of place, e.g. putting in his hands — therefore 22 amos are needed as above. In Beit Yaakov responsum, siman 16, he strained to answer Magen Avraham's difficulty and it does not satisfy the examiner — see there, for I abbreviated. Also what he extended to answer the Taz's difficulty cited above — we find waiting is worse than walking, as the Rama wrote in siman 90 regarding standing in prayer and a child urinating: better he go elsewhere than be silent lest he wait to finish the whole thing and return to the beginning; if so Tosafos precisely: waiting from walking a fortiori, until here his words. He was not precise: if so, in tzitzis too waiting alone would be an interruption; but the Taz already proved waiting does not invalidate tzitzis, nor any blessing over mitzvos; there in prayer regarding waiting to finish the whole thing is different. Also there it refers to walking four amos to distance from urinating — see there. According to this, one who goes to another room and returns to the first and wraps does not repeat the blessing if he did not interrupt. Magen Avraham does not seem to hold thus — what seems correct it seems to me I wrote. One may also answer Olas Tamid's difficulty from walking during Shema that it is not an interruption because the law is thus — "when you walk on your way"; and in this vein Berachas Avraham wrote there, page 155. Also eating and walking on the way — for all it is not an interruption, for there is no fixed practice, as below siman 178, seif 4. Further one may distinguish between the law here and their proof greatly; one should not extend.
