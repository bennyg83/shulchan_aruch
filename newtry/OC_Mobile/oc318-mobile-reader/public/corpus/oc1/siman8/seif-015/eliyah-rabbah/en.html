For walking from his house, etc. So the Beis Yosef explained the Tur’s words, where he wrote that one who usually wraps himself in a tallis gadol in the synagogue must bless over it even though he already blessed on the tallis katan he wore, end quote, because one may say that walking from his house to the synagogue is considered an interruption. The Taz wrote at length to disagree with this ruling, as did Olas Tamid, but in my humble opinion the words of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush are primary; therefore I must elaborate somewhat. The Taz challenged the Beis Yosef, saying that the reason the Tur requires a blessing is that he holds, as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 25, seif 2, that one places tefillin between the tallis katan and the tallis gadol, and that is the interruption, not the walking. This is not convincing, for if so the Tur should have distinguished between Shabbos and Yom Tov, when tefillin are not placed, and a weekday, when they are placed; since he wrote without qualification, it implies that one must always bless. Furthermore, from the Tur’s language at the beginning of siman 25, ‘after he has wrapped himself in tzitzis he should immediately put on tefillin so that they be present for him at the time of Shema and prayer,’ it implies that he holds that one wraps himself even in the tallis gadol before placing tefillin. Otherwise he should not have written generally, but should have explained. This also follows from the reason he wrote, ‘so that they be present for him,’ etc.; and the phrase ‘wraps himself’ implies a tallis gadol. So too it is implied in the language of the Rosh that the Beis Yosef brought in siman 28: one who is wearing a tallis katan and, when he prays, wraps himself, must bless, end quote. This implies that the phrase ‘wraps himself’ refers to a tallis gadol. The Taz and Olas Tamid also wrote that this is explicit from Terumas HaDeshen, siman 105, that walking is not an interruption at all, but only speech about another matter, end quote; Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 16, likewise wrote at length. In my humble opinion this can be rejected, for Terumas HaDeshen does not mention that it speaks of one who usually wraps in the synagogue; rather, it speaks of one who wraps at home. Possibly the same applies where he removes it at home when leaving the synagogue; see there and examine carefully. It also appears to me to be a proof from what we say below, siman 178, that a change of place requires a blessing according to all opinions for matters that do not require a blessing in their place, and the law of a tallis is comparable to them. I also found that Sefer Birkat Avraham, part 4, ruled explicitly and simply that walking to the synagogue is an interruption; it implies there that the reason is that walking and change of domain are an interruption. With this, the Taz’s difficulty from siman 104, seif 13, that walking is not an interruption, is answered, for there the case is different since there is no change of place. His difficulty from Tosafos, Sotah folio 39, can also be answered. Tosafos derive the measure of immediacy between washing the hands and the blessing from the immediacy of leaning and slaughter, which was not possible by a metzora because he could not enter Shaar Nikanor, and from there to the altar was twenty-two amos. The Taz asked how they know that delay is an interruption; perhaps walking is the interruption. One can answer that it follows the view that partial entry is not considered entry, and he inserts his hands within the gate and leans; from the gate to the altar there is no change of domain. Although now it may be that the place of the hand, where the animal is, is less than twenty-two amos, for the twenty-two amos begin from the gate, one can say that they were not precise about such a small amount. See below. With this I am satisfied regarding the Magen Avraham’s difficulty in siman 166 on Tosafos, from Zevachim folio 33, which proves that going from place to place is considered an interruption even with a small amount of walking. It is not difficult at all, for there it is a change of place, while Tosafos speak of walking without a change of place, such as when he inserts his hands; therefore twenty-two amos are required. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 16, pressed to answer the Magen Avraham’s difficulty, but it does not stand for one who examines it. He also wrote at length to answer the Taz’s difficulty by saying that delay can be worse than walking, as the Rama wrote in siman 90 regarding one standing in prayer when a child urinated, that it is better for him to go elsewhere and not be silent, lest he pause long enough to complete the entire prayer and have to return to the beginning. If so, Tosafos derived delay from walking by a kal vachomer. But this is not precise, for if so, delay alone should also be an interruption by tzitzis, and the Taz already proved that delay does not invalidate by tzitzis or by any mitzvah blessing. There, by prayer, the law of pausing long enough to finish the whole prayer is different. Furthermore, there it refers to walking four amos to distance himself from the urine. According to this, if one goes to another room and returns to the first room and wraps himself, he does not go back and bless if he did not interrupt. Sefer Magen Avraham does not imply this, but what appears in my humble opinion I have written. One can also answer Olas Tamid’s difficulty from walking during Shema, which is not an interruption, because that is the law there, as it is written, ‘and when you walk on the way.’ Similarly, Sefer Birkat Avraham wrote there, folio 155. Furthermore, one who eats while walking on the road, according to all opinions, is not interrupted, because that is not a fixed setting, as below in siman 178, seif 4. There are many other distinctions between our law here and their proofs, and one should not elaborate further.
