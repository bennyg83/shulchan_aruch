“He must.” Meaning if he conversed between them, as seif 13—see Beis Yosef. It appears to me the Rosh follows his own ruling in Yoreh Deah 19 that speech between one slaughter and another is an interruption since they are two mitzvos—so here; but shofar blowing is one mitzvah, so if he spoke between blasts he need not bless again—but the poskim there who say no new berachah apply here too: specifically for tefillin, two obligatory mitzvos, one must bless; here it is optional—if he wishes he need not slaughter—like speaking in the middle of a meal when no berachah is needed—therefore everyone should avoid speech between. If he did speak, later authorities ruled there—no new berachah (see siman 215 and 260:67); if he paused a major pause all agree he must bless, for that is distraction; but ab initio he should not pause, for it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary berachah—as stated.
“Unless.” Even though for slaughter some say no berachah is needed because they hold slaughter has a fixed continuity while engaged—they usually bring more animals (see siman 206; for milah therefore one blesses—not so siman 265)—unlike donning, where he changes his mind—see siman 176.
“If he removed.” This requires study: even though Taz inclined to say so, Rabbeinu Chananel’s responsum does not read that way—see there; also comparing to slaughter after one slaughter is like removing the first garment, yet all agree no berachah when he intended from the start for the second, as Chullin ch. 6 and Maharil responsum 10—only if he spoke must he bless—so Levush. It is not like seif 14, where at the berachah he did not intend to remove and redon, but here he did intend—not like Eliyah Rabbah. Nor did Taz write this primarily except following his earlier words citing Maharam: even if he blessed on the tallis, went to synagogue, and put on a small tallis again he need not bless if he did not interrupt speech, for it is one mitzvah—and therefore if he removed the large tallis it is surely distraction since he interrupted prayer and he must bless; but according to Rabbeinu Yerucham seif 13 that he must not interrupt at all, there is no difference whether he removed or not—and so Agudah, Parashas HaTekheles: on a silk tallis they were careful not to bless except they bless on wool, remove it, and don silk—end quote; Beis Yosef siman 34 cites sixty-five regarding two pairs of tefillin—see there.
