Likewise, if he removed the first [tallit] before donning the second, he must return and bless again; and some disagree about this—if at the time of the blessing he had in mind this one as well, and it was also placed before him at the time of the blessing, he need not return and bless again, as we find regarding fruit: one blessing exempts everything that was before him; even though the first [fruit] was not entirely eaten before he began eating the second, nevertheless he eats the second by force of the first blessing. The same applies to shechitah (Yoreh De'ah, siman 9). I wonder at Maharam Isserles how he ruled the law opposite the majority of poskim, as the Beit Yosef wrote; and my inclination is toward those who disagree—that is, the view of R' Meir of Rothenburg, who wrote that it likewise seems [one may] wear them without a blessing, to disagree with the rulings of great authorities who require us to bless again without any proof from the Talmud or the responsa of the poskim—and in particular that the later authorities Maharik and Rama agree on one ruling; and so it is implied from what Terumat HaDeshen wrote in siman 45, see there. And even according to R' Meir of Rothenburg, if he did not distract his mind he need not bless again, because he holds the matter depends on distraction of mind.
