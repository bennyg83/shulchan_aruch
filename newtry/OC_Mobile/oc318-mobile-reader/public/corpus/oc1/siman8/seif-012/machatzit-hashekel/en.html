(Seif kama 12) "And he shall bless to wrap himself," etc. Tosafot, Sukkah 38a, s.v. "over," and these are their words: and explicitly the Yerushalmi states, etc., "he wrapped himself in it, he says, etc., 'to wrap myself in tzitzit'"—and this is the reason: because the mitzvah extends as long as he is wrapped and standing, end quote. And Magen Avraham cited "and he shall bless to wrap himself," even though in Shulchan Aruch the word "to wrap" was not mentioned—perhaps Magen Avraham cited the language of the aforementioned Tosafot, or cited it expansively. Or perhaps if it is a tallit katan, one blesses on the mitzvah even though he already donned it—for "on the mitzvah" also implies past tense, as proven in Pesachim 7b; and likewise Tosafot there in Sukkah. But even "to wrap"—which certainly implies only the future, as is found in Pesachim—if so, one might think one should not bless "to wrap," for he utters falsehood from his mouth since he already wrapped himself—he teaches us that nevertheless it is permitted for the reason Tosafot wrote, as above.
