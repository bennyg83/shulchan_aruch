12) More. Magen Avraham wrote there—it seems from Kiddushin 29:2 that a bachelor who had not married a wife would not cover his head with a tallit, even if he is a Torah scholar—so it appears. Indeed Rambam at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchos Tzitzis, and Orach Chayyim 4:1a, and Kol Bo, siman 22, 15:3 wrote that it is a great disgrace for a Torah scholar to pray without wrapping his head in a tallit. Yafeh Lelev, letter 7. And what he further wrote from Magen Avraham, and on page 8:1a it states that one who is not a Torah scholar—even if married—would not cover his head; and so wrote the rabbi, the Mekubal, Mahariv in Sefer Etz Chayyim on the mitzvos, Zachu 74: the secret that one does not wrap in a tallit gadol except the wise alone—see there; this is the opposite of what he wrote in Knesset HaGedolah, 14:4, and in Sefer Nohag KaTzon Yosef 3:4, and Shaarei Berachah at the beginning of these laws; and what he wrote in the previous letter, Yafeh Lelev there. And in particular to what our Sages wrote in Shaar HaKavanos in the drashos of Tehillim, drush 2 and drush 3, that through wrapping in tzitzit and the blessing a repair is made in the supernal worlds—how can a person exempt himself without this? The words of Magen Avraham cited are not compelling, for there it does not discuss a tallit of mitzvah but a sudar of scholars, as written in Ahavat Tziyon at the end of siman 17; and so Beit Yosef in this siman; and so Moharik.
