Biur HaTaz — and see Turei Shlah siman 19 regarding slaughter: his opinion is like Taz there, not to bless because of doubtful blessings; and if so here too one should act likewise; and Magen Avraham wrote that the later authorities ruled in this dispute to bless — meaning Bach and Shach; nevertheless Turei Shlah wrote that sitting and not acting is preferable. And see in Turei Shlah, who wrote: if he spoke he diverts his attention from slaughter by uprooting from his place and engaging in other matters as though he does not wish to slaughter anymore now — then the blessing is by law, etc.; and it appears one should do likewise here according to Magen Avraham, that this dispute here is stated like that of slaughter; and see Magen Avraham that lekatchilah it is forbidden to speak so as not to enter a doubtful dispute; and even to make a great interruption requiring blessing — for all it is forbidden lekatchilah because of an unnecessary blessing — that is lekatchilah when he has not yet slaughtered; but if he transgressed and slaughtered, then it is good for him to make a great interruption and bless by law — and all the more so according to Magen Avraham, that if he slaughtered he should bless according to Shach's ruling in Yoreh De'ah; then it is better to bless without any doubtful dispute at all — that is, with a great interruption; but even according to Turei Shlah, who from doubt would not bless, nevertheless he wrote there in seif katan 17 that it is better to put oneself in doubt of causing an unnecessary blessing than to enter doubt of refraining from blessing on performing the mitzvah, etc.; and see there that he rejected Pri Chadash, who said one should intend and conclude in his mind not to fulfill with this blessing — only that he not speak idle talk; and he z"l wrote that this intention does not help to obligate him in a changed blessing since they are before him and he intends to slaughter them all, etc.; and likewise here:
