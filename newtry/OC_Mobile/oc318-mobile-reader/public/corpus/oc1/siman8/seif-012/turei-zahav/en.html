“If he did not have in mind at first,” etc. It implies specifically at the time of blessing he must intend for all; but if he blessed intending to wear one garment and before wearing they brought another and he decided to wear that too, he must bless again on the second wearing. At first glance this is difficult: what of fruits below siman 206 — if hide was brought while some of those fruits remained before him he need not bless — one could say: fruits are different, for it is usual to bring more before him; not so clothing — it is not usual to bring another garment he had not thought of. This distinction appears also in Tur between slaughter and fruits in Yoreh De’ah siman 19: he ruled if he had no intent at blessing he must bless on it even if brought while some of the first remained — but Semag there and Shulchan Aruch rule like him that while the first remained before him he need not bless on slaughtering the second — what distinguishes slaughter from tzitzis here? Thus Shulchan Aruch contradicts itself — here like Tur, there like Semag. Maharshal wrote here it does not compare to fruits and slaughter — one could say slaughter has a fixed time for eating but clothing does not — end quote; this is incomprehensible: Semag explicitly wrote for eating there is no fixed time — sometimes one prolongs from meal to meal; but for slaughter finishing is removal — end quote. Clearly “fixed” cannot apply more to slaughter than to tzitzis. It requires study how to justify especially blessing an unclear obligation.
