12) Magen Avraham further writes there that Kiddushin 29b implies that an unmarried man did not cover his head with a tallis, even if he was a talmid chacham. Rambam, at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchos Tzitzis; Orchos Chaim, page 4a; and Kol Bo 22, page 15c, however, write that it is a great disgrace for a talmid chacham to pray without wrapping his head in a tallis. Yafeh LaLev 7. Magen Avraham also writes that page 8a states that one who was not a talmid chacham did not cover his head even if married. Rabbi Moshe ibn Chaviv writes similarly, by way of sod, in Etz HaChaim Al HaMitzvos, page 27d, that only a chacham wraps himself in a tallis gadol; see there. This is contrary to Kitzur Shelah, page 54d; Noheg KaTzon Yosef, page 3d; Solel Boneh at the beginning of these laws; and Yafeh LaLev, cited in the preceding paragraph. It is particularly difficult in light of our Sages' teaching in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei Tefillas HaShachar, derushim 2–3, that wrapping in tzitzis and reciting the berachah effects a rectification in the supernal worlds. How could a person exempt himself from this? Even apart from that, Magen Avraham's words are not conclusive. The passage there does not discuss a tallis used for a mitzvah, but a rabbinic headcloth, as Be'er Heitev writes at the end of siman 17. Bach in this siman and Mor U'Ketziah likewise imply this.
