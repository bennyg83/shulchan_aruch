If it was not in his mind, etc. — see in the Mishna Berurah what we wrote there, that it deals with one accustomed to wear only one, etc. If his way is always to wear two or three four-cornered garments one on another, and now he put on a garment without specific intent: if all were before him at the time of the blessing he need not repeat the blessing. If not all were before him — only when they bring him the last garment — if some of the garments that were before him at the time of the blessing are still before him, or if they brought him the second garment immediately after the first blessing and before he put on the first, there are differing views among the decisors; the later authorities (Eshel Avraham, Taz, Turei Zahav, and Aruch HaChaim) ruled that out of doubt he should not repeat the blessing. [The Magen Avraham's view is also thus, like Eshel Avraham and the Taz — he did not distinguish between slaughtering and here; only that some say it is brought there in a gloss that he concedes this. What is not so is the ruling of the Semag and Shulchan Aruch there, and it is the Taz's ruling here — the Magen Avraham concedes to this too. Peri Megadim did not write thus, and it is not clear.] If he does not now need this last garment, it is better not to put it on until he diverts his mind from the first blessing so that he will be obligated to bless on it clearly. Aruch HaChaim.
