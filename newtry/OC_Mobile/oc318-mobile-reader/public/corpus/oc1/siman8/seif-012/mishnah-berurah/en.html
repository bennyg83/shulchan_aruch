(27) All of them are obligated—meaning: if he wears all of them. But if they are placed in his box—they are exempt, for we establish: tzitzis is an obligation of the person and not of the garment, as below in siman 19.

(28) If he interrupts between them—this means with a great interruption that is included in distraction of attention. But with a pause of some time, Magen Avraham and other later authorities ruled below in siman 206 that silence is not considered an interruption after the fact—even if he waited much longer than the measure of speech. And if he spoke between them: if the speech was for the purpose of donning, such as if he said, “Give me a garment to wear,” and the like—it is obvious that he need not return and bless. But if it was not for the purpose of donning, there are differing views among the poskim. And the view of the Mechaber below in seif 13, and Magen Avraham, and many of the later authorities [see Shach, Yoreh De'ah, siman 19, note 7] is like the poskim who hold that even ordinary speech is also included in interruption for this matter. But some of the later authorities rule that since there are those who hold in this that one need not return and bless—as if he spoke in the middle of a meal—it is better not to enter into doubt regarding a blessing; and Peri Megadim agreed. Therefore every person should be careful not to speak; and if he spoke, he does not return and bless. And all the more so he should be careful not to make a great interruption—for it is forbidden to cause a blessing that is not needed, as below in siman 266.

(29) It was not in his mind—meaning: it was not explicitly in his mind, but only that he blessed without specification.

(30) It is like interrupting—since it is unspecified, it implies that we deal even with a case where all the garments of the four corners were laid before him at the time of the blessing, and even so he must return and bless—for we deal with one who is accustomed to wear only one; and therefore, when unspecified, the blessing does not apply to all of them, as if he reconsidered, unless his mind was explicitly on them. And the same applies if he blessed on a small tallis “al mitzvas,” according to our custom, and immediately took the large tallis: if his mind was explicitly to exempt with “al mitzvas,” he need not return and bless; but when unspecified he returns and blesses—for when unspecified his mind is not on “al mitzvas” but only on the small tallis. Nevertheless, lekhatchilah one must certainly be careful not to bless on a small tallis if his intention is to wear the large tallis immediately—because of causing a blessing that is not needed. If he wore a tallis without inspection and found it invalid, or its tzitzis became invalid afterward, and he wears another tallis, he must bless again—unless at the time of the blessing he had explicitly in mind everything he would wear afterward.

(31) If he removed—the later authorities disagree about this, and it is reasonable to them that removing is not an interruption according to all. And it is not similar to what the Mechaber wrote in seif 14 that removing is an interruption—for there he did not have in mind at the time of the blessing that he would remove it and return to wear it; but here he had in mind at the time of the blessing to wear the second garment.
