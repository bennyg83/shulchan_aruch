(27) All of them are obligated. This means if he wears all of them. But if they are lying in his box, they are exempt, for we hold that tzitzis is an obligation upon the person and not upon the garment, as below in siman 19.
(28) He interrupts between them. This means a major interruption that is included in diverting one’s mind. But regarding waiting some time, the Magen Avraham and the other later authorities rule below in siman 206 that silence is not considered an interruption after the fact, even if he waited much longer than the time needed to say a short greeting. If he spoke between them, if the speech was needed for wearing, such as saying, “Give me a garment to wear,” or the like, it is obvious that he need not bless again. But if it was not needed for wearing, there are differing opinions among the poskim. The view of the Mechaber below in seif 13, Magen Avraham, and many later authorities, see Shach, Yoreh Deah, siman 19, s.k. 7, is like the poskim who hold that even ordinary speech is also included as an interruption for this matter. But some later authorities rule that since there are those who hold in this matter that he need not bless again, as it is like one who spoke in the middle of a meal, it is better not to enter into a doubtful blessing. Pri Megadim agreed to this. Therefore every person should be careful not to speak. If he did speak, he does not bless again. All the more so he should be careful not to make a major interruption, for it is forbidden to cause an unnecessary blessing, as below in siman 215.
(29) It was not in his mind. This means that it was not explicitly in his mind, but he blessed without specification.
(30) It is like an interruption. Since the wording is unspecified, it implies that it speaks even where all the four-cornered garments were lying before him at the time of the blessing, and nevertheless he must bless again, because it speaks of one who is accustomed to wear only one. Therefore, without specification the blessing does not apply to all of them, for it is as though he changed his mind, unless he had them explicitly in mind. The same applies if one blessed over a tallis katan “over the mitzvah,” as our custom, and immediately took the tallis gadol: if he explicitly intended to exempt it with “over the mitzvah,” he need not bless again; but without specification he blesses again, for without specification one’s intent with “over the mitzvah” is only for the tallis katan. Nevertheless, lechatchilah one must certainly be careful not to bless over a tallis katan if he intends to put on the tallis gadol immediately, because this causes an unnecessary blessing. If he wore a tallis without checking and found it invalid, or if its tzitzis became invalid afterward, and he puts on another tallis, he must bless again, unless at the time of the blessing he explicitly intended for whatever he would wear afterward.
(31) If he removed it. The later authorities disagree with this and hold that removal is not considered an interruption according to all opinions. It is not comparable to what the Mechaber wrote in seif 14, that removal is an interruption, for there he did not intend at the time of the blessing to remove it and then wear it again; but here he intended at the time of the blessing for wearing the second garment.
