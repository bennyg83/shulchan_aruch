Requires. See Taz and Beis Yosef, for the explanation of the Tur’s words is that interruption by tefillin is like interruption by tzitzis. If one removed them to go to the bathroom, it is also doubtful, and his view inclines that even by tefillin one need not bless again. Unintentional shifting is different. According to this, he attributes a great error to the Tur from Sukkah, where Rava blessed when he left the bathroom. But aside from this it is difficult: if so, why did the Tur not have a doubt by tefillin as by tzitzis? This shows that he knew the case of Rava in Sukkah, and there is different, as written in Darchei Moshe, letter 6, because it is forbidden to enter the bathroom with tefillin, so that is an interruption. This is not so with a tallis, even a tallis gadol, for although it is proper to remove it in the bathroom, there is no prohibition. Therefore he was uncertain only there. Indeed, with tefillin in a place where one need not remove them, it is an interruption like tzitzis, and my view is not to bless. So ruled the Taz in letter 12, that in a doubt about blessings we are lenient and one should not bless when he removed it intending to put it back, even if he goes to the bathroom and even if he has no tallis katan upon him. With Hashem’s help this will be further explained in siman 13.
