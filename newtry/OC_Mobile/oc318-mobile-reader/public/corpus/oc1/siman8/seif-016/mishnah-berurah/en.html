(42) He must bless — for we hold that night is not the time for tzitzis, and it constitutes an interruption. Some dispute this, since daytime garments one is obligated in at night according to some poskim, as below in siman 18, and doubtful blessings are resolved leniently. Nevertheless it is proper to intend to exempt it with a tallis gadol. All this applies as long as he did not remove it. But if he removed it intending not to wear it immediately, he must bless afterward when he wears it — according to all. Nevertheless lekatchilah it is not proper to use this device in order to become obligated in a blessing — according to all — because of a berachah she'einah tzerichah according to those who exempt. One who sleeps during an afternoon nap and removes his tallis katan from upon him — there are opinions among the poskim whether this is included in hesech ha-da'as. Therefore it is proper in any case to cover himself with it during sleep, and when he wears it afterward he need not bless — according to all.

(43) He should handle — see Taz, who wrote that this "and likewise" is imprecise, for in this case by law one must handle the tzitzis in the morning when he blesses on the tallis.
