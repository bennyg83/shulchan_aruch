[16] And so is the custom, etc. The Levush wonders: nearby it is written if he removed the first before donning the second he must repeat the blessing — learn from this that removing a garment departs the mitzvah, etc. Olas Tamid wrote there is no difficulty, for there he had no tallit katan left and the mitzvah departed entirely; but here, since part of the mitzvah remains on him, he does not repeat the blessing, until here. It seems to me this is rejected in what the Levush wrote — if so, even when he blessed on one, etc., it is easy to understand. But the Levush is difficult: why did he omit "some say" that the Rama brings — one should not bless at all even if he has no tallit katan on him. It is more surprising: below, siman 25, seif 12, regarding tefillin he ruled one need not repeat the blessing. In this vein it is difficult for the Rama, who distinguishes here whether he has a tallit katan on him or not, yet below ruled one need not bless. The Taz ruled from this difficulty that one should not bless at all even without a tallit katan on him. So emended Lechem Chamudos in the Rama's words to read "and so is the custom" on that one should not bless at all. But Nachalas Tzvi and remnants of Knesset HaGedolah ruled like the Shulchan Aruch and Levush that one always repeats and blesses. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote it seems his view is to return and don immediately — he need not bless even if he has no tallit katan on him. If he does not intend to return and don, he always must bless; but for plain removal it seems he would distinguish whether he has a tallit katan on him or not. So seems the Bach's view; so implies Magen Avraham; and so is the law regarding tefillin.
