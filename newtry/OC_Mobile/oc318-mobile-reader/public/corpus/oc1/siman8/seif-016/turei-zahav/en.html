“If he slept in his tallis,” etc. I wonder: tzitzis is not an obligation on the person but on the garment — this garment is not exempt from tzitzis even when worn at night according to Rosh siman 18 — so there was no exempt period for this garment — why bless again in morning any more than midday? This seems to me Tur’s reason who ruled no blessing in morning — and Beit Yosef wondered at him as Rosh responsa wrote one blesses only by handling as Beit Yosef cited. It appears Tur held: perhaps when Rosh wrote that responsa he held like Rambam siman 18 that a day garment is exempt at night — but from what Rosh later ruled in his rulings that a day garment is obligated even at night he surely retracted for the reason I wrote. Besides, Rosh’s responsa requires study — I set before you the Talmud and responsum. Rav Yehuda attached tekheles to his wife’s garment (set tzitzis on his wife’s cloth) and blessed on it every morning lehisatef be’tzitzis (another version: he did another thing — blessed every morning). They objected: he attached to a woman’s garment — hence he holds it is not a time-bound mitzvah — else women are exempt — why bless every morning? Rashi: he should bless only first time when new — from then it is one long day and night does not interrupt — end quote. They answer like R who said tefillin — whenever dons bless — so all times too — meaning even in day when removes and redons should bless. They answer R. Yehuda was modest and did not wear his cloak all day only at night — Rosh brought this responsum — “they answer like R,” meaning at night he removed and in morning when redons he blesses — it implies first he thought he did not remove at night — hence the question: since he held night is time for tzitzis why bless — if he removed what is the question if bless morning when redons — hence for us who hold night is not time for tzitzis one blesses in morning even without removing tallis. At first glance good to handle tzitzis at blessing — end quote. One should wonder according to his explanation: the main difference between questioner and answerer — questioner held he did not remove at night and answerer said he removed at night — who forced the questioner to this strained reading and question from this — there is no hint in Gemara — and why did answerer need to bring R — could say only removed at night — and also according to questioner Rosh would hold if removed at night must bless in morning — why then say afterward “so all times too” — for him too that should be difficult — hence certainly questioner too knew he removed at night like normal people — but he thought since there is no exempt interval between — night is time for tzitzis — he should bless only once — like Rashi I cited — they answer he holds like R on tefillin — each time dons bless even in day though no exempt interval — hence difficulty afterward “so all times even in day” — hence no proof from there for one who did not remove tallis at night. It appears to me therefore Tur did not rule like that responsum — it is not reliable — clear proof: at end of fifth chapter tzitzis Rosh wrote this sugya and explained like Rashi — he retracted from that responsum. For halacha it appears to me: one who follows Tur not to bless gains a blessing not needed — so it appears to me; I saw Maharshal too ruled no blessing.

“And it is good to handle.” Beit Yosef asked: here there is obligation to handle as we find for tefillin — it appears to me handling applies only when he wore during the day and did not bless at wearing — therefore must handle afterward at blessing so he not appear blessing on wearing time already passed and interrupted in between — not so here who did not wear yet today — main blessing is on fulfilling from now on — still good to handle here too — correct for this view — but we already wrote we do not rule like this clause — no blessing at all in morning.

“And so he should do,” etc. The wording “and so” is imprecise: in the previous ruling handling is not obligation only good counsel — but in this ruling before daybreak handling is obligation like tefillin — Beit Yosef brought.
