Seif 16, "one who slept," etc. So the Rosh learned from the incident of Rabbi Yehudah in Menachos, where it answers that he followed Rabbi, etc. See Taz and Bach, who challenged him. To me it appears that his proof is from the initial assumption that he held like the Rabbis; according to the Rabbis, he never blesses more than once, as stated in Sukkah: all seven days he blesses only on the first day. Even so, in the morning he blesses, unless he holds that night is a time for tzitzis. Likewise regarding lulav it says there: since nights separate days, he blesses all seven even according to the Rabbis; and the one who disagrees does so only because it is a mitzvah of the elders. The same applies according to Rabbi even without removal, for according to the Rabbis removal accomplishes nothing.
"And so he should do," etc. Tosafos, Menachos 36a, gloss beginning "and when he reaches," etc.; see there.
