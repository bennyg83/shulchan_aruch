(Seif katan 16) If he removed, etc. In the responsum of Maharch it does not imply this. These are the words of Maharch: if he spoke between the small tallis and the large one, it is an interruption and he must bless again. As for Maharam, who did not go with a small tallis to the synagogue, though afterward Maharam retracted, as explained in the Beit Yosef, but rather when he returned home from the synagogue he put on the small tallis and blessed over it, his reason appears to be that sometimes he did not need to speak between the small tallis and the large one, and he also was not permitted to speak without need in order to obligate himself in a blessing, since he could exempt himself with one blessing. He would then be uncertain whether to bless over the large tallis, if he had first blessed over the small tallis. Therefore he would wait until after the prayer, until he needed to speak in any case. The Terumas HaDeshen wrote about this: It would have seemed to say that specifically while he is still wearing one tallis, he is not obligated to bless over the second unless he diverted his attention between this one and that one. But if he removed this one and wants to wear another, he becomes obligated even if he did not divert his attention. However, the language of Maharch above does not imply this, for he wrote that he would wait until after the prayer, until he needed to divert his attention. This implies that if he had not diverted his attention, even though presumably he had already removed the large tallis when leaving the synagogue, nevertheless, if he did not divert his attention, we would not say that since he removed one tallis, its blessing has departed and the small tallis is not exempted through it. Thus, the matter depends on diversion of attention. This is not comparable to what is written in seif 14, that if he removed his tallis, even if he intended to return and wrap himself in it immediately, nevertheless according to the Mechaber there he must bless, even though he is wearing the first tallis. All the more so here, where he now wears another tallis after removing the first; this is how the text should read. And even though the Rama disagrees in seif 14 and holds there that he need not bless, nevertheless here, where he wears another tallis, he agrees that he should bless. So too wrote the Olas Tamid. About this the Magen Avraham wrote that it is not comparable, for there he did not have intent, etc.
And also the Terumas HaDeshen did not write initially; meaning, even according to the initial assumption of the Terumas HaDeshen, nevertheless for us it is not so.
For even, etc. And he went to the synagogue, etc., he need not bless, for he holds that a change of place and walking are not called an interruption, as the Magen Avraham wrote in seif katan 17.
That he interrupted with prayer is not precise; rather, it means the walking and the change of place. Meaning, even though removal alone is not an interruption, and the walking and change of place alone also are not an interruption, nevertheless the combination of the two together is an interruption.
But according to what the Beit Yosef wrote in seif 13; this is how the text should read. That he must not interrupt at all. Meaning, that walking and change of place are an interruption. If so, there is no distinction whether he removed it, etc.; for whichever way you look at it, if he changed his place, even if he did not remove it, it is an interruption, and if he did not change his place, the Terumas HaDeshen agrees that removal alone is not an interruption.
And so wrote the Agudah in Perek HaTekhelet.
And over a silk tallis, etc. These are the words of the Agudah, and from these words the Magen Avraham proved that removal is clearly not an interruption, for he removes the wool tallis before putting on the silk one, and nevertheless he is exempted by the blessing over the wool tallis. The law of a silk tallis is explained at the beginning of siman 9.
Regarding two pairs of tefillin in siman 34; and so it is later in Shulchan Aruch, siman 34, seif 2, that one who wants to put on tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam and cannot put them on together should put on those of Rabbeinu Tam or Rashi, bless over them, remove them immediately, and put on the others, and they are exempted by the first blessing. Thus, even after removing the first ones, nevertheless he need not bless over the others.
