(note 16) If he removed, etc. In the responsum of Maharach, siman 30, he wrote as follows: Maharach—if he spoke between a small tallis and a large one, it is an interruption and he must return and bless. And Maharam, who did not go to the synagogue wearing a small tallis (afterward Maharam retracted, as explained in Beit Yosef), but when he returned home from the synagogue he donned the small tallis and blessed over it—it appears his reason was that sometimes he would not need to distract his attention between the small tallis and the large one; and he was also not permitted to speak without need, to obligate himself in a second blessing, since he could exempt himself with one blessing—and then he would be in doubt whether to bless on the large one (if he had first blessed on the small tallis); therefore he would wait until after prayer, until he needed to distract his attention in any case. And Terumat HaDeshen wrote about this as follows: and it would appear to say that specifically while he is still wearing one tallis he is not obligated to bless on the second unless he distracted his attention between this one and that one; but if he removed this one and wishes to wear another, he is obligated even if he did not distract his attention. However, the language of Maharach above does not imply this—for he wrote that he would wait until after prayer until he needed to distract his attention, which implies that if he had not distracted his attention, even though presumably he had already removed the large tallis when leaving the synagogue, nevertheless if he had not distracted his attention we do not say: since he removed one tallis, its blessing has expired and the small tallis does not exempt it—behold, the matter depends on distraction of attention, end quote. And there is an answer to what the Mechaber wrote in seif 14: if he removed his tallis, even if his intention was to return and wrap himself in it immediately, nevertheless per the Mechaber there he must bless—even though he is still wearing the first tallis; all the more so here, where he is now wearing another tallis after he removed the first, as he should be. And even though Rama disagrees in seif 14 and holds there that one need not bless, nevertheless here, where he wears another tallis, he admits that one must bless; and likewise in Sefer Ayin-Tav; and Magen Avraham likewise explained that it is not comparable, for there he did not have intention, etc.

And Terumat HaDeshen also did not write this initially—meaning: even according to Terumat HaDeshen’s reasoning, nevertheless for us we need not hold this way.

For even if, etc. And he went to the synagogue, etc.—he need not bless, for he holds that change of place and walking are not called an interruption, as Magen Avraham wrote in note 17.

That he interrupted in prayer—not specifically; rather, he means walking and change of place—meaning: even though removing alone is not an interruption, and likewise walking and change of place alone are also not an interruption, nevertheless in combination of both together it is an interruption.

But according to what Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote in seif 13, as he should be. That one must not interrupt at all—meaning: walking and change of place are an interruption; if so, there is no difference between “if he removed,” etc.—for either way: if he changed his place, even if he did not remove, it is an interruption; and if he did not change his place, behold, Terumat HaDeshen admits that removing alone is not an interruption.

And likewise in Agudah, parashat HaTekhelet.

And regarding a silk tallis, etc. These are the words of Agudah; and from these words of his Magen Avraham proved that removing is clearly not an interruption—for behold, one removes the woolen one before wearing the silk one, and nevertheless he is exempted by the blessing on the woolen tallis; and the law of a silk tallis is explained at the beginning of siman 19.

Regarding two pairs of tefillin in siman 34—and likewise below in Shulchan Aruch siman 34, seif 2: one who wishes to lay tefillin of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam and cannot lay them as one—he lays those of Rabbeinu Tam or Rashi and blesses on them and removes them immediately and lays the others, and they are exempted by the first blessing—behold, even after removing the first ones, nevertheless one need not bless on the others.
