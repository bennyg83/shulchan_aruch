A tallis katan. See Baer Heitev. Levush agrees with Shulchan Aruch. So too, in the name of Netz and Knesses HaGedolah, one must repeat the blessing even if a tallis katan remains upon him. One who wishes to avoid the dispute cannot use the solution proposed by Tevuos Shor that I cited above. Since he remains in prayer for an extended period, it is impossible for him to remove his attention from the intention to put the tallis on again. Tevuos Shor similarly writes regarding Maharam that if he were wearing a tallis katan and had recited a blessing upon it, it would be impossible for him to remove his attention entirely from the tallis gadol, because wrapping in it during prayer is regarded as obligatory for him. He could not create a sufficient interruption between them merely to generate an unnecessary blessing. If, however, he can definitively remove his attention and decide that he does not wish to wear the tallis again, this does not constitute causing an unnecessary blessing. It therefore appears that at the time of the original blessing he may expressly intend that it cover only the first act of wearing. It will then not cover anything after he removes the tallis, even if he intends to put it on again and is still wearing a tallis katan. This resembles what Tevuos Shor writes in the name of Peri Chadash, cited above in seif katan 14. Although Tevuos Shor questions whether such an intention is effective, his objection concerns animals standing ready before a slaughterer when he intends to slaughter them. There, merely thinking that the blessing should not cover the later slaughter is ineffective. Here, however, one may properly say that the blessing applies only to this specific act of wearing. Once he removes the garment and puts it on again, a new act has arisen. For additional certainty, he should also interrupt with a few words concerning some need so that it counts as removal of attention. I already cited Tevuos Shor that this is not considered causing an unnecessary blessing, because it is preferable to enter this possible obligation rather than risk omitting a required blessing. This is my own practice. Nevertheless, it sometimes happens that one must remove the tallis while he is still in a section where speech is forbidden. I therefore intend initially that the blessing should cease to cover the tallis only if I later need to remove it at a time when speech is permitted. At that time, before putting it on again, I say a few words and then wear it with a blessing. If, however, I must remove it in a place where speech is forbidden, the force of the first blessing remains, since I remove it with the intention of putting it on again immediately and a tallis katan remains upon me. The first blessing can then cover the second wearing, at least according to Rama and those who agree with him. One who regularly follows this practice and usually forms this intention at the time of the blessing is presumed to bless in accordance with his established practice even if he does not consciously form the intention on a particular occasion. The places where speech is prohibited will be explained, with Hashem’s help, in the laws of Shema. See Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 16, which states that if one lives in a room adjacent to the synagogue and the walk is less than twenty two amos, the walking does not constitute an interruption. Magen Avraham, however, indicates that only where both locations are within one house is passage from room to room not considered an interruption. See Magen Avraham, Taz, and Chacham Tzvi. Responsa Penei Yehoshua, manuscript siman 18, discusses a scholar who wished to explain Tur’s uncertainty regarding one who removes his tallis in order to enter the privy as referring only to a person who ultimately did not enter but immediately put it back on. If he actually entered, the scholar claimed, he must recite a new blessing, as implied by the Gemara. Penei Yehoshua rejects this and explains that Tur refers even to one who entered. Since he intended to put the tallis on immediately after leaving, perhaps no new blessing is required. This is also the view of all the later authorities in explaining Tur.
