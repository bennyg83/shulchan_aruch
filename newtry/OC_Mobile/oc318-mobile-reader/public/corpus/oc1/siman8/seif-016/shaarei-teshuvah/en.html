Tallis katan. Biur HaTaz — and Levush's view is like Shulchan Aruch; and likewise in the name of Netziv and Knesset HaGedolah that he should return and bless even if tallis katan remained on him; and one who wishes to escape the dispute here cannot use the device of Turei Shlah that I wrote above, since he stands for the duration of prayer and cannot divert attention from returning to wear the tallis; and likewise in Turei Shlah there regarding Maharam: if he wore the tallis katan and blessed on it he could not divert attention at all from donning the tallis gadol, since it is an obligation for him to wrap in it during prayer, and he could not make so great an interruption between them to cause an unnecessary blessing; but if he could divert attention that he does not wish to wear a tallis anymore — that does not cause a blessing; therefore it appears he can intend at the time of blessing and conclude in his mind that this blessing exempts only the first wearing alone — then it does not exempt from removal onward, even though he intends to return and wear and tallis katan is on him — similar to what Turei Shlah wrote in the name of Pri Chadash that I brought above seif katan 14; and although in Turei Shlah he criticized this and wrote the thought does not help — that is according to those ready to slaughter before him and he intends to slaughter them; but what he thinks — that his slaughter is not exempted by this blessing — here it fits well: he blesses only for this act of wearing; and if he removes and returns and wears again, "new faces have come here"; and for clarity of the matter one may also interrupt with some speech for some purpose that will be considered hesech ha-da'as; and I already wrote in the name of Turei Shlah that in this case it is not like causing an unnecessary blessing — one may put oneself in this doubt rather than enter doubt of refraining from blessing; and thus I practice. Nevertheless, since sometimes it happens that one must remove while standing at first in places where interruption is forbidden, therefore I conclude in my mind that the blessing will not exempt when I must remove where speech is permitted — and then, before wearing, I interrupt with some words and return and wear with blessing; and nevertheless when it happens one must remove where interruption is forbidden, the power of the first blessing remains in place since he removes intending to wear immediately and tallis katan also remains on him — and it helps to fulfill blessing on second wearing at least according to Rama and his followers; and one who practices thus several times to intend at blessing for this purpose — even if he does not intend afterward and recites the blessing plainly — presumptively according to his custom he blesses; and the law of places where interruption is forbidden will be explained in the laws of Kerias Shema, with God's help. And see responsum Beit Yaakov siman 16: if one dwells in a room adjacent to the synagogue without a walk of twenty-two cubits — the walk is not interruption, etc.; but Magen Avraham explains that only if both are in one house is passage from room to room not considered interruption, etc.; and in Taz and Chacham Tzvi; and in responsum Penei Yehoshua (manuscript) siman 18 regarding what one sage wished to interpret Tur's words, who is uncertain about removing in order to enter synagogue, etc. — meaning he really did not enter but returned and dressed immediately; but if he entered he must bless as in the Gemara; and Pri Yitzchak z"l rejected his words — even if he entered, Tur speaks since his intent is to wear immediately upon exiting he may not need to bless, etc.; and so is the view of all the later authorities according to Tur:
