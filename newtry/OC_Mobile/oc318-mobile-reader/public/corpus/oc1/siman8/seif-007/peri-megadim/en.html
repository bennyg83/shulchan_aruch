Over. See Taz. See Levush, seif 5, that we are concerned that perhaps the mitzvah is specifically with wrapping, and such a tallis katan is not large enough for wrapping the head and part of the body; therefore we bless ‘over the mitzvah of tzitzis,’ as praise for the past, that Hashem gave us this mitzvah, and not that he is now fulfilling it. This resolves the difficulty of Maharai in the Beis Yosef, that ‘over’ indicates the past, as he concludes in Pesachim. Although in siman 432 the blessing is ‘over the removal of chametz,’ there is a reason there, as Tosafos wrote there, unlike here, where it would have been better to bless ‘to dress’ in the pattern of ‘to wrap.’ Rather, for the past one gives praise. Nevertheless, this is only a reason for the custom. The essential law is certainly that we hold wrapping is not alluded to and that one fulfills the mitzvah with it, for otherwise how could we invent a blessing after the sealing of the Talmud. What the Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 8, that one should have intent that Hashem commanded us so that we remember all His mitzvos, the Bach wrote that here, and in the laws of tefillin and sukkah, one must also have this intent, because the Torah explicitly states this reason. This is unlike other mitzvos, where it is enough if he intended only to fulfill the obligation. See there. Here, if he did not have this intent, he has not performed it properly. According to this, the same should apply to redeeming a firstborn son, for a reason is also written regarding it; some explain so.
