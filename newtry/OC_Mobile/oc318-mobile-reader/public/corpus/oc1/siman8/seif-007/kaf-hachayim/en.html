7) [Seif 2] The order of his wrapping as people wrap themselves in their garments, etc. So too our Rabbi the Tur in the name of Baal HaIttur—and not as the Gaonim wrote that we require Ishmaelite wrapping, which is complete wrapping. The Gaonim's reason Beit Yosef wrote: because we bless "to wrap ourselves in tzitzit"; but Baal HaIttur holds that "to wrap" means covering—see there. Nevertheless, to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzit in the optimal way, it is proper to wrap in Ishmaelite fashion at the time of the blessing and stand for the measure of walking four amos, as stated in the seifim after this; and through this one satisfies even the Gaonim's view, for their main concern is at the time of donning—for if he wished he could remove it afterward. Magen Avraham, seif kama 5, in the name of Terumat HaDeshen; and so Razah, letter 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch there—whose language is: Shaar HaKavanos in drashos on tzitzit, chapter 1: the Gaonim required Ishmaelite wrapping, and they return the two tzitzis on the right side to the left side so that all four tzitzis join on the left side behind him and hang behind on the left, etc. Also Rabbeinu Saadia wrote in his commentary that he composed in Arabic in Parashat Bo on this wrapping; and it is good and proper to do so, and at least he should remain thus wrapped for the measure of walking four amos while wrapping in tzitzit, and afterward remove the wrapping from his neck—and such was my teacher's custom—until here. And so Shaarei Berachah, letter 6, in the name of the writings of the Arizal; 11 in Hagahat Tashbetz; Machazik Berachah, letter 4; Beit Yosef Yashan, letter 25; Shaarei Tziyon, 32. And although in Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, he wrote that when a person wraps in a tallit he should grasp the right side of the tallit with its two tzitzis and throw them to the left around his throat, draping the left shoulder behind, and the other two left tzitzis should remain hanging before the person's face and not behind—see there; and so Matzmiach Yeshuah, page 24; and Chesed LeAlafim; and Midrash Talpiyot, page 170:3; and Amudei Shamayim for Yaavetz, 59:2—Yafeh Lelev brought them in Chayei Adam, letter 4. Already the Amli raised this difficulty in Kuntras Shaft Emet, siman 45, and wrote whose language is: both are found; the order of wrapping must be thus: first he throws the two right ones to the left side and leaves the two left on the side of his face, and intends to give the chotam gadol to Rachel and chotam katan to Yaakov; and after this intention he also throws the two left to the left side so that all four join there, as Rabbeinu Saadia wrote—and this is to nullify the power of the evil inclination; and so Machazik Berachah, letter 4—until here; see there. And Ben Ish Chai brought his words in the letter cited and wrote: and so it is explicit in the siddur of our Rabbi the Rashash. And Ben Ish Chai wrote there: although we do not have these kavanos, nevertheless after throwing the two right ones to the left behind him he pauses for the measure of walking four amos; and afterward he also throws the two left behind him to the left and pauses again for the measure of walking four amos; and then he drapes the tallit folded on his neck over his whole body to fulfill the instruction of this act—see there. And through this one satisfies all views—even those who brought only what is written in Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2: since he waited for the measure of walking four amos before throwing the two left behind him, there is nothing more. And the custom of Beit El: after throwing the two left tzitzis behind to the left, they strike the four tzitzis twice against the nape toward the left, corresponding to the two strikes mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos, drush 2 of tzitzit, and in the siddur of the Rashash.

7) After the wrapping he looks at the tzitzit twice, corresponding to the two lookings mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos in the drush cited; and so in the siddur of our Rabbi the Rashash. And see also Shaar HaKavanos in drashos on tzitzit, drush 7; and Sefer Nehora Shemesh, page 63:2 and page 54:1a (and the new [edition], page 12:2b and page 13:1a). And the custom of chassidim of Beit El: after wrapping, before lowering the tallit from their shoulders while it is still on their shoulders—two on the right and two on the left—they take all four tzitzis and look at them twice, and afterward lower the tallit on their bodies.
