7) [Se'if 2] The manner of wrapping is like people who wrap themselves in their garments, etc. This is the ruling of Tur in the name of Baal HaIttur, unlike the Geonim, who require the wrapping of the Yishmaelim, which is a complete wrapping. Beis Yosef explains that the Geonim infer this from the berachah 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis,' whereas Baal HaIttur holds that 'to wrap oneself' means covering; see there. Nevertheless, to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis in the most choice manner, it is proper at the time of the berachah to wrap oneself like the Yishmaelim and remain so for the time required to walk four amos, as stated in the next se'if. This satisfies even the Geonim, because the essential requirement concerns the initial donning; afterward, he could remove it if he wished. Magen Avraham 5 in the name of Terumas HaDeshen; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, ibid. Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, chapter 1, states: 'The Geonim required wrapping in the manner of the Yishmaelim. One brings the two tzitzis on the right side over to the left, so that all four tzitzis join behind him on his left side and hang behind him there. Rabbeinu Saadiah also explained this wrapping in his Arabic commentary to Parashas Bo. It is good and proper to do so, and one should remain wrapped this way at least for the time required to walk four amos when wrapping himself in tzitzis. He may then remove the wrapping from his neck. This was my master's custom.' Solel Boneh 6 writes likewise in the name of the Arizal's writings, as do Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Machazik Berachah 4; Siddur Beis Yaakov 25; and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 32. Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, however, writes that when a person wraps himself in a tallis, he should grasp the tallis's right side with its two tzitzis and throw them leftward around his throat, over his left shoulder toward the back. The other two tzitzis, on the left, should remain hanging in front of him, not behind. Matzas Shimurim, page 24; Chesed LaAlafim; Midrash Talpiyos, page 170c; and Yaavetz's Amudei Shamayim, page 59b, write likewise; Yafeh LaLev 1:4 cites them. Imrei Lev already posed this difficulty in Kuntres Sefas Emes 45 and writes: Both teachings are correct. The order of wrapping should be as follows. First, he should throw the two right-hand tzitzis to the left and leave the two left-hand tzitzis in front of him, intending to give the chasadim and gevuros to Rachel and the chochmah and binah to Yaakov. After this intention, he should also throw the two left-hand tzitzis to the left, so that all four join there, as Rabbeinu Saadiah writes; this nullifies the force of the evil inclination. Machazik Berachah 4 writes likewise. Ben Ish Chai cites this in the aforementioned paragraph and writes that it is stated explicitly in the siddur of Rashash. Ben Ish Chai adds that even though we do not have these intentions, after throwing the two right-hand tzitzis behind him to the left, one should wait the time required to walk four amos. He should then throw the two left-hand tzitzis behind him to the left and again wait the time required to walk four amos. He should then lower the tallis wrapped around his neck over his entire body, thereby carrying out the instruction expressed by this act; see there. This satisfies all opinions, including the works that cite only Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, because he waits the time required to walk four amos before throwing the two left-hand tzitzis behind him. Nothing further can be questioned. The custom of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, is that after also throwing the two left-hand tzitzis behind themselves to the left, they strike all four tzitzis twice against the left side of the nape, corresponding to the two strikes mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 2, and in Rashash's siddur.
7) After wrapping, one should look at the tzitzis twice, corresponding to the two gazes mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos in the aforementioned derush. Rashash's siddur likewise states this. See also Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 7, and Nehar Shalom, pages 53b and 54a; or pages 12b and 13a in the new edition. The custom of the chassidim of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, is that after wrapping, before lowering the tallis over their bodies, while it still rests upon their shoulders with two corners on the right and two on the left, they take all four tzitzis, look at them twice, and afterward lower the tallis over their bodies.
