Seif katan 7, with tzitzis, etc. According to grammar, saying it with a patach is not explained why it is so grammatically. The Levush wrote that some say it with a patach because it is the end of a matter, and one may say it with a patach as definite and separate from what precedes, unlike a sheva. He rejected this, because we find many times at the end of a verse vowelled with a sheva. On the contrary, one may say it with a sheva, for with a patach it implies the known tzitzis, namely techeles, and because of our sins we do not have techeles. Therefore it is better to say it with a sheva, meaning any tzitzis. But the Bach wrote the opposite, to say it with a patach in order to indicate the known one, namely techeles, according to what is written that techeles resembles the sea, the sea resembles the sky, and the sky resembles the Throne of Glory. He brought in the name of the Zohar that through remembrance of the Throne of Glory, where the place of judgment is, fear of Hashem will be upon his face. Since it is written “and you shall see it and remember,” it is proper to remember. Therefore, although we do not have techeles, nevertheless he should remember it in his blessing, and through this he will place the awe of judgment upon his heart. See there.
