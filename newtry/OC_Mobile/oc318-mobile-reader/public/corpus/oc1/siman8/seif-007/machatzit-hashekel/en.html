(note 7) “Tzitzis,” etc. — grammatically one should say it with a patach; with a sheva it is not clear why that would be grammatically correct. The Levush writes that one could say it with a patach, since it is the end of the matter; one could read the patach as indicating something known and distinct from what precedes it — unlike a sheva — and he rejects this because we find many times at the end of a parashah with a sheva. On the contrary, one could say with a sheva: for with a patach it implies the well-known tzitzis — namely tekheles — and alas we do not have tekheles; therefore it is better to say with a sheva, meaning any tzitzis whatsoever. But the Bach wrote the opposite: with a patach to indicate the well-known — namely tekheles — according to what is written, “tekheles is similar to the sea,” etc., and he cited in the name of the Zohar that through remembering this, at the place of judgment there will be fear of Heaven upon his face. And since it is written, “and you shall see it and remember,” it is fitting to remember; therefore even though we do not have tekheles, nevertheless one should remember it in his berachah, and through this he will set upon his heart the awe of judgment — see there.
