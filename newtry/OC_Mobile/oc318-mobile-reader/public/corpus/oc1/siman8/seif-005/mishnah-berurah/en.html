(10) "B'tzitzis" — in Sha'arei Teshuvah under the letter beis:

(11) On the tallit — and the same applies to tefillin and every blessing over mitzvos.

(12) As one — meaning, each one wraps in his own tallit, only that all the wrappings are done at one time; excluding [the case of] after kedei dibur before his blessing, which constitutes an interruption, and each must bless for himself. And see below, siman 206, seif 3.

(13) And if they wished — from this wording it implies that it is better for all to bless. But in truth, the opposite: lekatchilah it is better for one to bless and discharge the others, because of "in a multitude of people is the King's glory," and as stated below in siman 298, seif 14. But they are not accustomed to do so now. And perhaps because not everyone is expert in intending to fulfill [for himself] and to discharge [others]. And see Shaarei Teshuvah.

(14) One blesses — and discharges the others, even if they are expert. Provided the listener intends to fulfill [the mitzvah] and the one reciting intends to discharge. And moreover, even if the one blessing has already fulfilled for himself, or he is not wrapping at all, he can discharge others with his blessing, for all Israel are guarantors for one another. But regarding this there are those who say he discharges only specifically one who is not expert. Peri Megadim wrote: If several people bought new tallisim and attached tzitzis to them and did not bless Shehecheyanu — for they bless at the first wrapping as explained in siman 22 — although one may bless "l'his'ateif" on behalf of all, nevertheless the Shehecheyanu — each one blesses for himself.

(15) They answer Amen — although in any case one must answer Amen after every blessing, nevertheless regarding this one whereby one wishes to fulfill through it — the obligation is greater, for one thereby demonstrates in actual practice that he intends to fulfill through it. However, bedieved even if he did not answer Amen he has fulfilled, and as stated below in siman 213, seif 2.
