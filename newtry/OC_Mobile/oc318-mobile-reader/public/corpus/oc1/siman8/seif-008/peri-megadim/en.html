In order. See Taz. This is from the Tur and the Rosh at the end of Hilchos Tzitzis. In Be’er HaGolah there is a scribal error in letters 9 and 10, for letter 9 is from the Tur and letter 10 from the beginning of Hilchos Tzitzis, and letter 20 is Tosafos, chapter 14 of Berachos and Menachos 36. The Mechaber slightly changed the language of the Tur, which implies that in his view they were stringent specifically because of a blessing in vain, but otherwise not. This follows his approach in siman 13, letter 3, where regarding Shabbos they were lenient, while regarding the blessing they were more stringent; and Magen Avraham here, letter 11, and there, letter 5, follows his own approach. See what is written in Magen Avraham about this. From this the Taz derived that where one has many garments, etc., and similarly a silk tallis for which some practice to bless on wool, he need not check the silk. He also derived that even what is within the hole must be checked, unlike Magen Avraham, letter 11, who follows his view that it is likely to snap, unlike inside the hole. According to the Taz, the stringency is because of ‘You shall not bear’; therefore, with several garments, according to all opinions he need not check what is in the hole. But Eliyah Rabbah, letter 10, in the name of Taz and Shelah, says to check the place lying on the corners, for on the contrary, there if one string snaps it is invalid, and it commonly snaps there; see siman 12. So it is proper, for there is the main concern, unlike most of the strings, which are valid if enough remains to tie a knot. As to the distinction from Yoreh Deah siman 1, seif 1, regarding a slaughterer, where even according to the Mechaber there one must question him after the slaughter regarding the laws of slaughter and we do not rely on the majority, and if so here too he transgresses a positive commandment and it cannot be repaired, he answered that there we do not know at all what he is, whereas here it was checked once, and why should we say that it snapped, especially to check every day. Therefore he said the reason is the severity of ‘You shall not bear.’ One need not press and say that for this matter a presumption is stronger than a majority. Something similar is written in Magen Avraham regarding one witness against a presumption not being believed, while regarding majority one can say that he does not speak against it, for perhaps this is from the minority. See Pri Chadash, Yoreh Deah siman 1, in Mishbetzos Zahav. The Bach wrote that he mentioned the blessing and not the positive commandment because the practical difference is for Shabbos in siman 13 or a borrowed tallis in siman 14. The Taz was not satisfied with this, for it would have been better to mention the positive commandment of tzitzis, with many practical differences in several garments, etc.; at least he should have mentioned both. Rather, he was not concerned for the positive commandment, etc.

The humble writer has elaborated elsewhere, in Sefer Ginas Veradim, in my Pri Megadim to Yoreh Deah, siman 110, and in many places, and in Sefer Shoshanas HaAmakim, which is written in my hand, at very great length. Here I will speak briefly. See Pri Chadash, Eshel Avraham 436, regarding one who rents, and Pri Chadash, Yoreh Deah siman 29. There we clarified that we establish matters on a presumption even D’Rabbanan, except that in a case involving death one may say that they were concerned D’Rabbanan, as in Chullin, chapter HaZeroa, regarding the dough of a convert, which is obligated in challah, according to Rashi’s explanation there, folio 130a, because it involves death. According to this, on Shabbos in siman 13 it would be proper to be concerned; one may say that there it speaks where he already checked at the time of the blessing, so why should he check again in the brief moment when he goes out to the public domain. Also, by majority, and one may say all the more so by presumption, whenever it can be clarified easily, we clarify it, while the eighteen tereifos are not easy. Likewise by a double doubt, which one may say is stronger than a majority, nevertheless whenever it can be clarified easily, we clarify it; see Yoreh Deah 110 in the Shach, letter 66, and in Pri Chadash there. This is what Rabbeinu Tam meant: what is the practical difference, let us ask him, since he is not before us, and checking is a burden.
However. It is proper to note one point here. Chazal have authority to make a fence and safeguard, and were empowered to do so from the verse, ‘You shall guard My charge,’ meaning: make a safeguard for My safeguard; see Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchos Mamrim, and Tosafos, Menachos 61a, s.v. ha’omev, and 2b, s.v. he’ir. Although we find several times in the Gemara that they made a fence even for a positive commandment, one may say that this is specifically so as not to transgress it through active violation, such as Yevamos 20b regarding those prohibited by a positive commandment, etc. There too one may say it is impossible without a mitzvah involving kareis. There are many places; but by passive nonperformance they did not make a fence and safeguard. Reading Shema until midnight, and after that one still reads and is called negligent, is a caution, not a fence. If so, since tzitzis is passive nonperformance, as Tosafos wrote in Yevamos 90b, they did not make a D’Rabbanan fence to decree not to follow a presumption. But regarding the severity of ‘You shall not bear,’ which some say is according to the Torah, see siman 215, they made a fence not to follow the presumption. This is not the place to elaborate further. Below in siman 13, regarding the public domain, one might have said that it is a double doubt: perhaps they are valid, and perhaps all four are invalid and nullified; and a double doubt need not be clarified even easily, according to the Rav in Yoreh Deah siman 110, seif 9; see there. This is not correct, and see what I will write below. At the end of Acharei, the verse ‘You shall guard My charge, not to do from the abominations,’ also implies that they were authorized to make a safeguard against transgressing through active action, not passive inaction. There is some faint proof from the fact that Chazal made a distancing with chametz regarding eating at the fifth hour, but did not make a distancing regarding destroying it; this is active, and this is passive. Understand this. See Rambam, Hilchos Tzitzis, chapter 3, halachah 10: one who wears a tallis without tzitzis has nullified a positive commandment. This does not contradict the Mordechai below in siman 13, nor Tosafos in Yevamos 90, and with Hashem’s help it will be explained there. Since Sefer Chemed Moshe did not write so, I needed to mention this.
