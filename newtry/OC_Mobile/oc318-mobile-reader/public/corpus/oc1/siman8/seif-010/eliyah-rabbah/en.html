[10] He should look at the strings, etc. One must be careful and check also above at the corner, for there it is invalid even if one string snapped, and likewise the gedil. So wrote the Taz from Shelah, and not like the Magen Avraham. The Bach wrote: if he checked his tallit and placed it in a known place and found it as he left it, he need not check it — as the Rosh ruled regarding a slaughtering knife in Yoreh Deah, siman 18, until here. The remnants of Knesset HaGedolah wrote: I have seen careful people that even though they place the tallit in their pocket they check it every time they put it on. Perhaps the reason is lest some incident took it from the pocket without their knowledge, until here — a far-fetched reason. To me it seems because one may reject the Bach's proof: we check a knife after slaughter and rely on it that he placed it in a known place. Also the Rama ruled there, seif 14, even for a knife forbidden initially without checking. One may distinguish. Nevertheless one may rely on this when he cannot pray with the congregation if he checks it. One may also say: one who left the synagogue, removed his tallit, returned and entered and donned it with a blessing need not check it, since they already checked it today and left it in the presumption of checked — so implies Magen Avraham, siman 13. The Taz wrote, siman 13: one called to the Torah who wishes to wear a tallit — do not check at that time because of communal burden, as we find on Yoma they recite by heart out of respect for the congregation, until here. But I saw in Mateh Moshe, siman 16, that he wrote he saw from Maharash that he checked every time they called him to the Torah before wrapping. His proof from Yoma may be rejected — there is not so much communal burden as rolling the Torah in public; and in this vein Beit Yosef wrote, siman 244; especially as the Bach wrote that nowadays in any case they delay the congregation. The Taz also wrote in this siman: specifically because of a blessing in vain they were strict to check lest he transgress "you shall not carry" — not because he wears it without tzitzis. According to this, one who wears many garments with tzitzis need check only the first on which he blesses; but from the Bach it is proven the reason is also because he wears without tzitzis; so implies the Levush, siman 13, seif 2. One may bring some proof from Maharash — one may say Mateh Moshe testified he checked for Torah reading every time, even though he holds one need not bless, as I will write below, siman 14, borrowed tallit — strained to say it deals when it was not borrowed, blocking his language; and from "every time" it implies he always checked. Also presumably he always wore tzitzis. Beit Yaakov responsum, siman 84: if he is on the road and has no other tallit, he may close his eyes and bless without checking.
