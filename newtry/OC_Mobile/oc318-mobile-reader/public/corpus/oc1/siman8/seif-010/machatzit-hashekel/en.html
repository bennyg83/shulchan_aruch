(Seif kama 10) One must, etc. Beis Yosef wrote that it is proper to be precise and make a mark, etc.; and according to this, in my humble opinion it is possible that it suffices when one separates four threads on one side and four threads on the other side—for even in the era of tekhelet, when there was only one or two threads (for each corner, as stated above), one would separate the tekhelet from the white and that sufficed. But the Tur wrote, and these are his words: "And they are called tzitzit after the threads separated from it, as it is written, 'And He took me by the locks of my head'"—and therefore one must separate them one from another, end quote. From this wording it appears that one should separate all eight threads one from another, for such is the meaning of tzitzit. In my humble opinion I was uncertain regarding the Tur's view whether it is disqualifying if one did not separate them. But from the language of Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote per the Tur, and these are his words: "And it is written in the Ittur that in the era of tekhelet it states, etc., and it is proper to be precise, etc." (and as Magen Avraham wrote in his wording), end quote—from the fact that Rabbeinu Yerucham did not write "but in the Ittur" but rather "and it is written in the Ittur," etc., it appears that the Ittur does not disagree with the Tur, and such is also the Tur's intention.

Know what is written in Shulchan Aruch, seif 8: "One should intend in wrapping oneself," etc. Eliyahu Rabba wrote, and these are his words: that which compelled our Rabbi to rule that one must intend in wrapping oneself, and likewise in donning tefillin and in the sukkah—it appears according to what is written in the chapter of tzitzit, "that you may remember," etc., and in tefillin, "and it shall be for you as a sign," etc., "so that the Torah of Heaven shall be in your mouth, for with a strong hand Heaven brought you out," etc., and in sukkah, "so that your generations may know," etc.—it teaches that the essence of the mitzvah and its fulfillment depend on intention, that one should intend at the time of fulfilling the mitzvah. Unlike other mitzvos through which one fulfills his obligation even if he did not intend anything in it, but only that he performs the mitzvah for the sake of Heaven who commanded to perform it, end quote.

And this is lekhatchilah. However, siman 585, Beis Yosef wrote, and this is his language: it appears that he did not fulfill the mitzvah in its proper form if he did not intend that intention, end quote.
