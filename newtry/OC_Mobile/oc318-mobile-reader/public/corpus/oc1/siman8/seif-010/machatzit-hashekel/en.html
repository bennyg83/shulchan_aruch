Seif katan 10, one must, etc. The Bach wrote: it is good to be precise and make a remembrance, etc. According to this, in my humble opinion, perhaps it is enough to separate four strings to one side and four strings to the other side. For even in the era of techeles, when there was only one or two strings, each according to his view, he separated the techeles from the white and this sufficed. But the Tur wrote: they are called tzitzis because of the strings separated from it, as it is written, “and He took me by the locks of my head,” and therefore one must separate them from one another. From this language it implies that he should separate all eight strings from one another, for that is the meaning of tzitzis. In my humble opinion, I was uncertain according to the Tur whether it invalidates if one did not separate them. But from the language of the Beit Yosef, who wrote regarding the Tur: and the Itur wrote that this was said in the era of techeles, etc., and it is good to be precise, etc., as Magen Avraham cited his language, since the Beit Yosef did not write “but the Itur wrote,” but rather “and the Itur wrote,” it implies that the Itur does not disagree with the Tur, and this is also the intent of the Tur.
Know what is written in Shulchan Aruch, seif 8, that he should have intent when wrapping, etc. The Bach wrote about this: what compelled our teacher to rule that one must have intent when wrapping, and likewise in the laws of tefillin and sukkah, appears to be because in the passage of tzitzis it is written “so that you remember,” etc.; by tefillin it says “it shall be for you as a sign,” etc., “so that the Torah of Hashem shall be in your mouth, for with a strong hand He took you out,” etc.; and by sukkah, “so that your generations shall know,” etc. This indicates that the essence and fulfillment of the mitzvah depends on its intent, that one have intent at the time of fulfilling the mitzvah. This is unlike other mitzvos, where one fulfills his obligation even if he has no particular intent in it, only that he does the mitzvah for the sake of Hashem, who commanded him to do it.
And this is initially. However, at the beginning of siman 625 the Bach wrote as follows: It appears that he has not fulfilled the mitzvah properly if he did not have that intent.
