(section 6) “And he shall put it on him,” and the custom developed to make, etc. — not like the Levush, who at the beginning of his words in seif 70 challenges this custom, for through it it appears that the garment’s main purpose is head-covering and it is exempt from tzitzis, as above. Rather, the reason for the mark on those tzitzis, etc., is that there is no intention that it was made for head-covering.

To place it always on one side — meaning that he acted as the Sephardic custom of not making a mark, as above, and he was not particular; sometimes he would turn it over so the outer side was toward the face and sometimes the reverse. Per this textual version there is no contradiction to the words of the Shelah. Nevertheless one could say that he was particular that whichever side he placed on his head, he always placed that same side on his head.

“And per the intended textual reading,” etc. — and accordingly it appears that he was not particular, contrary to what the Shelah wrote.

And likewise siman 301, seif 34, as it should be — see there what the Taz wrote.
