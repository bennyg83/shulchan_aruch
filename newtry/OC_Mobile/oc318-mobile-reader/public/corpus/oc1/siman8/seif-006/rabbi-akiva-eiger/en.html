{Rama: Taz seif katan 8}Although without this. In my humble opinion one could say there is a distinction regarding a borrowed tallis when one blesses — that they give it to him as a gift on condition to return, as below siman 12 seif 3: in this case he can accept it as a gift on condition that if the tzitzis are valid he will accept it as a gift, and if they are invalid it will only be a loan. He would not need to check the tzitzis. This teaches that nevertheless, out of concern for a blessing in vain, one must check. Similarly, if he blessed on a tallis and wishes to wear a borrowed tallis as well — he need not check it, since after the blessing on his own garment he can intend to acquire it as a gift as above, conditionally; also simply when borrowing a tallis without tzitzis, for after thirty days he is obligated in tzitzis rabbinically since it appears as his own, and he would not need to check since it is only rabbinic. Likewise a silk tallis — for the Mechaber tzitzis is only rabbinically required. And also according to Rama's ruling that all kinds are biblically obligated — one finds on Shabbat or a weekday in a city where other tzitzis are not available: in this case biblically one may go without tzitzis, only rabbinically it is forbidden, as stated in siman 13. This teaches that nevertheless one must check so there not be a blessing in vain. The main point, in my humble opinion: out of concern that one wears a garment without tzitzis there is no obligation to check — for otherwise there is no end; one would have to check every hour lest they broke now and he walks without tzitzis — what difference whether at the time of wearing or afterward? Since this is impossible and one must rely on the presumption they did not break, one checks only because of the blessing; regarding the prohibition of a blessing in vain there is a remedy to check at the time of wearing. So it appears to me.
