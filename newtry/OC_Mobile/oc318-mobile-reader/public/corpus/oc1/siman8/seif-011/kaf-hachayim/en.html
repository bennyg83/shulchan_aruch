11) There. And it is proper that he cover his head with a tallit. And per the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos in the drashos on tzitzit, drush 5 and drush 6, one must cover the shel rosh tefillin with the tallit gadol; and so in P'nei Aharon, chapter 1, and in Shaar HaTefillin, chapter 11; and Olat Tamid, 24:1a. And from what he wrote, 24:4b, Shaarei Berachah there—it seems they should be completely covered, covered all twenty-four [hours] while they rest on the head; and so explicitly in Sefer Matzmiach Yeshuah. And what Magen Avraham wrote, seif kama 3, whose language is: and see the end of siman 27 that in any case one should not cover completely—and the Moharik wrote what Magen Avraham wrote, that one should not cover completely—meaning all day when not at prayer; however at the time of prayer they should be covered—see there. And so in Sefer Or Tzadikim printed in the supplement called Or HaYashar, siman 2, letter 17, whose language is: one must cover shel rosh with the tallit at the time of prayer and the tefillin should not be exposed—until here. And Machatzit HaShekel on the words of Magen Avraham: the intent is that the tallit cover the whole head and also the tefillin, and the tallit should be like a roof over the tefillin; but nevertheless it should be visible from before him because it is written "and all peoples shall see," etc.—he wrote there Yafeh Lelev: there is no proof that one may say that although it is covered with a tallit, nevertheless it is evident that he has tefillin on his head that protrude in the tallit or hat, as Magen Avraham himself wrote in Sefer Zachu, seif kaf—see there.
