11) There: It is proper that he cover his head with the tallis. According to the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derushim 5–6, the head-tefillin must be covered by the tallis gadol. Pri Etz Chaim, chapter 1, and Shaar HaTefillin, chapter 11, state likewise, as do Olas Tamid, page 24a; Matzas Shimurim, page 24d; and Solel Boneh, ibid. This implies that they should be entirely covered for the entire time they are upon the head, and Matzas Shimurim says so explicitly. Magen Avraham 3 writes: 'See the end of siman 27, that in any event he should not cover them completely.' Mor U'Ketziah explains that Magen Avraham's statement that they should not be completely covered means throughout the day, outside the time of prayer; during prayer, however, they should be covered. See there. Or Tzaddikim, printed with the supplement called Or HaYashar, 2:17, likewise states: 'One must cover the head-tefillin with the tallis during prayer; the tefillin should not be exposed.' Yafeh LaLev 1:6 cites this and writes that Matzas Shimurim, Ner U'Mitzvah, page 17b, and Solel Boneh imply that they should be covered even outside prayer; see there. Machatzis HaShekel explains Magen Avraham to mean that the tallis should cover the entire head and the tefillin, forming a sort of roof over them, but that the tefillin should nevertheless remain visible from the front because Scripture says, 'All the peoples shall see,' etc. Yafeh LaLev responds that this is no proof: even under the tallis, the presence of tefillin on his head is recognizable because they protrude through the tallis or hat, as Magen Avraham himself writes in siman 27:20. See there.
