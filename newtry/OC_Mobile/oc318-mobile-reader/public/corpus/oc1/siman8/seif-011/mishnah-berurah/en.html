(25) On his garments—and in writings it states that the small tallis is under his garments. And Magen Avraham wrote that in any case the tzitzis must be on the outside, and not like some who tuck them into the corners. But men who walk among gentiles go out with this. Nevertheless, at the time of the blessing they should be uncovered [for the measure of walking] four amos.

(26) That he see it—as it is written: “and you shall see it and remember,” etc. And those people who place the tzitzis in their trousers—it is not enough that they hide their eyes from what is written: “and you shall see it and remember,” etc.; they further disgrace the mitzvah of Hashem, may He be blessed, and in the future they will give account for this. And what they say—that they walk among gentiles—for this it would have been enough to place the tzitzis inside the corner. Whereas if they had some gift from a king of flesh and blood with the king’s name engraved on it, how they would adorn themselves with it before people always; and how much more so with tzitzis, which alludes to the name of the King of kings, the Holy One, blessed be He—as below in siman 24, seif 5: how honored is a person who bears His name upon him! And the Sages said: “Be careful with the mitzvah of tzitzis—one merits and sees the face of the Shechinah.” And it is implied from Scripture that the Jews who remain at the end of days will be marked by this mitzvah, as it is written: “and ten men from all the languages of the nations shall take hold of the corner of a Jewish man,” etc. (Zechariah 8:23). And the Sages said about this (Shabbos 32b): Whoever is careful with the mitzvah of tzitzis merits that two thousand eight hundred servants attend him, etc.
