Magen Avraham, se'if katan 16: 'According to what was written immediately above in Maharam's name, etc.' Magen Avraham cited Rosh's view that whenever one interrupts between putting on two talleisim, he must bless even if he did not divert his attention. He seeks to reconcile this with Maharam, whose words imply that although prayer and walking to the synagogue intervened, he would have blessed only because he needed to divert his attention; mere interruption would not have required a blessing. Terumat HaDeshen writes: 'It would appear that specifically while he remains clothed he need not bless unless he diverted his attention. If he removes this garment and wishes to put on another, he becomes obligated even without diverting his attention.' Magen Avraham explains that in the latter case Maharam can properly hold that a minor interruption, despite no diversion of attention, combines with removing his tallis so that the interruption and removal together are considered a diversion of attention. Under the Shulchan Aruch's rule, however, an interruption independently requires a blessing. The case there must consequently involve no interruption, and removal alone is not treated as a diversion of attention. This is Magen Avraham's intent. In my humble opinion, however, this is not Terumat HaDeshen's intent. He writes there that Rosh and Maharam do not disagree, and that Rosh's phrase 'without an interruption' means without a diversion of attention. He then writes that the rule appears to apply specifically while one remains clothed. What, then, was difficult about Maharam and Rosh? Both require a diversion of attention only while he remains clothed. Rosh was not discussing another interruption at all, and nevertheless Terumat HaDeshen says that if he removes the garment he must bless even without diverting his attention. See Terumat HaDeshen there. Rama's words here are understandable accordingly. Magen Avraham objects that Terumat HaDeshen concludes that Responsa R' Chaim does not imply this. In my humble opinion, Rama understands that Terumat HaDeshen nevertheless regards this reasoning as primary. In his collected writings, siman 120, Terumat HaDeshen writes: 'It appears to me that there is proof from Asheri in Hilchot Tzitzis, who writes that one who removes this tallis and then wraps himself in another must bless. He explains that this is because one is obligated to make tzitzis upon all his garments, etc.' It is explicit that Terumat HaDeshen attributes this reasoning to Rosh. We certainly do not abandon Rosh's words because of an implication from R' Chaim's wording. Yet Terumat HaDeshen's words astonish me. Asheri nowhere writes that one who removes a garment must bless. He writes only that one wears a tallis katan and, when praying, wraps himself in a tallis gadol and must bless. There he discusses a case in which the first garment remains upon him; the blessing is required only because of the interruption between them. That passage concerns the incident of R' Yehuda, who attached techeiles, etc. Rosh explains that he had not removed his nighttime garment, but put his cloak over it in the morning and blessed. He then writes, 'From here is proof, etc.' Only in Terumat HaDeshen's rulings, siman 45, does he write from his own reasoning that the rule appears to apply specifically while one remains clothed, as cited above; this is not Rosh's wording. In his collected writings, siman 120, Terumat HaDeshen infers from the phrase, 'and when he prays he wraps himself in a tallis gadol,' that he removed the tallis katan. He then understands 'but if he wraps himself in one after another without an interruption' to mean that he does not remove either, but wears one over the other. Perhaps this is also his intent in his rulings, and the words 'It would appear to me that specifically while he remains clothed, etc.' come to explain Rosh. The language does not imply this, however. As I have written, Rosh certainly does not discuss removing one garment at all. It appears likely to me that amid the sharp and rapid flow of Terumat HaDeshen's writing, Rosh's words ran together in his mind with his own words in the rulings. This requires further study.
