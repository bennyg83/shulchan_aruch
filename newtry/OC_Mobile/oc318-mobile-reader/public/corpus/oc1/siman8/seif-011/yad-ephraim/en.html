(Magen Avraham § 16) According to what was explained above, from his words Maharam, etc. — meaning: there he brought Rosh's words, who holds that whoever interrupted between the tallisim, even if there was no mental distraction, must bless; and he wishes to align his view with Maharam, from whose words it appears that even though he interrupted in prayer and in walking to the synagogue, he would not bless except because he had to distract himself — but from interruption alone he would not bless. On this Terumat HaDeshen wrote, and it would appear that specifically while he is still wearing he is not obligated to bless unless he distracted his mind; but if he removes this one and wishes to wear another, he is obligated even if he did not distract his mind. And Magen Avraham interprets his intent: with this it fits well that Maharam holds the interruption is [only] a minority — even though he did not distract, since he also removes his tallis, then through the interruption and through removing it is considered mental distraction; unlike in Shulchan Aruch, where we hold that if he interrupted in any case he must bless — if so, necessarily it deals with without interruption; then from removal alone we do not say it is like mental distraction — such is Magen Avraham's intent. However, to my mind this is not Terumat HaDeshen's intent, for there he wrote that Rosh and Maharam do not disagree, and what Rosh wrote "without interruption" means without mental distraction; and on this he wrote that it appears specifically while he is still wearing, etc. — if so, what is the difficulty against Maharam's words? And likewise Rosh, who requires mental distraction — meaning while he is still wearing, etc. — if so, Rosh is not dealing with a case where there was another interruption, and nevertheless Terumat HaDeshen says that if he removes he must bless even if he did not distract — end quote on Terumat HaDeshen. And accordingly Rama's words do not fit here; and what Magen Avraham wrote, that Terumat HaDeshen concluded there that in Rabbenu Chaim's responsum it does not imply so — to my mind Rama's view nevertheless holds that Terumat HaDeshen's main [view] is this reasoning. For in his writings, siman 120, he wrote: "And it appears to me proof from this, that Asheri wrote in the laws of tzitzis that one who removes this tallis and returns and wraps in another tallis must bless," and he wrote the reason because he is obligated to make tzitzis on all his garments, etc. — see there. Behold it is clear that Terumat HaDeshen holds that Rosh holds this view. And if so, it is obvious we do not abandon Rosh's words because of the implication of Rabbenu Chaim's language; but Terumat HaDeshen's words are puzzling in my eyes, for it is not found in Asheri that if he removes he must bless — only that he wrote that if one wears a small tallis and when he prays wraps in a large tallis he must bless, and there it deals while it is still on him, only because of the interruption that was between them — for there it refers to the incident of Rav Yehuda who attached tekheles, etc., and he wrote that it deals with one who did not remove night clothing and put the cloak on him in the morning and blessed on it — and on this he wrote "and from here proof," etc. Only that Terumat HaDeshen in siman 45, in his rulings from his own mind, wrote that it would appear specifically while he is still wearing, etc., as I copied above — but this is not Rosh's language at all; and Terumat HaDeshen in his writings, siman 120, infers from this language "behold when he wraps in that very tallis," etc. — it appears he explains what Rosh wrote, "and when he prays wraps in a large tallis" — meaning that he removed the tallis katan and on this he wrote "but if he wraps in this after that without interruption," etc. — he wished to explain that he does not remove one of them but wears them one on the other; and it is possible this is also his intent in his writings. And what he wrote "and it would appear to me that specifically while he is still wearing," etc. — to explain Rosh's words it fits. But this is not in the implication, and I already wrote that certainly Rosh is not dealing at all with removing one — see there. And it is close in my eyes that Terumat HaDeshen in his writings, in the flow of his argument, ran ahead with Rosh's words and they became confused for him with his own words in his rulings — and it requires study. (In Taz § 15) and Beit Yosef wondered at him from what Rosh wrote — so it should read.
