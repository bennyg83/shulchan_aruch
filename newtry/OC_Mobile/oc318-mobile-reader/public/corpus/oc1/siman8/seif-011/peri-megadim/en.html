Walking Taz. Beit Yosef wrote that Rosh disagrees with Maharam and holds that walking is considered an interruption; and thus he explained the words of Tur — therefore in the synagogue one requires kedei dibur to bless. And he challenged from Tosafos Sukkah 39a, s.v. kol — see there — for they proved that in any case 22 amos of waiting is an interruption between putting on [tefillin] and taking out [the Torah]; and so too in Kiddushin 66a — and this is from what we say in Zevachim 33a that the leaning of the guilt-offering of a metzora was not immediately adjacent to the leaning of the slaughter, for the north was 22 amos distant from the entrance — and what is the difficulty there? Walking is an interruption, but waiting without walking — whence do we know it was not immediately adjacent? Rather, we learn: wherever there is no waiting, there is no interruption; walking too is not an interruption. And therefore there, because of waiting 22 amos it was not immediately adjacent. And accordingly, regarding tzitzis where there is no waiting-interruption, certainly walking too is not an interruption (what he wrote that otherwise one should consider regarding the three immediate adjacencies of all blessings — I am perplexed; in Berachos 62a it states that one may not lekatchilah interrupt in silence between a blessing and the act more than kedei dibur; and Magen Avraham in Kiddushin 66, note 3, wrote that regarding kedei dibur one may not interrupt at all; however one may say that between garment and garment we are not concerned with waiting). And he raised that Ra'ah and Maharam do not disagree as it appears from what Mahari wrote: And here I must extend somewhat. Magen Avraham in Kiddushin 66, note 3, challenged Tosafos Sotah — and his intent appears to be: if they hold that partial entry counts as entry, it is not proven that 22 amos is an interruption from domain to domain — for the questioner holds like Rabbi that the north is not distant, etc.; and if they hold it does not count as entry (chapter 3 of Laws of Entry to the Sanctuary, 18 — dispute of Rambam and Ra'avad z"l) and like Rabbi Yosi bar Rabbi Yehuda that the north is distant and one extends his hand to the projections — then it is proven that even less than 22 amos is an interruption, for the metzora's hand is more than an amah and a half — approximately this is his difficulty (and see there note 15 — somewhat imprecise, and Magen Avraham was not settled on this that it is proven that approximately 20 amos is interruption). And to settle this: Tosafos were challenged from Rav Yosef there, to reject Ula's support — this is according to Rabbi Yosi bar Rabbi Yehuda, and he did not say perhaps as a rejected view; and furthermore, why establish the baraita according to Rabbi Yosi bar Rabbi Yehuda against the halachah, for the halachah follows Rabbi (see Rambam chapter 4 of Mechusarei Kapparah 2, and Kesef Mishnah there); and furthermore the halachah follows Ula and Ra'avad z"l in Laws of Entry to the Sanctuary — and Tosafos hold thus; but the questioner holds like Rabbi — and Rav Yosef answered that necessarily the baraita is according to Rabbi Yosi bar Rabbi Yehuda, for if according to Rabbi, why was it not immediately adjacent? — for he profanes the opening where he stands until inside the azarah — it would have been immediately adjacent, not as the questioner thought; and necessarily Rabbi Yosi bar Rabbi Yehuda — and now there is no support to say there is no entry and 20 amos interruption, or there is entry and 22 amos in any case interruption; and he leans in the airspace and brings the animal from there until a complete 22 amos north — and Magen Avraham's difficulty in Kiddushin 66:3 is resolved. And for this Taz wrote: thus it is proven from there that 22 amos — and walking from domain to domain from profaning the opening until the north — is not an interruption; only waiting is an interruption. And even though in Kiddushin it is explained that from corner to corner is interruption — one may say the tallit is upon him, as Magen Avraham wrote in note 17, according to Maharam — and so is proven this is the essence of Taz's words. However, one may distinguish: from domain to domain afterward is interruption; but profaning the opening inward in the azarah is not another domain, for although from the enclosure and outside is like outside (see siman 55, seif 13) — nevertheless domain afterward from what is written is not — and therefore waiting was not immediately adjacent; and Tosafos in Sotah proved well from this — unlike from his house to the synagogue, to another domain, interruption applies too; and I will explain further in Magen Avraham.
