That walking. See Taz. The Beis Yosef wrote that the Rosh disagrees with Maharam and holds that walking is considered an interruption, and with this he explained the Tur, that therefore in the synagogue one must bless again. He raised a difficulty from Tosafos, Sukkah 39a, s.v. kol; see there. Tosafos proved that in any event a delay of twenty-two amos is an interruption between washing the hands and hamotzi, as also stated in siman 166, seif 1. This is from Zevachim 33a, where the leaning of the metzora’s guilt-offering was not immediately followed by slaughter, since the north was distant twenty-two amos from the entrance. What is the difficulty? There, perhaps the walking is the interruption; how do we know that delay without walking would not be immediate? Rather, it must be that wherever delay is not an interruption, walking also is not an interruption. Therefore, there the delay of twenty-two amos was what prevented immediacy. According to this, by tzitzis, where delay is not an interruption, walking certainly is also not an interruption. What he wrote, that otherwise the Gemara should have counted all blessings among the three things requiring immediacy, I do not know, for in siman 206, seif 2, it is stated that lechatchilah it is forbidden to interrupt silently between blessing and performance for more than the time needed to speak; see Magen Avraham 166, letter 3, who wrote that ideally one should not interrupt at all. However, one may say that between garment and garment we are not concerned about delay. He concluded that the Ra’ah and Maharam do not disagree, similar to what Maharai, of blessed memory, wrote. I must elaborate here somewhat. Magen Avraham in siman 166, letter 3, challenged Tosafos in Sotah. His intent appears to be as follows: whichever way you say it, if they hold that partial entry is considered entry, there is no proof that twenty-two amos is an interruption, for change from one domain to another is an interruption, since the questioner thought like Rabbi that the north was not distant, etc. If they hold that partial entry is not considered entry, like Rabbi Yosei bar Rabbi Yehudah, that the north is distant and he inserts his hand to the thumbs, then even less than twenty-two amos is proven to be an interruption, for the metzora’s hand is more than about an amah and a half. This is his difficulty. To answer this I say that Tosafos were troubled by why Rav Yosef there rejected the support for Ulla by saying, ‘whose view is this? It is Rabbi Yosei bar Rabbi Yehudah,’ rather than saying only ‘perhaps’ as a rejection. Also, why establish the beraisa like Rabbi Yosei bar Rabbi Yehudah, not according to the halachah, since the halachah is like Rabbi? Furthermore, the halachah follows Ulla and the Raavad in Hilchos Bi’as Mikdash. Tosafos hold so, only the questioner thought like Rabbi, and Rav Yosef answered that the beraisa must be like Rabbi Yosei bar Rabbi Yehudah, for if it were like Rabbi, why would it not be immediate? From the space of the entrance, where he stands, into the Azarah it is immediately close, unlike the questioner’s reasoning. Necessarily it is like Rabbi Yosei bar Rabbi Yehudah. Now there is no proof, for one may say that partial entry is not considered entry and twenty amos is an interruption, or that partial entry is considered entry and in any event twenty-two amos is an interruption, since he leans in the space and brings the animal from there to the north, a full twenty-two amos. The difficulty of Magen Avraham in siman 166, letter 3, is thereby resolved. Therefore the Taz wrote that it is proven from there that twenty-two amos, and walking from domain to domain from the entrance space to the north, are not interruptions, only the delay is an interruption.
