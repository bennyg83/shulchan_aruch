Seif katan 11, and see, etc. For we place them on their presumption, only one who trembles at the word of Hashem should check. With this the Taz resolved what was written, that he should not recite a blessing in vain: why not say that he would transgress the positive commandment of “you shall make tassels”? Rather, since by law no checking is required and it is only good counsel, therefore regarding the positive commandment of “you shall make tassels” they were not stringent, and they left it according to the law. But regarding a blessing in vain, where there is the prohibition of “You shall not bear,” which is more severe, they were more stringent. Although according to Rambam a blessing in vain is a Torah prohibition, while according to Tosafot it is only D'Rabbanan, and the mitzvah of tzitzis is certainly according to the Torah, nevertheless one transgresses “you shall make tassels” only passively, while by a blessing in vain one transgresses actively, which is more severe. Based on this he derived a new law: if one wears several garments at one time, for which one blessing suffices for all of them as in seif 12, it is enough that he check one of them, and there is no concern of a blessing in vain even if the other tzitzis are invalid. However, the Bach wrote to resolve why he used the reason of a blessing in vain, because he stated a definite case: if one wears a borrowed tallis, which is exempt from tzitzis, nevertheless he may bless, as in siman 14, and likewise on Shabbos there is no Torah prohibition if he wears a garment without tzitzis, since he cannot make them on Shabbos, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 13 in the name of Mordechai. Therefore in these two cases it was preferable for him to state the reason of a blessing in vain, for in these two cases there is no positive commandment of “you shall make tassels.” Although on Shabbos it is at least D'Rabbanan forbidden to wear it without tzitzis, nevertheless a blessing in vain is more severe. According to this there is no proof to the law of the Taz, for one can say that just as they were concerned for a blessing in vain, so they were concerned that he not transgress “you shall make tassels.” Therefore, even if he wears several garments and exempts them with one blessing, nevertheless he must check them all.
And the Bach wrote, etc. On its presumption, since the presumption is not natural, etc. He answered regarding tzitzis. Know this, for even with a majority, which is stronger than a presumption, as we say at the beginning of the last chapter of Yevamos, nevertheless a majority dependent on an act is not considered a majority, as stated in Bechoros. This is the correct reading. In Bechoros we learned: one who buys an animal from a non-Jew and does not know whether it had previously given birth, Rabbi Yishmael says that if a goat within its first year gave birth, it certainly belongs to the kohen, for it certainly had not yet given birth and the offspring is a firstborn. After its first year it is doubtful. The Gemara asks: why is it doubtful? Let us follow the majority, and most conceive and give birth within their first year, and if so the offspring born after its first year is certainly not a firstborn. Rabbah established it according to Rabbi Meir, who is concerned for the minority. Ravina said it can even follow the Rabbis, because we do not follow a majority dependent on an act. If so, all the more so a presumption dependent on an act, since it is weaker than a majority, we do not follow such a presumption.
And I do not know, etc. Perhaps he did not have relations with her, etc. Meaning, one necessarily needs a reason to distinguish between something dependent on an act and something not dependent on an act. It is not a Scriptural decree. Rather, a majority not dependent on an act, such as that most people are not sarisim or aylonios, has no doubt about an act. But where it depends on an act, there is a doubt whether the act was done. Even though most times the act is done, this majority is weaker, and it is like a double doubt: perhaps he did not have relations with her, and perhaps she did not conceive. If we knew with certainty that he had relations with her, then even though it depends on an act we would follow the majority that women conceive. If so, the same applies here regarding tzitzis: although it depends on an act, nevertheless since we know the act was done and he repaired the tzitzis, by law we follow the presumption.

To clarify it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 1. Although most who are found by slaughter are experts, nevertheless if the slaughterer is before us he must be examined and we do not rely on the majority. If so, all the more so with a presumption, which is weaker than a majority. Although Magen Avraham below, siman 437, seif katan 4, wrote to explain the Beit Yosef’s words there, that one who rents a house on the fourteenth, although we hold that its presumption is that it was checked, nevertheless if the landlord is before us one must ask him and not rely on the presumption. The Beit Yosef asked: how is this different from all eighteen tereifos, where we rely on the presumption that most animals are kosher and do not check? He answered that with tereifos it never had a presumption of prohibition regarding tereifah, unlike the house, which had a presumption of chametz because it was used all year. Therefore the presumption that the landlord checked it is weakened and one must ask him if he is present. He wrote that a house is similar to the case where we do not rely on the majority that those found by slaughter are experts; nevertheless, if he is present he requires examination, since it originally had a presumption of prohibition regarding slaughter, for an animal stands in the presumption of being forbidden as a limb from a living animal. If so, the same should apply to tzitzis: since after the tzitzis were repaired there was no presumption of prohibition that they snapped, why should we not initially follow the presumption? One must say that since a presumption is weaker than a majority, therefore even where it had no prior presumption of prohibition, it is still no better than a majority where there had been a presumption of prohibition, which requires checking initially. See there in Peri Chadash.
In Yoreh Deah, siman 201, seif 65, a mikveh that is normally diminished and reduced to less than forty seah, and she immersed in it, she must immerse again whenever it is not known with certainty that at the time of her immersion it had forty seah. According to this, even if the time for prayer with the congregation will pass, he may not wear it without checking.
For this reason they did not practice checking the strings above the tied section, etc. According to the first reason, those strings too need checking, as the Taz wrote in seif katan 8. But according to this reason, nevertheless the practice still needs explanation, for it implies that each time they do not check those strings. This is difficult: granted, when the time for communal prayer would pass if he checked, the first reason is absent and there is only the reason that they are prone to snap, and therefore those strings need no checking because they are not prone to snap. But if there is time to check, he should have checked those strings, since initially we do not rely on a presumption where clarification is possible, as with the majority of those found by slaughter being experts, as written in the first reason. One must say that the world holds that the presumption here is stronger than the majority of those found by slaughter being experts, since there is a presumption of prohibition against the presumption that they repaired the tzitzis, whereas with the majority of those found by slaughter, there is no opposing presumption, as above.

And so it seems from the Bach here, who wrote that if he checked it, etc. This is the correct reading. As written in Yoreh Deah, siman 14, seif 18: although we hold that only if he checked a slaughtering knife before slaughter, and before he checked it again after slaughter the knife was lost, then it is permitted; but if he did not check it before slaughter and it was lost before he checked it after slaughter, it is forbidden. Nevertheless, if he has a designated knife and always stores it with the presumption that it is checked, even if he slaughtered with it without checking and after slaughter, before checking it, it was lost, it is nevertheless permitted. Although there the Rama wrote in the name of the Rashba that even so he should not slaughter with it initially without checking, one must say that the Bach holds that here is better, as above, because there is no presumption of prohibition against it as there is by slaughter. For this reason, in truth, Magen Avraham did not want to rely on this leniency except in pressing circumstances. In Eliyah Rabbah he brought in the name of Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 84, that if one is on the road and has no other tallis, he is permitted to close his eyes to it and bless without checking. I examined that responsum, and although all his proofs can be rejected, whether from the Semag, whether from what is written in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 31, seif 2, in the gloss, that one may throw away the head and sell it to a non-Jew without opening it to check whether water is found in it, and whether from what he brought as proof from the Rosh cited by Magen Avraham in this seif katan, as the reader will easily understand, nevertheless if he checked it before setting out on the road, one may rely on leniency and bless without checking, for there is no greater pressing circumstance than this, and in pressing circumstances Magen Avraham wrote that one may rely on it.
