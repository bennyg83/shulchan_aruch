(Seif kama 11) And see, etc. That we establish its presumption—except that one who is anxious about the word of Heaven must inspect; and through this the Taz is resolved regarding what he wrote, that one should not recite a berachah levatallah, lest one violate the positive commandment of "you shall make for yourself twisted cords." Rather, since from the perspective of law inspection is not required but only good advice, therefore regarding the positive commandment of twisted cords they were not stringent and established it according to its law; but regarding a berachah levatallah, where there is a prohibition of "you shall not take [the name in vain]," which is more stringent, they were more stringent (even though according to Rambam a berachah levatallah is a Torah prohibition, but according to Tosafos it is only rabbinic, as Magen Avraham wrote at the end of siman 215; and the mitzvah of tzitzit is certainly biblical—nevertheless regarding twisted cords one does not violate except while sitting; and you might ask: regarding a berachah levatallah one violates a positive commandment while standing, which is more stringent). And accordingly he generated a new law: if one wears several garments at once and exempts them with one berachah, as stated in seif 12—if so, when he inspects one of them, there is no longer concern for a berachah levatallah even if the other tzitziyot are invalid. However, Beit Yosef wrote to resolve that which he cited "so that one should not recite a berachah levatallah"—he cited a definitive matter: if one wears a borrowed tallit that is exempt from tzitzit and nevertheless may bless, as proven in siman 14; and likewise on Shabbat there is no Torah prohibition if one wears a garment without tzitzit, since it is impossible to make them on Shabbat, as Magen Avraham wrote in siman 13 in the name of Mordechai—if so, for these two reasons it is satisfactory for him to cite the reason of berachah levatallah, for in these two cases there is no positive commandment of twisted cords (even though on Shabbat in any event it is rabbinically forbidden to wear it without tzitzit, nevertheless berachah levatallah is more stringent). And from this there is no proof for the ruling of the author of the Taz that one could say: just as they were concerned for berachah levatallah, so too they were concerned lest one violate twisted cords—and if so, even if one wears several garments and exempts them with one berachah, nevertheless one must inspect all of them (and also Beit Yosef, according to his view, certainly that regarding twisted cords one must be concerned, for he holds that by law there is no basis to establish presumption; and even though according to this, what he cited—that one should not recite a berachah levatallah—is not a definitive reason, such as when wearing several garments at once—nevertheless, since there are cases where concern for berachah levatallah applies and there is no concern for the positive commandment of twisted cords, as Beit Yosef wrote, there is no longer a difficulty why he cited berachah levatallah and not nullification of the positive commandment of twisted cords.

And Beit Yosef wrote, etc. Its presumption—since presumption is not inherent in nature, etc.—the tzitzit will know, for regarding majority it is preferable to presumption, as we say at the beginning of the last chapter of "The Woman" (Yevamot 119b, beginning of 12a). Nevertheless, a majority dependent on action is not considered a majority, as is found in Bechorot (20a)—so too here. And this is in Bechorot, where we taught: one who buys an animal from a gentile and does not know whether it gave birth—Rabbi Yishmael says: a goat within its year, if it gave birth, certainly [the offspring belongs] to the priest, for certainly it has not yet given birth, and the offspring born after its year is in doubt. And the Gemara challenges: why is there doubt—let us follow the majority, and the majority of females conceive and give birth within their year; if so, the offspring it bore after its year is certainly not a firstborn. And Rabbah establishes it like Rabbi Meir, who is concerned for the minority; and Rabbina said: actually like the Rabbis, but a majority dependent on action we do not follow the majority—if so, all the more so presumption dependent on action, since it is weaker than majority, we do not follow presumption in such a case.

And "I do not know," etc. "Perhaps it did not come upon her," etc.—that is, we need a reason to distinguish between dependent on action and not dependent on action. For it is not a scriptural decree, but rather a majority not dependent on action, such as that the majority of men are not eunuchs or sexually incapable, where this majority has no doubt; unlike one dependent on action, which is doubtful whether the action occurred—even though the majority of times the action occurs, nevertheless this majority is weaker, and it is also like a case of doubt: perhaps it did not come upon her and perhaps she did not conceive. And if we knew with certainty that it came upon her, even though dependent on action, we would follow the majority of females who conceive; and if so, the same applies here regarding tzitzit, which is dependent on action—nevertheless, since we know the action was done and he fixed tzitzit in it, we follow presumption by law.

Like a mikveh that was drawn, etc. Not through drawing by bucket but only through channeling—and see Yoreh De'ah, siman 201, seif 49.

To clarify it, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 1: even though the majority found near slaughter are experts, nevertheless if the slaughterer is before us we must inspect him and not rely on the majority; and if so, all the more so regarding presumption, which is weaker than majority. And even though Magen Avraham wrote below, siman 437, seif kama 4, to explain the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham there, who wrote regarding one who rents a house on Passover: even though the halacha is that its presumption is inspected, nevertheless if the landlord is before us we must ask him and not rely on presumption—Rabbeinu Yerucham challenged: regarding all eighteen types of terefot we rely on presumption that the majority of animals are kosher and we do not inspect; and he answered that regarding terefot it always had a presumption of prohibition regarding terefah, unlike a house, which has a presumption of chametz that was used in it all year—the presumption that the landlord inspected is weakened, and one must ask him if it is so before us. And he wrote that a house is similar to this, that we do not rely on the majority found near slaughter who are experts—nevertheless, if it is before us, inspection is required, since it initially had a presumption of prohibition regarding slaughter, for an animal stands in presumption of prohibition of a limb from a living creature, end quote. And if so, the same applies to tzitzit: since after he fixed tzitzit it no longer has a presumption of prohibition that the threads snapped—why do we not follow presumption lekhatchilah? One must say: since presumption is weaker than majority, therefore regarding presumption, even if it did not have a presumption of prohibition, nevertheless it is not preferable to majority, which has a presumption of prohibition requiring inspection lekhatchilah—and see Peri Chadash.

Yoreh De'ah, siman 201, seif 65: a mikveh whose waters are accustomed to diminish and decrease and stand at less than forty se'ah, and one immersed in it—she must return and immerse again for as long as it is not known with certainty that at the time of her immersion there were forty se'ah in it. Accordingly, even if the time of prayer with the congregation passes, he is not permitted to wear it without inspection.

And for this reason they did not practice inspecting the threads above the gedil, etc. For according to reason one, those threads also require inspection, as the Taz wrote, seif kama 8; unlike reason two, nevertheless a reason is needed for this practice, for it appears that each time they do not inspect those threads—and it would be difficult: when the time of communal prayer passes, if he inspects, reason one does not exist, and there is only because they are made to snap; therefore those threads do not require inspection, for they are not made to snap. But if there is time to inspect, he should inspect those threads, because lekhatchilah we do not rely on presumption where it is possible to clarify, as with the majority found near expert slaughterers, as stated in reason one. And one must say that the world holds that presumption here is preferable to the majority found—for since there is a presumption of prohibition against the presumption that they fixed tzitzit, unlike regarding the majority found, where there is no presumption against it, as above.

In the name of Rosh, Levush likewise, etc. And he wrote that one may go out with a tallis on Shabbat to the public domain even if he did not inspect the tzitzit, and we are not concerned lest they snapped and are invalid, and it constitutes carrying and one is liable for the carrying.

Until all are invalidated—that is, the entire thread; but if from each thread enough remains for a knot, even if all snapped and from each one only enough for a knot remains, they are nevertheless valid; and to be concerned lest not even enough for a knot remained—this is not common.

But for us—that is, according to what Rama wrote in siman 12: the main thing is to be stringent like Rabbeinu Tam, who requires two complete threads, and it does not help if enough remains for a knot; if so, it is common that they snap such that two complete threads do not remain.

And likewise Beit Yosef here, who wrote that if they inspected, etc.—so too. And as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 14, seif 18: even though the halacha is that only if one inspected a slaughtering knife before slaughter and they inspected again after slaughter, and then the knife was lost—it is permitted; but if they did not inspect before slaughter and it was lost before they inspected after slaughter—it is forbidden. Nevertheless, if he has a designated knife and always stores it with a presumption of inspection, even if he slaughtered with it without inspection and after slaughter, before they inspected, it was lost—nevertheless it is permitted. And even though there Rama wrote in the name of Rashba that nevertheless one should not slaughter with it lekhatchilah without inspection—one must say that Beit Yosef is inclined that here it is preferable, as above, that there is no presumption of prohibition against it as with slaughter. And for this reason Magen Avraham did not wish to rely on this leniency except in a pressing situation. And in Eliyah Rabbah he brought in the name of the responsum of Beit Yaakov, siman 84: if he is on the road and has no other tallit, it is permitted to avert one's eyes and bless without inspection, end quote. And I examined the responsum there; and even though one can reject all his proofs—whether from Semag or from what is written in Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim, siman 31, seif 2, in Hagahah, that one may cut off the head and sell it to a gentile without opening to check if water is found in it, or what he brought as proof from the words of Rosh that Magen Avraham brought in this seif, which is easily understood for one who examines—nevertheless, if they inspected before setting out on the road, one may rely to be lenient and bless without inspection, for there is no greater time of pressing need than this; and in a time of pressing need, behold Magen Avraham wrote that one may rely on this.
