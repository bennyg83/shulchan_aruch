(א) Seif 2, "the order," etc. See Tosafos, Arakhin 2b, gloss beginning "hayode'a," etc. This is unlike the Geonim, whom the Mordechai and Tur cited, that it must be like the wrapping of Ishmaelites, as stated in Moed Katan 24a. We say in Berakhot 60b that one blesses "to wrap in tzitzis," but this is not so, for from the law of a mourner it is clear that elsewhere this is unnecessary. Wrapping is not written in the verse, but "with which you cover yourself," as people cover themselves, etc. "To wrap" means covering; only there, where Scripture revealed "do not cover," etc., is every wrapping that is not, etc., relevant in its place. Rather it is as Tosafos wrote in the name of Ri of Trani.

(ב) "And engage in their work." This is not exact; the language of the Baal HaIttur is: people cover themselves with their garments and go about their affairs.

(ג) "And it is proper," etc. This is the Tur's language: he covers his head so that he not be bareheaded. This is from the Baal HaIttur. The Beis Yosef wrote that it apparently does not mean completely bareheaded, for that is unrelated to tzitzis; moreover, how did he go until now to relieve himself and do other things? Rather, even though his head is covered, modest people place a scarf or tallis over their heads, etc.; perhaps this means that his head should not be uncovered by the tzitzis, etc. This is what he wrote: "and it is proper," etc. But his explanation is forced, for that is not called bareheaded. We find many places in the Talmud where it was their way to cover the head with a scarf: Kiddushin 8a, Berakhot 60b, Arvei Pesachim, Shabbos chapters 16 and 8, Moed Katan chapter 3, not with a hat and scarf; likewise Eruvin chapter 8. Per the Rif's reading there in Berakhot, the tzitzis blessing comes after "Who clothes the naked," and finally, "when he spreads a scarf over his head," etc. Also what he wrote, "how did he go," etc., implies that in his view it is legally forbidden to walk bareheaded. This is not so, since we say in Shabbos chapter 16, "may it come to me," etc.; and in Kiddushin chapter 1, Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua did not, etc.; and there, "how brazen is this man," etc.; perhaps he is from Mata Mechasya, etc. This shows that it is permitted, except before Torah scholars. So too in Maseches Derech Eretz with the two children, and there in Kiddushin regarding Rav Hamnuna. In Kiddushin one can perhaps explain like the Beis Yosef, because of modesty; also see Nedarim 30b. Likewise the end of Shabbos, "cover your head," is literal, as in Kiddushin 31a: Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua would not, etc., and like before Torah scholars. In Megillah chapter 3, "one who uncovers," etc., is explained in Maseches Sofrim as bareheaded. Likewise Kiddushin 8a is literal: another person need not have a scarf at all, as written in Shabbos chapter 8, Arvei Pesachim, and the Midrash Rabbah regarding Shema, cited by the Beis Yosef in siman 61: "I did not burden you," etc. Nevertheless the intent of the Baal HaIttur and Tur is that he should cover his head with the tallis, as written: the order of wrapping, etc., and the way, etc., and he returns, etc., and covers, etc., and blesses, etc. Because his wrapping is for prayer, he said to cover his head, as in siman 91, seif 3; see Magen Avraham there, small paragraph 2, and see there seif 5. Although the Tur already wrote in siman 2 to cover the head, there it is occasional and a measure of piety until he wraps; here he wrote it as law. Yet it is not strict law, for legally even for prayer and entering a synagogue everything is permitted, as written below; rather here everyone should be stringent, unlike siman 2, which is only for the modest, as in Kiddushin 8a. There any covering suffices until he covers his head; here, with a tallis or scarf. But see Zohar 3:122b: because she, etc., due to prayer and synagogue, and for ordinary people who do not have scarves. So it also appears from Shabbos 147a regarding a folded tallis; see Rashi there, and as written below, siman 301, seif 29, meaning after, etc.; and likewise Menachos, chapter HaTecheles: a tallis with which a minor covers, etc. See Zohar 3:122b. Also what Maseches Sofrim says, that some say it is forbidden to utter the Divine Name bareheaded, is also a measure of piety, for the Gemara in Berakhot 60b does not imply so, as written below siman 47, seif 1, and likewise from the above Midrash Rabbah, "I did not burden you," etc. In sum: there is no prohibition at all in being bareheaded, ever, except before important people; and at the time of prayer it is proper as a matter of conduct. For the rest of the day, it is for holy people who stand before Hashem always.
