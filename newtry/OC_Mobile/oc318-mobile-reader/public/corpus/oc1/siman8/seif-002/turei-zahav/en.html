“In the manner of people.” Even though for a mourner one requires wrapping that covers also the mouth — from “you shall not wrap upon the mustache” — for tzitzis, even though we bless lehisatef, nevertheless it is the covering of which it says “with which you cover yourself,” in the way people normally cover.

“It is proper that he cover his head with the tallis.” In the Tur it is written “and covers his head so he not be bareheaded,” and the Beit Yosef explains “not bareheaded from the mitzvah tallis — one should cover his head with tzitzis to fulfill the mitzvah in the best way.” Darkhei Moshe: even though one should heed the Beit Yosef’s explanation, nevertheless it appears to me our Tur meant literally — not to be bareheaded — for without tzitzis there is no prohibition to walk bareheaded, only a trait of piety as Maharam wrote in siman 2; therefore when wrapping tzitzis one needs to cover the head. To my mind, on the contrary our Tur comes to warn one must be so careful with head covering that even while wrapped in the mitzvah tallis one must not move off one’s head covering — because the mitzvah tallis sometimes slips from the head as our Tur cited from the Itur, and then one is bareheaded; but our Tur did not insist the tallis itself be on the head for the mitzvah of tzitzis. On the core issue whether bareheaded is forbidden, Rashbash responsa 72 wrote there is no prohibition from law, unlike Mahar”i; his proof from Shema “I did not trouble you to read bareheaded” implies no prohibition. He also wrote it is chassidus from “Kesubos: let it not be that the public walk bareheaded” — specifically public walking, and his conclusion: even without prayer one should be careful, and when learning if the garment is heavy he may cover with a light or silk cloth, and if he wishes to bless berachos hanehenin at night with no cap, in a bath — it suffices to cover the head with his hands, even though for “cover the heart” hands do not suffice as Mahar”i wrote, because people usually cover the heart with hands and it is not visible that the heart must be covered, but above the head it is visible, especially incidentally — so far his words. It appears to me there is a full prohibition for another reason: since it is now the custom among non-Jews to remove their hats as soon as they sit, this falls under “and in their statutes you shall not walk” — especially a custom that head covering indicates fear of Heaven, as end of Shabbos “cover your head so that fear of your Master be upon you,” and Kiddushin 33a on one who does not cover his head they said how impudent, and Moreh Nevuchim I:2 that our sages avoided baring the head so the Shechinah hover and spread over them, and they limited their words to that end. One must distance in this more than in other of their practices. Still, what Rashbash wrote that hands on the head suffice — that indeed suffices to remove the non-Jewish custom, since it shows he may not sit bareheaded, but it does not suffice for words of kedusha from his mouth, as in siman 74 — and it requires study as above.
