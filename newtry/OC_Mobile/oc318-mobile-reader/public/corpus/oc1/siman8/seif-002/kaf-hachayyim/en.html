7) [Se'if 2] The manner of wrapping is like people who wrap themselves in their garments, etc. This is the ruling of Tur in the name of Baal HaIttur, unlike the Geonim, who require the wrapping of the Yishmaelim, which is a complete wrapping. Beis Yosef explains that the Geonim infer this from the berachah 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis,' whereas Baal HaIttur holds that 'to wrap oneself' means covering; see there. Nevertheless, to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis in the most choice manner, it is proper at the time of the berachah to wrap oneself like the Yishmaelim and remain so for the time required to walk four amos, as stated in the next se'if. This satisfies even the Geonim, because the essential requirement concerns the initial donning; afterward, he could remove it if he wished. Magen Avraham 5 in the name of Terumas HaDeshen; Shulchan Aruch HaRav 6; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, ibid. Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, chapter 1, states: 'The Geonim required wrapping in the manner of the Yishmaelim. One brings the two tzitzis on the right side over to the left, so that all four tzitzis join behind him on his left side and hang behind him there. Rabbeinu Saadiah also explained this wrapping in his Arabic commentary to Parashas Bo. It is good and proper to do so, and one should remain wrapped this way at least for the time required to walk four amos when wrapping himself in tzitzis. He may then remove the wrapping from his neck. This was my master's custom.' Solel Boneh 6 writes likewise in the name of the Arizal's writings, as do Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur; Machazik Berachah 4; Siddur Beis Yaakov 25; and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 32. Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, however, writes that when a person wraps himself in a tallis, he should grasp the tallis's right side with its two tzitzis and throw them leftward around his throat, over his left shoulder toward the back. The other two tzitzis, on the left, should remain hanging in front of him, not behind. Matzas Shimurim, page 24; Chesed LaAlafim; Midrash Talpiyos, page 170c; and Yaavetz's Amudei Shamayim, page 59b, write likewise; Yafeh LaLev 1:4 cites them. Imrei Lev already posed this difficulty in Kuntres Sefas Emes 45 and writes: Both teachings are correct. The order of wrapping should be as follows. First, he should throw the two right-hand tzitzis to the left and leave the two left-hand tzitzis in front of him, intending to give the chasadim and gevuros to Rachel and the chochmah and binah to Yaakov. After this intention, he should also throw the two left-hand tzitzis to the left, so that all four join there, as Rabbeinu Saadiah writes; this nullifies the force of the evil inclination. Machazik Berachah 4 writes likewise. Ben Ish Chai cites this in the aforementioned paragraph and writes that it is stated explicitly in the siddur of Rashash. Ben Ish Chai adds that even though we do not have these intentions, after throwing the two right-hand tzitzis behind him to the left, one should wait the time required to walk four amos. He should then throw the two left-hand tzitzis behind him to the left and again wait the time required to walk four amos. He should then lower the tallis wrapped around his neck over his entire body, thereby carrying out the instruction expressed by this act; see there. This satisfies all opinions, including the works that cite only Shaar HaKavanos, chapter 2, because he waits the time required to walk four amos before throwing the two left-hand tzitzis behind him. Nothing further can be questioned. The custom of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, is that after also throwing the two left-hand tzitzis behind themselves to the left, they strike all four tzitzis twice against the left side of the nape, corresponding to the two strikes mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 2, and in Rashash's siddur.
7) After wrapping, one should look at the tzitzis twice, corresponding to the two gazes mentioned in Shaar HaKavanos in the aforementioned derush. Rashash's siddur likewise states this. See also Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derush 7, and Nehar Shalom, pages 53b and 54a; or pages 12b and 13a in the new edition. The custom of the chassidim of Beis El, may their Rock and Redeemer protect them, is that after wrapping, before lowering the tallis over their bodies, while it still rests upon their shoulders with two corners on the right and two on the left, they take all four tzitzis, look at them twice, and afterward lower the tallis over their bodies.
8) After wrapping, one should recite these four verses: 'How precious is Your kindness,' etc.; 'They shall be filled from the abundance of Your House,' etc.; 'For with You is the source of life,' etc.; and 'Extend Your kindness to those who know You,' etc. Matzas Shimurim, page 24c; Chesed LaAlafim, page 32b; Siddur Beis Yaakov; and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 32.
9) Regarding whether one must cover his head with the tallis, customs differ. Even within a single congregation, some cover their heads and others do not, and neither group protests or objects to the other. In a place where the majority would object to the minority changing the custom, however, an individual should not deviate from the majority practice. Responsa Devar Shmuel 123. Shaarei Teshuvah 3 cites this and writes that it applies specifically after the berachah, while one remains wrapped in the tallis for prayer. When reciting the berachah and immediately wrapping himself, however, he must cover his head with the tallis at that time and may not rely merely upon throwing it over his shoulders. In this respect, one should disregard a majority practice that is mistaken, through which they have not fulfilled their obligation associated with the berachah. See there.
9) Matzas Shimurim, page 23d, states: Those who wrap only their bodies in a tallis gadol and not their heads recite a berachah in vain, because the primary meaning of 'to wrap oneself in tzitzis' concerns the head, whereas they wrap only their bodies. This is also stated in Zohar, Bamidbar, in its phrase 'to spread over him the covering of a mitzvah.' Solel Boneh 6 and Shalmei Tzibbur 34c cite this.
9) Likewise, people who place the folded tallis around their neck and drape it over their shoulders, so that one folded end with two tzitzis hangs down in front on the right and the other end with two tzitzis hangs down in front on the left, do not fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis in this manner. They should be rebuked, because according to all opinions this is not wrapping. Masas Binyamin 48, cited by Magen Avraham 2; Eliyah Rabbah 3; Shaarei Teshuvah 3; and Chesed LaAlafim 4.
9) Likewise, those who cover only their head and the area around their neck with the tallis, taking no care to cover the body, must be protested. According to all opinions, the primary wrapping is of the body; the dispute concerns only whether the head must also be covered. Covering the head alone, however, is not called covering according to anyone. Shulchan Aruch HaRav 8; Siddur Beis Yaakov 27. After citing Devar Shmuel, Chida writes in Machazik Berachah 5: There are other customs regarding this; each river follows its course. According to every view, however, when reciting the berachah it is proper to wrap oneself like the Yishmaelim for the time required to walk four amos, as the Arizal writes, and afterward follow one's custom. Chida rules likewise in Kesher Gudal 2:8, as do Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid., and Siddur Beis Yaakov 26. As a matter of law, to avoid concern for a doubtful berachah, after the berachah one should wrap himself like the Yishmaelim as stated above. He should then lower the tallis over his body and remain with both his head and body covered simultaneously for the time required to walk four amos, after which he may follow his custom.
10) There: It is proper that he cover his head with the tallis. This is so that he prays with awe, for covering humbles a person's heart and brings him to fear of Heaven, as explained in the first chapter of Kiddushin. Bach; Olas Tamid 3; Shesilei Zeisim 5. He should cover his head from the beginning of prayer until its end. Eliyah Rabbah 4 in the name of Ateres Zahav; Solel Boneh 7 in that authority's name and in the name of Makor Chaim; and Chesed LaAlafim 3. During the Amidah, it is proper to cover his forehead and eyes as well, if he is not in public. Chesed LaAlafim, ibid.
11) There: It is proper that he cover his head with the tallis. According to the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei HaTzitzis, derushim 5-6, the head-tefillin must be covered by the tallis gadol. Pri Etz Chaim, chapter 1, and Shaar HaTefillin, chapter 11, state likewise, as do Olas Tamid, page 24a; Matzas Shimurim, page 24d; and Solel Boneh, ibid. This implies that they should be entirely covered for the entire time they are upon the head, and Matzas Shimurim says so explicitly. Magen Avraham 3 writes: 'See the end of siman 27, that in any event he should not cover them completely.' Mor U'Ketziah explains that Magen Avraham's statement that they should not be completely covered means throughout the day, outside the time of prayer; during prayer, however, they should be covered. See there. Or Tzaddikim, printed with the supplement called Or HaYashar, 2:17, likewise states: 'One must cover the head-tefillin with the tallis during prayer; the tefillin should not be exposed.' Yafeh LaLev 1:6 cites this and writes that Matzas Shimurim, Ner U'Mitzvah, page 17b, and Solel Boneh imply that they should be covered even outside prayer; see there. Machatzis HaShekel explains Magen Avraham to mean that the tallis should cover the entire head and the tefillin, forming a sort of roof over them, but that the tefillin should nevertheless remain visible from the front because Scripture says, 'All the peoples shall see,' etc. Yafeh LaLev responds that this is no proof: even under the tallis, the presence of tefillin on his head is recognizable because they protrude through the tallis or hat, as Magen Avraham himself writes in siman 27:20. See there.
12) Magen Avraham further writes there that Kiddushin 29b implies that an unmarried man did not cover his head with a tallis, even if he was a talmid chacham. Rambam, at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchos Tzitzis; Orchos Chaim, page 4a; and Kol Bo 22, page 15c, however, write that it is a great disgrace for a talmid chacham to pray without wrapping his head in a tallis. Yafeh LaLev 7. Magen Avraham also writes that page 8a states that one who was not a talmid chacham did not cover his head even if married. Rabbi Moshe ibn Chaviv writes similarly, by way of sod, in Etz HaChaim Al HaMitzvos, page 27d, that only a chacham wraps himself in a tallis gadol; see there. This is contrary to Kitzur Shelah, page 54d; Noheg KaTzon Yosef, page 3d; Solel Boneh at the beginning of these laws; and Yafeh LaLev, cited in the preceding paragraph. It is particularly difficult in light of our Sages' teaching in Shaar HaKavanos, Derushei Tefillas HaShachar, derushim 2-3, that wrapping in tzitzis and reciting the berachah effects a rectification in the supernal worlds. How could a person exempt himself from this? Even apart from that, Magen Avraham's words are not conclusive. The passage there does not discuss a tallis used for a mitzvah, but a rabbinic headcloth, as Be'er Heitev writes at the end of siman 17. Bach in this siman and Mor U'Ketziah likewise imply this.
