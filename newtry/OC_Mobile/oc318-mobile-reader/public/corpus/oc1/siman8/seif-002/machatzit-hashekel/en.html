(note 2) Standing. This means the blessing and the wrapping—for the Tur wrote as follows: and immediately he shall wrap himself in tzitzis while standing. Behold, he wrote that the wrapping shall be while standing. And in the Yerushalmi as well he derives wrapping through a gezeirah shavah: it requires standing, for regarding tzitzis it is written “for you,” and regarding the omer it is written “for you,” and regarding the omer it is written “standing,” and we expound “standing”; and from it the Yerushalmi derives that every blessing over mitzvos requires standing. And “for you” is written regarding wrapping—for this is a rabbinic blessing; if so, one must derive that the wrapping must be while standing. And since the Yerushalmi derives from it every blessing over mitzvos, therefore the blessing of a mitzvah has its law, and for this reason both the wrapping and the blessing must be while standing. See Bach and Eliyah Rabbah.
This implies that it is permitted, etc. — and this is a complete mishnah in tractate Challah. For we taught: a woman may sit and separate challah from her dough while naked — that is, when her face is downward and touching the ground. Even so, for a man in such a case it is forbidden to recite a berachah, because it is forbidden on account of uncovering his nakedness; but for a woman in such a case her nakedness is covered, and she is permitted to bless — thus one may infer that one may bless while seated.
It is not a mitzvah; and in the Yerushalmi we say this only regarding blessings over mitzvos.
And those who place [the tallis folded around the neck], etc. — they do not fulfill their obligation. For in this there is neither wrapping nor covering. He brings proof from below, siman 301, seif 29, where we say: one who goes out with a folded tallis on Shabbat is liable for a chatat, because it is not the normal manner of wearing and it constitutes carrying.
And likewise the Levush applies this to the Mishnah Berurah regarding those who wrap it only around their necks — they do not fulfill their obligation, as stated there in seif 70. He added that even if one covers his head with it and drapes it down over his neck while it does not cover his body at all, he still does not fulfill his obligation. He brings proof from siman 10: a garment whose main purpose is to cover the head is exempt from tzitzis even if it covers his head and most of his body. The same applies here: since it is only a head covering, it appears that its main purpose is to cover the head and it does not cover the body — therefore one does not fulfill his obligation.
