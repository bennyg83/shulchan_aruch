Seif katan 2, standing. Meaning, the blessing and the wrapping. For the Tur wrote: immediately, etc., he should wrap himself in tzitzis while standing. Thus he wrote that the wrapping should be standing. The Yerushalmi also derives the wrapping by a gezeirah shavah that it must be standing, for by tzitzis it says “for you,” and by the Omer it says “for you,” and by the Omer it says “standing grain,” which we expound as standing upright. From this the Yerushalmi learns that all blessings over mitzvos also require standing. Since “for you” is written regarding the wrapping, and the blessing is D'Rabbanan, necessarily we learn that the wrapping must be standing. Since the Yerushalmi learns from it all blessings over mitzvos, the blessing has the law of the mitzvah, and therefore both the wrapping and the blessing must be standing. See Bach and Eliyah Rabbah.
It implies that it is permitted, etc. This is an explicit Mishnah in tractate Challah, which teaches that a woman may sit and separate her challah from her dough while unclothed, namely when her lower face is pressed against the ground. Although for a man in such a case it is forbidden to bless because of exposed ervah, for a woman in such a case her ervah is covered and she may bless. Thus one may bless while sitting.
It is not a mitzvah, and in the Yerushalmi we say this only regarding blessings over mitzvos.
And those who place, etc. They have not fulfilled their obligation, for in this there is neither wrapping nor covering. He brings proof from below, siman 301, seif 29, where we say that one who goes out on Shabbos with a folded tallis is liable to a sin-offering, for it is not the manner of wearing and is considered a burden.
The Levush wrote similarly regarding the Magen Avraham, about those who wrap it only around their necks, that they have not fulfilled their obligation; see there, seif 70. He added that even if one covers his head with it and lets it hang down over his neck, but it does not cover his body at all, he has not fulfilled his obligation. He brings proof from siman 10, seif 10, that a garment made primarily to cover the head is exempt from tzitzis even though it covers his head and most of him. So too here, since it is only a head-covering and appears to be made primarily to cover the head and does not cover his body, he has not fulfilled his obligation.
