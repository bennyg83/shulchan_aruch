(3) With the head uncovered — for although we bless "to wrap oneself," covering the body alone constitutes atifah, and that is the essence of atifah. Wayfarers who place the tallis folded and hang it around their necks on their shoulders do not fulfill the obligation of tzitzis in this manner, for in this case — all agree — it is not atifah.

(4) "With a tallis" — for this covering humbles a person's heart and brings him to fear of Heaven. See Taz, whose view is that it is correct not to remove the small hat on his head during prayer, even though he also covers his head with the tallis. Bach wrote that the tallis must be on his head from the beginning of prayer until its end. In any case he should stand wrapped at least for a walk of four cubits. It is written in the intentions of the Ari z"l: he would cover the tallis over the head tefillin. See end of siman 27 — in any case one should not cover completely. At the time of wrapping he covers his head with the tallis until it reaches his mouth, and throws all four tzitzis to the left side for a walk of four cubits. Then he removes the wrap until the top of his head is visible somewhat, as above. He sets each tzitzis in its place, as stated in seif 4. This is the wrapping with which one fulfills according to all. Not like some people who pull the tallis over themselves until the head reaches half the length of the tallis and wrap themselves in it — this is not called atifah; see Moed Katan 24a. The Gemara implies that a bachelor would not cover his head with a tallis, even if he is a Torah scholar.
