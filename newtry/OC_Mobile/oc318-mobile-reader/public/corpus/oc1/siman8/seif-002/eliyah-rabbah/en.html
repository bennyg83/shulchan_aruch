And he should bless while standing. Meaning, both the blessing and the wrapping. In Eliyahu Zuta I wrote in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov as follows: if he sat, he does not go back, and it is not comparable to keriah in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 340; so wrote Maharash in the Derishah, end quote. This requires study, for if by keriah, which is D’Rabbanan, he does not fulfill it, all the more so by tzitzis, which is according to the Torah. Furthermore, according to him, also by counting the Omer, lulav, shofar, and milah he would fulfill it after the fact, since all are derived from one gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem’ from the Omer. Yet there we say that one must bless standing, as by keriah in Yoreh Deah, as the Beis Yosef wrote there. Furthermore, Abudraham, folio 19, wrote as follows: there are other mitzvos over which one blesses while standing for the preferred fulfillment, but if he did not bless while standing there is no concern, such as Hallel, Megillah, and sukkah, end quote. This implies that with those other mitzvos mentioned, even after the fact he did not fulfill it. It is difficult to say that ‘there is no concern’ means that even so he fulfilled the mitzvah properly, but in truth after the fact he fulfills all of them. It is also possible that Maharash’s view is that only by keriah, if he is not standing at the time of tearing, the mitzvah has passed, and the same applies to counting, shofar, lulav, and milah. But by tzitzis, even if he is not standing at the time of wrapping, nevertheless as long as he is wearing it the mitzvah is upon him; if he then stands a little afterward he fulfills it. This requires study. Olas Tamid wrote that it is proper to be stringent and stand for the time needed to walk four amos, as in seif 3 regarding wrapping in a tallis katan, end quote. According to what I wrote, one need not stand specifically that long at the time of wrapping. However, in Shulchan Aruch of Maharai Luria he wrote that one’s head must be covered with the tallis for the time needed to walk four amos after the blessing, like the wrapping of the Yishmaelim, end quote. This is what people practice, wrapping their heads in the tallis, winding the other end of the tallis around their throat and neck, and bringing all the tzitzis to one side. So wrote Sefer Divrei Chachamim. See below, siman 10, s.k. 12. He further wrote that when one wishes to wrap himself, he should take the sides of the tallis in his right hand and put on the right side first and afterward the left side. The reason for standing is derived through ‘lachem, lachem’ from counting the Omer. This is the language of the Levush: from here they learned that the same applies to all blessings over mitzvos, that they should be recited standing. It appears that with this he answers the Bach’s difficulty, for the Bach wondered why the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem’ is needed, since we learn in the Yerushalmi that all blessings are said standing. That is, the Bach’s difficulty is that since the Yerushalmi says so about all mitzvah blessings, it must not have learned it from the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem,’ for by other mitzvos ‘lachem’ is not written. Rather, the Yerushalmi holds by reasoning that all mitzvos are like avodah, which is performed standing, and the law of the blessing follows the law of the mitzvah. If so, the Bach properly asks why the gezeirah shavah of ‘lachem’ is needed for tzitzis, as above.
