[2] And he blesses standing. Explanation of the blessing and wrapping — and in Eliyahu Zuta I wrote in the name of my teacher the Maharil, whose language is: if he sat he does not return, and it is not comparable to tearing in Yoreh Deah, beginning of siman 340 — so wrote Maharash in Derishah, until here his language. Study is needed: if tearing, which is rabbinic, he does not fulfill — all the more so tzitzis, which is biblical. Moreover, according to his words, also counting the omer, lulav, shofar, and milah one fulfills after the fact, for from one gezeirah shavah we derive for them "for you" / "for you" from the omer; and there we say one must bless standing, as with tearing in Yoreh Deah, as Beit Yosef wrote there. Also Abudraham, page 19, whose language is: there are other mitzvos over which one blesses standing, the mitzvah in the optimal way; and if he did not bless standing one need not be concerned — such as Hallel, Megillah, and Sukkah, until here his language. It seems that in those mentioned, even after the fact he does not fulfill. It is strained to say that "one need not be concerned" means he still fulfills the mitzvah properly; in truth after the fact in all of them he fulfills. It is also possible that Maharash's view is specifically regarding tearing: if he is not standing at the time of tearing the mitzvah departs — and the same for counting, shofar, lulav, and milah. But for tzitzis, even if he is not standing at the time of wrapping, as long as he wears it the mitzvah is upon him; and if he stands afterwards a little he fulfills — study needed. Olas Tamid wrote it is proper to be strict to stand for the measure of four amos, as in seif 3 regarding wrapping the tallit katan, until here. According to what I wrote, one need not stand so much at the time of wrapping. However, the Shulchan Aruch [Mahari Luria] wrote his head must be covered with the tallit for walking four amos after the blessing, like Ishmael's wrapping, until here — and this is the custom: they wrap their heads in the tallit and wind the other end around the neck and turn all the tzitzis to one side. So wrote Sefer Divrei Chachamim; see below siman 10, comment 12. He also wrote: when he wishes to wrap he takes the sides of the tallit in his right hand and dons the right side first, then the left. The reason for standing: we derive "for you" / "for you" from counting the omer — this is the Levush's language; from this they learned that every blessing over mitzvos should be standing. It seems he resolves thereby the Bach's wonder at the gezeirah shavah of "for you" — why do I need it, since we have a text in the Yerushalmi that all blessings are standing, until here. Explanation: the Bach wondered — since the Yerushalmi says so for every blessing over mitzvos, perforce it is not from the gezeirah shavah of "for you," for in other mitzvos "for you" is not written; rather the Yerushalmi reasons that all mitzvos are like service, which is standing, and as the law of the mitzvah so the law of the blessing. If so, the Bach's challenge is sound: if so, why do I need the gezeirah shavah of "for you" for tzitzis, as mentioned above — unlike Olas Tamid, who wrote on the Bach's words, whose language is: he was not precise and overlooked that blessings are only rabbinic; and also we need the gezeirah shavah for wrapping even if he already blessed, until here his language. He did not plumb his words; in what I explained the difficulty is correct. The Levush seems to resolve that the Yerushalmi derives all mitzvos from this gezeirah shavah; so too in Kalbo, page 15, and Rabbi Eliezer there — know that all this is for blessings over mitzvos, not blessings of enjoyment, as Beit Yosef wrote. Now the Magen Avraham's difficulties from challah, like Rabbi Eliezer, siman 328 — it too is like a blessing of enjoyment; and even for other mitzvos study is needed, for it seems in Kalbo there that the Yerushalmi's intent is specifically the mitzvos mentioned, not other mitzvos. Also in Sukkah below, siman 643, in the Tur and Shulchan Aruch it seems thus; examine carefully, for I abbreviated.
