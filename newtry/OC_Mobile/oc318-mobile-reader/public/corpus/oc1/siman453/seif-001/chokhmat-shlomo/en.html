Seif 1: But not in rice etc. — It would seem by law to permit kitniyot on Pesach in time of duress, as we wrote in our works several times debating whether one can apply "a court cannot annul [the enactment] of its fellow court." Now Heaven enlightened my eyes and I found further permission from the Gemara Pesachim chapter 1 daf 3b: R' Zeira said to R' Elazar (and some say R' Eliezer to R' Yehoshua): R' Gamliel and the court — how can they annul the enactment of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel? Did we not learn: a court cannot annul the enactment of its fellow court unless it is greater in wisdom and number? He was astonished one hour. He said to him: say they stipulated among themselves that whoever wishes to annul may come and annul etc. From this it is clear that only where we know they did not so stipulate may a court not annul its fellow's enactment; but without such knowledge one may hang that perhaps they stipulated they could annul; and it is permitted to annul — and this is since it is only a rabbinic enactment, it is a rabbinic doubt, and we are lenient. Thus the language "say they so enacted" implies doubt, and on account of doubt they were lenient — not that R' Gamliel and the court knew they enacted for this; for then it would not say "say," since it is known R' Gamliel and the court annulled the enactment perforce knowing they so stipulated — then it should say "they stipulated among themselves" or "they knew they so stipulated"; and from saying "say" it is proven they too had doubt and permitted on account of doubt. And what the Gemara challenges in Megillah "a court cannot annul" and does not answer "say they stipulated" — that is because there it is words of tradition like Torah law, a doubt for stringency. And what it challenges in Gittin regarding prozbul — there it is a presumption of obligation, the borrower was certainly liable, and a doubt whether he was released by shemittah or not, like "I do not know if I paid you" — liable; therefore it challenges well they could not annul prozbul and release the borrower. And what it challenges in Avodah Zarah daf 36 regarding oil — although there it is only rabbinic, there it challenges two difficulties: first, a court cannot annul; then Rav Huna said in the name of R' Yochanan: in everything a court may annul its fellow's enactment except eighteen matters etc. — and it is astonishing how R' Yochanan can disagree with the Mishnah; behold an anonymous [Mishnah] implies in every matter a court cannot annul etc. But per the above, the Mishnah is specifically where it is known they did not stipulate initially that whoever wishes may annul; then in no matter may a court annul; but R' Yochanan deals with doubt whether they stipulated, and R' Yochanan according to his method in Pesachim answers "say they stipulated"; or even per the girsa there R' Avahu — nevertheless R' Yochanan may hold so, and therefore in doubt whether they stipulated initially, he holds in every matter a court may annul except eighteen matters; even in doubt one may not annul. Thus it fits: initially before knowing R' Yochanan's words it seemed truly even in doubt one cannot annul; but after bringing R' Yochanan's words one knows even in doubt one may annul, only it challenges regarding oil which was among the eighteen matters. And the upshot: where there is doubt whether they stipulated, they may annul a fellow court's enactment — all the more so in later enactments; and one should not be stringent. So it appears to me; and see in my work on YD laws of tereifot siman 33 edition 6 siman 207 what I wrote incidentally related to this law — see there and examine well.
