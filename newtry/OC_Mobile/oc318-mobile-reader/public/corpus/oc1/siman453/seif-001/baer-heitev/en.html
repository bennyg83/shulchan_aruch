Kitniyos. The same law applies to tartarki, tyekershi, and weitz: they do not become chametz.
The cooked dish. Even if there is no sixty, it is also permitted to eat; nevertheless a majority is required at least, for otherwise it is not called a mixture at all, since it is as if he is eating the cooked dish from the kitniyos themselves (Chok Yaakov). All types of groats that are called groifen, made from the five species, it is forbidden to keep in the house, for they moisten them with water before pounding. And if Pesach has passed over them, they are forbidden in benefit; see Chok Yaakov.
To kindle. And the Maharil forbids where there is concern that they may fall into a cooked dish. (For they pound them in a mortar in which they pound barley; and Darchei Moshe struck an axe into this, for even so the food is permitted, since it is nullified before Pesach; and one should not say that it absorbed taste because of pressure, for that applies only with a sharp food; Yoreh De'ah siman 90.) See what I wrote at the end of siman 447; and it is implied from the Maharil that it is permitted to eat olive oil on Pesach. Magen Avraham; see there.
And it is permitted. Nevertheless they require examination so that there not be among them one of the five species. Beina wrote that it is good to be stringent not to eat anise and caraway until the last day of Pesach, for it is impossible to clean them well; and the Taz wrote: I heard that they distinguish regarding what is called kremes kimmel brought from other countries, that it is forbidden, for its seeds resemble grain, but what is called feld kimmel is permitted on Pesach. And Chok Yaakov wrote: in my humble opinion one should not be more stringent, for kitniyos itself is only a stringency, and do not add upon it.
