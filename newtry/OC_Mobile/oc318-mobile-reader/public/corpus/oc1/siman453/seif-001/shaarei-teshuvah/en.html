Kitniyot — see in Mor u-Ketziah and She'elat Yaavetz part 2 siman 147 and in Kuntras Tov Ein of the Gaon HaAzulai zikhrono le-verachah at the end of Sefer Vaad LeChachamim: he wrote that in Ashkenaz and Poland, who accepted this stringency several hundred years ago, some great ones of Israel should not lighten for them, and one who is lenient breaches the fence etc.; and my uncle the Gaon zikhrono le-verachah in a responsum in manuscript wrote that the species called in our country pasulish is plainly a type of kitniyot, and he brought proofs from MaHarash on Mishnayot; and even where there is some duress, one should not permit them in these countries lest they come to breach the fence; and so the nation spoke; see in Lekut Maharil: after he brought the custom, 137, that one who eats them on Pesach transgresses the negative commandment "you shall not turn aside" and whoever transgresses words of the Sages is liable for death etc. — see there; and in one place some sages stood who wished to breach this and it did not succeed in their hand, because the sages of the generation, the great ones of Ashkenaz, were alarmed toward them and stood in the breach and upheld words of the Sages and decided the prohibition in these countries; and one should not learn from places that practice leniency from old and early times, before the decree of the great ones and their enactment spread among them — as regarding the ban on marrying two wives and the like; see in the aforementioned kuntras: a great one was stringent even in capers, although certainly it is fruit of a tree — it appears he wished to be stringent because the multitude did not know what it is and imagined it is also a type of kitniyot and would permit everything and breach the fence of the Rishonim; nevertheless even in places that accepted this stringency in capers, also if they were roasted before Pesach there is room to be lenient, as written in Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 5 — and not like Chak Yosef, end quote; and in these places they plainly practice leniency in capers; and even so the zealous precede roasting capers before Pesach, and also examine before buying them to separate from them mixtures of rice and other species, to be clean from every concern of Pesach stringency; and may a good blessing come upon them; see in Shevut Yaakov there: he wrote in Taya that he heard many refrain because there is concern of deceit that they use taya and later return and dry them to sell, and perhaps they were first used with chametz; and so I heard from Shlomim; and so many — therefore one who guards his soul should distance from them, for on Pesach extra distance is required etc. — see there; nevertheless it appears one need not be concerned for vessels in which taya was cooked or if taya spilled into food; and some practice leniency in taya that comes from merchants in countries of its growth; and also experts testify it is kosher for Pesach by recognizing in appearance, taste, and smell that there is a difference between taya that was cooked and dried and taya that comes in the normal way of its growth and light did not rule over it in cooking at all.
