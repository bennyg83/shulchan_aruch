(א) Through which one fulfills etc. — on the first night of Pesach.

(ב) And the practice etc. — because it is most beloved to a person and there is also the matter of beautifying the mitzvah; and if he has no wheat he should take for the mitzvah from one of the four species most important to him, so that he eat with appetite.

(ג) But not in rice — and likewise millet; the Gemara derives from a verse that one fulfills the obligation of matzah on the first night, which is obligatory, only with something that comes to chametz; and these do not chametz, as he concludes below.

(ד) And other kitniyot species — and buckwheat (tiktak), which among us is called (gerike), and caraway, which among us is called (tirkishe veitz), are also kitniyot species (Acharonim).

(ה) To chametz — and even if one kneaded rice flour and the like in hot [water] and covered it in garments until it swelled like dough that chametzed — this is not chametz but spoilage, and it is permitted to eat.

(ו) And some forbid — not from essence of law but a stringency they imposed; reason of this stringency: sometimes grain is mixed in kitniyot species and impossible to separate well, and it comes to chametz when baked or cooked; also often they grind rice and other kitniyot to flour, and often bake bread from them; there are common people not expert to distinguish between this flour and grain flour, between bread and grain bread — therefore they forbade all bread and cooked dish, even to cook whole rice and kitniyot, on account of lo pelug; also lest grain seeds be found in them; see Beur Halacha.

(ז) And one should not change — even on the last day of Pesach one should not lighten; nevertheless in duress when one has nothing to eat, permitted to cook all species except the five grain species; even then he should precede kitniyot to rice, millet, and ritzkale, for they resemble the five species more; plainly so too for an ill person without danger — permitted to cook for him if needed, but must check and separate well that no seeds of the five species be found; Chatam Sofer in responsum siman 122: even where there is room to permit, l'chatchila boil them, for whatever can be repaired we repair; so Chayei Adam.

(ח) If they fell etc. — for in this they did not practice stringency; nevertheless those grains one finds must be discarded if they are not unrecognizable.

(ט) Within the cooked dish — and it deals with a case where there is at least a majority in permitted [food], for otherwise it is not called a mixture at all but like eating a cooked dish of kitniyot themselves.

(י) And likewise permitted to light — meaning even to place them initially in water; in such a case in the five grain species they chametz; nevertheless it is permitted, for they did not practice prohibition except in eating and not in benefit.

(כ) And they do not forbid if etc. — meaning one need not be careful to hang an oil candle far from the table lest it drip on food; even if it dripped, we do not forbid, for in such a case they did not practice stringency, as Darkei Moshe explains; he further concludes there: even if groifen from which oil is made were crushed in a mortar in which chametz is soaked — we have no concern, for even if something mixed it is nullified in sixty before Pesach; one should not say it absorbs taste from chametz mortar, for cold does not expel and absorb; however all this is in sesame oil and the like that are not sharp; but olive oil if olives were crushed in a vessel of moistening etc., certainly absorbs taste of chametz soaking, for olive is sharp as above siman 447 seif 8; in sharp items we say under duress of crushing the mortar gives taste to olives, and there is not sixty in olives against the taste — as above siman 447 seif 8; therefore in olive oil all agree one must be careful not to hang the candle near the table lest it drip on food.

(ל) And likewise permitted to keep etc. — meaning even if water fell on them; with other grain in such a case it is forbidden to keep; here they did not decree, for they accepted upon themselves only to forbid eating them; and likewise permitted to benefit from them.

(מ) And permitted to eat on Pesach — nevertheless they require examination lest one of the five species be mixed in them; therefore some say good to be stringent not to eat anise and caraway until the last day, for impossible to separate them well; see Taz and Kaf HaChayim on this.
