And some forbid — so too in Hagahot Maimoniot chapter 5 in the name of Semak: kitniyot such as beans and lentils — some practice prohibition not to eat them on Pesach; and so Rabbi Shmuel of Ivria; but Rabbi Yechiel and other great ones practiced leniency with them; and the matter is very difficult to permit, since others practiced prohibition with them. And it appears they did not practice prohibition on account of chametz, for people do not err in this, for schoolchildren know it, for in Peirush we say in the Gemara it does not come to chametz but only the five species; rather they are forbidden because grain is a cooked-dish item and kitniyot are a cooked-dish item — a decree lest one be confused with the other; and also there are places that make bread from kitniyot and one comes to confuse it with dough that is a cooked-dish item; but types of vegetables that are not similar to grain at all do not come to be confused; and also sometimes grain is mixed in them and it is impossible to separate well; and mustard likewise some forbid on Pesach because it is considered grain, even though in their days it was permitted; nevertheless now one should be stringent in these generations who are not so expert in prohibition and permission as the early generations, as Rav Saadiah Gaon explained, end quote; and so Mordechai chapter Kol Sha'ah 27 in the name of Semak; and Bach and Beit Yosef wrote: none are concerned for these words except Ashkenazim; and so Darkei Moshe orot 2: and we Ashkenazim practice stringency; and such are his words here in gloss to Shulchan Aruch: and the custom in Ashkenaz is to be stringent and one should not change; and so Levush; and so Bach; and so Rashi orot 3: they practiced in these countries not to eat on Pesach a dish of rice, millet, beans, lentils, sesame, and other kitniyot, even on the last Yom Tov; and so Chayei Adam general rule 127 orot 1: all dispersions of Ashkenazi exile practiced so, and also in Poland, for they too are considered among Ashkenazim, to forbid even to cook kitniyot and ritzkale and whole rice and millet — everything on account of error — see there; and see further below siman 464 in the gloss that mustard too — the Ashkenazi custom is not to eat it at all on Pesach — see there; and so Levush and Bach and other Acharonim.
