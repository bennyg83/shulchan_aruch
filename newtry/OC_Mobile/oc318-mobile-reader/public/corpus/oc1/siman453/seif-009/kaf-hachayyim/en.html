Rabbi Shmuel of Evreux did likewise. Rabbi Yechiel and other great authorities, however, treated them as permitted. It is very difficult to permit them once others have adopted a prohibitive custom. The custom was apparently not adopted because of leavening, for people could not have erred about this; even schoolchildren know that the Gemara explicitly states that only the five grain species can become chametz. Rather, they were prohibited because grain and kitniyos are both cooked as porridge, so one might be confused with the other. In some places bread is also made from kitniyos, creating a risk of confusion with grain porridge. Vegetables, however, do not resemble grain at all and will not be confused with it. Grain is also sometimes mixed into kitniyos and cannot be perfectly sorted out. Mustard should similarly be prohibited on Pesach because it is gathered into a pile. Although it was permitted in earlier generations, in these generations one should be stringent, because people are not as knowledgeable about prohibitions and permissions as the earlier generations, in accordance with the explanation of Rav Saadiah Gaon. These are his words. Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah, likewise writes all this in the name of Sefer Mitzvos Katan. Beis Yosef cites it and writes that no one takes these concerns into account except Ashkenazim. Darkei Moshe, subsection 2, likewise writes, 'We Ashkenazim have customarily been stringent.' Rema similarly writes here in the gloss to the Shulchan Aruch that the custom in Ashkenaz is to be stringent and must not be changed. Levush and Bach write likewise. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3, writes that in these regions the custom is not to eat during Pesach cooked dishes made from rice, millet, beans, lentils, sesame, or other types of kitniyos, even on the last day of Yom Tov. Chayei Adam, rule 127, subsection 1, likewise writes that dispersed Ashkenazic communities everywhere, including the countries of Poland whose residents are also regarded as Ashkenazim, adopted the prohibition. They prohibit even cooking whole kitniyos, buckwheat, rice, or millet, because of the possibility of error. See there. See also the gloss below in siman 464, which states that the custom in Ashkenaz is not to eat mustard at all during Pesach. Levush, Bach, and the other later authorities write likewise.
