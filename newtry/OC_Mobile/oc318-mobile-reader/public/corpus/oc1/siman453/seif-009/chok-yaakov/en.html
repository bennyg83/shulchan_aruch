A day or two. Mahariv wrote in responsa, siman 193, that it is a proper custom not to grind for Pesach within three days before the eve of Pesach. The Mechaber, who wrote a day or two, means a full twenty-four-hour period, as Rashi explained in Parshas Mishpatim regarding the verse, if he stands a day or two. Similar precision is made in the language of the poskim, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 70, and in Terumas HaDeshen, klal 6. See there in my work Minchas Yaakov, seif katan 9, and see Choshen Mishpat, siman 306, seif 8, in the Sema. All this appears clear to me, unlike the Magen Avraham, who wrote that a full twenty-four-hour period is not needed, only that it stay overnight. It seems that he compares it to water, which requires staying overnight. In my humble opinion, the language of the poskim appears as I wrote. If one transgressed and kneaded, it seems one should not prohibit it after the fact, provided he guards it well. See seif katan 23.
At the time of grinding it is hot. The Keneses HaGedolah wrote that he heard from one sage that we are not concerned for this, because when they sift the flour it cools. He does not rely on this report, and it is against the implication of the poskim.
