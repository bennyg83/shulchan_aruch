(s.k. 8) Flour, etc. And the Gaon wrote, etc. And nowadays all of them, even villagers, soak. And for this reason, what Chok Yaakov wrote in the name of the responsum Nachalas Shivah, that he permitted to mill before thirty days without picking the millstones clean, and the same law applies even within thirty days before Pesach if it is a time of pressing need. And in my humble opinion the matter is very doubtful to me, whether one may rely on this nowadays; for I looked there in siman 12, that his reasons to permit were: (1) that his intent is not to nullify; (2) perhaps they did not mill soaked produce on it at all; and even if you say they milled soaked produce on it, perhaps afterward they milled on it several times produce that was not soaked, and the chametz was nullified in the produce that was not soaked. And further, since there is labor in picking clean, and expense, it is considered like after the fact. And it is enough that they were stringent within thirty days, and before thirty days we have not found a custom, so one should leave it on the law; end of his words. And the Radbaz already wrote in a responsum, and so wrote in a responsum the Gaon the Chacham Tzvi, that one should not rely on a permissive responsum unless in the case that comes before him there are all the sides that he wrote in the responsum; for perhaps the author of the responsum only permitted by combining all the sides. And if so, for us, who soak all the produce, the main basis of his permit is gone, namely perhaps they milled produce that was not soaked, and there remains only the permit that he does not intend to nullify. And yet the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Rashba in siman 447, and hinted to it here in s.k. 7, that one should not add upon it until there is sixty, but only specifically when there is already sixty; if so, how shall we permit making the mixture l'chatchila? And labor and expense are not sufficient for this, especially since regarding sorting sprouted wheat the Peri Chadash disagrees, as above; and how shall we be lenient in such a case? Therefore it appears to me in my humble opinion that nowadays one should not rely on this permit.
