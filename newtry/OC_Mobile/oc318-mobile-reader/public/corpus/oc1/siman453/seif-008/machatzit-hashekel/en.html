(Note 8) Flour etc. — the Gaon wrote etc.; and nowadays all, even village people, moisten. And from the reason Kaf HaChayyim wrote in the name of responsum of Nachal Shiva that he permitted grinding thirty days before without picking millstones — and similarly even thirty [days] before Pesach if time of duress. It appears very doubtful to me whether to rely on this nowadays; I examined there siman 12 that their reason to permit was: (1) he does not intend to nullify; (2) perhaps they did not grind grain for moistening in it at all; and if you say they ground grain for moistening, perhaps afterward they ground many times not for moistening and chametz in non-moistening grain was nullified. Also since there is trouble in picking and expense is important like b'dieved; and it suffices that they were stringent within thirty and before thirty we find no custom — we establish the law accordingly; and Radbaz already wrote in responsum, and so responsum of his son part 18: one should not rely on a lenient responsum unless the case comes before him with all sides, as he wrote in the responsum — perhaps the author of the responsum only permitted with all factors combined; if so for us who moisten all grain, the main permission was perhaps they ground non-moistening grain and only the leniency remains that he does not intend to nullify; and Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Rashba siman 447 and hinted here note 7 one should not add until sixty except when there is already sixty — how then permit making the mixture l'chatchila with trouble and expense insufficient, especially also on separating sprouted grain — Peri Chadash disagrees; how be lenient such? Therefore it appears nowadays one should not rely on this leniency.
