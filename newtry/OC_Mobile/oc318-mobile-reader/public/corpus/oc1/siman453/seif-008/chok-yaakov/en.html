The practice is to clean out the mill. In the responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 12, he wrote that thirty days before Pesach it is permitted to grind even without cleaning out the mill. See there, where he elaborated in permitting this. In question 13 he wrote that if they cannot easily clean out the mill because it is close to Pesach, or because of some other necessity, even for them this is considered pressing circumstances, and it is permitted to grind without cleaning. Nevertheless, one should bake everything before Pesach, as I wrote above in seif katan 13, and in any event one should grind everything before Pesach.
They guard it. My brother-in-law the Rav wrote in Soles Belulah: Now the practice is to cover the mill with linen, and according to this it may be possible to permit even the first flour. The Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Matzas Mitzvah: When grinding soaked grain in a mill, they should not grind in that house at that time even with another mill, because the dust flies and mixes in; and a minor should not guard the mill. I have not seen people careful about this, and they grind for Pesach in the same house in which other flour is ground. One must say this is because there is a doubt, perhaps the other grain was also not soaked, and even if you say that it was soaked, perhaps it was not soaked in a way that became chametz, as I wrote above in seif katan 15. Also, after the fact there is no prohibition, since it was nullified before Pesach, as explained above in seif 3. However, during Pesach one should be careful about this, so it appears to me.
