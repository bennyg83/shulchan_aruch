Mitzvah — see in responsum Divrei Yosef siman 41: some practice that all matzah one eats on Pesach should be from wheat guarded from harvest; and in this there is neither yohara nor dispute nor lo titgodedu — see there; and they brought in Rabbenu Yerucham; see Peri Chadash siman 491 in customs of prohibition seif 1: those who practice eating matzah all Pesach from harvest — it is not called a fence but an extra matter that one does; and if he wishes to eat from matzah not guarded from harvest all days of Pesach except the first night, he needs release; and in Rabbenu Yerucham he brought in the name of Nachpeh Bekesef that release is required; and there he wrote: also one who practiced not to eat matzah but fruits the rest of Pesach days — although there is room to side not to release him, and so one who practices eating all matzah from harvest — nevertheless the essence is that release in regret suffices — see there; see YD siman 334; and the correct view: whoever wishes to practice separation in these stringencies or the like should stipulate explicitly that he does not accept upon himself to practice so always, only this time, for sometimes it happens he cannot act so and it comes to ruin; and if he forgot to stipulate, it appears release through regret suffices, as Machatzit HaShekel wrote in the name of my teacher zikhrono le-verachah; see in Tashbetz part 3 siman 117: wheat on Pesach may be ground in water-millstones — see there; and so they practice even those who eat all Pesach from harvest, for it is no better than matzah of the mitzvah; for although most are scrupulous that it be guarded from harvest, nevertheless they are not particular to grind in hand-millstones — only some practice rolling themselves also in this, and there is no yohara as stated; and so I heard they practice in Eretz Yisrael whoever can; see Peri Chadash: his view is that for matzah of the mitzvah on the first night one does not fulfill with guarding from harvest; and in Mishna Berurah siman 79 it was asked whether to be concerned for Peri Chadash's stringency here, and he answered: has he not seen the custom of Israel in all countries of Poland — none are concerned except the extremely scrupulous etc.; and for Rambam it is an asmakhta; and Rosh and many great ones are lenient; and in rabbinic [matters] we listen to leniency, end quote; and nowadays many consider themselves scrupulous and return to the granaries that there be wheat guarded from harvest at least to fulfill with matzah of the mitzvah; and so Machatzit HaShekel siman 460: in most famous communities they practiced matzah of the mitzvah from harvest; and in Eretz HaTzvi many practiced that all Pesach matzah be from harvest — see there regarding grinding by a non-Jew in hand-millstones one must be stringent — see there.
