A mitzvah. See Baer Heitev; and see in Shaarei Teshuvah Divrei Yosef siman 41, that some practice that all the matzah one eats on Pesach should be from wheat guarded from the time of harvesting, and in this there is neither arrogance, nor dispute, nor "lo sisgodedu"; see there; and Birkei Yosef cited it. And see Peri Chadash siman 491, in the customs of prohibition, se'if 1, who wrote that those who practice to eat matzah all of Pesach from [wheat guarded from] the time of harvesting are not called making a fence, but rather an extra matter that one does; and if he wishes to eat from matzah that is not guarded from the time of harvesting on all the days of Pesach except the first night, he does not need release [from a vow]. And in Birkei Yosef he cited in the name of Nechpeh BaKesef that release is required; and there he wrote that also one who practiced not to eat matzah but only fruits on the other days of Pesach, although there is room to side that one should not release him, and likewise one who practices to eat all the matzah from [wheat guarded from] the time of harvesting, nevertheless the essence is that release with regret suffices; see there. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 228. And the correct [practice] is that whoever wishes to practice abstinence with these stringencies or the like should stipulate explicitly that he does not accept upon himself to practice so always, only this time, for sometimes it happens that it is impossible for him to do so, and he comes to ruin. And if he forgot to stipulate, it appears that release through regret suffices for him, as the Machazik Berachah wrote in the name of my teacher of blessed memory. And see Tashbetz part 3 siman 117, that wheat on Pesach it is permitted to mill with water-mills; see there. And so they practice even those who eat all of Pesach from [wheat guarded from] the time of harvesting, for it is no better than matzas mitzvah: for although most are careful that it be guarded from the time of harvesting, nevertheless they are not particular to mill with hand-mills; only some practice an extra refinement for themselves also in this, and there is no arrogance in this, as written above. And so I heard that they practice thus in Eretz Yisrael, whoever is able to do so. And see Peri Chadash, whose view is that for matzas mitzvah of the first night one does not fulfill his obligation except with [wheat] guarded from the time of harvesting. And in Noda BiYehuda, Tinyana, siman 79, he was asked whether one should be concerned for the stringency of the Peri Chadash in this, and he answered: has he not seen the custom of Israel in all the countries of Poland, that none are concerned for it except the most scrupulous of the scrupulous, etc.; and according to the Rambam it is an asmachta; and the Rosh and many great authorities are lenient; and in a rabbinic matter we listen [to those who] are lenient; end quote. And nowadays many have set themselves as among the scrupulous and go around to the threshing floors so that they have wheat guarded from the time of harvesting at least to fulfill the obligation of matzas mitzvah. And so wrote Machazik Berachah siman 460, that in most of the well-known communities they practiced that matzas mitzvah is from [wheat guarded from] the time of harvesting; and in Eretz HaTzvi many practiced that all the matzah of Pesach should be from [wheat guarded from] the time of harvesting. And see there regarding milling by a non-Jew, that with hand-mills one should be particular; see there.
