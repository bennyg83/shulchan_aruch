Se'if 2. One who makes, etc. And the Raavad wrote: and that is when there is a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras. And in the laws of challah the Raavad wrote that we require that there be grain in the [required] measure, like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for he ruled the halacha like him; however, we do not require a majority of grain. And that is to say, as is written in the Tosefta, chapter 2 of Challah: one who makes a dough from wheat and from rice: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says it is not liable for challah until there is grain in it to the [required] measure, and a person does not fulfill his obligation with it on Pesach until there is in it to the [required] measure. And in the Yerushalmi there, chapter 3 halacha 6: the Mishnah is not like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says, etc. R' Yose bar Idi in the name of Resh Lakish: the law is like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. Said R' Hila: whether like the Rabbis here or like the Rabbis there, we say until its majority is grain. And the reason of grain: Rav Huna said, the taste of grain, even though its majority is not grain, etc.; see there. And he ruled like Rav Huna, for so they said in the Gemara of Zevachim 78a: said Resh Lakish, pigul, etc., we learn from this that taste imparted in a majority is not according to the Torah. And Rava answered: "one who makes," etc., say the end, etc.; rather, a species with what is not its species, etc. Hence, even though the majority is rice, and specifically when there is a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, as is written in Avodah Zarah 67a: said R' Yochanan, whatever has its taste and its substance, etc. And Rashi explained, s.v. "its taste": fat that dissolved and fell, etc. And that is to say, even if there is a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, it is not forbidden according to the Torah, for we rule that taste like the substance itself is not according to the Torah, as is written in Chullin 98 and 99: Rava said, it is needed only for taste like the substance itself, that in consecrated things it is forbidden, etc., but in ordinary food it is permitted; and see there in Rashi. And there, 108a, Abaye said: we learn from this that taste without its substance in ordinary [cases] is according to the Torah, etc. He said to him: the manner, etc. And what is written in Pesachim 44 regarding "infusion," to give taste like the substance itself, the later Amoraim hold that it is a mere asmachta; for nazir and the vessels of Midyan are two verses; or alternatively, nazir is that the permitted joins the forbidden, and the vessels of Midyan are a novelty (Rashi there 98b, and Tosafos there). And in Avodah Zarah there they challenged Rashi from that Zevachim mentioned above regarding wheat and rice, that taste imparted in a majority is according to the Torah; and there: rather, a species with what is not its species by taste, a species with its own species by majority; and necessarily it is according to the Torah, similar to majority. And they explained in the name of R' Eliyahu that what is written in Avodah Zarah, "its taste and its substance," means a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, even if there is no substance, for example if it dissolved; and "its taste and not its substance" means that there is not a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras. And so they wrote in Chullin there in the second answer; and so wrote the Rosh there, and in the laws of challah in the name of R' Chaim. But Rabbeinu Tam explained that "its taste and not its substance," for which there are no lashes, is regarding a species with its own species; but regarding what is not its species, it is according to the Torah. And according to his words, even with less than the time of eating a pras; and so answered Tosafos in Chullin there in the first answer. And see the Rosh there. But it is difficult from what is written in Pesachim 44, "and it is impossible," like within the time of eating a pras according to the Torah, etc.; and see Tosafos there s.v. "they challenged him," etc., and one can say, etc. But Tosafos in Chullin 99a, end of s.v. Rava, answered that there it is a species with its own species; see there. And he wrote: however, it is difficult, etc., and one can say that this is the implication of "and according to your reasoning," and so they answered in Yevamos 82a, end of s.v. "rather." And one must say that the Tosafos of Pesachim mentioned above is according to the answer of R' Chaim; but this requires further study regarding those two mortars there, and likewise regarding Bavli kutach, which is a species with what is not its species; and likewise it is difficult for the words of the Rashba, who wrote that even according to the answer of R' Chaim, even with less than the time of eating a pras it is according to the Torah, like every half-measure according to R' Yochanan; and that sugya there is according to R' Yochanan. And from the words of the Rosh in Chullin there it does not imply so, for he wrote: and now according to the ruling of Rabbeinu Tam, if it was poured, etc.; and he did not also write according to the words of R' Chaim, because according to him there is a distinction whether within the time of eating a pras or not. And what is written in Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 98, "R' Chaim," must be "Rabbeinu Tam," and so wrote the Peri Chadash. And see the Raavad in chapter 5 of the laws of Nezirus, who wrote that even if there is not a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras it is forbidden according to the Torah; and so wrote the Tur in siman 98 in Yoreh De'ah; and he wrote that only for a ke-zayis alone is one liable if there is a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, for the permitted has turned into forbidden; and so wrote the Rosh in the laws of challah: for from this reason, one who makes a dough from wheat and from, etc. But the Rambam was silent here, and likewise in the laws of challah, and it is implied even if there is not a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras and there is not in it the [required] measure. And so wrote the Ramban, and the Rosh brought him in the laws of challah, and the Maggid Mishneh there; and he wrote: because of dragging, and specifically wheat and rice, for rice is dragged after wheat; and the reason is as they have established, that only the five species come to leavening, and therefore one does not fulfill his obligation on Pesach and one is not liable for challah. And so we rule, that rice is dragged after wheat even if there is not a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, and it becomes chametz, and therefore one fulfills, etc., and is liable for challah. And so they said in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Challah: you have nothing that comes to matzah and chametz except the five species alone; and correspondingly, you have nothing that is dragged with all of them except wheat and barley alone. R' Hila in the name of Resh Lakish: we learned only "one who makes," etc., and it is dragged only with wheat alone. And the Rambam goes according to his reason, for he explained "its taste and its substance" as that there is a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, and specifically that he ate the entire pras until he ate the ke-zayis within it within the time of eating a pras, and then they administer lashes for it; and if not, it is forbidden only rabbinically, as he wrote in chapter 15 of the laws of Forbidden Foods and in chapter 5 of the laws of Nezirus; and he wrote there: and regarding this it is said "and every infusion," etc. And here, "one who makes a dough," etc., implies that even one who eats a ke-zayis from it has fulfilled his obligation; and according to his view, specifically when he ate the entire pras; and therefore one must say it is because of dragging. And in the Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Orlah, halacha 7, and chapter 6 of Nazir, halacha 1: R' Abbahu in the name of R' Yochanan: regarding all that impart taste, they do not administer lashes for them except for taste imparted of a nazir. Said R' Zeira: regarding all that impart taste, they do not administer lashes for them until he tastes the substance of the prohibition itself; and regarding a nazir, even if he did not taste the substance of the prohibition itself. Said R' Abba bar Mamal: regarding all that impart taste, prohibition and permission do not combine; and regarding a nazir, prohibition and permission combine. A baraita supports R' Zeira: a ke-zayis of wine that fell into a pot, if he ate a ke-zayis from it he is exempt until he eats all of it. According to the view of R' Abba bar Mamal, once he ate a ke-zayis from it he is liable, etc.; see there. And the dispute of R' Zeira and R' Abba is the dispute of R' Akiva and the Sages in the chapter of "and every infusion," as is written in Pesachim and in Nazir; only that in our Gemara they said that according to the Sages he learns the entire Torah from nazir, and in the Yerushalmi even according to the Sages it is only regarding a nazir alone. And therefore even if there is a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, he is liable only regarding a nazir alone. And that is what he said: regarding all that impart taste, they do not administer lashes, etc.; and that is to say, as Rashi explained: specifically where the body of the prohibition fell and was not dissolved, since according to the Yerushalmi taste like the substance itself is only regarding a nazir; but regarding a nazir, where taste like the substance itself is according to the Torah, even if the prohibition dissolved he is liable, only that there be a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras and he ate the entire pras, as is written in the baraita, etc. And if so, according to our Gemara the same applies to all prohibitions; and this is like the words of the Rambam. And that is what the Rambam wrote in the laws of Nezirus there: a permitted thing does not combine with a forbidden thing for a nazir, etc., until there be from the forbidden thing a ke-zayis within the measure, etc.; and that is to say, not like R' Abba bar Mamal, for according to him even if he ate only a ke-zayis he is liable, as is written in the Gemara there, and there according to R' Akiva. And that is what the Rambam wrote in chapter 1 of the laws of Chametz: they are liable to kares only for chametz itself; but regarding a mixture of chametz, etc., if he ate them he receives lashes and there is no kares, as it is said "you shall not eat any leavening matter." When is this said? When he ate a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras; but etc., rather they strike him with rabbinic lashes. And so he wrote in Sefer HaMitzvos, negative commandment 198; and the Ramban challenged him: first, why did he explain like R' Akiva; and further, according to R' Akiva even with less than a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras he receives lashes, as is written in Pesachim there, for necessarily the Rabbis disagree with him only where there is not within the time of eating a pras; and further, within the time of eating a pras even kares he should be liable. But the Rambam goes according to his method, for he ruled so regarding all prohibitions in chapter 15 of Forbidden Foods, that in a mixture even with a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras there is no kares, only lashes; and so it is implied in the Rif there; see there, for he wrote: but etc., even according to the Rabbis he receives lashes; and as is written in Avodah Zarah: whatever has its taste and its substance, etc., they administer lashes. But he explained like the Sages, that regarding a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras they concede to R' Akiva. And even though they said there that according to the Rabbis the verse "any leavening matter" does not refer to a mixture, but only to what became chametz through something else; and likewise according to R' Akiva, only that R' Akiva expounded, etc., and the Sages do not expound: that is according to the initial assumption that the Rabbis disagree even regarding a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, and as is written there: if so, why do the Rabbis disagree with R' Akiva, etc., and a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras is according to the Torah, etc. But according to the conclusion, the Rabbis establish the verse regarding a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras; and necessarily one must say so, for if not so, why did they say in Avodah Zarah there "they administer lashes for it," which implies that there is no kares; either he should be liable to kares, or he should also be exempt even from a negative commandment, as the Ramban challenged the Rambam. Rather, all of them learn from chametz; and regarding chametz it is explicit that it is with a negative commandment and there is no kares, as is written in Pesachim there in Toras Kohanim of R' Yehudah: "any leavening matter," etc., to include, etc.; I might think he is punished with kares, etc.; and from this we learn regarding the body of the prohibition that is mixed into it; and if there is only its taste, we learn from "infusion"; and both are needed, for I might have thought that if the body of the prohibition is mixed, even kares he should be liable. And that is what the Rambam wrote in chapter 5 of the laws of Nezirus: a permitted thing, etc., and likewise, etc.; and regarding this it is said "and every infusion": they divided it into two clauses in the first clause, where the body of the prohibition is mixed into it, and it teaches us that the permitted does not combine with the forbidden; and therefore he did not write in the enumeration of the mitzvos [the case of] what became chametz through something else; for if his intent were to rule like R' Akiva, according to R' Akiva the main verse was said regarding this, and a mixture he learns from the inclusive wording of "any." And what he wrote in chapter 4, mixtures of chametz, they transgress regarding them because of bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei, and it is implied from his words even if there is not a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, since he did not distinguish and wrote without qualification; and as the Maggid Mishneh wrote there in the name of the Ramach: not because he explained like R' Akiva, for he ruled there that even regarding hard chametz [there is] bal yera'eh, as is written there regarding garments that they washed, etc., and likewise papers, etc., and there is not regarding them "shall not be seen" and "shall not be found," for the form of the chametz does not remain; which implies that otherwise one transgresses regarding hard chametz with bal yera'eh; and regarding hard chametz R' Akiva concedes that there is no negative commandment in eating them, as is written in the Gemara there. And one should not say that he explained like R' Meir, for the anonymous Mishnah, "se'or shall be burned," etc., is not like R' Meir; and likewise he did not enumerate in the negative commandments hard chametz, and he also did not include it in mixtures; rather, he explained that what was said "who is the Tanna" refers only to the end, "these are under a warning"; and therefore he cites regarding "these," etc. But one transgresses regarding a mixture and regarding hard [chametz] even according to the Sages: for a mixture, even if there is not a ke-zayis within the time of eating a pras, nevertheless it is not nullified and its prohibition remains; only regarding eating he does not transgress, since it is impossible to eat within the time of eating a pras, for even in its substance he would not transgress in such a case; and likewise hard [chametz], for the opinion of the one who eats it is nullified, since it is not fit for eating, and as is written regarding kutach, "if in its substance," etc. And therefore "se'or shall be burned," and not as [those who] wrote that it shall be burned by rabbinic [law], for the plain meaning of the Mishnah implies according to the Torah, like in the end; and one who eats it is exempt, for nevertheless it is forbidden, only he is exempt according to the Torah; and the same law applies to the beginning [clause].
