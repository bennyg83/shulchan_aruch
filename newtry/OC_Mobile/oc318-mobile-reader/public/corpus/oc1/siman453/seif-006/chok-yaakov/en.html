Flour throughout the year. The same applies if one put even Pesach flour into it once; if he wants to launder them, he must undo the stitching, as I wrote above, siman 451, seif katan 52.
And they launder them. The Taz wrote that it is good to buy new ones, and according to the law as well it appears to him that laundering is prohibited. His proof is from what the Rosh wrote, that if one rubbed a goose with bran, since it has holes it is forbidden, and certainly this applies to sacks, which have holes in their weave, and likewise to the cloths on which matzos are made. This is the substance of his words. I do not know why he brought his proof from afar and did not bring proof from a sieve, a grater, and baskets, which the Rav himself cited above in siman 451, seif 19 in the gloss, where hagalah does not help. There he wrote that with sacks the practice is to permit. Rather, certainly sacks are different, since they are laundered well against each other by hand from inside and outside, unlike a sieve and similar items, and certainly unlike a goose, where laundering is not relevant, since the chametz enters inside through rubbing. This is clear. See what I wrote above, siman 451, seif katan 52 and 53, and go out and see what the people do.
