(א) Nowadays etc. — meaning although in Gemara times they forbade only moistening barley because they are soft [and likewise oats and rye are included in barley for this matter], and they quickly chametz and one should fear lest they come to chametz at time of moistening; but wheat, being hard by nature, does not quickly chametz and was permitted to moisten; nowadays they forbade because we are not expert in moistening, as below.

(ב) Forbidden to moisten — moistening: Rambam wrote they soak wheat in water and grind immediately; Rashi explained to soak them a little in water before grinding to remove the bran so the fine flour is clean; and the Geonim forbade because we are not expert to moisten well so they not come to chametz at time of moistening; and also lest they come to leave them a little after completion of moistening before grinding and come to chametz; and if he transgressed and moistened, forbidden to eat and benefit from them on Pesach days; nevertheless permitted to keep moistened wheat flour on Pesach if they dissolved in a little water and were ground immediately after moistening; but barley perhaps even in this manner forbidden l'chatchila to keep, for perhaps since their way is [to chametz], they chametz through moistening.

(ג) Whether wheat whether barley — Mechaber's language is not precise, implying wheat and barley are equal; in truth barley, since their way is also by Gemara law forbidden to moisten; perhaps nowadays forbidden to keep in the house while moistened even if not near splitting — unlike wheat; see below siman 467 note 7 etc.; see Tur and Gemara Pesachim daf 46: rye is a type of barley for this matter — Eliyah Rabbah YD siman 324 Magen Avraham.
