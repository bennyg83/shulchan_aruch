Nowadays it is forbidden to soak. The explanation of soaking: the Rambam wrote that they moisten the wheat with water, and the Aruch explained it as an expression of steeping. Since we are not expert in soaking, the Geonim prohibited it. Nevertheless, it appears that it is permitted to keep the wheat in one's house, since according to the law of the Gemara, wheat that was soaked is permitted as long as it is not close to splitting, as explained below in siman 467, seif 8. See there. This is specifically with a steeping or slight washing, but extensive soaking is complete chametz, like grain that sank in a river, as explained at the beginning of siman 467. See there in the Beit Yosef. So it is also implied in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 119, in Tzemach Tzedek, siman 32, and in the other later authorities. See what I wrote in seif katan 16. So too Maharil wrote in responsa, siman 59, that if they leave it in water for some time, it is complete chametz. The Taz wrote that it is forbidden to buy wheat from the mill owner, for they regularly rinse the wheat first.
Whether wheat or barley. The language of the Mechaber is not precise, for it implies that wheat and barley are equal. In truth, barley, since it is soft, is forbidden to soak even according to the law of the Gemara. Perhaps nowadays it is forbidden to keep it in the house when it has been soaked even if it is not close to splitting, unlike wheat. See what I wrote below, siman 467, seif katan 7 and 26. See there in the Tur and in the Gemara, Pesachim 46, that rye is like a type of barley for this matter. See Yoreh Deah, siman 324, Magen Avraham.
