From a mouse's eating. Because the small amount of saliva in the mouse's mouth does not cause a hard kernel of wheat to become chametz. Furthermore, some say that it is like fruit juice, which does not cause leavening; see the responsa of the Ramban, siman 150. The Magen Avraham wrote: Therefore, even if there is not sixty times against them, it is permitted after the fact. Possibly this applies specifically to wheat, but barley hastens to become chametz. See below, siman 466, regarding a mouse eating flour and what I wrote there.
That there not be sixty times of permitted matter against it. This refers only to grain that sprouted, and so wrote the Magen Avraham. The agreement of the later authorities is that the primary view follows the poskim who hold that flour mixed with flour is called liquid mixed with liquid, and it mixes and becomes nullified before Pesach, and it is permitted to bake it even during Pesach, unlike the view of the Semag. See Terumas HaDeshen, siman 114, and in his Pesakim, siman 146, where what he wrote in the name of the Semak seems to contradict what he wrote in his name in siman 190 regarding challah, and it implies that they are equal. See there. A conscientious person should be stringent for himself and bake everything before Pesach. See what I wrote above, siman 430, seif katan 1. Nevertheless, it is certainly permitted to cook from those matzos during Pesach, unlike the Olas Shabbos, as the Magen Avraham challenged him below in siman 458. See there. But if the flour became moistened and turned into crumbs, nullification does not help even to bake it before Pesach, as explained below in siman 467.
There should be sixty times. This is not called nullifying a prohibition initially, since his intent is not to nullify it. So wrote the Tur. The act of selecting makes it even clearer that his intent is not to nullify, as written in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 114. See there, and see my work Minchas Yaakov at the beginning of klal 61.
