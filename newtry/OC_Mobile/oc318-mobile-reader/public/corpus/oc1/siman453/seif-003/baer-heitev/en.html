A mouse. For the little saliva in a mouse's mouth does not leaven hard wheat; therefore even if there is not sixty against it, it is permitted after the fact (and this is not like nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, for his intent is not to nullify it, but only to mill it) (Rashba and Ramban). And the Peri Chadash challenged: is he not nullifying it before Pesach, and it is entirely permitted? And he concludes that since he mixes it in order to eat it on Pesach, it is like nullifying a prohibition; see there. And Chok Yaakov wrote in the name of the Ramban that a mouse-bite is like fruit juice; and it is possible that this is specifically wheat, but barley quickly becomes chametz (Magen Avraham); and see above, siman 466 se'if 4.
To see. This refers to grain that has sprouted. And one need not bake it before Pesach, for flour mixed with flour is called wet with wet, and it does not again awaken (Terumat HaDeshen and Bach). And Chok Yaakov wrote that a conscientious person should be stringent for himself to bake everything before Pesach. Nevertheless it is certainly permitted to cook from those matzos during Pesach; see Magen Avraham in siman 458. And if the flour became moist and turned into crumbs, nullification does not help even for baking before Pesach, as is explained below in siman 467 (Chok Yaakov).
