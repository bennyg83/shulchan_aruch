(15) Wheat — and likewise other grain species.
(16) It is of no concern — for the grain does not chametz from the small amount of saliva in the mouse's mouth; therefore even if there is not sixty against them it is permitted b'dieved; see below siman 466 regarding law of eating a mouse in flour and what is written there.
(17) And likewise etc. — that sprouted grain (called ausgewachsen), meaning that although grain that sprouted from moisture of the ground is full chametz, nevertheless when he did not separate, there is no prohibition, for they are nullified within the permitted wheat and permitted to grind them all together. Many Acharonim agreed that it is permitted to bake even on Pesach; and although on Pesach its prohibition is in any amount, already before Pesach it was nullified in sixty; flour in flour is called dough in dough and does not return and awaken; however one scrupulous should be stringent for himself to bake before Pesach when there is sprouted grain.
(18) However one must see etc. — this refers only to sprouted grain and not to the above.
(19) That there is not so much etc. — and if he sees there is not sixty against it, he must separate the sprouted grain; and he is not obliged to clear all of them but at least enough of them until there are sixty against them.
(20) Sixty against it — for if there are not sixty against them, even b'dieved they are forbidden in benefit, for sprouted grain is full chametz [and even if there is doubt whether there was sixty against the sprouted grain, likewise forbidden]. And they must be cleared before Pesach; and if Pesach passed and he did not clear them, he sells them to a non-Jew except for the value of the prohibition in them; and if there was not sixty against the sprouted grain, some say permitted to add more permitted wheat upon them in order to nullify them in sixty and grind before Pesach; and this is not called nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila that is forbidden, since it is before Pesach and the time of its prohibition has not yet arrived; and most poskim forbid, for since he mixes with intent to eat them on Pesach, it is as nullifying at the time of prohibition; nevertheless in duress it is possible to rely on those who are lenient.
