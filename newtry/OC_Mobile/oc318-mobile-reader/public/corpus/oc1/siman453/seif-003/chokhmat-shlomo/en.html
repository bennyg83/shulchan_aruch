(There, seif 3 in the gloss) And similarly if they did not separate sprouted grain — however one must see it is not so much that there is not sixty against it from the permitted. It appears by law: if there is not sixty in the wheat against the sprouts, whether it is permitted to add another species such as buckwheat etc. — it appears forbidden; not only to fulfill matzah obligation — certainly forbidden; one: concern for those who hold even in such a mixture one does not fulfill matzah except with rice, not other species; two: even for those who permit other species, perhaps combined with buckwheat there will not be an olive-bulk within the time of eating a meal from the permitted species for matzah alone; three: even for those who hold taste suffices, that is when the taste is only from what is permitted on Pesach — then since taste is sensed it is as if in essence; but here there are sprouts too, also a grain species — in the sprouts one does not fulfill matzah by taste; and from taste there is no proof it is as if in essence, lest it be in combination with sprouts; therefore certainly one does not fulfill; only for permitted eating it does not appear so. Bach is brought in Magen Avraham end siman 447: forbidden to mix even before Pesach to eat on Pesach; so implies Tzemach Tzedek whom Magen Avraham brought; even Magen Avraham who disagrees only because he does not intend to mix to eat on Pesach — if he mixed to eat on Pesach he would concede forbidden; so Mishnah Berurah siman 442 notes 18, 14; and Tur brought there; even per Taz who permits siman 447 — examination is needed. I answer: in Shulchan Aruch Shabbat siman 254 seif 4 he wrote: even if one put a little oil etc. one may not light with them — see Taz and Magen Avraham: reason — we decree lest one come to light in essence; however one could say specifically if he put a little oil in his hands intentionally to permit lighting — that is forbidden by this decree since he does so intentionally; but if it mixed by itself without intent we do not decree; and truly l'chatchila one does not nullify prohibition; and one could say from that reason itself we decree lest one eat in essence; yet this is difficult: why for a rabbinic prohibition may one add and nullify per Mechaber, and why not decree lest one eat in essence — in oil too it is rabbinic and we decree lest one eat in essence; it would seem there it deals when only a little of permitted was put and forbidden was majority — therefore we decree lest one light in essence; so Pri Megadim Shabbat laws wrote; but one who examines the Gemara proves the opposite — if so the Gemara should challenge from Rabbenu Gershom etc.; also more clear from Tosafot there s.v. simchat: it is different, since of permitted there is only a quarter, nullified among other species, as if no flax at all — thus explicitly where there is little permitted one need not decree lest one light in essence, only otherwise forbidden even in the Temple though there this decree does not apply; thus the Gemara's question "what if one puts any amount of oil" — the reason is the decree; yet by law it is forbidden otherwise since oil is little and perforce only Abaye was doubtful perhaps even any amount is as if in essence; but Rabbah who said the reason "one does not light" perforce forbids even much; therefore the reason was needed that for any amount otherwise it would be forbidden by law, especially poskim who gave reason lest one light in essence — how do they disagree with the Gemara's anonymous [view] that any amount is as if no permitted at all? Perforce it deals with majority — and from Tosafot it would be proof for R' Chaim Kohen that minority permitted in majority forbidden joins forbidden to permitted and one is lashed — see there and examine; poskim disagree. From my words it appears: even in majority we decree lest one light in essence — why permit adding to rabbinic prohibition? One must distinguish: there it is rabbinic without a Torah strand, no Torah-prohibition concern — therefore we do not decree a decree on a decree; but in Shabbat laws, since there is a Torah strand lest one tilt to violation, we decree decree on decree; or distinguish: adding a species one sometimes mixes without intent to nullify — we do not decree lest one use in essence; but in oil one mixes other oil not of its kind — no way to mix them, recognizable he does so only to nullify prohibition — we decree lest he use alone. Thus here chametz on Pesach is Torah-forbidden in less than sixty — Taz permits mixing in time of permission, adding its own kind, not recognizable intent to nullify to cancel, for often one mixes wheat with wheat; but if he mixes what is not customary to mix, intent is only to nullify — certainly forbidden; and what helps from time of permission if we fear use in essence, and here Torah prohibition if used without sixty nullification — in Shabbat too mixture and lighting were in time of permission, yet chametz is worse, some say its name clings and it is always in prohibition, but oil was certainly on erev Shabbat in time of permission and we decreed — all the more here; therefore it appears forbidden to add another species — Taz would agree forbidden to mix, especially most forbid nullifying before Pesach intending to eat, as stated; examine well. Regarding onions found in wheat: in my Pesach laws I wrote to permit in sixty relying on Peri Chadash — see there; only in duress, not otherwise. Now I saw even without duress if they are so little that in a thousand they are nullified for all — one may say permitted: combine Rashba brought in Beit Yosef siman 100: bran is nullified in 960 — we see nullification near a thousand even what is not nullified in sixty; even disputants explain Yerushalmi deals with taste — thus taste not nullified in sixty is nullified in 960; so here even if we say fruit juice does not help in sixty, in a thousand it is nullified, as in bran: since not explicit it is not nullified even in a thousand per Rashba, only in sixty — so one may say for a sharp item, especially onions not clearly sharp or fruit juice; so it appears to be lenient; examine well.
