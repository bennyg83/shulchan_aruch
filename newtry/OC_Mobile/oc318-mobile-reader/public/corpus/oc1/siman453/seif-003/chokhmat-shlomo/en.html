(There, se'if 3 in the gloss) And likewise if they did not remove from it that grain which has sprouted; however, one must see that there is not so much of it that there not be sixty of the permitted against it. Note: Behold, regarding the law if there is not sixty in the wheat against the sprouted, whether it is permitted to add upon it by putting into it another species, such as tartarki and the like: it appears that it is forbidden. Not only to fulfill the obligation of matzah is it certainly forbidden: first, one should be concerned for those who hold that even within the time of eating a pras one fulfills the obligation of matzah only with rice and not with other species; and even according to those who permit also with other species, nevertheless one should be concerned that perhaps in the combination of the chametzes with the tartarki there will not be within the time of eating a pras a ke-zayis from the kosher for matzah alone. And further, one can say that even according to those who hold that within the time of eating a pras it helps, that is because in any case there is taste of grain, as is explicit in the Mishnah, "if there is in it taste of grain," namely if the taste is only from what is kosher for Pesach; then, since the taste is sensed, it is as if it is in its substance. But in this, since there are also chametzes in it, and it too is a type of grain, then either way: with the chametzes one does not fulfill the obligation of matzah, and from the taste there is no proof to say that it is as if it is in its substance, for perhaps it is in combination with the chametzes; therefore to fulfill, certainly one does not fulfill his obligation with it. But even for a permit of eating, so it does not appear. And behold, the view of the Bach, brought in Magen Avraham at the end of siman 447, is that it is forbidden to mix even before Pesach in order to eat it on Pesach; and so is implied the view of the Tzemach Tzedek that Magen Avraham brought there; and even Magen Avraham who disagrees is only because he does not intend to mix into it in order to eat it on Pesach; but if he were mixing it in order to eat it on Pesach, he would concede that it is forbidden. And so it is implied from the words of Chok Yaakov, siman 442 s.k. 18, and siman 453 s.k. 14, and from the language of the Tur that he brought there. And even according to the view of the Taz who permits in siman 447, one should look into this. And I will answer and say: Behold, in Shulchan Aruch, laws of Shabbos, siman 264 se'if 4, he wrote: even if he put a little oil, etc., one does not kindle with them; and see there in the Taz and in Magen Avraham the reason: because we decree lest one come to kindle with it in its substance. However, one can say that specifically if he put by hand a little oil so that it be permitted to kindle it, this is forbidden because of that decree, since he does so intentionally; but if it became mixed by itself without his knowledge, we do not decree. And yet in truth l'chatchila one does not nullify a prohibition; and one can say that for this very reason itself, for we decree lest one come to eat it in its substance. But this is difficult: why regarding a rabbinic prohibition is it permitted to add upon it and nullify it according to the view of the Mechaber, and why do we not decree lest one come to eat it in its substance? And yet regarding oil as well it itself is rabbinic, and we decree lest one come to [use] it in its substance. And at first glance it would appear that there he is dealing with a case where he put only a little of the kosher, and the unfit was the majority; therefore we decree lest one come to kindle with them in their substance. And so I found in Peri Megadim, laws of Shabbos, who wrote so. But one who looks in the Gemara will see the opposite is proven: for if so, what does the Gemara challenge from Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel? Let us say that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel deals with a case where the majority is from things with which one kindles, therefore one kindles; and he deals only with a minority of permitted and a majority of forbidden. And further it is proven more from Tosafos there, who wrote s.v. "the rejoicing of the Place of the Drawing": it is different, and this is their language: since there is of the kosher in it only a quarter, it is nullified in the other species, and it is as if there is no flax in it at all; if so, it is explicitly proven that where there is a minority of the permitted, one does not need a decree lest he kindle with them in their substance; rather even without that it is forbidden even in the Temple, even though that decree does not apply there. And if so, it is puzzling: why does the Gemara say in the inquiry "what is the law that he put into it any amount of oil," the reason being because of a decree? Behold by law it is forbidden even without that, since the oil is a minority. And necessarily specifically Abaye according to the initial assumption was in doubt that perhaps even any amount is as if it is in its substance; but Rabbah who said to him the reason, since one does not kindle, necessarily forbids even much; therefore he needed that reason, for regarding any amount, even without that it would be forbidden by law. And especially the poskim who gave a reason because of lest one come to kindle with them in their substance: and how do they disagree with the anonymous Gemara that holds that any amount is as if there was no kosher in it at all? And necessarily he is dealing with a majority. [And while I am speaking of this, it appears that from the words of Tosafos mentioned above there will be a proof for the view of R' Chaim HaKohen, that a minority of permitted in a majority of forbidden combines the permitted to the forbidden, and they administer lashes for it; see there carefully. And the poskim disagree with him, and this requires further study from here; and understand.] And behold, according to this, since even regarding a majority we decree lest one come to kindle with them in their substance, the difficulty above remains: why regarding a rabbinic prohibition is it permitted to add upon it? And one is forced to distinguish: that there is different, for he is dealing with a rabbinic matter in which there is no trailing Torah concern, and there is no concern of a Torah prohibition; therefore we do not decree a decree upon a decree. But in the laws of Shabbos, since there is a trailing Torah concern lest he tilt, therefore we decree a decree upon a decree. Or one can distinguish between if he adds upon it from a species that was there at the outset: for in this the manner is to mix sometimes even without intent of nullification, and regarding this we do not decree on account of lest he come to use it in its substance; but regarding oil he is dealing with mixing into it other oil that is not of its species, for this is not the manner to mix them together, and it is recognizable that he does so only in order to nullify the prohibition; therefore we decree lest one come to use it alone. And if so, here regarding chametz on Pesach, which according to the Torah is forbidden on Pesach with less than sixty: therefore, granted that the Taz permits to mix in a time of permit, that is to add upon it from its own species, for in this it is not recognizable that his intent is to mix to nullify it, for many times his manner is to mix thus wheat with wheat; but if he mixes something whose manner is not to mix them together, then it is recognizable that his intent is only to nullify, and certainly it is forbidden. And what does it help that it is in a time of permit, if we are concerned lest one come to use it in its substance? And here it would be a Torah prohibition if he uses it without nullification in sixty. And yet on Shabbos as well the mixture and the kindling are in a time of permit; and it is even better than this, for regarding chametz there is an opinion that its name remains upon it and it is always in prohibition, but regarding oil certainly it is on erev Shabbos in a time of permit, and we decree; and all the more so in this. And therefore it appears that it is forbidden to add upon it another species; for regarding this one can say that even the Taz concedes that it is forbidden to mix; and especially since the majority of those who forbid to nullify before Pesach in order to eat it [hold so], as it appears to me in my humble opinion regarding this; and look carefully. And behold, regarding the onions that are common in wheat, whether they are kosher for Pesach: Behold, in my composition on the laws of Pesach, from the early print, in this siman, I wrote to permit with sixty, and I relied on the words of the Peri Chadash there; see there. But that is specifically under pressing circumstances, and not otherwise. And behold now I have seen that even without pressing need, if in any case they are very little, little, until it is permitted with a thousand according to all, one can say that it is permitted: for one can combine with this the view of the Rashba brought in Beis Yosef siman 100, that a whole creature is nullified in 960; behold we see that there is nullification near a thousand even in something that is not nullified in sixty. And even those who disagree with him, nevertheless they explain the words of the Yerushalmi as dealing with its taste; if so, we see that there is a reason for something that is not nullified in sixty and is nullified in 960; if so, here too, even if we say that fruit juice does not help for nullification, that is in sixty, but in a thousand it is nullified; and just as regarding a whole creature, since it is not explicit that it is not nullified even in a thousand, the Rashba holds that specifically in sixty it is not nullified, but in 960 it is nullified, so one can say regarding a sharp thing likewise. And especially regarding the matter of onions, where it is not so clear that they should be considered a sharp thing or fruit juice; therefore it appears to me in my humble opinion to be lenient in this, as it appears to me correct; and look carefully.
