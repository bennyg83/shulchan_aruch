Flour. See Taz, and see Magen Avraham letter 8, who was stringent; see there. And at first glance it is implied that the reason is that it is a double doubt: perhaps they did not soak it, and even if you say they soaked it, perhaps in a way that it did not become chametz; and see Yoreh De'ah regarding a doubt whether the gullet was broken while alive or after slaughter. And in Chok Yaakov letter 17 it is implied that even if it is known that they soaked it a little, under pressing circumstances it is not so grave. And what he wrote: where they mill beside the maltzin (malt), it is forbidden under pressing circumstances; and see Chok Yaakov there: if they bake before Pesach, one can say that it is nullified in sixty, for flour is called "to it," if he has no other flour. And it is better to take rye flour for matzos, or oat flour and the like, than wheat flour from the market, for this one they soak and that one they do not soak; and all this is under pressing circumstances.
