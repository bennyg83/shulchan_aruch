Tur's reason: moreover even if there is mouse-bitten [grain], it is not one in a thousand and they are nullified when ground; and here one is not nullifying prohibition l'chatchila, for he does not grind to nullify them — unlike Hagahot Semak who wrote one must separate well that there be sixty hundred of those that did not sprout; and although dry in dry, nevertheless it gives taste at baking; also baking must be before Pesach, for if on Pesach even any amount is forbidden; therefore correct to beware that none of that sprouted wheat be mixed in Pesach grain, for it is his habit to bake on Pesach, end quote — behold he holds flour does not mix with flour le-shemah as one entity like dough in dough, only after baking it becomes one piece; therefore needed to bake before Pesach; so Terumat HaDeshen siman 114: we do not hold like Semak but like Semag: flour in flour mixes and they are like dough in dough; so Tosafot Avodah Zarah daf 44 s.v. it was brought etc. who wrote: and one could say it deals with wheat flour and barley flour that are not recognizable — thus not nullified in majority, since species in non-species; perforce implies but in its kind such is well nullified in majority from Torah; and rabbinically sixty required in every kind in moist. Therefore here if there is sixty, permitted to bake even during Pesach, since nullified before Pesach and does not return and awaken like moist siman 447; but difficult from Shulchan Aruch siman 466: if before Pesach the moisture dried, shaking does not help, it crumbles and mixes and forbidden to eat — Mordechai in the name of Mahari of Vienna; already mentioned in Mordechai in the name of Mahari of Vienna: what was nullified before Pesach does not return and awaken — if so one must say when moisture dried it was not nullified at all, flour in flour does not mix per Semak we mentioned — if so Shulchan Aruch rulings contradict each other; one must distinguish: Semak holds flour in flour is not considered one piece unless ground together as Tur wrote "and they are nullified when ground" — grinding makes mixture as one piece, and that is like baking per Semak; therefore Shulchan Aruch and Rama here permit if there is sixty since mixture was before grinding; but siman 467 where chametz came after grinding, moisture on sack became chametz — then during Pesach forbidden to bake to make one piece; therefore also before Pesach forbidden to bake, for he will certainly bake on Pesach; thus stricter than Semak who is lenient in baking before Pesach — to reconcile Shulchan Aruch: here lenient on one side more than Semak, siman 466 stricter on another side than Semak — where permitted, permitted even during Pesach; where prohibited, prohibited even before Pesach. But from Tosafot I mentioned it is clear flour in flour mixes immediately when it falls there — therefore permission when mixed before Pesach even after grinding. Levush ruled here like Semak: must bake before Pesach sprouted grain; it appears correct as Shulchan Aruch ruled.
