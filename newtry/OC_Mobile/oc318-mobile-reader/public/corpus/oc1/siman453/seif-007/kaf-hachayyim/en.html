Even if one kneads rice flour or a similar flour with boiling water and covers it with cloths until it swells like dough that became chametz, it remains permitted for consumption. It is not chametz but spoilage. Rambam, chapter 5, halachah 1; Kol Bo, siman 48; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. This also follows from Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, which write that these substances do not become chametz, implying that they cannot become chametz under any circumstances. It likewise follows from Rashi and Levush cited above in subsection 2, and from Rabbeinu Yerucham, path 5, section 3, who writes that one may prepare every type of cooked food from them. See there. Raavad's objection there to Rambam, that this matter is not clear and not everyone agrees, refers to halachah 2, concerning dough kneaded with fruit juice, as Maggid Mishneh explains there while reproducing the objection in halachah 2. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur writes likewise. This also appears from Maran in Kesef Mishneh, who does not address Raavad's objection because he relies upon Maggid Mishneh. It is also true that not all authorities agree regarding dough kneaded with fruit juice, as stated in Tur and Beis Yosef, siman 462. See there. See Chok Yaakov, subsection 4.
