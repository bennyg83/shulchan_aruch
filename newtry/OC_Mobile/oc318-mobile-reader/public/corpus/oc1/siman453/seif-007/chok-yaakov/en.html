It is forbidden to place it upon an animal. The reason is written in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 118: because the flour becomes heated by the animal's body, and also because it is not clear to us whether an animal's sweat renders food susceptible to impurity and causes leavening. Although logically it should not render susceptible or cause leavening, like human sweat, nevertheless one should not be lenient in a matter that is unresolved. According to this, it would seem that after the fact one should prohibit flour that became moistened from an animal's sweat because of doubt. However, the Magen Avraham wrote: It appears to me that after the fact one should rule that an animal's sweat does not cause leavening. This appears to me to be primary, for what Terumas HaDeshen was uncertain about, whether animal sweat renders susceptible, is certainly not so for one who examines chapter 2 and chapter 6 of Machshirin and the Rambam, chapter 10 of Tum'as Ochlin. See below, siman 467. Nevertheless, it requires study whether it is permitted to knead matzos from it, since fruit juice mixed with water causes leavening. One might say that it is an independent derivative, and also that once the fruit juice inside it has dried, it no longer causes leavening. It appears to me that this is the reason of the opinion cited in the Tur in this siman regarding a mouse's eating, that it is fruit juice and does not cause leavening. However, in the glosses of the Semak brought by the Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Peretz, it implies that even in such a case it causes leavening. See below, simanim 462 and 466, and what I wrote there in seif katan 7 and 8.
And likewise one should be careful initially. If he transgressed and placed the sacks one on top of another, there is still a remedy: he should leave them for a day or two before kneading. This is also implied by the words of Shibbolei HaLeket, who compares the law of placing many sacks one on top of another to kneading on the same day that the flour was ground. This is also explained in the Magen Avraham at the end of this siman. If he transgressed and kneaded, there is no prohibition after the fact, as implied by the language of the Rav, and so wrote the Tzemach Tzedek.
