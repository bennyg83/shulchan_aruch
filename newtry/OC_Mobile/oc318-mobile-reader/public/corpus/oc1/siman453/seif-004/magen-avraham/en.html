From the time of milling. Because then they bring them close to water (the Rosh). This implies that in a place where they mill with a donkey-mill, or where the wind drives the millstones, guarding is not required; however, there too they are accustomed to wash the wheat.
Flour from the market. For we do not presume a prohibition, lest water touched it (Shiltei Gibborim and Tanya). And the Gaon Moreinu HaRav R' Yitzchak of blessed memory of Gombin, who was head of the beis din of the community of Lisa, wrote that in our country, where they are accustomed to wash the wheat and leave them soaking in water, it is forbidden to take flour from the market, for it is literally like grain that was submerged, as is written in siman 467 se'if 2. And there is further proof from what the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 442 in the name of Terumat HaDeshen, that they treat fine flour as absolute chametz because they soak the wheat (see Sefer Charedim). And so ruled all the sages of Prague: that once a certain teacher ruled to take flour from the market, and he retracted and decreed a fast for his error. All this I heard from the mouth of my teacher Moreinu HaRav R' Chaim Katz of blessed memory, who was head of the beis din in the community of Posen; end quote. And so he wrote in the sefer Tzemach Tzedek; see there.
