See Taz; see what I wrote in letter 3; and see siman 467 se'if 1; and see Chok Yaakov letter 17: if crumbs became mixed, they sift, and to keep it until after Pesach; and see siman 466 se'if 4. And under pressing circumstances it is implied that he permits; and from Kol Bo one can say that he speaks of a doubt, not of a certainty that it became mixed; see there. Nevertheless, if he has no matzah except this in which crumbs became mixed that was sifted with a sieve, one should permit and fulfill the positive commandment of eating matzah. And if a dry crumb of a minute amount became mixed into flour, and it is impossible to separate the crumbs, and he has no sieve, and he has no other flour for matzah except this: this requires further study; for one can say that in such a case we rely on the She'iltos, that in a sefer, or an opinion, that dry is nullified on Pesach like other prohibitions, even though a rabbinic prohibition does not [join]; and God willing it will be explained in siman 475 and siman 485 regarding this.
