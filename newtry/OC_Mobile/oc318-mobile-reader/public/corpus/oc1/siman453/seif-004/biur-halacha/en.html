It is good to guard them, etc., from the time of harvesting. And the view of the Peri Chadash is to decide that guarding from the time of harvesting is required by law, and as an absolute prerequisite, even after the fact. And like his words is found in several Rishonim, that the guarding is from the time of harvesting. And even though if one does not find matzah that was guarded from the time of harvesting for the sake of matzah, we can certainly rely on the view of the Shulchan Aruch and recite the blessing on eating matzah, nevertheless l'chatchila it is certainly proper to be stringent for matzas mitzvah to take specifically matzah guarded from the time of harvesting. And in the name of the Gra they also cited that he was very stringent not to eat except what was guarded from the time of harvesting, and he was careful in this all the days of Pesach, for the reason that perhaps water fell upon them while still attached after the produce had dried.
And at least from the time of milling. And whether it is permitted to send guarded flour on a freight wagon without a Jewish watchman, see in the sefer Tosafos Yerushalayim, who is in doubt about this: for even if we are not concerned for substitution and for rainfall, nevertheless explicit guarding there is not, and the Merciful One said "and you shall guard"; and if so, perhaps it is not worthwhile to send without a watchman. And see Sedei Chemed, section Chametz U'Matzah, siman 11, letter 6, who spoke at length on this matter.
And under pressing circumstances, etc., from the market. See Mishnah Berurah; and even under pressing circumstances it is better to take rye flour or oat flour for matzos than wheat flour from the market, for this one they soak and that one they do not soak (Peri Megadim).
From the market. From the terse wording it is implied that even from a non-Jew it is permitted, for we do not presume a prohibition; only that one must sift them at the outset, lest there be crumbs of chametz in them, as is explained in Shibolei HaLeket siman 210 in the name of Rashi, and so it is explicit in several Rishonim. However, in the novel insights of the Ritva on Pesachim he is very stringent about this, and this is his language: it stands to reason that flour of non-Jews is forbidden on Pesach, for through milling with water-mills water commonly enters them; and so is the view of the Ra'ah of blessed memory. And even flour of a Jew that remains from the rest of the days of the year, regarding which they were not careful properly, he raises a doubt there. Nevertheless at the end he concludes: and regarding flour of a Jew we do not forbid after the fact, but regarding that of a non-Jew it is forbidden, for that one he certainly leaves in a vulnerable place as much as he can; and his teachers agreed that even under pressing circumstances we do not permit them at all. And the Geonim of blessed memory wrote that it is permitted to take flour from the market under pressing circumstances, and we do not presume a prohibition, and one fulfills his obligation with this. And he of blessed memory wrote about this: wherever he has no other flour in any way at all, he makes matzah from flour of a non-Jew and eats a ke-zayis from it for the mitzvah, for this concern is from a rabbinic prohibition of a minute amount, and where there is the Torah mitzvah of matzah we are not concerned; and even though a beis din can stipulate to uproot a Torah matter passively, that is specifically where there is a definite prohibition, but here it is a remote concern, and it is possible that there is no prohibition; end quote. And from the other Rishonim it is implied that we are not stringent after the fact, and even all the days of Pesach it is permitted; and see Bach.
