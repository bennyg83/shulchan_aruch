From the marketplace — from the conclusion of the wording it implies that even from a non-Jew it is permitted, for we do not establish a prohibition unless one must pick them initially lest there be crumbs of chametz, as explained in Shibolei Leket siman 200 in the name of Rashi, and so explicitly in several Rishonim. However in Chiddushei Ritva to Pesachim he is very stringent here and thus: It is reasonable that their flour is forbidden on Pesach, for through grinding in water-millstones it is common for water to rise in them; and so is the view of Ra'ah; and even flour of Israel left from the rest of the year that was not guarded properly — he doubts there. Nevertheless at the end he concludes: and in flour of Israel we do not forbid b'dieved, but of a non-Jew it is forbidden, for that one certainly leaves them in a ruinous place as he can, and his teachers agreed that even in a time of duress one does not permit them at all. And the Geonim wrote that it is permitted to take flour from the marketplace in time of duress and we do not establish prohibition, and one fulfills his obligation thereby. And he wrote on this: wherever one has no other flour in any way at all, one makes matzah from flour of a non-Jew and eats from it an olive-bulk for the mitzvah, for this concern is [only] a rabbinic issur of something [forbidden in] mixture; and in a place of the Torah mitzvah of matzah we are not concerned — even though a court may stipulate to uproot something from the Torah in shev v'al ta'aseh, that is only where there is a definite prohibition; but here the concern is remote and it is possible there is no prohibition, end quote. And from other Rishonim it implies we are not stringent b'dieved, and even all the days of Pesach it is permitted; see Bach.
