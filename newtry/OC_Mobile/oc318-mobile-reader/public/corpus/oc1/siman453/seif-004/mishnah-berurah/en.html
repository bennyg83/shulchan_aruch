(21) That they make matzah of mitzvah with them — meaning the matzot one eats on the first two nights to fulfill the mitzvah of "in the evening you shall eat matzot" require extra guarding; and it is not enough that we have no concern of chametz fear, but extra guarding is required le-shem matzah, as it is written "and you shall guard the matzot" — meaning he must guard le-shem matzah.

(22) From time of harvest — meaning he should guard from that time onward that water not fall on them le-shem matzah of mitzvah; but before harvest while still connected to the ground there is no concern even if we know rain fell on them; see below siman 467 seif 5 that even while connected, if they dried completely and no longer need the ground, they accept chametz if rain falls on them; therefore the practice is to harvest guarded grain while still somewhat moist.

(23) From time of grinding — because then they bring them near water and guarding is required.

(24) Permitted to take etc. — for we rely on those poskim who hold the essence of guarding is from kneading onward; before kneading we do not require explicit guarding, and even by default we do not establish prohibition; even where they moisten wheat — usually they do not soak until it chametzes, only sprinkle water and rinse and immediately grind; although Mechaber in seif 5 forbade nowadays to moisten — here is time of duress, and perhaps these wheats were not moistened; Acharonim wrote: now that the custom is to launder wheat and soak in water, forbidden to take wheat flour from marketplace even in duress, literally like sunk grain explained siman 467 seif 2 — even b'dieved forbidden by Gemara law, worse than moistening, forbidden all Pesach days, all the more to take for matzah of mitzvah; many poskim hold even to keep them in the house forbidden because of bal yera'eh, as siman 467 seif 2 in gloss there; even if he transgressed and kept, some forbid; Agur in the name of his ancestor permits selling to a non-Jew after Pesach, for at most it is chametz doubt.

(25) Flour from the marketplace — and wheat is permitted to take even not in duress, if he will guard from grinding onward; however forbidden to take wheat from the mill-owner who measures all year for grinding wages — even b'dieved, for millers regularly launder them in water first; one should warn the multitude (Taz); Taz further wrote: where they grind wheat in millstones in which they grind malt, forbidden to take marketplace flour even in duress (even where there is no way to moisten them initially), for malt is full chametz and mixes; Acharonim wrote: in duress even this is permitted, for even if something of malt remains and mixes, certainly nullified before Pesach; flour in flour is dough in dough and does not return and awaken (and must shake them lest malt crumbs remain inside); nevertheless correct in duress to bake everything beforehand, as seif 2 in Mishna Berurah; Acharonim wrote: although by law guarding le-shem matzah is only for first nights, other days when no chametz concern — Israel are holy and practiced guarding le-shem matzah in all matzot from grinding, at least from kneading; see below siman 460 what we wrote.
