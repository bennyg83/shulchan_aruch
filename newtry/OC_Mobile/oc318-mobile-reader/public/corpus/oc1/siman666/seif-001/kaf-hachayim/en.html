(א) [Seif 1] He shall not dismantle his sukkah — for the entire day he is obligated to dwell and study there, and if a meal occurs he must eat inside it (Rashi Sukkah 45a). Therefore even if he finished eating on the seventh in the morning he does not dismantle; but he removes utensils from Mincha onward and prepares the house for the final Yom Tov. If he lacks space to remove utensils and wants to eat in it on Shemini Atzeret, he must reduce at least four by four in the sekhakh to show he sits not for the mitzvah of sukkah, so it not appear he is adding. If he must eat the rest of the day he eats in the sukkah, for the mitzvah applies all seven days. In chutz la'aretz, where they sit also on Shemini Atzeret, when finished eating on Shemini Atzeret he removes utensils; if he lacks space and wants to eat on the ninth he cannot reduce because it is Yom Tov — what distinction can he make? If the sukkah was small so a candle was forbidden on other days, he brings a candle in; if large so a candle was permitted, he brings pots and vessels to show it is invalid and the mitzvah time has passed.
