(S.k. 1) A place, etc. See Tosafos, folio 48a, s.v. 'he reduces,' etc., and this is their language: He reduces it by four by four tefachim, and it is invalid; so Rashi explained. This is surprising, etc., for empty space invalidates with three. They left the matter in astonishment. The Magen Avraham hinted to resolve why Tosafos placed their difficulty on Rashi's explanation; they should have placed their difficulty on the Talmud, which gave a measure of four tefachim, although empty space invalidates with three. According to the words of the Magen Avraham it works well, for the Talmud was not difficult to Tosafos, since one could say the reason is as the Magen Avraham wrote, that for recognition we require four tefachim. But Tosafos' wonder was at Rashi's language, where he wrote, 'he reduces it, etc., and it is invalid,' implying that the reason is because this is the measure to invalidate it; but for the measure of invalidation, three tefachim suffice.
