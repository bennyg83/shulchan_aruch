1) [Se'if 1] A gallery. It is long, and many entrances of upper stories open onto it. Rashi, cited by Beis Yosef. Rabbeinu Yerucham describes a gallery as follows: Houses surround a courtyard, with upper stories above the houses. A gallery runs in front of the entrances to the upper stories, and all the upper stories open onto the gallery just as the houses open onto the courtyard. A ladder in the courtyard leads up to the gallery. All those who live in the upper stories descend by that ladder into the courtyard and leave through the courtyard into the public domain. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2.
2) There. They do not prohibit, etc. This refers to a case in which they did not establish an eruv together. Rather, the courtyard residents established their own eruv, and the gallery residents established their own. This teaches that they are not comparable to a single courtyard in which the residents of one half established a separate eruv and those of the other half established a separate eruv, in which case they prohibit one another. Instead, they are regarded as two courtyards. Beis Yosef.
3) There. They do not prohibit, etc. This discusses a gallery ten handbreadths high. If it is not ten handbreadths high, everything is considered one courtyard, and the gallery residents prohibit carrying for the courtyard residents. Rashi and Tosafos explain the Mishnah this way, and the ruling is obvious. Bach; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1.
4) There. They do not prohibit carrying for the courtyard residents, etc. This applies with respect to utensils that began Shabbos in a house. Regarding utensils that began Shabbos in the courtyard, however, even if they established no eruv at all, it is permitted to carry from the courtyard to the gallery, for they are included among roofs and courtyards, which constitute one domain. Beis Yosef; Tosefes Shabbos, there. See above, at the beginning of siman 372.
