The Levush writes: Ten tefachim high, etc. For if it is not ten tefachim high, the entire area is considered one courtyard and they prohibit the residents of the courtyard, as Tosafot explain.
And they ascend to it by a ladder, etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham describes a balcony as follows: In a courtyard there are houses around it, and above the houses are upper stories. In front of the entrances to the upper stories is a balcony, and all the upper stories open onto the balcony just as the houses open onto the courtyard. There are steps in the courtyard by which one ascends to the balcony, and all those who live in the upper stories descend by the ladder into the courtyard and leave through the courtyard to the public domain.
Utensils, etc. Meaning, where they did not make an eruv.
Even though, etc. The Tur and poskim imply that anything less than ten tefachim is assigned to the courtyard residents if it is four tefachim distant from the balcony or ten tefachim lower than it, because a person does not prohibit his fellow through mere airspace, as written in siman 376, seif 2, according to Magen Avraham. The Bach distinguishes there. Yet I found an even broader ruling in Rabbeinu Yerucham, p. 89, that even if it is ten tefachim above the courtyard it is assigned to the courtyard because it is four tefachim distant from the balcony. This is also implied by Tosafot 87 s.v. Hachi, since it is well known that the halachah follows Rav in matters of prohibition.
