Se'if 1: To stroll, or etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. "a horse," etc. And Beis Yosef inferred from the fact that it says "for free" that strolling is called a need of the moed; and ostensibly that is what the Gemara implies according to our reading: "even a horse that one rides," etc. And if it is for a need of the moed, what is "even"; how is it inferior to all other needs of the moed there?
And to prepare etc. The Rosh there; and we learn it from the preceding case.
And it is permitted etc., even for free. Not like the aforementioned Tosafos and Mordechai, who wrote that it is forbidden; and the Mordechai brings a proof from the aforementioned Yerushalmi in the preceding siman. For our Gemara disagrees with what is stated at the beginning of chapter 3 regarding swimming, that according to everyone etc.; and it is specifically from Eretz Yisrael to chutz la'aretz, as written in siman 531, se'if 4, and likewise one who comes etc.; and this can be dismissed. And see Magen Avraham there, s.k. 6, in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, that nevertheless his coming home was not for strolling; and it is possible that that is what is meant by "he went out without permission," namely that he was forced to return on Chol HaMoed; with this the Yerushalmi that the Rosh cites there is also resolved, and one need not say that he went from Eretz Yisrael to chutz la'aretz. And that is what the Mordechai means, that it is forbidden to go from place to place, for one may not move from dwelling to dwelling because of the exertion, and all the more so to go far away if it is not for food or merchandise as a matter of impending loss; that is to say, as written there: for food, according to everyone etc., so too for profit. And the Rav wrote in the preceding siman, se'if 1, "all the more so to transport etc.," according to his view, for he does not hold like the Mordechai in this; and "from where may one clear out etc." is proof only for transporting one's property; and likewise the "some say," as above.
Even etc. For even without this, even on Shabbos it is permitted, as written in Shabbos 53b.
