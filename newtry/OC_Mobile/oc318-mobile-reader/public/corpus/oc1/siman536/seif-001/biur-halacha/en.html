To stroll. And if it is from Eretz Yisrael to chutz la'aretz, it is forbidden to prepare the needs of riding [Magen Avraham and other Acharonim].
And it is permitted to ride. See Mishnah Berurah. And according to Beis Yosef's reason, it would seem specifically regarding riding, for even on Yom Tov it is only a shvus, and we do not decree this on Chol HaMoed; but regarding machmer, on Shabbos there is a full Torah prohibition of "you shall not do melacha, you and your animal," for Chazal received that this refers to an action done through a person and an animal, namely machmer; ostensibly one should not be lenient when it is without [festival] need. However, when we examine the matter, one may be lenient based on one double-doubt: perhaps the halacha follows the poskim who hold that a person is not commanded regarding resting his animal on Yom Tov, for Yom Tov is not equated to Shabbos except regarding the thirty-nine melachos, and not regarding the positive commandment of resting one's animal and the prohibition of machmer; and as is the view of the gloss above in siman 245, se'if 3; see there in Mishnah Berurah. And what is written in siman 495, se'if 3, that one may not take out a load on an animal on Yom Tov, is only rabbinic, as written in Mishnah Berurah there. And even if we say like the poskim who hold that Yom Tov and Shabbos are equal from the Torah also regarding this, perhaps the halacha follows the Ran in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, who holds that the prohibition of machmer was stated only regarding a public domain, where a person is liable for it when he carries out; for that the Torah forbade also when one takes out a load on an animal, but not in a courtyard (and he disputes the Rashba there, who holds that the prohibition of machmer applies in all cases). If so, according to what the world is accustomed to hold in several matters, namely that we have no Torah-level public domain nowadays, then the prohibition of machmer from the Torah does not apply at all, only rabbinically; and if so we should not forbid it on Chol HaMoed. However, in siman 345 we brought in Biur Halacha the view of several Rishonim who hold that there is a Torah-level public domain even in our time; nevertheless we need not protest those who are lenient in this, for they have authorities on whom to rely.
