One may not set [a hen], etc. Because it is not a need of the moed, nor a matter of impending loss.
One may return her. Since the Mechaber ruled without qualification, it is implied that even if the hen has not yet sat upon them for three days, and the eggs have not been entirely ruined so that they are still fit to sell cheaply, nevertheless it is permitted, for in any case there is a minor loss if one does not return her. And see the Rosh and the Tur, from whose view it is clear that one should be lenient in this only according to their approach that melacha of Chol HaMoed is rabbinic; but if we hold that it is according to the Torah, one should be lenient to return her only if she has already sat upon them for three days, for then the eggs are not fit at all for eating, having begun to form a chick, and it is a matter of impending loss if one does not return her. And if so, according to what we brought above at the beginning of siman 530, that several Rishonim hold that melacha of Chol HaMoed is from the Torah, one should be lenient in this only when he has a loss from several eggs, for then one may rely to be lenient even within three days of her sitting.
Another. Even after three days of her sitting, when they are entirely lost, it is nevertheless forbidden. And even though every matter of impending loss is permitted on Chol HaMoed, one may say that with excessive exertion there is no permit even for a matter of impending loss; and see Biur Halacha.
Within three, etc. For then the return helps, and there is also not so much exertion in returning her; but after three days it is forbidden even if the eggs will be lost, for there is great exertion in returning her, and it is also possible that returning her will not help at all, since her heat has already left her.
