The first one died. One who examines Beis Yosef will see that the one who forbids in this is the Rambam, and according to his view, what is stated in the Mishnah, "and if she died, one sets another," applies only to the fourteenth of Nisan before Pesach. And behold, in Beis Yosef he leans to say that this is also the Tur's view; therefore he did not copy this law regarding our topic. And for all this, Beis Yosef also omitted this leniency in the Shulchan Aruch regarding our topic, and copied it only in siman 468 regarding erev Pesach; and the Rav followed him, copying it explicitly as a prohibition. However, in the Taz and likewise in the Derishah, their view is that the Tur is lenient in this also on Chol HaMoed; and see Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, whose view is that according to the Rif as well it is permitted to set another if she died, also on Chol HaMoed. And I searched and found several Rishonim who were explicitly lenient in this, namely Rabbeinu Chananel in our sugya, and likewise the Rokeach, and Maharam Chalawah in his novellae. And apparently Beis Yosef and the Rama did not see them, for otherwise they would not have ruled stringently without qualification. Therefore it appears that in a place of major loss one may rely to be lenient.
