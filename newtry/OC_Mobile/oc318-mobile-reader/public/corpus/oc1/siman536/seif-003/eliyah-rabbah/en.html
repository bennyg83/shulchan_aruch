One may not breed, etc. Meaning: one may not hold the animal to bring a male upon her; but on a weekday it is permitted even in the manner of a brush in a tube, for he does not entertain [such] thoughts while occupied with his work; and so it is implied in the Gemara and poskim. And to bring them into a barn so that they breed on their own is permitted even on Chol HaMoed. Beis Yosef wrote: even though R' Yehuda disagrees and says that an animal that demands [mating], one may put a male upon her so that she not cool off, the Rif and the Rosh mentioned only the words of the Tanna Kamma alone, and not like what is implied from the words of Hagahos Ashri that the halacha follows R' Yehuda, end quote. And the Bach challenged him, that everyone holds that the halacha follows the Tanna Kamma, and Hagahos Ashri cited R' Yehuda's words only because of the law of bringing them into a barn, end [of Bach]. And this is not clear, for if so, why does the first clause deal with a jenny that demanded [mating], one may breed, etc.? And it is even more astonishing that he did not see in the Agudah and the Ran, chapter "One who turns over," who ruled explicitly that it is permitted regarding a jenny, for it is a matter of impending loss; and it is implied that in their view the Tanna Kamma does not disagree with R' Yehuda, for R' Yehuda speaks specifically of a jenny, and the Tanna Kamma speaks of other animals, regarding which cooling off is not relevant and it is not a matter of impending loss. And Beis Yosef, who copied in the words of R' Yehuda "an animal that demanded," was not precise, for it says explicitly "a jenny that demanded" in Moed Katan folio 12, as Hagahos Ashri wrote; and likewise in Kenesses HaGedolah I saw that he cites the words of R' Yehuda. Also from Beis Yosef it is evident that he did not see the aforementioned poskim; therefore it appears to me to be lenient like Hagahos Ashri.
