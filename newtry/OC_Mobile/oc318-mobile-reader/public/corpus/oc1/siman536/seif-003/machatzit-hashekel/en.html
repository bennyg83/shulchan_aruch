(א) (sk 3) Within, etc. — it is fitting to sell the eggs cheaply. A soft-boiled egg is not entirely repulsive, and a person who is not fastidious will eat it; therefore one must sell it cheaply because one will not find many buyers; unlike within three days of its sitting, when the loss is complete, for it becomes entirely repulsive and impossible to eat at all.

(ב) And all the more it is forbidden to seat another. For in Shulchan Aruch the prohibition of seating another is not explained except within three days of its sitting we only saw minor loss — and it teaches that even after three days of its sitting where the loss is complete, nevertheless seating another is forbidden; for it is all the more from that hen itself — if it is after three days of its escape that it is difficult to return it, therefore returning it is forbidden even after three days of its sitting because of extra exertion — all the more to seat another where there is more exertion. And it is difficult — behold from the words "it is permitted to do complete melacha," etc. — and it appears to me it is not all end quote. And this answer for the prohibition of seating another afterward he brought the words of Shach, which is an answer for the prohibition of returning after three days of escape. However, Magen Avraham concluded end quote and I did not know who is the questioner and who is the answerer. In truth in Rabbeinu Yehudah this reasoning is explained to reconcile the Rambam's view that even after three days of escape the return does not help, as Shach's view; but Magen Avraham's question and answer I did not find who they are. And behold what he challenged — that complete melacha is permitted in davar ha'aved — so it is explained at the beginning of siman 537 that watering an irrigated field was permitted per the conditions explained because it is davar ha'aved; although watering is d'oraisa melacha as explained in siman 356 in Magen Avraham sk 5: "and one who waters seeds is liable for plowing and sowing" — first chapter of Moed Katan — end quote; and although it is explained in siman 537 seif 2 that if there is extra exertion the watering is forbidden even though it is davar ha'aved — meaning because there are two matters: complete melacha and also extra exertion. And the intent of Magen Avraham's challenge is: since in davar ha'aved complete melacha was permitted where there is no exertion, all the more one should permit extra exertion where there is no complete melacha. But if it is difficult to me — behold it is clear in siman 537 seif 12 one does not worm trees, etc., and Magen Avraham wrote in sk 10: one does not worm when one removes, etc., even though the tree is lost — forbidden because it is extra exertion — end quote; and they are the words of Tosafot in Avodah Zarah daf 50b s.v. "and one does not worm," etc.: it deals with a case where it is not lost if delayed until after the moed — otherwise it would be permitted, etc.; for that which in davar ha'aved is permitted on the moed — or even if it is truly lost — Tosafot say it is forbidden on the moed because of extra exertion, as we say in the first chapter of Moed Katan that they forbade watering seeds with kilon water because of extra exertion, although watering with rainwater is permitted because of davar ha'aved — end quote Tosafot. And if so Magen Avraham copied in siman 537 sk 10 the latter answer of Tosafot to be stringent. Behold worming, although it is not complete melacha, nevertheless is forbidden because of extra exertion. Perhaps the questioner Magen Avraham brought here regarding the hen holds the main first answer of Tosafot in Avodah Zarah above — that if it were davar ha'aved worming would be permitted even though there is extra exertion; but Magen Avraham who copied the latter answer — I do not know why he brought the question and answer here regarding the hen.
