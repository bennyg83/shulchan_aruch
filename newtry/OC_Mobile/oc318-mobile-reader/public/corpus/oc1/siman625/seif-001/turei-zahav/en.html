The Tur wrote that Scripture ties the mitzvah of sukkah to the Exodus from Egypt, and so for many mitzvot etc. It appears our master's intent is that he made the mitzvah of sukkah more particular than other mitzvot, and similarly in what he wrote afterward, "and the sukkot that Scripture says" etc., implying a connection to what he wrote before. This is as follows: there is a dispute in the first chapter of Sukkah — R' Akiva holds Scripture literally speaks of actual sukkot when the Omnipresent seated them at the time of the Exodus, and R' Eliezer holds clouds of glory surrounded them. It appears there is a difference in explaining the verse between them: for R' Akiva there is no special quality in those sukkot — if so what remembrance would there be? Rather certainly the remembrance is that then we left Egypt, and the main purpose of our sukkah is to remember the Exodus. But for R' Eliezer it would be remembering another miracle, namely the surrounding clouds of glory, only Scripture tied the mitzvah to the Exodus like other mitzvot such as tzitzit which has Exodus in it — and it is obvious the essence of tzitzit is not to remember Exodus but Scripture attached the mitzvah after Exodus. Our master wants to conclude like R' Eliezer as Rashi explained in Chumash like R' Eliezer, and he wrote there the Raavan in parashat Emor that Rashi's reason appears that divine providence was in those sukkot — and this is our master's intent here that he wrote Scripture tied sukkah to Exodus and many mitzvot — meaning the essence of the mitzvah is not to remember Exodus, for otherwise Scripture should have said "commanded us sukkah to remember Exodus" — rather certainly the mitzvah is for another miracle only Scripture tied this mitzvah to Exodus like many mitzvot where there is only attachment to Exodus, not that this is the reason for the mitzvah. If so, what is the relevance of this attachment? He answered: because it is something we saw with our eyes etc., and there is great feeling here — our Master blessed be He — and His fear should be upon our faces to fulfill the mitzvah. As written in parashat Kedoshim many times "I am the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent" and Rashi explained He wanted to show awe of who decrees. And he asks regarding sukkah because they said it is a light mitzvah — therefore He said do not treat it lightly in your eyes so you know who decrees upon you, that He is the great Power blessed be He. And similarly in the beginning of Avodah Zarah in what Scripture writes Exodus in tzitzit, interest, and weights — "I am He" who distinguished between a drop of firstborn etc. So too here Scripture warned that the mitzvah of sukkah not be light in our eyes, and therefore it fits well after what our master wrote afterward, "and the sukkot — they are clouds of glory" etc., namely like R' Eliezer we mentioned, and this is a mitzvah in its own right not for remembering Exodus like R' Akiva. And afterward he said although we left Egypt in Nissan etc. — for R' Akiva who says the essence is remembering Exodus, it is reasonable this remembrance is all year and He wanted us to remember specifically twice a year, once at Pesach beginning of summer and once in Tishrei beginning of winter; not so for R' Eliezer who says to remember clouds of glory that were for our ancestors — that is not the time of those clouds of glory — therefore our master explained that thereby it appears to all it is the King's mitzvah blessed be He, as explained — very correct.
