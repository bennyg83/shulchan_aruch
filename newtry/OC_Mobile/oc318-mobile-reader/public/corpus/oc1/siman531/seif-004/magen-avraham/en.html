In the hand of a Yisrael. For since he was in distress, he did not shave.
Whom they released on the festival. This is the view of the Rambam; and Bach ruled like the Rosh and the Tur, that specifically they released him before the festival and the days of his nidui were not completed until the festival, for our nidui is not less than seven days; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 334, se'if 1 and se'if 13. But if he did not ask (note) that they release him until the festival, it is not considered ones.
One who vowed. It appears to me that in this the words of the Rif and the Rambam seem correct: even though he could have had it released, he is not called negligent, for it was his intention to fulfill his vow and his neziros.
From overseas. [Meaning] that he did not arrive in a settled place until Chol HaMoed (Beis Yosef; Rabbeinu Yerucham). This implies that if he was on erev Yom Tov in a settled place, it is forbidden to shave on Chol HaMoed. And it appears to me that if he was two or three days before Yom Tov in a settled place, and on erev Yom Tov he was not in a settled place, it is permitted to shave on Chol HaMoed; however, specifically when he comes from overseas, for then the matter becomes public knowledge, but not by another route.
To travel about. But if he went out to earn a living or to see his friend's face, it is permitted, for it is called a mitzvah; see above, siman 248.
