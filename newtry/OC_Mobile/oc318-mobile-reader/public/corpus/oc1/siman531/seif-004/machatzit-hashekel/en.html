(s.k. 4) That they released him, etc. And Bach ruled, etc.: for in the Yerushalmi they raise a difficulty on the Mishnah which states "and one under nidui whom they released on the festival." Whichever way you take it: if they released him before the festival, he should have shaved before the festival, so why was shaving permitted for him on the festival? And if they did not release him before the festival, there is likewise no reason to permit him to shave on the festival, for he should have appeased the beis din to release him before the festival, and he could have shaved before the festival. And they answer that it refers to a case where they released him before the festival, but the thirtieth day from the time and day that they placed him under nidui falls on Yom Tov. And his explanation is: although we hold that whether they placed him under nidui for a monetary matter or for apostasy, they may release him immediately, that is where he has appeased the beis din; but when he has not appeased the beis din, [and] only [then] appeased the beis din: although they may release immediately, nevertheless the release does not take effect until he completes thirty days from the day they placed him under nidui. And of this sort the Mishnah speaks: when he appeased the beis din and the thirtieth day falls on the festival. And so ruled the Rosh and the Tur. But the Rambam did not cite the aforementioned Yerushalmi; and Beis Yosef wrote because in our Gemara this is not mentioned, rather the matter is left without specification, implying even if he could have been released before the festival and did not come to beis din for release until the festival, even so it is called ones and he is permitted to shave on the festival.
And our nidui is not less, etc. And he means to say: although the Yerushalmi establishes it as a case where the thirtieth day is on the festival, that is specifically for them, for a generic nidui of theirs was thirty days; but for us, for whom a generic nidui is seven, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 334, one must say that the seventh day is completed on the festival.
See Yoreh De'ah, siman 334, as should be written. For there these laws are set out. But the Taz there raised a difficulty on Bach: according to this, why is he permitted to shave on the festival when the thirtieth day falls on the festival? Behold, he could have appeased his litigant, and would have been released at once from his nidui before the festival, see there. And according to the dissenting view that Beis Yosef and the Rama in Yoreh De'ah, siman 334, se'if 13, bring, that they never release before thirty days, even if he appeased his litigant, it is fine. And according to those who say that if he appeased his litigant they release immediately, one must say that he was ones, for example that his litigant went overseas and the like, so that he could not appease his litigant; and see there.
