Seif 4, and he had no, etc. There, 17b; see Taz.
And even if he had, etc. Yerushalmi, brought by the Rosh there.
That they permitted him on the festival. Rambam, unlike the Rosh in the name of Yerushalmi. His reason is that our Gemara disagrees, for it says there, 14b, that he went and appeased him, etc. Moreover, it is impossible to establish the Mishnah according to the Yerushalmi's explanation, since we hold that when he appeases his litigant, we permit him immediately, as Rif and Rambam wrote. See Rif there on the statement of Abaye, 16a: "Abaye said, what should he do..." And see Mordechai at the beginning of chapter 3.
And he requested release from his vow on the festival. Rambam, unlike the Yerushalmi; this is learned from the above.
And he did not have, etc. The Gemara, as above.
Provided that, etc. The Gemara there: Rabbi said, "to sail..." And so the Rosh wrote in the name of Raavad, that this is also implied in the Yerushalmi. This requires study, for the Yerushalmi implies the opposite. This is the Yerushalmi's language there: it was taught in the name of Rabbi Yehudah: one who comes from overseas is forbidden to shave. Rabbi Yehudah follows his own view, for Rabbi Yehudah says it is forbidden to set out to the great sea. If so, a kohen who went outside the Land, since he went against the will of the Sages, should be forbidden to shave. One kohen came before R. Chanina; he said to him, etc. Shimon bar Ba, etc. This implies that Rabbi speaks not specifically of outside the Land, and the reason is as written, that it is forbidden to set out to the great sea because of danger. It also implies in the Yerushalmi that the prohibition of leaving the Land applies specifically to a kohen. Shimon bar Ba was also a kohen, as written in the episode of the daughters of Mar Shmuel: "Go tend to..." But our Gemara at the end of Ketubot does not imply this.
