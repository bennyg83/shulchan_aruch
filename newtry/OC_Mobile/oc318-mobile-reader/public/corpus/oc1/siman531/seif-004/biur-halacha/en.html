And these may shave on the Moed, etc. Know that those whom they permitted to shave [may do so] even in the ordinary way, without a change: for in the Mishnah it taught shaving and laundering together as one category; and just as regarding laundering we permit even in its ordinary way, as in siman 534, the same applies regarding shaving. And furthermore from Pesachim 54b, where it says: the tailors, for a layman sews in his ordinary way on Chol HaMoed; the barbers and the launderers, for they are permitted on Chol HaMoed for one who comes from the sea; and according to the first Tanna, the cobblers are forbidden on Yom Tov, even though they too are permitted to change [their mode] on Chol HaMoed, as in the Yerushalmi; and necessarily one does not learn from a change on Chol HaMoed to permit on that account in the ordinary way on Yom Tov. And accordingly it is proven explicitly for our matter that for barbers and launderers they permitted them in the ordinary way (afterward I found in the Chiddushei Ritva on chapter 2 that he holds for the law as we have written). And at first glance it is difficult: behold, a matter that is not food preparation and is for the need of the Moed, we hold that it requires a change; and perhaps because until now they were ones in the matter, the Sages treated them as a davar ha'aved, for which we hold that a davar ha'aved does not need a change. And it still requires further study.
