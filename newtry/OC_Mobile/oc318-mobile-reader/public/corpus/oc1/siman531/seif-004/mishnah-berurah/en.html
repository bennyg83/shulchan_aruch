(7) And he had no free time: for example, that he left during the Moed, or even on erev Yom Tov close to nightfall, so that he had no opportunity to shave while it was still day.
(8) And one who left prison: so too when there was no opportunity to shave on erev Yom Tov while it was still day, as above.
(9) In the hand of a Yisrael: for since he was in distress, he did not shave.
(10) Whom they released on the festival: for beforehand he was forbidden to have a haircut, and therefore he is ones in that he did not shave beforehand; and there are those who disagree on this and hold that where he could have asked and appeased so that they release him before the festival and he did not appease, he is negligent and it is forbidden to shave on the festival. And nevertheless one who is lenient has authorities on whom to rely, since it is a rabbinic matter.
(11) And he requested release from his vow on the festival: and there are those who disagree on this as well, that it is specifically if he did not find a sage to release his vow until within the festival; whereas if he did not wish to seek release beforehand and afterward reconsidered, it is not considered ones. And the view of Magen Avraham is to be lenient in this like the Mechaber, that we do not consider him negligent for not seeking release beforehand, since before the festival it was not at all his intention to release his vow, and afterward he reconsidered.
(12) One who comes from overseas: specifically from overseas, for the matter becomes public knowledge; but by another route, no (Magen Avraham); and see Machatzit HaShekel, that the same applies from another very distant place where the matter becomes public knowledge (and in the Chiddushei Ritva he wrote that whoever comes outside the techum is called as from overseas, even though it is not a distant place; and this requires further study).
(13) On Chol HaMoed: and specifically that he did not arrive in a settled place until Chol HaMoed; for if he arrived on erev Yom Tov in a settled place and arrived at a time when there was still opportunity while day remained to shave, even if he did not reach his home until Chol HaMoed, this is not ones, for he should have shaved on erev Yom Tov in that city. And see Magen Avraham, who wrote that even when he was in a settled place two or three days before the festival, and afterward on erev Yom Tov he was not in a settled place, for example that he then sat on a ship and arrived home on Chol HaMoed, or that he arrived in the city close to nightfall and there was no daytime opportunity to shave, it is also permitted to shave on Chol HaMoed, since on erev Yom Tov itself he was ones.
(14) Provided that he did not leave, etc.: meaning that from Eretz Yisrael to Eretz Yisrael, or from outside the Land to outside the Land, even if he left to travel about, which is not a matter of mitzvah, even so he is permitted to shave when there was no free time to shave while it was still day, since his departure was with permission, for he did no prohibition in this; but from Eretz Yisrael to outside the Land he is permitted only if he left to earn a living or to see his friend's face, which is considered a matter of mitzvah for which it is permitted to leave Eretz Yisrael; whereas for mere traveling about, for which in such a case it is forbidden to leave Eretz Yisrael for outside the Land, they did not permit him to shave.
