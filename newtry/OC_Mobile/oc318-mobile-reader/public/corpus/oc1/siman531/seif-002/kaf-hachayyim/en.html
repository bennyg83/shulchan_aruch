3) [Se'if 2] One may not remove hair during the festival, etc. This decree was enacted so that one would remove it before Yom Tov and not enter the festival unkempt. Magen Avraham, subsection 1.
4) There. One may not remove hair during the festival, etc. This applies both to the hair of the head and to all other body hair. Tosafos, Moed Katan 14a, s.v. u'menudeh; Pischei Olam, subsection 2. See below, se'if 8.
5) There. One may not remove hair during the festival, etc. This is forbidden even through a non-Jew. Whenever the law below permits it, it may be performed even by a Jew. Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah. See below, subsection 13.
6) There. Even if he removed his hair before the festival. The reason is that an observer who sees him removing hair will not know that he also removed it on erev Yom Tov and will say that removing hair during the festival is permitted. Levush.
7) There. Even if he removed his hair, etc. Rabbeinu Tam writes that if one already removed his hair before the festival, he may remove it during the festival. Tur comments that this does not appear to follow from the Talmud. Beis Yosef likewise writes in the name of several halachic authorities that even if one removed his hair before the festival, he may not remove it during the festival. See there. Shulchan Aruch therefore rules here that it is forbidden. Levush, Bach, and the other later authorities rule likewise. Noda BiYehudah, siman 13, writes that one who removed his hair on erev Yom Tov may remove it during Chol HaMoed through a worker who lacks food. Several great authorities already dispute this ruling, as written below, siman 542, se'if 2. See there.
8) There. Even if he removed his hair before the festival. Bathing, however, is permitted, as are all forms of bathing pleasure, because people ordinarily bathe every day. Mordechai, first chapter of Moed Katan, brings a proof from the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah and Rashi there, who writes that for us it is permitted to heat bathhouses on Chol HaMoed. See there. This is also stated in Rashi's commentary to Moed Katan 19b. See there. Ritva likewise writes in his novellae at the beginning of the chapter VeEilu Megalchin that bathing and all work associated with a bathhouse are permitted. See there. Beis Yosef, siman 544, writes in the name of Responsa Rashba that repairing bathhouses is permitted even when the work was deliberately scheduled for the festival. Bathhouses also involve necessities of life because bathing the body prevents boils and sores. See there. Responsa Beis David, siman 305; Shulchan Gavoah, siman 534, subsection 7; Maharikash in this siman; Erech HaShulchan, subsection 2; Ruach Chaim, subsection 3, write likewise. See also below, at the end of this siman, in the gloss.
