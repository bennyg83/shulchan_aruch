A minor. See Baer Heitev; and see Gan HaMelech, who wrote as follows: And it is permitted to defer the celebration of the haircut that they make for a minor, in order to set the joy in the Moed, and there is also a mitzvah in this, etc., see there. And the matter of the joy is also a custom of the Land of Israel, that they make a celebration at a minor's first haircut, through which they initiate him in the mitzvah of having peyos of the head; and see Pri Etz Chaim, Gate of Sefiras HaOmer, who wrote regarding those who go to the grave of Rashbi and R' Elazar his son in Meron on Lag BaOmer, that he saw his master the Arizal, who went there to cut his son's hair amid feast and joy in those days, see there.
(In Shulchan Aruch se'if 7) But if [the seventh day] falls, etc. And see Kenesses HaGedolah: one whose thirty days for his father were completed on the Moed and whose fellows rebuked him is forbidden to shave on the Moed; and in Halachos Ketanos, siman 285, he wrote to permit in this; and below, siman 545, Baer Heitev brought it, and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah, and so wrote R' Yaakov in the name of Shaarei Yeshuah (manuscript); but in Noda BiYehuda, siman 14, his view is to forbid, and he wrote that so it is explicit in Rashi; and in Machatzit HaShekel he wrote to reconcile the words of Rashi, that there is no decisive proof from this; and nevertheless one should not be lenient except for a great need, for so in Sefer Beis David, siman 312, it appears that he forbids, see there. And see Noda BiYehuda there, who wrote that one could say that even if he is still within the thirty days for his father on erev ha-regel, but the measure for ge'arah has arrived and his fellows rebuked him, there is room to permit cutting hair on erev ha-regel; but he wrote that he writes only for the theoretical law and not for practice, see there; and therefore one should also be stringent in this when there is no great need. And see below, siman 548, what is written there in the name of Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah.
