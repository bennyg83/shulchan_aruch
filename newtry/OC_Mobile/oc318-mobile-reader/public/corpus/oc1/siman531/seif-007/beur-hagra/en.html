Se'if 7, and his practice is to shave. See Rif and Rosh at the end of chapter 19 of Shabbos; and they practiced thus according to what is written in Parashas Behaalosecha, "and he shall pass a razor," and see Rashi there, s.v. "and he shall pass," etc.; and if so, he should be included in what the Mishnah says, "the nazir and the metzora," etc.; and they say in the Gemara even if they had free time, etc.; and so too here, that they should not delay, for they do not include him for anything of sanctity until he shaves there; and see Yoreh De'ah, siman 268, se'if 2 in the gloss, "and some say," etc.
Every person is permitted. The wording of the Rambam; and so wrote the Rif; and Maggid Mishneh wrote that although they said specifically a mustache that impedes, or one who is fastidious, the Rambam holds that it refers to mourning and not to Chol HaMoed; and his words require further study, for behold regarding mourning the Rambam wrote in chapter 6 that it is entirely forbidden; and therefore one must say that he holds it refers to Chol HaMoed; and such is the view of the Tur here, see there; and so ruled Nimukei Yosef and other poskim; and like the view of the Raavad from the Tosefta; and so it appears in the Yerushalmi, which said a mustache is like cutting nails for every matter, etc.; and see Toras HaAdam; and in Beis Yosef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 390; and in Kesef Mishneh; and Beis Yosef wrote that the Rambam's reading is like our reading in the Gemara, and he explains not like R' Ami, as the Rosh wrote; and his words require further study, for according to our reading R' Ami explains his words; and furthermore Ravina, who is a later authority, holds like him, that specifically before Abaye and Rava the halacha is not like him; granted, according to the reading of Bahag and Raavad, Ravina said like everyone [else]; and see Yoreh De'ah, siman 390, which explains like R' Ami; and the Rif's words can be explained as Maggid Mishneh explained: that what they said regarding a mustache and a remnant that impedes was said regarding mourning, but on Chol HaMoed it is permitted in every respect, as written regarding cutting nails, and that applies in the days, etc.; and in the Yerushalmi, mustache and cutting nails: there is a beraisa that teaches on the festival it is permitted and in mourning forbidden, etc.; Rav said a mustache is like cutting nails for every matter; and with this the words of Shulchan Aruch likewise do not contradict; but the Rambam's words require further study. And after consideration it appears to me that the essence is that the Rambam explains like the Aruch, entry "zavit," and this is its language: a mustache from corner to corner; a remnant all the more so impedes; meaning: a mustache made from corner to corner, that is, if all of it needs repair it is permitted, but part of it not; a remnant all the more so impedes; meaning: if he made his mustache and left a part unfinished, it is permitted to take it off, because it impedes him in his eating and the like, and therefore it is permitted, end quote. And accordingly, what R' Ami said, "a mustache all the more so impedes," means even if he never shaved at all, it always impedes, and it is always permitted; and he ruled like R' Ami, and as the Raavad wrote that he is a later authority; and even the Rosh admits this, that in matters of the Scribes one follows the more lenient; and this is the view of Rav Alexsandri, not like the Rosh there, see there. And in any case the Rambam's view is like what was said last.
Even, etc. As above in se'if 6, and as written in siman 534, se'if 1; and "and publicly," etc.: Nimukei Yosef wrote specifically regarding linen garments, which are recognizable to all, unlike the Mordechai; and the Hagahos Maimuni there answers Beis Yosef's difficulty, and one need not resort to what was forced there in the gloss; and that which is permitted, etc.; and see siman 533, se'if 4 in the gloss, "and it is permitted," etc., when it is recognizable; and not like what is written at the beginning of siman 534.
And it is permitted to shampoo, etc. For even shaving is permitted by the law, [and was forbidden] only so that one not enter, etc.; which is not so in a case like this; and more than this: even heating a bathhouse is permitted, as written at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, 21b; all the more so a case like this; and since there is in it no [concern] of "so that he not enter," etc.; and a proof is that they permitted one who comes from overseas and the like: thus there is in it no [issue] of melacha.
