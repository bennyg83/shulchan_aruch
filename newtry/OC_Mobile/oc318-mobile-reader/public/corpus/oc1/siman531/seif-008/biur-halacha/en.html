Every person is permitted to trim the mustache. See Magen Avraham, who proved that at the place of a wound on the hand, even when it does not reach pikuach nefesh, it is permitted to remove the hair; and see Machatzit HaShekel, and it implies at first glance from him that if this was on his head it is forbidden to remove the hair; and see Chayei Adam, who brought a proof from Tosefta Pesachim chapter 3 that even on his head it is permitted (for its language there is: one upon whom a wound arose on his head is permitted to cut hair on the Moed); and according to how Minchas Bikkurim explains there, the intent of the beraisa is where he was healed on the Moed, and therefore it is permitted because he could not shave beforehand on account of the wound; and if so, there is no proof at all for the words of Chayei Adam. And in passing I will remark somewhat regarding Magen Avraham's difficulty on the words of Likkutei Pardes, from which it implies that when it does not reach pikuach nefesh it is forbidden, and this is not so according to what we hold that even a mustache that does not impede eating is permitted; and in my humble opinion, Likkutei Pardes, who wrote of hair at the place of bloodletting like "rivda de-kusilta," is speaking of bloodletting called Köpfe setzen, for there too the custom is to remove the hair first from that place and afterward to strike that place with cupping; and therefore he permits only because it reaches pikuach nefesh; and from the Tosefta that Chayei Adam brought as a proof to be lenient, it already is not [proof], for it is possible its explanation is as Minchas Bikkurim explains, as above. And in any case, where he could have removed the hair before Yom Tov, it is possible one should not be lenient for a boil on his head; and what we brought in the name of Orach Chaim to be lenient regarding Köpfe setzen, it is possible he speaks of a case where one need not shave the hair first, or that the necessity arose for him on Chol HaMoed, or that he holds like Likkutei Pardes that this reaches pikuach nefesh; and aside from all this, where he needs this so that the illness not weigh heavily upon him, it is already set out in siman 532 that any medical treatment is permitted on Chol HaMoed, and even for a sick person whose life is not in danger, as set out there in Mishnah Berurah; and the same applies where he has a boil on his head and his whole body suffers on account of this: he is also considered like a sick person, as above in siman 328, and it is permitted to do medical treatment for him.
