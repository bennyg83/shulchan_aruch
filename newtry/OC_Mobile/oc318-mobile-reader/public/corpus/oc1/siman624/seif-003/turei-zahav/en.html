And one does not recite the blessing over the spices. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote: However, in Responsa Maharil, siman 34, he wrote to recite the blessing over the spices when it falls on Shabbos, and so wrote Abudraham. This also seems to me primary in practice, since the Mordechai ruled to recite the blessing over the spices according to the custom of Rabbeinu Gershom Me'or HaGolah even when it falls during the week. Although in that case we do not follow him, nevertheless when it falls on Shabbos it is proper to follow his practice, since many hold this way, and so ruled our teacher Rabbi Shlomo Luria. It also seems to me that the rule that in doubtful blessings we are lenient does not apply here, since he derives benefit from the spices. End quote. Seemingly one could reject what he wrote, 'since he derives benefit,' for nevertheless there is an interruption here between the blessing of Borei Peri HaGafen and the blessing of Hamavdil if the spices are not necessary. Nevertheless, it seems better to satisfy all opinions than to be concerned for an interruption. A proof is from the words of the Terumas HaDeshen, siman 142, regarding the tekiah-shevarim-teruah-tekiah blown in the order of the blessings, for with this one satisfies all possibilities, namely either tekiah-teruah-tekiah or tekiah-shevarim-tekiah, and we are not concerned for an interruption: if tekiah-shevarim-tekiah is primary, the teruah is an interruption, and if tekiah-teruah-tekiah is primary, the shevarim are an interruption. Since in any case he has fulfilled his obligation, we are not concerned for an interruption, even though the main shofar blasts are those sounded over the order of the blessings. Here too, since he fulfills the obligation whichever way it is, we are not concerned for an interruption.
