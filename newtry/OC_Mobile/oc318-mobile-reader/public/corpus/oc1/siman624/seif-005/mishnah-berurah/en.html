(10) That one does not recite the blessing on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, etc. - The reason we are lenient in this on Motza'ei Shabbos is that he recites the blessing over the added flame of permitted fire that was added to the Jew's candle, and not over the source of the fire that he drew from the non-Jew's candle. But this added flame never existed on Yom Kippur; it was born only now, and is similar to fire that comes from wood and stones, over which it is forbidden to recite the blessing on Motza'ei Yom Kippur. See the Magen Avraham, for according to this, even regarding fire that emerges from wood and stones one should not recite the blessing even over another candle lit from it, unlike the 'some say' opinion in se'if 4.
(11) Over a synagogue candle - Meaning, when it was lit from before Yom Kippur and burned permissibly, it is literally considered to have rested.
(12) That one should not make havdalah over it - Because it was made only for the honor of the day and not to illuminate, and it is explained in siman 298 that one does not recite the blessing over this. The first opinion holds that they are made to illuminate, and it is better to make havdalah over this than over another candle lit from it, for that is not so fully called a fire that rested, since it did not exist on Yom Kippur.
(13) And the correct practice is to make havdalah over both together - Meaning, through this he certainly fulfills his obligation. The same applies if he had one candle in his home that was lit from before Yom Kippur: he should light another one from it and recite the blessing over both together. For that candle in his home alone is also not sufficiently fit for the blessing, since perhaps it too was made only for the honor of the day [Chayei Adam]. From Derech HaChayim it implies that it is better initially for him to leave in his house a candle lit from Erev Yom Kippur that will burn until Motza'ei Yom Kippur, in order to recite the blessing over it. He thereby satisfies all opinions, since he placed it initially for this purpose.
(14) Over the synagogue candle itself alone - If it is impossible to make havdalah over both, it is better to make havdalah over another candle lit from it alone than over the synagogue candle alone. Nevertheless, if he transgressed and recited the blessing over the synagogue lights alone, he does not need to go back and recite a blessing over another candle, for our candles are also made to illuminate, since we pray by their light. One does not fast from Yom Kippur until Sukkos even for the day on which his father or mother died, for these are days of joy, when they dedicated the altar in the days of Shlomo. On the day after Yom Kippur one rises early to go to the synagogue, so that it should not appear, Heaven forbid, that we humbled ourselves before Him only at the time when we needed to request judgment in our favor.
(15) For it is somewhat of a Yom Tov - As stated in the Midrash, that a heavenly voice goes out on Motza'ei Yom Kippur and says, 'Go, eat with joy,' etc.
(16) To make two days of Yom Kippur - Because of the doubt of the day. Nevertheless, they should pray only the weekday prayer and put on tefillin. However, they may say piyyutim and selichos as they wish, and even this they should not say in the middle of the prayer, but only after Shemoneh Esrei. They should also not read from the Torah, even 'Vayechal.' When Yom Kippur falls on Thursday, others are forbidden to prepare Shabbos needs for them on Friday, which is their Yom Kippur; rather, they should eat with others who did not increase the preparation for them. Some say that one who did this once, making two days, cannot withdraw and must do so his whole life, because it is as though he accepted it upon himself as a vow.
(17) And this can be annulled - That is, if he regrets this and does not want to do so, he may have it annulled before three people through an opening and regret. If he explicitly stipulated that he was doing so only for this year, and was not accepting upon himself to do so every year, he does not need annulment. What the Rema wrote, that one should not practice this stringency, is because in truth according to the law there is no concern, since we are expert in the fixing of the month. We observe two days only because of the custom practiced by our fathers, and regarding Yom Kippur our fathers did not practice it; if so, why should we practice it?
(18) And not Tzidkascha Tzedek, etc. - And not the chapters, and not Shir HaMa'alos. But 'Lamnatze'ach' and 'Kel Erech Apayim' are said. There are places where on Erev Sukkos they do not say 'Lamnatze'ach,' but they do say 'Kel Erech Apayim.'
(19) They begin - And on the next day he should make all of it, as written in siman 625.
