A Jew who kindled [a flame] from a non-Jew — we do not recite a blessing over it on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, even though on Motza'ei Shabbos we do recite a blessing over it; for on Motza'ei Yom Kippur we recite a blessing only over a flame that rested [i.e., was burning] while it was actually still day, or over a flame that was kindled from it. And so is the custom, to kindle from the lamps of the synagogue. However, even if it was kindled on Yom Kippur, if it was kindled permissibly — such as for a sick person — one may recite a blessing over it. {Rama: Some say to make Havdalah over a synagogue lamp (Maggid; Avudraham; Or Zarua); and some say not to make Havdalah over it, but rather to kindle another lamp from it; and the correct [practice] is to make Havdalah over both together — that is, to kindle one lamp from the synagogue lamp — and not to make Havdalah over the synagogue lamp itself alone [Maharil]. And the other laws of the [Havdalah] lamp, see above, siman 298. And we eat and rejoice on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, for it is somewhat of a Yom Tov (Tur; Mahari Weil; Or Zarua). Some are stringent to observe two days of Yom Kippur; but there is annulment for this [vow], and one should not conduct oneself with this stringency, for there is concern that it may lead to danger [Or Zarua]. One who fasts a ta'anis chalom on the day after Yom Kippur need not fast [it] all his days [Minhagim]. We do not say supplications nor "Tzidkascha" from Yom Kippur until Sukkos; and the meticulous begin immediately on Motza'ei Yom Kippur with the building of the sukkah, in order to go from mitzvah to mitzvah [Maharil; Minhagim; Hagahos Maimoni, chapter 2 of Berachos; Mahari Weil].}
