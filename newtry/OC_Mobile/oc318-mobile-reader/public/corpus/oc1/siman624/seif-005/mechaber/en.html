A Jew who lit from a non-Jew's fire — we do not bless on it at Motzaei Yom Kippur even though at Motzaei Shabbat we bless on it, because we bless at Motzaei Yom Kippur only on fire that rested from before Yom Kippur itself, or on fire lit from it; and so is the custom to light from the synagogue lamps. However, even if it was lit on Yom Kippur, if lit permissibly such as for a sick person, one may bless on it.Some say to make Havdalah on the synagogue candle (Maggid, Abudraham, and Or Zarua); some say not to make Havdalah on it but to light another candle from it; the correct practice is to make Havdalah on both together — meaning light one candle from the synagogue candle and not make Havdalah on the synagogue candle alone (Maharil). Other candle laws see above siman 298. We eat and rejoice at Motzaei Yom Kippur for it is somewhat a festival day (Tur and) Mahariv and Or Zarua). Some are strict to observe two days of Yom Kippur; there is permission for this and one should not follow this stringency lest one come to danger (Or Zarua). One who fasted a dream fast for the day after Yom Kippur need not fast all his days (Minhagim). We do not say tachanun nor Tzidkatcha from Yom Kippur until Sukkot; the meticulous begin immediately at Motzaei Yom Kippur to build the sukkah to go from mitzvah to mitzvah (Maharil, Minhagim, Hagahot Maimoniyyot chapter 2 of Berakhot, Mahariv).]:
