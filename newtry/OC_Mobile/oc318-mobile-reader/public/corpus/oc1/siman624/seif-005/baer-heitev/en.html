Two days. This is the language of the Maharil in responsum siman 212: Those who observe two days of Yom Kippur should not alter the prayers at all. Even if there are ten or one hundred of them, they should pray only the weekday prayer, for otherwise it is a case of 'lo tisgodedu,' forming separate factions. The Magen Avraham wrote: It seems to me that they should also put on tefillin for this same reason. However, they may say piyyutim and selichos as they wish, and the Thirteen Attributes as one who is reading from the Torah. The Magen Avraham wrote: Nevertheless, it seems to me that they should not say them in the middle of the prayer, but only after Shemoneh Esrei. Nor should they read from the Torah, even 'Vayechal.' When Yom Kippur falls on Thursday, it is forbidden to prepare Shabbos needs for them on Friday, which is their Yom Kippur; rather, they should eat together with others who did not increase the preparation for them. See the Magen Avraham there, who concludes that one should not make two days of Yom Kippur, for when he makes the second day Yom Kippur, then the previous day was Erev Yom Kippur, when it is forbidden to fast. If so, how did he fast? The two practices contradict one another. Therefore one should not act this way, and certainly according to our custom, that we do not fast between Yom Kippur and Sukkos. In Likkutei Pardes it implies that they would not fast at night, but only by day.
Supplications. One does not fast on these days, even for a yahrzeit, because they are days of joy, when they dedicated the altar in the days of Shlomo. Maharil. One recites Kiddush Levanah on Motza'ei Yom Kippur; see siman 426, se'if 2. On the day after Yom Kippur one rises early to go to the synagogue. Minhagim.
