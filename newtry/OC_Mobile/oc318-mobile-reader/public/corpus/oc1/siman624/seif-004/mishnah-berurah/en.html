(6) One recites the blessing over the fire - Because his benefit from it ceased on that day, for the entire day they could not use it, and now it became permitted to him.
(7) On Motza'ei Yom Kippur over a fire that they produced, etc. - The same applies when they produced it from sulfur wood [called shvebelich] and anything similar. What distinguishes Yom Kippur from Shabbos is that the reason we recite the blessing over fire on Motza'ei Shabbos is only as a remembrance that fire was created on Motza'ei Shabbos, when Adam HaRishon struck stones against each other on Motza'ei Shabbos and produced fire. Therefore it is permitted to recite the blessing over this kind of fire, which they produced now. But on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, the blessing over fire is to indicate that this day was holier than other Yamim Tovim, and lighting fire was forbidden on it, and now it has become permitted; therefore the fire is a new matter over which one recites a blessing. This applies only to a fire that rested, meaning that it existed in the world on Yom Kippur during the time of rest, and benefit from it ceased on that day and now became permitted to him. It does not apply to this fire, which did not exist at all on Yom Kippur. However, if Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, according to the law it is permitted to recite the blessing on Motza'ei Shabbos over this fire, but the common custom is to be stringent. In any case, regarding a candle lit from that fire, one may certainly be lenient.
(8) Now from the stones - This excludes a case where they produced it yesterday from the stones; that is certainly permitted, since it rested on Yom Kippur.
(9) From the first pillar onward - That is, they lit one candle from that flame. Their reasoning is that this too is called a fire that rested, and not a newly created fire.
