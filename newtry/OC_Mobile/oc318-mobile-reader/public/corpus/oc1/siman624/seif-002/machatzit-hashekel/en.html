(Se'if katan 2) And one does not, etc. Because we require a fire that rested. So it is in Pesachim 54a, that specifically on Motza'ei Shabbos one recites the blessing even on a fire newly produced from wood and stones. The reason on Motza'ei Shabbos is that then was the beginning of the creation of our fire, as stated there in Pesachim: On Motza'ei Shabbos, the Holy One, blessed be He, prepared two stones for Adam HaRishon, and he struck them against each other and fire emerged, etc. Therefore one recites the blessing also over a fire now produced from wood and stones, for it resembles its initial creation. This is not so on Motza'ei Yom Kippur, where this reason does not apply. Rather, the reason is that benefit from fire ceased for him on Yom Kippur and now became permitted to him. This does not apply to a fire newly produced now, but only to a lamp that was burning from Erev Yom Kippur, or was lit on Erev Yom Kippur permissibly for a woman in childbirth or for a dangerously ill person, even by a Jew; and where there is no danger, only by a non-Jew. For where it was lit in prohibition, even on Motza'ei Shabbos one may not recite the blessing over it, since it was made through prohibition, as written in siman 298. In such a case the idea of resting applies. But when one produces fire now on Motza'ei Yom Kippur from wood, the idea that it rested does not apply.
