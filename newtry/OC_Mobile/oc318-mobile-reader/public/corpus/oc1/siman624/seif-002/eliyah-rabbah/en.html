[2] And even if, etc. The Bach ruled that on Motza'ei Shabbos one should recite the blessing, and there is no blessing in vain here since one derives benefit. So too ruled Tosafos, the Taz, and the Magen Avraham. It seems that for himself a person may act this way, but not protest against or instruct the congregation to do so, since they have already practiced this way on the basis of the Shulchan Aruch, the Levush, and the Minhagim. So too, Mateh Moshe and Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote that this is the widespread custom. Furthermore, the Kol Bo cited by the Beis Yosef brought the opinion of those who disagree and concluded, 'And so we do,' which shows that one should not cancel the custom in this matter. In addition, the Taz wrote that there is an interruption between the blessing of Borei Peri HaGafen and the blessing of Hamavdil when the spices are not necessary. Although he wrote that it is better to satisfy all opinions than to be concerned for an interruption, this is necessarily not correct; for if it is an interruption, the blessing of Borei Peri HaGafen is in vain, and the drinking of the wine is also without a blessing. The proof that the Taz brought from the tashrat blasts recited in the standing prayer, where we blow three forms, for if tekiah-teruah-tekiah is primary the shevarim are an interruption, etc., is no proof at all, because there he has already fulfilled his obligation with the blasts while sitting, as he himself wrote. Nor should one bring proof by saying that in the seated tashrat, where either teruah or shevarim is an interruption between the blessing and the blowing, nevertheless we proceed, because there the Sages instituted it that way because of the doubt, and therefore the blessing 'Who commanded us' applies. Furthermore, the tekiah is certainly the beginning, and whenever one has begun the mitzvah we rule that there is not so much of an interruption, as with the Torah reading in siman 140. But with spices, the beginning after the blessing of Borei Peri HaGafen is immediately the blessing over the wine, and if spices are unnecessary it becomes in vain. Therefore our custom should not be cancelled at all.
