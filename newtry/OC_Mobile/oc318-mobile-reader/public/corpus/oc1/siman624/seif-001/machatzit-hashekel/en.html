The Magen Avraham wrote: There are places, etc. And further: for in kiddush, etc., and even so we do not decree with regard to children. The reason we do not decree is written by the Magen Avraham in siman 269 in the name of the Rashba. First, anything that is essentially permitted, only it is at a time when it is forbidden, and this is the child's ordinary way, we give to the child and do not decree. Second, the primary institution of havdalah in the synagogue was initially made for guests, so that they would fulfill their obligation with it, and the guests would drink the cup. Only now, when there are no guests, the enactment remains; and so that there should not be disgrace to the cup, it is given to children to drink. If there are guests, it is not given to a child, but to the guests. Therefore it is not a fixed matter for children, and regarding anything not fixed we do not decree lest the child come to be drawn after it. Similar to this is explained above in siman 559 regarding a circumcision that falls on Tishah B'Av, where the cup is given to a child to drink because it is not a fixed matter; see there. Although regarding a circumcision on Yom Kippur we do not give the cup to children, as written in siman 621 and in the Magen Avraham, siman 559, see there, that is because on Yom Kippur itself one must be more stringent than on Tishah B'Av. But on Motza'ei Yom Kippur one should not be more stringent than on Tishah B'Av.
And it seems to me that it is permitted, etc. Before he makes havdalah. Above in siman 491, the Magen Avraham left unresolved whether it is permitted to eat chametz on Motza'ei Pesach before making havdalah. One must distinguish: that applies specifically to washing and the other afflictions like it, but eating and drinking are forbidden even on Motza'ei Shabbos before havdalah. If he has no cup, see siman 296.
