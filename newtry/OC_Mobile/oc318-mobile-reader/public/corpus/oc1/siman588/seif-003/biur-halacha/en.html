And if two blew as one the entire series.. See what is written in the Mishnah Berurah regarding if three blew together, each one one series, and he intended with this to fulfill all of them, and that is from the Ritva, and for the reason that two sounds are distinguished; and the latter "some say" is also from the Ritva; and the Rashba likewise brought this view in his novellae on the sugya of nine tekios in the first answer. And it appears to my humble opinion that according to this view, if he intended to fulfill all of them, he has not even fulfilled one; and when he said "one of them," his intent is where he intended only for one, then he has fulfilled, because where it is dear to him and he sets his mind, the second sound that he does not desire does not prevent him from hearing; which is not so where he needs both sounds; he has fulfilled with neither of them, for it is impossible to hear two sounds well. And even though in the sugya there it is proven that one who hears shofar and trumpets together fulfills, and even though he must also hear the trumpets; one must say that the trumpets are not the primary obligation independently, and are only of the trappings of the shofar, and even if he did not hear the sound of the trumpets well we also do not care; and so I found the Ritva on folio 27 who likewise wrote as our words, and these are his words: for there one needs only to hear one sound, namely the shofar, for the mitzvah of the day is with the shofar, and therefore they are properly distinguished so that he can intend for the sound of the shofar that he needs to hear amidst the sounds of the trumpets; and he concludes there: but to hear both of them actually when he needs them (even from two people they are not distinguished, as we will say below) end quote. And certainly he intended the sugya of "he heard nine tekios," from which we copied his words; and accordingly, when intending for both of them he has not fulfilled at all according to this view; and the Ritva already wrote that one should be concerned to be stringent.
