(s.k. 3) And some say specifically etc. And specifically regarding a blower do we say etc. that if he blew two teruos one after the other it is an interruption.
For Rabbeinu Tam and the Rashba etc. Another sound etc. (regarding that which we say at the end of Rosh Hashanah: R' Abbahu enacted in Caesarea that they blow tekiah, shevarim, teruah, tekiah) for he was in doubt what a teruah is: whether moans, namely shevarim, or wails, namely teruah, and he does both. Rav Aveira challenged: perhaps it is moans, and the teruah interrupts between the shevarim and the final tekiah. And the Ran asked: what of it; did not R' Yochanan say that one who heard nine tekios in a day has fulfilled, even though there is interruption between them? And Rabbeinu Tam answered, and so the Rashba holds for one answer: granted that after the fact he has fulfilled, nevertheless ideally one should not interrupt. And the Ramban answered: granted that waiting is not an interruption; nevertheless if he blows between them a tekiah for the sake of the mitzvah it is an interruption; and we hold like the Ramban below in siman 590. If so, according to Rabbeinu Tam and the Rashba, even though he heard another sound for the sake of the mitzvah, nevertheless he has fulfilled, for this addresses Rav Aveira's challenge that the teruah blown out of doubt is an interruption; and even so he concludes that specifically ideally [one should not], but after the fact he has fulfilled, like one who heard nine tekios, as above, behold, even another sound, namely a teruah that is irrelevant if it is moans, nevertheless after the fact is not an interruption.
And granted that we hold like the Ramban that it is an interruption (that is specifically regarding a blower, or even a hearer if he heard another sound; but a hearer who heard an appropriate sound twice) for example two teruos one after the other; we do not stringently say that it is an interruption.
But in the Ran it is implied etc. For if not so he raises no challenge at all, namely the aforementioned challenge against Rav Aveira from "he heard nine tekios." And what is the challenge if there is a reason to distinguish between hearer and blower, for there, "he heard nine tekios" deals with a hearer, and one may say it is not an interruption; but Rav Aveira challenges R' Abbahu: how does the blower himself fulfill, for for a blower who performs an act it is an interruption? Must be that the Ran does not hold to distinguish between hearer and blower. And see the Taz who disputes the Levush.
