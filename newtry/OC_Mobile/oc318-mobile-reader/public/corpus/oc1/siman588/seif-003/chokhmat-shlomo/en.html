(There, se'if 3) He heard nine tekios from nine people who all blew as one; he has not fulfilled etc. Note: Here there are variant readings in the Talmud: some read "he has fulfilled," and there is a version that he has not fulfilled. And in my humble opinion the essential [reading] is like that version that he has not fulfilled, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote; and it is compelled in the Talmud from what we read there in tractate Rosh Hashanah, folio 34: R' Yochanan said, one who heard nine tekios from nine people in nine hours of the day has fulfilled etc. and did R' Yochanan say thus, for R' Yochanan said regarding Hallel and Megillah: once he waited long enough to complete all of it, he returns to the beginning etc.? And it is very difficult for me: what does he challenge according to those who hold (brought in the Shulchan Aruch, Hilchos Krias Shema, siman 64) that waiting long enough to complete all of it [causing] return to the beginning is only when he waited because of coercion, but when not because of coercion he does not return, if so what does he challenge: perhaps R' Yochanan here deals with interruption not because of coercion, therefore he has fulfilled? And this is a great wonder according to that view, and I have not seen anyone who noticed this; therefore it appears to answer thus: the reason of the view that distinguishes between waiting because of coercion or not must perforce be the reason that it resembles what we hold; whatever is fit for kneading, kneading does not prevent it, and what is not fit for kneading, kneading prevents it; so too here, if it was not under coercion and it was fit to be together, therefore even though it is not together it also helps; but where it was under coercion and not fit for kneading, kneading prevents it; and therefore it fits well there; but regarding tekios, since he heard nine tekios from nine people as one he has not fulfilled by force of there not being a plain sound before it and a plain sound after it, and why should we not say: behold it is fit for kneading and fit for them to be heard one after another? It is proven from this that regarding plain before it and plain after it, being fit for kneading does not help; rather we require that it actually be before him and after him; and if so here too, if there was interruption of another matter, this is not within "before it and after it." Only granted according to the view that waiting long enough to complete all of it does not [require] return even where it was under coercion and not fit for kneading; perforce according to him waiting is not considered an interruption by itself; if so automatically it is also considered "before it and after it"; but according to the view that waiting long enough to complete all of it [requires] return to its beginning (perforce this is considered an interruption; if so, granted that where it was not under coercion it is not an interruption) that is for another reason, that it is fit for kneading; but if so regarding tekios, since he heard from nine people as one he has not fulfilled, for we require before it and after it, and behold it is fit for kneading, and perforce regarding before it and after it being fit for kneading does not help, and we require actually before it and after it; if so where there is interruption it is not actually before it and after it, and being fit for kneading does not help on this; and he challenges well. Therefore the matters compel that we read here "he has not fulfilled" for if the reading were "he has fulfilled," our difficulty stands in its place: what does he challenge? Therefore in any case for those who hold there that specifically through coercion he has not fulfilled, this reading is compelled; and even for those who hold that in every case he has not fulfilled, nevertheless our difficulty is hard, since the Talmud wants to prove from this that in every case of waiting he does not return, and if so, whence do we know this: perhaps in truth there is a distinction between where it is fit for kneading or not, as we hold in several places, and we may say that what R' Yochanan said that he returns is where it was not possible at all, but where possible he does not return, and what does he challenge? And perforce as above; and it is proven that this reading is essential; and study carefully, for it is a great proof with God's help; and see in my work on Orach Chayim of the year 5601, folio 6a, in siman 587, se'if 5, what is written there on this (see there and study); and see the Taz, s.k. 5 here; and similarly I wrote in Yoreh De'ah, siman 117; and see in my new work on Yoreh De'ah what is written there (a great wonder on the Taz from the Talmud of Kesubos) see there and study carefully.
