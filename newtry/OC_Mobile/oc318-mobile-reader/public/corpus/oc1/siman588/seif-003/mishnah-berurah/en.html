(9) That they all blew as one etc. That is, one did a tekiah and the second a teruah and the third the second tekiah, and so all of them; and one does not fulfill with this, as he concludes "for there is no plain sound etc.," for we require that there be a teruah in the middle and before it and after it a tekiah, and not that all the sounds be at once; and he must blow anew; nevertheless he has not lost the blessing if he did not interrupt with speech. And there is one who says that at least he has fulfilled one tekiah and must complete upon it eight sounds from the teruah onward; and some say that even one tekiah he has not fulfilled, and he must begin anew all nine sounds.
(10) And even if one blew with trumpets, Even though the sounds are different.
(11) He has fulfilled etc. And even though in every place we say that two sounds are not distinguished (see above, siman 141), this case is different, for it is a time-bound mitzvah and is dear to him, and he sets his mind to hear well. And where three people blew at once, each one three sounds; some say he has fulfilled with his hearing because of nine sounds, since two sounds are distinguished regarding shofar; and because he heard all three Tarat series at once there is no concern, for we are particular only that he hear a tekiah before it and a tekiah after it and a teruah in the middle, and that he heard; and likewise the same law applies for us who blow Tashrat, Tasht, and Tarat: if he heard them from three people at once, each blowing a series, he fulfills all the blowings by hearing them at once. And some say that even regarding shofar two sounds are not distinguished so as to fulfill with both of them, and he has fulfilled only with one of them; and it is proper to be stringent like this view; and see the explanation of this view in the Biur Halacha.
