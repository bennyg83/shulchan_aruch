In the Taz, s.k. 4. A single tekiah, half of it for this one etc. Apparently it appears that he is dealing with a case where this one intended his heart only for the beginning of the tekiah and that one only for its end, and they did not fulfill even though each alone has the measure of a tekiah, for we do not split [it]; and so wrote Peri Megadim; and this requires further study, for according to that view above in siman 587, that he fulfills if there is the measure of a tekiah in the portion that is under obligation, if so, why did he not fulfill, and what does it matter to him that his fellow intended to fulfill at the end of the tekiah, and likewise the reverse? And this is not similar to one person who wants to fulfill with the beginning of the tekiah by itself and with the end of the tekiah by itself. And it is possible that it fits according to that view in siman 590, se'if 6, that even for [that] one alone it did not count for him, namely because his intention was to interrupt the tekiah, and not [that] the beginning [should count as] the end nor the end as the beginning; if so, here too: granted that from the hearer's side we do not care; nevertheless, since the blower's intention to discharge others is required, and the blower's mind was to interrupt the tekiah, part for this one and part for that one, therefore the tekiah is spoiled and does not count at all; but if he did not intend to interrupt it, even though his intention is to use it for this one and for that one, since in all of them he is using it; what of it? And it is possible that even the first view agrees to this when he intended to interrupt it for the sake of two people.
