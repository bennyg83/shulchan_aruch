Se'if 1. The time of blowing the shofar is by day and not by night, and its mitzvah is from the time of sunrise etc. Note: So it is in the Tur; and one should examine why in the Mishnah of Megillah, in the latter clause (regarding every matter whose mitzvah is by day, it is valid all day) it also listed there taking the lulav and shofar; while in the first clause, regarding "they do not circumcise and do not sprinkle etc.," it did not list shofar and lulav, only milah and sprinkling and immersion. Also the Tur's words have no understanding: here he wrote this law, and in Hilchos Lulav he wrote simply that its mitzvah is from when dawn rises, and did not write at all that ideally its mitzvah is from sunrise. And to reconcile the Tur's words it would appear that he holds that since the main concern of before sunrise is by force of a doubt of night (as Rashi explained, and it is brought in Magen Avraham in this siman) and one may say the intent is: by force of "the diligent advance to mitzvos," there is concern that because he is eager to be among the diligent he will not examine carefully and it will seem to him that it is day while it is still night; therefore they set him a fixed time at sunrise, for it is impossible to err. And accordingly one may say that because of concern of a wasted blessing they would not have enacted, for they hold like the view of Tosafos that every [ordinary] blessing is only rabbinic and it is a rabbinic doubt; rather, for concern of nullifying the body of the mitzvah they so enacted; and Megillah too, since it is words of kabbalah [Prophets], is like words of Torah, and its doubt is to be stringent. One may say: specifically regarding Megillah and milah and sprinkling and immersion (where people do not tend to do so several times in one day (for milah is impossible; and likewise sprinkling) he would become tamei again with a second sprinkling; and likewise immersion (people do not tend to do so several times in one day; and likewise Megillah) therefore there is concern that they may err that it is day and fulfill with that reading and not read a second time and be nullified from their mitzvah; therefore they enacted with sunrise. But regarding lulav, where the meticulous tend to hold the lulav in their hand all day, and likewise even other people tend to take it several times a day, if so there is no concern of nullifying the mitzvah, for even if it was still night his end is to take it again in actual day; therefore they did not decree on this to wait until sunrise. And with this one may explain why the Mishnah did not list shofar: one may say that specifically Megillah and sprinkling and immersion (which people do not enjoy and certainly will not add to do so many times for nothing) therefore they were concerned lest he take it before day and be nullified from the mitzvah; but regarding shofar, where many people enjoy when they blow, as Kol Bo wrote, if so, automatically even if he already fulfilled, his end is to blow further, and there is no basis to decree to wait until sunrise; only according to the view of several that after sunset one should not blow further for nothing, if so again it is proper to be concerned as above. And the She'elas Yaavetz in a responsum of Hilchos Rosh Hashanah discusses at length that one may say it is permitted to blow all day by force of "mitochn"; but in my opinion this is not so, for "mitochn" applies only to what was permitted by way of optional [use], for example melachah of Yom Tov that one is not obligated to do on Yom Tov and is only optional and was permitted, therefore "mitochn" applies; but what is not optional but obligatory. "mitochn" does not apply to it, and not for the sake of the mitzvah is forbidden; however that is according to the view that women are forbidden to blow; but according to the view that women are permitted to blow, if so in that respect it is optional and permitted, and "mitochn" applies, and automatically it is permitted to blow entirely; and therefore the Mishnah comes like the view that women are permitted to blow (that women may lean [on a korban] as optional) and automatically every person may blow as he wishes; therefore they did not decree on this to wait until sunrise by force of something like a double doubt: perhaps it is already day, and even if you say it is night, perhaps he will blow further and afterward fulfill his obligation, which is not so regarding Megillah and the others, where people do not tend to do so for nothing, and anything uncommon does not enter the category of a double doubt; which is not so regarding shofar, where this is the way, since many people enjoy when they blow, and it is properly a double doubt. But the Tur goes according to his view that it is forbidden to blow for nothing, as Magen Avraham wrote in his name in siman 596; and therefore if he has fulfilled his obligation he will not blow further; therefore ideally he must wait until sunrise; and regarding lulav, where certainly his way is to take it several times, it is an excellent double doubt (therefore he does not require waiting until sunrise; and he proved so from the Mishnah, since it did not list lulav and not shofar) perforce as above; and it fits well. And according to the above one may also reconcile what is in the Yerushalmi of Megillah on the Mishnah "they do not read etc. until sunrise etc.," and these are its words: "But do not the Rabbis advance for intercalation of the year?" and it answers: "It is a mitzvah to advance in order to spur on in mitzvos" (end quote) and it has no understanding. And it appears in intent according to Rashi's explanation that the concern of sunrise is because not everyone is expert; and if so there is no difficulty from intercalation of the month, for there one may say the Sages are meticulous and will not err, as written in the Talmud in Pesachim and Eruvin that one holds "members of a fellowship are meticulous"; and even though we do not hold so, one may say regarding the Sages who go up for intercalation of the year that certainly one may say so, for they are meticulous; only the Yerushalmi's assumption was that the main reason we ideally require sunrise is because until then it is night by rabbinic law, and not by force of concern of error, and therefore there is no reason to distinguish between intercalation of the year and other matters, and therefore it challenges from its intercalation; and to this it answers that the reason is not because until sunrise it is night, but because it is a mitzvah to advance and spur on in mitzvos; and therefore by force of being eager we are concerned that he will come to err and do before dawn; since the concern is only by force of error, automatically there is no challenge from intercalation, for the Rabbis are meticulous, and it fits well; and study carefully.
