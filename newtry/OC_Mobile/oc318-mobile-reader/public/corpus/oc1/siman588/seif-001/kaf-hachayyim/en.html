1) [Seif 1] The time for sounding the shofar is by day and not by night. For it is written, "It shall be a day of sounding for you." Megillah 20b.
2) Ibid. By day and not by night. If, for some reason, one was delayed and did not hear the shofar blasts until bein hashmashos, he should then sound the shofar without a blessing, because the time of bein hashmashos is uncertain whether it is part of the day or part of the night, as stated in tractate Shabbos 34b, see there, and in a case of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently. So ruled Chayei Adam, klal 141, os 1. See above, siman 260, os 1, regarding the time at which bein hashmashos begins, see there. Also see below, siman 652, regarding taking the lulav if one was delayed until bein hashmashos, see there.
3) Ibid. Its mitzvah begins from sunrise onward. So that one has emerged from the uncertainty of night. Rashi, Megillah 20a; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. See below, os 7.
4) Ibid. Its mitzvah begins from sunrise, etc. Meaning, the rising of the sun in that particular place, for the time of sunrise in each place varies from place to place, as the Ibn Ezra, of blessed memory, wrote in Parashas Vayishlach on the verse, "The sun shone for him," see there. Yafeh LaLev, vol. 2, os 1. We have already written the time of sunrise above, siman 28, os 10, see there.
5) Ibid. Its mitzvah begins from sunrise, etc. Regarding the practice of many to sound on Rosh Hashanah the series of blasts sounded while seated before the prayer, at sunrise, as the most preferred fulfillment of the mitzvah, see Mateh Yehudah, who wrote to reject this view on the basis of the Mishnah, Gemara, and commentaries, see there. So too wrote the Kisei Eliyahu, os 1, that it is not proper to sound the shofar in the morning at all, but only after the prayer, the repetition, and the Torah reading. This was also the practice in Alexandria, and he abolished the custom of certain individuals who had been accustomed to sound the shofar in the morning, see there. So too wrote the Birkei Yosef, os 1, that according to the mystical approach as well, it is not proper to sound the shofar in the morning at all. The blasts must be sounded in the place established by the early great authorities, after the Shacharis prayer, its repetition, and the Torah reading. This is the primary ruling. It is a great principle that passive inaction is preferable, and what place is there for alacrity where objections arise according to both the plain meaning and the mystical teaching? See there. So too he wrote in his Machazik Berachah, os 1, and in his Chaim Sha'al, vol. 2, siman 10, os 3, attributing it to the eminent kabbalist, the Rashash, of blessed memory, who discussed it with him orally, see there. The Shaarei Teshuvah cited his words. So too wrote the Ruach Chaim, os 1, proving it from several Rishonim and Acharonim, see there. This is also evident from what we wrote above, siman 585, os 28, that the shofar blasts complete the drawing down of the mochin that were drawn down during the silent prayer and the repetition, see there. Also see above, siman 585, os 9.
