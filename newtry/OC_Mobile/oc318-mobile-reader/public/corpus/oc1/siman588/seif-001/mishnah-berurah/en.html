(1) By day and not by night - For it is written, "a day of teruah it shall be for you." If it extends until twilight, he should blow without a berachah.
(2) He has fulfilled - By strict law, from alot hashachar it is day for all matters. But because not everyone is expert in identifying it, and at times one may come to do it too early, the Sages required lechatchilah to wait until sunrise, which is clear day for everyone. It is not necessary to wait until the entire body of the sun has risen over the earth; from the beginning of sunrise it is also permitted lechatchilah, as Peri Megadim writes. However, all this is from the basic Torah law, or in pressing circumstances such as when he must set out on a journey and the like. But the Sages established shofar blowing during Mussaf, and the Gemara explains that in a time of decree they would not allow Israel to blow the shofar and would lie in wait for them during all six hours of the Shacharit time; therefore they moved it to Mussaf. For this reason it appears that all Israel have practiced from ancient times, even regarding the seated blasts, not to be among the eager who hurry and blow at the beginning of the day, but rather near Mussaf after Torah reading. Even an individual blowing for himself should also be careful to blow after roughly three hours, so that his blowing occurs when the congregation is engaged in blowing.
(3) He has not fulfilled - For the end of a blast without its beginning is of no effect at all, as above in siman 587 seif 3. The same applies if he heard part of a blast by day and part afterward at night: he has not fulfilled, for a beginning without an end is also of no effect, as above there. It appears that even today, when the practice is to blow thirty sounds to satisfy every doubt, one must also be careful that even the first tekiah should not have any part before daylight.
(4) It was explained in siman 587 seif 3 - According to what we wrote there, that the main view is the first opinion, in every case he has not fulfilled.
