(There, se'if 5) A Yom Tov of Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos; one does not blow the shofar. Note: I was in doubt regarding Rosh Hashanah that fell on Shabbos, and one transgressed or forgot and blew one tekiah or two and did not complete the third; do we say that he should complete the third or not? For one may say: granted that the Sages upheld their words in the face of a mitzvah not to blow; that is by passive abstinence, then it is comfortable for them to uphold their enactment and they uprooted words of Torah by passive abstinence; but in this case where he has already transgressed a positive commandment of their words by active deed and has already blown, and if he blows further, granted that he transgresses their words, nevertheless he performs a mitzvah and fulfills the Torah mitzvah of blowing; but if he does not blow further, then he has not fulfilled the mitzvah at all, and in the end he has transgressed their prohibition and already blown, and if so he loses: he has already done a prohibition and not fulfilled any mitzvah, since he did not complete the tekios, for every tekiah we require a plain sound before it and a plain sound after it; in such a case perhaps the Sages did not uphold their words, since in the end their rabbinic enactment was not fulfilled, for he has already blown, whether we say that in this they permitted him to complete the tekios or not. And apparently I wanted to resolve this doubt from Beitzah, folio 21, from the beraita of the sheep of Atzeres that one slaughtered not for their sake or slaughtered before their time etc. and if it was Shabbos he should not throw [the blood], and if he threw, it is accepted for burning the emurim in the evening; and there the Talmud says: if he threw; after the fact yes, but ideally no; and behold there if he will not throw, a Torah prohibition is made retroactively, and even so they upheld their words not to throw on Shabbos, even if by not throwing he has not fulfilled the mitzvah at all, and if he throws he has fulfilled the mitzvah of the korban which is valid at least; but when we examine carefully there is still room to distinguish: there, even if the korban would be valid, he has done a Torah transgression (for ideally it was forbidden to offer this korban) and he has still desecrated Shabbos according to the Torah even if he throws; and further, there it is not the day's obligation since he slaughtered them not for their sake or before their time, which is not so here, where it is the mitzvah of the day according to the Torah thereby, and if he blows according to law he fulfills his obligation according to the Torah and has done no transgression except that he transgressed a decree of their words. And with this one may say that the Sages did not uphold their words in such a case; and this requires further study at present for the practical law; and study. And the reason why we do not blow on Shabbos according to Aggadah I wrote in Parashas Ki Savo in my work on the Torah of the year 5597, and I wrote there several ways on this with God's help; also regarding confession on Shabbos of Rosh Hashanah I wrote there (see there and study); and see here in the Taz what he wrote regarding a matter stated explicitly as permitted etc. and so it is in Tosafos, alternatively that it was an assumption. And according to his words one must say: at least why did they not decree on Shabbos by force of "lest he repair a musical instrument" (namely, since it is impossible to enact entirely for that reason, and that reason always applies) if so, if we permit on Yom Tov and not on Shabbos it would be mockery and ridicule. And it appears there is a proof for this from what is written in tractate Bechoros, folio 35b: R' Papa said to Abaye; according to R' Meir who holds that one suspected of one matter is suspected of the entire Torah, and who holds that one suspected regarding a matter may not judge and may not testify ([are] kohanim likewise that they may not judge a judgment? But it is written "by their mouth shall every dispute and every plague be") and apparently it is astonishing to me what he challenges: whence do we know that R' Meir holds that kohanim are suspected according to the Torah; perhaps according to the Torah they are not suspected, only by rabbinic law; or that what he holds that one suspected of one matter is suspected of the entire Torah is only rabbinic; or that what he holds that one suspected regarding a matter may not judge and may not testify is only rabbinic, and what difficulty from what the Torah said "by their mouth shall every dispute and every plague be"? And perforce it is proven like the Taz's words; and therefore the Talmud's challenge fits thus: since it is written explicitly as permitted, we have no power to forbid it; and if so, since regarding kohanim even though they are suspected they are valid to judge, automatically regarding all the suspected it is proper to say so, for one reason is equal for them; and regarding kohanim it is impossible to forbid, so again it is impossible to enact at all, and it is exactly like here regarding shofar; and study. And see Baer Heitev who brought the dispute of Rashi and the Rambam: according to the Rambam Jerusalem is not included in "Medinah," and according to Rashi Jerusalem is included in "Medinah"; and I saw in a midrash at the beginning of Megillas Esther, and these are its words: wherever "field" is said it means a city, and a city means a medinah, and a medinah means a province; and it brings there a proof from what is written "pass through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem" etc., if so it is proven explicitly that what is called in the language of Scripture "city" is called in the language of the Mishnah "medinah," and it is proven that Jerusalem is called in the language of the Sages "medinah," like Rashi's view (see there and study). And I saw in the small Shulchan Aruch in Eshel Avraham, siman 600, who brought in the name of a certain responsum that was in doubt: if one transgressed and took the shofar on Shabbos and blew with it and recited the blessing; has he fulfilled the obligation of shofar or not? And the practical difference is regarding that he need not recite Shehecheyanu on the second day according to those who hold that if he recited the blessing on the first day he does not recite Shehecheyanu on the second day (see there); and in my humble opinion it appears to resolve this from that which is challenged in Rosh Hashanah and Sukkah and in chapter 6 of Chullin against Rava who said "if he blew he has not fulfilled; if he took he has not fulfilled" (see there; and if we say that one who took the lulav on Shabbos or blew the shofar has fulfilled) if so what does he challenge? For this entire law that mitzvos were not given for enjoyment. Rashi explained the reason by force of that they were given as a yoke upon their necks; and if so it is proven that that is specifically regarding a mitzvah that is a necessity and obligation upon him, then it applies to say they were given as a yoke upon their necks; but where it was not a mitzvah and necessity upon him, and even so if he did it he fulfills the mitzvah; in this Rava agrees that they were given for enjoyment; at least there is no difficulty, for one may say Rava did not say "mitzvos were not given for enjoyment" except where he was forced to do it, but where there was no necessity Rava agrees they were given for enjoyment; and if so it is difficult: what does he challenge; let us say that beraita of "if he blew he has not fulfilled; if he took he has not fulfilled" deals with Shabbos, where ideally he was not obligated to do it, and since in any case he was not forced to do it it is enjoyment for him, and even so if he blew or took, if it was not of forbidden benefits he would fulfill and perform a mitzvah and be exempt on the second day from Shehecheyanu; but if it was of forbidden benefits, since if we say he has fulfilled then he has enjoyed forbidden benefits and done a transgression, then again it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, therefore automatically he has again not fulfilled; and what does he challenge? Rather certainly it is proven from this that on Shabbos even if he took of the permitted and blew of the permitted he has not fulfilled, and he is obligated to recite Shehecheyanu on the second day according to law. One may also say on this that therefore it does not help for him even after the fact by force of it being a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since he transgressed a rabbinic [law]; and behold even regarding a rabbinic prohibition it is considered a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, as is proven in chapter 2 of Pesachim regarding tevel that is tevel by rabbinic law, and Rashi explained there (see there); and Eshel Avraham there did not notice this, and there is no leisure to elaborate. And one should note on this: why then did Rava in Beitzah folio 19 give another reason for immersing vessels (because of repairing) and not say the reason "lest he carry it" and behold that reason is compelled regarding shofar and lulav; and see the commentators; and according to what I wrote one may say: for it is implied according to Rava in all these that even after the fact it does not help, for if not so no Tanna would have omitted teaching us that it is specifically ideally, and after the fact he has fulfilled; and perforce after the fact also it does not help; and accordingly it fits well: granted regarding these that are a mitzvah it is fine, since they forbade ideally because of "lest he carry it," automatically after the fact he has not fulfilled, for it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; but regarding immersing vessels, which is not a mitzvah, by force of the decree of "lest he carry it" it would be proper that after the fact it help, since what was was, and even if we say he has not fulfilled, the concern we already had is still not removed for us; and therefore it would be proper to say that after the fact it helps; therefore he needed the reason of "repairing," and with this after the fact properly does not help, for by this the prohibition is repaired, since if it does not help he has repaired nothing; but if after the fact it helps he has properly repaired; therefore to repair the prohibition we say that even after the fact it does not help; but by force of "lest he carry it," that concern is not repaired even if we say that after the fact it does not help; and since it would not be repaired it would be proper to say that after the fact it helps; therefore Rava did not want to answer on this "lest he carry it" why should it not help after the fact? But there regarding a mitzvah, that it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, that reason properly suffices, since ideally at least it is forbidden by force of "lest he carry it," and automatically even after the fact it does not help since it was a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and therefore the aforementioned answer fits better: according to Rava to his view who answers in Beitzah by force of "lest he repair," if so perforce he holds that after the fact it does not help in these; and therefore he challenges from "if he blew he has not fulfilled," and it is impossible to answer regarding Shabbos that on Shabbos even with the permitted he has not fulfilled, and the aforementioned law is compelled that after the fact also he has not fulfilled; and not by force of Eshel Avraham's reason that after the fact it does not help, for if so Rava still would not have needed another reason for immersing vessels, only as our words by force of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and therefore regarding vessels he needed another reason by force of repairing; and it fits; and study carefully on this; and in my work on Orach Chayim of the year 5592 in siman 588 I elaborated on this (see there and study carefully).
