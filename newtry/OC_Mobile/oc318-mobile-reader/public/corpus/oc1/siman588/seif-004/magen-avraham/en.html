One does not blow. Even though blowing is a shevus, and a positive Torah commandment comes and overrides a shevus of their words; nevertheless we decree lest he carry it four cubits on Rosh Hashanah (Rambam). Maharam Padua raised a difficulty in his glosses: for blowing as a shevus is itself also a decree lest one repair a musical instrument, and if so, let us say it is forbidden because of that decree, and they upheld their words in the face of a positive commandment; and he answered that they did not decree that decree regarding blowing the shofar, for if so they would never blow, since even on Yom Tov "lest he repair" applies, and it would turn out that they uproot a positive Torah commandment; but the decree "lest he carry it" applies only on Shabbos, and they properly decreed. And see the Bach and the Re'em who strained over this; but according to what the Ran wrote in chapter 4 of Sukkah and chapter 1 of Megillah, that the reason they set aside the blowing because of this decree is because they were not expert in the fixing of the month (and so it is implied in the Gemara) if so the difficulty returns to its place: let us say the decree is lest one repair a musical instrument, as with all optional blowings; and what is permitted on Yom Tov is because either way: if it is Yom Tov they perform a mitzvah, and if it is a weekday they have done nothing, which is not so on Shabbos, when they would transgress a shevus. And in Tosafos at the beginning of chapter 3 of Sukkah, s.v. "inhu d'yad'ei etc.," it is implied that in truth regarding blowing the reason is because of shevus (see there); and at the beginning of chapter 4 of Rosh Hashanah; and this requires further study.
