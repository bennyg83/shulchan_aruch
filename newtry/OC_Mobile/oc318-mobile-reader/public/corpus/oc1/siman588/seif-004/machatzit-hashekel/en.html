(s.k. 4) One does not etc. Even though blowing is a shevus, that is to say, like optional blowing, for which we decree lest one repair a musical instrument.
Lest he carry it, and they upheld their words, namely the decree of "lest he carry it," in the face of the positive commandment of blowing; [that decree] applies only on Shabbos, for on Yom Tov there is no prohibition of carrying out.
But according to what the Ran wrote etc. That they were not expert in the fixing of the month. This is the language of the Rif; and the Razah raised a difficulty upon him, for the reason is "lest he carry it," and not because they were not expert. And the Ran answered according to what is stated in Sukkah at the beginning of the chapter "Lulav and aravah": a lulav is sometimes six [days] and sometimes seven, for the mitzvah of taking the lulav is seven days, and it is impossible for seven days without Shabbos; therefore when the first day of Sukkos falls on Shabbos, then it is seven, for the first day overrides Shabbos.
And in the Mikdash the mitzvah is all seven [days], but outside the Mikdash its mitzvah is one day according to the Torah; therefore the first day, which is significant (for even in the provinces it is according to the Torah) therefore when it falls on Shabbos it overrides the decree of "lest he carry it," and one takes it even in the provinces; but when one of the other days of the festival falls on Shabbos, since the other days are not according to the Torah except in the Mikdash, it is not so significant and does not override Shabbos even in the Mikdash; and nowadays when they are not expert in the fixing of the month, even if the first day falls on Shabbos, nevertheless they do not take the lulav (these are the words of the Gemara; and the Ran asked: if so, regarding shofar, which even in the provinces is according to the Torah) why when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbos does the Mishnah teach that specifically in the Mikdash they blew and not in the provinces? And he answered: shofar on Rosh Hashanah is different, for it is on the day of the coming of the witnesses to testify about seeing the moon, and the time of blowing was in the morning; and in the morning by the majority the witnesses would not come; and for this reason they enacted that even if witnesses came in the morning, nevertheless over the morning Tamid offering they would recite the weekday song.
If so, if they were blowing in the provinces when it fell on Shabbos, if witnesses came in the morning; we decree lest next year witnesses not come in the morning until the morrow and the day be a weekday, and nevertheless they will blow in the morning and say: last year we blew in the morning, this year too let us blow in the morning, and they will not know that last year was different because witnesses came in the morning, but this year witnesses have not yet come in the morning and perhaps no witnesses will come today at all; which is not so in the Mikdash, which is a place of the meticulous, where they did not decree.
And he wrote that this is the intention of the Rif in what he wrote as the reason that they were not expert in the fixing of the month, that is to say, on Rosh Hashanah, that on that day we are not certain that witnesses will come today; this the Rif calls "they were not expert etc."; for either way: if it is Yom Tov, namely that witnesses will come today.
And if it is a weekday (that is to say, that witnesses will not come today at all) which is not so on Shabbos: that is to say, when it falls on Shabbos they properly decree to forbid the blowing, lest witnesses not come and the day not be Rosh Hashanah, and he would perform a prohibition (transgressing a shevus of "lest he repair a musical instrument") for in any case today is certainly Shabbos.
And in Tosafos etc. Who likewise raised the aforementioned difficulty of the Ran; they answered that lulav is different, for it is only mere carrying etc. but regarding blowing the shofar there is an act of skill, and they were more stringent with it (end quote of Tosafos. And Magen Avraham explained their words in what they wrote "there is an act of skill") namely, lest he repair a musical instrument. And that is to say: because of one shevus alone they would not uphold their words; therefore regarding lulav there is only one shevus ("lest he carry it") therefore the first day that falls on Shabbos overrides Shabbos; but regarding shofar there are two shevusim (lest he repair a musical instrument and lest he carry it) therefore they upheld their words when it falls on Shabbos, that it does not override Shabbos, even though it is according to the Torah in the provinces. And accordingly, even without this, Maharam Padua's difficulty is not difficult (that one should have said [the reason is] lest he repair a musical instrument) for in truth that is the Gemara's intention in what it wrote "lest he carry it": that is to say, there is also the decree of "lest he carry it" in addition to "lest he repair a musical instrument," so that now there are two shevusim; but because of one shevus they would not uphold their words.
