He heard nine tekios etc. (See what we wrote in the Mishnah Berurah regarding if he interrupted because of coercion); and it is difficult for me according to what the author of Magen Avraham decided in siman 65, not to be stringent regarding interrupting because of coercion unless the coercion is in the body of the matter, for example that the person was not fit because of a clean body, or that the place was not fit because it was not clean, if so, apparently this applies only to prayer or Birkas HaMazon and Hallel and Megillah, where in any case it is forbidden to mention the Name and say words of Torah in a filthy place; which is not so regarding tekios, which are only an act of mitzvah, and where do we find that it is forbidden to fulfill a mitzvah when his body is not clean or in a place that is not clean? Is it forbidden to wear a tallis of four corners when his body or the place is not clean? We have not found this anywhere; and this requires further study. And in Mateh Ephraim I found that he wrote, and these are his words: It appears that if he began to blow and water is dripping upon his knees, he stops until the water ceases and returns to blow from the beginning onward in order; and if he blew while the water was dripping, or even if he found excrement in his place, he has fulfilled; nevertheless he should return and blow without a blessing (end quote). He compared this to the law of Krias Shema; and it is possible that the reason is that since we hold that intention is required to fulfill the mitzvah that Hashem commanded us, this itself is also considered like words of Torah [and similar to the Rama's ruling in siman 84 that it is forbidden to answer Amen in the bathhouse], and it is no worse than contemplating words of Torah. And one may also say simply that while one is fulfilling a mitzvah in practice it is service, and one should not perform the service of God in a degrading way, for that is included in treating a mitzvah with contempt. And greater than this they said in Rosh Hashanah, folio 33, that it is forbidden to polish a shofar of Rosh Hashanah with urine because of respect, and that is only a preparatory act for the mitzvah, and all the more so the mitzvah itself.
And some say specifically when he did not interrupt.. He wrote this in the language of "some say" because Rabbeinu Tam and likewise the Ba'al HaMaor disagree with this. However it is difficult, for the Mechaber himself in siman 590, se'ifim 7 and 8, ruled unqualifiedly to be stringent and did not bring any dissenting view, and even regarding teruah after teruah, which is lighter, as we in fact find in the Or Zarua in the name of the Riva to be lenient on this; and it is possible that he used the language of "some say" because the interruption is only by hearing, as the Levush and Magen Avraham distinguished on this regarding teruah after teruah; and this requires further study; and I found that the Peri Chadash noted this.
