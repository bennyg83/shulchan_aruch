[1] [Levush] For the Sages were concerned, etc. Why they did not make such a decree regarding circumcision was explained in siman 588. In Temim Deim, siman 77, it says that for mitzvos dependent on the fixing of the new month they decreed, lest we be mistaken in sanctifying the new month and come to desecrate Shabbos not for the sake of a mitzvah. But regarding circumcision, which is on the eighth day and cannot be attributed to an error, they did not decree, for even if he would bring the knife there would be no Torah prohibition, since by Torah law the preparatory acts for circumcision override Shabbos, as it is written 'on the eighth day,' even on Shabbos. End quote. This is somewhat difficult, for he should have answered briefly that there is no Torah prohibition in circumcision. Perhaps the reason is that the preparatory acts for lulav also override Shabbos by Torah law, as stated in Shabbos 131 according to R. Eliezer: 'on the day,' even on Shabbos. From this there is difficulty with what the Levush answered on his own in siman 588, that circumcision is different because it says 'on the day.' So too in Matteh Moshe, siman 548, he wondered about all the great authorities who asked why circumcision is different from lulav, since regarding circumcision it says 'on the day.' In my humble opinion, this is not difficult at all, for according to R. Eliezer we expound 'on the day' also regarding lulav, even on Shabbos. If so, the difficulty for R. Eliezer would be: what is the difference between shofar and lulav? So too it is implied in Sefer Yereim at the end of siman 127.
