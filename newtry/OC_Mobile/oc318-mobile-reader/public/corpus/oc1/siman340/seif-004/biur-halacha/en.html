In liquids on the table — see in Mishna Berurah where we copied laws of writing and erasing; its essence is from Rambam chapter 1 and the Gemara there; and it is further proven there, in the Gemara that if he wrote in teria water [Rashi explained in one language it is a type of fruit, and in second language rain water] or in dust [meaning outside water, as concluded in Gittin 19] or in soot [see Rashi and Tosafos there; and see in Even HaEzer siman 125 that for Rambam he would be liable in all such cases] — liable; and in Tosefta it further states if he wrote on walnut shells, pomegranate shells [meaning he wrote in their juice that dyes — as explained in Sefer Chasidei David] — all these are called writing in something enduring and he is liable to a chatat; and Rambam included all this in what he wrote "and the like"; and see Rashba Shabbos 115b s.v. had amrinan that regarding Shabbat liability we do not require writing on something that endures forever, only that endure somewhat until people's manner is to write in them things not made to endure forever but for a time, such as memorandum books and the like — regarding Shabbat it is melacha thought and we do not require it endure forever like ink; and similarly implied is Tosafos s.v. aval hacha — see there; and accordingly it appears simple that one who writes on a lead tablet as is customary today among merchants to write their accounts as a reminder — liable, and this is not comparable to writing in dust, for it is clear there in Gittin that it is not called writing d'oraisa, for we see that on a lead tablet its mark is recognizable and endures; and all the more writing with charcoal mixed in water — the Gemara implies liable to a chatat; and all the more according to Mechaber there in Even HaEzer siman 125 who ruled anonymously like Rambam that in dust and soot in all such cases valid — if so in our matter certainly liable in all cases. And regarding if he wrote in non-enduring matter — forbidden — it further states there, in the mishnah if he wrote in road dust and scribe's dust — see there in Rashi and Rambam took part of them; and the same for all these whose mark does not endure; and likewise what we require he write on something enduring — Tosefta states if he wrote on olive, carob, and gourd leaves, or wrote in congealed blood and milk [meaning by scratching, for it does not say on congealed blood, as it concludes on olive leaf — see there] — liable; and on lettuce, leek, onion leaves and vegetables — exempt, for it is not permanent; and Rashba brought this Tosefta for halacha — see there; and Rambam took what occurred and included the rest in his words in what he wrote he writes enduring matter on enduring matter; and what he did not mention milk and blood — a great expansion — requires study. And behold I saw an astonishing matter in Peri Megadim who wanted to distinguish between equals between Shabbat and gittin against Rashba from what Rambam and Yerushalmi ruled that on vegetable leaves exempt, and in Gittin 17a it states they write on an olive leaf and it was hidden from him this Tosefta that on Shabbat too liable on olive leaf; and know further that if he wrote letters on foods — likewise liable, for it is called on something enduring, as proven in Gittin 19a — according to Rabbanan if he wrote on them a get it is valid, for it is called something enduring; only according to R' Yehuda HaGelili they limited regarding get from scripture — see there, in the Gemara his reason they require similar to a book; and likewise explicit in Mordechai chapter Kol Gadol that erasing applies and automatically writing applies also. And regarding writing one letter see Mishna Berurah — specifically if he completed the book; and it implies if he completed only a word with this he is not liable; and Tosefta states the reverse; and see Rashba. And know that even though Rashi s.v. vehishlimah lesfar explained last letter of one of the twenty-four books — it appears not precise, for the same if some letter was missing in the middle of the book — through this it is not a complete book and he fixed it — liable, for through this the book was completed; and likewise proven in Rashi s.v. rava amar and Ri'az brought in Shiltei HaGibborim — see there. And regarding correcting one letter we copied Rambam's words; and in the Gemara there is further an establishment such as he took the roof of a dalet and made a resh; and this is brought in Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham; and what Rambam did not copy — because Rava deals such as through this the book was completely corrected with no further error except this — therefore liable, for it is like completing the book, as Rashi explained; and if so this is included in what he already copied regarding completing the book. And regarding writing equal letters we copied Rambam's language such as a word is formed thereby; and if they were equal and no word formed — such as alef-alef, bet-bet and the like — from Rambam's language it appears exempt, as Rambam wrote; and his view is from all the conditions of the baraita it appears he holds alef-alef of "I girded you" does not obligate; and how does the tanna of the mishnah argue on all these — therefore what the mishnah said "whether from one name" means specifically such as gag-tav and the like; and he ruled like the anonymous mishnah and like R' Gamliel who is R' Yehuda's teacher and not like R' Yehuda [and also possible he holds like R' Yaakov's establishment that the mishnah is R' Yosi who obligates because of marking and therefore liable in all cases; and he rules like all baraita conditions that argue on this]; but Rashi's, Ri'az's, and Ra'avad's view — even on alef and alef liable; and likewise implied from Rif and Rosh who copied the mishnah that said whether from one name whether from two names anonymously and did not copy R' Yehuda's baraita from R' Gamliel — see there, in the Gemara it appears they hold also on alef-alef liable. One who examines Rashi's view throughout the sugya will see he holds the baraita tanna'im do not argue on the mishnah at all, and all hold if he intended from the start to write two alefs and the like — liable; and what they said exempt — specifically when he intended from the start to write one large word with this — they hold that even though the Torah revealed letter by letter that even if he wrote part of it he is liable — nevertheless we require that in that part there be some word as he originally intended; therefore exempt on alef-alef of "I girded you," for there is no word at all; and R' Shimon who exempts until he writes the whole name — also deals thus, as proven from Rashi s.v. et kulo; but if from the start he intended to write only two letters — for all liable, even alef-alef, even for R' Shimon. And regarding two symbols we copied from Rambam that liable; and at first glance difficult, for Yerushalmi states on this: who is the tanna of two symbols? R' Yosi [meaning he obligates even in two marks, therefore liable even without letters only in symbols alone] — implies for Rabbanan exempt; and if so Rambam who ruled not like R' Yosi — since he ruled writing with left exempt — automatically exempt with two symbols; and one can say Rambam holds the Bavli argues on this and proof from what they challenge on Shabbat 103 on R' Yaakov's answer "but from the end R' Yosi the beginning is not R' Yosi" — implies even for Abbaye it works; or that the Gemara holds even for Rabbanan liable, for symbols are like letters. And regarding read together — question if he wrote one letter and afterward wrote another above it — whether this is called two letters after they are not read together; and do not bring proof from one in land and one on beam exempt — because they were not adjacent but one above the other; one can say the mishnah uses "not this and not this" language — see there, in the mishnah. And see Yerushalmi that commentator explained it is included in writing atop writing — meaning one letter written above another and exempt because not read together; and their explanation in Yerushalmi is not forced, as I wrote in my chiddushim; and also our Gemara does not hold this explanation, for otherwise whence that it is not like R' Yehuda. And regarding marking lines we copied Rambam's language; its source is Yerushalmi chapter Kol Gadol: the first who outlines a form is liable for writing and the second for dyeing; explanation: the first marked the form on hide and the like and the second passed dye on it — therefore the first liable only for writing for the marked image he made, and the second who passed dye on the marked place is only dyeing; and Rambam's intent is also that he made the mark of the image as painters mark. And behold Rambam did not explain explicitly whether we require two forms specifically like in letters or here liable even in one, for it is considered independently; and from this Yerushalmi it is clear even one form liable; and see in Eshel Avraham who challenged Rambam's words here against what he ruled in chapter 10 and we copied in Mishna Berurah that one who outlines in a formed utensil — his liability is striking the final hammer-blow; and with our words it is somewhat resolved, for in chapter 10 it does not deal with mere marking as in our matter, for here the complete melacha for adornment is not yet done but he made the form at once — therefore marking does not apply, for it is a different matter; unlike in our matter it deals that he made only the mark of the form initially so the dyer knows thereby afterward what to dye — similar to writing letters that indicate something; and that Rambam does not obligate in a utensil for dyeing — perhaps it does not deal when he outlined in dye but engraved a form in a utensil as done on silver utensils — therefore he also did not mention in our Gemara daf 76b dyeing, only striking the final hammer-blow; and in what we wrote Peri Megadim's challenge in s.k. 6 is removed. And incidentally since we deal with writing laws I saw fit to note one matter that troubled me: Rashi on the mishnah of writing on two pages of a ledger wrote they coat with wax — implies wax is called something enduring; and likewise reasonable it is not inferior to congealed milk that Tosefta states liable for writing on it; and below in siman 645 seif 7 it states on wax tablets it is non-enduring; and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote there that even on Shabbat exempt. And regarding erasing we copied its essence from Rambam's language; and what he wrote in order to write in the place of erasure — it appears he learned thus from what the Gemara said and Rambam ruled thus that demolishing in order to build in its place is demolishing, etc. — see there daf 31b.
