(א) (s.k. 4) Wax and even if it dripped, etc. And Bach wrote: if one removes wax from one letter there is d'rabbanan prohibition; and if from two letters liable, for removal is called writing — for as long as it is not removed one cannot read the letter. And responsum Me'il Tzedakah siman 25 wrote: according to Bach's words if such a case is found in a sefer Torah — letters covered with wax that dripped on them — forbidden to read with it, for the sefer Torah lacks those letters, for he considers removal of wax as writing; and accordingly before removal it is as if those letters were not written; and Me'il Tzedakah wrote it does not appear in his eyes to obligate thus for writing, for we do not find such in the Mishkan. Whether if part of a sefer Torah is rolled and tied with a permanent knot — even on Shabbat when forbidden to open and untie the knot — whether we say forbidden to read in the unrolled part of the sefer Torah because the tied part is as erased; or if one unties that knot on Shabbat and they did not permit because of untying a knot but because of writing — whether we say he would be liable — certainly not reasonable; and the same if one removes wax from letters — no liability for writing; and furthermore wrote most of our sifrei Torah are full of wax drippings and would be invalid to read in them — except Bach's words flow from Tosefta Rosh brought chapter Klal Gadol; and in truth Tosafos' explanation is not as Bach understood it — therefore the above law he derived from there does not exist. And this is Tosafos' language: ink fell on the sefer and he erased it — wax on the ledger and he erased it — if in its place there is enough to write two letters liable, end of his words; and Bach understood regarding wax on the ledger the reason is because of writing; and in responsum Me'il Tzedakah there he wrote Tosafos' language does not imply thus; and furthermore why distinguish sefer and ledger — combine them: ink or wax fell on sefer or ledger and he erased, etc.; and furthermore from logical necessity of the above — waw, this is not the Baraisa's intent; rather what Tosefta stated: wax on the ledger (meaning as Rashi on two pages of ledger) — they had boards smeared with wax and they marked on them; and after all was written they smoothed the wax on the board and wrote again on it — and his liability is because of smoothing which is a toldah of erasing, not because of erasing in order to write two letters:

(ב) And nevertheless (meaning even though his liability is because of smoothing and not because of writing) it requires the measure of two letters to obligate a chatas, for all Shabbat liabilities require melacha fit for something; etc. But because of removal of wax and thereby the writing beneath is found — therefore he permitted if wax dripped on a sefer Torah in a place that is not the weekly portion; but if the dripping is on a place that must be read — then impossible, for it is like reading by heart; and if permitted to remove it — this depends on his dispute with Bach — for Bach forbidden and according to responsum Me'il Tzedakah permitted:

(ג) Nevertheless it appears he did not resolve to rely on his view to permit removal (and I did not understand — if he was concerned for Bach's view, then even dripping not in the place of reading there is reason to invalidate reading, for according to Bach it is as if those letters under the wax were erased); and he concluded further: and perhaps experts can scrape on parchment until it is removed from above two letters; and on paper one should be stringent, for from paper the writing is removed with the wax and it is actual erasing and forbidden, end of his words.
