[11] [Levush] "But," etc. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch is missing here: one must be careful not to write with liquid on a table or in ashes, but, etc. If one writes with a finger in honey or in congealed fat in the same manner as one writes in ashes, it is forbidden. [Perishah] I found in the Tosefta, chapter 12: if one writes with nutshells, pomegranate peels, congealed blood, congealed milk, on an olive leaf, carob leaf, gourd leaf, or upon any enduring material, he is liable. If he writes upon a lettuce leaf, leek leaf, onion leaf, vegetable leaf, or upon any non-enduring material, he is exempt. The rule is this: if he writes with an enduring substance upon a non-enduring surface, or with a non-enduring substance upon an enduring surface, he is exempt; he is liable only when he writes with an enduring substance upon an enduring surface. This teaches that both the substance used for the writing and the surface upon which it is written must endure for Torah liability. Writing in dust, ashes, liquids, or similar temporary substances is therefore not Torah-level writing, though it remains forbidden rabbinically. Likewise, forming recognizable letters with a finger in a substance that retains their form temporarily is forbidden even though the writing will not endure permanently. The laws of erasing follow the same basic distinction: Torah liability requires erasing in a manner connected with enduring writing, while temporary erasure can nevertheless be rabbinically forbidden. Accordingly, one must be careful with letters formed on foods, wax, or other materials when breaking, smoothing, or manipulating them would intentionally create or erase recognizable writing.
