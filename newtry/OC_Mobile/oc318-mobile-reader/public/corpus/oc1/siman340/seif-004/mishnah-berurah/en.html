(א) One should beware — meaning although there is no liability to a chatat, for we require that he write specifically in ink or in something similar whose mark endures, as below — nevertheless rabbinically forbidden even in what does not endure:
(ב) In liquids, etc. — meaning he dipped his finger in some liquid and wrote with it on the table; and the same on paper and parchment and the like; and Acharonim wrote that even if he wrote in liquids on something that does not endure, such as on a vegetable leaf and the like — nevertheless rabbinically forbidden:
(ג) Or in dust — meaning he scratched with his finger like letters in dry dust; and the same on a weekday; and likewise forbidden to mark like letters on a windowpane on cold days when they are moist from the cold. And if he marked like letters in things that congeal, such as in blood and milk that congealed — Tosefta implies he is liable:
(ד) To mark — in order to hint to his fellow some matter, and we do not say he trains his hands in writing thereby; and the same permitted to see a craft on Shabbat even though it teaches it [Acharonim]; and it appears this is specifically incidentally when it happened that he sees; and also in this he should beware not to speak then with the non-Jew about this, as above in siman 307 — see there; but to go on Shabbat intentionally to the non-Jew's house for this — forbidden on account of speaking of your matters; and see above in siman 306 seif 1:
(ה) In the air — the same if he moves his finger on a dry page like the form of letters — likewise permitted since its mark is not recognizable at all and is not comparable to liquids and dust [Taz]. And incidentally we explain here from the Gemara and poskim the principles of laws of writing and erasing in a matter involving liability to a chatat and in a matter that is exempt and forbidden, for Shulchan Aruch abbreviated in this. A) The writer is not liable until he writes in something that marks and endures, such as ink and black [meaning charcoal — Rashi], sikra [red dye], komos [tree resin], gallnut water, lead utensils, and all similar — something that endures; and likewise he is not liable until he writes on something on which the writing endures, such as on hide, parchment, paper, wood, and the like; but one who writes in something whose mark does not endure, such as in liquids and fruit juices, or who wrote in ink and the like on vegetable leaves and on anything that does not endure — exempt; and he is not liable until he writes something enduring on something enduring; and likewise the eraser is not liable until he erases enduring writing from atop something enduring; and all this regarding liability to a chatat; but rabbinic prohibition exists even if he wrote something non-enduring on something non-enduring, and likewise regarding erasing. B) One who writes on his flesh with ink is liable because it is hide even though the warmth of his flesh removes the writing after time — this resembles erased writing; but one who scratches on his flesh the form of writing is exempt, for there is no manner of writing thus. One who tears on the hide the form of writing is liable for writing; one who marks on the hide the form of writing is exempt [and in our Tosefta it states the reverse — tearing exempt and marking liable — and the Rishonim already struggled with this; see in Rashba's chiddushim and in Rambam here and in chapter 4 of Gittin]. C) One who writes writing atop writing — meaning he passed the quill over already-written letters and renewed them — exempt, for he accomplished nothing; and this is when it was ink atop ink or sikra atop sikra; but if he passed ink atop sikra he is liable twice — once for writing, for now it is proper writing more than before, and once for erasing, for through his writing he erases the two lower letters; and if he passed sikra atop ink — exempt, for it is spoiling. D) The writer is not liable to a chatat until he writes two letters; but if he wrote one letter, even if it was as large as two — exempt from chatat; nevertheless d'oraisa prohibition exists even in one letter, as above at the beginning of the siman. And the one letter we said exempt — even if adjacent to writing that was written before, since on Shabbat he wrote only one letter; but if with this letter he completed the book — liable, for his action accomplished much. One who wrote one letter and dotted on it as a sign — even though they read from it a complete word thereby — exempt; how so? Such as he wrote the letter mem and all read from it maaseh, or he wrote it in place of forty, for behold it is as if he wrote forty — nevertheless exempt. One who corrected one letter and made it two — such as he split the roof of a chet and it became two zayins and he needs this — liable; and all similar. If he intended to write a chet and wrote two zayins, or intended to write mem and it came out kuf because the quill skipped and the letter was not drawn properly, and all similar — exempt, for he intended only one letter. E) The two letters we said for liability — even if no word was formed thereby, such as alef-gimel and the like. And even if both were from one name, such as tav-tav, gimel-gimel, chet-chet, dalet-dalet and the like — likewise liable. And even if his intent from the start was to write more, such as titnu or gagot and he wrote only these two letters from them — nevertheless liable. And know that according to what was ruled above in siman 336 that the tagin of the letters she'atnez gatz do not invalidate — evidently it is called writing without them; if so, if one writes two letters even in a Torah scroll without tagin — likewise liable; and all the more in ordinary writing, which is called writing and liable; and likewise the practice in gittin that they write without tagin, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 126 — evidently it is complete writing even without tagin. Also the melacha of writing is d'oraisa in every script and every language; and see above in siman 306 in Biur Halacha that we explained that such is the agreement of all poskim; and Rambam wrote that even from two symbols one is liable — meaning such as he made symbols indicating a number. F) The two letters we said for liability — even if he wrote them on two pages of a ledger and they are read together — meaning he wrote them on their edges so one can read them as one; and even if he wrote them on two walls of a corner internally — liable, since they were opposite one another and one can read them as one. And even if he took a scroll and the like and wrote on its edge one letter in this province and went that day and wrote a second letter in another province on another scroll on its edge — liable, for when brought near they are read together and no act is lacking to bring them near; but if he wrote one letter in one land and one on a beam, or wrote on two walls of the house that were far from one another, or on two pages of a ledger such that they are not read together — exempt. G) One who writes with the left or back of the hand [meaning on the back of the hand where he held the quill in his fingers and turned his hand and wrote], with the foot, mouth, or elbow [forearms] — exempt because there is no manner of writing thus; nevertheless prohibition exists even with the foot and the like. A left-handed person who wrote with his right, which is to him like everyone's left — exempt; if he wrote with his left — liable. One who is equally proficient with both hands — liable whether with right or left. A minor holds the quill and an adult holds his hand and writes — the adult is liable, for he is primary in the melacha and the minor did nothing. An adult holds the quill and a minor holds his hand and writes — exempt, for here the adult did nothing; and if the one holding the quill intended to assist him — liability depends on the one holding the quill [so is according to Gra's version in Tosefta — see there]. One who writes in order to spoil the hide — liable, for his liability is not on the place of writing but on the writing; but one who erases in order to spoil — exempt. H) One who marks lines and forms on a wall with scarlet and the like as painters mark — this is a toldah of writing and he is liable for writing [and if he makes a form in a formed utensil [meaning the utensil stands for adornment — Rashi] — even if he makes part of it he is liable for striking the final hammer-blow, which completes the melacha of the utensil]; and likewise one who erases the marked line in order to fix — this is a toldah of erasing and he is liable. Forbidden to attach silver letters to a parochet and the like, for it is like writing; and likewise forbidden to remove from it, for it is like erasing; and see in Chayei Adam general rule 37 what he wrote on this. And now we explain some matters of erasing and marking. One who erases writing in order to write two letters in the place of the erasure — liable; and even one letter if it is large and in its place there is room to write two — liable. One who erases in order to fix — even not in order to write, such as when there were extra letters in the Torah and he erased them — likewise liable. Chayei Adam wrote that all melachot except writing — one is liable whether with right or left; and he brought proof for this from Tosefta chapter 10; but with foot, mouth, and elbow it implies there exempt and forbidden. One who rules paper and parchment and the like in order to write two letters under that ruling — liable; and likewise when he wishes to cut hide or other things and first rules it in order to aim his cut — this too is included in ruling. And the same for wood craftsmen who pass a scarlet thread on the beam in order to saw evenly, or builders who do so on stones in order to split the stone evenly — all this is included in ruling; and whether one rules with dye or without dye — liable.
