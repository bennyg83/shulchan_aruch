Wart. One who cuts a wart with a utensil is liable if it is moist. One who shears — whether from the living, whether from the dead, whether from domestic or wild animal, even from the flayed hide — liable. One who plucks a wing from a bird — liable. One who spins from the living — exempt, for this is not the manner of shearing. It implies one who plucks wool from an animal is exempt. And if he does not need the wool or hair it is melacha she'eino tzricha legufa. Magen Avraham. And Taz siman 336 wrote a person must be careful in a garment made from animal hide not to pluck hair from it, for there is chatas liability, for so it is in Yerushalmi chapter Klal Gadol: one who plucks from a dead animal is liable, for its plucking is its shearing; and one must also be careful not to pluck from his own skin on his hand or other places, for they are as attached to the ground, as is implied at the beginning of Sanhedrin that a person is like ground, end of his words. And Shulchan Aruch HaRav forbids taking lice from hides; see there. And Yad Aharon from Rambam's words implies he holds one who plucks from an animal is not liable to a chatas, for he wrote in chapter 9: one who shears wool or hair whether from domestic or wild animal, whether from the living, whether from the dead — liable, end of his words — behold he did not write one who plucks is liable for shearing but specifically one who shears is liable — from which one who plucks has no chatas liability; and the proof is that he wrote explicitly one who plucks a wing from a bird — behold this is a toldah of shearing — and from that he wrote one who plucks a wing from a bird and did not write regarding an animal that one who plucks wool from an animal is liable for plucking — infer that he holds regarding an animal plucking is not a toldah of shearing; and this appears the opposite of the Yerushalmi Taz brought to rule halachah; and it appears this Yerushalmi is not like the halachah, for from our Talmud it appears the opposite, and this is the sugya in the chapter HaLokeiach Behemah on the Mishnah of R' Yosi ben Meshullam who says one who slaughters the firstborn makes room for the axe and plucks the hair — they asked Rav Huna opposite him on Yom Tov what is the law, etc., and they conclude a wing is different, meaning its manner; see there. Behold explicitly from our Gemara it appears clear that one who plucks from an animal is not shearing, and a bird's wing is different, meaning its manner — and therefore Rambam did not rule that Yerushalmi Taz brought; and I am astonished at Taz how he did not pay attention to our Talmud; and it appears explicit there is no chatas liability in plucking wool from an animal; but nevertheless the law is true law that one must be careful not to pluck hair from an animal even if dead, for even though there is no chatas liability nevertheless there is a prohibition, end of his words.
