2) Taz writes in Yoreh De'ah, siman 198, that if a woman forgot to cut her nails before Shabbos and her immersion is Friday night, she should not ask a non-Jew to cut them. She can clean beneath them instead, so why should instructing a non-Jew be permitted? Moreover, she moves her hand and assists, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 181. She should therefore clean them thoroughly. Shach in Nekudos HaKesef there, however, discusses the matter at length and permits it, because assistance has no substance, as stated in siman 328 at the end of seif 17 in the gloss, and because it is labor not needed for its own purpose, as Tosafos writes. Nevertheless, if they can be removed by hand, that is preferable. These are his words. It appears to me that she should tell the non-Jew to remove them by hand, because this creates two layers of rabbinic prohibition, which are permitted for a mitzvah, as stated in siman 307, seif 5. If removal by hand is impossible, they may be cut with a utensil. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. According to what we wrote in the preceding paragraph, however, Tur and Shulchan Aruch follow Rivash that cutting hair or nails with a utensil incurs Torah liability even when one does not need the hair or nails. A non-Jewish woman may therefore be permitted only to remove them by hand or teeth, creating two layers of rabbinic prohibition permitted for a mitzvah. This is Shulchan Aruch HaRav's view in paragraph 2; see there. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Ki Sisa, paragraph 16, writes that the practice in his city follows Shach, permitting a non-Jewish woman to remove them, but she is instructed to draw the Jewish woman's hand toward herself to cut, rather than having the Jewish woman bring her hand forward. Where this is the custom, it remains the custom. If no non-Jewish woman is available, it appears that in pressing circumstances one may rely upon thoroughly cleaning beneath the nails. For toenails, even when a non-Jewish woman is available, cleaning beneath them is preferable. So write the later authorities.
