(א) Permitted, etc. — behold Bach and Taz challenged Shulchan Aruch's words from the mishnah in chapter HaBoneh that marking is like writing, for so they mark on Mishkan boards to know which is its mate; and if he marked two marks on something enduring — liable to a chatat; if so on non-enduring matter must be forbidden rabbinically, as in the mishnah there regarding writing on non-enduring matter, as above; and therefore Bach ruled against Tur and Mechaber and wrote forbidden to mark on a fingernail even one mark; and Magen Avraham and Taz expanded on this and reconciled with difficulty — see there. And the truth is as Eshel Avraham sided to say Tur holds the halacha is not like R' Yosi, as Ra'avad wrote; and if so mere scratch-marks are not included in writing at all and forbidden only rabbinically; therefore on non-enduring matter entirely permitted — see there; and it is clear this is also Rif's, Rosh's, Rambam's, and Rabbeinu Yerucham's view, not like R' Yosi, for behold all ruled writing with left exempt unless proficient with both hands — this is not like R' Yosi, as stated in the Gemara; and this is also Shulchan Aruch's reason; yet Darkei Moshe brought in name of Or Zarua to forbid, concerned for R' Yosi's view that marking is liable like writing; and if so on non-enduring matter forbidden rabbinically; and Kenesset HaGedolah wrote proper for Heaven-fearing to be stringent; and likewise in Birkei Yosef; and see what I further wrote below on Shulchan Aruch's reason [gloss: incidentally I write an astonishing matter I saw in Maggid Mishnah chapter 1 of Hilchot Shabbat law 17 s.v. marking, etc., on what Rambam wrote there law 17: marking is a toldah of writing — how? The first marks lines and forms on a wall with scarlet and the like, etc.; and Maggid Mishnah wrote: marking is a toldah of writing. Mishnah in HaBoneh: R' Yosi said they did not obligate two letters except because of marking, for so they write on Mishkan boards to know which is its mate; and Rambam explained in explanation of the mishnah R' Yosi's view was that marking is an av by itself and writing two letters alone is because of marking, for so they did in the Mishkan; and the halacha is not like him but a toldah of writing — end Maggid Mishnah; and I did not understand his explanation, for behold writing two letters is actual writing and not a toldah, as in the mishnah daf 73; and if to say his intent regarding mere scratch-marks that it is a toldah of writing and what Rambam wrote there that halacha is not like R' Yosi — meaning it is not an av by itself — also not, for Rambam there does not deal with scratch-marks at all but whether writing two letters' liability is for writing or marking — see there; and what is there, in the Gemara 72b according to R' Yosi if he scratched two marks liable — automatically we derive this, since R' Yosi holds two letters are not yet included in writing but only because of marking liable, for it is a mere sign — if so even two scratches are included; and automatically for Rambam who explicitly ruled in explanation of the mishnah halacha is not like R' Yosi but two letters' liability is for writing — we have no proof for marking included in melacha, for on Mishkan boards they wrote letters, as implied in mishnah explanation there and likewise in R' Yosi's baraita for his reason he innovated this law that scratching is included in melacha, concluding therefore if he scratched one mark, etc. — see there implies for the anonymous tanna exempt in scratching; and likewise implied there, in the Gemara therefore exempt for the anonymous tanna in one proficient with left; and also all Maggid Mishnah's proofs he brought R' Yosi's words — here is astonishing, for this marking Rambam mentioned is a different matter, for behold he concludes how one marks lines, etc. [and his words are explicit in Yerushalmi chapter Kol Gadol law 2 — see there] and he did not mention scratch-marks at all — implies he holds this is not included in writing at all; and likewise from what he ruled writing with left exempt, as above; and likewise from what he ruled in law 10 regarding writing two letters — specifically when there is some word such as tav-tav, chet-chet liable, but alef-alef exempt, as Maggid Mishnah wrote there; and if we say even two marks liable — this is not inferior to two marks; and likewise all baraita conditions that hold alef-alef of "I girded you" does not obligate — they also hold mere marks exempt]:

(ב) To mark, etc. — see in Peri Megadim who wrote: nevertheless when he marks on a fingernail a mark on an error in a book — it appears forbidden, for correcting fixes thereby and I am concerned for chatat — see Shabbos 104b; and specifically to mark as a reminder and the like one can say permitted — end; and from other Acharonim' words it does not appear so; and the law is with them that there it deals correcting the letter through his correction, but here he only makes a line as a reminder and the fixing will be done after Shabbat; and perhaps Peri Megadim's intent is when he erases the letter through marking with his nail — strained, for certainly Shulchan Aruch does not deal thus and what does it teach us:

(ג) As one marks — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote that to make like a letter forbidden — from Levush and Acharonim. And behold from Gra's words in seif 4 where he proved the source for the gloss there permitting to mark in the air like letters [even many] — from this seif it appears he explains Shulchan Aruch's words even like letters; and accordingly I did not know any reason for Shulchan Aruch permitting l'chatchila because it is non-enduring matter — at minimum rabbinically should be forbidden; and perhaps this is Gra's reason that he did not copy any source for Shulchan Aruch's words in this seif; nevertheless question why he did not challenge this and was silent; and perhaps the reason is he holds Shulchan Aruch holds marking is not considered writing at all in all cases; and proof from Yerushalmi chapter 23: one who marks on light the form of writing exempt; and likewise Rambam ruled — see there Maggid Mishnah whose source is this Yerushalmi; and therefore on non-enduring matter permitted l'chatchila; and one can say Yerushalmi deals marking on a fingernail because it is non-enduring; and therefore exempt; and it is known exempt matters of Shabbat exempt but forbidden is stated anonymously; and furthermore if not they should have distinguished in marking itself between in a utensil and on a fingernail; and why distinguish there between tearing on hide like the form of writing and marking on hide like the form of writing; however in Or Zarua the Yerushalmi reads the reverse: one who marks on light the form of writing liable; and likewise in our Tosefta; and Gra did not emend this at all — implies he holds this is primary; and accordingly to mark on a fingernail, which is non-enduring matter — at minimum forbidden l'chatchila, as the aforementioned Acharonim; and according to our words this entire law depends on Tosefta's version whether like Rambam or like Or Zarua; and likewise Maggid Mishnah wrote there are variant readings in Tosefta; and Gra did well not to write on this neither source but critique; and still requires study in all this:
