(ב) That endures. And Rashal wrote specifically on hard parchment — for in the time of Tur they wrote sefarim on parchment; but on paper forbidden, for it endures; and so Taz's view; see there. Bach forbids even on parchment. It implies in Gittin daf 20 that if one inserts silver letters on a garment it is called writing; Or Sameach; and if so perhaps forbidden to do on Shabbat. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote chapter 1 of a sefer Torah. That which is stated in siman 307 seif 11: they write on Shabbat — meaning in glachat script, for it is only shevut, for they decreed on theirs because of ours; and so implied somewhat in Rama's words there; and its explanation is one tells a non-Jew to write, for it is shevut d'shevut. And at end of seif 7 he wrote that light writing that we write documents of commoners is not considered writing, end of his words. And Magen Avraham wrote: I am astonished — for Rambam wrote one who writes in any script and any language is liable, even from two dyes; and Maggid Mishneh explained even if they are not known letters but merely a sign, such as inverted nuns next to "and it came to pass when the ark set out," end of his words. And that which requires adornments — this is in Assyrian script, for as long as they are not adorned their melacha is not complete; and perhaps in light writing too exempt since it is taken from Assyrian script; but in gentile script, since it is their writing — why should he not be liable? And so Rabbeinu Yerucham in Even HaEzer siman 126 regarding a get; also in Tosafos Gittin daf 8 and end of Marbeh it is stated explicitly that it is d'oraisa; and perhaps he disputes Rambam; and see Shach on the matter of adornments who elaborated — until here his words.
