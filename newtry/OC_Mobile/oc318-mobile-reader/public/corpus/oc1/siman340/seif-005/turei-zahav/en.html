Permitted to mark on a fingernail, etc. Even though in chapter HaBoneh it is stated in the Mishnah that one who marks is like one who writes, for so they marked on the boards of the Mishkan for a sign — and accordingly liable if he makes two marks like a writer; and even in something that does not endure the Mishnah implies exempt but nevertheless forbidden — here he marks one and it does not endure since two for two purposes are permitted as above — apparently. However it is difficult: if so he should have taught us that specifically one mark is permitted but two forbidden. Rather it appears specifically similar to the Mishkan where they marked two marks for recognition lest the boards be switched — and it turns out that for one sign they made two marks, and in this forbidden d'rabbanan even in something that does not endure — not so when marking on a sefer in two places, for each is a sign for a particular matter — therefore permitted even d'rabbanan; and it is plain that marking is forbidden if he marks letters, for in writing they forbade even in something that does not endure. And Rashal wrote: and Semak forbids marking on a sefer; and I heard from a wise man who decided between them and said this on hard parchment and this on soft paper — and this is a correct reasoning, end of his words; and this distinction I heard also from my brother the gaon Mahar'i Halevi of blessed memory because our eyes see that a mark a person makes on a fingernail on paper endures even after many years.
