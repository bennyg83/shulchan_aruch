10) From here comes a life-giving rebuke to those who grow their hair to resemble non-Jews. This is forbidden even during the week, and on Shabbos they add further prohibitions by combing it, detaching hairs, and applying oil or similar substances. Even when done by a non-Jew, it is rabbinically forbidden and warrants excommunication. The excommunication may be treated leniently if the act was inadvertent because the person did not know it was forbidden, but he must nevertheless observe forty fasts, as Rema writes above in the gloss to siman 334. Magen Avraham there, paragraph 33, writes even more strongly that one should fast for violating a rabbinic techum, and even an inadvertent transgressor must fast, as Zera Emes, volume 2, Yoreh De'ah, siman 89, writes; see there. From here I warn others when I see them removing earwax or nasal matter on Shabbos in a manner that detaches hairs. Although an unintended result is permitted, one must be stringent when the result is inevitable. When using a cloth, squeezing may also be a concern if it is wet. Similarly, one washing his face who runs his hands over his beard and sidelocks and grasps them cannot avoid detaching hair. The incident involving Arizal is known: he inadvertently placed his hand into his beard and did not remove it until after Shabbos. For this reason, the precise phrase is, 'Anyone who observes Shabbos according to its law,' meaning that no halachic dispute should remain, but it should be observed in a manner universally accepted as lawful and without any halachic doubt. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1.
