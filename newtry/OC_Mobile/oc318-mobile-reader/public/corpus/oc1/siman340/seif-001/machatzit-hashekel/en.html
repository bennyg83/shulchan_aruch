(א) (s.k. 1) Magen Avraham wrote: one who cuts, etc. So Maggid Mishneh explains Rashi — for we learned in Shabbos daf 94b: one who removes his nails one by one or with his teeth, and likewise his hair, etc. — R' Eliezer obligates and the Sages forbid because of shevut, and we rule like the Sages; and it was stated on this: R' Elazar said: the dispute is by hand, but with a utensil liable; and furthermore R' Eliezer said: the dispute is for himself, but for his fellow all agree exempt. And at first glance one might think: what does "but for his fellow all agree exempt" mean? — meaning even with a utensil, for the Sages too agree liable, nevertheless his fellow is exempt; for if we say what is "but for his fellow all agree exempt" — meaning by hand where R' Eliezer obligates — this is for himself, but for his fellow R' Eliezer agrees exempt; if so R' Eliezer's words "but for his fellow" etc. are said not per the halachah, for we rule like the Sages — rather what "but for his fellow exempt" means is even with a utensil. And if so his words are also per the halachah, for with a utensil the Sages agree liable. On this Magen Avraham wrote: there is no distinction — with a utensil even for his fellow liable; and if so for us who rule like the Sages there is no distinction between for himself and for another — by hand even for himself exempt, and with a utensil even for his fellow liable. And the practical difference is regarding cutting nails for a woman for immersion, as Magen Avraham wrote afterward; and on this he brought proof from Rashi's words who wrote: the dispute for himself — here R' Eliezer obligates because he can train his hand for himself to fix without a utensil, but for his fellow he cannot train his hand to remove well without a utensil, end of his words — behold it is explicit as Magen Avraham's words:

(ב) And so Maggid Mishneh regarding a wart — meaning from Maggid Mishneh's words it is proven that with a utensil even for his fellow liable. However Magen Avraham's primary proof is from the sugya in Arakhin end of Eruvin daf 103: R' Eliezer — even though he holds in general that preparatory mitzvah overrides Shabbat — nevertheless whenever possible we change; for we learned: a kohen on whom a wart grew (and it is a blemish and as long as the wart is on him he is forbidden to serve) — his fellow cuts it for him with his teeth; and this Baraisa must be according to R' Eliezer, for if per the Sages — why teach "his fellow cuts it for him with his teeth"? It would be that even he himself may cut it for himself; one can say it deals when the wart grew on his back and between his shoulders where he cannot remove it himself — only this is difficult: why was only permission to remove with teeth given? It should have taught that he is permitted to remove even with his hands! And resolve R' Eliezer who said dispute by hand but with a utensil all agree liable. And Tosafos explained: if it taught "his fellow cuts with his hand" it would teach two things — one that preparatory mitzvah does not override an av melacha, and furthermore we hear that with a utensil the Sages agree chatas liability; but now we cannot explain and infer that with a utensil it is because it is an av melacha, for by hand too it is shevut and they do not permit, end of Tosafos' words — rather it must be according to R' Eliezer; and according to him, since the matter is light and by law for preparatory mitzvah even with a utensil which is av melacha it would be permitted — only the Sages forbade because whenever possible we change; if they permitted shevut the lenient matter by hand would lead to permitting even with a utensil; but for the Sages who did not permit d'oraisa melacha at all for a mitzvah — why "lest he do with a utensil"? Now the Gemara is clear: from what the Gemara said it should have taught "his fellow is permitted to remove with his hand" — and thereby it would be resolved according to R' Eliezer that with a utensil the Sages agree liable; and this Baraisa deals with removing for his fellow which is for others — therefore with a utensil even for others even for the Sages liable; and therefore what R' Eliezer said "dispute for himself but for his fellow all agree exempt" applies only if he removed by hand and according to R' Eliezer who obligates also by hand — but for his fellow he agrees exempt by hand; but with a utensil even for his fellow and even for the Sages liable; and if so for us who rule like the Sages there is no distinction between for himself and for others — by hand even for himself exempt for the Sages, and with a utensil even for others liable, as Magen Avraham wrote and as above. However this proof is not sufficient, for perhaps one can distinguish between wart and nail, and perhaps a nail is lighter than a wart for his fellow even with a utensil exempt — therefore Magen Avraham needed to bring proof from Maggid Mishneh's words regarding wart in chapter 9 of Laws of Shabbat:

(ג) Assisting has no substance — meaning specifically the Mishnah teaches the nikaf is liable like the one who encircles — assists because it did not write "do not encircle" in plural to include the encircler and also the nikaf; but the Gemara challenges: the Merciful One forbade also the nikaf — nevertheless why should the nikaf be liable? He did nothing, and it is a negative commandment without an act; and on this they answer: since he assisted by tilting the head it is called there is an act — and this is specifically regarding nikaf where the verse already forbade him — therefore the assistance helps to consider it a negative commandment with an act; but if the verse had not forbidden the nikaf, it would be logical they forbid the nikaf — all the more to obligate him even though assisting has no substance; and therefore in cutting nails on Shabbat where there is no verse we say assisting has no substance:

(ד) As Tosafos there (daf 94b) s.v. but with a utensil all agree liable — and this is according to R' Yehuda who obligates in melacha she'eino tzricha legufa, end of Tosafos' words; and if so, even if an Israelite did it according to Tur who rules like R' Shimon that melacha she'eino tzricha legufa exempt — it would only be shevut unless he needs the nails for something:

(ה) Nevertheless if it is possible to remove by hand — meaning the gentile should remove by hand, for it is shevut d'shevut — preferable to the gentile cutting with a utensil:

(ו) And see s.k. 303 in name of Rivash — perhaps he intended with this to resolve the above difficulty on Tur who ruled melacha she'eino tzricha legufa exempt and nevertheless wrote in siman 328 s.k. 31: a nail that separated but not most of it — removed by hand exempt, but with a utensil liable; and why — behold it is melacha she'eino tzricha legufa? And it is strained to say Tur disputes the above Tosafos whose words are clear and reason compels them — rather Tur in siman 321 was not lenient regarding melacha she'eino tzricha legufa; and the practical difference is with a utensil liable if he needed the nail, which is melacha she'eino tzricha legufa; and in order to resolve this Magen Avraham wrote see s.k. 303 in name of Rivash that according to him it implies in removing a nail even if he does not need the nail nevertheless liable even for R' Shimon who exempts in melacha she'eino tzricha legufa — for removing nails is a toldah of shearing, and in shearing one is liable even if he does not need the wool, for all melachot are derived from the Mishkan — in the Mishkan they sheared tachash skins even though they did not need the wool at all; and if so one can say Tur disputes Tosafos and holds like Rivash, even though Tosafos' words reason compels them — nevertheless Rivash's words are words of reason:

(ז) But actual shevut — meaning the Israelite herself should remove them by hand, for it is only one shevut — forbidden:

(ח) As is written s.k. 326 forbidding immersion, etc. — this is Magen Avraham's explanation in siman 326 s.k. 8 in name of Rabbeinu Yerucham who wrote in name of Kolbo to forbid immersion on Shabbat because of squeezing hair; and even though we rule there is no squeezing in hair and there is only shevut if he squeezes them — nevertheless forbidden; and it is that we do not rule like him in this — meaning because it is not certain he will squeeze the hair but there is a decree lest he squeeze them; even if he squeezes there is only shevut — therefore permitted; but if we knew he would certainly squeeze — forbidden even though there is only shevut; and this is what Magen Avraham brought proof from siman 307 that one shevut is forbidden; rather he wanted to challenge from immersion itself. And to tell a gentile to do d'oraisa — meaning the gentile should cut them with a utensil which is nevertheless only shevut of amira l'goy; and it is that he already wrote one may not permit an Israelite shevut — meaning the woman should cut and remove by hand — because it is a severe shevut; and the Gemara uses shevut that has an act; nevertheless one might think to tell a gentile to do melacha where there is only shevut of amira l'goy which is a light shevut called in the Gemara shevut without an act — this he teaches is also forbidden:

(ט) As is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 197 because we rule immersion in its time is not a mitzvah but because of the mitzvah of onah — therefore we push it off lightly; and also requires study whether one may permit through scraping — aside from perhaps she will not scrape well and a little dirt and filth will remain; also concern for Ra'avad's words Shach brought in Yoreh De'ah siman 198 s.k. 25 who wrote the nail itself is a barrier:

(י) To permit through scraping — nevertheless permitted to tell a gentile to cut, as it should read; and meaning — until now he wrote the gentile should remove by hand which is shevut d'shevut; and at the end of his words now I have resolved and conclude that on the contrary it is preferable to tell a gentile to cut them — meaning with a utensil, for etc. melacha she'eino tzricha legufa exempt; and if so you might ask: telling a gentile to cut with a utensil would be shevut d'shevut — and we rely also on Tosafos who dispute Rivash that in shearing too we require needed for itself:

(כ) And Even HaEzer siman 303 s.k. 66 one cannot, etc. — meaning to resolve his own words that he wrote permitted to tell a gentile to cut with a utensil and rely on Tur and Tosafos that for them it is shevut d'shevut; on the contrary if he tells a gentile to remove with teeth or hands it is preferable — for this is certainly shevut d'shevut for all; for this he brought proof from Yoreh De'ah siman 303 s.k. 66 one cannot remove well with teeth; also clear there one cannot remove well by hand — therefore better the gentile cut with a knife to rely on Tur in Tosafos as above:
