Certainly his words are correct that if there is prohibition in letters also in a form it would be forbidden — and this is impossible, for they said one fulfills with decorated sheets on Pesach and behold fulfilling is on the first night which is Yom Tov, and they did not forbid making them l'chatchila except because of leavening — but if they are already made one fulfills with them l'chatchila; and see Pesachim daf 37a and decorated sheets means figures of birds and fish are drawn on them — see Maggid Mishneh chapter 5 of Laws of chametz and Matzah; therefore if this law existed all would be compelled to accept the distinction of R' Shmuel HaLevi that specifically when writing from another substance but if from the cake itself — permitted as I wrote. And further in my humble opinion it is not a question for Terumat HaDeshen whom Magen Avraham brought in siman 314 s.k. 5 that in a d'rabbanan prohibition psik reisha is permitted — if so this erasing which is not in order to write is only a d'rabbanan prohibition and since he does not intend — permitted; but even per the view of Magen Avraham there that even in a d'rabbanan prohibition psik reisha is forbidden — nevertheless behold where he does not intend and ruins and acts kil'achar yad — Magen Avraham permits there even with psik reisha — all the more so here where all are present that it is ruining and also kil'achar yad for erasing is not done thus — all the more so if he does not break with his hand only with his mouth in the manner of eating; and also a d'rabbanan prohibition that is not in order to write — obviously when he does not intend it is permitted even with psik reisha. Therefore in my humble opinion complete permission in this matter, and likewise all the Acharonim wondered at this prohibition only they did not resolve to be lenient since this came from the mouth of Maharam of Rothenburg see there in Mordechai; and in my humble opinion Maharam there was asked about children's cakes on which they write letters and words — how the children eat them on Yom Tov etc. see there in Mordechai; and since he wrote "the children's cakes" with the definite article — in my humble opinion they were accustomed to write intentionally that the children eat them as a segulah to become wise — see Aruch entry kaf-lamed-vav; and if so this was intentional and also not ruining — therefore he permitted only for children; but in another matter permitted even for adults, and one who is stringent should be stringent for himself and not for others:
