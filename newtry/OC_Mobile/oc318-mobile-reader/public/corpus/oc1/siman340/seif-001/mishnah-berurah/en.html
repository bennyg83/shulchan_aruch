(ב) Whether with a utensil — even though Mechaber equates hand to utensil only for the prohibition, for in all of them there is prohibition; but regarding liability there is a distinction between them, for by hand one is exempt in all cases where there is no manner of shearing thus on a weekday, and with a utensil one is liable in all cases, for it is a toldah of gozez; and what he wrote afterward that one is liable for two hairs is specifically with a utensil:

(ג) And liable for two hairs — and for one hair there is d'oraisa prohibition like every half-measure of all prohibitions; and the same law applies in all melachot of Shabbat — we do not require a measure for prohibition, only for liability to a chatat. And regarding cutting nails — Eshel Avraham wrote that even for one nail one is liable; and so it is proven above in siman 328 s.k. 31. And a woman who forgot to cut her nails from erev Shabbat and her immersion falls on Shabbat night — Magen Avraham concludes she should tell a non-Jew to cut with the hand, for it is shevut di-shevut in a place of mitzva; and if impossible by hand — permitted through his hand even with a utensil; and if there is no non-Jew — it appears one may rely in time of pressing need on scraping that she scrape under the nail, provided she examine well that there is no filth:

(ד) Even for one he is liable — and the reason is that even for one he is particular that he not appear like an elder; therefore it is considered melacha; and in Tosefta it states the same regarding one who picks white from among black:

(ה) One shall not wear — for it is the manner of women to be particular about this for adornment. And now we will explain in part the law of the melacha of shearing. A) One who shears wool or hair whether from domesticated animals or from wild animals is liable; and what is its measure for liability — enough to spin from it a thread whose length is close to four tefachim according to Rambam, and according to Rashi half of this. B) Liability for shearing is whether he shears from the living or from the dead, even from their shalach [meaning hide when stripped from the animal]; and specifically shearing — his liability is in all cases; but plucking from the living is exempt, for there is no manner of plucking because it hurts it, only to shear; but one who plucks by hand from the dead — the manner is to pluck as to shear, and he is liable; and accordingly those people dressed in hides of domesticated animals and wild animals must beware not to pluck hair from them on Shabbat; and in Sefer Chasidim it is forbidden to take lice from the hides on Shabbat; and all this in domesticated animals and wild animals; but in birds their manner is to remove feathers by plucking as in domesticated animals by shearing — therefore one who plucks a wing from a bird whether from the living or after death is liable, for their plucking is their shearing.
