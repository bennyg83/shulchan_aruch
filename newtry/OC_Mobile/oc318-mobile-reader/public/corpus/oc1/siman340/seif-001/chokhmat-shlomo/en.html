Seif 1: It is forbidden to remove his hair, etc. NB: See Magen Avraham s.k. 1, who discusses the dispute between the Taz and the Shach in Nekudot HaKesef; the Nekudot HaKesef wrote that this is assisting, and assisting has no substance. We read in the Yerushalmi, Megillah chapter 1 halacha 11: "And non-Jews nowadays are permitted to do so. Rav Ba said in the name of R. Yehudah: it is forbidden for a Jew to assist them, and forbidden to become an agent for them." I saw that the Korban HaEdah in Shirei Korban wondered at the Mishneh LaMelech at the end of Hilchot Ma'aseh HaKorbanot, who wrote on the Rambam's statement "and it is forbidden to assist them" that this is in the Yerushalmi, Megillah chapter 1. The Korban HaEdah wondered: this is an explicit Bavli in Zevachim 116b, so why did he attach it to the Yerushalmi? He left it requiring study. In my humble opinion the Mishneh LaMelech had a great intent. There is a distinction between the wording of the Bavli and the Yerushalmi. In our Talmud it says, "it is forbidden for a Jew to assist them and to become their agent"; in the Yerushalmi it says, "it is forbidden for a Jew to assist them, and forbidden to become their agent," repeating "forbidden" twice and dividing them into two clauses. There is great intent here. At first glance one might say the Bavli teaches one combined law: agency alone is certainly forbidden, because there is no agency for a non-Jew and the Jew is the one acting, but mere assistance might be permitted because assisting has no substance. Only where he acts as the non-Jew's agent by assisting would it be forbidden. It is proven in Bava Metzia chapter 1, regarding migo dezakhi lenafshei, that although there is no agency for transgression, where he has a share in the matter there is agency for transgression. So too one could say regarding agency for a non-Jew: where the non-Jew also acts, the Jew has the status of agency, and then it is not merely assistance; since there is agency, the two are considered like one body, and it does not have the law of assisting. Thus the Bavli teaches that assisting is forbidden, meaning where he performs the non-Jew's agency through that assistance. Therefore the Yerushalmi teaches by using "forbidden" twice, proving that even assistance without agency is forbidden. The Mishneh LaMelech cited the Yerushalmi to prove that the Rambam's wording, "it is forbidden to assist them and to become their agent," teaches each separately, either-or, and does not mean both together. The reason one might have thought the Bavli meant both together, despite the apparent either way argument, is this: if there is agency for a non-Jew, the act is the non-Jew's and there should be no issur; and if there is no agency for a non-Jew, it is only assisting, which has no substance. Yet one may say that just as where two people perform a melacha and each could not do it alone, each is considered as having done the whole act and each is liable for a chatat, so too here: where two act by agency of the other, each is considered as doing the whole, because one's agent is like oneself; only because the other acted with him is it considered as though he did all. But without agency, the one who assists has no substance. Therefore the Yerushalmi teaches that assistance itself is forbidden. In any event, it is proven from the Yerushalmi that it is forbidden to assist them even though the non-Jew is permitted, because it is forbidden for a Jew, and we do not say that assisting has no substance. This is proof for the Taz and against the Nekudot HaKesef. Examine very carefully.
Now regarding what is explained in Shulchan Aruch, that it is forbidden to remove his hair or nails, etc. We read in Keritot 17a: Rava can say to you that Rabban Gamliel holds like Rabbi Eliezer, who says that Shabbatot are like separate bodies, etc. My sharp friend R. Yaakov Kallir asked me: according to what Rashi wrote there on the previous folio, s.v. velo kibel minei, that according to Abaye, even according to Rabbi Eliezer he is liable only once, R. Chisda's question remains in place; see there. In truth there is no question, for one can say that indeed it does remain, and Rava only said that he himself can answer. But by way of analysis it occurred to me to say as follows. At first glance it is difficult: why does the Gemara set the baraitot against one another? Let us say that Rabban Gamliel's view that there is no awareness for a half-measure applies where there was deliberate awareness of Shabbat and error regarding the melachot. Then the error concerns the melacha, and when he later becomes aware, he becomes aware not of Shabbat but of the melacha; regarding this he holds there is no awareness for a half-measure. But where there was deliberate awareness of the melachot and error regarding Shabbat, the error concerns Shabbat, while he knew the melacha. When he later becomes aware, it is Shabbat that becomes known to him, and it is not relevant to say there is no awareness for a half-measure, for what became known is not the half-measure but Shabbat. What, then, is the difficulty? Perhaps Rabban Gamliel agrees in this case. If so, the baraita that teaches liable speaks of deliberate Shabbat and mistaken melachot, where there is no awareness for a half-measure, and the baraita that teaches exempt speaks of deliberate melachot and mistaken Shabbat, where there is awareness for a half-measure. And the earlier baraita in the dispute between R. Natan and the Rabbis can be read this way: "one who writes, etc., in two lapses," where Rabban Gamliel obligates, means deliberate Shabbat and mistaken melachot; because there is no awareness for a half-measure, he is liable. When it then says Rabban Gamliel agrees that if he wrote one letter on this Shabbat and one letter on another Shabbat he is exempt, that means the reverse case, deliberate melachot and mistaken Shabbat; there he agrees he is exempt. What is the Gemara asking? One cannot say that if it speaks of mistaken Shabbat and deliberate melachot, why mention one letter on this Shabbat and one on another Shabbat, when it could have stated the same Shabbat and taught that Rabban Gamliel agrees where there is deliberate melachot and mistaken Shabbat that awareness for a half-measure counts. For one may answer that it wanted to teach that the days between are awareness to divide. Why, then, does the Gemara ask? But it can be resolved: the Gemara asks only according to Rava, since according to him it is obvious that the days between are awareness to divide and there is no doubt. If so, Rabban Gamliel did not need to teach this, and the baraita should have taught the case within the same Shabbat if it speaks of mistaken Shabbat and deliberate melachot. But according to Abaye, who holds that this itself is his question, whether the days between are awareness to divide, one may well say as above: Rabban Gamliel agrees in the case of mistaken Shabbat and deliberate melachot, and the baraita mentions two Shabbatot for the novelty that the days between are awareness to divide. Therefore it is not difficult for Abaye, only for Rava does the Gemara ask properly. Examine carefully. This may be said by way of analysis. Since this question is a matter of reasoning and is a proper question, I did not refrain from writing it down. Perhaps God will grant me another time to explain it further. Examine very carefully.
