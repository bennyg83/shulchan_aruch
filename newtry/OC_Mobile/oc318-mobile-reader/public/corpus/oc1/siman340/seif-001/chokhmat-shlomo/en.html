Seif 1 forbidden to remove one's hair etc. Note: see in Magen Avraham s.k. 1 what he wrote the dispute of Taz and Shach in Knesset HaGedolah that the silver dots — he wrote it supports that there is no substance. Behold we learn in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Megillah halacha 11 and these are its words: and a gentile in our time is permitted to do so — Rav came in the name of R' Yehuda and it is forbidden for an Israelite to assist them and forbidden to be made his agent — end of his words. And I saw in Korban HaEdah in Shiray Korban that he wondered at the Moreh LaMelech at the end of Laws of Beit HaBechirah who wrote on what Rambam wrote there "and forbidden to assist them" — it is in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Megillah — end of his words. And Korban HaEdah wondered: behold the Talmud is explicit in tractate Zevachim daf 116b — why did he tie it to Yerushalmi see there that he left requires study. And in my humble opinion the Moreh LaMelech intended a great matter here — for there is room to distinguish between the language of the Babylonian Talmud and the Yerushalmi, for in our Talmud it is said "and forbidden for an Israelite to assist them and to be made his agent" and in Yerushalmi it says "and forbidden for an Israelite to assist them and forbidden to make their agency" — behold it uses the word forbidden twice and separated them into two clauses, and there is great intention in this — for initially it would appear that in the Talmud the intent is that one clause teaches that agency alone is certainly forbidden since there is no agency for a gentile the Israelite is the doer; but to assist alone it would appear permitted since assisting has no substance — only where he does his agency to assist then forbidden; for it is proven in the Talmud chapter 1 of Bava Metzia regarding miggo dezaki lenafshei that even though there is no agency for a matter of transgression where he has partnership in it there is agency for a matter of transgression too; and likewise one can say here regarding agency for a gentile where the gentile also acts he has the law of agency and if so in this he does not have the law of assisting — only since he has agency they are considered as one body and he does not have the law of assisting; therefore the Talmud teaches that forbidden to assist them — that is where he does his agency in this assistance then forbidden; therefore Yerushalmi uses forbidden twice — it is proven that even to assist without agency is also forbidden; and therefore it uses thus to prove what Rambam wrote "and forbidden to assist them and to make their agency" — each one separately or or, and their intent is not both together; and the reason why it would seem in our Talmud his intent is both together — even though necessarily if there is agency for a gentile then the act is the gentile's and there is no prohibition, and if there is no agency for a gentile then it was only assisting and there is no substance — it appears one can say like two who did it — this one cannot and that one cannot and they did it as two — each is considered as having done it all and each is liable for a chatat as if he did everything; likewise one can say here that with two who acted in the agency of the second each is considered as having done it all since his agent is like himself — only because he made him act with him it is as if he did it all; but without agency the one who assists has no substance; therefore it teaches that forbidden; and if so at any rate it is proven from Yerushalmi that forbidden to assist with them even though the gentile is permitted — nevertheless since it is forbidden for an Israelite therefore forbidden to assist, and we do not say assisting has no substance; and behold a proof from this for the view of Taz and not like Knesset HaGedolah — understand well. And behold in what is explained in Shulchan Aruch forbidden to remove one's hair or nails etc. We learn in tractate Kerisos daf 17a Rava told you R' Gamliel holds like R' Eliezer who said Shabbats are like themselves etc. And behold my friend the sharp R' Yaakov Kaller z"l asked me on what Rashi wrote there on the previous daf 17b s.v. velo kibel minei etc. that according to Abbaye even according to R' Eliezer he is liable only for one — the difficulty of R' Chananel remains in its place see there. And behold if in truth there is no difficulty one can say it is so — only that Rava says he can answer for himself; nevertheless by way of pilpul it arose in my heart to say that initially it is difficult — why does the Talmud challenge the Baraisos against each other — let us say thus: what R' Gamliel holds there is no awareness for half a measure — that is if it is with intentional Shabbos and unintentional melachos, for then the unintentional act is regarding the melacha and when he becomes aware he did not become aware of Shabbos — he became aware only of the melacha — in this R' Gamliel holds there is no awareness for half a measure; but if it is intentional melachos and unintentional Shabbos — then the unintentional act is regarding Shabbos but he knew the melacha — and if so after he becomes aware of Shabbos it is that he became aware and therefore it is not relevant to say there is no awareness for half a measure — for he did not become aware of the half measure but of Shabbos — and what difficulty that perhaps R' Gamliel agrees in this and if so on the contrary what is taught liable — that is with intentional Shabbos and unintentional melachos, for in that there is no awareness for half a measure therefore liable; and what is taught exempt above in the Baraisa in the dispute of R' Nachman and the rabbis — the intent is thus: one who writes etc. in two concealments — R' Gamliel makes liable — that is if it was with intentional Shabbos and unintentional melachos — in this since there is no awareness for half a measure therefore liable; and afterward it says and R' Gamliel agrees that if he wrote one letter on this Shabbat and one letter on that Shabbat he is exempt — that is for example when it was the reverse with intentional melachos and unintentional Shabbos — in this he agrees he is exempt — and why does it challenge. And one should not say that if it deals thus with unintentional Shabbos and intentional melachos — why specifically mention that he wrote one letter on this Shabbat and one on that Shabbat — it should mention on the same Shabbat itself that R' Gamliel agrees with intentional melachos and unintentional Shabbos that there is awareness for half a measure — one can say it wants to teach us that on days intervening there is awareness for a portion — and why challenge. However one can say it comes out well — the Talmud challenges only Rava since according to him it is obvious that on days intervening there is awareness for a portion and there is no doubt in the matter — if so R' Gamliel did not need to teach us this — and if so it should challenge that it should teach us on this Shabbat itself if it deals with unintentional Shabbos and intentional melachos; but for Abbaye who holds this is his question — if on days intervening there is awareness for a portion — in this one can say well as I wrote that on Shabbat R' Gamliel agrees and it deals with unintentional Shabbos and intentional melachos and mentions two Shabbats as an extra that on days intervening there is awareness for a portion — therefore there is no difficulty for Abbaye, only for Rava it challenges well — understand well. Likewise one can say by way of pilpul.
