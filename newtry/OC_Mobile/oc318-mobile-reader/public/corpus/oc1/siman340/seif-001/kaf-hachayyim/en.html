1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to remove, etc. One who removes hair or nails by hand, whether from himself or another person, is exempt but the act is forbidden. If he uses a utensil, he is liable to bring a sin offering, and liability for hair requires two hairs. Tur. Rambam writes the same in chapter 9, halachos 8 and 9. One who uses a utensil is liable even when removing another person's hair. Bach; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, likewise writes that since we rule like the Sages, there is no distinction between cutting one's own hair and another's: with a utensil one is always liable, while by hand one is always exempt. He adds that Yerushalmi, chapter Elu Devarim, proves that all opinions exempt one who removes another person's hair even with a utensil; see there. In practice, we follow the Babylonian Talmud, with practical relevance to the required rectification. See Tosafos, Shabbos 94b, beginning with the word 'But,' who writes that liability follows Rabbi Yehudah, who holds one liable for labor not needed for its own purpose. Rabbi Shimon exempts. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1, writes that this is Shulchan Aruch's view; see there. Rivash, siman 394, however, holds that one is liable here even though the labor is not needed for its own purpose, because in the Mishkan they sheared the hides of techashim and did not need the hair, but only the hides; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1, cites him, and in paragraph 2 writes that Shulchan Aruch follows Rivash, not Tosafos; see there. Nishmas Adam, klal 21, paragraph 3, likewise writes that Tur follows Rivash; see there. This also appears to be the view of Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2, and Chayei Adam there, paragraph 4. See below, paragraph 6, and above, siman 278, and examine carefully.
