(א) Whether by hand — see in Mishna Berurah that by hand one is exempt and forbidden, and this is per the Sages against R' Eliezer; and see in Ran who wrote that according to R' Chananel the halacha follows R' Eliezer, since they explain the reason of the Sages and I did not understand his explanation, for R' Eliezer's first statement is specifically per the Sages; and furthermore what is the language "they explain its reason" that he says; and furthermore that Rabeinu Asher in chapter Kol Gadol siman 16 proves explicitly that R' Chananel holds that plucking wool by hand is exempt like the Sages against R' Eliezer; but now that we merited to have R' Chananel's explanation we found explicitly that R' Chananel's words refer to R' Eliezer's second case of a large woman and a dwarf, where several Sages explain the reason why he is liable; and in the first case of R' Eliezer R' Chananel rules explicitly like the Sages — see there; and likewise on daf 74b — see there; and it appears Ran did not have the R' Chananel explanation we have, only some abbreviation mistakenly copied to him:

(ב) And liable, etc. — behold Tosafos wrote that this is specifically according to R' Yehuda, for melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah one is liable; but according to R' Shimon exempt even with a utensil; and because of this Acharonim wondered at Rosh and Tur who copied this law — behold they ruled like R' Shimon; and also Mechaber — how did he rule anonymously here, and in siman 316 seif 8 he sided with the first view to exempt, and Magen Avraham in s.k. 1 wrote that according to one who holds melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah exempt — it deals when he needs the hair; but he challenged his words, for if so why did he rule that in picking white from among black even for one he is liable — for nevertheless he does not need the hair itself, for even if we say it deals when he needs the hair — that one alone has no measure for liability; and because of this challenge one found someone who challenged thus; and it appears from this challenge Gra moved from Magen Avraham's words and cited on the law of picking black, etc., anonymously like Rambam's view that the halacha follows R' Yehuda in melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah — end; and likewise in Makos 20b he cited their words in name of Rashbam also on this statement of picking, etc. (and perhaps one can say with difficulty Magen Avraham's words, since once shearing is considered melacha even in one hair because through this he beautifies — automatically it is considered needed for its purpose also in what this hair alone needs); and also according to Magen Avraham's words it would be forced to explain that which is above in siman 328 s.k. 31 regarding hanging skin-tags and nails — see there also as needed; and likewise in responsum Chacham Tzvi siman 82 he rejected with two hands because of this challenge. And behold Gra wrote that Shulchan Aruch ruled anonymously here like Rambam's view in melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah that one is liable; but what shall we do with the challenge of all Acharonim that Shulchan Aruch contradicts itself, as above; and behold Rivash in siman 394 [and it was brought above in siman 303 and also in this siman] wrote that the melacha of shearing is considered melacha for all, even when he does not need the hair, for in the Mishkan they sheared tachash skins and not for the wool and hair but for the hide, such as tachash skins — and therefore one is liable whenever it is for its purpose even though he does not need the hair; and melacha needed for its purpose is, end; and similarly Rivash wrote almost the same in Ran's chiddushim on Shabbos: dispute by hand — Rashi [reason] on account of gozez, for even though he does not need the shearing, since he needs to beautify himself with this shearing — even in this he is liable for gozez, similar to shearing the shalach, where he is liable for gozez even where he does not need shearing but that the hide remain without hair so it be fit for tanning, end; and it is possible to say this is Shulchan Aruch's view and not like Tosafos; and I found in Chacham Tzvi that he also agreed that Shulchan Aruch's view is like Rivash and not like Tosafos; and behold I saw in Sefer Seder Taharah who wondered at Chacham Tzvi for agreeing to Rivash's view and wrote — behold Rosh wrote explicitly in Bechoros 25 and also brought there in name of Ramban that plucking wool from the animal before slaughter and likewise feathers from the bird is melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah — and therefore they hold also like Tosafos; and in truth it is no proof at all, as he himself rejected afterward, for it is different there, for that plucking is not for the hide but for slaughter; but where it is for the hide, even though he does not need shearing — they hold he is liable, as is written regarding gozez from the shalach; and likewise in our matter, since he takes nails or hair to beautify himself — this is called for his purpose, as above in name of Ran. And now that we clarified that this sugya in Shabbos 94b deals when he does not need the hair and nails — we are forced to say according to many early great ones who also hold like Rivash and Ran above — behold they are R' Chananel, Ramban, Semag, Rosh; and we explain them one by one. R' Chananel — he himself ruled like R' Shimon, and also ruled explicitly in our matter that with a utensil one is liable to a chatat; and also Ramban — he himself brought in chapter Kol Kitvei and proved that Rif rules like R' Shimon, and here Rif himself copied the entire sugya of hanging skin-tags and nails; and also Rosh and Semag — they explicitly agreed the halacha follows R' Shimon that melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah is exempt, and nevertheless both copied the entire sugya of hanging skin-tags and nails and picking white from among black that there is liability to a chatat — and therefore we must say all these poskim hold like Ran and Rivash above that this is called for its purpose and therefore Shulchan Aruch ruled anonymously thus. And behold I saw that Gra in this siman wrote from this sugya proof for Rambam's ruling that melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah one is liable; but according to all the aforementioned geonim who hold melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah exempt and nevertheless all copied the sugya — therefore they hold like Ran and Rivash above; and see in siman 316 end of seif 18 in Beur HaGra where he sided there the halacha is melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah exempt — and therefore he shows favor to every view that has a great source in the Talmud. And behold regarding a woman who forgot to cut nails from erev Shabbat and her immersion night falls on Shabbat — certainly l'chatchila proper that she beware to tell a non-Jew to cut with the hand and not with a utensil, for it is complete melacha according to Rambam; but when impossible by hand Magen Avraham sided to be lenient through a non-Jew even with a utensil, like Nekudot HaKesef in Yoreh De'ah siman 198, as we wrote above that here it is called melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah, for her intent is not adornment but for immersion; and it appears regarding toenails certainly better to permit through scraping than through a non-Jew; see in Perishah there in name of Maharar Daniel:

(ג) For two hairs — see in Mishna Berurah, and it is from Magen Avraham; and likewise is the agreement of all Acharonim for prohibition, and not like Eliyah Rabbah; and likewise Rambam explicitly in chapter 9 law 23 that all melachot of Shabbat — if one did half the measure there is prohibition; and automatically included is writing one letter or removing one hair and the like. And regarding whether it is d'oraisa or rabbinically — in Magen Avraham it appears it is d'oraisa like every half-measure of forbidden foods, for we rule like R' Yochanan that it is forbidden from the Torah, for the reason is it is fit to combine, as stated in Yoma daf 74 regarding shearing; and likewise if we say because it is written "all chelev" — reasonable we derive from it also for the entire Torah, as Tosafos wrote there that R' Yehuda holds because it is fit to combine therefore the Torah forbade; and if so the same in anything fit to combine [and Chacham Tzvi's words in siman 86 who wanted to distinguish with one answer between equals and wanted to prove his view from Tosafos are very strained]; and likewise is Rashi explicitly daf 74 s.v. vekhi and in Hagahot chapter 23 siman 11; and likewise Or Zarua in Halakhot Gedolot letter 59 and in Meiri's chiddushim daf 74 and Rabbeinu Yerucham part 2 chapter 14; and do not challenge our words from what they said daf 3: all exempt matters of Shabbat — exempt but forbidden, and Rashi explained forbidden rabbinically; and behold on daf 102b it states if they are not read together exempt, like one letter and similarly several mishnayot; and according to our words above it is Torah prohibition, and only exempt from chatat — one can say what Rashi wrote forbidden rabbinically intended to include all exempt matters that in any case have rabbinic prohibition; and the same here that there is Torah prohibition; and with this Nachalat Tzvi's words on writing are somewhat challenged, for he contradicts what he wrote at the beginning of the section; and perhaps what he wrote in all these is not precise and for practical law one should not move at all from all the aforementioned poskim's words:
