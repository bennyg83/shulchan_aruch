(א) One who cuts with a utensil — whether for himself or for another — is liable, and by hand exempt. So Maggid Mishneh explains Rashi, and so Maggid Mishneh regarding a wart. And Taz wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 198: a woman who forgot to remove her nails from erev Shabbat and the night of Shabbat is her night of immersion — she should not tell a non-Jew to cut them, for it is possible through scraping; and why should we permit telling a non-Jew? And also she tilts her hand and assists, as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 181; and therefore she should scrape well. And the author of Shach in his book Nekudat HaKesef elaborated and wrote it is permitted, for assisting has no substance, as is written siman 378 s.k. 7; and also it is melacha she'eino tzricha legufa, as Tosafos wrote. Nevertheless, if it is possible to remove them by hand, it is better to remove them by hand, end of discussion; and see s.k. 303 in name of Rivash; and it appears to me one may tell a non-Jew to remove them by hand, for it is shevut d'shevut, and as is written siman 307 seif 5 it is permitted in a place of mitzvah; but actual shevut forbidden to do, as is written siman 326 forbidding immersion because of the decree of squeezing hair even though it is only shevut; and to tell a non-Jew to do d'oraisa melacha too does not seem correct to permit, for we push off immersion nowadays lightly, as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 197; and also requires study whether one may permit through scraping; therefore better to tell a non-Jew to cut them by hand; and if impossible by hand — permitted with a utensil, for Tur and his colleagues rule melacha she'eino tzricha legufa exempt; and see Eliyah Rabbah; and Even HaEzer siman 303 s.k. 66 one cannot remove well with teeth; and in Laws of Simcha for Mahariv wrote that in a doubtful case one tells a non-Jew to cut them, for shevut in a place of mitzvah is permitted, as they say in the chapter HaDar, end of his words.
