[14] [Levush] That opened, etc. Shabbos 75 states that Rav said one who tightens a sewing thread is liable. Rashi explains that this is a sewn garment whose thread was left long and whose pieces separated, and he pulls the thread to tighten the seam. The Beit Yosef writes that the Rambam appears to mean one who stretches the thread he intends to sew with so that it will not be bent, and is liable because this is a necessary preparation for sewing. Olat Shabbos wonders why the Shulchan Aruch omitted this. In my humble opinion, the Beit Yosef overlooked the Maggid Mishneh, who makes clear that the Rambam also rules like Rashi, differing only in the reason: according to Rashi the act is actual sewing, while according to the Rambam it is a preparation necessary for sewing. It appears that this is why the Shulchan Aruch omitted any separate law. As practical halachah, it appears to me that one should not impose liability for merely stretching the thread one intends to sew with. I saw that the Semag, Kol Bo, Rabbenu Yerucham, and all the compilers rule like Rashi concerning a sewn garment, and not one cites a distinct Rambam law. It is therefore clear that the Rambam too explains the case like Rashi and differs only in conceptual reason. When I lectured here in Prague on the three adjacent statements in that Gemara, I explained at length why the Rambam disagrees with Rashi's reason. There is a major difficulty in the Gemara's ordering, for it places this law beside tearing in order to sew rather than earlier beside sewing. I therefore explained that according to the Rambam tightening is not actual sewing but only a need of sewing, like tearing in order to sew. According to Rashi, this ordering remains difficult. Incidentally, I explained in that derashah why the Gemara says that one who knows how to calculate astronomical seasons and constellations but does not do so may not have teachings reported in his name. At first glance, what measure-for-measure relationship is there? In Avodah Zarah 35, Rava says not to report a teaching in the name of Ivo because he eats non-Jewish bread. Torat HaBayit explains that one should not repeat a halachic teaching in his name. Bedek HaBayit explains that the Sages used their wisdom to prohibit non-Jewish bread, and one who refuses to observe that wisdom is unworthy of being regarded as a sage whose teaching is cited. Likewise, 'it is prohibited to relate from him' regarding one who refuses to calculate the astronomical seasons means not to report a teaching in his name. The purpose of calculating them is that he be regarded as wise and understanding, as stated there. Since he refuses to act as a wise man should, the response is measure for measure that his teachings are not cited as those of a sage.
