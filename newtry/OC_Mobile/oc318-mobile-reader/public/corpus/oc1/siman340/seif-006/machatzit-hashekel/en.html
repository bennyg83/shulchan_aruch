(א) (s.k. 6) Like letter forms, etc. And Rama wrote in responsum, etc. permitted to open it, etc.; and he brought proof from what they say in chapter HaBoneh: one who writes on two pages of a ledger and they are accustomed to use them together — liable. And Rashi explained: shopkeepers have boards smeared with wax and they mark on them as above — if they are accustomed and read them together, such as he wrote on two edges of the boards adjacent to each other — liable:

(ב) And now one must infer: why teach "one who writes" — let it teach a greater novelty: even if already written on the edges of the boards — if he opens or closes the ledger liable, for it is writing or erasing; or that this is not called writing or erasing — and if so, meaning it is proven there is no Torah liability; but nevertheless d'rabbanan prohibition exists. For if so in the Mishnah that teaches on two pages of ledger and they are not accustomed together — exempt — this law that when not accustomed they are exempt but forbidden was already taught there in a Baraisa elsewhere more — it should have taught if already written on the edges of the boards but distant from each other and he closes and thereby brings them together — exempt but forbidden; or that in such a case even l'chatchila permitted; and furthermore he brought proof from what is stated: he wrote one letter in Tiberias and one in Tzippori — this proof is also brought in Taz s.k. 2; and see there where he elaborated:
