(א) Wax. And Bach wrote: and therefore one must be careful when wax drips on a sefer not to remove it, and even if it dripped only on one letter — and if on two letters he is liable to a chatas, end of his words. And in responsum Shevut Yaakov vol. 2 siman 4 he wondered on this — what does erasing have to do with this? — and from this he wrote specifically in a ledger that has a wax receptacle it is forbidden, but on parchment there is no concern of prohibition unless he wants to remove the wax with a finger and fingernail, for there is concern of smoothing; but to remove wax from parchment in a manner that he does not touch the wax with his hand or fingernails — permitted; Or Sameach; and Yad Aharon agreed with him; see there; and see in Shachnei HaGolah. And regarding a sefer Torah whether it is fit to read if a drop of wax dripped on it or if this happened on Shabbat what is its law — I wrote its law in siman 143 s.k. 9; see there what I wrote. Ink that fell on a sefer — one should not lick it with his tongue and not rinse it, for he erases. Shulchan Aruch HaRav siman 496. Forbidden to put wax on a sefer for a mark because of smoothing. (ב) Like letter forms. Requires study — for if so, even if forms are written on it it should be forbidden, for one who forms a shape is liable — and if so one who erases is also liable; requires study. And Rama wrote in responsum siman 119: a sefer on whose pages letters are written — permitted to open and close it, for since it is made to close and open there is no concern of erasing; Or Sameach elaborated. And Levush wrote it is close to chatas liability; and as above to be stringent — Rama's proofs are not compelling; and in Shachnei HaGolah wrote the practice is to permit; and so R' Shalom Halevi siman 56; and Taz too ruled to permit; see there. And Shachnei HaGolah wrote further: specifically when they write on cakes letters from another substance — but when the writing is from the cake itself in a mold or by hand it is permitted; and therefore they eat cakes that have designs on them, end of his words; see there; requires study.
