3) One who shears wool or hair from a domesticated or wild animal, whether living or dead, or even from its hide, is liable. Likewise, one who plucks a feather from a bird, whether living or dead, as Chayei Adam, klal 21, paragraph 2, writes, performs a derivative of shearing. One who spins wool directly from a living animal, however, is exempt because this is not the ordinary manner of shearing. Rambam, chapter 9, halachah 7. This implies that one who plucks wool from an animal is exempt because this is not the ordinary manner of shearing. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, distinguishes that one who plucks from a living animal is exempt because its normal method is shearing, while one who plucks from a dead animal is liable. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 5, writes the same, based on Taz at the end of siman 336 in the name of Yerushalmi; see there. We already wrote there in paragraph 67 that Yad Aharon proves from the Babylonian Talmud and Rambam, chapter 9, contrary to Yerushalmi, that even plucking from a dead animal incurs no liability to bring a sin offering but only a rabbinic prohibition; see there. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah here writes the same; see there. Some, however, hold that one who plucks from an animal is liable for uprooting something from its place of growth, as stated in Beis Yosef, siman 498, and Magen Avraham there, paragraphs 22 and 23; see there. Eshel Avraham here, paragraph 3, writes the same; see there. Chayei Adam, klal 21, paragraph 1, however, writes that reaping does not ordinarily apply to skin except with a utensil; see there. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes the same in the name of Chiddushei Rashba; see there. All this applies when one needs the wool or hair. If he does not need it, this is labor not needed for its own purpose. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4. See above, paragraph 1, where we wrote that some hold one liable for shearing even when not needed for its own purpose, because in the Mishkan the hides of techashim and rams were sheared although their hair and wool were not needed; see there and our words on siman 278.
