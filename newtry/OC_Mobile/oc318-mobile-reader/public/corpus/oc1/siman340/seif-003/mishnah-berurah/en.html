(א) One who erases ink, etc. — and all the more if he erases two letters that were written improperly in order to write two other letters in their place — he is liable, for this is the primary av melacha of erasing:

(ב) That is on parchment — in their time they were accustomed to write on parchment; and the same applies to anything similar; and even on wood there is writing and erasing, for so it was in the Mishkan on the boards in order to match them; and see in Biur Halacha that we explained that Mechaber deals when not in the place of the letters and erases in order to write in that place; and see in Bach that likewise one should beware when ink falls on letters or wax drips not to erase the smudge, for through erasing it the letters become unrecognizable and it is like erasing in order to write, and he is liable; and even if the smudge was on one letter one should beware, for there is prohibition in any case; and they brought Eliyah Rabbah and Taz and Eshel Avraham; also in Magen Avraham it appears he holds that in wax on a book there is erasing; and the same applies to dripping milk; and see in Biur Halacha. And if it is found thus in the middle of reading — although forbidden to remove the wax and milk — nevertheless one should not take out another scroll for this, and he reads that word by heart [Peri Megadim; and so in Chayei Adam in laws of Torah scrolls; and in Darkhei Chayim he wrote that if found thus between one person and another one may take out another if the letters are not recognizable — I copied this above in siman 332 in Biur Halacha — see there]:

(ג) Wax on the ledger — meaning their manner was to coat pages of their ledgers with wax in order to inscribe on it; and if some wax smudge fell on the page and he erases the upper wax so he can inscribe on the wax as is his manner — he is liable for erasing:

(ד) Two letters — and for less than this there is also prohibition, only the distinction is for liability to a chatat, as above:

(ה) Liable — and this is when his intent at the time of erasing is that it be fit whenever he wishes to write two letters, and we do not require that he intend to write on Shabbat; and the same in demolishing in order to build, and tearing in order to sew. If ink or another moist thing fell on a book — he should not lick it with his tongue and should not rinse it with water because he erases. Forbidden to place wax on the book as a marker in the place where he wishes to study tomorrow because of concern for smearing [Acharonim]:

(ו) Forbidden to break — and permitted to give to a child:

(ז) That is written on it — and specifically when they write letters on cakes from another matter; but when the writing is from the cake itself by mold or by hand — permitted, for there is no writing on it and consequently erasing does not apply. And likewise if he wrote the letters in honey mixed with water or other fruit juices — likewise no stringency:

(ח) Like letters — and some are stringent even in drawings:

(ט) That it is erasing — and even though not in order to write — rabbinic prohibition; however even without intent to erase it is psik reisha and forbidden even rabbinically, as is proven in siman 316 seif 4; and see in Sefer Dagul MeRevavah who sided to be lenient in the essence of this law, and they brought it in Shaarei Teshuvah; and one may rely on him when he does not break in the place of the letters with his hand but with his mouth in the manner of eating. And a book written on the edges of its pages with letters or words — some forbid opening and closing it on Shabbat, for through opening he breaks the letters and it is like erasing, and likewise when closing it is like writing; but Rama's view in responsum is to be lenient, and so is the view of many Acharonim; their reason is since it is made to open and close constantly there is no erasing and writing, and it is like a door constantly closed and opened — there is no building and demolishing; and such is the custom.
