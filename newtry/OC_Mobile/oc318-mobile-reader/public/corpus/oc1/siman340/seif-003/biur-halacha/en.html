(א) One who erases ink, etc. — behold the primary av melacha of erasing is when he erases two letters in order to write two other letters in their place, for all melachot of Shabbat we derive from the Mishkan; and in the Mishkan regarding this matter they sometimes wrote erroneously on the boards that they wrote on them as a sign in order to match them, and erased in order to write others in their place [and even though in the Mishkan sometimes an error fell in one letter — Chazal hold erasing is not considered from writing and we require specifically two letters also]; nevertheless we derive from this that likewise erasing ink or another smudge like this in order to write two letters in this place — liable, for the letter first written in error is as if it did not exist; and the primary liability of erasing is because he intended to write in this place; therefore liable even for erasing an ink smudge [Rosh, brought in Beit Yosef]. Peri Megadim wrote: just as we say in the Gemara that if he corrected one letter to complete the book he is liable because there one letter is considered significant — the same if he erased a corrupted letter in order to write another in its place and thereby the book is completed — liable for erasing — see there; and if so according to what we explain below from Rashi and Ri'az that even if he corrected one letter in the middle of the book he is liable for writing — automatically the same when he erases in this manner in order to write another in its place properly — liable for erasing; and if so what Mechaber wrote that we require in the place of erasing ink room to write two letters specifically — it deals when ink fell not in the place of letters, and therefore we require the place be fit to write two letters; but if ink fell on some letter and was corrupted and he erases in order to write another in its place properly — liable for erasing on this letter alone. And since we deal with laws of erasing I saw fit to copy what Peri Megadim sided on this: if in the erasing alone there was some fixing — such as something was written there that he was obligated to his fellow or his fellow to him and the like, and now we need to erase it — liable for the erasing alone even though not in order to write; and specifically in erasing a smudge and the like we require intent in order to write two letters; but here where the erasing itself is fixing — end. And I saw fit to bring several proofs for his words: just as it states in the mishnah regarding tearing in order to sew, for so it was in the Mishkan as stated in the Gemara — nevertheless it is known that even not in order to sew, whenever there is fixing in the tearing he is liable, for it is like in order to sew; and as we clarified at the end of the siman — the same in erasing, even though the mishnah states erasing in order to write and the reason is because so it was in the Mishkan that they erased some letter they erred in in order to write another in its place — nevertheless where in the erasing itself there is fixing it appears like in order to write; and there is proof from Tosefta chapter 12 law 7: you have none liable except one who erases in order to write, and one who erases in order to fix anything — liable; and see Rambam chapter 10 law 15 that wrote in demolishing we require in order to build, but if he demolished by way of spoiling — exempt — implies if not by way of spoiling but for some need it is like in order to build, since it was for some fixing it emerges from spoiling; and the same in our matter regarding erasing, for Rambam includes demolishing and erasing in one category, as is proven in chapter 1 law 18 — see there:

(ב) That is on parchment — see in Mishna Berurah where I copied Beit Yosef's practical law and his support; and even though in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 4 he disagrees that removing wax from parchment is not included in erasing but in smearing when he spreads it by hand or with fingernails on parchment and paper [and a ledger is different, for their custom was to coat pages with wax in order to inscribe on the wax, as Rashi explained; and therefore when more wax dripped on the page the pages stick together; and therefore when he erases the upper wax in order to have place to write on it — included in erasing in order to write; unlike when wax drips on parchment or paper it does not stick so much and the writing is inside like placed in a box; and proof for this is in tefillin it is not invalidated because of lack of order when he removed the wax, as above in siman 332 s.k. 23 in Magen Avraham — therefore when he scrapes and removes wax it is not erasing but removing cover from above] — therefore he wrote that if he wants to remove wax from parchment in a manner that he not touch the wax by hand or fingernails, such as when the wax dried and he can remove it back-handed so the wax falls by itself — permitted to do so; nevertheless in practice one should not rule leniently against Bach and all the many great ones who hold like him; and Bach's view and all the above is since in any case the writing was smudged by the wax and through removing it the writing is revealed — included in erasing in order to write; and even though regarding tefillin we hold that through dripping wax above the letters beneath the wax are not nullified so as to later invalidate for lack of order — nevertheless regarding Shabbat, since initially they could not read it and now when he removes the wax he fixes the letters so they can read — included in erasing in order to fix, as written above; again I found one who also challenged Bach's words from this law of tefillin, as above; and it appears to me to answer as I wrote that nevertheless it is included in erasing in order to fix. And see in Chiddushei R' Akiva Eiger on siman 332 that Shevut Yaakov's view is in essence there is no erasing regarding wax, and nevertheless he agreed to the practical law also with all the great ones that one should not remove wax from letters of a book on Shabbat on account of fixing a utensil by fixing the book — see there. And Peri Megadim's view in Eshel Avraham appears he also holds like Bach that in wax on parchment and paper there is erasing and not like Shevut Yaakov — they did not mention except that he sided that specifically not in the place of letters then liable for erasing when intent is in order to write in that place; but in the place of letters there is no erasing and the lower writing automatically exists — only prohibition of moving wax exists — see there; nevertheless he did not fill his heart to rule thus in practice, for he wrote regarding scraping wax from two letters through a non-Jew — requires study; and one letter he permitted scraping through a non-Jew; and in Sefer Birkei Yosef it also appears he sided with Bach's practical law. And know that according to Bach and supporters the same when pages stuck together with wax in the place of letters — beware not to separate them [Peri Megadim]. And know further Shevut Yaakov's words are specifically regarding wax; but regarding ink that stuck on a letter — he also agrees there is erasing, for here the ink is not like cover from above, for behold the letter was nullified initially thereby, as above in siman 332 seif 17; and through erasing the ink a letter is newly formed; and even though it is engraved — no distinction for Shabbat, as Rosh and Rashba wrote end of chapter HaBoneh; and Rashba's view who holds engraved applies also to Shabbat is solitary, as Chiddushei R' Akiva Eiger wrote on siman 332 — see there [but accordingly his liability would be for writing as there, in the Gemara regarding removing the roof of a chet and making two zayins; and from Bach it appears somewhat his liability is for erasing, as in Eliyah Rabbah and Eshel Avraham — requires study].
