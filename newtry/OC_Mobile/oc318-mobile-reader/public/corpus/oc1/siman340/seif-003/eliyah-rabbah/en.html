[5] Or wax, etc. It is prohibited to put wax on a book because of smoothing. Sefer Zichronot prohibits even touching the wax, as cited by Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah. If ink or another liquid falls on books, one should not lick it with the tongue or wash it with water because of erasing, as stated in Sefer Chasidim 902. If wax drips upon letters, one must take care not to remove it on Shabbos, because removing it entails liability for erasing. Even with one letter there is at least a prohibition.
[6] [Levush] Because of the intention to write, etc. Olat Shabbos writes that the Shulchan Aruch implies one is liable for erasing even if he does not intend to write in that place, and he rejects this. I found in Tosefta, chapter 12: one who writes destructively is liable; one who erases destructively is exempt. Liability exists only for one who erases in order to write. One who erases in order to repair even the smallest amount is liable.
[7] To break the cakes, etc. The Rama in responsum 119 permits this specifically when one eats the cake, because then he performs an irreversible act. If he merely breaks it without eating, it is permitted. According to the Taz, however, once the cake has been broken it cannot be restored into one connected piece, so it should be prohibited even without eating. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that the prohibition applies only when the writing on the cake is made from another substance. If the writing is formed from the cake itself by a mold or by hand, it is permitted. Therefore people eat cakes bearing designs. The same applies on Yom Tov. See siman 519, seif katan 6. The Agudah writes that he was accustomed to write with honey mixed with a little water, because it is fruit juice and does not constitute erasing. The Magen Avraham questions this because the mark is nevertheless visible. There is no difficulty: even though it is temporarily visible, it does not endure and its prohibition is only rabbinic, as explained below. Since one does not intend to write, it is permitted.
[8] [Levush] The same law applies, etc. The Rama there permits it because the object is designed to be opened and closed, so this is not the normal manner of erasing. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah cites many great authorities who agree and writes that this is the practice. The Perishah likewise compares it to a door that opens and closes, and the Taz argues at length to permit.
[9] [Levush] It is rabbinically prohibited, etc. For a minor it is permitted, because beit din is not commanded to separate him from the act, as stated by the Mordechai. See siman 342.
[10] In the form of letters, etc. This requires study, because if so, even drawings should be prohibited, since one who forms a picture is liable, and one who erases it should likewise be liable, as stated by the Magen Avraham. He therefore understands that 'letters' is not precise and the same applies to drawings, as he writes in seif katan 4. Yet this requires study, because Shabbos 75 states that one who forms a picture on a vessel is liable because of the finishing blow. Why, then, should erasing the picture incur liability for erasing? This also creates a difficulty for the Rambam I will cite in seif katan 1, who holds that pictures fall under writing. See Rambam, chapter 10, halachah 16.
