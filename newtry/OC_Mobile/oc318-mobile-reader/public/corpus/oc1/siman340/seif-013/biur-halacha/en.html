(א) One does not break, etc. — this seif and the adjacent one are from Rambam's language; his words in this seif derive from what he said in Beitzah daf 32: the reason is he makes it a utensil; and at first glance according to what is proven in the adjacent seif that tearing in order to fix is liable for tearing — why not say here on account of tearing; and furthermore from Rambam's language in chapter 23 it appears it is rabbinic, for he included it there with several matters known to be rabbinic; and if there were tearing concern it would be d'oraisa prohibition; and in truth Peri Megadim already challenged this and it remains in difficulty; and what one of the Acharonim wanted to derive that tearing applies only in something sewn and woven joined from many bodies as in curtains, but not in something of one body like hide and paper — this is not, for Yerushalmi explicitly deals tearing also in hide; and Neshmat Adam wanted to say thus and rejected it from this Yerushalmi. And were I not afraid I would say a novel matter that tearing in order to fix applies only when he tears something in the middle and needs fixing of both parts, as in the Mishkan where a curtain had a tear they tore and sewed it [Shabbos 74]; and likewise what is said there Shabbos 104 regarding a servant like a pouch — the benefit is for the whole garment; and when he tears in anger Rambam obligates because through this he soothes himself — like fixing the whole body of the garment; unlike when he tears something from the side of the garment to fix the garment that was long or defective at its edge — the torn piece is not fixed at all — tearing in order to fix does not apply, only another name fixing a utensil by fixing the garment [and if particular about measure it appears there is also mechatech concern; see above siman 322 what is written on mechatech]; and the matter depends on the fixing whether complete fixing — liable for striking the final hammer-blow completing the utensil or garment; and if not complete fixing — rabbinic; therefore in our matter where he tears some piece of paper from a whole page in order to use it for something and in the page from which paper was torn he does not fix it at all and possibly spoils it — perhaps tearing in order to fix does not apply and depends only on fixing done to the torn piece of paper; and Rambam holds what the Gemara said there he makes it a utensil means it appears like fixing a utensil that is not a complete utensil thereby; therefore Rambam included this law in every matter rabbinically; and accordingly if he tears the paper into many pieces and needs each — liable, for he tears in order to fix; and he supported from what is said in Moed Katan 22b on the verse "and he strengthened in his garments and tore them into two tears" — implies plain tearing is into two and not tearing some piece from the side and discarding it while the garment remains in place [and some support for this from Yerushalmi chapter Kol Gadol where it was difficult: is tearing cutting? and it says tearing in the middle, cutting from the side; and according to our words likewise tearing is only in the middle in order that both sides be fixed through the tearing as in the Mishkan, as above; but cutting applies even from the side when he cuts at the edge of the hide to even it, as Yerushalmi says there a woman weaving who cuts the threads — there is mechatech concern; but one can reject Yerushalmi took customary language; and its intent in saying tearing in the middle means at its edge sometimes impossible except by cutting, for nothing to grasp and tear; and all that we needed to reconcile the Gemara's language that it says because he makes it a utensil and not tearing is for Rambam's view that opening the neck of a garment is also forbidden on account of tearing; but according to Rashi there who also [because he makes it a utensil — see there daf 48 and his reason as we wrote in Biur Halacha below] holds every matter whose fixing is recognizable immediately at the time of tearing itself — tearing name does not apply at all since he makes a utensil in his tearing — therefore the Gemara could not say the reason tearing; and it can be according to Rashi there is d'oraisa prohibition of striking the final hammer-blow as there daf 48 where Rashi explicitly wrote his liability is striking the final hammer-blow; and the same here; however requires study somewhat, for from Rambam's language it does not appear he holds thus, for he wrote in law 10 one who tears to destroy exempt because he spoils — implies specifically to destroy, but here where there is fixing on some side liable; and one can reject this piece he tears he discards — therefore included in destroying; and see Rambam chapter 1 law 17 regarding one concerned for the candle and extinguishes the wick — liable even though extinguishing is included in demolishing and spoiling as implied in the Gemara 41b — nevertheless liable because he fixes the candle thereby; and at first glance contrary to our words; and even if we say Rambam holds like our rabbis' view brought there in Rashi and calls all this for him demolishing in order to build in its place, as we clarified above in Biur Halacha — at minimum for Rashi proven explicitly demolishing in order to build not in its place is called spoiling here and fixing elsewhere and nevertheless liable per the anonymous tana; but already we clarified for Rashi from one reason it is not included in tearing — requires study in all this]:

(ב) Paper — see in Mishna Berurah what we wrote regarding opening a letter; and I will explain somewhat. Perach in Yoreh De'ah 118 wrote an Israelite who opens it — chatat liability for all, for tearing paper is spoiling in order to fix and its fixing is what is needed to open it; and likewise Sefer HaZichronot — spoiling in order to fix; and in responsum Chacham Tzvi siman 39 he disagrees and permitted through a non-Jew and did not explain his reason; and it appears he holds melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah, for he does not fix the paper itself immediately; and Tosafos daf 94 wrote therefore he tears in anger to frighten members of his household exempt according to R' Shimon because there is no fixing to the torn thing itself; but accordingly for Rambam who rules melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah liable — d'oraisa prohibition here and forbidden through a non-Jew; and in Neshmat Adam general rule 29 I saw he wrote for all exempt; and his intent is different that case of tearing for a dead person and in anger where the tearing itself is the fixing [for through tearing he soothes anger or frightens household members] unlike here where for the paper it is pure spoiling and fixing is from another side; and in truth neither Chacham Tzvi's nor Neshmat Adam's view is clear here, for it is known the essential fixing of a letter is to read it and its fixing is that he can read it; and if so when he tears in order to read it — melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah; however where the letter is enclosed within another paper as is customary — certainly one who tears the upper paper is included in spoiling and exempt even for R' Yehuda, as we say Shabbos 93b spoiling exempt even for R' Yehuda who holds melacha she'einah tzerichah legufah liable; and therefore permitted through a non-Jew where needed, for it is shevut di-shevut; but according to what we brought above that according to Rashi where it is demolishing in order to build not in its place — spoiling here and fixing elsewhere — liable for R' Yehuda; if so here in all cases d'oraisa melacha for R' Yehuda, included in tearing in order to fix; and even through a non-Jew beware; and likewise Magen Avraham in siman 307 s.k. 20 sided to forbid like Sefer HaZichronot; nevertheless it appears for great need one may be lenient to open through a non-Jew, especially if the letter is placed within another paper; and l'chatchila better to do as I wrote in Mishna Berurah in name of Agudah:
