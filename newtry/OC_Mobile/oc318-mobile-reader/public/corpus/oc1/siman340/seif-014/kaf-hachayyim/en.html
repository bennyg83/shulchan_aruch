14) There: Even for one hair, he is liable, etc. Chayei Adam, klal 21, paragraph 6, writes that this applies even if he plucked it by hand. Kol Bo, page 27, writes the same, and Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, cites him. Eliyah Rabbah adds, however, that the halachic authorities and Gemara imply that one who uses his hand is exempt; see there. Rashi in Makkos 20b also appears to imply this; see there. Tosafos there writes in the name of Rashbam that this follows specifically Rabbi Yehudah, who holds one liable for labor not needed for its own purpose; see there. We already wrote above in paragraph 1, however, that some hold one liable for shearing even when the labor is not needed for its own purpose; see there. Nevertheless, when one plucks a white hair by hand and does not need the hair, it is sufficient for a transgressor seeking rectification to address a rabbinic prohibition. This is in addition to rectifying the negative prohibition against a man wearing what is associated with a woman, which applies no less than during the week. See the end of siman 334, where we wrote some forms of rectification.
