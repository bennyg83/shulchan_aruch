This is a derivative of sewing. Sewing means taking two separate things and joining them into one; the same applies through adhesion. It appears to me that if one glues wood to wood with glue, as is customary, the name of sewing also applies, for we find in the Gemara, Shabbat 74b, that sewing applies to wood as well: one who makes a reed basket, if he sewed its mouth, etc.; see Rashi there.
And he is liable. See Mishnah Berurah regarding the measure. Although regarding grinding the Rambam holds that one who saws wood to benefit from the sawdust is liable for any amount, even though it is a derivative of grinding whose measure is a dried fig, that case is different because the sawdust is significant in any amount, like filing metal. But for gluing leather, which is like sewing, we do not find a distinction between leather garments and other garments; all require the usual measure for liability, and the same should apply to gluing. Paper apparently has the same law from the Shulchan Aruch's language. One must still examine tearing them apart, whose liability is because of tearing. Even though it is not in order to sew, since he intends some useful purpose and not destruction it is considered like tearing in order to sew, as below, or like tearing in anger according to the Rambam who obligates. Does the measure of two stitches apply there too, or only where the liability is because he intends to sew? Perhaps where the repair is different, such as needing the paper or leather separated, or tearing in anger to settle his mind, or any tearing in order to repair, liability depends on the measure of the intended repair, not on two stitches. Proof is from tearing for a deceased relative, where the Gemara says he is liable because there is a mitzvah repair through the tear; there the measure must be a tefach, for less is not a valid keriah even for other relatives. Thus it does not depend on two stitches. Perhaps every case follows its repair, whether leniently or stringently. The Mishnah's rule that one who destroys in order to repair has the measure of the repair applies where the repair has a known measure, such as erasing to write, tearing to sew, or demolishing to build. So too, tearing for some other repair depends on the measure needed for that purpose and not necessarily on two stitches. This requires study.
And he did not intend to damage, etc. All this is the Rambam's language in chapter 10 of Hilkhot Shabbat, halakhah 10. His view is clear: the Gemara requires tearing in order to sew because otherwise it is destructive; but if one intends some repair, even not in order to sew, he is liable. This explains why the Rambam includes opening a neck-hole on Shabbat among the laws of tearing, even though it is certainly not in order to sew, because he repairs the garment through the tear. It is surprising that the Beit Yosef in siman 317 wrote simply that opening a neck-hole is not tearing because it is tearing not in order to sew, while he himself rules in siman 340 like the Rambam that any tearing in order to repair is liable. This is explicit from Shabbat 105b regarding one who tears in anger or for a deceased relative for whom he is obligated to tear, where even not in order to sew he is liable if some repair is accomplished. Rabbi Akiva Eiger cites this proof in his gloss, and the main points are also explained in Nishmat Adam. Peri Megadim also hints to it. What Rabbi Akiva Eiger wrote, that Tosafot on 73b imply actual intent to sew is needed, may be rejected: Tosafot use the standard phrase, but mean that without needing the wood there is no act of kotzer for liability because he effects no repair in the thing cut, just as tearing requires intent to sew or to repair. The Rambam certainly does not require actual sewing, and yet he requires needing the wood for pruning to be liable as kotzer; without that, it is destructive and not a melakhah. Tosafot themselves on 94a write that in destructive labors such as extinguishing and demolishing one must act in order to repair as in the Mishkan, and do not mention sewing specifically. Rashi below on 73 similarly means tearing with no repair. The conclusion is that for Rambam, Rashi, Tosafot, and the poskim, tearing in order to repair is liable because it is like tearing in order to sew. According to Rabbi Shimon, who exempts melakhah she-einah tzerikhah legufah, one is also liable here, though according to Tosafot the repair must be in the torn object itself. The Ritva's view, cited by Kesef Mishneh, that actual tearing in order to sew is required, is a lone view and the sugya of HaOreg is against it, as Rabbi Akiva Eiger asks. The Beit Yosef too, although he suggested otherwise in siman 317, here in Shulchan Aruch retracts and rules that tearing in order to repair is liable even not in order to sew.
The conclusion of our words is that tearing in order to repair is liable according to the Rambam and according to Rashi, Tosafot, and the poskim, for it is equivalent to tearing in order to sew. The Ritva's stricter formulation that actual sewing is required is a minority view. We still must explain Rashi on Shabbat 48, who says that opening a neck-hole is liable because of makeh be-patish, since he makes it into a vessel, and not because of tearing in order to repair. It appears that Rashi holds that the name "tearing" applies where the act itself is destructive, but it is done for a later repair, as in the Mishkan where a worm-eaten curtain was torn and sewn. Tearing in anger or for a dead relative likewise damages the garment, though some repair results. But opening a neck-hole completes the garment through the very act of opening; the name "tearing" does not apply, and he is liable under another category, makeh be-patish, which applies to completing work. This is also evident from the Gemara there, which groups it with putting stuffing into a pillow; just as that is makeh be-patish, so too this. The Rambam too was concerned for this, but follows his own approach in chapter 22. Thus the Shulchan Aruch in siman 317:3 can rule that opening a neck-hole is makeh be-patish without contradicting this seif. The Magen Avraham there rejects the Olat Shabbat's statement that opening a neck-hole is tearing; according to what we clarified, the Magen Avraham is correct for a neck-hole, but not for all tearing cases, for here the Mechaber rules generally that tearing in order to repair is liable.
And he is liable. See Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote about cutting apart newly joined pages. Peri Megadim implies this is because of tearing in order to repair. In my view this is not necessary, for such a melakhah is included in makeh be-patish: bookbinders customarily inspect and finally cut apart all pages joined to one another. According to what we wrote above in Rashi's view, when the repair occurs immediately through the act, it is not called tearing but repairing a vessel, which is makeh be-patish. This applies here as well. Tiferet Yisrael also writes that this is tikkun mana. Perhaps the Rambam too agrees here that it is completion of the work, although regarding opening a neck-hole he holds it is not yet the final completion.
