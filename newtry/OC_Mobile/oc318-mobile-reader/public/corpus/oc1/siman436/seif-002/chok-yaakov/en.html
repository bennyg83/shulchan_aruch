Although he intends to return before Pesach. Even so, no inspection is required. This implies that the leniency applies specifically before thirty days. Within thirty days, inspection is required even if he intends to return, according to Rashi. This is unlike the interpretation attributed to Hagahos Semak. Beit Yosef appears to derive this from Ran’s effort to align Rashi with the Yerushalmi.
When Pesach arrives. He should then nullify it. Although Rama already added this in seif 1, he repeats it here to teach that even according to this lenient opinion, which does not require inspection despite an intention to return before Pesach, nullification is still required according to all opinions. Magen Avraham offers another answer.
During Pesach. Although Hagahos distinguishes ordinary travel from sailing or caravan travel, no distinction applies where he intends to return during Pesach. In every case he must inspect so that he does not violate the prohibitions of seeing and finding chametz. Bach writes that the Gemara mentions sailing and caravan travel only to emphasize the leniency of the first clause, that if he does not intend to return during Pesach we do not fear that such travelers may nevertheless be delayed into Pesach. Alternatively, it teaches that even though such travelers are commonly delayed, if they intend to return during Pesach they must inspect.
Even from Rosh Hashanah. Even from the beginning of the year after the previous Pesach, if he intends to return for the following Pesach, he must inspect. No blessing is recited over such an early inspection. Bach rules that within thirty days a blessing is recited, but Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah disagrees. The latter is primary, because in cases of doubtful blessings we rule leniently.
His wife should also inspect. Although women are trusted regarding inspection, initially one is still obligated to inspect personally. Bach and Maharil write that initially the mitzvah should be performed by men. Accordingly, the Mechaber’s initial phrase, “if he leaves no one to inspect,” must mean that he leaves no adult men. Magen Avraham offers another answer. In my opinion, if he leaves his wife and children in the house, this entire law does not ordinarily apply. They will continue bringing chametz into their rooms and will need to inspect for themselves. He should fulfill the mitzvah of removal wherever he is. When the poskim say that he leaves no one in his house, they mean in the room or dwelling set aside for his own use. Even if his wife and household remain in other rooms used separately, he must inspect his own room before leaving if no one will inspect it. If he forgot, his wife is obligated to inspect his room as well. Taz writes similarly.
