From a non-Jew’s house. If he leaves a Jew’s house and another Jew lives there, he is never obligated to inspect, because the second Jew bears the obligation of inspecting the house in which he lives. Magen Avraham is correct, unlike Knesses HaGedolah. For he will fulfill the mitzvah of removal. He will fulfill it in the other house, either personally or through the homeowner, who is regarded as his agent for removal. A person’s agent is equivalent to himself. The later authorities. But if he sails. Rama rules at the end of siman 433 that pockets and sleeves in which chametz is sometimes placed require inspection. A sailor or caravan traveler can therefore also fulfill the mitzvah of inspection and removal through inspecting his clothing. Perhaps one may answer that Scripture says, “You shall remove leaven from your houses.” The primary mitzvah is to inspect and remove chametz from an actual house. Although the non-Jew will enter the house. All the more so, if no non-Jew will enter, he should remove the chametz in order to fulfill the mitzvah. If he moves into another house in that city or goes to another city, no inspection is required even within thirty days. The house is not his, but belongs to a non-Jew, and any chametz he leaves there is effectively ownerless because he leaves it in the non-Jew’s property. This follows the plain meaning of the Mechaber and the other poskim. It is unlike Magen Avraham, who writes that if no one will enter the house within thirty days, the obligation of removal falls upon him as in the beginning of the siman. There the case concerns his own house; here it is the non-Jew’s house.
