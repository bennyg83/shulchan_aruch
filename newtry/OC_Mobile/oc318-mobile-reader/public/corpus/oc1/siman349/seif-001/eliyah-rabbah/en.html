[1] He has four amot, etc. See siman 396, which grants a broader measure, and the Bach rules so here. This is difficult for the Magen Avraham, who rules here according to a precise four amot. It appears that he is stringent specifically regarding carrying in a public domain, while siman 396 concerns techum. This is supported by the fact that the Rama and Levush do not write the broader measure here. The Beit Yosef explains that the Rambam requires a precise measure. The Bach wonders why the Rambam did not state this explicitly. That wonder is itself surprising: the Rambam defines the measure as the length of a person when extending his hands and feet, and Rashi, the Rif, and Rosh explain that this is a precise measure. Moreover, the Beit Yosef brings a strong proof from the Rambam's ruling like Rabbi Meir against Rabbi Yehudah. See there.
[2] [Levush] His limbs are large, etc. The Tur writes this, but I do not understand it. If his limbs are large, then his amah is also large. The Gemara and Tur in siman 396 speak instead of a dwarf with disproportionate limbs, which Rashi explains as a large-bodied person whose limbs are small.
