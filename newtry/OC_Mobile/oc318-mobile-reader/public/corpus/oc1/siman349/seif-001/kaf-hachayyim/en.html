1) [Se'if 1] Every person has four amos, etc. For it is written, 'Remain, each man, in his place,' meaning an area corresponding to the space beneath him. How much is the space beneath him? His body occupies three amos, with one amah to extend his hands and feet; these are the words of Rabbi Meir. Rabbi Yehudah says: His body occupies three amos, with one amah so that he can take an object from beneath his feet and place it beneath his head. What is the practical difference between them? The difference is precisely measured four amos. This concludes the words of the Gemara. Rashi explains that according to Rabbi Yehudah they are precisely measured, whereas according to Rabbi Meir they are ample, since extending one's hands and feet requires more than one amah. However, the Rif and the Rosh write that according to Rabbi Yehudah they are ample, and we rule in accordance with Rabbi Yehudah, as is known, for when Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah disagree, the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah. The Tur rules likewise. However, since the Shulchan Aruch did not cite the words of the Tur, it appears that he follows the opinion of Rambam cited in the Beis Yosef, who did not rule in accordance with Rabbi Yehudah in this matter, and these four amos are precisely measured. See there. The Bach likewise writes that the Shulchan Aruch followed the words of Rambam. The Magen Avraham, subsection 1, writes likewise, as does the Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1, though he writes that we rule in accordance with the Tur. See there. However, Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, rules in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch, that these four amos are precisely measured. The Chayei Adam, rule 53, subsection 18, rules likewise, and this is also the opinion of the later authorities. See below, subsection 7.
