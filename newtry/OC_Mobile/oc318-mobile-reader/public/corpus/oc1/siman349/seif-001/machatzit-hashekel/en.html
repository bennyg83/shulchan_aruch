(s.k. 1) Four amot confined, etc. — so wrote from the view of Rabbenu Yehuda and Rambam; that is, even though the Rama wrote below in siman 396 that we give spread amot, and as he hinted here in the gloss that he wrote "and see below siman 396" — nevertheless the view of Rabbenu Yehuda appears like Rambam that they are confined; for in Eruvin daf 40 R' Meir and R' Yehuda disagree on the reason for those four amot — R' Meir: three amot for his body and an amah in order to spread his hands and feet; and R' Yehuda holds: three amot for his body and an amah in order to take an object from beneath his feet and place it beneath his head; and he asks what is the difference between them, and concludes that confined — one amah; and explains that for R' Meir spread amot are required and for R' Yehuda confined suffices; and some explain the reverse — that for R' Meir confined suffices and for R' Yehuda spread is required; and however it may be, we rule like R' Yehuda, for the principle is in our hands that R' Meir and R' Yehuda — the halacha is like R' Yehuda; and Rambam wrote the measure of four amot: two amot for his body and an amah in order to spread his hands and feet in it; and Rabbenu Yehuda wondered why he took the measure of R' Meir, for we rule like R' Yehuda against R' Meir; and Rabbenu Yehuda explained that Rambam explains that R' Meir requires confined and R' Yehuda holds they are spread; and since we conclude at the beginning of Eruvin that tefachim we measure from the sweat of the toilers — stringently — automatically also four amot are confined, and that is like R' Meir; at present Rambam proves that he holds confined from the necessity of Rabbenu Yehuda's difficulty; and so it appears that Rabbenu Yehuda holds it is impossible that they are spread, since he knew it is a dispute among the poskim — he should have explained that they are spread; and from his silence it appears his view is to be stringent that confined suffices:
