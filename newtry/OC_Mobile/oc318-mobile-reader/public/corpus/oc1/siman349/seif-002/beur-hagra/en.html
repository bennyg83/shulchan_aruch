(א) Seif 2 — four amot that they spoke of. Like Rashi there — so they wrote; and it is not so, for according to Rashi specifically in passing where it is impossible to say corners; but here where corners are possible he has only square four amot, and like the view of Rambam; and likewise Rashi in the Mishnah 45a s.v. if there were, etc., six amot, etc.; except the view of Rabbenu Tam, and this is what is written in the Tur; and nevertheless Rashi, etc., and Rabbenu Tam explained, etc.:

(ב) And some. And Maggid Mishneh gave a reason for his words from what they said "he is not liable until," etc.; but for prohibition we do not require diagonal; and it appears to me that since there it says the dispute is for walking — but for moving, similar to walking; and all the more so according to him who explains like R' Yehuda, as written in siman 396 seif 1 and siman 405 seif 1; and in walking certainly without diagonal, as written 2b Baal HaMaor, etc.; and further it appears that also for Rambam it is permitted with diagonal, only not in every direction but in a square, as written law 15 law 18; and in whichever direction he chooses for himself the four amot — there they measure for him straight and make it a square, as stated — moving equals walking; and as in walking he explains like R' Yehuda that if he chose, etc., and they measure the four amot with their corners, and as written in siman 308 seif 1 in the gloss — only in the width of four amot with their corners, etc., and they measure the square in whichever direction he chose the four amot straight — the first who explains like R' Yehuda, and that is in moving; but passing four amot — it is impossible to say thus, for it is incidental; and as Rashi explained s.v. they and regarding passing, etc.; and also in moving with permanence; but one who moves incidentally — certainly without diagonal, for this is the first four amot he chose; and this is what is written 5 law 19 — behold here, etc.; and in such a case as stated:
