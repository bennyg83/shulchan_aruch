[4] And give it, etc. It is obvious that this method helps only regarding moving beyond four amot in a public domain or karmelit. It does not help to transfer from a private domain to a karmelit. Maharshal responsum 51 writes so and brings proof. The Taz calls this obvious and says it is an explicit Mishnah in the first chapter of Shabbos. He was not sufficiently precise in reading Maharshal's responsum. Maharshal himself treats that point as obvious and brings proof only for the further prohibition of carrying from a domain lacking an eruv chatzerot, even when the route passes from a private domain into a public domain. The Taz writes that communities which bring a Sefer Torah to the synagogue on Shabbos by passing it from person to person in increments of less than four amot violate the prohibition when they first take it from the house into the street, because passing in short increments does not help for that transfer. Doing it through a non-Jew is also prohibited because it would be a mitzvah accomplished through a transgression. See siman 331, seif katan 6.
