(summary 3) And the main point, etc. — therefore one may not deviate from customs, meaning the view of Rama that stems from customs; and therefore when Rosh Chodesh falls on Sunday, or on Shabbat when the custom is to fast instead of Erev Rosh Chodesh on Thursday (and see in Magen Avraham siman 417); and the custom is to lay tefillin then also at Mincha — if so, then at Mincha too it is fit to fall on the right, since tefillin are on his left; but one must study if this is correct per Kabbalah. And what Magen Avraham agreed with customs — since the words of the kabbalists are not uniform in their views; and for all Israel one may say support to rely.

Mateh Moshe Shatz wrote, etc. — meaning if one sits on a bench or step before the ark in the morning on the north side: in the morning it is fit to fall to the right; and if so, when he sits in the north and leans to the right he turns his face from the ark of the sanctuary, which is not proper conduct; therefore he wrote that on the contrary he should turn his face toward the aron — the ark; and likewise at Mincha if he sits on the south side; and from this Magen Avraham added that likewise those sitting on the side of the menorah near the ark — the law is so that they lean toward the aron. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he disagrees on this — for if so, what is mentioned in Shulchan Aruch on which side one leans does not refer per this view, except for those sitting in the west; but those sitting in the east, north and south, must in any event lean toward the aron; but this is not Maharil intent; and per what Rama wrote — in the morning lean on the right and at Mincha on the left — the shatz can sit on the north side or south side; if so, in the morning if he sits in the north, if he wishes to lean on the right he must turn his face from the aron, which is not correct; therefore it is good he sit on the south side, and then if he leans on the right his face is toward the aron; and likewise at Mincha he sits on the north side for that reason; and nevertheless he wrote that if he did not do so, b'dieved the shatz should in any event turn toward the aron when sitting on whichever side; but other people — even if they turn their faces from the aron, they need not do so, and they do as Rama wrote.
