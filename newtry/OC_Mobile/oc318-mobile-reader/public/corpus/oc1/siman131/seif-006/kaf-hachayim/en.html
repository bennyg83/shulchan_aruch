(6) And he wrote in Olas Tamid in Shaar HaKavanot, and these are his words: a person must also say all the details of vidui — all of Ashamnu Bagadnu, etc.; and although a person knows in himself that he does not have all those matters and it appears like speaking falsehood, there is no concern in this; on the contrary he is required to say so; and through this you will understand the correct reason why vidui was enacted in plural language — for it should have been "my guilt, I betrayed," etc.; but the matter is that aside from what a person is punished for his sins, he is also punished for his fellow because of mutual responsibility, for all Israel are one body comprised of many limbs; therefore it is fitting for a person to confess in plural language in order to confess also for his fellow; and therefore even if there are transgressions in which he did not sin, behold it is possible his fellow sinned in them, and therefore he must confess in plural language even though he did not do that transgression, end of his words; and they brought Shatz daf 148 side 2; and see there in Shatz what he wrote of an incident in the days of the Rambam, see there — Berkei Yosef note 6; and see further in Chasam Sofer note 3 that he wrote other reasons — because it is possible he sinned in other incarnations; and he wrote that this is the reason they say in the vidui text "we have sinned, we and our fathers" — the previous incarnation is called father; and he wrote another reason — because there are some transgressions a person tramples with his heels and they are severe, such as everyone who gets angry is as if he worships idols, and everyone who whitens his fellow's face is as if he sheds blood, and the like, see there; and there are those who explained that even though he did not sin in those transgressions, it is possible one of the roots of his soul sinned, and therefore he confesses for it in order to repair it.
