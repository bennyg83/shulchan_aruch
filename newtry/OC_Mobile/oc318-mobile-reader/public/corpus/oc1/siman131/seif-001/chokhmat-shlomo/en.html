Seif 1 — one should not speak between prayer and nefilat apayim. Note: behold Rashba's proofs are from Bava Metzia from the incident of R' Eliezer; and if for law one may reject this proof — per what is written in Ateret Zekenim that even though R' Eliezer was alone in his house, nevertheless it was at the time the congregation prays, see there; and perforce one may say his wife's intent was only to interrupt him slightly until the congregation's tachanun recitation passes, and then he could not fall, for he has no Torah scroll, or even if he falls he will not be answered so much — and this is simple; examine well. Behold in my work Sefer HaChayim I wondered at Taz's words that one should not say "u'mitpateh" in supplications — that it is false language, for it is written "piteni Heaven va'efat," and likewise "hitah" is also false language, as written "ki yasitcha achicha ben imecha," and it is written regarding it "va'tesiteni bo livl'o chinnam," see there. And now Heaven reminded me to look in Zohar parashat Bo — and I saw Heaven spoke truth and the spirit of Heaven spoke in the mouth of Taz — may his memory be blessed: Zohar beginning of parashat Bo writes that if it were written "tesitani bo" without vav, it would imply that in truth it is incitement; but since it is written "va'tesiteni bo" with vav, the intent is per Iyov's view as he thought — "va'al atzat resha'im hofata," but in truth everything is as it should be; and he brought also the verse "piteni Heaven va'efat" which is also only per the speaker's view and not per truth — only per his view is it flattery, see there. And therefore Taz's words fit very well: in language of appeasement and conciliation in supplications, which is language of praise to Heaven — one may not praise Him in something not true to Him; and "mitpateh" in truth does not apply to Him, and on the contrary it appears like disgrace, Heaven forbid; unlike in the verse "piteni Heaven va'efat" which is not praise but narrative; and likewise "va'tesiteni bo" — his intent is only per man's view, that it seemed like incitement and flattery, but in truth everything is as it should be, per Zohar mentioned; and a wise man is better than a prophet, as his words imply in that Zohar, see there; examine well.
