(1) One should not speak, etc. — for it appears his supplication in nefilat apayim is not accepted as much; and specifically when he stops and occupies himself with other matters entirely; but ordinary speech we have no concern (and specifically not when the shatz repeats the prayer, for then one must be silent and listen well to each berachah, as above siman 124 summary 4); and all the more so to speak sacred words such as when they interrupt in supplications like "and He is merciful" — certainly one may interrupt, and even within nefilat apayim one may answer Amen and every sacred matter (Acharonim).
(2) Between prayer, etc. — however if one transgressed and spoke, obviously he must fall on his face. One may pray in one place and fall in another, provided he does not interrupt in the middle.
(3) When one falls on his face — behold nefilat apayim in early days was actual kneeling — falling on one's face to the ground, not with hands and feet extended; and now they do not practice actual falling but only tilting the head and covering the face alone. They cover the face with a garment, for covering with the hand onto which his face falls is insufficient, since hand and face are one body and the body cannot cover itself (Magen Avraham).
(4) On the left side — because we find when a person prays the Shechinah is to his right, as it is said "Heaven is your shadow on your right hand"; and when he leans on his left his face is toward the Shechinah; and if he leaned on his right it would be the opposite, and it is improper to turn one's back to one's Master.
(5) On the right side — because the Shechinah is opposite the person, and the left of the Shechinah is opposite his right; and when he leans on his right and the Shechinah is opposite him, he should intend "my left under my head" and "His right shall embrace me."
(6) And the main point, etc. — And likewise is the view of the Taz and Magen Avraham that one may not deviate from this custom, and so the world has practiced (and even per the Gra who holds that the main rule is like the Mechaber that one always [falls] on the left, nevertheless if one prays in the synagogue and the world practices like the Rama, nevertheless it is forbidden to deviate because of lo titgodedu). And the shamash standing to the right of the ark — it is good if he tilts his head slightly toward the ark.
(7) And at Maariv — meaning toward the side of Maariv, that is at Mincha when we do not practice putting on tefillin, and one who puts on tefillin there as well shall fall on his right; and see Chayei Adam who leans [toward the view] that then he may rely on the view of the Gra and fall on his left.
(8) According to his custom — and in these countries it is customary to say Rachum veChanun, etc.
(9) To say "and we do not know" — because we prayed in every manner a person can pray — sitting, standing, and nefilat apayim — as Moshe Rabbeinu did, peace be upon him, as it is said "and I sat on the mount," etc., "and I stood on the mount," etc., "and I fell before Heaven," etc.; and since we do not have strength to pray alone we say "and we do not know what we shall do," etc.; and it is fitting to say "and we do not know" while sitting [the part] "what we shall do" while standing (Shelah and Acharonim).
