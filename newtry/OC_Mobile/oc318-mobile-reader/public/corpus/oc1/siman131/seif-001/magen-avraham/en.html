One should not speak. This requires study, for at times interruptions are made with supplications such as El Rachum. One must be careful not to interrupt with speech. Nevertheless, siman 51, seif 4, implies that the matter is not treated so strictly. Beit Yosef likewise writes in the name of Terumas HaDeshen that it is forbidden specifically when one interrupts and occupies himself with other matters, but casual conversation is not of concern. This also appears from siman 124, seif 4, which states that the congregation must remain silent specifically because of the chazzan’s repetition, implying that otherwise there is no prohibition. This may be rejected, but what I wrote nevertheless appears correct. Rivash, siman 412, writes that Rabbi Elazar’s wife would engage him in conversation until he forgot to perform Nefilas Apayim. See there. There is therefore no proof that interruption is prohibited. See Beit Yosef. One may pray in one place and perform Nefilas Apayim in another. Sefer Chassidim; see Megillah 22b.
To incline. The reason for inclining is as written in seif 8 and in the gloss. Nevertheless, the custom is to place a garment over the hand as an interposition, for the hand itself is not considered a covering, since an object of the same type does not interpose, as stated in siman 98.
And the primary view, etc. There is a reason for this according to Kabbalah. Levush writes that during Shacharis as well one performs Nefilas Apayim on the left side, but inclines slightly toward the right, and that this is likewise correct according to Kabbalah. Rekanati, however, writes according to Kabbalah that one should fall on the right side. One should therefore not depart from customs recorded as ancient practices. Mateh Moshe writes that the chazzan should incline his head toward the lectern. The same applies to one sitting beside the menorah before the aron.
And we do not know. Shelah writes that it is proper to say ‘And we do not know’ while seated and ‘what we shall do’ while standing, both aloud.
