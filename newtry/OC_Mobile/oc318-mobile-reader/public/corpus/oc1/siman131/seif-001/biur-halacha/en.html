(To lean on the left side) — the matter of leaning is because in their days they would fall on their faces to the ground on the pavement; therefore they needed by law to lean on one of the sides so it should not appear that he prostrates to a stone floor, which is forbidden d'oraisa, as all this is written below in seif 8 in the gloss; and for this reason we also lean in nefilat apayim. Magen Avraham below summary 20 sides — that by law leaning is required in nefilat apayim; but from Rivash's language it does not imply so for one who examines [and it appears he did not have Rivash before him, only what Beit Yosef copied]. And so Gra copied in Shulchan Aruch. What Magen Avraham noted — see in Tur, meaning in what he brought in the name of R' Nissim that one must raise his face above the ground, and afterward he brought Abbaye and Rava who pray leaning — evidently that otherwise it would be forbidden; it is possible that raising the face for everyone is considered like leaning for an important person, for an important person requires leaning specifically — see Beit Yosef; and it is already written in Magen Giborim on Magen Avraham that there is no proof from Tur's words, see there. And it is also possible that Magen Avraham's intent to be strict is specifically in actual nefilat apayim on the ground against the pavement, even if his face is not attached to the ground; but not in our nefilat apayim which is only covering the face — and with this it is reconciled with Rivash's explicit words permitting.
