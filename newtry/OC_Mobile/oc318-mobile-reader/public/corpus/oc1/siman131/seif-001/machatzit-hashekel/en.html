(summary 1) One should not speak, etc. — not to interrupt with speech, meaning even ordinary conversation.

However in siman 51, etc. — he wrote one may not interrupt with speech from the beginning of Baruch Sheamar until after the eighteen [blessings], and it should have said until after nefilat apayim; if so, ordinary conversation is permitted after the eighteen [blessings] before nefilat apayim.

And so in Beit Yosef in the name of Tur David — this law flows from the words of Rashba, who wrote on that which they said in the chapter HaZahav in this incident that they blessed R' Elazar, and Asher his brother-in-law, brother of R' Elazar wife — Rabban Gamliel was then Nasi and was counted to bless him; R' Elazar wife did not let R' Elazar fall on her face so he would not be punished because of her brother Rabban Gamliel; and Rashba wrote — can it enter your mind that she did not move from him one hour? Rather she would interrupt his focused prayer to stop for other matters, etc., end of his words; and the wording "to stop for other matters" implies a complete break and not ordinary conversation. And so siman 124 — that the congregation must be silent when the shatz repeats the eighteen [blessings] in order to answer Amen.

And one may reject, etc. — for from the words of Rashba there is not so much proof.

And that which siman 51 and siman 54 — one may say they cited the word "interruption," for there are days when they do not fall on their faces, such as Rosh Chodesh or the days explained in this siman; nevertheless it appears to me from what he wrote, meaning from reasoning that ordinary conversation is not needed there.

And in Rivash, etc. — and if so there is no proof, etc. — for there because of forgetfulness she did not fall on her face at all, and it is not heard from this that conversation is interruption and that she refrained from falling on her face even with long speech.

See in Megillah daf 22 — meaning there is some proof to the words of Shulchan Aruch, for they said Rav came to Babylonia and everyone fell on their faces and Rav did not fall; and they said there on one explanation — why Rav did not fall: in his place there was a pavement of stones, forbidden to fall on his face, as explained in this siman. And in the place of the congregation there was no pavement; and they challenged — let him go to the place of the congregation and fall on his face there; and they answered because of troubling the congregation, for they would stand before him; and what is the challenge — let him go to the place of the congregation? And if you think one must fall on his face in the place where he prayed — even if he had gone to the place of the congregation he could not have fallen on his face, since he had not prayed there. And it is strained to say the Talmud challenges — let him go immediately when they came to the synagogue to the place of the congregation and pray the eighteen [blessings] there and also fall there.
