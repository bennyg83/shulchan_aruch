2) There: One should not speak between the tefillah and nefilas apayim. And although nefilas apayim is considered like one long tefillah, and it is stringent according to the holy Zohar, nonetheless if, while he is still falling upon his face, he heard Kaddish or Kedushah, its law is like the law of Krias Shema and no more. Mateh Yehudah, ot 1; Machazik Berachah, ot 7; Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 149b; Zechor L'Avraham, ot 6; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 13. And as for what he wrote in Kesher Gudal, siman 19, ot 14, that according to the way of the hidden teachings there is room to hesitate, I do not know what hesitation there is in this according to the way of the hidden teachings, if one answers Kaddish or Kedushah and the like. And afterward I saw in the Beis Oved, ot 6, that he too wrote that the essential ruling is that one may answer, like the Mateh Yehudah, since it is no better than Krias Shema, and there is no room to hesitate even according to the way of the hidden teachings. End of his words. And so wrote the Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1, that the custom is clear to answer Kaddish and Borchu and every davar shebikdushah after he has finished the Shemoneh Esreh, before nefilas apayim or during nefilas apayim, since nefilas apayim is optional, as stated below. End of his language. However, that which is implied from his words, that even before nefilas apayim one should answer only a davar shebikdushah, does not appear so in my humble opinion; rather, specifically during nefilas apayim is the law so, but before it one may answer all Amens, even those of berachos, as we wrote above at the beginning of siman 122; see there. And afterward I saw that so wrote the Mishbetzos Zahav, ot 1. See below, ot 28.
