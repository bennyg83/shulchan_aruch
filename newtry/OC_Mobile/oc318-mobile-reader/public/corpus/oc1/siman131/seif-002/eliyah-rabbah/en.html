(2) The left side, etc. For on the left is the support of the angels; therefore one must bow; but in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 19 this is his language — in order that one establish a way of freedom, for in nefilat apayim all one's sins are forgiven, for one confesses and falls on one's face as Moshe Rabbeinu, peace be upon him, did — "and I sat on the mountain," etc. — until here. He further wrote a reason to lean to the left — in remembrance of the tamid offering that was slaughtered, which lies on its left side; and Mateh Moshe wrote because Yitzchak Avinu at the akedah lay on his left side — until here. And that which there are those accustomed to lean to the right because of "And His right shall embrace me" — Beit Yosef already wrote in the name of Shibulei HaLeket that on the contrary, for this reason one should lean to the left, for the right of the Divine Presence is opposite his left, since the Divine Presence is opposite him; and Beit Yosef wrote that so is the practice. And that which Rama wrote that because of honor for tefillin one should lean in the morning to his right — a contradiction to his words; I found in Shibulei HaLeket: because of disgrace to tefillin there is no concern, for its place is at the height of the arm on his burial when he lies on his left side — the tefillin is found above and the arm below; and since he is bowing toward the heart, all that the heart presses upon him — behold it is for remembrance the most — until here; and likewise wrote in Sefer Tanya. Therefore I say that it is fit to practice in any event as the compromise that the Levush wrote, and likewise wrote the Bach, and not like the Taz and Magen Avraham who rejected his words without reason. Mateh Moshe wrote in the name of Maharil: a chazzan who sits with his back to the south should fall to the right side so that he bend his head toward the ark — and the sign is "And His right shall embrace me" — until here; and likewise wrote Olas Tamid to Magen Avraham — for the law, to the left. And to me it requires examination, for one who examines in Maharil will see that it seems to him like those who practice leaning to the right, as the verse "And His right shall embrace me" is written; and that is what he wrote — the chazzan should sit facing south, and the same law when he went and sat facing north — he should lean to the right, not toward the ark; but l'chatchila the chazzan sits facing south. And if so, for us who hold that one does not put on tefillin — he should lean to the left; l'chatchila his back should be to the north, and if he sits with his back to the south, nevertheless he should lean to the left; and likewise Rama and all the early poskim did not distinguish between one who leans toward the ark; and it is possible that it seems to Mateh Moshe to decide on his own from the reasoning that for a chazzan who sits with his back to the south one may rely on those who practice leaning to the right because of honor for the ark — and it requires examination. And in any event it appears to me that he speaks specifically of the chazzan who sits directly before the ark; and you should know that if not so, all the people sitting in the synagogue on the east, south, and north sides would have to write that they lean toward the Holy Ark, as Shiyurei Keneset HaGedolah wrote in the name of Mahari Brunna; and why did he mention specifically the chazzan — but certainly as we said, and not like Magen Avraham — see there. Sefer HaManhig wrote to cover one's face with a tallit or sudar and not with one's hand, because one's sins are written there.
