Where there is an Aron and a Sefer Torah, etc. The same applies even to an individual in his home: he performs Nefilat Apayim if there is a Sefer Torah there even without an Aron. So writes the Rokeach, siman 324. Olat Tamid writes in the name of Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah that other sacred books have the same status as a Sefer Torah, but I have not seen this practiced. Moreover, according to that, an ordinary house containing a mezuzah would suffice. The practice is also based on the verse 'before the Ark of Hashem,' where a Sefer Torah was present. Knesset HaGedolah itself also somewhat implies that this rule applies specifically in a beit midrash. This requires study.
Which opens into the synagogue, etc. Based on this, the custom is to perform Nefilat Apayim in the synagogue courtyard. If the door is locked, they say Tachanun without covering the face. So Olat Tamid writes in the name of Maharil. This is surprising: if he does perform Nefilat Apayim, why should covering make a difference? Maharil himself does not write this. Rather, he says that one should recite the supplication without covering the eyes, because then one cannot fulfill 'and he fell, etc.' This implies that he means one should say Tachanun without Nefilat Apayim at all, and he calls Nefilat Apayim 'covering the eyes' because the eyes are covered when falling. This is likewise the intention of the Rama and Levush when they say that Tachanun is recited without covering the face. The Rokeach explicitly writes that one does not perform Nefilat Apayim where there is no Sefer Torah, and the same is implied by Minhagim and all the later authorities. Clearly, therefore, 'covering the face' means Nefilat Apayim. See Hagahot Minhagim.
Even an individual in his home, etc. The Levush explains that the individual's house is adjacent to the synagogue and he can hear when the congregation is praying there. It seems to me that this applies even if the synagogue door is locked. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah gives this as one interpretation. The synagogue courtyard discussed above refers to a time when the congregation is not praying. I find support for this because the Agur explains the rule concerning an individual in his home by saying that even an iron barrier does not separate Israel from their Father in Heaven. If so, what difference does a locked door make? This is also implied above in siman 55, seif 20, unlike the second interpretation of Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah there. For the same reason, it appears that there must not be filth interposed between his house and the synagogue, as above, because there too the reason given is that even a barrier does not separate. The same applies above in siman 128, seif 24, to people standing in front of the kohanim or to their sides, where even a barrier does not separate, as stated. Magen Avraham also implies this. See Be'er Sheva, folio 56. The Taz holds that when the congregation is praying, an individual in his house performs Nefilat Apayim even if he cannot hear that the congregation is praying. Magen Avraham writes that he does so specifically when the congregation is saying Tachanun and he says it with them.
