While seated — as Beit Yosef, the reason is per Kabbalah; and in Rivash he wrote there is no strictness.

There is an ark and Torah scroll — "ark" is not precise, but a Torah scroll alone; and so Rokeach in Beit Yosef, and these are his words: and a sign to speak, for it is written in the war of Ai "and he fell on his face before the ark of Heaven" — end; and as Beit Yosef, and I say: if Kabbalah we accept, and if by law there is an answer — end of his words; and it appears to us the answer is that it is written in the incident of the concubine at Gibeah "and they wept and returned before Heaven" — shall we say there too weeping and returning is specific where there is a Torah scroll and not elsewhere? Rather certainly we do not learn so from "before" Scripture; here too from the words of Rosh that Beit Yosef cites it appears he does not hold so — for he wrote specifically when praying with the congregation and for the sake of the congregation; but where he prays between himself and himself at home it is fine — where he prays with the congregation he is embarrassed, etc.; implies that also between himself and himself at home the law of nefilat apayim applies, and this is certainly not only when his house opens to the synagogue; and further that was with the congregation; and nevertheless it appears to Rama to establish the law so, as Rokeach.

And courtyard of synagogue, etc. — meaning he prays in the courtyard and sees the synagogue door open, as Maharil wrote there.

Or when the congregation, etc. — Levush wrote on this and I have not seen them practice so; and it is possible his intent is one who hears the prayer of the congregation — therefore; and this is not implied at all; and since fundamentally by law, as Beit Yosef, there is an answer to what is written nearby, one need not be strict in this at all — for even an individual at home falls on his face when the congregation prays; and the reason is it is as if he stands with them, for even an iron partition does not separate between Israel and their Father in heaven; and all the more when they pray in a minyan at home at that hour and they have no Torah scroll — there is nefilat apayim, as explained.
