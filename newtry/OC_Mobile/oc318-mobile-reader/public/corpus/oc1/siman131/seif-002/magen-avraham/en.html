And not while standing. Rivash writes that one who wishes to perform Nefilas Apayim while standing may do so. Maharil likewise records that when he finished his Amidah as the congregation began Tachanun, he remained standing in his place and performed Nefilas Apayim. Mateh Moshe writes similarly; see siman 123, seif 2. Beit Yosef, however, writes that according to Kabbalah it must specifically be performed while seated. Darkei Moshe implies that even one who has just completed his Amidah should not perform it while standing. If he cannot sit there, he should wait the time needed to walk four amos, return to his place, and perform it seated. In these countries the passages Rachum VeChanun, etc., are recited. Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Zohar that one should recite the chapter of Tehillim ‘To You, Hashem, I lift my soul.’ It concludes that if one says ‘To You I lift my soul’ without proper intent, but with a distant heart, he causes his soul to depart from the world before its appointed time, etc. See there. It appears to me that this is why that chapter of Tehillim is not recited in these countries.

Even an individual in his home. Darkei Moshe writes: Maharil referred to a courtyard opening into the synagogue, etc. Agur writes in the name of Maharil that even an individual standing outside the synagogue may perform Tachanun together with the congregation, because an iron partition does not separate. It appears that he refers to covering one’s face. This implies that he recites it with them specifically while the congregation is saying Tachanun. See siman 55, seif 2; the same applies here.
