And not while standing. Rivash wrote that if he wished to do so while standing he is permitted; and so Maharil — when he finished his prayer at the hour the congregation begins tachanun he stood in his place and fell on his face; and so Mateh Moshe, see siman 123 seif 2; and Beit Yosef wrote per Kabbalah it must specifically be seated, end of his words — Darkei Moshe implies that even if he finished his prayer now he should not fall while standing; and if he cannot sit there he waits four amot and returns to his place to fall seated. In these countries they say Rachum veChanun, etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Zohar to say the psalm "To You Heaven I lift my soul," and he concludes — if one said "To You I lift my soul" and did not intend the full wording but in his heart from afar, he caused her to depart from the world before her days ended, etc., see there; and it appears the reason they omit it in these countries.

And Torah scroll within it. Even an individual at home falls so — it implies from the incident of R' Elazar.

Open to the synagogue — for it is as before the ark; but if the synagogue is locked — no (Darkei Moshe, Maharil); and in the synagogue of women they fall (Hagahot Maimoniyot, Maharil).

Even an individual at home — Darkei Moshe explains Maharil wrote open courtyard, etc.; and in Agur in the name of Mahari Molina — even an individual standing outside the synagogue may fall in tachanun with the congregation, for a partition of iron does not separate; and it appears he speaks regarding covering the face, end of his words; it implies specifically at the hour when the congregation says tachanun he says with them — see siman 55 seif 2; and the same applies here.
