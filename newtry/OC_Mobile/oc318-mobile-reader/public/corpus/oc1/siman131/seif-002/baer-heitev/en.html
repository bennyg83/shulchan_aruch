And not while standing. Rivash writes that one who wishes may perform Nefilas Apayim while standing, and Maharil likewise rules. See above, siman 123, seif katan 5, where Maharil is cited. Beit Yosef writes that according to Kabbalah it must specifically be performed while seated. This implies that even one who has just completed his Amidah should not perform it while standing. If he cannot sit there, he should wait the time needed to walk four amos, return to his place, and perform it while seated.
Within it. An aron is not specifically required; a Sefer Torah alone suffices. So write Rokeach and Taz. Even an individual in his home performs Nefilas Apayim if he has a Sefer Torah. Magen Avraham. Knesses HaGedolah writes that Rokeach agrees that other sacred books have the same law as a Sefer Torah, and upon this I rely to perform Nefilas Apayim wherever I study. See there.
To the synagogue. If the synagogue is locked, however, he should not perform Nefilas Apayim, but should recite the supplication without covering his eyes. In the women's section of the synagogue, Nefilas Apayim is performed. Maharil.
In his home. He recites it with them specifically while the congregation is reciting Tachanun. See siman 55, seif 20, for the same rule applies here. Magen Avraham. All the more so, when a minyan is praying in a house at that time and they have no Sefer Torah, Nefilas Apayim is performed there. Taz. See Knesses HaGedolah.
