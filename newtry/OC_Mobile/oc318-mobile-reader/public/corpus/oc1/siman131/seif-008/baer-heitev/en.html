His face. And when he tilts slightly it is permitted. Magen Avraham.

The congregation. Meaning: and before the congregation. HaRosh. And for example when the congregation does not fall in Tachanun; but if the congregation falls in Tachanun it is permitted — Bach, and see Beit Yosef. We derive that specifically when he prays for the congregation before the congregation and the congregation does not fall in Tachanun — that is forbidden; and therefore in his home it is permitted even when he prays for the congregation. And the same applies in the congregation when he prays for himself. And so wrote Etz Chaim. And Peri Chadash disagrees and holds that before the congregation, even when he prays for himself — see there.

Mashchit. Even though it is not forbidden from the Torah except with outstretched hands and feet on a mashchit stone, nevertheless the sages decreed because of a mashchit stone. And Rivash wrote that we are concerned for a mashchit stone even if there was no stone floor there, lest it was there and was built upon — see Beit Yosef. And specifically when his face is pressed to the ground; but if he bows in prayer, even if there is a floor there it is permitted. However, nifilat apayim is forbidden even if his face is not pressed to the ground, since his intent is not to prostrate but only to fall on his face. And a brick floor is not forbidden, for it is written stone, and a brick is not a stone — see Magen Avraham.

Mashchit. He meant to say: even with outstretched hands and feet — see Beit Yosef.

In the Temple. Meaning: they would tilt to their sides.

Or they would spread. So it should read — meaning: and then even without tilting it is permitted, for specifically on the ground it is forbidden without tilting, lest there be beneath him a stone floor and it was built upon it; for the ground is not considered an interruption — the ground of a house is like it until the abyss; but grass is considered an interruption — Magen Avraham see there, and Peri Chadash did not write so — see there. On Rosh Hashanah one does not need grass, for they do not fall on their faces but only kneel and bow on their knees; but in the Temple they fall on their faces in the order of the service. One must protest against those who say the supplications in the Maamadot that conclude with Elokei netzach — Baruch Elokei netzach without the Name. Taz.
