5) There: One should not speak between the tefillah and nefilas apayim. The language of the Tur: After the shaliach tzibbur concludes the repetition of the tefillah, they fall upon their faces and supplicate. And such is the language of the Rambam in chapter 9 of Hilchos Tefillah, law 5. But according to the words of the Arizal, of blessed memory, in Shaar HaKavanos, at the end of the fifth discourse on the repetition of the Amidah, one must say the viduy immediately after the repetition of the Amidah, and after it 'Vaya'avor,' and afterward nefilas apayim, as seen there, for a reason rooted in the hidden teachings. And so it is written in the text of the tefillah, folio 51c; and see there, where he wrote that even on Monday and Thursday one should not change this order; and it was cited above in siman 48; see there. And so it is written in the Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 147b; Kesher Gudal, siman 19, ot 1; Zechor L'Avraham, ot 6; Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 1; Beis Menuchah, ot 2; Chesed LaAlafim, ot 1; Kaf HaChaim of R' Chaim Palagi, siman 16, ot 1. And he wrote further there in Shaar HaKavanos, and this is his language: For at the time that you confess the viduy, you should strike with your right hand, bent like a fist, upon your heart, which is on your left side, blow after blow, gently, while you confess, whether at Shacharis or at Minchah; therefore, for each and every word of 'Ashamnu, bagadnu,' etc., one blow, etc. And through these blows you cause a striking there and a bringing down of the chasadim which are above, in the concealed place, downward to the place of the heart, which is the revealed place; and since the right hand is the secret of the five chasadim in their supernal root, therefore one must strike with his right hand upon his heart, and should intend to shake down the chasadim which are above and bring them down below to the revealed place. End of his language. And the Magen Avraham in siman 607 wrote in the name of the Midrash Koheles that one should strike upon the heart, saying, 'You caused this to me.' And the Shalmei Tzibbur cited it, folio 148c. And the Chesed LaAlafim, ot 4, wrote that it is good to close one's hand when he strikes upon his heart, with the thumb beneath the fingers, which is an allusion to the attribute of mercy, and he should intend to subdue the attribute of judgment. And the Ben Ish Chai, of blessed memory, parashas Ki Sisa, ot 3, wrote that the place of the heart is beneath the breast by four or five fingers, drawn toward the front, and every person should take care to strike there; see there.
