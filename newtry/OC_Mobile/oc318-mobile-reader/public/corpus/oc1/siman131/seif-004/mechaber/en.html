They practiced not to fall on their faces in a house of mourning, nor in a groom's house, nor in a synagogue on a day of milah, nor when a groom is present.Specifically when the milah or groom is in that synagogue; but if the milah is not in that synagogue, even in another synagogue in the city, they say tachanun (Piskei Mahari"a siman 81). And on the day of milah when tachanun is not said — only at Shacharit when they circumcise the infant; but at Mincha, even when praying by the circumcised infant, they say tachanun — unlike a groom, for they do not say tachanun all day when praying by the groom (Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 5 Hilchot Tefillah). And he is called groom only on the day he enters the chuppah.
