(4) There one should not speak, etc. — and if it happened that he interrupted, nevertheless he falls on his face, but it is not accepted as much as if he had not interrupted — Olas Tamid note 2, Eliyah Rabbah note 1, and Magen Avraham summary 1 wrote that specifically when he stops and occupies himself with other matters it is forbidden, but ordinary speech we have no concern, see there; and they brought Eliyah Rabbah there and likewise Razah note 1; but per the words of the Zohar and the Ari it implies that even ordinary speech is forbidden, as we wrote above note 1.
