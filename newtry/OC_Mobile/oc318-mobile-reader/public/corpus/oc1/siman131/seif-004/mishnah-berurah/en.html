(20) Not in a house of mourning — meaning all seven [days], and the reason is then the attribute of judgment is stretched upon him; therefore one must be careful not to strengthen the attribute of judgment, as the reason there is no nefilat apayim at night (Levush). Even after he left his house he need not say that tachanun takes its place immediately after the eighteen [blessings], as explained at the beginning of the siman; but "and He is merciful" he says afterward at home, and some are lenient in this. And behold all this is specifically in the mourner's house — no tachanun so as not to strengthen judgment; but when the mourner prays in the synagogue or another house the congregation is not drawn after him and they say tachanun, and nevertheless the mourner himself does not say. See in Eliyah Rabbah who is uncertain — even when praying at the place of the dead when the mourner is not in his house — not to say tachanun. They do not say Hallel in a house of mourning. On Rosh Chodesh when praying in a mourner's house, even when he goes afterward to his house he need not say it; and on Chanukah when they finish Hallel he must say it afterward at home; but on Shabbat when Rosh Chodesh falls on it, or if he buried his dead on a festival, even if they pray in a mourner's house they say Hallel since there is no mourning.

(21) And not in the house of the groom — meaning from the hour he entered the chuppah; but before they say, and some are lenient even at Shacharit (Shulchan Shlomo, and so the Gra somewhat implies). Acharonim wrote that even if he went afterward to his house he also need not say tachanunim, since he was in the groom's house or in the synagogue at the time of prayer the joy applied to him; and likewise at milah the law is so.

(22) And not in the synagogue on the day of milah — meaning in the synagogue where they will circumcise the baby afterward, even if the baalei brit do not pray there; Acharonim wrote that if the baalei brit pray there (and they are called baalei brit: the father of the son, the sandak, and the mohel — not the one who brings out and the one who brings the baby) even though the milah is in another house they do not fall on their faces.

(23) And not when there is a groom there — because it is a mitzvah to rejoice with him; the joy extends to all attached to him (Acharonim).

(24) In that synagogue — and those praying in the ezrah, since they are drawn after the synagogue when there is no ark and aron — therefore just as in the synagogue they do not say, they also do not say.

(25) Even though they pray, etc. — and the view of Rashal, Bach, and Taz is they do not say when praying by the baby; and in Eliyah Rabbah he ruled that if they pray Mincha in the baby's house before the meal or in the middle of the meal — no tachanun; but after birkat hamazon they say tachanun, and so in Kaf HaChayim; and Chayei Adam wrote all this when praying by the baby, but when praying in another house, even though the baal brit is there, they say tachanun even before the meal. He also wrote that baal brit, sandak, and mohel never [say tachanun] at Mincha, even after birkat hamazon, for it is their Yom Tov.

(26) When he entered the chuppah — and in our time they practice not falling all seven days of the feast; and even if the seventh day is the first day of the chuppah — if they prayed before the chuppah was, such as if the chuppah was a little before evening on day one — they do not say tachanun until the next day one a little before evening; and specifically if the groom was a bachelor or the bride a virgin; but a widower who married a widow — this is not practiced except three days; and the law is the same for "and He is merciful"; therefore it is good to be careful that the groom not enter the synagogue all seven feast days, for they refrain from saying tachanun on his account.
