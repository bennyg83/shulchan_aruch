In a mourner’s house. Tachanun and Lamnatzeach Mizmor Shir are not recited there. Beit Yosef, Yoreh Deah 393, in the name of Kol Bo. This is likewise the practice in Constantinople, but VeAni Zos Brisi is recited. Hallel is not recited there even on Chanukah. Maharil, Hilchos Chanukah. Tanya, however, writes that Hallel is omitted specifically on Rosh Chodesh, when its recitation is only a custom, but on Chanukah it is recited. Rokeach writes: Hallel is not recited because it consists of joyful matters; moreover, ten people who separated from the congregation are regarded as individuals. This implies that on Chanukah it is recited, since all agree that an individual completes Hallel then. See what is written in the Tur, siman 691, that any group of ten is called a congregation. It therefore appears to me that on Chanukah it is preferable for each person to recite Hallel at home. I likewise found this written in the name of Maharan.
The bris is not held in the synagogue. All the more so, if it occurs on Shabbos, the piyutim are not recited, since they are readily set aside. Mahari. It appears there that during cold weather, when the bris is held in another house, Tachanun is recited in the synagogue. This requires study. Knesses HaGedolah writes that the universal practice is that even when the bris is held in another house, Nefilas Apayim is not performed in the synagogue where the baal bris prays. Hagahos Minhagim, Hilchos Shabbos, writes that at a bris Av HaRachamim is not recited, while Tzidkascha is recited at Minchah, even in places where the meal is held only at night.
At Minchah. Bach writes that Maharshal ruled that Tachanun is not recited when the congregation prays in the presence of the infant, and this is the practice in Brisk of Lithuania. Hagahos Maimoniyos, he explains, discusses a case where they do not pray in the infant’s presence. Its wording, however, is: ‘Nefilas Apayim is not performed during the Yotzer prayer on the day of a bris, and likewise on the wedding day in the place where the groom prays.’ Since it mentions the groom on the wedding day but the bris specifically during the Yotzer prayer, this implies that at Minchah Nefilas Apayim is performed even when praying in the infant’s presence. Otherwise it should have combined the two cases and stated that on the wedding day and the day of the bris it is omitted where the groom or infant is present. Nevertheless, where a contrary custom exists, it should be followed. Mahari writes that in the Rhineland grooms would leave the synagogue on Thursday before Retzei so the congregation could recite Tachanun, and all the more so on Friday, the principal day of the wedding. Darkei Moshe. In these countries too, the custom is that a groom does not enter the synagogue for one or two days before the wedding, perhaps for this reason. If he does enter, however, Tachanun is recited except on the wedding day itself, as Rama writes here. Some have the custom not to perform Nefilas Apayim throughout all seven days when the groom is present in the synagogue. Even on Friday, which was the eighth day from the wedding, they did not perform it because the wedding had taken place close to evening. Knesses HaGedolah.
