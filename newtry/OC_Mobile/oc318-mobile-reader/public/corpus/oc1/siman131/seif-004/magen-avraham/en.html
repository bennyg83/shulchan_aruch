In a house of mourning — no tachanun there and Lamenatzeach, a song, a psalm (Beit Yosef 14, siman 693 in the name of Kol Bo); and so they practice in Constantinople — but "and I, this is My covenant" they say (so it is). And no Hallel there even on Chanukah (Maharil laws of Chanukah end); but the Tosefta wrote that specifically on Rosh Chodesh they do not say Hallel, for it is only custom; but on Chanukah they say; and these are the words of Rokeach — no Hallel, for they are matters of joy; and further — ten who left the congregation are considered like individuals, end of his words; it implies on Chanukah they say, for an individual completes it for everyone; and see siman 691 in Tur that every ten are called a congregation; therefore it appears on Chanukah it is good each one recite Hallel at home; and so he wrote in the name of Maharan.

The milah is not in the synagogue. And all the more if it falls on Shabbat when they do not say piyyutim, for we push them off with a lighter prohibition (Maharai); it implies there that in the time of cold when they circumcise in another house they say tachanun in the synagogue — it requires study; and in Keneset HaGedolah he wrote the practice of all places that even if the milah is in one house they do not fall in the synagogue where the baal brit prays; Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in Laws of Shabbat on milah — no Av Harachamim and for Mincha they say Tzidkatcha, even though there are places that make the meal only at night.

(At Mincha.) Bach wrote that Rashal ruled one does not say tachanun when praying by the infant; and so they practice in Brisk of Lithuania; and in Hagahot Maimoniyot Iyar — when they do not pray by the infant — until here. And behold the language of Hagahot Maimoniyot: there is no nesiat kapayim in Yotzer prayer on the day of milah, and likewise on the day of marriage in a place where the groom prays there — until here. From his citing groom on the day of marriage and milah in Yotzer prayer it implies that at Mincha they fall even when praying by the infant; for otherwise he should have cited one law for the day of marriage and the day of milah by the infant and groom together; nevertheless where they practice, they practice. Mahari wrote, and in Breinus grooms leave the synagogue on Thursday before Rosh Chodesh so the congregation can say tachanun, and all the more so on Friday which is the main chuppah (Darkhei Moshe); and also in these countries they practice not to enter the synagogue a day or days before the chuppah, and perhaps for this reason; however if he enters they say tachanun except on the day of the chuppah as Rama wrote here. Some practice not to fall all seven days when the groom is in the synagogue, and even on Friday which was the eighth day before his chuppah they do not fall, since the chuppah was close to evening (Kolbo).
