4) There: One should not speak, etc. And if it happened that he interrupted, he nonetheless falls upon his face, but it is not as favorable as if he had not interrupted. Olas Tamid, ot 2; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1. And the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, wrote that it is forbidden specifically when he interrupts and engages in speaking of other matters, but mere conversation is of no concern to us; see there. And the Eliyah Rabbah cited him there, and so wrote the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 1. But according to the words of the holy Zohar and the Arizal, of blessed memory, it is implied that even mere conversation is forbidden, as stated above in ot 1.
