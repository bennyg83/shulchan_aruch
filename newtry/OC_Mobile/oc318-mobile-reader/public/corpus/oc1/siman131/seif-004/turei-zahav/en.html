Not in a house of mourning — the reason in Beit Yosef in the name of Shelah, because it is written "and I will turn your festivals into mourning" — end; and if so he speaks of all seven, for they compare to the festival, which is seven days of the festival; and it is explained there that also other confessions and supplications are not there; and nevertheless it appears to me to distinguish: nefilat apayim others should not say at all, neither in the mourner's house nor after they left, for it is already proven at the beginning of the siman that it must be immediately after the eighteen [blessings]; but other supplications such as "and He is merciful" — certainly the others praying say it at home after leaving there for their homes; and why should they be exempt from this? Behold it is an individual obligation among us that we established on every Monday and Thursday, as written in siman 134, as explained.

And not in the groom's house — and from this it appears that even if he goes afterward to his house he need not say supplications such as "and He is merciful," since he was in the synagogue at the time of prayer and joy applied to him; and likewise at milah, unlike a mourner's house where we mentioned the reason is not the mourner's sorrow — for the mourner himself is not exempt because of sorrow, but the attribute of judgment rules over him and it is not fit to awaken then nefilat apayim which hints at judgment, as Levush wrote; therefore others too should not awaken judgment there; but after they leave they are obligated; except per what is written in seif 1 that one should not speak between prayer and tachanun — automatically also "and He is merciful" is so, for they say it between the eighteen and tachanun; if so, since it is not relevant to say it in its place — after the eighteen — he should not say it afterward either; and what Rama wrote that specifically on the day of his chuppah — so Beit Yosef in the name of Shelah. And I wonder — for all seven days of the feast are called moed regarding a groom, as Riba wrote siman 132; and why would they say tachanun on the other days of the feast when the groom is there? For on Rosh Chodesh we do not [fall], because it is called moed, as Beit Yosef in the name of Shelah himself; and here all seven feast days are called moed — therefore it appears all seven days of the feast the groom should not come to the synagogue for this reason, so they will not refrain from saying tachanun.

Specifically Shacharit — Maharach wrote on this that he saw in Hagahot Maimoniyot that there is no nefilat apayim in the Yotzer prayer on the day of milah — implying specifically in Yotzer and not at Mincha; and this is no proof, for it comes only to exclude at Mincha not by the baby, but by the baby they do not fall even at Mincha; and so Rashal ruled and they practice so in Brisk of Lithuania; and so it appears to be lenient, since nefilat apayim is optional as Rav Neturai wrote in Tur — end; and so I have seen practiced in Krakow.
