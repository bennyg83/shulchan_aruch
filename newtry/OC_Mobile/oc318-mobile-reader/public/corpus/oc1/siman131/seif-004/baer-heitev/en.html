The mourner. Meaning, no supplication is recited there at all. Lamnatzeach Shir Mizmor is also not recited, but "Vezot Beriti Otam" is recited. Maharil writes that Hallel is not recited there even on Chanukah. Tanya, however, writes that only on Rosh Chodesh is Hallel omitted, because its recitation then is only a custom, whereas on Chanukah Hallel is recited. Magen Avraham writes that on Chanukah it is proper for each person to recite Hallel at home, and Ran writes likewise. See Taz, who writes in seif katan 9 that VeHu Rachum, which is said on Monday and Thursday, should be recited at home by the other worshippers after they leave the mourner's house. Rachum VeChanun, however, should not be recited at all, even at home; see there for the reason. On the day of a brit milah. The authorities disagree whether the omission of Tachanun in the presence of the infant applies only at Shacharit or also at Minchah. The custom in Brisk of Lithuania and in Krakow is to omit it also at Minchah, and it appears proper to be lenient because Nefilat Apayim is optional. Taz and Knesset HaGedolah write accordingly; see Magen Avraham. Eliyah Rabbah writes that if they pray Minchah in the presence of the infant after Birkat HaMazon was already recited following the festive meal, it is possible that Tachanun should be recited, because one may rely upon Rama in this matter. To the chuppah. Knesset HaGedolah writes that the statement that he is considered a groom only on the day he enters the chuppah is intended only to exclude the period before he enters the chuppah. Once he has entered the chuppah, Nefilat Apayim is omitted for all 7 days if he comes to the synagogue, and certainly when he prays at home. Whether a bachelor married a virgin, a bachelor married a widow, or a widower married a virgin, Nefilat Apayim is omitted for 7 days from the chuppah wherever they pray. If a widower married a widow, it is omitted only during the first 3 days of marriage; afterward it is recited. Pri Chadash and Taz rule likewise. Taz concludes that throughout all 7 days of celebration the groom should not come to the synagogue, so that the congregation will not thereby refrain from reciting Tachanun. On the wedding day itself, even if the chuppah will not take place until evening, Terumat HaDeshen, siman 80, holds that Nefilat Apayim is omitted at Shacharit. Pri Chadash disagrees and writes that as long as he has not entered the chuppah, Nefilat Apayim is recited. See Knesset HaGedolah, Magen Avraham, and Halachot Ketanot, part 1, siman 21.
