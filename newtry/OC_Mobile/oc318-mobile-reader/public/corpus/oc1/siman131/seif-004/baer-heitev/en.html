The mourner. Meaning, no supplication is recited there at all. Lamnatzeach Shir Mizmor is likewise omitted, but Vesamti Es Brisi is recited. Hallel is not recited there even on Chanukah. Maharil. Tanya, however, writes that Hallel is omitted specifically on Rosh Chodesh, when its recitation is only a custom, but on Chanukah it is recited. Magen Avraham writes that on Chanukah it is proper for each person to recite Hallel at home, and Ran writes likewise. See Taz. In seif katan 9 he writes that the other people praying there must recite VeHu Rachum, which is said on Monday and Thursday, at home after leaving. Rachum VeChanun, however, they should not recite at all, even at home; see there for the reason. In the following seif katan he writes that even VeHu Rachum should not be recited later at home, since it was not appropriate to say it in its proper place. See there. In Yoreh Deah, siman 376, he cites Rokeach without qualification that after completing the prayer they recite Tachanun. See there. He does not distinguish between Rachum VeChanun and VeHu Rachum, and this requires study. The Baer Heitev before me states: “It appears from his words that even in a mourner's house they need not say it afterward, since they did not say it in its proper place.” I do not know how that inference is relevant here. It also appears that he overlooked what Taz writes in Yoreh Deah in the name of Rokeach, as stated above. See there, seif katan 1. Knesses HaGedolah writes that even in the presence of an orphan whose father died before he was born, Tachanun and Nefilas Apayim are omitted. See there. Taz in Yoreh Deah writes that if no mourner is present in the place where the deceased died and people pray there, they recite Tachanun.
A groom. Taz writes that even if he later returns home, he need not recite supplications such as VeHu Rachum, since he was in the synagogue during the prayer. The same applies to a bris milah. See there. Regarding a mourner, see the preceding seif katan. I was asked: Since a groom and a mourner are alike in that Nefilas Apayim is not performed in their homes, why is it omitted when a groom comes to the synagogue, while it is recited when a mourner comes to the synagogue? I answered that their reasons differ. The groom is immersed in joy, and since he is regarded as a king, the entire congregation follows his status. The reason concerning a mourner, however, is not to intensify the attribute of strict judgment. Therefore, when the mourner comes to the synagogue, the status of the rest of the congregation, who are not mourners, is primary. Knesses HaGedolah.
The bris milah in the synagogue. This implies that during cold weather, when the bris is held in a house, Tachanun is recited in the synagogue, and this requires study. Knesses HaGedolah writes that the custom everywhere is that even when the bris is held in a house, Nefilas Apayim is not performed in the synagogue where the baal bris prays. If a child was born circumcised and hatafas dam bris is performed, Nefilas Apayim is not recited. Halachos Ketanos, part 2, siman 168.
Tachanun. Bach, citing Maharshal, writes that Tachanun is not recited at Minchah when they pray near the infant. This is the custom in Brisk of Lithuania and in Krakow, and it appears proper to be lenient because Nefilas Apayim is optional. Taz and Knesses HaGedolah. See Magen Avraham. Eliyah Rabbah writes that if they pray Minchah near the infant after Bircas HaMazon was recited following the meal, it is possible that Tachanun is recited, for one may rely upon Rama in this matter.
To the chuppah. Knesses HaGedolah writes that the statement that one is considered a groom only on the day he enters the chuppah intends merely to exclude the period before entering the chuppah. Once he has entered the chuppah, Nefilas Apayim is omitted for all seven days if he comes to the synagogue, and certainly when he prays at home. Whether a bachelor married a virgin, a bachelor married a widow, or a widower married a virgin, Nefilas Apayim is omitted for seven days from the chuppah wherever they pray. If a widower married a widow, it is omitted only during the first three days of marriage, but afterward it is recited. See there. Peri Chadash and Taz rule likewise. Taz concludes that throughout all seven days of celebration the groom should not come to the synagogue, so that the congregation will not thereby refrain from reciting Tachanun. See there. On the wedding day, even if the chuppah will not take place until evening, Nefilas Apayim is omitted at Shacharis. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 80. Peri Chadash disagrees and writes that as long as he has not entered the chuppah, Nefilas Apayim is recited. See there, and see Knesses HaGedolah, Magen Avraham, and Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 21.
