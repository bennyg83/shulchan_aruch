The mourner. Meaning that they do not say Tachanun there at all. Also they do not say Lamnatzeach shir mizmor. But Zot Briti otam they do say, and they do not say Hallel there even on Chanukah — Maharil. But the Tenai wrote that specifically on Rosh Chodesh they do not say Hallel, for it is only a custom; but on Chanukah they say. And Magen Avraham wrote that on Chanukah it is good that each one read Hallel in his home — and so too the Ran. And see in Taz that in note 9 he wrote that VeHu Rachum that they say on Monday and Thursday — the others who pray there need to say it in their homes after they leave from there. But Rachum veChanun they should not say at all, even in their homes — see there the reason. And in the note afterward he wrote that even VeHu Rachum they should not say afterward in their homes, since there is no need to say it in its place — see there. And in Yoreh Deah siman 376 he wrote anonymously in the name of Rokeach that after finishing prayer they say Tachanun — see there. And he does not distinguish between Rachum veChanun and VeHu Rachum — this requires further study. And in Baer Heitev that is before me he wrote in these words: and it appears from his words that also in a house of mourning one need not say afterward, since they did not say it in its place — end of his words. And I do not know what appears from his words that applies here. Also it appears that he omitted what Taz wrote in Yoreh Deah in the name of Rokeach as written. And see there note 1 — from Mahari Segal he wrote in Shakh Nachalat Tzvi that even a child whose father died before he was born — they do not say Tachanun, and nifilat apayim — see there. And Taz in Yoreh Deah there wrote that if there is no mourner in the place where the deceased died and they pray there, they say Tachanun.

The groom. Taz wrote, and from this it appears that even if he goes afterward to his home, likewise supplications such as VeHu Rachum — since he was in the synagogue at the time of prayer. And so too regarding milah — see there. And regarding a mourner see the note before this from Mahari Segal. I was asked: since groom and mourner are from one reason — they do not fall on their faces in their homes — why when the groom comes to the synagogue they do not fall on their faces, but when the mourner comes to the synagogue they fall? And I answered that there is a difference between them in the reason of the matter. For the reason regarding the groom is because he is immersed in joy, and since he is king we follow him — the whole congregation; but the reason regarding the mourner is so as not to increase the attribute of strict judgment; therefore when the mourner is in the synagogue we do the main practice from the rest of the congregation who are not mourners. Shakh Nachalat Tzvi.

Milah in the synagogue. And it implies that in times of cold when they circumcise in a house they say Tachanun in the synagogue — this requires further study. And Shakh Nachalat Tzvi wrote that the custom of all places is that even when the milah is in a house they do not fall in the synagogue where the baal brit prays there. If one was born circumcised and they draw from him blood of the covenant, they do not fall on their faces. Hagahot Leket Yosher volume 2 siman 168.

Tachanun. But Bach in the name of Maharshal wrote that they do not say Tachanun at Mincha when they pray at the infant, and so is practiced in Brisk in Lithuania, and so is practiced in Krakow, and so it appears to be lenient since nifilat apayim is optional — Taz, Shakh Nachalat Tzvi, see Magen Avraham. {Rama: And it is written in Eliyah Rabbah: however, if they pray Mincha at the infant after they blessed Heaven on the meal, it is possible that they say it, for one may rely on this on Rama.}

To the chuppah. Shakh Nachalat Tzvi wrote: this that he wrote and one is not called groom except on the day he enters the chuppah — his intent is to exclude before entering the chuppah; but all who enter the chuppah, all seven days they do not fall on their faces if he comes to the synagogue, and all the more so if he prays in his home. A young man who married a virgin — a young man who married a widow — a widower who married a virgin — they do not fall on their faces within seven days from the day of their chuppah in the place where they pray. But a widower who married a widow — specifically within three days of the marriage; but after three they fall. See there, and so too Peri Chadash, and so too Taz. And he raised that all seven days of feasting the groom should not come to the synagogue for this reason, so that they not refrain from saying Tachanun — see there. And on the day of the chuppah, even though he does not enter the chuppah until the evening, they do not fall on their faces at Shacharit — Maharav Avraham Isserles siman 80; and Peri Chadash disagrees with him and wrote: it appears to me that all who have not entered the chuppah — they fall on their faces — see there. And see Kenesset HaGedolah and Magen Avraham and Hagahot Leket Yosher volume 1 siman 21.
