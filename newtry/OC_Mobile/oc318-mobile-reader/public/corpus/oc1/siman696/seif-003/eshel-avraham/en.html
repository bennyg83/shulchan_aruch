And fasting. A fast for a dream is permitted, except that he must sit a fast for his fast, as explained above in siman 568, seif 5. Magen Avraham, siman 604, elaborated greatly to reconcile what is written regarding Yom Kippur eve with Purim and Shavuos, and concludes as follows: Since on Yom Tov it is forbidden according to the Torah to fast, one should be stringent because of the doubt not to fast a dream fast on Shavuos. And if he holds that Mar son of Ravina does not refer to a dream fast, then on Yom Kippur eve also one should fast; see there. When I merited to receive the face of the famous Gaon, av beis din and rosh mesivta of the Ashkenazic community in Amsterdam, may his light shine, this past Purim, his heart was joyful, and some words about the matter of the day came from his mouth. First, he raised a difficulty against the words of the Mechaber cited above: if according to the Torah it is forbidden to fast on Yom Tov, then also on other Yamim Tovim, how do they fast? And how did Mar son of Ravina do so? If he holds like R. Eliezer, who says either entirely for Hashem, we at least should not act like him, for we rule like R. Yehoshua. He answered and resolved it incidentally, that the matter is explained in Magen Avraham himself, siman 597, in the name of Darkei Moshe, as follows: the reason it is permitted to fast a dream fast on Yom Tov is because the fast is pleasure and joy for him. According to this, the rulings of the Rama are clear and well directed: on Atzeres and Purim the prohibition to fast is because of withholding pleasure and joy, and therefore a dream fast is certainly permitted, since the fast is pleasure and joy for him. But on Yom Kippur eve, where the Merciful One placed upon him the mitzvah of eating, as we derive from "and you shall afflict yourselves, etc.," and also the reason is so that he will fast the next day with the strength of that eating, it certainly does not help that he has pleasure and satisfaction from his fast, and he fulfills his obligation only through actual eating. Therefore, this mitzvah was not given to be pushed aside by a D'Oraisa mitzvah because of a dream fast. The Rama therefore ruled well that it is forbidden. These words are reasonable, and they left him room to distinguish himself. On the basis of these words, he further explained the words of some Geonim who hold that it is a mitzvah to fast on Rosh Hashanah. See Birkei Yosef and the later authorities, who raised difficulties against this view from many verses and from the Talmud, Rosh Hashanah 19. For one who understands, this is easy, and this is not the place to elaborate.
