And some say Orach Chayyim — superficially from Tur who brought Maharam of Rothenburg's words; but on Purim night after praying Maariv at home he goes to synagogue to hear Megillah — one could say specifically Maariv they pray somewhat from the outset, unlike morning he goes to synagogue to pray, for he holds there is no mourning on Purim; and Orach Chayyim 5: but Rosh brought Maharam's language that Beit Yosef brought: there is no mourning on Purim, and after praying Maariv at home he goes to synagogue for Megillah, for Megillah avodah defers it — all the more mourning d'rabbanan; it implies Megillah defers it — behold prayer even morning at home; and it implies also even if he has a minyan at home public reading is preferable like avodah — 13 kohanim gain in tamid, nevertheless in public preferable; Magen Avraham 6; and Eliyah Rabbah there will be explained. Hence private matters are practiced on Purim even without leaving his house's door at all for discretionary matters; and for Taz even for prayer he does not go out, only in public removing shoe etc. are not practiced on Purim. Also difficult for "some say": the mourner for a matter of mitzvah to pray in ten or Kiddush Levanah leaves his house's door — for Mechaber 65 it implies even if he has no minyan at home he does not go out; Rabbenu Yerucham ruled he goes out — publicizing the miracle; also there is no mourning on Purim — if not so no; see Knesset HaGedolah; Eliyah Rabbah 431; and siman 425 for Kiddush Levanah — requires study. One should not read for a mourner, for in public there is no mourning on Purim; see siman 688.
