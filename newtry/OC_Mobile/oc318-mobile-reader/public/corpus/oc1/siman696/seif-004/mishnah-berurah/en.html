And some say that there is no, and so is the custom. Although the Bach and other later authorities wrote that in their locality the custom was to observe mourning, the Shaarei Teshuvah has already written in the name of several later authorities to be lenient, and that this is the widespread custom in these lands. Especially regarding removing shoes and sitting on the ground, one certainly should not be stringent. Nevertheless, he should try to minimize forms of joy that are done. In Responsa Binyan Olam, siman 35, he wrote regarding a mourner reading the Megillah in the beis haknesses: if there is no one else there who knows how to read it so well with vowels, cantillation, and precision of the letters, it is better that the mourner read it, as is written in Yoreh De'ah regarding davening on Shabbosos and Yom Tov.
Matters done privately apply. The Taz wrote that going from his house to davening is forbidden even according to this view, and this is not called public mourning, for it is not evident that he does so because of mourning. But the Magen Avraham wrote that it implies from the Tur that in the morning he goes to the beis haknesses to daven. See the Pri Megadim, who wrote that one who is lenient to conduct himself this way has not lost, and so wrote the Derech HaChaim, that in the morning he goes to daven in the beis haknesses.
