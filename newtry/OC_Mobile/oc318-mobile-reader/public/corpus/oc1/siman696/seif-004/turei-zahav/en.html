And some say mourning does not apply etc. Superficially it implies this "some say" disputes what was written first that he goes to synagogue to hear Megillah — behold at time of prayer he should not go there — this "some say" disputes and holds he may go to synagogue even at time of prayer; and this is not so, for this "some say" is Maharam of Rothenburg whom Tur brought, and wrote there he prays at home and afterward goes to hear — nevertheless; if so this "some say" does not dispute except on what is written that mourning applies on Purim, namely doing mourning acts in public — he holds one should not do so, and going to prayer is forbidden and this is not called public for it is not recognizable he does so because of mourning. It appears to me: since there is no mourning on Purim in public, one should not read in synagogue on Motzei Shabbat before Barchu to walk opposite the mourner, for one should not mention mourner's name in public; rather he goes to synagogue without reading.
