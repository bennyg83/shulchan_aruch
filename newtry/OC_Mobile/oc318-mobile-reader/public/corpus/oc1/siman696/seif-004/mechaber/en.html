All matters of mourning are observed on Chanukah and Purim. Gloss: Nevertheless, he should go to the beis haknesses to hear the Megillah, as Tosafos writes in the first chapter of Moed Katan. If he can gather a minyan in his house to read the Megillah, that is preferable, as the Maharil writes. Some say that mourning does not apply on Purim, neither on the fourteenth nor on the fifteenth, as in the Rosh and Minhagim, and so is the custom. Even the mourning of the first day is deferred because of Purim, as appears to me. But matters done privately do apply. And even though mourning is not observed on Purim, it counts for him toward the seven days of mourning, like Shabbos; and the Rav himself ruled so in Tur Yoreh De'ah, siman 401.
