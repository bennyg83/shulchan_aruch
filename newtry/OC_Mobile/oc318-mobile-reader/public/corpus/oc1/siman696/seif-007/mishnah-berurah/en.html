(א) It is called “the dead shall not be buried.”

(ב) It is permissible in the flesh and wine – even though the author above is used in the Purim of the Dao Sea, and as the book of taste and the Gemara has written And Ka, on the day of his joy, is filled with joy and joy, but not even at night. And then, in the second and second night, and then At night, I will not only be jealous of its time and humiliation:

(כה) And all the more he is obligated in Megillah etc. — meaning: just as for the mitzvah of joy of the day we defer the positive mitzvah of mourning and permitted him in meat and wine, certainly it is not fitting to exempt him at night from Megillah and other mitzvot since there is no way to bury then, similar to onen on second day of Yom Tov whom many poskim obligated in mitzvot for this reason; but one may question this: one, that on second night of Yom Tov itself there are views among poskim; another, that there at least it is Yom Tov, therefore aninut does not apply because there is no way to bury at night — unlike Purim whose main joy is by day, and at night is like other weekday nights, certainly aninut applies even if there is no way to bury then; therefore Darkei Chayyim wrote on the night of the 14th he hears Megillah reading afterward.

(כו) Burial of his dead first as explained etc. — meaning there it is proven not specifically a mitzvah corpse before Megillah reading — likewise any dead person who has no burying enough for his need, the one encountering him buries him first and then reads. In truth Magen Avraham already wrote many poskim dispute this and hold that for any dead person Megillah reading precedes his burial, and so they practice to bury after leaving synagogue; Magen Avraham challenged: fine for other people, but how is the onen obligated in Megillah reading — behold he is exempt from all mitzvot, and how did he fulfill through what he heard in public after he was not obligated at all? Per force Magen Avraham concludes: even though he heard the reading in public he reads it again after the dead is buried. Regarding Shema and prayer he is like other onen on weekdays: before burial he is exempt, after burial see above siman 71 in Mishna Berurah s.k. 4 there all details are explained. Regarding tefillin, view of many acharonim he does not put them on, as above siman 38 seif 5.
