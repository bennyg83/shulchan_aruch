Is permitted in meat and wine. Although above the Mechaber's view is that mourning applies on Purim, nevertheless regarding aninus he holds that it does not apply, as he wrote the reason. See the Magen Avraham, who concludes that this is specifically by day, when the mitzvah of the joy of Purim applies, for “days of feasting and joy” is written, but not at night, even the first night. All the more so the second night. That which he wrote afterward, “and it appears to me, specifically at night,” refers only to Megillah, davening, and Krias Shema.
And all the more so he is obligated in reading, and so on. This means that just as because of the mitzvah of the day's joy we set aside the positive commandment of mourning and permitted him meat and wine, certainly it is not proper to exempt him at night from Megillah reading and the other mitzvos, since it is not the way to bury him then. This is similar to an onen on the night of the second day of Yom Tov, where many authorities obligated him in mitzvos for this reason. But there is room to question this. First, the night of the second day of Yom Tov itself is subject to differing opinions among the authorities. Furthermore, there at any rate it is Yom Tov, and therefore aninus does not take effect because it is not the way to bury him at night. This is unlike Purim, where the primary joy is by day, while at night it resembles an ordinary weekday night, when aninus certainly takes effect even though it is not the way to bury him then. Therefore the Derech HaChaim wrote that on the night of the fourteenth he should hear the Megillah reading from someone else.
The burial of his deceased comes first, as was explained, and so on. This means that there it is proven that it is not only a meis mitzvah that comes before Megillah reading, but the same applies to any deceased person who does not have enough people to bury him: one who encounters him buries him first and afterward reads. In truth, the Magen Avraham already wrote there that many authorities disagree with this and hold that with any deceased person, Megillah reading comes before his burial, and the custom is to bury him after leaving the beis haknesses. The Magen Avraham asked: this is understandable regarding other people, but how is the onen obligated in Megillah reading? Is he not exempt from all mitzvos, and how did he fulfill his obligation with what he heard in the community, when at that time he was not obligated at all? Therefore the Magen Avraham concludes that even though he heard the reading with the community, he should read it a second time after the deceased is buried. Regarding Krias Shema and davening, he is like any other onen on weekdays: before burial he is exempt, and after burial see above in siman 71, Mishnah Berurah seif katan 4, where all the details are explained. Regarding tefillin, the view of several later authorities is that he should not put them on, as above in siman 38, seif 5.

Therefore he concludes that it is no better than Shabbos and Yom Tov, meaning the second day of Yom Tov when he buries him, and similarly on Shabbos when he waits near the boundary for the needs of burial, when he is then exempt from all mitzvos; all the more so here.
