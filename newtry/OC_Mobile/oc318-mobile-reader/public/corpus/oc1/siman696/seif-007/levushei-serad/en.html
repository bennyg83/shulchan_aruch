S.k. 12. Its law is like Shabbos. For the law of portions is like the law of greeting with peace. There in Yoreh Deah, seif 3, he wrote that on Shabbos there are places that permit greeting a mourner with peace, and some had the custom to prohibit it. Therefore, in places where they had the custom to permit, portions are also permitted; see there. The same applies here on Purim, whose law is also like Shabbos regarding mourning: portions are permitted in a place where they have the custom to permit greeting with peace on Shabbos. For according to them, the same law is that greeting with peace is permitted on Purim; therefore portions are also permitted. But the Taz, s.k. 3, understood the Rama's intent here as referring to what the Rama wrote there in seif 2, that nowadays we are lenient in greeting a mourner with peace after thirty days, and there is no reason for this unless we say that the greeting we practice is not called the greeting with peace of their days; see there. Therefore the Taz found this difficult regarding portions, where this permissive reason does not apply, and therefore wrote that one should not rely on this, etc. But according to the explanation of Magen Avraham, it is well understood, for the Rama's intent is to permit portions on Purim in places that permit greeting a mourner with peace on Shabbos, since he holds that even the greeting with peace of their days was permitted on Shabbos, and this is easy to understand.
