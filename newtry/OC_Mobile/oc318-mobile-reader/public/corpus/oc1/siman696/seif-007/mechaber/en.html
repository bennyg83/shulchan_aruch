There is one who says that an onen is permitted in meat and wine, because the positive commandment of an individual, namely mourning, does not come and override the positive commandment of the public from the Torah to rejoice on Purim, for words of Kabbalah are like words of Torah. Gloss: All the more so, he is obligated in Megillah reading, davening, and Krias Shema. It appears to me that this is specifically at night, even though his deceased lies before him. But by day, when he wants to bury him, the burial of his deceased comes first, as was explained above in siman 687, seif 2; therefore he reads and davens afterward, for it is no better than Yom Tov and Shabbos, as explained above in siman 71. So it appears to me.
