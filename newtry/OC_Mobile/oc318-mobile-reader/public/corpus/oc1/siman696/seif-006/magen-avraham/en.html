That a mourner is obligated to send. It is written in the Maharil that he should not send anything joyous, and they should not send it to him, and so wrote the Sefer Chasidim. Mateh Moshe wrote that the custom is not to send to a mourner even something not made for joy, and so it also seems from the words of the Rema. This is the language of the Sefer Chasidim: One whose father died on Purim, in the following year he sends portions, but in that year he sends money or meat to the poor, only not delicacies made for joy; and anything of joy he should not send and they should not send to him. This is the end of his words in siman 713. It is obvious that if the mourner is poor, it is permitted to send him money, for this is no worse than tzedakah.
In Yoreh De'ah, siman 385; see there. It appears to me that his intent is to teach that in a place where they do not observe mourning on Purim, as written in seif 4, its law is therefore like Shabbos, and it is permitted to send in a place where the custom is to inquire after his welfare; see there.
