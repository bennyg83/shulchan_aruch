1) [Seif 1] It is permitted to perform melacha on Purim. Although it is written, 'days of feasting and gladness and a festival,' they did not accept upon themselves a prohibition against melacha. At first it is written, 'gladness and feasting and a festival,' but afterward it is written, 'to make them days of feasting and gladness,' while 'festival' is not written. Nevertheless, there are places where the custom is to prohibit the performance of melacha. Megillah 5b; Beit Yosef; Levush.
2) Ibid. In a place where the custom is not to perform melacha, it may not be performed. At night, until sunrise, it is permitted even in a place where the custom is not to perform melacha. Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1. However, Responsa Chasam Sofer, siman 195, states that it may be said that it is forbidden at night as well; see there.
3) Ibid. It may not be performed. It is permitted through a non-Jew. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Eshel Avraham, os 1; Erekh HaShulchan, os 1; Chayei Adam, klal 155, os 34; Mishna Berurah, os 2. The same applies when he has nothing to eat: it is permitted for him to perform melacha and to sew his fellow's garments for payment, because all of this brings him joy. Eshel Avraham there; Mishna Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, os 2. Likewise, if a loss is involved, it is permitted, because anything that brings joy is permitted on Purim. Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1; Mishna Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, os 3.
4) One who had been building his house through a non-Jew before Purim should suspend the work on Purim, because the matter is publicly noticeable. Beit David, siman 495; Birkei Yosef, os 3; Erekh HaShulchan there; Shaarei Teshuvah, os 1. In Chikrei Lev, volume 2, Yoreh De'ah, in the addenda to Orach Chayyim, siman 20, he permitted building a store on the 14th and 15th, and he acted accordingly; see there. Construction of a synagogue and another mitzvah matter is permitted. Ruach Chaim, section 1, os 10, seif 1, and in his sefer Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 31, os 31.
5) Ibid., Rama's gloss. Nowadays the custom everywhere is not to perform melacha. It appears that commerce is permitted, since it brings him joy. Taz, s.k. 1. So too wrote Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 5. However, he wrote that although we reason by analogy, we should not act upon it to permit commerce on Purim in a place where the custom is not to perform melacha. Yet if one transgressed and did so, beit din does not penalize him, unless it is necessary to establish a safeguard so that people not become lax in performing melacha. All of this depends upon the judgment of beit din; see there. So too wrote Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, os 3. It appears that if beit din sent him a warning not to conduct business and he disregarded it, beit din may place him under a ban. Ruach Chaim, os 2. See above, siman 554, seif 22, and our words there, with G-d's help.
6) Ibid. One who performs it does not see a sign of blessing, etc. See the Rambam, whose words imply that even in a place where the custom is to perform melacha, he does not see a sign of blessing. So too wrote the Re'em in his commentary to the Semag in the Rambam's name. However, the Beit Yosef implies that this applies specifically in a place where the custom is not to perform melacha. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2.
7) Ibid. He does not see a sign of blessing, etc. This implies that it is only a sign of blessing that he does not see, but he is not placed under a ban. Such is the view of most poskim. However, Rav Achai Gaon in the Sheiltos, cited by Hagahos Maimoniyos, and the Razah in Sefer HaMaor, and Orchos Chaim, wrote that he is placed under a ban. Birkei Yosef, os 5; Erekh HaShulchan, os 2. In practice, Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, at the end of Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 2, agreed that nowadays the custom everywhere is not to perform melacha, but if one did so he is not placed under a ban and is not penalized; rather, he does not see a sign of blessing. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, os 4.
8) It appears to me that even according to Orchos Chaim, the penalty was imposed only upon one who intentionally performs melacha. But if he acted unintentionally, thinking that performing melacha was permitted, he is not punished as though he acted intentionally. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah there, os 3. If he acted intentionally, although he is not placed under a ban because the matter is uncertain due to the dispute among the poskim, nevertheless he should be penalized by requiring that all the profit he earned from that melacha be given to the poor. Everything depends upon the judgment of the dayan. Ruach Chaim, os 3. So too wrote in his sefer Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 31, os 30.
