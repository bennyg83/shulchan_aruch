"Seif 1, 'And one who performs,' etc." Pesachim 50b, and similarly wherever there is any trace of transgression. "And it is permitted to perform any mitzvah-related labor." In the first chapter of Shekalim, on the 15th they read, etc., and repair, etc., even in the walled cities, because it is a mitzvah, as stated there in the Gemara. They prepare it so that if it were not, etc. "For example, to write." The Mishnah, 17a, states: "He was writing it." Although the Yerushalmi there implies that melacha is permitted, this is because they did not accept Purim as an actual Yom Tov. As a matter of custom, however, melacha may still be restricted, as stated in the Gemara, 5b: "If you wish, say that they always practiced," and regarding Rabbi's planting, etc. This implies that had Purim been an actual Yom Tov, even labor connected to rejoicing would be forbidden. "And likewise it is permitted." As above regarding planting for rejoicing. The same rationale applies to halachic rulings, as in the verse, "the ordinances," etc.
