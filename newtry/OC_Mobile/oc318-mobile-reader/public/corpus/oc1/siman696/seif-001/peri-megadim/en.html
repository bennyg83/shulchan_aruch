In every. See Taz, "not to do work." In Divrei Adam the word "that one should not" is missing, and this is a scribal error. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, and in Darkei Moshe, s.k. 1, in the name of Kol Bo, that nowadays they accepted not to do work, and to prohibit this one on that one, meaning the fourteenth for walled cities, etc. There is no custom even now. See the Ran at the beginning of the second chapter of Megillah, regarding one who was writing it: "This says that Purim is permitted in doing work." That means that if it were prohibited by law, there would be no distinction between a mitzvah need and otherwise; but if it is only a custom, one can say that for a mitzvah need, writing halachic rulings and a Megillah is permitted. There he rejects this, saying that it speaks of villages; see there. See siman 468, that on Pesach eve at night until sunrise it is permitted even where they had the custom not to do work. It appears that all the more so this applies to Purim. The same applies where there is a loss involved, that it is permitted, for every matter of joy is permitted on Purim. Darkei Moshe brought the words of the Ran on "one who was writing the Megillah," from the Yerushalmi: "This says that Purim is permitted in work," regarding the fourteenth for walled cities. The Ran explained that this refers to when the fifteenth falls on Shabbos and they read on the fourteenth, etc.; all the more so villages that read earlier on the day of assembly are permitted in work on the day they read. In Magen Avraham we wrote about this in letter 2 from our own reasoning. The Yerushalmi holds that one should not distinguish between villages and walled cities. One can also say that it holds that when villages read not at its time, ten are required indispensably; if so, how would it be relevant to gather ten and not have intent? This is not so for walled cities on the fourteenth, where one can say that it is its time. But there is no need to explain it this way.
And know. What is written in the gloss, that nowadays they had the custom not to do work, is from Kol Bo. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of Orach Chayyim, and in Bedek HaBayis he wrote Kol Bo. The Rav appears not to have seen the Beit Yosef's Bedek HaBayis, as I wrote in the rules of issur veheter in Yoreh Deah. The Beit Yosef concludes that one who does work is excommunicated, since its prohibition spread throughout all Israel. In Eliyah Rabbah, s.k. 1, in the name of Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, he says that one is not excommunicated, and this requires study. It implies that for a custom one is not excommunicated unless its prohibition spread through most of Israel, but not for a custom of one city. See Pesachim 52, and the Rambam, Hilchos Yom Tov, where they excommunicate for Yom Tov Sheni even though it is a custom. This implies that it is because of degrading Yom Tov that they excommunicate; otherwise, for a custom that is not widespread through all Israel, they do not. See Pri Chadash, Orach Chayyim 496, letter 1, s.v. "And if it is difficult," see there. See Yoreh Deah 214, seif 1, which implies that a custom is like a vow; this requires study, and the matters are lengthy.
