He should not leave from there, and so on. This law was written by the Rokeach, and in his place they would eat seudah shelishis before Minchah. Therefore he said that when he comes to daven Minchah, he should not leave from there. But for us, he must go to his house after Minchah to eat seudah shelishis. At night he should daven Maariv and read the Megillah in his house if he has a minyan; if not, he should go to the beis haknesses while it is still day and remain there for davening and the Megillah reading.
The entrance of his house. Only to hear the Megillah reading if he does not have a minyan in his house, as above. The Mechaber, who copied the words of the Rokeach, follows his view in seif 4 that mourning applies on Purim. But the Rema has already written above that we follow the authorities who say that mourning does not apply on Purim. According to this, even if he has a minyan in his house, he goes to the beis haknesses to hear the Megillah reading with the community. To leave the entrance of his house for optional matters, it implies from the Pri Megadim that one should be stringent. All of this is by day, for days of feasting and joy are written. But at night, if he has a minyan, he should daven and read the Megillah in his house; and if he does not have a minyan, he should daven in his house and go to hear the Megillah reading in the beis haknesses, but other than the reading he should not leave the entrance of his house. See the Derech HaChaim, that on the fifteenth day he should not go from his house to the beis haknesses even to daven Shacharis. Nevertheless, regarding removing shoes and sitting on the ground, it implies from the authorities that even on the fifteenth he need not do so. The Levush wrote: one does not recite Tzidduk HaDin or Kaddish except for a sage in his presence after they expound about him; and although one does not recite Tzidduk HaDin, they do say Kaddish.
