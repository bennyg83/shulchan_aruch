In Shulchan Aruch, seif 7. Like words of Torah. Although regarding the second day of Yom Tov the Mechaber ruled above in siman 548 that mourning is observed, one must say that words of Kabbalah are stronger. It also appears that the fact that he is not prohibited in meat and wine is certainly only D'Rabbanan, and is overridden by the joy of Purim, which is from words of Kabbalah. But this requires study, for above he ruled that mourning is observed, and it implies even on other days that are not D'Oraisa. It does not appear correct to say that his intent is that this "there is one who says" disagrees with the ruling above. Perhaps his reason is that since mourning has already taken effect, Purim does not come and override it, as the Ran wrote. If so, regarding an onen whose relative died on Purim, it is the opposite: aninus does not come and override the positive commandment of Purim. But the language of the Shulchan Aruch does not imply this, for we approach it from the reason that words of Kabbalah are like words of Torah; if so, Purim should not be overridden even if aninus or mourning came first. I found that someone wrote that this appears contradictory. See Tashbetz, in the siman cited above, whose view is that the essential enactment of the Purim meal is only not to fast, while increasing the meal is only D'Rabbanan. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 15.
