[5] [Levush] And they pray in his house, etc. In my humble opinion, it is implied from the Rama that since mourning is not observed, it is permitted to go to the synagogue even for prayer. So too Mateh Moshe ruled, that specifically at night, when the days of feasting and joy have not yet entered, he goes after the prayer, but by day it is permitted for prayer. This also appears to be the view of the Tur, and not as Hagahos Maimoniyos understood; examine this carefully. Perhaps the Levush distinguishes this on his own, because the Rama had a difficulty from the words of the Shulchan Aruch here against Yoreh Deah, siman 401, where he ruled that it is not observed. Therefore he distinguishes that matters dependent on joy, such as drinking joyfully and not sitting on the ground, are not observed, but regarding not leaving the doorway of his house it is observed, except for reading the Megillah. Perhaps the same applies that he should remove his shoes when he comes home. I explained the language of the Rosh in Bava Kamma this way in my work on Yoreh Deah. This is proper to decide, for the Shach testified in the name of Maharshal and Bach that they had a tradition to observe all matters of mourning. I also expressed wonder there at the great later authorities who raised contradictions between the words of Maharam of Rothenburg; see there. For I found in Teshuvos Maimoniyos at the end of Sefer Shoftim that he brought both, and his implication is that for deciding the halachah one should not observe mourning, while regarding custom he conducted himself to wear shoes for the Megillah and afterward return to his mourning and remove his shoes. This may also be the view of the Shulchan Aruch, that in Yoreh Deah he wrote according to the law and according to custom. Nevertheless, regarding removing shoes one may perhaps be lenient. Magen Avraham wrote that it is forbidden to see all kinds of joy, end quote; but this is not compelled, for they are days of joy. See s.k. 8.
