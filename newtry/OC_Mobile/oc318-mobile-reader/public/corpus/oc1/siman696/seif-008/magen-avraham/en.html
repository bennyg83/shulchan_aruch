It is permitted to marry. For regarding the festival it is written, “and you shall rejoice in your festival,” and not with your wife, but this is not so regarding Purim. Also, Purim is one day, and because of one day a person does not delay himself; and this follows the one who says it is because of neglecting the mitzvah of being fruitful and multiplying, as cited by the Beis Yosef in the name of the Rashba. At first glance it implies that this is the explanation: granted, on a regel we decree lest he delay until the regel in order to profit by having the seven days of feasting fall within the seven days of the festival. But for Purim, because it is one day, he will not delay. This requires analysis, for the Gemara says that it is permitted to marry on Erev Yom Tov because, for one day, a person does not delay himself. Rashi and the Ran explain that since if they delay it further they cannot do it on the moed. If so, he will not schedule the marriage on Erev Yom Tov, and that does not apply to Purim. Perhaps the Rashba stated his own reasoning, but adopted the language of the Gemara. Nevertheless, it is difficult: from where does he know this? Perhaps his proof is from the fact that it is permitted to marry on Erev Shavuos, and we are not concerned that he will delay the marriage, because there he is not concerned if it is postponed until after Shavuos, since it is only one day. Rather, it must be that since it is only one day, he does not care to delay for this, for in any event he will still need to make the seven days of feasting. The Levush wrote: according to what I wrote in siman 688, seif 6, that one should not make the Purim meal on Shabbos, here too it is forbidden to make the wedding meal on the fourteenth. This is the end of his words. But it does not appear to me so, for the cases are not comparable. There the reason is that if so, the Purim meal will not be recognizable, but here what does it matter if the wedding meal is not recognizable? See what I wrote there. Rather, if there is a difficulty, this is the difficulty: we rule that one does not mix one joy with another joy, as I wrote at the beginning of siman 546, and we derive this from a verse. If so, how is it permitted to mix the joy of marriage with the joy of Purim? One may say that for this reason the Rashba concluded that these matters are Rabbinic and we follow the lenient view; that is, according to the opinion that one does not mix joys, it is indeed forbidden, but since according to the other view it is permitted, we follow the lenient view. If so, according to what I wrote at the beginning of siman 546, that we rule according to the one who says that one does not mix one joy with another joy, here too it is forbidden. Therefore it appears to me that he should make the chuppah on the thirteenth. So it appears to me.
