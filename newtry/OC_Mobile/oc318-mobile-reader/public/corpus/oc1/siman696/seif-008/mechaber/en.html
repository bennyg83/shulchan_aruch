It is permitted to marry a woman on Purim. Gloss: This is both on the fourteenth and on the fifteenth. All the more so, it is permitted to make a pidyon haben, as Tosafos writes in the first chapter of Moed Katan. Regarding the custom to wear costumes on Purim, and for a man to wear a woman's garment and a woman men's clothing, there is no prohibition in the matter, since they intend only ordinary joy. The same applies to wearing Rabbinic kilayim. Some say it is forbidden, but the custom follows the first view. Likewise, people who snatch from one another in a joyous manner do not thereby violate “You shall not steal,” and this is the custom, provided that one does not do anything improper according to the leaders of the city, as in Responsa Mahari Mintz, siman 17.
