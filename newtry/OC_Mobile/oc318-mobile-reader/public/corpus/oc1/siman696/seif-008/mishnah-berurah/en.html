(28) It is permitted to marry, etc. It is not comparable to a festival, for regarding it the verse says, "and you shall rejoice on your festivals," but not with your wife, which is not so regarding Purim. The Magen Avraham wrote that one should make the chuppah on the thirteenth and the meal in the evening. See Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote that in these countries the custom spread to permit it, in accordance with the view of the Shulchan Aruch. In Chemed Moshe he wrote that it is more proper to make the chuppah at the end of the fourteenth day, and to make the Purim meal beforehand.
(29) Redeeming the firstborn son. And a bris milah, for in these there is not so much joy that it would be relevant to say regarding them that one may not mix one joy with another joy.
(30) The first opinion. See Yoreh Deah, siman 182, where the Taz wrote in the name of the Bach that this custom should be abolished, whether on Purim or at the joy of a marriage. So too wrote the Baer HaGolah there. If all the garments are male garments and only one garment is a woman's, and they are recognizable, perhaps one should not protest against them. Peri Megadim. See Knesses HaGedolah and the Shelah, who warned to distance oneself from this.
(31) Because of "you shall not steal." According to this, it is permitted to recite a blessing over what they snatch. However, the Shelah wrote that this is not the teaching of the pious, and one who guards his soul should distance himself from garments of kilayim and from snatching from his fellow. Eliyah Rabbah.
(32) And they had this custom. This means from the time of reading the Megillah until the night of the Purim meal, which is two nights and one day. Eliyah Rabbah.
