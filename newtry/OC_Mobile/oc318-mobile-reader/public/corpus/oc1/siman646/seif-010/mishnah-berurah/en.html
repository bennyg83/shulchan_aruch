Its head was cut off. This means even if the heads of all three branches were cut off.
Valid. It is not comparable to lulav and aravah, where if its head was cut off it is invalid, because by hadas its branches cover its head, meaning the leaves, and its cutting is not recognizable in them.
Even if no date-like growth arose on it. This is a kind of date-like growth that emerges on willow leaves; with this it is certainly valid according to all opinions, because the growth covers the cut and the cut is not recognizable.
And some invalidate, etc. The same applies to its head drying according to this view. It is obvious that 'its head dried' is called so only when its surface whitened, which is not common at all among hadasim that grew in this year. But if it is only somewhat withered, even to the point that it crumbles under the fingernail, it is not called dry, for even regarding other leaves it is not called dry in such a case, as above in se'if 7.
When its head was cut off. Because it is not beautiful. According to this view, one must examine whether this is specifically when the heads of all three branches were cut off, or even if the head of one branch was cut off it is also invalid. See below, siman 651:1 in the gloss.
And it is good to be stringent, etc. Regarding what is common, that small branches emerge between the stems and one must cut them off so that they not interrupt between the stems, if this is cut off it is valid according to all opinions, even if it is found this way in all three branches, since it is not at its head.
Where another is possible. The Bach wrote that if its head dried, meaning the upper leaves dried, even if another is not possible one should not bless over it. But there is a remedy: he should remove the upper leaves, and then it is valid ab initio according to all opinions.
The wood. But if only the upper leaves were cut off, this is not called 'its head was cut off.'
