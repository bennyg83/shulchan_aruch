The Nachalas Tzvi and the Eliyah Rabbah rejected his words, and their view is that if another is not available, one should not be stringent and one may recite a berachah. The Bigdei Yesha also agrees with this as practical halachah, since the Mishnah teaches 'its top was cut off' and does not teach 'its top dried.' Rather, since the opening clause teaches generally that a stolen or dry myrtle is invalid, it implies that the entire myrtle dried, because it is said, 'the dead do not praise Hashem.' Also, if there are three branches of moist leaves at the top, it is valid, as stated in the Gemara. But if only its top dried, it should not be invalidated at all. These are his words. See Eliyah Rabbah, who proves his view from the Ra'avad in Tamim De'im, who is also lenient where the top dried, meaning the upper leaves. Bikkurei Yaakov also found in Tamim De'im, siman 232, that he wrote explicitly: We have a question regarding a myrtle, if the three upper branches at its head dried but all the rest remained moist, what is the law? It is reasonable to be lenient, for it has been clarified to us that 'its top was cut off' is invalid when the top of the spine itself was cut off; but if only the upper leaves were cut off, it is not invalid. Since if they were cut off they would not invalidate it, all the more so if they dried they would not invalidate it, for if he wanted, he could remove them. These are his words. Bikkurei Yaakov wrote that the Shulchan Aruch necessarily does not accept this reasoning of 'if he wanted, he could remove it,' from siman 648, se'if 14, regarding chazazis on an esrog: if, when he peels it off, it returns to the appearance of an esrog, it is valid, and we do not say that since he can peel it, it should immediately be valid. But this is no proof, for there we do not know beforehand whether it will return to the appearance of an esrog, and also whether nothing will be missing. What he wrote, that the Tur's validation of a dry myrtle refers only to its wood drying and not to the leaves, is forced, for 'dry' is stated plainly, implying whether the wood or the leaves. See below at the end of siman 649. Therefore it appears that one who is lenient about this where he has no other myrtle should not be protested.
