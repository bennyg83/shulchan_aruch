What the Mechaber wrote, that it must be at its top, according to the Rosh means that the one leaf is over the other two, and 'at its top' means the leaf that rides over the two. He is lenient that even if no moist leaf remains at the top of the myrtle except one leaf in the nest riding over the two, it is valid. This is difficult, for in se'if 3 the Mechaber wrote that this is a hadas shoteh, and I do not know how to resolve it. The Levush is also difficult. It is forced to say that he speaks of a case where at its top there is a nest, and throughout the rest of the myrtle's length there are truly three leaves in one nest, and they dried. This requires analysis.
