(Subparagraph 3) And onward it is valid because, etc. The reference printed here is mistaken; it belongs to what the Shulchan Aruch wrote above: if he transgressed and reduced them, or picked them for eating, it is valid. On that the Magen Avraham wrote the reason, that it is in his power to repair it. The Beis Yosef asked: why is this different from the case of a myrtle whose berries were green and then turned black, where we say that since it was initially disqualified, it is disqualified? He answered that here it is in his power to repair it by reducing the berries, and anything that is in his power is not called rejected. The Beis Yosef brought proof from Tosafos in Sukkah 32a, regarding an asherah of Moshe. Tosafos wrote that where it is in his power to fix the matter, even through a prohibition, it is not called rejection, for since he can do it, it is considered fit. Therefore, although reducing the berries on Yom Tov is forbidden, since he can reduce them before Yom Tov or on Chol HaMoed, it is not considered rejected from the mitzvah. This explains why, if he transgressed and reduced them, or if he picked them for eating, it is valid.
