See Bikkurei Yaakov, who wonders about this: in any case, it is not included in the avos stated in the Torah, which is specifically three leaves. See there, where he proves that even those who are lenient in pressing circumstances are lenient only with two and one, but for two and two we have no basis, unless we rely on the opinion of the Baal HaHashlamah, who permits reciting a berachah in pressing circumstances over three species. But aside from the fact that all the poskim disagree with him, this reasoning does not apply to us in any event, since fine myrtles of three upon three grow among us. Although the Shevus Yaakov wanted to invalidate them on the ground that they are grafted, the Chacham Tzvi and Panim Meiros already disagreed with him in responsa, and the law is with them. The custom has now spread everywhere to validate them, for it is known that they are not grafted. Since one can fulfill the mitzvah with these, even if there is only one in the city, it is no longer called a pressing circumstance that would allow one to recite a berachah over three species without a valid myrtle. Therefore, fortunate is one who buys for himself even a single myrtle of three upon three and completes the set with two other myrtle branches that are two upon two, if he cannot afford to buy three branches of myrtle that are three upon three. Alternatively, the congregation should buy a lulav with myrtles of three upon three, and the entire congregation should fulfill their obligation with it. These are his words.
