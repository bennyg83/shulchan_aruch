Seif 3: It is invalid even, etc. As stated there, 12a: a wild myrtle may be used for the sukkah. The verse also places “a leafy tree” between “an olive tree” and palms. Furthermore, since Scripture writes both “myrtle” and “leafy,” it follows that a myrtle that is not leafy is invalid. Maharik.
And there are, etc. This has no substance.
And there is an authority who, etc. See Maharik, shoresh 41. He inferred from Rashi's words, “two and one,” meaning two leaves from one node and one leaf from below, etc., that Rashi disqualifies specifically in such a case. If the single leaf is above the other two, it is possible that Rashi would regard it as valid. The principal basis of the leniency he proposed, however, is that one may follow Baal HaHashlamah, who maintains that one may recite a blessing when one of the four species is missing. Maharik wrote all this in response to a questioner who asked him to inform those communities whether any justification could be found for their practice of reciting a blessing. He answered that perhaps in pressing circumstances they relied upon Baal HaHashlamah, or upon the combination of Rashi's wording and Baal HaHashlamah's opinion. By combining these two grounds one may rely upon them in pressing circumstances, as stated in chapter 2 of Chullin: “Come, and let us rely upon the words of Rabbi Chiya,” etc. This shows that two positions may be combined even when they are not identical, and all the more so in pressing circumstances. He nevertheless wrote: “All this remains very distant in my eyes,” and refuted the position from the statement in chapter 3 of Menachos concerning the four species of the lulav, “This was taught only,” etc. The author himself did not rely upon his suggestion, and at the beginning of his responsum wrote that he was greatly alarmed by the practice; see there. In the writings of Mahari, he likewise wrote: “I carefully examined all the sefarim and found no basis for leniency even after the fact.” He sought to infer a leniency from Rabbeinu Ephraim cited in Mordechai, chapter 8 of Berachos, which the Tur cites in siman 297. Maharik there refuted those who made this argument and wrote that, on the contrary, Rabbeinu Ephraim's words indicate the opposite. See Beit Yosef in siman 297, who cites this briefly. Their words provide no support at all.
