But if there were two leaves, etc. In the Gemara we say that avos means that three leaves stand in one nest. Rav Kahana said: even two and one. Rashi explains: two leaves on one stem, and one leaf above, rising and riding over the two. Rav Acha son of Rava would seek out two and one, since it came from Rav Kahana's mouth. Mar bar Ameimar said to Rav Ashi: my father called that a hadas shoteh. Therefore the poskim invalidated it. The Rema labored to uphold our custom, that we recite a berachah over such myrtles. It appears to me that there is basis and proof in this way: Rav Acha labored to uphold Rav Kahana's words, even though this is not the choicest performance of the mitzvah, for he said it with the expression 'even,' but he did so out of honor for Rav Kahana. About this Mar bar Ameimar said that his father called it a hadas shoteh, meaning that it is not the choicest form, and he disagrees with specifically seeking it out. But as a matter of law, it is valid. This seems to me a basis for the custom.
