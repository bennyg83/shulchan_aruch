On one stem. Meaning in each and every nest, and they should be adjacent to one another in one circle, so that one is not lower than another, even though each is on its own stem.
Above them. Or below them.
It is called a wild hadas. Because its leaves do not go in order, but are disordered like a fool.
Even in pressing circumstances. For it is not included in hadas at all, and it is invalid even on the other days. Likewise, if there were two one above the other, this too is not included in the 'avot' stated in the Torah.
That it is valid, and therefore the custom is, etc. Meaning that they rely on that authority who validates with two and one, and the same applies to two above two.
Therefore they were accustomed to be lenient, etc. See the Gra's commentary and the other later authorities, who all greatly questioned that custom, for it has no foundation, neither in two and one nor in two above two. The Rema pressed only to uphold the custom. Therefore, one who fears the word of Hashem should trouble himself to find an 'avot' according to law, namely three leaves equal in each and every nest. See below in se'if 5. Chayei Adam wrote that among these dry hadasim that are brought with the esrogim, with difficulty one in a hundred is found that has three in one nest; therefore they do not have a presumption of validity until one soaks them in hot water and softens them. If he does not find such a one, he should take it without a blessing; see there.
