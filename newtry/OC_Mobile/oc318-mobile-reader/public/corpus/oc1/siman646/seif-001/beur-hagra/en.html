Seif 1: A myrtle whose tip was severed, etc. This follows Shmuel's ruling there in accordance with Rabbi Tarfon. Below, in seif 10, the Mechaber cites a dispute, because these first two seifim reproduce Rambam's language, whereas from seif 3 onward he follows the Tur's wording. Here he continued with Rambam's formulation together with the other laws.
Most of its leaves fell, etc. This too is Rambam's language, as stated above. Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam means most of the leaves in each individual cluster, as is written in seif 4. This is contrary to the Tur's understanding of Rambam, for the Tur wrote that in Rambam's view it is sufficient if even one cluster remains. Beit Yosef expressed surprise at this interpretation; see there.
