Taz se'if katan 6 It is proper to be stringent since it is a Torah doubt. In my humble opinion, Tosafot wrote that even though something in his power is not called rejection, even regarding offerings, it is not so much a rejection. This is as Tosafot wrote in Zevachim, because it is prohibited to reduce them; see there. If so, this is not a doubt in a Torah matter, for it is obvious that by Torah law it is permitted to pick them, as Tosafot wrote explicitly in Yoma 34b, s.v. hanach milei, at the end of the discussion, that this repair of reducing its berries does not appear so much like repair that it should be considered repair by Torah law; see there. Likewise the Levush here wrote that it appears like repairing. If so, by Torah law it is not rejection, because it is in his power. Rather, since rabbinically it is prohibited to pick them, it is rejection rabbinically; if so, it is a rabbinic doubt. However, the Mordechai at the beginning of the third chapter of Sukkah wrote that this repair of reducing its berries is biblical. See what I wrote above in the margin of Magen Avraham, siman 314, se'if katan 5. The practical difference is whether it is permitted to reduce them through a gentile: according to Tosafot it is permitted, for it is a double rabbinic prohibition in the place of a mitzvah, like that case at the beginning of siman 586:21; but according to the Mordechai it is prohibited. Perhaps even according to the Mordechai it is permitted if one tells a gentile to reduce them for eating, for in that case, even though it is an inevitable consequence, nevertheless instructing a gentile is permitted. See Magen Avraham, siman 253, se'if katan 18. Similarly, I am puzzled by Sukkah 33b: when did he reduce them, etc.; it is initial rejection. But before Yom Tov, when it is entirely in his power, it is obvious that it is not rejection, for even by offerings it is not rejection. This requires great investigation.
