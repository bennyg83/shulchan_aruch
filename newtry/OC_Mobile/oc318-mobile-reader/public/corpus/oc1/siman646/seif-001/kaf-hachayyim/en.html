1) [Seif 1] A hadas whose tip was severed is valid. Below, in seif 10, he cites a dispute. The reason is that these first two seifim are the language of the Rambam, while from seif 3 onward it is the language of the Tur. Here he followed the Rambam's wording incidentally together with the other laws. Biur HaGra; Mishna Berurah, sec. 2. See Machatzis HaShekel.
2) Ibid. A hadas whose tip was severed, etc. This refers to the wood. Regarding a leaf alone, the concept of its tip being severed does not apply at all, as stated below in seif 10 in the Rama's gloss; see there. All the laws of a severed tip will be explained there.
3) Ibid. If the majority of its leaves fell off, if three remained, etc. This too is the language of the Rambam, as stated above. The Maggid Mishneh explains that he means that three leaves remained in each and every whorl, as written in seif 4. This is contrary to the Tur's understanding of the Rambam, namely that according to the Rambam even one whorl suffices, regarding which the Beit Yosef expressed astonishment; see there. Biur HaGra. So too wrote Bigdei Yesha, that the Shulchan Aruch's statement, 'If the majority of its leaves fell off,' refers to what is written in seif 4, as Taz, s.k. 2, wrote. So too wrote Mishna Berurah, sec. 3.
4) The invalidations of avos apply equally on the first day of Yom Tov and on the remaining days. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 226, in the name of the Raavad; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Olas Shabbos, sec. 1; Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman. See Responsa Penei Yehoshua, siman 3, regarding a hadas whose majority of leaves fell off, that since it is invalid, it remains invalid even on the remaining days according to the opinion that beauty is required all seven days. Even a lulav and aravah are invalid on the remaining days just as on the first day, and likewise all invalidations except for something deficient and a borrowed species. It goes without saying that a hadas whose leaves fell off is invalid, since the Torah explicitly calls it avos, thickly leaved, etc.; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah. See above, siman 645, sec. 26.
5) Ibid. If three leaves remained, etc. Regarding our hadasim, which have two leaves above two, in accordance with what is written below in seif 3 in the Rama's gloss, it is obvious that even if only one leaf from each whorl was severed throughout the majority of the required length of the hadas, it is invalid, since initially there were only two leaves in each whorl and the majority of the whorl did not remain. Bach; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, sec. 2; Olas Shabbos, sec. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, sec. 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 7. This implies that if one leaf from each whorl was severed only throughout the minority of the length, while the majority remained complete, it is valid. Chayei Adam, klal 150, sec. 3, wrote that even if one leaf fell from one whorl within the majority of the required length of the hadas, it is invalid; see there. This will be further explained below, sec. 39; see there.
6) It is the manner of hadas branches that shoots emerge from their sides, and these interrupt the whorls of the hadas so that there are not three leaves in one whorl. They must be cut off. Ran. This applies when they interrupt between one leaf and another. Even if those emerging shoots themselves have three leaves in one whorl, they nevertheless interrupt between the leaves of the main stem and the bases of the shoots. One cannot fulfill the mitzvah with the shoots emerging from the side because they lack the required length of a hadas. If, however, those shoots emerge between one whorl and another, they do not need to be cut off. Yet if the shoots emerging from the sides do not have three leaves in one whorl, I am uncertain whether they must be cut off. Machatzis HaShekel, s.k. 1. Nevertheless, the common practice is to cut them off in every case. One should take care to cut them off before Yom Tov.
