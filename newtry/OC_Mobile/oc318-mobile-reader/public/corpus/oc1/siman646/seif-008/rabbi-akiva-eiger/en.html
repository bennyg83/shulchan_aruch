Se'if 8 That even if from the three that he chose, a nest, etc. This is puzzling to me, for from the words of the Rosh it appears that we require that three nests remain on the hadas branch, and in each nest the leaf at the top of the one nest should be one leaf that is set over the two of them. This requires great investigation. Afterward I found that the Gaon Rabbi Eliyahu of Vilna, of blessed memory, wrote so. I am further puzzled: if one leaf is set over the two of them, this is a wild hadas. The Korban Netanel addressed this. According to his words, the difficulty still remains against the Shulchan Aruch, which ruled in se'if 3 that such a case is called a wild hadas.
