What he wrote, that those that come from Italy are invalid, means that the Chacham Tzvi wrote so in response to what the Shevus Yaakov wrote, namely that one should not recite a berachah over the myrtles found in Prague, which are also found in Amsterdam and Hamburg, in order to fulfill the mitzvah with them, because of a concern that they are grafted. The older ones brought from Italy have small round berries, while the newer ones are elongated, and some larger seeds are also found. Therefore one should recite the berachah over the customary myrtles brought from afar, and as long as they are still green one fulfills the mitzvah with them. But in pressing circumstances, when the customary myrtles are not found, perhaps one may permit fulfilling the mitzvah with these, etc.; see there. Because of this the Chacham Tzvi wrote that the opposite is true: those brought from afar, namely from Italy, should be used only in a place and time when it is impossible to find the valid ones; see there. See my Beis Ephraim on lung tereifos at the beginning of siman 35, on the phrase 'even like a myrtle leaf,' where I brought the words of Machazik Bedek, wondered about him, and also explained this in my Responsa Beis Ephraim, Yoreh De'ah, siman 7. There I brought the above words of the Chacham Tzvi and wondered about the Kapos Temarim, who wanted to force into the words of the Rosh the idea that if one leaf is above, it is not a hadas shoteh. This is not so, for it is clear that specifically the three standing in a circle are required. I brought there the words of the Remazim, that they must stand in the same circle, except that one leaf is mounted over the other two and extends somewhat across their width; this is the avos of the verse. See there, where I wrote that according to this one can properly resolve the words of Machazik Berachah. What the Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of the Panim Meiros, that one violates bal tosif, see there: he did not write this decisively, but wrote only that they should have been more concerned that they might violate bal tosif according to some great authorities brought by the Rosh. It appears from there that according to the Rabbis, and it seems there is a scribal error and it should read that according to the Rabbis who hold that lulav does not require binding, one can say that even according to the Rabbis we do not say each item stands alone, but rather one violates bal tosif; see there. According to the view that lulav requires binding, one violates the Torah prohibition of bal tosif with a non-species, even if he intended it for beauty; see there. See Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 108, who brought the words of the Chacham Tzvi and wrote that his words are not compelling, and that the main ruling is as he wrote in part 1, that my grandfather, the Gaon Rabbi Shmuel Shapiro, did not want to change the custom at all. However, regarding taking old myrtles together with a new one, he also wrote that they did not act properly, because there is a decree on account of observers, due to bal tosif. In my humble opinion, a decree on account of observers does not apply here, since no change is recognizable except for the seeds, and who can determine that this is not its species? See also Responsa Panim Meiros, siman 9, and see Magen Avraham, siman 655, subparagraph 16, that intent is needed for this: he intends to fulfill the obligation only with one, whichever is correct according to law. So too we rule regarding chalitzah, that for a left-handed man they perform chalitzah with both left and right. Nevertheless, what forces me into all this? See there. His proof from chalitzah requires analysis. See my Responsa Beis Ephraim, siman 1, and the Turei Zahav above, siman 39. See Mor U'Ketziah, who wrote that regarding a myrtle, grafting does not invalidate at all, since the change is not recognizable; see there. His words require analysis. See Machazik Berachah, who brought from Responsa Rema, siman 117. Machazik Berachah wrote that if it is certainly grafted, it must be invalidated; see there. Also according to the reason of the Levush, who invalidates a grafted esrog because a transgression was committed with it, one should invalidate here, for perhaps it was grafted onto a food tree, and this is the sign, that its seeds are somewhat large, as we wrote above from the words of the Shevus Yaakov about this.
