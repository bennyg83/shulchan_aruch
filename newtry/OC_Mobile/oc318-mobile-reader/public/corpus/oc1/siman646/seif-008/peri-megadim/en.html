Even. See Turei Zahav, and see Chok HaRei'ah. It also implies that he validates a single nest of three leaves where one fell and two remained. With an Egyptian myrtle, however, three must specifically remain. See Eliyah Rabbah, letter 2, who writes that if there are two throughout the myrtle and one fell, it is invalid. From the Turei Zahav it implies that with two in every nest, if one fell it is valid. Only where there are three and two fell, which is the majority, is it invalid. In practice one should be stringent, namely if, throughout the majority of the required length of the myrtle, one leaf fell from each nest in a two-upon-two myrtle, as in se'if 5.
