Seif 2: Because it is, etc. Gemara there.
Or another person plucked them, etc. Maggid Mishneh's version of Rambam, however, reads: “another person plucked them for eating,” meaning a person who does not need to fulfill the mitzvah with this myrtle because he has another one, as stated in the Gemara there. See Taz and Magen Avraham. The poskim omitted the discussion there concerning when it first turned black, etc., because the Gemara asks, “Resolve it from this,” etc., and then says that Rav Pappa himself, etc. This implies that if we rule stringently, even an object disqualified from the outset remains disqualified, as in Rabbi Yirmiyah's question, because for purposes of stringency mitzvos are treated like sacred offerings, and concerning sacred offerings even an initial disqualification remains effective, as concluded in chapter 9 of Pesachim, chapter 1 of Kiddushin, chapter 1 of Zevachim, the final chapter of Kerisos, and elsewhere. Since the conclusion establishes that an initial disqualification does not remain effective, it necessarily follows that even leniently the principle of permanent disqualification does not apply to mitzvos. The same therefore applies where it was initially fit, became disqualified, and then became fit again, for Rav Pappa's case involved an object that was fit and then became disqualified. See Tosafos there, who struggled with this point.
And from the first day, etc. The reason for the disqualification is the requirement of beauty. Therefore, when the berries are green it is valid. They accordingly said in two or three places that the issue is its mottled appearance. As stated regarding a lichen, this follows Tosafos in the name of the Yerushalmi, that a lichen disqualifies only on the first day of Yom Tov. We, however, do not rule this way, as explained above in siman 649.
