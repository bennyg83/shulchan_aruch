If the defect is in the avot quality, it is invalid for all 7 days. [Terumat HaDeshen, siman 226] "Green ones are valid." Because they are a species of myrtle. "One by one for eating." In the Maharam edition of Rambam, the statement "or he picked them one by one for eating" means that another person who does not need this myrtle may initially pick the berries for eating. Although he thereby automatically repairs the myrtle, this is a davar she'eino mitkaven. One who needs this myrtle, however, even if he picks the berries for eating, creates a psik reisha and it is forbidden. This is the Gemara's rule, and Beit Yosef writes likewise. Even according to the authorities at the end of siman 320 who do not rule like the Aruch, see Beit Yosef there, for a mitzvah need it is nevertheless permitted through another person. [Tosafot] "And onward it is valid." Because it is within his power to repair it. "In every case." Bedek HaBayit writes: "This does not appear correct to me; rather, so long as he did not reduce the berries, it is always invalid." It appears to me that this is correct. Orchot Chaim follows the Yerushalmi cited in siman 648, according to which a chazazit on an etrog is valid on the remaining days. We, however, hold that such a defect is invalid on all days, as Rama himself writes in siman 649, seif 4, that an etrog with speckling is invalid throughout all 7 days. If so, this myrtle too should be invalid, because the Gemara explicitly compares its berries to speckling. Perhaps the opposing view holds that according to the Gemara's conclusion the problem is not speckling except when it appears in 2 or 3 places, whereas in a single place the defect is instead lack of hadar. Yet the truth indicates that even in 1 place the reason is hadar, for otherwise what would make it invalid there? Rama's words therefore require study, and one should not be lenient.
