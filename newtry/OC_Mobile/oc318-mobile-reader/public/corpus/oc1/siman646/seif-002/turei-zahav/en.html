Green are valid. For the myrtle itself is also green, so there is no change of appearance because of the berries. This is unlike black berries. Red berries are also considered black, for we say that this black is really red that has deteriorated.
And one may not reduce, etc. Meaning, they were detached before Yom Tov, and this fruit that grows on the myrtle grows even when detached. So Rashi explained.
One by one for eating. This reading is not correct in the Rambam. In the Gemara we say: Rabbi Shimon bar Elazar says, one may reduce them on Yom Tov. The Gemara asks: but he is repairing a utensil, for at first it was invalid and he is making it valid. Rav Ashi answers: this is where he picked them for eating and not to make it valid. The Gemara asks: but it is a pesik reisha. It answers: he has another hosha'ana. The Rambam is difficult, for it sounds as if he wants to permit reducing them initially when he picks them for eating, and he should have mentioned that he has another hosha'ana. The Maggid Mishneh reads: or another person picked them for eating. This means another person who does not need this repair, for presumably he has another one of his own. This is like the Gemara's answer. The Beis Yosef also agreed to this. Therefore it is surprising that here he copied this reading, 'one by one, etc.,' which is not a correct reading.
And from the first day onward, etc. I do not know any special quality in this invalidation of its berries being more numerous than its leaves over other invalidations, such as its leaves being split apart and the other invalidations of myrtle. For regarding all invalid items we say in siman 649 that they are valid from the first day onward, and on the second day of Yom Tov as well its law is the same as the other invalid items, that they are taken without a berachah, as explained there below. Why did the Rema need to write it here? Further, it is difficult for me: why is there leniency here on the other days? It has a remedy, for he can reduce them on Chol HaMoed. This is rejection from the outset, and we say explicitly in Perek Lulav HaGazul, Sukkah 33, that rejection applies to this. It says there regarding what the Mishnah teaches, 'if he reduced them it is valid': when did they blacken? Meaning, when did they become black and invalid? If they blackened before Yom Tov, that is rejection from the outset. Rashi explains: the time of the mitzvah arrived and it was not fit for him, so it is rejection from the outset, and it is stronger than fit and then rejected and then fit again. Shall we resolve from this that rejection from the outset is not rejection? Rather, it means they blackened on Yom Tov, which is fit and then rejected. Thus you learn that there is never rejection when they blackened from the outset, and you may resolve that this is not rejection, but you cannot resolve from here that fit and then rejected is not rejection. If so, here, where the berries were more numerous, it is rejection from the outset; why should he not reduce them on Chol HaMoed and take them? As to what is written in this sugya, that the question of fit and then rejected was not resolved, namely when they blackened on Yom Tov, and there is permission only when they blackened before Yom Tov: why did the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur write without qualification that if he reduced them it is valid, and not write that this is specifically when they blackened before Yom Tov, but if they blackened on Yom Tov one should be stringent, since it is a Torah doubt? This is the question Rabbi Yirmeyah asked nearby, whether rejection applies to mitzvos, whether it should be compared to korbanos, where rejection is from the Torah. The Beis Yosef answered that since it is in his power to repair it, it is not rejection, as stated in Zevachim that wherever it is in his power, it is not rejection. The repair is that he can reduce them, as stated in the Gemara, where he picks them for eating and has another hosha'ana. In my humble opinion this does not appear correct, for on Yom Tov he cannot repair it so that it becomes valid. The Gemara's answer, that he picked them for eating, means that his intent is only for eating, unlike here, where you say that he can repair it, which means he will reduce them for the sake of repair, and that is forbidden on Yom Tov. Further, if so, the Rosh should have mentioned that it speaks specifically where he has another hosha'ana, as the Gemara says. Further, if so, even fit and then rejected should also be permitted, as the Ran wrote; the Beis Yosef wrote about this that in truth it is permitted and the question was resolved, except that at first they did not know this reasoning. This is not at all implied in the sugya. It appears to me that there is no difficulty, for there above in the sugya Rav Papa said, 'This teaches that there is no rejection regarding mitzvos,' implying completely, even leniently, and that rejection of a mitzvah is not like rejection of a korban. Rabbi Yirmeyah was merely uncertain about this inference of Rav Papa, whether it applies even leniently. We do not leave Rav Papa's certainty and follow Rabbi Yirmeyah's uncertainty, as we say similarly at the beginning of Bava Kamma regarding half-damages of pebbles, where Rav Papa was uncertain and Rava was certain, and the poskim rule there like Rava, who was certain. So too here. Therefore it is permitted even if they blackened on Yom Tov. Although the Ran wrote here that it is permitted only if they blackened before Yom Tov, the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur do not agree with him. This appears clear to me.
