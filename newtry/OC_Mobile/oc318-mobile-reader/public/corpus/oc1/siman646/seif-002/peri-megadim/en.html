Three leaves. See Turei Zahav. In the Rambam, of blessed memory, chapter 8, halachah 5, of Hilchos Lulav, and in the Gemara, Sukkah 32, and in Rashi and Tosafos there, it appears that the text of the Gemara is: three leaves in one nest. See Lechem Mishneh there. So too the reading in the Rif appears to be: in one nest. Nevertheless this requires analysis, for in the Rambam only the baraisa there about most of its leaves falling is mentioned, which concerns an Egyptian myrtle, whereas the Mechaber states this twice, here and in se'if 5, which appears redundant. There is no distinction at all whether one complete nest remained at its top or not, as written in se'if 5. With an ordinary myrtle of three leaves in one nest, if one leaf remains, even though the majority, two leaves, remains, it is invalid. See letter 4, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there.
