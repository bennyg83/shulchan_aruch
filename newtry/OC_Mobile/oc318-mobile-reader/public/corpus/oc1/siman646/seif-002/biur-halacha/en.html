If he transgressed and picked them, etc. The Mechaber writes this without qualification, implying that in his view even if they blackened on Yom Tov, in which case it was fit and then rejected, nevertheless it becomes fit again when he repairs it. In truth, this is a question in the Gemara. See the Beis Yosef, who resolves this, and the Turei Zahav and the Gra also worked to explain why we rule leniently here. The reason the Mechaber wrote this without qualification is that the Rif, the Rosh, and the Rambam also stated the law without distinguishing. In my humble opinion, however, this is not proof, for the plain expression 'black' does not imply that they blackened today; it means that they were black from yesterday. In any event, since they did not explicitly rule leniently, and we have found several very early Rishonim who are stringent about this, one should not be lenient. These are Rav Yitzchak Giat, and so it also appears from Rabbeinu Chananel; so too wrote the Baal HaMaor, whose words are also brought in Shibbolei HaLeket, the Ittur, the Or Zarua, and the Ran. See all their words, for they took the view that one may be lenient only when they blackened before Yom Tov, which is merely rejection from the outset and is not called rejection, but not when they blackened on Yom Tov.
