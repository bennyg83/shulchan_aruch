(Subparagraph 2) One says, etc. The language of the Rambam, as cited by the Beis Yosef, is: if he transgressed and picked them, or if another picked them for eating, it is valid. This must be the correct reading. For it says in the Gemara, page 32, and so the Rambam rules: if one picked them on Yom Tov, or if he picked them one by one for eating, it is valid. Granted, according to Rabbi Shimon bar Elazar, who said that an act not intended is permitted, picking for eating is permitted. But according to the Sages it is still difficult, for they hold that an act not intended is forbidden, and nevertheless they said that it is valid after the fact. The Gemara answers that the case is where he has another hosha'ana. If so, he is not repairing a utensil. Rav Ashi said: even if he does not have another hosha'ana, it is permitted, for since if he finds another hosha'ana he can take it, even now he is not considered to be repairing a utensil. Thus the Rambam ruled like Rav Ashi. But if the reading were 'or if he picked them for eating,' it would imply that even the owner himself picked them for eating, and that would be permitted even initially, since it is an act not intended. The Maharam Padua therefore corrected the text to read: 'or another picked them for eating.'
And even according to the poskim, etc. Tosafos rejects Rashi's explanation there. For if he has another hosha'ana, it is a case of pesik reisha that is not beneficial to him, and it is permitted according to the Aruch. And even according to the poskim who disagree with the Aruch and forbid a pesik reisha even when it is not beneficial to him, they nevertheless permit it here because it is for the need of a mitzvah.
Furthermore, even those who disagree with the Aruch concede that the prohibition here is only rabbinic, for it is a melachah she-einah tzerichah legufah, an act of labor not needed for its own constructive purpose. The Rambam, however, rules that one is liable for a melachah she-einah tzerichah legufah. Therefore the Rambam cannot explain the sugya in that manner, and he must explain it either like the Aruch or like Rashi.
