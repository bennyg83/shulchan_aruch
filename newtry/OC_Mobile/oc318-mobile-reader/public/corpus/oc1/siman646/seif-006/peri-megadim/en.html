And from the first day. See Turei Zahav, and see Magen Avraham, letters 3 and 4, and the Levush, who wrote that from the first day onward it is rabbinic, etc. At first glance one could say that 'from the first day onward' means, for us, from Chol HaMoed onward: if they blackened on Chol HaMoed and he reduced them, it is valid, for it is not a case of fit, rejected, and fit again, since it is in his power to repair them on Chol HaMoed. But if they blackened on the first or second day of Yom Tov, that is the question of fit and then rejected, whether it returns and becomes fit. However, from the Mechaber's plain wording it implies that even on the first day, if he transgressed and picked them, it is valid in every case, even if they blackened on Yom Tov. The Turei Zahav wrote that Rav Papa resolved it from, 'This teaches that there is no rejection, etc.,' while Rabbi Yirmeyah was uncertain about the language of the Mishnah, as Rashi wrote there, 33a, s.v. mifshat pshit lei letanna. If so, we do not abandon Rav Papa's certainty because of Rabbi Yirmeyah's uncertainty. Now, where a new shoot grew, it is not in his power; where the wind covered it and it became uncovered again, it is in his power to uncover it. See the Bayis Chadash about this and see Chok HaRei'ah. Regarding a myrtle whose top was cut off and a new shoot grew, one can say that it is a double doubt: perhaps it is valid, and perhaps there is no rejection regarding mitzvos. See Chok HaRei'ah. A matter that is valid after the fact but invalid initially, when it returns to validity, is not called fit and then rejected according to all opinions, and it is valid there in Chok HaRei'ah. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham.
