And for indispensability, in its majority. See Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that several Acharonim agreed to be lenient like the Ra'ah, that even if one leaf fell from every nest along its whole length, it is still valid, since two leaves remain in each nest, which is a majority. I was uncertain about a case where some leaves fell from part of the length of the myrtle, where the Mechaber rules like the Ra'avad that it is valid because avos is required only in the majority of its length: if, from that majority section, one leaf also fell from each nest, do we say two majorities to validate it? I saw one of the Acharonim whose words imply that it should be validated even in this case, and so it also appears from Chayei Adam. But in my humble opinion this requires analysis. From where do we know to count each matter by majority, both regarding its width and regarding its length? It is enough for us to be lenient like the Ra'ah and validate when one leaf is missing from each nest along its whole length, or like the Ra'avad when the minority of the length is missing all across its width, from the majority and downward. But we should not combine both leniencies. After all, through this most of its leaves will be called fallen. For example, if the myrtle branch has seven rows of nests, three leaves in each nest, and three rows fell entirely, and from the four remaining rows one leaf is missing in each row, thirteen leaves are missing in all, and only eight leaves remain on the myrtle, which is the minority. There is no proof from the Bayis Chadash, who wrote that even in one nest that is within the majority section, if two leaves fell from it, it is invalid because the majority measure of the myrtle did not remain avos. It is possible that when he validates where one leaf fell, he means only where it is missing from one nest, for then most of the leaves still remain on the myrtle, as in the above example, where eleven leaves would remain and only ten leaves would be missing, but not more than that. This requires analysis. In any event, one should be lenient only where no other myrtle can be found in the city, as it appears to me, and perhaps that was their intention as well. Know further that the Bayis Chadash wrote that if the majority of the required measure of the myrtle is avos, even if the myrtle is very large, we do not require the majority of its full growth to be avos, but only the majority of its required measure, meaning close to two tefachim. See Pri Megadim, who is uncertain whether this helps even if those avos sections are scattered along the length of the myrtle, or only if they are at least in one place; Bikkurei Yaakov is lenient even about this.
