And for indispensability, most of it. This means that a hadas whose measure is no more than twelve thumbbreadths, as below in siman 650, does not need to be 'avot' throughout; only seven thumbbreadths must be 'avot,' meaning three leaves in each nest. The five thumbbreadths, even if completely lacking because their leaves fell off, or they were not 'avot,' meaning their leaves were two above two or two and one, are nevertheless valid, since most of it was 'avot.' If a hadas had three leaves in each nest along its full required length, and one leaf fell from each nest over most of the required length of the hadas, there is a dispute among the Rishonim. Some validate, for since two remained in each nest, the majority is like the whole, just as we validate when some of its leaves fell off along the length of the hadas. Others invalidate, for they hold that through this the name 'avot' does not remain upon it at all, since 'avot' is only with three. Regarding practical halachah, we take the lenient view in pressing circumstances, for so several later authorities agreed. This is specifically where at least two leaves remained in each nest, so that there is at least a majority; but if two leaves fell off through most of the required length of the hadas, it is invalid according to all opinions. If so, in a place where the custom is to fulfill with hadasim of two above two, as above in the gloss, if one leaf fell from each nest over most of the required length of the hadas, it is invalid according to all opinions, for no majority remains.
Even if it is not at its head. Such as where from the bottom until most of it, three leaves emerge in each nest, and above that its leaves fell off or it is not 'avot,' it is also valid. And even though regarding leaves that dried he ruled in se'if 8 that it is valid only if three moist leaves remain at the head of each branch, there the reason is because of beauty, and beauty is recognizable when it is at its head. But here the reason is 'avot,' and any place suffices.
