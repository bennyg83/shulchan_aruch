Even if most of them fell, etc. In the Gemara the Rabbis taught: if most of its leaves fell and a minority remained, it is valid, provided that its avos remains. This itself is difficult: you said that if most of its leaves fell it is valid, and then you said, provided that its avos remains. Once two have fallen, how can avos be found, since altogether there are only three? Abaye said: it is found with an Egyptian myrtle, where seven stand in one nest; when four fall, three remain. The Ran wrote: regarding an ordinary myrtle, its law was not explained. There is one who says that it must all be avos, and if even one leaf from its entire required measure remains missing, it is invalid, etc. This is what we say, that its avos must remain, and we established it where three remained, proving that with fewer than three it is not called avos. But the Ra'ah holds that when we say three in a nest, it means that the species must be of this type. If it is of this species, but some of its leaves fell, as long as the majority remains in each nest it is valid and is properly called avos. He infers this, etc. One should not be lenient this way, for his proof is not clear. These are the Ran's words. The Rosh wrote that what the baraisa teaches, that most of its leaves fell, refers to the leaves beyond the required avos. 'Nifrezu' in the Mishnah means that most of the avos of the myrtle's required measure fell and the minority remained. These are his words. The Beis Yosef wrote that this implies that if the entire required length of the myrtle was avos and most of the leaves fell, meaning most of the nests, even if only one leaf fell from each nest, it is invalid. This follows the words of the Geonim who hold that avos is required throughout the whole required length of the myrtle. These are his words. I do not know the basis for his implication, for the Rosh distinguishes between the falling of leaves in the baraisa, which refers to the surplus beyond the avos, that is, from valid avos in one place, namely an Egyptian myrtle that has seven leaves, four of which are surplus, and the 'nifrezu' of the Mishnah, which speaks of most of the measure being broken in one place. With an ordinary myrtle, whose measure is three leaves, most of them being broken means two. The Rosh wrote this explicitly regarding willow: the final clause teaches that if some of its leaves fell it is valid, to teach that 'its leaves were split' means the majority; he taught it in the last clause, and the same applies to all of them. Regarding the invalid case he used the expression 'nifrezu' because it is the majority. These are his words. This also refers to an ordinary myrtle, all of whose leaves are only three, and nevertheless it is invalidated only when the majority was broken. Do not ask: what is the real difference, that we do not require all the avos, meaning all three, as the baraisa said, provided that its avos remains? One can answer that the Mishnah speaks of an ordinary myrtle that has only three leaves, but the baraisa speaks of an Egyptian myrtle that has seven. When most fell, we require that the whole measure of avos remain, similar to an ordinary myrtle, which is not validated when only one remains, since the majority fell from it, but rather the majority must remain. So too with an Egyptian myrtle, whenever the majority fell, three must remain, which is the measure of avos. So the Tur also wrote: first he wrote about an Egyptian myrtle of seven leaves that three must remain, and afterward he wrote that if most of the leaves of the avos measure fell, it is invalid. It is clear that he speaks of an ordinary myrtle, whose measure is three, and it is invalid specifically when two fell from it. It follows that no one is stringent to invalidate an ordinary myrtle when one of the three leaves fell except the Ran alone. Therefore, for us, where our myrtle has two leaves above two, if one leaf from the required myrtle measure, which must be avos, meaning three tefachim, fell, it is invalid according to the Ran; but according to the Rosh, if two leaves fell from most of this required measure, then it is invalid. One must be careful about this, because many times many leaves fall from the myrtle and people do not pay attention to it.
