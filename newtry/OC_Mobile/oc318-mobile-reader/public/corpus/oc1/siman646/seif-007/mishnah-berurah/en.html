When their surface whitens. After it crumbles under the fingernail. [Magen Avraham] In truth, it is not found in reality that its surface whitens and it does not crumble under the fingernail; if so, when its surface whitens it is certainly dry. One who is not expert in this measure, it implies from Tamim De'im, can measure by soaking them for a day or two in water: if they return to what they were in their feel and appearance, they are still moist, and if not, they are dry. The Peri Megadim wrote that if the wood of the hadas dried, even though the leaves are still green, it is invalid. Bikkurei Yaakov challenges this, and his view is that invalidity does not apply at all to dryness of the wood; see there.
