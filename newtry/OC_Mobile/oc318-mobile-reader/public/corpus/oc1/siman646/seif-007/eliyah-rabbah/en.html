[7] In a place where it is possible, etc. The Bayis Chadash wrote that if its top dried out, even if no other is available, one should not recite a berachah over it; this is brought by the Magen Avraham. But the Nachalas Tzvi ruled leniently even when its top dried out if no other is available. This also appears from the Tamim De'im in the name of the Ra'avad: if most of its leaves remain moist, even if the top dried out, it is valid. A dried top is better than one that was cut off. This is comparable to what is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 59, regarding a flayed animal.
