They designated it for a latrine, etc. — there is no difference between designation by speech and designation by action [so too Tosafos Yeshanim in Nedarim]. It would seem that we require permanent designation; but designation for a time is not called designation here; proof from above siman 42, seif 3. But one may reject this, for otherwise there it is ruled that designation even permanently without action is not a matter at all; whereas here it is in any case forbidden rabbinically; therefore it is because of disgrace to recite Shema and pray in a place where such a repugnant name is mentioned upon it, as the poskim wrote. If so, it is possible that the same applies even when one designated it only for a time — so it is, and it requires study. Nevertheless, from all this there is a practical difference regarding what we wrote in Mishna Berurah: if there was designation and action, it is a complete beit hakisei for all its laws, and therefore its prohibition is from the Torah; for certainly if one did not designate it only for a time, its prohibition is not from the Torah, as above. And what is written further — if by chance one relieved himself there, etc. — in truth action without designation is worse than designation without action, for in this, according to all in general, it is not a matter, as stated in Berachos 23b — see there; if so, whence do we have to be stringent in this in our matters? Moreover, if so we bury all the paths of Neharde'a — so writes Yevaser Tov, and his words are correct. Even though I found in the Shulchan Aruch of the Gra that if one used it only once, it becomes an old beit hakisei and its prohibition is from the Torah — therefore his intention is in a beit hakisei built for this purpose, or even if it has no partitions at all but one designated this place initially for a beit hakisei; but otherwise not. The main point of the Gra's law that he wrote it is forbidden from the Torah seems that he inferred from the wording of Rambam and Shulchan Aruch that wrote "he has not yet used it" — it implies; but if he used it, it becomes an old beit hakisei for all its laws. And one may say on this the verse "and a spade shall be for you," etc. — that is, a designated place; behold there was designation there, and nevertheless the Torah permitted after covering the feces to speak words of Torah there, as Terumat HaYerushalayim wrote at the end of the chapter "One whose relative died." And what is better — covering, or if one removed the feces entirely that it is forbidden? One may say that designation did not take effect there, since at the time of designation he knew he would need to cover immediately; or alternatively, since each person would have a stake on his belt to dig immediately and cover — this is similar to what is explained in siman 87, seif katan 1 in Magen Avraham — see there. Also this is not better than a place designated for burial where one explicitly placed the dead there with intent to relieve himself — as stated in Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 364 that it is permitted, and brought above in Magen Avraham siman 42 — see there.
