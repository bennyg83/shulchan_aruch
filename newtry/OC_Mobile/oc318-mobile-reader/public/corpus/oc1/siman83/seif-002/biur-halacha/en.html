He designated it as a privy, etc. - There is no distinction here between designation by speech and designation through an act, as Tosafos Yom Tov writes in Nedarim. At first glance, the designation must be permanent, whereas temporary designation would not be considered designation here, as may be proved from siman 42, seif 3 above. This proof may be rejected, however, because there we rule that even permanent designation without an act has no effect at all, while here it is at least prohibited D'Rabbanan. This must be because it is degrading to recite Krias Shema or pray in a place upon which such a repulsive designation has taken effect, as the poskim wrote. If so, perhaps the same applies even when it was designated only temporarily, and this requires study. Nevertheless, this distinction has practical relevance to what we wrote in Mishnah Berurah, that when there was both designation and use it is a full privy for all its laws, and its prohibition is therefore D'Oraisa. Certainly, if it was designated only temporarily, its prohibition would not be D'Oraisa, as explained. Regarding what we further wrote, that if one happened to relieve himself there, in truth an act without designation is weaker than designation without an act, for in other areas all agree that this has no effect, as stated in Berachos 23b. If so, from where do we derive stringency in our matter? Moreover, if so, all the paths of Nehardea would become defiled. Sefer Yevaser Tov writes this, and his words are correct. Although I found that the Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes that if it was used even once it thereafter becomes an old privy and its prohibition is D'Oraisa, he must mean a privy that was built for this purpose, or even a place without any partitions that was initially designated as a privy, but not otherwise. The principal ruling of Shulchan Aruch HaRav, that it is prohibited D'Oraisa, appears to be inferred from the language of Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, who write that it has not yet been used, implying that once it was used it becomes an old privy for all its laws. One cannot challenge this from the verse, 'You shall have a designated place outside the camp,' which refers to a designated place, yet the Torah permitted speaking words of Torah there after covering the excrement, as Rabbeinu Yonah writes at the end of the chapter Mi Shemeiso. Why should covering be better than removing the excrement entirely, which remains forbidden? It may be answered that the designation did not take effect there, because at the time of designation he knew that he would immediately have to cover it. Alternatively, since each person had a spade among his implements with which to dig and cover immediately, this resembles the case explained by Magen Avraham in siman 87, seif katan 1. It is also no stronger than a place designated for burial in which a corpse was placed explicitly on condition that it later be removed, which Tur and Yoreh Deah siman 364 permit, as cited above by Magen Avraham in siman 42.
