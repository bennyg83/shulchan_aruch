(6) And it has not yet, etc. - But if it was used even once, it thereafter has the status of an old privy for all its laws, since there were both designation and an act. If there was no designation, but one happened to relieve himself there, and afterward the excrement was removed and no foul odor remains, it appears to me that it has no status of a privy at all and it is permitted even inside it. I found this likewise in Sefer Yevaser Tov. See Biur Halachah.
(7) To recite. This is permitted even within four amos of it, although some authorities are stringent.
(8) Opposite it - For we rule that designation alone has no effect, as stated above in siman 42, seif 3. Nevertheless, inside it is prohibited D'Rabbanan, because reciting Krias Shema or praying there constitutes a great degradation. Even after the fact, if one recited or prayed inside it, he did not fulfill his obligation, as Levush rules.
(9) But not inside it - Meaning, if that place has no partitions, only the specific area that he designated is forbidden. If the designated sitting area is enclosed by partitions, the designation forbids the entire enclosed area within the partitions. See nearby, seif katan 7. Outside it, however, it is permitted even adjacent to it. Baer Heitev writes that it is permitted to think words of Torah inside a new privy that has not yet been used, and Eliyah Rabbah writes likewise. It is obvious that builders constructing a privy may not recite any blessing inside the area of the partitions, even before the partitions have been built, because once the place has been marked out for them, that area has been set aside as a privy and is forbidden.
