(6) And it has not yet been used, etc. — but if it was used even only once, it has the name of an old bathroom for all its laws, since there was designation and act. And if there was no designation and one only happened to relieve himself there, and afterward they removed the feces and there is also no bad smell, it appears to me it has no name of bathroom at all and it is permitted even inside; and so I found in Yevaser Tov. See in Biur Halacha.

(7) To recite — and even within its four amot; and some are stringent about this.

(8) Opposite it — for we hold designation is not a significant matter [as above in siman 42, seif 3 — see there]; nevertheless inside it is forbidden rabbinically, for it is great degradation for Kerias Shema or prayer, and even after the fact if he recited and prayed inside he has not fulfilled his obligation (Levush).

(9) Not inside it — meaning if that place is without partitions, only that designated place is forbidden; but if the designated seat area is surrounded by partitions, the designation forbids the entire area within the partitions; see nearby in note 7. But from outside it is permitted adjacent to it. Beit HaTefillah wrote that to think about words of Torah is permitted inside a new bathroom that has not yet been used; and similarly Eliyah Rabbah. And it is obvious that builders building a bathroom are forbidden to recite any blessing there from inside the partition area, even before they built the partitions, since once the place was marked off for them this place has become designated for a bathroom and is forbidden.
