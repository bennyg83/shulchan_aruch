(siman kuf alef) If he has partitions — even if they are not ten [tefachim] high; and Bach inferred thus from Shulchan Aruch's wording, since it was left unstated and wrote "if he has partitions" without specifying ten [tefachim] high — it implies even less than ten helps according to the Ri, as he wrote in his long work; for it seemed to him, etc., they do not help even for ten high; and it suffices if we are lenient like Bach for ten high, but not for less — as Magen Avraham concludes at the end of this siman kuf alef.
New privy, etc. The ground is forbidden even though there is no feces in it, through designation for the need of the privy; likewise partitions — even though there is no feces on them — are graf shel rei, namely the vessel in which feces rests, as below seif 7.
Partitions between him and the privy, and the plain meaning, etc., on the one who says thus — as explained above seif 6 seif 4: he put his hand into a hole of the privy, and on this Rashi and Baal HaMaor [explain]: partitions between him and between the privy, etc. And likewise in Rashba's novellae on the above gemara "he put his hand into the privy" — and we say thus, etc. For it teaches "one who enters" — when he has partitions it does not mean entering; and if so, even though it says to him "partitions," it must be at a distance of four amot; it implies partitions do not help.
However, this "if he has" means even if he has no partitions, the language "one who enters" applies — namely, he entered that field in which the privy is within it; and such we learned in tractate Taharot chapter 6 mishnah 5: one who enters a valley in the rainy season.
For we do not call it a privy except the place of sitting — for more than the house of sitting one cannot recite [Shema] in the privy; what boundary did he establish to recite [Shema] in the privy since he has no partitions? If so, in a new [privy] it is permitted to recite within the partitions, etc. — for in a new one only its inside is forbidden but outside it, opposite it, is permitted, as seif 2 wrote; and if so, if there were no partitions, it would not be forbidden in a new one except on the place of sitting, which alone is called privy and no more; and if so, when he has partitions, only the place of sitting is forbidden, but opposite it — even within the partitions — is permitted, for the partitions do not cause prohibition more than if he had no partitions.
Ravina from Nedarim. And this is from here in siman 53, and it is explained there in Nedarim that Ravina had two inquiries: one — they designated a privy: does designation help? And if you say designation helps, he must inquire whether there is "hand" to the privy — namely, if he said "this house shall be a privy" and "this too." And certainly the second too is a privy, except that he said about the second "and this" and did not say the word "too," so it is only "hand" — for the law is not clear whether he meant "and this" shall be a privy and "this" for ordinary use, and it is not resolved; and if so, what Magen Avraham wrote "and this too — what?" — the word "too" is an error, for in such a case he would not need it at all, and it is plain to him that he meant for the privy and it is forbidden; only if he did not say the word "too" would he need it, as above.
With this, ordinary use does not apply — for the place of sitting is not fit for anything else except the privy.
And if he has partitions — it is forbidden to recite, etc. — and not as Magen Avraham wrote above; rather, the partitions reduce — namely, when he has no partitions he is not forbidden in a new one except the place of sitting, for more than that one cannot recite [Shema] in the privy; what boundary did he establish? Shall the whole world recite [Shema] in the privy? Not so: when he has partitions, then on the contrary the partitions limit, and we say: all that is within the partitions is called privy. And if so, Ravina's inquiry is in a new privy in which he has partitions and said "this" — whether he meant all that is in the space of the partitions shall be privy, or the space is for another use.
If so, what does the gemara in Berachos chapter Mi sheMeto challenge — that Ravina should resolve his inquiry that designation does not help for a privy, and a new privy is permitted? For Rephaim [in the name] of R' Chisda said: a person stands opposite the privy and prays — and they establish it there in Berachos that perforce Rephaim deals with a new privy and permits opposite it even though before him in an old privy it is forbidden as far as his eyes can see, and nevertheless he permits opposite it in a new one; perforce designation is not a [significant] matter and it has no law of privy. And they answer: Ravina's inquiry is regarding its inside, and Rephaim deals outside it, where it is permitted in a new one — the gemara's words are brought in Magen Avraham siman kuf hei; and Magen Avraham's view is: that which the questioner does not bring the gemara's answer — for perforce Rephaim deals outside it, for it teaches "opposite it"; perforce the presumption was that Ravina's inquiry too was outside it, until the answerer innovated for him that Ravina deals specifically inside it. And now that perforce the second inquiry is regarding "hand" when he said "and this" — it deals when he has partitions, as Magen Avraham wrote — for otherwise ordinary use would not be needed. If so, the first inquiry too — whether designation helps for a privy — deals when he has partitions, for the two inquiries are asked together; if so, since it deals with one who has partitions, perforce he would not need to ask only its inside, but outside it is certainly permitted — for even in an old one partitions help to be a separation and it is permitted outside according to the Ri; and since Ravina perforce deals inside it and Rephaim's wording implies he deals outside it, since he said the language "opposite it," as Magen Avraham wrote siman kuf hei — if so, perhaps he deals with one who has no partitions, as plain privies were in the time of the gemara according to the Ri; and if so, the gemara's answer is explained: Rephaim deals outside and Ravina inside — if so, what is the challenge, and how did it not bring the gemara's answer? See in Magen Avraham siman kuf hei.
And further: if partitions help, etc. — meaning, if partitions cause the whole space to be forbidden, how will they not cause the partitions themselves to be forbidden?
From the challenge on Rava who said, etc. See Hagahot Maimoniyot in Magen Avraham siman kuf hei: behold, partitions interrupt. Meaning, if a plain privy in the time of the gemara had partitions. Not so Rava, who stands at feces where there is no interruption of a partition — or that their plain privy had no partitions — and he challenged well. And if he has partitions — why did the Ri infer: if a privy in the time of the gemara had partitions, what does Rava challenge on Rephaim? Rephaim deals with one who has partitions — if so, partitions interrupt; but if we say that in the time of the gemara there were partitions, perforce even partitions would not be an interruption — and perforce the Ri did not say partitions interrupt except because he proved to his view that in the time of the gemara privies had no partitions, and therefore distancing from the privy was required; but when he has a partition, we say it is reasonable that it interrupts. If so, what proof does the Ri bring — that perhaps always in the time of the gemara there were partitions, and nevertheless he challenged well Rava on Rephaim — that Rephaim deals with one who has partitions: behold, partitions are not an interruption; or that in the time of the gemara there were partitions — nevertheless the Ri inferred thus: if in the time of the gemara there were partitions, then partitions do not interrupt; but nevertheless, whence does the questioner challenge Rava? Rava deals without partitions and Rephaim with partitions — and perhaps partitions interrupt.
And whence does he challenge that there is no feces in it? This is the gemara's first answer — and why did we not bring the questioner up [to it]? Because he knew to challenge on this as he challenged afterward on this answer; and "R' Yirmiyah: privy — they said that even when there is no feces in it" — if so, Taz too should not have been inclined to distinguish that Rephaim deals with interruption of partitions, for he knew the truth that partitions do not interrupt — as above.
It should appear they are ten [tefachim] high. Then one may rely on Bach as above; and see Taz, who also agreed partitions do not interrupt — except he wrote specifically when that partition is designated for the privy alone; but if it is designated for the house and the privy — e.g. he uses one side for the house and the other side for the privy — it does not have the law of privy partitions, and it is permitted to recite even near it.
Forbidden according to all. Even according to the one who says in siman 79 that a partition interrupts even for smell — nevertheless privy partitions are inferior for this matter.
