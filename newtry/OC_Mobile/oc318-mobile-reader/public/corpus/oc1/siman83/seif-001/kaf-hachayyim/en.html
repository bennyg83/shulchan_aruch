(a) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to recite [Shema] opposite an old privy, etc. Meaning: any privy in which there was feces (even if they removed it) it is as though the feces is still inside, and one must distance before him as far as his eyes can see and behind him four amot from where the smell ceased. Bach. Etz Chaim ot 1. Eliyah Rabbah ot 1. Maamar Mordechai ot 1; and see there what he wrote on the Ri's words; see there. Rav Zundel ot 1.
(b) There; it is forbidden to recite [Shema] opposite an old privy. Even if they washed it, cleaned it, and coated it with lime; nevertheless, since an hour has passed, it has no allowance. Beit Yehudah chelek 1 Orach Chayyim siman 10. See in Mishna Berurah siman 84 in Biur Halacha ot 1, Chasam Sofer ot 1. But if he reconsidered regarding the privy and demolished it and made the place a house, it is permitted; for the reason is on account of disgrace; and since the name of privy was uprooted from it, there is no further disgrace; go out and see how the public treats it. And likewise in responsum of Peri Megadim regarding a bathhouse. Chasam Sofer there. See below siman 84 ot 3.
(c) There; old privy, etc.; and if there is doubt whether it is new or old, we go according to stringency. Ein Tov ot 1, Eliyah Rabbah there.
(d) There; but if he has partitions, etc. Since it did not write how high the partition must be, it implies even if it is not ten [tefachim] high, as Beit Yosef wrote. However, Bach wrote that we require a partition ten [tefachim] high; see there; and likewise Knesset HaGedolah in Biur Halacha ot 3, Peri Chadash ot 1, Etz Chaim ot 2. Yet the view of Taz in siman kuf alef is to forbid even with a partition ten [tefachim] high, since the partitions are designated to receive feces (they are considered like feces) even though there is no bad smell there; see there. And likewise Magen Avraham in siman kuf alef. And Beit Din, Eliyah Rabbah ot 2, and likewise Shaarei Teshuvah ot 1, Rav Zundel ot 1, Chayei Adam kelal 3 ot 11, Chasam Sofer ot 2, Ruach Chaim siman 79 ot 2; and specifically regarding partitions that are made only for the privy, e.g. when one makes in his courtyard a room designated for this alone; but if he built the privy in a partition of the house that he adjoined to that partition (even though that partition is literally the privy partition) that partition does not have the law of a privy for this matter, and it is permitted to recite [Shema] even nearby, since the partition is not designated for the privy alone. And from this: whoever has a privy in his courtyard in a designated room; one may not recite Shema or pray in the courtyard as far as opposite the privy as far as his eyes can see, like the law of feces; and this is in a privy that has no pit but the feces rests on the ground, like those in the days of the Sages of the Talmud at the edge of the field. Taz there. Eliyah Rabbah there, Shaarei Teshuvah there, Rav Zundel there, Chayei Adam there, Chasam Sofer there, Ruach Chaim there; and see there in Chasam Sofer that he was uncertain if there is a pit but it is under the drain and is not ten deep; and moreover even if it is not lacking that there is feces and urine outside; it is possible this too is considered like graf shel rei and it is forbidden to recite opposite it, as Beit Yosef wrote to this effect. See Ruach Chaim there and Ben Ish Chai parashat Bo ot 25. And all this if no bad smell reaches him; but if bad smell reaches him, it is forbidden according to all; for partitions do not help for smell. Magen Avraham there, Chayei Adam there, Chasam Sofer there. See what I wrote above siman 79 ot 19 and ot 21.
(d) Toilet rooms adjoining a synagogue; if they have partitions ten [tefachim] high and they also pray in the synagogue surrounded by partitions; we have no concern for these two separations, provided no bad smell reaches them. Shvut Yaakov chelek 1 siman 13. But if bad smell reaches them, there is no separation and a change of domain does not help, and neighbors must distance even though they have held it for many years; Or HaNe'elam book 5, and Beit Din Ikkei HaDat siman 5 ot 15. See further below siman 86 ot 3.
