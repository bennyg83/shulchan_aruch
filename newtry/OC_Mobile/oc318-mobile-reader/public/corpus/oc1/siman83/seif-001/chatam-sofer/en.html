In the Mechaber, seif 5. It seems to me that all this is when the feces falls into the bench itself — in this Magen Avraham is stringent. But those of ours who place a receptacle beneath it and the feces falls into it — it is found that the bench was never designated for a latrine at all; and it turns out that as long as the vessel is beneath it, it is forbidden, even though the rabbi Beit Yosef wrote in siman 87 that he found written in Ohel Mo'ed in the name of Ri'atz Ne'ot that a receptacle inside another vessel — one may recite Shema [opposite it], and the reason that it is reasonable — until here. Nevertheless it seems to me specifically in a vessel that is not a toilet vessel; but here it is a toilet vessel, and it is forbidden to recite as long as the vessel is in it. But if they removed that vessel from there, or covered the bench with another vessel that is not a toilet vessel, it seems to me that then it is permitted; and it seems to me that so is the practice — and I have elaborated.
