(In Magen Avraham, note 8) Opposite the seat area, etc. — it appears his intent is that he holds: certainly if moist it is forbidden only the seat area. If so, when one said plainly "this house for a bathroom" and did not designate the seat area, the house is not forbidden at all in such a case — there is no designation; therefore he designates the seat area and points to a place that this shall be the bathroom seat area. If so, when he said regarding the second "and this house" it is difficult: either he did not designate a place for it — then in any case it is not forbidden at all; or he points to a place and says "this place" — then one must again be uncertain: perhaps he meant for ordinary use; perhaps he saw this place clearly and said it is for another use, for the whole house is for another use and not a bathroom — rather necessarily what he points to in a special place in the house and says about it "and this place" is to designate it as the seat area. And again Magen Avraham wrote: he answered that with partitions it is forbidden to recite inside; if so, what is the question, etc. — meaning in Berachos he challenges from Ravina's question: if one designated it for a bathroom, what is its law, against R' Chanina who said a person stands in a bathroom and prays; and the Gemara answers there: to stand inside to pray, but opposite is permitted, as written in note 5. And for the questioner it must be that it seemed to him that Ravina's question too regarding opposite deals with inside — it seems obvious it is forbidden; rather necessarily "opposite" needs to be asked, and it is difficult for him from R' Chanina. And now it is difficult: what is the question — even if we say Ravina's question is not literally about the seat area, which is certainly forbidden, nevertheless one may well say Ravina: although he said plainly "designated for a bathroom," it deals when there are partitions, similar to designating a bathhouse, and he asks whether it is permitted to recite within the partitions opposite the seat area, or perhaps within partitions in such a case is forbidden; and R' Chanina who permits opposite deals when there are no partitions. With this there is no reason to say the questioner erred and thought this law obvious to forbid, and they answered that there it means to stand inside — and he needs to ask whether within partitions is like within the seat area, for why should this be obvious to the questioner? And also accordingly they should have answered: there it deals within partitions and opposite the seat area — but certainly it deals in an old one with partitions too, and there is no difference between inside partitions and outside the partition. And what Magen Avraham wrote further: if partitions help, etc. — at first glance requires study what this has to do with that; and it appears he means: granted, if partitions do not help in a new one regarding inside, then one could say in an old one it is permitted opposite, for the surrounding partitions are not part of the waste body; whereas if we say partitions help for the waste body to forbid all inside partitions — because partitions are made for their interior — whence do we lighten in an old one opposite, lest it be like a cesspool receptacle? And what Magen Avraham wrote: it should challenge the Beit Yosef's question, "and according to your view," etc. — at first glance requires study, for in truth the Gemara answered it deals so; and it appears Magen Avraham holds: granted, if a bathroom has partitions it is reasonable that the questioner deals when there is feces in it and nevertheless it is permitted opposite the partitions, and on this it answers it deals when there is no feces; whereas if we say the bathroom has no partitions, then necessarily the bathroom R' Chanina mentions is one without feces, for otherwise why mention bathroom at all, since the main stringency of a bathroom is because of feces; and if R' Chanina comes to permit when there is feces, it should have said a person stands opposite feces and prays — why mention bathroom? Rather necessarily the questioner too knew it deals when there is no feces; if so there is no question on Rava. And this is Magen Avraham's intent writing it should challenge, "and according to your view, perhaps even though it has no partitions nevertheless it deals when there is no feces" — meaning for him it is reasonable that the questioner thought a plain bathroom has feces, unlike Beit Yosef who necessarily deals when there is feces in it (and Magen Avraham's wording "perhaps" means: perhaps since it deals without feces therefore it is more lenient than feces itself). And one may also say Magen Avraham holds it is not reasonable to say the questioner did not know one could establish it without feces, for certainly the term bathroom applies also without feces (and the wording "perhaps" Magen Avraham wrote is literal); but certainly the questioner too knew this R' Chanina deals without feces, only he holds bathroom law and feces law are equivalent and he did not hold to distinguish between them, as Rav Yosef actually holds here; and he holds that R' Chanina mentioned bathroom to inform us that opposite is permitted but inside is forbidden even without feces, but opposite is at least permitted even though it has feces law; therefore he challenges Ravina and the answer holds there is a difference between a bathroom without feces and one with feces, and he challenges from Rav Yosef; if so, whence does the questioner hold reasonably that partitions too are like a cesspool receptacle and have feces law, and therefore partition interruption does not help.
