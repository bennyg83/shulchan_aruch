Partitions. Even if they are not ten tefachim high — Beit Yosef. Bach ruled that specifically partitions ten tefachim high are required. The Acharonim disagreed with Beit Yosef and challenged him, and Magen Avraham raised that one may not be lenient even if he has partitions ten tefachim high. One who is lenient should at least see that they are ten tefachim high. If a bad smell reaches him, it seems to me that it is forbidden according to all; see siman 79, seif 2. So too Lechem Chamudos wrote that partitions of a beit hakisei do not help for smell. These words of Lechem Chamudos seem to have been omitted by the Mechaber from Olas Tamid — see there. Taz raised likewise that one may not be lenient because of partitions, and Taz concluded: it seems this stringency of beit hakisei applies specifically to partitions made only for a beit hakisei, such as if one made a special room in the courtyard for this; but if one built a beit hakisei in a wall of the house that he leaned against that wall — even though that partition is literally a partition of a beit hakisei — that partition does not have the law of a beit hakisei for this matter, and one may recite even nearby, since the partition is not designated for the beit hakisei alone. Therefore, whenever there is a beit hakisei in one's courtyard in a special room, one may not recite Shema or pray in the courtyard wherever the latrine is before him as far as one can see, like the law of feces — that is, a beit hakisei without a pit but with feces placed on the ground, as in the days of the Sages of the Talmud at the edge of the field — until here, see there.
