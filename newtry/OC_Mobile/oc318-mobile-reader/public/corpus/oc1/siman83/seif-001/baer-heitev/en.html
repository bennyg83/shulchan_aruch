Partitions. Even if they are not ten handbreadths high, according to Beit Yosef. Bach rules that the partitions must specifically be ten handbreadths high. The later authorities disagreed with Beit Yosef and raised objections against him, and Magen Avraham concludes that one should not be lenient even when the partitions are ten handbreadths high. One who is lenient should at least ensure that they are ten handbreadths high. If a foul odor reaches him, it appears to me that it is forbidden according to all opinions. See siman 79, seif 2. Lechem Chamudos likewise writes that the partitions of a privy do not help with respect to odor. These words of Lechem Chamudos appear to have escaped the author of Olas Tamid; see there. Taz likewise concludes that one should not be lenient because of the partitions. Taz ends: It appears that this stringency regarding a privy applies specifically to partitions made solely for the privy, such as when one constructed a room in the courtyard specifically for this purpose. If, however, one built the privy against a wall of the house, so that the privy wall is joined to that wall, then even though that wall actually serves as a wall of the privy, it does not have the status of a privy wall for this matter, because it was not designated exclusively for the privy. It is therefore permitted to recite even adjacent to it. Consequently, whenever there is a privy in one’s courtyard in a room designated for it, one may not recite Krias Shema or pray in the courtyard while the privy is before him within his line of sight, as is the rule for excrement. This refers to a privy that has no pit, but in which the excrement rests on the ground, like those that existed in the days of the Sages of the Gemara in the open fields. These are his words; see there.
