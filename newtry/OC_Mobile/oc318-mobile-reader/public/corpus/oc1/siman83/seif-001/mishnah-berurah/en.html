(1) It is forbidden to read - Meaning, before it as far as his eyes can see, and behind him four amot. If there is a foul odor there, he must distance four amot from the place where the odor ceases.
(2) It was cleared out - Its prohibition is from the Torah, since this place was formerly normally used for excrement and therefore "your camp" is not holy. Accordingly, if one is uncertain whether it is an old privy or a new one, we rule stringently, like every doubt concerning excrement above in siman 76 seif 7.
(3) When it has no partitions - Such as behind a fence or behind houses. It is obvious that even if they did not initially designate that place as a privy, since people are accustomed to relieve themselves there it is called an old privy. A proof is from above siman 42, where the poskim wrote that if one always uses a cloth to wrap tefillin, it is forbidden to use it for another purpose as though he had designated it; see there.
(4) Partitions - It appears that this is speaking where they reach the ground, or at least within three tefachim of the ground, where we apply lavud; otherwise it is forbidden according to Rav Hai Gaon cited in Beit Yosef siman 87; see there.
(5) Does not reach - If the foul odor reaches him, it is forbidden until he distances four amot from where the odor ceases; this suffices even in front of him according to the Mechaber. Know that the Mechaber's view is to permit even with partitions that are not ten high, as stated in Beit Yosef, but all the later authorities disagree with him on this. With partitions ten high, some later authorities hold like him, while many later authorities disagree even in this. Their reason is that since the partitions are designated to receive excrement, we judge them like excrement and like a chamber pot below in siman 87, which is forbidden even without a foul odor. From the words of the Gra in his Biur it also appears that his view is to be stringent, and so Peri Megadim, Derech HaChayim, and many other recent later authorities are all stringent. They hold that one must distance four amot from the partitions, and before him as far as his eyes can see, even if the excrement was cleared out and there is no foul odor. Therefore one must be careful not to read, pray, or speak words of Torah in a courtyard opposite a privy as far as his eyes can see, even if it is closed and the excrement inside cannot be seen. It is therefore proper to be careful, when making a sukkah in a courtyard, to do it in such a way that the walls of the privy are not directly opposite the sukkah entrance when it is open, for then he would need to be careful when speaking words of Torah and other sacred matters. Likewise, if there is a privy opposite the window of his house, and the window is open and he stands in his house facing the privy, it is proper to be careful not to speak words of Torah then. Nevertheless, in pressing circumstances one may rely on leniency if no foul odor reaches him, for there is a double doubt: the Beit Yosef and Bach in this siman permit with partitions ten high, and the Rosh in siman 79 seif 2 permits even with excrement when it is in another domain although he sees it. Closing his eyes is effective according to all opinions in such a case. The later authorities further wrote that our stringency regarding privy partitions, judging them externally like excrement, applies specifically to partitions made only for the privy, such as when one made a special room for this in a courtyard. But if he built along a wall of a house, so that the same wall is actually a wall of the privy, that wall does not have the law of a privy for this matter, and one may read even near it, since the partition is not designated exclusively for the privy.
