It appears to me that this applies when it has no partitions - As was the case in the time of the Sages of the Gemara, when their privy was behind the fence at the edge of the field. Accordingly, it appears that in small towns, where each person does not have a fixed privy behind the houses, the place where people ordinarily relieve themselves has the law of a privy, like the area behind a fence. Regarding the practice of some people who, when Sukkos arrives, clear such a place so that it will be clean and make a sukkah there, it has the law of an old privy. What does clearing it accomplish? Perhaps because the structure that he builds there, even though it is only for several days and afterward the place will again serve as a privy as before, removes the designation of privy from it. It is implied by Panim Meiros, part 1, siman 87, that removing the designation is effective for this purpose. However, what he wrote there, that an old privy containing no excrement is prohibited only D'Rabbanan, overlooked the words of Rabbeinu Yonah, Beit Yosef, and the other poskim in this siman, all of whom write that it is prohibited D'Oraisa. The other statement of Rabbeinu Yonah on this very page, where he writes that it is D'Rabbanan, was explained by the commentators as referring only to a new privy, so that his statements do not contradict one another, and this is also implied by the Rosh. Yet even if we say that this is considered removal of the designation, one must at least first inspect the walls of the structure and clean them. Otherwise, it will be forbidden to recite blessings or Kiddush there, for perhaps they are soiled from the entire year, since this is a place in which there is reason for concern, as stated above in siman 76, seifim 7 and 8. I have personally seen on Sukkos many stumbling blocks of this and similar kinds, such as taking a door or board for the sukkah that had previously been lying in a filthy place. One who guards his soul should avoid making a sukkah in such places. Rather, he should place it farther behind the houses, in a clean place; and if this requires making an eruv, he should carry by means of the eruv.
But if it has partitions - Peri Megadim was uncertain whether specifically four partitions are required, or whether even three partitions suffice. See there further.
If no foul odor reaches him - The Mechaber rules here without qualification in accordance with the first opinion above, at the end of siman 79, seif 2, as noted by the Gra. The later authorities there likewise rule this way as practical halachah. Lechem Chamudos and Magen Avraham wrote that, according to all opinions, the partition of a privy does not serve to interrupt a foul odor. I therefore likewise ruled stringently in Mishnah Berurah that one requires four amos from the place where the odor ceases, especially since many later authorities there maintain this in any event. See there, and see the introduction to siman 79, paragraph 7.
