And it seemed to me that this means when it has no partitions — as in the time of the sages of the Gemara, when their beit hakisei was behind the fence at the edge of the field; therefore it seems that in small towns where not everyone has a fixed beit hakisei behind the houses in the place where they are accustomed to relieve themselves, the law of a beit hakisei applies as with behind the fence. What some people practice when Sukkos time arrives — they clear the place to be clean and build a sukkah there — behold, it has the law of an old beit hakisei; and what will clearing help? Perhaps because of the building one builds there, even though it is only for some days and afterward it will return to being a beit hakisei as before — the beit hakisei is uprooted from it; and it is implied in Penei Me'orot, Chachmat Adam, siman 87, that uprooting the name helps for this [but what is written there — that an old beit hakisei without feces is rabbinic — omitted the words of Terumat HaYerushalayim and Beit Yosef and the other poskim in this siman, for all write that it is from the Torah; and the other words of Terumat HaYerushalayim on this page itself that write it is rabbinic — the commentators explained that it refers only to a new beit hakisei so his words do not contradict each other, and so it seems in Rosh]. But even if we say this is called uprooting the name, by law one must at least initially check these walls of the house and clean them, for otherwise it will be forbidden for him to bless and sanctify the Name, lest they be soiled all year, for it is the place where one is likely to relieve oneself, as above in siman 76, seifim 7 and 8. With my own eyes I saw in sukkos stumbling-blocks from such matters and the like, many (that they take some door or sheet for a sukkah and it had originally been placed in a repugnant place); one who guards his soul should distance himself from making a sukkah in such places, but rather place it behind the houses in a clean place; and if he must make an eruv because of this, he should carry by eruv.

But if it has partitions — Pri Megadim was uncertain whether we require specifically four partitions or even three partitions, etc.; see there further.

If a bad smell does not reach him — the Mechaber here is unstated, like the first opinion above in siman 79 end of seif 2 [Gra]; and so the Acharonim ruled there for halacha. Lechem Chamudos and Magen Avraham wrote that a partition of a beit hakisei, according to all, does not help to interrupt a bad odor; therefore I too concluded in Mishna Berurah to be stringent that we require four amot from where the smell ends, for otherwise many Acharonim there hold so — see there; and in siman 79 in the introduction, letter 7.
