It appears to me - See Taz, who discusses this at length and concludes that partitions designated for a privy may themselves have the law of a privy, requiring four amos behind them and, in the case of an old privy, distance as far as one can see in front. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, who writes similarly. What Taz writes about a perforated bench follows his own view in paragraph 3; see Magen Avraham, seif katan 5. According to Perishah, however, a perforated bench has the law of a new privy. One distances four amos from a new privy, and the phrase that one may recite 'opposite it' means outside four amos. This is evident from the Gemara's question in Berachos 26a, which concerns an old privy opposite him at a distance of four amos, as Rashi explains there; likewise on 22b, where one saw excrement opposite him at a distance of four amos, and for that reason need not repeat the prayer, as in siman 81, seif 2. Although in the case of an old privy with partitions one may recite adjacent to it opposite the partitions and need not distance four amos, a perforated bench is not considered partitions. It is merely a perforated bench, less than ten tefachim high and also not four by four tefachim wide. The unqualified wording of Shulchan Aruch implies that this applies even when it is not ten tefachim high. See Taz in siman 79, paragraph 3, regarding a block. In truth, an area four by four tefachim wide but less than ten high is a karmelis; see there. What Beit Yosef writes, that it resembles something covered, requires study, because there the waste is as though buried, whereas here it is not in a different domain. Peri Chadash permits when it is ten tefachim high, and possibly also four tefachim wide, in accordance with Bach. When one sits in a sukkah and a partitioned privy faces the entrance, there is a double doubt: perhaps the law follows the Mechaber, and perhaps it follows the Rosh in siman 79, seif 2. Nevertheless, one may argue that initially one should not deliberately create a double doubt, just as one may not deliberately create a D'Rabbanan doubt, as we will explain below, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham, paragraphs 2 and 3. Closing one's eyes in such a case appears sufficient; see Magen Avraham 79:8. Even Eliyah Rabbah, who is stringent there, may agree here. Know that according to the Mechaber, whenever the privy is in another domain this is effective. In siman 79, excrement in another domain is permitted. What constitutes another domain? Must there be four partitions, or do three suffice, or even two partitions ten tefachim high and four by four wide, which form a private domain? See siman 363 and Taz there. I saw, however, that Bach writes here that there must be partitions on every side, ten tefachim high. This implies that a complete private domain with an entrance is required; if the fourth side is entirely open, it is a karmelis, while we require specifically a private domain, similar to what Peri Chadash writes in siman 79 according to the Rosh. Understand this, and the matter requires study.
