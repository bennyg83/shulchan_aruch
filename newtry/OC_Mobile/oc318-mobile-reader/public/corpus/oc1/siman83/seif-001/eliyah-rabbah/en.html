[1] Even if it was cleared out, etc. Ateres Tzvi writes that in front of it one must distance himself as far as he can see, and behind it four amos from the point where the odor ends. These are his words. This is also evident from the Gemara at the end of the chapter Mi Shemeiso; see there and examine it carefully. The words of Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rashi on the Rif, require study; see there. I have abbreviated. If there is doubt whether it is an old or a new privy, it appears that we rule stringently, as in a case of doubtful excrement in siman 76.
[2] If he has a partition, etc. Even if it is not ten tefachim high — so wrote Beit Yosef; and Bach in section 4 holds that specifically we require ten tefachim high, and Lechem Chamudos explains likewise; and the view of Taz and Magen Avraham is that even when it is ten tefachim high it is forbidden. However, this is specifically regarding partitions that are made only for the privy — e.g. when one makes in his courtyard a room designated only for this — but if he adjoined the privy to the partitions of the house, even though that partition is literally the privy partition, it is permitted to recite [Shema] even nearby, since the partition is not designated for the privy alone; and all this is in a privy that has no pit but the feces rests on the ground.
[3] If there is no smell, etc. See above siman 79 seif 2 what I wrote on this.
