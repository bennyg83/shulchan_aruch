But not inside it, etc. So Beit Yosef wrote, because Rambam and Rosh ruled thus; and Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote so in the name of Rosh. What Beit Yosef wondered about Rabbeinu Yeruham — that afterward he wrote Rosh ruled to be lenient — I examined Rabbeinu Yeruham's words and there is no difficulty: his intention is that Rosh explained the words of Ri'f, who wrote that it is an unresolved question without resolution, and Rosh explained it leniently; but Rosh himself clearly holds to be stringent — see there, for I abbreviated. So too Ba'al Halachot Gedolot is stringent, and even after the fact if one already recited or prayed he has not fulfilled his obligation — this is rabbinic, and a practical difference emerges regarding a doubt; see responsum of R' Yom Tov Tzahalon siman 72. It seems that just as they forbade inside it, so too it is forbidden within its four amot, for Rav Chisda said: a person stands in a latrine and prays, and Rashi explained there — distant four amot; even though one may say he explained so to challenge — ostensibly this is the law for the conclusion, for it enters your mind that the Talmud that even afterward it is forbidden as far as one can see — if so, for the conclusion it suffices that we permit as far as one can see, but within four amot is forbidden; and so Derishah wrote explicitly in the name of Mordechai. There seems some proof for this from that it uses the language "opposite" and does not say "standing at its side and praying," as stated on folio 25a — if it were ten high, sitting at its side and reading; rather perforce specifically distant four amot is permitted. Study is required, for the implication of Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and Levush would seem that no distancing is required at all; see above siman 81; and for practical law one may be lenient after the fact.
