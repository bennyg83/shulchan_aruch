(11) It is considered as sealed — meaning it is as if this place is not a bathroom at all, whether it has partitions above the pit or not.

(12) But if they urinate — meaning even at a time when there is no urine there and no bad smell, because there is bathroom law on this place outside the pit where they urinate. And the Taz wrote that nevertheless the partitions of this bathroom do not have the law of bathroom partitions to require distancing from them opposite them, as in note 5 above, for with urine there is no Torah prohibition except opposite the stream alone, and after it falls on the ground its prohibition is only rabbinic, and these partitions are not called bathroom partitions for that reason. And it appears that those places where it is customary to make a special urinal for everyone — and it is known those places become very foul — even on their partitions there is full bathroom law, similar to a urine container explained [in siman 87], for many poskim hold its prohibition is from the Torah; and although urine itself is only rabbinically forbidden, nevertheless since it is designated for this it becomes more foul and is stricter; and the same applies here. See siman 79 in Peri Megadim, whose view is that foul-smelling urine is forbidden by Torah law.
