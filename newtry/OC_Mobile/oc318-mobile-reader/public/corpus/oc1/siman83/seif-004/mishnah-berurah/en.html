(11) It is considered sealed - Meaning, it is as though this place is not a privy at all, whether or not there are partitions above the pit.
(12) But if they urinate - Meaning, even when no urine is presently there and there is no foul odor, because the area outside the pit where people urinate has the designation of a privy. Taz writes that the partitions of this privy nevertheless do not have the law of privy partitions, requiring distance from the area opposite them, as explained above in seif katan 5. The Torah prohibition concerning urine applies only directly opposite the stream; after it falls to the ground, its prohibition is only D'Rabbanan. These partitions are therefore not considered privy partitions on that account. It appears, however, that in places where a special public urinal is made, and it is known that those places and their walls smell extremely foul, the walls also have the full status of a privy. This is comparable to a urine receptacle, which many poskim explain in siman 87 is prohibited D'Oraisa. Although urine itself is only D'Rabbanan, because the receptacle is designated for it and becomes especially foul, it is more severe; the same applies here. See Peri Megadim in siman 79, who maintains that foul-smelling urine is prohibited D'Oraisa.
