People who have, etc. - See what I wrote in Mishnah Berurah regarding a small perforated chair. If the child for whom this chair, called a shtoyelke, was made has not yet reached the age at which his excrement is prohibited by law, and no other children have used it, it would appear that one may be lenient and need not distance himself from it when no foul odor reaches him. Independently, the law of this children's chair requires examination, because its primary function is for the child to sit upon, and only incidentally does he also relieve himself there. It would therefore appear comparable to the ruling of Derech HaChaim cited in Mishnah Berurah, according to which one may be lenient when it is clean of excrement and no foul odor comes from it. I had thought that Taz, when he mentioned a small perforated chair, did not mean what we call a shtoyelke, but an actual chair made like a perforated bench so that the child could relieve himself upon it. The words of Shulchan Shlomo, however, do not imply this, and the matter requires study. In any event, it is proper to be stringent and cover the shtoyelke while words of Torah are being spoken, because it is uncommon for it to be completely clean according to the law governing excrement, and at times dried excrement remains upon it.
The chamber pot is not beneath the opening - If the chamber pot is beneath the opening, and certainly when excrement lies on the ground beneath the bench, all agree that it has the law of a privy. It is forbidden in front of it as far as one can see, and behind it for four amos from the place where the foul odor ceases. It requires examination, however, whether this causes the entire room to have the law of a privy, so that even behind it one would be forbidden throughout the room, even if the room is large. This is implied by Chayei Adam, klal 3, law 10, according to his explanation of Mordechai, and Eliyah Rabbah likewise writes this. Although one might say that according to Eliyah Rabbah's final conclusion, where he explains it in accordance with Perishah, this explanation of Mordechai is no longer needed, it is nevertheless possible that he did not retract this law. This also appears from Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, who forbids even in a new privy the entire area within the partitions, and all the more so in an old one. There is no proof from Beit Yosef and Perishah, cited by Chayei Adam, for one who examines Perishah. Regarding the proof he brings from Taz, see Levushei Serad. It is possible that if the room is also designated for other purposes, all agree that the entire room does not have the law of a privy, comparable to the leniency of Magen Avraham in siman 87, seif katan 1, regarding a chamber pot. All our discussion concerns a place beyond four amos from where the odor ceases; within that distance, all agree that it has the law of a privy D'Oraisa. I saw an evil practice in the house of a frivolous person who had a room designated for relieving himself, with the chamber pot beneath the bench and the foul odor reaching throughout the room, yet his sefarim were permanently kept in that room. I applied to him the verse, 'For he has despised the word of Hashem,' and he received his punishment: he had a son who departed from the proper path, may Hashem protect us, and his own person also became dishonored among people because of this, in accordance with the teaching in Avos: Whoever desecrates the Torah, his person is desecrated among people.
And anyone who recites, etc., must recite again - Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes in his novellae that regarding urine, since its prohibition is D'Rabbanan, after the fact he need not recite again. This is not implied by Shulchan Shlomo or by the words of Shulchan Aruch HaRav. Nor is there a strong proof from Magen Avraham above, at the end of siman 76, because his intent there is the case stated in Shulchan Aruch, where one found it in a place in which there had been reason for uncertainty, but not where he knew of it initially. He abbreviated his language, as is his manner. See what I wrote below in Biur Halachah to siman 185.
