People who have them, etc. — see in Mishna Berurah what is written regarding a small perforated chair. If the child for whom this chair was made [called a potty chair] has not yet reached the age that his feces are forbidden by law, and other small children did not use it — it would seem one may be lenient not to need to distance from it if no bad odor reaches from it. Otherwise one should examine the law of this children's chair, for its main function is for holding the infant, only that incidentally he also relieves himself there; if so, it would seem this resembles the law of a chamber pot that I brought in Mishna Berurah, that one may be lenient if it is clean of feces and no bad odor reaches from it. I would have thought that Taz's intention in what he wrote — small perforated chair — is not about what we call [potty chair], but literally a chair made like a perforated bench for the child to relieve himself; but from the words of Shulchan Shlomo it does not imply so, and it requires study. In any case it is correct to be stringent and cover [the potty chair] at the time of words of Torah, since it is not found to be completely clean like the law of feces, and sometimes there is dry feces on it.

There is no receptacle under the hole — but if there is a receptacle under the hole, and all the more so when feces are placed on the ground under the bench — according to all its law is like a beit hakisei, and it is forbidden before it as far as one can see and behind it four amot from where the bad smell ends. But one should consider whether perhaps thereby the entire room has the law of a beit hakisei and it is forbidden even behind it throughout the room, even if it is large; and so it seems in Chachmat Adam, general rule 3, law 10, according to how he explains the words of Mordechai; and so the "some say." Even though one may say that per the conclusion of the "some say" there, who explains like the separation — we do not need this explanation in Mordechai's words; nevertheless it is possible he did not retract this law, and so it seems ostensibly from Magen Avraham, seif katan 1, that even in a new one the entire area within the partitions is forbidden, and all the more so in an old one. And from Beit Yosef and the separation that Chachmat Adam brought — there is no proof to examine the separation; and what he brought from Taz's words — see Levush Shulchan. It is possible that if that room is also designated for other things, according to all the entire room does not have the law of a beit hakisei, similar to what Magen Avraham was lenient in siman 87, seif katan 1, regarding a receptacle. All our discussion is after four amot from where the smell ended; and within this measure, according to all, its law is a beit hakisei from the Torah. The evil of the deed I saw in the house of a simple person — he had a special room to relieve himself there, with a receptacle under the bench, and the bad odor reached throughout the room; in that room his books were fixed, and I recited on him this verse: "for the word of God is disgraced in this," and I took his tefillin — he had a son who went out to bad culture, may God save us — and his body too was profaned among people because of this, as the tanna said in Avos: whoever profanes the Torah, his body is profaned among people.

And whoever recites, etc., returns and recites — in the novellae of Ra'avad he wrote that regarding urine, since it is rabbinic, after the fact he does not return; in Shulchan Shlomo and in the words of the Gra it does not imply so; and from Magen Avraham's words above at the end of siman 76 there is also not so much proof — his intention is on what Shulchan Aruch wrote "if he found" in a place where one should have been concerned, but not when he knew from the start; and he abbreviated in his language as is his way.
