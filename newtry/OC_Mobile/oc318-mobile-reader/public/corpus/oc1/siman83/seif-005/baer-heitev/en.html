Perforated. Beit Yosef writes as follows: Mordechai writes at the end of the chapter Mi Shemeiso that Rafram said, 'A person may stand opposite a privy at a distance of four amos and recite.' I was asked concerning people who have a perforated seat in their room and relieve themselves upon it, whether they may recite Krias Shema there. I permitted it and brought proof from here, because there is no excrement on the opening, the chamber pot is not beneath the opening, and the opening is also constantly covered with a board. These are the words of Mordechai. Beit Yosef writes that it appears from his words that this perforated seat has the status of a privy, and accordingly one must distance himself four amos from it. Taz writes that Beit Yosef's requirement of four amos applies when it is behind him, but in front of him that distance does not suffice, and he must distance himself until it is outside his line of sight. Magen Avraham likewise concludes that a perforated seat has the status of an old privy that contains no excrement, and one must distance himself from it in front until it is outside his line of sight. One must therefore be careful in houses that contain small perforated chairs for the children of the household that they be covered during Krias Shema and words of Torah, even when they contain no excrement, or that they be removed from the room in which one prays. Taz. In the earlier edition of Baer Heitev it is written: 'It appears to me that one may be lenient, based on what is written in seif 4. Examine this carefully.' His intention is to compare this case to that of a privy situated over a pit in seif 4, but I cannot understand this. In my opinion, the cases are as distant from one another as east is from west, and this is clear. Excrement in water is not nullified unless the water is turbid, as stated in the glosses to the Rif. See above, siman 76, seif katan 8. Magen Avraham cites this there in seif katan 9. In the earlier Baer Heitev this was cited in the name of 'I found written,' because the author had then overlooked the words of Magen Avraham in siman 76; see there.
