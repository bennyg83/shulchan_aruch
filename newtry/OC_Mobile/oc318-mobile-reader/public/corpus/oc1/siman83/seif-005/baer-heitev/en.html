Perforated. Beit Yosef, of blessed memory, wrote: Mordechai, in the chapter "One whose relative died," — Rafram said: a person stands opposite a beit hakisei at a distance of four amot and recites. I was asked about people who have a perforated bench in their room and relieve themselves on it — whether one may recite Shema; I permitted them and brought proof from here, since there is no feces on the hole and also no receptacle under the hole, and moreover the hole is usually covered with a sheet — until here, Mordechai. Beit Yosef wrote: it seems from his words that this perforated bench has the law of a beit hakisei, and therefore one must distance four amot from it — until here, Beit Yosef. Taz wrote: what Beit Yosef said — four amot — is behind it; but before it does not help, and one must distance as far as one can see. Magen Avraham raised likewise that a perforated bench has the law of an old beit hakisei without feces in it, and one must distance before it as far as one can see; therefore one should be careful in those houses that have small perforated chairs for the children of the house that they be covered at the time of Shema and words of Torah, even if there is no feces in them, or remove them from the room where one prays. Taz. In Baer Heitev before me it is written in this language, and it seems to me to be lenient compared to what he wrote in seif 4 — examine closely, until here. His intention is that he wants to compare this law to the law of a beit hakisei that is in a pit in seif 4; I cannot understand this. In my opinion this is as far from that as east from west, and it is simple. Feces in water is not nullified except in murky water — Hagahah of the Rif, see above siman 76, seif katan 8; Magen Avraham brought it there in seif katan 9. In Baer Heitev before me this is written in the name "I found," and it was hidden from him then the words of Magen Avraham in siman 76 — see there.
