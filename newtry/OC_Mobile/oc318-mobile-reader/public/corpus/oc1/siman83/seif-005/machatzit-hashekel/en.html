(siman kuf hei) Beit Av, etc. And behold Rashi, etc. And behold it is difficult for Rashi, etc. — for he did not write what Mordechai [wrote] at a distance of four amot, for Rashi also wrote thus; and what compelled Rashi is because it was difficult for Rashi why he challenged, etc.; and since perforce per the presumption Rephaim meant at a distance of four amot even though he said plainly — from this Mordechai was born, that also per the conclusion one may say thus under compulsion, as Magen Avraham wrote after this, even though Rephaim said plainly — behold we see Rashi explained thus for the questioner and we do not consider it strained. Learn from this that inside it is forbidden — for he did not say "more," that even inside it is permitted; perforce "opposite it" and not inside it. It deals with an old one in which there is no feces — and this is the answer the gemara rejected, from R' Yehoshua: privy — they said that even when there is no feces in it.

And R' Yehoshua stands, etc. — meaning, how will Ravina answer the gemara's challenge from Rav Yosef? Therefore he said Ravina holds R' Yehoshua stands on the mishnah — meaning, in the mishnah it teaches the measure of distancing four amot, and Rav [Chisda] innovated specifically behind him, but before him one needs as far as his eyes can see; and since in the mishnah the measure of as far as his eyes can see was not mentioned, on this R' Yehoshua stands — that even when there is no feces in it, this measure of distancing is required, namely four amot; and regarding the measure of four amot it is equal whether there is feces in it or not; but regarding before him there is certainly a distinction between there being feces in it and not — when there is feces in it, distancing as far as his eyes can see is required, and when there is no feces in it, even before him four amot suffices. And if so, Rephaim's words that he said "a person stands opposite the privy" — one may say at a distance of four amot — and this is not strained, for also per the questioner's view it must be so, as Rashi explained above. And what Magen Avraham wrote that he stands on the mishnah — even though in the mishnah he did not mention the law of privy but only the law of feces — nevertheless one may say: since in truth we hold a privy has the law of feces, it is well to say he refers to the measure mentioned in the mishnah regarding feces. Accordingly, it is impossible in a new one that inside it is permitted; perforce Rephaim deals with an old one in which there is no feces, and four amot suffices. With this, what Mordechai wrote is resolved: Rephaim said, etc., at a distance of four amot and reads, and he explained it in a privy in which there is no feces — for Mordechai ruled the inquiry leniently, as Magen Avraham wrote afterward; and if so, perforce it must be so as above — only that accordingly it should be asked: what did the gemara challenge initially, as it establishes Rephaim in a privy in which there is no feces, and R' Yirmiyah: privy — they said that even when there is no feces in it? Let us say R' Yehoshua stands on the mishnah and four amot suffices, and Rephaim too meant at a distance of four amot, as Magen Avraham wrote for Ravina; therefore one must say: since the questioner did not bring up distinguishing in a new one between inside and outside until they answered him — this is because he held there is no distinction in privy law: either it has full privy law and is forbidden even outside, or it has no privy law at all and even inside is permitted; and if so, likewise regarding an old one without feces he would not know to distinguish — for since it has the law of "there is feces in it" for distancing four amot, likewise it should have the law of "there is feces in it" for before him as far as his eyes can see. Mordechai's words according to Ravina — what Magen Avraham wrote that he holds R' Yehoshua requires only distancing four amot — are per the conclusion, that also regarding a new one they distinguish between inside and outside (and four amot is the law of inside); likewise one may say for a privy in which there is no feces that it should have the law of "there is feces in it" only in part and not be equal in all its laws to "there is feces in it." And indeed per the conclusion there is no need again to establish Rephaim specifically in a new one; likewise the questioner deals with an old one without feces — and it is also settled that which the gemara said only "since" — for there is no need to veer from the first presumption and it deals specifically in a new one, since there is another establishment in an old one without feces as above; and therefore it said only "and such is the gemara's flow everywhere." But it is still to be asked: who compelled Magen Avraham to write that Mordechai recorded the inquiry leniently? Perhaps he recorded it stringently, as most poskim hold — and nevertheless per the conclusion he establishes Rephaim in an old one without feces at a distancing of four amot as above. One reason: for Mordechai wrote and explained it in a privy without feces — and this is not explicit so in the gemara; it is only that per the conclusion one may establish Rephaim in an old one without feces. But it is not explicit so in the gemara; on the contrary, it somewhat implies the reverse; and further it is impossible that he ruled the inquiry stringently — and this is because it is a Torah doubt; if so, all the more since it is possible to say that also per the conclusion the gemara's answer remains that Rephaim deals in a new one and in an old one without feces one must distance as far as his eyes can see — whence do we lighten from a doubt that without feces suffices even before him at four amot? Nevertheless he ruled the inquiry leniently; and if so, perforce Rephaim deals with an old one without feces, as above.

And it is as though explicit in the gemara that per the conclusion Rephaim deals with an old one without feces; and if so, for us who hold the inquiry stringently out of doubt — if so, likewise one may say that also per the conclusion the gemara's answer remains that Rephaim deals in a new one. But in an old one, even without feces, before him must be as far as his eyes can see, as it answered initially — whence would we find a new leniency without necessity? And it is still to be asked accordingly: perforce the questioner did not bring up in his mind distinguishing the laws of privy, and he held in every matter either it has full privy law with all its stringencies or it has no privy law at all; if so, what does Magen Avraham challenge in siman kuf alef? See Magen Avraham, and such are his words: and if he has partitions — it is forbidden to recite inside it even from new. If so, what does the gemara challenge — that perhaps Rav Chisda deals, etc., and Ravina, etc.? Behold the questioner held: if inside is forbidden, outside too is forbidden; and if so, it would be that Rav Chisda deals without partitions — nevertheless it is fit to forbid as far as his eyes can see, like the law of an old privy — for the questioner held one may not distinguish privy laws as above. One reason that here Magen Avraham wrote he is inclined to explain Mordechai's words as we explained, not like the Ri; but in siman kuf alef Magen Avraham challenged to reject the Ri there, for he holds privy partitions are the interruption, and the Ri perforce holds the questioner too brought up distinguishing, as Magen Avraham rejected in siman kuf alef the Ri's proof from the gemara's challenge from Rava to Rephaim; examine carefully.
