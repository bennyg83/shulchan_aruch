And furthermore, the opening, etc. This is the language of Mordechai cited by Beit Yosef. Beit Yosef writes that it appears from Mordechai's words that a perforated seat has the status of a privy, and accordingly one must distance himself four amos from it. Meaning, four amos suffice only when it is behind him, but when it is in front of him that distance does not help. One must therefore be careful in homes containing small perforated chairs for the children that they be covered during Krias Shema, prayer, and words of Torah, even when they contain no excrement, or that they be removed from the room in which one prays.
