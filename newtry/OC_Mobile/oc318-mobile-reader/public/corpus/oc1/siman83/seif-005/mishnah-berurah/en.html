(13) Kerias Shema opposite it — he holds it does not have the law of an old bathroom, for we hold it is forbidden before it as far as the eye can see even if the feces was removed, but only the law of a house designated as a bathroom explained in seif 2, since there was never feces on it and the cesspool receptacle does not stand under it except when needed. But many later authorities disagree and hold it has the law of an old bathroom since people relieve themselves on it; therefore one must be careful when wishing to recite Kerias Shema, pray, or speak holy words — to remove the chair from the house, or place it four amot behind him, or cover the entire chair with a garment or board, even if it is clean, has no bad smell, the receptacle is not under it, and the hole is covered above with a board. And similarly one must be careful in those houses that have small perforated chairs for the children in the house that they be covered with a garment at the time of Kerias Shema and words of Torah, even if there is no feces in them, or remove them from the room where one speaks holy words — especially since a child is accustomed to soil the chair when relieving himself, and for all opinions it has bathroom status even when clean. Derech HaChayyim wrote regarding a perforated bench: if the bench is designated for sitting like a chair designated for sitting and covered with a cushion, and when needed he removes the cushion and relieves himself into the vessel placed there — one may permit reciting and praying while it is covered with the cushion, even if it is not covered with a board at all, for it has no bathroom status; see there for his reason. And see in Biur Halacha what we wrote further on this.
