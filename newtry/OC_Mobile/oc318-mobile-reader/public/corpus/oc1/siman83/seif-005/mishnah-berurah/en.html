(13) Krias Shema opposite it - He maintains that it does not have the law of an old privy, which we rule is forbidden in front of a person as far as he can see even after the excrement has been removed. Rather, it has only the law of a house designated as a privy, explained in seif 2, because excrement was never upon the chair itself and the chamber pot stands beneath it only when needed. Many later authorities disagree and maintain that it has the law of an old privy, since people relieve themselves upon it. Therefore, one who wishes to recite Krias Shema, pray, or speak words of holiness must be careful to remove the chair from the house, place it four amos behind him, or cover the entire chair with a garment or another object. This applies even when it is clean, has no foul odor, the chamber pot is not beneath it, and the opening above is covered by a board. One must likewise be careful in homes containing small perforated chairs for the children, covering them with a garment during Krias Shema or Torah study even when they contain no excrement, or removing them from the room in which words of holiness are spoken. This is especially so because a child commonly soils the chair while relieving himself, in which case all agree that it has the designation of a privy even when clean. Derech HaChaim writes regarding a perforated bench that if the bench is principally designated for sitting, like a chair used for ordinary sitting and covered with a cushion, and when needed one removes the cushion and relieves himself into a vessel placed beneath it, one may recite and pray while it is covered by the cushion, even if it is not otherwise covered by another object, because it has no designation as a privy. See there for his reasoning. See Biur Halachah for what we wrote further on this matter.
