People, etc. — thus wrote the Rabbenu Yosef: the Mordechai wrote that R' Perem said a person stands opposite a bathroom at a distance of four amot and recites, and he explained it regarding a bathroom that has no feces; and from this I brought proof regarding people who have a perforated bench, etc., until "always covered with a board," end quote. And it appears from his words that a perforated bench has the law of a bathroom, and therefore one must distance four amot from it, end quote — Beit Yosef. And it requires study: if so, before it too should be forbidden as far as the eye can see; why does the Shulchan Aruch write here that Kerias Shema opposite it is permitted? And it is difficult: the Mordechai wrote in R' Perem's words "at a distance of four amot and recites," which is not in the Gemara. And it is difficult that he wrote "and he explained it regarding a bathroom that has no feces," whereas in truth the conclusion establishes it in a new bathroom. And behold, Rashi also explained R' Perem's words "at a distance of four amot," and thus the Gemara: "How far must one distance from them and from feces? Four amot." Rava said: this was stated only behind him, but before him as far as the eye can see. And R' Perem said: a person stands opposite a bathroom and prays. The Gemara asks: regarding a bathroom that has no feces? And Rav Yosef said: a bathroom that they designated — even though there is no feces in it, the Gemara asks. And Ravina asked: if one designated it for a bathroom, what is its law? There, to stand inside to pray; but opposite it is permitted, end quote. And behold, it is difficult for Rashi why Rava challenges R' Perem and does not challenge from the mishnah; rather he holds R' Perem deals when one stands at a distance of four amot, and therefore he challenges Rava that before it is forbidden as far as the eye can see. And it is difficult for the Mordechai: what does Ravina need to ask — let him resolve from R' Perem's statement that a person stands opposite a bathroom and prays; necessarily it deals with a new one, from which we learn that inside is forbidden. And it is strained to say R' Perem himself needs to be asked; rather Ravina must be uncertain — perhaps R' Perem deals with an old one that has no feces, and therefore one need not distance as far as the eye can see, only four amot; and Rava refers to the mishnah, and if there is feces one must distance as far as the eye can see. And the Mordechai ruled the question leniently, per some poskim; therefore he holds that in a new one even inside is permitted. Therefore R' Perem said a person stands opposite a bathroom at a distance of four amot and recites, and he explained it regarding a bathroom that has no feces — for if there is feces in it, even as far as the eye can see is forbidden. Therefore he ruled this bench has the law of a bathroom without feces, and one need not distance as far as the eye can see, only four amot as above. And if so, for us who hold Ravina's question stringently — in an old bathroom without feces one must distance as far as the eye can see — the same applies to this bench: one must distance as far as the eye can see, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, that one may not distinguish between an exposed bathroom and a covered one. And so is the plain meaning of the poskim, who equated the law of feces and a bathroom without feces; examine this.
