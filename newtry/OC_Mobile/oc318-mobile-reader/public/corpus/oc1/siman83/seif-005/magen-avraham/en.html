People, etc. Beit Yosef quotes Mordechai as follows: Rafram said that a person may stand opposite a privy at a distance of four amos and recite. He explains this as referring to a privy that contains no excrement. From here I brought proof concerning people who have a perforated seat, etc., through, 'it is constantly covered with a board.' These are his words. Beit Yosef writes that it appears from Mordechai's words that a perforated seat has the status of a privy, and accordingly one must distance himself four amos from it. This requires study. If so, it should also be forbidden in front of him as far as he can see, and why does the Shulchan Aruch write here that one may recite Krias Shema opposite it? A further difficulty is that Mordechai writes in Rafram's statement that one stands four amos away and recites, whereas this does not appear in the Gemara. Moreover, he writes that the Gemara explains it as referring to a privy containing no excrement, when in fact the conclusion establishes it as referring to a new privy. Rashi too explains Rafram's statement as referring to a distance of four amos. The Gemara states: 'How far must one distance himself from them and from excrement? Four amos. Rava said: This applies only behind him, but in front of him as far as he can see. But did Rafram not say that a person may stand opposite a privy and pray? The case concerns a privy that contains no excrement. But Rav Yosef said that a privy, as the Sages described it, is forbidden even though it contains no excrement. The case concerns a new privy. But Ravina raised the question whether one who designated it as a privy may pray inside it. There the question concerns standing inside it and praying, but opposite it is permitted.' Rashi's explanation is difficult, for why does the Gemara challenge Rava from Rafram rather than from the Mishnah? It must be that Rafram refers to one standing four amos away, and therefore the Gemara challenges Rava, who forbids it in front of him as far as he can see. Mordechai's position is also difficult: why did Ravina raise his question? It should be resolved from Rafram's statement that one may stand opposite a privy and pray, which necessarily refers to a new privy, showing that inside it is forbidden. It is strained to say that Rafram himself was uncertain. Rather, one must say that Ravina was uncertain whether Rafram referred to an old privy containing no excrement, in which case it is unnecessary to distance oneself as far as one can see and four amos suffice, while Rava referred to the Mishnah and to a case where excrement is present, in which case one must distance himself as far as he can see. Mordechai ruled leniently regarding Ravina's unresolved question, following some authorities, and therefore held that in a new privy one may recite even inside it. Accordingly, Rafram's statement that one may stand opposite a privy at a distance of four amos and recite is explained as referring to a privy containing no excrement. If excrement were present, it would be forbidden as far as one can see. He therefore ruled that this seat has the status of a privy containing no excrement, so one need distance himself only four amos and not as far as he can see. According to us, however, since we rule stringently regarding Ravina's unresolved question, and from an old privy containing no excrement one must distance himself as far as he can see, the same applies to this seat. Beit Yosef likewise cites Hagahos Maimoniyos that one should not distinguish between an exposed and a covered privy. This also appears from the plain meaning of the authorities, who equate excrement with a privy containing no excrement. Examine this carefully.
