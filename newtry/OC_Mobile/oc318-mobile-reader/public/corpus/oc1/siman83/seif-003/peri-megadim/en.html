And further — the Taz; I did not understand this, for the Mechaber's wording "opposite it" indicates before it and outside four amot, and similarly in the Mordechai who wrote he permitted them a perforated bench from R' Perem: a person stands opposite a bathroom at a distance of four amot; and as the Perishah wrote to the questioner — necessarily at a distance of four amot, and plain "opposite it" means four amot (there is a difference between at its side and opposite it). And consequently even according to what he concludes in a new designated one, "opposite it" means outside four amot; and so is the Mechaber's view in seif 2: permitted opposite it, not adjacent, as written in seif 1. And what he wrote "but not inside it" — within four amot is called inside. And a perforated bench has the law of a new bathroom that was designated, for there was never feces in it, only in the vessel beneath it; if so he properly said one distances four amot even from before it. And it suffices; for otherwise what is this that the Mordechai wrote "and I permitted them" — on the contrary, he should have forbidden them like the law of feces; rather he permitted them before it at a distance of four amot like that case of R' Perem. And one could say for the Shulchan Aruch that a bench is not called partitions, as the Taz wrote, letter 1, Magen Avraham note 5; and there it will be explained further; and Eliyah Rabbah, note 7.

If it is permitted to think words of Torah in a new bathroom — in Nitzotz Chaim, siman 85, he wrote it is permitted; and in Kitzur Maamarim chapter 3 on Kerias Shema, halacha 4, it seems a new bathroom is forbidden for thought — see there. And in truth they are not entirely equivalent: opposite it is permitted in a new one; I would say thought is also permitted, as he wrote. Nevertheless, thought is permitted except in a bathroom and bathhouse and the like of a bathhouse in a new one. And from what Rashi wrote in Berachos 26a, s.v. bathhouse — thought is forbidden — it would seem he means the same in a bathroom without feces, thought is forbidden like a bathhouse.

And to say praying of R' Chanina is not through thought — this is not so, for several reasons; and there is no need to expand.
