(1) Who forgot and did not make an eruv — it mentions 'forgot' to teach us that even in such a case he forbids when he does not renounce; but the same applies that even when he intentionally did not make an eruv, renunciation is effective:
(2) He forbids them — to carry from houses to the courtyard:
(3) And he need not acquire with a formal acquisition — for it is merely removal of domain in general, removing himself from his domain so as not to forbid them; and therefore it is permitted to renounce even after nightfall:
(4) To each and every one — and we do not say that when he renounced his domain to one of them his intent is to renounce to all the members of the courtyard so as not to forbid them, and what he said to one of them he said to him in place of all; and if he did not renounce to all of them, even one who renounced is forbidden:
(5) And one says that it suffices that he say, etc. — it implies that per the first opinion it is not effective when he said to all of you; and the reason Taz wrote: because in general the majority is like the whole; and therefore if he said to all of you, it is possible his intent was only on the majority of them; and what the Mechaber wrote at the beginning of the seif 'my domain is renounced to you' — there it deals with when there were only two; and for the halacha the Pri Megadim concludes that the primary [opinion] is like one opinion that it is effective also when he said to all of you; and all this is when he stands facing all of them and said this wording to them:
(6) His domain without specification, etc. — and all the more so if he said 'the domain of my courtyard is renounced to you,' for the house is not included:
(7) That he is a mere guest — and a guest does not forbid the members of the courtyard, and also they do not forbid him; as above at the end of siman 370; see there:
(8) From his house to the courtyard — for he did not renounce to them the domain of his house:
(9) And also he — meaning that he is forbidden to carry out from his house to the courtyard, for one cannot say regarding his house that he is like a guest in it, since he did not renounce to them:
(10) That he must lock his house, etc. — meaning when he did not renounce the domain of his house; for when he renounced, it is permitted l'chatchila to carry out from his house — both he and they — as written in seif 2:
(11) So that he not come, etc. — and in a time of need one may rely to be lenient that locking the house is not required:
