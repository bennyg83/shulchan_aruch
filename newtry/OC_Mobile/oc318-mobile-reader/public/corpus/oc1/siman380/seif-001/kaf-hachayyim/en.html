1) [Se'if 1] One of the courtyard's residents who forgot, etc. The Shulchan Aruch states that he forgot in order to teach that even in such a case he prohibits carrying if he does not renounce his rights. The same law applies even if he intentionally failed to establish an eruv: renunciation is effective, as stated in the Gemara on folio 70a. Rambam likewise writes this at the beginning of chapter 2.
2) There. Prohibits carrying for them. He prohibits carrying from the houses into the courtyard. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1.
3) There. He need not transfer ownership through a kinyan sudar, etc. This is because it is merely a withdrawal of rights. For the same reason, he may renounce his rights even after nightfall. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2. The eruv is effective retroactively, and it is regarded as though he had participated in the eruv from the outset. Orchos Chaim, subsection 2.
4) There. He must renounce his rights to each person, etc. If he did not explicitly renounce his rights to each person, carrying is forbidden even for the person to whom he did renounce them. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, halachah 2; Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
