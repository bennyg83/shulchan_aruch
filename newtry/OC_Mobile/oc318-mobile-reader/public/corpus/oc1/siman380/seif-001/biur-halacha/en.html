Even after nightfall — see in Mishna Berurah; and even though it is forbidden for part of Shabbat, nevertheless renunciation is effective, as stated in the baraita 69, 2 — that regarding renouncing domain we do not concern ourselves with what was forbidden at the beginning of Shabbat:

Must renounce to each and every one — see Mishna Berurah; and even though when one renounces his domain to one of them he makes an eruv with his fellow, since he made the eruv from yesterday with the intent of this partner he did not make an eruv [Rashi 26, 2 s.v. hag — this is not needed] — and therefore if the renunciation was intentional before he made the eruv, since when he made the eruv he also made it with the intent of this partner, the renunciation that he renounced to him alone is effective; and likewise it is proven from the end of Rashi's words there that he brought from the baraita that it returns and teaches there: one from the market who died etc. — intentionally, that is, before they made the eruv, etc.; and know that per the Rosh who holds that the intent of the baraita when it says 'intentionally' is even if he died after they made the eruv, since it is before the time of acquiring the eruv — that is, before bein hashemashot — the eruv also takes effect on the house he inherited, as the Gra wrote in siman 371; the same applies in our matter — even if the renunciation that he renounced to him was after he made the eruv, since it is before bein hashemashot the eruv also takes effect on the portion that was renounced to him:
