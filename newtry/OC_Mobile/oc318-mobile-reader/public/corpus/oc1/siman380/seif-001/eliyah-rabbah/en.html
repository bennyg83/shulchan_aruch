And some say, etc. The first opinion is primary, for Avodat HaKodesh, p. 26, the Tur, and Tzeidah LaDerech rule accordingly. The Rambam too may be understood this way. If he did not relinquish his rights to everyone, it is prohibited even to the person to whom he did relinquish, as stated by Avodat HaKodesh and the Gemara. There is a mitzvah upon a person to relinquish his rights; if he is an eminent person, the matter is within his discretion, as stated in Tosefta, chapter 5.
His rights without qualification, etc. Meaning, even if he did not mention the courtyard at all, but said only 'my rights.' I found this stated explicitly in Semag, and the Bach overlooked it.
And he too, etc. The Perishah writes that it is permitted even before they take possession. This differs from seif 2, where if he also relinquished his house he may not remove items before they take possession. There, it is presumed that he is removing his own utensils, so it appears that he is retracting his relinquishment. Here, however, when he removes items from their houses, they are presumably their utensils, so it does not appear that he is retracting. End quote. It appears to me there is a proof from the Mishnah on 69, which teaches, 'and theirs are permitted to him and to them.' Since it mentions him before them, this implies that it is permitted immediately.
The Levush writes: That he retracts, etc. Although he cannot legally retract once the residents of the mavoi have taken possession, it is nevertheless prohibited because it appears ridiculous and absurd, or because observers will think that he is using the area in violation of the prohibition, as Rabbeinu Yonatan explains.
