That many do not become guests — see Mishna Berurah, what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham that even if the individual preceded and took possession, they are also forbidden to carry out; and see in Beit Meir who discusses this at length; and likewise in Chemed Moshe; and I found in the chiddushim of Rashba explicitly like Magen Avraham; however Rashba concludes that it is reasonable that although they are forbidden to carry out in every manner, nevertheless they cannot forbid him through their carrying out if he preceded and took possession:

And the same applies if there were two etc. — and as he concludes that one relative to one is not considered a guest; and see in Even HaEzer who disagrees with this ruling, and his opinion is that specifically many relative to one is not considered a guest; and likewise in Perishah — from the words of Tur and Tosafos it does not appear so; and likewise it is explained in Ritva daf 64 that one relative to one becomes a guest; and therefore it appears that in a time of need one may rely on this. And know further that if many dwell in one house, per the view of Chemed Moshe in siman 381 seif 4 they are called 'many'; and conversely if one dwells in several houses he is called an individual, for we do not concern ourselves with houses at all; however from the words of Shulchan Aruch HaRav in siman 391 it is proven that if many dwell in one house they are considered like an individual and are included among guests relative to many; see there; this requires further study:
