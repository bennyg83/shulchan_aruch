(s"k 4) His house, etc. — why do we say daf 79, etc.; and the same applies there; and it teaches: a house between two courtyards (and Rashi: and two houses from two courtyards open to it) and they filled it with straw — two make an eruv and one does not (meaning that each courtyard must make an eruv by itself); but the two courtyards cannot make an eruv together, even though that house is open to both of them, since it is full of straw it is as if a wall separates between them; the straw diminished, and it is still not ten handbreadths high — both are forbidden; how does he act (Rashi — to permit however the one): he locks his house (open to that house that has straw in it) and renounces his domain — he is forbidden and his fellow is permitted (Rashi — he is forbidden to carry out from that house to his courtyard, for he renounced his domain and the whole house belongs to the other courtyard):

For there it deals with two individuals, etc. — meaning that certainly in each courtyard many people dwell, for the reishah teaches 'two make an eruv'; and if in each courtyard there is only one individual, with whom will he make an eruv; and the sifah refers back to the reishah. And also unspecified courtyards are such — only that all the members of the courtyard do not have a foothold on the house that has straw in it except those two houses standing on its two sides; and also that house that has straw in it has no foothold on the courtyards except on the two houses, or the straw house is fully breached to the two houses; and therefore if there were no straw here in the middle house they could have made an eruv for the two courtyards together, since they are open to one another; and now that they did not make an eruv, the middle house forbids only on the two houses and not on the courtyards, as above; and if so, when one of them renounces he does not renounce except to the second homeowner and not to the members of the other courtyard, for they have no need and also no connection:
