(14) He is permitted, etc. — for since they renounced their domain to him, their courtyard and his house become one domain:
(15) And not from their houses — for presumably when they renounced their domain to him they renounced only their courtyard alone:
(16) Even from his house — as it should read; and meaning: not only is it obvious that from their houses that they did not renounce they are forbidden to carry out to the courtyard which is a different domain, but even from the house of this one to whom they renounced, which is one domain with the courtyard, it is also forbidden; and as he concludes that they are not considered as mere guests when they are many; and if so, when they return and carry out into it, it is as if they are retracting and wish to acquire it; and Magen Avraham wrote that we say this only when they carry out from house to courtyard, but when they bring in from courtyard to house this is not called use of the courtyard and it is not like wishing to acquire the courtyard; but the Pri Megadim wrote in the name of many poskim that to bring in from courtyard to house is also forbidden; and likewise in Even HaEzer:
(17) And we do not say, etc. — and even if they also renounced to him the domain of their houses, they are forbidden to carry out both from their houses and from his house to the courtyard:
(18) That many do not become guests, etc. — meaning in such a case that in truth the courtyard and houses belong to them, but in order that carrying be permitted they removed themselves from their domain; and Magen Avraham wrote that even if the individual preceded and took possession it also does not help regarding many that they cannot return and acquire, and they are forbidden in every manner; and see in Biur Halacha:
(19) Even regarding his own — the later authorities wrote that we do not have the text 'even,' for it implies from this that all the more so regarding his fellow's; and this is not so, for the Mechaber deals with one who renounced only the domain of his courtyard and not his house; and if so it is forbidden to carry out from his fellow's house to the courtyard; and likewise it is in old editions of Shulchan Aruch:
(20) Two forgot and did not make an eruv, etc. — meaning that just as with an individual who did not make an eruv renunciation is effective, so too with more:
(21) To each one — and as written in seif 1:
(22) To one who did not make an eruv — meaning that there were in the courtyard only three people and none of them made an eruv; and therefore when those two renounced their domain to the third, it turns out the courtyard belongs to an individual and an eruv is not needed; and it is explained in Beit Yosef that even if the two did not renounce at once but one after the other, it is also effective:
(23) And even if one said to one, etc., on condition, etc. — meaning that it is not only if he did not specify that he should return and renounce to his fellow, but even if he renounced without specification to the first and the first on his own returned and renounced his own portion and also the portion he received — it is not effective, for his own portion he can renounce but not the portion his fellow transferred to him [for since at the time he renounced his domain to him he had no permission in the courtyard because they forbid one another, it turns out the renunciation did not help at all and he did not acquire it for him; and therefore he cannot transfer this renunciation to his fellow]; but even if he said to him from the outset 'I renounce to you on condition that you return and renounce to your fellow' — we do not say he made him an agent and it is as if he himself renounces to the last one; rather since to the first this renunciation is not effective, he did not acquire it for him that he could return and transfer to the last one:
(24) It is not effective — and as above, that his portion he can renounce but not the portion that was transferred to him; and it is reasonable that if that one who renounced to the first returned and on his own renounced to the second, it is effective properly, for now all the portions are his, for the first renounced to him from the outset his own portion:
