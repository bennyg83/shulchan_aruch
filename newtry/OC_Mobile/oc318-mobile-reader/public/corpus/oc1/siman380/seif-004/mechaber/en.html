If the members of the courtyard who made an eruv renounce their domain to one who did not make an eruv — he is permitted to carry out from his house to the courtyard, and not from their houses (unless they explicitly renounced the domain of their houses as well) (Beit Yosef); and they are forbidden even from his house to the courtyard; and we do not say that they should be like guests, since many do not become guests relative to an individual; and the same applies if there were only two and one renounced to his fellow — the one who renounced is forbidden even regarding his fellow's domain, and his fellow is permitted even regarding his own; and likewise many who did not make an eruv who gave their domain to many who made an eruv — since an individual becomes a guest relative to many, and many do not become guests even relative to many, nor does an individual relative to an individual; if two forgot and did not make an eruv, they can renounce their domain — that each one of them renounce his domain to all the members of the courtyard — whether they renounce to many who made an eruv, provided that each one of them renounces to each individual, or whether they renounce to one who did not make an eruv; but they cannot renounce to two who did not make an eruv — whether many who made an eruv are renouncing or a single individual who did not make an eruv — for even after they renounce to them they are two and one forbids his fellow;
