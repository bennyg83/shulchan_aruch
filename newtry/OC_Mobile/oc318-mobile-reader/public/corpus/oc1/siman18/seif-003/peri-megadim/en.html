Yet a nighttime garment worn during the day is likewise doubtful; see Taz here. If Rambam's view is understood in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam's second explanation, which is a third opinion, as Rama writes here, namely that only a daytime garment worn during the day is obligated, then Rambam's statement, “lest he cover himself with it at night,” and the Gemara's phrase “a nighttime garment,” can refer to wearing it during the day. Although this is not a decisive indication that Rambam holds this way, see Beit Yosef and understand, the rule that doubtful blessings are treated leniently is readily explained. See Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav, who writes that our talleisim, which are used only for the mitzvah of tzitzis and not as clothing, are not considered garments designated for daytime. A practical consequence would be whether, if a tzitzis became severed at night, one may continue walking in the garment. Beit Yosef rejects this and rules that it is considered designated for daytime. According to Rosh it therefore requires tzitzis at night in such a case; see there. This is the practical halachah. Regarding what I wrote in letter 1, that tzitzis attached inadvertently on Shabbos are valid, we do not apply the rule that whenever the Torah says not to perform an act, if one performed it the act is ineffective. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 208, and Reish Lakish regarding a transaction performed in violation of Shabbos. See Sema there: since the prohibition in the transaction concerns only the time at which it was performed, it is not included in the rule that a forbidden act is ineffective. Slaughtering on Shabbos inadvertently proves the same. See what I wrote concerning this in Pri Megadim on Yoreh Deah.
