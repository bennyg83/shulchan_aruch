(Seif katan 3) Sheets, etc. I do not know the reason. We are stringent in accordance with Rambam and Rosh. A garment designated for nighttime, if worn during the day, requires tzitzis in order to satisfy Rambam, although no blessing is recited in order to satisfy Rosh. Therefore, although linen sheets are exempt because we rule in this matter like Beis Shammai, as stated at the end of siman 9 and in Beit Yosef there, a wool sheet ought to require tzitzis.
Even by way of placing it upon oneself, which is prohibited because of kilayim. Meaning, when it provides warmth, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 301. Tzitzis is juxtaposed to kilayim, as the verse says, “You shall not wear shaatnez,” followed by, “You shall make fringes for yourself.”
Rather, for wearing. Meaning, where it is worn in the manner of clothing. In the verse from which tzitzis is compared to kilayim, only wearing is mentioned. The prohibition against placing kilayim upon oneself is derived from another verse: “A garment of kilayim, shaatnez, shall not come upon you.”
According to him, one must explain, etc. Regarding tzitzis upon a sheet. Menachos 41a and Shabbos 25b discuss whether kilayim is permitted, meaning techeiles, which is wool, in a linen sheet. Yet if the sheet is entirely exempt from tzitzis, such a combination is certainly forbidden. When he wraps himself in it. Meaning, according to Rosh, when the sheet is designated for wrapping.
As Rabbi Yehudah bar Ilai did. Shabbos 25b states: on Erev Shabbos they would bring him a basin filled with hot water, he would bathe, etc., then wrap himself and sit in fringed sheets, etc.
And Tosafos on Menachos 41a, etc. They distinguish between spreading it beneath oneself and wearing it. This is the opposite of Mordechai's position, for Mordechai requires tzitzis even when it is spread beneath a person, although there is no Torah prohibition of kilayim in that manner, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 301.
