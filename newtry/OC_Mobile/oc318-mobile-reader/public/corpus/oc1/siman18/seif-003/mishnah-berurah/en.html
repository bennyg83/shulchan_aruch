(9) From the time he can distinguish, etc. This is the time when he can recognize an acquaintance at a distance of four amos, as mentioned below in siman 58 regarding Krias Shema. Beit Yosef in siman 58, in the name of Rosh, writes that these are the same measure; see there and the Gra's commentary here.
(10) And this is the custom. See Biur HaGra, who rules like Shulchan Aruch. Peri Megadim writes that in pressing circumstances, one who is lenient and puts it on from dawn onward should not be rebuked. Initially, however, it is certainly very proper to wait before reciting the blessing until one can distinguish between techeiles and white. Eliyah Rabbah likewise appears to rule this way. After the fact, it appears that even if he recited the blessing before dawn he does not recite it again, because perhaps the halachah follows Rosh, who maintains that a daytime garment is obligated even at night.
(11) He should handle it. Meaning, he should touch his tzitzis.
