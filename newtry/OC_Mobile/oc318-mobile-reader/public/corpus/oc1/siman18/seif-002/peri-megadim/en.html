Rambam writes in chapter 3 of Hilchos Tzitzis, halachah 7, that one does not place tzitzis upon a linen garment, as a decree lest one cover himself with it at night. The Gemara, Menachos 40b, states that the decree is because of a nighttime garment. This indicates that Rambam maintains that even a daytime garment worn at night is exempt. The Gemara states the greater concern of a garment designated for nighttime, which is never permitted. Rosh has proof from the Gemara's phrase “a nighttime garment,” indicating that everything depends upon the garment's designation. Rosh's statement that it was permitted on one occasion is cited by Beit Yosef. This is difficult, because according to Rabbeinu Tam and Rosh the reason is not that it was once permitted, but that a daytime garment is obligated at night. According to Tosafos' second explanation there, that only a daytime garment worn during the day is obligated, the wording is understandable. See Tosafos on Menachos 40b, s.v. “because,” and s.v. “techeiles,” where Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation appears to contradict the earlier explanation. See Beer HaGolah there. It can be somewhat reconciled as follows: even one who owns a four-cornered garment designated for nighttime may wear a daytime garment at night with kilayim, and this is not considered a case where both requirements can be fulfilled without conflict. See Tosafos in the chapter Eilu Na'aros regarding this.
