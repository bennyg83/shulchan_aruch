"According to the Rambam." Rashi explained likewise in Menachot 40b, but in Shabbat 25b he did not explain it that way.
"And according to the Rosh, etc." This is because in every place, in Shabbat, Zevachim, and Menachot there and there, the expression used is "a night garment." See Tosafot in Kiddushin 34, in the comment beginning "Tefillin, etc." The Ran wrote there that one is obligated only in a daytime garment when wearing it by day. But in the Jerusalem Talmud in Kiddushin there it does not sound this way. This is the language of the Jerusalem Talmud there: "And what is a positive commandment that is not time-bound? Returning lost property, sending away the mother bird, making a parapet, and tzitzit. Rabbi Shimon exempts women from tzitzit because it is a positive commandment that is time-bound. Rabbi Shimon said to them: Do you not agree with me that it is a positive commandment that is time-bound, since a night garment is exempt from tzitzit? Rabbi Ila said: The reason of the Rabbis is that if they were designated for him for day and night, it is obligated in tzitzit." If the matter were as the Ran says, it would still be exempt at night. See the Ran there. It is certainly decisive in favor of Tosafot and the Rosh.
"Or for the day, etc." This is from the Jerusalem Talmud, chapter 1 of Kiddushin, and from Sifrei, cited by Tosafot on 40b at the end of the comment beginning "Because," and on 41a at the end of the comment beginning "Blue." From there there is a proof for the explanation of the Rosh.
