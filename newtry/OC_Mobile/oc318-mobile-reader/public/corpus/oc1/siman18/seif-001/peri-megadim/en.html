For the Rambam — Taz. And these are the words of the Rambam, may he rest in Eden, in chapter 3 of Hilchot Tzitzis halacha 8 and in Hilchot Shabbat halacha 20 — see there. If tzitzis were torn on a day garment at night — per the Rambam one need not remove it from upon him; per the Rosh one must remove it from upon him.

Question: If tzitzis tore from a garment designated for day and he made tzitzis on it at night — is he permitted to do so or not? And tzitzis that he made on Shabbat — are they valid or not?

Answer: It appears they are valid even if he made them at night, and it is not tolameh. For although a day garment at night is exempt per the Rambam, an analogy: one whose heart lacks time during the day and time comes of itself — it is not tolameh. And it does not compare to a three-cornered garment, which is not obligated at all and is not at all fit for tzitzis. And a four-cornered garment that had kilayim in its hem and he made tzitzis and afterward cut the kilayim from it — one could say it is tolameh, since at the time of making we do not read on it "that you may cover yourself with it," now that we have no kilayim; even if we say mitchila it was permitted for kilayim only rabbinically (see Peticha), nevertheless rabbinically it is tolameh.

And if he made tzitzis on Shabbat — if he did it unwittingly, one could say they are valid even according to Mordechai in siman 13 that on Shabbat one may walk in a four-cornered garment without tzitzis — see there; nevertheless it is not tolameh and better than if he made tzitzis at night, which is valid as I wrote. But if he did it knowingly on Shabbat, in this there is room to consider; and there is a difference if he made them on Shabbat day — its law is explained in Even HaEzer siman 123 seif 2 and seif 5 in Hagah — knowingly on Shabbat the get is void mitchila, and av bet din there letter hei — but we certainly do not exclude from Israel a mumar for this alone, for he is among Israel and his kiddushin are valid; only for sacrifice it is a willful sin from knowledge, and writing not lishmah — if so here in siman 14 seif 2 they depended on lack of intent... therefore one should say they are invalid; but on erev Shabbat one could say it is a safek; and of Shabbat afternoon one could say presumption of prohibition applies (see Orach Chayyim siman 342 in Magen Avraham and Yoreh De'ah 310). And if the question is to whom does he say this — the Rambam [says they are] valid in siman 14, and the Rosh and other poskim — they derive from Torah (see Orach Chayyim siman 261) — one could say mumar for violating Shabbat specifically on Shabbat day, not on erev Shabbat; and this is not bein ha-shemashos — it will be explained in Hilchos Shabbat. See end of siman from this.

Furthermore, briefly I will speak that I was uncertain: if he designated a garment for night and made tzitzis on it when it was exempt, and afterward he changed mind and designated it as a day garment — is it tolameh, since at the time he made it it was exempt; or perhaps since it was not lacking an act, it is not tolameh — unclear now.
