If the tzitzis of a daytime garment became severed at night, according to Rambam one need not remove the garment, while according to Rosh one must remove it.
Question. If the tzitzis of a garment designated for daytime became severed and one attached new tzitzis at night, may he do so or not? Likewise, if tzitzis were made on Shabbos, are they valid or not?
Answer. It appears that they are valid even if made at night, and this is not “you shall make,” and not something already made. Although according to Rambam a daytime garment is exempt when worn at night, this resembles the principle that something whose appointed time has not yet arrived during that same day is not considered lacking an act, because time arrives automatically. It is therefore not “you shall make,” and not something already made. This differs from a three-cornered garment, which is entirely exempt and wholly unfit for tzitzis. If a four-cornered garment had kilayim along its hem, one attached tzitzis, and afterward removed the kilayim, one may say that this is “you shall make,” and not something already made. At the time the tzitzis were attached, the verse, “with which you cover yourself,” did not apply to it, since nowadays we do not permit kilayim. Even if one says that according to the Torah kilayim is permitted in tzitzis and that the present restriction is only D'Rabbanan, see the introduction, it would still be “you shall make,” and not something already made, at least D'Rabbanan.
And if. If one attached tzitzis on Shabbos inadvertently, one may say that they are valid, even according to Mordechai in siman 13, who maintains that on Shabbos one may wear a four-cornered garment without tzitzis. Nevertheless, this is not “you shall make,” and not something already made, and it is even more clearly valid than tzitzis made at night, as I wrote. If he attached them deliberately on Shabbos, the matter requires analysis. There is a distinction depending upon whether he made them during Shabbos itself. The law is explained in Even HaEzer, siman 123, seifim 2 and 5, in Rama's gloss: where a get was written deliberately on Shabbos, it is invalid according to the Torah. See Beis Shmuel there, letter 5. Certainly the phrase “the children of Israel” does not exclude a Jewish apostate from making tzitzis, for he remains included among the children of Israel and his kiddushin are valid. Yet regarding korbanos he is not considered one who would desist upon becoming aware of the prohibition, and he writes without proper intention. Accordingly, here in siman 14, seif 2, where tzitzis attached without intention are discussed, one may say that tzitzis attached deliberately on Shabbos are invalid. If they were attached during twilight on Erev Shabbos, one may say there is a double doubt. During twilight after Shabbos, however, one may say that the prior prohibition remains established. See Orach Chayim, siman 342, Magen Avraham, and Yoreh Deah, siman 110. One might ask according to whom this analysis is stated. According to Rambam, tzitzis attached without intention are valid in siman 14, while according to Rosh and the other poskim, the addition to Shabbos before nightfall is a Torah obligation; see Orach Chayim, siman 261. One may answer that one is considered an apostate for desecrating Shabbos specifically during Shabbos itself, not during the added period before Shabbos. With Hashem's help, this will be explained in the laws of twilight. See the end of this siman regarding this.
I shall add another brief point. I was uncertain regarding one who designated a garment for nighttime, attached tzitzis while it was exempt, and afterward changed his mind and designated it for daytime. Is this “you shall make,” and not something already made, since it was exempt when the tzitzis were attached? Or perhaps, because no physical act was lacking and only its designation changed, it is not considered “you shall make,” and not something already made. This presently requires study.
