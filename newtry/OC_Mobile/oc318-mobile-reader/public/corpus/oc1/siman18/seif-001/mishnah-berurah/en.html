(1) Per the Rambam, etc. — nevertheless it is permitted to go out to reshut ha-rabbim on Friday night in a tallis with tzitzis, and it is not considered carrying, for they are ornament of the garment and its jewelry.

(2) To be lenient — this is specifically regarding blessing; but it is forbidden to wear a night garment by day without tzitzis, and likewise a day garment at night out of doubt. Nevertheless it appears that the shaliach tzibbur wearing a tallis at night need not check the tzitzis, for in such a case we can certainly rely on their presumptive validity from before.

(3) Also for day — means also for day, and even if also designated for night, and all the more so for day alone.

(4) Maariv prayer — and on a public fast when they wear a tallis at Minchah, he should remove the tallis when they reach Barchu, since now one does not wear the tallis except for the mitzvah of tzitzis; if it remains on him he appears as if he holds that night is time for tzitzis. Nevertheless the shaliach tzibbur before the amud — if he is not going in the upper garment, for there is no honor to the congregation in this — it is obvious he should not remove the tallis from upon him.

(5) And on Yom Kippur night — everyone passing before the ark must wrap. And in Lechem Chamudot he wrote that even one who says Mourner's Kaddish before the ark should wrap because of the honor of the congregation, and one does not bless on it.

(6) He should wrap — to resemble angels who wrap in white; therefore they should not remove it at night until after Maariv of motza'ei Yom Kippur.

(7) While still day — but if he delayed wrapping until night, he should not bless again, lest the halacha follows the Rambam. And it is implied from the words of the Magen Avraham in siman kaf-daled that even during bein ha-shemashos it is permitted to bless until tzeit ha-kokhavim; his reason is that in tzitzis it does not say "day" but only "and you shall see them," which implies whenever we can see. And so ruled Ba'al Derech HaChaim; in Matteh Ephraim I saw he is strict about this. But if he himself is uncertain whether bein ha-shemashos has already arrived, it appears he can certainly bless, for otherwise the view of Tosafos and the Rosh that a day garment is obligated at night, and the Gra in his Beur strongly supports this view as well — see there.
