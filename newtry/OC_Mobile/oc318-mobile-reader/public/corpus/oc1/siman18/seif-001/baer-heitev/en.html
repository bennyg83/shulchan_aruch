To be lenient. This refers specifically to the blessing. The garment itself, however, requires tzitzis. Olas Tamid.
Also for daytime. Meaning, it is designated also for daytime, even if it is also designated for nighttime, and all the more so if it is designated for daytime alone. Knesses HaGedolah and Olas Tamid.
Maariv. Regarding a communal fast, Bach writes that one should remove the tallis when the congregation reaches Barchu. Knesses HaGedolah writes that it should remain on until after Maariv; see Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah. See also siman 30, seif katan 3. The writings of Mahari state that one who forgot or deliberately failed to wrap himself in tzitzis throughout the day should wrap himself without a blessing after the congregation has prayed Maariv.
And on the night of Yom Kippur. Anyone who leads the prayers must wrap himself in a tallis. Lechem Chamudos writes that even one who recites Mourner's Kaddish should wrap himself because of the honor of the congregation, but he does not recite a blessing over it.
