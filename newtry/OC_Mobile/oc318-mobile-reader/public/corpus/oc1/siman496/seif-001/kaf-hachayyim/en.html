1) [Se'if 1] In the Diaspora, where two days of Yom Tov are observed, etc. Although we are now expert in determining the new month, the Sages nevertheless instituted that two days of Yom Tov be observed outside Eretz Yisrael. The Sages were concerned that the government might issue a decree forbidding Torah study, causing the method of calculating the calendar to be forgotten. People would then designate a deficient month as full and a full month as deficient, and they might eat chametz on Pesach. Beitzah 4b and Rashi there. See below, subsection 10.
2) The residents of Eretz Yisrael nowadays observe one day, in accordance with their custom, for they never observed two days. It follows that the second day we presently observe in the Diaspora is ordained by the Sages, who instituted this practice. Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Kiddush HaChodesh, law 6.
3) It follows that the essential rule is as follows. Every place more than ten full days' journey from Yerushalayim always observes two days, in accordance with its earlier custom, because the messengers sent in any Tishrei could reach only a place ten or fewer days' journey from Yerushalayim. For every place exactly ten or fewer days' journey from Yerushalayim, which the messengers could have reached, the following distinction is made: If that place is in Eretz Yisrael and was inhabited by Jews at the time of the sighting under the second conquest, such as Usha, Shefaram, Lod, Yavneh, Nov, Tiberias, and similar places, only one day is observed. If the place is in Syria, such as Tyre, Damascus, Ashkelon, and similar places, or outside Eretz Yisrael, such as Egypt, Ammon, Moab, and similar places, its ancestral custom is followed: if that custom is one day, one day is observed; if it is two days, two days are observed. Rambam there, law 11.
