(In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 1) And they place in niddui, etc. And see in Responsa Mahari Halevi, siman 11, regarding a sage who permitted setting sail in a ship on the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora, whether they place him in niddui like one who treats [Yom Tov] lightly. (•) (In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 2) There is no difference between the first and the second, etc. And see in Responsa Noda BiYehuda, siman 30, regarding an infant born at twilight, and his eighth day, which is a doubtful ninth, falls on the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora: on this he wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 266 to be stringent that it does not push aside Yom Tov; and the Shach is lenient, and even in a certain case; and he [Noda BiYehuda] wrote that even the Rosh instructs regarding a doubt that it is permitted, and even on Yom Tov of Rosh Hashana; and also regarding a certain milah not in its time on the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora, one who wishes to rely on the Shach to be lenient, we do not protest against him; see there.
(*) In Shulchan Aruch seif 2: there is no difference. Between the first and second, etc. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah siman 30 regarding an infant born at twilight whose eighth day, possibly ninth, falls on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora. In Yoreh De'ah siman 266 he wrote stringently that it does not override Yom Tov, while the Shach is lenient even in a definite case. The Noda BiYehudah writes that even the Rosh indicates that in a doubt it is permitted, even on the second day of Rosh Hashanah, and regarding a definite delayed milah on the second Yom Tov of the Diaspora, one who wants to rely on the Shach to be lenient is not protested.
