Rather. See the Taz; see siman 526, note 5: even the second day of Rosh Hashana on a weekday; and what is written here, "except the second day of Rosh Hashana," refers to applying medicine to the eye; and what is written "a rabbinic shevus" is apparently explained in the gloss. However, you will see that the Rema marked above "and the same applies to another illness without danger," and afterward marked "and specifically a shevus," and did not include them together at the end as "and the same applies to another illness without danger, and specifically." Apparently regarding applying medicine to the eye, even when there is no illness at all, it is permitted on the second day of Yom Tov through a Jew, for it is bodily benefit, and that is the view of the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov, brought by Darkei Moshe and the Bach; see there. And the Rema marked that likewise an illness without danger is also forbidden on the first day through a Jew as a shevus; see siman 328, se'if 17: regarding an illness without danger it is also forbidden (and apparently the Rambam according to his view there). And at the end he marked "and specifically": regarding applying medicine to the eye when there is no illness at all, specifically a shevus. And for this the Taz wrote that this is not so: even regarding an illness without danger, specifically a shevus; but a Torah melachah on the second day through a Jew, no; and through a gentile it is permitted even on the first day. And when there is no illness at all, regarding applying medicine to the eye it is implied from the Rema here in the words "another illness" that likewise for the eye we require some illness; but without any illness it is forbidden on the second day of Yom Tov through a Jew; and through a gentile this requires further study; see siman 307 regarding a shevus of a shevus where there is need; see there.
Further. I will explain to you that what the Mechaber wrote, "in the Diaspora where they observe out of doubt," is not the wording of the Tur, nor the wording of the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 21; rather only custom, for they are expert in the fixing of the month. And to say that he intended what is written in Yoreh De'ah 294, that even on a doubt they do not administer lashes, nevertheless [that concerns] grafting of diverse kinds; see there; and he comes here to contradict that, that even on a doubt they administer lashes: nevertheless this is forced; and see what I wrote in the inclusive introduction and there. However, one can say that he comes to say the opposite: even in a place where they are not expert and they observe from custom, and also from a Torah doubt to stringency; and according to the view that a Torah doubt is to stringency, therefore perhaps niddui does not suffice until they flog him nevertheless; or conversely, that even for a Torah scholar lashes do not suffice and they specifically place in niddui. Do not say that even regarding the doubt it is likewise so; and see the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Yom Tov, law 21, "and even the second day of Rosh Hashana," and on the Lechem Mishneh there; and according to what I wrote one can settle the word "and even"; see there; and understand this.
