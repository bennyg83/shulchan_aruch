(s.k. 1) And they place in niddui. Etc.: "for any matter, etc." These are the words of the Ran in the chapter "Makom SheNahagu," for we say there: Nasan bar Assya went outside the techum on the second day of Yom Tov, and R' Yosef placed him under shamta; and the Ran raised a difficulty: from here it implies that one who transgresses a D'Rabbanan they place in niddui; yet in the chapter "Kirah" we say that one who cooks in the hot springs of Tiberias on Shabbos, where there is only a D'Rabbanan prohibition, receives makkas mardus. And he answered: regarding a matter whose essence is D'Rabbanan, such as desecrating the second day of Yom Tov, for when he uproots it he uproots the entire mitzvah, in such a case they place him under shamta, for it is a severe offense; but one who cooks in the hot springs of Tiberias, where the essence of the prohibition, namely [cooking] with fire, is D'Oraisa: if so, if he transgressed and cooked in the hot springs of Tiberias, nevertheless the essence of the mitzvah, namely cooking with fire, remains in its place, and he has not uprooted the entire mitzvah, and makkas mardus suffices; end of his words. From this it is proven that even if on the second day of Yom Tov he transgressed only a shevus, nevertheless they place in niddui; for regarding techumin we hold that they are D'Rabbanan, and even so they placed him under shamta.
And in the Shach there, who raised a difficulty on the words of Beis Yosef. Nevertheless it is clear from the words of Beis Yosef there that one who casts doubt on one of the Divrei Sofrim is more severe than one who transgresses the words of the Sages. And Beis Yosef at the end of siman 524 wrote that they flog and place in niddui; and the Peri Chadash wrote that the vav of "and they place in niddui" is a disjunctive vav, and he means "or": either they place in niddui or they flog, and all is according to the eyes of the dayyan, according to the severity of the offense and according to the person; and even if the Gemara mentions niddui, authority is in the dayyan's hand to flog; and likewise even if the Gemara mentions flogging, the dayyan may place in niddui; and he disagrees with the aforementioned Ran; and the Peri Chadash agreed with him as a matter of law.
