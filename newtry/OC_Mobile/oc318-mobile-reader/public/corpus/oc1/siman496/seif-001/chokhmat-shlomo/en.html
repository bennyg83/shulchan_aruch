Se'if 1. In the Diaspora, where they observe two days of Yom Tov out of doubt, whatever is forbidden on the first day is forbidden on the second, etc. Marginal note: I was asked by the rabbinic Moreinu HaRav Yisrael Neta, dayyan of the holy community of Zlazhitatz, and afterward appointed dayyan here in this holy city, regarding an incident that occurred in the city on Simchas Torah: they found written in a Sefer Torah in the verse "mimeged shamayim mital" that there was an ayin in place of a tet, and the rav ordered a man from Eretz Yisrael to cover with a tallis and erase the leg of the ayin and make it a tet; and the aforementioned rabbinic R' Yisrael Neta cried out like a crane against this. And I answered him that the law is with him in all his words, for certainly the Sefer Torah is invalid, for it is chak tochos, and it requires correction. And aside from this he did a prohibition in that he ordered a man from Eretz Yisrael to repair it; for aside from what his eminence wrote, that three men stood there on the bimah and saw, and it was done publicly, and also people below sensed the matter; and even where we require privacy, if even one person sees it is not privacy. And if so, since the rav ordered him to do it, it is not privacy. And furthermore there is the view of several poskim who forbid doing so even in private, and they also brought the view of the Radbaz and the Peri Chadash in siman 496. Aside from all this: is a man of Eretz Yisrael inferior to a gentile, whom it is forbidden to tell to do melachah even on the second day of Yom Tov, and it is a shevus? All the more so it is forbidden to say so to a Jew; and regarding a Jew there is agency, and it is an absolute prohibition, and worse than a shevus. Therefore this ruling is a great error, and it is forbidden to read from it until they repair it according to law. And his eminence spoke well in this. Thus far what I answered the aforementioned dayyan briefly.
