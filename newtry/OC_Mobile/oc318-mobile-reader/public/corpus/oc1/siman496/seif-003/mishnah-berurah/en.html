{9} They are forbidden to do melachah: for we place upon him the stringencies of the place to which he went, and even in private it is forbidden, as was explained above in siman 468, se'if 4; and nevertheless if he did melachah they do not place him in niddui.
{10} In a settlement: to exclude if he left the settlement and came to the wilderness, he need not conduct himself like the custom of the Diaspora since his intent is not to settle there. The poskim wrote that a "settlement" is called specifically a settlement of Jews, and so long as he has not reached a Jewish city he need not conduct himself like the stringencies of that country. And they further wrote that within the techum it is like within the city, and he must conduct himself like the stringencies of that place.
{11} But if he reached a settlement: even a settlement of gentiles, for since he has no intent to return he is drawn after the country to which he came once he reached a settlement of human beings; and there are those who are lenient even in this like one whose intent is to return, and require that he specifically reach a settlement of Jews.
{12} Whether in the wilderness: meaning after he entered a settlement, even if afterward he goes out to the wilderness his obligation is not cancelled.
{13} And all outside the techum, etc.: meaning whether with intent to return or without intent to return, so long as he has not reached the techum of the settlement it is called that he has not yet reached a settlement. The poskim wrote: people of Eretz Yisrael who came to the Diaspora with intent to return, and the second day of Yom Tov falls on erev Shabbos, need not set eruvei tavshilin, because it is a private matter; and therefore they need not conduct themselves in this like the stringencies of the place to which they went, since their intent is to return; and for this reason they must pray the Eighteen Blessings silently on the second day of Yom Tov. And likewise to don tefillin in private, since their intent is to return, we place upon them the stringencies of the place from which they left, as is written in siman 468; but they must wear Yom Tov garments because it is a public matter. And a Diaspora Jew who came to Eretz Yisrael: if his intent is to return to his place he must observe two days of Yom Tov; and nevertheless the Yom Tov prayer he must pray in his house in private; but if his intent is never to return to his place he should conduct himself like people of Eretz Yisrael, as is explained above in siman 468; see there in Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha. The Acharonim wrote that one who uproots his dwelling with his wife and children from place to place to buy and sell and earn a living, even though at the time of uprooting his intent was to return to his place, is like one who has no intent to return; for the usual nature of the matter is that since he uprooted his household, so long as he finds his livelihood ample in that place to which he went he does not move from there. And they further wrote that one who has no intent to return, even though his wife remained in her first place, nevertheless is called one who has no intent to return.
