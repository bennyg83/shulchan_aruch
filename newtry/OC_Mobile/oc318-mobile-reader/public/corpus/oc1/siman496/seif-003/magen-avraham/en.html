People of Eretz Yisrael, etc. This entire se'if is the wording of HaMeor, chapter 4 of Pesachim; and he wrote: even though regarding other matters, anyone whose intent is to return is permitted to do [melachah] in private (as is written above in siman 468), here, since it is a great custom that has spread throughout the entire Diaspora, one may not breach its fence; end quote. And the Tosafos wrote that melachah is forbidden, for it is impossible to do it in private; and so wrote the Kesef Mishneh in the name of the Radbaz, part 1, [simanim] 73 and 258, and Mitz, part 2, 79, and the Rashdam 15; not like the Mabit, part 2, 149.
In a settlement. Specifically a Jewish settlement; and if he is within the techum, it is as if he is inside the city (Radbaz).
But if, etc. Even a gentile settlement, since he has no intent to return (Radbaz).
Outside the techum. For outside the techum it is called a "wilderness," as is written in the Milchamos; and therefore if his intent is to return it is permitted to do [melachah], and if he has no intent to return he has still not become fixed to be like them. People of Eretz Yisrael who came to the Diaspora and did melachah: they do not place them in niddui (Mabit, part 2, siman 149); and they pray the Eighteen Blessings like their place, and wear Yom Tov garments; and when Yom Tov falls on erev Shabbos he cooks without an eruv tavshilin. And one who uproots his dwelling with his wife from Eretz Yisrael to the Diaspora, even though his intent is to return, his law is like one who has no intent to return (Radbaz). And anyone who has no intent to return, even though his wife remains there, is treated as having no intent to return (Mitz, part 2, 49; so it is); and see there, for he wrote further other reasonings; and one should not move from the ruling of Beis Yosef; see siman 574, se'if 2.
