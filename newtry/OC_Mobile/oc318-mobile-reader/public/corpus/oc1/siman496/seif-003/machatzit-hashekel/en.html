(s.k. 3) To apply medicine, etc. Even on Shabbos it is permitted to do a shevus. And applying medicine to the eye is only a shevus; if so, if it encompasses the whole body, even on Shabbos it is permitted through a Jew, all the more so on the first day of Yom Tov.
However, his words require further examination. For above in siman 328 Beis Yosef brought four views on this, and wrote that the words of the third reasoning are principal; and Magen Avraham explained there that where there is no danger to a limb: granted that a shevus is permitted through a Jew where it encompasses the whole body, nevertheless it must be done by means of a change. And if so, perhaps here we are always dealing with [an illness] that encompasses the whole body, and on the second day of Yom Tov it is permitted for a Jew even without a change. And one must say that Magen Avraham inferred from what Beis Yosef wrote, "even though it is forbidden on the first day except through a gentile," implying that there is no permit whatsoever through a Jew; and to this he found difficulty, for we find a permit even through a Jew at least by means of a change, so how did Beis Yosef write categorically "except through a gentile"? Perforce he is dealing with [an illness] that does not encompass the whole body. And in this, applying medicine to the eye is lighter on the second day of Yom Tov: for in such a case on the first day even by means of a change it is forbidden, but on the second it is permitted even without a change. And one should not ask against Beis Yosef himself, from where did he have [grounds] to be so lenient: one can say that he decided so from his own reasoning, for the Rambam is lenient to apply medicine to the eye even where there is no illness at all, and Maggid Mishneh wrote the reason that it is bodily benefit; and granted that we do not hold like him, nevertheless an ill person suffices even if it does not encompass the whole body. And therefore Beis Yosef omitted "or another illness," meaning that regarding another illness we require that it encompass the whole body; and if so it comes a fortiori from applying medicine to the eye, which is permitted even when it does not encompass so.
The Rema comes to teach that even according to those who forbid, etc. Meaning that the Rema's words are themselves the words of the Ran; and the Ran according to his reason holds that on Shabbos, even regarding an illness that encompasses the whole body, nevertheless a shevus was not permitted for a Jew even by means of a change; and according to this it is not such a simple matter to permit a Jew a shevus on the second day of Yom Tov: even though it is an a fortiori from the permit of applying medicine to the eye, nevertheless it is not as clear a matter as it is for Beis Yosef.
