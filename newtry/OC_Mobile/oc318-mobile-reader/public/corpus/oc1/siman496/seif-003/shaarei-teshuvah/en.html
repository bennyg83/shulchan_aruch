(In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 3) People of Eretz Yisrael, etc. And see in Maharitatz, siman 139: a Diaspora Jew is permitted to increase [cooking] on Yom Tov for a person of Eretz Yisrael who left intending to return, in one pot, and in two pots, and of two kinds; and they cook for themselves and for the guest; even though if the guest were not there they would cook only one dish, it is likewise permitted, for there is oneg Yom Tov in that they eat and take delight; and also to cook for the guest alone there are several sides of permit, since he is in the house for some time his meals are upon him, etc.; and also that in any case for the guest it is Chol HaMoed or Isru Chag, on which it is also a mitzvah to take delight on Chol HaMoed and Isru Chag with meat; nevertheless it is good to request a large pot or to eat with him from his pot so that it not be recognizable that it was made for him alone, to remove oneself from doubt; and if it is impossible, it is permitted; see there. And in Shalmei Chagigah in the name of Mahari Ayash he wrote to permit plainly; and see there. And see in Birkei Yosef in the name of Maharikash that a Diaspora Jew who is standing in Eretz Yisrael is permitted to tell a person of Eretz Yisrael to do melachah on the second day of Yom Tov; and Mahari Prago and Mahari Malko rejected his words and agreed to forbid; and see in Ginas Veradim, klal 4, simanim 16 and 17; and see in Zera Avraham, siman 9. And he wrote in Kuntrus Acharon to Machazik Berachah in the name of Mazeh Avraham: one who went to the Diaspora and sent for his wife who was in Eretz Yisrael and she did not wish to go, and afterward she consented to send her household vessels and was ready to travel after Pesach: so long as her body is still in Eretz Yisrael she should conduct herself like Eretz Yisrael; but because of the stringency of chametz she should not eat chametz on the eighth day; and likewise her husband, so long as his wife is in Eretz Yisrael, should conduct himself in Eretz Yisrael. And a bachelor who came from the Diaspora to Eretz Yisrael: the ruling has spread that he should act like Eretz Yisrael (and see in Halachos Ketanos in Yoreh De'ah, and in Leket HaKemach, and in Ginas Veradim, klal 4, simanim 14 and 15: many great ones agreed and decreed a severe decree that even a bachelor, if he came only temporarily, is obligated to observe two days like the custom of his place; see there); but if this bachelor became betrothed to a daughter of the Diaspora with a kinyan, an oath, and a handshake, and to dwell with her in the Diaspora, he should act like people of the Diaspora. And see there in Shiyarei Berachah on this siman, who wrote that he concluded in a responsum that a person of Eretz Yisrael who went down to the Diaspora with intent to return: the law is that he acts like the custom of Eretz Yisrael, and so he did for several festivals; and afterward it became known to him that his wife whom he left in Eretz Yisrael had died, and his intent is to marry a woman in the Diaspora and return to Eretz Yisrael: so long as he has not married he should act as he had been acting, like people of Eretz Yisrael; and when he marries in the Diaspora he should act like people of the Diaspora until he goes to Eretz Yisrael, he and his household; and a great one of the generation agreed with him; see there. See in Sedei Chemed regarding an incident that happened to him of this sort, that he married a woman in the Diaspora but was delayed there because of several false accusations, and it was not his intent to remain there, only to return with his wife as he had stipulated with her, and he conducted himself like people of Eretz Yisrael; but I testify about myself that I never ate chametz on the eighth of Pesach, and I did not conduct myself like Eretz Yisrael except regarding prayer, for the reasons the Radbaz wrote, that if he prays the Eighteen Blessings he is not found a liar in his prayer, which is not so if he would pray "Me'ein Sheva," etc.; see there. And see in Birkei Yosef regarding a Diaspora Jew who lingered some time like a person of Eretz Yisrael with intent to return to the Diaspora, for there is his home, and became a partner in a shop with a person of Eretz Yisrael, and a fair fell on the second day of Yom Tov, which for his partner of Eretz Yisrael is a weekday: it is permitted for his partner to sit in the shop and sell at the fair, since both always deal in the shop, as the Ran wrote, brought by Beis Yosef, siman 245, regarding a Jew and a gentile both dealing in heating the bathhouse; see there. And see in Sedei Chemed in the name of a responsum of the Radbaz that a person of Eretz Yisrael who is in the Diaspora is permitted to cook from the second day of Yom Tov for Shabbos without an eruv tavshilin, and Baer Heitev brought it; and the reason is that an observer will say he set an eruv tavshilin or relied on others. And see in Shaarei Teshuvah regarding people of the Diaspora who go up to Eretz Yisrael for a "Zohara" (that is the matter of the circuits and going around from city to city and from village to village to pray at the graves of the righteous) with intent to return, and they observe two days, and among the Diaspora people there is none who will ascend the duchan: a kohen from people of Eretz Yisrael may ascend the duchan and bless them; and so is the view of Maharam ben Chaviv; but Mahara Halevi is in doubt about this, because for people of the Diaspora who pray Musaf that day, it is a weekday for people of Eretz Yisrael and the Avodah does not pertain to him; and therefore his view is that he should not recite the blessing "Who has sanctified us with His commandments," etc., but only Birkas Kohanim alone. And see there that the glossator wrote that a kohen of Eretz Yisrael who left for the Diaspora intending to return: if he can slip away to leave before they reach Birkas Kohanim, it is better; and if not, if there are other kohanim there he should ascend with them, and since others are with him he can swallow the Name in a melody and it will not be noticed; but if there is no kohen there but him, it appears that he should ascend and bless in his usual way with mention of the Name, and one may rely on Maharam ben Chaviv; and Moreinu Avraham Yitzchak of blessed memory, and also Mahara, did not decide that he should not bless; see there. And the words of Mahara Halevi (who is the author of Responsa Ginas Veradim) you will find elsewhere in Ginas Veradim, klal 1, siman 13; and there are the responsa of Maharam ben Chaviv, whose view is that if there are other kohanim there from people of the Diaspora, kohanim of Eretz Yisrael should not ascend with them; and if there are none there, kohanim from people of Eretz Yisrael may ascend and bless, just as regarding another congregation; see there. And the words of Mahara Yitzchaki are in his responsum Zera Avraham, siman 12, and were also brought in Shalmei Tzibbur, Hilchos Birkas Kohanim; see there. And see there in Responsa Ginas Veradim that Maharam ben Chaviv's view regarding people of the Diaspora who came is that they need to pray on Yom Tov; if they do not know how to pray, one of the people of Eretz Yisrael may be shatz to be motzi them of their obligation; and the author of Ginas Veradim, and likewise in Zera Avraham, disagreed with this; and so leans Birkei Yosef, siman 124; see there. And he wrote regarding a Diaspora Jew who on the second day of Yom Tov was in a village in Eretz Yisrael for whom it is a weekday, and he had already prayed the Yom Tov prayer, and they asked him to spread aloud for them over Shema and to pass before the ark to be motzi them of the weekday prayer: there is room to say that he can be motzi them, for it is not comparable to the case above, for this case is different, since one can say that the Eighteen Blessings properly pertain to him; for even on Shabbos the reason is that the Sages did not trouble them; see there, where he wrote that one must deliberate on the matter; see there. And in Sedei Chemed my view is that if he has not yet prayed the Eighteen Blessings for himself, then he may pass before the ark aloud only, and say Ya'aleh VeYavo in the Avodah; for even though the congregation does not pertain to him, it pertains [to the prayer], and he is not like a liar in his prayer; and he should not pray a silent prayer at all, for then he would need to pray the Yom Tov prayer in his usual way, and then the loud prayer would be only for the congregation, and he would not be permitted to say Ya'aleh VeYavo, and it would be for them like an interruption; see there. And according to my humble opinion it is preferable that he pray silently for himself the Yom Tov [prayer] in his usual way, and aloud say the Eighteen Blessings to be motzi the congregation of their obligation, and in truth he should not mention Ya'aleh VeYavo at all in the repetition of the prayer, since he comes to be motzi the congregation, for for them it is a weekday.
