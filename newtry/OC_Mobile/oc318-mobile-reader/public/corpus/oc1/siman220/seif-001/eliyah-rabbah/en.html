And his spirit is distressed, etc. Perishah writes that this phrase is precise: even if the dream is not among those for which one fasts and contains no indication of anything bad, if his spirit is distressed he should fast, because although he does not see the danger, his mazal sees it, and therefore his spirit is distressed. I do not know how he infers fasting, because the Tur mentions a distressed spirit only regarding the dream-improvement procedure, not fasting, in siman 288. Nevertheless, below it will be explained that Bach also rules this way. Perishah further writes that the dream improvement should be done at the end of the day after Maariv. Shelah, page 200, cites Rabbi Shlomo Almoli that it is done at night after the fast, but himself writes that it should be done immediately in the morning because the zealous hasten to mitzvot. One cannot say that the merit of the fast assists only later, because once he accepted and began it, it is as though he has fasted. This proof is not decisive, for the merit is still greater after completion. Nevertheless, Shelah, page 134, cites Seder HaYom that one should perform dream improvement on Shabbos, even though he asks for personal needs, because danger to life overrides the restriction. This indicates it is done immediately in the morning. Perhaps, though with difficulty, he speaks where one does not fast on Shabbos. One should call the dream to mind during the improvement. If two people are having their dreams improved in one sitting, the third participant must also say the responses to each one, even though the second person did not relate his dream to him at that moment, as stated in Berachot Maharam MiRothenburg.
[Levush] For good, seven, etc. It is a mitzvah to be among the three who improve the dream because this revives the spirit of the fasting person. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that according to one explanation in Tosafot, one should not actually say the phrase "seven times"; rather, the Gemara is instructing them to repeat the response seven times, and this is his own practice. The other poskim do not appear to agree. Bach cites Maharshal that one should say "I saw a good dream" three times and the others answer "It is good" four times, completing seven; then he says "I saw a good dream" another three times and they answer three times, thereby satisfying both explanations in Tosafot. Bach nevertheless concludes that the world practices saying everything seven times. Perishah also rejects Maharshal and concludes one should not change from saying each entire formula seven times. Beit Yosef and the later authorities cite Kol Bo that one should not say a phrase whose initials spell a word of mourning, but should reorder it to "With joy eat your bread." I examined Kol Bo itself, siman 30, and its text is actually "They shall say to you: eat your bread with joy."
