Seif katan 2: “Seven times,” etc. “Some say, in the manner,” etc. The Gemara states that after the dreamer says, “I saw a good dream,” those who ameliorate it answer: “It is good and may it be good. May the Merciful One make it good, seven times,” and so forth. Some therefore hold that the two words “seven times” are themselves part of the incantation, meaning that those ameliorating the dream must also say these two words. Their meaning is unknown, as the meaning of all incantations is obscure. According to this view, it is sufficient for them to recite the incantation three times, since the Gemara does not state how many times it must be said. Presumably, as with other incantations, three times suffice. Others hold that the words “seven times” are not part of the incantation. The incantation is only: “It is good and may it be good. May the Merciful One make it good.” The Gemara’s words “seven times” are an instruction that they must repeat the incantation seven times.
Therefore, etc. Four times. Meaning, during those four repetitions they do not conclude with the words “seven times.” “And afterward,” etc., “with seven times” means that they repeat the incantation three times and conclude each repetition by saying the two words “seven times.” In this way they satisfy both opinions. If the incantation must be recited seven times, the words “seven times” are not themselves part of it, and the first four repetitions, in which those words were omitted, combine with the last three to make seven. If the words “seven times” are themselves part of the incantation, then three repetitions suffice, and the final three were recited with those words. The first four repetitions, which did not conclude with “seven times,” are unnecessary according to that opinion.
This applies when they ameliorate it for him in the evening, meaning, from the fact that they say to him, “Go, eat with joy,” etc. Necessarily, they ameliorate the dream in the evening. If it were done in the morning, how could they tell him to eat joyfully when he must fast? The ordinary practice is that one who has a dream requiring amelioration also fasts. This is the reason he waits until evening to ameliorate it, so that the merit of the fast should assist the amelioration.
For as soon as he accepted the fast upon himself, etc. As stated in Taanis 8b: In the days of Rabbi Zeira, a decree was issued, and they decreed that people should not observe a fast. Rabbi Zeira said to them, “Let us accept the fast upon ourselves now, and when the decree is annulled we will observe it.” They asked him, “From where do you know this?” He answered from the verse, “He said to me: Do not fear, Daniel, for from the first day that you set your heart to understand and to fast before your God, your words were heard.” This concludes the Gemara’s statement.
One who dreamed, etc. About another person should fast. Sefer Chassidim, siman 444. It writes there: “For his fellow is under censure, and therefore the dream was not shown to him,” and so forth. “If the dreamer will not fast, it is proper to tell the other person so that he may pray for himself,” and so forth.
