See Taz, for one can say: even though regarding piercing the ear of a firstborn animal they did not fine, and likewise a field that was improved, even for a Torah prohibition they did not fine his son; nevertheless, when he deliberately directed his melacha and intentionally waited for Chol HaMoed, acting improperly in his intention, and he also committed an issur on the moed by doing melacha that is not for something lost on the moed, they fine his son after him, and the beis din causes him to lose it and declares it ownerless for everyone by their hands, even though the melacha was completed. And the Mechaber who wrote "if it is something that would be lost" is not such a strong proof to say that when it is not something that would be lost they cause him to lose it if he did [the melacha], for one can say that the novelty is regarding something that would be lost, that it is permitted to do l'chatchila; and see Magen Avraham 2, and God willing it will be explained there. And behold, this is according to the one who holds "they shall be destroyed," that it is forbidden to benefit from it; even after the fact, when he did the melacha, the beis din declares it ownerless; and see Magen Avraham 2, and God willing it will be explained further there. And see Prishah 22, that there are two prohibitions: he directed his melacha and committed an issur; there is reason to fine his son after him, as the Taz [said]; and see Eliyah Rabbah.
