[4] One does not fine his son, etc. From Shulchan Aruch and Levush it implies even if he did a matter that is not lost; and it follows Beit Yosef's reasoning, who challenged Rabbeinu Yerucham who wrote they fine his sons from that which we resolve in Bechorot that we do not fine for piercing the ear — see there; and I am astonished how he did not see what Ran wrote in the chapter "one who overturned" — this is his wording: piercing the ear — we do not fine; and likewise one who intends his melacha on the moed in a lost matter — but in a matter that is not lost they fine — until here is Beit Yosef's wording; behold perforce one must distinguish; and in truth it appears to me to distinguish: since here they fined him in a lost matter which is d'rabbanan, if so one must give extra stringency in a matter that is not lost which is d'oraisa; and further that here he does two prohibitions — he intends his melacha on the moed and does a matter that is not lost; and this distinction I found now in Perishah and Taz, and in Eliyah Zuta I wrote more from this.
