(•) In Shulchan Aruch (seif 1) davar ha-aved is permitted; and the Rabbenu Yerucham in the name of Maharam Provencial in a responsum wrote that printing books on chol ha-moed even if he has workers for whom he fixed wages whether or not there is work is not called davar ha-aved because of possibly losing workers' wages, since printing itself is not davar ha-aved; and even taking workers who have nothing to eat is forbidden, for a person may not do melacha on chol ha-moed to pay a worker who has nothing to eat, see there; and the Beit Rivai wrote on this; see below siman 542. In what the Acharonim wrote, see there; meaning there it implies one may give work to a worker who has nothing to eat, and likewise in siman 542 Maharam's words need explanation; in my humble opinion the leniency is only when the work has some aspect of Yom Tov, unlike printing which is only for profit; see in Nekudat HaKesef and Magen Avraham that I brought in siman 542; see below siman 545 in the name of Moreh u-Ketziah: (א) davar ha-aved on erev Yom Tov; see responsa Peri Megadim part 2 siman 61 reconciling Beit Yosef's difficulties on Rabbenu Yerucham in what he wrote they fined his son after him, see there; and Machatzit HaShekel challenged this, Netiv 24 chapter 2; also Chida noted this in siman 448 seif 141—it appears from his words that biblically they do not fine the son after him; and it is difficult from “one who squeezes the ear” and chametz—see in Nekudat HaKesef siman 24 that chametz is a quarter prohibition upon him, etc., see there; see in Acharonim in Tashbetz siman 16.
