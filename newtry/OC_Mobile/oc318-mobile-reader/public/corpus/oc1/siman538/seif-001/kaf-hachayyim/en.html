1) [Se'if 1] One who turned over his olives, etc. This means that he gathered his olives, placed them in the olive press, and turned them over in a manner such that they will be ruined if not pressed immediately. Tur; Levush. This follows the words of Rashi and Nimukei Yosef on Rif. See there.
2) There. One who turned over his olives, etc. Rambam, chapter 7, holds that even initially one may turn them over. His reason is that once they were placed in the storage vat and the time for turning them arrived, failure to turn them would create a matter subject to loss. Raavad disputes this, and Maggid Mishneh writes a resolution of Rambam's position. See there. Beis Yosef cites them. Ritva writes in his novellae that perhaps Rambam speaks of the second turning. Alternatively, in Rambam's locality, the turning itself was part of preventing a loss. Everything depends upon the place and time. See there. Erech HaShulchan, subsection 1; Pischei Olam, subsection 1; Mishnah Berurah in Biur Halachah, cite this. See below, se'if 5.
3) There. Or his wine was in the pit, etc. He is afraid that it will turn sour. Rashi on Rif.
4) There. Or his wine was in the pit, etc. The vessel into which the wine falls from the winepress is called the pit. Rashi, Moed Katan 12a. Nimukei Yosef on Rif writes that the pit is an excavation beneath the winepress, plastered inside with lime so that not a drop is lost, into which the wine flows from the press. Rashi there gives a similar explanation. It is possible that some people use one arrangement and others the other. Alternatively, one who ordinarily makes much wine constructs an excavation and structure in the ground, while one who does not ordinarily make much wine places a vessel into which the wine falls.
5) There. And circumstances beyond his control occurred. This means that because of those circumstances, he could not complete the processing of the olives or wine before the festival.
6) There. He pours and completes the work. This means that he places the olives upon the press and squeezes all of them. Every act of pouring is called ziluf. Rashi, Moed Katan 11b. Likewise regarding the wine, transferring it from the pit into a barrel is called ziluf. Rashi there, 12a.
