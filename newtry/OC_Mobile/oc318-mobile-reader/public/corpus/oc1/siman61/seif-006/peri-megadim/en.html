"Blessed is the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever." It is said quietly. See Taz, and see siman 619 regarding saying it aloud on Yom Kippur, in Tur, Levush, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 8. Moshe did not state it in the Torah, but took it from the angels. At first glance, this is difficult: Why do we need the reason that Yaakov said it, when Moshe brought it down to Israel? They say it quietly because of the parable, etc., as Tur states there in siman 619.
Between. See Taz. The Rama in his gloss rules that there are two pauses, one between "One" and "Blessed is the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever," and another between that declaration and "You shall love." The unqualified wording of the Mechaber, however, implies only a pause between "Blessed is the name" and "You shall love." See Levush and Bach regarding this.
