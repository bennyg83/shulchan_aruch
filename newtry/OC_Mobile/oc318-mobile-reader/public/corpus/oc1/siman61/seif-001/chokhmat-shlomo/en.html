Seif 9—it is forbidden to say Shema Shema twice, etc. NB: Behold in Selichot for Monday they enacted to say "and nevertheless we are to Him and our eyes to Him." And in my humble opinion this is not correct, for behold in the mishnah in Sukkah chapter 5 it states there that he would say "we are to Him and to Him our eyes," and the Gemara challenges there on page 53—for one who says Shema Shema we silence him, and it answers that they would say "we are to Him and our eyes to Him" in hope, and Rashi explained since two matters are mentioned he mentions it two times and not one matter two times we have no concern. If so it implies that without this it is forbidden to say two times. And one should not say that in the mishnah it grouped them as one "we are to Him and to Him our eyes"—for this it is forbidden, but for us who separate with the word "and our eyes" we have no concern—this is not correct at all, for from Rashi's words it depends whether he mentions two matters; if so what difference if he says "and to Him praise" or at the end, and behold in the end one word "to Him" is said on "we" and one on "and our eyes," and from this if this is considered two matters even when saying consecutively we have no concern, and if it is not considered two matters what difference if he separates with "and our eyes" first. And further—if the separation helps, why does the Talmud add length "and our eyes to Him in hope"—it should have answered briefly that it must be reversed "we are to Him and our eyes to Him," and perforce that even this does not help if he does not explain the language of hope. If so for our nusach it is like saying Shema Shema and is not correct. Therefore it is fitting to correct to say the Name only once: "and nevertheless we and our eyes to Him," as in my humble opinion is correct—examine well. Seif 13—after the first verse one must say the bedtime Shema, etc. NB: Tzelach in chapter 4 of Pesachim wanted to recommend for those accustomed to say the bedtime Shema in a loud voice that what the Talmud says Nemeira did not say Moshe means only to interrupt between them between the parashiyot—with this, since they did not say Moshe one should not interrupt between them in our own person and must say quietly; but not in the middle of the parashah it is permitted to say in a loud voice. And behold although his words seemed plausible, his words are not correct according to truth—for the custom on Yom Kippur is to say it in a loud voice in the middle of Keriat Shema. It is satisfactory if the Talmud's intent is on account of the body of the verse that it should not be said in a loud voice because it is great praise fit only for ministering angels—it works well for Yom Kippur when we are like ministering angels it is permitted to say it in a loud voice; but if the intent is only on account of interruption between parashiyot—what difference all days of the year, what difference Yom Kippur, and what relevance that we are like ministering angels to interrupt between parashiyot. And also what it says Nemeira said Jacob—where is it proven Jacob said thus between parashiyot. Therefore certainly the main point is as plain. And therefore according to our custom that on Yom Kippur in Keriat Shema we say in a loud voice, it is not fitting to say it at all in a loud voice on other days of the year even not in Keriat Shema—for otherwise there would be two voices contradicting each other—as in my humble opinion is correct in this—examine well.
