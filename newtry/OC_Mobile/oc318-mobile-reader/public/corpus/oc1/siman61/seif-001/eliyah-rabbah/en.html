(1) With kavanah, etc. One must intend that in the parashah of Shema until "and in your gates" there are forty-two words, and it corresponds to the name of Gevurah—the name of forty-two—in order to include the left in the right, for the parashah itself of ve'ahavta is the right; therefore the precise ones at the time of reading the parashah of ve'ahavta place the left hand into the right hand—thus wrote Be'er Mayim Chayyim. Rabbi David Abudraham wrote: the ayin of Shema and the dalet of echad are a sign of seventy, a hint to testimony; alternatively Shema is a notarikon: raise high your eyes, to whom is enough, King of the world, when—Shacharit, Minchah, Arvit—and if you do so you accept upon yourself the yoke of the kingdom of heaven, which is Shema out of order. Rabbi Eliezer of Germiza wrote: since the merit of na'aseh was lost, the Holy One, blessed be He, said—in what remains in hand, the merit of shema—through it they will unite My name; therefore He gave them Shema Yisrael in the language of hearing, so far. Kolbo page 5 wrote it is good to reflect while reading Keriat Shema on the Ten Commandments, for all are hinted in these three parashiyot. "I am the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent," "You shall have no," "the Omnipresent is one." "You shall not take" and ve'ahavta—one who loves does not swear falsely in His name. "Remember"—"in order that you remember." "And you shall do all My mitsvot"—this is Shabbat which is equivalent to all the mitsvot. "Honor"—"in order that your days increase." "You shall not murder" and "you shall perish quickly"—one who kills is killed. "You shall not commit adultery"—"you shall not explore." And I found in Yerushalmi like Kolbo's words: heart and eye are agents of sin. "You shall not steal" and "you shall write on the doorposts of your house," and "not your fellow," so far. And in Shelah page 53 he wrote in the name of Sefer Be'er Mayim Chayyim the hints in another matter. And since Shelah wrote, it seems to my eye it is good to intend on the Ten Commandments, and I already wrote thus in the name of Kolbo—explained that he did not see it.
