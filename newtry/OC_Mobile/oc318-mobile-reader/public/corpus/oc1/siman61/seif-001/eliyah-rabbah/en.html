With intent, etc. One should have intent that in the parsha of Shema until 'uvish'arecha' there are forty-two words, corresponding to the Name of Gevurah, the forty-two-letter Name, in order to include the left within the right; for the parsha of 've'ahavta' itself represents the right. Therefore, those who are exacting, when reading the parsha of 've'ahavta,' place the left hand into the right hand. So wrote Be'er Mayim Chaim. Rabbi David Abudraham wrote that the ayin of Shema and the dalet of echad form the sign 'ed,' testimony. Another explanation: Shema is an acronym for 'raise your eyes on high,' 'to Whom? Shaddai, King of the world,' and 'when? morning, afternoon, evening.' If you do this, you accept upon yourself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, which is Shema read in reverse. Rabbi Elazar of Worms wrote that since the merit of 'we will do' was lost, Israel said Shema.
With awe, etc. It appears that this awe and fear is in the following manner: when he reads Shema he should intend to be killed for the sanctification of the Name. This is the meaning of 'with all your soul'; with such intent he will read it with awe, trembling, and shuddering. Bach. See above, siman 59, seif 4.
