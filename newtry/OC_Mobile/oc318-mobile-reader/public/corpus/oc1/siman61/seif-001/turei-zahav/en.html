In awe, in fear, etc. In the Tur he brought the midrash: "I did not burden you to read standing, nor with uncovered head," etc.—it implies that burden is not required; but if a person acts with uncovered head, heaven forbid—and even though regarding "not standing" we say in siman 53 that one who reads standing is a rebel, there it is different because he has no reason to stand gratuitously, for in this he transgresses the words of Beit Hillel; and were it not for this there would be no prohibition in the matter per se. Therefore he said that the explanation is that He did not burden us to stand, but there is no prohibition in the matter. But regarding uncovering the head it is difficult, for there is prohibition in uncovering the head, as is explained in chapter Kol Kitvei and as the Tur wrote above in siman 2 that one must cover his head, for there is lightness of head in this, and as written in siman 8. And it seems to me that according to our reasoning the reason for prohibition in uncovering the head is lightness of head, as if there is no yoke upon him; but here, where one needs in any case awe, trembling, and sweating—if so, in uncovering the head there would also be a way of awe; rather the Holy One, blessed be He, did not burden us with this. Nevertheless it appears that now, since their custom is so, there is certainly prohibition in this because of "and in their statutes you shall not walk."
