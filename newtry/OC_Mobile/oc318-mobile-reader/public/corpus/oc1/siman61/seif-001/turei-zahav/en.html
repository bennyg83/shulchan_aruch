With dread, with awe, etc. Tur cites the Midrash: "I did not burden you to recite it standing or with uncovered head," etc. This implies that the burden was not required, but if one did recite with uncovered head it would be permitted. Although regarding standing we say in siman 63 that one who reads while standing is a transgressor, that case is different, for he must not stand without reason, since by doing so he violates the words of Beis Hillel. Were it not for that, the act itself would not be prohibited. The Midrash therefore properly says that He did not burden us to stand, although standing would not itself be prohibited. Regarding uncovering the head, however, this is difficult, since uncovering the head is prohibited, as stated in the chapter Kol Kisvei and as Tur writes above in siman 2, that one must cover his head because uncoveredness displays lightheadedness, as stated in siman 8. It appears to me that there is no difficulty. The entire reason uncovering the head is prohibited is that it displays lightheadedness, as though one bears no yoke. Here, however, one must in any event recite with awe, trembling, and trepidation. Were he then to recite with his head uncovered, that too could be a manner of awe, except that the Holy One, blessed is He, did not burden us with it. Nevertheless, it appears that now, when this is their established practice, there is certainly an additional prohibition because of "you shall not follow their statutes."
