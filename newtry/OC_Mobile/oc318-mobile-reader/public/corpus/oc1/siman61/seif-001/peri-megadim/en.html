In awe—Taz, and see siman 200, note 2, and below 91, seif 3, that even placing his hand on his head, which is not in their statutes, nevertheless it is forbidden to utter the Divine Name with his mouth; and from the Midrash it may be inferred that from the Torah there is no prohibition and the Sages forbade it. And behold, what the Mechaber wrote "with intention"—Levush explained intention of the heart, that he understand what he says, not the intention of the mitzvah alone—this matter is in siman 60, Magen Avraham 4, and siman 63, s.v. "and therefore" compared it to a proclamation, that one must understand what is written in it, not read alone; see Perishah. And what Rav Amram added in Tur and in "as new today," delet chapter 1, part 2—that one must understand what is written in it, and from here onward they read hurriedly just to discharge the reading obligation, but every day as new to understand what he says; and the matter is in siman 62, Magen Avraham 1; and in any case the first verse one must understand even in a foreign language, and it will be explained there. A ba'al keri who strikes his head while reading Shema—siman 88; a drunkard—his law is explained in 99, seif 1, in Hagahot, Peri Chadash; and siman 185, Taz note 2, from this.
