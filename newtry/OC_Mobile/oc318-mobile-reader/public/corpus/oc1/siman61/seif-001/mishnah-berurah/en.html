(1) "Shema"—meaning all of Keriat Shema; what was explained at the end of the previous siman is only for the matter of bedi'eved.

(2) With kavanah—Eliyahu Rabbah brought in the name of Kol Bo, and it is in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 1 of Berakhot, that the Ten Commandments are alluded to in Keriat Shema. In "Hashem Elokeinu" is alluded "Anokhi Hashem Elohekha"; "Hashem echad"—the utterance "Lo yihyeh lekha"; in the verse "Ve'ahavta"—the utterance "Lo tisa," for one who loves the king does not swear falsely in His name; in the verse "Uchtavtam"—the utterance "Lo tachmod," as it is written "your house" and not your fellow's house; in the verse "Ve'asafta degankha"—the utterance "Lo tignov"—your grain and not your fellow's grain; in the verse "Ve'avadtem meherah"—the utterance "Lo tirtzach," for one who kills is killed; in the verse "Lema'an yirbu yemeikhem"—the utterance "Kaved et avikha"; in the verse "Ve'lo taturu" and "acharei eineikhem"—the utterance "Lo tinaf"; in the verse "Lema'an tizkeru"—the utterance "Zachor et yom haShabbat," which is equivalent to the entire Torah; in the verse "Ani Hashem Elokeikhem"—the utterance "Lo ta'aneh bere'akha ad sheker." Therefore a person must reflect on them at the time of saying Keriat Shema so that he does not come to transgress one of them.

(3) In awe and fear—and it seems that this awe and fear is in this manner: that he intend when reading Shema to accept upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, to be killed for kiddush Hashem—which is "b'chol nafshekha," even if they take his soul; and on this the verse said "For Your sake we are killed all day"—for then with this kavanah he will read it in awe, fear, trembling, and sweating.

(4) In trembling and sweating—Tur wrote in the name of Rav Amram: one should read Keriat Shema at any time as a new proclamation [this is the king's decree upon the people of his realm]; and he should think in his heart: if a king of flesh and blood sent a new proclamation, surely all the people of the realm would read it in awe, fear, trembling, and sweating—a fortiori for Keriat Shema, which is the proclamation of the King, the Holy One, blessed be He, that each person is obligated to read in awe, fear, trembling, and sweating. Perishah wrote: therefore they compared it to a proclamation and the king's decree—to tell you that one should not read Keriat Shema hurriedly and in haste and with words jumbled together, but slowly word by word, with a pause between matter and matter, like one who reads the king's decree—who reads with great slowness, each decree by itself to understand its content; so too one should read Keriat Shema—each command and punishment mentioned in it he should set upon his heart to understand, for it is the decree of the great King, blessed be He.
