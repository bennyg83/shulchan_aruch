In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 9: It escaped his notice. The complaint also applies to Ri cited in Beit Yosef, and Beit Yosef himself rejects the custom only from the Yerushalmi, not from our Gemara. Perhaps those who permit it follow the view that only repeating each phrase twice is forbidden. This is the case in Sukkah, where they repeated 'To You' twice. Repeating verse after verse is merely unseemly because it makes one appear foolish, which does not apply to a congregation; hence they repeat 'Shema Yisrael.' It further appears that they follow Rosh's view, ruled by Rama in Yoreh De'ah 141 at the end of se'if 4, that suspicion does not apply publicly. Magen Avraham rules similarly below, siman 244, se'if katan 8. Here too, a congregation is not suspected of believing in two authorities. The Gemara in Sukkah follows the other version in Avodah Zarah 43b, which Rif, Rambam, and the Mechaber there in Yoreh De'ah adopt. Nevertheless, where the custom was not established, it should not now be introduced, as Shach writes there, se'if katan 27, that all agree the practice is unseemly even publicly; the same applies here. The incident of Todos, who instituted a practice for the Jews of Rome, requires some study: They were a public body, yet the Sages feared suspicion because it resembled offering sacred animals outside the Temple. One may answer that this is why they sent him, 'Were you not Todos, etc.' Todos generally held that suspicion does not apply publicly. He expounded, 'What did Chananyah, Mishael, and Azaryah see, etc.?' Tosafos explains that an idol statue was involved, implying that Todos held that because all Israel bowed publicly, suspicion did not apply; they should have bowed were it not for their inference from the frogs and the exceptional severity of sanctifying Hashem's Name. See the first chapter of Megillah, where R' Shimon bar Yochai says they deserved annihilation for bowing and did not derive this inference. Perhaps R' Shimon bar Yochai applies the inference more stringently because he holds that punishment may be derived through a kal vachomer.
