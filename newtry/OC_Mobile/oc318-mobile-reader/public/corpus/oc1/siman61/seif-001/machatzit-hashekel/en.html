(s.k. 1) "And he returns," etc., and in the book, etc.; but if he says, etc.—it is 249 words, for in Keriat Shema there are 248 words besides "emet"; if so, when he says "emet" twice and also "Hashem Elokeikhem," it is 249 words. And the individual, since it is possible for him to intend not to join the word "emet" that the prayer leader says so that it will be 248 with the word "emet" that the individual says in a whisper—therefore it is preferable that he say "emet" in a whisper so as not to interrupt between "Elokeikhem" and the word "emet," as is written "Hashem Elokeikhem emet," as written in siman 66, seif 5, and so too in the Gemara.

With this it is resolved, etc.—he does not return and say "emet."

Page 14 side 2—regarding what R' Yehuda holds in the middle of the chapter: one asks on account of fear and answers on account of honor, and between the chapters one asks on account of honor and answers to any person; and R' Yehuda said: between the word "Elokeikhem" and "emet" he has the law of the middle of the chapter. And if he must interrupt to ask on account of honor or to answer any person, he waits until he also says the word "emet"; and the amoraim disagree whether if he interrupted between "emet" and "veyatziv" he returns and says after he interrupted—and they conclude that he does not return and say "emet." And we say: this one who went down before Rava—Rava heard that he said "emet" twice. Rava said: every "emet"—"emet" catches this man (and Rashi explained: the haste of "emet" catches this man). And Rabbi Yosef challenged: if so, why do we say "emet" twice? And answer: since we interrupt between "emet" and "emet" with the words "Hashem Elokeikhem," we do not need it. But he challenged: if so, this one who went down before Rava—perforce he said "emet emet" adjacent to each other and did not interrupt to say "Hashem Elokeikhem emet"; if so, Rava should have said to him: why did you not return "Hashem Elokeikhem emet"? And answer: and one can say that in the days of Rava they did not return "Hashem Elokeikhem emet," and to complete 248 words in Keriat Shema they would say "El melekh ne'eman." Further answer: another answer—see there; and Magen Avraham wrote that per the ten utterances one does not say—for this one who went down before Rava returned "Hashem Elokeikhem emet" but also said "emet" in a whisper, and therefore Rava said "every emet," etc.—for this is not proper, for if so it is 249 words as above.
