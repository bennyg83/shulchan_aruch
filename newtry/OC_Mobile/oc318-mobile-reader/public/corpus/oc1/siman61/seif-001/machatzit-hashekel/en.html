(Seif katan 1) And repeats, etc. And in the book, etc. But if he says, etc., there are 249 words, for Krias Shema has 245 words apart from ‘emes.’ If so, if he says ‘emes’ twice and also ‘Hashem Elokeichem,’ there are 249 words. Since an individual can have intent not to join the word ‘emes’ said by the shaliach tzibbur, and there will be 248 with the word ‘emes’ that the individual says quietly, therefore it is preferable that he say ‘emes’ quietly so as not to interrupt between ‘Elokeichem’ and the word ‘emes,’ as written, ‘and Hashem God is true,’ as stated in siman 66, seif 5, and so it is in the Gemara.
And with this it is resolved, etc. He does not go back and say ‘emes.’
Page 14b, regarding what Rabbi Yehudah holds: in the middle of a chapter one may greet because of fear and respond because of honor, and between chapters one may greet because of honor and respond to any person. Rabbi Yehudah said that between the word ‘Elokeichem’ and ‘emes’ it has the law of the middle of a chapter. If he needs to interrupt to greet because of honor or to answer any person, he should wait until he has also said the word ‘emes.’ The Amoraim dispute whether, if he interrupted between ‘emes’ and ‘veyatziv,’ he goes back and says it after he interrupted, and the conclusion is that he does not go back and say ‘emes.’ We say: the one who descended before Rava, Rava heard him say ‘emes’ twice. Rava said: this man has been seized by ‘emes, emes.’ Rashi explained: the momentum of saying ‘emes’ seized this man. The Beit Yosef asked: if so, why do we say ‘emes’ twice? He answered that we, since we interrupt between one ‘emes’ and the other with the words ‘Hashem Elokeichem,’ have no issue with it. But he asked: if so, the one who descended before Rava must have said ‘emes, emes’ adjacent to each other, without interrupting to say ‘Hashem Elokeichem emes.’ If so, Rava should have said to him: why did you not repeat ‘Hashem Elokeichem emes’? He answered: it may be said that in Rava’s days they did not repeat ‘Hashem Elokeichem emes,’ and to complete the 248 words in Krias Shema they would say ‘El Melech Ne’eman.’ He gave another answer as well; see there. The Magen Avraham wrote that according to the words of Asarah Maamaros, this is well understood: the one who descended before Rava did repeat ‘Hashem Elokeichem emes,’ but he also said ‘emes’ quietly, and regarding this Rava said ‘every emes, etc.,’ for this is not proper, since if so there are 249 words, as above.
