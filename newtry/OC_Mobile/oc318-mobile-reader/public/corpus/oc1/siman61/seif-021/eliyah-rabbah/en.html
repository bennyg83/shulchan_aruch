Such as 'u'lemadtem,' etc. However, the Shelah in the name of Sefer Be'er Mayim Chaim wrote that with every alef following a mem one must pause, even where the reason of it sounding like 'mosam' does not apply, such as 'va'avadtem elohim acherim,' 'eineichem asher zonim achareihem,' 'l'Elokeichem ani,' 'Elokeichem asher,' 'l'elohim ani,' and 'Elokeichem emes.' He further answered that with every word beginning with alef, where it is possible that it will not be perceived in pronunciation, a pause is required, such as 'asher Anochi,' 'mitzvosai asher Anochi,' 'es Hashem Elokeichem,' 'matar artzechem,' 've'atzar es,' 'asher Hashem,' 'divrei eileh,' 'vayomer Hashem el,' 'daber el,' 'mitzvos Hashem,' and 'asher atem Hashem Elokeichem.' Likewise in 'uv'derech' and 'uv'shochbecha' one must pause between 'nafsh'chem' and 'ukshartem,' and between 'hadevarim ha'eileh,' so it should not seem as though he says 'the things from these'; and likewise 'u'chesavtam al.' One should also be precise with the yud of 'yihyeh,' 'yiftach,' 'yirbu,' and similar words, so it should not seem as though the word begins with an alef, as 'ihyeh,' 'iftach,' 'irbu.'
