Like one who reads 'their death.' The Eliyah Rabbah brought in the name of the Shelah that the same applies to every alef after a mem: one must pause, such as 'va'avadtem elohim acherim,' 'eineichem asher zonim achareihem,' 'Elokeichem asher,' 'l'Elokeichem ani,' and 'Elokeichem emes.' Also every word that begins with alef, where it is possible that it will not be perceived in pronunciation, requires a pause, such as 'asher Anochi,' 'matar artzechem,' 've'atzar es,' 'asher Hashem,' 'divrei eileh,' 'vayomer Hashem el,' 'daber el,' 'mitzvos Hashem,' 'asher atem,' and the like. Also every word that begins with yud must be emphasized in pronunciation, so that it not appear as though it is drawn after the preceding word, such as 'pen yifteh,' which should not appear as though he reads 'penifteh' or 'pen ifteh'; and likewise with 'yirbu' and all similar cases.
