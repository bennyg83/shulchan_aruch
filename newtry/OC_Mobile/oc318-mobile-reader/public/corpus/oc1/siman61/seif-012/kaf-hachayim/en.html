(12) There—the shaliach tzibbur concludes "the Lord your God" emes and returns and says in a loud voice "the Lord your God" emes. It implies that the shaliach tzibbur should say emes once at the conclusion and once in the repetition. But in Sefer Asarah Ma'amarot, ma'amar Im Kehalot, vol. 1 siman 17, he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur does not say emes in a whisper and the individual says emes in a whisper and does not return to say emes—and it turns out there are 248 words with kavanah; but if he says emes in a whisper and says emes aloud, there are 249 words—end quote. And they brought Magen Avraham note 1, Shitah Mekubbetzet 99b. And likewise the Rama in Alfasi Zuta, chapter "One who was reading," and thus: the halachah is he does not return and say emes—meaning the individual says it only at the end of the parshah, and the shaliach tzibbur does not say it except in the repetition of "the Lord your God"—so emes is with each person and not in the repetition at all—end quote. And they brought from the author siman 3, and the custom of the world that the shaliach tzibbur concludes "the Lord your God" in a loud voice and the congregation answers after him emes, and he does not say emes; and afterward he returns and says "the Lord your God" emes in a loud voice, and the congregation does not return to say emes but say emes ve-yatziv, etc.—and it turns out emes is in each one's hand alone. And what Knesset HaGedolah wrote siman 3—that if he finished before the cantor he should join "the Lord your God" to emes, for they said between "and He said" to emes ve-yatziv one should not interrupt, and there one waits for the cantor and then begins emes ve-yatziv, and not as the custom of the world that waits for the cantor for everyone to say emes as one and interrupts between Eloheikhem and emes—and they err, for they did not permit interruption there even for fear, etc.—end quote—it does not appear they said only not to interrupt in an interruption of speech, but an interruption without speech, only waiting alone, is not called interruption, as the Mateh Yehudah wrote siman 66, note 2, and Zekher LeChaim vol. 3 siman 100, siman 133—see there. And further, one who hears is as one who answers, and he intends what he says after the cantor's reading as though he says it after his own reading—and there is no concern in this.
