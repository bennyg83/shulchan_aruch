And Beit Yosef disagrees and wrote that it is permitted, for it is written "Kara amen va'amen"—and one need not distinguish between saying with a vav or without a vav; and so too Bahya in parashat Beshalach—and one who intends and doubles "amen amen" merits two worlds, and so David said at the end of Psalm 41; and in the Yerushalmi chapter "HaKore et haMegillah" it states that not specifically "modehim modehim" do we silence him, but likewise "Shema Shema," "amen amen," "the Omnipresent the Omnipresent." If so, perforce we must say that the verse "One who says amen and amen"—the vav separates the matter; and wonder at Beit Yosef that the Yerushalmi escaped him. Yad Aharon—and see Perishah. One who happened to answer amen on two matters—it is possible one ascends here and there; and if he says "amen amen" it is even better.
