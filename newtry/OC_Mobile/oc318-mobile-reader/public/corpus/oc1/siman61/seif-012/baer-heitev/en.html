Amen. The Beit Yosef disagrees and wrote that it is permitted, for the verse says ‘amen ve'amen,’ and one should not distinguish between saying it with a vav or without a vav. So too wrote Bachya in Parashas Beshalach: one who has intent and repeats ‘amen, amen’ merits two worlds, and so David said at the end of Tehillim 41. In the Yerushalmi, chapter HaKorei es HaMegillah, it states that not only ‘Modim, Modim’ do we silence him, but the same applies to ‘Shema, Shema’ and ‘Amen, Amen,’ see there. If so, we must say that in the verse which says ‘amen ve'amen,’ the vav separates the matter; and it is surprising that this Yerushalmi escaped the Beit Yosef. Yad Aharon; and see Peri Chadash. One who has occasion to answer amen to two things, it is possible that one [amen] counts for both; and if he says ‘amen ve'amen,’ that is preferable. Halachos Ketanos, part 1, siman 85.
