“With all your heart.” See Baer Heitev and Shalmei Tzibur in the name of Sefer Shtei Yadot. To join words marked with a maqaf, one should pause slightly on the preceding word. For example, in “VeNatati-metar artzekhem,” he should pause slightly on “VeNatati,” and it will then be easier to read “metar artzekhem” together with the maqaf. I wrote this because I saw many people who did not know it and had not learned it from their teachers, as I received it, etc. Maharam Negrin writes that one should likewise pause between “af” and Hashem’s name. The reason appears to be that it should not sound as though he is saying that Hashem Himself is af, etc. I say that one should not heed those who invent pauses of their own. On the contrary, one must be careful not to pause at all between “af” and Hashem’s name, since there is a maqaf, etc. Perhaps Maharam Negrin meant only that one should articulate the word “af” clearly so that it not sound as though he were reading an unrelated combined word; see there. It is also possible that the reason for a slight pause is that it should not sound as though he were saying “even Hashem will become angry,” meaning that Hashem too will become angry in addition to someone else. Since one must in any event pause slightly between “vecharah” and “af” so that it not sound like one word, “vecharaf,” the word “af” should not be joined too strongly to Hashem’s name. Nevertheless, the pause should not be as noticeable as the pause I described regarding “with all your heart.”
