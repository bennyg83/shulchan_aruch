It is a proper custom. For they praise the blessed Creator, Who dwells above the seven firmaments. We also find twice in the verse, 'Hashem, He is Elokim,' regarding Eliyahu. What we say three times on Yom Kippur, 'Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed,' is also permitted, for the concern of two authorities applies only to the verse Shema, which is the primary acceptance of the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven; once he has accepted upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven once, there is no concern in saying 'Baruch shem' more than once, for it refers back to Shema Yisrael and no one will err.
Twice amen. This too appears like two authorities. See Magen Avraham, who is lenient, but the later authorities agreed to forbid it. If he says 'amen ve'amen,' it is permitted, as it is written, 'Blessed is Hashem forever, amen ve'amen.' See Peri Megadim, who wrote a novel distinction here: there are two types of amen. One means that I believe and strengthen that this is true; the other is an expression of request, meaning that the matters should be confirmed and our requests fulfilled. If so, in a blessing that contains two themes, for example, 'Heal us, Hashem, and we shall be healed,' and 'Healer of the sick,' etc., one may properly say amen twice. If he happens to need to answer on two matters, perhaps one amen can apply to both; and if he says 'amen ve'amen,' that is preferable.
