(27) It is a proper custom—that they praise the Creator, blessed be He, that He sits above the seven heavens; and we also find in Scripture twice "the Omnipresent Hu HaElokim" regarding Eliyahu. And what they say twice "Baruch shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed" on Yom Kippur is also permitted, for it is not considered two authorities—but in the verse of Shema, which is the essence of accepting the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven, since he already accepted the yoke once, there is no concern in saying "Baruch shem" more than once, for it refers to the verse "Shema Yisrael," and there is no one who will come to err.

(28) Twice "amen"—for three times it appears like two authorities; see Magen Avraham who is lenient in the matter, but the acharonim agreed to forbid. And if he says "amen" and "amen," it is permitted, as it is written "Baruch the Omnipresent le'olam amen va'amen"; see Pri Megadim who wrote a novel matter—that there are two types of amen: one, that I believe and affirm that so is the truth; the second, that it is the end of a request—meaning, that the matters be believed and our requests fulfilled. Accordingly, in a berakhah that has two matters—for example "Refa'einu the Omnipresent venirafeh" (Heal us, the Omnipresent, and we will be healed)—it is fine to say twice "amen." And if he happened to answer on two matters, perhaps one amen suffices for both; and if he says "amen amen," it is even better.
