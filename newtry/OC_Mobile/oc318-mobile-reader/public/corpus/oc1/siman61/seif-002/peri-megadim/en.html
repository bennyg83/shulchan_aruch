Eliyah Rabbah. He brought valuable teachings, which I will copy. The first section contains forty-two words, corresponding to the forty-two-letter Divine Name, which represents strength, while the section itself represents the right side, and the left hand is thereby made serviceable through the right, as stated in Be'er Mayim Chayim. The large ayin and dalet extend through Shemoneh Esrei. The words "upon your eyes" allude to "Shaddai, King of the world," and the times of recitation are morning, Minchah, and evening. "The yoke of the kingdom of Heaven" is indicated in reverse order, as stated by Abudraham. When Israel lost "we will do" and only "we will hear" remained, the Holy One, blessed is He, said: With what remains in their hands, namely Shema, let them proclaim the unity of My Name through Shema, as stated by Rabbi Elazar of Worms. See Bach, who writes that when saying Shema Yisrael one should intend that he is willing to be killed for the sanctification of Hashem's Name, and Scripture considers it as though he had done so. This is the meaning of, "For Your sake we are killed all the day." Eliyah Rabbah further cites Kol Bo that the Ten Commandments are alluded to in Krias Shema. "I am Hashem your God" is alluded to in "Hashem is one." "You shall have no other gods" is likewise included there. "You shall not take" is alluded to in "and you shall love," for one who loves does not swear falsely by His Name. "Remember" is alluded to in "so that you shall remember and perform all My commandments," for Shabbos is equivalent to all the mitzvos. "Honor" is alluded to in "so that your days may be prolonged." "You shall not murder" is alluded to in "and you will perish," for one who kills will be killed. "You shall not commit adultery" is alluded to in "and you shall not stray." "You shall not steal" is alluded to in "and you shall write them upon the doorposts of your house," not upon your fellow's house. It is also possible to explain that a human king is guarded by his servants, whereas the Holy One, blessed is He, guards us. Let the thief take instruction and not steal, since Hashem sees and guards. "You shall not bear false witness" is alluded to in the teaching that one who recites Krias Shema without tefillin bears false testimony. "You shall not covet" is possibly included in "and you shall not stray." With Hashem's help, this will be explained in Sefer HaMaggid. The Mechaber's statement in seif 2, that it should be new and beloved to him, I have explained as possibly also meaning that each time he should understand what he says. Beer HaGolah cites Vayikra Rabbah, section "In the third month." This is a scribal error and should read the section "An ox or a sheep," page 197b. In Shemos Rabbah, on page 144d, there is another parable, which is not relevant here.
