A rabbi brought good things—I will copy them in chapter 1: 42 words corresponding to the name 42 (Gevurah); "and the pamphlet itself" right, and servants—left hand by right (Be'er Mayim Chayim). See, and the large dalet until Shema that they say "upon your eyes"—(For whom) Shakkai, King of the universe; (When) morning, afternoon, evening offering; accept the kingdom of Heaven retroactively (Rada). When Israel were lost, "na'aseh" remained and "nishma"—the Holy One, blessed be He, said: in what remained in their hands—and what remained in their hands is "Shema"—they shall unite My name in Shema (R' Eliezer of Germiza). (And Abudraham: in Shema Yisrael one should intend that he wishes to be killed for sanctification of the Name, and the verse elevates him, etc., and this is what is meant "for You we are killed all day.") He further brought—a rabbi, in the name of HaKol—in Keriat Shema the Ten Commandments are alluded: Anokhi the Omnipresent Elohekha; Lo yihyeh; the Omnipresent echad; Lo tisa; Ve'ahavta—one who loves does not swear falsely in My name; Zachor; Lema'an tizkeru; Ve'asitem kol mitzvotai; Shabbat... Kaved; Lema'an yirbu; Lo tirtzach; Ve'avadtem—one who kills is killed; Lo tinaf; Lo taturu; Lo tignov; Uchtavtam on your doorposts and not your fellow's; (And it is possible—a king of flesh and blood, his servants guard him, and the Holy One, blessed be He, guards us; "Yikach haganav"—take the thief to report and he will not steal, for the Omnipresent sees and guards;) Lo ta'aneh; Lo tachmod—all who read Shema without tefillin testify false testimony; and "you shall not covet" in general; and "you shall not explore"—possibly, and it will be explained in Sefer HaMagid. What the Mechaber wrote in seif 2 "as new today" should be precious to him in its explanation—one may also say he should understand each time what he says. Be'er HaGolah noted Vayikra Rabbah, parashah "In the third month"—it should read parashah "ox or sheep" (97b). And Shemot Rabbah, parashah (144:4)—there is another parable not applicable here.
