Your God is true. Baal HaTov and Be'er Heitev wrote that Bach and Peri Chadash agreed to the Yerushalmi that if one reads individually he may repeat "Hashem Elokeikhem"; and I heard that Maharach likewise practiced so—our master the Ari, and the Raavad divided regarding the requests and mentions of praise of the Holy One, blessed be He, and they brought this in Shulchan Arukh siman 61. Nevertheless "emet" alone he should not double, as written below s.k. 3 on Shulchan Arukh, for the Magen Avraham likewise wrote so in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham. And see in responsa Mayim Rabim part 1, siman 1, that a shaliach tzibbur who says the word "emet" in a loud voice and then returns and says "Hashem Elokeikhem emet" to complete 248 words does not act properly; rather he should say the word "emet" the first time quietly, and afterward return and say the three words in a loud voice—and specifically the shaliach tzibbur, but the congregation should not return at all, on Shulchan Arukh. And he wrote in Seder HaYom: one who did not complete Keriat Shema and the shaliach tzibbur already preceded him and finished—it is better that he stand in his place and be silent and listen to the shaliach tzibbur when he returns "Hashem Elokeikhem emet," and afterward finish Keriat Shema, and it helps him since he stands within Keriat Shema; and this is preferable to intending in the fifteen vavins, on Shulchan Arukh. And in Sha'ar HaTziyun he wrote in the name of Mateh Yehudah that one who intends to hear from the shaliach tzibbur in the middle of Keriat Shema—it is not considered as reading retroactively, only for the matter that he fulfills his obligation; but not for the matter that it counts as an interruption, and what he hears from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur is not considered as reading himself. Rather he fulfills what is incumbent upon him in obligation to complete 248 words, and he reads Keriat Shema in order; and so too in Mordechai, on Shulchan Arukh.
