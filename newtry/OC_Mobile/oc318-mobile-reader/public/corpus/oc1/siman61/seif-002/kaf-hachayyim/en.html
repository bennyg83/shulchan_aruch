8) [Se'if 2] 'Which I command you today.' This means that each day they should be as new in your eyes, etc. Maharchu writes in Shaar Maamarei Razal, in his commentary to Pirkei Avos: 'Our Sages established three times for prayer: Shacharis, Minchah, and Maariv. Although they arranged an order for prayer and Krias Shema, these differ according to their respective times, for each has a new aspect and does not resemble another. One therefore should not pray as a merely fixed routine, Heaven forbid, but with his full concentration, like one praying a new prayer and reciting a new Krias Shema. Entirely new mochin are renewed with every prayer. There is no prayer in which a new light and flow are not generated, and one is not like another, etc. There is a great difference between a weekday prayer and a Shabbos prayer, between a Rosh Chodesh prayer and those of Yom Tov and Chol HaMoed. Not only that, but the Yom Tov prayers themselves differ - the prayer of Sukkos from that of Pesach, and so forth. Within each day, Shacharis does not resemble Minchah or Maariv, and so forth. Even on weekdays, the prayer of one day does not resemble that of the next; one is not like another. From the creation of the world through the future, no prayer has ever resembled another in any respect. The secret, briefly, is that the prayers refine the refinements of the seven kings. Each day and each prayer involves new and different refinements, not the same ones. In every prayer, sparks and refinements ascend that had never previously ascended. The order of the supernal union, yenikah, mochin, and their extension corresponds precisely to the order and value of the ascending sparks. This cannot be discussed at greater length here. This is the secret of our praying every day and at every time; one prayer a day does not suffice, because each prayer accomplishes a new aspect that no other prayer accomplished. This is sufficient. The same applies to every Krias Shema: each differs from another. All this concerns the mystery of the particulars. Regarding the general principles, however, all weekday morning recitations of Krias Shema have a single kavanah, all weekday evening recitations have a single kavanah, and so forth.' Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the variation among prayers, page 59, writes likewise; see there. See Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 56; Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on the difference between Shabbos and Yom Tov, page 77a; and Rabbi Rashash's comments in Nehar Shalom, page 19c in the new edition. Examine carefully.
9) There: Each day they should be as new in your eyes, etc. Berachos 63b, chapter HaRoeh, says: 'Listen and hear, O Israel; this day you have become a people.' Was the Torah given to Israel that day? It was already the end of the forty years. Rather, this teaches that Torah is as beloved to those who study it each day as on the day it was given at Mount Sinai. Know this, for a person may recite Krias Shema morning and evening, yet if he fails to recite it one evening, he resembles one who never recited Krias Shema. Pri Chadash 2 and Yafeh LaLev 2 cite this. The words, 'This day - behold, the day has come,' also teach that in serving Hashem, a person should place before his eyes only the light of the present day. When one seeks to enter Hashem's service, it appears like a heavy burden that he cannot carry. If he thinks that he has only this day, however, it will not be a burden at all. Nor should he defer from day to day, saying, 'Tomorrow I will begin; tomorrow I will pray with concentration, vigor, and propriety,' and the like. A person has nothing in his world except the present day and hour, for tomorrow is an entirely different world. Thus concludes the pious author of Likkutei Etzos, entry Yirah VeAvodah 16, based upon, 'Today, if you will heed His voice' - specifically today; see there. Yafeh LaLev, ibid.
