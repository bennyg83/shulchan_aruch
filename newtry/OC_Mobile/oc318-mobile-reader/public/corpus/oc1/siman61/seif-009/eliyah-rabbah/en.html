In heaven and on earth, etc. The language of the Semak is that one should think that He is unique in the seven firmaments and on the one earth, making eight. Rabbi David Abudraham wrote that the three letters of echad include three worlds: one should intend with the alef to the Holy One, blessed is He, Who is first; with the ches to the eight spheres; and with the dalet to the four elements upon which this world is founded. Etz Chaim wrote: when saying 'Hashem Elokeinu Hashem echad,' one should have intent for the four letters of the Name of Havayah; when saying 'Baruch shem kevod malchuso,' one should have intent for the four letters of the Name Adnus; and when saying 'le'olam va'ed,' one should have intent for the unified Name.
