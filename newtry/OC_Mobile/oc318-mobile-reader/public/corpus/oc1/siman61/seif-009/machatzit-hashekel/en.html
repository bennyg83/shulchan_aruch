(Seif katan 9) That one should not, etc. The Bach wrote, etc., but the Gemara in Sukkah 53b, chapter 5, escaped him.
That they would say, etc. On the festival of Sukkos, at the Simchas Beis HaShoevah, when they would go to draw water for the water libation.
There, all of them would say, etc. It is possible that ‘all of them’ is not precise, for the Simchas Beis HaShoevah was performed by the pious, men of deeds, and great Torah scholars, while the rest of the men and women came to see and hear, as the Mishnah teaches there. It implies somewhat that the saying ‘we are to Him, etc.’ was also said specifically by the pious and men of deeds. Nevertheless, in any event, since the saying was from a gathering of most of the pious, according to the Bach it should have been permitted.
Rather, it must be that even the whole congregation, etc. One must distinguish between this case here and that which we say in Rosh Hashanah 24b, that with the public there is no suspicion, and they are permitted to make types of forms where keeping them would raise suspicion of prohibition; the Rama cites this in Yoreh Deah, siman 141, seif 4, in the gloss.
