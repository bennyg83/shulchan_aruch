Whether he repeats, etc. See Mishnah Berurah regarding what it states, that after the fact, when he repeated the verse, etc. It is not only according to Rabbeinu Chananel, who holds that repeating the verse is merely unseemly, that the first recitation certainly is not uprooted thereby. Rather, even according to Rashi, who holds that he is silenced, it is also reasonable that he has fulfilled his obligation. There was no prohibition in his first recitation, and the second recitation does not constitute an interruption in Krias Shema, since he did not pause long enough to complete the whole of it. This resembles the Gemara's question there: Perhaps initially he did not direct his mind, etc. There too, he then recited the verse again and had not directed his mind the first time, yet this is not deemed an interruption, and he fulfills his obligation through the second recitation. The same applies here, that he fulfills his obligation through his first recitation. Regarding repetition of individual words, the matter requires consideration. According to Rashi, who explains that this is only unseemly and that he is not silenced because his recitation is regarded merely as mockery, it is possible that according to this he has not fulfilled his obligation. According to Rabbeinu Chananel, however, who explains that in such a case he is silenced, implying that he does not accept Rashi's rationale, it appears that he does fulfill his obligation. A proof may be brought from the Gemara's question there: Perhaps initially he did not direct his mind, but afterward he did direct his mind. According to Rabbeinu Chananel, the question concerns repetition of the word, for which he is silenced. Evidently, inserting an improper word between one word and the next is not considered an interruption. The same applies in our case.
