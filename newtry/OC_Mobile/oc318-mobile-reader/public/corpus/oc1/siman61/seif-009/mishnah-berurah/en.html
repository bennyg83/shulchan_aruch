Shema twice. Because it appears as though he accepts upon himself two authorities, G-d forbid. If he did not have intent at first, he should go back and read it quietly; and if there are no listeners, even aloud is permitted. But if he had intent at first, even quietly is forbidden. In Selichos it is permitted to say Shema each time one says Vaya'avor, since much interruption occurs between them and it does not appear like two authorities. See the Taz in siman 63, seif 3, who likewise wrote that if he waits some time between the first and second reading it is permitted, for they silence him only when he reads twice consecutively; it is not even improper.
Whether he repeats. After the fact, if he repeated the verse he fulfilled his obligation; if he repeated the words, it requires study. See Biur Halacha.
The first verse. It is stated in the Beit Yosef that from the first verse onward there is no concern; but from the wording of the Rif it appears, at first glance, not so. See there, and see Magen Gibborim, for the Meiri also is stringent in this. However, regarding Shema on one's bed, certainly one should not be stringent about this.
