(9) There—"each day they should be as new in your eyes," etc. And so we say in chapter HaRo'eh (Berakhot 63b): "Be attentive and hear, Israel: this day you have become a people"—was the Torah given to Israel on that day? Was not that day the end of forty years? Rather, to teach you that Torah is beloved to its learner every day and every day as on the day it was given from Mount Sinai. Know that a person reads Keriat Shema morning and evening, and one evening that he does not read is like one who never read Keriat Shema at all—end quote. And they brought Peri Chadash, siman 2. Yafeh Lelev, siman 2, also: "This day, behold, a day comes"—to say that in the service of God one should not set before his eyes anything but the light of the day he stands in alone; for when one wishes to enter the service of God it seems to a person as if it is a heavy burden and he cannot bear such a heavy burden—but when he considers that he has only that day, he will have no burden at all; also he should not postpone himself from day to day saying "tomorrow I will begin, tomorrow I will pray with kavanah and strength as fitting," and the like—for a person has in his world only that day and that hour he stands in, for tomorrow's day is an entirely different world. And when the pious rabbi finished Likutei Mitzvot on the topic of fear and service, siman 16, from what is written "today if you heed His voice"—"today" specifically—see there; Yafeh Lelev there.
