One should infer from the words of the Gemara, where it says that we silence him, that this refers to where there are others who hear him. But where there are no others who hear him, namely if he is in a congregation and says it quietly, or if he is alone even aloud, we have no issue with it. Bach; and so wrote Sefer Chasidim, siman 18. See Taz, seif katan 3. One should not ask: if so, why does the Talmud in Berachos 33b challenge from one who reads Shema and repeats it; perhaps it speaks of one who says it quietly or alone. For if so, it would not even be disgraceful; see there. In Selichos, where there is a large interruption in between, it is permitted to say it each time one says ‘Vayaavor.’ Shelah, Magen Avraham. The same applies to two distinct matters: it is permitted to repeat, as we say in chapter HaChalil regarding the Mishnah, ‘We are to Him and to Him are our eyes,’ where the conclusion there is that this is what it means: to Him we give thanks, and to Him our eyes hope. Rashi wrote there that since he mentions two matters, we have no issue with it; see there. This sugya escaped the Bach in what he ruled that where the entire congregation says it, there is no objection, for there the whole congregation would say it, and nevertheless the Gemara challenged: do we not say that one who says ‘Shema, Shema’ is silenced? See Magen Avraham, seif katan 9.
