In two matters. And if he did not intend he returns and reads quietly; and one must be precise from the words of the Gemara that we say "we silence him"—it implies when there are others who hear him; but when there are no others who hear him—i.e. if he is in a congregation and quietly, or individually even in a loud voice—we have no concern—Bach, and so too in Sefer Chasidim siman 18; and see Taz s.k. 3. And one should not challenge: if so, what does the Gemara in Berakhot 33b challenge on one who reads Shema and doubles it—perhaps it deals with quietly or individually, for then even disgrace there is none, end quote. And in Selichot where the rabbi interrupts much in between, it is permitted to say each time he says "vaya'avor." Shelah, Magen Avraham. And likewise on two separate matters it is permitted to double, as we say in the chapter HaChalil on "we are His and to Him our eyes"—we conclude there that this is what he said to Him: "we admit and our eyes hope to Him"; and Rashi wrote there: since two matters are mentioned, we have no concern, end quote. And this sugya escaped Bach in what he ruled where the entire congregation says—there is no objection, for there the entire congregation would say it and nevertheless he challenged there "we say one who says Shema Shema—we silence him." Eshel Avraham s.k. 9.
