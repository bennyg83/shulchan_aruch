19) There, in the gloss: Some write that anyone reciting Krias Shema alone should say El Melech Neeman, etc. Tur writes in Ramah's name that one who interrupts with El Melech Neeman commits three errors. First, he interrupts between the berachah and the reading. Second, he utters a Divine Name in vain and violates 'You shall not take,' because mentioning the Name El here has no relevance: he relates it neither to 'Who chooses His people Israel' nor to 'Hear, O Israel,' so it is in vain. Third, since the words are not needed for the berachah but are merely intended to complete 248 words, they are an addition to Krias Shema. Neither Yaakov nor Moshe said this addition; see there. Maharshal similarly writes in Responsa 64: 'Regarding Krias Shema, I received the practice to say Amen, Shema Yisrael in a congregation, but not El Melech Neeman, whether alone or with a congregation. Pay no attention to Maharik, who writes that El Melech Neeman should be said.' Matteh Moshe, laws of Krias Shema 90, cites this and writes that it is correct: If the concern is an interruption, what difference is there between an individual and a congregation? Knesses HaGedolah and Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef 1, cite this, as do Magen Avraham 4 and Eliyah Rabbah 6. Beis Yosef and Bach, although writing that this was an ancient custom, state that it was subsequently abolished so as not to interrupt between 'Who chooses His people Israel' and Shema. The custom based upon Zohar and Tikkunim became for the shaliach tzibbur to repeat Hashem Elokeichem emes; see there. We already wrote in paragraph 16 that the Arizal's words demonstrate that El Melech Neeman has no place here. Saying it here confuses the order. Even an individual may instead repeat Hashem Elokeichem and then say emes.
