(19) Gloss. And some wrote that whoever reads Keriat Shema alone should say El Melekh Ne'eman, etc. Tur wrote in the name of the Ramah that one who interrupts with El Melekh Ne'eman has three matters of interruption between the blessing and the reading; and further he takes the Name in vain and transgresses "you shall not take [the Name in vain]," for mention of the Name here has no context—it does not mention for Him who chose His people Israel nor Shema Yisrael—and it is in vain; and further since it is not for the need of a blessing but to complete 248 words, it is an addition in Keriat Shema—and this addition neither Jacob said nor Moses said—see there. And likewise Rashal in responsum siman 64 and thus: in Keriat Shema I received to say amen at Shema Yisrael in congregation and not El Melekh Ne'eman, whether alone or in congregation—and do not heed Maharik who wrote one should say El Melekh Ne'eman—end quote. And they brought Mateh Moshe, laws of Keriat Shema siman 90, and wrote: and this is correct—if they were strict because of interruption, what difference to me alone, what difference to me congregation—end quote. And Bach as Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef; and in Shakh Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef note 1, Magen Avraham note 4. Eliyah Rabbah note 6. And also Beit Yosef and Bach who wrote this is an ancient custom—nevertheless they wrote that afterward they abolished it so as not to interrupt between "who chose His people Israel" and "Hear, Israel," and they practiced per the words of the Zohar and Tikkunim that the shaliach tzibbur repeats "the Lord your God" emes—see there. And we already wrote above siman 16 that it is proven from the words of our Rabbi the Ari, may his memory be a blessing, that they have no place here; and if one says it here, behold he mixes the order—but even if reading alone he may repeat "the Lord your God" and afterward say emes.
