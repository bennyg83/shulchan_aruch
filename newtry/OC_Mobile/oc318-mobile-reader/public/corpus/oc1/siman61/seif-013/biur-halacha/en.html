After, etc., in the bedtime Shema—see in Mishnah Berurah regarding bedi'eved if they did not say it. Behold Magen Avraham brought in the name of Sefer HaGibborim and Bach that they do not return him, and Levush rules that they return him. And behold even though from Magen Avraham's words it implies that he agrees with Levush that they return him—and the intent is to return him to the beginning as proven in Sefer Chayyim Adam general rule 21, or at minimum to the bedtime Shema—nevertheless in my humble opinion it seems the law is with Sefer HaGibborim and Bach. See in Pesachim 56a: Rabbi Yehuda says they would interrupt, except that they would not say it in the bedtime Shema. And in the second baraita: these and those—not with the will of the Sages, etc., except that the Sages did not protest them. And if it were so that they return him and he did not fulfill his obligation of Keriat Shema through this—they would have had to protest them as on other matters in which there was no Torah prohibition, as the Gemara states there, and nevertheless they protested them. And further—why should we make a distant dispute between Rabbi Yehuda and Rabbi Meir who holds there that they would not interrupt, and Rashi explained between echad and ve'ahavta; and see there in Maharsha that according to Rabbi Meir's view he would not need at all to say it in the bedtime Shema per Rashi's explanation, but certainly that even for Rabbi Yehuda bedi'eved they do not return him. And further—from the beginning of chapter 2 of Berakhot it is proven as the statement there: he was reading in the Torah [parashat Keriat Shema as Rashi explained] and the time of recitation arrived—if he intended in his heart he fulfilled, and behold he did not say it in the bedtime Shema—or that he fulfilled bedi'eved. And it works out if we explain "if he intended in his heart to fulfill" (for the Gemara says there—we learn mitsvot require kavanah—what is "if he intended in his heart to read"—to read? But he is reading while reading to revise) we can also say the case is where he said it in the bedtime Shema; but if we explain he intended in his heart to read in the Torah generally and nevertheless fulfilled as the Gemara rejected there—certainly the case is not where he said the bedtime Shema and nevertheless fulfilled. [Hagahah: I thought to place this matter in the words of Yerushalmi chapter 2 of Berakhot in what it says there "and he does not interrupt and we did not teach it," etc.—meaning whether he does not interrupt to say the bedtime Shema and nevertheless we did not teach this thing; and likewise regarding berakhot—perhaps only if he blessed them, see there in Yerushalmi; and if so there would be support for Levush's words—but since poskim omitted this, apparently they do not explain Yerushalmi thus.] And one should not reject that the mishnah follows Rabbi Meir mentioned above that one need not say the bedtime Shema—first, Rabbi Meir himself holds one must say the bedtime Shema as stated explicitly in Yerushalmi Pesachim chapter "Makom shehagu"; and even for Rashi above who had a different text in Yerushalmi on this as commentators wrote—nevertheless one should not say thus, for if so how did all poskim copy the mishnah's law in general and not one of them omitted to say that for us who rule like Rabbi Yehuda he does not fulfill in general unless he said the bedtime Shema—but certainly in every manner he fulfilled. And what Magen Avraham brought as proof from siman 66 seif 6 that there he rules one should not interrupt in saying the bedtime Shema unless he fears lest they kill him—thus it is included in accepting the yoke of heaven like the first verse of Shema—is no proof, for we too concede that the very saying of the bedtime Shema is a great matter; but there is no proof from this that if he skipped he must return to the beginning, for also if he skipped from the body of the parashah—if the Torah had not revealed to us and there were those who would not read out of order—he would not need thereby to return to the beginning; and whence do we derive to be stringent also in saying the bedtime Shema that the Sages enacted and added? And were I not afraid I would say that Levush too concedes in this that he only said one must return and it is not like other verses in Keriat Shema where one need not return on account of kavanah even if he had not yet begun the verse after it—and the practical difference is if he is standing before ve'ahavta; but if he already read Keriat Shema but skipped the bedtime Shema he need not return on account of this, only say it when he remembers—and it is possible that even this is not required by law.
