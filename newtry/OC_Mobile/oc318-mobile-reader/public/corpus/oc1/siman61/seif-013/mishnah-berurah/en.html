(29) "Baruch shem"—and if he did not say it, there are differing opinions among the poskim whether they return him; see Magen Avraham, and see Beiur Halachah.

(30) Quietly—when Yaakov Avinu, peace be upon him, called his sons, he wished to reveal to them the end, as it is written "And I will tell you what will befall you in the end of days," and the Shechinah withdrew from him and did not permit him to reveal it. He said to his sons: perhaps there is among you one who is not fit. They all opened and said Shema Yisrael the Omnipresent Elokeinu the Omnipresent echad. The elder opened and said Baruch shem kevod malchuto le'olam va'ed. The Rabbis said: how shall we act? Shall we say it? Moses did not say it. Yaakov fixed that we say it quietly—and it is a sign that it is not from the parasha written in the Torah; only Yaakov said it.
