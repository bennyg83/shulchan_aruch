Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed. If he did not say it, there are differing opinions among the poskim whether we make him go back; see Magen Avraham and Biur Halacha.
Quietly. For when Yaakov Avinu, peace be upon him, called his sons, he sought to reveal the end to them, as it is written, 'and I will tell you what will happen to you at the end of days,' but the Shechinah departed from him and did not allow him to reveal it. He said to his sons: perhaps there is someone unfit among you. They all opened and said, 'Shema Yisrael, Hashem Elokeinu, Hashem echad.' The elder opened and said, 'Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed.' The Sages said: what should we do? Shall we say it? Moshe did not say it. Shall we not say it? Yaakov said it. They enacted that it be said quietly, as a sign that it is not part of the parsha written in the Torah, but Yaakov said it.
