(15) There—and if he reads alone he should have kavanah, etc.; and according to what was said in the Yerushalmi—if one reads alone he may repeat "the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent"—and so agreed the Bach and Peri Chadash as the Yerushalmi's words. And I heard that Maharach, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, likewise wrote that our Rabbi the Ari, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, was accustomed thus; and likewise the Ra'avad divided regarding the requests and mention of His praise, may He be blessed—they brought R' Shabtai HaLevi siman 8, Berkei Yosef siman 1, and Bakashat Shmuel siman 11 note 19. Shitah Mekubbetzet 94d, Siddur Beit Yosef siman 3. Zekher LeChaim vol. 3 siman 100 note 136. Sha'arei Teshuvah note 2, Kaf HaChayyim siman 14 note 49. And see Sha'ar HaKavanot in the derashot of the night derashah 9, who wrote and thus: after you complete Keriat Shema you should return to say passage one "the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent" emes as the shaliach tzibbur does, and thereby all 248 words are completed—end quote. And Emek Malach wrote in Kuntras Sefat Emet that this is in Sha'ar Keriat Shema 109b—that likewise in morning Keriat Shema when said alone it is like Keriat Shema on the bed, and the repair is done by our hands even though the essence of the repair depends on the shaliach tzibbur—see there. And see what I wrote in the note before this.
