Regarding the ruling in siman 63, seif 4, that he returns and reads, one may answer that he says the entire section twice, as Magen Avraham writes there in seif katan 6, or that he waits briefly, as Taz writes there in seif katan 3. Taz found Sefer Chassidim difficult, and answered that Rav Pappa's question was why they silence and rebuke him. Whenever he did not initially have intent, although he repeated it aloud, we should not have rebuked him. Abaye answered that they properly rebuke him, for he should have had intent initially. One cannot readily say that the question there concerns dividing a verse or repeating a word, and that the Gemara does not answer that he lacked intent because such repetition would still be unseemly, though the matter may be resolved. Bach writes that the Shulchan Aruch follows Tur and does not distinguish between an individual and a congregation, but this is incorrect. See there. I do not know why, since in siman 63, seif 4, "he returns and reads" means quietly. At any rate, the Yerushalmi distinguishes an individual, implying that even if he initially had intent, he may repeat it, because it is a supplication for him, as Peri Chadash writes. Tur disputes this on the ground that the Bavli does not accept that distinction, since it asks regarding the difference between "unseemly" and "we silence him." Peri Chadash answers that for an individual it is not even unseemly. Nevertheless, we have only the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch: If he did not have intent, he repeats it quietly, and for an individual, aloud is comparable to quietly. If he initially had intent, however, even an individual may not say Shema twice, as stated. See Peri Chadash, who cites Sukkah 53b that where the two statements concern different matters there is no concern. He proves that this does not follow Rashi, for there they repeated each word. In my view, one may answer that it is nevertheless unseemly. Rabbi Zeira there selected the baraisa because one could have said that repetition of verses is merely unseemly, and therefore he cites Rabbi Zeira. This is not the place to elaborate. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 9, where, with Hashem's help, this will be explained.
