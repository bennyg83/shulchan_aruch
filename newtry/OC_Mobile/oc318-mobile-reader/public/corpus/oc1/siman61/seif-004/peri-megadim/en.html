Forbidden—Taz challenged from Berakhot 33b, see there; and from what is in siman 63, seif 4, "he repeats and reads"—one may say he says the entire parashah aloud, as Magen Avraham wrote there note 6, or waits a little as Taz wrote there note 3. And for Maharshal there is a difficulty, and he answered: the difficulty is why we silence and rebuke him who did not intend from the outset even though he says loudly—we should not have rebuked; and he answered that rebuking is proper and he should have intended from the outset. However one should not say the challenge there is about difference of verse or word and not answer "he did not intend" is disgraceful—and one may say. Abudraham wrote Shulchan Aruch follows Tur and does not distinguish individual from congregation—but it is not so, see there; and I say—for in 63, seif 4, "he repeats and reads" means in a whisper. But in any case from Yerushalmi distinguishing individual it implies even if he intended from the outset he may say aloud—it is [like] supplications for him, as Peri Chadash wrote; and on this Tur disagrees that Bavli does not hold so, since it challenges "disgraceful" we silence; and Peri Chadash explained that for individual it is also not disgraceful; nevertheless we have only Shulchan Aruch's words—if he did not intend he repeats and says in a whisper, and for individual aloud is like whisper; but if he intended from the outset, even individual is forbidden to say Shema aloud as we said. See Peri Chadash brought Gemara Sukkah 53b, two matters—we have no concern and proved not like Rashi, for word by word they say; and to my view one may still say it is disgraceful, and he holds there R' Zeira that from a braita one may say disgraceful in sections, therefore he brings R' Zeira, and need not elaborate; Magen Avraham note 9 and there it will be explained on this.
