With all your heart. Meaning that he should read it in a manner that makes it audible that there are two lameds. Nevertheless, there must be a hyphen between them, for without a hyphen one must read ‘bechol’ with a cholam, and with a hyphen with a kamatz. The reason is that a sounded resting consonant never follows a large vowel unless the cantillation mark comes under the large vowel, as is explained by the grammarians. Therefore, with a hyphen the cantillation mark never comes except under the second word, and automatically the cholam changes to a chataf-kamatz, which is a small vowel, so that a sounded resting consonant can follow it. Even so, it does not depart from its form, as Rashi wrote in the Chumash, Parashas Yisro, on the verse ‘lest He burst forth among them’; see there. So too, ‘es’ is with a tzeirei, and when it comes with a hyphen it changes to its derivative, a segol, for this reason.
