With this—Baer Heitev. And see in the name of Sefer Shetei Yadot, who wrote that in most communities they read with trop but do not pause at the end of a verse as the Torah reader does; on the contrary, one must pause more at the end of a verse without any trop, and one should be careful with the trop tarcha and not be careless with it when reaching "kol ha-karov ha-karov el mishkan Hashem yamut mecharef." And at the end of parashat tzitzit, "Ani Hashem Elokeikhem emet"—in the word ani there is trop tarcha, and I will explain why David said "Ana Hashem ki ani avdekha," etc.; and behold, when he says "ani avdekha" he does not exclude that there should not also be another man His servant—only "I am Your servant," and already others too are found His servants, and likewise "and to me, I am Your servant," etc., and to Solomon "Your servant" he did not call; therefore if you say "Ani Hashem Elokeikhem" without tarcha, it implies "I am Hashem Elokeikhem" and it is possible another too will be your God; but when you read ani with trop tarcha, then it implies I and not another, etc. And it is not far in my eyes that initially the Holy One, blessed be He, ended this parashah thus to allude to unity at the end of Keriat Shema, and the one who concludes concludes with what he begins—end quote, see there. And see there further in the name of Sefer Shetei Yadot, who wrote in the name of the Maharam, that it is in the Zohar: Rabbi so-and-so found a dead man and examined his mouth and nostril and eyes and did not find them in him, and said: certainly he was not careful with the glues in "emet veyatziv"—end quote.
