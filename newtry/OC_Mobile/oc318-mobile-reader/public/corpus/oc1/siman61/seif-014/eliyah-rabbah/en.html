Baruch shem kevod malchuso va'ed, etc. Shiltei Gibborim and the Bach wrote that if he did not say it at all, we do not make him go back; end quote. The reason is that it is only an enactment because Yaakov said it. But the Magen Avraham wrote about this as follows: the Levush wrote that even if he said it but did not say it with intent, he must go back. See below, siman 63, seif katan 9.
In order to interrupt, etc. There is another reason in Lechem Chamudos: 'Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed' is a blessing and not language of acceptance. The Shelah wrote that one should pause between Shema and Yisrael, and also between Hashem and Elokeinu, and between Hashem and echad.
