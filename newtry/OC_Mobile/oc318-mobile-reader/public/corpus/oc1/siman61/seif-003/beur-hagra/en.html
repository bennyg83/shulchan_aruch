Seif 3: the shaliach tzibbur concludes, etc. However, the Gemara implies that an individual concludes with 'emes' and does not repeat it, as it says there that he returns and says, etc.; but the shaliach tzibbur does not first conclude with 'emes,' as is implied there from the case of the one who descended before the ark, etc. Certainly 'emes' is included in the count, even though it belongs to the blessing; proof is that even the fifteen vavs are counted. All the more so according to the Rav's explanation in the gloss, 'and there are another nine,' etc., it is evident that 'emes' is included in the count. His words are primary, as stated there; and so wrote the Magen Avraham in the name of some commentators, unlike the Beit Yosef, who was very strained.
And with this, each person, etc. As written in the Zohar Chadash, seif katan 1, that whoever says Shema not with the congregation, etc.; and even though he does not say it with the shaliach tzibbur, as stated in the Gemara that he returns, etc., Rabbeinu Yonah explained that this is when he finishes and waits for the shaliach tzibbur, as explained above in siman 66, seif 6.
And if, etc. For only 'emes' alone may not be repeated. Darkei Moshe.
And if he is, etc. Zohar Chadash there.
Which amount, etc. So the Beit Yosef explained. But the Rav's words are primary: first, thirteen vavs suffice; also, 'emes' interrupts between them; and also, it corresponds with 'Hashem Elokeichem emes,' and here too there are these two Names.
And some wrote, etc. So it is written in Tanchuma and in the Tikkunim, tikkun 10, 21b.
And it is in place of, etc. As written in chapter 16 of Shabbos.
And it appears to me, etc. For the Ramah protested this practice, because they make an interruption. Indeed, even one who answers amen to his own blessings was called an ignoramus for this reason; and regarding 'Baruch shem kevod malchuso le'olam va'ed' they said: shall we say it, when Moshe did not say it? And in Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 10 there, they said: because they do not make an interruption, etc. It would seem there, however, that this is because the words can be repeated, which is not so for an individual. And even though one can have intent for the vavs, nevertheless in the Zohar Chadash there it is called a crookedness that cannot be corrected.
Only, etc. For it is in place of 'El Melech Ne'eman,' as written in Shabbos there; see Tosafot there. He holds like the Rosh that amen after the shaliach tzibbur is not an interruption, as explained above in siman 59, seif 4; see there.
