And in order to complete the 248 [words], the chazan concludes "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet," etc. And the chazan may say "emet" quietly and then repeat it aloud (Ra'avan in his responsa).

And he repeats and says aloud "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet," etc. And some say one must begin "Ani the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet" (Maharshal siman 64 and in his responsa Ra'avan siman 8); and the first reasoning is primary—to begin "Ani the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet," and so is the custom (Maharal ben Chaviv siman 73, and Maharam Alashkar his siman, and Maharshach siman 33). And some say one need not compel to be lenient where they were not accustomed to return the three words that they would return (Ra'avan siman 12). And it is permitted for the chazan to say "emet" quietly and then repeat it aloud (Ra'avan there).

And if he reads individually he should intend in the fifteen vavins in "emet veyatziv." And some say the fifteen vavins in "emet veyatziv" correspond to "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet," and the first vavins until "vene'eman" correspond to the the Omnipresent mentioned in their words "Ani the Omnipresent ne'eman le'shalem sachar tov"; "the Omnipresent" vavins until "venora" correspond to "Elokeikhem," for the name of the Omnipresent is very awesome; and until "veyafeh" correspond to "emet," for the beauty of the matter is the truth (Tzror HaMor). And if he reads Ma'ariv individually he should intend in the verse "Pedita oti the Omnipresent El emet," which corresponds to "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet" (Tzror HaMor). And some say that if he reads Keriat Shema of Ma'ariv individually he should say "El melekh ne'eman" (Maharshal per Rokeach). And some say one need not complete 248 in Ma'ariv, because the 248 words of Keriat Shema heal 248 limbs of his body—because Keriat Shema is directed against tefillah and they are bound together in one bond, therefore his prayer is heard and ascends on high, unlike Ma'ariv tefillah which is optional; and in order to remove his soul from dispute he must be careful to read Keriat Shema with the congregation and to intend in what the chazan repeats in order to complete his limbs—and one who lacks any of them is flawed in his soul, and no blessing rests on a flawed thing (Tikkunei Zohar). And some say one must say "El melekh ne'eman" individually.

He should only say amen after the chazan when he finishes the berakhah; and some say one must say "amen, Shema," etc., both individually and in a congregation—unless if he reads Ma'ariv individually, then he must say "El melekh ne'eman, Shema Yisrael," etc. (Maharshal).
