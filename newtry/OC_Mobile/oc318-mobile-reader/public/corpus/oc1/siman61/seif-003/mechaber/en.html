In the recitation of Shema there are 245 words, and in order to complete 248, which corresponds to the limbs of the human body, the prayer leader concludes "Hashem your God is true" and repeats and says aloud "Hashem your God is true." And if he's reading alone, have in mind regarding the fifteen vavs in "Emet V'yatziv" which equal 90, and they correspond to three divine Names, where each Name equals 26 and its four letters are 30. {Rama: Rema: And through this every person fulfills their obligation, since they hear these three words from the mouth of the prayer leader (Beit Yosef in the name of Orchot Chaim). And if the individual wants to say them along with the prayer leader, it is not forbidden (his own words). Rema: And there's another reason for doing so, since the fifteen vavs add up to 90, and the reading counts as one, this is then 91, which is the value of the God's name as it is pronounced and written, and it's as if he said "Hashem Adonai emet" (Maharik Root 42 and Agur). And there are those who wrote that anyone who recites Shema alone should say "El melech ne'eman Shema, etc.," because these three words complete the count of 248, and this comes in place of the Amen that one is required to answer after "Blessed is He who chooses His people Israel with love," and so is the custom. And it seems to me nevertheless that when reciting with the congregation, one should not say "El melech ne'eman" but rather should say "Amen" after the prayer leader concludes the blessing, and this is our custom and it is correct.}
