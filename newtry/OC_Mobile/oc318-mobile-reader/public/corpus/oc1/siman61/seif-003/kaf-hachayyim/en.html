10) [Se'if 3] Krias Shema contains 245 words. To complete 248 corresponding to a person's limbs, the shaliach tzibbur concludes, 'Hashem Elokeichem emes,' and repeats, etc. One who does not follow this practice should not be compelled to repeat them. Re'em 1:1; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham 1. Zohar Chadash, Midrash Rus 95a, however, is very stringent: 'Anyone who recites Krias Shema outside the congregation does not complete his limbs, because he lacks the three words repeated by the shaliach tzibbur. What is his remedy? He should concentrate upon the fifteen vavs in Emes VeYatziv. Despite all this, Abba would apply to him the verse, "That which is crooked cannot be made straight," etc.' Beis Yosef writes likewise in Midrash HaNeelam's name; see there. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 8 on Krias Shema, likewise requires repeating the three words Hashem Elokeichem emes and gives a mystical reason; see there. Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 25, writes likewise.
11) There: The shaliach tzibbur concludes, 'Hashem Elokeichem emes,' etc. Pri Chadash 3 writes: 'It is proper to teach all chazzanim that when they reach the end of Krias Shema, they should say these three words quietly. This vindicates the custom. Anyone who does not do so disparages the words of our Sages, the masters of the Talmud, deserves rebuke, and one who heeds me will dwell securely.' Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, however, writes: 'We have only the words of our holy master, who says that he repeats Hashem Elokeichem emes, as the gaon Re'em, Maharam Alashkar, and Maharshach write. This does not contradict the Talmud, for our author has already reconciled its words so that they do not conflict with Zohar and Tikkunim.' Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99a, writes likewise. See what we wrote above in siman 25:75: On a matter not stated explicitly in the Talmud, the received tradition decides; see there. Here too, since the Talmud does not explicitly prohibit repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes, as Beis Yosef writes, Zohar and Kabbalah establish that these three words must be repeated. It therefore makes no difference even if the shaliach tzibbur concludes Hashem Elokeichem emes aloud and repeats them aloud. See the next paragraph.
12) There: The shaliach tzibbur concludes Hashem Elokeichem emes and repeats aloud, 'Hashem Elokeichem emes.' This implies that the shaliach tzibbur should say emes twice: once upon concluding and once when repeating. Asarah Maamaros, maamar Em Kol Chai 1:17, however, writes that the shaliach tzibbur does not say emes quietly. An individual says emes quietly and does not repeat it. There are thereby precisely 248 words; if the shaliach tzibbur says emes quietly and again aloud, there are 249. Magen Avraham 1 and Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99b, cite this. Rama MiPano likewise writes in Alfasi Zuta, chapter Hayah Korei: 'The halachah is that he does not repeat emes. An individual says it only at the end of the section, while the shaliach tzibbur says it only in the repetition of Hashem Elokeichem. Each person thus has emes, and it is never repeated.' Machazik Berachah 3 cites this. The universal custom is for the shaliach tzibbur to conclude Hashem Elokeichem aloud; the congregation answers emes, but he does not say emes. He then repeats Hashem Elokeichem emes aloud. The congregation does not repeat emes but continues VeYatziv, etc., so each person has only one emes. Chesed LaAlafim 3 writes that if one finishes before the chazzan, he should join Hashem Elokeichem immediately to emes, for the Sages said that one may not interrupt between Vayomer and Emes VeYatziv. He should wait there for the chazzan and afterward begin VeYatziv. He should not follow the common practice of waiting to say emes together with the chazzan, thereby interrupting between Elokeichem and emes. Those who do so err, since even an interruption due to fear is not permitted there, etc. This does not appear correct. The prohibition concerns interruption through speech; a silent pause alone is not an interruption, as Matteh Yehudah, siman 66:2, and Zechor LeAvraham 3, entry kuf 133, write; see there. Moreover, hearing is equivalent to answering. Since he says emes after the chazzan's reading, it is considered as though he says it after his own reading, and there is no concern.
13) There, in the gloss: In this manner everyone fulfills the obligation, since they hear it from the shaliach tzibbur, etc. This too requires the listener and speaker to intend respectively to fulfill and discharge the obligation. Since the Arizal is reported to have repeated these three words when reciting Krias Shema alone, it follows that they were instituted even for an individual. Therefore, if one is uncertain whether the chazzan knows to intend to discharge the congregation's obligation, he should repeat the three words himself and not rely upon the chazzan. Chesed LaAlafim 3. See below, paragraph 15, and the next paragraph.
14) There, in the gloss. If an individual also wishes to say the words together with the shaliach tzibbur, there is no prohibition. This is the custom of Beis El, may it be rebuilt speedily in our days: Each person repeats Hashem Elokeichem emes for himself together with the shaliach tzibbur. They do not rely upon the shaliach tzibbur's recitation, because each person wishes personally to have the kavanah associated with these three words that is recorded in Rabbi Rashash's siddur. See the next paragraph.
15) There: If he recites it alone, he should concentrate, etc. According to the Yerushalmi's statement, one who recites Krias Shema alone may repeat Hashem Elokeichem. Bach and Pri Chadash agree with the Yerushalmi. I heard that Maharchu likewise writes that the Arizal followed this practice. Raavad also takes this position concerning supplications and mentioning the Holy One's praises, as cited by Shibolei HaLeket 8; Birkei Yosef 1; Kesher Gudal 11:19; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99d; Siddur Beis Yaakov 3; Zechor LeAvraham 3, entry kuf 136; Shaarei Teshuvah 2; and Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi 14:49. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 9 on the night, states: 'After completing Krias Shema, repeat Hashem Elokeichem emes once, as the shaliach tzibbur does. Through this, all 248 words are completed.' Emes LeYaakov, Kuntres Sefas Emes, question 52, page 109b, writes that the same applies to the morning Krias Shema when recited alone: it is like the bedtime Krias Shema, and the rectification is performed by us, although the principal rectification depends upon the shaliach tzibbur; see there. See the preceding paragraph.
16) There: If he recites it alone, he should concentrate upon the fifteen vavs, etc. If an individual is still reciting Krias Shema when the shaliach tzibbur finishes, the individual may remain silent and intend to hear Hashem Elokeichem from the shaliach tzibbur. This benefits him even though he subsequently completes Krias Shema. Mahari Ayash, Matteh Yehudah 2, Likkutei Orach Chaim, page 60c, cited by Machazik Berachah in the Kuntres Acharon to this siman 2. Maamar Mordechai 2 writes likewise in Seder HaYom's name, as do Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99b; Zechor LeAvraham 3, entry kuf 135; Shaarei Teshuvah 2; and Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi, ibid. 50. According to the Arizal's words in Shaar HaKavanos, end of discourse 5 and discourses 8 on Krias Shema and 9 on the night, and in Rabbi Rashash's siddur, however, the first section from VeAhavta contains forty-two words corresponding to the forty-two-letter Name of alef-beis-gimel, etc., and to Chochmah, Binah, and Daas. The second section through Vesamtem contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from the initial letters of Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet - Vayisa in straight order, Vayavo in reverse, and Vayet in straight order - and to Chesed, Gevurah, and Tiferes. From Vesamtem to the end of the section are fifty words, corresponding to the fifty gates of Binah. Forty correspond to ten Names Ehyeh containing forty letters; ten correspond to the aggregate of the ten Names Ehyeh. They correspond to the lower two-thirds of Tiferes. The tzitzis section contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from the initial letters of Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet, all in straight order, and to Netzach, Hod, and Yesod. Repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes draws influence from the upper joints of the Netzach, Hod, and Yesod of Zeir Anpin, and from the moach within them, into Zeir Anpin's Netzach, Hod, and Yesod, thereby forming the Chochmah, Binah, and Daas of Malchus; see there at length. Those familiar with the mystical wisdom know that this kavanah can occur only after one recites the passages in order - after completing all three sections through Hashem Elokeichem and then repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes. Otherwise he reverses and confuses the order effected above, Heaven forbid. The only remedy is therefore either to hear the shaliach tzibbur and intend to fulfill the obligation when he is at the same point, or to repeat the words himself. Hearing them out of their proper place with intent to fulfill cannot accord with the kabbalists. Nor, according to them, can concentration upon the fifteen vavs substitute for repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes, because it confuses the order. Rather, he must hear them from the shaliach tzibbur or say Hashem Elokeichem emes himself, as stated. All the more does saying El Melech Neeman instead confuse the order, for those words have no place here, as the Arizal's words demonstrate and as stated below in paragraph 19. Although we do not perform the kavanos, we have written in many places that one must follow the order appropriate to them. Independently, we already wrote above that an individual may repeat Hashem Elokeichem, and nothing is lost thereby. Maamar Mordechai further writes that if one did not finish Krias Shema at precisely the same time as the shaliach tzibbur but reaches that point while the shaliach tzibbur repeats these three words, he should remain silent and hear them from the shaliach tzibbur, then complete his reading. They count toward his 248 words, for what difference does it make whether the shaliach tzibbur says them first or he says them first? This appears incorrect, for the repetition has a separate kavanah, as explained above. How can it precede his completion? He reverses the order. If this occurs, he should not intend to fulfill through the shaliach tzibbur but repeat the words himself, as stated. See also paragraphs 1 and 2 above.
17) There: He should concentrate upon the fifteen vavs, etc. See Magen Avraham 3. Maamar Mordechai 3 writes that at Maariv there is less reason for concern. According to the Arizal, however, there is no distinction between Shacharis and Maariv. Even the bedtime Krias Shema must be completed to 248 words, as stated above in paragraph 15.
18) There: They correspond to three Names Havayah, etc. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 8 on Krias Shema, page 27d, states concerning the fifteen vavs in Emes VeYatziv: 'Concentrate through them upon the Name Havayah that emerges from them as follows. The fifteen vavs have the numerical value represented by Yud-Heh. Multiply Yud-Heh by vav, yielding the value of the fifteen vavs; together with the heh at the beginning of the word hadavar, one complete Havayah is formed. I also received this kavanah in another form: The fifteen vavs are six times Yud-Heh, totaling ninety. Thus the two letters Yud-Heh are present in each of the six extremities represented by vav. This is the mystery of the Name Yud-Heh-Vav. Together with the heh at the beginning of hadavar, etc., Havayah is completed. One must therefore connect the word hadavar to these fifteen vavs and make no interruption between them.' See there, where another kavanah is given. The practical consequence is that one must take exceptional care not to lose one of these vavs and thereby lose the kavanah. I have seen pious men of deeds count them on a finger so none will be omitted. See also our comments below to siman 66:1, laws of Emes VeYatziv, with G-d's help.
19) There, in the gloss: Some write that anyone reciting Krias Shema alone should say El Melech Neeman, etc. Tur writes in Ramah's name that one who interrupts with El Melech Neeman commits three errors. First, he interrupts between the berachah and the reading. Second, he utters a Divine Name in vain and violates 'You shall not take,' because mentioning the Name El here has no relevance: he relates it neither to 'Who chooses His people Israel' nor to 'Hear, O Israel,' so it is in vain. Third, since the words are not needed for the berachah but are merely intended to complete 248 words, they are an addition to Krias Shema. Neither Yaakov nor Moshe said this addition; see there. Maharshal similarly writes in Responsa 64: 'Regarding Krias Shema, I received the practice to say Amen, Shema Yisrael in a congregation, but not El Melech Neeman, whether alone or with a congregation. Pay no attention to Maharik, who writes that El Melech Neeman should be said.' Matteh Moshe, laws of Krias Shema 90, cites this and writes that it is correct: If the concern is an interruption, what difference is there between an individual and a congregation? Knesses HaGedolah and Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef 1, cite this, as do Magen Avraham 4 and Eliyah Rabbah 6. Beis Yosef and Bach, although writing that this was an ancient custom, state that it was subsequently abolished so as not to interrupt between 'Who chooses His people Israel' and Shema. The custom based upon Zohar and Tikkunim became for the shaliach tzibbur to repeat Hashem Elokeichem emes; see there. We already wrote in paragraph 16 that the Arizal's words demonstrate that El Melech Neeman has no place here. Saying it here confuses the order. Even an individual may instead repeat Hashem Elokeichem and then say emes.
20) There, in the gloss: Rather, he should answer Amen after the shaliach tzibbur, etc. See what we wrote above in siman 59:26.
