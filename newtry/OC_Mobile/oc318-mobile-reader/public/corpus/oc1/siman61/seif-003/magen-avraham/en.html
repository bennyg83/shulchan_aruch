(א) And he returns and says. And in a place where they were not accustomed to repeat, one does not protest against them [Piskei HaDin in the name of R' Levi ibn Chaviv siman 73, and Maharam Alashkar siman 15, and Maharshach siman 33, and R' Eliyahu Mizrachi vol. 1 siman 61, and see there siman 2]. And in Sefer Asarah Ma'amarot, ma'amar Im Kehalot vol. 1 siman 13, he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur does not say emes in a whisper and the individual says emes in a whisper and does not return to say emes—and it turns out there are 248 words with kavanah; but if he says emes in a whisper and returns and says emes, there are 249 words—end quote; and with this the plain sense of the Talmud that says he does not return and say emes is settled; and the Rabbeynu Yonah forced it there—see there; but Maharam Alashkar wrote that it may be explained: also the first time emes is so as not to separate between them, and the second emes is not from the count but is the formula of the blessing emes ve-yatziv.

(ב) There is no prohibition. But he does not say emes alone with the shaliach tzibbur, for it is like Shema Shema [Maggid Mishneh]. And he wrote in Knesset HaGedolah that one does not silence him—and specifically if he waited in the meantime.

(ג) In the fifteen vavs. And at Ma'ariv one has kavanah in the vav of emunah (written) and in Yeshu'ot Ya'akov he wrote that emes alone ascends in place of the three names mentioned above.

(ד) He says El Melekh Ne'eman. And in Mateh Moshe he wrote that Rashal did not want to interrupt; and in Beit Yosef it implies that it is an ancient custom—and likewise Bach.
