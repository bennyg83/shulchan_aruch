Levi Chaviv, siman 73; Maharam Alashkar, siman 15; Rashach, siman 33; Ra'em, part 1, siman 1, and see there, siman 2. In Sefer Asarah Maamaros, Ma'amar Chikur Din, part 1, chapter 13, he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur does not say ‘emes’ quietly, and an individual says ‘emes’ quietly and does not go back and say ‘emes,’ and it comes out exactly 248 words. But if he says ‘emes’ quietly and then goes back and says ‘emes,’ it is 249 words; end quote. With this, the simple meaning of the Talmud is resolved, where it says that he does not go back and say ‘emes,’ while the Rav Beit Yosef was pressed in this; see there. But Maharam Alashkar wrote that one should say ‘emes’ the first time as well, so as not to separate them, and the second ‘emes’ is not part of the count, but rather is the formula of the blessing ‘Emes VeYatziv.’
There is no prohibition. But he should not say ‘emes’ alone with the shaliach tzibbur, for that is like saying ‘Shema, Shema.’ Darkei Moshe. In Kenesses HaGedolah he wrote that we do not silence him, and specifically if he paused in between.
With fifteen vavs. At Maariv he should have intent with the vav in ‘ve'emunah.’ Writings. In Yad Moshe he wrote that ‘emes’ alone takes the place of the three aforementioned Names.
