And so. "They place their hands," see Taz. The reference mark belongs on the first occurrence of "and so he wrote." This is impossible, for what proof is there from an individual to a congregation? It appears that it belongs on the second occurrence of "and this is the custom," which concerns a congregation answering Amen. This disagrees with the Mechaber in siman 59, seif 4. He did not record the dispute there, but since there are differing opinions, one may say that only those accustomed to interrupt may answer Amen. They hold that Krias Shema contains 244 words, because "forever and ever" counts as two words even though the two form one concept, meaning eternally, as Rashi explains in the section Beshalach. See what I wrote below. They therefore complete four words by saying "Amen, El Melech Ne'eman," and they intend in Amen the initial letters meaning "God, faithful King," so that it counts both as the response to the blessing and toward completing the word count. This differs from the present custom, which relies upon the Zohar that only the three words "Hashem your God, true" are added, since Krias Shema itself contains 245 words. Accordingly, they should not answer Amen. Nevertheless, one may say that an individual says "true" and hears three words from the chazzan, totaling four words. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 1. Yet the Rama's statement that in a congregation one says Amen, while an individual says El Melech Ne'eman, can in any event be explained as ensuring compliance with the opinion that there are 244 words. See what I wrote in siman 51, Magen Avraham, seif katan 3. In siman 66 I will explain further, with Hashem's help, that even according to the Rama here the matter is not certain. Krias Shema is also different, because one does not recite a blessing saying that He commanded us concerning Krias Shema, and therefore there is less concern for an interruption. By contrast, when one recites a blessing over fruit and finishes the blessing, and then his fellow finishes, the Rama agrees that he should not answer Amen. The Rama therefore wrote this ruling here and not in siman 59, as stated, but merely referred from there to here. Understand this. After the blessing Yotzer HaMe'oros, however, one answers Amen if he is himself at the conclusion of the blessing, but not if he is in its middle. See siman 51, Magen Avraham, seif katan 3, and siman 66, seif 3 and Magen Avraham, seif katan 5, regarding this.
