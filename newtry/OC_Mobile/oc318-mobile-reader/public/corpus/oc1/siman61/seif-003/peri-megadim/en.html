And so Taz gives a citation on "and likewise the first"—there is no proof from individual to congregation; and it appears on "and so is the practice, the second in congregation—amen"—and disagrees with the Mechaber, siman 59, seif 4, and did not write the dispute there; but since there are opinions, one may say one should not interrupt with amen—only those who practice interrupting, who hold Keriat Shema has 248 words, as Bach wrote "forever and ever" two words that are one explanation, worldliness, as Rashi in parashat Beshalach (as written below); and they complete four words amen el melekh ne'eman and intend in amen also initials, and it amounts to blessing and completion—whereas now they rely on the Zohar that only the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet—for there are 245 words; therefore they do not say amen. However one may say a lone person says emet and hears three words from the shaliach tzibbur—behold four words; Magen Avraham note 1. However what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote—in congregation amen, and alone it cannot be fixed—nevertheless one should say el melekh ne'eman to express the view that there are 245. And as written in siman 51, Magen Avraham 3, and siman 66 I will explain further that even for Rabbeinu Yonah here this is not clear; also that Keriat Shema is unlike [other blessings] in that we do not say "amen, blessed be He..." upon Keriat Shema, and therefore no concern for interruption—unlike when one blessed on fruit and finished the blessing and another affirms, Rabbeinu Yonah holds one may not answer amen; and therefore Rabbeinu Yonah wrote here this is not in siman 59 as said, only showed it here, understand. But after Yotzer HaMe'orot they say amen if he stands at the conclusion of the blessing, not when standing in the middle—matter in siman 51, Magen Avraham 3, and siman 66 seif 3, and Magen Avraham note 5 on this.
