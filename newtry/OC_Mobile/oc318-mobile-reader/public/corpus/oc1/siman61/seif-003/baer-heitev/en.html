Hashem Elokeichem emes. In a place where they did not practice repeating it, we do not protest against them. In Sefer Asarah Maamaros he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur does not say ‘emes’ quietly; an individual says ‘emes’ quietly and does not repeat and say ‘emes.’ But Maharam Alashkar wrote that one should say ‘emes’ the first time as well, so as not to separate them, and the second ‘emes’ is not part of the count. So too wrote the Radbaz, part 1, siman 55, and the Ra'em, part 1, siman 2. See Peri Chadash and Magen Avraham; and see Responsa Nachalas Shivah, part 2, siman 26, who wrote that one should rebuke the shaliach tzibbur when he repeats ‘Hashem Elokeichem emes,’ that he should not also repeat the word ‘Ani,’ see there; and see Yad Aharon.
With a word. But he does not say ‘emes’ alone together with the shaliach tzibbur, for this is like saying ‘Shema, Shema.’ Darkei Moshe; and see Kenesses HaGedolah.
With fifteen vavs. At Maariv he should have in mind that ‘emes’ takes the place of the three Names mentioned above. Yad Moshe, Magen Avraham.
Shema. In Mateh Moshe he wrote that the Rashal did not want to interrupt; but from the Beit Yosef it implies that this is an ancient custom, and so wrote the Bach. See Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah.
