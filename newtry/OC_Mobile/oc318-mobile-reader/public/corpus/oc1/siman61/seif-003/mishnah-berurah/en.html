(6) To complete 248—as stated in the hidden midrash: R' Yehuda opened and said "Let them be healing to your navel and refreshment to your bones"—the Torah is healing for body and bones in this world and the next. For R' Nehorai said in the name of R' Nehemiah: In Keriat Shema there are 248 words corresponding to a person's limbs, and one who recites Keriat Shema properly—each limb takes one word and is healed through it, and "Let them be healing to your navel," etc. But in Keriat Shema there are only 245 words, etc. What is the fix? They fixed that the shaliach tzibbur repeats three words—which are "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet"—to complete 248 words for the congregation, etc. And anyone who says Keriat Shema not with the congregation does not complete his limbs, because he lacks the three words that the shaliach tzibbur repeats. What is his fix? He should intend the fifteen vavins in emet veyatziv; and with all this, if his father was crooked he cannot straighten, and a deficiency he cannot count—those three words of Keriat Shema that the shaliach tzibbur repeats he cannot count them to complete 248 like the rest of the congregation, etc., in brief [Beit Yosef].

(7) The shaliach tzibbur concludes—and the individual quietly also concludes with the word emet, but afterward he does not repeat saying emet; rather he begins veyatziv venakhon, etc., as below in siman 66 seif 10 in the Hagahah.

(8) "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet"—and in Sefer Asarah Ma'amarot he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur should not conclude quietly with the word emet except when he repeats and says aloud; thereby the count is 248, otherwise it would be 249. But the custom is as the Shulchan Aruch that one should say emet also the first time so as not to separate them; and the second emet is not counted—only the berakhah emet veyatziv counts as such—so writes Peri Megadim; and the Gra wrote that the essential ruling is as Asarah Ma'amarot.

(9) And he repeats and says—and in a place where they did not practice to repeat, they rebuke them.

(10) "the Omnipresent Elokeikhem emet"—and he should not repeat the word Ani either.

(11) "Bidvar" (regarding this matter)—but he does not say emet alone with the shaliach tzibbur, for it is like Shema Shema; nevertheless they do not silence him if he paused in the meantime.

(12) In the fifteen vavins—and in the evening he should intend that emet equals the place of the three Names mentioned above.

(13) Behold 91—like the numerical value of amen and like... [Darkei Moshe].

(14) He should say El melekh ne'eman—see Magen Avraham and Bach that this is a holy custom [Peri Megadim].

(15) He should say amen—nevertheless one should not learn from this regarding if one blessed on fruit or finished hamotzi and has not yet eaten and meanwhile his friend finished that berakhah whether to answer amen after him—see Pri Megadim. And this is also proven above in the Gra's explanation in siman 59 ot 12—see there that the reason for this Hagahah is because berakhot were not instituted specifically for Keriat Shema.

(16) And so is the custom—see Pri Megadim and see what we wrote above that lekhatchilah it is better that he conclude together with the shaliach tzibbur and not need to answer amen after him; see there.
