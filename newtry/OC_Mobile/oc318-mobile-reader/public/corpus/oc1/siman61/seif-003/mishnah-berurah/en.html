To complete 248. As stated in Midrash Hane'elam: Rabbi Yehudah opened and said, 'It shall be healing for your navel and marrow for your bones.' The Torah is healing for the body and bones in this world and the next. Rabbi Nehorai said in the name of Rabbi Nechemiah: in Shema there are 248 words, corresponding to a person's limbs; one who reads Shema properly, each limb takes one word and is healed through it. This is 'healing for your navel,' etc. But there are only 245 words in Shema. What is the remedy? They enacted that the shaliach tzibbur should repeat three words, namely 'Hashem Elokeichem emes,' to complete 248 words for the congregation. Whoever says Shema not with the congregation does not complete his limbs, because he lacks the three words that the shaliach tzibbur repeats. What is his remedy? He should intend with the fifteen vavs of 've'yatziv,' which amount to ninety, corresponding to the three Names.
The shaliach tzibbur concludes. The individual also concludes quietly with the word 'emes,' but afterward he does not repeat 'emes'; rather, he begins 've'yatziv venachon,' etc., as below in siman 66, seif 10, in the gloss.
Hashem Elokeichem emes. In Sefer Asarah Ma'amaros he wrote that the shaliach tzibbur should not conclude quietly with the word 'emes,' but only when he repeats it aloud; in this way the count is 248, for otherwise it would be 249. However, the custom follows the Shulchan Aruch, that it is said the first time as well, so as not to separate them; the second 'emes' is not part of the count, but belongs to the blessing 'Emes VeYatziv.' So wrote the Peri Megadim. The Gra wrote that the primary view is like the Asarah Ma'amaros.
