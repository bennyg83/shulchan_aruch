(17) One should teach them, etc. But Bach wrote that no sage or great one may abolish the custom in places where they practiced thus—specifically when an individual says it before the public it is forbidden; but when the entire public says thus to arouse kavanah it is permitted, so far. And it is difficult—for the Talmud challenges in Sukkah page 45 on the mishnah that says "we are to Him and to Him our eyes"—"but behold one who says Shema Shema," etc., and there in public they said—from where do we know, Levush. And one must say it is different there because they only do it for song and praise in general and not to arouse kavanah. Again Sefer Magen Avraham was printed and Bach rejected entirely and wrote Bach omitted the Talmud mentioned above and it is not so. It also seems to my humble opinion a bit of proof for Bach's words from Sukkah page 45 that says they say "we thank You and to You we praise"—and apparently it is difficult, for behold it is like saying Shema Shema; rather it is different in public, and since Rashi explained there this language "we thank" is not language of confession but language of thanks that we admit to Him and are not deniers, so far—it is possible it is like accepting the yoke of the kingdom of heaven. It further seems to me to resolve: since he interrupts with "we praise You" it is not like saying Shema Shema one after another; and in this manner Shelah and Nachalat Tzvi wrote that those accustomed to say Shema, etc., when the shaliach tzibur says vaya'avor are not included in this because there is no immediacy in their statement and selichah always interrupts, so far. And I saw in Sefer Ta'avah LeEinaim that he resolves: for this Rashi enacted in what he wrote "we thank" is not language of confession, etc.—meaning in "modehim" there are two explanations, either language of confession or language of admitting that they are not deniers; and if so once language of confession and the second language of admitting; and since each is a separate matter there is no concern for doubling, so far. And one may wonder: perforce also in the first it is not language of confession, for if so the Talmud's difficulty stands in its place that he associates the Name of Heaven with an altar; and it seems to me for this Rashi enacted and explained it is not language of confession. And further one may wonder on him in this matter except that the intent of this book is on a halakhic ruling. And regarding that they say Shema Yisrael when the shaliach tzibur says vaya'avor—Shelah page 213 settles it and it is a good custom in my eyes, for one is in gematria thirteen and it is fitting to mention in the thirteen attributes. But I saw in Lechem Chamudot page 41—it seems to me it is included in those we silence, and at minimum it is disgraceful and fitting to abolish it, so far. And I already wrote Nachalat Tzvi wrote like Shelah, and one may wonder he did not mention the words of Lechem Chamudot; and in any case it seems one should not protest at all those who do so. It also seems the first time certainly every person should practice saying it. And regarding that they say "Barukh shem kevod malkhuto le'olam va'ed" three times on Yom Kippur—Taz wrote: since it refers to Shema Yisrael that he said once, there is no concern for error.
