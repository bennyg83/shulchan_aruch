21) [Se'if 5] The custom is to place one's hand over one's face while reciting the first verse, etc. Shaar HaKavanos, discourse 5 on Krias Shema, page 21c, states: 'Before saying Shema Yisrael, close both eyes with your right hand, etc. With your left hand hold the four tzitzis and place them over your heart, as explained above. Before reciting Krias Shema, one must also intend to fulfill the positive commandment to recite Krias Shema twice each day.' Pri Etz Chaim, Shaar Krias Shema, chapter 6; Eliyah Rabbah 7.
22) There: The custom is to place one's hand over one's face, etc. It is obvious that although one closes his eyes and recites from memory, there is no prohibition against reciting written Torah orally. Maran himself writes above in siman 49 that any passage familiar and fluent in everyone's mouth, such as Krias Shema, may be recited from memory. Pischei HaDevir 3; see there.
23) There: So that he not look at something else that prevents him from concentrating. Anything a person does to arouse concentration is beneficial. Tur. Zera Kodesh, cited in Derech Yesharah, page 20a, writes that through the unification of Krias Shema one repairs the blemish caused by the sin of emitting seed in vain, which separated those who were joined; see there. Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi 14:42 cites this. How greatly, then, must a person exert all his strength to concentrate upon the unification of Krias Shema and clear his heart of every kind of thought, so that this may assist him in repairing the blemish.
