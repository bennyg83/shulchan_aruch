(11) There—the shaliach tzibbur concludes "the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent" emes, etc. And Peri Chadash wrote siman 3: it is fitting to teach all cantors that when they reach the end of Keriat Shema they should say these three words in a whisper; and thus one may learn merit for the custom; and whoever does not do so dismisses the words of Chazal, masters of the Talmud—he is worthy of rebuke, and whoever listens to me will dwell securely—end quote. But R' Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef wrote and thus: we have only the words of our holy master, who wrote that one repeats "the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent" emes, as the gaon R' Eliyahu Mizrachi and Maharam Alashkar and Maharshach wrote; and this is not against the Talmud, for our author, may his memory be a blessing, already settled the words of the Talmud that they should not contradict what is said in the Zohar and Tikkunim—end quote. And likewise Shitah Mekubbetzet 99a. And see what I wrote above siman 25, siman 75—that every matter not mentioned explicitly in the words of the Talmud, the words of Kabbalah decide—see there. If so, here too—since it is not mentioned explicitly in the Talmud to forbid repeating these three words "the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent" emes, and as Beit Yosef wrote—the words of the Zohar and Kabbalah prove that they said one must repeat these three words; if so, it does not matter even if the shaliach tzibbur concludes "the the Omnipresent your the Omnipresent" emes in a loud voice and returns to say it in a loud voice. And see in the words that follow.
