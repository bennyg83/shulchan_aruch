(16) And if he reads alone he should have kavanah in the fifteen vavs, etc. If the individual is still reading Keriat Shema and the shaliach tzibbur has already finished, the individual may be silent and intend to hear "the Lord your God" from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur—and it helps him even if afterward he completes Keriat Shema. The rabbi Mahari Ayash in Mateh Yehudah vol. 2, Likutei Ach, page 60 side 3; and they brought from the author in Kuntras Orach Chayyim, siman 77 note 2. And likewise Ma'amar Mordechai note 2 in the name of Sedei Chemed. Shitah Mekubbetzet 99b. Zekher LeChaim vol. 3 siman 100 note 135. Sha'arei Teshuvah note 2. Kaf HaChayyim there note 50. However, according to what the rabbi, may his memory be a blessing, wrote in Sha'ar HaKavanot at the end of derashah 5 and derashah 8 of Keriat Shema and derashah 9 of the derashot of the night, and in the siddur of Rashash—the first parshah of Keriat Shema from "and you shall love" has 42 words corresponding to the 42-letter name of Avag, etc., and corresponding to Chabad; and the second parshah until "and you shall bind them" has 72 words corresponding to the 72-letter name emerging from three verses Vayisa Vayavo Vayet—acronym straight of Vayisa and reverse of Vayavo and straight of Vayet—and it too corresponds to ChGaT; and from "and you shall bind them" until the end of the parshah are fifty words corresponding to the fifty gates of Binah—they are forty corresponding to ten Eheyeh that have forty letters, and Yud corresponding to the totality of ten Eheyeh, and they correspond to the lower two-thirds of Tiferet; and the parshah of tzitzit has 72 words corresponding to the 72-letter name from the acronym of three verses Vayisa Vayavo Vayet—all straight—and they correspond to NeHiY; and in the repetition of the three words "the Lord your God" emes to draw from the supernal parshiyot of NeHiY of Ze'ir Anpin and from the brains within them into the NeHiY of Ze'ir Anpin to make Chabad of Malchut—as written there. And it is known to those who have kavanah—if so, this kavanah does not come except when he says in order—that is, until he completes all three parshiyot until "the Lord your God," and afterward he returns to say "the Lord your God" emes; and if not, he reverses and mixes the order made above, God forbid. If so, there is no remedy except either he hears from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur and intends to fulfill if he is equal with him, or he himself repeats to say them; but to hear them not in their place and intend to fulfill does not work per the kabbalists; and also to intend in the fifteen vavs and to fulfill through repetition of the three words "the Lord your God" emes does not work per the kabbalists, for he mixes the order—except either he hears from the mouth of the shaliach tzibbur or he himself says "the Lord your God" emes as mentioned; and all the more if he says in their place El Melekh Ne'eman, for he mixes—for they have no place here, as is proven from the words of the rabbi, may his memory be a blessing, and as I wrote below siman 19. And although we are not commanded in kavanot, we already wrote in many places that one must make the order per the order belonging to the kavanah; and in any case we already wrote above that the individual may repeat "the Lord your God"—and to the Maggid Mishneh. And also what Ma'amar Mordechai wrote there—that if he did not finish Keriat Shema with the shaliach tzibbur exactly, but when the shaliach tzibbur repeats these three words he reached there—he should be silent and withdraw from the shaliach tzibbur and afterward he finishes his reading and it counts for him toward completion of 248—what difference whether the shaliach tzibbur says them first or he says them first—end quote—it does not appear so, for the repetition is a different kavanah as stated above; and how can it be before he finishes, behold he reverses; except if such happened he should not intend on the shaliach tzibbur but return to say himself as written. And see further what I wrote above siman 1 and siman 2.
