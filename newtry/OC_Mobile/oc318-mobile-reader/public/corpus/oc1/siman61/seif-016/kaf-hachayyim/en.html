This benefits him even though he subsequently completes Krias Shema. Mahari Ayash, Matteh Yehudah 2, Likkutei Orach Chaim, page 60c, cited by Machazik Berachah in the Kuntres Acharon to this siman 2. Maamar Mordechai 2 writes likewise in Seder HaYom's name, as do Shalmei Tzibbur, page 99b; Zechor LeAvraham 3, entry kuf 135; Shaarei Teshuvah 2; and Kaf HaChaim by Rav Chaim Palagi, ibid. 50. According to the Arizal's words in Shaar HaKavanos, end of discourse 5 and discourses 8 on Krias Shema and 9 on the night, and in Rabbi Rashash's siddur, however, the first section from VeAhavta contains forty-two words corresponding to the forty-two-letter Name of alef-beis-gimel, etc., and to Chochmah, Binah, and Daas. The second section through Vesamtem contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from the initial letters of Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet - Vayisa in straight order, Vayavo in reverse, and Vayet in straight order - and to Chesed, Gevurah, and Tiferes. From Vesamtem to the end of the section are fifty words, corresponding to the fifty gates of Binah. Forty correspond to ten Names Ehyeh containing forty letters; ten correspond to the aggregate of the ten Names Ehyeh. They correspond to the lower two-thirds of Tiferes. The tzitzis section contains seventy-two words, corresponding to the seventy-two-letter Name derived from the initial letters of Vayisa, Vayavo, and Vayet, all in straight order, and to Netzach, Hod, and Yesod. Repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes draws influence from the upper joints of the Netzach, Hod, and Yesod of Zeir Anpin, and from the moach within them, into Zeir Anpin's Netzach, Hod, and Yesod, thereby forming the Chochmah, Binah, and Daas of Malchus; see there at length. Those familiar with the mystical wisdom know that this kavanah can occur only after one recites the passages in order - after completing all three sections through Hashem Elokeichem and then repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes. Otherwise he reverses and confuses the order effected above, Heaven forbid. The only remedy is therefore either to hear the shaliach tzibbur and intend to fulfill the obligation when he is at the same point, or to repeat the words himself. Hearing them out of their proper place with intent to fulfill cannot accord with the kabbalists. Nor, according to them, can concentration upon the fifteen vavs substitute for repeating Hashem Elokeichem emes, because it confuses the order. Rather, he must hear them from the shaliach tzibbur or say Hashem Elokeichem emes himself, as stated. All the more does saying El Melech Neeman instead confuse the order, for those words have no place here, as the Arizal's words demonstrate and as stated below in paragraph 19. Although we do not perform the kavanos, we have written in many places that one must follow the order appropriate to them. Independently, we already wrote above that an individual may repeat Hashem Elokeichem, and nothing is lost thereby. Maamar Mordechai further writes that if one did not finish Krias Shema at precisely the same time as the shaliach tzibbur but reaches that point while the shaliach tzibbur repeats these three words, he should remain silent and hear them from the shaliach tzibbur, then complete his reading. They count toward his 248 words, for what difference does it make whether the shaliach tzibbur says them first or he says them first? This appears incorrect, for the repetition has a separate kavanah, as explained above. How can it precede his completion? He reverses the order. If this occurs, he should not intend to fulfill through the shaliach tzibbur but repeat the words himself, as stated. See also paragraphs 1 and 2 above.
