(8) [Seif 2] "Which I command you this day"—meaning to say that every day they should be as new in your eyes, etc. Thus wrote Maharach, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, in Sha'ar Ma'amarei Razal in his commentary on Pirkei Avot. Behold, the Sages established three times for prayer—Shacharit, Minchah, and Ma'ariv—and even though they arranged the order of prayer and Keriat Shema, there is a difference among them as each time requires, for each one has its renewal and is not similar to its fellow; therefore one should not pray in a merely fixed routine manner, but rather pray with all his kavanah as one praying a new prayer and a new Keriat Shema—for in every prayer and prayer entirely new minds are renewed, for you have no prayer in which abundant light of influence does not renew; but this sight is not like that sight, etc., and there is a great difference between weekday prayer and Shabbat prayer, and between Rosh Chodesh prayer and Yom Tov prayer and Chol HaMoed prayer; and not only this, but even the Yom Tov prayers themselves—such as Sukkot prayer versus Pesach prayer and the like. Also, in every single day itself, Shacharit prayer is not similar to Minchah and Ma'ariv prayer and the like. Also, even on weekdays themselves, this day's prayer is not similar to the prayer of the day after it; this sight is not like that sight, and you have no prayer and prayer from the day the world was created until the future that resembles its fellow at all in essence. The secret of the matter, in brief: behold, prayers are in their aspect for clarifying clarifications of the seven kings, and in every day and in every prayer and prayer they are renewed and changed and are not the clarifications themselves—for in every prayer and prayer sparks rise and clarifications that had not yet risen in that time rise; and in the order of the value of those sparks that rise, so too corresponding to them is the order of the supernal union, suckling, minds, and their drawing in the order of the value of the clarifications—and one cannot elaborate more on this. And this is the secret of what we say every day and every hour the prayer, and one prayer in a day is not enough; the reason is that in every prayer and prayer something is made new that was not made in another prayer, and this suffices. And likewise the matter with every Keriat Shema, for every Keriat Shema differs from its fellow. However, all this is in the secret of particulars; but in the secret of generals, all Shacharit Keriat Shema of a weekday have one kavanah for them, and all Ma'ariv Keriat Shema of a weekday have one kavanah for them, and so on—end quote. And likewise in Sha'ar HaKavanot in the derashah on the change of prayers, page 59—see there; and see in Tikkunim tikkun 56 and Sha'ar HaKavanot in the derashah on the difference between Shabbat and Yom Tov, page 77 side 1; and what Rashash, may the memory of the righteous be a blessing, wrote in Nahar Shalom page 19 side 3 on the matter of the Nefesh HaChayim—examine closely.
