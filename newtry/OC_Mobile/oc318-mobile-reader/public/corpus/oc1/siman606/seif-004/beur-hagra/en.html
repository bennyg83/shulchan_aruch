Se'if 4. He may, etc. This excludes Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who wrote in the seventh hour before Minchah. This is not so, for the main reason is because of Yom Kippur.
To immerse. As written in Midrash Tanchuma, parashas Va'eschanan: you have one nation on earth like the ministering angels; just as the ministering angels are clean, etc. See the Rosh.
And to receive lashes. See above siman 607.
And he does not, etc. According to chapter 3 of Berachos, and so wrote Tosafos there, 22b, end of the comment beginning "and the law is not."
And he need not, etc. Because the reason for immersion is according to the midrash mentioned above, that Israel are clean like the ministering angels, as the Rosh wrote above. The main meaning of clean is clean of all sin, as written in Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer and brought in the Rosh there. Nevertheless, we imitate the ministering angels as much as possible. See the end of siman 610.
Because of a seminal emission. Not because of Rabbi Yitzchak, for this is impossible nowadays. See the beginning of chapter 4 of Bechoros.
And the same applies that pouring, etc. See Rashi, Berachos 22b, comment beginning "and the law is not, etc.," and Magen Avraham subparagraph 9. "And if he entered, etc." is a question at the beginning of chapter 2 of Gittin, and we rule leniently. What he wrote, "and he may, etc.," is from the end of chapter 3 of Taharos. What he wrote, "and R. Yonah wrote, etc.," is from the Mishnah mentioned above. What he wrote there, "forty se'ah, etc.," is in chapter 3 of Berachos according to Rabbi Yehudah and according to one opinion, and the Mishnah in chapter 8 of Taharos implies like that opinion.
One whose, etc. Chapter 3 of Moed Katan.
Although, etc. As written in Yoreh De'ah siman 381, se'if 1 in the gloss, and above siman 548, se'if 10 in the gloss.
Immersion, etc. See Yoreh De'ah there, se'if 5 in the gloss, that even in a place where it is forbidden by law, they said it is forbidden only with washing for pleasure, and they said: all those obligated in immersions, etc.
