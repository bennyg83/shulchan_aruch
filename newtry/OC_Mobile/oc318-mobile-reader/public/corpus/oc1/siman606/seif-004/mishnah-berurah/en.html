(17) He may immerse - The Mechaber was brief, for he should first have written that the custom is to immerse and receive lashes on the eve of Yom Kippur, and even youths and maidens immerse once they are obligated in mitzvos.
(18) Before night - It is good that he immerse before the Minchah prayer, where he confesses, and so is the custom. Some customarily immerse after the final meal so it will be close to Yom Kippur.
(19) And he does not recite a blessing - For this immersion is only a custom.
(20) Without confession - To exclude those accustomed to confess at the time of immersion.
(21) Because of a seminal emission - Therefore once is sufficient, and he also needs no confession. A woman who had relations within three days must clean herself with hot water before immersion, so that she not emit semen and return to the impurity of a seminal emission, unless it is close to her immersion or close to her period, for then women are commonly impregnated, and if she cleans herself there is concern for destruction of seed. So wrote the Magen Avraham. In Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger he questions this and leans toward saying that she should never clean herself. Some say the reason for immersion is repentance; according to this, one should immerse three times, and so wrote the Rokeach. See the Magen Avraham, that even according to the one who holds the reason for immersion is a seminal emission, nevertheless one must always immerse on the eve of Yom Kippur, even if he already immersed on the eve of Rosh Hashanah and did not see a seminal emission afterward.
(22) Also helps - If he is pained by immersion (Mahari Weil). For the details of the laws of nine kavim, see above in siman 88 in the Mishnah Berurah.
(23) Between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur - The same applies even on the eve of Yom Kippur.
(24) It is permitted to wash - Meaning even with hot water. Immersion in the mikveh is also permitted even if it was hot.
(25) For Yom Kippur cancels seven - Meaning that its law is like any other festival that encountered mourning, which cancels the seven. Because of this he is permitted to wash after Minchah close to dark, according to the permissive opinion above in siman 548, se'if 10. See the Magen Avraham, that in our matter too one should be lenient only an hour or two before night, and not earlier.
(26) Although the custom is, etc. - Meaning that although the decree of seven was cancelled by Yom Kippur, we might have forbidden washing and immersion because of the decree of thirty. To this he answered that this immersion is a mitzvah, and because of this washing is automatically permitted too, so there should be no interposition for the immersion.
(27) Immersion for a mitzvah is permitted - Although it is not obligatory, as above, nevertheless the prohibition of washing throughout thirty is also only a custom.
