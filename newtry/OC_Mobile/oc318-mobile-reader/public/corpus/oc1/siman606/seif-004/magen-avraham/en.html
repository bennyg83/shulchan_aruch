Because of a seminal emission. One who says confession is following a foolish custom (Mahari Weil). According to this, a woman who had relations within three days needs to clean herself with hot water so that she not emit semen. This is specifically when it was not close to her immersion or close to her period, for at those times women are commonly impregnated, and there is concern she will destroy seed of conception. Some say the reason for immersion is repentance, for even youths and maidens who are obligated in mitzvos immerse, and even one who immersed on the eve of Rosh Hashanah and did not see a seminal emission should immerse again. According to this, one should immerse three times; so wrote the Rokeach. One should immerse before Minchah, and so Maharil practiced. Some customarily immerse after the final meal so it will be close to Yom Kippur. All this is in Maharil; see what I wrote at the end of siman 607. See the Shelah. It seems to me that even one who holds the reason for immersion is a seminal emission should nevertheless immerse again if he immersed on the eve of Rosh Hashanah, for perhaps he saw separated or cloudy water; see the Mishnah, Mikvaos chapter 8.
Also helps. If he is pained by immersion (Mahari Weil). If half of him entered the mikveh and nine kavim were poured over the other half, he fulfilled the requirement. He may rely on two or three vessels, provided the second begins before the first stops, but four vessels do not combine (Mishnah in Mikvaos and Beis Yosef siman 88). R. Yonah wrote in chapter Mi Shemeiso that even if he empties from one vessel, it must be in a way that it can be emptied without interruption, excluding a vessel that absorbs somewhat or is very heavy. So it appears to me to explain his words, unlike the Beis Yosef there. One may immerse in forty se'ah of drawn water that is in the ground, but not in a vessel (Beis Yosef there and siman 159 in the name of the Raavad).
And to immerse on the eve of Yom Kippur. This is difficult, for on other festivals too it is permitted. Perhaps he holds that in practice one should permit only close to dark; and since on the eve of Yom Kippur it is impossible to be exact, they permitted washing while the day is still long, even before Minchah (Bach). But the Rema resolved this difficulty by writing "although they customarily do not wash all thirty," meaning that even on a festival it is forbidden, as written in siman 548, se'if 10 in the gloss; immersion for a mitzvah is permitted. See what I wrote there. It seems to me that even according to the Bach, it is permitted only an hour or two before night, but not before then, and so one should practice, for even aside from this there are those who customarily immerse after the meal, as above. If so, regarding a mourner one should be stringent. See the Beis Yosef. What the Levush wrote, that it is permitted to immerse on the eve of Yom Kippur until midday, is a textual error and should read after midday. Nevertheless, one should not be so lenient, for there are those who forbid until night, as written in siman 548, and all the more so according to us. Since he can fulfill it afterward, why should he enter into prohibition? It is obvious that the other laws of mourning, such as sitting on the ground, apply until dark, as written in siman 548, se'if 10, for the eve of Yom Kippur is not a day of joy and is no better than Purim, regarding which some say mourning applies. Do not err from the language of Maharil, who wrote in the laws of mourning that that day cancels the decrees of seven, for there he refers to Yom Kippur itself. Even regarding Yom Kippur there is a dispute in the Gemara, but the eve of Yom Kippur is certainly not called a festival at all, and by law eulogy is permitted on it. See siman 131, se'if 6. It has no special status except that it is a mitzvah to eat on it.
