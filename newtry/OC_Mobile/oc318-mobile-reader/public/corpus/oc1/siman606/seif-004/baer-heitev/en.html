Once. Nowadays the custom is to immerse three times and to confess in the mikveh according to the kabbalah of the Ari, of blessed memory. The custom is also to immerse before the Minchah prayer; Acharonim. There are pious people who immerse thirty-nine times; see Shelah.
Kavim. If he is pained by immersion. The measure is fifteen Polish quarts; Taz. One must pour it over him with a vessel from which it can be emptied without interruption. He may pour over himself from two or three vessels, provided the second begins before the first stops, but not from four vessels. One may immerse in forty se'ah of drawn water that is in the ground, but not in a vessel. If half of him entered the mikveh and nine kavim were poured over the other half, he fulfilled the requirement; see Magen Avraham. The Shelah wrote to cut the nails as a woman does for her husband. The Magen Avraham wrote that according to the implication of the Rema, one who has not seen a seminal emission since the eve of Rosh Hashanah need not immerse. Nevertheless, a healthy person should be stringent upon himself and immerse because of repentance, and also perhaps he saw separated or cloudy water. A woman who had relations within three days needs to clean herself with hot water so that she not emit semen. This is specifically when it was not close to her immersion or close to her period, for at those times women are commonly impregnated, and there is concern that she would destroy seed of conception. See Magen Avraham there.
To wash. Meaning an hour or two before night, and not earlier, for even aside from this there are those who customarily immerse after the final meal so that it be close to Yom Kippur. If so, regarding a mourner one should be stringent. All other laws of mourning apply until night; see Magen Avraham.
