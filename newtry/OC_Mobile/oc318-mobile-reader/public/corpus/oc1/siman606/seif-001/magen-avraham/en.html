At the time of forgiveness he must specify the sin to his fellow (Bach). It seems to me that if his fellow would be embarrassed by this, he should not specify it. He should return and go. He should appease him each time with a different kind of appeasement (Bach).
He need not attend to him. If he wants to appease him more, he may, if there is no disgrace to Torah (Bach).
He is his teacher. Even if he is not his primary teacher, but he heard words of Torah from him (Me'il Tzedakah, part 2, siman 49).
Unless he intends - The same applies if he intends for his benefit, as stated in the Gemara, that Rabbi Chanina did not want to forgive Rav because he saw in a dream that Rav would become head of the yeshivah, and he feared that they would remove him. Therefore he did not want to forgive him, so that Rav would go elsewhere, to Bavel, and become head of the yeshivah there (Bach).
He need not forgive him. Because some hear the slander but do not hear the appeasement, and this man does not leave suspicion. Nevertheless, it is a trait of humility to forgive (Yam shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 8, siman 63, in the name of Terumas ha-Deshen, siman 212).
