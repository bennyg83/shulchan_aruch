(1) He must appease him - For even with this he transgressed the prohibition of verbal wronging. Although during the rest of the year one is also obligated to appease one against whom he sinned, nevertheless, if he has no time he can wait to appease him on another day. But on the eve of Yom Kippur he is obligated to repair everything so that he be purified from all his sins, as it is written, "for on this day He shall atone for you from all your sins, etc." All the more so if he has robbery, fraud, or anything involving money in his hand, he should see to repair it. This is the great accuser against a person, as the Sages said: a se'ah full of sins, who accuses? Robbery accuses first. If his fellow has money in his possession over which he has a claim, he should inform him, even though his fellow knows nothing of it. In any event he should set out the matter fully and truthfully before the rabbi and halachic decisor, without falsehood, and ask how to conduct himself. The general rule: in every monetary matter he should not rely on his own ruling, for the evil inclination has many permissions (Chayei Adam).
(2) To appease him - It is proper that he go to him himself, and not first send an intermediary to see whether he is willing to accept appeasement. But if it is difficult for him to go himself first, or he knows the appeasement is more likely through an intermediary who will mediate between them, he may do it through an intermediary.
(3) He should return and go - He should appease him each time with a different kind of appeasement. At the time of asking forgiveness, he must specify what he sinned against his fellow, unless he knows his fellow would be embarrassed if he specified the sin; then he should not specify it. One who asks forgiveness from many people generally has not fulfilled this if he knows he did something specific to an individual.
(4) And if he is not appeased after three times - Meaning in this manner, with an increase of people.
(5) He need not attend to him - If he wants to appease him more, he may, if there is no disgrace to Torah.
(6) Before ten - In order to publicize that the prevention is not from his side.
(7) His teacher - Even if he is not his primary teacher, but he heard words of Torah from him.
(8) He should not be cruel by withholding forgiveness - For anyone who yields on his measures, Heaven yields for him on all his transgressions. If he does not want to forgive, they will also not forgive him.
(9) For the benefit of the requester, etc. - So that his uncircumcised heart be subdued and he not become accustomed to this. Nevertheless, it appears that in his heart he must remove hatred from him, since in truth he asked forgiveness from him.
(10) Forgiveness - The same applies if he intends for his own benefit, because he fears damage will come to him if he forgives.
(11) He need not forgive him - For there are those who heard the suspicion and did not hear the appeasement, and the slander remains. Nevertheless, it is a trait of humility to forgive even in this.
