1) [Seif 1] Yom Kippur does not atone for transgressions between one person and another, etc. As we say at the end of tractate Yoma, 85b: "From all your sins before Hashem you shall be purified." Yom Kippur atones for transgressions between a person and Hashem; Yom Kippur does not atone for transgressions between one person and another until he appeases the other person.
2) Ibid.: Transgressions between one person and another, etc. Maharash Garmizan wrote that regarding a transgression between one person and another that also contains an element relating to Hashem, such as when he disgraced him verbally, thereby transgressing "You shall love your fellow as yourself," and similarly in comparable cases, as long as he has not appeased the other person, even the element between him and Hashem is not atoned for. Pri Chadash; Birkei Yosef, os 1.
3) There is one who wrote that when a person has not repented for the transgressions between himself and another person, even his transgressions between himself and Hashem are not atoned for, and he supported this from the Mishnah. I say that his Master is not pleased with this. Birkei Yosef, ibid. However, Yafeh LaLev, volume 5, os 1, wrote in the name of several poskim that as long as he has not appeased the other person, even the element between him and Hashem is not atoned for, see there. In my humility, I wrote in the sacred work Edus B'Yaakov, discourse 2 for Shabbos Teshuvah, that when he did not request forgiveness from the person against whom he sinned, there is reason to be concerned that the Attribute of Judgment will prosecute him on Yom Kippur, saying: This person is unconcerned about transgressions, for he did not request forgiveness from the other person; why, as it were, should You forgive him? See there.
4) One who had relations with another man's wife has committed a transgression with two elements. The first element is between himself and Hashem, for he committed a severe transgression, and repentance and Yom Kippur are effective for this. However, there is a second element, consisting of transgressions between one person and another, for he causes the man's wife to become forbidden to him. Repentance and Yom Kippur are not effective for this until he has appeased him. Noda B'Yehuda, first edition, Orach Chaim, siman 35; Ruach Chaim, os 1.
