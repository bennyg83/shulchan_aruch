[1] To go to him, etc. Meaning he should go on his own. The custom to first send an intermediary to see whether he will accept appeasement is a mistake; Mahari Levi Chaviv. He should say: I ask pardon, forgiveness, and atonement from Hashem, God of Israel, and afterward from so-and-so, that he forgive me (Yam shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma chapter 8, siman 49). A person must bring himself to the one against whom he sinned in order to ask forgiveness from him; Raavan. The kabbalists wrote: one who does not remove hatred on Yom Kippur, his prayer is not heard. One who asks forgiveness generally from many people has not fulfilled the requirement if he knows that he did something specific to an individual. One who has relations with an unmarried woman, if it is with her consent, is called a transgression between man and the Omnipresent; but if by force, or if he had relations with her when she was faint, it is called a transgression between man and his fellow; Maharik 129. One who asks forgiveness must specify at the time of forgiveness; Bach. If his fellow is embarrassed by this, he should not specify it; Magen Avraham. It is somewhat difficult, for the Gemara in Yoma 86 should have established the two verses as both speaking of sins between man and his fellow, except that there is a distinction whether he is embarrassed.
