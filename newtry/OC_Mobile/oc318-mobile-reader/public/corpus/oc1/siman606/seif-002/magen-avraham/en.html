If he died. If he said to someone, "mamzer," he also blemished the honor of his ancestors (Mahari Weil). In Yam shel Shlomo there, siman 46, he wrote that he need not, because he can say, "I intended you," unless he called him "mamzer son of a mamzer." So it also appears in Kiddushin 18 in Tosafos: one who calls his fellow a mamzer receives forty lashes because he suspected his fellow of violating the prohibition, "a mamzer shall not enter, etc." This is difficult: did he not suspect his father of relations punishable by kareis? Rather, perforce he meant only him.
At his grave. He must go there barefoot. If it is more than three parasangs away, he should send his agent (Yam shel Shlomo, siman 20). If he insulted him after death, he need not go to his grave, but asks forgiveness from him in the place where he shamed him (Kenesses ha-Gedolah).
