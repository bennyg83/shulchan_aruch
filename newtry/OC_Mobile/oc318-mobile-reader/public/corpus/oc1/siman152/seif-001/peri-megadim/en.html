Lest. See Taz. Bava Basra 3b gives two explanations: one authority is concerned for negligence, and the other for the absence of a place to pray. Each explanation has a stringency. According to the concern for negligence, it is forbidden even when there is another place to pray. Conversely, according to the concern for a place to pray, it is forbidden even where negligence is not a concern, such as when construction of the new synagogue has already begun and it will not be diverted to redeem captives. See Magen Avraham, siman 153, seif katan 32, concerning the reading in Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilchos Matanos Aniyim, halachah 11: “But if they built it, they must complete it.” It is not “and they completed it” with a vav, but “they must complete it” with a yud; this is the reading in Beit Yosef, siman 153. See Taz, Yoreh Deah 252, seif katan 2, who writes that Rambam's language as quoted by Beit Yosef implies that the entire structure must already have been completed. I do not understand this, because the reading is “they must complete it” with a yud. In some editions the reading is “and they completed it” with a vav. Accordingly, even after construction has begun, where negligence is no longer a concern and it would not be sold for redeeming captives, it is nevertheless forbidden if there is no place to pray. This explains Hagahos Ashri to Megillah 26b, who writes that taking stones from the old synagogue to build the new one is forbidden because there is no place to pray. Hagahos Ashri may also accept the concern for negligence, but in this case there is no such concern, because what has already been built of the new synagogue will not be sold to redeem captives, since “once they have built it, they must complete it.” Even if one argues that the stones and bricks might still be sold and the structure left unfinished, here they are taking stones from the old synagogue. Such stones would not be sold to redeem captives, since people prayed there for many years and a person does not sell his dwelling. Although it has now been demolished, perhaps a prohibition still applies in such a case, as we will explain, with Hashem's help, below in siman 153, seif 13, were it not for the reason that there is no place to pray. This is what Rama means in the gloss, “and it is forbidden to take stones from the old one,” as explained. Magen Avraham, seif katan 5, however, does not explain it this way; with Hashem's help, it will be clarified there. Regarding Taz's reference to Ran's reading, see Tosafos to Bava Basra 3b in the gloss concerning a summer synagogue. What Taz writes as Rama's view in siman 153 appears in seif 7: “All this does not discuss,” etc., where another synagogue exists. This appears difficult according to Beit Yosef: how could he understand the Gemara to mean that a village synagogue may be sold but may not be demolished, all the more so? Magen Avraham there, seif katan 16, resolves this by distinguishing a case where they have only one synagogue and first build another, from a case where they already have two, in which it is forbidden. He writes the same here in seif katan 1. See what I will write, with Hashem's help, concerning Magen Avraham there.
