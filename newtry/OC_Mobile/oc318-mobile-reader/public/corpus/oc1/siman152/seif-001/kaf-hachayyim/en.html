1) [Seif 1] One may not demolish a synagogue in order to build another synagogue, etc. For so it is stated in Bava Basra 3b: Rav Chisda said, a person should not demolish a synagogue until he has built another synagogue. Some say that the reason is the possibility of negligence, and some say that the reason is the need for a place to pray. What practical difference is there between them? There is a difference where another synagogue is available. According to the one who says that the reason is the need for a place to pray, there is no concern, since another synagogue is available. According to the one who says that the reason is the possibility of negligence, there remains a concern. The Rif similarly writes in Megillah, chapter Bnei HaIr, that the reason is the possibility of negligence. So too the Rambam writes in chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 12. The Rosh also rules there in Bava Basra according to the opinion that the reason is the possibility of negligence; see there. The Tur likewise writes that the reason is the possibility of negligence. Maran, of blessed memory, comments upon this in the Beit Yosef: although there is an opinion there that the reason is the need for a place to pray, Rabbeinu did not take that reason into account. The Rif and the Rambam likewise wrote only the reason stated by Rabbeinu, and Rabbeinu Yonah writes the same. It is also evident at the beginning of Bava Basra that we rule that the reason is the possibility of negligence, even if they have another synagogue in which to pray; see there. This also appears to be Maran's view here in the Shulchan Aruch. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1, similarly writes regarding Maran's words that according to this reason, even if they have another synagogue in which to pray, they may not demolish this synagogue because of the possibility of negligence. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1, writes the same. The Ran there, however, writes that whenever they have another established synagogue, all opinions permit it, since there is neither a concern about a place to pray nor a concern about negligence; see there. The Beit Yosef and Olas Tamid there cite him. The Levush also brings both views, that of the Ran and that of Maran, of blessed memory; see there. The Taz, paragraph 1, however, rules in accordance with the Ran; see there. This also appears to be the view of Yad Aharon in Hagahot Beit Yosef. R' Shlomo, paragraph 1, writes the same in the name of the Agudah, Tosafos, and Rabbeinu Yerucham. However, what Eliyah Rabbah writes there, that the Shulchan Aruch agrees with the Taz, does not appear correct from the words of the poskim cited above. This is also the view of Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, and the Beur HaGra writes the same. But Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1, and Nehar Shalom discuss the matter at length, reject the Taz's words, and agree with Maran, of blessed memory; see there. Mateh Yehudah writes there that even if a city has ten synagogues, none of them may be discontinued until another has been established in its place; see there. This also appears to be the view of the Peri Megadim in Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1, in accordance with Maran, of blessed memory. See the following paragraph.
