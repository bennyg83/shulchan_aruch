Magen Avraham, seif katan 5 Bach wrote. He holds that although Hagahos Ashri concludes this ruling by writing, “when there is no place to pray,” Hagahos Ashri nevertheless agrees that the halachah follows the opinion concerned for negligence. Since they are already in the middle of construction, having begun building the new synagogue, and wish to demolish the old one piece by piece and place each piece immediately into the new one, there is no concern for negligence. Therefore, Hagahos Ashri prohibits it only when there is no place to pray. This is how Bach explains Hagahos Ashri, and he therefore rules that where there is another place to pray, it is permitted. Magen Avraham objects: Why should we not be concerned for negligence in such a case? Even if there is no concern for negligence, there is still concern that an unavoidable mishap may occur. Rather, Hagahos Ashri must rule according to the opinion that the concern is the absence of a place to pray. Accordingly, since we rule according to the opinion concerned for negligence, it is forbidden even where there is another place to pray. These are also the words of Taz, seif katan 3.
