Lest something occur. Likewise, even if they have two synagogues, it is forbidden. This is implied by the Gemara.
And afterward they demolish. This is permitted even in large cities, since the entire public is satisfied when another synagogue is being built for them. See siman 153, seif 7, Masas Binyamin, siman 33, and what I wrote in seif katan 16. See also what I wrote in siman 153, seif 4.
The same law applies. They first build the new wall and afterward demolish the original one.
Quickly. The Gemara says that they brought their beds there so they would not delay rebuilding. This refers to bringing them near the synagogue. See siman 151, seif 11.
And it is forbidden to take. Bach writes that if the old synagogue stands in the middle of the new construction, so there is no concern for negligence, and they also have another place to pray, it is permitted. This requires study. From where did he derive this? Rashi explains that the Gemara's question concerned a case where they had begun building the new synagogue and wished to demolish the old one piece by piece in order to use the material immediately in the new one. Although there was no concern for negligence, the Gemara resolved that it is forbidden, because an unavoidable mishap might occur and both structures could remain ruined, as written at the beginning of the siman. If so, even where the old one stands in the middle, it is forbidden for this reason, and even if another place to pray exists. Moreover, why should there be no concern for negligence merely because it stands in the middle? If the reason is that otherwise they would have nowhere to pray, the same is true whenever they demolish it, yet it is nevertheless forbidden. Further, when the Gemara asks what practical difference exists between the two reasons, it should have answered that the difference is where the old synagogue stands in the middle of the new construction. Necessarily, concern for negligence exists in every case. The poskim therefore altered the Gemara's wording and wrote, “lest an unavoidable mishap occur.” Rama accordingly omitted the end of Hagahos Ashri's statement implying that where there is another place to pray it is permitted, because we rule according to the opinion concerned for negligence, namely lest an unavoidable mishap occur, and it is forbidden in every case.
And it is forbidden to demolish. This is like removing a stone from the Sanctuary. The prohibition against demolition applies specifically to something attached. Mahar"am Padua, siman 65.
