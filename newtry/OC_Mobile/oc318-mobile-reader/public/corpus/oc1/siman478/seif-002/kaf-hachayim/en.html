(ב) And the custom is not to eat anything, even fruit, after eating afikoman, so the taste of afikoman remain in one's mouth, as our ancestors practiced with the Pesach offering and called it afikoman — see the Mishna we learned: one does not conclude after the Pesach offering with afikoman. 'Maftirin' means opening the mouth for speech, from 'yafteru befisa.' Alternatively, language of taking leave of one's friend — when they take leave of the meal they do not conclude it to say afikoman. Afikoman is two words: afiku mani — meaning they do not open their mouths after eating Pesach to say afiku lanu minei metika, types of sweets like fruits to eat afterward. And one can say we call the matza itself afikoman simply, since it is in place of fruits that one customarily eats after a meal another time; and by the affection of the mitzvah that is dear to us like sweet fruits we call it afikoman — as if one says to his household or each to his friend: afiku lanu minei metika — and that is the matza sweet to us through its mitzvah. Levush. Maaseh Zion ot alef.
