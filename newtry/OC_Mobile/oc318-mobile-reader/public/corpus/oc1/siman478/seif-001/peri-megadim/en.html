After afikoman. Taz: afikoman means a commemoration of Pesach, and it is forbidden to eat in two places like Pesach which is forbidden; and also one may not eat even in one place other things after afikoman, like Pesach. And for Rashi and Rashbam, afikoman is matza of mitzvah, and at first they bless on it — Magen Avraham — because after one filled his stomach one does not bless; per them one does not eat other things after afikoman, but in two places one could say it is permitted per Rashi and Rashbam; see Pesachim 120a and siman 477, and see in Magen Avraham 3 I will explain that even per Rashi and Rashbam one may not eat afikoman in two places. And all poskim ruled there — even though it is d'rabbanan, the anonymous ruling is like them, and the dispute is per Mar Zutra; see Rif and Rosh Pesachim 120a, and see in 482 it will be explained further, see there. See Levush: since afikoman is in place of Pesach, one may not eat after it — it is somewhat difficult, for even per Rashi and Rashbam that it is obligatory matza, one may not eat after it. He further wrote: afikoman — afiku mani, meaning they brought out all types of sweetness (from the word man and the like); and 'ein maftirin' — they do not open their mouths (like 'yafteru befisa' — pours water) to say afikoman after Pesach and after matza, etc. Some explain 'ein maftirin' like one who takes leave of his friend and goes from him — and the explanation: they do not take leave and go from Pesach to say afikoman. Another explanation: for matza itself they call afikoman, since it is dear to us and sweet like man and types of sweetness, see there. And behold the explanation afiku mani is also true — that one does not eat in two places, and to say afiku kelim — take utensils from here and go to another place; see Pesachim 119b.
