1) [Se'if 1] After the afikoman, one may not eat, etc. The Mishnah teaches that no afikoman may follow the Pesach offering. This means that after people finished eating the Pesach offering, they could not leave their location to eat elsewhere, and even in the same location they could eat nothing afterward. The afikoman takes the place of the Pesach offering for us. Therefore, once people begin eating it, they may not move from place to place and may eat nothing afterward, even in the same place. Tur; Taz, subsection 1.
2) The custom is not to eat anything, even fruit, after eating the afikoman, so that its flavor remains in one's mouth, as our ancestors practiced when eating the Pesach offering. It is called afikoman after the Mishnah's statement that no afikoman may follow the Pesach offering. The word maftirin can mean opening the mouth to speak, as in the phrase, 'They open their lips.' Alternatively, it derives from taking leave of another person: when departing from the meal, one does not conclude it by requesting afikoman. Afikoman may be understood as two words meaning, 'Bring out delicacies.' The Mishnah thus means that after eating the Pesach offering, people do not open their mouths to say, 'Bring us sweet foods, such as fruit, to eat afterward.' It is possible that we call the matzah itself afikoman because it replaces the fruit customarily eaten after the meal at another time. Because of our affection for the mitzvah, which is as beloved to us as sweet fruit, we call it afikoman. A person says to the household, or each person to another, 'Bring us delicacies,' meaning the matzah whose mitzvah is sweet to us. Levush; Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1.
