(א) Forbidden in benefit — see in Mishnah Berurah, and know that although we ruled stringently even b'dieved if baked in an oven on chametz coals and ash because Acharonim ruled stringently, Shach's view in Yoreh De'ah 142 is not so — he holds there that although baking or cooking on coals alone is certainly forbidden, nevertheless baking inside an oven kindled on coals is permitted as this-and-that cause, and we do not say it is only one cause except when baking opposite the fire of the chametz body which is significant, and therefore we disregard the oven; unlike coals and ash alone. Possibly, and all the more if all coals and ash were removed and then he baked — certainly one need not be stringent b'dieved. So Magen Avraham at the beginning of s.k. 5 (and even though he later retracted and forbade this-and-that cause in chametz, many Acharonim rejected his view, and the Gra disagrees). One who examines Rabbeinu Manoach's novellae on the Rambam will see his view permits even on coals themselves b'dieved; nevertheless in practice it is hard to be lenient after all Acharonim ruled stringently, and the Gra in his commentary holds no distinction between cooking on chametz coals and ash versus cooking on them inside an oven kindled in every manner — forbidden. However, if he kindled the oven with chametz and also with permitted wood together and then baked bread there, it is literally this-and-that cause; although Taz there in Yoreh De'ah and Magen Avraham at the end of his words forbid this-and-that cause, nevertheless Shulchan Aruch and Shach there on orlah are lenient, and so in our case regarding chametz; and many Acharonim and the Gra ruled leniently on this-and-that cause, as I wrote above. Therefore in a pressing situation where he has nothing to eat or there is great loss, one may rely to be lenient — so Gra Zal.

(ב) And likewise the coals — regarding the flame: since it is attached to the coal, it is like the coal, as the Rashba and Rosh proved in Beitzah 39 — see there; so Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 142. If it is not attached to the coal, Chok Yaakov is stringent for chametz, as we are stringent in the Gemara for hekdesh lest one come to use the coal itself; Chak Yosef rejected this based on Pesachim 4a: he goes after it to burn it — a dog eats it — and there is no room to reject Chok Yaakov's words, for here one in any case wants to benefit from its flame, and properly one should fear he will benefit from the coal; it is simple. Therefore it is forbidden to warm oneself by the flame or light a lamp from it and similar benefits.
