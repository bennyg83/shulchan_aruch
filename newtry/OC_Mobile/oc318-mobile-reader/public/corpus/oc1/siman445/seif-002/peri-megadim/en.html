If he fulfilled the mitzvah of burning in the fifth hour and afterward found chametz, he destroys it like any other matter, for during Pesach, if he found it on Yom Tov, he covers it with a utensil, and on Chol HaMoed he burns it, although he already fulfilled the mitzvah of burning on the eve of Pesach. For according to Rabbi Yehuda, who requires burning, one may say like Rabbeinu Tam, that this means on Pesach, similar to nosar, which has kares and the negative commandment of bal tosiru; the same applies to chametz. This is unlike the eve of Pesach until night, where there is no kares and no bal yera'eh, only the positive commandment of tashbisu; one cannot derive by analogy from nosar, which has kares and a negative commandment. Therefore we are also lenient regarding a doubt and other leniencies, since there is no kares until night, as in siman 447. If so, granted that in the sixth hour we burn by custom like Rabbi Yehuda and like Rashi's explanation, but this is unlike where he already fulfilled it; see letter 3. See Tosafos, Pesachim folio 12b, s.v. when, where it is explained that according to Rabbeinu Tam, Rabbi Yehuda also requires burning from the seventh hour. See Tosafos folio 28a, s.v. and chametz, and folio 27b, s.v. ein; this requires examination. Know that we rule the halachah like the Sages, that they are among those buried and their ashes are forbidden, not that there is a doubt whether the halachah follows Rabbi Yehuda or the Rabbis, and we rule stringently both ways. The practical difference is where there is a doubt whether matzah bread was baked with a chametz coal; should this be considered a double doubt? Certainly the halachah follows the Sages. In my Peri Megadim to Yoreh Deah, in Kuntres HaSefeikos, we wrote that a doubt in a dispute that became mixed with others is also forbidden; see there. See Beis Shmuel, Even HaEzer siman 28, s.k. 54, and Chelkas Mechokek there, which imply that it is a doubt whether the halachah follows Rabbi Yehuda or the Sages. What the Beis Shmuel wrote there, that she can sell it to a sick person, requires examination, for to a healthy person it is forbidden according to him. See Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchos Ishus; this is not the place. The law requires examination, whether ash is definitely forbidden or only doubtful.
