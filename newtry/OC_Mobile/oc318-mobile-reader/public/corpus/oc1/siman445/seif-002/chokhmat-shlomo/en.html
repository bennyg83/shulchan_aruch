(Same seif 2) And if he gave to a non-Jew before the sixth hour, he need not destroy, etc. Note: I saw Chok Yaakov and Bach citing Bach responsum: forbidden to give to worker or gentile servant if abundant chametz — like paying debt with issurei hana'ah — see there. Their words are very surprising: paying debt with issurei hana'ah is when at payment time it is forbidden in benefit; if permitted at payment, what matter that later forbidden — may one not pay gentile debt in chametz even before due? So too here: gentile receives and is released from sustenance before prohibition; if sold to gentile who owes, permitted — here like sale to gentile, gentile owes wages, pays with chametz, releases sustenance — permitted as siman 450. Also siman 450 permits telling grocer to give worker chametz and employer pays — not paying debt with issurei hana'ah (Magen Avraham); even stringent agree when paying before Pesach. Therefore any acquisition valid generally works here; not on Jew's responsibility if lost — must feed more — same if little chametz remains; like accepting responsibility on gentile's chametz in gentile's domain — permitted. Why is worker permitted dinar for chametz — even when chametz comes to worker, Jew responsible if lost — like responsibility on gentile chametz — permitted. Main point as my novellae: chametz responsibility not forbidden; he wants it to exist only if obligation stems from chametz itself; not when obligation elsewhere, chametz only releases debt — not responsibility, not wanting existence — like scaring lion (Bava Kama) — not benefit; paying debt permitted knowingly only if giving in hand after prohibition; telling grocer or giving in hand while still permitted — not he who gives later, mere benefit — not worse than paying debt unknowingly — scaring lion. Bach's words surprising. In my humble opinion Bach deals only when gentile eats in Jew's house — Agudah brought by Magen Avraham siman 450: do not eat in his house — suspicion of paying debt with issurei hana'ah; Bach's intent: suspicion they say he pays debt with issurei hana'ah at Pesach. If not in Jew's house, certainly permitted; only in Jew's house forbidden lest suspicion. Why not mention transgressing bal yera'ah — since nullification suffices, presumably nullified, won't leave permitted and eat forbidden; rabbinic prohibition permitted in inner rooms (Magen Avraham 431). Bach cited paying-debt reason — Torah prohibition, forbidden even inner rooms. Without this, Bach's words have no explanation; for law, seems full permission except not in his house per Agudah. Later I received Bach responsa — not siman 147 as Chok Yaakov said but 143; Bach follows stringent view siman 450; for lenient view certainly permitted here. Chok Yaakov surprising to cite in stam; Bach's text surprising — cites Agudah forbidding dinar for chametz — there at prohibition time; here still permitted time — can one not pay before due if creditor agrees to release debt? Without proof I bring Gemara Pesachim: Rabbah Gamliel betrothed with Kordyene wheat from six onward — no concern for kiddushin. Why not teach greater leniency — betrothal before permitted time, kiddushin not effective until six when chametz forbidden, especially according to those who hold after six d'oraisa? Must be Rabbah's view kiddushin effective — Ran: money consumed with her permission; here chametz already hers, only debt remains — kiddushin in debt permitted, not in chametz though chametz visible — like not in domain. All the more giving worker chametz in permitted time — not benefiting from issurei hana'ah. Do not rely on Bach here — study. I permitted stews except pot adjacent to challah likely touched — forbade that. Tur permits morayis in benefit — lenient, no concern not charred well; especially some hold this-and-that cause permitted in chametz — rely in pressing case. Concern taste from challah on adjacent pot — not this-and-that cause — Magen Avraham forbids even benefit — I forbade that dish, permitted others.
