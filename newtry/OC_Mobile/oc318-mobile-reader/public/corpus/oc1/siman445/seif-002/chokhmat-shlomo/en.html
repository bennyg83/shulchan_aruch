And if he gave it to a non-Jew before the sixth hour, he need not destroy it, etc. Note: I saw in Chok Yaakov and Baer Heitev that they brought in the name of Responsa Bach that it is forbidden to give it to his worker or to his non-Jewish servants whose sustenance is upon him if it is a large amount of chametz, because it is like paying his debt with prohibitions of benefit; see there. His words are very astonishing. Paying one's debt with prohibitions of benefit means where at the time of payment it is forbidden for benefit. But if at that time of payment it is permitted, what concern is there that afterward it will become forbidden for benefit? Would a person not be allowed to pay a debt to a non-Jew with chametz, even a debt whose time has not yet arrived before Pesach? Is that forbidden? So too here: since the non-Jew receives it and exempts him from his sustenance before its prohibition, and if he sold it to the non-Jew and the non-Jew became obligated to him, it would be permitted, here too it is as if he sold it to the non-Jew, and the non-Jew became obligated to him for money, and with that debt-money he exempted him from his sustenance. It is like giving him money to buy his sustenance for him; even though he buys chametz for him, it is permitted, as written in siman 450. Furthermore, there are those who permit in siman 450 saying to a shopkeeper: give chametz to my workers and I will pay, and it is not considered paying his debt with prohibitions of benefit, as the Magen Avraham wrote there, even though his sustenance is upon him. It is not considered paying his debt with prohibitions of benefit. Even those who are stringent would agree here, for there he gives on Pesach itself, but where he gives before Pesach, it is certainly permitted. Therefore it appears that whenever the non-Jew acquired it in a manner that is generally effective, it is effective here too. One cannot say that it remains under a Jew's responsibility, for if it is lost he will need to give him other sustenance; for if so, even with a small amount of chametz, if it remains for him into Pesach, the same would be true. Furthermore, it is like accepting responsibility for a non-Jew's chametz in the non-Jew's domain, which is permitted. Furthermore, if so, why is it permitted with a worker to give him a dinar to buy chametz with it, and according to those who permit, even to tell a shopkeeper to give him chametz is permitted? When the chametz comes into the hands of the non-Jewish worker, it would be under the Jew's responsibility, for if it is lost he will need to pay him another. Perforce, this is like accepting responsibility for a non-Jew's chametz in the non-Jew's domain, which is permitted. Furthermore, and this is the main point, as I wrote in my chiddushim long ago: responsibility for chametz is forbidden, and likewise wanting its preservation, only where the primary responsibility arises because of the chametz, such as one who accepts the chametz of others under responsibility, so that now the obligation of responsibility begins; and likewise wanting its preservation, where one buys chametz for a non-Jew or hires himself as a worker for chametz or for yayin nesech. But where the obligation is not because of the chametz, but from another source, only that through the chametz he is exempted from his debt, and if the chametz will not be there, another prior obligation that he already owed from another source and not because of the chametz will take effect, this is not considered responsibility and not considered wanting its preservation. The body of his benefit or responsibility does not begin from the chametz, but from elsewhere, and the chametz is only removal of damage, as I wrote in my chiddushim long ago with this proof; see there. If so, here too it is like this, and it is only like driving away a lion, which is not benefit but only removal of damage, as Tosafos wrote in Bava Kama regarding an animal that fell into a garden. Although paying his debt is permitted only without his knowledge, but with his knowledge it is forbidden, that is only where he gives it to him by hand and it is also after it has already become forbidden. But if he does not give it to him by hand, but only says to a shopkeeper to give it to him, or where he gives it to him by hand but it is still the time of permission, then at the time of giving it is permitted, and afterward, although it becomes forbidden for benefit, at that time he is not giving it to him; he benefits automatically, and it is no worse than paying his debt without his knowledge, which is only driving away a lion. Therefore the words of the Bach are astonishing. My heart therefore tells me that the Bach speaks only where the non-Jew eats in the Jew's house, and these are the words of the Agudah that the Magen Avraham brought in siman 450, that he should not eat in his house because of suspicion. What is the suspicion? That they will say he is paying his debt with prohibitions of benefit. This too is the intent of the Bach: there is a concern that people will say it is paying his debt with prohibitions of benefit, for they will think he gave it to him on Pesach. But if it is not in the Jew's house, it is certainly permitted; only in the Jew's house is it forbidden, because people will say it is like paying his debt with prohibitions of benefit. Why did he not state the reason that people will say the Jew transgressed bal yera'eh? One can say that since according to Torah law mere nullification is enough, presumably he nullified it, for one does not abandon the permitted and eat the forbidden. Although it remains forbidden rabbinically, nevertheless it would then be permitted in private chambers, for with a rabbinic prohibition, it is permitted in private chambers where there is concern of maris ayin, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 301. Therefore the Bach stated the reason because of paying his debt with a prohibition of benefit, for that is a Torah prohibition and is forbidden even in private chambers. This appears correct to my humble opinion in the Bach's intent. Without this, his words have no explanation. In any case, as a matter of law it appears to be completely permitted; only in his house he should not eat because of suspicion, like the words of the Agudah above. This is clear to my humble opinion; examine it. Afterward Responsa Bach came into my possession, and it is not in siman 147 as the Chok Yaakov wrote, but in siman 143. In truth, it all follows the stringent view in siman 450; but according to the permissive authorities, certainly all the more so it is permitted here too. Therefore the words of the Chok Yaakov are astonishing, that he brought this without qualification. The substance of the Bach's words is also astonishing, for he brought proof to this, that even greater than this is written in the Agudah, that it is forbidden to give a worker a dinar to buy chametz; see there. In my opinion it is not comparable at all, for there it is during the time of prohibition, but in our case it is still during the time of permission. Can a person not pay before its time if the creditor agrees to accept it to exempt him from his debt? In order not to leave the matter smooth without proof, I will bring a proof from the Talmud in the first chapter of Pesachim, where Rab Gidal said in the name of Rav: one who betroths with Kurdani wheat from six hours and onward, we do not take the kiddushin into account. This is difficult: why did he not teach a greater novelty, that even if he betrothed before, during the time of permission, and stipulated that the kiddushin not take effect until the sixth hour, we do not take the kiddushin into account, since when the time of kiddushin arrives the chametz has already become forbidden, especially according to those who explain that he speaks after the sixth hour, when it is according to Torah law? Why did he not teach this? Perforce, in such a case she is betrothed, for Rav follows his own reasoning in Kiddushin folio 59, that even if the money was consumed, she is betrothed, because it was consumed in her domain. If so, here too with chametz, the chametz has already become hers, and what remains upon him is only a monetary debt; if so, he is not benefiting from a prohibition of benefit, but from the debt, which is permitted, and she is betrothed with the debt and not with the chametz. Although the chametz is then physically present in her hand, it is as though it were not present. If so, all the more so one who gives chametz to a worker during the time of permission is not considered to be benefiting from prohibitions of benefit. This is clear and correct with the help of Hashem. Therefore the Bach's words in this matter do not stand; examine it. There, in the same se'if: But if he burned it from the sixth hour and onward, since it is forbidden for benefit, he may not heat an oven or stove with it, etc. Note: an actual case came before me where a woman threw the challah into the fire on Shabbos HaGadol before Pesach and thought it had burned. Afterward they swept the coals from the oven and left them for Pesach, and they took the coals to cook pots with them, and found the challah among the coals. I ruled to permit the cooked dishes; only the pot that was adjacent to the challah, where it was likely that the challah touched that pot, I forbade. My reason was that since if he charred it before its time it is permitted for benefit after its time, then if we knew that the challah had been well charred before Pesach, there would be no concern at all. Only since we did not know, we are concerned perhaps it was not thoroughly charred, as the Rosh wrote regarding muryas. Nevertheless, that which we require that it be fit for a dog applies only when it is present as itself; but through a mixture, it is enough if it became unfit for human consumption, as is proven in siman 442. If so, here too, granted it was not well charred, in any event it was certainly unfit for human consumption. Since this and this cause it is only like through a mixture, and the reason this and this cause it is permitted with prohibitions of benefit is because of nullification, it is proven that it is a kind of mixture. If so, it is chametz through a mixture, and it is enough if it became unfit for a dog's consumption. What the Rosh was concerned about, perhaps it was not thoroughly charred, follows his earlier words, where he wrote that Rabbi Shimon permits only where it became mixed by itself, but not to mix it and keep it intentionally. Therefore, regarding that, he properly wrote that since the Rosh holds that intentionally mixing is as though it were present as itself, and regarding something present as itself we require that it become unfit for a dog's consumption, he is concerned perhaps it was not thoroughly charred. But where it became mixed by itself, since it is through a mixture and it is enough that it became unfit for a dog's consumption, this is considered certain. Furthermore, the Tur permits muryas even for benefit; if so, it is proven that he holds leniently regarding benefit, and we are not concerned that perhaps it was not thoroughly charred. Especially since there are views that even with chametz, this and this cause it is permitted, therefore in any event one may rely on this in such a case, for it is a double doubt: perhaps this and this cause it is permitted, and perhaps it was thoroughly charred. But where it is likely that it touched the actual body of the challah, there is concern for flavor that imparted flavor, and this does not fall under the law of this and this cause it; there one should properly be concerned that perhaps it was not thoroughly charred. Some of the above reasons do not apply there, and the Magen Avraham also forbids in such a case even for benefit. Therefore in that matter my heart did not allow me to be lenient, and I forbade that cooked dish; the rest I permitted. This is correct with the help of Hashem; examine it. Since in this siman I spoke at length, with the help of Hashem, in my work Sefer HaChaim regarding the law of this and this cause it, and regarding where something is not nullified even in a thousand and even this and this cause it is forbidden, I will copy here what arose in my thought with the help of Hashem when I studied the Rambam in Hilchos Temurah. This is the language of the Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchos Temurah, law 5: one who consecrates an animal with a passing blemish, etc., and it is redeemed, and he substituted another animal for it after it was redeemed, this is a temurah but is not offered, etc. I saw in Lechem Mishneh that he wondered: since we say in chapter 2 of Bechoros that the reason their offspring are forbidden is specifically where it conceived before its redemption and was born after its redemption, etc.; but if it conceived after its redemption, they are chullin. If so, here too regarding temurah it should be proper to say this. After all, the offspring of kodshim is more severe than temurah, for the offspring of partners is sanctified, whereas they do not make a temurah; see there. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty at all, since we say in Pesachim that although this and this cause it is permitted, nevertheless with hekdesh it is forbidden, because it is not nullified even in a thousand. If so, here too, the fact that the offspring of a korban is permitted when it conceived after its redemption is because of this and this cause it. Granted, where it conceived before its redemption, it is not considered this and this cause it, since it has a permitted outcome through redemption and therefore is not considered this and this cause it. But where it conceived after redemption, since it no longer has a permitted outcome, it is this and this cause it; therefore it does not have the status of the offspring of a korban and is permitted. But with temurah, since in any event it is the temurah of a korban and is not this and this cause it, even after its redemption it is forbidden. With a partners' korban, since they have redemption, it is not this and this cause it, and it is considered the offspring of a korban; therefore the offspring of a korban is more severe than temurah. But where it is this and this cause it, it is not considered the offspring of a korban at all, and in this temurah is more severe. This works well; examine it. While dealing with this, I was uncertain regarding these laws of the Rambam, where he wrote that it is a temurah: does one also receive lashes or not? This requires examination; examine it. See Magen Avraham here, who wrote that where a mashehu is forbidden, even this and this cause it is forbidden. As for the difficulty against him, how this differs from a davar hama'amid, where we hold that even in a thousand it is not nullified, and nevertheless this and this cause it is permitted, see what I wrote in my work on tractate Avodah Zarah, Avodas Avodah, folio 68b, to resolve this; examine it.

"But if he burned it from the sixth hour and onward, since it is forbidden in benefit, he may not heat an oven or stove with it, etc." NB: A case came before me: a woman threw challah into the fire on Shabbat HaGadol before Pesach and thought it had burned. Later they swept the coals from the oven, left them for Pesach, and used those coals to cook pots. They then found the challah among the coals. I ruled to permit the cooked foods, except for the pot that was adjacent to the challah, where it was likely that the challah touched the pot; that one I forbade. My reason was that if one charred it before its time, it is permitted in benefit after its time. If we knew the challah had been well charred before Pesach, there would be no concern at all. Since we do not know, we are concerned perhaps it was not thoroughly charred, as the Rosh wrote regarding muries. Nevertheless, the requirement that it be unfit for a dog applies only when it remains intact. Through mixture, it suffices if it became unfit for human consumption, as is proven in siman 442. Here too, although it may not have been thoroughly charred, it was certainly unfit for human consumption. Since zeh v'zeh gorem is like a mixture, and for that reason zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted in issurei hana'ah because of nullification, this is chametz through mixture, where being unfit for human consumption suffices. The Rosh's concern lest it was not thoroughly charred follows his view that Rabbi Shimon permits only where it became mixed on its own, not deliberate mixing and keeping; deliberate mixing is like the item remaining intact, and for an intact item it must be unfit for a dog. But where it mixed on its own, it is through mixture and being unfit for human consumption suffices. Moreover, the Tur permits benefit even with muries, proving that he is lenient and is not concerned lest it was not thoroughly charred. Especially since there are views that even with chametz, zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted, one may rely on this here as a double doubt: perhaps zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted, and perhaps it was well charred. But where the pot was close enough that the actual challah likely touched it, there is concern for imparted taste, and this does not fall under zeh v'zeh gorem. There one should be concerned lest it was not well charred, and some of the above reasons do not apply; the Magen Avraham also forbids such a case even in benefit. Therefore I did not have the heart to be lenient there, and I forbade that dish while permitting the rest. This is correct, with God's help. Since in this siman I discussed at length in my Sefer HaChayim the law of zeh v'zeh gorem and the law that where something is not nullified even in a thousand, zeh v'zeh gorem is also forbidden, I will copy here what arose in my mind while learning the Rambam in Hilchot Temurah. The Rambam writes in Hilchot Temurah 3:5: one who consecrates an animal with a temporary blemish, and it was redeemed, and afterward he made temurah with it after redemption, it is a temurah but is not offered. The Lechem Mishneh wondered: in Bekhorot chapter 2 we say that its offspring is forbidden only where it conceived before redemption and was born after redemption, but if it conceived after redemption it is chullin. The same should apply to temurah, and offspring of sanctified animals is more stringent than temurah, for the offspring of partners' korbanot is sanctified while partners do not make temurah. In my humble opinion this is not difficult. In Pesachim we say that although zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted, in hekdesh it is forbidden because even in a thousand it is not nullified. The reason offspring of a korban is permitted when it conceived after redemption is zeh v'zeh gorem. If it conceived before redemption, it is not considered zeh v'zeh gorem because it has a heter through redemption and is a davar sheyesh lo matirin. But if it conceived after redemption, since it has no further heter, it is zeh v'zeh gorem and therefore is not considered offspring of a korban. With temurah, however, since it is at least the temurah of a korban and not zeh v'zeh gorem, even after redemption it is forbidden. With partners' korbanot, since they have redemption, they are not zeh v'zeh gorem and are considered offspring of a korban; in that respect offspring is more stringent than temurah. But where it is zeh v'zeh gorem, it is not offspring of a korban at all, and temurah is more stringent. This resolves it. I was also uncertain in these Rambam cases, where he says it is temurah, whether one also receives lashes; this requires study. See the Magen Avraham here, who wrote that where even the smallest amount is forbidden, zeh v'zeh gorem is also forbidden. Regarding the difficulty why this differs from davar hama'amid, where even in a thousand it is not nullified and nevertheless zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted, see what I wrote in my work on Avodah Zarah, Avodat Avodah, fol. 68b. Examine carefully.
