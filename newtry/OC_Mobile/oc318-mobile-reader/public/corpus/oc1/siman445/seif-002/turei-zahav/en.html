And if he burned it before the sixth hour, etc. In the Gemara, folio 12b, Rabbi Yehuda said: when is this so? When it is not at the time of its destruction; but at the time of its destruction, its destruction may be by any means. Rashi explained: not at the time of its destruction means at the beginning of the sixth hour and all of the sixth, for according to Torah law it is still permitted; but at the time of its destruction means in the seventh hour, etc. In the Tur he brought the view of the Rosh, that according to Rashi's view, in the fifth hour its destruction may be by any means, since it is permitted for benefit. The Tur objected to him from what Rashi explained in the Gemara on folio 27b, regarding what they said to Rabbi Yehuda: if he did not find wood to burn it, should he sit idle? Rashi wrote as follows: although we say in the first chapter, according to Rabbi Yehuda, that at the time of its destruction, its destruction may be by any means, nevertheless regarding their dispute this is a leniency, in the case of one who wants to leave in a caravan, etc., where we say he is required to destroy it, and when he has no wood to burn it, should he sit idle? End quote. The Beis Yosef wants to answer his difficulty; see there. To me it appears that when you examine Rashi's explanation, there is no difficulty at all against the Rosh. For there is a difficulty: why did Rashi need to mention a caravan? He should have asked from the sixth hour, that one who has no wood should sit idle and not destroy it. Rather, perforce this is the explanation: certainly according to Rabbi Yehuda, the primary destruction is burning; nevertheless, one who has no wood must in any event destroy it from the world so that he should not transgress bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei. If so, one cannot ask from the sixth hour, from one who has no wood, etc.; for although he will not have wood, nevertheless the general obligation of destruction is upon him so that he should not transgress bal yera'eh, and automatically he will not sit idle. Therefore Rashi mentioned one leaving in a caravan, for in that case, if he has no wood, he would certainly sit idle: the obligation of destruction, namely burning, is not upon him because he has no wood, and the general destruction because of bal yera'eh is also not upon him, since bal yera'eh has not yet taken effect at all. If so, he would sit idle. According to this, there is no difficulty at all against the Rosh, for if he destroys it by general destruction in the fifth hour, this is not called sitting idle, since he has already destroyed it from the world at the outset and has nothing to burn. The Tur's difficulties against him are removed.
And so too the coals, etc. That is to say, from the chametz itself and not from the wood. The Tur wrote: even after they went out, according to the Rambam's view, for he holds regarding the destruction of chametz that every method is included in what is written that all items that are buried have forbidden ashes. If so, even if there are no chametz coals here at all, but only ordinary ash, it is forbidden according to the Rambam. The coals mentioned here are not precise.
