Se'if 2: And if he burned it before, etc. There: after its time passed, etc., and he should not heat, etc.; thus before its time it is permitted. And there in the Gemara: if he charred it before its time, it is permitted for benefit after its time.
But if. The Mishnah: and he should not heat, as above.
And if he cooked or baked, etc. This is the language of the Rambam, and since he wrote without qualification, it implies even over coals, and even dimming coals. So too he wrote without qualification in chapter 7 of Hilchos Avodah Zarah regarding idolatry. But in chapter 16 of Hilchos Ma'achalos Asuros he explained regarding orlah that over coals it is permitted, even if they are glowing. This is as the Rosh wrote in Perek Kol Shaah: this applies specifically to orlah and kilayim, whose ashes are permitted, and likewise their coals; but with chametz, whose ashes are forbidden, all the more so its coals are forbidden, and even if one cooked over dimming coals it is forbidden. See Tosafos there, s.v. if he cooked it, and there folio 21b, s.v. together with, etc. So too with an asheirah, whose ashes would be forbidden, as it says there: except for wood of an asheirah. So too they said at the end of Beitzah, that coals of idolatry are forbidden, unlike the Kesef Mishneh, who wrote there and there that this is only where the flame is opposite it. So too he wrote in Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah siman 142, and so wrote Bedek HaBayis there. See the Shach there, who challenged them at length, and his words are very correct, unlike the Magen Avraham here, who followed the Kesef Mishneh. Therefore Rami bar Chama asked the question regarding hekdesh wood according to the Rabbis, and did not also ask according to Rebbe regarding coals, since the ashes of hekdesh are forbidden, as written there. This is unlike the Rabbis, according to whom there is no improvement of wood in bread at all, even where the flame is opposite it. What the Magen Avraham wrote, that although chametz, etc., nevertheless it is a case of this and this cause it, etc., shows that he did not examine the Ran in Perek Kol Shaah, who wrote: although this and this cause it is permitted, etc., here we say that the improvement of the wood is in the bread, and it is more severe than this and this cause it, because the prohibition is recognizable in the permitted item. So too what the Magen Avraham wrote to forbid for another reason, since it is forbidden by any amount, as with hekdesh wood according to the Rabbis, is not so; for with hekdesh the reason is not because of any amount, but because of its severity. See Tosafos there, s.v. hekdesh, etc.: this is difficult, etc.; that is to say, unlike where they heated it, for the prohibition is burned away and nullified, as written in Beitzah folio 4b, unlike Magen Avraham. All his words here do not appear correct.
And so too the coals. Since he charred it after its time, as above. Even its ashes are forbidden, as written at the end of Temurah, as above; all the more so coals, which are more severe than ashes, as Tosafos wrote, s.v. if he cooked it.
