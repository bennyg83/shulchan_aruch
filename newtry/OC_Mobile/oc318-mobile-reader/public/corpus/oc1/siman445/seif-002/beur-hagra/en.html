(א) Seif 2 — and if he burned before, etc. His time passed, etc., and he shall not kindle — behold, before his time it is permitted; and in the Gemara: if he charred it before its time, it is permitted in benefit after its time.

(ב) But if. No, no matter how:

(ג) And if he cooked or baked, etc. Rambam's wording; and from that he taught 40 — even on coals and even glowing coals; and so stam in chapter 7 of laws of orlah regarding orlah; but in chapter 16 laws of forbidden foods regarding orlah on coals — permitted even if they smolder — as the Rosh wrote in chapter 21 specifically for orlah and kilayim whose ash is permitted and so coals; but for chametz whose ash is forbidden, all the more coals — even if he cooked on glowing coals it is forbidden. Tosafot there s.v. bishlah and there 21b s.v. behadi, etc.; and in asherah whose ash was forbidden as stated, except asherah wood; and they said end Yom Tov coals of orlah are forbidden — not like what is written there, and there specifically with flame opposite; so Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah 142 and Bach there; Shach there challenged them at length and his words are very correct — not like Magen Avraham here who followed the Kesef Mishneh; therefore the question was raised for Rami bar Chama on wood of hekdesh for the rabbis, and not raised for R' Yehudah on coals, since hekdesh ash is forbidden as stated there — unlike the rabbis, for there is no enhancement of wood in bread even with flame opposite. What Magen Avraham wrote — and even though chametz, etc. — nevertheless it is zuz gavra, etc. — he did not look in the Ran chapter 21 who wrote: and even though zuz gavra is permitted, etc. — we say there is enhancement of wood in bread and it is stricter than zuz gavra because the prohibition is recognizable in the permitted; and what Magen Avraham wrote to forbid for another reason — since forbidden in benefit like orlah wood for the rabbis — that is not so, for in hekdesh the reason is not because of benefit but because it is stringent; see Tosafot there s.v. hekdesh, etc., "one might say," etc., meaning unlike when he kindles where the prohibition is consumed and nullified as Yom Tov 4b — not like Magen Avraham; and so here they are not plausible.

(ד) And likewise the coals. Since he charred it after its time as above, and even its ash is forbidden as stated end Temurah as above, and all the more coals which are stricter than ash as Tosafot s.v. bishelah.
