And if he burned it, etc. In the Tur it implies that even if he burned it before the sixth hour, it is good to make a separate fire for it. The Maharshal wrote to burn it in the courtyard, and so is the custom. See Rabbi Yehuda Levi, siman 83.
They are forbidden for benefit. Because the improvement of the wood is present in the bread. Ran; Maggid Mishneh. This is specifically when the flame is opposite it. Ramach; Kesef Mishneh. Although with chametz even its coal and ash are forbidden, nevertheless it is a case of this and this cause it, for the oven is also a cause of its baking, and it is permitted. But if he cooked a dish, even over ash or coals, it is forbidden. See Yoreh Deah siman 142 and examine it. However, the main ruling appears to me like the view of the Taz and Shach there, that the view of the Rambam and the halachic decisors is that with idolatry, even if he cooked over coals or with the heat of the oven, it is forbidden. Not for their reason, but because we say in Pesachim folio 26b that although the Rabbis permit where the flame is opposite it, that is specifically with orlah, which is nullified in two hundred, and therefore it is not more severe than where the prohibition has been consumed and burned away. But with hekdesh, which is not nullified even in a thousand, it is forbidden. If so, the same applies to dimming coals, that they are forbidden. This is the view of the Tur, who did not distinguish here regarding bread between a new oven and an old oven, because he wrote in Yoreh Deah in the name of the Ramah that since idolatry is not nullified, even this and this cause it is forbidden. The same applies to chametz, for there is at least some benefit. If so, even if other wood was lying on the fire and he placed the chametz on it as well, so that it is this and this cause it, nevertheless it is forbidden. So too it appears, for it is impossible to light chametz by itself except through wood, and nevertheless he wrote that the bread is forbidden. See there, siman 142. This is the language of Maharil: its coals and ashes are forbidden, and even through nullification it is forbidden. It is not comparable to wood that fell from a palm tree on Yom Tov, for which nullification helps, as written in siman 507 se'if 2, because there the wood is not edible; and even if some of it comes into the cooked dish, the prohibition is not in its normal manner of benefit. End quote. In Tosafos, Shabbos folio 25, s.v. this applies on a weekday, etc., it says that oil, meat, and grain are considered more present at the time of heating than wood; see there.
They are forbidden for benefit. And it has no remedy; this is the main ruling. Maggid Mishneh. His intent is that it has no remedy by bringing the benefit to the Dead Sea, for this helps specifically with idolatry, whose value attaches to its exchange and which has redemption. See Yoreh Deah, Shach siman 110, s.k. 2. Furthermore, only when that bread became mixed with others do we say that he should bring the benefit away, but not when it is present as itself, as appears to me, unlike the Levush, who is lenient by bringing the benefit to the Dead Sea. The Shach wrote that it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew excluding the value of the prohibition in it, and so he wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 142. But from the language of the Maggid Mishneh, who wrote that it has no remedy, it implies that it has no remedy at all. The reason is as the Mordechai wrote in chapter 2: the drop of moisture around the wheat kernel is chametz, and we hold that yayin nesech, since it is according to Torah law, forbids its mixture liquid with liquid, and selling it excluding the value of the yayin nesech in it does not help; the same applies to chametz. End quote. In Tosafos, Pesachim folio 75, it implies that where the flame is opposite it, it is forbidden according to Torah law; if so, no remedy helps to sell it to a non-Jew. So too it appears from the Ran, and somewhat from Milchamos in chapter 2, and from Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, that there is no remedy for that bread. Perhaps in a case of great loss one may be lenient, especially with this and this cause it; furthermore, some say that even chametz has a remedy through redemption. See the Mordechai at the end of chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah and the Ran in chapter 5.
