(א) And if they burned as well. In the Tur — source., it means that Affith burned an hour ago and 'good to make him a break in the fire, and the PA wrote to burn him in the yard, as well as to the Dr. Levi C.C.:

(ב) Forbidden in benefit — because there is enhancement of wood in bread (Ran, Maggid Mishneh), specifically with flame opposite (Rambam, Kesef Mishneh); and even though chametz coals and ash are forbidden, nevertheless it is this-and-that cause — the oven also causes baking and is permitted; but if he cooked stew, even on ash or coals it is forbidden. Yoreh De'ah 142 requires study. In my humble opinion the essence is Taz and Shach: Rambam and poskim hold orlah — even cooking on coals or in oven heat is forbidden, not for their reasons but Pesachim 26b: even rabbis permit only with flame opposite in orlah because nullified in 200, therefore not stringent; hekdesh not nullified even in 1000 — forbidden; so glowing coals forbidden. Tur did not distinguish new vs. old oven because Rambam in Yoreh De'ah: since orlah not nullified, even this-and-that cause forbidden — so chametz, any benefit. Even if other wood were on the fire and chametz also — this-and-that cause — still forbidden; impossible to kindle chametz alone without wood; Tur wrote bread forbidden — see Yoreh De'ah 142; Maharil: his coals and ash forbidden, even nullification does not help; unlike palm branches fallen on Yom Tov where nullification helps, siman 497:2 — there wood is not food; even if some goes to food, issur is not through its benefit — end quote. Tosafot Shabbat 25b s.v. ha bechol: oil, meat, grain weigh more at kindling — see there.

(ג) Forbidden in benefit — and it has no remedy; so the essence (Maggid Mishneh). Meaning: no remedy by casting benefit to the Dead Sea — only for orlah it helps that value attaches and has redemption; see Shach Yoreh De'ah 110 s.k. 2. When that bread mixed with others they say cast benefit; when distinct, not like Levush who is lenient on Dead Sea. Shach permits selling to non-Jew except prohibition value; Yoreh De'ah 142. From Maggid Mishneh "no remedy" implies no remedy at all; reason as Mordechai Beitzah 2: moisture around grain is chametz, yayin nesech is d'oraisa, forbids mixture, sale does not help except nesech value; so chametz — end quote. Tosafot Pesachim 75: with flame opposite forbidden d'oraisa, so no sale to non-Jew; Ran and Machatzit HaShekel chapter 2; Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah: no remedy for that bread; possibly with great loss one may be lenient, especially this-and-that cause; some say chametz has redemption via orlah — see Mordechai chapter 3 and Ran chapter 5.
