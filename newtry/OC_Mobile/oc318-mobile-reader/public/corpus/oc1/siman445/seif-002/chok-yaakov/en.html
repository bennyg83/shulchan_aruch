It is permitted to benefit from its coals. In the Tur it implies that nevertheless one should burn it separately and not benefit from it at the time of its destruction. This is why he is precise that it is permitted to benefit from the coals, but not at the time of burning. So wrote Maharshal in responsum siman 86, and so wrote Maharil in Hilchos Bedikas Chametz in the name of Mahari Segal. However, in Mahariv siman 193 it implies that before six hours it is permitted to benefit from it even at the time of burning.
One may not heat with it. The Beis Hillel wrote in siman 142, s.k. 1, that the flame of chametz is permitted, for it is no worse than wood of a non-Jewish idol, whose ashes are forbidden and nevertheless its flame is permitted; if so, regarding chametz too, although its ashes are forbidden, nevertheless its flame is permitted. This is obvious. End quote. I did not grasp the end of his thought, for the opposite is obvious in Beitzah folio 39, where the Mishnah teaches: a flame of hekdesh, one may not benefit from it; and of idolatry, it is permitted. The Talmud asks: what is the difference? It answers that people keep away from idolatry, so the Sages did not decree regarding it, unlike hekdesh, from which people do not keep away, so the Sages did decree regarding it. Rashi and Tosafos there explain that although in chapter 6 of Pesachim it says that the blood of hekdesh need not be covered because people keep away from it, implying that people do keep away from hekdesh, nevertheless relative to idolatry it is called something from which people do not keep away; whereas relative to chullin it is called something from which people do keep away. End quote from Tosafos. Thus, with hekdesh it is forbidden to benefit from its flame although people keep away from it; all the more so chametz, from which people do not keep away at all, as is explicit at the beginning of Pesachim, folio 18, certainly its flame is forbidden. This is clear and obvious, unlike Beis Hillel.
Forbidden for benefit. See the Beis Yosef in this siman and in Yoreh Deah siman 142, where he compares chametz to other prohibitions of benefit, where the bread and cooked dish are forbidden only when the flame is opposite it. So too is the implication of Tosafos, Pesachim folio 5a, s.v. and I say; see there. However, the consensus of the later authorities there and here is to forbid even where it was cooked or baked near coals or with the heat of the oven. This is also the view of the Beis Yosef and the Rav, for he did not gloss here, specifically when the flame is opposite it, as he glossed in Yoreh Deah siman 142 se'if 4; see there. It is also explained from the words of these later authorities that there is no remedy by bringing the benefit to the Dead Sea. The Magen Avraham further wrote that even if there was also permitted wood here, it does not help, although it is this and this cause it; see there. The Shach wrote there in Yoreh Deah, s.k. 8, that it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew excluding the value of the prohibition in it, provided it is in a manner that it will not return and be sold to a Jew. So too the Shach wrote there in Yoreh Deah siman 110, and the Pri Chadash there, and so is the implication of the halachic decisors. However, the Magen Avraham wrote as follows: but from the language of the Maggid Mishneh, who wrote that it has no remedy, it implies that it has no remedy at all. The reason is as the Mordechai wrote in chapter 2, that with yayin nesech, which is according to Torah law, and the same applies to chametz, selling excluding the value of the prohibition in it does not help. In Tosafos, Pesachim folio 75, it implies that when the flame is opposite it, it is forbidden according to Torah law; if so, selling to a non-Jew does not help. End quote. But there is no proof from the words of the Maggid Mishneh, because he follows the Rambam's view, that casting the money of benefit away and selling excluding the value of the prohibition are all one law, as is explained many times in his words; and we do not hold like him. See Yoreh Deah, Beis Yosef siman 136. As for the reason he wrote in the name of the Mordechai, he did not examine it carefully there, for the Mordechai refers only to where the body of a Torah prohibition is mixed into it, unlike here, where he benefits only from the prohibition, as is clear from the proof itself that the Mordechai brought from Perek HaSocher Es HaPo'el, that the halachah follows Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai with barrels among barrels, but not wine with wine, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 136. Therefore the main ruling appears to me like the Shach there, for his words are clear, with necessary proofs; see there. This is certainly so when the flame is not opposite it and it is only a rabbinic prohibition. See Tosafos, first chapter of Pesachim, folio 5.
Its coals. The same applies to their ash. If one betrothed a woman with them, since there are halachic decisors who hold that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehuda, that the destruction of chametz is only by burning, and with all items that are burned their ash is permitted, therefore it is a doubtful kiddushin. So wrote the later authorities in Even HaEzer siman 22. Even without this, it appears to me that his kiddushin takes effect and she requires a get, since their coal and ash are only rabbinically forbidden. Even if we compare it to rabbinic chametz during hours that are according to Torah law, nevertheless it is a doubtful kiddushin, as explained there in Even HaEzer siman 28 se'if 21. See above, siman 443, s.k. 6.
Forbidden for benefit. Nevertheless, it is permitted to cover the blood of slaughter with it, because mitzvos were not given for benefit. For this reason it is permitted to cover with the earth of an idolatrous city at the end of Perek Kisui HaDam, and it implies there that Rava permits covering with it even initially. But Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah followed Responsa Maharam di Boton, folio 100, who presses to explain his father's words this way; in truth, however, this is explicitly against the implication of the Talmud there. It is not comparable to the shofar of an idolatrous city, with which one may not blow initially because it is repulsive, unlike regarding covering blood, as Tosafos wrote there. See above, siman 655.
