To a non-Jew before, etc. It is forbidden to give it to his non-Jewish slave or maidservant whose sustenance is upon him if it is a large amount that he cannot eat before the time of prohibition. So wrote Chok Yaakov in the name of the Bach. In Sefer Chemed Moshe he disagreed with them, saying that we do not care that his sustenance is upon him, since he does not pay him at the time of prohibition, but rather pays him before Pesach what he owes him; when Pesach arrives, he no longer owes his sustenance, for he already paid him beforehand. Only it is forbidden to say explicitly that he should keep the chametz throughout the days of Pesach, for if so he wants the chametz to remain, which is forbidden. Rather, he should give it to him without specification and say to him: take this chametz for your debt, and you will no longer have sustenance upon me throughout the days of Pesach. So too the Gaon Maharshak agreed in practice in his chiddushim, and he wrote that in any event one must be careful that the slave not eat the chametz in his house, so that they should not suspect him of paying his debt with chametz, as explained below in siman 450 se'if 6 in Mishnah Berurah.
He does not need to destroy it. Nevertheless, it is proper for one to conduct himself not to remove all his remaining chametz by giving it to a non-Jew, and instead to fulfill the mitzvah of destruction in the sixth hour with crumbs that he leaves to burn. Crumbs are already considered destroyed, and even if he leaves them he does not transgress bal yera'eh; rather, he should leave at least a kezayis of his chametz in order to fulfill the mitzvah of tashbisu properly. Beis Meir, and so wrote Chayei Adam and Chemed Moshe.
To benefit from its coals, etc. For the chametz was not yet forbidden for benefit at that time. The same applies that in the fifth hour it is permitted to cook with it and to have other forms of benefit. However, the custom is to make a separate fire for it even in the fifth hour, so that people should not come to be lenient also in the sixth hour. The custom is to burn it in the courtyard and not in the stove. Later authorities.
They are forbidden, etc. Although this is a case of this and this cause it, for the oven is permitted and the wood, namely the chametz, is forbidden, and we generally hold that this and this cause it is permitted, here it is different, because the cause of the forbidden wood through which the bread is baked is more recognizable, and its improvement is visible to the eye, so it is like one cause. Even if when he placed the bread into the oven the chametz had already become coals, or even hot ash, and it was baked through them, it is also forbidden, for with chametz even coals and their ash are forbidden; and since the bread was baked from their heat, their improvement is recognizable in the bread as with wood. Even if he swept away the ash and coals and baked or cooked only opposite its heat, several later authorities also forbid the bread and the cooked dish. See Biur Halacha.
For benefit. Even if the bread became mixed with other loaves and is not recognizable, all of them are forbidden for benefit. There is no remedy to permit this bread or cooked dish by redeeming the value of the benefit of the chametz and casting the money away to destruction, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 110 in the Shach, s.k. 2, and in siman 142. The later authorities wrote that in any event there is a remedy to sell the bread to a non-Jew excluding the value of the prohibition in it, meaning that he deducts the value of the benefit of the chametz with which the oven was heated from the price of the bread and cooked dish that he takes from the non-Jew, so that he does not benefit at all from the chametz with which he heated it. He should only be careful to crumble the bread before selling it to the non-Jew, if it is a place where they permit non-Jewish bread, because if he does not crumble it we are concerned that the non-Jew will resell it to a Jew. Even if this bread or cooked dish was not mixed with others, this remedy of sale also exists. There is one who forbids if it was not mixed; in a case of great loss or pressing need, one may rely on the lenient view.
And so too the coals. The same applies to their ash, as above. Therefore it is forbidden to warm oneself by the coals, or to benefit from their light and the like; and likewise with their ash, it is forbidden to launder clothing with it and the like. Nevertheless, it is permitted to cover the blood of slaughter with them, for mitzvos were not given for benefit. Later authorities.
Its. That is to say, of chametz. If coals of chametz became mixed with coals of wood with which they burned the chametz, it is forbidden to benefit from all of them, and they are not nullified even in a thousand, for they have a permitted use without nullification, namely to keep them until after Pesach; and a davar sheyesh lo matirin is not nullified.
