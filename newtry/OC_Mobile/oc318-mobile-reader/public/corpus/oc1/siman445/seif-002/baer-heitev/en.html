(א) To a non-Jew. Bach wrote in a responsum siman 147 that it is forbidden to give to his male or female gentile slave chametz remaining on the 14th if the chametz is abundant so it cannot be eaten before its prohibition time, since their sustenance is upon him — it is like paying his debt with items forbidden in benefit — see there.

(ב) Oven. Beit Yosef wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 142 that the flame of chametz is permitted, for it is no worse than wood of a non-Jew whose ash is forbidden yet the flame is permitted; so too for chametz — even though its ash is forbidden, nevertheless its flame is permitted — see there. Chok Yaakov challenged him and ruled the flame is forbidden — see there.

(ג) Forbidden in benefit. Acharonim agree to forbid even when there is no flame opposite it, only if it was cooked or baked near coals or in oven heat; also clear from Acharonim that it has no remedy by casting benefit to the Dead Sea, but one may sell to a non-Jew except for the value of the prohibition in it. Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 110 permits only on condition he not return and sell to a Jew; so too Peri Chadash. But Magen Avraham wrote it has no remedy at all — see there; Chok Yaakov agreed with Shach when there is no flame opposite, for it is only a d'rabbanan prohibition — see there. Magen Avraham wrote even if permitted wood were also here it does not help, even though it is this-and-that cause — see there.
