(א) Or — Taz: his holy words need explanation. Mechaber's wording is Rambam chapter 3 law 11; Maggid Mishneh ruled like R' Yosef; so Kesef Mishneh there. He explains wheat and bread opposite Rashi 28a s.v. bechitei — scattering from sack; soft bread sometimes needs crumbling. Rambam: wheat need not crumble even if gathered (except in sack) — scatter; hard bread sometimes needs crumbling. "If hard and sea does not cut quickly" — if wheat required grinding, soft bread would not be included; he wrote stam, should have said wheat specifically — opposite Rashi. In Laws of Idolatry 8:6 how a non-Jew destroys — grind and cast to wind or burn and cast to Dead Sea, like R' Yosef — end quote there. I thought Rambam's "how to destroy — burn or crumble and cast" means l'chatchila burn and cast so ash not food and no this-and-that cause; crumble also for one not meticulous like R' Yosi in Avodah Zarah 43b — this-and-that cause permitted; for non-Jew the opposite — wood and stone ground do not grow, burned grows, see Chullin 88 Tosafot s.v. shechikat. Darkei Moshe: l'chatchila best grind and cast without burning — no growth, no this-and-that cause; burning best to Dead Sea — end quote. If so, why not write for the less meticulous he may burn and cast? Understand; see there. And if so, why write stam — should say wheat specifically? Opposite Rashi entirely. In ch. 8 idolatry 6 how non-Jew destroys — grind, cast, or burn and cast to sea like R' Yosef. I thought Mechaber means l'chatchila burn/cast; crumble for less meticulous; burning to sea best. If so, why not write less meticulous may burn/cast? Understand; see there and in ch. 3 Orach Chayim law 11 he elaborated four disputes between Rabbah and R' Yosef.

(ב) And behold Taz explains Rambam like Rabbah — where no gentiles, even metals need not grinding; chametz in sea needs no crumbling even with gentiles — water cuts fast. Other rivers need crumbling. R' Yosef: no repugnance in water; chametz is repugnant in water though not cut — people won't take it (Rashi: root maas; Rambam per Taz: root maas). If explaining like Rashi, wheat and bread dissolve — Rambam would follow R' Yosef. In truth halacha follows Rabbah over three; Rambam should explain accordingly; Taz: Rambam explains like R' Yosef because of repugnance — cannot explain like Rashi lest one think he rules R' Yosef; must say Rambam takes leniency Rabbah did not mention — if not hard, no crumbling. Tur is clearer — rules Rabbah, does not distinguish Dead Sea from rivers; Beit Yosef: stringent where we are not expert which waters are strong — distant from us. Taz Yoreh De'ah 146 note 11.
