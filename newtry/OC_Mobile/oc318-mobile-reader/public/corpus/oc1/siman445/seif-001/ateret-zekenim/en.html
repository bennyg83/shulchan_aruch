(1) And the custom of burning the chametz in it, and there is throwing it in the oven when baking unleavened bread, and these and these are the words of the living the Omnipresent (from the Rashel in this section in the context):

(ב) But if one wants to burn immediately, etc. — it appears to me that nevertheless tomorrow he fulfills the mitzvah of biur through burning, like that chametz left on the 14th, or he burns the utensil for two reasons: one, to fulfill the mitzvah of biur on the 14th itself, which is primary; and also so people will be careful afterward not to eat chametz. If he burned before the sixth hour, it is permitted to benefit from the coals. The Tur concludes that nevertheless it is good to make a bonfire by itself, and one should not deviate from custom; especially where there is no zagir (watchman), one burns chametz in the courtyard and not in the house so as not to benefit. They are accustomed to burn even in the fifth hour like R' Yehudah, and also to be stringent like the Sages that ash is forbidden, for we take here and there stringently — so writes Maharshal. If he cooked or baked that bread and that dish, they are forbidden in benefit, and even the coals are forbidden in benefit; and even though we hold that for all burned items ash is permitted, nevertheless since it was after the sixth hour, the ash is also forbidden in benefit (Beit Yosef and the Rosh).
