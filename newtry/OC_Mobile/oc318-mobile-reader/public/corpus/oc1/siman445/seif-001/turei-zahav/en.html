(א) Or crumbles, etc. Rabbah holds chametz needs crumbling even for throwing to sea; distinguishes Dead Sea — no ships, no crumbling — unlike other rivers. R' Yehudah: chametz needs no crumbling since repugnant; baraita needing crumbling — wheat vs. bread per Rashi: fermented wheat in sack — scatter on water; Rosh ruled Rabbah; Shulchan Aruch here is Rambam's wording — implies R' Yosef distinguishing wheat and bread; "if chametz hard," etc. Why rule R' Yehudah when we rule Rabbah over three? Tur did not distinguish hard chametz — holds Rabbah. Rambam also — difference Dead Sea vs. rivers not per Rashi but Dead Sea's cold salty water cuts fast. R' Yosef's wheat/bread distinction — Rambam per repugnance: lenient only for bread that repulses in water; wheat can be rinsed. Rambam rules Rabbah — Dead Sea vs. rivers from water strength; Rambam omitted wheat/bread — only R' Yehudah. Now Tur's omission of Dead Sea distinction is fine — we do not decide; be stringent in all waters requiring crumbling — so appears to me, though Maggid Mishneh wrote Rambam like R' Yehudah — my soul is divided; he is correct in my humble opinion.

(ב) However, if the loaf is small — thus in Tur, and his reason is because it is a double doubt, doubt matza doubt chametz, and say chametz — perhaps they ate; and it appears at first glance that for Rambam, who holds that even in rabbinic doubt checking is required, this too would not help with a double doubt; but in truth even for him it helps because of a double doubt in a rabbinic matter; and it is difficult for Tur, who holds that a double doubt helps — if so, it is difficult from what is in the Gemara: R' Yehudah in the mishnah is not concerned lest a weasel dragged — if so there is no end to the matter; and the Gemara challenges: we see a mouse takes, etc. — why not say it ate; and answers there is no doubt of eating removing from certainty of chametz — see there. And therefore it is difficult why the mishnah needs the reason because there is no end — it should follow because it is a double doubt whether they dragged or not, and say they dragged — perhaps it ate; rather certainly a double doubt does not help here, as a rabbinic doubt does not help for Rambam; and on this it is difficult — if so, even for a large loaf one might say a double doubt, doubt chametz doubt matza, and say chametz — perhaps it called its fellow and they ate it; even a large loaf — in such a case they said in one who deposits, daf 11a, that Tosafot brought in the name of Yerushalmi: these mice are wicked — when they see pregnancy they do not nurse but call their fellows who eat with them — see there; rather certainly we do not say here that a double doubt should help; and Rambam did not bring this distinction at all, as stated.
