Or he crumbles it, etc. Rabbah holds that chametz requires crumbling even when throwing it into the sea, and he distinguishes between the Dead Sea, where it does not require crumbling, which Rashi explains is because ships do not travel there, and other rivers. Rav Yosef holds that chametz does not require crumbling, since it becomes repulsive; and that which the baraisa teaches that it requires crumbling is not difficult, for this refers to wheat kernels and that refers to bread. Rashi explains that wheat kernels that became chametz should not be thrown into the water in a sack, but should be scattered over the water. The Rosh ruled like Rabbah. The words of the Shulchan Aruch here are the language of the Rambam, and it implies that he holds like Rav Yosef, who distinguishes between wheat kernels and bread; therefore he wrote, and if the chametz was hard, etc. This is difficult: why did he rule like Rav Yosef, when we hold like Rabbah against Rav Yosef except in three cases? Furthermore, the Tur did not distinguish hard chametz, which means he holds like Rabbah, and he wrote that so wrote the Rambam, but this is not so. The Beis Yosef wrote at length to reconcile the words of the Tur and the Rambam. In my humble opinion, it appears that the Rambam too rules like Rabbah, but he holds that the distinction between the Dead Sea and other seas is not like Rashi's explanation; rather, the waters of the Dead Sea are sharp by the nature of the salt and cut the chametz quickly, unlike other waters. Rav Yosef, who distinguishes between wheat kernels and bread, also does not mean as Rashi explained. Rather, the Rambam explains that according to Rav Yosef, who makes the matter depend on repulsiveness, he is lenient only with bread, which becomes repulsive in the moisture of the water; but wheat kernels do not become repulsive, for they can be rinsed well. If so, the Rambam also rules like Rabbah: according to Rabbah the distinction between the Dead Sea and other waters is because of the strength of the water. This matter cannot be measured precisely, for sometimes there is such a distinction among other waters, this one strong and this one weak. Therefore he gave a rule according to the nature of the chametz, whether it will be cut apart quickly in the place where he throws it. The Rambam did not mention the distinction between bread and wheat kernels at all, since it belongs only to Rav Yosef. Now it is understandable that the Tur did not mention the distinction between the Dead Sea and other waters, because the matter is not fixed for us, as I wrote; therefore he was stringent with all waters, requiring crumbling. So it appears to me, although the Maggid Mishneh wrote that the Rambam ruled like Rav Yosef. My soul has said my portion, and it is correct with the help of Heaven.
And the custom is to burn it, etc. The reason for this burning appears to be that there is a dispute between Rabbi Yehuda and the Sages whether burning is required, and the dispute among the halachic decisors is extensive; therefore one should practice like the stringent authorities. It therefore appears that one who fulfilled the mitzvah of burning with his chametz from the sixth hour and afterward pieces of chametz were found does not need to burn them, but should destroy them from the world. For from the sixth hour and onward we are lenient with chametz as with other prohibitions, both regarding a mashehu and regarding a spoiled flavor, as I wrote below in siman 447. Therefore, since even on Pesach itself we hold like the Rabbis, only that because of mere custom we practice like Rabbi Yehuda, this is unlike here, where it is before night and he already fulfilled the mitzvah of burning in the sixth hour; therefore he need not burn it. So it appears to me.
