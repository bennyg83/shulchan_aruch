(א) He burns it. In our Gemara: "and the Sages say, even crumbles," etc. — certainly burning is preferable; "even crumbles" suffices (Bach). But Rif, Rosh, and Sages' girsa: "crumbles," etc. — implies specifically crumbling; so Tur — for the Sages it is among buried items whose ash is forbidden; we learned end Temurah all buried items shall not be burned lest one benefit from ash. It is astounding the Rambam ruled ash forbidden yet permits burning; end Hilchot Pesulei HaMukdashim he ruled all buried items shall not be burned — though stringent in burning, lenient in ash, for buried ash is forbidden. Possibly: he shall not burn to treat it like burned items; but if they want to burn without benefiting from ash, it is permitted, and they did not decree lest one benefit from ash; so Maharil burns and buries coals.

(ב) Hard chametz — such as wheat requires crumbling; so Maggid Mishneh for the Rambam. One must say he holds each grain is cut in two or three parts — he does not hold like Rashi that scattering suffices; if so, why say "if chametz was hard," etc. — the reason is not hardness but being in a sack. Rather as we said; soft bread does not need crumbling. Rabbeinu Yehudah explains soft bread needs crumbling, wheat does not — see there. In my humble opinion one should be stringent and crumble both. If throwing into privy, no crumbling; no concern a dog or pig will eat it — if so, fish in water would eat too; we are not concerned since he does not place it for eating. If collected water with fish, forbidden to throw — he benefits. Forbidden in all melachot when burning time arrives until he burns, as siman 431.
