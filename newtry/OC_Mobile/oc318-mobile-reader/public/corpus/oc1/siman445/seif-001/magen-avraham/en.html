He burns it. In our Gemara the text reads: and the Sages say, even crumbling, etc. Certainly burning is preferable, only that even crumbling is enough. Bach. But the text of the Rif and the Rosh is: and the Sages say, he crumbles, etc., implying specifically crumbling. So too it appears from the Tur, who wrote that according to the Rabbis, it is among those items that are buried, whose ashes are forbidden; and we learned at the end of Temurah that all items that are buried should not be burned, because we decree lest one come to benefit from their ashes. It is astonishing that the Rambam ruled that its ashes are forbidden, and nevertheless ruled that it is permitted to burn it; and at the end of Hilchos Pesulei HaMukdashin he ruled that all items that are buried should not be burned, for although one is stringent by burning them, he is lenient regarding their ashes, since the ashes of buried items are forbidden. End quote. Perhaps this is what he means: one should not burn it in order to treat it like items that are burned, so as to benefit from its ashes; but if they want to burn it without benefiting from its ashes, it is permitted, and we do not decree lest he benefit from its ashes. So wrote Maharil: he burns it and buries the coals.
The chametz is hard. Such as wheat, it requires crumbling. So wrote the Maggid Mishneh according to the Rambam's view. One must say that he holds that he should cut each wheat kernel into two or three pieces, for one cannot say that he holds like Rashi, that it is enough to scatter the wheat kernels; if so, what does he mean by saying, and if the chametz was hard, etc.? The reason would not be because it is hard, but because they are placed in a sack. Rather, it is certainly as we said. Soft bread does not require crumbling, for it is soft and dissolves in water. The Beis Yosef explains that soft bread requires crumbling and wheat kernels do not require crumbling; see there. To me it appears that one should be stringent and crumble both. However, if he threw it into the privy, it does not require crumbling, and there is no concern that a dog or pig will eat it; for if so, in water too the fish would eat it. Rather, we are not concerned, since he is not placing it before it to eat. But it appears to me that if he has collected water containing fish, it is forbidden to throw it there, for he benefits from it. One is forbidden in all labors when the time of burning arrives until he burns the chametz, as written in siman 431.
