Seif 1 — How to destroy chametz: burn or crumble, etc. Note: see Magen Avraham s.k. 1 who cites Rambam end Hilchot Pesulei HaMukdashim: all buried items shall not be burned — though stringent in burning, lenient in ash, etc. I questioned Pesachim 27: why did the Sages say to R' Yehudah every din beginning stringent ending lenient is not a din — if no wood to burn, etc., better to say: stringent in burning, lenient in ash, ending lenient? Possibly they said so to R' Yehudah per his view end Temurah: if he wants to be stringent and burn, he may; he does not consider leniency in ash a leniency, or holds ash of burned items is forbidden — therefore for him it is not "ending lenient" from this. Better: at biur time "you shall destroy" is in all ways; for R' Yehudah it is among burned items so ash permitted — leniency; for rabbis among buried, ash forbidden — only leniency in burning before biur when even buried ash permitted. They said: if you judge before biur, when even buried ash is permitted, if no wood he sits idle — necessarily ending lenient. Burning is stringent for even buried then ash permitted. See Ohr Chadash on my question; Pri Megadim siman 449 note 1 seems like my view. Further question on Gemara: R' Yehudah returned and judged another din from notar — chametz lighter than notar? Notar is lav linked to aseh only when violating lav; leaving first to fulfill aseh forbidden — but Tosafot Pesachim 29: holding chametz to destroy does not violate lav — proves one may violate lav initially to fulfill aseh; chametz lighter than notar — why proof? Requires study. A scholar asked: proof from kodshim to hullin? Kodshim stricter. It fits: we learn leniency from kodshim as Taz Yoreh De'ah 3 — kal va'chomer hullin from kodshim; R' Yehudah: if burning stringent, kal va'chomer from kodshim; what will they answer "ending lenient"? If burning lenient, still learn leniency hullin from kodshim as Taz — study well. (Same seif) Note Magen Avraham s.k. 2: chametz is not called communal positive mitzvah; see Hilchot Megillah 696:7 — meat and wine on Purim are communal aseh; opposite Magen Avraham here; see my novellae Pesachim 92 and Or LaChayim 5681 41a. My resolution: Shulchan Aruch Megillah why not write opposite — communal aseh of Purim overrides rabbinic aninut of individual, or aseh overriding aseh; when mitzvah needs intent (or stam intent), intentional for mitzvah — aseh overrides lav or stricter aseh; when act permitted without intent but forbidden by another aseh/lav blocking — blocking aseh overrides blocked aseh; Purim eating without intent is mitzvah, only blocked by aninut — aninut communal somewhat like Torah — cannot override; Megillah wording "permitted in meat and wine" — forbidding would be aninut overriding Purim aseh, impossible; chametz needs intent for biur, cannot do on Shabbat — Shabbat aseh overrides chametz aseh; chametz not true communal aseh since individual does not discharge public; for aseh-docheh-aseh need great stringency — Purim not enough; Megillah precise wording explains Magen Avraham; resolves my Pesachim question why not Purim aseh overrides aninut on Pesach — Pesach needs intent, not communal aseh, aninut stronger; see my work.
