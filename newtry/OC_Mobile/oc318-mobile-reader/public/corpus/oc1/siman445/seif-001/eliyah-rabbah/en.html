[1] And if the chametz was hard, etc. Magen Avraham was stringent that even wheat requires crumbling — cutting each grain in three parts — as the Rambam and Chok Yaakov rejected his view. In my humble opinion the essence is Magen Avraham, as clear from Rambam's explanation on the mishnah beginning chapter Kol Sha'ah; also in the Rif there: "wheat of chametz requires grinding and then throwing to the sea" — end quote. Although Beit Chadash implies it is not from the Rif, it is a gloss of a great authority — but specifically dry wheat. Magen Avraham: hard chametz thrown into privy needs no crumbling — no concern dog or pig will eat since not placed for eating; if he has collected water with fish, forbidden to throw — he benefits.
