He burns it, or crumbles it and scatters it to the wind. This is the dispute in the Mishnah at the beginning of Perek Kol Shaah: Rabbi Yehuda says the destruction of chametz is only by burning, and the Sages say even crumbling it and scattering it to the wind or throwing it into the sea. The Rambam ruled, in the language of the Mechaber, like the Sages. Although at the end of Hilchos Pesulei HaMukdashin the Rambam ruled like the Sages at the end of Temurah, that all items that are buried, meaning that scattering to the wind or throwing into the sea is included among buried items, as the Tur wrote, should not be burned, one must say that this applies specifically to those items about which it teaches only that they are buried. This is unlike chametz, regarding which the Sages explicitly taught that it may be burned and buried; this is not included in the ordinary category of buried items. Therefore the Rambam did not count chametz there, neither among those that are buried nor among those that are burned. So wrote Responsa Emunas Shmuel, siman 55. One should not ask: since in any case items that are buried are forbidden to burn because, since the buried items' coal and ash are forbidden and the ashes of items that are burned are permitted, one might come to be lenient with their coal and ash, and the Rambam wrote similarly there at the end of Hilchos Pesulei HaMukdashin explicitly, as follows: all items that are buried should not be burned; although you are stringent by burning them, you are lenient with their ashes. If so, since with chametz its coal and ash are forbidden like buried items, how may they be burned? One can answer that because of the stringency of chametz, more than other prohibitions of benefit, in that one transgresses bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei, as Tosafos wrote at the beginning of Pesachim, it is preferable to be stringent with burning it, for then he will not come to the prohibition of bal yera'eh and bal yimatzei, even though he was lenient regarding its ashes. One can further say that since everyone destroys it according to its law before six hours, as above in siman 434, then its coal and ash are permitted for benefit. Only if he did not destroy it does he destroy it afterward, and this is uncommon; the Sages did not decree in all this. All this appears to me according to the Rambam. But Ri and the Rosh, although they have another version in the words of the Sages and do not read even crumbles it, but only crumbles it, etc., nevertheless hold that even according to the Sages it is permitted to burn it, as is explicit there in the Talmud according to the Sages: their reason is, if he did not find wood to burn it, should he sit idle when the Torah said tashbisu? In any event, the Rosh ruled explicitly in Perek Kol Shaah that regarding all items that are buried, if one wants to be stringent upon himself and burn them, he may, like Rabbi Yehuda at the end of Temurah. The reason must be that since the anonymous Mishnah regarding a firstborn donkey is established there like Rabbi Yehuda. This is the reason for our custom, that we are accustomed to burn the chametz, which satisfies everyone, even those halachic decisors who rule like Rabbi Yehuda that the destruction of chametz is only by burning. See Beis Hillel, Yoreh Deah siman 294, and Issur VeHeter, klal 21, law 13. I wrote at length about all this because I saw that the Magen Avraham explained the Talmudic sugya according to the Rambam in a very forced and distant manner. What he wrote as the view of the Rif and the Rosh is also incorrect, and the matters are clear as I wrote.
The chametz is hard. The same applies if there were wheat kernels in a sack: he scatters them from the sack and throws them into the sea, but he need not grind them or crumble them. So is the implication of Rashi and the halachic decisors. In Magen Avraham he wrote that the implication of the Maggid Mishneh, according to the Rambam's view, is that the wheat kernels themselves also require crumbling, but his words are not at all compelled, and I have not seen people practice this way. The Magen Avraham further wrote that if he throws it into the privy, it does not require crumbling, and we do not decree lest a living creature, such as a dog, pig, or fish in the water, come to eat from it, since he is not placing it before them to eat. But if he has collected water with fish, it is forbidden to throw it into it. End quote. In Issur VeHeter, klal 21, law 13, he wrote as follows: the custom is to pour a cooked dish of meat and milk into the privy, and this is its burial, or into a river, which is like burning, for no creature can benefit from it; but not into the street, even if there is no dog there. End quote.
And it is good to burn it by day. That is, before the sixth hour. Maharshal wrote in responsum siman 26 that one should burn it with the hoshanah. Similarly, Maharil wrote in siman 153 that one should heat the oven with the lulav; Maharil was accustomed to use it for heating at the mouth of the oven at the time of baking the matzos. See above, siman 25.
The choice is in his hand. In Responsa Maharik, siman 170, he wrote as follows: even so, he fulfills the mitzvah of tashbisu in its time, since at the time of destruction he has no chametz in his house, even if he destroyed it thirty days before Pesach. End quote. And if he gave it to a non-Jew, etc. The Bach wrote in responsum siman 147 that it is forbidden to give his slave or maidservant the chametz remaining on the fourteenth day if the chametz is so much that it cannot be eaten before the time of prohibition, since their sustenance is upon him, and it is like paying his debt with a prohibition of benefit. End quote.
