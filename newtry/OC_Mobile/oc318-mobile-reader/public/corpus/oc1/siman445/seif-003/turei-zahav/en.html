This is the language of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that the Beis Yosef brings: Rabbeinu Baruch Kohen forbade throwing chametz, and brought proof from the Yerushalmi, which says that one may not feed chametz even to other dogs. However, before its prohibition, one may throw it wherever ravens are found, since it is not yet forbidden for benefit; but if he finds it after its time of prohibition, he may not leave it there so that the ravens should take it, for the essential destruction of chametz is burning. End quote. In the next siman I will write the view of Rashi. End quote from the Beis Yosef. Now, what is written here, and if he finds chametz after its time of prohibition: if its meaning is that if he found it in the house after the time of prohibition, he may not place the chametz in his domain in a place where ravens eat it and have that be its destruction, because destruction is only by burning, then so it appears in the Beis Yosef in the next siman, where he wrote, in one explanation of Rashi's words according to the Tur's view, that removing it from his domain is sufficient after the time of prohibition, unlike the view of the Hagahos Maimoniyos that I wrote in the previous siman. This implies that this gloss also speaks of removing it from his domain to another ownerless domain. If so, one cannot settle what the Shulchan Aruch wrote here, and if he found it afterward, etc., that the ravens had not eaten it, which implies that it refers to the previous case, namely that he threw it before the prohibition and the ravens did not eat it, meaning that after he found that chametz in the place where he threw it; this contradicts his explanation in the next siman. Furthermore, according to the Shulchan Aruch's explanation, it is difficult, for it is not reasonable at all that after he threw it permissibly he would afterward become obligated, since the place is ownerless; it is as though he gave it to a non-Jew before the prohibition. Would one think that he must return and take it from the non-Jew after the prohibition? Perforce one must say that here in the Shulchan Aruch this is the explanation: the ownerless place he mentions does not mean truly ownerless, but it is his domain, and there is a place there that is ownerless for the ravens. Therefore he said that even if he found it in the place where he placed it before the prohibition, he must take it and destroy it. So too it appears well from the Levush, who wrote that although he made it ownerless, it is considered his, since it is not in the domain of others. If so, the Beis Yosef contradicts the explanation he wrote in the next siman, as I wrote. Furthermore, this explanation is not possible; for if so, the first clause of the gloss also speaks in this way, that the ravens will eat it in his domain, and nevertheless it is permitted because it is before the prohibition. This is not so, for then it contradicts an explicit Talmud at the first chapter, regarding the mouse, from which it is proven that it speaks even before the prohibition, and there is no permission on account of perhaps the mouse will eat it, for doubt does not remove certainty. Would we say here that it is permitted to throw it for this reason? The Beis Yosef already wrote in Shulchan Aruch, siman 433, that definite chametz requires checking, and the Rema challenged there from this case before us, but did not decide his view there. The main point appears to me to be that the Shulchan Aruch's ruling there is certainly true: definite chametz requires checking even before the time of prohibition. Here the Hagahos Maimoniyos does not speak of chametz in one's domain, to be lenient because mice may eat it; that is forbidden even before its prohibition unless one stands over them so they will eat it before the prohibition. Rather, the Hagahos Maimoniyos speaks where he throws it into a truly ownerless place. This he may do even close to the time of prohibition, when they certainly will not eat it before the prohibition, for what concern is it to him, since the prohibition will not be in his domain? And there is no prohibition of benefit, even to other dogs, because that applies specifically if he gives it before them after the prohibition. Therefore the latter clause of the Hagahos Maimoniyos says, and if he finds it after the time, meaning that he finds it in his house after the prohibition, he cannot exempt himself from destruction on the basis that ravens will take it in an ownerless place, even during the time of rabbinic prohibition, namely the sixth hour. Above, where it is forbidden with definite chametz, that is because it is in his domain; this is forbidden even before the prohibition, lest it remain after the prohibition. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, explained the words of the Hagahos Maimoniyos in siman 433 by distinguishing between a courtyard shared by many people, which is called ownerless, and a courtyard designated to one person. This does not settle the matter, for partners are fully obligated like one person in all matters.
