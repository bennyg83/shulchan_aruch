In Shulchan Aruch, se'if 3: and if he found it after the time it became forbidden, etc. In Responsa Pnei Yehoshua, siman 12, he wrote about one who had chametz in a non-Jew's house and forgot to sell it. After midday he remembered, and he had nullified his chametz before midday as is customary, and he asked what to do. He instructed him by way of advice to place it in an ownerless place for dogs or pigs; and if non-Jews acquire it, he will be able to buy it from them cheaply after Pesach, or they may return it to him. For presumably the non-Jew in whose house the chametz is located will not allow dogs to ruin it, but will acquire it, and afterward perhaps return it to him, since he knows him. See there, where he discusses at length the words of Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of the Rivash, from whom this law originates. See the words of the later authorities above, siman 433 se'if 6.
