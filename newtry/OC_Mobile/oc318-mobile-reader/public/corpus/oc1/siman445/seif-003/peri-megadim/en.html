And if. See the Taz; his words are difficult to understand. If we explain what he wrote, of blessed memory, that from the sixth hour, according to Rabbi Yehuda, when there is wood, the mitzvah is to burn it in the sixth hour, but if there is no wood, he need not wait until the beginning of the seventh hour, when in one moment he will also transgress a positive commandment or a negative commandment according to the one who holds so, then his words are certainly very difficult. One can say that this is the explanation: on the contrary, from folio 27b there is proof like the Rosh. For if not, why did Rashi, of blessed memory, not explain the leniency in the sixth hour according to Rabbi Yehuda, that when there is no wood he sits idle during the sixth hour until the beginning of the seventh and transgresses a positive commandment for a moment? And according to his own view, the Torah said tashbisu a moment before the sixth hour; see folio 4b. Rather, if at the end of the fifth hour he sees that he has no wood, he is obligated to destroy it by any means at the end of the fifth hour, and not sit idle in the sixth, since he already destroyed it at the end of the fifth. Only in such a case does the Rosh hold according to Rashi, of blessed memory, that at the end of the fifth hour, when he sees he will not have wood in the sixth, he destroys it in the fifth by any means. This is unlike one traveling in a caravan: when he has no wood he sits completely idle, for the time of destruction has not yet arrived and he has no wood. Even according to the Rosh, in the fifth hour it is specifically by any means for the reason that perhaps he will not have wood; this is unlike a long time before Pesach, as stated. In the Rosh he did not conclude, in the fifth hour when it is permitted for benefit, as written in the Tur, and this is as I wrote. A practical difference emerges regarding one who leaves in a caravan, who checks and destroys it by burning according to the custom, because perhaps the halachah follows Rabbi Yehuda, and he nullifies on the night of checking; but on the day, when he burns it, he does not nullify, only when Pesach arrives.
