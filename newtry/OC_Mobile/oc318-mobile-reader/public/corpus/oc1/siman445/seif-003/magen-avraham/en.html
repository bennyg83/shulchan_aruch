In a place where ravens, etc. This is the case of that law that a courtyard does not require checking. Hagahos Maimoniyos. If so, it is proven that it speaks of his own courtyard, for if it were an ownerless courtyard, such as in the street, what checking would be relevant? Even his own chametz, if he made it ownerless before six hours, is permitted, as the Tur wrote in siman 437 and the Beis Yosef in siman 448 in the name of the Yerushalmi, and Darchei Moshe in the name of Or Zarua, for what difference is there between giving it to a non-Jew and making it ownerless? Rather, perforce it does not speak of an ownerless place. What he concluded, although it is lying in an ownerless place, nevertheless, etc., means that since it is lying in his courtyard, it is ownerless to anyone who wants to take it; nevertheless, since it is lying in his domain, he is obligated to destroy it according to its law, namely by burning according to Rabbi Yehuda. So too it appears from Hagahos Maimoniyos, as I wrote. This is the language of the Kesef Mishneh: those people who place chametz at the sides of the public domain at the sixth hour are acting improperly, for once the sixth hour arrives one must destroy it from the world; so too it appears from Hagahos Maimoniyos. End quote. This implies that before the sixth hour it is permitted. See what I wrote in siman 433 and in Yoreh Deah siman 146.
