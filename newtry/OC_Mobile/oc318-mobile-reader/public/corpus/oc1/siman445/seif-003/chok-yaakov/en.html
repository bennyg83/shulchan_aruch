He may throw it in a place where ravens are found. See the Taz, who asks: how is this case different from above in siman 438, regarding a mouse that dragged a loaf before us, where a doubt that a mouse ate it does not remove certainty, and checking is required? In truth there is no difficulty at all, for specifically with a mouse, whose way is to drag into covered places such as holes and cracks, checking is required. This is unlike placing them in an exposed place before ravens, where presumably he will see it if they do not eat it, since ravens do not ordinarily hide and cover from people as mice do. As for the further difficulty of the Taz and other later authorities from above in siman 433, I already resolved everything there properly in s.k. 12; see there.
For the place is ownerless. The Taz and Magen Avraham wrote that he does not mean literally ownerless, such as where it lies in the public domain, for then according to everyone he need not destroy it, since it is not lying in his domain; and before the time of its prohibition, even initially it is enough to make it ownerless. See the Talmud in Bava Kama folio 29; see there. Rather, here he speaks of his courtyard, and he calls it ownerless, meaning that the chametz is ownerless for ravens and the like. What the Taz asked about the language of Hagahos Maimoniyos that was brought in the Beis Yosef is not difficult at all, for certainly the words of the gloss at first speak at the time of destruction, when destruction specifically is required, and even placing it in an ownerless place does not help, as is clear from the proof he brought from the Yerushalmi. But the end of his words speaks of his own courtyard, and this is clear, unlike the Bach, who explained in siman 433 that Hagahos Maimoniyos speaks of a courtyard of two partners. The Taz properly challenged him and wrote as follows: partners are obligated just like one person in all matters. End quote. One should not ask that it is written, no chametz shall be seen to you, and from the word to you one should exclude partners, as stated in Chullin folio 136, and see Tosafos in Sukkah folio 72. One can say that it is written, you shall destroy leaven from your houses, which implies even of partners; and to you excludes that of hekdesh and that of others, as stated there in Pesachim.
