Magen Avraham s.k. 5. That the view of the Rambam and the halachic decisors is that with idolatry even, etc., they are considered more present at the time of heating than wood; see there. This is how his words should be explained: specifically where the flame is opposite it, for although the oven is also a cause, nevertheless, since the improvement of the wood is recognizable in the bread and the improvement of the oven is not so recognizable, or the cause of the oven is nullified relative to the cause of the wood, it is forbidden. This is unlike its coal and ash, where its improvement is not so recognizable, and it is like the cause of the oven; and since this and this cause it is permitted, it is permitted. But with cooking over coals, where there is only one cause, it is forbidden. Afterward the Magen Avraham wrote that the main ruling appears to him like the view of the Shach, that with idolatry even cooking over coals, one word is missing and it should read dimming coals, or with the heat of the oven, is forbidden. That is to say, he cooked the bread over dimming coals or with the heat of the oven; so too is the language of the Talmud, cooked it over coals, and it refers there to cooking bread; see there. But not for the Shach's reason, for the Shach holds that since with idolatry its coal and ash are forbidden, it is like a flame opposite it, and its improvement is recognizable in the bread and is stronger than the cause of the oven. According to this, if there were idolatry wood and permitted wood here, it should be permitted. The Magen Avraham holds that this is not the reason; rather, it is because we say there that with hekdesh wood, which is not nullified even in a thousand, even according to the Rabbis, who hold that even where the flame is opposite it there is no improvement of wood in the bread, nevertheless it is forbidden because since it is not nullified, we treat the cause as a mashehu of forbidden benefit. If so, according to this, even with dimming coals, granted that relative to them the cause of the oven is also significant, it is nevertheless forbidden; for what does it help that it is this and this cause it, since it is not nullified at all? With this the view of the Tur is understandable, for he did not distinguish between a new and old oven; he should have written that with an old oven it is permitted if he cooked with dimming coals or with its ash. But the Tur wrote without qualification that according to the Rabbis it is forbidden in every case. Perforce, the reason is that since it is not nullified, this and this cause it does not help, as with hekdesh and idolatry. The practical difference between this reason and the Shach's reason is that according to the Shach's reason, if there is chametz wood or idolatry wood and permitted wood, one may permit it, for just as the improvement of the forbidden wood is recognizable in the bread, so too the improvement of the permitted wood is recognizable. But according to this reason, even in such a case it is forbidden, since chametz or idolatry is not nullified. However, in my humble opinion, in truth one can say that the Shach also agrees to this, for the Shach's reason is not that the cause of the baking of the coals or the heat of the oven is more recognizable in the bread than the cause of the oven; rather, the reason is that the improvement of the wood recognizable in the bread is like the wood itself being present in the bread, and it does not help that there is another cause here. If so, even if he placed permitted wood as well, it also does not help, since the improvement of the forbidden wood is like being present; what does it help that the improvement of the permitted wood is also present? Afterward the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of Maharil that even for its coal and ash, nullification does not help. It is not comparable to wood that fell from a palm tree on Yom Tov, where one may add to it and nullify it, although it is a davar sheyesh lo matirin and is not nullified even in a thousand. Nevertheless, it is permitted there because the prohibition is burned away. If so, here too, granted that chametz is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, nevertheless its prohibition is burned away. Although with chametz, even its coal and ash are forbidden, nevertheless the same is true with muktzeh wood, for the coals are also muktzeh, and even so, since the prohibition is burned away, nullifying it is proper. Maharil answered this: wood is not food, and therefore the prohibition that comes from it into the cooked dish is not in its normal manner of benefit, and for this reason the reasoning that the prohibition is burned away helps, so that it is not forbidden because of davar sheyesh lo matirin. This is unlike chametz, which is food; if so, that benefit which comes into the cooked dish is somewhat in its normal manner of benefit, for it comes from food to food. Although that benefit comes through nullification, nevertheless chametz forbids by a mashehu, and burning away the prohibition does not help except where the benefit that comes from the prohibition comes from a prohibition that is not edible. But with chametz, nullification does not help. I heard that one may say that even according to the one who says chametz is not a davar sheyesh lo matirin because it returns to its prohibition the following year, nevertheless regarding the prohibition of the ash, the ash is indeed not nullified because of davar sheyesh lo matirin, for the ash does not return to its prohibition; by the following year it is already like chametz charred before its time, which is permitted. The Magen Avraham then wrote that Tosafos wrote in Shabbos that oil, meat, and grain are considered more present than wood at the time of heating. If so, where he placed other wood together with the chametz, it does not help even without the reasoning he wrote above that it is not nullified even in a thousand, because the chametz is considered more present. Nevertheless, there is a practical difference where he also placed grain or matzah and the like with the chametz at the time of heating. Still, one needs the distinction of Maharil regarding coals of chametz, for in such a case, where they have already become coals, it requires examination whether we say they are considered more present; Tosafos wrote only that at the time of heating it is considered more present. See Magen Avraham siman 507, s.k. 2, and siman 325, s.k. 9, regarding muktzeh wood; see there. See also siman 509, s.k. 15.
