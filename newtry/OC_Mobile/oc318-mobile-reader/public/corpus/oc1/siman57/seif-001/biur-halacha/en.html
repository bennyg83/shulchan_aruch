And they respond after him - See Magen Avraham, and see Mishnah Berurah citing Eliyah Rabbah. See also Peri Megadim in siman 124, letter 18, who leaves the matter unresolved, because the words of Magen Avraham there explicitly indicate that he too maintains that one may respond together with the congregation even though he did not initially hear the chazzan say 'Barechu.' In truth, according to their understanding of Magen Avraham's intent in this siman, it is difficult to understand why Magen Avraham specified a case where the chazzan said it quietly. The same should apply even if he did not say it quietly, but the individual stood at a distance and did not hear him. Therefore, it appears obvious to me that Magen Avraham agrees with Eliyah Rabbah, as he himself wrote at the end of siman 124. Rather, if the chazzan said it quietly and nine people did not hear him, but only a few individuals heard and responded to 'Barechu,' and through them the sound was then heard by others, who also responded to 'Barechu,' an individual who hears the congregation responding may certainly not respond to 'Barechu' together with them. They too responded improperly, since at least nine people did not hear the one who recited the blessing himself. This is Magen Avraham's intent when he writes that the chazzan said it quietly. He means that the individual is standing near the chazzan, sees that the chazzan is speaking quietly, and estimates that nine people certainly did not hear him, but only a few individuals, through whom the congregation then heard the response. He should therefore not respond to 'Barechu' after them, but should answer only Amen, according to all opinions. Regrettably, many people stumble in this matter when they recite the blessing over the Torah quietly, so that only those standing near them at the bimah hear them. Through those nearby, the congregation farther away hears the response and answers, 'Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One, forever and ever.' Through their indolence, they cause the public to sin.
