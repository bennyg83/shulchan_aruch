"And they answer after him"—see Magen Avraham; and see Mishnah Berurah in the name of Ohr Zaru'a; and see Pri Megadim in siman 124 letter 18, who left the difficulty that from Magen Avraham's words there it is proven explicitly that he too holds one may answer with the congregation even if one did not initially hear the shaliach tzibbur saying Barekhu. But indeed according to their words, per Magen Avraham's intention in this siman it is difficult why Magen Avraham wrote that if the shaliach tzibbur said silently—even if he did not say silently but only stood far away and did not hear, likewise. Therefore it seems simple that Magen Avraham too concedes to Ohr Zaru'a, as he himself wrote at the end of siman 124; except that if the shaliach tzibbur said silently and nine people did not hear from him but only some individuals answered Barekhu and through them the voice reached others and they too answered Barekhu—certainly an individual who hears the congregation blessing may not answer Barekhu with them, for they too answered improperly since they did not hear at least nine from the blesser himself; and this is Magen Avraham's intention in what he wrote. And "if the shaliach tzibbur said silently" means one standing next to the shaliach tzibbur who sees that the shaliach tzibbur says silently and estimates that certainly nine did not hear but only some individuals and through them it reached the congregation—therefore he should not answer after them Barekhu but only Amen, per all opinions. And in this matter many people err, Heaven forfend: people who bless on the Torah silently so that only those close to him standing on the bimah hear, and thereby it reaches the congregation from afar and they answer with the full formula—and through their laziness they cause the many to sin.
