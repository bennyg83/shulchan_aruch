(1) The shaliach tzibbur says—in a loud voice so that all the people hear and answer "Barukh the Omnipresent l'olam va'ed."

(2) And they answer after him—and if one did not hear from the shaliach tzibbur when he said Barekhu but only heard from the congregation that they said "Barukh the Omnipresent l'olam va'ed," he answers with them likewise with the full formula; but if he heard from the shaliach tzibbur alone, when they answer with the full formula he should not answer with them but only answer Amen on his words [Ohr Zaru'a, and this is the halakhah—not like Magen Avraham].

(3) And the shaliach tzibbur returns—in order to include himself too among the blessers. And if there are not ten there besides him, if he did not answer with them he does not fulfill his obligation—Magen Avraham in the name of Agudah [it is difficult for me: even if there are ten besides him he likewise does not fulfill his own obligation since he did not answer with them; and regarding the rest it seems they fulfill in every manner, for it is no worse than if one were praying or sleeping, for we hold above siman 55 seif 6 that they combine].

(4) And he says "Barukh the Omnipresent ha-mevorakh"—Sefer Shnei Luchot haBrit wrote [regarding laws of birkat haTorah, and the same here] that some shlichei tzibbur, when the congregation say "Barukh the Omnipresent ha-mevorakh," etc., answer Amen after them and return and say "Barukh the Omnipresent," etc., and this is an error in their hands—for since they immediately say "Barukh the Omnipresent," etc., there is no need to say Amen [and likewise Pri Megadim in siman 198 and Chayei Adam]. But the congregation should say Amen after the shaliach tzibbur even though they already said at first "Barukh the Omnipresent ha-mevorakh," etc.; but Chayei Adam wrote they need not say Amen, and likewise Pri Megadim in simanim 57 and 198: since they already said with the full formula, why should they say Amen a second time—and believing, behold they already said so. Nevertheless he ruled finally that it is optional for the congregation if they wish to answer Amen on this. In any case, one standing in a place where it is forbidden to interrupt should not answer this Amen.

(5) While he prolongs—specifically during the melody; but when he says the words they should say nothing. And if the chazzan does not prolong in Barekhu, it is better that they be silent and have intention for what the shaliach tzibbur says so that they may answer with the full formula [later authorities]. Those accustomed to say "At the Omnipresent Elohekha tira" when the shaliach tzibbur reaches "et"—Maharshal wrote this is not accepted and is not from the foundation of wisdom.
