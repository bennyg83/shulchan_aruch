(1) The chazzan says. He says it aloud so that the entire congregation will hear and answer, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed forever and ever.”
(2) And they answer after him. If a person did not hear the chazzan say Barchu, but heard only the congregation saying, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed forever and ever,” he should answer that response together with them. If he heard only the chazzan as the chazzan answered, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed forever and ever,” he should not repeat the response with him, but should answer Amen to his words. This is the view of Eliyah Rabbah and Derech HaChayim, unlike Magen Avraham.
(3) And the chazzan repeats. He does so in order to include himself among those who bless. If there are not ten men besides him, and he did not answer together with them, he has not fulfilled his own obligation. Magen Avraham cites this in the name of the Agudah. It is difficult to me, for even if there are ten besides him, he seemingly has not fulfilled his own obligation if he did not answer with them. Regarding the others, however, it appears that they fulfill their obligation in all circumstances, for this is no worse than where one person is praying or sleeping, whom we rule above in siman 55, seif 6, may be counted toward the minyan.
(4) And he says, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed.” Shaarei Efrayim writes, in the laws of Bircas HaTorah, and the same applies here, that some chazzanim answer Amen after the congregation says, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed,” and then repeat, “Blessed is Hashem,” etc. They are mistaken, for since they immediately say, “Blessed is Hashem,” etc., there is no need to say Amen. Peri Megadim in siman 198 and Chayei Adam rule likewise. The congregation, however, may answer Amen after the chazzan, even though they initially said, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed,” etc. Chayei Adam writes that they need not answer Amen, and Peri Megadim in simanim 57 and 198 likewise writes that since they have already said, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed forever and ever,” why should they again say Amen and affirm what they have already stated? Nevertheless, he concludes that the congregation has the option to answer Amen if they wish. In any event, one who is at a point where interruption is forbidden should not answer this Amen.
(5) While he prolongs it. This applies only while the chazzan prolongs the melody. While he is pronouncing the actual words, one should say nothing. If the chazzan does not prolong Barchu, it is better to remain silent, concentrate on what the chazzan says, and then answer, “Blessed is Hashem, Who is blessed forever and ever.” The Acharonim. Regarding those who say, “Hashem your God you shall fear,” when the chazzan reaches the word es, Maharshal writes that this is not a received custom and has no foundation in the wisdom of Kabbalah.
