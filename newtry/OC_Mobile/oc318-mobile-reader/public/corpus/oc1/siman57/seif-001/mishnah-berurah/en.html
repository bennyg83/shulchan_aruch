(א) The shaliach tzibbur says [Barchu] — in a loud voice so that all the people hear and respond "Blessed is the Lord, the blessed God, forever and ever."
(ב) And they respond after him — and if one did not hear from the shaliach tzibbur when he said "Barchu" but only heard from the congregation when they said "Blessed is the Lord, the blessed God, forever and ever," he responds with them as well in "forever and ever." But if he heard only from the shaliach tzibbur when he responds with "forever and ever," he does not respond with him; rather he responds Amen to his words [Agur, and this is the halacha, not like Magen Avraham].
(ג) And the shaliach tzibbur repeats — so that he includes himself also among those who bless. And if there are not ten there besides him, if he did not answer with them he does not fulfill his obligation. Magen Avraham in the name of Agudah [it is difficult to me: even if there are ten besides him, he also does not fulfill his own obligation since he did not answer with them; and regarding the rest, it appears they fulfill in all manners, for it is no worse than if one was praying or sleeping, for we rule above siman 55 seif 6 that one joins].
(ד) And he says "Blessed is the Lord, the blessed" — Sha'agat Arye wrote [regarding the laws of Torah reading, and the same applies here] that some prayer leaders, when the congregation says "Blessed is the Lord, the blessed..." respond Amen after them and repeat saying "Blessed is the Lord..." — and this is an error on their part, for since they immediately say "Blessed is the Lord..." there is no need to say Amen [and so wrote Pri Megadim in siman 198 and Chida]. But the congregation should say Amen after the shaliach tzibbur even though they already said at first "Blessed is the Lord, the blessed..." However Chida wrote that they need not say Amen, and so too Pri Megadim in simanim 57 and 198: since they already said "forever and ever," why should they say Amen a second time — "and believe" they already said so. Nevertheless he ruled finally that it is optional for the congregation: if they wish they respond Amen to this. In any case, one who is standing in a place where it is forbidden to interrupt should not answer this Amen.
(ה) While he prolongs — specifically during the melody; but while he says the words, one should say nothing. And if the chazzan does not prolong in Barchu, it is better that one be silent and intend what the shaliach tzibbur says so that one responds with "forever and ever" [Achronim]. Those accustomed to say "you shall fear the Lord your God" when the shaliach tzibbur reaches "you" — Maharshal wrote this is not accepted and is not from the foundation of wisdom.
