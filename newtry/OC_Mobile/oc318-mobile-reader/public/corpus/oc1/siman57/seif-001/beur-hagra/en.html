Seif 1: The blessed One, etc. This follows Rabbi Yishmael, for that is the Gemara's conclusion there.
And they respond, etc. As stated in the Mishnah, 'In the same manner that one blesses, so do they respond,' and the same applies here.
And he repeats, etc. As stated regarding Bircas HaMazon there, 46b, that he returns to the place where he stopped, in accordance with the Rif's explanation there. Although the Yerushalmi states: Shmuel said that one should not exclude oneself from the collective. They challenged this from the reader of the Torah, who says it. Rabbi Avin answered: Since he says 'the blessed One,' he does not exclude himself from the collective. Here too, in Bircas HaMazon they say 'Nevarech,' yet nevertheless he repeats, etc.
And the custom is, etc. Rambam and the Geonim did not record this, and the Tur likewise protested against it, as stated in Sotah 40a. It was therefore omitted from the Shulchan Aruch. However, in the name of the Rosh, the Tur wrote to justify the practice by explaining that they recite it while the chazzan prolongs the melody, as stated here. He wrote that an allusion to this practice is found in Modim D'Rabbanan. See below, siman 128, seif 26.
