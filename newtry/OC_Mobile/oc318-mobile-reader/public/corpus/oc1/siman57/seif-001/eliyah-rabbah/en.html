[1] "the Omnipresent ha-mevorakh," etc. And that the congregation say "m'vorakh"—the chazzan must say it in order to include himself in the category of the blessing, as Matteh Moshe and the poskim wrote; I explained in Eliyahu Zuta on Chullin 91a: Israel are dearer than ministering angels, for Israel mention the Name after two words "Shema Yisrael the Omnipresent," whereas ministering angels mention only after three words "Kadosh kadosh kadosh the Omnipresent"; and the Gemara asks: but there is "Barukh kevod the Omnipresent mi-mekomo," and answers: since permission was given, it was given. Rashi explained: since they already mentioned in kedushah after three words, permission was given to mention even after two words. It seems to me the same applies to Israel—it is difficult at first glance how they say "Barukh the Omnipresent" after one word; except that since the shaliach tzibbur already mentioned after two words "Barekhu et the Omnipresent," they are permitted to mention; therefore they say "ha-mevorakh," meaning that the shaliach tzibbur already blessed as if they had mentioned.
