[1] Hashem, the blessed One, etc. The congregation says 'the blessed One,' whereas the chazzan must say it in order to include himself among those blessing, as Matteh Moshe and the poskim wrote. I explained in Eliyah Zuta that Chullin 91b states that the Jewish people are more beloved than the ministering angels, because the Jewish people mention the Name after two words, 'Hear, Israel, Hashem,' whereas the ministering angels mention it only after three words, 'Holy, holy, holy, Hashem.' The Gemara asks that there is also the phrase, 'Blessed is the glory of Hashem from His place,' and answers: once permission has been granted, it has been granted. Rashi explains that once they have already mentioned the Name in Kedushah after three words, permission is granted to mention it even after two words. It appears to me that the same principle applies to the Jewish people. For at first glance, it is difficult to understand how they say, 'Blessed is Hashem,' mentioning the Name after only one word. Rather, since the chazzan has already mentioned it after two words, 'Bless Hashem,' they are permitted to mention it. They therefore say 'the blessed One,' meaning that the chazzan has already blessed Him, and it is considered as though they themselves had already mentioned the Name.
