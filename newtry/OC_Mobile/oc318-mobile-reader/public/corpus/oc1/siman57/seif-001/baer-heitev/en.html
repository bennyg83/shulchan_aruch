And they respond. If the chazzan says it quietly and one did not hear him say 'Barechu,' but only heard the congregation responding, he should answer Amen after them, as stated in siman 139, seif 6, in the gloss. Regarding why the chazzan says 'Barechu' rather than 'Nevarech,' as is said in a zimun, see Tosafos Yom Tov on Berachos, chapter 7, mishnah 3, and Responsa Matteh Yosef, part 2, siman 15.
And the chazzan repeats and says. If there are not ten people there apart from him, and he did not respond together with them, he has not fulfilled his obligation. Agudah; Magen Avraham.
Prolongs. This applies specifically during the melody, but while he is pronouncing the words one should say nothing. If the chazzan does not prolong 'Barechu,' it is preferable to remain silent and concentrate on what the chazzan says, so that one can respond, 'Blessed is Hashem, the blessed One, forever and ever.' Levush and Hagahos Yesh Nochalin. Regarding those who customarily say, 'Hashem your God you shall fear,' when the chazzan reaches the word 'es,' Maharshal wrote that this practice is neither from Kabbalah nor founded upon wisdom.
