They are obligated. See Turei Zahav, who concluded that a minor who does not know to Whom we bless cannot discharge another's obligation with his blessing. What he wrote, that he cannot discharge with the reading without a blessing, perhaps is not precise, for blessings do not prevent fulfillment, only rabbinically one must also bless. Yet it is possible to understand the sacred words of our master, the light of Israel, literally, according to what Ramban wrote in his critique of the enumeration of the commandments, that there is a commandment to bless over Torah reading whenever it is read, for, When I call the name of Hashem; see there in commandment 15. If so, one could say that rabbinic blessings do not prevent fulfillment, but a blessing that is from the Torah does prevent fulfillment. I later saw that this is not so, for regarding communal reading and blessing over it, they already blessed at the beginning of the day and had in mind the whole day; and the communal blessing was instituted rabbinically. This requires examination. Also, what he wrote in the name of Bach, that Torah reading is from the Torah every Shabbos: see above in the laws of Sukkah, where it implies that it is rabbinic, and Moshe instituted it on his own, and from the words of Moshe there is only a support. There is no Torah-level reading except Zachor and Parah, and some say all of them are rabbinic; see Magen Avraham. In any event everyone should be careful. Know that if one has a minyan to go hear Parashas Parah, but there will be no place there to read the Megillah at all, certainly Megillah reading takes precedence, even though some say Zachor is Torah law, for he already fulfilled that on the Shabbos when he heard And Amalek came.

If they did not recite the Torah blessings in the morning, and it happened that they read Parashas Zachor from the Torah with ten people without a blessing, one may say that they must go back and read it, if we say that a Torah-level blessing prevents fulfillment, as I wrote above; and if so he has not been exempted, provided that he studies immediately afterward. This requires examination. Also, if it is Torah law, perhaps even a minor who knows to Whom we bless cannot discharge the obligation, for a rabbinic obligation cannot come and discharge a Torah obligation. See siman 688 in the Tur: if they erred and thought it was an ordinary year, and they read the parshiyos in the first Adar, they do not need to read them in the second Adar. But ab initio the four parshiyos are read in the second Adar; see Bach and Beis Yosef there, siman 688. For the mnemonic of fixing the month of Adar, see the Tur and above, siman 428; see there. Regarding what I wrote above, if they erred in the first Adar concerning the parshiyos, see Darkei Moshe, and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah here, paragraph 21, that they must go back and read them in the second; see there.
