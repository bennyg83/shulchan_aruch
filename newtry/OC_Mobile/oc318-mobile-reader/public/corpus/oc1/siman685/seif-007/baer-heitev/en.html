From the Torah. In Shlah he extended to side that Parashat Parah is not d'oraisa — so Bach; so in responsum of Maharil siman 182 he mentioned only Parashat Zachor. In Tur HaShulchan siman 108 he wrote: in Ashrei chapter 7 of Berachot he wrote positive mitzvah d'oraisa to read Parashat Zachor with ten; in Tosafot Shantz they wrote no Torah reading is d'oraisa except Parashat Zachor; in Tosafot Ketuvot on Berachot chapter 2 they explained Parashat Zachor and Parashat Parah are obligatory from Torah, etc.; if so one must be more careful that they hear Parashat Zachor on the 14th from Megillah reading in its time — but the world is not careful thus — end. Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me to resolve world custom — is it written in Torah they must read specifically this Shabbat? Rather Chazal instituted this Shabbat since many are in synagogue near Purim to connect Amalek's action to Haman's action — if so when hearing on Purim Parashat Vayavo Amalek he also remembers Amalek's action and fulfills positive mitzvah; so Raavan siman 2. But Parashat Parah we do not find d'oraisa — end there. Rashal wrote a minor does not ascend as maftir for Zachor and Parah — end. Bach wrote whether minor who knows for whom they bless or does not know — he ascends — end; Taz wrote specifically minor who knows for whom they bless ascends. In siman 282 Rama ruled a minor may read in additional portion or four portions added in Adar — appears when he knows for whom they bless — end there; see Ginat Veradim Orach Chayim klal 1 siman 37. Maharil wrote in all four portions do not mention souls and say tzavtzav dam. Some practice in leap year to establish fast every Thursday from Parashat Sheuvim until Ta'anit Esther; some say also Vayakhel and Pekudei and say Shomer Yisrael, etc. Plain the shaliach tzibbur says its matter in Shacharit and Minchah. Ari z"l wrote in Torah commentary these days are auspicious to repair keri sin more than all year for those fasting — therefore begin from Parashat Shemot when bondage began, repaired through Parashat Ki Sisa when Hebrew slave goes free — end:
