Section 7. Some say that Parashas Zachor and Parashas Parah Adumah must be read by Torah law, etc. Note: I was asked by one scholar about what the Magen Avraham writes in this siman, that one fulfills the positive commandment of remembering Amalek by reading the parashah of And Amalek came on Purim. If so, it is difficult: why did they say in Megillah 30 that when Purim falls on Friday, Parashas Zachor is advanced, so that the performance should not precede the remembering? How would the performance precede the remembering? After all, he fulfilled his obligation with the parashah of And Amalek came, so the performance and the remembering are together. At first glance the scholar who asked this asked well. But I answered that in truth there is no difficulty, and it is well understood. For the question is: if one fulfills the obligation with the parashah of And Amalek came, why did the Sages institute reading Parashas Zachor first? Rather, the matter is that one who studies the Magen Avraham will see that his intent is as follows. His primary wonder about the world was according to the view that Parashas Zachor is Torah law: why are people lax about it? It follows from his words that if it were rabbinic, he would not have found this difficult. Likewise, what he writes afterward, that Parashas Parah is also not Torah law, proves from his words that his difficulty was only if it were Torah law. To this he answers as follows: there are two matters here. By Torah law there is a positive commandment to remember what Amalek did to you, but it is enough to mention the actual deed of Amalek, namely the parashah of And Amalek came. But the Rabbis instituted that they also read the parashah of the commandment, where the Torah commanded with a positive commandment to mention the deed of Amalek, so that this parashah too should be read, just as they instituted Parashas Shekalim and Parashas Hachodesh, which are rabbinic. So too, in this they instituted reading the actual parashah of Zachor, and this reading is rabbinic. Therefore the Magen Avraham answers as follows: at first he thought that we had no other remembering of the deed of Amalek except Parashas Zachor. He therefore asked that it is Torah law, and how are people lenient about it? Afterward he answers that the actual positive commandment of the Torah is fulfilled with the parashah of And Amalek came; only the Rabbis instituted also reading Parashas Zachor, and regarding a rabbinic matter it is not difficult that people are lax about it. If so, our common practice is settled, in that people fulfill their Torah obligation. But for us, since the Rabbis instituted that the remembering should be through Parashas Zachor, the statement is correct that one should not let rabbinic performance precede rabbinic remembering, for rabbinically we require the remembering to be through Parashas Zachor and not through the parashah of And Amalek came. If so, we require that the rabbinic performance not precede the rabbinic remembering. It further seems possible to say that since we rule that commandments require intent in matters of Torah law, here too regarding Parashas Zachor, if it is from the Torah, one must intend to fulfill the obligation with it. According to this it is well understood that the Magen Avraham is explaining the practice of the world: those who do not come to hear Parashas Zachor intend to fulfill the obligation through the parashah of And Amalek came, and they fulfill it with that. But for us, since in practice we are accustomed every year to read Parashas Zachor, we intend to fulfill the obligation with that and not with the parashah of And Amalek came, especially since in this year too they will read Parashas Zachor on the following Shabbos. And know this, for even Shmuel agrees that Parashas Zachor is read; he only says that it is delayed, and he does not say that they fulfill the obligation with the parashah of And Amalek came. Perforce, he wanted to fulfill the commandment in the preferred manner, by reading Parashas Zachor according to the ordinance of the Sages. If so, now as well they will read Parashas Zachor tomorrow, and therefore we are concerned that they will not intend to fulfill the obligation with the parashah of And Amalek came for the sake of Parashas Zachor, as they do in all years. And if they do not intend, they have not fulfilled the obligation, since commandments require intent. Therefore the performance would again precede the remembering, and for that reason they instituted reading Parashas Zachor first; and it is well understood. It may also be said simply: since it is written, remembered and performed, and from this we derive that remembering must be before performance, we require that it be in language of actual remembering, namely Remember what Amalek did to you. By contrast, in the parashah of And Amalek came, it does not state the command of remembering, to remember, and the term remembered is not applicable to it. Understand this. This is what I answered the aforementioned scholarly questioner; examine it well.
