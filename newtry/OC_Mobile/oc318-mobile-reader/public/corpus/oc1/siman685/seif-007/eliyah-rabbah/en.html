[ז] [Levush] Fast in leap year in first Adar, etc. If they did not die in leap year in second Adar, then fast in second in leap year; some are stringent always to fast in both (Levush siman 568), but Rama did not write "always" — implies for all: if died in first or second, fast thus in leap year; so Bach Yoreh De'ah end siman 402 — only if died in simple year some say fast in both as if leap, some dispute first Adar. Magen Avraham siman 568 challenged Ta'anit daf 18a for leap month — Rashi explained leap year, etc. — implies plainly practiced in second Adar; also we say there 18b Tyranos day and Nikanor day all practiced in second Adar — end. In my humble opinion no difficulty: it appears to me it was leap in second Adar — for in Terumat HaDeshen siman 294 he brings proof from fast seventh Adar and fasting in first — he challenged perhaps Moshe died first Adar and needed this proof in simple year — if so same here appears second. Rule: if died in Adar in simple year, fast in leap in first as Levush wrote and Rama in Yoreh De'ah there as practiced; Shach wrote there practiced fasting in both. In my view where no known custom, be stringent for oneself not for others; mourners need not give Kaddish except in first. Terumat HaDeshen wrote in name of Sefer Chasidim: one whose father died first Adar fasted simple year in Shevat and Adar — end. Magen Avraham wrote mourners need give him Kaddish only once — see siman 433 s.k. 2. Magen Avraham there: one whose father died first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev — next year Cheshvan deficient and Rosh Chodesh Kislev only one day — establish 29 Cheshvan; so Taz wrote; but when first year also full, must establish on Rosh Chodesh — therefore even later deficient years establish Rosh Chodesh — it is like a vow — we follow people's language; see siman 55 s.k. 9. Taz there: if his deceased died on Hanukkah, yahrzeit is not that Hanukkah day but after day of Rosh Chodesh — we follow how many days in month was death day, so yahrzeit — end. What Magen Avraham wrote there if died first day of Rosh Chodesh Adar he fasts, etc. — wondrous — see there.
