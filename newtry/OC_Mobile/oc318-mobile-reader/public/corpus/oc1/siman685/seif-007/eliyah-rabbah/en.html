Levush: He fasts in a leap year in the first Adar, etc. Unless the person died in a leap year in the second Adar, in which case he fasts in the second Adar in a leap year. Some are stringent to fast always in both of them; this is in Levush, siman 568. But Rema did not write the word always, which implies that according to everyone, if he died in the first or second Adar, they fast that way in a leap year. So too it implies in Bach to Yoreh Deah, end of siman 402. Only if he died in an ordinary year, some say they fast on both when a leap year comes, and some say they disagree and he fasts in the first Adar. Magen Avraham in siman 568 raised a difficulty from Taanis 18a, regarding an intercalated month, which Rashi explains as meaning that the year was intercalated, etc.; this implies explicitly that it applies in the second Adar. The Gemara also says there on 18b that the Day of Trajan and the Day of Nicanor are all observed in the second Adar. Until here. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for it seems to me that there it had been intercalated in the second Adar. For Terumas HaDeshen, siman 294, brings proof from the Seventh of Adar that they fast in the first Adar, and he asked: perhaps Moshe Rabbeinu died in the first Adar. He had to bring proof that this was in an ordinary year. If so, here too it seems to me that it was in the second. The rule of the matter is: if he died in Adar of an ordinary year, he fasts in a leap year in the first Adar, as Levush wrote, and so wrote Rema in Yoreh Deah there, that this is the practice. Shach wrote there that the practice is to fast on both. It seems to me that in a place where there is no known custom he should be stringent for himself and not for others; also, mourners should give him Kaddish only in the first Adar. In Terumas HaDeshen he wrote in the name of Sefer Chasidim that one whose father died in the first Adar would fast in an ordinary year in Shevat and in Adar. Until here. Magen Avraham wrote that mourners need give him Kaddish only once; see siman 133, paragraph 2. Magen Avraham wrote there: one whose father died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and in the following year Cheshvan is deficient and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day, should establish it on the twenty-ninth of Cheshvan; and so wrote Turei Zahav. But when the first year was also full, then he must establish the day on Rosh Chodesh, and therefore even in the following years, though they are deficient, he establishes it on Rosh Chodesh, for it is like a vow, and we follow the language of people. See siman 55, paragraph 9. Turei Zahav wrote there: if a relative died on Chanukah, the yahrzeit is not on that day of Chanukah, but we follow the day of Rosh Chodesh, determining which day of the month was the day of death, and so the yahrzeit will be. Until here. What Magen Avraham wrote there, that if one died on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Adar he should fast, etc., is astonishing; see there.
