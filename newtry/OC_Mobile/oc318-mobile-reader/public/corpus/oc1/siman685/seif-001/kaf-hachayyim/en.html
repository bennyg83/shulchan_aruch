1) [Seif 1] Rosh Chodesh Adar that is adjacent to Nissan, etc. The reason it was necessary to say, 'that is adjacent to Nissan,' is to teach us that in a leap year the reading is not performed in the first Adar, which is adjacent to Shevat, but in the second Adar, which is adjacent to Nissan, as stated in Megillah 6b, see there. See also the reason below, at the beginning of siman 697:
2) Ibid. Rosh Chodesh Adar, etc., that falls on Shabbos, etc. Meaning, the second day of Rosh Chodesh, which is 1 Adar adjacent to Nissan, falls on Shabbos; on that day we read Parashas Shekalim, etc. This accords with what we learned in the Mishnah, 'On 1 Adar they make announcements concerning the shekalim,' meaning that on that day they read Parashas Shekalim, to announce it publicly when it falls on Shabbos. Yafeh LaLev, vol. 3, letter 1:
3) Ibid. We read Parashas Shekalim, etc. For the verse states, 'This is the burnt offering of each month in its month.' The Torah said: Renew, and bring the offering from the new terumah. We derive through a gezeirah shavah that this month is Nissan. Since in Nissan the offering must be brought from the new terumah, we precede it on 1 Adar, so that the shekalim will reach the Beis HaMikdash. Afterwards comes Parashas Zachor, before the reading of the Megillah on Purim, so that remembrance should precede action, as it is written, 'remembered and performed.' Afterwards comes Parashas Parah, in order to remind those who are tamei of the warning concerning Pesach. Taz, s.k. 1. In Tikkun Yissachar, 72b, he wrote another reason in the name of Maseches Soferim: Since the Holy One, blessed is He, foresaw that Haman would weigh out shekalim against the Jewish people, He therefore caused their shekalim to precede his shekalim. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, letter 1:
4) Ibid. We read Parashas Shekalim, etc. If they erred and read Parashas Shekalim on the preceding Shabbos, they must return and read Parashas Shekalim again on the Shabbos that is 1 Adar. Yafeh LaLev, ibid. See below, letter 6 and letter 23:
5) If the congregation forgot on Rosh Chodesh Adar to read Parashas Shekalim, they must return, take out a Sefer Torah, and read the parashah. Mahar"i Shababi, the Av Beis Din, in Ginas Veradim, Orach Chaim, klal 1, siman 36; Mahar"a HaLevi agreed with him in siman 37. Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur. However, if the time for Minchah has already arrived, it can no longer be made up. Yosef Ometz, siman 27:
6) If they erred and read the order of the four parshiyos in the first Adar, they must return and read them in the second Adar, as stated in Megillah 6b, for the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, see there. So too ruled the Tur in siman 688, based on the emendation in Bedek HaBayis that appears there in the Beit Yosef. So too wrote Darkei Moshe and Bach there. Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, at the end of letter 21. Imrei LaYosher, in Mishpat Im Nefal Ta'us, letter 14. Derech HaChaim, siman 9, letter 14. Shaarei Ephraim, sha'ar 8, letter 96:
7) If on a Shabbos of one of these four parshiyos they erred and forgot that the parashah required for that day must be read for maftir, and they read for maftir in the manner of all other Shabbosos of the year, and then remembered, they must take out a Sefer Torah and read the parashah required for that day. This applies even if at first they took out only one Sefer Torah, and there is no concern for the discredit of a second scroll. Even if they already recited the haftarah of the weekly portion with its blessings, they must take out another Sefer Torah. One person should be called up, and they should simply announce, 'Let so-and-so son of so-and-so stand,' without saying 'maftir.' He should read with him from the subject of the day. After the concluding blessing, he should recite Kaddish, they should lift the Sefer Torah, and he should read the haftarah from Nevi'im without a blessing before it or after it.
