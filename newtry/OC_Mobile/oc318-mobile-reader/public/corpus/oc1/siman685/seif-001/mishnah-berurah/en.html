(1) Rosh Chodesh Adar, etc. I will briefly explain the four special parshiyos. Chazal instituted the reading of four parshiyos each year from Rosh Chodesh Adar until Rosh Chodesh Nisan in remembrance of four matters. The first is Parshas Shekalim, in remembrance of the mitzvah of the half shekel, which will be explained below. Each year the Jewish people were obligated to contribute it to the chamber for the daily offerings. The second is Parshas Zachor, to remember what Amalek did. It is read on the Shabbos before Purim so that it is adjacent to the event involving Haman, who descended from Amalek, and so that the remembrance of eradicating Amalek precedes its performance, as the verse states, “These days are remembered and performed.” The third is Parshas Parah Adumah, read on the Shabbos before Parshas HaChodesh. The red cow was burned in the wilderness shortly before Nisan so that the Jewish people could be sprinkled with the ashes of the sin offering immediately after the Mishkan was erected. They would thereby be pure and able to offer the Pesach sacrifice at its proper time. We therefore read this parshah and pray before Hashem that He should likewise sprinkle pure water upon us speedily. The fourth is Parshas HaChodesh, read on the Shabbos near Rosh Chodesh Nisan in order to sanctify the month of Nisan, as the Torah states, “This month shall be for you the first of the months.” This reading is not the essential sanctification of the month, because the essential sanctification occurs when the new moon is seen and the beis din sanctifies the month. This reading is only a rabbinic enactment.
(2) Parshas Shekalim is read. The verse states, “This is the burnt offering of each month in its month, for the months of the year.” The word “for the months” is superfluous. The Torah thereby teaches that there is a month whose offerings must be renewed through the use of a new contribution for the daily and Mussaf offerings. This is Nisan, as the Gemara derives through a gezeirah shavah that the month referred to here is Nisan. Since the offerings in Nisan must come from the new contribution, the announcement concerning the shekalim is made in the preceding month of Adar so that the people will bring their shekalim by Rosh Chodesh Nisan. We fulfill the verse, “Our lips will substitute for bulls,” by reading the portion of Ki Sisa, which discusses the shekalim. Yotzer piyyutim are recited at Shacharis and also at Mussaf on Parshas Shekalim and Parshas HaChodesh, but no Mussaf piyyutim were instituted for Parshas Zachor or Parshas Parah, as stated by Peri Megadim. If the special parshah of that day was not read, it cannot be made up on the following Shabbos, as stated by Shaarei Ephraim. If the congregation forgot to read Parshas Shekalim, and the same applies to the other three special parshiyos, and they already recited the weekly haftorah with its blessings before remembering, they must again take out a Sefer Torah and read the special parshah with the blessings before and after it. They then recite Kaddish and read the special haftorah without a blessing before or after it. If they remembered after the reader began the opening blessing over the haftorah and had already pronounced Hashem's Name, he should complete that blessing and recite a few verses from the weekly haftorah so that the blessing has a subject upon which to take effect, but he should not recite its concluding blessing. They should then take out a Sefer Torah, one person should read the special parshah, and after the concluding Torah blessing Kaddish should be recited. The special haftorah should then be read without its opening blessing, but the concluding blessings should be recited. If they remembered immediately after the concluding blessing over the Torah reading, they should take out another Sefer Torah, place it beside the first, and recite Kaddish. Another person should then read the special parshah as maftir and recite its haftorah with the blessings, as stated there. If they mistakenly read the special parshiyos and the Megillah during the first Adar of a leap year, the Megillah must be read again during the second Adar. Regarding the special parshiyos, Beis Yosef implies that they need not be repeated, but Darkei Moshe and Eliyah Rabbah write that they must be repeated.
(3) The haftorah Vayichros Yehoyada is read. This haftorah mentions contributions for the repair of the Beis HaMikdash. The haftorah of Rosh Chodesh is not read, despite its greater frequency, because the Torah reading concluded with Parshas Shekalim and the haftorah follows that subject.
(4) The weekly parshah. Six people are called to the Torah, and in places where the custom is to add aliyos, additional people may be called, as stated by Shaarei Ephraim.
(5) From the second Sefer Torah, the reading of Rosh Chodesh. They begin with the verse U'veyom HaShabbos, as stated by the Levush. The third Sefer Torah is placed beside it and Half Kaddish is recited. If, after completing the weekly parshah, they mistakenly took the Sefer Torah that was rolled to Parshas Shekalim, recited the blessing before reading, and began to read, they should complete Parshas Shekalim. The person called as maftir should then read the Rosh Chodesh portion from the third Sefer Torah, and the haftorah Hashamayim Kis'i should be recited, since the Torah reading concluded with a subject relating to Shabbos and Rosh Chodesh. The same applies when Parshas HaChodesh occurs on Rosh Chodesh Nisan that falls on Shabbos and they mistakenly read Parshas HaChodesh before the Rosh Chodesh reading, as stated by Shaarei Ephraim.
