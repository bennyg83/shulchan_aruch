And most of this species grows by a river. The Rosh wrote this to answer the question: since most poskim hold that there is more of a mitzvah with one from a river than with one from the mountains, and he did not see his teachers being careful about this, he explained that the verse means a species that grows by a river. This is not enough for us, for in any case they should have fulfilled according to most poskim and not relied on this explanation. It appears to me that this is like the matter we find in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin regarding the blessing of hamotzi: even though if one said motzi it is preferable, nevertheless one should say hamotzi, to show a novelty, as the Rosh wrote in the first chapter of Pesachim regarding the blessing 'al bi'ur chametz,' that a person should show novelty in his blessings. Here too, it is preferable to show a novelty and take even one not from the river.
And there is a kind of aravah, etc. In the Gemara they call them chilfei gili. Tosafot asked: why do we need the signs of tzaftzafah, since he revealed to us the signs of aravah? They answered: in order to validate chilfei gili, which does not have these three signs. Therefore he taught the signs of tzaftzafah, that specifically these are invalid. But this is difficult: let him not mention tzaftzafah, and also the sign that its edge is like a sickle and its edge is smooth, and I would know that chilfei gili is valid, for it has the other signs of aravah. One can say that I might have thought chilfei gili are prohibited even though they have the other signs of aravah, because they have a modified name, as stated in the Gemara. Therefore he mentioned tzaftzafah, that specifically this is invalid, while chilfei gili is valid. And even though we include it from 'arvei,' nevertheless I might have thought that the verse comes only to teach us that 'arvei' means two. It teaches us that this too is learned from it. Still, it is difficult: let him say only two signs, namely drawn like a river and a red stem, and I would know that chilfei is valid; for otherwise, why would we need the signs of tzaftzafah? One can say that I might have said the opposite, that the tanna teaches us to prohibit chilfei, so that we not say we include it from 'arvei.' Therefore the tanna taught tzaftzafah, to learn from it prohibition regarding chilfei. For this reason he taught the sign that its edge is divided; automatically, without the signs of tzaftzafah, you would learn prohibition. Therefore he mentioned tzaftzafah specifically to infer that chilfei is permitted, as it appears to me. Regarding the verse, it is difficult: according to the derivation of 'arvei nachal' as drawn like a river, why did it mention this sign and not the other signs? And if they are not needed, why did the tanna mention them? One can say that certainly they are needed, but this verse does not come to teach signs for permission; rather, it comes to exclude, so that we not say that the extra 'arvei' comes to include tzaftzafah. It teaches us that we include only a species that grows by a river, as it appears to me. Do not ask: from where did the Tur know that this chilfei has two signs of aravah, and resembles tzaftzafah in only one sign, namely that it is like a sickle? One can say that since one baraita mentions that something like a sickle is valid, it implies that in the other signs it resembles aravah, and it teaches us that regarding this sign of the edge being divided, we find validity even when it is not smooth but only like a sickle; it excludes only one like a saw.
