An aravah that dried. This means most of its leaves.
Or that fell off, etc. One must be precise about this, for sometimes they fall off by being inserted into the lulav, and also by shaking. In particular with the communal esrog and lulav, which everyone's hand handles, it is very common that most of the leaves fall off through the shaking of some people. Automatically, the other people who bless afterward not only fail to fulfill the mitzvah, but also recite a blessing in vain. It is proper that at least on the first day, which is a biblical positive commandment, they appoint a designated person to supervise this.
Most of its leaves. This means within the three-handbreadth measure of the aravah. [Bach and Sha'arei Teshuvah in siman 646] If its leaves were loosened from the stem, or its leaves split, or divided, the Maggid Mishneh wrote that this is called 'its leaves were broken apart' and it is invalid. All this is where most of its leaves are this way. The Magen Avraham wrote that one should be stringent like him, because aravot are common.
Or its head was cut off. This means that its wood was cut off; but if its leaves were cut off, even at its head, there is no practical difference in this, and it is considered like the other leaves. Know that dried and its head was cut off are invalid because of beauty. Regarding most of its leaves falling off, the view of the Ritva is that it is because through this its name is not upon it; therefore his view is that in this case it is invalid all seven days. From the words of Rabbeinu Manoach it implies that this too is because of beauty.
Valid. This implies that ab initio it is not worthwhile to take it. [Magen Avraham] The Eliyah Rabbah validates even ab initio. However, since aravot are common, it is good to seek a better one ab initio even in this.
Validates. We follow the first view.
