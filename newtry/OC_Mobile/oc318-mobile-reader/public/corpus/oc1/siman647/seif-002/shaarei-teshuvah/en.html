That fell off. See Ba'er Hetev. In Responsa Pnei Yehoshua manuscript he wrote: an aravah that was cut on the first day of the festival, it is prohibited to move it and place it in the lulav on that day. This is not comparable to what is written in siman 655 regarding a lulav brought from outside the techum, etc., for there it is not muktzeh, since it is fit for another Jew; only because it was brought from outside the techum it is prohibited for eating and benefit, and mitzvot were not given for benefit. But something detached from what was attached, which is prohibited to everyone and is complete muktzeh to move, the Sages upheld their words in the place of a mitzvah through passive non-action, etc.; see there. There, in the language of the question, he wrote to be uncertain: if you say it is prohibited, nevertheless, if the first day of Yom Tov fell on Shabbat, is it permitted on the second day of Yom Tov? The responsum to this is missing there. Now the Magen Avraham wrote that if it was detached on the first day it is permitted on the second; see there. But it appears that this is on a Yom Tov that falls on a weekday. If the first day fell on Shabbat, it is prohibited, for we rule in siman 513 regarding an egg that when Shabbat and Yom Tov are adjacent, if it was born on this one, it is prohibited on that one. The same applies here. But if it was detached on Shabbat and afterward came Yom Tov day one and day two, it is permitted on the second, as explained there in the gloss; see there.
That was cut off. See Ba'er Hetev. See Eliyah Rabbah, whose view is to be lenient with an aravah whose head was cut off when there is no other, as with hadas. If a date-like growth arose on it, it is valid even ab initio, unlike the Magen Avraham; see there.
