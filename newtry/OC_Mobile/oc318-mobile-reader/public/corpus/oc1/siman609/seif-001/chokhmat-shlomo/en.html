Se'if 1 It is permitted to insulate hot food from the eve of Yom Kippur for Motzaei Yom Kippur. Note: In truth, the view of those who are stringent is astonishing, as the Tur wondered about them, and the Beis Yosef agreed with his words. I wonder even more, for the Talmud is explicit in the first chapter of Shabbos, and so we rule in Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim siman 252, se'if 1, that it is permitted to place bundles of flax into an oven and they are completed throughout Shabbos, and likewise to place wool into the cauldron; if not for the reason lest he stoke, it would be permitted to place it even when it is on the fire. So too it is permitted to place incense beneath vessels and they absorb its fragrance throughout Shabbos; see there in se'if 5. If so, why should it be forbidden to insulate on the eve of Yom Kippur for weekday? How is this different from all those cases? Shabbos is more severe than Yom Kippur, for one has kareis and the other stoning. Therefore it seems that the main reason is because of a decree lest he eat. Since on an ordinary Shabbos the usual way is to insulate in order to eat that same day, if he insulates on Yom Kippur he may come to eat. Just as they decreed not to touch foods because he may eat, so too they forbade insulating, even though he is not touching, because of this concern, so that he not come to err from an ordinary Shabbos. With this, the reason of the Gaon is understood, for he holds that it is forbidden to eat on the night after Yom Kippur until one waits the time it would take to do it, and the Tur wondered about him. According to what I wrote, just as where it is forbidden to cook on Shabbos, if one cooked in prohibition he must wait the time it would take to do it, so that he not benefit from forbidden labor, so too here: since they forbade insulating on Yom Kippur because of concern lest he eat, if he transgressed and did the forbidden act and benefits from forbidden labor, they decreed to penalize him by making him wait the time it would take to do it. This appears correct to my humble opinion, with the help of Heaven.
