(1) The practice etc. — it implies it is only a custom; and the custom is based because matzah eating is equated to the Pesach offering, as written "and you shall slaughter the Pesach to your God" etc. "seven days you shall eat matzot upon it" etc.; and we say: just as slaughtering the Pesach was after six, so making matzah for the mitzvah is after six hours; but from the essence of law the poskim proved one need not be concerned for this, for the primary hekesh is said regarding eating matzah on the first night that one be careful to eat before midnight like the Pesach that is eaten until midnight, as explained below siman 477; but not regarding making the matzah; and therefore b'dieved if he baked the matzah before six, and even a month or two months before Pesach, and made them for matzah, they are valid.

(b) We had to leave his duty in the first two nights, but the rest of the inventions do not take care of it:

(3) Until after etc. — and see below siman 464 seif 3 what one must be careful regarding kneading at the time of the chametz prohibition. And behold most of Israel do not practice to be careful even regarding matzah for the mitzvah to knead on erev Pesach except some of them; and I searched among Acharonim and found in Bigdei Yesha a reason to advocate for the custom: because there are several views that even on erev Pesach in something, as explained above siman 452; and it is hard to be careful that they not find even something chametz in the wheat that is not sprouted; and therefore they bake beforehand so the chametz will be nullified. And in Ma'amar Mordechai I found he wrote: nowadays they are very lenient in the matter because of duress, except some who are careful in this to knead and bake after six the matzot with which one fulfills on the two nights; and so it is fit to do.

(4) Six hours — behold Kaf HaChayyim sides with Bach that specifically after six and a half; however in Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah he sides with Shulchan Aruch that after six; and so ruled Beit Meir; and so implied in Biur HaGra. And know that from the law it is permitted to bake matzah on Pesach; however the meticulous practice to bake everything before Pesach so that if something chametz becomes mixed in them it will be nullified, unlike on Pesach where its prohibition is in any amount. And it is obvious that when baked before Pesach, even if there was something chametz in the flour, it already mixed and was nullified, and permitted afterward to cook the matzot on Pesach, for there is no chimutz after baking; and there are men of action who are stringent on themselves and do not soak and do not cook matzot on Pesach from concern lest a little flour remain inside the matzot internally that was not kneaded well and through soaking it will chametz; and see in Sha'arei Teshuvah siman 464 that from the law one need not be concerned for this, for we do not establish a prohibition presumptively, and especially nowadays when they practice to make thin wafers. Nevertheless one who practices this stringency one does not dismiss him; and see there who elaborated on this. Poskim wrote: the baker on the night of Yom Tov for matzah mitzvah should bake only what is needed for that night and not for the second night; however if he baked while still day on the first night he may say "I eat today warm bread" and may bake others on the first night, and what he baked while still day he hides for the second night; and Acharonim wrote that l'chatchila it is not worthwhile to bake on Yom Tov lest one come to ruin in separating challah on Yom Tov, and likewise in measuring flour and washing vessels, and lest the children sleep, and lest thereby one come to eat afikoman after midnight.

(5) Knead on erev Shabbat — meaning unlike those who are stringent to bake on the night of Yom Tov, as above at end of s.k. 4.
