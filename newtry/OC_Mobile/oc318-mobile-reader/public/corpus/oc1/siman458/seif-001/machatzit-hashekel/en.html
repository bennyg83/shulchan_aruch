(s.k. 1) After etc. As Tosafot wrote Pesachim daf 5 and Yoma. For in Pesachim Rava said: this that is written "only on the first day you shall rest" — necessarily means from Yom Tov from midday, as written "you shall not slaughter on chametz the blood of my sacrifice" — youremeans do not slaughter the Pesach while chametz still exists; and he concludes this is the time of slaughtering the Pesach. Proven that from the time of slaughtering the Pesach there is a positive commandment of "you shall rest" — meaning from Yom Tov from midday. Rashba challenged in Tosafot s.v. lo tishchat etc.: behold the time of slaughtering the Pesach is after the tamid, and the tamid slaughtered before midday is invalid; if so necessarily since one must delay the Pesach after the tamid, slaughtering the Pesach is not immediately after midday. And answer: since b'dieved if he slaughtered the Pesach before the tamid it is valid, it is properly called midday the time of slaughtering the Pesach — end quote; and still difficult: behold the time of slaughtering is from six and a half. And necessarily must say as we say in Yoma daf 28: in truth its time begins at seven; however the walls of the Temple were wide and it was impossible to determine midday precisely in the shadow of the walls until six and a half; and so Rashi wrote there in Pesachim.

Tur wrote etc. everything before Pesach etc. — excluding those who bake on the night of the first Yom Tov and on the second Yom Tov; and see there the reason of the two views. And in Darkei Moshe he further wrote a reason for those who practice baking on Yom Tov: for we say maror must be dipped in charoset because of kafa — meaning poison of lettuce. And he said there in this sugya kafa of all of them is hot — meaning poison of all vegetables, their medicine is hot; therefore they bake on the second night so the matzot will be hot and nullify the poison of maror — end quote from Eshel Avraham. And Darkei Moshe wrote: one may learn from this that it is good to keep the matzah hot if possible in order to be guarded from kafa — end quote. And Eshel Avraham's words siman 453 in this are astonishing, for he wrote: since there are poskim that flour in flour is called dry in dry, therefore one should bake everything before Pesach; you have no grain without wheat that sprouted except that they are nullified before Pesach; and since we hold dry in dry, it returns and awakens, therefore must bake before Pesach so it not return and awaken on Pesach at time of baking. And so in the name of Bach. And he wrote: one who is concerned for this stringency should not make a cooked dish from matzah on Pesach as he wrote from its baking. And those meticulous in baking before Pesach and on Pesach make a cooked dish from matzot — there is no reason for their words — end quote.

And his intent is that in a cooked dish likewise it returns and awakens. On this Magen Avraham wondered: if so, even plain they are forbidden; and in this apparently the law is with Magen Avraham, but for another reason one should be stringent for a ba'al nefesh not to make a cooked dish from matzot on Pesach: for sometimes not all the flour is kneaded and mixed, and a little flour remains on the surface of the dough; and if it remained on the matzah above there would not be so much concern, for since it was given into the oven that little flour was scorched; and it is like flour of abshina, and in such a case where it is only a small amount one may be lenient. But sometimes that flour is in the middle of the matzah inside: for sometimes flour remains on the surface of the dough when it was a little hard, and by rolling afterward the helpers roll the pieces of dough to make matzah from them and by rolling it that flour comes inside the matzah. And in such a case Magen Avraham wrote siman 459, and especially siman 463 s.k. 4, to forbid putting into a cooked dish; and he left in doubt if they put it into the cooked dish whether to forbid; and he concluded one must forbid matzah in such a case since the matzah separates between the flour and the fire and it is not scorched. Therefore one does not dismiss one who practices this stringency.
