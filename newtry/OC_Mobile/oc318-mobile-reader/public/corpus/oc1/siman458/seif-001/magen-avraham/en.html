After six hours. Even though the time of offering the Pesach is after six hours and a half, nevertheless its primary time is from midday, as Tosafot wrote Pesachim daf 5 and Yoma daf 28 and Niddah 63b. Tur wrote: the meticulous practice to bake everything before Pesach so that if something chametz becomes mixed it will be nullified — end quote; and it is obvious that when baked before Pesach and mixed in kneading, it is permitted afterward to cook the matzot on Pesach — for otherwise even to eat them plain is forbidden; and Eshel Avraham's words siman 453 in this are astonishing. Maharil wrote that some bake during the festival to eat fresh matzot every day; and Matanot Kehunah wrote: and the baker on the night of Yom Tov may not bake doubtful matzot; likewise forbidden to bake on the first night for the second night; however if he baked while still day on the first night he may say "I eat today warm bread" and may bake others on the first night, and what he baked while still day he hides for the second night — see above siman 407 seif 6. If one of the matzot was lost he may bake others on Yom Tov because of asmakhta that they practice to make three from one tenth, even though he has others, lest they were made by minors; and although for afikoman we rely on them (as written siman 477), for matzah d'oraisa we do not rely on them (Matanot Kehunah, Maharil) — and see siman 482. If dough was kneaded close to nightfall and he did not manage to bake until nightfall — uncertain if permitted to bake on Yom Tov so it not chametz, for challah is forbidden to bake so it not chametz; and one may say it is permitted to use a stratagem and say "I eat warm bread" as above, even though he will not take that dough for matzot, since he did not knead it for the mitzvah, nevertheless he may eat it during his meal or tomorrow on the first day — and see siman 406 seif 3 and what is written there.
