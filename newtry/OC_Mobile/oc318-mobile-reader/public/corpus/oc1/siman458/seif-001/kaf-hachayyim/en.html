1) [Se'if 1] The custom is not to knead, etc. A responsum records an incident in which someone baked matzos before four hours of the day had passed, after already eliminating the chametz. Our teachers prohibited the matzah because matzah is compared to the Pesach offering. Just as the Pesach offering is slaughtered only from six hours onward, so too matzah should be prepared then. Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol and Rabbi Shmuel HaKohen, however, permitted it. Initially, one should nevertheless be careful because a mitzvah is cherished when performed at its proper time. Avi HaEzri cites a Tosefta stating that one fulfills the obligation with old matzah, provided it was prepared for the sake of Pesach. He writes that the Yerushalmi disagrees with the Tosefta and prohibits it even if prepared for the sake of Pesach. Since the Yerushalmi prohibits it, it is proper to be stringent not to prepare it before midday. Tur. The comparison to the Pesach offering appears in Pesachim 120b. Rashi, Rashbam, and Rabbeinu Tam explain there that it is derived from the verse, 'They shall eat it with matzos and bitter herbs.' See there. Levush writes likewise. The permitting authorities maintain that matzah was compared to the Pesach offering only regarding eating, not regarding preparation. Bach.
