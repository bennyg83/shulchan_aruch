Six hours. And a half. So wrote Bach and Taz and Kaf HaChayyim, not like Magen Avraham. Bach wrote one does not fulfill with matzah baked thirty days before, even if made for Pesach, and most poskim disagree — see Kaf HaChayyim. The meticulous practice to bake everything before Pesach so that if something chametz becomes mixed it will be nullified; and so practiced Maharshal. Magen Avraham wrote and it is obvious that when baked before Pesach and mixed in kneading, it is permitted afterward to cook the matzot on Pesach — for otherwise even to eat them plain is forbidden, unlike Eshel Avraham siman 453. Maharil wrote that some bake during the festival to eat fresh matzot every day; and Taz wrote it is not proper to do so — see there his reason; and so Acharonim. Matat Moshe wrote: the baker on the night of Yom Tov may not bake doubtful matzot; and likewise forbidden to bake on the first night for the second night; however if he baked while still day on the first night he may say "I eat today warm bread" and may bake others on the first night, and what he baked while still day he hides for the second night — see above siman 437 seif 7. If dough was kneaded close to nightfall and he did not manage to bake it until nightfall, it is permitted to bake it on Yom Tov so it not chametz — Magen Avraham.
