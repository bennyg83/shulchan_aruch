Knead on erev Pesach after six. Meaning after six and a half, for such is the time of slaughtering the Pesach; and not as some Geonim brought in Tur that one may bake only on erev Shabbat, and thus: therefore do not bake matzah except on erev Shabbat because of mar'it ha'ayin — if you bake from erev Pesach others will treat it lightly and be lenient; and although permitted to bake from erev Pesach, strengthen and adorn the mitzvah and bake in its time — end quote; and all this regarding matzah mitzvah. Beit Yosef challenged from what is written nevertheless he makes three matzot etc.: it appears his intent is in time of duress — meaning when there is fear to bake matzah properly, meaning all four together, for in this there is praise for matzah of the mitzvah as in chapter 5 Yom Tov; and this is impossible because of non-Jews who will notice if there is great smoke; nevertheless they make a small smoke on the night of Yom Tov only for the three matzot of Pesach; and thereby Beit Yosef's question is resolved. And this mar'it ha'ayin means others will think they eat on Shabbat also matzah; and afterward they wrote: and although permitted to bake it — meaning the three matzot of Pesach night alone, for in this there is no room for error; nevertheless because of the mitzvah in its time they bake on erev Shabbat; and Beit Yosef brought in the name of Mordechai: this mar'it ha'ayin means it will appear like preparing from Shabbat to Yom Tov — meaning on Shabbat forbidden to prepare and arrange the matzot on the table for the night as they do on weekdays, rather wait until night; and if he bakes the matzot on erev Pesach they will think he preceded himself in order to prepare tomorrow on Shabbat for Yom Tov to arrange the table — for otherwise why did he not wait also in baking matzot; and Beit Yosef explained another way which is strained; and Tur concluded in the name of R' Yehudah HaKohen: we have only the custom of our fathers to bake from erev Pesach and not to trouble on Yom Tov and after eating matzah; rather the zealous precede for the mitzvah — end quote; and as Shulchan Aruch ruled; and in my humble opinion another reason not to bake on the night of Yom Tov: there are many laws in baking challah as above siman 457, and not everyone knows the law thoroughly, and they will come to bake also on Yom Tov and not know the law of challah and come to transgress burning kodshim on Yom Tov; and I saw this stumbling block with my own eyes. And another reason: they hurry in eating so the children not sleep as written siman 493; and if he delays in kneading and baking certainly the children will sleep. And in Rokeach it is written to bake on the second night of Pesach because of love of the mitzvah that it is elevated thereby; and it is not proper to practice so in my humble opinion for the reason I mentioned; and see siman 495 in explanation of the words of Raavan who holds also one should not delay from eating immediately at night. Tur wrote: and I saw in Barcelona among the meticulous that they baked all they needed for the festival before the festival, so that if something chametz becomes mixed it will be nullified before its prohibition time — end quote; meaning before its prohibition in something — from the beginning of Pesach night; and so practiced Maharshal.
