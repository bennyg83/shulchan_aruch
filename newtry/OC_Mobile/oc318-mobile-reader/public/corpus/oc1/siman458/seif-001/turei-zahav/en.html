Knead on erev Shabbos after six. Meaning after six and a half, for that is the time of slaughtering the Pesach offering, and not as some Geonim wrote, whom the Tur cited, that one should bake only on the night of Motzei Shabbos; and this is their language: therefore do not bake matzah except on Motzei Shabbos because of maris haayin, for if you bake from erev Shabbos others treat it lightly and are lenient about it; and even though it is permitted to bake from erev Shabbos, strengthen and beautify the mitzvah and bake it in its time, end quote. And all this is regarding the matzah of the mitzvah. And Beis Yosef asked from what Maggid Mishneh wrote, "nevertheless he should make three matzos," etc. It appears that this is what he means: in a time of pressing need, that is, where there is fear of baking matzos properly, namely many together, for in that there is praise for the mitzvah matzos, as is stated in the laws of Yom Tov, and that is impossible because of non-Jews who will notice if there is a large oven-heating; nevertheless they should make a small heating on Yom Tov night only for the three matzos of Pesach; and with this Beis Yosef's difficulty is resolved. And this maris haayin: meaning that others will suppose that they also eat matzos on Shabbos. And afterwards they wrote, "and even though it is permitted to bake it," it appears the meaning is the three matzos of Pesach night alone, for regarding this there is no room for error; nevertheless, because of performing the mitzvah in its time they should bake on Motzei Shabbos. And Beis Yosef brought in the name of the Mordechai regarding this maris haayin that it means it would appear as though one is preparing from Shabbos for Yom Tov. It appears the explanation is that on Shabbos it is forbidden to prepare and arrange the matzos on the table for the need of the night as one does on a weekday; rather wait until night. And if he bakes the matzos on erev Shabbos, they will suppose that he advanced himself so that he could prepare the next day on Shabbos for Yom Tov to arrange the table; for if not, why did he not also wait regarding baking the matzos? And Beis Yosef explained it another way, and that is forced. And the Tur concluded in the name of R' Yitzchak HaKohen: and we have only the custom of our fathers to bake from erev Shabbos and not to trouble ourselves on Yom Tov and delay eating the matzah; rather the zealous ones advance to the mitzvos, until here. And this is like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch. And it further appears to my humble opinion another reason not to bake on Yom Tov night: for there are many laws in baking regarding challah, as above in siman 457, and not everyone is expert in the law, and they will come to bake also on Yom Tov and will not know the law of challah and will come to the prohibition of burning sanctified items on Yom Tov; and I saw this stumbling-block with my own eyes. And another reason further: that they hurry with eating so that the children not fall asleep, as is written in siman 443; and if he delays in kneading and baking, the children will certainly fall asleep. And in the Rokeach it is written to bake on the second night of Pesach for the sake of cherishing the mitzvah, for that is elevated in this; and in my humble opinion it is not proper to practice thus for the reason I mentioned; and see what I wrote in siman 455 in the explanation of the words of R' Eliyahu Mizrachi, who also holds that one should not delay from eating immediately at night. The Tur wrote, and I saw in Barcelona among the meticulous ones who would bake all that they need for the festival before the festival, so that if any chametz became mixed with it, it would be nullified before the time of its prohibition, end quote. Meaning before its prohibition even in a minute amount, that is from the beginning of the night of Pesach; and so practiced Maharshal.
