[Levush] After midday etc. — meaning after six and a half, similar to Pesach (Bach); and in Rokeach and Maharil after seven and a half; and it appears to me their reason: we learned the tamid was slaughtered at seven and a half and afterward the Pesach offering; and other poskim hold: since b'dieved it is valid when the Pesach was slaughtered before the tamid, and this mitzvah is only asmakhta, they were lenient l'chatchila; and it appears this is the reason of Magen Avraham who disagrees with Bach and permits after six immediately, since its primary time is after six; and Chok Yaakov challenged from what Magen Avraham wrote beginning siman 433: since we do not know the measure of hours they were stringent until six and a half — see there; and in my humble opinion here is different since it is only asmakhta they were not stringent except unlike minchah prayer. Tur wrote: the meticulous practice to bake everything before Pesach so that if something chametz becomes mixed it will be nullified — end quote; and permitted afterward to cook the matzot on Pesach when mixed in kneading, for if not so even to eat them plain is forbidden (Magen Avraham); and in Matat Moshe he wrote on Tur in the name of R' Shimon it is an excessive stringency; and they also practice to bake on chol ha'moed for honor of Yom Tov that there be fresh matzot, as Maharil wrote; but on the night of Yom Tov itself the view of all Acharonim is not to practice baking for several concerns. Matat Moshe wrote: if one of the matzot was lost he may bake another on Yom Tov even though he has another, lest they were made by minors; and although for afikoman we rely on them as written siman 477, for matzah d'oraisa we do not rely on them — end quote; however if there are still enough there is no concern. Matat Moshe wrote: the baker on the night of Yom Tov should not make doubtful matzot; and all the more so one should not bake from the first night for the second.
