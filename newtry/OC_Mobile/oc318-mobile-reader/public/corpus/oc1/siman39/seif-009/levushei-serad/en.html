It must be said that the case in Gittin is as follows: When the Ran writes in Gittin that even one who leaves permitted food available and eats prohibited food is disqualified only rabbinically, he means that the person explicitly says that he is not acting in order to provoke but only to taste the prohibited food, etc. In an unspecified case, however, we do not assume that he acted merely to taste it, and he is disqualified by Torah law and is also deemed a sectarian. According to the second resolution, even in an unspecified case we assume that he acted merely to taste it, and his disqualification is only rabbinic. Only when he acts this way many times does he become a sectarian by Torah law.
