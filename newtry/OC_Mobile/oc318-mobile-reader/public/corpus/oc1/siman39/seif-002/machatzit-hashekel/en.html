(Seif katan 2) A Kuti, etc. So it should read, for an idolater is invalid in any case, etc., as written in siman 32 seif 9, and even a Jew standing over him does not help according to Rambam. In Eliyah Rabbah, in the name of his son Maharash, he asked: Tosafot in Menachot 42, s.v. ve'el, ask regarding the baraita in Gittin: a Sefer Torah, tefillin, or mezuzah written by a min, informer, idolater, slave, woman, minor, Kuti, or apostate is invalid. Regarding an idolater, let it derive from the fact that he does not write lishmah. Tosafot answered: it was needed because of slave and woman, etc.; although it taught idolater, so too it taught Kuti, even though ordinarily he writes for Mount Gerizim, end quote. Thus Tosafot answered that it mentioned idolater because of the others; if so, why did Magen Avraham erase it? In Shulchan Aruch too one could say that it used the language of the baraita because of the others. This is the substance of his expanded question. In my humble opinion, he understood that before Magen Avraham both words were written, idolater and Kuti, and Magen Avraham erased idolater and retained only Kuti. If so, the author of Eliyah Rabbah would have asked well. But in truth, before Magen Avraham his version had only idolater and not Kuti. Therefore it was reasonable to Magen Avraham that this came from a printer's error, for such is the way of printers with such words, and instead of idolater it should read Kuti. For we see that in any case he did not copy the entire language of the baraita, for even if we read idolater, he omitted Kuti, which is mentioned in the baraita. If so, it is preferable to him to say it should read Kuti, so we need not say "because of the others." What Eliyah Rabbah wrote further, that Tosafot also wrote regarding a Kuti that he writes for Mount Gerizim and is worse than an idolater: in my humble opinion, Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 27 also wrote as in Menachot but did not conclude by bringing proof from the fact that it taught Kuti, that ordinarily he writes for Mount Gerizim. That is because one can say the baraita speaks before they found the image of a dove on Mount Gerizim, as stated in the first chapter of Chullin 6, but that this tanna holds like the view that Kutim were lion converts; and so Rashi and Ran explicitly wrote in Gittin. If so, it does not appear regarding them that ordinarily it is for Mount Gerizim. Magen Avraham holds that nevertheless he acts lishmah and his law is like an apostate Jew, who is not subject to the mitzvah of tying. Although for us, after they found the image of a dove, they are like complete idolaters, as stated in Chullin, and so we rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 2 seif 8, nevertheless there is no further difficulty on Beit Yosef here in Shulchan Aruch, who copied Kuti; for according to us he is in any case invalid because he writes for Mount Gerizim. One can say he used the language of the baraita, which speaks before they found the image of a dove, so it should read.
Even according to Rosh, who is lenient regarding processing, that is when a Jew stands over him. He establishes it as a convert who returned to his former ways, for in Gittin 45b the baraita teaches: there was an incident with one idolater in Sidon who wrote a Sefer Torah, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permitted buying it from him. The Talmud asks: but Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel requires processing lishmah; all the more he requires writing lishmah, and an idolater does not act lishmah. It answers that the above idolater of Sidon was not an actual idolater, but a convert who returned to his former ways out of fear, meaning, as Rashi explains, so idolaters would not kill him, and such a person acts lishmah. According to Rosh, it would have been better to establish it as an actual idolater with a Jew standing over him. Rather, necessarily, regarding writing Rosh agrees that this does not help.
