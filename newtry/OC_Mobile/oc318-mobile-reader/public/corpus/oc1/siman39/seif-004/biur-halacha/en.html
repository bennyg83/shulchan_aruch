Written by a heretic. Here this means one devoted to idolatry, as Beis Yosef states in Rashi's name and Kesef Mishneh states in Rambam, Hilchos Tefillin 1:13. It excludes the erroneous printed reading in Rashi, Gittin 45b, defining him as a Jewish rejecter of rabbinic teaching. Though such a person is a heretic for other laws, his writing is not burned on that account. In practice, passages written by such a scribe should be buried: perhaps the parchment was not prepared lishmah, the passages or Divine Names were not written lishmah, or he ignored chok tochos; moreover, he is suspected of causing others to sin. If observers ensured every requirement (for example, he wrote only a few letters before them) one must still ask whether he is subject to binding. There is an apparent proof from slaughter: only one subject to slaughter may slaughter, yet Yoreh De'ah 2:9 validates a Sadducee or Boethusian's slaughter after checking the knife and supervision. Since he adheres to that mitzvah, he remains subject to it; similarly, supervised writing might qualify. This can be rejected because contemporary people of this type are generally indifferent to mitzvos and commonly desecrate Shabbos publicly; they have the status Shach assigns to Karaites. It is therefore best to erase their letters and replace them. If erasure is impossible because of kesidran or because they are letters of the Divine Name, practical law requires further consideration.
