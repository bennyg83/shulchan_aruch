That an apikores wrote them - This refers to one attached to idolatry; so it is stated in Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi, and in Kesef Mishneh on Rambam, Hilchot Tefillin 1:13, excluding what was emended in some printings of Rashi, Gittin 45b, "a Jewish apikores who does not believe the words of Chazal," for that is not from Rashi's commentary, as is known. Although for other matters this too is included in apikores, it is not so for this matter, that they must be burned. As to the practical law, it appears simple to me that a scribe such as this requires genizah for his parshiyot, lest he did not process the parchment lishmah, lest he did not write the parshiyot and Divine Names lishmah, and lest he was not careful about the law of chak tokhot, since he does not believe in the words of Chazal; he is also suspected of causing others to stumble. However, if it happened that others stood over him and saw that everything was done according to halachah, for example he wrote only some letters in these parshiyot before them, or in sheets of a Sefer Torah, even so one must consider whether such a person is included among those subject to the mitzvah of tying. At first glance there is proof to permit, for regarding slaughter too we expound that one who is included in slaughter, "eat of his slaughter," but not one who is not included in slaughter, as in Chullin 3b, Tosafot s.v. kasavar; nevertheless in Yoreh De'ah siman 2 seif 9 it is ruled that a Sadducee or Boethusian, if his knife was checked and others stand over him, his slaughter is kosher. Thus, since he is established with regard to this mitzvah, even though it is not written, we call him among those who are capable of slaughter and who believe in the mitzvah of slaughter, as Peri Megadim explains there, see there. If so, here too in our case, apparently if others stand over him and see that there was no defect of chak tokhot, which is very common in the writing work, and also that it was written lishmah, one should not disqualify it, for he is still included in the mitzvah of tying. But this can be rejected, for it is known that if one abandons the permitted and eats the forbidden, even in one matter, he is thereby included as an apikores; and such people in our time are known not to be concerned for mitzvot at all, and public Shabbat desecration is also common among them. If so, their law is as Shach concludes there regarding Karaites. Therefore it is better that he erase them and write others in their place; but if he cannot erase them because of kesidran or in the letters of the Divine Name, this requires study in practice.
