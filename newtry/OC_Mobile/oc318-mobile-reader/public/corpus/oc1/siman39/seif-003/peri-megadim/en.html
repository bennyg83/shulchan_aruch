An apikores: they should be burned. See Taz. Beis Yosef's version of the Tur reads, "If an apikores wrote them, they should be buried," and this is the opinion introduced here as "some say." Bach's version, however, reads, "They should be burned." As a matter of law, Shulchan Aruch also agrees with the first opinion, which is stated without qualification. Bach nevertheless concludes as practical law that whether an apikores wrote them or they were found in the possession of a non-Jew, they should be buried rather than burned, but they are not valid. His proof is that the Baraisa in Gittin 45b lists a sectarian and a non-Jew together, indicating that their law is the same. See Menachos 42b, Rashi, beginning with the words "a Tzeduki": Rashi mentions a non-Jew because such a person is ordinarily proficient in writing. It appears that Rashi's version reads "a Tzeduki non-Jew," meaning a sectarian non-Jew who is proficient in writing, so that this is a single category. According to this, one can say that Rashi holds that the Baraisa provides no proof; otherwise it would be puzzling why it uses that expression merely to teach that the writings should not be burned. See Shach, Yoreh Deah 281, seif katan 5, who writes that nowadays non-Jews do not know how to write even a valid Torah scroll, and certainly not tefillin. See also Magen Avraham in siman 11 concerning this.
