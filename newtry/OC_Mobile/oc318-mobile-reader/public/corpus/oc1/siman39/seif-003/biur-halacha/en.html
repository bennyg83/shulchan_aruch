"Fit to write tefillin." All of this concerns a full convert. Regarding a ger toshav, Pri Megadim, Levush Serad, Machatzit HaShekel, and many others agree that he is invalid because he is not subject to the mitzvah of binding tefillin. Shaarei Teshuvah proposes a novel interpretation, that Darchei Moshe's validation refers to a ger toshav who accepted all the mitzvot except the prohibition of neveilah, so that he is subject to tefillin. In my humble opinion this too is incorrect, because in any event he is not commanded in binding tefillin. If voluntary acceptance were enough, then a woman or a slave who accepted the mitzvah of tefillin upon themselves would become fit to write tefillin, which is untenable. One cannot answer that women are protested against for wearing tefillin, as stated above in siman 38, because that is only an extra stringency based on concern for the Pesikta, whereas our Talmud holds that we do not protest, as Beit Yosef states there. Slaves likewise prove the point, since they are not protested against, as I wrote above in Mishnah Berurah in the name of Tosefet Shabbos. Likewise, according to this reasoning, if a woman undertook the mitzvah of tzitzit she would be fit even according to Rabbeinu Tam to make tzitzit for men. This cannot be correct, for then Rabbeinu Tam would agree in such a case and the Rosh's proof from Gittin 45b, from the basket of the wife of R' Chanina ben Dosa that she would one day place techelet in it for the righteous in the future, would fall away. Beyond all this, conversion cannot be partial; see Masekhet Gerim. Furthermore, one who underwent brit milah but did not immerse, regarding whom the Rosh writes in Avodah Zarah that he is no worse than a ger toshav because he accepted all the mitzvot, would according to this logic be fit to write sacred texts and, according to R' Yitzchak's view that slaughter depends on belonging to the class obligated in slaughter, would be fit to slaughter as a Jew. Yet the plain Gemara in Yevamos 46, which says, "since he did not immerse he is a non-Jew," implies otherwise.
