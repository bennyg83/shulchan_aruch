He is qualified to write tefillin. This entire ruling concerns a full convert. Pri Megadim, Levush Serad, Machatzis HaShekel, and many others agree that a resident alien is invalid because he is not subject to binding. Shaarei Teshuvah innovates that Darkei Moshe's validation concerns a resident alien who accepted every mitzvah except the prohibition of neveilah and is therefore subject to binding. This too appears incorrect: he is not commanded in binding. If voluntary acceptance sufficed, a woman or slave accepting tefillin would qualify to write them, which is untenable. The concern that women should not don tefillin is only a stringency based on Pesikta and is not shared by the Bavli; slaves demonstrate the point, since no protest applies to them. Likewise, a woman accepting tzitzis would then qualify according to Rabbeinu Tam to make tzitzis for men, undermining the Rosh's proof from the basket of Rabbi Chanina ben Dosa's wife. Moreover, conversion cannot be partial. A person circumcised but not immersed accepted all mitzvos and is no worse than a resident alien, yet Yevamos 46 plainly still calls him a gentile; he cannot therefore write sacred texts or slaughter as a Jew.
