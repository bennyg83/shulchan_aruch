(Seif katan 3) An apostate, etc. The correct reading is: nevertheless, anyone who does not put on tefillin, etc.
As written in Yoreh Deah, siman 2, seif 5 in the gloss, that in such a case regarding shechitah he is not considered one who is subject to slaughtering. The same applies here regarding binding.
So it appears from the language of the Ran in Gittin 45, and it requires study, for in Yoreh Deah, etc. Meaning that regarding the law explained in Yoreh Deah, the Ran ruled in the first chapter of Chullin, for in Avodah Zarah 26b, etc.
Alternatively, in Gittin it speaks where he ate for the first time. That is, even there it may speak without his specifying that he acts out of appetite; even ordinarily we judge that his intent was not to provoke, but for a taste of prohibition. In Avodah Zarah, however, it speaks where he ate many times and therefore acts to provoke.
But according to the Rashba in the Tur, Yoreh Deah, this is the correct reading. The Tur brought the words of the Rashba, though the Tur himself does not hold like the Rashba. The sugya in Chullin 5b works hard to establish the baraisa, and according to the Ran one could have established it as an apostate for one matter who acts to provoke, which would make him like an apostate to the entire Torah.
And in Tosafot there in Avodah Zarah it implies that even for appetite, etc. His words here are difficult, as Chemed Moshe asked. The Gemara says that minim, informers, and apostates are lowered and not raised; Rabbi Yochanan removed apostates from that teaching because of the verse including the lost object of an apostate. Tosafot explain that only regarding lowering into a pit did Rabbi Yochanan remove an apostate, but in other matters we find minim and apostates taught together. Magen Avraham understood this to mean even an apostate for appetite is invalid for writing a Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzah.
