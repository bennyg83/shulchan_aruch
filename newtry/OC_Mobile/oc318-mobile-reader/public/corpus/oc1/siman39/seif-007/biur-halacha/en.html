One may not purchase them, etc.; for more than their value, etc. The Mishnah Berurah states that the same applies when they are intrinsically invalid. This is plain according to this view: there is no difference between concern that an invalid person wrote them and a defect such as chok tochos. Rambam, however, rules that one may read from a scroll purchased from a gentile, so the Gemara does not prove that it must be purchased for burial. 'Their value' seemingly means their value if valid, for if a gentile wrote them they are actually worthless. The Gemara's price for burial must mean the price of a valid Torah scroll; that amount will not encourage the gentile, who does not know that this scroll is worthless to us, just as Taz, Yoreh De'ah 281, rules that it may not be read even privately. The same plainly applies here, though the matter remains slightly difficult.
