Or a minor — If a deaf-mute or imbecile wrote them, Pri Megadim left in his introduction a doubt: perhaps they are better than a minor, and it helps if others stand over him and signal the deaf-mute or imbecile to write leshmah. Signs of an imbecile are explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 1 s.h. 5. Regarding when a minor becomes an adult if we do not know he brought two hairs — I wrote in Mishna Berurah: if his beard is full, and in Choshen Mishpat siman 35; I omitted Magen Avraham's words that until eighteen, for all acharonim challenged him. Nevertheless we cannot disqualify after Chayei Adam and Shelah are lenient l'chatchila. I saw Pri Megadim strove to reconcile Magen Avraham, citing siman 53 s.h. 8 in hagahah: from eighteen onward it is called "his beard is full"; Pri Megadim explains that from eighteen even a little beard is called full — likewise Magen Avraham's intent with a little beard. Still, this truly requires study: perhaps that case is different, for it discusses one who brought two hairs who is biblically adult and the beard is only for communal honor — as siman 53 Shulchan Aruch shows, where even age twenty without any beard is called full for that reason; not so here, where we do not know if he brought two hairs and whether the beard makes him adult — perhaps true beard-filling is required. Certainly dean's nails [scattered beard hairs] are not called full beard; proof from Yevamot 102b Tosafos s.v. "who brought two hairs" at the end, and Or Zaru'a — not significant; likewise dean's nails are not beard-filling, else Tosafos' difficulty on Rabbah bar Avuha per the Gemara's answer would remain; and it is known the law that full beard needs no further check derives from those Tosafos, as Tur Even HaEzer siman 169 Beit Yosef ibid. — although Beit Yosef Choshen Mishpat siman 35 head seems Turei Zahav holds even without full beard it helps in his Shulchan Aruch only if full — needed for "little beard" in siman 53, possibly per Maharshal from geonic responsa: large hairs on both cheeks and by the chin — possibly full beard everywhere even biblically, or perhaps only communal honor; still perhaps leniency per Gra Choshen Mishpat: Tur's reason is that usually the lower sign comes before the upper, so we presume the lower already came. If so, Tur Even HaEzer "full beard" is not precise; Choshen Mishpat "beard sprouted" is precise — also much time after two hairs — perhaps dean's nails help; Yevamot 102b regarding R. Nachman is different since they knew he lacked two hairs — requires study. In any case, before any beard one must not let him write tefillin while we do not know he brought two hairs, even if already eighteen, as in Mishna Berurah. B'dieved, if a bar mitzvah youth wrote and we cannot check — requires study; Noda B'Yehuda MT siman 1 validates; Pri Megadim siman 39 and 55 letter 7 similarly compares to lung inspection — if lost without check, valid; responsum Re'a Choshen Mishpat siman 7 disqualifies; Yeshuot Yaakov siman 32 is strict; but for one who wrote b'dieved at eighteen, do not be strict after lenient authorities l'chatchila:

Or apostate for idolatry — It discusses one not meticulous in it; otherwise he is a heretic requiring burning [Eshel Avraham]. See Mishna Berurah: if apostate out of spite, his law is like a gentile; "out of spite" is when permitted and forbidden are before him and he leaves the permitted and eats forbidden — included in spite. If he does not insist on clarifying permission and takes what comes to hand, permitted or forbidden, he is not yet "out of spite" — only for slaughter Rama Yoreh De'ah siman 2 s.h. 5 hagahah considers it spite because he is not a valid slaughterer; see Beit Yosef and Pri Megadim Sha'ar HaTevunah s.k. 88. From this Magen Avraham learned likewise for tefillin; and as Mishna Berurah wrote: if he does not care about tefillin, even not out of spite he is not fit for binding [and per Ran who is lenient in Beit Yosef and Ran at length, likewise here — I saw one write the opposite, unclear]. See Biur HaGra s.k. 16: Rashi holds even if not out of spite, for other sins he is not fit for binding. Know: all these disqualified as spite, idolatry, or public Shabbat desecration — Shach Yoreh De'ah rules even once invalid without repentance; apparently likewise here if he does not care about tefillin — do not infer from "apostate" that only habitual apostasy, for Beit Yosef discusses spite, even once. If he does not care, perhaps he is like spite and immediately invalid — this doubt applies to Yoreh De'ah ruling one who does not care in slaughter is like spite — requires study. Inner note: stricter even for appetite is Tosafos brought by Magen Avraham — for writing Torah scroll stricter than other matters; Pri Megadim introduction siman 32 wrote much study on apostate for appetite. Especially apostate for tefillin for appetite — such a person must be known to know laws of sacred writing, engraving laws, etc., like slaughter where they must know shechita laws — Yoreh De'ah ibid.: for such a person we do not say most experts are available. It seems plain: apostate for one prayer, shel rosh or shel yad — not fit for binding, for Scripture links binding and writing. Yet if a scribe habitually wears shel rosh on his forehead and they warned him and he does not listen — great sin, yet perhaps do not prevent writing sacred texts, lest a moment they were properly placed and he remains fit for binding.
