1) Seif 1, Tefillin that a slave wrote, etc. Rashi explained: a slave and a woman are not included in tying, for it is a time-bound positive mitzvah, since night and Shabbos are not a time of tefillin. Bach, Shulchan Gavoah, letter 2.
2) There, or a minor, etc. Since we derive this from a verse, he must be a true adult, meaning that he brought two hairs after becoming thirteen years old. This is stated by Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, Mateh Yehudah, and other later authorities, unlike Nehar Shalom. The Magen Avraham's statement that in a doubtful case he is invalid to write until age eighteen is questioned by the later authorities, for we find no such measure anywhere to presume he brought two hairs. Many authorities reject this, and the words of Magen Avraham remain difficult.
3) We do not rely regarding the count of years except through valid witnesses, as written in Even HaEzer, siman 169, seif 11. Although there is an opinion there to rely if he was established as such in the eyes of the neighbors, Responsa Perach Mateh Aharon wrote that one should rely on this only in pressing circumstances. For tefillin and mezuzos there is not such pressing need, and one should be lenient only through clear proof. For the law of the two hairs and their examination, see Even HaEzer, siman 155, seifim 15 through 19, and siman 169, seif 11.
4) And if his beard filled in, one need not check for the hairs; see Even HaEzer, siman 169, seif 10, and Choshen Mishpat, siman 35.
5) And if he reached twenty years and did not bring two hairs, and one of the signs of a saris was born in him, he is an adult; see Choshen Mishpat there, seif 2. See also Even HaEzer, siman 155, seif 12, regarding how the age of twenty is calculated, and Even HaEzer, siman 172, seif 5, for the signs of a saris.
6) And if signs of a saris were not born in him, he is not called an adult until most of his years have passed, meaning thirty-five years and one day; see Choshen Mishpat there and Even HaEzer, siman 155, seif 13.
7) And if he was made a saris by human action, once he reaches thirteen years and one day he is called an adult, for such a person will never bring signs; see Even HaEzer, siman 172, seif 7.
8) There, or a minor, etc. The same applies to a deaf-mute or a mentally incompetent person, who are invalid. So too a tumtum or androgynos; if the tumtum was opened and found to be male, his writing is valid, as stated by the cited authorities.
9) And in these laws it is not a great pleasure and kosher that stands because the point is that there is a difference between binding and writing.
