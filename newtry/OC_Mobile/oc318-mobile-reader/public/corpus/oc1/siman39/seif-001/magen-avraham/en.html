Apostate regarding the entire Torah. Or out of spite — even for one transgression; but for appetite, for one transgression he is not disqualified [Beit Yosef]. Nevertheless, whoever does not lay tefillin — even if not out of spite — is invalid, for he is not obligated in binding, as written Yoreh De'ah siman 2 s.h. 5. However, one who does not lay tefillin for appetite — such as one who follows his business — is fit to write, as we say regarding slaughter that he is called a valid slaughterer; and so from Ran's words, he disqualifies only one who casts off the yoke, abandons the permitted and eats the forbidden. Ran wrote he is disqualified only rabbinically, for biblically an apostate for idolatry is not an apostate for the entire Torah — and this requires study. In Yoreh De'ah siman 2 s.b. Ran ruled that out of spite his law is like a gentile; and one could say here it discusses one who does not act out of spite and also not for appetite — for in Avodah Zarah ch. 2 Ran wrote: if permitted and forbidden are before him and he leaves the permitted and eats the forbidden, he is called acting out of spite; and there he ruled that out of spite is a sectarian, and if he wrote a Torah scroll it is burned — therefore one must say here it discusses not out of spite; if so, how did he write that he abandons the permitted and eats forbidden, which would be out of spite for him? One must say: in Gittin it discusses one who explicitly states he does not act out of spite but for the taste of the forbidden; or in Gittin he ate once and in Avodah Zarah he ate many times, tasting forbidden the first time — so Tosafos in Avodah Zarah and so in the Gemara, all per Ran. But per Rashba and Tur and Yoreh De'ah ibid., out of spite for one matter is not suspected for the entire Torah; and Chullin implies their view, for it labors to establish "apostate" for what, and not to establish "apostate out of spite" — and this requires study on Rabbeinu Yerucham in Yoreh De'ah siman 258, who wrote out of spite is a sectarian and if he wrote a Torah scroll it is burned, like Rashi Avodah Zarah 26 — if so, necessarily here it discusses one who does not act out of spite, yet here it writes it discusses one who acts out of spite — requires study.
