A minor. Because the verse requires an actual adult, he must have produced two pubic hairs after reaching thirteen years of age. Because of doubt, one should disqualify his writing until he reaches eighteen. See Even HaEzer, siman 148, se'if 25; Choshen Mishpat, siman 35, regarding testimony; siman 55, se'if 5 and se'if 9; and siman 199, se'if 10.

An apostate. One who rejects the entire Torah, or who transgresses even one prohibition out of spite, is disqualified. One who transgresses one prohibition out of appetite is not disqualified. Beis Yosef. One who does not put on tefillin is disqualified from writing them even if he does not act out of spite, because he is not included among those who bind them; see Yoreh Deah, siman 2, se'if 5. One who transgresses out of appetite, such as for business gain, remains qualified to write, just as he remains a participant in ritual slaughter. Ran's phrase "one who casts off the yoke" means one who abandons permitted food and eats forbidden food. Ran holds that this disqualification is rabbinic, for biblically one who is an apostate concerning a single matter is not considered an apostate concerning the entire Torah. This is difficult in light of Yoreh Deah, siman 2, se'if 2, where Ran treats one who transgresses out of spite like a non-Jew. It cannot refer to one acting neither out of spite nor appetite, for Ran in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah defines the category otherwise. There, one who acts out of spite is a min and a Torah scroll that he writes must be burned. Consequently, the case here must concern one who does not act out of spite, though the phrase "abandons permitted food" remains difficult. The answer may be that in Gittin the Gemara explicitly says he did so for the taste, or that there is a distinction between a single occurrence and habitual conduct; see Tosafos in Avodah Zarah and the Gemara there. Rashba and Tur in Yoreh Deah hold that one who violates one matter out of spite is not thereby suspected concerning the entire Torah, and tractate Chullin supports them. Yet Beis Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 158, writes that one who acts out of spite is a min, and Rashi in Avodah Zarah 26 says his Torah scroll must be burned. Thus the case here must not involve spite, despite the wording. Tosafos, s.v. "Remove this from here," implies that even one who acts from appetite is invalid for writing.
