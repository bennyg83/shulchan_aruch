Beis Yosef considers whether, according to Rashi and Rabbi Meir, an informer and an apostate Jew are disqualified by Torah law, while other authorities say that they are disqualified only by rabbinic law; see there, in the name of Ran in the chapter HaSholeiach. Taz adopts a compromise: one who is an apostate regarding Shabbos or who transgresses out of spite is disqualified by Torah law, because he does not believe in the binding of the tefillin, whereas the Sages disqualified an informer only by rabbinic law. This applies specifically to tefillin and a Torah scroll, as stated in Yoreh Deah 281:3. Regarding other prohibitions, however, an informer is trusted, as the Mechaber writes in Yoreh Deah 119:10 and Shach writes there in seif katan 23. See also Yoreh Deah, siman 2, Taz, seif katan 13, where an informer is treated like an apostate who sins to satisfy his appetite. Regarding the words "and many will become wise," see Bach and Perishah. They write that the Tur omitted the case of an informer because it follows a fortiori from a non-Jew: if a non-Jew, whom one does not lower into a pit, is disqualified, then certainly an informer, whom one does lower, is disqualified. The difficulty is that, if so, why does Gittin 45 state explicitly that it is forbidden? See Drishah, who writes that the distinction between sinning out of spite and sinning to satisfy one's appetite applies to mitzvos between man and his Creator, but not to a thief or violent robber, for to satisfy his desire he destroys his fellow's livelihood; see there. This requires great study. According to this, one who violates a mitzvah between man and his fellow to satisfy his appetite would likewise be treated as one who sins out of spite. He would consequently be an apostate with respect to the entire Torah, and his slaughter would be disqualified by Torah law. We have never heard such a ruling. A robber is disqualified from testimony, but not from other matters, because he is termed wicked, etc. I saw in Choshen Mishpat 425, concerning the words "one does not raise the shepherds," that Perishah likewise writes something similar; see there. This requires extensive study.
