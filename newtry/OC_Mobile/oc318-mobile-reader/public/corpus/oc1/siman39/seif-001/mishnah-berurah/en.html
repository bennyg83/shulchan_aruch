Tefillin. The same applies to a Torah scroll and mezuzah.
Or a woman. The same applies to a tumtum and androgynos, for they are included in a doubtful woman.
Or a minor. Since we derive this from a verse, we require an actual adult, namely one who brought two hairs after becoming thirteen years old. If it is doubtful whether he brought two hairs, he is invalid to write unless his beard filled in, most of his years passed, or signs of a saris appeared. Scribes who allow youths to write tefillin without verifying signs of adulthood should be rebuked; see above, siman 32, seif katan 103, and Biur Halacha.
For education. In truth, even one who is an adult by years is not valid whenever we do not know that he brought two hairs. But for an adult, if afterward we checked and found two hairs, we presume he had them at the time of writing; this does not apply to a minor, for his hairs then do not help.
Or a Kusi. This is the correct reading, for a non-Jew is invalid in any event since he does not write for its required purpose.
Or an apostate to idolatry. In this he is like an apostate to the entire Torah. The same applies to one who publicly desecrates Shabbos. But an apostate for other transgressions is not considered an apostate to the entire Torah unless he acts to provoke. Some are more stringent and require genizah. If he is an apostate regarding tefillin and does not put them on, even if not to provoke, he is invalid, unless he neglects them for appetite or business and is still included in binding. Initially, one should avoid all apostates for any transgression.
Or, etc. See Baer Heitev, that this applies even for appetite alone and even one time.
Who is not included in binding, etc. A slave, woman, and Kusi are not commanded in binding, and a minor who reached education is obligated only D'Rabbanan. In all these cases it does not help even if an adult stands over him and watches that it be for its required purpose.
