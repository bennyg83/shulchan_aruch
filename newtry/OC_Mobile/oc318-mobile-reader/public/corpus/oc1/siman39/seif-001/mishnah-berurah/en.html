(1) Tefillin — and likewise a Torah scroll and mezuzah:

(2) Or a woman — and likewise a tumtum and androgynous, who are included in the doubt of a woman:

(3) Or a minor — since we derive from Scripture, we require that he be truly an adult, meaning he brought two hairs after age thirteen; but if we doubt whether he brought two hairs, he is invalid to write unless his beard is full — meaning abundant hair on his beard even if very small [Sema in Choshen Mishpat siman 35], or most of his years have passed, or signs of a saris developed, as below siman 55 s.h. 5. One should rebuke scribes who let youths write tefillin without checking signs of maturity. See above siman 32 s.k. 103 in the name of Levush on this matter, and see Biur Halacha:

(4) For training — in truth, even an adult: whenever we do not know he brought two hairs. But regarding an adult, if afterward we checked and found two hairs, we presume he had them at the time of writing [Choshen Mishpat siman 35] — not so a minor, for hairs found later do not help:

(5) Or Samaritan — so too. A gentile is in any case invalid, for he does not write leshmah:

(6) Or apostate for idolatry — for in this he is like an apostate for the entire Torah; and likewise if he publicly desecrates Shabbat. But if apostate for other sins, we rule an apostate for one matter is not apostate for the entire Torah — except if he acts out of spite, for then even for idolatry his law is like a Samaritan, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 2. Some are stricter — burial is required. All this if apostate for other sins; but if apostate regarding tefillin — not laying tefillin, even not out of spite — he is invalid, for he is not fit for binding, unless he was only apostate for appetite, such as following business — he remains fit for binding. All this b'dieved; l'chatchila one should be strict not to let such a person write sacred writings, for some are strict even for appetite, even with others standing over him telling him to write leshmah. See Biur Halacha. Pri Megadim wrote: even if he publicly desecrates Shabbat in a rabbinic prohibition, such as muktzeh or carrying to a karmelit — one should be strict l'chatchila not to let him write sacred writings; and even b'dieved requires study:

(7) Or, etc. — see Beit HaTefilah: even for appetite alone, even once:

(8) Not obligated in binding, etc. — slave, woman, and Samaritan are not warned about binding; a minor, even if he reached chinuch, is only rabbinically obligated [Beit Yosef]. See Biur Halacha. In all these, an adult standing over him does not help even if he sees them written leshmah:
