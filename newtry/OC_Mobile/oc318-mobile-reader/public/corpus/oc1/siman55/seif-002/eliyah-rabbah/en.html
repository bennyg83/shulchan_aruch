With Avos, etc. So the Bach ruled, and he wrote that the Rama wrote likewise. It appears to me that according to this, what the Rama wrote, 'after he began aloud and Kedushah,' does not specifically mean Kedushah, for the same law applies even if he began only Avos, and so the Magen Avraham wrote. Alternatively, they mentioned Kedushah as an added novelty, that even so they do not read from the Torah; and this requires study. Regarding Birkas Kohanim, the Kenesses HaGedolah discussed it at length and concluded that he should say 'Our G-d, etc.'; see there. I wrote in siman 121 that even an individual should say it.
