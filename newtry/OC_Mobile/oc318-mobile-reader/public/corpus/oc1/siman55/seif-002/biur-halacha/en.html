If he began, etc., that kedushah — see Mishna Berurah and Tur, who also used this language; we can say simply: according to what Beur HaGra implies in siman 69 — according to Tur, spreading Shema is in the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur; he says until after kedushah and goes no further; here Tur follows his reasoning.

And nevertheless it is a transgression, etc. — if the tenth passed and left, whether the ninth may also leave, since otherwise there are not ten for resting of the Shekhinah; and otherwise it is permitted to finish — uncertain.

But if they remained, etc. — from this wording it seems at first glance that even in the middle of kedushah it is permitted to leave; and below siman 125 it does not imply so. The truth will show its way: Mordechai does not discuss kedushah there at all, but the main law — if he began, he finishes; for he said about this in Yerushalmi that leaving is a transgression, and Mordechai hints at this — when ten did not remain; and Yerushalmi does not discuss kedushah there at all; and Rama too does not refer to kedushah but to the main law — if he began, he finishes — which applies to the entire prayer, as below in the gloss.
