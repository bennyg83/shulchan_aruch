If he began, etc., that kedushah. See the Mishnah Berurah and the Tur, who also used this wording. We can explain this straightforwardly according to what is implied by the Beur HaGra in siman 69, that according to the Tur, spreading Shema means that in the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur he says only until after Kedushah and no more. Thus here the Tur follows his own view.
Nevertheless it is a transgression, etc. If the tenth man transgressed and left, whether the ninth is also permitted to leave, since in any case there are not ten for the resting of the Shechinah and in any case they are permitted to finish, requires study.
But if ten remained, etc. From this wording it would seem at first glance that even in the middle of Kedushah it is permitted to leave, yet below in siman 125 it does not appear so. The truth points the way: the Mordechai there is not discussing Kedushah at all, but the basic law that if they began, they may finish, regarding which the Yerushalmi says that those who leave commit a transgression. The Mordechai alludes to this, namely that this applies where ten did not remain. The Yerushalmi there is not discussing Kedushah at all, and the Rama also is not referring to Kedushah, but to the basic law that if they began, they may finish, which applies to the entire prayer, as appears just below in the gloss.
