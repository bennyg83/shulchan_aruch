(6) If he began—and the same applies to everything requiring ten, such as nesiat kapayim, keriat haTorah, and haftarah from the Prophets—they finish everything pertaining to that matter once they already began with ten; but they do not begin what is a different matter.

(7) To say Kaddish—and if he began to say Yishtabach with ten and some left, and likewise if he began to say Ashrei before Minchah with ten and some left, Peri Megadim is uncertain whether he may say the Kaddish after it, for that Kaddish belongs to Yishtabach and Ashrei. And see Sha'arei Tefillah in the name of Eliyah Rabbah; however, in Sefer Maamar Mordechai, Derech HaChayim, and Chayei Adam, all ruled that he may not say Kaddish in this case.

(8) Or kedushah—not specifically; for the same applies even if he began only with Avot, as below in se'if 53; and possibly he mentioned it to include the end—that even in this case it applies only if a majority remained.

(9) That Kaddish—and if the Kaddish was before Barekhu, Derech HaChayim wrote in the name of Eliyah Rabbah that he may also say Barekhu, for it is all one matter. And in Sefer Maamar Mordechai he disputes this; see there.

(10) That kedushah—and the same applies to the entire Shemoneh Esreh. And according to what is explained in the gloss in se'if 3, he also finishes the half-Kaddish and full Kaddish after it. And the later authorities wrote that he should also say the blessing "Elokeinu veElokei avoteinu, beracheinu babrachah hameshuleshet," etc., for whoever began the Shemoneh Esreh finishes it, even though we learned "we do not raise hands with fewer than ten"; and even if he began the prayer with ten—as Ran and Beit Yosef explain—that refers specifically to actual nesiat kapayim, because it is a different matter; but the prayer "Elokeinu" he says, since he began the Shemoneh Esreh, and this too belongs to the Shemoneh Esreh.

(11) A majority of them—that is, six; and we do not require a recognizable majority [Peri Megadim].

(12) It is a transgression—it seems this means if he does not wait until they finish that matter; but if he waits until they finish the matter, that suffices. For example, if one joined with nine for Barekhu, he is not obligated to wait for the joining of the shaliach tzibbur's repetition, which is a different matter; but if he was with them in their joining when the shaliach tzibbur began aloud, he must wait until they finish the entire kedushah section and the full Kaddish after it; and likewise in every similar case.

(13) But if they remained, etc.—see Biur Halachah.

(14) It is permitted to leave—provided they already heard kedushah and the Kaddishim up through Aleinu.
