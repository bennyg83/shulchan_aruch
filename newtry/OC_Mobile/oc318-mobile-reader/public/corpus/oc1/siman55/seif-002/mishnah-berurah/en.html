(6) If he began. The same applies to any matter that requires ten, such as lifting the hands, Torah reading, and the haftarah in the prophets: they complete all that belongs to that matter, since they already began it with ten, but they do not begin something that is a different matter.
(7) To say Kaddish. If he began to say Yishtabach with ten and some left, or if he began to say Ashrei before Minchah with ten and some left, the Peri Megadim is uncertain whether perhaps he may say the Kaddish after it, since the Kaddish belongs to Yishtabach and Ashrei. See Shaarei Teshuvah in the name of Eliyah Rabbah. However, Maamar Mordechai, Derech HaChaim, and Chayei Adam all ruled that he may not say Kaddish in this case.
(8) Or Kedushah. This is not precise, for the same applies even if they are only at Avos, as below in seif 3. Perhaps it mentioned this as a novelty in the latter clause, that even for this, only if the majority remained.
(9) That Kaddish. If it was the Kaddish before Barchu, Derech HaChaim wrote in the name of Eliyah Rabbah that he may also say Barchu, for it is all one matter. Maamar Mordechai disagrees with this; see there.
(10) The Kedushah. The same applies to the entire Shemoneh Esrei. According to what is explained in the gloss in seif 3, he also completes the half Kaddish and the full Kaddish after it. The later authorities wrote that he should also say the blessing Elokeinu u’Elokei avoseinu, bless us with the threefold blessing, etc., for once he began Shemoneh Esrei he completes it. Although we learned that the kohanim do not lift their hands with fewer than ten, even if the prayer began with ten, as in the Ran and Beit Yosef, that is specifically actual nesias kapayim, because it is a different matter. But the prayer Elokeinu should be said, since he began Shemoneh Esrei, and this too belongs to Shemoneh Esrei.
(11) Their majority. That means six, and we do not require a conspicuous majority, as the Peri Megadim writes.
(12) It is a transgression. It seems to me that he means if he does not wait until they finish that matter. But if he waits until they finish the matter, this is sufficient. For example, if one joined with nine for Barchu, he is not obligated to wait to join for the repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, which is a different matter. If he was with them in their combination when the shaliach tzibbur began aloud, he must wait until they finish the entire order of Kedushah and the full Kaddish after it. The same applies in similar cases.
(13) But if there remained, etc. See Biur Halacha.
(14) It is permitted to leave. This is provided that they already heard Kedushah and the Kaddishim up to Aleinu.
