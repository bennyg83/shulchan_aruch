(Seif 10.) If there is one youth, etc., and the thirteenth year is not intercalated, etc. See note: we have the text in Zevachim [daf 25 amud bet]: Rava said — it is taught: a lamb, unblemished, male, one year old, that it be unblemished and one year old at the time of slaughter, at acceptance, at carrying, at sprinkling — from where? Scripture teaches: "it shall be" — all its states shall be only unblemished and one year old, etc. And is there anything that at slaughter is one year old and at carrying and sprinkling is two years old? Rava said: this teaches that hours disqualify in consecrated animals — thus the Gemara's words; and see Rashi. At first glance it was difficult for me — what is the question? One finds it: he was born on Rosh Chodesh Nisan and the like, and his year did not end until the next Rosh Chodesh Nisan; and on the thirtieth of Adar witnesses had not yet come to sanctify the month, and they thought it would be intercalated, and his year had not yet ended until the morrow when it would be Rosh Chodesh — and at slaughter he was one year old, and afterward between slaughter and sprinkling witnesses came and the court sanctified the month, and the day had already become a second year. However, one may say that here we say the matter was revealed retroactively, that even at slaughter he was two years old; and there is a practical difference in such a case: a minor who recited Birkat HaMazon or prayed and discharged others who were eighteen, and he was still in the category of minor, and afterward the court sanctified the month — he becomes an adult retroactively and those eighteen were discharged through him, and he need not bless and pray again; and likewise if he ate forbidden fat then, he is flogged and brings a sacrifice retroactively. However, this is difficult for me only according to those who explain in the name of Yerushalmi on the verse "I will call to Hashem, who is exalted over me" — for behold, if she was three years and one day old and the year was intercalated, her virginity returns; and Rama brought them in Even HaEzer siman 20, Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 189, and Magen Avraham here. If so, we may also say the baraita's intent is thus: we require that he be one year old specifically at slaughter — but if at slaughter he was not one year old but two years old, only that afterward he became one year old, such as when at slaughter the year was not intercalated and he already had more than a year, and afterward the court intercalated the year and retroactively he became within his year — that does not help here, since at slaughter he was not one year old; and we require specifically that at slaughter he be in the status of one year old. And for this reason the verse teaches: specifically if after the entire offering he becomes one year old retroactively it does not help, since all his offerings were in the presumption of two years; but if in one of the services, such as carrying, acceptance, or sprinkling, he then becomes one year old, it helps to validate it — therefore it says regarding carrying, acceptance, sprinkling — from where? It does not help if in that service he becomes one year old; and we require specifically at slaughter — therefore it says as written "it shall be" — all its acts shall be in one category; but if in one act it was as two years old, it does not help. And why does Rava challenge? Therefore it seemed to prove from this that Yerushalmi does not say so except regarding virginity returning, and you have in it only its novelty; but for other laws, if at first he was established in the presumption of a [regular] year, even if afterward they intercalated the year, he does not return retroactively — and specifically if they knew from the start it would be intercalated, then his year did not begin until the second, because the main existence was in the second and the first was only supplementary. And in what Yerushalmi wrote in the first chapter of Megillah — the second is main and the first supplementary — but if at the time of Adar they still did not know it would be intercalated and they established him for the month of Adar, truly the years do not return retroactively; and there is a practical difference from this for Magen Avraham's ruling, who wondered why the author wrote "and the thirteenth year is not intercalated" — for even if it is intercalated, one born on the twenty-ninth of first Adar becomes bar mitzvah on the twenty-ninth of the second — see there. And in my humble opinion it seems not as his words: granted, when one was born in a non-intercalated year and at the time it becomes intercalated, he does not become [bar mitzvah] until second Adar — one may say the second is main and it is Adar, and he was born only in Adar, therefore he does not become bar mitzvah until second Adar. And conversely, if born in an intercalated year in first Adar and in the thirteenth year it is not intercalated, there is concern that the first is the main Adar, and then his year is not fulfilled until the month of Adar day for day. But if at the hour he was born it was intercalated, when he becomes thirteen years the year is intercalated — if so, either way: if the first is main, even now the first is main and his year was fulfilled in the first; and if the second is main, behold he was not born in its main part but in the supplementary part, and his thirteenth year was fulfilled in the supplementary part — either way he already became thirteen, and why should he wait further? Magen Avraham seems to base mainly on Yerushalmi "I will call to Hashem who is exalted over me," that it is like a scriptural decree without reason — for regarding virginity returning, which reason does not suggest, we say so; therefore he holds we say so here too. But according to what I proved, we do not say so except there, and we do not derive from it generally; if so, here too in such a case where the thirteenth year is intercalated, he becomes bar mitzvah in the first — and this is correct; it requires study [I again saw in later authorities who brought in the name of Or Zarua and responsa Me'il Tzedakah that they also disagreed with Magen Avraham on this — my intent was their view, which I raised in the law]. And since I spoke in the Zevachim sugya above, I will mention what puzzled me in what Rava said — this teaches hours disqualify in consecrated animals: what relevance has the language of disqualification? On the contrary, per the matter the hours are validating in consecrated animals — for if we did not say that in consecrated animals we count hour by hour, then immediately at the day's start he becomes a second year, as Rashi's meaning there and Tosafos in Rosh Hashana and Niddah and Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 35 and Magen Avraham here — and he would be disqualified from morning; but if we require hours, then all whose hour is not complete is one year old and fit for offering — if so, on the contrary they would be hours validating in consecrated animals. And it is true one finds disqualification too in those requiring two or three years, and then one finds disqualification through requiring hours; nevertheless, why should Rava mention a different matter not under discussion and leave the body of the law he speaks of? And since one finds validation too, he should have listed this side that hours validate in consecrated animals — for the force of permission is preferable everywhere; this too is dealt with in its place — uncertain; I have no business with this now to examine it; perhaps Hashem will grant me another time to explain more — it requires study.
