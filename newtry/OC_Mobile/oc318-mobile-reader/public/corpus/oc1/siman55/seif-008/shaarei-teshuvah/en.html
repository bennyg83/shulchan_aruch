“Presumably.” See Baer Heitev. This implies that the presumption is ineffective for a Torah-level obligation. In truth, many halachic authorities hold that Rava’s presumption has Torah authority. See above, siman 39, what I wrote from Noda BiYehudah, second edition, and Responsa Beit Ephraim, Even HaEzer, where this subject is discussed at length. Regarding the reference to siman 53, se’if katan 13, which states that at the beginning of the day on which he was born he is considered one day old: this means from the beginning of the night that introduces the date of his birth, which is called the beginning of the day. The Bach likewise writes explicitly in siman 145 that as soon as night falls he becomes an adult, even though thirteen complete years have not yet elapsed from the precise hour of his birth; see there. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 6, who writes that the established custom follows the Magen Avraham: immediately when night falls on the anniversary date of his birth, he becomes bar mitzvah, and one should not depart from the custom. See there. See also Magen Avraham, siman 69, se’if katan 4, where he refers to siman 236.
