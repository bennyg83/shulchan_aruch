A deaf-mute who speaks and does not hear — see Peri Megadim, who is uncertain about a deaf-mute who speaks and does not hear, who intends to fulfill his obligation through his fellow's blessing and knows at that time which blessing his fellow is reciting to discharge him — whether he fulfills his obligation thereby even if he answered Amen. And see there further, that from his words it implies that one who speaks and does not hear is not fit to be shaliach tzibbur according to Taz in siman 489 seif 1. Here it implies that according to what the later authorities ruled [Bach, Magen Avraham, Gra, and other later authorities], not like Taz, one need not remove him from being shaliach tzibbur for this reason. However, in Chiddushei Ra'ah in siman 53 it implies that according to all it is not proper that such a one be shaliach tzibbur, for lekhatchilah according to all he must hear with his ears.

He is like a fool and a minor — see siman 199 seif 10 in the gloss, that it implies that even a fool, if he is attentive and understands, combines for zimmun; if so, the same applies to prayer, at least in a time of need [for according to Rama above in seif 4 in the gloss it implies that for prayer combining is lighter than for zimmun, for in prayer in a case of need it implies from him that one may rely on the lenient authorities, as Darkei Moshe wrote explicitly; whereas in siman 199 regarding zimmun he is strict and does not distinguish there at all in this, and the reason is so that Jews should not remain without Barchu and kedushah] — and see in Magen Avraham s.k. 8 in the name of Levush Chayei, who explains that there it deals with one who is not a complete fool, and it is not clear, for he thought to resolve the difficulty from this siman and did not resolve it according to what we rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 1, that even with one sign of folly he is a complete fool by Torah law; and if he has no sign of folly it is obvious. But the truth is as Magen Avraham's words, that Maharil's words are only per the view that a minor lying in a cradle combines; but according to what we rule, that a minor lying in a cradle does not combine, a deaf-mute and fool are no better than a minor lying in a cradle; if so, it is obvious that for prayer too they cannot combine them even in a time of need. And know further that the same applies if he was sound-minded from birth but afterward became a deaf-mute who does not hear and does not speak — he too is considered in the category of fool [Peri Megadim in Yoreh De'ah siman 1 in Sha'ar HaTaharah s.k. 22]. And one who is sometimes ill and sometimes a fool — when he is ill he is like a sound-minded person for all his matters [Choshen Mishpat in siman 35 seif 9].
