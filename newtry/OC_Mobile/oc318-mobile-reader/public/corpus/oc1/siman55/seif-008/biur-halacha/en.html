A deaf person who speaks but does not hear. See the Peri Megadim, who is uncertain regarding a deaf person who speaks but does not hear, who intends to fulfill his obligation through his fellow's blessing, and knows at that time the blessing that the other is reciting to discharge him, whether he fulfills his obligation through this even if he answered Amen. See there further, for his words imply that one who speaks but does not hear is not fit to be shaliach tzibbur according to the Taz in siman 689, seif 1. It follows from this that according to what the later authorities ruled, namely the Bach, Magen Avraham, Beur HaGra, and other later authorities, not like the Taz, one should not remove him from being shaliach tzibbur on account of this. However, in the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger in siman 53 it implies that according to all opinions it is not proper for such a person to be shaliach tzibbur, for initially, according to all opinions, one must make the words audible to his own ears.
He is like one mentally incompetent and like a minor. See siman 199, seif 10, in the gloss, which implies that even one mentally incompetent, if he has intent and understands, combines for zimun. If so, the same should apply to prayer, at least in a pressing situation. According to the Rama's view above in seif 4 in the gloss, it appears that combining for prayer is more lenient than for zimun, for regarding prayer in a pressing situation it implies that one may rely on the lenient opinions, as he wrote explicitly in Darkei Moshe, whereas in siman 199 regarding zimun he is stringent and makes no distinction there. The reason is so that Israel should not remain without Barechu and Kedushah. See there in Magen Avraham, seif katan 8, in the name of Lechem Chamudos, who explains that there it speaks of one who is not fully mentally incompetent. This is not clear, for he intended to resolve the difficulty from this siman but did not resolve it according to what we rule in Yoreh Deah, siman 1, that even with one sign of mental incompetence he is fully considered mentally incompetent according to the Torah; and if he has no sign of mental incompetence at all, the matter is obvious. However, the truth is as the Magen Avraham says, that the words of Maharil apply only according to the opinion that a minor lying in a cradle combines. But according to what we rule, that a minor lying in a cradle does not combine, a deaf person and one mentally incompetent are no better than a minor lying in a cradle. If so, it is obvious that the same applies to prayer, and they cannot be combined even in a pressing situation. Know further that the same applies if he was born sound-minded but afterward became deaf, not hearing and not speaking; he too is considered within the category of one mentally incompetent, as stated by the Peri Megadim in Yoreh Deah, siman 1, Sifsei Daas, seif katan 22. One who is sometimes sound and sometimes mentally incompetent, when he is sound, he is like a fully competent person for every matter.
