A Chumash, etc. Meaning a Chumash written on parchment like a Torah scroll; but our Chumashim, according to all opinions, do not combine, and so the Bach wrote.
They are accustomed to be lenient, etc. The Levush wrote: I have not seen people accustomed to do so. At first glance this is difficult, for perhaps it is because we do not have the Chumashim mentioned above, and therefore he did not see people accustomed to do so. Rather, he means that it would have been possible through a Torah scroll itself. So my grandfather, the Gaon, of blessed memory, would regularly rule in practice, that one should not combine him even in a pressing situation. The Bach likewise ruled that the Rama's intent was certainly not to practice this way, Heaven forbid, nor that he saw people practicing this way. Rather, he wrote that some are accustomed, meaning that in some place where they have practiced this way from earlier times they have whom to rely on, and one should not protest against them; but certainly he did not see people in our country practice this way, as the Levush testified. My grandfather, the Gaon, of blessed memory, was elderly and sat in yeshivah more than fifty years. A clear proof to this appears to me, for in siman 199, seif 10, the Rama himself wrote that one does not combine him for zimun at all, and so is the custom, and one should not change. If so, all the more so for prayer, since zimun is more lenient than prayer, as the Maharshal and the Bach wrote. Rather, it is as we said, and not like the Magen Avraham, who wrote that in our time they are accustomed to combine him by means of a Chumash for Kedushah, Barechu, and Kaddish, but not for the Kaddish after Aleinu. This is certainly not an established custom, and those who do so are acting because they did not understand the Rama's words. Aside from this, I saw that the Maharshal brought many poskim prohibiting it, and also concluded that even in an emergency one should not combine.
