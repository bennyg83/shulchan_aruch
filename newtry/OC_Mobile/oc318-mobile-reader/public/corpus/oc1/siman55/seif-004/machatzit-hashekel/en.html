(Seif katan 4) With nine, etc. At the end of siman 690 it appears that the matter is doubtful. Rashbatz inferred from the fact that a minor may be counted among the seven called to the Torah that he has some degree of obligation and may therefore be included in a minyan of ten.
To this Magen Avraham responds that women are certainly obligated in the Megillah reading. They too were included in that miracle, as stated at the beginning of siman 689. Nevertheless, regarding the requirement that the Megillah be read before ten, Rama writes at the end of siman 690 that it is doubtful whether women combine. Magen Avraham there writes that the Tur records a dispute on this point. See there.
By means of a Chumash. Bach writes that even according to the view that holding a Chumash is effective, it must be written as a scroll like a Sefer Torah, as Tosafos writes, unlike our printed chumashim. Accordingly, the child would need to hold a Sefer Torah. Eliyah Rabbah, however, writes in the name of his grandfather Rabbi Shmuel that even in pressing circumstances he was unwilling to rely upon this. Responsa Shav Yaakov, siman 6, rules likewise. He writes that Rama also did not intend that one should rely upon this, even though he writes that some are accustomed to do so. For in siman 199, seif 6, Beit Yosef writes that a minor may be included for zimun, whether with three or with ten. Rama writes there: “Some say that he is not included until he is thirteen years old, and this is the custom and should not be changed.” Thus Rama himself rules regarding Bircas HaMazon that a minor may not be included, whether with three or with ten, and that the custom should not be changed. One cannot say that Rama considers prayer more lenient than Bircas HaMazon. On the contrary, Beit Yosef holds the reverse, for regarding prayer he rules without qualification that a minor is not included, while regarding Bircas HaMazon he writes that a minor is included both with three and with ten. How could we say that Rama holds views exactly opposite to Beit Yosef? Rather, Rama holds that prayer and Bircas HaMazon are equal, and that in neither case is a minor included. Responsa Shav Yaakov writes that in siman 55 Rama likewise agrees that a minor is not included even when holding a Chumash. He merely writes that some are accustomed to be lenient, meaning that those who follow this custom rely, in pressing circumstances, upon the view cited by the Mechaber. Rama does not write that those who act this way are acting properly, and he relies upon what he wrote in siman 199, seif 6. He discusses this at length, and I have abbreviated it. See also what Taz writes in seif katan 3, and Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 234, who disagrees.
