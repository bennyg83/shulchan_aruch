4) Kaddish should not be recited at all except at the established places in the prayers, after reading some words of Torah, or after an explanation or exposition of a verse. It should not be recited after the prayers composed by Rav Saadiah Gaon or after supplications for mercy. Rambam in his responsa, Pe'er HaDor, new edition, 130. Orchos Chaim, page 96 at the beginning of column 4, writes in Maharam's name that Kaddish is not recited over Torah verses, which require no reinforcement, while Ashrei was made like a rabbinic prayer; see there. The aforementioned words of Rambam prove the opposite. Raavad likewise writes in the responsum cited by Maran in Beis Yosef that Kaddish is recited after a psalm; see there. This is the practice. Birkei Yosef 2; Kesher Gudal 8:3–4; Shalmei Tzibbur 81b; Chesed LaAlafim 54:2; and Yafeh LaLev 2. Those who recite Kaddish after a circumcision without first reciting a psalm therefore act improperly. Chesed LaAlafim, ibid. The people customarily recite Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya, etc., after any Oral Torah and then Kaddish DeRabbanan. After Written Torah, they recite 'The salvation of the righteous is from Hashem,' etc., and then Kaddish Yehei Shelama Rabbah.
