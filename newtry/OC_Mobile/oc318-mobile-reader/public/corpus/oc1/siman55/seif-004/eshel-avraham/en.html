“To whom they pray.” See what Baer Heitev writes in the name of Ateres Zekeinim. See also Responsa Shav Yaakov, siman 6, which rules stringently even in pressing circumstances, because most poskim are stringent. Even were one to say that the views are evenly balanced, this would still be comparable to a case of doubtful blessings, in which we rule leniently, all the more so since there are two Rabbinic elements here: prayer itself is D'Rabbanan, as is the requirement to pray with ten. See the Rosh. See also Rama, siman 199, who writes explicitly that they do not combine and that one should not deviate, whereas here he writes that some are accustomed to be lenient. His rulings would therefore appear contradictory. One cannot answer that the Rama holds that combining a minor with three for zimun is more stringent than combining him with ten. See siman 194, where for three it is sufficient that they call to him and inform him, while for ten he must actually sit with them. This would seem to show that combining for three is more stringent. That is incorrect, because there the reason is that we require “Magnify Hashem with me,” and it is therefore sufficient that he hear and respond. With ten, however, the reason is that the Shechinah rests among them, which applies only when they sit together in one company. Regarding the inclusion of a minor, it is certainly more lenient. See siman 194, which states explicitly that a minor does not combine even with ten, for a davar shebikdushah is certainly more stringent than zimun. Nor do we find any posek who holds that zimun with ten is more stringent than a minyan of ten for prayer. See there.
