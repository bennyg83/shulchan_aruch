They pray — in Be'er HaTov in the name of Or Zarua he wrote; and see Shevut Yaakov siman 6, that he was stringent in the matter even in a time of pressure, because most decisors were stringent; and if you would say they are equal, nevertheless it is like a doubt in blessings to be lenient — all the more so since there are two rabbinic matters: prayer itself is rabbinic, and likewise praying with ten. And see in Rosh. And see Rama siman 199, that he wrote explicitly they do not combine and one should not change; and here it is written that some are lenient — if so his words contradict one another. And one may say Rama holds it is stringent to combine three for ten; and see siman 194, that three suffice when one reads and informs them, but with ten one must specifically sit with them. From now we have found we see the stringency of combining for three — that is not so, for here it is different because the reason is that we require "Magnify the Omnipresent" — with three, one who hears and answers suffices; but with ten the reason is that the Divine Presence rests, and this is as if all sit in one gathering. But regarding combining a minor, certainly they are more lenient. And see siman 194, that he wrote explicitly a minor does not combine even for ten — for certainly a matter of kedushah is more stringent than zimmun. And likewise we do not find any decisor who holds that zimmun with ten should be more stringent than ten for prayer — see there.
