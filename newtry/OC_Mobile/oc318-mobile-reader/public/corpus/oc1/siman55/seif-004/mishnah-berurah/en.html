(23) Through a Chumash, etc.—for we do not count the minor as a man by virtue of his holding a Chumash in his hand.

(24) In a time of pressure—meaning: even without a Chumash, and according to those who permit the aforementioned. And specifically one [minor] and not two; and the Levush wrote that he did not see the practice to combine him even in a time of pressure; but in our time the practice is to combine a minor through a Chumash in his hand. However, specifically to hear Barekhu and Kaddish, which is an obligation; but the Kaddish after Alenu they may not say [Magen Avraham]. And many later authorities are strict not to combine any minor even in a time of pressure until he has completed thirteen years. An onen does not combine for a minyan—and this means: every onen who is obligated to mourn him, even if he is not obligated to bury him, such as when he is not his heir—if he is in the city where the deceased is, he does not combine; and even if he has someone who will deal with him in burial, if they did not hand him over to the bearers, as will be explained in siman 71. But if the deceased is in another city and he knew before the burial, and there is someone who will exert himself for him—his law is: if he wished to read [the Torah] he is permitted, and they do not protest against him; he combines for the minyan. However, all this is regarding an onen; but in mourning he certainly combines for Birkat HaMazon and for prayer.
