(23) By means of a Chumash, etc. For we do not consider the minor a man by virtue of his holding a Chumash in his hand.
(24) In pressing circumstances. Meaning even without a Chumash, following the aforementioned permissive opinion. This is only one minor and not two. The Levush wrote that he had not seen the practice to join him even in pressing circumstances. In our times the practice is to join a minor by means of a Chumash in his hand, but only to hear Barchu and Kaddish that are obligatory; the Kaddish after Aleinu should not be said, as the Magen Avraham writes. Many later authorities are stringent not to join any minor even in pressing circumstances until he has completed thirteen years. An onein does not join a minyan, meaning any onein for whom he is obligated to mourn, even if burial is not incumbent upon him, such as where he is not an heir, if he is in the city where the deceased is, he does not join. Even if he has someone to attend to the burial, unless he has handed it over to the carriers, as will be explained in siman 71. But if the deceased is in another city and he learned of it before burial, and there is someone there to attend to it, such that his law is that if he wishes to recite Shema he may and we do not protest, he joins the minyan. However, all this concerns an onein; a mourner certainly joins for Birkas HaMazon and prayer.
