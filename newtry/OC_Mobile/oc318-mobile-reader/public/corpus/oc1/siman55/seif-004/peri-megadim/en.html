Or. See Taz. One who examines Beit Yosef may say that Mahari Bei Rav did not derive the ruling regarding a sleeping person from the case of one who is praying. Rather, Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, letter 9, derives the case of one who is praying from Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. Even those who disagree with Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi regarding a minor may concede regarding a sleeping adult, because the Shechinah rests wherever ten adult Jews are present. Mahari Bei Rav holds that one who is praying cannot listen and remain silent, because listening is equivalent to responding and would constitute an interruption, as discussed in Beit Yosef in siman 104. According to our ruling, however, one who is praying certainly combines, because he can listen silently; perhaps the principle that anything fit for mixing is treated as though mixed also applies. A sleeping person differs, yet the ruling is nevertheless derived from Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, as stated. Regarding Taz's assertion that the principle of anything fit for mixing applies only after the fact and not initially, see Menachos 18. There too, the Torah initially required mixing. See also Kiddushin 25a. See Hilchos Tefillin, siman 32, in Beit Yosef, citing Rashba, that when letters are joined to one another no correction is required even initially, under the principle that anything fit for mixing is treated as though mixed. According to our ruling, however, the writing remains invalid until the joining is scraped away, because each letter must be surrounded by blank parchment. With Hashem's help, I will explain this elsewhere. Regarding what Taz writes from siman 125, seif 4, Peri Chadash writes the same and agrees with Taz. This implies that if only one person is praying, the others may proceed, and he may remain silent at the end of the blessing and listen to the chazzan's conclusion. See siman 124, seif 4, Magen Avraham letter 8, and Eshel Avraham there; with Hashem's help, it will be explained there. Peri Chadash further adduces proof from Even HaEzer 136. According to Rosh, a sleeping woman cannot be divorced, for she is treated like one lacking legal competence or like a deaf-mute; the same should apply to counting her counterpart toward a minyan. According to Ran and Rashba there, however, the reason is that she cannot safeguard the get. Yet in Shulchan Aruch there, seif 3 in the gloss, if the get is placed in the hand of her agent, even Rosh agrees, as Beit Yosef writes at the end of that siman. Otherwise, how could one ever rely on an agent for acceptance, since perhaps she was asleep when he accepted it? There is no need to elaborate. Certainly, initially one should awaken him. If that is impossible, one who relies on Shulchan Aruch has not lost. Perhaps in places where Kaddish and Kedushah are recited aloud and the remainder of the repetition is said silently, the Amidah is not repeated. In that case, there is no contradiction from siman 124. See what I write, with Hashem's help, in Magen Avraham letter 8 on this subject.
