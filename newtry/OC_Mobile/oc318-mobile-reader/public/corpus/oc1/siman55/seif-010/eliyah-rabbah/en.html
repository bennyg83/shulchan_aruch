On this I wrote in Eliyah Rabbah to Yoreh De'ah siman 337 — Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote: if the court saw fit to excommunicate, he may not be included in three, nor pray in ten, nor may they circumcise his son — until here. And Shach wondered: behold, in any case every excommunicated person is forbidden in zimmun and prayer, as Shulchan Aruch wrote there seif 2 — until here. And I wrote: certainly every excommunicated person is forbidden in zimmun and prayer — but that means to combine him for zimmun or for ten; but when there are three or ten apart from him, it is permitted to pray and invite with them, for they are permitted to pray while he is still in the synagogue; therefore it says: if the court saw fit to excommunicate that he should not invite in three, etc. — meaning: even though there are three or ten apart from him, they have permission as written here, unless they specified explicitly, etc. — and see Eliyahu Zuta. Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah wrote: I was uncertain whether they combine for the count, and I agreed they do not combine — until here. And in my humble opinion, proof is from siman 199 that one does not combine for zimmun, and all the more for prayer.
