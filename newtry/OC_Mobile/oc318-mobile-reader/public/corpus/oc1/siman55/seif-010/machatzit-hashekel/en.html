(Seif katan 10) And the thirteenth year, etc. And likewise at the end of Eruvin that a complete year is required. Meaning: that it implies there, as Rama wrote in the gloss, that from the outset Magen Avraham raises a difficulty. And it is: one who sells a house in walled cities—the Torah gave him permission to redeem within a year, and if he did not redeem within a year it is permanently forfeited to the buyer and never returns to the seller. And if the year was intercalated, the seller has time for twelve months; and the same applies here—even though one could say it is different there because it is written "complete" to include the intercalated month, nevertheless we derive from it as Beit Yosef wrote there. And what he wrote about the thirteenth year is another proof: for within thirteen years there were several intercalated years, and they give him thirteen months for each intercalated year. And what he wrote that in the thirteenth year he becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar.

Her signs of virginity return—for if she was married within three years from the day of her birth, her signs of virginity return; but not when she was married after three years, for they do not return. And if she was born at the beginning of Adar and in the third year was married at the end of Adar, her signs of virginity do not return. And if afterward the court ruled when they would sanctify by sight and they intercalated the year—then she still stands within her three years, and her signs of virginity return again.

He cited a settled matter—meaning: that most years are not intercalated. But in Sefer Ateret Zekenim and in responsa Shevut Yaakov siman 9 he wrote that Magen Avraham surely did not examine the body of responsa Mahari Mintz, who is the master of this law, who wrote plainly to the contrary; and these are the words of Mahari Mintz. It seems plain regarding one born in an intercalated year in the first Adar and one born in the second Adar, and in the thirteenth year there will also be an intercalation—then certainly we would say that the one born in the first becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar and the second in the second Adar; and we say: the end of the law is like its beginning—this one in his own and that one in his own, etc. And he concluded in responsa Shevut Yaakov, and so is the plain custom among all decisors, etc.

And one who was born, etc.—for it implies in Nedarim that we taught there: one said, "Konam upon me one thing this month when Rosh Chodesh of the coming month arrives"—he is permitted; and it challenges: obvious; and it answers: it was needed only for an intercalated month. Meaning: that the month in which he vowed is intercalated, and therefore the coming month has two days of Rosh Chodesh. The mishnah teaches that even the first day of Rosh Chodesh is permitted, since people call it Rosh Chodesh; and in vows we follow the language of people. It implies that if we did not follow the language of people, from the law it would belong to the previous month and it is day thirty of the previous month.

There are different opinions how to write in a get on the first day of Rosh Chodesh—whether it is day thirty of the past month or the coming Rosh Chodesh. And one who says one should write Rosh Chodesh—his reason is that also in documents we follow the language of people, as Ran wrote there in Beit Yosef. In any case, since it appears that the first day of Rosh Chodesh belongs to the past month and it is day thirty of the past month—therefore he was not born on Rosh Chodesh Kislev but at the end of Cheshvan. Therefore in the thirteenth year, when Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day, nevertheless he becomes bar mitzvah at the end of Cheshvan—that is, on the twenty-ninth of Cheshvan.

Until twenty-nine days of Cheshvan pass. Meaning: it is as written in siman 53 seif katan 13: once the beginning of the day on which he was born arrives, we say "part of the day is like the whole" and he is thirteen years and one day. And the same applies here: once the beginning of the night of the twenty-ninth of Cheshvan arrives—which is the day on which he was born, for he was born on the last day of Cheshvan as above—he is thirteen years and one day. That is: he was born at the end of Cheshvan, and in the thirteenth year the twenty-ninth is the end of Cheshvan; nevertheless twenty-nine days of Cheshvan must pass over him before he becomes bar mitzvah, just as twenty-nine days of Cheshvan passed before he was born. And therefore the same law applies at the beginning of Tevet: if he was born on Rosh Chodesh Tevet and in that year Rosh Chodesh Tevet was two days, and in the thirteenth year Rosh Chodesh Tevet is only one day—see in Sefer Ateret Zekenim. And see what is written in siman 568 seif 7 regarding yahtzeit the law is not so, etc., and see there.
