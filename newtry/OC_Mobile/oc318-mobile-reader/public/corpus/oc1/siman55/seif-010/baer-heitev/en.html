Intercalated month. Magen Avraham wrote only regarding siman 44, but similarly: if the thirteenth year is intercalated, one born in the first Adar becomes bar mitzvah in the second Adar, as Rama wrote in the gloss, etc. And he did not examine the source of the law—for Rama's gloss is from responsa Mahari Mintz siman 9, who wrote plainly and ruled that even if one was born in a leap year in the first Adar and becomes bar mitzvah in a plain year, he becomes bar mitzvah after one born in the second Adar; and one born in Adar in a plain year who becomes bar mitzvah in an intercalated year does not become bar mitzvah until the second Adar. Nevertheless he wrote in Levush Chadash, and it seems plain regarding one born in an intercalated year in the first Adar and afterward one born in the second Adar, and in the thirteenth year there was also an intercalation—then certainly we would say the one born in the first becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar and the second in the second Adar, each in his own month. Responsa Shevut Yaakov chelek 1 siman 9; and the custom is plain among all decisors, and he agreed lehalakhah lema'aseh with the Gaon R' David Oppenheim z"l, not like Magen Avraham; and likewise Ateret Zekenim and Peri Chadash, etc. Magen Avraham also wrote: one born on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and when he becomes bar mitzvah Cheshvan is missing and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only on Sunday—he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh, for a full year has not yet been completed for him until twenty-nine days pass from Cheshvan, for we require thirteen complete years, etc. This also caused him not to look in responsa, because there he explained this law explicitly, etc. See siman 568 seif 6 regarding yahtzeit, and Taz in Yoreh De'ah siman 246.
