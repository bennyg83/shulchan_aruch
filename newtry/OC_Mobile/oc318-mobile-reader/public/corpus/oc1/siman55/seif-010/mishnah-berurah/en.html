(43) It is not a leap year. But if the thirteenth year was also a leap year, then we would certainly say that one born in first Adar becomes bar mitzvah in first Adar, and one born in second Adar becomes bar mitzvah in second Adar. This is the consensus of the later authorities, unlike the Magen Avraham.
(44) Until the twenty-ninth of Adar. For one might have thought that the first Adar in which he was born should be reckoned in place of Shevat, and once the twenty-ninth of Shevat arrives in the thirteenth year he becomes bar mitzvah. Therefore it teaches that we do not say this.
(45) He does not become. Although the Mechaber already wrote in seif 9 that the leap month is included, one might have thought that this applies specifically when he was born in another month, such as on Rosh Chodesh Nisan, and the thirteenth year is a leap year, where he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh Nisan. But if he was born on Rosh Chodesh Adar, one might have thought that he becomes bar mitzvah on Rosh Chodesh of first Adar. Therefore it teaches that first Adar is not called Adar but is called the leap month, for the Megillah is not read in first Adar, and we require thirteen complete years. One who was born on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, when there were then two days of Rosh Chodesh, and in the thirteenth year Cheshvan was deficient and Kislev had only one day of Rosh Chodesh, nevertheless does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh Kislev. One who was born on Rosh Chodesh Kislev when there was only one day of Rosh Chodesh, and in the thirteenth year there were two days of Rosh Chodesh, becomes bar mitzvah on the first day of Rosh Chodesh.
