(43) Not intercalated—however, if in the thirteenth year there was also an intercalation, then certainly we would say: the one born in the first [Adar] becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar and the second in the second Adar [agreement of later authorities, not like Magen Avraham].

(44) Until the twenty-ninth of Adar—for lest one think the first Adar in which he was born counts as Shevat, and when the twenty-ninth of Shevat arrives in the thirteenth year he becomes bar mitzvah; it comes to teach that we do not say so.

(45) Is not made—although the Mechaber already wrote in seif 9 that the intercalary month is included, nevertheless this is specifically when he was born in another month, such as Rosh Chodesh Nisan and the thirteenth year is intercalated—he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh Nisan. But one born on Rosh Chodesh Adar—it comes to teach that he becomes bar mitzvah on the first Rosh Chodesh Adar, for the first Adar is not called Adar but is called the intercalary month, since we do not read the Megillah in the first Adar; and we require thirteen complete years. One born on Sunday on Rosh Chodesh Kislev when there were then two days of Rosh Chodesh, and in the thirteenth year Cheshvan was missing and Kislev has only one day of Rosh Chodesh—even so he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh Kislev. And one born on Rosh Chodesh Kislev when there was only one day of Rosh Chodesh, and in the thirteenth year there were two days of Rosh Chodesh—he becomes bar mitzvah on the first day of Rosh Chodesh.
