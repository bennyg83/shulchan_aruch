And the thirteenth year is not a leap year. This implies that if the thirteenth year is a leap year, one born in first Adar becomes thirteen in first Adar. So too wrote the Levush in siman 685. This requires study, for one born in an ordinary year does not become bar mitzvah until second Adar, as written in the gloss. If so, even this one, although he was born in a leap year, nevertheless the previous year was ordinary and he became twelve in plain Adar; if so, his thirteen years are not complete until second Adar. This also appears from the end of Arachin, where we require a complete year. Why should the thirteenth year differ from all other years, since a leap year has thirteen months? So too we say regarding three years for her virginity to return, that if the year was intercalated, the additional month is included, as stated in the Yerushalmi on the verse, I will call to G-d Most High, to G-d Who completes for me. Therefore it seems to me that he becomes bar mitzvah in second Adar, and when it says that the thirteenth year is not a leap year, it stated a clear-cut case. One who was born on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and when he becomes bar mitzvah Cheshvan is deficient and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day, requires study whether he becomes bar mitzvah on the twenty-ninth of Cheshvan. It appears in Nedarim 60 that in truth the first day of Rosh Chodesh is the thirtieth day of the previous month, only in people’s language it is called Rosh Chodesh. In Even HaEzer siman 126, seif 6, there are different views. Nevertheless, it seems to me that he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh, for his year is not yet complete until twenty-nine days of Cheshvan have passed, since we require thirteen complete years, as stated above.
