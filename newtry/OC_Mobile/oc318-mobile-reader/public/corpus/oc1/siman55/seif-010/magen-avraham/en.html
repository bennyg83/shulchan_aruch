And the thirteenth year is not intercalated. It implies that if the thirteenth year is intercalated, one born in the first Adar becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar; and likewise in Levush siman 695. It requires examination—for one born in a plain year does not become bar mitzvah until the second Adar, as written in the gloss; and therefore also this one, even though he was born in an intercalated year, nevertheless last year was a plain year and he became twelve in plain Adar—therefore thirteen years were not completed for him until the second Adar year; and likewise at the end of Arakhin that a complete year is required. And what he wrote that the thirteenth year—from all the years, the intercalated year has thirteen months, and so we say regarding the three years that her signs of virginity return: if the year was intercalated, the intercalated month is also included, as explained in Yerushalmi on the verse "I will call to God Most High, to God who completes for me"—therefore it seems to me he becomes bar mitzvah in the second Adar. And that he cited "and the thirteenth year is not intercalated"—he cited a settled matter. And one born on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and when he becomes bar mitzvah Cheshvan is missing and Rosh Chodesh Kislev is only one day—it requires examination whether he becomes bar mitzvah on the twenty-ninth of Cheshvan, for it implies in Nedarim page 60 that in truth the first day of Rosh Chodesh is day thirty of the past month, except that in the language of people they call it Rosh Chodesh; and likewise in Orach Chayim siman 126 seif 6 there are different opinions. Nevertheless it seems to me he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh, for a full year has not yet been completed for him until twenty-nine days of Cheshvan pass, for we require thirteen complete years as above; and see what is written in siman 568 seif 7.
