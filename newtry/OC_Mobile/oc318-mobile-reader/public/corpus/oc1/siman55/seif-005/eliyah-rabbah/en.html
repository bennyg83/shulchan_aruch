They are accustomed to be lenient, etc. — and Levush wrote: I have not seen people act so. And at first glance it is difficult — perhaps because we do not have the chumashim mentioned above, therefore he did not see people act so. But he meant to say that it was possible through a Torah scroll itself; and so my grandfather the Gaon, may his memory be blessed, was accustomed to rule practically that one should not combine even in a time of need; and so Bach ruled, that Rama too did not intend, heaven forbid, to act so, or that he saw people act so — rather he wrote "there are those who act," etc., meaning in some place where they act so from ancient days and have whom to rely on, and one should not protest them; but he certainly did not see in our country to act so, as Levush testified. And also my grandfather the Gaon, may his memory be blessed, who was old and sat in yeshiva more than fifty years — and clear proof of this seems to me, for in siman 199 seif 10 Rama himself wrote that one does not combine him for zimmun at all, and so is the custom and one should not change — until here. And if so, all the more for prayer, for zimmun is lighter than prayer, as Rashal wrote and so in Bach — but as we said, and not like Magen Avraham who wrote that in our time they combine him through a chumash for kedushah, Barchu, and Kaddish, but not for Kaddish after Aleinu; for that is certainly not an established custom, and those who act so do so because they did not understand Rama's words. And apart from law I saw Rashal page 3 brought many poskim to forbid; he also concludes one should be stringent and not lenient, lest the sanctity of God's Name leave our mouths without ten; and so I found in responsum Nachalat Yaakov siman 9, who ruled as Levush's words.
