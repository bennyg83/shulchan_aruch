The Taz did not side to say that although there is no minyan, if a minyan came afterward they may say Kaddish; and he brought Magen Avraham. And apparently that on Yishtabach alone one says Kaddish only on verses (and see what he wrote in letter alef) — so it is explained thus. And even if we say that on Yishtabach as well one says Kaddish because it has something like verses, nevertheless, since he gave the reason not to interrupt between Yishtabach and Kaddish, one could derive that a minyan is required — we learn that it is not required. And what he wrote in the name of Rama, that one may wait with Yishtabach — how long? Certainly with a great interruption it is not necessary to say Kaddish on what one already recited. And what he wrote about the Levush — I am uncertain; for apparently he understood from the Levush that his intention is that on Aleinu there is no obligation to say Kaddish since there are no verses (or they say it as supplication); and therefore on Tehillim or Haggadah the Levush would concede. However, one who examines the Levush from what he concluded there — rather the custom is to say it because of the orphan's Kaddish that follows it; and the beginning too is explained thus: that at evening the Sages obligated saying the blessings after Kriat Shema and the Kaddish is drawn after it; and whether there will be ten at the blessings or at the mourner's Kaddish — but Aleinu there is no obligation to say Aleinu at all, etc. And in the beginning too one could say his words are thus — see there. And Magen Avraham siman 69 letter dalet, and Rama in siman 234 letter alef dispute and hold like the Levush; and it appears there even Hodu in Minchah that one is obligated to say twice — nevertheless ten are needed at Hodu, for its obligation is likewise like the blessings of evening; only he is guaranteed even though the obligation is upon him — and further, sometimes one says Tehillim at Shacharit, and similarly Hodu is exempt from obligation; and see Rabbi Akiva Eiger here letter gimel. Therefore what HaTaz wrote — proof from Kol Bo for his view, that since he brought Rabbi Akiva as proof that one should say Yishtabach standing so as not to interrupt (even though there are already ten) — and what proof is Ashrei at Minchah where there is no ten? One could say Ashrei is like an obligation twice, etc., and necessarily the reason not to interrupt — understand. And what he wrote "but that which was not mentioned in the verses of zemerah" — I am uncertain about the connection with the previous; and in Rama too it is explained that Yishtabach requires waiting; and one could say: one says Yishtabach and Kaddish refers to Yishtabach — and one need not elaborate. And the correct practice is that they say afterward three times with a minyan and say Kaddish; and Rav in siman 581 letter yod gimel challenged the words of Kol Bo and it appears there like the Levush.

And behold I have seen some who in Minchah prayer say Vayidaber, Ashrei, and afterward take the tallit and check tzitzit and bless — and who permitted them to interrupt so much between Ashrei and Kaddish, since they could set this aside before Ashrei or Vayidaber? See Av Beit Din siman 53 on Kol Bo, see there on this.
