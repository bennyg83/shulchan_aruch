If some of the congregation leave after the chazzan has begun the repetition aloud and Kedushah, those who remain may complete the entire order of Kedushah, including U’va LeTzion and the Kaddish that follows it. The same principle applies to Maariv. If they began Vehu Rachum with ten and some then left, they complete the service through Yir’u Eineinu and also recite the Kaddish that follows, because that Kaddish belongs to Shema and its blessings. They may then recite the silent Maariv Shemoneh Esrei, since ten are not required for the silent prayer. The Kaddish following Shemoneh Esrei, however, may be recited only when ten are present. Similarly, even if Aleinu was begun with ten and some then left, the Mourner’s Kaddish that follows may not be recited with fewer than ten. If Aleinu was recited without ten, the Kaddish afterward may not later be recited even after ten arrive. The same applies to Tehillim and Shir HaYichud. If they were completed without ten, the Kaddish afterward may not be recited even if ten are now present. Conversely, if ten were present during Tehillim or Shir HaYichud but were no longer present when the time for Kaddish arrived, the Kaddish is not recited. This is the accepted practice, as stated by Lechem Chamudos. I have likewise seen the practice in some places that when Tehillim is being recited without ten, they leave one chapter of Tehillim to be recited after ten arrive. The reason appears to be that this Kaddish, being of Rabbinic institution, does not possess sufficient force to be recited merely because ten were present earlier, nor does the reverse case permit it.
