And if they left after he began aloud with kedushah, they may complete the entire kedushah section, etc.—that is, "uvah l'tziyon" and the Kaddish that follows it.

And the evening prayer—therefore if they began "Hu rachum" with ten and some left, they finish through "yeir'u eineinu" and also say Kaddish because it belongs to Kriat Shema and its blessings; and the evening prayer is in a whisper because ten are not required for prayer in a whisper. But they do not say the Kaddish after the Shemoneh Esreh except with ten; and Aleinu l'shabe'ach—even if he began with ten and some left, nevertheless they do not say the orphan Kaddish that follows when fewer than ten remain. And likewise if they said Aleinu without ten, one may not afterward say Kaddish even with ten. And the same law applies to Tehillim and Shir haYichud: if they finished them without ten, they do not say Kaddish even with ten—or the reverse, if there were ten during Tehillim and Shir haYichud but not ten for Kaddish, they do not say it—and this is the custom (Levush Chadash). Likewise I have seen practiced in some places: when they say Tehillim without ten they leave one psalm to recite after ten arrive; and the reason appears to me that that Kaddish is not strong enough, for it is Rabbinic, to say it even with ten—and likewise the reverse.
