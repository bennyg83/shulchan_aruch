(Seif katan 3) Maariv, etc.—for people who leave, as written in Beit Yosef: Beit Yosef in the name of Agur and Shiltei Gibborim, that Maariv prayer is optional; and some go on their way before eighteen [blessings] and do not listen to Kaddish, etc. Even though Maariv is now accepted as obligatory.

And so wrote Tur siman 236: and what is practiced—to pause at the verses "v'yiru eineinu" and Kaddish—because in early days their synagogue was in the fields, and they were afraid to delay there until after the time of Maariv prayer. They instituted for him these verses that contain eighteen mentions [of God's name] corresponding to the eighteen blessings in Maariv prayer, and they take leave with this Kaddish (and likewise there Tur in the name of Ran Gaon: and he pauses at Kaddish when all have finished prayer—whoever wishes to leave may leave, etc.). Now that they have returned to pray Maariv in the synagogue in the city, the first custom was not abolished, etc., Tur. And therefore, since we do not say the verses and Kaddish except because of the first custom—and then at that time the Kaddish before eighteen belonged to the verses and Kriat Shema, and whoever wished to leave would leave—therefore now too there is relevance to this Kaddish for the verses and Kriat Shema: if some left before the verses and Kaddish, they may say the Kaddish before the prayer of eighteen.
