(Seif katan 3) Maariv, etc. For the sake of those who leave, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Agur and Shibbolei HaLeket: Since Maariv is optional, some people leave before the Amidah and would otherwise not hear Kaddish. This is so even though nowadays Maariv has been accepted as obligatory.
The Tur writes in siman 236: The custom to interrupt with the verses of “May our eyes behold” and Kaddish arose because in earlier times their synagogues were in the fields, and they feared remaining there until after the time for Maariv. They therefore instituted the recital of these verses, which contain eighteen mentions of Hashem corresponding to the eighteen blessings of the Maariv Amidah, and they discharged their obligation through this Kaddish. The Tur likewise writes there in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon that the Kaddish marks the conclusion of the prayer, meaning that one who wishes to leave may leave. Now that they have returned to praying Maariv in the synagogue within the city, the original custom was not abolished. These are the words of the Tur. Accordingly, since we recite these verses and Kaddish only because of the earlier custom, and at that time the Kaddish before the Amidah belonged to the verses and Krias Shema, after which one who wished to leave would leave, even now this Kaddish remains connected to the verses and Krias Shema. Therefore, if some of the minyan left before the verses and Kaddish, the Kaddish before the Amidah may still be recited.
