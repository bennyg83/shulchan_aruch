Kedushah. And all the Shemoneh Esreh prayers and the full Kaddish that follows, as explained in Rama afterward. And he wrote in Shakhnehag that one should also say the blessing "Elokeinu veElokei avoteinu" with the triple blessing in the Torah—for whoever began the Shemoneh Esreh finishes it, even though we learned one may not raise the hands with fewer than ten; that is specifically for raising the hands, but the prayer of our God may be said since he began the Shemoneh Esreh, and the blessing of our God belongs to the Shemoneh Esreh, etc.; and likewise on the matter.

And kedushah. Not specifically—for even if they have not yet said kedushah, since they began to pray aloud they finish even the full Kaddish afterward—Taz, Magen Avraham. He wrote in responsa Mateh Yosef volume 2 siman 12: what he wrote, that if some left after praying Yotzer, etc.—is not specifically so; rather, even if they said Kaddish and Barekhu with ten and left before they began the blessing Yotzer Or, they complete through the Shemoneh Esreh, etc.

And its blessings. Therefore if they began the evening service with ten and some left, they finish the Kaddish that precedes the prayer, because that Kaddish belongs to the blessing of Kriat Shema; but they do not say the Kaddish after the Shemoneh Esreh, because the prayer is not connected to the blessings of Kriat Shema, for the evening prayer is optional—and all the more so the kedushah section and Kaddish, as he wrote. And if they said Aleinu and some left, they do not say Kaddish, for it is only custom—and all the more so for Shir haYichud or a psalm—Magen Avraham. Taz wrote from the words of the poskim who wrote "and one says Kaddish" and one may not say it with fewer than ten: this implies that pesukei dezimra do not require ten—for even if one says them individually, whenever there are ten at the time of saying the Kaddishim it suffices; and this is clear from what Rama wrote in siman 53 seif 3, that if there is no minyan in the synagogue the shaliach tzibbur waits with Yishtabach, etc.; and in responsa Mahari Mintz siman 8 he wrote that one may also wait after saying Yishtabach for a minyan; and likewise it seems to me regarding every study of verses and aggadah that if a minyan happens to be present immediately so that he can say Kaddish—and all the more so Aleinu l'shabe'ach, which they said with fewer than ten and afterward ten became available, that one should say Kaddish; and they practice thus every day in reciting Tehillim in the morning without a minyan, saying Kaddish afterward when a minyan comes—Taz, etc. Magen Avraham in siman 234 seif katan 1—not so much, etc. And the public practices: when they say Tehillim without a minyan they leave one psalm to say after ten arrive; and from the words of responsa Sha'ar Ephraim siman 67 it appears like the words of Taz, etc.
