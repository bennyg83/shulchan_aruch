Kedushah. And all the Shemoneh Esrei prayers, and the full Kaddish after it, as explained in the Rama that follows. The Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah wrote that he should also say the blessing of 'Our God, bless us with the threefold blessing in the Torah,' for whenever he began the Shemoneh Esrei, he completes it. Although we learned that the kohanim do not lift their hands with fewer than ten, that applies specifically to nesias kapayim, but the prayer 'Our God' should be said once he began the Shemoneh Esrei, and the blessing 'Our God' belongs to the Shemoneh Esrei; see there. So too wrote the Olas Tamid.
And Kedushah. This is not precise, for even if they have not yet said Kedushah, since they began praying aloud, they complete even the full Kaddish. Terumas HaDeshen; Magen Avraham. In Teshuvos Mateh Yosef, part 2, siman 12, he wrote that what was stated, that if some left after they began to pray Yotzer, etc., is not precise; rather, even if they said Kaddish and Barchu with ten and some left before they began the blessing of Yotzer Or, they complete until the Shemoneh Esrei; see there.
And its blessings. Therefore, if they began Maariv with ten and some left, he completes the Kaddish before the prayer, because that Kaddish belongs to the blessings of Kerias Shema. But they should not say the Kaddish after the Shemoneh Esrei, because the prayer is not joined to the blessings of Kerias Shema, since the Maariv prayer is optional. All the more so this applies to Seder Kedushah and Kaddish, as stated. If they said Aleinu and some left, they do not say Kaddish, for it is only a custom; all the more so for Shir HaYichud or a chapter of Tehillim. See Magen Avraham. The Taz wrote from the words of the poskim, who wrote 'and he says Kaddish, and one does not say it with fewer than ten,' that this implies that Pesukei D'Zimrah do not require ten, for even if he recited them alone, as long as there are ten at the time of saying the Kaddishim, that is sufficient. This is also implied by what the Rama wrote in siman 53, seif 3, that if there is no minyan in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibbur should wait at Yishtabach, etc. In Teshuvos Maharam Mintz, siman 8, he wrote that he may likewise wait after saying Yishtabach for a minyan. So it appears to me regarding any learning in which a person studies verses and aggados, that if a minyan immediately becomes available to him, he may say Kaddish. All the more so with Aleinu Leshabeiach, which they said with fewer than ten and afterward ten became available, that Kaddish should be said. This is the daily practice when Tehillim are recited in the morning without a minyan, and afterward Kaddish is said when a minyan arrives. Taz; see there. The Magen Avraham in siman 234, s.k. 1, did not write this way; see there. The common practice is that if Tehillim are recited without a minyan, they leave over one chapter of Tehillim to say after ten have arrived. From the words of Teshuvos Shaar Ephraim, siman 67, it appears like the words of the Taz; see there.
