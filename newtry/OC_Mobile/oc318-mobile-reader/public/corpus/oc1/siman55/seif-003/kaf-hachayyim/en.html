3) There: Kaddish is recited. Rambam writes in the Mishneh Torah that whenever ten people engage in Oral Torah (even Midrash or Aggadah) they recite Kaddish DeRabbanan. Magen Avraham, above at the end of siman 54, cites Be'er Sheva, who writes that Rambam contradicts himself: in his commentary to the Mishnah at the end of Avos, Rambam writes concerning Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya that the people customarily recite it upon completing the chapters because Kaddish is not recited over Mishnah, but only over Aggadah, as the Master said, 'Yehei Shmei Rabbah' following Aggadah, etc. One may answer that by law Kaddish is certainly recited over all Oral Torah. This is somewhat supported by the phrase 'Yehei Shmei Rabbah following Aggadah': the fact that it specifies the response following Aggadah implies that Kaddish is also recited over other Oral Torah, but the response following Aggadah possesses exceptional importance, for the world endures through it. The reason may be that Aggadah draws a person's heart. Yisrael rejoices, and when people respond 'Yehei Shmei' out of the joy of a mitzvah, it brings satisfaction to their Master and sustains the world. For this reason the custom developed to recite Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya at the end of a halachic discourse, so that the people rejoice, for it is Aggadah and speaks of Yisrael's merit. Kaddish is then recited so that it falls into the category of 'Yehei Shmei following Aggadah.' This is a custom, not the underlying law, and this is why Rambam says in his commentary to the Mishnah, 'They customarily recite,' etc. Birkei Yosef 1 and Kesher Gudal 8:2. Therefore, when one recites Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya after Oral Torah in order to say Kaddish, he must say it with heartfelt joy and fervor so that Yisrael rejoices. In particular, when mentioning God's Name in the verse, 'Hashem desired, for the sake of his righteousness,' etc., one must say it with intention. As explained in many places in our work, great care is required to articulate God's Name with intention. Because a person is constantly accustomed to reading God's Name in verses, there is concern that he may forget, when mentioning it, and utter it without intention. One must therefore exercise great care at all times never to let any Divine Name pass his lips without intention. See the wondrous incident related above in siman 5:5.
