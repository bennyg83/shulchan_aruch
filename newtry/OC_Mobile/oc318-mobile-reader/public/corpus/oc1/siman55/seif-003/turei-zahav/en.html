It does not pertain to Krias Shema and its blessings. Meaning, if they began Maariv with ten and some left, they complete the Kaddish before the Amidah, because that Kaddish pertains to the blessings of Krias Shema. They do not recite the Kaddish after the Amidah, however, because the Amidah is not connected to the blessings of Krias Shema. From the words of the poskim, who write that Kaddish is recited but may not be recited with fewer than ten, it appears that Pesukei DeZimrah itself does not require ten. Even if it was recited privately, it suffices that ten are present when Kaddish is recited. This is also implied by Rama in siman 53, seif 3, who writes that if there is no minyan in the synagogue, the chazzan should wait before Yishtabach. Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 8, writes that he may also wait after reciting Yishtabach until a minyan arrives. The same applies at Maariv: one who prayed privately may wait afterward for a minyan. It likewise appears to me that whenever a person studies verses, aggadah, or Gemara, if a minyan becomes available immediately afterward he may recite Kaddish. Certainly, if Aleinu was recited with fewer than ten and ten then arrive, Kaddish may be recited, because Aleinu contains verses. Levush writes that if Aleinu was recited without a minyan, a minyan arriving afterward does not help, because the recitation of Kaddish after Aleinu is not a Rabbinic enactment but merely a custom. This does not appear correct to me, since Aleinu contains verses. Levush further writes that if some of the minyan left while Aleinu was being recited, Kaddish may not be recited afterward, because the recitation of Aleinu is not sufficiently weighty to draw Kaddish after it without a minyan. This is certainly correct. The same applies to Kaddish after study: ten must be present specifically when Kaddish is recited. One should not object from Rama in siman 234, who writes that Ashrei before Minchah should not be recited until a minyan is present in the synagogue so that Kaddish may be recited over it before Minchah. The reason there is not that Kaddish cannot refer to something previously recited without a minyan. Rather, initially one should not make an interruption between Kaddish and what preceded it. This is explicit in Kol Bo, the source of that ruling, who writes: “Tehillah LeDavid should not be recited at Minchah until enough people have arrived to form a minyan, so that Kaddish before Minchah may be recited over it. For this reason Rabbi Amram ruled that the chazzan should recite Yishtabach at Shacharis while standing before the lectern, so that Kaddish may be recited over it.” Thus, his comparison to the chazzan's recital of Yishtabach while standing, which is intended to prevent an interruption between Yishtabach and Kaddish, shows that the same concern applies to Ashrei before Kaddish at Minchah. They did not similarly warn against beginning Pesukei DeZimrah before a minyan is present because its recital takes time, and a minyan ordinarily arrives during that interval. Ashrei is brief, so a warning is needed to avoid an interruption. Certainly, if a minyan did not arrive until after Ashrei, Kaddish is not omitted for that reason. This is also the daily practice when Tehillim is recited in the morning without a minyan and Kaddish is recited when a minyan later arrives. They act properly, as stated.
