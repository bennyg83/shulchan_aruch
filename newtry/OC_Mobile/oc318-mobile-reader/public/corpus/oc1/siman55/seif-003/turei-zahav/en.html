It does not belong to Kriat Shema and its blessings. Meaning: nevertheless, if they began Maariv with ten and some left, he finishes the Kaddish before the prayer, for that Kaddish belongs to the blessing of Kriat Shema; but they do not say the Kaddish after the Shemoneh Esreh prayer, for the prayer is not connected to the blessings of Kriat Shema. From the words of the decisors who wrote "one says Kaddish" and one may not do it with less than ten—it implies that pesukei dezimra do not require ten; for even if one recites them individually, as long as there are ten at the hour of saying the Kaddishes it suffices. And thus is clear from what Rama wrote in siman 53 se'if 3, that if there is no minyan in the synagogue the shaliach tzibbur waits with Yishtabach, etc.; and in responsa Maharam Mintz siman 8 he wrote that one may likewise wait after Yishtabach for a minyan; and the same law applies to Maariv prayed individually—one waits afterward for a minyan; and so I say regarding any study when a person learns verses and aggadot or Talmud: if a minyan happens immediately, he may say Kaddish; and all the more Alenu leShabeach, which they said with less than ten and afterward ten arrived—one may say Kaddish, for Alenu has verses. And in Levush it is written: when one said Alenu without a minyan, afterward a minyan does not help, since it is not a takanat chachamim to say Kaddish on it but rather a mere custom; and I do not think so, for Alenu has verses. But what Levush further wrote: if some left from the minyan at the time of Alenu, one should not say Kaddish after it—for saying Alenu is not strong enough to draw Kaddish afterward even without a minyan—this is certainly correct. And the same applies to Kaddish said after study: a minyan is required specifically at the time of Kaddish. And one should not infer from what Rama wrote in siman 234 that one does not say Ashrei before Minchah except when there is a minyan in the synagogue so they may say Kaddish on it before the Minchah prayer—for there the reason is not that Kaddish does not attach to what was said at first without a minyan, but rather that one may not make an interruption at the outset between Kaddish and what was said first. And this is explained in the words of all, from whom is the source of that law that he wrote: "One does not say Tehillah leDavid at Minchah until they finish coming to a minyan, so that one may say on it the Kaddish of the Minchah prayer." Therefore Rabbi Akiva explained that the chazan should say Yishtabach at Shacharit before the Ark standing, in order to say Kaddish on it—so too here with Ashrei before Kaddish at Minchah. And that they did not warn regarding pesukei dezimra at Shacharit not to say until there is a minyan—because in pesukei dezimra there is pause during their recitation, and it is usual for a minyan to arrive in the course of their recitation; unlike Ashrei, in which there is no pause during its recitation—therefore a warning is needed not to interrupt. But certainly if it happened that a minyan did not arrive until after Ashrei, for this reason one does not omit Kaddish; and so they practice every day in reciting Tehillim in the morning without a minyan, and say Kaddish afterward when a minyan comes—and they act properly, as explained above.
