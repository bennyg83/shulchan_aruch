There they say Kaddish. The Rambam wrote in his composition that every ten who engage in Torah shebe'al peh—even in midrashim and aggadot—say the Rabbinic Kaddish. And Magen Avraham above at the end of siman 54 brought from Rav Be'er Sheva, who wrote that the Rambam contradicts himself, for he wrote in his commentary on Mishnah at the end of Avot: Rabbi Chanina ben Akavya—people practiced to say it at the end of chapters, because one does not say Kaddish on Mishnah but on aggadah, as the master said: "May His great name be exalted" is the aggadah, etc. And one may say that certainly by law one should say Kaddish on all Torah shebe'al peh; and this is somewhat proven from what we say: "May His great name be exalted" of aggadah—for since it was necessary to explain "May His great name" of aggadah, it implies that one says Kaddish on other Torah shebe'al peh; only that the Kaddish of aggadah is greater in its matter, for it sustains the world. And it seems the reason of aggadah is that since aggadah draws the heart of a person, Israel rejoices—and when people rejoice, "May His great name be exalted" from joy of mitzvah is pleasant to the Master of the world and sustains the world; and from this reason they practiced to say at the end of a halakhic discourse "Rabbi Chanina ben Akavya" so that the people rejoice, for it is aggadah and the merit of Israel; and they say Kaddish so that it be in the category of "May His great name be exalted" of aggadah—and this is custom, not law; and this is what the Rambam z"l said in commentary on Mishnah: "they practiced," etc. Kaf HaChayyim letter 1. And in his book Kaf HaChayyim siman 8 letter 2. Therefore one must be careful when saying "Rabbi Chanina ben Akavya" after Torah shebe'al peh, in order to say Kaddish, to say it with joy of heart and enthusiasm so that Israel rejoice; and in particular one must be careful when mentioning the Name that is in the verse "Hashem desires for the sake of righteousness," etc., to say it with intention, as explained in our words in many places—that great care is required to utter Hashem with one's lips with intention; for since a person is accustomed at all times to read Hashem in verses, we are concerned lest he forget when he mentions the Name and come to mention it without intention. Therefore great care is required at every time and season that he not forget and not pass over any divine name without mentioning it with intention.
