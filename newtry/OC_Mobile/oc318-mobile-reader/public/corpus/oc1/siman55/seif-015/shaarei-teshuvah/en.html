House. Ba'er Heitev. And he wrote in Shiurei Knesset HaGedolah: attics that are not closed with a complete wall but only pillars ten high, and below there is a large courtyard toward which the attics face and they see one another—they do not combine; and Avudraham brought this, and in Mateh Yosef part 2 siman 13 he wrote that they do combine, and thus is common practice; and Malchei Bakodesh rejected his words; and in Machatzit HaShekel it appears he also agrees with Knesset HaGedolah, and he wrote that responsa Zera Emet likewise; nevertheless he wrote that if women stand in the attics or in the women's section, it is not easy to descend below to the synagogue, and they strive to show their faces from above—one may rely to be lenient. And he wrote "or" in Machatzit HaShekel in Lazarte (meaning a place guarded against foul air—called quarantine) that there are two groups, six in one house and four in another, and they cannot touch one another; one sage was uncertain whether the four can stand before the door of the house and combine for ten since they see one another; and he wrote: since these are not permitted to enter inside, and the guard is with them in the field before the opening, and those in the house also cannot go outside because the place is narrow outside and also because of passing crowds, and these four strive to come to the opening and show their faces—it is like showing face through a window that combines; and all the more so, since they cannot in any way be together; and by inference from the ruling above one may rely on the decisors that forty days should not pass without saying Kaddish and kedushah.
