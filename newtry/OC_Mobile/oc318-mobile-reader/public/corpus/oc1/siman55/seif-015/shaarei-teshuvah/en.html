“A house.” See Baer Heitev. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah writes concerning upper galleries that are not enclosed by a complete wall, but only by railings ten handbreadths high, with a large courtyard below toward which the galleries face: even though the people can see one another, they do not combine. Eliyah Rabbah cites this. Matteh Yosef, part 2, siman 13, writes that they do combine and that this is the common practice. Malki BaKodesh rejects his words. Machazik Berachah appears likewise to agree with Knesset HaGedolah and writes that Responsa Zera Emet rules similarly; see there. Nevertheless, Machazik Berachah writes that if it is not easy for those standing in the upper galleries or women’s section to descend into the synagogue, and they make an effort to show their faces from above, one may rely on the lenient view. Machazik Berachah further discusses a lazaretto; that is, a quarantine place used to protect against contaminated air, called a contumaz; where two groups are present, six people in one house and four in another, and they cannot reach one another. A sage was uncertain whether the four may stand by the doorway of their house and combine with the six, since they can see one another. He writes that, because those people are not permitted to enter farther inside, the guard remains with them in the field before the doorway, and those in the house cannot go outside because the area outside is narrow and also serves as a public passage. If the four make an effort to come to the doorway and show their faces to the others, it is comparable to showing one’s face through a window, which allows one to combine. This is all the more so because it is altogether impossible for them to be together. In this pressing circumstance one may rely on the authorities cited above, so that they will not go forty days without reciting Kaddish and Kedushah; see there.
