With this I resolved in Eliyah Rabbah to Yoreh Deah, siman 337, the words of the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, who wrote that if beis din saw fit to place a ban that he not join a zimun of three, not pray with ten, and that they not circumcise his son, they are permitted. The Shach wondered: in any case, every banned person is forbidden in zimun and prayer, as written there in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 2. I wrote that certainly every banned person is forbidden in zimun and prayer, but that means counting him for zimun or for ten. However, when there are three or ten without him, it is permitted to pray and make zimun with them, since they are permitted to pray while he is in the synagogue. Therefore it says that if beis din saw fit to ban him so that he not make zimun with three, etc., meaning even where there are three or ten without him, they have authority, as written here: unless they specified explicitly, etc. See Eliyah Zuta. Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah wrote: I was uncertain whether he combines for a minyan, and I concluded that he does not combine. In my humble opinion there is proof from siman 199 that he does not combine for zimun, and all the more so for prayer.
