(Seif katan 11) The inner edge, etc. And in Sefer Tanya, etc., and likewise in the Gemara in Avodah Zarah—meaning: this law has its source in Pesachim page 85b: we taught, from the edge inward is considered inward (for kodshim kalim that may be eaten throughout Jerusalem but not outside Jerusalem; for kodshei kodashim that are eaten only within the azarah), from the edge outward is considered outward. Rav said: and likewise for prayer. And the Gemara raises contradictions in the mishnah's inferences against each other: you said in the first clause "from the edge inward is considered inward"—behold the edge itself is considered outward; and in the last clause it teaches "from the edge outward is considered outward"—behold the edge itself is considered inward. And it answers: regarding the gates of the azarah it is considered inward, for all the gates of the azarah were sanctified with the sanctity of the azarah except the eastern gate, which is the Nikanor gate, because of the metzora who was healed and fulfilled as the Torah commands. On the eighth day he was still lacking atonement and forbidden to enter the azarah, yet he had to bring his hands into the azarah to place the blood of his guilt-offering on his big toes. Since the Nikanor gate was not sanctified, he could stand in the hollow of the gate and extend his hands in—and by this he does not violate the prohibition of partial entry, for what one puts of his body into the azarah is not called entry. But the gates of the azarah were sanctified; therefore the edge itself is considered inward—unlike the gates of Jerusalem, which were all not sanctified so that metzoraim sent outside Jerusalem, forbidden to enter the city, could stand in the hollow of the gates for shelter from sun and rain, as Beit Yosef wrote. And it seems at first glance that for joining a matter of sanctity the edge itself is considered inward—for in general kodshim are considered inward, unlike the gates of the azarah; and also regarding the gates of Jerusalem, were it not for the metzoraim, they would have sanctified them for that reason. But Rabbenu Yerucham wrote regarding prayer that the edge itself is considered outward; and likewise Rambam chapter 9 of laws of Korban Pesach regarding taking the Pesach offering out of its group at the time of eating—that it is forbidden, for the edge itself is considered outward. And it seems we derive from this for prayer as Rabbenu Yerucham's words, etc., Beit Yosef. Therefore it is written here in Shulchan Aruch and he did not bring any disputant that the edge is considered inward—even though it seems to Beit Yosef that the edge is inward for prayer since the gates of the azarah were sanctified, and one must also give a reason why the gates of Jerusalem were not sanctified as above—nevertheless he set aside his view against Rabbenu Yerucham as above, since he found proof from the aforementioned Rambam. However, had Beit Yosef seen Sefer Tanya that disagrees, he would certainly have brought his view; and it is possible he would have ruled like him, since so it implies in the Gemara.
