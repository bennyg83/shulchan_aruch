“A leap year.” See Baer Heitev. The same ruling cited from Shevut Yaakov is stated by Meil Tzedakah, siman 21. Korban HaEdah, at the beginning of the first chapter of Megillah, proves that one born in an ordinary year does not become bar mitzvah until Adar II when his thirteenth year is a leap year. This accords with the Rama and Peri Chadash here, who disagree with Maharshal HaLevi. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Even HaEzer, siman 59; Levush and Eliyah Rabbah in siman 685; and Yad Ephraim in siman 565. Responsa Bach, siman 175, writes that if one was born on Rosh Chodesh in a deficient month and in the current year the preceding month is full, this presents no difficulty. Eliyah Rabbah writes that if Kislev was deficient in the year of his birth, so that Rosh Chodesh Tevet had only one day, while now Kislev is full and Rosh Chodesh has two days, he nevertheless becomes bar mitzvah on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Tevet. Eliyah Rabbah further writes that according to the Magen Avraham, who holds that the first day of Rosh Chodesh is regarded as the thirtieth day of the preceding month, one may say that he does not become bar mitzvah until the second day of Rosh Chodesh. There is some room to distinguish. See Even HaEzer, siman 126, and Yoreh De’ah, siman 65, in the Taz and Knesset HaGedolah there.
