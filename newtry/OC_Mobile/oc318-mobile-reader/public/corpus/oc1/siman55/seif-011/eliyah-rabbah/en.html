That is, when the door is closed, etc. — it is explained that even the side itself — namely those standing in the synagogue doorway in the thickness of the opening — is as outside; for Rashi explained that the side itself is called from the inner lip of the thickness of the opening until the place of the hinge. And even though Shulchan Aruch's language is "thickness of the door" and Rashi's language is "thickness of the opening," nevertheless from Shulchan Aruch's language it implies they are the same — it requires study; and so it implies from Atteret Tzvi, who so explained Shulchan Aruch's words; and so Magen Avraham explained. The reason seems to me: even though Beit Yosef wrote that to him the Talmud implies it is as inside, nevertheless since he brought at the end Rabbeinu Yerucham's words, who wrote explicitly it is as outside, he set aside his view before Rabbeinu Yerucham's view. But Olas Tamid wrote that Shulchan Aruch did not write regarding the side itself what its law is — meaning from the place of the inner lip of the thickness of the door and inward, i.e., all the thickness of the threshold — until here; and he was not precise. Moreover, from the place of the inner lip inward is the synagogue itself, as Rashi's explanation that Beit Yosef brought. Moreover, according to his understanding of Shulchan Aruch, that from the side outward is spoken and not the side itself — if so, it should have been clear from Shulchan Aruch that he holds the side itself is as inside, since he mentioned only from the side outward — it requires study. Nevertheless for law Olas Tamid too wrote that it implies in Beit Yosef that he agrees with Rabbeinu Yerucham that the side itself is as outside; and now we must explain Levush's view, who was silent on this. And greater study is needed, for I saw in Sefer Shibolei HaLeket — this is his language regarding prayer: it is as inside; so my teacher explained — until here; and so Sefer Tanya wrote in the name of Rabbi R. bar Binyamin; and he wrote the reason: for regarding Shabbos we say an open opening is as inside. And had Beit Yosef seen their words, he would not have been silent from holding his view.
