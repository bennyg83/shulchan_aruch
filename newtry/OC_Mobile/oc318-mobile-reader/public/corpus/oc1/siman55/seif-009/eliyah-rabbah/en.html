Thirteen is not, etc. — and Olas Tamid wrote there is reason to wonder, for he wrote before that one does not become an adult until thirteen, and an intercalated month is included; if so, why did he mention "and the thirteenth year is not intercalated"? Therefore it seems he did not mention it precisely; and so it seems from Rama's words, since he wrote anonymously "one who was born" and did not write "some say" — until here. And I wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that he was not precise at all in this, for responsum Mahari Mintz siman 9, which they published for this law, ruled explicitly that if one was born in an intercalated year in first Adar, and in the thirteenth year the year became intercalated, he becomes bar mitzvah in first Adar. And even though he ruled there that one becomes bar mitzvah in second Adar — that is specifically one born in a regular year, or also born in second Adar; but one born in first Adar we say the end of his law is like its beginning; and so I found below in Levush siman 485 — only that he ruled so from his own reasoning and did not see the responsum mentioned above; and if so everything is settled. And Rama and Levush wrote precisely "and the thirteenth year is not intercalated" — and Levush wrote therefore: one born in a regular year, etc. — that if born in an intercalated year he becomes bar mitzvah in first Adar, and that is clear. Again Sefer Magen Avraham was printed and I saw in it too as Olas Tamid's words; and one may wonder on him that he did not see responsum Mahari Mintz. Magen Avraham wrote: one born on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and when he becomes bar mitzvah Kislev is lacking — he does not become bar mitzvah until Rosh Chodesh — until here. And see siman 485 s.k. 5 — yahrzeit is the reverse. He wrote responsum Bach siman 145 — this is his language: the year the boy was born, on Rosh Chodesh Tevet it was lacking, and now it is full — we have no concern with this — until here, his language. It seems his explanation: in the year he was born Kislev was lacking and there was only one day Rosh Chodesh Tevet; and now Kislev is full and there are two days Rosh Chodesh Tevet — nevertheless he becomes bar mitzvah on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Tevet. And to me it seemed according to Magen Avraham mentioned above that he does not become bar mitzvah until the second day of Rosh Chodesh, for the first day we consider the thirtieth day of the intercalated month; and one may distinguish somewhat.
