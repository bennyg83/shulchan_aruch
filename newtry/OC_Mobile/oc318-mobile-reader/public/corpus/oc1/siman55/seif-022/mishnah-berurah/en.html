(70) On the Days of Awe—meaning, if there are eight or nine men they can compel one another not to remain here to pray alone; but if one of them wishes to go to another place where there is a minyan, they cannot compel him at all.

(71) The city's inhabitants—even if only three or four baalei batim reside in the city, if they have servants and teachers to complete the minyan.

(72) To hire a chazzan—in order that he fulfill their obligation with Barekhu and kedushah and the like; and even all year they compel for this.

(73) The tamid—for since there is a minyan in their city, the duty of the mitzvah applies to them. And in small communities, one should compel the youths and scholars to go to the synagogue where there is not often a minyan, for Torah study time is separate; and specifically in large communities then scholars may pray in their place of study, since there is nevertheless a congregation in the synagogue; unlike in such a small kibbutz. Our Sages said that Rabbi Eliezer came to the synagogue and did not find ten there, and he freed his slave and they completed to ten in order to fulfill the many through kedushah—from this we learn all the more that a person should not be lazy with ordinary exertion to gather a minyan for prayer; and how great is the mitzvah of the man whose residence is in a small kibbutz when he sees to gather the minyan constantly so the tamid not be voided—for even one who is only among the first ten, our Sages said he receives reward corresponding to all who come after him; how much more so one who labors to gather them as well. And our Sages said: whoever causes the many to fulfill a mitzvah, no sin comes through his hand.
