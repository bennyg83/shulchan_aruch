(70) On the Yamim Noraim. Meaning, if there are eight or nine men, they may compel one another not to remain there and pray individually. If, however, one of them wishes to go elsewhere where there is a minyan, they cannot compel him at all.
(71) Of the residents of the city. Even if only three or four householders live in the city, provided that they have servants and teachers who can complete the minyan.
(72) To hire a chazzan. So that he may enable them to fulfill their obligation through Barchu, Kedushah, and the like. They may compel one another to do this even throughout the year.
(73) The regular offering. Since there is a minyan in their city, the obligation of the mitzvah rests upon them. In small communities, the young men and Torah students should be compelled to go to the synagogue where there is no regular minyan, for Torah study has its own time. Only in large communities may the students pray in their place of study, since there is a congregation in the synagogue regardless. This is not so in a small gathering. Our Sages said that R. Eliezer came to the synagogue, did not find ten men there, and freed his slave so that the slave could complete the minyan and enable the public to fulfill its obligation of Kedushah. From this we learn that a person should certainly not be indolent merely because of the effort required to assemble a minyan for prayer. How great is the mitzvah of one who lives in a small community and continually endeavors to gather the minyan so that the regular service will not be discontinued. Our Sages said that even one who is merely among the first ten receives reward corresponding to all who arrive afterward. All the more so does this apply to one who also labors to gather them. Our Sages further said that one who brings merit to the public will not be caused to sin.
