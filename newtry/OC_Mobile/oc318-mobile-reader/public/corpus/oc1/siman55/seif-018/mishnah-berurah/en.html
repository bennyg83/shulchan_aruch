(57) And some in the Azarah. The same applies even if nine are in the synagogue and one individual is in the Azarah. The Peri Megadim wrote that the cases in seifim 17, 18, and 19 refer to when they do not see one another, for if they see one another, even in two actual houses they combine, similar to zimun below in siman 195. Some are stringent even when they see one another, and in pressing circumstances perhaps one may be lenient.
(58) They do not combine. Because there is no breach there, and the doorway itself, even without a door, is considered like a partition, so they are two houses. The Chayei Adam wrote that according to this, when there is a house and within it another room, and that room is not breached in its entirety, one who prays there is like one who prays alone; only Kaddish and Kedushah he may answer, as below in seif 20. In Responsa Radbaz, siman 650, I found that he wrote this is specifically regarding joining, but regarding being considered as praying with the congregation, if the room has no other entrance but only through the large house, it is considered as praying with the congregation.
