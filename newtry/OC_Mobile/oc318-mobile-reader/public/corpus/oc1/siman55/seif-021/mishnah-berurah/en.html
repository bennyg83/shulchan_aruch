(66) In its place—meaning, since the custom in all Jewish Diasporas is that even those who do not have a complete minyan all year hire one or two on the Days of Awe, or go to a place with a minyan—this is comparable to a sefer Torah; and in the synagogue that the townspeople compel one another.

(67) Eleven and wish—and the same applies: twelve and three wish to leave, or thirteen and four wish to leave—they all need to hire one jointly in partnership.

(68) Half, etc.—the later authorities wrote that this is a scribal error; for in this case both pay equally, for each is obligated to place a man in his place, since initially there was a complete minyan and one may not cancel the minyan. And that which they give half according to wealth applies when they do not live in a settlement of only eight who need to hire two—for then it also depends on wealth, since the poor can go to the nearby city and the rich cannot leave their homes and property.

(69) Upon those who leave—one who cannot return home due to compulsion who is not permitted to enter the country is exempt even when the festival time arrives; specifically if he is truly compelled. But if due to a monetary debt or negligence he cannot come, his fellows do not lose their share. All this is regarding completing the minyan and the chazzan's wages; but etrog is not comparable to all these—see below siman 658 seif 9 and what we wrote there. Settlements that need to hire a minyan and chazzan, and there are surroundings accustomed to come there with their households—they need not give anything to the settlement, for if they wish they can go to another city where there is a minyan without them and they would not have needed to give anything even for hiring the shaliach tzibbur, since even without them they need to hire a shaliach tzibbur.
