(66) In his place. Since the custom throughout the Jewish diaspora is that even those who do not have a complete minyan the entire year hire one or two people for the Yamim Noraim, or go to a place with a minyan, this is comparable to a Torah scroll and synagogue, regarding which the residents of a city can compel one another.
(67) Eleven, and they wish. The same applies to twelve when three wish to leave, or thirteen when four wish to leave; they must all hire one person in partnership.
(68) Half, etc. The later authorities wrote that this is a scribal error, for in this case the two pay equally, since each one is obligated to place a person in his stead, as there was initially a complete minyan and he is not permitted to nullify the minyan. The case where they pay half according to wealth is when only eight live in the settlement and they need to hire two; then it also depends on wealth, because the poor can go to the nearby city, while the wealthy cannot leave their homes and property.
(69) Upon those who leave. One who cannot return home because of duress, since he is not permitted to enter the province, is exempt even if the festival time has arrived. This is specifically if he is truly under duress; but if because of monetary debt or negligence he cannot come, his colleagues do not lose their share. All this concerns completing the minyan and hiring the chazzan. But an esrog is not comparable to all these; see below in siman 658, seif 9, and what we wrote there. Settlements that need to hire a minyan and chazzan, and there are people from the surroundings who usually come there with their households, do not need to give anything to the settlement. For if they wished, they could go to another city that has a minyan without them, and they would not need to pay anything even toward the shaliach tzibbur’s hire, since even without them the settlement needs to hire a shaliach tzibbur.
