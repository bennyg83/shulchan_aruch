A city that does not have, etc., they compel. It appears to me that if they were accustomed from earlier times to go to a nearby city where there is a large gathering and the prayer is with a multitude of people, and now nine want to hire a shaliach tzibbur so that they will not need to go to the city, but the tenth refuses, they cannot compel him according to their view. A proof for this can be brought from the Mordechai in the first chapter of Bava Basra.
And the wage of the chazzan. At first glance this refers specifically to the above case, where there were eleven, and so Sefer Olas HaYom writes, that it speaks where they left in such a way that it is necessary to hire another in place of the one who remains, since there are fewer than ten. But if he left a minyan behind him, he need not pay the wage of the shaliach tzibbur, for since it is permitted in our time for him not to be at home, and the shaliach tzibbur is only for the Days of Awe, why should he pay? If so, however, it is very difficult why the Mechaber wrote that those who leave are like those who remain. These two who are not at home should need to pay only the amount of one share. For although if both of them were at home, the chazzan's wage would be divided among eleven people, nevertheless now that he is not at home and derives no benefit at all from the chazzan, and is obligated only because he became bound to the minyan, he should not need to complete more than the share that would fall upon a minyan. Unless we say that the Shulchan Aruch did not speak precisely when saying that those who leave are like those who remain, and came only to say that even though he is not at home, he cannot exempt himself. Or perhaps it is like Sefer Olas HaYom, but the Mechaber holds that even if he left a minyan in his home, he is nevertheless obligated to pay the chazzan's wage, for it is among the needs of the city, similar to a wedding house or mikveh, explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 163, at the end of seif 3 in the gloss. Afterward I found in a responsum of Maharil that it implies like Olas HaYom, and this requires study.
