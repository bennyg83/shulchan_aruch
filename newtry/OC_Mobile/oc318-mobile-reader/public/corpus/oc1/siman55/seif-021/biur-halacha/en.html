A city that has none, etc., they obligate — it seems to me: if they were accustomed from before to go to the nearby city where there is a large gathering and prayer is with the majority of the people, and now nine want to hire a shaliach tzibbur so they need not go to the city — and the tenth refuses — they cannot compel him according to their view; and from Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Batra, proof may be brought for this.

And the cantor's wages — apparently this refers specifically to what is above, where there were eleven; and so in Sefer Eretz Chaim — it deals when they left in a manner that requires hiring another in place of this one who remains with fewer than ten; but if he left a minyan after him, one need not pay the shaliach tzibbur's wages, since it is permitted for him not to be at home at this time and the shaliach tzibbur is only for the Days of Awe — why should he pay? But if so, it is very difficult why the author wrote about those who leave as about those who remain — they should not give for those years they are not at home only according to one account; for even if both were at home the cantor's wages would be divided among eleven people — nevertheless now that he is not at home and does not benefit from the cantor at all, only because he became bound to the minyan, he need not complete more than the share that would have been imposed on the minyan — unless we say Shulchan Aruch did not mean specifically those who leave as those who remain, and only came to say that even though he is not at home he cannot exempt himself; and perhaps this is as Sefer Eretz Chaim — only that the author thought even if he left a minyan at home he is also obligated to pay the cantor's wages, for it is among the needs of the city, similar to a wedding hall or mikveh explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 163 end of seif 3 in the gloss; afterward I found in responsum Maharil that it implies like the work of the day — uncertain.
