In the gloss. 'If some departed after he began the repetition aloud and Kedushah, they may begin the entire Seder Kedushah and say the full Kaddish.' This implies that, for the Kaddish as well, he must have begun the repetition aloud. If some departed before the repetition began, they do not say the full Kaddish. This is difficult: Why should they not say the full Kaddish on account of the silent prayer, which was recited with ten? How is this different from the evening prayer, concerning which Rema subsequently writes that the evening prayer and the Kaddish following it do not belong to Keriat Shema and its blessings? This implies that if they began the Amidah, they say the Kaddish afterward, because the Kaddish belongs to the prayer. Why should Shacharit be different? Does the institution of the repetition cause the Kaddish no longer to relate to the silent prayer? This requires examination. Regarding the law that, if some departed, they finish provided a majority remains, I am uncertain what the law is if they began with ten, five departed, and then another person arrived, so that there is now a majority: Does this permit them to finish, given that there is no majority of the original ten who began? Even if one of the five who departed returned, it remains uncertain, for perhaps once there was a period in which no majority remained, the matter was entirely terminated and it is now like beginning anew.
