1) [Se'if 1] Kaddish is recited. This means after Pesukei DeZimrah. Taz 1. Agur 98 writes: Raavad explained the reasons for the Kaddishim recited during prayer. The first follows Pesukei DeZimrah, because it marks the conclusion of a unit of verses. The second follows completion of the Amidah, which is a mitzvah in its own right. The third follows Kedushah, which is a sacred matter. The fourth follows recitation of a psalm, chapter, aggadah, or Mishnah, as Sotah 49a states: On what does the world endure? On the Kedushah of U'Va LeTziyon and Yehei Shmei Rabbah following aggadah. The fifth follows Ashrei at Minchah, which is a mitzvah in its own right, for anyone who recites Tehillah LeDavid three times each day is assured, etc. The sixth follows Krias Shema of Maariv, because the Maariv prayer is optional and some leave before the Amidah and would otherwise not hear Kaddish. The seventh follows the Maariv prayer, when the prayer has been completed. No fewer than seven Kaddishim are recited daily, corresponding to 'Seven times a day I praise You.' Thus appears in Shibolei HaLeket. Beis Yosef and Perishah 1. He does not count the Kaddish after the Minchah Amidah, before the beginning of Maariv, because it is certainly included by inference from the Kaddish after the Shacharis Amidah. This also resolves why he says, 'No fewer than seven,' since Kaddish following aggadah is not recited every day. In any event, there are seven when the Kaddish following Minchah is included; he wished only to explain here the reason for each type. Perishah, ibid. See Levush. According to the Arizal, twelve Kaddishim must be recited each day. Six are at Shacharis: one before Hodu, the second before Yotzer, the third after the Amidah, the fourth after U'Va LeTziyon, the fifth before Kaveh, and the sixth before Aleinu. Although the Rav writes that the final Kaddish is not included among the Kaddishim, etc., he concludes that this Kaddish pertains to Asiyah, where the aspect of death exists, in order to elevate all souls and nefashos through the mystery of resurrection; therefore orphans recite it upon the death of a father or mother. See there. Toras Chacham, page 139b, writes that its intention is to elevate Asiyah to Yetzirah at the source of the souls of the dead. This applies from the lowest Asiyah through the highest levels, because every aspect is called Asiyah relative to the level above it. It is therefore also obligatory. Two Kaddishim are recited at Minchah, one before and one after the Amidah. Four are recited at Maariv: one before the Maariv berachos, one before the Amidah, one after the Amidah, and one before Aleinu. This is explained in Shaar HaKavanos, Derush HaKaddish, and Nusach HaTefillah, page 51, cited above in siman 48; see there. See also our comments to siman 48:1, s.v. VeDa Hakdamah Achas, for the kabbalistic reason for reciting the Kaddishim. Some additionally have the custom after Minchah to recite 'For the conductor, with instrumental music,' etc., followed by Kaddish and Aleinu. This produces thirteen Kaddishim, corresponding to the Thirteen Attributes. Fortunate is one who serves his Master with joy and intention. See below, paragraph 19.
2) Levush writes that 'Tiskabel tzeloshon' is never recited except after the Amidah; see there. Taz 1 cites this and objects that it is not an absolute rule: during Selichos before dawn, Tiskabel is recited after Selichos, because they too contain many petitions like the Amidah. Tiskabel is not recited immediately after the Amidah because U'Va LeTziyon is drawn after the prayer, since it relates to prayer and praise. We therefore delay saying Tiskabel until after U'Va LeTziyon so that it applies to that order as well. Consequently, on a day with Mussaf, a full Kaddish with Tiskabel is recited immediately after Shacharis; after Mussaf, a second full Kaddish with Tiskabel is recited for Mussaf, and it also applies back to U'Va LeTziyon. Levush in the gloss; Ateres Zekeinim. See below, siman 423.
3) There: Kaddish is recited. Rambam writes in the Mishneh Torah that whenever ten people engage in Oral Torah (even Midrash or Aggadah) they recite Kaddish DeRabbanan. Magen Avraham, above at the end of siman 54, cites Be'er Sheva, who writes that Rambam contradicts himself: in his commentary to the Mishnah at the end of Avos, Rambam writes concerning Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya that the people customarily recite it upon completing the chapters because Kaddish is not recited over Mishnah, but only over Aggadah, as the Master said, 'Yehei Shmei Rabbah' following Aggadah, etc. One may answer that by law Kaddish is certainly recited over all Oral Torah. This is somewhat supported by the phrase 'Yehei Shmei Rabbah following Aggadah': the fact that it specifies the response following Aggadah implies that Kaddish is also recited over other Oral Torah, but the response following Aggadah possesses exceptional importance, for the world endures through it. The reason may be that Aggadah draws a person's heart. Yisrael rejoices, and when people respond 'Yehei Shmei' out of the joy of a mitzvah, it brings satisfaction to their Master and sustains the world. For this reason the custom developed to recite Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya at the end of a halachic discourse, so that the people rejoice, for it is Aggadah and speaks of Yisrael's merit. Kaddish is then recited so that it falls into the category of 'Yehei Shmei following Aggadah.' This is a custom, not the underlying law, and this is why Rambam says in his commentary to the Mishnah, 'They customarily recite,' etc. Birkei Yosef 1 and Kesher Gudal 8:2. Therefore, when one recites Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya after Oral Torah in order to say Kaddish, he must say it with heartfelt joy and fervor so that Yisrael rejoices. In particular, when mentioning G-d's Name in the verse, 'Hashem desired, for the sake of his righteousness,' etc., one must say it with intention. As explained in many places in our work, great care is required to articulate G-d's Name with intention. Because a person is constantly accustomed to reading G-d's Name in verses, there is concern that he may forget, when mentioning it, and utter it without intention. One must therefore exercise great care at all times never to let any Divine Name pass his lips without intention. See the wondrous incident related above in siman 5:5. See also my sacred work Chafetz BaChaim, derush 6, maareches HaTzaddikim, where I wrote that the sins of one who hears Kaddish from a chacham expounding the wisdom of Kabbalah are forgiven; see there.
4) Kaddish should not be recited at all except at the established places in the prayers, after reading some words of Torah, or after an explanation or exposition of a verse. It should not be recited after the prayers composed by Rav Saadiah Gaon or after supplications for mercy. Rambam in his responsa, Pe'er HaDor, new edition, 130. Orchos Chaim, page 96 at the beginning of column 4, writes in Maharam's name that Kaddish is not recited over Torah verses, which require no reinforcement, while Ashrei was made like a rabbinic prayer; see there. The aforementioned words of Rambam prove the opposite. Raavad likewise writes in the responsum cited by Maran in Beis Yosef that Kaddish is recited after a psalm; see there. This is the practice. Birkei Yosef 2; Kesher Gudal 8:3-4; Shalmei Tzibbur 81b; Chesed LaAlafim 54:2; and Yafeh LaLev 2. Those who recite Kaddish after a circumcision without first reciting a psalm therefore act improperly. Chesed LaAlafim, ibid. The people customarily recite Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya, etc., after any Oral Torah and then Kaddish DeRabbanan. After Written Torah, they recite 'The salvation of the righteous is from Hashem,' etc., and then Kaddish Yehei Shelama Rabbah.
5) Some say that the words over which Kaddish is recited must themselves be said in the presence of the minyan. Therefore, if no minyan is present in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibbur should wait at Yishtabach and remain silent until a minyan arrives; he then recites Yishtabach and Kaddish. When studying Oral Torah, after the minyan arrives he should recite the baraisa, 'The Holy One, blessed is He, desired to confer merit upon Yisrael,' or the final two or three verses of Ashrei. If no minyan was present for Hodu, they should recite through Baruch She'amar; the shaliach tzibbur should then recite Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya and say Kaddish DeRabbanan. Chesed LaAlafim 54:3. See Kesher Gudal 8:1, what was stated above in siman 50:6, and what we shall write below in paragraph 39, with G-d's help.
6) There: It is not recited with fewer than ten, etc. Scripture says, 'I shall be sanctified among the Children of Yisrael.' This is derived through the shared word 'among': here it says 'among,' and elsewhere it says, 'Separate yourselves from among the congregation.' A congregation contains no fewer than ten, as the verse concerning the spies says, 'How long shall I bear with this evil congregation?' Yehoshua and Calev are excluded, leaving ten, as explained in Megillah 23b. Bach; Taz 1; Birkei Yosef 3; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav 2. See below, paragraph 17.
7) There: It is not recited with fewer than ten, etc. Initially, one should seek six people who have not heard Kaddish and Barchu. After the fact (when six such people are unavailable) it is recited even for one person who did not hear, as stated below in Shulchan Aruch 69:1; see there and Magen Avraham 4 there. Ateres Zekeinim 1 in this siman writes likewise.
8) There: It is not recited with fewer than ten, etc. Just as it is proper to minimize berachos, it is proper to minimize Kaddishim. Mahari Fas in a handwritten responsum; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. Responsa Devar Shmuel 183 likewise protests the practice of gathering ten people and reciting numerous Kaddishim over verses of Torah, Mishnah, or Gemara; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, cites him, as do Shalmei Tzibbur, pages 80b and 81c; Chida, Kesher Gudal 8:9; Siddur Beis Yaakov 2; and Chesed LaAlafim 11. Rather, one Kaddish should be recited over all the Written Torah studied and one Kaddish over the Oral Torah. Chesed LaAlafim, ibid.
9) Just as it is forbidden to pass in front of one who is praying, it is forbidden to pass in front of one reciting Kaddish. The pious Rabbi Yaakov Molcho, handwritten responsum 135; Birkei Yosef 9 and Kesher Gudal 8:5; Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid.; Siddur Beis Yaakov 50; Shaarei Teshuvah at the beginning of siman 56; Zechirah LeChaim 100; Chesed LaAlafim 12; and Ben Ish Chai, ibid., 10. The prohibition applies from the beginning of Kaddish through 'daamiran be'alma, ve'imru amen.' Afterward, when he says 'Al Yisrael,' etc., and 'Yehei shelama,' etc., there is no concern, because this is merely a custom and not part of the original Kaddish text, as stated above in siman 51:10 and 21. Ben Ish Chai, ibid., writes likewise.
10) There: With fewer than ten males, etc. This applies to every Kaddish, even Kaddish DeRabbanan after study, for all Kaddishim have the same law. Maamar Mordechai, page 58a; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur 4; and Solel Boneh 1.
11) There: With fewer than ten males, etc. One must take care not to count Jews by their heads to determine whether a minyan is present. The beginning of the second chapter of Yoma states that it is forbidden to count Yisrael even for a mitzvah, as it is written, 'Shaul summoned the people and counted them with lambs.' Pri Chadash 1. Chesed LaAlafim 10 writes likewise and concludes: Many err by counting alef, beis, gimel, etc., imagining that the prohibition applies only to saying uno, dos (meaning one, two) while counting by letters is permitted. This is their error. Rather, they should assess visually and count in thought. Pele Yoetz 2, entry moneh, rules likewise, as do Rav Chaim Palagi in Kaf HaChaim 13:10 and Yafeh LaLev 3. See Magen Avraham 156:2 and Pischei HaDevir 3 there.
12) There: Adult freemen. Regarding a person created through Sefer Yetzirah, Rabbi Tzvi Ashkenazi concludes in Responsa Chacham Tzvi 93 that he does not join a minyan. He derives this from Sanhedrin 65b, where such a being was returned to dust. Birkei Yosef 4 and Machazik Berachah 1 agree. Divrei Menachem, Hagahos HaTur 2, and Ikrei HaDat 3:15 cite this.
13) An onen does not join ten for any sacred matter. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos Beis Yosef 4; Olas Tamid 3; Eliyah Rabbah 10; Pri Chadash 8; Yad Aharon, Hagahos Beis Yosef, in the names of Perach Mateh Aharon 1:19 and Shevus Yaakov 2:25; and Beis Yehudah 45. Chida rules likewise in Birkei Yosef 5 and Kesher Gudal 8 in the names of the later authorities. See also Shalmei Tzibbur, pages 80a and 177b; Beis Yaakov 25; and Ben Ish Chai, Vayechi 6. If, however, someone else is attending to the burial arrangements on his behalf, so that if he wishes to recite Krias Shema he is not prevented, as stated in siman 71, it is certainly reasonable that he joins. Pri Chadash, Yad Aharon, Birkei Yosef, Shalmei Tzibbur, and Beis Yaakov, ibid.
14) A drunk person: if he has not reached Lot's degree of intoxication, it is proper to include him in a minyan for Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barchu, even if he cannot speak before a king. If he has reached Lot's degree of intoxication, he is like an insane person and may not be included for any sacred matter. Responsa Beis Yehudah 45, cited by Ikrei HaDat 3:3; Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of Kaddish 24; and Pischei Olam 18. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 79c, however, cites Beis Yehudah to the opposite effect: if he cannot speak before a king, he does not join ten; see there. Chesed LaAlafim 8 writes likewise. Their words require further study. See below, siman 99:1 in the gloss, and Eshel Avraham on the margin of Shulchan Aruch there, citing Responsa Beis Yehudah 45, that one unable to speak before a king does not join. Chida, Kesher Gudal 8:7, and Ben Ish Chai, Vayechi 6, rule likewise. Siddur Beis Yaakov, however, writes that this is not the accepted custom; rather, he is included even if unfit to speak before a king.
15) An androgynos may under no circumstances be permitted to join a minyan of ten. Responsa Orach LaTzaddik 2-5, unlike one who entertained the possibility that such a person joins; Siddur Beis Yaakov 22; and Ikrei HaDat 3:18. Pri Megadim, Eshel Avraham 4, likewise writes that a tumtum or androgynos does not join. This is not a double doubt (perhaps nine men plus a woman or slave join, and perhaps this person is male) because according to our ruling, a slave or woman certainly does not join. The same applies to one who is half slave; see Eliyah Rabbah 128 and 143. The word 'freemen' also indicates this. Although Yeshuos Yaakov writes that one who is half slave and half free joins because he can attain full obligation, since his master is compelled to free him, one should nevertheless be stringent. If a tumtum was opened and found to be male, he may be accepted, as I wrote in Kol Yaakov 39:8; see there.
16) An apostate to idolatry or public Shabbos desecration, or one who transgresses even one matter specifically to provoke, is like a non-Jew and does not join. Se'if 11, which says that a transgressor joins, concerns one who sins out of appetite; otherwise he does not. Pri Megadim, ibid. See what I wrote in Kol Yaakov 39:11; Yoreh Deah 2:5; Rav Zevach Tzedek's comments there on that se'if; Rav Pe'alim 2:11 and 3:12; and below, paragraph 62.
17) A convert joins a minyan of ten for every sacred matter. Although Scripture says, 'I shall be sanctified among the Children of Yisrael,' and Tosafos, Sukkah 28b, s.v. Lerabos, state that 'Children of Yisrael' excludes converts absent an inclusion, here the verse is only an asmachta. A convert is qualified to serve as shaliach tzibbur, as stated in siman 53:19, and there is no reason he should not join ten. Pri Megadim, ibid.; Pischei HaDevir 1; and Levushei Serad on Magen Avraham 7. A convert who was circumcised but has not immersed does not join until he immerses. Siddur Beis Yaakov, laws of Kaddish 16, in the name of Responsa Radbaz 3:478, Fürth edition.
18) There: Who have grown two hairs. See below, se'if 5 in the gloss, and our comments there, paragraph 46, with G-d's help.
19) There: The same applies to Kedushah and Barchu, etc. Regarding the custom for minor orphans to recite Mourner's Kaddish and Barchu publicly while adults respond, Devar Shmuel 301 protests the practice. Pri Chadash likewise protests it below, at the beginning of siman 69, and writes that in several places he objected but they did not heed the teachers; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, cites him. See Responsa Beis Yehudah, Orach Chaim 22, which discusses this at length and writes that there is some support for the custom when minors of six or seven who know to Whom they sanctify recite Kaddish. He concludes: All this applies specifically to the Kaddish containing Barchu, which resembles repeating Barchu for those few individuals who did not hear the Barchu preceding Yotzer, as Rivash writes in Responsa 334, cited by Beis Yosef 133. Although we have provided some support for the custom, it is proper to institute that the final Kaddish containing Barchu be recited only by an adult of thirteen years who has grown two hairs. Other Kaddishim that are optional may be recited even by a minor who has not reached the age of education. Seven Kaddishim are obligatory each day, as it is written, 'Seven times a day I praise You,' etc. It is proper for the shaliach tzibbur to recite all these. Our custom is for the shaliach tzibbur to recite the seven, except the Kaddish containing Barchu. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 80b, and Siddur Beis Oved, laws of Kaddish 48, cite him. Mahari Chazan, Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim 1, page 78d, defends the custom because the shaliach tzibbur also recites Kaddish together with the minors; in that case there is no concern. If the minor alone recites an obligatory Kaddish without the shaliach tzibbur, however, they have no authority on which to rely; see there. Beis Yaakov 49 cites him. Accordingly, given what was stated above in paragraph 1 in the Arizal's name; that there are twelve Kaddishim in the order of Shacharis, Minchah, and Maariv, all obligatory for the ascent of the worlds; they must all be recited by the chazzan or an adult of thirteen years and one day who has grown two hairs. Other, non-obligatory Kaddishim may be recited by a minor.
20) Regarding the Kaddish recited by orphans, according to the Arizal specifically the final Kaddish before Aleinu benefits the deceased's soul. Shaar HaKavanos, Derush HaKaddish, states: 'The final Kaddish is not included among the Kaddishim, because all the worlds have already ascended. This Kaddish is nevertheless called Kaddish Yasom, meaning the Kaddish recited by orphans. This Kaddish pertains to the world of Asiyah, which possesses the aspect of death, in order to elevate all souls and nefashos through the mystery of resurrection. Therefore orphans recite it upon the death of a father or mother,' etc. See what was stated above in paragraph 1 in the name of Toras Chacham. 'It appears to me that I heard from my master that it is proper to recite this Kaddish for one's deceased father or mother throughout the entire year, even on Shabbos and Yom Tov. The reason is not, as the masses think, merely that it helps save the deceased's soul from the judgment of Gehinnom. It has another benefit: bringing the soul into Gan Eden and elevating it from level to level. Accordingly, it should be recited even on Shabbos and Yom Tov. I saw my master recite the final Kaddish every year on the date of his father's death at all three prayers.' The Rav's words show that specifically the final Kaddish benefits the deceased's soul. Here in our holy city Yerushalayim, may it be built and established, some have the custom for orphans also to recite the Kaddish before Hodu and the Kaddish before Kaveh, while the chazzan alone recites all other Kaddishim. It appears to me that these two Kaddishim are also recited because they correspond to the ascent of the world of Asiyah, which is the aspect of nefesh, into the world of Yetzirah; they may therefore also benefit the deceased's nefesh.
21) If the orphans are minors, we have already stated above that the chazzan must recite Kaddish together with them in order to discharge the congregation's obligation. The same applies if the orphan stammers and does not articulate the letters correctly and distinctly, even if he is an adult; if he possesses any disqualification from discharging the public's obligation, as stated in siman 53; or if he possesses one of the characteristics stated above that prevents him from joining ten. In all these cases, the chazzan must also recite Kaddish in order to discharge the congregation. Attention must be paid to this. See also below, siman 56:30.
22) In a place where the custom is for orphans to recite all the Kaddishim, if they are in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah they should not interrupt to recite Kaddish, as stated above in siman 53:13; see there.
