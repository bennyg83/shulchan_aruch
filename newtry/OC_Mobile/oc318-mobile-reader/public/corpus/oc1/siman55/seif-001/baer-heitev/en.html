Kaddish. As it is good to minimize blessings, so it is good to minimize Kaddish recitations—as Hagahot Maimoniyot, and likewise in responsa Dvar Shmuel siman 183, who also protests that ten men gather and say many Kaddishim on Torah verses or Mishnah or Gemara, etc. See Rambam's commentary on Mishnah at the end of Avot and Sefer Mateh Yosef volume 2 siman 12; and above siman 54 seif katan 3. Ten Jews who are commoners and none knows the holy language to take them out for the Shemoneh Esreh—one of them can be the shaliach tzibbur and say Kaddish and kedushah in the vernacular. Responsa Dvar Shmuel siman 321—see there also siman 301, who protests against the orphans, very small children, who say Kaddish and Barekhu and the congregation answers after them, for they act not according to law; and likewise Peri Chadash siman 69 protests this—etc.—when two or three say Kaddish together and one precedes: if each comes within kedei dibbur, one answers Amen on the first or the last and it counts for all; and if there is a break, one answers on each separately. Leket Yosher volume 2 siman 48.

Males. Whenever there is a minyan in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibbur may not wait for an important or great person who has not yet come—Binyamin Ze'ev siman 168; and likewise Rama siman 124 seif 3, etc. A person created through Sefer Yetzirah—if he joins for ten and for any matter of kedushah, see responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 93.

Free men. And all the Kaddishim are equal, even the Rabbinic Kaddish after study—as brought in Shakhnehag in the name of Magen Avraham.
