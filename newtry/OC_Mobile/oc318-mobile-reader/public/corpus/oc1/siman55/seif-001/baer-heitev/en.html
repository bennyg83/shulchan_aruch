Kaddish. Just as it is good to minimize blessings, so too it is good to minimize Kaddishim of this sort. Likewise, in Teshuvos Devar Shmuel, siman 183, he also objects to this practice, that ten people gather and recite several Kaddishim over verses of Torah, or Mishnah, or Gemara; see there. See also Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah at the end of tractate Avos, and the work Mateh Yosef, part 2, siman 12. Above, siman 54, s.k. 3: ten Jews who speak a foreign language and among whom no one knows the Holy Tongue well enough to discharge them from their obligation, one of them may be shaliach tzibbur and say Kaddish and Kedushah in the foreign language. Teshuvos Devar Shmuel, siman 321. See further there, siman 301, where he challenges the practice of very young orphans saying Kaddish and Barchu while the congregation answers after them, for they are acting improperly. The Peri Chadash in siman 69 likewise objects to this; see there. When two or three say Kaddish together and one begins first, if each of the others comes in within toch kedei dibbur, he should answer Amen to the first or to the last, and it counts for all of them. But if there is an interruption, he should answer to each one separately. Halachos Ketanos, part 2, siman 48.
Males. Whenever there is a minyan in the synagogue, the shaliach tzibbur should not wait for an important or great person who has not yet arrived. Binyamin Ze'ev, siman (161) [168]. The Rama likewise wrote in siman 124, seif 3; see there. Whether a person created through Sefer Yetzirah joins for ten and for every matter of sanctity, see Teshuvos Chacham Tzvi, siman 93.
Free men. All Kaddishim are the same, even the Rabbinic Kaddish after learning. Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah in the name of Maggid Mishneh.
