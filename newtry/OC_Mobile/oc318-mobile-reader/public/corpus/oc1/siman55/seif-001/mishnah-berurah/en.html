(1) They say, etc.—Just as it is good to minimize blessings, so too it is good to minimize Kaddish recitations. See the later authorities who protested that ten gather in a synagogue and say many Kaddishim on Torah verses or Mishnah or Gemara. Ten Jews who are commoners and none knows the holy language to lead them in the Shemoneh Esreh—one of them may be the shaliach tzibbur and say Kaddish and kedushah in the vernacular.

(2) Kaddish—meaning after pesukei dezimra; and we require ten, for every matter of kedushah such as Kaddish, kedushah, Barekhu, keriat haTorah, and nesiat kapayim is not said with fewer than ten, as it says "and I shall be sanctified among the children of Israel," and we derive by gezeirah shavah "among" "among" from the spies, as written "how long for this evil congregation"—Joshua and Caleb remained, ten—and they are called a congregation. And all the Kaddishim are equal to this, even the Rabbinic Kaddish after study. Taz wrote: at the time of saying pesukei dezimra ten are not required—for even if they said them individually, whenever there are ten at the time of saying Kaddish it suffices; and likewise if they began the evening prayer individually and ten came at the time of Kaddish, he may say Kaddish; and likewise in every study of verses or aggadah, or in saying Aleinu, or in saying Ashrei before Minchah, or in saying Tehillim in the morning—if it was without a minyan and a minyan happened to be present immediately, one should say Kaddish. Magen Avraham in siman 69 and 234—not so much; and likewise from the Gra in siman 234 it is proven like Magen Avraham. Therefore the later authorities agreed [Peri Megadim; Derech HaChayim; Sha'ar Ephraim] that the proper practice is to say afterward a psalm or three verses and then say Kaddish. And see in Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav note 3 that it is proven from there that regarding pesukei dezimra and the evening prayer Magen Avraham also agrees with Taz.

(3) Males, etc.—to exclude women, slaves, and minors; and likewise a tumtum, androgynus, and half-slave half-freeman do not combine [Peri Megadim].

(4) Adults—see Peri Chadash siman 69, who disputed places where the custom is that a minor says Kaddish and Barekhu and the congregation answers after him—that they act not according to law; and even if there are ten adult men besides him. And see in Peri Megadim there that he also sides to be strict lekhatchilah. Hagahot Leket Yosher siman 48 wrote: when two or three say Kaddish together and one precedes—if each comes within kedei dibbur, one answers Amen on the first or the last and it counts for all; and if there is a break, one answers on each separately. A person created through Sefer Yetzirah—if he combines for ten and for every matter of kedushah, see responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 93 and Sefer Ikrei Dinim what he wrote on this matter.

(5) And likewise for kedushah—and likewise for every repetition of the shaliach tzibbur that requires ten, for it is called a matter of kedushah because of the kedushah in it [Megillah 23a; Ran there]. One does not reduce from seven Kaddish recitations each day, corresponding to "seven times a day I praise You"—that is: (1) one after pesukei dezimra, which is after Yishtabach; (2–3) two half-Kaddishim after the eighteen blessings; (4) after the kedushah section, in which they also say titkabel, which also belongs to the eighteen blessings; (5) after Aleinu, which includes saying verses; (6) after Ashrei at Minchah; (7) after the Minchah prayer; (8) after the evening Kriat Shema before the eighteen blessings. The Kaddish after the evening prayer—even though it is also a mitzvah—is not counted among these seven; see there in Levush the reason.
