(1) They say, etc. Just as it is good to minimize blessings, so too it is good to minimize Kaddishim. See the later authorities who objected to the practice of ten people gathering and saying several Kaddishim over verses of Torah, Mishnah, or Gemara. Ten Jews who speak a foreign language and have no one who knows the Holy Tongue to discharge them may have one of them serve as shaliach tzibbur and say Kaddish and Kedushah in the foreign language.
(2) Kaddish. Meaning after Pesukei D’Zimrah. Ten are required, for every matter of sanctity, such as Kaddish, Kedushah, Barchu, Torah reading, and the lifting of the hands, is not recited with fewer than ten, as it is stated, and I shall be sanctified among the Children of Israel. We derive by gezeirah shavah from the spies, from among and among, as it is written, how long for this evil congregation; Yehoshua and Kalev left, leaving ten, and they are called a congregation. All Kaddishim are equal in this respect, even Kaddish D’Rabbanan after learning. The Taz wrote that during Pesukei D’Zimrah ten are not needed, for even if they recited it individually, as long as there are ten at the time of Kaddish, this suffices. Likewise, if they began Maariv individually and ten came by the time of Kaddish, he may say Kaddish. The same applies to any learning, when a person says verses or aggadah, or when saying Aleinu, or Ashrei before Minchah, or Tehillim in the morning, if it was without a minyan and a minyan immediately came, Kaddish may be said. The Magen Avraham in simanim 69 and 234 did not write this way, and from the Gra in siman 234 it is evident like the Magen Avraham. Therefore the later authorities agreed, the Peri Megadim, Derech HaChaim, and other poskim, that the proper practice is that they afterwards say a psalm or three verses and then say Kaddish. See the Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, note 3, from whom it is evident that regarding Pesukei D’Zimrah and Maariv prayer, even the Magen Avraham agrees with the Taz.
(3) Males, etc. This excludes women, slaves, and minors. The same applies to a tumtum, an androgynous person, and one who is half slave and half free; he does not join, as the Peri Megadim writes.
(4) Adults. See Pri Chadash, siman 69, who challenged places where the practice is that a minor says Kaddish and Barchu and the congregation answers after him, for they are acting improperly, even if there are ten adult men besides him. See the Peri Megadim there, who also tends to be stringent initially. Helekes Yaakov, siman 48, wrote that when two or three say Kaddish together and one precedes the others, if each comes within the time needed for speech, one answers Amen with the first or with the last and it counts for all of them; if there is an interval, one answers each one individually. Whether a person created through Sefer Yetzirah joins ten and every matter of sanctity, see Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 63, and what is written in Sefer Ikkarei Dinim on this matter.
(5) The same applies to Kedushah. The same applies to the entire repetition of the shaliach tzibbur, which requires ten, for it is called a matter of sanctity because of the Kedushah in it, as in Megillah 23 and the Ran there. One should not have fewer than seven Kaddishim each day, corresponding to seven times a day I praised You. These are: one after Pesukei D’Zimrah, after Yishtabach; two, half Kaddish after Shemoneh Esrei; three, after the order of Kedushah, where they also say Tiskabel, which also relates to Shemoneh Esrei; four, after Aleinu, which contains verses; five, after Ashrei at Minchah; six, after Minchah prayer; seven, after Krias Shema of Maariv before Shemoneh Esrei. The Kaddish after Maariv prayer, although it too is a mitzvah, is not counted among these seven; see there in the Levush for the reason.
