They recite Kaddish. See Taz. After Yishtabach they recite Kaddish, and it refers back to Pesukei DeZimrah, for Kaddish is recited only after verses from Scripture or after aggadah. See Magen Avraham 54:3. Regarding what is written that Kaddish is recited after the Amidah, one may say that the Amidah contains several verses, such as “Heal us, Hashem,” and the like. The Levush enumerates the seven Kaddishes recited each day, corresponding to “Seven times a day I praise You”: first, after Yishtabach; second, after the Amidah; third, after the order of Kedushah; fourth, after Aleinu, which contains verses; fifth, after Ashrei at Minchah; sixth, after the Minchah Amidah; and seventh, after the blessings of Krias Shema at Maariv. The Kaddish after the Maariv Amidah is optional. I, in my humble capacity, have several thoughts on this matter. I discussed them at length in my work on customs, but here I shall be brief and set them forth, with Hashem's help.
Know that we have three forms of Kaddish: first, full Kaddish with Tiskabel; second, full Kaddish without Tiskabel; and third, half Kaddish. Levush gives a rule that Tiskabel is recited only after the Amidah, while Taz writes that it is also recited after Selichos. In siman 693, according to the view that Tiskabel is recited twice, once after the Amidah and once after Ve'Atah Kadosh, this rule does not hold. Levush, however, follows his own view there, that half Kaddish is recited after the Amidah and full Kaddish with Tiskabel after Ve'Atah Kadosh; Taz there likewise holds this view. Yet Levush's rule that whenever the chazzan cannot recite Tiskabel he does not complete the Kaddish is disproved by Tisha B'Av, when the chazzan recites full Kaddish without Tiskabel after Ve'Atah Kadosh, as Levush himself writes in siman 559. See Taz there.
Now half Kaddish requires examination. Why did they choose half Kaddish rather than full Kaddish without Tiskabel, as is recited after verses or aggadah? In my humble opinion, the rule is as follows. Whenever Kaddish is recited on account of verses or aggadah, full Kaddish is recited. By contrast, when the chazzan recites it to mark a separation after Pesukei DeZimrah, which ends at that point, as explained in Beit Yosef, before beginning the Amidah, which is an independent mitzvah, only half Kaddish is recited. Likewise, after Tehillah LeDavid at Minchah, although whoever recites it is assured of a portion in the World to Come, it is recited as supplication rather than as one reading Scripture, and therefore only half Kaddish is recited. After the Torah reading on Monday and Thursday, although Scripture has been read, it is nevertheless a distinct mitzvah and an enactment of Ezra, so only half Kaddish is recited, for the reason stated.
Know that half Kaddish is always recited after the Torah reading, even at Shabbos Minchah and at Minchah on a public fast, because the Kaddish recited at the lectern before the Amidah refers back to the Torah reading. It is not recited on the bimah at Minchah, as Levush writes in siman 292 regarding the Monday and Thursday fasts, so that the congregation will know when to begin the Amidah. This concern does not apply when Ashrei is recited aloud. One might ask why, on Monday and Thursday mornings, Kaddish is recited on the bimah after the Torah reading. Full Kaddish with Tiskabel refers back even to the Amidah, and certainly it could refer to the Torah reading. Levush answers in siman 292 that public Torah reading is exceptionally important, and therefore they instituted a separate Kaddish for it. On Shabbos, in any event, it also separates between the seven aliyos and Maftir. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further. See my work on customs; this is not the place to elaborate.
