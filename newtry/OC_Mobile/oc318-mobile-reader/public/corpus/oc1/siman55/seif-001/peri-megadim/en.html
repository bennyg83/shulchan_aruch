They say Kaddish after "Yishtabach." After Yishtabach they say Kaddish, and it refers to pesukei dezimra, for they say it only after written Torah or after aggadah [Magen Avraham 54:3]. And what is written "after the eighteen-blessing prayer" can be explained that there are several verses there, "Heal us, the Omnipresent," and the like. And the seven Kaddishim each day—on "seven times a day I praise You"—the Levush counts them: (1) after Yishtabach; (2) after the eighteen blessings; (3) after the kedushah section; (4) after Aleinu, which has verses; (5) after Ashrei at Minchah; (6) after the Minchah prayer; (7) after the evening Kriat Shema blessing. And the Kaddish after the evening eighteen blessings is optional. For my part, the layman, I have reflections here; and in my customs I expanded, and here I shorten, and I enumerate them with the Omnipresent's help.

Know that we have three types of Kaddish: (1) Kaddish with titkabel; (2) Kaddish without titkabel; (3) half-Kaddish. And the Levush gave a rule that titkabel is said only after the eighteen blessings; and Taz wrote also after Selichot. And here in siman 593, section 44, that they say twice titkabel—once after the eighteen blessings and once after "VeAtah kadosh"—there is no general rule, but the Levush according to his view there, that they say half-Kaddish after the eighteen blessings and after "VeAtah kadosh" Kaddish with titkavel; and likewise is Taz's view there. However, what the Levush wrote as a rule—that wherever titkavel is not said, the shaliach tzibbur does not complete the Kaddish—Taz proves that the shaliach tzibbur says after "VeAtah kadosh" Kaddish without titkavel, as the Levush himself wrote in siman 559 and Taz there.

And here, half-Kaddish requires investigation: why did they choose half-Kaddish and not Kaddish without titkavel, as they say Kaddish without titkavel after verses or aggadah? I say according to my lay opinion that the rule is: wherever they say it for the sake of verses or aggadah, they say Kaddish; but when the chazzan pauses between pesukei dezimra that are completed here [Avudraham], and Shulchan Aruch wrote that it is a mitzvah on its own; and after "Tehillah leDavid" at Minchah, for whoever says Tehillah is assured, etc., and they say it as supplication—not like one reading Torah—they say only half-Kaddish; and after keriat haTorah on Monday and Thursday, even though he reads Torah, nevertheless it is a mitzvah and an enactment of Ezra—we say only half-Kaddish. What is the reason? As we said.

Know that they always say half-Kaddish after keriat sefer Torah, even on Shabbat at Minchah and on a public fast at Minchah, for the Kaddish they say before the eighteen blessings on the bimah refers to sefer Torah. And that which they do not say on the bimah at Minchah—as the Levush wrote in siman 592 regarding a fast on Monday and Thursday, so the people know when they begin "extremely"—unlike when they say Ashrei aloud. And if you ask on Monday and Thursday morning why they say Kaddish on the bimah after sefer Torah—is not Kaddish with titkavel the subject, and it returns to the eighteen blessings, all the more so to sefer Torah? The Levush in siman 592: keriat sefer Torah in public is very significant, and they enacted a separate Kaddish for it (however on Shabbat, if not for necessity, they interrupt between the seven readers and the haftarah—and it will be explained further). And see my customs; here there is no need to expand.

What Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 2: if a majority remained — it is reasonable that we do not require a majority that is recognizable, but six is also fine. I have not found it now, and what he wrote below will be explained there regarding this.
