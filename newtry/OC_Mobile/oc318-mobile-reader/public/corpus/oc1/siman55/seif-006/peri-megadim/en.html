Half. See Taz and Peri Chadash. According to the Mechaber there, the expense is paid from the communal fund. Here, however, the poor person's departure creates the need to hire another chazzan. Therefore, even according to the Mechaber, half is assessed per capita. Even according to the view there that where there is no established custom the collection is assessed according to wealth, there they seek a chazzan with a pleasant voice, unlike here. See Eliyah Rabbah and Magen Avraham, letter 16, where, with Hashem's help, this will be explained. Regarding the rationale that it should be assessed according to wealth so that the poor will not need to leave, meaning, the primary purpose of the enactment is that they should not need to leave. Levush writes that, ordinarily, a poor person's departure is not comparable to that of a wealthy person who leaves to pursue profit. See Eliyah Rabbah.
Regarding bar mitzvah, see Magen Avraham, letter 10. A remarkable case arises from Rama's comments to Proverbs 8:16: it is possible for intercourse with one girl to incur the death penalty while intercourse with the other is exempt, even if both acts occurred on the same day. Examine it carefully there. If a girl was born in a leap year on the twenty eighth of Adar I, and her third year is also a leap year, intercourse with her on that date creates a doubt whether she thereby acquires the status of a zonah. In a second comparable case, should one say that the status of zonah is Rabbinic, the doubt is resolved leniently. See Even HaEzer, simanim 7 and 20, and examine the discussion there.
