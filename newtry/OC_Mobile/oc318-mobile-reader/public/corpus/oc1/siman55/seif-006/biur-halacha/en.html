Or he is asleep. See the Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote that initially one should at least wake him. If that too is impossible, the practical ruling requires study. For although the Peri Megadim wrote that if it is impossible, one who relies on the Shulchan Aruch has not lost, and so it also seems from the Chayei Adam, nevertheless the Birkei Yosef and the Derech HaChayim agreed with the Taz and the Peri Chadash. Perhaps in a place where Kaddish and Kedushah are recited aloud and the rest is said quietly, one may be lenient in a pressing situation regarding one who is asleep, since there is no concern there of a blessing in vain. So too it seems from the Peri Megadim, that this is more lenient.
