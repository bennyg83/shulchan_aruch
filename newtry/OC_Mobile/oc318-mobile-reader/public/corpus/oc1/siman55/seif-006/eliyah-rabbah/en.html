And one day. Meaning from the beginning of the night, as written in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 55, and so the Levush explained. Thus one whose thirteenth birthday falls on Shabbos becomes bar mitzvah from the entrance of Shabbos, and he may be counted for all matters of sanctity.
If he was born in Adar. See Levush and Magen Avraham. The rule is that if he was born in a leap year in the first Adar, and the thirteenth year is also a leap year, he becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar. But if he was born in a simple year, or in the second Adar, he becomes bar mitzvah in the second Adar. This follows the responsum of Mahari Mintz and the Levush below in siman 685.
His thirteenth year is not, etc. Olas Tamid wrote that this is surprising, for he wrote beforehand that he does not become an adult until thirteen, and an intercalated month is included; if so, why did he specify that the thirteenth year is not intercalated? Therefore it appears that he did not mean this precisely. I wrote in Eliyah Zuta that he was not precise in this at all, for the responsum of Mahari Mintz, siman 9, from which this law is derived, ruled explicitly that if one was born in a leap year in the first Adar, and in the thirteenth year the year becomes a leap year, he becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar. Although he ruled there that one becomes bar mitzvah in the second Adar, that is specifically one who was born in a simple year, or was also born in the second Adar. But one born in the first Adar, we say that the end of his law follows its beginning. I also found this below in the Levush, siman 685, except that he ruled so from his own reasoning and had not seen the responsum mentioned above. If so, everything is resolved, and the Shulchan Aruch and Levush wrote precisely, 'and the thirteenth year is not intercalated.' This is what the Levush wrote: therefore, one born in a simple year, etc.; for if he was born in a leap year he becomes bar mitzvah in the first Adar, and this is clear. Afterward the Magen Avraham was printed, and I saw that he too wrote like Olas Tamid. It is surprising that he did not see the responsum of Mahari Mintz. The Magen Avraham wrote that one who was born on the first day of Rosh Chodesh Kislev, and when he becomes bar mitzvah Cheshvan is deficient, does not become bar mitzvah until the following day.
