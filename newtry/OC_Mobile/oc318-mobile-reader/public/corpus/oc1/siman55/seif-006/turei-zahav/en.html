In Beit Yosef he wrote that Mahari Bei Rav, teacher of Beit Yosef, originated this law and derived it for me from the beginning here—that he cannot pray with them, and nevertheless he combines. I wonder at him: for at the beginning, although he is praying and cannot interrupt to answer with the congregation, nevertheless he can be silent and hear as one who answers, as written above siman 104 se'if 7—unlike one who is asleep. And Beit Yosef brought further in the name of Agur in the name of Maharil, who first began to teach that a sleeper combines with them, since according to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi in the Gemara one combines even a minor lying in a crib—and he is the more wondrous: for we do not hold like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, as explained above in Beit Yosef; and even Rabbenu Tam, who rules like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, nevertheless did not wish to act according to his own words—and shall we stand and act? And more so, it seems similar matter to similar matter: what one could say about him—that perhaps a sleeper is worse than a minor, for he has no kedushah, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 4 in the name of the Zohar that his soul ascends and he is not in the category of kedushah; the reason of death and an impure spirit rests upon him is mistaken. And further, it seems to me that even Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi agrees he does not combine when it is possible to wake him from his sleep—why combine him when he is asleep lekhatchilah? And regarding a minor, one cannot make him an adult; and regarding what is written "all that is fit for soaking," etc.—there it deals with delay if one already did so, unlike doing so lekhatchilah in a repulsive matter that one may fix. And it is written in Agur there in Beit Yosef: although Rosh wrote that ten who listen are required, nevertheless we see the world combines even though they engage in idle talk—the heart wavers, perhaps "all that is fit for soaking," etc. One must sail far on this: how does he bring proof from those fools who make a prohibition of interrupting with idle talk, whose sin is very great? In truth I say: heaven forbid to combine with such wicked people when there is no minyan besides them; kal vachomer one should not learn from this permission to sleep at all. For Shulchan Aruch already established the law in siman 125, that if there are not nine who are attentive to the words of the shaliach tzibbur at the time of repetition of the prayer, it is likely to be a blessing in vain—all the more when he is asleep one should not be lenient lekhatchilah, as explained above.
