Beit Yosef writes that his teacher, Mahari Bei Rav, originated this ruling and derived it from the first case here, where one who is praying cannot respond with the congregation yet nevertheless combines. This is surprising, because in the first case, although he is praying and cannot interrupt to respond with the congregation, he can remain silent and listen, and listening is equivalent to responding, as stated above in siman 104, seif 7. This does not apply to one who is asleep. Beit Yosef further cites Agur in the name of Maharil, who initially wished to rule that a sleeping person combines, since Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi in the Gemara holds that even a minor lying in a cradle combines. This is even more surprising, since we do not rule like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, as Beit Yosef explains above. Even Rabbeinu Tam, who rules like Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, did not wish to act in accordance with his own ruling. Shall we then arise and act upon it? Moreover, even if one compares the cases, perhaps a sleeping person is worse than a minor because he lacks sanctity, as Beit Yosef writes in siman 4 in the name of the Zohar, that his soul departs from him, he is not included in holiness because he has tasted the taste of death, and an impure spirit rests upon him. It further appears to me that even Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi would concede that a sleeping person does not combine, since he can be awakened. Why should we initially count him while asleep? This is unlike a minor, whom one cannot make into an adult. It is also unlike the principle that anything fit for mixing is treated as though mixed, because that principle addresses whether an act already performed is indispensable after the fact. Here, by contrast, one would intentionally proceed initially despite a defect that can presently be corrected. This is astonishing. Agur further writes there, as cited by Beit Yosef: “Although Rosh writes that ten who listen are required, we see that the common practice is to count people even when they engage in ordinary conversation. Nevertheless, my heart hesitates, for perhaps this is based on the principle that anything fit for mixing is treated as though mixed.” One must strongly object to this. How can he adduce proof from fools who commit a prohibition by interrupting with idle conversation, whose sin is very great? Indeed, I say that Heaven forbid one should combine with such transgressors when no minyan exists without them, and certainly no permission regarding a sleeping person may be derived from them. In sum, Shulchan Aruch has already established in siman 125 that if nine do not concentrate on the chazzan's words during the repetition, the blessings are close to being in vain. Certainly, therefore, one should not initially be lenient regarding one who is asleep. This appears correct to me.
