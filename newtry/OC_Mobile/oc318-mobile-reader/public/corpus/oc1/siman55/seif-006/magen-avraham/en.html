He joins with them. For wherever there are ten, the Shechinah rests. Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv wrote in siman 15: perhaps according to this the same would apply to three or four, if the majority remains. To me this does not seem so, for one should not be so lenient. The main proof of Maharil is from the fact that there is an opinion that a minor lying in a cradle joins, even though he cannot answer. Although we do not rule that way, that is because the Shechinah rests only upon adults, etc.; see there. If so, it is enough that what is derived from the law should be like the case from which it is derived: just as by a minor only one joins and not two, so too by one who is asleep, only one joins and not two. Maharil also wrote that although the Rosh wrote that nine must have intent for the shaliach tzibbur’s blessing, as written in siman 124, seif 4, the practice is not so, for we see that even one who is speaking and does not hear joins.
