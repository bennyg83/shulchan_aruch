(19) There — and likewise for kedushah and Barchu, etc., in the custom of orphan Kaddish and Barchu that small orphans say in the congregation and the adults answer after them — Dvar Shmuel protests on this in siman 301; and so Peri Chadash below at the beginning of siman 69 protests on this, and wrote that in some places they protested to them and they did not listen to the voice of teachers — see there; and Beit David, Yad Aharon in Hagahot Tashbetz; and see responsum Beit Yehudah Orach Chayyim siman 22, who expanded on this matter and wrote there is some support for the custom that children of six or seven years know to whom they sanctify when they say Kaddish; and he concluded: and all this is specifically for Kaddish of Barchu, which is like the one who recites Shema aloud, arranged for a few individuals who did not hear Barchu of Yotzer, as Rivash wrote in responsum siman 134, and Beit Yosef brought it siman 133; and even though we have shown some support for the custom, nevertheless it is fit to lead in the final Kaddish of Barchu not to lead saying it except an adult of thirteen who brought two hairs; but in other Kaddishim that are optional, a minor who has not reached education may say them; and seven Kaddishim are obligatory for the day, as written "seven times a day I praise You," etc. — and all these are fit for the shaliach tzibbur to say them; and our custom is that the shaliach tzibbur says them for the seven except Kaddish of Barchu — so far. And they brought Shulchan Aruch HaRav page 80 column 2; Siddur Beit Yosef in laws of Kaddish letter 48; and Rabbi Mahari Chazan in Sefer Chok LeYisrael Orach Chayyim part 1 page 78 column 4 defended the custom, since the shaliach tzibbur also says Kaddish with the minors together — we have no concern; but that a minor alone should say obligatory Kaddish without the shaliach tzibbur — they have nothing to rely on — see there; and they brought Beit Yosef there letter 49. And if so, according to what I wrote above letter 1 in the name of the Arizal z"l that there are twelve Kaddishim in the order of Shacharit, Minchah, and Maariv, and all are obligatory for the sake of ascent of worlds — all must be said by the chazzan, or an adult of thirteen years and one day who brought two hairs; but other Kaddishim that are not obligatory, a minor may say them.
