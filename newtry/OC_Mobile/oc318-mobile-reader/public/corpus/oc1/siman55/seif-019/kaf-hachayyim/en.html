Pri Chadash likewise protests it below, at the beginning of siman 69, and writes that in several places he objected but they did not heed the teachers; see there. Yad Aharon, Hagahos HaTur, cites him. See Responsa Beis Yehudah, Orach Chaim 22, which discusses this at length and writes that there is some support for the custom when minors of six or seven who know to Whom they sanctify recite Kaddish. He concludes: All this applies specifically to the Kaddish containing Barchu, which resembles repeating Barchu for those few individuals who did not hear the Barchu preceding Yotzer, as Rivash writes in Responsa 334, cited by Beis Yosef 133. Although we have provided some support for the custom, it is proper to institute that the final Kaddish containing Barchu be recited only by an adult of thirteen years who has grown two hairs. Other Kaddishim that are optional may be recited even by a minor who has not reached the age of education. Seven Kaddishim are obligatory each day, as it is written, 'Seven times a day I praise You,' etc. It is proper for the shaliach tzibbur to recite all these. Our custom is for the shaliach tzibbur to recite the seven, except the Kaddish containing Barchu. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 80b, and Siddur Beis Oved, laws of Kaddish 48, cite him. Mahari Chazan, Chikrei Lev, Orach Chaim 1, page 78d, defends the custom because the shaliach tzibbur also recites Kaddish together with the minors; in that case there is no concern. If the minor alone recites an obligatory Kaddish without the shaliach tzibbur, however, they have no authority on which to rely; see there. Beis Yaakov 49 cites him. Accordingly, given what was stated above in paragraph 1 in the Arizal's name; that there are twelve Kaddishim in the order of Shacharis, Minchah, and Maariv, all obligatory for the ascent of the worlds; they must all be recited by the chazzan or an adult of thirteen years and one day who has grown two hairs. Other, non-obligatory Kaddishim may be recited by a minor.
