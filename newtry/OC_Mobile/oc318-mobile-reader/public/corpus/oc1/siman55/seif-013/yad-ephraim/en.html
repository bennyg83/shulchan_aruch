On Shulchan Aruch se'if 13—lip. It seems his intention: from the inner lip of the thickness of the door outward is considered outside; but from there inward is considered inside—for example, if the door is in the middle of the threshold and lintel, then the thickness of the door is considered outside. For what they wondered in the Talmud about the agaf itself was the place of the thickness of the door; and on this Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rabbeinu Yerucham, that Beit Yosef explained it is considered outside. And what Rashi wrote—from the inner lip of the thickness of the opening to the place of knocking—it implies at first glance that the threshold and lintel are included in agaf; and if so, for Rabbeinu Yerucham it is considered outside—one must say Rashi explained thus because the doors opened inward and were fixed on the inner lip of the thickness of the opening, and closed into the thickness of the opening—that is, the thickness of the doors was swallowed within the thickness of the hollow of the opening, and there was the place of knocking in the middle of the thickness of the hollow. Thus the inner lip of the thickness of the opening and the inner lip of the door are one matter—and so is the intent of Eliyahu Rabbah. See there. Therefore Rashi wrote "from the agaf inward"—that is, inside the city proper: since the door is on the inner lip of the opening, necessarily inward from it is inside the city proper. And what forced Rashi to explain thus: because we say, "Why were the gates of Jerusalem not sanctified? Because of lepers," etc.—it implies that the entire thickness of the gate—that is, the entire hollow of the opening—was not sanctified, because the agaf itself—that is, the thickness of the gate where the door is—is at the end of the inner lip in the hollow. But if the door is in the middle of the threshold and lintel, there is no reason to say it should be considered as outside from the lip of the hollow of the opening, even though the door is far from there because the threshold and lintel are wide—since it is from the door inward. And what Rashi wrote, "the agaf itself, the thickness of the gate from the knocking inward"—was likewise because the thickness of the door is at the end of the thickness of the gate. I found clear proof for the words of Shulchan Aruch from Nedarim 56: one who vows from the house is forbidden from the agaf inward. And Ran wrote there on the mishnah at the beginning of 56a: from the closing of the door inward, etc.; and likewise Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch Yoreh De'ah siman 214—likewise see there. And so it is implied in the Talmud there from "the priest goes out," etc.—that what is from the door outward is called mashkof because the door leans upon it; and agaf is also the language of hugging doors; but what is on the other side of the door inward is not called agaf.
