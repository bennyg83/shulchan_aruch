In Shulchan Aruch, seif 13, “from the edge.” It appears to me that his intent is that from the inner edge of the thickness of the door outward is considered outside, whereas from that point inward is considered inside. For example, if the door is positioned in the middle of the threshold and lintel, the thickness of the door is regarded as outside. The Gemara’s uncertainty concerning the doorway itself refers to the place occupied by the thickness of the door. In this respect Shulchan Aruch rules like Rabbeinu Yerucham, cited by Beit Yosef, that it is considered outside. Rashi writes, “from the inner edge of the thickness of the entrance until the place against which the door strikes,” which might appear to imply that half the threshold and lintel are included in the doorway and, according to Rabbeinu Yerucham, are outside. It must be explained that Rashi describes doors that opened inward and were fixed at the inner edge of the entrance’s thickness. When closed, they entered into the thickness of the opening, so that the thickness of the doors was absorbed within the thickness of the entrance cavity, and the striking point was in the middle of that thickness. Consequently, the inner edge of the entrance’s thickness and the inner edge of the door were one and the same. This is also the intent of Eliyah Rabbah; see there. Rashi therefore explains “from the doorway inward” as meaning actually within the city, since the door stood at the inner edge of the entrance and everything beyond it was truly within the city. Rashi was compelled to explain this because the Gemara asks why the gates of Yerushalayim were not sanctified and answers that it was because of the metzoraim. This implies that the entire thickness of the gateway, meaning the whole cavity of the entrance, was not sanctified. This was because the doorway itself, meaning the thickness of the gateway where the door stood, was at the far inner edge of the cavity. If, however, the door is in the middle of a broad threshold and lintel, there is no reason to regard the area from the outer edge of the entrance cavity as outside merely because the door is distant from it, since that area lies inward of the door. Rashi’s statement that the doorway itself is “the thickness of the gateway from the striking point inward” likewise reflects the fact that the door’s thickness was at the far end of the gateway’s thickness. I found clear proof for the ruling of Shulchan Aruch in Nedarim 56: one who vows not to benefit from a house is forbidden from the doorway inward. The Ran explains there, at the beginning of 56a, that this means from the place where the door closes inward. Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 214, rule likewise. The Gemara there also implies this from the passage concerning the kohen going out, for the area outward of the door is called the lintel because the door strikes against it, while “doorway” likewise denotes the closing of doors. What lies on the other side of the door, inward, is not called the doorway.
