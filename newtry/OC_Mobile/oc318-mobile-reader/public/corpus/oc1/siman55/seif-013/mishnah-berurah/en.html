(48) All ten. Even if they do not see one another, since they are in one house, as the later authorities write. But if some are in this room and some are in another room, they do not join, even though the doorway between them is open, because there is no breach there, and the doorway itself is considered like a partition, so they are two houses. Even when there is no door between them, as long as they are in two domains and do not see one another, they do not join. Some are stringent even when they see one another.
(49) One. If there were nine in a house and one in a sukkah, some say he joins and some disagree. When there are nine in one place and one behind a curtain spread for modesty, they combine. This is specifically if it was spread only for modesty. But if there are tefillin or a Torah scroll there, and they spread a sheet so that it should serve as a partition for marital relations, it also constitutes a partition for joining ten, and they do not combine, as the Pri Chadash writes. The Peri Megadim tends to say that in all cases they combine.
(50) The inner edge. Meaning that the place where the door closes is considered outside, even though the entrance is now open. See the Magen Avraham, who wrote that some disagree with this and he rules like them, that this place is considered inside. See also Even HaOzer, who ruled simply that where the minority stands within that area, they join with the ten, for it is no worse than a small courtyard breached into a large one, explained in seif 16. So too it appears from the Gra’s explanation.
(51) Of the thickness of the door. See Yad Ephraim, who wrote that regarding the space of the entrance apart from the place of the door, such as where the door is fixed on the outside and from the door inward there remains space within the thickness of the entrance, according to all views it is considered inside, and one may rely on this. Although the Peri Megadim implies that in his opinion Rabbeinu Yerucham holds even there that it is considered outside, in any case many disagree with Rabbeinu Yerucham, as above in seif katan 50; see Biur Halacha.
