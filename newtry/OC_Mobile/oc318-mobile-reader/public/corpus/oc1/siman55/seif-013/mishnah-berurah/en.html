(48) All ten—even if they do not see one another, since they are in one house [later authorities]. But if some are in this room and some in another room they do not combine, even though the doorway is open between them, because there is no breach and the doorway itself is considered a partition—and it is as two houses; and even when there is no door between them, whenever they are in two domains and do not see one another they do not combine. And some are strict even when they see one another.

(49) One—if nine were in the house and one in a sukkah, some say they combine and some disagree. When there are nine in one place and one beyond the curtain spread for modesty—they combine, specifically when they spread it for modesty in general; but if there are tefillin or a sefer Torah and they spread a sheet so there will be a partition for the sun from his bed—it is also a partition for joining ten, and they do not combine [Peri Chadash]; and Peri Megadim sides that in all cases they combine.

(50) Inner lip—meaning: the place where the door closes is considered outside, even though now the doorway was open. And see in Magen Avraham, who wrote that some disagree on this and rules like them that this place is considered inside. And see in Sefer Even haEzer, who ruled plainly that where the minority stand within this place they combine for ten, for it is no worse than a small courtyard breached into a large one, explained in Taz; and likewise it appears from the Gra's explanation.

(51) Of the thickness of the door—see Yad Ephraim, who wrote that in the hollow of the doorway alone, apart from the place of the door—for example where the door is affixed on the outside and from the place of the door inward there is another hollow within the thickness of the doorway—according to all opinions it is considered inside, and one may rely on this [for though in Peri Megadim it appears that Rabbeinu Yeruham also holds in this case that it is considered outside, nevertheless many disagree with R' Yehuda, as explained above in se'if kaf nun, and see in Biur Halachah].
