(Seif katan 14) They combine, etc. Regarding standing before a Sefer Torah, one need not stand, because the Sefer Torah is in another domain. During the Torah reading, see below, at the end of siman 146.
Nevertheless, it is subordinate, etc. Peri Chadash writes: “The rulings of nine in the larger courtyard and one in the smaller courtyard, and of a chazzan in the smaller courtyard with the congregation in the larger courtyard, apply specifically when they cannot see one another. If they can see one another, they combine in every case. What the Mechaber writes in seif 19, that a chazzan in the bimah and nine in the synagogue combine because the bimah is subordinate to the synagogue, applies even where they cannot see one another. The reason that it is subordinate is therefore necessary.”
It should read: See siman 370, seif 3. This refers to the view cited by the Mechaber as “some say,” whose reasoning is that they compare it to the ruling in siman 370 regarding an eruv made by five groups who spent Shabbos in a single hall. Although partitions divided them, if the partitions did not reach the ceiling and there were three tefachim between the partitions and the ceiling, so that the rule of lavud did not apply, all five groups are regarded as one and contribute only a single eruv. See there.
