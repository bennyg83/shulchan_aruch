(1) [Seif 1] The reader may not raise his voice, etc. — we learn in the first chapter of HaKoreh: in Torah one reads and one translates, provided there are not one reading and two translating; in Prophets one reads and two translate, provided there are not two reading and two translating; further we learned there: one may not read to the translator more than one verse, and in Prophets three; and if the three were three portions they read one by one; Ran wrote that the same applies to three divided matters; further we learn in Yerushalmi chapter HaKoreh: R. Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak entered the synagogue, saw a certain man standing translating leaning on the platform, said to him: forbidden to you, as it was given in awe and fear, so we must conduct with it in awe and fear; further we learn there: he should not translate from writing, for matters said orally must be oral; they did not practice thus, as they permitted writing Torah orally, as we say in chapter HaNizakin that R. Yose and R. Shimon ben Lakish looked in a book of aggadah on Shabbat due to "a time to act for the the Master — they violated Your Torah," Peri Chadash there.
