1) [Seif 1] The reader is not permitted [to read to the translator more than one verse], etc. We learn at the beginning of the chapter 'HaKorei omed': in the Torah, one reads and one translates, provided that not one reads and two translate; and in the Prophets, one reads and two translate, provided that not two read and two translate. Further we learned there: one does not read to the translator more than one verse [at a time], and in the Prophets, three; and if the three were three [separate] parshiyos, they read them one by one. And the Ran, of blessed memory, wrote that the same applies to three different matters. Further we learn in the Yerushalmi, chapter 'HaKorei omed': Rabbi Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak entered a synagogue, saw one person standing and translating leaning against a pillar, [and] said to him: it is forbidden to you; just as it was given with awe and fear, so must we conduct ourselves with it with awe and fear. Further we learn there that one should not translate in writing, for matters that were said orally [must remain] oral; but they did not practice so, just as they practiced [the] leniency in writing down the Oral Torah, as we say in the chapter 'HaNizakin,' that Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish would study the sefer of Aggadah on Shabbos, on account of 'it is a time to act for Hashem, they have voided Your Torah.' Pri Chadash there.
2) There: And the reader is not permitted to raise his voice, etc. For it is said, 'Moshe would speak and God would answer him with a voice' -- with the voice of Moshe. Berachos, folio 45a; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. And if it is impossible for the translator to raise his voice to [match] the reader, the reader should soften his voice and read. Pri Chadash, ot 1.
3) The Rambam wrote, chapter 12 of Hilchos Tefillah: and not all the passages are translated in public. The incident of Reuven, and the Priestly Blessing, and the incident of the [Golden] Calf from 'and Moshe said to Aharon' until 'and Moshe saw the people,' etc., and further another verse, 'and Hashem struck the people,' are read but not translated. And in the incident of Amnon, in the place where it says 'Amnon son of David,' it is not read and not translated. Until here. And all this is a Baraisa and Gemara at the end of the chapter 'HaKorei omed'; and the verse of 'and [Hashem] struck' is taught in the Tosefta. And since the Baraisa set out to teach all of them, and yet the incident of Bas Sheva it does not teach, therefore that which it teaches in the Mishnah 'the incident of David and Amnon is not read and not translated' means the incident of Amnon in which David is mentioned; but the incident of Bas Sheva is read and translated, either because it is agreeable to him [David] that he have atonement, or because it is his praise, for he confessed. Pri Chadash there.
