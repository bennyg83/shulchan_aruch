1) [Seif 1] The reader is not permitted [to read to the translator more than one verse], etc. We learn at the beginning of the chapter 'HaKorei omed': in the Torah, one reads and one translates, provided that not one reads and two translate; and in the Prophets, one reads and two translate, provided that not two read and two translate. Further we learned there: one does not read to the translator more than one verse [at a time], and in the Prophets, three; and if the three were three [separate] parshiyos, they read them one by one. And the Ran, of blessed memory, wrote that the same applies to three different matters. Further we learn in the Yerushalmi, chapter 'HaKorei omed': Rabbi Shmuel bar Rav Yitzchak entered a synagogue, saw one person standing and translating leaning against a pillar, [and] said to him: it is forbidden to you; just as it was given with awe and fear, so must we conduct ourselves with it with awe and fear. Further we learn there that one should not translate in writing, for matters that were said orally [must remain] oral; but they did not practice so, just as they practiced [the] leniency in writing down the Oral Torah, as we say in the chapter 'HaNizakin,' that Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish would study the sefer of Aggadah on Shabbos, on account of 'it is a time to act for Hashem, they have voided Your Torah.' Pri Chadash there.
