Seif 1. It is a mitzvah to eat on Erev Yom Kippur and to increase the meal on it. Marginal note: In the year 5586 I was asked by one scholar whether women are also obligated in the positive command to eat on Erev Yom Kippur, or not, since it is a time-bound positive command. I answered: at first glance, from the second chapter of Sukkah, where the Gemara derives the affliction of Yom Kippur from the word ha-ezrach, and asks, why do I need a verse, for it follows from Rabbi Yehudah said Rav? It answers that this is for the addition to Yom Kippur, where there is no punishment and warning; I might have thought women are exempt, therefore ha-ezrach teaches otherwise. According to this, regarding eating on Erev Yom Kippur, where there is no punishment and warning, and ha-ezrach is not written concerning it, they would be exempt. But it appears that they are obligated. Just as women are obligated in eating matzah because eating matzah is compared to eating chametz, to teach that whoever is included in the prohibition not to eat chametz is included in the positive command to eat matzah, etc., so too here, since it is written, And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month in the evening; from evening to evening you shall rest your rest day, eating on Erev Yom Kippur is compared to Yom Kippur itself. Therefore whoever is included in from evening to evening you shall rest your rest day is also included in And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month. These women, since they are obligated on Yom Kippur, as is explicit in the second chapter of Sukkah, are therefore also obligated in eating on Erev Yom Kippur, for the two are compared, and we expound a hekesh on our own. In this way one can explain the wording of Chiya bar Rav, who said: Whoever eats and drinks on the ninth, Scripture credits him as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth. Everyone asked that the word tenth is extra; what relevance does the tenth have here? It should have said only: whoever eats and drinks on the ninth, Scripture credits him as though he fasted on that very day, or as though he fasted on the ninth. But according to what I wrote, it is well: he comes to teach that even women are obligated in this mitzvah. That is, whoever eats and drinks on the ninth, Scripture credits him as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth, implying that if he did not eat on the ninth, then the fast of the tenth also is not a fast. The fast of the tenth is considered a fast only when he also eats on the ninth; but if he does not eat on the ninth, the fast of the tenth is not considered a fast at all. Therefore these women, since they are obligated to fast on the tenth, are obligated to eat on the ninth as well, for otherwise even the tenth would not be considered a fast for them. He learned this from the hekesh. Or, even without the hekesh, one may say that this is proven from the body of the verse: it says, And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month. If you afflict yourselves on the ninth, then from evening to evening you shall rest your rest day, meaning the fast of the tenth will be considered a fast for you. But if you do not afflict yourselves on the ninth, the tenth will not be considered a fast for you. Therefore, automatically, women who are obligated on the tenth are also obligated on the ninth. This is clear and correct in my opinion. With this there was room to resolve further what the commentators asked: why did the Torah express this mitzvah in the language of affliction and not write that it is a mitzvah to eat? According to the above, one may say that the Pnei Yehoshua wrote at the beginning of the fourth chapter of Beitzah that the rule that women are exempt from time-bound positive commands applies only to something involving an action, but where it is only sitting and not acting, women are obligated even though it is a time-bound positive command; see there. According to this, all is well: if the Torah had expressed it in language of action, that it is a mitzvah to eat, women would be exempt from it, for it would be a time-bound positive command. But since it wrote that it is as though he fasted, it is only like a positive command through sitting and not acting; therefore women are also obligated in it. With this one can resolve the very words of the Pnei Yehoshua there, regarding what the Tzelach challenged him from the second chapter of Sukkah, which derives the addition from ha-ezrach, and also from Tosafos in Kiddushin 34, that women are exempt from positive commands of Yom Tov even though they are through sitting and not acting; see there in Tzelach, and see also in my work Bigdei Yom Tov on tractate Beitzah what I wrote there about this. Now it appears that it is well, because this teaching of Chiya bar Rav is subject to dispute, and the authority who derives from this verse the addition to Yom Kippur does not accept the derashah of Chiya bar Rav. Therefore it works well: according to Chiya bar Rav, it was necessary to state the Pnei Yehoshua's reasoning, for otherwise the question remains why the Torah expressed eating in the language of affliction. It must be because of the reasoning of the Pnei Yehoshua above. And in truth, he does not accept the derashah of ha-ezrach; rather, from reasoning we know that women are obligated in the addition to Yom Kippur, and he does not expound the verse ha-ezrach at all. The addition to Yom Kippur itself he learns from the repetition. But the Gemara in Sukkah and the beraisa that learn from ha-ezrach do not hold like Chiya bar Rav, but expound And you shall afflict regarding the addition to Yom Kippur. Therefore they hold that even where there is no action, women are exempt, and a verse of ha-ezrach is needed. If so, Tosafos in Kiddushin raised their question according to that authority who derives women's obligation in the addition to Yom Kippur from ha-ezrach; according to them, women should be permitted to kindle with oil of terumah that became tamei on Yom Tov. We have not found anywhere a dispute that according to one authority women would be permitted to kindle with such oil on Yom Tov. Therefore they asked well according to the Gemara in Sukkah. But the Pnei Yehoshua wrote this according to our ruling, for we rule like Chiya bar Rav; therefore the Pnei Yehoshua correctly wrote that it is forbidden by reasoning. Examine this carefully. Know that the difficulty of Tosafos in any case is only from the fact that we have not found a tanna who says so, as Tosafos explicitly write there in their language; otherwise there would be no proof, for perhaps women are indeed permitted. Therefore they had to ask from the fact that no tanna is found who says so; see there. If so, it works well: they asked that at least according to the one who derives the addition to Yom Kippur for women from ha-ezrach, and derives from And you shall afflict the addition to Yom Kippur, according to him women should be permitted to kindle with oil of terumah that became tamei on Yom Tov, and we have not found a tanna who says so. But the Pnei Yehoshua wrote according to our view, for we rule like Chiya bar Rav. Consequently, since the Torah did not express it in language of eating in order to obligate women, it is proven that he does not expound ha-ezrach, but holds by reasoning that where it is through sitting and not acting women are obligated. This is well; examine it carefully. Although it is far-fetched to load this into the intent of the Pnei Yehoshua, nevertheless it is proper and correct to vindicate the righteous one at the outset, for the spirit of Hashem spoke through him, and, Heaven forbid, no uncorrected matter emerged from under his hand. Now, regarding what is stated in Shulchan Aruch, that it is a mitzvah to eat on Erev Yom Kippur and to increase the meal on it, see Taz, s.k. 1, that the Tur brought the Gemara, etc. Everyone asked why the Torah did not explicitly write to eat on Erev Yom Kippur. In my opinion it appears that this is well, for the answer is alluded to in the answer of the tailor brought by the Tur and Taz, who answered that we are confident in Him, may He be blessed, etc. The meaning is that the Holy One, blessed is He, wanted to give Israel merit in judgment. The merit is that through the confidence with which they trust in Him, may He be blessed, that He will vindicate them, and they rejoice over this, this confidence and faith are sufficient to vindicate them, as I wrote several times in derashos for Rosh Hashanah and above in siman 581, that because of this confidence that we display, we merit judgment. Therefore, with Rosh Hashanah, where it is possible to make rejoicing on Rosh Hashanah itself, we do so on Rosh Hashanah: we drink, eat, and rejoice. But on Yom Kippur, when these things are forbidden, the Holy One, blessed is He, commanded us to display this confidence on Erev Yom Kippur, that we rejoice then to show our confidence in Him, may He be blessed, and through this power we merit judgment. Therefore, how could the Holy One, blessed is He, have written to eat on Erev Yom Kippur? If so, we would be compelled to eat by force of the Torah's command, and how would our confidence be recognizable? Then no benefit would grow for us from the eating. Therefore the Torah did not write the mitzvah of eating, and similarly regarding Rosh Hashanah it did not write so for this reason: that we ourselves should be awakened to this, to eat and drink and show that we trust in Him, may He be blessed. Scripture therefore wrote that if we do so on our own, then as reward for this confidence He credits us as though we fasted on the ninth and the tenth. But to write the eating itself was impossible, for then there would no longer be confidence and joy, and the reason for the eating would be nullified. Therefore the eating of Rosh Hashanah and Erev Yom Kippur have one reason, and this is good and correct; examine it carefully. Now, the Rav Beis Yosef wrote in siman 570 that eating on Erev Yom Kippur is only rabbinic and the verse is a mere asmachta, and the Magen Avraham wondered about him there, for there it is proven the opposite. See siman 570, and see in my earlier work on Orach Chayim what I wrote on the sugya in Rosh Hashanah, folio 9, and what I wrote about this in the derashah for Erev Yom Kippur in the year 5603; see there and examine it carefully. The source of this law is in tractate Yoma: Rabbi Chiya of Difti said, And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month, etc.; rather, whoever eats and drinks on the ninth is as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth. To understand the reason for the matter, with Hashem's help, one may say according to the words of the Rav Bina LaItim on the verse, Fools, because of the way of their transgression, and because of their iniquities, are afflicted; all food their soul abhors. The meaning is that fasting applies to one who had a desire to eat but fasts. But one who has no desire at all to eat is not called fasting. Therefore he said that they are fools who say they will fast because of their sins; in truth, it would be fitting that they not desire to eat at all, and that all food their soul abhor because they have reached the gates of death. See there in his pleasant language; his words are fine. Let us consider a person who has a harsh judgment before earthly judges. If for one moment his case were forgotten from him and he ate and drank, and afterward he remembered the harsh judgment against him, he would be very distressed that he ate and drank beforehand. Since a harsh judgment is upon him, how could eating and drinking be pleasant to his mouth? He would have distress over the past, asking why he ate and drank. So too here: on Yom Kippur, a person eats and drinks on the ninth and the fear of Yom Kippur is not before his eyes. But when the time of Kol Nidrei arrives, the fear of Yom Kippur falls upon him. Automatically he has distress over why he ate and drank beforehand, since he faces a great and awesome judgment. Therefore through this it is as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth. This is what the verse says: And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month, meaning that even the ninth of the month, on which you ate, will be considered affliction for you on the evening of the tenth, when the fear of Yom Kippur falls upon him. But from evening to evening, on Yom Kippur when you fast, you shall rest your rest day, meaning that there will be delight for you in not eating on it; this will be your rest. One may allude to this from what is written in Yoel, chapter 2: You shall eat, eating and being satisfied, and you shall praise the Name of Hashem your God, Who has dealt wondrously with you, and My people shall never be ashamed. It appears that this alludes to eating on Erev Yom Kippur. Now, when we eat on Erev Yom Kippur, when Yom Kippur arrives we are ashamed: why did we eat, since the great Day of Judgment is upon us? But the prophet said, You shall eat, eating and being satisfied, and you shall praise the Name of Hashem your God, Who has dealt wondrously with you, for it is a wonder that the eating will be considered like affliction; and My people shall never be ashamed, for even after the eating you will not be ashamed of having eaten beforehand. This is good; examine it carefully.
