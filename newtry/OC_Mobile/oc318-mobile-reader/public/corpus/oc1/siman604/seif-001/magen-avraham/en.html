One should reduce his learning on Erev Yom Kippur in order to eat and drink; Berachos, folio 8. And it is forbidden to fast. In Maharil it is stated that when Mar son of Ravina sat the whole year in fasting except for Erev Yom Kippur, Purim, and Shavuos, it means that throughout the year, whenever he had a bad dream, he would fast, except on these days. So the Beis Yosef wrote in siman 288 in the name of the Rashba. If he wants to fast until the concluding meal, it is permitted. End of his words. So too he wrote in Hilchos Taanis. This requires analysis: why did the Rema write here that it is forbidden to fast a fast for a dream, while regarding Purim he wrote that it is permitted to fast a fast for a dream, and regarding Shavuos he did not mention this at all? Perhaps one can say that since some say Mar son of Ravina was speaking about other fasts, then for a fast for a dream one should fast; therefore he ruled regarding Purim and Shavuos that one should fast. But on Erev Yom Kippur, since the eating is considered affliction, one should not fast. So the Shelah wrote. Nevertheless this requires analysis, for on Yom Tov it is forbidden by Torah law to fast; if so, because of doubt one should be stringent not to fast a fast for a dream on Shavuos. And if he holds that Mar son of Ravina was not referring to a fast for a dream, then on Erev Yom Kippur too one should fast. Therefore it appears to me that one should follow Maharil, for certainly if he fasts until the concluding meal there is no prohibition. Even according to the one who says that this is D'Oraisa, it is not comparable to Shabbos, for there it is written, You shall call Shabbos a delight, and it is forbidden to fast on it at all. Here, by contrast, the mitzvah is to eat, and if he ate once during the day that is enough. He need not observe a fast for his fast. What is written in siman 568, seif 5, refers to where he fasted the entire day, as written in Hagahos Maimoniyos, where Maharam ruled to fast two consecutive days; so too in Sefer Chasidim, siman 220. However, one who is not afraid need not fast at all, as written at the end of siman 220. The Shelah wrote: Those accustomed not to eat meat except on days when Tachanun is not said, or in places where they do not increase Selichos in the dawn watch, then the night too is Yom Tov and it is permitted to eat meat at night. In places where they increase Selichos in the dawn watch, they do not treat the night as Yom Tov and it is forbidden to eat meat. End quote. Nevertheless this requires analysis, for whenever the night is treated as Yom Tov, Tachanun is not recited at the Minchah before it, while here Tachanun is recited. This proves that the night is not treated as Yom Tov. Therefore it appears to me that only one who eats at actual night may eat meat, but if he eats before tzeis hakochavim he should not eat meat even though he prayed Maariv. So it appears to me. One who vowed not to eat meat except on Yom Tov, see siman 570, end of seif 1, where it is permitted on Erev Yom Kippur. Nevertheless it appears to me that at night it is forbidden, since he vowed explicitly. See what I wrote at the end of siman 608.
