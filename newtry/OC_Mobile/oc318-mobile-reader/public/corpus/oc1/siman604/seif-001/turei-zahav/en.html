It is a mitzvah to eat, etc. The Tur brings: Rabbi Chiya of Difti taught, And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month. Does one fast on the ninth? Is it not on the tenth that we fast? Rather, this teaches you that whoever eats and drinks on the ninth, Scripture credits him as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth. This is what the verse says: prepare yourselves on the ninth for tomorrow's affliction. Since the Merciful One expressed eating in the language of affliction, it is therefore considered like affliction, as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth, meaning as though they had been commanded to fast on both. End quote. Tosafos explained: Although one might say that on the ninth in the evening he should begin to fast, just as it says, In the evening you shall eat matzos, there it is different, because it writes at the end, you shall eat; whereas here it writes, And you shall afflict, before the word evening, implying that immediately on the ninth there is affliction. This is difficult to me: why does it ask, Is it not on the tenth that we fast? The wording implies that it asks from common practice, not from the implication of the verse; otherwise it should have said, But does the verse not say so? If so, what is the question? Perhaps in truth people do improperly by not fasting on the ninth but only on the tenth. Further, what difficulty is there from the fast of the tenth? Is it impossible to fast on both? One can say that this is its meaning: it would have been possible to say that the fast should also be on the ninth. But there is a difficulty, for the fast must be from evening to evening and no more, as written in the verse. Since the beginning of the fast would be from the day of the ninth, and on the tenth one must certainly fast, as it is written, But on the tenth of the month you shall afflict, etc., it would be more than from evening to evening, and the verse wrote only from evening to evening. This settles well the wording, Does one fast on the ninth? meaning, how is it possible to fast on the ninth and begin fasting from the start of the day of the ninth, when on the tenth we fast by force of the verse? If so, you are fasting more than from evening to evening, and the verse did not write this. It answers: indeed, there is a fast on the ninth, but this fast is not like other fasts, for this fast is through what one eats and drinks, and it is considered a fast. Yet it remains difficult: why did it not say explicitly, And you shall eat on the ninth? One must say that then the reward would be for the mitzvah of eating, and would not be as great as the reward of one who fasts. According to this it is difficult, for one who is not commanded and acts does not have the reward of one who is commanded and acts; if so, the reward for the mitzvah of eating, had they been commanded in it, would be greater than the reward for a fast in which he was not commanded. To this it answered, as though he fasted on the ninth and the tenth, for the fast of the tenth he was commanded in, and the eating of the ninth is considered important and subordinate to the command of the fast of the tenth. Thus the command of the fast of the tenth applies to both. So it appears to me, and this is very correct. In the incident of the tailor that the Tur brings, this is his language: He said to him, Why did you buy a fish worth one gold coin for five gold coins, and furthermore, you took it from my servant whom I sent to buy it for me? He answered him, How could I not buy it even for ten gold coins in order to eat it on this day, on which the Holy One, blessed is He, commanded us to eat and drink, and on which we are confident that the Holy One, blessed is He, will atone for our sins? He said to him, If so, you acted well, etc. It is difficult: what did he answer concerning having taken it from his servant? The Maharshal answered that the incident was that the seller demanded five gold coins or more for it, and it was not worth the price he demanded. Therefore he answered him: your servant would not have bought it so expensively, at the price the seller demanded, and I did not buy it out of your servant's hand, for even if no other buyer had been there except me alone, and he had demanded ten gold coins, I would not have left it because of the command of the Omnipresent, blessed is He, and the honor of the day. If so, this answer is sufficient. In my humble opinion there is another precision: he lengthened and said, and we are confident, etc., which seems unnecessary for this. Rather, since he had posed two questions, he had to give two answers. To the first he said that even for ten gold coins he would not leave it, because of the command of the Omnipresent; consequently it is worth that much to him. To the second, when he said, Why did you put yourself in danger by taking it from my servant, and did the Holy One, blessed is He, command you to do such a thing? he answered: certainly this is not included in the command, but I did so by reasoning, since we have confidence in Him, may He be blessed, that He will atone for our sins; included in that confidence is that you will not be angry with me when you hear my words, since our deeds are pleasing before Him, may He be blessed. And so it was. Therefore he said, and we are confident, meaning that even if there had been no command, nevertheless on our own we are confident, etc. Similar to this we explained in the aggadah, Wait for me until I appease you with words, as I wrote above in siman 66. And it is written in the Levush that nevertheless one should be careful to eat foods that are light and easy to digest, such as fowl and fish, so that he not be full and arrogant.
