In Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. And if he holds that Mar son of Ravina was not referring to a fast for a dream, if so, etc. It appears that he holds one cannot rely on this rationale, that on Erev Yom Kippur eating is considered affliction, unless we say that there is a doubt: perhaps Mar son of Ravina was referring to a fast for a dream. In that case one can well say that even if you say he refers to other fasts, nevertheless one can also say the rationale that the eating is considered affliction. But if we say that it was clear to him that he was not referring to a fast for a dream, and for this reason he ruled regarding Shavuos that one fasts a fast for a dream, then on Erev Yom Kippur as well one should not rely on this rationale alone. Still, this requires analysis: from where did the Magen Avraham know this? Perhaps although Maharil approached it from the reason that he holds it refers to a fast for a dream and the Rema does not hold so, nevertheless the reason that eating is considered affliction was clear to him. In my humble opinion, the intent of the Magen Avraham is that he holds it is not reasonable to say that although the verse considers the eating of Erev Yom Kippur as though he fasted, this does not include the statement that a fast is beneficial for a dream, that this eating should have the power to tear up the decree as though he fasted; for in a fast for a dream, the fast is for distress, not for pleasure. Rather, what the Magen Avraham means by writing, unlike on Erev Yom Kippur, etc., is directed to what he wanted to say, that every doubt concerning a fast is treated stringently. Therefore, since we are in doubt that perhaps it refers to other fasts, one should be concerned stringently and fast even on Purim and Shavuos. But on Erev Yom Kippur one cannot say this, to be stringent that he must fast; that is not so, for it is a stringency that leads to a leniency, since through it the fast that the Torah stated regarding Erev Yom Kippur, namely eating that is considered affliction, is nullified. What did you see to go stringently regarding the doubt of a fast for a dream? Go stringently regarding the doubt of Erev Yom Kippur. About this the Magen Avraham says: nevertheless this requires analysis, since on Yom Tov they are forbidden. Certainly, if it were a doubt, there would be no room to say that a doubtful fast should be treated stringently; on the contrary, the stringency is the reverse. It must be that he holds like the poskim that it does not refer to a fast for a dream. If so, there is no basis to prefer Erev Yom Kippur over Erev Yom Tov and Shabbos, as written above, because the affliction of eating does not count for the fast that is beneficial for a dream. As for what he wrote, certainly if he fasts, etc., this requires analysis, for in Magen Avraham, siman 570, s.k. 9, it states that if Rosh Chodesh were D'Oraisa it would be forbidden to fast on it even for hours, as is proven in Eruvin; see there. Perhaps one can distinguish between Yom Tov, where fasting on it is forbidden by Torah law, so even a fast for hours should be forbidden, and similarly Rosh Chodesh if it were D'Oraisa, whereas Erev Yom Kippur does not have the law of Yom Tov to forbid fasting; rather, it has a mitzvah of eating, and if one eats once at the end of the day that is enough. This is the Magen Avraham's intent in making this distinction. What the Magen Avraham wrote, And so wrote the Shelah, means that there he wrote this rationale, that the eating of the day is considered a fast; unlike if we say there is a doubtful fast, there would be room to be stringent about this doubtful eating, since it is considered a fast.
