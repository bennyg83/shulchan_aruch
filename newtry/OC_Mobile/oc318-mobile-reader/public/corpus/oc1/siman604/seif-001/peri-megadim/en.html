It is a mitzvah. See Turei Zahav, Berachos 1b, Tosafos s.v. Ke'ilu, and Vayikra 23, parashas Emor: But on the tenth of the seventh month, and you shall afflict your souls. Another verse there says: And you shall afflict your souls on the ninth of the month in the evening; from evening to evening you shall rest your rest day. See Yoma, chapter 1. For us, the addition to Yom Kippur is derived from another verse; see siman 608. The verse that writes, And you shall afflict, on the ninth of the month, means that it is as though he also fasted on the ninth. Since there is greater reward, according to the principle that reward is according to distress, when one is commanded to fast than when one is commanded to eat, the verse therefore expressed eating as though he fasted. See Bach. Still, the reward is greater for one who is commanded and acts, and the command here is eating, not fasting; on the contrary, it is forbidden to fast on the ninth. It should have written, You shall eat on the ninth, and there would be greater reward. The answer is that the command of the fast of the tenth pertains to the ninth, to eat on the ninth so that he can fast on the tenth. If so, on the ninth he is commanded and acts; understand this. It is implied from here that there is a positive Torah command to eat on the ninth, or at least a mitzvah in general, though not a formal positive command. See Pri Chadash, and a parallel in Tosefes Shabbos, in Kesef Mishneh, chapter 3 of Hilchos Nedarim, halachah 9, who wondered that it is an asmachta; and it is written that an asmachta does not need reinforcement, see there. See what I wrote in the general introduction regarding asmachta, and see simanim 418 and 570; with Hashem's help it will be explained there. And see Yoreh Deah 216. If so, there is uncertainty on Erev Yom Kippur whether it is actually from the Torah or not; and see siman 677 regarding pushing aside a rabbinic mitzvah before a Torah command. Understand this.
The incident. Of the tailor; see Beis Yosef. According to their understanding there, this is the explanation: the term shoter sometimes refers to a servant and sometimes to an officer; see Parashas Shoftim regarding judges and officers, in Rashi, Ibn Ezra, and Re'em, of blessed memory. According to one explanation, a shoter is not a minister but only an appointee to search out thieves, gluttons, and drunkards. This resolves three questions. First, why did he ask, What is your trade? Second, why did he give five gold coins for one gold coin's worth, and a person may not squander. Third, how was he appeased regarding the fact that he took it from the shoter's servant? The answer is that the shoter was not a minister, etc. He asked: How did you give five for one? Are you a glutton and drunkard, or a thief? And he wanted to seize him. He answered: I am a tailor, not a thief, and I may spend my own money. Then he asked: True, so it is; nevertheless the matter is astonishing, for one who benefits from the labor of his hands does not squander so much like a merchant, though this point would still have been acceptable. And furthermore, you took it from my servant, meaning that the seller had already agreed to sell it to the servant for one gold coin, and you added until five and raised its price. You thereby raise the market price in this matter, and I as shoter ought to seize you for this. This is unlike where he squandered in that way and paid what the seller asked; then it is not called raising the market price if the seller is foolish, etc. It emerges that he did only one wrong. Therefore the Beis Yosef made the meaning there depend on the words and furthermore. The tailor answered: Granted that on Erev Shabbos, Yom Tov, and the like there is a mitzvah to eat, nevertheless I raise the market price only once a year, specifically on Erev Yom Kippur, when the Holy One, blessed is He, commanded us to eat and drink and we are confident that He will atone for our sins. This is unlike Yom Tov and Shabbos, where eating is a mitzvah; here it is as though he fasted on the ninth, eating so that we can fast on the tenth and sins will be atoned. Once a year is not called raising the market price, and all is well. In Sefer HaMaggid, by way of allusion: a tailor sews garments. The Levush, of blessed memory, wrote in the introduction that he sews so that the soul should not enter in shame, without clothing; there is a greater canopy in ascent because there are more documents of debt that obligate one. Understand this. The tailor knows the measure of shame, for were it not for shame all of man's toil would be for his mouth, and they would be naked and not ashamed; this is the livelihood of a tailor. If so regarding the garments of the body, all the more so regarding the garments of the soul, to remove soiled garments, and through repentance to acquire merits. Therefore he said this to him. A second answer: the shoter was the city officer, and there were two wrongs. First, something worth one gold coin was bought for five, and it was proper to fine him because he cheapened money greatly beyond measure. Second, he also bought it from the officer's servant. He answered: To me it is worth a thousand, and on such a day I bought it cheaply. Also, I did not rebel against the officer, but acted because of a positive command and the honor of the day; you have no reason to be angry. See there. The Bach, of blessed memory, in the name of Maharshal, of blessed memory, wrote that the incident was not that the servant offered one gold coin and the seller agreed; rather, the seller demanded five gold coins, the servant offered one gold coin, and the seller did not agree. Immediately the tailor said, Five gold coins. The tailor answered: Even if your servant had not wanted to buy it and I had not feared another buyer, nevertheless I would immediately have given even ten gold coins. This answer suffices regarding the spending, for to me it is worth much, and he did not take it from the servant. The Turei Zahav, of blessed memory, answered that there are two answers to two questions. Regarding his spending more, to me it is worth much because of the command of Hashem; see siman 656 regarding one who spends for a mitzvah. And even though we were not commanded to eat on this day, meaning as above, And you shall afflict on the ninth, as though he is not commanded and acts, nevertheless since this eating is pleasing before the Holy One, blessed is He, we are confident that the officer too will not be angry. See siman 66, os 1; and one may say this in another way.
