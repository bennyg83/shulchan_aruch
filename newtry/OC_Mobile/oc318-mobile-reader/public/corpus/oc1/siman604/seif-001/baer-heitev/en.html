To eat. One should reduce his learning on Erev Yom Kippur in order to eat and drink. Berachos, folio 8; and see Yad Aharon.
A dream. The Magen Avraham concluded that if one fasts until the concluding meal there is no prohibition; see there. The Shelah forbids fasting at all; see Magen Avraham. If one erred and fasted the entire day, he must observe a fast for his fast, as written in siman 568, seif 5. The Taz wrote that he need not observe a fast for his fast after Yom Kippur; see siman 288. Rabbi Beis Yaakov wrote that he must observe a fast for his fast after Yom Kippur; see there, and see siman 288, what is written there, and see Yad Aharon. The Shelah wrote: Those who are accustomed not to eat meat except on days when Tachanun is not said, then in places where they do not increase Selichos in the dawn watch, the night also has the status of Yom Tov, and one may eat meat at night. But in places where they increase Selichos in the dawn watch, the night is not treated as Yom Tov, and it is forbidden to eat meat. End quote. The Magen Avraham wrote: Nevertheless this requires analysis, for whenever the night is treated as Yom Tov, Tachanun is not recited at the Minchah before it, whereas here Tachanun is recited. This shows that the night is not treated as Yom Tov. Therefore it appears to me that this applies specifically to one who eats at actual night; but if he ate before tzeis hakochavim, he should not eat meat even though he already prayed Maariv; see there. And one who vowed not to eat meat except on Yom Tov, see siman 570, end of seif 1, that it is permitted on Erev Yom Kippur. Nevertheless it appears to me that at night it is forbidden, since he vowed explicitly. Magen Avraham.
