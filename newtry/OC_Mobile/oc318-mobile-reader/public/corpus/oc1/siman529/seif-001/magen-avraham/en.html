From the Rambam it appears that they give the derasha after the morning meal. But it seems to me that they should give the derasha before the meal, as we find in the incident of Rabbi Eliezer; and also Rava would not appoint an Amora over him from one Yom Tov to the next because of drunkenness. Therefore it is better to give the derasha in the morning, and in any case one should not draw it out past midday (Yam Shel Shlomo). And in Chullin, chapter 1, siman 50, he wrote that what the chazzanim extend with melodies is not [fulfilling] "half for Hashem and half for you," end quote. But it seems to me that nowadays, when they do not expound laws within the derasha, it is better to give the derasha after the meal because of the joy of Yom Tov; and so wrote the Bach, and such is the custom; see siman 290. And it is forbidden to eat. And the same applies on the first day of Yom Tov, because it is erev of the second day of Yom Tov.
However, if erev Yom Tov, etc. This is from the words of the Maharil, and he goes according to his own view, for he holds that it is forbidden to eat on erev Shabbos and erev Yom Tov from Mincha Gedolah onward, and for the third meal it is forbidden to eat before Mincha Gedolah; therefore he wrote that one may eat after Mincha. But we hold that it is permitted to eat until Mincha Ketanah, as is written in siman 249; accordingly one should make the third meal before Mincha Ketanah; see siman 291. And if he forgot to make it beforehand, he should make it afterward. And it is obvious that a bris meal is permitted, as is written in siman 249. And see siman 251, that it is forbidden to do melacha after Mincha. One is obligated to eat bread on Yom Tov at night and in the morning; and so it appears at the end of siman 188. And it seems to me that if he did not eat on the night of Yom Tov, he should eat twice during the day, as is written in siman 298 at the end of se'if 1.
Upon the wine. And it is a mitzvah to eat meat (Rambam; Semak; Bach; Yam Shel Shlomo). And it is stated in Abudraham, folio 5, that they enacted to increase the meal on the first day of Yom Tov of Pesach, and on Atzeres, and on Rosh Hashana, and on the last day of Yom Tov of Sukkos; see there in Tosafos for the reason.
And Yom Tov clothing, etc. Because one is obligated in simchah; and for this reason they customarily increase types of foods more than on Shabbos.
And they did not customarily make. And so it appears in Sukkah, chapter 2, that one is obligated to eat only two meals; and so wrote the Tosafos Yom Tov in chapter 5 of Maaser Sheni, mishnah 10. But in Seder HaYom he wrote that it is good to eat fruit at a third meal; and the Kol Bo wrote that from this the custom spread to eat one extra cooked dish on Yom Tov, to count in place of a third meal, even though one does not interrupt with Birkas HaMazon (Rabbeinu Yerucham; Yam Shel Shlomo). Nevertheless, when Yom Tov falls on Shabbos one should not rely on this.
They come late. Except for Rosh Hashana, when they rise early to the synagogue; and Bach in siman 584 and in siman 644 wrote that nowadays, when they extend with piyutim, they rise early on every Yom Tov, end quote. That is to say: Rashi explained the reason they come late as so that one can toil over the Yom Tov meal before going to the synagogue; and nowadays they can prepare while they are extending with melodies and piyutim.
And they hurry to leave. And one should rebuke the chazzanim who extend past midday, namely eighteen hours from the beginning of the night, as on Shabbos (Yam Shel Shlomo). And according to what is written in siman 157, it appears that we reckon seasonal hours, namely half the day; and that is the main ruling.
