And he should not stint in the outlay for Yom Tov. But on the other days every person must stint in his expenses (Tur). And its source is from what is stated in the Gemara: a person's sustenance is allotted to him from Rosh Hashana, etc. And Rashi explained: to be careful not to make excessive expenditure, for they will not add for him beyond what they fixed for him, end quote. And this is a strong rebuke for our times, when due to our many sins many people transgress this and do not set their mind how to manage the expenses of their household so as to keep distant things that are permitted; and many have been cast down by this bad practice, which ultimately brings a person through it to theft and robbery and also to disgrace and shame. And there are many causes that lead to this bad practice. And the greatest cause of all of them is through women, whose minds are light and who do not foresee what will be born [from their actions]; and fortunate is he who strengthens his heart and pays no heed to enticements, and manages the expenses of his household with reckoning according to the measure of his profit and not more. And see in the kuntres Sefas Temimim, chapter 5, what we wrote on this.
To honor it and to delight in it as on Shabbos. See in the Mishnah Berurah, because it is written "to the Holy One of Hashem, honored," and regarding Yomim Tovim as well it is said of them "a holy convocation." And all this is the wording of the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchos Yom Tov; and according to this, the verse concludes upon this (Yeshayahu 58:13): "and you honor it by not doing your ways, by not seeking your affairs and speaking a word." And all this is explained above in siman 306 and siman 307: which affairs one may speak of on Shabbos, and that your speech on Shabbos should not be like your speech on a weekday; see there. And if so, the same applies on Yom Tov, for the verse refers to both of them; see there.
From Mincha onward. Not precisely so; rather even a half hour before the time of Mincha Ketanah, namely from nine hours onward. And see in Eliyah Rabbah and in Eshel Avraham, who wrote that one should not do melacha on erev Yom Tov from Mincha onward as on erev Shabbos; see at the beginning of siman 251.
On erev Yom Tov as on Shabbos. See in Magen Avraham, who wrote that the same applies on the first day of Yom Tov: one should refrain from fixing a meal from nine hours onward (which is erev of the second day of Yom Tov) so that he will eat at night with appetite; and the Acharonim copied this. But in my humble opinion this law is not clear, for in truth the principal day is the first day of Yom Tov, since we are expert in the fixing of the moon; and even though we certainly must be stringent regarding it not to do any melacha, and likewise all the shevusin, as on the first day of Yom Tov (except for the need of a sick person, as above at the beginning of siman 496), that is with respect to the essence of Yom Tov once its time has already come. But that we should be stringent not to eat on the first day of Yom Tov at a time when one has an appetite to eat, for example on the festival of Shavuos when the day is long, and turn it into a matter of doubt lest today be a weekday and it be erev Yom Tov, so that he will eat at night with appetite: from where do we have such a thing?
