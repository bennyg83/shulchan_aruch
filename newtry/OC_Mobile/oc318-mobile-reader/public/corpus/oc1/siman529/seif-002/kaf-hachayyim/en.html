2) There. Half in the beis midrash, etc. Rambam, chapter 6, law 19, implies that the public discourse is delivered after the morning meal. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 2 of Beitzah, siman 5, however, writes that the discourse should be delivered before the meal, as found in the incident involving Rabbi Eliezer in Beitzah there. Rava likewise did not appoint a lecturer from one Yom Tov to the next because of intoxication. It is therefore preferable to deliver the discourse in the morning, and in any event it should not continue beyond midday. See there. In Chullin, chapter 1, siman 50, he writes that a prayer leader who excessively prolongs melodies is blameworthy, because this is neither 'half for Havayah' nor 'half for yourselves.' See there. Magen Avraham at the beginning of the siman cites this and writes that nowadays, when halachos are not taught during the discourse, it is preferable to deliver the discourse after the meal because of the joy of Yom Tov. Bach writes likewise, and this is the custom. These are his words. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 9, writes likewise. Since some hold one way and others hold the other way, it appears that each place should follow its custom, provided that no less than half the day is devoted to the beis midrash, thereby fulfilling the teachings of our Sages. Some therefore have the custom to study in a group on every day of the festival, from after the morning meal until Minchah. This is especially appropriate for merchants and craftsmen who lack time to study on weekdays. Many who fear Heaven have the custom to study in a group on every Shabbos and festival, and for several hours on weekday nights as well. The primary study should be halachah, so that a Jew will know what to do. See above, siman 238, and our words there, with Heaven's help.
