There, in the same seif. "And he is obligated to feed the convert, the orphan, and the widow together with the other poor." NB: There is an allusion to this in the verse in Kohelet, "On a good day be in good, and on a bad day see, for God made this corresponding to that." The intent appears as follows. The Holy One, blessed be He, commanded in the Torah, "You shall rejoice in your festival, etc., and your poor who are in your land." What God commanded, to gladden the poor on the festivals, may be for the sake of the joy of the wealthy person himself, for God wants a person to rejoice on Yom Tov with complete joy. But the tanna said in Avot, "Do not despair of calamity," so his joy is not complete, lest, God forbid, harm come upon him. Therefore God commanded him to give tzedakah to the poor, and it is known that tzedakah saves from calamity. Since he is thereby assured from calamity, he has complete joy. This is what the verse says: "On a good day be in good, and on a bad day see," meaning remember also the bad that could come. The author of Maaseh Hashem explained the passage Ha Lachma Anya in his way along these lines; see there. So too it may be explained in our way: therefore give tzedakah to the poor so that you will have complete joy. This is why the verse concludes, "for this," meaning what God commanded, to give tzedakah to the poor on a day of joy, "God made corresponding to that," meaning corresponding to the bad day, so that he not see harm and will have complete joy. Amen. Examine carefully.
