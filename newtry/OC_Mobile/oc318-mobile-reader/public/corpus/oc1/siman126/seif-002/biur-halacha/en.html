And if it was in the first three [berachos], etc. — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote, or if he became mute in them in the middle of a beracha; and to exclude the author of Petach on Orach Chayyim who brought in the name of responsum of Noda B'Yehuda regarding a prayer leader who fainted at Attah Gibbor — that the second should return to the place where the first stopped, not to the beginning, per the innovations of Rabbi Akiva Eiger; and also from Noda B'Yehuda it is proven explicitly that they do not hold thus; and also in Birkhei Yosef he hinted to that responsum that one should waver over it and did not copy it in his book, see there. And know further that it is evident from the words of Gra and Noda B'Yehuda that their words apply specifically if the one standing in his place did not have intent from the beginning for the prayer leader's prayer; but if he had intent for the prayer leader's prayer and did not lapse in between, he need not return if the error was in the piyut of the kedushah order, but only to the place where the first stopped. And the same law applies in the last three: if he erred or became mute in the third beracha, he need not return except to that beracha if the one standing in his place had intent for the prayer leader's prayer — and even if he only had intent from the beginning of Retzei, it is aided there by "Give ear." But I did not find clearly in their words whether if he erred in the middle of one beracha of the three, or became mute, and the one standing in his place had intent for the prayer leader's prayer from its beginning — whether he must return to the beginning of this beracha or whether it suffices from the place where he stopped alone, since the three berachos are considered as one; nevertheless we are lenient because he had intent from the beginning — if so it is one in his regard — and therefore perhaps it suffices on this account even if he begins in the middle of a beracha. And perhaps one can distinguish; and from Beit Yosef on the passage "and know in Petach" etc., from which we learn etc., it appears that even in such a case he must return to the beginning of the beracha — and Noda B'Yehuda and Gra did not address this. See their words — it seems at first glance that even according to their words it is so — requires study. And a prayer leader who is a kohen and it became known that someone died in adjacent houses in the middle of prayer — see Mishna Berurah; and after the fact, if they told him even though it is only rabbinic impurity — Peri Megadim ruled that he must stop in the middle; and from the words of She'eris Yehudah it appears that he need not stop for this which is rabbinic in the middle of prayer — see there.
