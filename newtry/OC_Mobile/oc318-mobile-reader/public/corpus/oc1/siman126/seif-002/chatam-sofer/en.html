Regarding the Mechaber, seif 2. See responsa Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayim 21. He resolves the question of my father-in-law, the great Rabbi Akiva Eiger, on Beit Yosef here. Beit Yosef writes that one who prompts the kohanim begins with Sim Shalom. Why does he not begin with Retzei, since the first three and last three blessings are considered a single unit? This requires study in a case where the person praying reaches Retzei, knows that he must interrupt for Bircas Kohanim, and another will replace him, who will need to begin with Retzei. It would seem that he should withdraw immediately upon reaching Retzei. See there for his resolution of this difficult question. He further writes there regarding a chazzan who became unable to continue during the Selichos of Mussaf on Yom Kippur. Another replaced him and began from the place where he stopped, not from the beginning of Atah Vechartanu, in the presence of leading Torah scholars of the city. Chasam Sofer writes that in his opinion they acted correctly. Since the second chazzan still had to say, “Our God and God of our fathers, forgive our sins,” through “Blessed are You, Hashem, Who sanctifies Israel and Yom Kippur,” there is no concern if he shortened the middle portion and omitted Atah Vechartanu. Even in Mussaf of Yom Kippur, where the second chazzan does not mention the Avodah in the standard text of Umipnei Chata’einu, even if that omission would otherwise invalidate the prayer, it is of no consequence here because the Selichos mention matters of the Avodah many times. The precise wording is not indispensable, provided that he does not shorten it to the form of a blessing over produce. See Magen Avraham, siman 114, seif katan 9.
