From the beginning of the blessing in which he erred, etc. Even if he erred only at the end, that entire blessing is regarded as though it had not been said. Ideally the replacement should be someone who concentrated on the entire prayer together with the chazzan and did not speak while the chazzan was praying. If no such person is available, they may appoint even one who did not concentrate. Nevertheless, because of the burden on the congregation, he begins only from the beginning of the blessing where the error occurred. This is also implied by the Beit Yosef. In Eliyah Zuta I recorded an incident involving a kohen who was serving as chazzan and reciting the Amidah aloud when it became known that there was a corpse in a house adjoining the beit midrash. My master and father, the rav, may his Rock protect and sustain him, ruled that they should not tell him until he completed the prayer, because this form of tumah is only d'rabbanan, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 372.
