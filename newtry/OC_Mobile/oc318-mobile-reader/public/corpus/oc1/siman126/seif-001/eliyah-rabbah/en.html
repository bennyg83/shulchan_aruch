Immediately, etc. This means that they do not wait for him to remember the correct text, but remove him immediately from the lectern. It appears, however, that they do not remove him permanently from serving as chazzan. So write Magen Avraham and the Perishah.
And if he began, etc. The same applies if he omitted the beginning of the blessing but recited its conclusion. All the more so, says the Taz, if he also omits the blessing of the resurrection of the dead or Boneh Yerushalayim, then even if he has already begun another blessing he is removed. This is not conclusive. See the Bach and Beit Yosef, who apparently overlooked the Tanchuma in Parashat Vayikra, which compels this conclusion; see there, as I have abbreviated. The Bach holds that the Gemara's rule likewise applies to the blessing of resurrection, etc., and I find support for this from the Tanchuma there. Note this well.
