If: Eliyah Rabbah ruled that if he skipped two blessings — even the resurrection of the dead and who builds Jerusalem — even if he had begun them, they remove him. Not so with one blessing: if he subdues the wicked, if he had begun they do not remove him; if he had not begun, they remove him. And the resurrection of the dead or who builds Jerusalem — even if he had not begun, they do not remove him. That which he takes "erred and skipped" (see Magen Avraham 119:5) — if he did so intentionally and skipped, even in the other blessings they remove him immediately, per Alef Beit Chayyim. And one could say that the words of Taz are on a different matter.

In Eliyah Rabbah (3): A kohen who is prayer leader, and someone died in the adjacent house — one need not tell him until he finishes the prayer; see above. And as cited in Shach 1, one need not wake him for rabbinic tumah — the same applies here. But certainly, if he knows that they told him, he must leave from there, because there is a positive commandment and a negative prohibition regarding tumah. And it appears that the second begins from the beginning of the blessing, even though there is no error, whenever he interrupted in the middle of a blessing. But certainly, if he finished the prayer silently and did not begin aloud, they tell him to go out, and another returns aloud. What the author wrote in seif 2, etc. — "if there is someone who had intention from the start, it is good that he go" — that is "perhaps"; see Eliyah Rabbah. And even if he skipped many blessings, they do not remove him; see above on this.

There are several halachic doubts in what the author wrote in seif 4; I will write a bit here, and im yirtzeh Heaven in Magen Avraham I will explain further.

1. If the prayer leader erred aloud in Shacharis and did not mention dew and rain — it is obvious that he returns; even "who makes rain descend" he returns. Mention of Rosh Chodesh, Shabbos, and Yom Tov — b'dieved he relies on the mention in Musaf, and one remembrance suffices for him; not so "who makes the wind blow," etc.

2. On Shabbos the law is literally like Rosh Chodesh, and similarly if he did not uproot his feet he returns and says the Shabbos blessing and the three final blessings. So concluded Pri Megadim citing Mahari Tiktin 12, who wrote because of this small matter, etc. Nevertheless, the same applies to Shabbos, and so Maharik — the law seems literally so.

3. In silent prayer he does not return — apparently Rambam, Piskei Mahari Tiktin, learned it from Shacharis to Musaf; therefore, when he did not uproot his feet, he returns. And this is because in silent prayer, if he erred in "who makes the wind blow," he does not return; hence it is logical, and therefore logically even if he did not uproot his feet he does not return — as Levush, that in the first three, if he uprooted his feet he does not return; and from this, etc.

4. A prayer leader who prayed silently at Maariv returns, since he has nothing on which to rely; but on Shabbos he may rely on the abbreviated seven-blessing form, as it is not less than an individual who relies on it, as in siman 268; see above.

5. And know: an individual or prayer leader who did not pray Mincha at all, who prays Maariv twice on Shabbos — the second is more stringent than the first; for on the first, if he did not mention Shabbos, he relies on the abbreviated seven-blessing form, as in siman 268, since it is optional; and the second, for making up the obligatory Mincha, does not raise for him the abbreviated seven-blessing form for this.
