Ateret Zekenim, note 1: From the beginning of the blessing where the prayer leader erred — hence in the first three he begins at the head; and in the last three he begins at R'tzei, as is found in seif 2. And certainly there is no difference whether he erred at the head and another passes in his place, or became mute and another passes — Yerushalmi, chapter Ein Omdin, regarding one who becomes mute in various ways. Therefore, if the other did not have intention, he must begin at R'tzei. Therefore the words of Beit Yosef wonder me in what he wrote: "Know that in Piskei Mahari Tiktin," etc. And from there we learn that when we say, etc. — but what is the proof? One could say the reason of the Gemara that wrote one must be careful that another should not stand to lead the reading and say "establish peace" — except for one who directed his heart to the entire prayer, meaning he will not need to begin from the start at R'tzei. And it is possible that for this it would suffice if the other had intention from the start of R'tzei; from this it implies that the other would have had to return for the entire prayer. So occurred in the community of Pozna on Yom Kippur 5531, in the Shacharis prayer in the piyyut of the Kaddish order: weakness seized the cantor, and another had to descend before the ark; and I ruled that the other, who did not have intention together with the prayer leader, should return to the head of the prayer, without saying the piyyut.
