3) There: But if he skipped the berachah of 'HaMalshinim,' etc. The Tur wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: A shaliach tzibbur who skipped two or three berachos, they do not remove him, except for one who did not say 'Techiyas HaMeisim,' lest he deny the Resurrection of the Dead; and 'Machnia zeidim,' lest he be a heretic; and 'Boneh Yerushalayim,' lest he not believe in the coming of the Mashiach, and I say he is a heretic. Until here. However, the Beis Yosef was puzzled by the Tur for citing the aforementioned Yerushalmi, and wrote: since it disputes our Talmud, which stated without qualification that if he erred in all the berachos they do not remove him except for the berachah of 'V'lamalshinim'; and so wrote the Rif, the Rambam, and the Rosh, and they did not mention this Yerushalmi at all; see there. And so wrote the Birkei Yosef, ot 2, that we do not rule in this like the Yerushalmi, and the resolution with which the Taz settled the matter I do not find acceptable. End of his words. And so wrote the Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, that the words of the Taz have no reason; and also concerning the resolution of the Bach he wrote that it does not appear correct; see there. However, as for what the Derishah wrote, ot 1, that the Tur cited it [the Yerushalmi] to teach that it is not specifically one berachah, but even if he skipped two or three, in which our Talmud does not dispute; see there. The Maamar Mordechai cited him there and wrote that so wrote also the Vayigash Yehudah, and it is correct; but concerning another resolution that the Derishah wrote, he wrote that he could not grasp his intent, and there is no doubt that it contains several errors; see there.
3) And know that it is proven according to all opinions that if we know that he denies the Resurrection of the Dead, or does not believe in the future Redemption, and all the more so if he does not believe in Torah from Heaven or in reward and punishment, he is according to all opinions a complete heretic, and it is forbidden to allow him to be a shaliach tzibbur; and if he stood as chazzan by force, one does not answer Amen after him. Mishnah Berurah, ot 2.
