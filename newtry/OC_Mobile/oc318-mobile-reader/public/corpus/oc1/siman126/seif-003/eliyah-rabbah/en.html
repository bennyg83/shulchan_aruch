And such is the custom. The Rama and Levush omit this because the Gemara mentions Rosh Chodesh, as the Beit Yosef and Lechem Chamudot explain in several places. Menachem Azaryah writes that Rosh Chodesh is mentioned as a greater novelty, because one is not obligated to delight in it as on Shabbos and Yom Tov, yet nevertheless the Sages were concerned for the burden on the congregation. All the more so on Shabbos and Yom Tov. It seems that for this reason they were more lenient at the beginning of siman 268 regarding the Shabbos prayer than regarding Rosh Chodesh. Based on this, I can explain the Bach's ruling that in Mussaf of Shabbos the chazzan is not required to repeat because of an omission, whereas in Mussaf of Rosh Chodesh he is required to repeat. Note this well. Although the Bach earlier suggested the opposite, that on Rosh Chodesh they were more concerned about burdening the congregation because of loss of work, that reasoning follows Baal Halachot Gedolot, who holds that Shabbos is more stringent. The Rif and those who follow him, however, do not mention such a distinction. Accordingly, where they say that in Mussaf of Rosh Chodesh he must repeat, that statement is specifically about Rosh Chodesh. Magen Avraham writes that the Bach's statements contradict one another and that there is a scribal error. In my humble opinion they can be reconciled without emending the text. Moreover, Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah also records the Bach's conclusion according to its plain meaning. Furthermore, if we assume a scribal error, we would have to say that in Mussaf of Rosh Chodesh he does repeat and that this is the opinion of most poskim. This is difficult, for the Rif, Rosh, Rambam, Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 15, and the Tur all rule that he repeats. It is even more surprising regarding the Semag and Tur, because in the Baal Halachot Gedolot text before me, at the end of the chapter Tefillat HaShachar, he rules that he repeats. For the law concerning an individual, see siman 422, seif 1. In any event, the individual may fulfill his obligation by listening to the chazzan, as at the end of siman 124. So write Maharam, Malbushei Yom Tov, and Lechem Chamudot.
