(Seif 4): If the prayer leader erred when he prayed quietly he never returns, etc. I would say: see Magen Avraham what he wrote if he already completed his prayer, see there. And in my humble opinion the law is with the author of Biur Halacha, for it is evident from the wording of Shulchan Aruch that wrote "always returns" — and what is the wording "always"? One cannot say his intent is that even though he already stands in another place he returns to the beginning, for that would not need to write "always," for from the wording "returns" it already implies thus if he still stands in the place where he erred — what is the wording "returns"? Rather perforce the intent is that even though he already prayed further he returns to the beginning; and why write the wording "always"? Rather perforce the intent is always, even if he completed his prayer he returns to the beginning — and it is evident like the view of Biur Halacha; and so it is correct and requires good study.
