And if. See Taz. Avodah Zarah 22a leaves the case of an unspecified division unresolved. Although we generally rule leniently in a D’Rabbanan doubt, Rama permits it only where there is substantial loss. This is so even though the Gemara does not conclude with the word teiku, and in such a case it would seem that everyone should rule leniently in a D’Rabbanan matter. See Yam Shel Shlomo, Beitzah, chapter 1, siman 43, who writes that where the Gemara concludes teiku, its silence may indicate prohibition, unlike where it does not say teiku; see also Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 2. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained in the general principles. Ran gives a reason for stringency: according to the first version, in which Rava was embarrassed, the case involved an unspecified division, showing that such a division is forbidden. The question arises only according to the second version reported by Rav Geviha of Bei Kesil. We follow the first version. This is what Beit Yosef means here when citing Ran’s reason.
