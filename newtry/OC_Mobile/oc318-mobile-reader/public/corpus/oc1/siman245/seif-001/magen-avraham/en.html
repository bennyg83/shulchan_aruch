An oven. Although the Jew’s share does not increase when the non-Jew works on Shabbos, as it would in a field, it is nevertheless forbidden. Since the Jew is obligated to work on the weekday in exchange, when he says, “You work on Shabbos and I will work during the week,” it is as though he appoints a worker to perform labor on his behalf. Tosafos. Accordingly, if they themselves do nothing, but the non-Jew heats the oven and bakes in it and they receive rent, then although the Jew may not receive the rent because it is Shabbos wages, he may nevertheless divide it without specification through inclusion, as Beit Yosef writes at the end of this siman in the name of Rivash. We are not concerned that people will say the non-Jew works for the Jew, since the non-Jew owns a share in it. Otherwise, even a prior stipulation should be forbidden, as Ran writes and as stated in the gloss.
Whether little or much. It is forbidden, however, to calculate with him and say, “You took more on Shabbos and I took less; let us divide the remainder,” as wrote Raavad, Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of the Geonim, and Bach. See seif 2. Regarding a field, the Jew must say to him, “You exert yourself to sow and plow on Shabbos and at harvest take a share corresponding to your Shabbos labor, while I will exert myself during the week and take the corresponding weekday share.” It is permitted because they stipulated initially, so no obligation to work on Shabbos ever fell upon the Jew and the non-Jew is not his agent. Mahari ben Lev, siman 106, writes that if the amount for which the property was rented on Shabbos is unknown, as with a shop, they may divide equally. See what I wrote in siman 246, seif 5.
And they divided without specification. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: “If they did not stipulate and later came to an accounting, and the Jew said, ‘You take Shabbos and I will take a weekday,’ it is forbidden. If the non-Jew took Shabbos on his own, without any statement by the Jew, the matter was left unresolved and we rule leniently.” Rashi explains: “They accepted the arrangement without specification, the non-Jew worked on Shabbos and the Jew on Sunday, and the Jew never said, ‘You take Shabbos and I will take a weekday.’ May they divide equally without specification, without the Jew mentioning Shabbos?” According to Rambam, both cases are forbidden. Beit Yosef writes that if the non-Jewish baker owns no share in the oven, then even if the Jew initially tells him, “Take for yourself a certain number of days, including Shabbos, so that you will also heat it during the week,” it is forbidden. One cannot say that the non-Jew acquires the oven on Shabbos unless it is permanently his. Tosafos in the name of Rabbeinu Elchanan. Beit Yosef infers that Tosafos did not accept this view, for they permit any case where there was an initial stipulation, and therefore Rosh and the other poskim did not cite this law. In my view the opposite is true. Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham recount the incident of one who collected half an oven in payment of a debt, but do not mention, as Tosafos does, that the baker took a fixed number of days. This shows that regarding a baker, a prior stipulation is ineffective, which explains the gloss, “If he has no share,” etc. It appears to me that Tosafos and Hagahos Ashri prohibit only where the Jew says, “You take Shabbos,” for this resembles renting out his oven on Shabbos on condition that the non-Jew heat it for him during the week. He receives Shabbos wages, which is like giving the non-Jew money as payment, as Tosafos explicitly writes in the name of Rabbeinu Elchanan. But if he says to the baker, “You take two or three days and I will take two or three days,” it is permitted, for the Shabbos wages are included with weekday wages. In this manner Rabbeinu Tam permitted a stipulation, contrary to Beit Yosef’s claim that Tosafos retracted. All this applies only where it is permitted to rent out an oven, as explained in siman 243. See there and siman 246.
Works on his day. Since the Jew works in exchange for what the non-Jew did on Shabbos, the non-Jew is regarded as his agent on Shabbos. The same applies where the Jew is obligated to perform all the work but the non-Jew performs it on Shabbos. See Knesses HaGedolah.
For his own benefit, etc. He is like a sharecropper, and there is no concern of Shabbos wages at all, as explained in siman 243, because the non-Jew acts for his own benefit. There is also no concern of appearance, since everyone knows that the non-Jew owns a share in it. Olas Shabbos writes that siman 243 permits explicitly hiring the worker, but he was not precise, for there the case is renting out the oven, not hiring workers for oneself. See there.
Through inclusion. Although it is technically permitted even when separated, it nevertheless appears like Shabbos wages. Ran. Likewise, in the incident cited above from Tosafos, it is permitted if the non-Jew receives half the profit each day. The Jew is not obligated to work at all, for only the baker works, so the baker is not his agent. Therefore, even without an initial stipulation it is permitted, provided that the payment is included with weekday payment. In the earlier case, however, if the non-Jew did not heat the oven on Shabbos, the baker would not heat it for the Jew during the week. It therefore resembles renting out one’s oven, as explained above. See the end of seif 3.
