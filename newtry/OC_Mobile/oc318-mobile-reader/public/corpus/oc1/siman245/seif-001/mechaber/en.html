A Jew and a non-Jew who jointly own a field, oven, bathhouse, water mill, or merchandise in a shop: if, when they first entered the partnership, they stipulated that the profits of Shabbos, whether little or much, would belong solely to the non-Jew, and that the profits of 1 weekday corresponding to Shabbos would belong solely to the Jew, it is permitted. If they did not stipulate this at the outset, then when they later divide the profits, the non-Jew takes all the profits of the Shabbosos and the Jew takes the corresponding profits of the weekdays; the remainder is divided equally. If the profits of Shabbos are not known separately, they may divide all the profits equally. {Rama: Some authorities say that all of this applies only when each partner's work is fixed for a particular day, but if they work together whenever they wish and the non-Jew works on Shabbos of his own accord, they may divide all the profits equally, because the Jew did not instruct the non-Jew to work for him and the melacha is not recognizable as belonging to the Jew. [Rosh and other authorities]}
