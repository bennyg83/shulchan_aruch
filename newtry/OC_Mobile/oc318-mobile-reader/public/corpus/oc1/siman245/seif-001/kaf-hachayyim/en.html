1) [Seif 1] A Jewish person and a non-Jew who jointly own a field or an oven, etc. Although the Jewish person's share does not improve during the week through the non-Jew's work in the oven on Shabbos as it does in a field, it is nevertheless forbidden, since he is obligated to work on Shabbos as well. When the Jewish person tells him, 'You work on Shabbos and I will work during the week,' it is as though he directly appoints a worker to perform labor. Tosafos, Avodah Zarah 22a. Accordingly, if the owners of the oven do nothing, but a non-Jew who owns dough heats the oven, bakes in it, and pays rent, although the Jewish person may not receive the rent because it is Shabbos wages, he may nevertheless divide the proceeds without specification when they are subsumed within other payment, as Beis Yosef writes at the end of this siman in the name of Rivash; see there. We are not concerned that people will say the non-Jew works for the Jewish person's benefit, since the non-Jew has a share in it, for otherwise it should be forbidden even when they stipulated initially. Ran writes the same, as stated in the gloss. Magen Avraham and Machatzis HaShekel, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 7.
2) There: If they stipulated at the outset, etc. Since they stipulated from the outset, it is as though the Jewish person has no share in the field at all on Shabbos. Rather, it belongs entirely to the non-Jew on Shabbos and entirely to the Jewish person on the corresponding weekday. Thus no Shabbos labor was imposed upon the Jewish person, since the field is not his on that day. Beis Yosef. Regarding a field, he must tell him, 'You exert yourself to sow and plow on Shabbos and at harvest take an amount corresponding to your Shabbos labor, while I will exert myself during the week and take a corresponding amount.' Beis Yosef in the name of Sefer HaTerumah; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2. A practical difference is that if one of them did not work the entire day, at harvest he takes only in proportion to the work he performed. Perishah, paragraph 1. Nevertheless, if something then remains for them to divide equally, it is permitted, as stated in seif 2; see there.
3) There: If they stipulated at the outset, etc. Similarly, when a convert and a non-Jew inherited from their father, it is permitted if they stipulated at the outset. Rash, Demai, chapter 6, mishnah 14.
4) Two Jewish people who were partners with a non-Jew in animals and in operating a mill may act as follows: if, when they first entered the partnership, they stipulated with the non-Jew, 'Take Shabbos for yourself and we will take another day in its place,' it is permitted. Since they stipulated at the outset when purchasing the animals, it is as though the animals belong to the non-Jew on Shabbos and the Jewish people have no share in them, while on the corresponding weekday the animals belong to the Jewish people and the non-Jew has no share in them. There is also reason to permit it because the non-Jew will not refrain from working with the animals, since he has a share in them, and the Jewish person does not receive Shabbos wages, since they stipulated at the outset. If, however, they did not stipulate at the outset, it is forbidden. Beis Yosef in the name of Responsa Rivash, siman 151; see there.
4) A Jewish person was partnered with a non-Jew in purchasing wheat. They sifted it with a sieve and a tray to clean away straw and chaff, then sent it outside the city to a watermill for grinding and sold the flour in their shop. The non-Jew opened the shop on the sixth day of the week and publicly performed his work by sifting the wheat and selling the flour. When the Jewish person initially entered the partnership with the non-Jew, however, he reduced his own share by eight percent and added it to the non-Jew's share, expressly stating, 'This eight percent from my share shall be yours alone in exchange for the profit produced by the Shabbos labor.' See Petach HaDevir, paragraph 2, who discusses this at length and concludes that this is entirely permitted; see there.
5) There: Whether little or much, etc. It is forbidden, however, to calculate with him and say, 'You took a large amount on Shabbos and I took a small amount; let us divide the surplus,' as Raavad writes, and as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Responsa of the Geonim and Bach. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Beis Yosef, however, writes in the name of Mahari Abuhav that when they stipulated, it is permitted in every manner; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 3, similarly writes that according to Mahari Abuhav and the Shulchan Aruch in seif 2, Raavad's arrangement was permitted completely, because they maintain that when an initial stipulation was made there is never any prohibition. As a matter of practical halachah, we have only the Shulchan Aruch's ruling, which permits every arrangement when they stipulated, contrary to Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; see there. Taz, paragraph 2, also explains the Shulchan Aruch's words this way; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, however, writes that even Beis Yosef agrees that if he mentions, 'Make up the difference for me,' it is forbidden, since it is apparent that their original stipulation was a subterfuge. Beis Yosef's statement in the name of Mahari Abuhav that when they stipulated it is permitted in every manner means both when they calculate and say how many Shabbos days one took and how many weekdays the other took, whether many or few, and when they divide equally, provided they did not say, 'Make up for me from the larger amount received on Shabbos'; see there. Levush Serad on Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, and Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, write the same, except that Eliyah Rabbah writes there that as a matter of practical halachah it is possible to be lenient; see there. Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 294, paragraph 20, writes, however, that even if they stipulated at the outset, it is forbidden if they subsequently make a calculation. Shach there, paragraph 27, writes the same; see there. Therefore, since this matter is disputed, and Maran's view in seif 2 can also be explained like Eliyah Rabbah and those who follow him, namely, that he permitted only an unspecified equal division, while explicitly telling him, 'Make up for me from the larger amount you took on Shabbos,' is forbidden, this is the ruling we adopt.
6) There: Whether little or much, etc. If it is not known how much was earned on Shabbos, as in the case of a shop, they may divide equally. Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, siman 106; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 5.
