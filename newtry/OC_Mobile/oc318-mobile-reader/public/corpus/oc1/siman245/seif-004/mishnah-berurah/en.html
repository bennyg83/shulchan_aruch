(16) Because it is not, etc. If the work were incumbent upon the Jew, it would be prohibited by law, as stated in seif 1 regarding a case without an initial stipulation.
(17) As it is stated, etc. There is no concern that it appears to be Shabbos wages, since the payment is absorbed with other days. See below, siman 307, seifim 3 and 4, and Mishnah Berurah there.
(18) Likewise, there is no, etc. Meaning, there is also no concern of appearance that people will think the non-Jew is the Jew's employee, because the business is not publicly identifiable as the Jew's. If it were identifiable and called by the Jew's name, it would be prohibited, as stated in siman 243, seif 1, regarding a bathhouse. Even this leniency applies only when the Jew does not instruct the non-Jew to deal with the property on Shabbos, but the non-Jew does so on his own initiative, as stated similarly in seif 5.
(19) It is prohibited. Unless he stipulated this with the non-Jew before entering the partnership, as explained in seif 1.
