Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Beis Yosef, however, writes in the name of Mahari Abuhav that when they stipulated, it is permitted in every manner; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 3, similarly writes that according to Mahari Abuhav and the Shulchan Aruch in seif 2, Raavad's arrangement was permitted completely, because they maintain that when an initial stipulation was made there is never any prohibition. As a matter of practical halachah, we have only the Shulchan Aruch's ruling, which permits every arrangement when they stipulated, contrary to Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; see there. Taz, paragraph 2, also explains the Shulchan Aruch's words this way; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, however, writes that even Beis Yosef agrees that if he mentions, 'Make up the difference for me,' it is forbidden, since it is apparent that their original stipulation was a subterfuge. Beis Yosef's statement in the name of Mahari Abuhav that when they stipulated it is permitted in every manner means both when they calculate and say how many Shabbos days one took and how many weekdays the other took, whether many or few, and when they divide equally, provided they did not say, 'Make up for me from the larger amount received on Shabbos'; see there. Levush Serad on Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, and Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2, write the same, except that Eliyah Rabbah writes there that as a matter of practical halachah it is possible to be lenient; see there. Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 294, paragraph 20, writes, however, that even if they stipulated at the outset, it is forbidden if they subsequently make a calculation. Shach there, paragraph 27, writes the same; see there. Therefore, since this matter is disputed, and Maran's view in seif 2 can also be explained like Eliyah Rabbah and those who follow him, namely, that he permitted only an unspecified equal division, while explicitly telling him, 'Make up for me from the larger amount you took on Shabbos,' is forbidden, this is the ruling we adopt.
