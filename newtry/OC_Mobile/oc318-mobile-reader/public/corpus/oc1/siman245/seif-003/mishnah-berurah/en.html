(14) The seller should transfer it back to them. Choshen Mishpat states that a new act of acquisition is now required to transfer it back to the seller; merely returning the money and deed is insufficient. See simanim 189 and 194 regarding a non-Jew's acquisition.
(15) If he wishes, etc. See Magen Avraham and Taz, who question this ruling sharply: why should it be permitted, when the leniency in siman 244 applies specifically because of financial loss? Magen Avraham concludes that the case concerns renting the Jew's share to the non-Jew with absorbed payment, meaning that he rents him all his Shabbosos together with two or three weekdays, so direct Shabbos wages are no longer involved. The allowance through contracted work likewise applies only where Shabbos was not specified, but the Jew says generally, “Whenever you work and earn such-and-such for me, I will pay you such-and-such.” Rama's principal novelty is that there is no concern of appearance that the non-Jew works for the Jew, because it is known that the non-Jew has an ownership share. The phrase “as explained” refers to the earlier case, where appearance was not a concern; all the more so here, where none exists because of the partnership. Taz likewise concludes that the arrangement is permitted only with absorbed payment. Nevertheless, the Jew should avoid discussing the business with the non-Jew on Shabbos, as the later authorities write at the end of siman 244.
