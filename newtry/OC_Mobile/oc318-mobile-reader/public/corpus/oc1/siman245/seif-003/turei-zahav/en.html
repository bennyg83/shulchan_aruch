If he wishes to rent it to the non-Jew, etc. I do not know who authorized Rama to permit renting it for Shabbos alone without absorbed payment. We find such a leniency only regarding customs collection above, because of financial loss, lest the Jew otherwise commit the greater prohibition of collecting it himself, as explained in the name of Maharam in Mordechai. If one would apply that reasoning here as well, it is difficult: regarding a field, the Rosh and Tur in siman 243 permitted only absorbed payment, so why did they not permit renting it for Shabbos alone? They should likewise have permitted a bathhouse for this reason, and many laws in siman 246 require specifically absorbed rental. Necessarily, Maharam permitted this only regarding customs collection and coin minting, which are major enterprises about which a person is especially anxious. Here, the fact that the non-Jew is a partner does not help the Jew receive Shabbos wages; this is no better than a field or bathhouse. Only a contracted arrangement, meaning absorbed payment, is permitted here, as with a bathhouse or field where ownership is publicly known. It therefore appears that one should not rely upon this leniency to rent the property for Shabbosos alone. Rivash states this explicitly, as cited by Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, regarding an animal jointly owned with a non-Jew: “After they became partners, the Jew said, ‘Take your portion on Shabbos and I mine during the week.’ This is prohibited. Even though there is no concern of the animal resting on Shabbos, because the non-Jew owns a share and is not restrained from working it, the Jew is still prohibited from taking weekday profit in exchange for Shabbos profit, since he thereby receives direct Shabbos wages.” All the more so is renting specifically for Shabbos without absorbed payment prohibited.
