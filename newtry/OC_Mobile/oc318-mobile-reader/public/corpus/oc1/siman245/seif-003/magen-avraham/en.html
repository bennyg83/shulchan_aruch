To rent it to a non-Jew. It appears to me that only initially, before they accepted the partnership, may the Jew say, “Give me a certain sum and take Shabbos for yourself.” After they have already accepted it, this is forbidden unless there is no other option, in which case it is permitted to avoid loss. Likewise, one may hire a non-Jew as a contractor where the partners do not themselves perform labor but only collect rent from the oven, mill, or the like, as explained at the beginning of this siman. Where they themselves perform labor, such as heating the oven, it is forbidden to hire a non-Jew even by contract, for this resembles appointing a worker. It is permitted only where there is no alternative, such as where they cannot dissolve the partnership, because of financial loss, as explained at the end of siman 244. Rama’s language, however, implies that it is permitted in every case. One must say that since the non-Jew owns a share, we are not concerned for appearance, as stated in seif 1. It is therefore permitted to rent it to the non-Jew through inclusion, and likewise to hire him by contract, provided that Shabbos is not designated specifically. See siman 243 and the beginning of siman 246.
