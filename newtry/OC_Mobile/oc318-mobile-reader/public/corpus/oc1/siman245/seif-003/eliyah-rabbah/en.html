[3] [Levush] And they came to an accounting, etc. Meaning, even if they wish to calculate without specification, without mentioning Shabbos, and even if the Jew wishes to take a weekday in exchange, whether much or little, without saying that it is in exchange for Shabbos, it is forbidden because they did not stipulate initially. In seif 3, which states regarding this, “Some say that after the fact it is permitted,” the permission applies only when they divided without specification. If they mention the Shabbos proceeds, all agree that it is forbidden where there was no prior stipulation. All this is clear from the Gemara and the poskim. Olas Tamid writes: “One may wonder about those who prohibit a division made without specification, for the question remained unresolved, and in a D’Rabbanan doubt we rule leniently.” He overlooked the words of Ran at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, who explains that according to the first version it was obvious to the Gemara that an unspecified division is forbidden. Since the first version is the anonymous formulation of the Gemara, whereas the second version is reported in the name of Rav Geviha of Bei Kesil, we follow the first version, as is the practice of the Geonim in every such case.
