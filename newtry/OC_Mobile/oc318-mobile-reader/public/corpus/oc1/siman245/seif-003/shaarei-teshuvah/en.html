Permitted to give merchandise, etc. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 29, discusses one who has a store open on a street of non-Jews and employs a non-Jew to sit in the store and sell, paying him a fixed weekly or annual wage. This is forbidden. Rather, he must add and stipulate that the worker receive a fixed amount from every hundred, every thousand, or every measure sold, even one perutah per item, in addition to his wage. We may then say that he acts for his own benefit. If the store is in the Jew’s home or elsewhere, but it is publicly known that the business belongs to the Jew, such as where the government granted that Jew the annual franchise and this is publicly known, it is forbidden even if the Jew instructed him before Shabbos to work on Shabbos, for instructing a non-Jew before Shabbos to perform a Rabbinically prohibited act is also forbidden, as Beit Yosef writes regarding customs duties. Yet because the arrangement is contractual and the only issue is suspicion, one may argue: why should people suspect that the Jew instructed him on Shabbos itself? There is therefore room for leniency, but only where financial loss is involved, such as concern that closing on Shabbos will cause him to lose the franchise. In addition, he must lease the store itself to the non-Jew, so that when the non-Jew enters he enters his own premises, for a lease for a day is considered a sale. The arrangement must also be as described, that beyond his regular wage the non-Jew receives a fixed share from the sales, and no Jew may sit there during the week to assist him with the sales. See there. He further writes that one need not be concerned that the worker must record the sales in a ledger, which is a Torah prohibition, as Chavos Yair writes regarding customs duties. One may distinguish, because here there is no necessity that he write on Shabbos itself. Furthermore, Rama in siman 306 is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon regarding their script. See there. See above, siman 244, seif katan 7, where Noda BiYehudah is cited as ruling stringently regarding printing in their script. See also Birkei Yosef in the name of Rabbi Shlomo Salemi, cited in the manuscript Shaarei Yeshuah, concerning one who owns a vinegar warehouse and whom the official compels not to close on Shabbos. On Friday after midday he should tell the non-Jew: “For everything you sell from these barrels I will give you a fixed amount. Beginning today I give you this wage. If you wish, sell them today.” If some barrels remain on Sunday, the non-Jew sells them on Sunday and receives his full wage. Rabbi Yosef Ayash, author of Shaarei Yeshuah, disagrees and writes that there is no remedy unless the Jew gives him additional time, either before Shabbos or after Shabbos, during which the non-Jew can sell them. Once the Jew has extended the time, he may receive from the non-Jew whatever remains in his possession after Shabbos. See there.
