[6] [Levush] Some say that after the fact it is permitted, etc. This does not resemble the ruling at the end of siman 243, where some say that if one violated the law and rented it out, the proceeds are forbidden. Here the Jew did nothing, for the non-Jew acts on account of his own partnership. The words of Olas Tamid on this point are unclear and plainly contain a scribal error. His statement that “above he permitted hiring the worker explicitly” appears to refer to the end of siman 244, and his answer, that there it is contracted work because the worker receives a fixed amount when the revenue is collected, proves this. Magen Avraham understood him to refer to what was mentioned earlier in siman 243 and therefore challenged him, but the challenge is unnecessary. See there; I have abbreviated.
