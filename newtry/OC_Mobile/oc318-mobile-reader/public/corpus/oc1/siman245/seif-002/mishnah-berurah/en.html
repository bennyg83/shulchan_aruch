(13) The non-Jew agreed. This means that according to their initial stipulation, whether the amount was much or little, the non-Jew's Shabbos portion exceeded the Jew's one weekday portion, and at the time of division the non-Jew nevertheless agreed to divide equally. We do not say that it thereby becomes evident retroactively that the non-Jew had been the Jew's Shabbos agent. Rather, because they stipulated initially that Shabbos belonged exclusively to the non-Jew, the extra amount is merely a gift. This applies where the non-Jew agreed to equal division without calculating the profits of the two days. If the agreement followed an explicit calculation, with the Jew saying, “You earned one hundred zuz on Shabbos and I earned fifty on the weekday; make up my deficiency,” and the non-Jew then agreed to supplement him, the dispute noted above in seif katan 5 applies.
