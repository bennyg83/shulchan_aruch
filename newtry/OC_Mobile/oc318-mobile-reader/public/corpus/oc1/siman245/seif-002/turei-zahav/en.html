Where they stipulated, etc. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Mahari Abuhav: According to the stringent poskim, there is room to question a case where they stipulated initially and afterward divided without specification. It appears to me that since Rambam, who rules stringently in this doubt, interprets the clause “if they came to a reckoning” as referring specifically to a case where they did not stipulate, rather than to one where they did stipulate, as Raavad explains, it follows that everything depends upon whether they stipulated initially. Wherever they did so, the arrangement is permitted in every form. Raavad holds that even after an initial stipulation, equal division is prohibited because it reveals that the stipulation was merely an evasion, as Beit Yosef cites earlier from Ran. Mahari Abuhav argues that if there were any prohibited form even after an initial stipulation, why would Rambam interpret “if they came to a reckoning” specifically as referring to the case without a stipulation? That reading is somewhat strained, since the clause follows immediately after the case where they did stipulate. Rambam should have interpreted it as referring to that case and to its prohibited form. Necessarily, he holds that every form is permitted once they stipulated initially. Accordingly, even if at the time of division the Jew says, “I received fifty zuz on Shabbos and you received one hundred on Sunday; make up the difference,” a case Beit Yosef prohibits in the name of the Geonim, Rambam disagrees and permits every form after an initial stipulation. Rashi likewise agrees with Rambam.
