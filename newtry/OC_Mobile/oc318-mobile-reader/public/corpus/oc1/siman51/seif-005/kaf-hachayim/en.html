(5) And if the chazan finished a different blessing and he said a different blessing — he may answer Amen. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Razah there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot bet. However, Rabbi Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTaz disagreed with the above Magen Avraham and wrote that it does not seem proper to distinguish between equal blessings and unequal ones; according to Rechasim, all appear as answering Amen after his own blessings, until here his wording. And so appears the view of Ma'amar Mordechai, ot alef. But Birchas Yitzchak, ot gimel, ruled like the Magen Avraham and wrote not like Rabbi Yad Aharon; and so he ruled in his book, Kaf HaChayyim, siman 7, ot kaf-vav. And so the revising rabbi in Sha'arei Teshuvah on siman 67, amud alef; see there, where he wrote on the words of Ma'amar Mordechai that it is a great stretch, and concluded: since according to those who say one is obligated to answer Amen — if he does not answer, his punishment is greater than [that for] a married woman, as is known regarding the punishment for neglecting Amens; and according to Yad Aharon, who holds he should not answer — it is only because it appears as answering Amen after his own blessings, and this is not such a great prohibition; they only said it is unseemly, as Peri Chadash z"l wrote — one who answers Amen after his own blessings is unseemly, but for silence we do not silence him, as we say in the chapter "Ein Omdin"; see there. If so, in every such case under our discussion he should answer Amen because of the doubt regarding the severity of neglecting it, and there is not even unseemliness, until here. And so Sha'arei Teshuvah, ot bet.
