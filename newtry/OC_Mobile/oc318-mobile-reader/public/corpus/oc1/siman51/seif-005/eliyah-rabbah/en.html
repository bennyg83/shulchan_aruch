(5) To pause, etc. Nevertheless, one should not pause too long, so that it not appear to be the end of the verse. One should pause between ki and chol, and pronounce the kaf of chol strongly. Shelah in the name of Derech Chayim. Shelah further writes that one should pause slightly between “Your mercies are many” and Hashem; between the two Names in “Hashem, Hashem shall reign”; between Hashem and “will guard you from all evil”; between Hashem and “will guard your departure”; and between “the praise of Hashem” and “my mouth shall speak.” In the verse “Peoples will thank You, God,” there is a pause between peoples and God, for otherwise it would sound as though the nations are gods. The Yerushalmi says that one must articulate the words ki le'olam carefully so they not sound like kol ha'olam. The same applies to the samech of chasdo, so it not sound with a shin as chashdo, meaning suspicion. Lechem Chamudos notes that this does not mean to add a strong dagesh and turn the sheva into a silent sheva, contrary to the rules. One should not alter the vocalization. In “Our soul waited,” the chet should be pronounced carefully so that it not sound, Heaven forbid, like “our soul struck.” Know that the chapter of Tehillim Hodu LaHashem Kir'u appears in Divrei HaYamim, chapter 16. Although much of it also appears in Tehillim, there are several textual differences, as Abudraham notes in the laws of Shabbos. One should say “To you I shall give the land of Canaan,” without es; ya'aloz has a tzadi, not a zayin; veyomru chol ha'am has a patach under the vav and a strong yod; and in veyishecha titen lanu the yod has a segol. The passage Hodu LaHashem Kir'u was instituted by David before the Ark. In the morning daily offering they would say from the beginning until “Do not touch My anointed,” and in the afternoon daily offering from “Sing to Hashem” until “Praise Hashem.” For this reason Lechem Chamudos writes that his teacher Mahari would repeat from “Sing to Hashem” before Minchah. Presumably he recited only through “Praise Hashem,” as Levush writes. I am therefore surprised at Magen Avraham at the end of siman 237, who cites him as saying through “And He is merciful.” See also siman 100.
