(5) To interrupt, etc. — In any case, he should not pause so long that it appears like the end of a verse. He pauses between "ki" and "kol," and emphasizes the kaf of "kol," as the Shelah wrote on folio (252) [213 amud bet] in the name of Derech Chayyim. He further wrote that one should pause slightly between "rachamecha rabim" and "the Omnipresent"; between "the Omnipresent" and "the Omnipresent yimloch"; between "the Omnipresent" and "v'yishmerecha mikol ra"; between "the Omnipresent" and "yishmor tzeitecha"; between "tehilat the Omnipresent" and "yedaber fi." In the verse "Yoducha amim Elokim" there is a pause between "amim" and "Elokim," for if he does not pause it appears as if he says the nations are the Omnipresent, until here. In Yerushalmi one must sprinkle [water] on "ki le'olam" so it not be heard "kol ha'olam"; and likewise the samech of "chasdo" must be sprinkled so it not be heard with a shin as "chashdo," the language of suspicion. Lechem Chamudot wrote: it is not to be said that one should emphasize the samech with a strong dagesh and make the shva nach — contrary to the rule that one may not change the vowels at all, until here. In "Nafsheinu chakesah" one emphasizes the chet so it not be heard, Heaven forbid, "hachtah." So wrote the Shelah. Know that the psalm "Hodu laHashem kir'u" is from Divrei HaYamim, siman 16; and although it is also in Tehillim, nevertheless there are some differences in certain words, as Abudraham wrote in the laws of Shabbat. One must say "Lecha eten eretz Kena'an" without "et"; "Ya'aloz hasadeh" with tzadi, not zayin; "Vayomeru kol ha'am" — the vav with patach and the yod with dagesh. Also "Veyish'acha titein lanu" — the yod with segol. That which we say "Hodu laHashem kir'u" — the explanation is that David enacted it before the Ark to say in the morning at the tamid of dawn until "Im al teiru," and at the tamid between the evenings from "Shiru laHashem" until "Ve'halelu laHashem." Lechem Chamudot wrote: for this reason my teacher Mahari would repeat and say "Shiru laHashem" before the Minchah prayer, until here. Presumably he would say it only until "Ve'halelu laHashem," as the Levush wrote; therefore I wonder at Magen Avraham at the end of siman 237, who wrote in his name to say until "VeHu rachum."
