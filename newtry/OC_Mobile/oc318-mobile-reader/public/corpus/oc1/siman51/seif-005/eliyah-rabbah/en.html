[5] To pause, etc. Nevertheless, one should not pause more than this, so that it not appear like the end of a verse. One should pause between "ki" and "khol," and emphasize the kaf of "khol"; so wrote Shnei Luchot HaBerit, p. 213b, in the name of Derekh Chaim. He further wrote that one should pause slightly between "Your mercies are many" and "Lord," between "Lord" and "the Lord will reign," between "Lord" and "and He will guard you from all evil," between "Lord" and "will guard your going out," and between "the praise of the Lord" and "my mouth will speak." In the verse "Let the peoples thank You, God," there is a pause between "peoples" and "God," for if he does not pause it appears as though he is saying that the peoples are God; end quote. The Yerushalmi says one must articulate "ki le-olam" so it not sound like "kol ha-olam." Likewise, one must articulate the samekh of "chasdo" so it not sound with a shin, "chashdo," an expression of suspicion. Lechem Chamudot wrote: this does not mean to emphasize the samekh with a strong dagesh and make the sheva quiescent contrary to the rule, for one may not alter the vowels at all; end quote. In "our soul has waited," one should emphasize the chet, so it not sound, Heaven forbid, like "struck"; Shnei Luchot HaBerit. Know that the psalm "Give thanks to the Lord, call upon His name" is from I Chronicles, siman 16. Although it is also in Psalms, there are slight differences between them in some words, as Abudarham wrote in the laws of Shabbat. One must say "to you I will give the land of Canaan" without "et"; "let the field exult" with a tzadi and not a zayin; "and all the people said" with the vav with patach and the yod with dagesh; also "and grant us Your salvation" with the yod in segol. The reason we say "Give thanks to the Lord, call," is because David instituted it before the ark to say in the morning with the daily morning offering until "do not harm," and with the afternoon daily offering from "Sing to the Lord" until "and praise the Lord." Lechem Chamudot wrote that for this reason my teacher Mahari customarily repeated "Sing to the Lord" before Minchah; end quote. Presumably he said it only until "and praise the Lord," as Levush wrote. Therefore I am surprised at the Magen Avraham at the end of siman 237, who wrote in his name to say it until "and He is merciful."
