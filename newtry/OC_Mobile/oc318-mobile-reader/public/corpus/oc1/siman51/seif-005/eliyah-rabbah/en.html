(4) In the middle of a psalm, etc. The same law applies between verses that are connected to one another even if they do not form a complete psalm. Such places have the law of the middle of a section. Examples include: from “Give thanks to Hashem, call upon His Name” through “amen”; from “Ascribe strength to Hashem” through “Blessed is God”; from “God of vengeance, Hashem” through “upon the arrogant”; from “Our soul waited for Hashem” through “as we hoped in You”; from “May the Name of Hashem be” through “His glory above the heavens”; from “Blessed is Hashem, the God” through “amen and amen”; from “And David blessed” through “for the Name of Your splendor”; from “You alone are Hashem” through “in mighty waters”; and from “Hashem saved” through “Hashem shall reign.” One should not interrupt at all between “Hashem reigns, Hashem reigned, Hashem shall reign forever and ever,” just as one may not interrupt between “Hashem your God” and “true.” This is implied by Piskey Tosafos. Beit Yosef cites Abudraham: if a need to interrupt arises, one should say the verses beginning “Blessed is Hashem, the God of Israel, from eternity to eternity,” continuing through “Amen and amen,” “Blessed is Hashem from Zion,” “Blessed is Hashem, the God of Israel, Who alone performs wonders,” and “Blessed is His glorious Name forever; may His glory fill the entire earth, amen and amen.” After speaking, he repeats these verses and continues from where he stopped. Knesses HaGedolah writes in the margin that this appears to him an excessive leniency not recorded by the poskim. He apparently understood it to permit interruption even for an optional matter. That is not correct. The discussion concerns a situation where interruption is already permitted, such as because of fear or honor, and even then these verses are recited. Levush and Lechem Chamudos imply this, and I found it explicitly in Kol Bo and Tashbetz, siman 204. Levush writes that for this reason these verses were placed after Kol HaNeshamah, because most interruptions occur there. Lechem Chamudos objects that interruption is permitted only when an occasion of fear or honor arises, which has no fixed place. He therefore explains that the verses were inserted so people would know them fluently whenever needed, and they were placed before Vayevarech because they use language of blessing. I am surprised, because Abudraham explicitly explains that these verses are found at the conclusion of Tehillim and were therefore placed after Kol HaNeshamah, upon completing Tehillim. Magen Avraham also records extended explanations. Levush writes that one should not say “Who can recount the mighty acts” before the zemiros, because it would sound like mockery, as though he now intends to do so. After completing the zemiros it is permitted. Mateh Moshe cites Maharshal likewise. In “Say among the nations: Hashem reigns,” the second letter is soft; the mem and lamed are vocalized with a kamatz. In veyishecha, the yod has a segol.
