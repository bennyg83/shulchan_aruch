(4) And in the middle of a psalm, etc. — And likewise between adjacent verses, even when they are not a complete psalm, their law is as in the middle of a section — that is, from "Hodu laHashem kir'u" until "Amen"; and "Halelu laHashem tenu oz," etc., until "Baruch Elokim El nekamos Hashem"; until "Al ge'im"; "Nafsheinu chakesah laHashem" until "Ka'asher yichalnu lach"; "Yehi shem" until "Al hashamayim kevodo"; "Baruch Hashem Elokim" until "Amen va'amen"; "Vayevarech David" until "Leshem tifarteicha"; "Atah Hu Hashem levadecha" until "Bemayim azim"; "Vayoshea" until "Hashem yimloch." One may not interrupt at all between "Hashem malach, Hashem malach, Hashem yimloch le'olam va'ed," just as one may not interrupt between "Hashem Elokeichem" and "emes" — so it implies in Piskei Tosafos. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Abudraham: if it happens that he needs to interrupt, he says, "Baruch Hashem Elokei Yisrael min ha'olam ve'ad ha'olam," and all the people say Amen; "Halelu laHashem," etc.; "Baruch Hashem le'olam amen va'amen"; "Baruch Hashem miTziyon"; "Baruch Hashem Elokei Yisrael oseh nifla'os levado"; "Baruch shem kevodo," etc.; "Baruch Hashem Elokei Yisrael," etc.; "Baruch shem kevodo," etc., "et kol ha'aretz amen va'amen." And after he spoke, he returns to say these verses and completes from where he stopped, until here. Knesset HaGedolah wrote — this is its wording written in the margin: "This is an excessive leniency in my opinion, for the poskim did not write it," until here. It appears he understood that one may interrupt when saying these even for a discretionary matter, even not out of fear or honor. This is not clear, for the case discussed is where one may interrupt — and even so they say the verses; and so it implies in the Levush and in Lechem Chamudot that I will cite; and I saw that Kol Bo and Rashbatz, siman 200, wrote thus. The Levush wrote: for this reason these verses were established after every "Nishmas," etc., for most times when they interrupt, they interrupt there, until here. Lechem Chamudot challenged him, folio 39 — for one may not interrupt except when it happens out of fear or honor, and for every chance occurrence there is no fixed place and time. Therefore it seems to me the reason they were fixed in the zemirot is so people would be accustomed and know them by heart to say when needed; and they enacted them first as a blessing, since they speak in the language of blessings, until here. I wonder — for Abudraham wrote this reason in his wording: that we find these verses written at the end of Sefer Tehillim, and therefore they enacted saying them upon completing Tehillim after every "Nishmas," etc., until here; and Magen Avraham wrote similarly and expanded further on the reasons; see there. The Levush wrote that one should not say "Mi yemalel gevuros" before the zemirot, for it is like blasphemy — afterward one wants [to praise God]. But after finishing the zemirot it is permitted; and so wrote Mateh Moshe in the name of Rashal. And they say "Bagoyim Hashem malach" — the bet is rafeh; "Refui" — the mem is rafeh; "Velamed" — with kamatz; "Veyish'acha titein" — the yod with segol.
