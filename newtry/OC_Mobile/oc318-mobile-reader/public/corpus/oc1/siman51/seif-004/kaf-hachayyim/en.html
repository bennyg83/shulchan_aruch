7) [Seif 4] One must take care not to interrupt by speaking, etc. Baruch SheAmar is the blessing before Pesukei DeZimrah, and Yishtabach is the concluding blessing. Tur; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 4. See below at the beginning of siman 54. It is forbidden to interrupt even in Hebrew. Wherever interruption is forbidden, it is forbidden even in the holy tongue. Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 66; Siddur Beit Yaakov, ot 5; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 14, ot 1. See below, ot 15 and siman 61, ot 1, and above, siman 25, ot 53. If one transgressed and spoke between Baruch SheAmar and Pesukei DeZimrah or during Pesukei DeZimrah, he need not repeat the blessing of Baruch SheAmar. This follows from Maran's wording, 'One must take care, etc.,' which implies that this is a caution but does not require repeating the blessing after an interruption. It is unlike blessings over mitzvos and enjoyment: there the blessing was instituted for that mitzvah or enjoyment, whereas Baruch SheAmar was not instituted because of the zemiros. It was instituted on the basis of a note that fell from Heaven and was merely positioned before the zemiros. Understand this carefully. This is unlike Zechor LeYitzchak Harari, simanim 7 and 8.
8) There: One must take care not to interrupt, etc. Abudraham writes that when Maharam needed to interrupt between Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach, before speaking he would recite these verses: 'Blessed is Hashem, God of Israel, from eternity to eternity; and all the people said Amen, and praise Hashem'; 'Blessed is Hashem forever, Amen and Amen'; 'Blessed is Hashem from Zion'; 'Blessed is Hashem God, the God of Israel'; and 'Blessed is His glorious Name forever,' etc. When resuming from the place where he stopped, he would recite these verses again, because they resemble a blessing. Beit Yosef; Bach; Taz, se'if katan 2; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 4; see Taz; Solet Belulah, ot 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 5; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67c. Knesset HaGedolah writes in Hagahot Beit Yosef: 'In my opinion this is an excessive leniency. Come and see that the poskim did not record it.' This is a manuscript gloss. Eliyah Rabbah and Shalmei Tzibbur cite it there. Eliyah Rabbah explains: Knesset HaGedolah apparently understood that reciting these verses permits an interruption for an optional matter, even when not required because of fear or honor. This is not persuasive. The discussion concerns a situation in which interruption is already permitted, and even then the verses are recited. This is also implied by Levush and Lechem Chamudos. So write Solet Belulah there; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 2; Maamar Mordechai, ot 3; Shalmei Tzibbur there; and Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 3. When interrupting for Kaddish, Kedushah, or the like, where interruption is permitted, one need not recite these verses, for the rule cannot be stricter than Keriat Shema and its blessings. This is our widespread custom. Maamar Mordechai, Shalmei Tzibbur, and Shaarei Teshuvah there.
9) There: One must take care not to interrupt, etc. Solet Belulah cites the following Midrash: R' Yose said, 'Once I was traveling and encountered Eliyahu, of blessed memory, accompanied by four thousand laden camels. I asked him, “What are these carrying?” He answered, “Wrath and fury, to exact retribution with wrath and fury from one who speaks between Kadosh and Baruch, between one blessing and another, between one section and another, between Amen Yehei Shemei Rabbah and Yitbarach, or between redemption and prayer. Moreover, his prayer is not heard, as stated: ‘But you did not call upon Me, Yaakov.’”' Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67b, writes likewise. See Beit Yosef, siman 56, and the later authorities there, who also cite this Midrash.
