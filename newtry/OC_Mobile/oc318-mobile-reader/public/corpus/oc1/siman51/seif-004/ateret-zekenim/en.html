One must be careful not to interrupt etc.; it is more severe than between Yishtabach and Yotzer Or (Kol Bo siman 4). From when one begins Baruch she'amar he should cast his eyes downward and not look outside except within four cubits until after Nefilat Apayim, which is the conclusion of the individual [prayer]. He should strive to have a siddur from which to pray from Baruch she'amar onward and not say by heart. He should unify his words to Him, and every time he mentions the name Adonai he should intend the name Y-H-V-H through that hidden and concealed [unification]. When he must interrupt between Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach he says these verses: "Blessed be the Lord forever, Amen and Amen" until "And David blessed"; and when he returns to begin from where he left off he says these verses as well, for it is like a blessing—and it is a blessing at the beginning and end of all the zemirot. One must intend especially in the eighteen blessings and remove every other thought, which is a kelipah between the Holy One blessed be He and His Shechinah; for since one unifies, special intention is required without any other thought interrupting. If he did not do so, it is better for him that he not have been created—and the Holy One blessed be He said, "Write this man childless" (Zohar parashat Pekudei; Tzitzuat Chaim).
