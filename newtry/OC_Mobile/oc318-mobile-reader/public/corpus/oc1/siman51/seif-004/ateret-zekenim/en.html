One must be careful not to interrupt, etc. An interruption here is more severe than an interruption between Yishtabach and Yotzer Or. Kol Bo, siman 4. From the time one begins Baruch SheAmar, he should keep his eyes lowered and not look beyond four amos until after Tachanun, which completes the individual's prayer. He should endeavor to have a siddur from which to pray from Baruch SheAmar onward, and should not recite it by heart. He should direct all his words to Hashem. Whenever he mentions the Divine Name Adonai, he should intend the combined Name represented by the letters yud-alef-hei-dalet-vav-nun-hei-yud, through the hidden and concealed One. When he must interrupt between Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach, he should say the verses beginning, “Blessed is Hashem forever, amen and amen,” through “And David blessed.” When he returns to continue from where he stopped, he should again say these verses. They function like a blessing, providing a blessing at the beginning and end of all the praises. He must concentrate especially during the eighteen blessings and remove every other thought, which forms a barrier between the Holy One, blessed is He, and His Shechinah. Since he is effecting unification, he requires an exclusive concentration without any foreign thought interrupting. If he does not do this, it would have been better for him not to have been created. Concerning such a person the Holy One, blessed is He, says, “Write this man childless.” Zohar, parashas Pekudei, and Totzaos Chayim.
