(6) To be careful. Baruch She'amar is the blessing before Pesukei DeZimrah, and Yishtabach is the blessing afterward. Interruption between Yishtabach and Yotzer is also forbidden, as explained below in siman 54, and from Yotzer onward one is within the blessings of Krias Shema. Responsa Mekor Baruch, siman 1, writes that the custom of some communities to interrupt between Baruch She'amar and Yotzer in order to bring a bridegroom into the synagogue should be abolished, and that this custom has insufficient force to be followed. See there.
(7) From interrupting. Wherever interruption is forbidden, speaking is forbidden even in the sacred tongue. When one must interrupt because of unavoidable circumstances, or in a case like that of seif 5, before speaking he should recite the verses beginning, “Blessed is Hashem forever, Amen and Amen,” through “And David blessed.” When he returns to the place where he stopped, he should again recite these verses, because they are comparable to a blessing before and after.
(8) By speaking. Even “Blessed is He and blessed is His Name” may not be said, since this response is not mentioned in the Gemara. Amen, however, may be answered to any blessing one hears, even in the middle of a verse of Pesukei DeZimrah, provided he is at a point where the subject has concluded. Likewise, he may recite any blessing of thanksgiving, such as Asher Yatzar after relieving himself, and the like. He may also answer Modim D'Rabbanan and recite Krias Shema with the congregation, for which he must recite the first verse with them. All the more so, he may interrupt for Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barechu.
(9) The end of Shemoneh Esrei. When Tachanun is recited, one should not speak until after Tachanun. This is stated by Eliyah Rabbah and Chayei Adam in the name of Shitah Mekubetzes.
(10) For the sake of a mitzvah. It is obvious that if one fears that the time for Krias Shema will pass before he reaches it in the order of prayer, and he forgot to recite it before Baruch She'amar, he may interrupt and recite it. Likewise, if he forgot to recite Birkat HaTorah before prayer, he may recite it during Pesukei DeZimrah and afterward say the customary verses, because some hold that even Pesukei DeZimrah may not be recited before Birkat HaTorah. One who is in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah should not initially be called to the Torah, except when he is a kohen and no other kohen is present, or a levi and he is the only levi. He may read quietly together with the Torah reader, but he should not interrupt to ask the gabbai to recite a Mi Sheberach. If the gabbai began it on his own, forgot his name, and asks him, he may answer because of honor. If he is near the end of a section and can finish it without delay before going up, he should do so. If finishing would require delay, he should not wait, because of the burden upon the congregation. This is from Shaarei Ephraim. One may not recite the verse “Who can recount the mighty acts of Hashem,” etc., after Baruch She'amar and before Hodu. Some have the practice of reciting it after completing the Tehillim. Some recite Kaddish before Baruch She'amar, and others before Hodu.
(11) Until Yishtabach. The permissibility of speaking for the sake of a mitzvah between Yishtabach and Yotzer is discussed at the end of siman 54, where some authorities permit it. See reference 100.
