(6) Be careful. Baruch SheAmar is the opening blessing of Pesukei DeZimrah and Yishtabach the concluding blessing. Interruption between Yishtabach and Yotzer is forbidden, and from Yotzer onward one is within the blessings of Krias Shema. A custom of interrupting to bring a bridegroom into the synagogue between Baruch SheAmar and Yotzer should be abolished.
(7) Not to interrupt. Wherever interruption is forbidden, even Hebrew speech is forbidden. When one must interrupt because of unavoidable circumstances, he should first say the verses beginning “Blessed is Hashem forever, amen and amen” through “And David blessed.” When he resumes, he should repeat these verses, because they function like a blessing before and after the praises.
(8) By speaking. Even “Blessed is He and blessed is His Name” should not be said. Amen may be answered to any blessing one hears, even in the middle of a verse, provided it is at the end of a subject. One may also recite Asher Yatzar, answer Modim DeRabbanan, recite the first verse of Krias Shema with the congregation, and certainly interrupt for Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barechu.
(9) The end of the Amidah. Where Tachanun is recited, one should not speak until after Tachanun.
(10) For the sake of a mitzvah. If the time for Krias Shema may pass before he reaches it, and he forgot to recite it before Baruch SheAmar, he may interrupt and recite it. If he forgot Birchas HaTorah, he may recite it during Pesukei DeZimrah and afterward say the customary verses. One should not initially call to the Torah a person in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah, except a kohen or levi when no other is present. He may read quietly with the reader but should not request a Mi SheBerach. If the chazzan asks his name, he may answer. If he can finish the section without delaying the congregation, he should do so; otherwise he should not delay. One should not say “Who can recount the mighty acts of Hashem,” etc., after Baruch SheAmar before Hodu. Some recite it after the psalms. Some say Kaddish before Baruch SheAmar and others before Hodu.
(11) To Yishtabach. The law of interrupting for a mitzvah between Yishtabach and Yotzer is explained at the end of siman 54, where some permit it.
