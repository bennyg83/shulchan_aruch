(6) To be careful — for Baruch She'amar is a blessing before Pesukei Dezimra, and Yishtabach is a blessing after it; and between Yishtabach and Yotzer it is forbidden to interrupt, as below in section 54; and from Yotzer onward they are the blessings of Keriat Shema. He wrote in the responsum Makor Baruch, section 1, that it is fit to abolish the custom that some practice of interrupting in order to bring the groom to the synagogue between Baruch She'amar and Yotzer, and this custom has no proper strength to follow it — see there.

(7) From interrupting — in any place where it is forbidden to interrupt, even in the holy tongue it is forbidden to speak. And when it is necessary to interrupt on account of some constraint or weeping — in the manner of seif 5 — one must say before speaking these verses: "Blessed is the the Omnipresent forever, Amen and Amen," etc., until "And David blessed"; and when he returns to begin from the place where he stopped, he says these verses as well, for they are like a blessing before and after them.

(8) In speech — and even Baruch Hu and Baruch She'amar it is forbidden to say, since it was not mentioned in the Gemara; but Amen is permitted to answer to any blessing one hears, even in the middle of a verse of Pesukei Dezimra if it is at a place where the matter is concluded; and likewise any blessing of thanks one may bless, such as "Who formed" after performing one's needs, and the like; and likewise to answer Modim derabbanan; and likewise to answer Keriat Shema with the congregation, for one must read the first verse with them — he reads; and all the more so that it is permitted to interrupt for Kaddish, Kedushah, and Barchu.

(9) The end of the Eighteen [Benedictions] — and when there is bowing [of the head], he should not speak until after the bowing [Eliyah Rabbah and Chayei Adam in the name of Shitah Mekubetzet]:

(10) For the sake of a mitzvah — it is plain that if one fears the time of Keriat Shema will pass before he reaches reciting it in the order of prayer and forgot to say it before Baruch She'amar, he is permitted to interrupt and recite it; and likewise if he forgot to bless Birchas HaTorah before prayer, he is permitted to bless in the middle of Pesukei Dezimra and afterward say the customary verses. Some say (11) it is forbidden to say even Pesukei Dezimra before Birchas HaTorah. One should not call up to the Torah lekhatchilah someone standing in the middle of Pesukei Dezimra, only for a kohen if there is no other kohen, and likewise for a Levi if only he is there; and he may read in a whisper with the reader, but he should not interrupt to tell the cantor to do Mi Shebeirach; and if the cantor began on his own and forgot his name and asks him, it is permitted to answer him out of respect. And if he is standing near a chapter where he can finish until the end of the chapter without delay before ascending, he should do so; but if delay is needed for this, he should not wait because of trouble to the congregation [other later authorities]. It is forbidden to say the verse "Who can recount the mighty acts of the Omnipresent," etc., after Baruch She'amar before Hodu; and after finishing the psalms some have the custom to say it.
