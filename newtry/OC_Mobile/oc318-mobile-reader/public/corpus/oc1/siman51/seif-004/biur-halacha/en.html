One must be careful, etc. — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote, that interrupting for Amen is permitted only where the matter is concluded — that is, from Chayei Adam. And although this ruling is not clear even regarding the Shema blessing, as we wrote below in siman 66 in Biur Halacha, nevertheless it is proper to rule thus — that this ruling too, permitting one to answer Amen in the middle of pesukei dezimra, is not so entirely clear; for in truth, although Baruch she'amar is not mentioned in the Talmud, it is nevertheless an ancient enactment from the days of Knesset HaGedolah and is mentioned in the holy Zohar, and the Yishtabach blessing is mentioned in Yerushalmi, as the Shach said between Yishtabach and Yotzer, and as all the later authorities wrote. Nevertheless, regarding Kaddish, Kedushah, Barchu, Modim, Amen de'elokeinu, and Amen of Shema Koleinu — it seems to me obvious that in pesukei dezimra one may interrupt for all of these even where the matter is not concluded. And what I further wrote — that one may interrupt for Yotzer — is from Chayei Adam. And although Chayei Adam wrote that one may recite this blessing after prayer, this is not clear, lest he then need to go out again and the blessing be invalidated; it is better that he refrain from reciting it until between the sections.

And even for the sake of a mitzvah — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote, that one should not call up to the Torah lechat'chilah; this is from Sha'arei Efraim and the Peri Megadim and Chayei Adam, and not like Chayei Adam who wrote that one may call up lechat'chilah during pesukei dezimra; and possibly his intent was also when there is no other kohen present. And what he further wrote — that one may say along with the reader in a whisper — so wrote Sha'ar Efraim; and although Chayei Adam is strict about this, the correct view is as Sha'ar Efraim; for even regarding the Shema blessing the Peri Megadim concludes in siman 66, ot het, that this matter is in doubt lest there be concern for a berachah levatalah through this, and all the more so here one should not be strict about it. And what I further wrote — that if the chazan asks those questions, one may answer him — is obvious.
