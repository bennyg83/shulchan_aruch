(Se'if vav) One must repeat — and the reason the Sages valued saying Tehillah leDavid, as written in se'if shin-alef afterward: because it is said in alphabetical order at the heads of the verses; and also this psalm contains "Pote'ach et yadecha," which is great praise that He sustains and nourishes all worlds, great and small.

As written in siman 101 — for although by law, if one did not have intent in the blessing of the Fathers of the eighteen blessings, he must return and pray; nevertheless, nowadays we do not repeat, for even upon repetition he is likely not to have intent unintentionally.

See section 63, section 64, as should be. That is, he wrote there that if one did not have proper intent in the first verse of Keriat Shema, one must go back and say Shema Yisrael as well. What is the reason? Since it is only one verse.
