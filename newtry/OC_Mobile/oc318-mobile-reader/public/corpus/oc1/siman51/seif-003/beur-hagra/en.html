Siman 63: after etc.—there, and as Rashi and Tosafot explained in the name of R' Chananel and Bahag and all the poskim; and as the Rosh wrote: one may not say specifically after Boneh Yerushalayim because of laborers—for R' Eliezer answered him in a whisper; and in Yerushalmi chapter 5 it teaches: one who breaks [the Shema], one who passes before the ark, one who lifts his hands, one who reads the Torah, one who reads the haftarah, one who blesses on any of the mitzvot stated in the Torah—should not answer Amen after himself; and if he answered, behold he is a boor. There is a version that teaches "this one is a boor" and a version that teaches "this one is wise." R' Chisda said: one who says he is wise—by answering at the end; and one who says he is a boor—by answering on every blessing and blessing—and they did not say in the Gemara "Boneh Yerushalayim" except as an extra case, even though HaTov veHaMeitiv follows it; and the Geonim's view is that after every conclusion of a matter one answers Amen—even after the final blessing on eating fruit. As they wrote, he is a boor because he interrupts; but Rashi's method is specifically at the conclusion of blessings, and likewise the conclusion of praises or prayer. And so the conclusion of Keriat Shema morning and evening; and so wrote Rosh and Mordechai—and the same for Yishtabach and Yehi chevod and the end of the eighteen blessings. Rambam wrote: specifically when one or more blessings preceded it—and he means that there were two or more blessings adjacent to each other. The second condition is that it be at the end of the matter, not at the beginning—as with the king's blessing and the high priest's blessing and the last blessings of Keriat Shema of the evening, unlike that of morning which has only one blessing; likewise the final blessing on fruits and the like; unlike the end of the two blessings before Keriat Shema, and likewise before the Megillah and the Chanukah candle which are at the beginning—and so that he not interrupt as above. R' Yitzchak wrote in the name of the Tosefta: after the mitzvot and after fruits one answers Amen at the end—behold this is the way of boors; except he explains that it refers to the blessing before them—but the language "at the end" implies Rambam's words apparently; except the aforementioned Yerushalmi that lists reading the Torah and blessing on mitzvot—apparently not like Rambam. For the Geonim one may say: the sons of the West bless "to observe His statutes"—but one may say "this one is wise" does not refer to all of them. And after the blessings of Keriat Shema of the evening, R' Yitzchak and Rosh wrote in a responsum—that is, after Shomer Amo etc., as with Boneh Yerushalayim in Beit HaMikdash. And as written below siman 236; and in siman 215 seif 1 it is stated simply like Rambam's view, except that he wrote "and they practiced" etc. Lechem Chamudot wrote that this follows another rationale—for certainly Rambam's reasoning is correct, specifically with adjacent blessings; therefore the Gemara cited Boneh Yerushalayim—except specifically regarding something whose beginning and end are not grasped together, where one may interrupt between them—unlike Yishtabach and Yehi chevod which are adjacent; and this is Tur's view: even though in siman 215 he wrote that the Rosh practiced like Rambam, nevertheless here he wrote to answer Amen after Yishtabach; and in siman 111 after Go'el Yisrael without dispute—and nevertheless they practiced not to answer Amen after Go'el Yisrael per the Zohar, as written below siman 111.

And see below etc.—for there he wrote that they practice as Tosafot and Mordechai wrote there—that they practiced not to answer except at Boneh Yerushalayim alone, and not by law but because they so practiced.
