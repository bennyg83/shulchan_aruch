Seif 3: After, etc. This follows Rashi, Tosafos in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, Baal Halachos Gedolos, and the poskim. The Yerushalmi distinguishes between answering amen after each blessing, which is ignorance, and answering at the conclusion of a series, which is wisdom. The Bavli mentions Boneh Yerushalayim as a novelty even though HaTov VeHaMeitiv follows it. The Geonim allow amen after the conclusion of any subject. Rashi limits this to the conclusion of blessings, praise, or prayer, including the blessings of Krias Shema. Rosh and Mordechai likewise include Yishtabach, Yehalelucha, and the end of the Amidah. Rambam requires at least two adjacent blessings and the conclusion of the subject. This excludes isolated blessings and opening blessings where amen would interrupt. Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh write that amen is answered after Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad at Maariv, like Boneh Yerushalayim. Shulchan Aruch in siman 215 rules like Rambam, though the custom differs. Rav Lechem Chamudos explains that Yishtabach and Yehalelucha are treated like adjacent blessings. Nevertheless, the custom is not to answer amen after Ga'al Yisrael, based upon the Zohar.
See below, etc. There it is written that the custom follows Tosafos and Mordechai, who say that amen is answered only after Boneh Yerushalayim. This is customary practice, not the strict law.
