The Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Abudraham that when Maharam needed to interrupt between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach, before speaking he would recite these verses: “Blessed is Hashem, the God of Israel, from eternity to eternity, and all the people said Amen and praised Hashem”; “Blessed is Hashem forever, Amen and Amen”; “Blessed is Hashem from Zion”; “Blessed is Hashem, the God, the God of Israel”; and “Blessed is His glorious Name forever,” etc. When he returned to begin from the place where he had stopped, he would also recite these verses, because they are like a blessing. End quote. The Levush writes that one should not say, “Who can recount the mighty acts of Hashem, or make all His praise heard?” before beginning, “Give thanks to Hashem, call upon His Name,” because it would then appear as though he were saying: Who can recount them other than one who is able to make them heard, and I will now make them heard. Rather, he should say this verse after the Tehillim, to indicate that there is still more to mention beyond what we have already recalled, but it is impossible to mention everything.
