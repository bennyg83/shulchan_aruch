After Yishtabach, etc. Because it is the conclusion of the blessing of song, and Amen indicates departure from the praise that was said. As Beit Yosef in the name of Abudraham: when the Rosh needed to interrupt between Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach he would say before speaking these verses—"Baruch the Omnipresent Elokei Yisrael min ha'olam ve'ad ha'olam," and all the people say Amen; "Halelu laHashem," etc.; "Baruch the Omnipresent le'olam amen va'amen"; "Baruch the Omnipresent miTziyon"; "Baruch the Omnipresent Elokei Yisrael"; "Baruch shem kevodo le'olam," etc.; and when he returned to begin from where he stopped he would say these verses as well, because they are like a blessing, until here. And he wrote in Levush: one should read "Mi yemalel gevurot the Omnipresent, yashmi'a kol tehilaso" before beginning "Hodu laHashem," for then it appears as one who says "Who can recount?"—only one who can make heard—and I will make heard; but after the psalms he says thus to hint that there is still more to mention apart from what we mentioned, and it is impossible to mention all of them. So too [writes] Magen Avraham.
