(Regarding) "And they say" — Taz: "Who is like You" with hard kaf [degusha], and likewise "like stone" and "You redeemed" — although they come after targum without cancelling because of these reasons; he wrote in Sefer Da'at Chochmah Mekor Chochmah in section 49:42 that one must pause between "in the waters" and "mighty," and there is a tipcha under "mighty waters" and a sof pasuk on "mighty" — as the Gemara states, "Adir will come and exact vengeance from mighty ones" — "mighty" refers to Egypt; and likewise in Midrash "the mighty sank like lead in the water" — end quote.

(Regarding) answering Amen and the intent therein — see siman 124:6; and there is a small booklet bound in my siddur where I gathered scattered laws regarding the synagogue — I will copy here a few words. (1) Amen has in it two intentions: either that I believe the one who blessed spoke truth and it is certain; and there is a second intention that I ask and pray the matter be fulfilled. And now in the Shemoneh Esreh, the first three are praise — the explanation of Amen "true and firm" is that it is so — he is not asking to fulfill our requests, lest we appear, God forbid, as apostates; for surely the dead will live too. And a blessing that is request alone — one may intend Amen yehi ratzon that we believe his words; and the blessing "You grant knowledge" begins with praise of the Place and ends with request "grant us from You," etc. — one must intend both intentions — Shlah, Ran, A; and Magen Avraham 124, letter 10, see there; and okhel chullin there. And in Sefer Mekor Chochmah section 46 he also copied this.

(Regarding) "And accordingly" — in Kaddish "yitgadal v'yitkadash" — the world answers Amen; the explanation is yehi ratzon that we believe the words of the speaker, and that is that it should be in our days, for otherwise we appear as apostates — for surely on that day (at the time of the end) Hashem will be One of necessity; and if so why do they say a second time Amen yehei shmei rabbah, etc. — Amen refers to what the blesser finished "be'it kalah," etc. — and why do we need both? Also we wrote there that one must pause between Amen and yehei shmei rabbah, and likewise Amen modim anachnu lach — for that is on the blessing and modim is a matter on its own — Magen Avraham siman 56, letter 7, and Shlah, Ran, and Ranah. (2) Amen — one should say Amen with kamatz gadol, omitting the aleph as though he said "I believe" — as one speaking for him: "I believe in it"; and even Amen after a request — one can say, perforce; and furthermore it is fine to apply "I believe Hashem will fulfill our requests if one prays with whole heart — Hashem is close to all who call Him in truth"; but in Eliyah Rabbah 124, letter 13, with chataf sefaradiya, see there. And behold even with kamatz chataf, that is small kamatz, we do not — only with kamatz gadol — okhel chullin, Levush there. And I will explain below in 56 and 57 and 124 more than that booklet.
