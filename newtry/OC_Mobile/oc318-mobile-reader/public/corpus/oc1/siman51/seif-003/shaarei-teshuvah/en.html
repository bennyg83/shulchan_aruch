In speech—Ba'er Heitev. See in responsum Penei Yehoshua part 1 volume 1 siman 5: a congregation that did not have a minyan before Baruch she'amar, and he began saying pesukei dezimrah, and ten came—it is forbidden to say Kaddish within pesukei dezimrah—see above; and they brought the Beit Yosef below siman 53, and the reason is explained in the responsum that saying Kaddish after Tehillim is not comparable to answering Kaddish and Barchu between chapters, for since there were not ten at the time of saying Tehillim they were not obligated; and even though the holy kavanot of the Ari z"l require Kaddish before Baruch she'amar even without Tehillim—this is not according to my view but only a mitzvah according to his holy kavanot, etc.; and also according to his kavanah the time has already passed if they did not say it before Baruch she'amar, and one who understands will understand, etc.; and see above that even before "And David blessed," which is the end of pesukei dezimrah, and likewise between the Song of the Sea and Yishtabach—one may not interrupt—see above. (3) In speech—Ba'er Heitev. And what is written, "One who must interrupt," etc.—in Knesset HaGedolah he wrote in the name of Hagahot Tikkun Yisrael that it is an excessive leniency; and in Sha'arei Teshuvah he wrote in the name of Maamar Mordechai that there, when one had to interrupt out of great necessity—that is what is discussed; but when he interrupts for Kaddish and Kedushah he need not say these verses at all—see above; and he wrote in Pri Chadash that one may not interrupt in a verse between the verses "Yehi kevod" and "Tehillah le-David" even on Shabbat; and also the verse "VeHu rachum" one says and one may not skip it on Shabbat and Yom Tov—not as She'elat Yaavetz wrote.
