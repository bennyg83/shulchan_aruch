Through speech. See Baer Heitev. Responsa Penei Yehoshua, part 1, siman 5, discusses a group that did not have a minyan before Baruch SheAmar. They began Pesukei DeZimrah, and then ten people arrived. They may not recite Kaddish in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah. Baer Heitev cites this below in siman 53. The reason is explained in the responsum. Reciting Kaddish after Tehillim is not comparable to answering Kaddish or Barechu between sections. Since there was no minyan when they recited Tehillim, no obligation of Kaddish arose. Although the holy Arizal requires Kaddish before Baruch SheAmar even without Tehillim, in my opinion that is only an ideal practice based upon his sacred intentions. According to those intentions, once its proper place before Baruch SheAmar has passed, the opportunity has already been missed. One who understands will understand.
The same responsum states that one may not interrupt even before Vayevarech David, which concludes Pesukei DeZimrah, nor between the Song at the Sea and Yishtabach.
Through speech. See Baer Heitev. Regarding Baer Heitev’s statement that one who must interrupt should recite the designated verses, Knesses HaGedolah cites a manuscript gloss that calls this an excessive leniency. Shalmei Tzibbur cites Maamar Mordechai that the source concerns a case of extremely great necessity. When interrupting for Kaddish or Kedushah, one need not recite these verses at all. Pri Etz Chayim writes that one should not interrupt with any verse between the verses beginning Yehi Chevod and Tehillah LeDavid, even on Shabbos. One should also recite the verse VeHu Rachum and not omit it on Shabbos or Yom Tov, unlike Sheilas Yaavetz.
