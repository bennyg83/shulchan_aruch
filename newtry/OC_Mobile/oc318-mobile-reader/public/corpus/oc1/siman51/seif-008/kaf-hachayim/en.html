(8) There — one must be careful not to interrupt, etc. Radak wrote that when the Rosh needed to interrupt between Baruch she'amar and Yishtabach, he would say before speaking these verses: "Baruch Hashem Elokei Yisrael min ha'olam ve'ad ha'olam," and all the people say Amen; "Halelu laHashem," etc.; "Baruch Hashem le'olam amen va'amen"; "Baruch Hashem miTziyon"; "Baruch Hashem Elokei Yisrael," etc.; "Baruch shem kevodo le'olam," etc. And when he returns to begin from where he stopped, he says these verses again, for they are like a blessing, until here — Beit Yosef, Bach; Taz, se'if bet; Magen Avraham, se'if dalet; Eliyah Rabbah, ot dalet; Eretz Tzvi; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot gimel; Razah, ot heh; Sha'arei Teshuvah on siman 67, amud gimel. Knesset HaGedolah wrote in Hagahos Beit Yosef: "This is an excessive leniency in my opinion; come and see that the poskim did not write it" — proofread 21, until here; and Eliyah Rabbah and Sha'arei Teshuvah cited it there. Eliyah Rabbah wrote there: it appears he understood that one may interrupt when saying these even for a discretionary matter, even not out of fear or honor. This is not clear, for the case is where one may interrupt — and even so they say the verses; and so in the Levush and Lechem Chamudot, until here; see there; and so Shulchan Aruch HaRav there. Peri Megadim in Mishbetzos Zahav, ot bet; Ma'amar Mordechai, ot gimel; Sha'arei Teshuvah there; Sha'arei Teshuvah, ot gimel. When interrupting for Kaddish, Kedushah, and the like — where interruption is permitted — he need not say these verses, for it is not as strict as Shema and its blessings. And so is the simple custom among us — Ma'amar Mordechai there; Sha'arei Teshuvah there; Sha'arei Teshuvah there.
