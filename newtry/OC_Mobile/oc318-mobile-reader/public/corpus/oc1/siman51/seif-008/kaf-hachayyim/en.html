8) There: One must take care not to interrupt, etc. Abudraham writes that when Maharam needed to interrupt between Baruch SheAmar and Yishtabach, before speaking he would recite these verses: 'Blessed is Hashem, God of Israel, from eternity to eternity; and all the people said Amen, and praise Hashem'; 'Blessed is Hashem forever, Amen and Amen'; 'Blessed is Hashem from Zion'; 'Blessed is Hashem God, the God of Israel'; and 'Blessed is His glorious Name forever,' etc. When resuming from the place where he stopped, he would recite these verses again, because they resemble a blessing. Beit Yosef; Bach; Taz, se'if katan 2; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 4; see Taz; Solet Belulah, ot 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 5; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67c. Knesset HaGedolah writes in Hagahot Beit Yosef: 'In my opinion this is an excessive leniency. Come and see that the poskim did not record it.' This is a manuscript gloss. Eliyah Rabbah and Shalmei Tzibbur cite it there. Eliyah Rabbah explains: Knesset HaGedolah apparently understood that reciting these verses permits an interruption for an optional matter, even when not required because of fear or honor. This is not persuasive. The discussion concerns a situation in which interruption is already permitted, and even then the verses are recited. This is also implied by Levush and Lechem Chamudos. So write Solet Belulah there; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzot Zahav, ot 2; Maamar Mordechai, ot 3; Shalmei Tzibbur there; and Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 3. When interrupting for Kaddish, Kedushah, or the like, where interruption is permitted, one need not recite these verses, for the rule cannot be stricter than Keriat Shema and its blessings. This is our widespread custom. Maamar Mordechai, Shalmei Tzibbur, and Shaarei Teshuvah there.
