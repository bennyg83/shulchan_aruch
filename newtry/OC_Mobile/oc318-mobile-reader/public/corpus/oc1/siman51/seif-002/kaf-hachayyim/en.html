3) [Seif 2] If he completed Baruch SheAmar before the chazzan completed it, etc. This implies that if he completed it simultaneously with the chazzan, he should not answer Amen, because he would be answering Amen after his own blessing. So write Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 2; Solet Belulah, Mekor Chayyim, ot 1; Halacha Berurah, ot 1; Birkei Yosef, ot 2; Kesher Gudal, siman 7, ot 26; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67a; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 3; Siddur Beit Yaakov, ot 11; and Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Vayigash, ot 8.
4) There: He answers Amen after him. If possible, it is preferable that he also begin Mizmor LeTodah and then answer Amen, so that he does not interrupt between the blessing and that to which the blessing applies. Chesed LaAlafim, ot 3.
5) If the chazzan concluded one blessing while he himself concluded a different blessing, he may answer Amen. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Solet Belulah, ot 2. Yad Aharon, Hagahot HaTur, however, disputes Magen Avraham and writes that according to R' Chaim Shabtai there appears to be no distinction between identical blessings and different blessings: in every case it appears that he answers Amen after his own blessing. Maamar Mordechai, ot 1, appears to agree. Birkei Yosef, ot 3, however, rules like Magen Avraham and explicitly rejects Yad Aharon. He rules likewise in Kesher Gudal, siman 7, ot 26. The annotator to Shalmei Tzibbur, page 67a, writes likewise. See there, where he describes Maamar Mordechai's reading as very strained and concludes: According to those who require him to answer Amen, if he does not answer, his sin is exceedingly great, as the punishment for neglecting Amens is known. According to the view that he should not answer, the concern is only that he appears to answer Amen after his own blessing. That is not such a severe prohibition; they said only that it is unseemly. Peri Chadash writes that one who answers Amen after his own blessings acts unseemly, but he is not silenced, as stated in the chapter Ein Omdin; see there. Accordingly, in our doubtful case he should answer Amen because of the severity of omitting it, and there is not even anything unseemly about doing so. Shaarei Teshuvah, ot 2, writes likewise.
