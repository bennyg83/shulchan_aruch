If he finished — see in the Mishna Berurah what he wrote regarding if he did not finish, etc.; so it implies from the plain meaning of the wording, and so it implies in the Beit Yosef. See in the Peri Megadim, for it implies from his words that according to what the Magen Avraham concludes in se'if kaf-gimmel — that one may answer Amen in the middle of Baruch she'amar — likewise one may answer this Amen. In my opinion, however, this is not clear at all: regarding this Amen, either way — if we consider this a blessing, he would need to answer Amen after it, and then he may not interrupt in the middle; whereas regarding other Amens we say that since this blessing was not mentioned in the Gemara, he need not refrain from answering Amen on its account. The Peri Megadim's subtle point can be resolved easily; see in siman 59 in the Beit Yosef there and in siman 61 in the Tur. Nevertheless, one can reconcile the Peri Megadim's words, for he follows the Magen Avraham, and the Magen Avraham holds later in siman 66, se'if kaf-yud-alef, that even in the middle of a section one may answer Amen whenever the Amen is from that very blessing. But the Peri Megadim and all the later authorities there have already challenged him, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote there — that if one answers Amen before he finishes the blessing, it is like interrupting in the middle of a blessing. If so, it is obvious that the same applies here: although Baruch she'amar is lighter than the Shema blessing, its Amen is also lighter, as stated.

Baruch she'amar, etc. — The same applies in the Yishtabach blessing: if he is standing in the middle of the blessing, he may not answer Amen to this blessing after the chazan finishes, as stated. The blessing begins from the start of the Yishtabach blessing, as is clear in siman 54, se'if alef; see there in Biur Halacha.

The chazan — he mentioned the usual case, and the same applies if he heard from other people as well: he must answer Amen to each one.

He answers after him — see in the Beit Yosef, who concludes that he is not obligated to hurry in order to finish and answer; rather, if it happens that he finished, he answers Amen.
