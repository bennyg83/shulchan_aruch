If he finished. See Mishnah Berurah. If he had not finished, he should not answer amen. Peri Megadim implies otherwise according to Magen Avraham, but this is not correct. If this amen is considered necessary for the blessing, it may not interrupt the blessing itself. Other amens are different because Baruch SheAmar is not mentioned in the Gemara. Peri Megadim and the later authorities reject Magen Avraham's view that one may answer an amen belonging to that very blessing before completing it. Such an amen is an interruption. The same applies here.
Baruch SheAmar, etc. The same applies to Yishtabach. If one is in the middle of the blessing, he should not answer amen when the chazzan concludes it.
The chazzan. This is the usual case. The same law applies if he hears the blessing from other people; he answers amen to each one.
He answers after him. Beit Yosef concludes that one need not hurry in order to finish and answer amen. Only if he happens to finish then should he answer.
