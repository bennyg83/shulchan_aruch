(2) If he finished. If he did not finish, he should not answer amen. This applies only once he has begun “Blessed are You, Hashem,” etc.; before that point the passage is merely praise. This concerns amen after Baruch SheAmar or Yishtabach. Other amens may be answered even in the middle of these blessings, since they are not mentioned in the Gemara. However, after “Blessed are You, Hashem,” before completing “King praised through praises,” he may not answer, because he would disrupt the blessing.
(3) The chazzan. If he finishes together with the chazzan, he should not answer amen, because it appears that he is answering after his own blessing. The same applies to all blessings, except Yishtabach, Yehalelucha after Hallel, and Shomer Amo Yisrael La'ad. In those cases, one may rely upon the authorities who permit answering amen after one's own blessing. If he concludes one blessing while the chazzan concludes another, he may answer amen.
(4) Amen after him. This is not an interruption between the blessing and Pesukei DeZimrah, because both Pesukei DeZimrah and amen are forms of praise. If he spoke idle words between Baruch SheAmar and Hodu, he may need to repeat the blessing. Even one word is an interruption. Accordingly, one should also avoid an extended silent delay.
