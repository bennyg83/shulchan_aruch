(2) If he finished — but if he did not finish, he should not answer Amen; and it is plain that specifically from the time he began "Blessed are You, Lord," etc., for at first it is mere praise. And all this is specifically regarding the Amen of Baruch She'amar or of Yishtabach; but other Amens, Magen Avraham concludes in SA note 3 that it is permitted to answer even in the middle of the blessing of Baruch She'amar or Yishtabach, since this blessing was not mentioned in the Gemara; however, if he is standing after the words "Blessed are You, Lord" before he finishes "King, Who is extolled with praises," Chayei Adam wrote that it is forbidden for him to answer, for thereby he completely ruins the blessing.

(3) The cantor — but if he finished with the cantor in one breath, he should not answer Amen, for it appears like answering Amen after his own blessings; and we rule that this is repugnant; and likewise in all blessings the law is thus, except for Yishtabach or "Praise God" after Hallel, or after "Who keeps His people Israel forever," if he finished together with the prayer leader, or if another person answers Amen — for many decisors hold that in this one answers even after his own blessing; and even though we do not practice thus, as written in section 215, nevertheless in such a case one may certainly rely on this; and if he finished one blessing — whichever blessing it may be — and the prayer leader another blessing, it is permitted to answer Amen [Magen Avraham and Shulchan Aruch HaRav; and so is implied by the explanation of the Gra, not like R. Aharon].

(4) After him, Amen — and it is not an interruption between the blessing and speech, for Pesukei Dezimra is praise and Amen is praise and like a song, and it is not an interruption. And if he spoke idle words between Baruch She'amar and Hodu, it is possible that he must repeat and recite the blessing on account of interruption; and even one word is an interruption [Peri Megadim]; and it seems that therefore even in silence it is good to be careful initially not to wait long between them [see section 206 in Magen Avraham, SA note 4, and in the novelae of R. Akiva Eiger there].
