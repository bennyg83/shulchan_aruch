(Regarding) "After" — Taz (textual variant: "and not" etc.); and we rule in section 266 that one answers only in Barchu etc. to interrupt between Torah and rabbinic (and even if one ate a rabbinic measure, some say); and although all of it in the name of R. Shemaiah — Beit Yosef brought that here too, between Yishtabach which is rabbinic and the blessings of Keriat Shema which are d'oraita — behold the blessings of Keriat Shema are also rabbinic, and even though Keriat Shema is d'oraita the blessings are rabbinic — and there is no reason to give an interruption between a matter one blesses upon rabbinically and a matter one blesses upon d'oraita alone in Barchu etc., as said. And what is written in the name of Abudraham — that is when he interrupts under constraint or where it is permitted to interrupt as explained, but without constraint, even though he says these verses, it is forbidden to interrupt; and likewise Eliyah Rabbah, and it is plain.
