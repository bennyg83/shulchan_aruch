After. See Taz. The word “and” is a scribal error and should be omitted. We rule in siman 215 that one answers amen after one's own blessing only in Bircas HaMazon, to mark the division between Torah-level and Rabbinic blessings. Even if one ate only a Rabbinically significant amount, there is no distinction. Kol Bo in the name of Rabbi Shemaya, cited by Beit Yosef, writes that here too one answers amen between Yishtabach, which is Rabbinic, and the blessings of Krias Shema, which are Torah-level. This is difficult. The blessings of Krias Shema are themselves Rabbinic, even though Krias Shema is D'Oraisa. There is no reason to make a division between a Rabbinic blessing and a Torah mitzvah over which a Rabbinic blessing is recited. This applies only in Bircas HaMazon, as stated. What Magen Avraham cites from Abudraham applies where one must interrupt because of unavoidable circumstances or where interruption is otherwise permitted, as in seif 4. Without such a reason, interruption remains forbidden even if one recites those verses. Eliyah Rabbah writes likewise, and this is obvious.
