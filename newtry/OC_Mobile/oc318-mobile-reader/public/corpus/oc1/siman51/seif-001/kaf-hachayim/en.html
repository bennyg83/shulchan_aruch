(1) [Se'if 1] They say Baruch she'amar, etc. — and one must say it with melody and pleasantness, for it is a beautiful and pleasant song. He wrote in Sefer Heichalot that it has eighty-seven words, and the sign "His head is fine gold." Tur. Toldot Yaakov wrote per Or Zarua that the Men of the Great Assembly enacted this praise based on a note that fell from heaven; they found written on it that it has eighty-seven words, and they received [the practice] to say it standing, etc., until here — Bach, siman 53; Taz, se'if kaf-alef; Eliyah Rabbah, ot alef; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot alef; Sha'arei Teshuvah on siman 66, amud dalet. Shulchan Aruch HaRav wrote there, citing Sefer HaGan: "In Beit Yosef there are eighty-seven words, to inform that whoever says Baruch she'amar with a pleasant voice and intent — the Holy One blessed be He makes for him a crown of gold, as it says 'His head is fine gold,'" until here. With his mouth, with segol. In "beteshbachot" with chirik; and he holds the two tzitziyot of the tallit that are opposite his face at the time of saying Baruch she'amar — Magen Avraham, se'if kaf-alef, in the name of the kavanos; and Bach above, siman 48; see there. And so wrote the later authorities. See Mechaber in Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, ot dalet, and Bach in Sha'arei Teshuvah on siman 66, amud bet; see there.
