1) [Seif 1] We recite Baruch SheAmar, etc. It should be recited melodiously and pleasantly, for it is a beautiful and delightful song. Sefer Heichalot writes that it contains eighty-seven words, for which the mnemonic is, 'His head is fine gold.' Tur. Tola'at Yaakov writes in the name of Or Zarua that the Men of the Great Assembly instituted this praise based upon a note that fell from Heaven and was found bearing this text. It contains eighty-seven words, and they received a tradition to recite it while standing, etc. Bach, siman 53; Taz, se'if katan 1; Eliyah Rabbah, ot 1; Solet Belulah, ot 1; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 66d. Solet Belulah writes there in the name of Sefer HaGan: 'Baruch SheAmar contains eighty-seven words, teaching that whoever recites it in a pleasant voice and with concentration, the Holy One, blessed is He, makes him a crown of fine gold, as stated: “His head is fine gold.”' The word b'fi in 'b'fi amo' has a segol, and betishbachot has a chirik. While reciting Baruch SheAmar, one should hold the two tzitzis of the tallis that are in front of him. Magen Avraham, se'if katan 1, in the name of Kavanot HaArizal. This was cited above in siman 48; see there. So write the later authorities. See Machazik Berachah, Kuntres Acharon, ot 4, cited by Shalmei Tzibbur, page 66b; see there.
2) There: We recite Baruch SheAmar, etc. The eighty-seven-word text of Baruch SheAmar recorded above in siman 48 in Arizal's name must never be changed: not on a weekday, Shabbos, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, or Yom Kippur, because it contains hidden mysteries, as stated in Shaar HaKavanot, Discourse on the Morning Prayer, page 17c. So write Mishnat Chasidim, Tefillat HaYetzirah, chapter 1, ot 4; Yad Aharon, Hagahot HaTur; Peri Chadash, ot 1; Chida in Tziporen Shamir, siman 2, ot 19; Divrei Menachem, Hagahot Beit Yosef, ot 6, in the name of R' Avraham Nachum; and Chesed LaAlafim, ot 2; see there. This is unlike Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, who cites Abudraham that on Shabbos one says, 'Blessed is He Who grants rest,' etc., and on Yom Tov, 'Blessed is He Who gave appointed festivals,' etc. Chida writes in Machazik Berachah, Kuntres Acharon, siman 51, ot 2, that even if the congregation recites another text, he alone should recite the aforementioned established text, for its interior is filled with awesome mysteries. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 66b, cites this. So Chida writes in Tziporen Shamir there and Kesher Gudal, siman 7, ot 25; see also Siddur Beit Yaakov, ot 2.
