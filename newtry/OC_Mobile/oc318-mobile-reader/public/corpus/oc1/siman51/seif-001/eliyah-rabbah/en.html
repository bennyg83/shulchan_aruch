(1) Baruch SheAmar, etc. The mnemonic “His head is fine gold” indicates that the opening blessing should contain eighty-seven words. It should be recited with melody and pleasantness. Sefer Heichalos writes that the Men of the Great Assembly composed this praise based upon a note that fell from Heaven and was found with this text written upon it. Tolas Yaakov writes that they received a tradition that it must be recited while standing, to teach that it corresponds to an exalted form, as the verse states, “Seraphim stood above Him.” Bach at the beginning of siman 53 writes likewise, and this is the custom, as explained below at the end of the siman. Lechem Chamudos, page 19, writes that the original text was “And we shall crown You and mention Your Name, our King.” It was later emended to “And we shall mention Your Name and crown You, our King,” to connect the language of kingship to “our King.” The original wording is preferable, because it groups four praises together: “We shall exalt You, praise You, glorify You, and crown You,” corresponding exactly to the four names of the supernal chariot. The siddur of Mahari Luria gives the following vocalization and directions: the word habriyos is pronounced with a sheva, chirik, and cholam; one must say “with the mouth of His people,” because this completes the count of eighty-seven words; in betishbachos, the tav has a chirik; and during Baruch SheAmar one should hold the two front tzitzis. Abudraham writes that one should not say “the God, the merciful Father,” but only “the merciful Father,” because we already said “our God.” The same principle applies to the blessing after the Megillah, “Who fights our fight.” It therefore appears to me that one should say “Blessed is He Who performs the act of Creation,” because the word maaseh completes the count of eighty-seven in place of the omitted word HaKel. Mateh Moshe, however, writes in the name of Maharam that maaseh is omitted so as not to add to the count. Since our text is otherwise missing one word, it appears proper to complete it with maaseh.
