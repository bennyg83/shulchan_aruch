(1) Baruch she'amar, etc. — And the sign: "His head is fine gold" — meaning the first blessing should be eighty-seven words, and one must say it with melody and pleasantness. He wrote in Sefer Heichalot that the Men of the Great Assembly enacted this praise based on a note that fell from heaven, and they found written on it [the praise], until here. Toldot Yaakov wrote that they received that one must say it standing, to learn that it corresponds to the supernal form — this is what is written, "Seraphim stand above Him" — and so wrote the Bach at the beginning of siman 53, and so is the practice, as below at the end of the siman. Lechem Chamudot, folio 19, [wrote] that in early texts it is "venamliechah venazkir shemecha malkeinu," and afterward it was emended to "venazkir shemecha venamliechah malkeinu" in order to join the language of kingship to "malkeinu." But the first text is correct, for there are four praises together — "venagdilecha venishbachacha venifarecha venamliechah" — and they correspond exactly to the four names of the supernal chariot. Mahari Luria wrote in the Shulchan Aruch that "haberiyot" is said with sheva under the bet, chirik, and cholam on the yod; and one must say it with his mouth, for it is like the count of eighty-seven words. Also one must say "beteshbachot" with chirik on the tav, and hold the two tzitziyot in front of him at the time of saying Baruch she'amar. Abudraham wrote that one should not say "haKel av harachaman" but "av harachaman," for we say "Elokeinu," until here; and so we hold regarding the blessing after the Megillah, "Rav attah," etc. And it seems to me that for this reason one should say "Baruch oseh ma'aseh bereishis," for the word "ma'aseh" completes the count of eighty-seven in place of the word "haKel." But Mateh Moshe wrote in the name of Maharam that for this reason they do not say "ma'aseh," so as not to add to the count of eighty-seven; see there. And since we lack one word, we may complete it with the word "ma'aseh" — so it seems to me.
