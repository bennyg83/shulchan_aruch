In the Magen Avraham, se'if kaf — I wondered; see note. See the responsa of Chasam Sofer, Orach Chayyim, siman 10 [there he cites in the name of my father-in-law, Rabbi Moharan R' Yosef Lehmann zt"l of Amsterdam, a resolution to the Rashba's question in Berachos, folio 14 amud bet: In the West they recite in Maariv, "Speak to the children of Israel and say to them, I am Hashem your God" — yet any verse that Moses did not pause at, we do not pause at. And the aforementioned rabbi answered that this is a full verse in the parashah of Acharei, at the beginning of the parashah of forbidden relations, for there it says, "Hashem spoke to Moses, saying: Speak to the children of Israel and say to them, I am Hashem your God," etc. And Chasam Sofer praised this and wrote that this answer is undoubtedly true for us; but he also reconciles why the Rashba did not answer thus. See there further what he wrote to reconcile the custom that they begin Kiddush on Shabbat night with "Vayehi erev vayehi boker yom hashishi," which is in the middle of the verse; he wrote that it is because of the combination of the Divine name in the acronym of Yom Hashishi Vayechulu Hashamayim — and it is not proper to begin with "yom hashishi," which has no meaning by itself, so they had to combine "vayehi erev," etc. And at the beginning of the reading, "Vayar Elokim," etc. — "and behold, it was very good" — it is not proper to begin on Shabbat night because of the derashah of Chazal, "tov me'od — this is death." Now that it is not possible otherwise, it is permitted to interrupt a verse; so one may say regarding the custom.
