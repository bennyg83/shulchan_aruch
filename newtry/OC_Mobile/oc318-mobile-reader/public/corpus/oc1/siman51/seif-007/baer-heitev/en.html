Another. Even though nowadays we do not repeat for lack of intention, as written in siman 101—here, since it is only one verse, one repeats so that he may intend.

And until. In Sefer Nagid uMetzavah in the name of the Ari he wrote that one should also say the Targum of this verse—that is, two readings and one translation. And that one should say the verse "for Pharaoh's horse came," for this verse too is from the essence of the song—Orach Chayyim. And see Ginat Veradim part 1, klal 2, siman 8. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Orach Chayyim: "Who is like You"—the second kaf is dagesh; but "Who is like You" the first, and likewise "all my bones shall say, the Omnipresent, who is like You"—the kaf is rafeh. "With this You redeemed"—the gimel is dagesh. "They shall be silent as a stone"—the kaf is dagesh—Orach Chayyim. One should pause between "in the waters" and "mighty." Some say the song [should be recited] with intention and joy—Zohar in Beshalach.

And David blessed. At "And David blessed" one stands until he says "You are the the Omnipresent the the Omnipresent" forever and ever; and when a governor arrives in every place he gives charity—three perutot: two he gives to the collector and one to the charity box. Mishnat Chassidim page 64 side 2. Magen Avraham wonders at the custom on the day of circumcision that the mohel says and cuts and divides some verses—for we say in the first chapter of Berachot that any verse that Moshe did not pause in the Torah we do not pause—Orach Chayyim; he reconciles the custom.
