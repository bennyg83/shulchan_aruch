Se'if 7 in the gloss: "And they say the verse 'Ve'anachnu nivarech,' etc." — Note: In these days the renowned rabbi, Moharan R' Shmuel, sent me his book Iyun Tefillah; and whenever I set my study upon it I saw that he picks a quarrel with the world's custom in pesukei dezimra — that they repeat "Halleluyah" and wait on the word "hamotar" for the chazan — and he wonders, for any verse that Moses did not pause at in the Torah, we do not pause at. I say, Heaven forbid to pick a quarrel with the custom of Israel; for besides that there are several customs that divide the verse, as the Magen Avraham wrote in several matters — and one may say that they hold that only in the Torah do we not pause, but in Nevi'im and Ketuvim we do pause; and similarly several poskim hold regarding matters written [in Scripture] that one may not recite them orally, for it is like Torah — so one may say here as well. And apart from this, it appears that one may say: since here they conclude with "Halleluyah," therefore even though he returns and says "Halleluyah," which is the beginning of the following verse — one may say that perhaps one repeats only the preceding word where the break is recognizable; then we do not pause. But where the break is not recognizable, we pause properly; only to repeat the word itself one is not permitted, for it is like one who says "Shema Shema," and they silence him. But this depends on intent: if he truly repeats, it is forbidden, for it appears, Heaven forbid, like two authorities. But here, in truth, his intent is not to repeat but to begin from the psalm that follows; and so, in truth, there is no repetition here, and the verses are not considered interrupted, since one may say he is repeating the preceding word — and from where do we forbid in such a case? I found support for me in the Beit Yosef [siman 51] that some early authorities hold to repeat every verse at the end of each chapter in this manner — only some challenged this because it is like saying "Shema Shema," etc. If so, it appears they did so intentionally to be a compromise between the views — to repeat only the last word, which is the beginning of the second chapter; this is not like "Shema Shema" and not like interrupting a verse in the middle [and one who examines the Beit Yosef, siman 51, will find what he wrote on the reason they say "Ve'anachnu nivarech Yah" — for otherwise one need not continue "Halleluyah" from the beginning of the following verse upward; the above will show his intent, as stated — that if he had not concluded with "Halleluyah" it would not be possible to continue upward, because one may not interrupt Scripture; but if they conclude with "Halleluyah," one may continue upward for the reason stated]. And behold — for Israel, their custom is Torah; understand and examine. And if they are not prophets, they are sons of prophets; may my portion be with them in the World to Come, Amen.
