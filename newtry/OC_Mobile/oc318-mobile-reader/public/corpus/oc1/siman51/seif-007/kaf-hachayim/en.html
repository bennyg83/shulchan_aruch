(7) [Se'if 4] One must be careful not to interrupt in speech, etc. — for according to Beit Yosef, Baruch she'amar is the blessing before pesukei dezimra and Yishtabach is the concluding blessing. Tur; Magen Avraham, se'if gimel; Razah, ot dalet. See below at the beginning of siman 54; and even in sacred speech it is forbidden to interrupt. And wherever interruption is forbidden, it is forbidden even in holy language — Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 66; Siddur Beit Yosef, ot heh; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 14, ot alef. See below, ot tet-vav, and in siman 61, ot alef, and above siman 25, ot gimmel-nun. If he transgressed and interrupted in speech between Baruch she'amar and pesukei dezimra, or in the middle of pesukei dezimra — he need not return and recite Baruch she'amar, as is clear from Maran z"l, who wrote "one must be careful," etc. — it implies only ordinary caution, but not that he must repeat the blessing if he interrupted; and this is not comparable to blessings on mitzvos and pleasures, where the blessing was enacted for that mitzvah or pleasure. But here it was not enacted for the zemirot but based on a note that fell from heaven; only they fixed it before the zemirot; understand and examine. And not as Zakor LeYitzchak Harari wrote, simanim 7 and 8.
