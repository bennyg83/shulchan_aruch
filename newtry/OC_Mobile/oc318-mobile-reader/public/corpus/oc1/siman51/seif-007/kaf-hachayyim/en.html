7) [Seif 4] One must take care not to interrupt by speaking, etc. Baruch SheAmar is the blessing before Pesukei DeZimrah, and Yishtabach is the concluding blessing. Tur; Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ot 4. See below at the beginning of siman 54. It is forbidden to interrupt even in Hebrew. Wherever interruption is forbidden, it is forbidden even in the holy tongue. Magen Avraham at the beginning of siman 66; Siddur Beit Yaakov, ot 5; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 14, ot 1. See below, ot 15 and siman 61, ot 1, and above, siman 25, ot 53. If one transgressed and spoke between Baruch SheAmar and Pesukei DeZimrah or during Pesukei DeZimrah, he need not repeat the blessing of Baruch SheAmar. This follows from Maran's wording, 'One must take care, etc.,' which implies that this is a caution but does not require repeating the blessing after an interruption. It is unlike blessings over mitzvos and enjoyment: there the blessing was instituted for that mitzvah or enjoyment, whereas Baruch SheAmar was not instituted because of the zemiros. It was instituted on the basis of a note that fell from Heaven and was merely positioned before the zemiros. Understand this carefully. This is unlike Zechor LeYitzchak Harari, simanim 7 and 8.
