(15) One must concentrate. The principal reason Chazal instituted the daily recitation of Tehillah LeDavid is the verse “You open Your hand,” which praises Hashem for supervising and sustaining His creatures.
(16) Once more. Chayei Adam cites Levush that one must repeat from “You open Your hand” through the end of the psalm in order. If he remembers only after reciting other psalms and has no time to return, he should nevertheless say after prayer from “You open Your hand” through the end of the psalm.
(17) Forever and ever. Some write in the name of the Arizal that one should also recite the Aramaic translation of this verse, twice Scripture and once translation, and should add “For Pharaoh's horse came,” because it is part of the Song. In the name of Gra, however, they write not to add that verse. The second Mi Chamocha has a strong kaf, while the first and the phrase “Hashem, who is like You” have a soft kaf. The gimel of ga'alta and the kaf of ka'even are strong. One should pause between “in the waters” and “the mighty,” because “the mighty” refers to the Egyptians. The Song should be recited joyfully, imagining as though one crossed the sea that very day. One who recites it with joy is forgiven for his sins.
(18) When reciting Baruch SheAmar. Beit Yosef implies that on Shabbos it is also proper to stand for “Hashem reigns, Hashem reigned,” etc. Magen Avraham writes that the Arizal would stand with the congregation when they recited it, even if he had not yet reached that point.
(19) Vayevarech David. One should remain standing until saying, “You are Hashem, the God.” When the Arizal said, “And You rule over all,” he gave charity while standing.
