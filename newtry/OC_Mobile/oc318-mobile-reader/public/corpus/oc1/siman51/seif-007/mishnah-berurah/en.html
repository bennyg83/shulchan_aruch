(15) One must concentrate. The principal reason the Sages instituted reciting Tehillah LeDavid every day is the verse that praises the Holy One, blessed is He, for supervising His creatures and sustaining them.
(16) A second time. See Chayei Adam, who cites Levush that one must repeat from the verse “You open Your hand” through the end of the psalm, in order. If he remembers only after having recited other psalms and lacks time to return, he should nevertheless say after prayer from “You open Your hand” through the end of the psalm.
(17) Forever and ever. It is written in the name of the Arizal that one should also recite the Aramaic translation of this verse, meaning twice in Scripture and once in translation. One should also recite the verse “For Pharaoh's horse came,” because that passage is part of the essential Song. In the name of the Gra, however, they write not to say “For Pharaoh's horse came,” etc. In the second occurrence of Mi Chamocha, the kaf is pronounced with a dagesh. In the first Mi Chamocha, and in “all my bones shall say, Hashem, who is like You,” the kaf is soft. In “this people You redeemed,” the gimel is pronounced with a dagesh. In “they sank like stone,” the kaf is pronounced with a dagesh. One should pause between “in the waters” and “the mighty,” because “the mighty” refers to the Egyptians. One should recite the Song at the Sea joyfully and imagine that on that very day he crossed the sea. One who recites it with joy is forgiven for his sins.
(18) When saying Baruch She'amar. It is implied by the Beit Yosef that on Shabbos it is also proper to stand when saying the verse “Hashem reigns, Hashem has reigned,” etc. Magen Avraham writes this in the name of Baal HaItur. He also writes in the name of the Arizal that when the congregation recited “Hashem reigns,” etc., the Arizal would stand with them even if he had not yet reached that passage.
(19) And David blessed. One should remain standing until he says, “You are Hashem, the God.” When the Arizal said, “and You rule over all,” he would give tzedakah while standing.
