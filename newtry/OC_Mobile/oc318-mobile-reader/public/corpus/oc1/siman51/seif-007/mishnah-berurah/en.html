(15) One must have intent — that the main thing they enacted to say Tehillat Ledavid every day is for that verse in which it mentions the praise of the Holy One Blessed be He that He watches over His creatures and provides for them:

(16) Another time — see in Chayei Adam who brought in the name of Levush that one must say from the verse "You open" until the end of the psalm in order. And if he did not remember until he already said other psalms and has no time to return, nevertheless he should say after prayer from the verse "You open" until the end of the psalm:

(17) Forever and ever — and in the name of the Ari they wrote that one should also say the Aramaic translation of this verse, that is, twice he says Scripture and once translation; and that one should say the verse "For the horse of Pharaoh came," since this portion too is from the essence of the song [and in the name of the Gra they wrote not to say "For it came," etc.]. "Who is like You" — we read it with hard kaf [degusha]; but "Who is like You" at first; and likewise "All my bones shall say, 'Hashem, who is like You?'" — soft kaf [rafoya]. "This people You redeemed" — hard gimel [degusha]. "They sank like stone" — hard kaf [degusha]. One must pause between "in the waters" and "mighty," for "mighty" refers to Egypt. And one should say the song of the sea with joy and imagine in his mind as though on that day he crossed the sea; and one who says it with joy, his sins are forgiven:

(18) When they say Baruch She'amar — it is implied in Beit Yosef that on Shabbat it is also proper to stand when saying the verse "Hashem shall reign," etc.; and likewise Magen Avraham in the name of Ba'al HaTanya; and he wrote further in the name of the Ari that when the congregation would say "Hashem shall reign," etc., he would stand with them even though he had not reached there:
