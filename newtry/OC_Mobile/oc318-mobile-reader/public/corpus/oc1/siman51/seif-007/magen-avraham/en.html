He must repeat. Although nowadays one does not repeat prayer because of a lack of concentration, as explained in siman 101, here it is only one verse and he should repeat it, because he can concentrate. See siman 63, seif 4.
And they say the verse, etc. Beit Yosef cites Kol Bo that the reason is that we pray to merit blessing Hashem also in the World to Come, in accordance with the Sages' statement that anyone who recites Tehillah LeDavid three times each day is assured of being a member of the World to Come. For this reason it is also said at Minchah. Maharshal writes in a responsum that the reason is to link one Hallelujah to the next. He therefore writes that it should not be said at Minchah, and that at Shacharis one should omit “Fortunate are those who dwell” and begin directly with Tehillah LeDavid. The custom follows the first view. In the second Mi Chamocha the kaf is strong, while in the first Mi Chamocha and in “All my bones shall say: Hashem, who is like You,” the kaf is soft. In “This people You redeemed,” the gimel is strong. Beit Yosef in the name of Orach Chayim provides allusions explaining some of these pronunciations. Baal HaItur writes that on Shabbos and Yom Tov the verse “Hashem reigns, Hashem reigned,” etc., should be recited while standing. The Arizal would stand together with the congregation when they recited it, even if he had not yet reached that point. When saying “And You rule over all,” he would give charity while standing.
One should not bow. Maharil bowed while seated, facing east. Perhaps the concern applies only when one bows while standing. Baal HaItur. See what I wrote in siman 113, seif 3.
And David blessed. During Vayevarech David one should stand until saying “You are Hashem, the God,” and intend the initial letters of the Divine Name Ehyeh. Afterward he may sit. Kabbalistic writings. It is good to recite with the Song the verse “For Pharaoh's horse came,” etc. I am surprised by the custom on the day of a bris that the mohel says “And You made” and divides several verses. The Gemara at the end of the first chapter of Berachos states that any verse not divided by Moshe may not be divided. See siman 422, seif 3. It appears to me that the reason is based upon Sotah, chapter 5: Moshe said “I shall sing to Hashem,” and the people answered “I shall sing to Hashem.” Thus the Song was originally recited in separated segments, and we may therefore recite it in this manner. Rokeach, siman 319, writes that his brother Rabbi Chizkiyah questioned why people divide the verse “Let them praise the Name,” etc., when returning the Sefer Torah, and likewise “And one called to the other and said.” He gives a reason there. This requires study. How can we invent a reason when the Gemara explicitly prohibits such division? See the beginning of siman 282.
