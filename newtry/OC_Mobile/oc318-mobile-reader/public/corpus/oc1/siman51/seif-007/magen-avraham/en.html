One must repeat. And even though nowadays we do not repeat on account of lack of intent, as stated in section 101 — here it is only one passage that one repeats, for one can have intent — section 63, section 64.

And they say the passage, etc. As Beit Yosef in the name of Kol Bo: the reason we say "that we merit to bless God" also in the World to Come, as the Sages said: whoever says Tehillah le-David three times every day is assured that he is a child of the World to Come — end quote; and therefore they say it at Minchah as well. And Rashal wrote in his responsum that the reason is to chain Hallelujah psalms after Hallelujah; therefore he wrote that he said in Minchah, and at Shacharit he said Ashrei yoshevei, only he begins Tehillah le-David — but the custom is as stated. "Mi kamokha" of the Tanna recitation — the kaf is hard; but "Mi kamokha" of the earlier occurrence, and likewise "All my bones shall say, O Lord, Mi kamokha" — the kaf is soft. With this is explained the gimel with dagesh; and Abudraham in the name of Orach Chayyim gives a slight hint of the reason for the matter. He wrote in Beit Avraham: on Shabbat and Yom Tov they say the passage "The Lord is King, the Lord was King, the Lord will be King," etc., standing; and the Ari, when the congregation said "The Lord is King, etc.," would stand with them even though he had not reached there [in the kavanot]; and when he said "And You rule over all," he gave charity standing [in the writings].

One must not prostrate extensively. And Maharil would prostrate while seated with his face toward the east; and it is possible that there is no stringency except when prostrating from standing [see there]; see section 113, section 63.

And David blessed. At "And David blessed" one stands until he says "You are the Lord God," and he intends the acronym Eheyeh, and afterward he sits [in the kavanot]. It is good to say with the song the passage "For Pharaoh's horse came, etc." [in the writings]. I was astonished at the custom on the day of circumcision that the mohel says "and cut" and divides some verses, for we say in the first chapter of Berachot: any passage that Moses did not divide in the Torah, we do not divide — see section 422, section 63; and it seems to me the reason is that we say in Sotah chapter 5: Moses said "I will sing to the Lord" and they said "I will sing to the Lord" — if so, at first it was said in stanzas; therefore we too say it thus. And the Rokeach, section 319, wrote: my brother R. Yechezkel was astonished at those who divide the passage "Let them praise the name of the Lord, etc." when they bring in the Torah scroll; and likewise the verse "And one called to another and said" — and he gave a reason for the matter, see there; and it requires investigation — from where is there license to divide, since in the Gemara it is explained as forbidden? And to find a reason is burdensome for us — see section 482.
