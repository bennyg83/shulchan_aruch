(SA note 7) "And we say, etc." — "that we merit, etc." That is, the intent of our recitation of the verse "And we will bless, etc." is a prayer that His promise be fulfilled in us, that we merit the World to Come and bless God from now and forever.

Therefore he wrote that he said in Minchah. He abbreviated in his responsum, and so it states there: at Minchah he did not need to conclude with the verse "And we will bless, etc.," since it is not applicable to chain Hallelujah psalms after Hallelujah, for he does not say any Hallelujah psalms at Minchah (and at Shacharit the reason is that since after Ashrei they say psalms that begin with Hallelujah and end with Hallelujah, Ashrei too ends with a verse that concludes Hallelujah). Nevertheless he would also say at Minchah after Ashrei the passage "And we will bless," since he would first say Tehillah le-David — the Ashrei passage that ends "Yet they shall still praise You, Selah"; if so, he began with the language of praise; therefore he would conclude with "And we will bless" in order to conclude with the language of praise. But at Shacharit, before Lamenatzeach, he would not conclude that Tehillah le-David in "And we will bless," for he would not begin Tehillah le-David in Ashrei beforehand, but only at Minchah, as Magen Avraham wrote. After this: the reason they add Ashrei is because one must wait one hour before beginning to pray; therefore the Sages saw fit to enact saying one passage — and that is the measure of the waiting — and since from Ashrei "The inhabitants of Your house, etc." they expounded this exposition that one must wait one hour, therefore they enacted saying Ashrei before Tehillah le-David; unlike at Shacharit, where Tehillah le-David is in the middle of the songs, so this reason does not apply — he would not say them; and if so, likewise before Lamenatzeach he would also not say Ashrei before Tehillah le-David, for the same reason that it is in the middle of prayer; and therefore he would not conclude the verse "And we will bless" either, for before Lamenatzeach there is no reason to chain Hallelujah after Hallelujah, nor the reason that since he began with the language of praise — for he did not begin with the language of praise, since he would not say Ashrei first — see above for another variant. But the custom is as stated: the reason for saying "And we will bless," as Kol Bo stated — it applies whenever it is said; and Ashrei we say every time as well, because of lo plug (not making distinctions).

"Mi kamokha" of the Tanna recitation — that is, in the song; and that is the fourth "Mi kamokha" said at the sea, and Micah's idol passed with them in the sea; and if he had said it with a soft kaf, it would appear like blasphemy, God forbid, as though he were saying Micah, and it is adjacent to the Divine Name; but "Mi kamokha" of the earlier occurrence, that is, "Mi kamokha ba-elohim," even though it is said at the sea, nevertheless since it is not adjacent to the Name — for the word ba-elohim interrupts — we have no concern with it. And likewise "All my bones, etc., Mi kamokha" — even though it is adjacent to the Name, nevertheless since this verse is not said at the sea, we have no concern with it, and it is fit to be said as required with a soft kaf, since it comes after the letters alef-hei-vav-yod.

The gimel with dagesh of the soft reading appears as though it is the language of something abominable and detestable; and Taz also wrote "They shall be like stone" — say the kaf with dagesh so that it does not appear yidmukha (like you).
