(Seif katan 7) And they say, etc. The intention of “But we shall bless” is a prayer that Hashem's promise be fulfilled for us, that we merit the World to Come and bless Him forever.
Therefore he writes that it should not be said at Minchah. At Minchah there is no need to conclude with “But we shall bless,” since no Hallelujah psalms follow. Nevertheless, Maharshal said it after Ashrei at Minchah because Ashrei begins with language of praise, and he wished to conclude likewise. At Shacharis before Lamnatzeach he did not say it, because neither reason applied. Our custom follows Kol Bo's reason, which applies whenever Tehillah LeDavid is recited. We also say “Fortunate are those who dwell” each time because no distinction was made.
The second Mi Chamocha in the Song at the Sea has a strong kaf, lest it sound like the name Michah adjacent to Hashem's Name. The first Mi Chamocha is separated from Hashem's Name by the word ba'eilim. In “All my bones shall say: Hashem, who is like You,” the verse was not said at the sea, so the kaf remains soft.
The gimel of ga'alta is strong, lest it sound like a word meaning repulsive or defiled. Likewise, the kaf of ka'even is strong, lest the phrase sound like “they compared You,” Heaven forbid.
