Seif katan 7: And we say, etc., that we should merit, etc. Our intent in reciting the verse, "But we shall bless Hashem, etc.," is a prayer that His promise be fulfilled for us, that we merit the World to Come and bless Hashem from now and forever.
Therefore he wrote that it should not be said at Minchah. The responsum is abbreviated. Its full meaning is as follows. At Minchah there would seemingly be no need to conclude with the verse "But we shall bless Hashem, etc.," because the reason of linking one Halleluyah to another does not apply. At Minchah no Tehillim that begin and end with Halleluyah are recited. At Shacharis, by contrast, after Ashrei we recite Tehillim that begin and end with Halleluyah, and therefore Ashrei is also concluded with a verse ending in Halleluyah. Nevertheless, at Minchah he would also recite after Ashrei the verse "But we shall bless Hashem," because before Tehillah LeDavid he recited the verse "Fortunate are those who dwell in Your house, etc., they will yet praise You, Selah." Since he began with language of praise, he concluded with "But we shall bless Hashem" so as to end with language of praise. At Shacharis before LaMenatzeach, however, he did not conclude that Tehillah LeDavid with "But we shall bless Hashem," because he did not begin it with Ashrei. He recited Ashrei before Tehillah LeDavid only at Minchah, as Magen Avraham writes below. The reason is that one must remain in waiting for one hour before beginning the Amidah. The Sages therefore instituted the recitation of a chapter of Tehillim, which provides the measure of that waiting period. Since the requirement to wait is derived from the verse "Fortunate are those who dwell in Your house," they instituted Ashrei before Tehillah LeDavid. At Shacharis, where Tehillah LeDavid appears in the middle of Pesukei DeZimrah and this reason does not apply, he would not recite Ashrei beforehand. Likewise, before LaMenatzeach, since it is already in the middle of prayer, he did not recite Ashrei before Tehillah LeDavid. He therefore did not conclude with "But we shall bless Hashem," because neither reason applied: there was no need to link one Halleluyah to another, and he had not begun with language of praise by saying Ashrei. See there for additional variations. Our custom, however, follows the explanation of Kol Bo that "But we shall bless Hashem" is recited because of its own meaning, which applies whenever the chapter of Tehillim is said. Ashrei too is always recited because the Sages made no distinction.
The second occurrence of "Who is like You." This refers to the phrase in the Song at the Sea, the second of the four occurrences of "Who is like You." The idol of Michah passed with them through the sea. If the kaf were pronounced softly, the words could sound, Heaven forbid, like "Michah, Hashem," placing the name Michah next to the Divine Name. The first occurrence, "Who is like You among the mighty," presents no concern even though it too was said at the sea, because the word "among the mighty" separates it from the Divine Name. Likewise, in the verse "All my bones shall say: Hashem, who is like You," although the phrase is adjacent to the Divine Name, the verse was not said at the sea and there is no concern. It should therefore be pronounced according to the ordinary rule, with a soft kaf, since it follows one of the letters alef, hei, vav, or yud.
The gimel of "ga'al" should be pronounced with a dagesh, because if it is soft the word can sound like "defiled" or "repulsive." The Taz likewise writes that in "they were still as a stone" the kaf should be pronounced with a dagesh, so that it not sound like "they compare You." See citations 50, 100, 130.
