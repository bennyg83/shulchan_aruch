(9) And the custom is to stand, etc. — In the Shulchan Aruch, Mahari Luria wrote that one stands from "Vayevarech David" until "Atah Hu Hashem haElokim." The Ari's kavanah was that he stood when the congregation reached "Hashem malach, Hashem malach, Hashem yimloch," even though he had not yet reached there — but he would say the order of zemirot wherever he was saying. Abudraham wrote: in these psalms there is an allusion to the ten utterances — "Baruch she'amar" corresponds to "Bereishis bara"; "Mizmor letodah" to "Yehi or," to which all must give thanks; "Yehi kevod" — it is written "Hashem bashamayim heichin kiso" — to "Yehi rakia"; "Ashrei yoshvei" to "Yikavu hamayim," for otherwise creatures would have no place to dwell; "Halelu nafshi" — it is written therein "Noten lechem l're'evim," to the fifth utterance "Tadshe ha'aretz"; "Halelu ki tov" — "Moneh mispar lakochavim," etc., to "Yehi me'oros"; "Halelu es Hashem," etc., "Hachayah ve'chol behemah," etc., to "Yishretzu hamayim"; "Halelu shiru laHashem shir chadash" to "Totzei ha'aretz nefesh chayah"; "Halelu Kel bekadsho kol haneshamah," etc., to "Na'aseh adam"; "Vayevarech David," etc., "Ve'atah moshel bechal," to "Peru u'revu ve'chivshuha," that He gave man dominion over all — and we say that nevertheless "Atah moshel bechal."
