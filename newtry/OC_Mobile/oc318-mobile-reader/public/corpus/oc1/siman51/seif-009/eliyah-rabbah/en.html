And the custom is to stand, etc. In the Shulchan Aruch of Mahari Luria it is written that one should stand from Vayevarech David through “You are Hashem, the God.” The writings concerning the Arizal state that he would stand when the congregation reached “Hashem reigns, Hashem reigned, Hashem shall reign,” even if he himself had not yet reached that passage. He would then recite the order of the zemiros from the point where he was holding. Avudraham writes that these psalms allude to the ten utterances with which the world was created. Baruch SheAmar corresponds to “In the beginning God created.” Mizmor LeSodah corresponds to “Let there be light,” for which all must give thanks. Yehi Chevod, which says, “Hashem established His throne in the heavens,” corresponds to “Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters,” since the glory of the Omnipresent was that He separated His throne above. Ashrei Yoshvei Veisecha corresponds to “Let the waters gather,” because otherwise creatures would have had no place to dwell. Halleluyah, Hallel Nafshi, which says, “He gives bread to the hungry,” corresponds to the fifth utterance, “Let the earth sprout vegetation producing seed.” Halleluyah Ki Tov Zamerah, which says, “He counts the number of the stars,” corresponds to “Let there be luminaries in the firmament.” Hallelu Es Hashem Min HaShamayim, which mentions wild animals and all beasts, corresponds to “Let the waters swarm with living creatures.” Halleluyah, Shiru LaShem Shir Chadash corresponds to “Let the earth bring forth living creatures according to their kinds.” This teaches a great moral refinement, that one should not desire the mating of one species with another. Hallelu Kel BeKodsho, which says, “Let every soul praise,” corresponds to “Let us make man.” Vayevarech David, which says, “You rule over all,” corresponds to “Be fruitful and multiply and subdue it,” by which man was granted dominion over everything. Yet we still say, “You rule over all.”
