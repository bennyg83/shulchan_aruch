(21) On Shabbat and Yom Tov — since the thanksgiving offering is not brought then. Some places say the psalm of thanksgiving on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur because it is written therein "Make a joyful noise to the Omnipresent, all the earth." In our countries the custom is not to say it [Peri Megadim]:

(22) Because of chametz — for with the thanksgiving they had to bring ten loaves of chametz; also on Erev Pesach they could not bring a thanksgiving offering lest they be unable to eat it until the time of the prohibition of chametz and would need to burn it, and it is forbidden to cause consecrated items to come to burning; and for this reason likewise on Erev Pesach, because it shortens their eating time and brings them to invalidation. And for this reason likewise one accustomed to say daily the passage of sacrifices belonging to an individual should not say it on Erev Yom Kippur, for they are not brought on Erev Yom Kippur like thanksgiving, except for the passage of olah one may say [Peri Megadim in the name of Taz]. And they say the psalm of thanksgiving also on Erev Tisha B'Av and on Tisha B'Av [later authorities]. One should not skip on Yom Tov the verse "A psalm, a song for the day of Shabbat," for Yom Tov too is called Shabbat [Bach]:
