(21) On Shabbos and Yom Tov. This is because a thanksgiving offering was not brought then. Some communities recite Mizmor LeTodah on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur because it contains the verse, “Sound the shofar to Hashem, all the earth.” In our countries, however, the custom is not to recite it, according to Peri Megadim.
(22) Because of chametz. A thanksgiving offering required ten loaves of chametz. It could not be brought on erev Pesach, lest there be insufficient time to eat it before chametz became forbidden, forcing the sacred food to be burned. One may not cause sacred food to become subject to burning. For the same reason, it was not brought on erev Yom Kippur, because doing so would shorten its permitted eating time and bring it to disqualification. For this reason, one who customarily recites each day the Torah sections concerning private offerings should not recite them on erev Yom Kippur, because such offerings were not brought then, just as a thanksgiving offering was not brought. He may, however, recite the section of the burnt offering. This is from Peri Megadim in the name of Tevuot Shor. Mizmor LeTodah is recited on erev Tisha B'Av and on Tisha B'Av, according to the later authorities. On Yom Tov one should not omit the verse “A psalm, a song for the day of Shabbos,” because Yom Tov is also called Shabbos, according to Baer Heitev.
