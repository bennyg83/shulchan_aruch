And Yom Tov. Mizmor LeSodah is omitted because a thanksgiving offering was not brought on those days. Some places recite it on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur because it contains the verse, “Call out to Hashem, all the earth.” Knesses HaGedolah.
On the eve of Pesach. Perhaps the thanksgiving offering could not be eaten and would have to be burned. For the same reason it is omitted on the eve of Yom Kippur. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes that their custom is to recite it on the eve of Pesach and during Chol HaMoed Pesach. Hagahos Minhagim writes that it is omitted on the eve of Tisha B'Av and on Tisha B'Av itself. Maharshal, responsum 64, writes that this is a great error. In the time of the Beis HaMikdash, Tisha B'Av would have been a Yom Tov and thanksgiving offerings would have been brought. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes that in some places, when reciting Mizmor Shir LeYom HaShabbos on Yom Tov, they omit the opening words “A psalm, a song for the Shabbos day” and begin “It is good to thank Hashem,” following Beit Yosef in the name of Orach Chayim. We do not follow this and should not change the text, because Yom Tov is also called Shabbos, as the verse says, “From the day after the Shabbos.” Lechem Chamudos and Peri Chadash likewise rule that the opening verse is recited on Yom Tov.
