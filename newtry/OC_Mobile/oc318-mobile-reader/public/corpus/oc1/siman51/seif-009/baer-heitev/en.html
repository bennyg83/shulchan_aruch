By heart. Lest they be unable to eat it and need to burn it—and for this reason one does not say it by heart in such a case. And in the remnants of Knesset HaGedolah he wrote that our custom is to say it by heart; and on Passover eve—Orach Chayyim. And in Hagahot Maimoniyot he wrote that one does not say it on the Ninth of Av or on the Seventeenth of Tammuz. Maharshal wrote in responsa siman 64 that this is a great error—for in the time of the Temple the Seventeenth of Tammuz was a festival and they would offer the thanksgiving offering. He wrote in the remnants of Knesset HaGedolah: in some places, when they say "A psalm, a song for the Sabbath day" on Yom Tov, they skip "A psalm, a song for the Sabbath day" and begin "It is good to give thanks to the Lord" as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Orach Chayyim—but one need not be concerned for this and should not change, for Yom Tov is also called Shabbat, as it is written "And you shall count for yourselves from the morrow of the Shabbat"—and so wrote the Lechem Chamudot; also on Yom Tov they say the verse "A psalm, a song for the Sabbath day"—and so Peri Chadash.
