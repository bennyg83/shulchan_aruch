Levush: When he reached the tower, etc. Beit Yosef writes that until one reaches Tzofim, tearing is not required, because the sight is not considered a significant viewing. Bach disagrees and writes that the Gemara’s statement that one tears upon reaching Tzofim refers specifically to one who hears but does not see. One who actually sees must tear even from a great distance. This is imprecise. It is obvious that one who merely hears must tear even at the end of the world. The phrase “from Tzofim” does not refer to hearing, but to seeing. The Gemara’s phrase, “whether one hears,” etc., concerns the basic law that one must also tear separately for the Beis HaMikdash. I found this stated explicitly in Maseches Semachos, chapter 9, and likewise explicitly in the Jerusalem Talmud, Moed Katan. Tosafos, Pesachim 49, holds that Tzofim is not the name of a particular place, but refers to any location surrounding Jerusalem from which the city can be seen. The later authorities cite this. This requires study. If so, why did Moed Katan and the cited Jerusalem Talmud need to say concerning one who sees that he tears once he reaches Tzofim? Obviously, one cannot be called a person who sees before the city is visible. Perhaps the meaning is that even at the first place from which the city begins to be visible, the sight is considered sufficient, and one need not see it clearly. For practical halachah, if one tore upon first seeing it, even without seeing it clearly, he need not tear again when he reaches a clearer vantage point. The same law applies to the cities of Yehudah.
