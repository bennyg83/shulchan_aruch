1) [Seif 1] One who sees the cities of Yehudah. Specifically the cities of Yehudah, but not the cities of Yisrael. As for that which is written, "Men came from Shechem, from Shiloh, and from Shomron, with their beards shaven and their garments torn," although these were cities of Yisrael and not cities of Yehudah, one can say that they did not tear their garments until they saw Mitzpah, which is among the cities of Yehudah. Beit Yosef. Levush. Taz, s.k. 1. Regarding what the Bach wrote, the Eliyah Rabbah wrote concerning him that he was not precise; see there:
2) Ibid. One who sees the cities of Yehudah. Specifically the cities of Yehudah, because they are close to Yerushalayim. Levush. The Bach wrote that it is because the cities of Yisrael are not as significant as the cities of Yehudah:
3) Ibid. The author of Chesed LeAvraham, of blessed memory, wrote in handwritten glosses: Concerning the common practice not to tear one's garment upon seeing Chevron, may it be speedily rebuilt, I heard in the name of great authorities that this is because it was among the cities of refuge and among the cities given to the Leviim, and is not called one of the cities of Yehudah. End quote. Maharar Avraham Yitzchaki, of blessed memory, wrote in the margin: I do not know who these great authorities are; however it may be, these are weak arguments. End quote. We too, in our poverty, say that one should not rely upon this. Birkei Yosef, os 1. Shaarei Teshuvah:
4) Ibid. In their destruction. Even if Jews dwell in them, since the nations rule over them, this is called destruction. Beit Yosef. Bach. Taz, s.k. 1. Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. Mishna Berurah, os 2:
5) Ibid. And he tears his garment. When he tears it, he recites a blessing and says: "Blessed is the true Judge, for all His judgments are righteous and true. The Rock, His work is perfect, for all His ways are justice; a God of faithfulness and without iniquity, righteous and upright is He. You are righteous concerning all that has come upon us, for You have acted truthfully, while we have acted wickedly." Bach in the name of the Likkutim. The Bach wrote concerning this that the statement that he should recite "Blessed is the true Judge" means without Hashem's Name and kingship, for this blessing was instituted only for bad tidings that newly occur and arrive. This, however, is an old mourning, and it has no direct connection to his seeing its destruction; see there. The later authorities cited his words. Afterwards he should say: "Gladden us according to the days You afflicted us, etc.; let Your work be seen by Your servants, etc." He should also say: "May it be Your will before You that, in Your abundant mercy, You privilege us to see the rebuilding of the Beit HaMikdash, Yerushalayim, and the cities of Yisrael and Yehudah, amen, may it be His will."
