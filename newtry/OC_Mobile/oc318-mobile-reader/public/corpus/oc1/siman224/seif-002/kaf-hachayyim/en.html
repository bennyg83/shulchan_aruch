6) [Seif 2] One who sees a place from which it was uprooted, etc. One who uproots idolatry in Eretz Yisrael must recite, 'Who has sanctified us with His mitzvos and commanded us to uproot idolatry from our land,' like every blessing over a mitzvah. Maharsha, Chiddushei Aggados, 57b.
7) There: If it is in Eretz Yisrael, etc. Mordechai writes that even when idolatry was uprooted in Eretz Yisrael, one says, 'Blessed is He Who uprooted idolatry from our land,' only if it was uprooted from all of Eretz Yisrael. If it was uprooted from only one location, he recites, 'Blessed is He Who uprooted idolatry from this place.' Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1, cites this. It is also implied by Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter HaRoeh in the name of the Yerushalmi. Regarding why Rema did not include this in his gloss to the Shulchan Aruch, Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1, writes that the Bavli implies the blessing is recited even when it was uprooted from one location, as Bach writes; see there. See Nehar Shalom, paragraph 3.
8) There: And restore the hearts of their worshippers, etc. This is because they are destined to convert, as it is stated, 'Then I will transform the peoples to a pure language.' Berachos 57b, cited by Beis Yosef.
9) There, gloss: And over a place where they placed it, etc. It appears that Rema writes this according to the Shulchan Aruch's view, while he himself holds that nowadays this blessing is not recited, as stated above in the gloss at the end of seif 1. See Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3, and our words above in paragraph 5, and understand this.
