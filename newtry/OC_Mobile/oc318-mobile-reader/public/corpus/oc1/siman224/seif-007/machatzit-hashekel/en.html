(s.k. 7) To exert oneself implies in the Gemara, etc. — and nevertheless, since it concludes because of honor of kings, etc., as they say in Berachot daf 19b: R' Yosi said — Rav said: one who finds kilayim in his garment removes them even in the market; what is the reason? There is no wisdom… where there is desecration of Heaven's name one does not apportion honor to a rabbi, etc.; and he challenges: see that which R' Avraham bar Tziyada said — we leap over coffins of the dead to greet kings of Israel, and not only for kings of Israel did they say but even for kings of the nations of the world — that if he merits he will distinguish between kings of Israel and kings of the nations of the world. Why should we not say there is no wisdom, etc.? And he answers: per Rava — for this leaping over coffins there is no impurity d'oraisa but only d'rabbanan, and because of honor of kings the Sages did not decree — end quote of the Gemara. And from this it implies they permitted only for kings of Israel, who are obligated in their honor, and not for kings of the nations of the world, who are not obligated in their honor, to go see them except because if he merits he will distinguish, etc.; for this reason they did not permit impurity d'rabbanan — for otherwise it should have said because of honor of kings and because if he merits he will distinguish, etc., the Sages did not decree. And in Sefer Chokhmat Adam he wrote, and so it is: Rashi explains in the Chumash parashat Bo "and Pharaoh king of Egypt will pursue them" — to apportion him honor in their words, etc.; and it implies that even for kings of the nations of the world one must apportion honor. And in my humble opinion — specifically when speaking with kings of the nations of the world one must apportion them honor; and this is the trait of derech eretz, for the Holy One, blessed be He, desires their honor — even a head of the laundry is appointed from Heaven. But to trouble oneself to see them one is not obligated because of their honor; and know that otherwise why would he need to state the reason that it is a mitzvah to run to see kings of the nations of the world because if he merits he will distinguish, etc.; it would be more fitting to say one is obligated in their honor:

(ב) And so too — since Yoreh De'ah siman 372, which wrote the law of permitting a kohen's impurity for a mitzvah matter, did not write this law that it is permitted to see kings of the nations of the world, but only permitted for kings of Israel — and we do not have this; and for a king of the nations of the world it was not permitted — therefore it did not write this law for us:

And what the Gemara says "and not to greet, etc." — these are the words of R' Avraham bar Tziyada: we leap, meaning, etc.; and it concludes and not only for kings of Israel did they say but even for kings of the nations of the world — that if he merits, etc.; and it implies that also for kings of the nations of the world it was permitted to leap over, etc. For this he wrote there is no such intent; rather, since he said we leap, meaning, etc., perforce there is a mitzvah in this — namely, that this mitzvah: not only for kings of Israel did they say but even for kings of the nations of the world there is a mitzvah; however for kings of the nations of the world there is another reason than for kings of Israel — for with kings of Israel the reason is because one is obligated in their honor, and therefore impurity d'rabbanan was permitted; and for kings of the nations of the world the reason is only if he merits, etc.; and automatically impurity was not permitted regarding them unless he comes with a great army, etc.; for then again applies the reason that if he merits, etc.; for now he sees them with greater honor than once; and nevertheless he will see if he merits whether the honor of Mashiach will be greater:

And in Rambam, etc. — because of honor of kings, and even kings of the nations of the world, so it is. And so it is: we leap over coffins of the dead to greet kings of Israel, and even to greet kings of the nations of the world in order to distinguish, etc. — end quote:

(ה) And Tosafot wrote that specifically there in Bechorot it is written thus:

Requires blowing. Meaning impurity of bet ha-peras — and this is a field in which a grave was plowed, up to a length of one hundred cubits — is impure d'rabbanan, lest the plowshare carried a bone the size of a barley grain from the grave; and for more than one hundred cubits they were not concerned that the plowshare carried it, and it is pure. And as proof — Rambam chapter 17 of Ohalot. And they said that to learn Torah, or to marry a woman, or to comfort mourners, or to greet kings, or to litigate with gentiles — because it is like saving from their hands — for all these a kohen was permitted to walk in bet ha-peras; and so Tosafot — specifically for these mitzvot it is permitted, but for other mitzvot it requires blowing. And Rashi explained that a bone the size of a barley grain does not impart impurity in a tent except through contact and carrying, unless it is a majority of number or a majority of structure — then it imparts impurity in a tent; or if a person tents over the corpse — therefore the Sages permitted blowing with the mouth before his feet, for if there is a large bone that cannot be blown, behold he will see it and distance himself from it so as not to touch it and not to move it.
