1) [Seif 1] One who sees Markulis. The idol's actual name is Kulis, not Markulis. It is called Markulis as an alteration of 'kilus,' for they call praise 'kilus,' while the Sages changed it to a term of disgrace meaning mockery and derision, because it is forbidden to mention an idol's name. 'Mar' indicates an alteration. Tosafos on Bava Metzia 25b, beginning with the words, 'like stones,' and Tosafos on Avodah Zarah 50a, beginning with the words, 'stones.' See Magen Avraham, seif 1. Markulis is constructed by placing one stone upon two others and throwing stones upon it. Rashi on Avodah Zarah 50b.
2) There: One who sees Markulis, etc. If one saw through a window something whose sight requires a blessing, and the window was covered by clear glass, whether he may recite the blessing upon seeing it is obviously resolved affirmatively. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1; see there. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 3; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 14.
3) There: Or other idolatry. Beis Yosef writes that this includes even a house of idolatry. Bach, however, writes that it applies specifically when one sees the idol exposed; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 7, likewise writes that it applies specifically when one sees it exposed; see there. The Rambam in chapter 10 of Hilchos Berachos, law 10, however, implies like Beis Yosef; see there. Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 177, likewise writes that the blessing is recited even over the building, contrary to Bach; see there. See the following paragraph.
4) There, gloss: Nowadays, this blessing is not recited. Tosafos explain that the reason is that we see it every day, while all these blessings upon seeing are recited only from one thirty-day period to another. This implies that if one happened not to see idolatry for thirty days, he must recite the blessing. Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, writes that even one who did not see it within thirty days does not recite the blessing, since most people see it, contrary to Olas Tamid; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1, writes that the reason the blessing is not recited now is that people rely upon the commentators who hold that it applies specifically to Markulis, and this form of idolatry is not common nowadays. It appears that since the matter is disputed, and since many are lenient regarding these blessings, as stated above in the gloss to siman 223, seif 1, one should recite this blessing only without the Name and kingship, whether seeing the idol itself or a house of idolatry, even if he has never before seen that location or idol.
5) If a spectacle of non-Jews occurs in your city, such as a joust or tournament, guard yourself from seeing it. The same applies to their dancing or anything connected with them. You will thereby merit to see the sport involving the Leviathan and the wild ox. If you hear a multitude of non-Jews celebrating, playing flutes, and rejoicing, sigh and say: Master of the worlds, Your people whom You brought out of Egypt sinned doubly and were punished doubly, etc. May it be Your will to rebuild Yerushalayim, Your holy city, speedily in our days, Amen. Bach in the name of Sefer HaEshkol; Ateres Zekeinim. Avodah Zarah 18b states that even one who attends a spectacle of Jewish people is included among those who sit in a gathering of scoffers. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Chayei Adam, rule 63, paragraph 17.
