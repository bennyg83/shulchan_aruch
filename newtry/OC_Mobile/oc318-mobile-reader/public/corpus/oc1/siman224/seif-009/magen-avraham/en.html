It implies in the Gemara that one may transgress a d'rabbanan mitzvah to see kings, even kings of the nations of the world; and nevertheless, since it concludes because of honor of kingship the Sages did not decree — it implies specifically for kings of Israel they spoke, and because of their honor; and so too — since Yoreh De'ah siman 372 did not write this law, it implies specifically for kings of Israel it is permitted, and we do not have this now among gentiles. And what the Gemara says "and not to greet, etc." — meaning there is a mitzvah to run to greet kings of the nations of the world that if he merits to see the king Mashiach who will come speedily he will distinguish what is between their greatness and his greatness and know the reward of doers of mitzvot; and nevertheless it is forbidden to transgress even a d'rabbanan mitzvah for this. And in Shulchan Aruch HaRav siman 490 he wrote that if one sees the king once he should not cancel more of his study to see him unless he comes with greater force and with greater honor. And in Rambam chapter 3 of Avel he wrote explicitly that it is permitted to become impure with the impurity of their words because of honor of their kings, even for kings of the nations of the world, or because of honor of creatures, such as to comfort a mourner; and so too Tosafot in Bechorot daf 29 and so in Yerushalmi Berachot; and Tosafot wrote that specifically for these mitzvot, but for other mitzvot it requires blowing; and so in Pesachim chapter 8; and so Ramban in Hasagot:
