[7] Houses of Israel, etc. It appears to me that the primary ruling follows the Rif, that this refers specifically to synagogues, and likewise the reference to gentile houses concerns their houses of worship. So wrote Maharshal, and this is also implied by Kol Bo, siman 87, Sefer Zikaron, and the Yerushalmi. As for the ruling above that one recites a blessing over gentile worship, it must refer to the idolatry itself when it stands openly, as Beit Yosef writes. From Kol Bo it appears as though there is a dispute, and the authority who stated one ruling did not state the other. This requires study, for the Mishnah states, “A place from which idolatry was uprooted,” etc. It appears to me from the silence of the poskim that there is no distinction between Eretz Yisrael and outside the Land, nor between the era of the Beis HaMikdash and afterward. This is also implied by Abudraham, contrary to Beit Yosef and Chiddushei Aggados. Regarding gentile houses of worship, all agree there is no distinction. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that the Tur also omitted the words “and He knows the number of all of you” from the text of the blessing, but the Rif, Rosh, and the Remezim include them. This is also implied by Baal Halachos Gedolos, Rokeach, Abudraham, Sefer Zikaron, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Kol Bo. Derashos Maharash writes that when he walked by a grave he would say: “May it be His will that the resting place of so-and-so, who is buried here, be honorable, and may his merit stand for me.” He further writes that the practice of plucking grass from a grave, or taking a pebble and placing it upon the monument, is only out of honor for the deceased, to show that one visited his grave. One should not visit the same grave twice in one day. Writings of the Arizal. Likutim at the end of Maaneh Lashon states that reading the inscription upon a monument is harmful to memory, and that one should recite Ahavah Rabbah until “with love,” which is highly effective against forgetfulness. The custom is not to erect a monument until after twelve months, because a monument appears to honor the deceased, while during the twelve months he is in distress. Alternatively, the purpose of the monument is that he not be forgotten from the heart, and the dead are not forgotten until after twelve months. The custom is not to take anything, large or small, from a mourner’s house throughout the seven days of mourning, because a spirit of impurity rests there for seven days. Some also have the custom not to measure the cemetery within seven days of a burial. The early authorities placed a ban against taking water from the river after accompanying a funeral, because on one occasion the gentiles fabricated an accusation and killed many Jews. When burying, people are careful not to take the shovel or hoe directly from another person’s hand, but rather to throw it to the ground and then pick it up. Likewise, when washing hands, they are careful not to take the vessel directly from the hand of the person washing. He further writes that they distance themselves at least four amos before reciting Tzidduk HaDin and Kaddish. See Yoreh Deah, siman 377. This requires study. See also the end of siman 581.
