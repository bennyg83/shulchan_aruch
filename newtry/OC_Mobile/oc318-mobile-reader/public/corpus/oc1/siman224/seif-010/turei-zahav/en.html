One who sees houses of Israel. Rashal wrote, and these are his words: the Rif explains synagogues; per this, what he wrote afterward "one who sees houses of idolatry" must also mean their houses of prayer; and if so, there is difficulty — is there not a special blessing for them as he wrote "one who sees Mercurius or other avodah zarah" says Baruch Who gave long patience? One may answer: there, when one sees the body of the idol; but here one sees the house, a structure designated for them where they set up idolatry, and one does not see the idol itself. And some explain it refers to all houses of Israel of leaders and wealthy in their settlement — as he said, meaning when they dwell in their strength and power; therefore Rashi intended matsiv gevul almanah, such as the settlement of the Second Temple, and he does not speak specifically of the building of the Temple, for then it should have said one who sees the Temple; rather all houses of Israel when they are in their settlement after their days of widowhood — this is in the Second Temple; and so in the Gemara he speaks of all houses, and so in the sugya he brings the incident of Ulla and R' Chiya when they arrived at the entrance of Rav Chana, etc.: from the day the Temple was destroyed a decree was issued on the houses of the righteous, etc. — it implies that houses of Israelites in their destruction that he mentions are houses of wealthy Israelites and masters of charity; and automatically the same applies to houses of Israel in their settlement; and people did not practice so except on synagogues, and conversely on their houses of prayer. Beit Yosef wrote that one does not bless on houses of Israel unless they were in Eretz Yisrael and in the time of the Temple — and this is also Rashi's intent, etc.
