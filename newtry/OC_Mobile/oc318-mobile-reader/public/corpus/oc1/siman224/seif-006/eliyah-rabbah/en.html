[ו] Kings of Israel, etc. It implies in the Gemara that even a blind person who knows the king is passing blesses — Magen Avraham. And requires study in the language of the Gemara: Rav Sheshet opened and was blessing him — it implies he was blessing the king like a greeting and not reciting the blessing to the Holy One, blessed be He; and so is reasonable — for the Gemara "one who sees kings of Israel" speaks, and likewise all sight-blessings — a blind person does not bless; and above at the beginning of siman 59 is different regarding one who benefits — see there; however Maharsh"a in Chiddushei Aggadot understood plainly that he blesses. And Radbaz wrote in his responsa: the governors in their cities upon whom there is no king's yoke to change their words from them to no, and they fulfill for them and execute by law — one blesses on them. Further there: if one sees several kings or sages in one day — one blesses on each one individually; but for that same king or sage one does not bless except from thirty to thirty. Further we hold that it is a mitzvah to exert oneself to see kings — even within four amot of a grave a kohen is permitted to go to see him, for one may transgress a d'rabbanan to see kings.
