It is clear that this refers to the blessing. But in the Beis Yosef he wrote: however, this means, and so on, and it emerges that he did not hear it, and so on. This implies that it refers to the Megillah itself. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, also explained it this way, and he found it difficult why one should not protest against someone who interrupts during the Megillah reading. I do not know any basis for this difficulty, for certainly here it speaks only of an interruption during the reading of the blessing. But regarding interruption during the reading of the Megillah itself, it is obvious that one must protest against him. Even for words of Torah it is forbidden to interrupt in any way, as written in siman 690, seif 3, all the more so for ordinary matters. Nevertheless, if he spoke even during the Megillah itself, he does not return to the beginning, for so wrote the Rashba, brought by the Beis Yosef, that it is an interruption only if he interrupted between the blessing and the beginning of the Megillah reading.
