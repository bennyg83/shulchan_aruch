There, seif 2. One may not speak while it is being read. Note: see the Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Shiltei HaGibborim in the second chapter of Berachos, but he did not copy it well. He should have concluded with what the Shiltei HaGibborim left in need of analysis: what is considered “between the sections” in the Megillah, since it is read like a letter? See there. In my opinion, one can say that since there are opinions in the Mishnah about from where the Megillah is read, although we rule that it must be read from the beginning, in any event this is considered “between the sections,” meaning between the beginning and “There was a Jewish man,” and between “There was a Jewish man” and “On that night,” and so on. See what I wrote in my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5404, in the laws of Rosh Chodesh, siman 422. There I wrote that there is a dispute about this among the authorities, and the reason why the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch omitted this law; see there and examine it carefully.
