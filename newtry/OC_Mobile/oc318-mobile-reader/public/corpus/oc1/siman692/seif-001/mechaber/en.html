One who reads the Megillah recites
three blessings beforehand: “al mikra Megillah,” “she’asa nissim,” and “shehecheyanu.” During the day he does not repeat the blessing shehecheyanu.Some say that even during the day one recites shehecheyanu (Tur in the name of Rabbenu Tam, the Rosh, and the Maggid}, and this is the custom in all these lands; one person may recite the blessings while another reads (Hagahot Ashiri, ch. 2}. Afterward it is customary to recite “Ha-rav et riveinu…” If he did not recite the blessings either before or after, he has nevertheless fulfilled his obligation. {Rama: It is customary to say “Asher heni” at night but not during the day (Kol Bo; Beit Yosef in the name of Orḥot Ḥayyim}, and the concluding blessing is recited only in the presence of a congregation.] ולאחריה נוהגים לברך הרב את ריבנו וכו' אם לא בירך לא לפניה ולא לאחריה יצא: הגה ונהגו לומר בלילה אשר הניא אבל לא ביום [כל בו וב"י בשם א"ח] ואין לברך אחריה אלא בצבור:
