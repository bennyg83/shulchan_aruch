1) [Seif 1] One who reads the Megillah, etc. It is proper for two people to stand beside the shaliach tzibbur, one on his right and one on his left, when he reads the Megillah. Even a permanently appointed shaliach tzibbur should request permission before beginning. Orah V'Simchah, 25; Moed L'Chol Chai, siman 31, os 104; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Tetzaveh, os 12. This means that before reciting the blessing he should say, 'With the permission of my teachers and masters.' The custom of the pious men of Beit El, may it be rebuilt and established speedily in our days, is that after the chazzan says, 'With permission,' etc., he says, 'For the sake of the unification,' etc.: 'Behold, we come to fulfill the mitzvah of reading the Megillah, to rectify its root in the supernal realm. May the pleasantness,' etc. He then begins the blessings.
2) Ibid. Concerning the reading of the Megillah. Why do we not recite the blessing, 'to hear the reading of the Megillah,' as we say, 'to hear the sound of the shofar'? The Abudraham answered that regarding shofar, one must hear it with his ears, since comprehension of the heart is not involved. But regarding the Megillah, even if he did not hear his own voice, he fulfilled his obligation, because reading is the essential act, since comprehension of the heart is involved. One can further say that had the blessing been formulated 'to hear,' there would be concern that listeners might understand the term 'hearing' as meaning comprehension, as in, 'For Yosef understood.' If so, women and unlearned people would not fulfill their obligation, yet we rule that they do fulfill it. Therefore, the blessing is, 'concerning the reading of the Megillah.' Regarding shofar, however, one cannot recite, 'to sound the shofar,' because its mitzvah includes teruah and shevarim; nor can one recite, 'to sound a teruah,' because it also includes tekiah. Therefore, one must recite, 'to hear.' Eliyah Rabbah, os 1; Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah, os 2. See further above, siman 585, os 16.
3) Ibid. Who performed miracles, and Shehecheyanu. If one has no Megillah, he should recite Shehecheyanu, but he should not recite the blessing of 'Who performed miracles' or the blessing of 'Who champions our cause.' Mahari Molcho, manuscript responsum, siman 78. However, the later authorities wrote that he should not recite Shehecheyanu either. Birkei Yosef, os 1. See below, os 5.
4) Ibid. And Shehecheyanu. When reciting Shehecheyanu, he should also intend that it apply to mishloach manos and the Purim meal, which are also mitzvos. Shelah. It appears to me that he should have this intention during the Shehecheyanu blessing of the daytime reading, because their time is during the day. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 2; Yad Aharon in Hagahos HaTur. This should be made known to the shaliach tzibbur, because both the listener and the one enabling him to hear require intention. However, according to the Rambam and the Mechaber, who hold that Shehecheyanu is not recited during the day, he should intend during the nighttime Shehecheyanu to fulfill the requirement also for mishloach manos and gifts to the poor, even though their time is not at night. Eshel Avraham, os 1.
