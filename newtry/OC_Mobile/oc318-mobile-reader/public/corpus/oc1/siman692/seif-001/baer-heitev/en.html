Shehecheyanu. He should also have intent in the Shehecheyanu blessing for mishloach manos and the Purim meals, for they too are mitzvos, as the Shelah writes. It appears to me that he should have this intent in the daytime Shehecheyanu, since their time is by day. It appears to me that one who has no Megillah should not recite Shehecheyanu over mishloach manos and the meal, for these are things practiced every day and on every Shabbos and Yom Tov. This is from the Magen Avraham.
By day. For the primary mitzvah of reading it is by day. If the reader became unable to speak in the middle of the Megillah, the second reader must begin at the beginning, as written in siman 284, seif 5. But he does not need to recite the first blessing, because he fulfills it with the blessing of the first reader. This is from the Magen Avraham.
