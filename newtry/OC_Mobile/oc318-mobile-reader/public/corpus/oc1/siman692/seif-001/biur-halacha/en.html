And Shehecheyanu. See Mishnah Berurah, seif katan 1, where we wrote that some say it is proper to bless, and so on. This is from Mor Uketziah, who challenged the Magen Avraham. This is his language: I do not know what difference it makes that it is a customary thing. Even without the mitzvos of mishloach manos and the meal, it would be proper to recite Shehecheyanu over the day itself. Although it is from words of Kabbalah, it needs strengthening like Torah law, and even more so for an individual, because of the power of the miracle that occurred on it. Since it comes from time to time, it is in this respect like all the festivals of Hashem over which we recite Shehecheyanu. Shehecheyanu does not require a cup, for the halachah is that one may say it even in the marketplace. Yom Kippur has no eating and drinking, only cessation and rest, and nevertheless we recite it, and so on. He concludes there that initially, for one who has no Megillah, it is proper to attach Shehecheyanu to the cup of the meal. This is the end of his words. In truth, according to his reasoning that one should recite Shehecheyanu over the day itself because of the power of the miracle that occurred on it, he should also recite the blessing “Who performed miracles.” In fact, I found in the Meiri on Shabbos regarding Chanukah, and the same applies in our matter regarding Purim, as follows: One who has nothing with which to kindle and is not in a place where he can see the lights, some say that he recites for himself “Who performed miracles” and Shehecheyanu on the first night, and “Who performed miracles” on all the nights, and the words appear correct. This is the end of his words. Regarding his basic wonder at the Magen Avraham, that even without the mitzvos of mishloach manos and so on it is proper to recite Shehecheyanu over the day, I do not know that this is a difficulty. It is possible that the Magen Avraham also agrees to this, but he is speaking where at night one had a Megillah and recited Shehecheyanu then, and automatically fulfilled Shehecheyanu on the day itself. What the Pri Megadim suggests, that according to the Mechaber one fulfills with the Shehecheyanu of the night for mishloach manos and the daytime meal, is not clear, and the flow of the Magen Avraham's language also does not imply this. But if by day he had no Megillah at all, perhaps even the Magen Avraham agrees. Afterward I found in the Birkei Yosef: If one has no Megillah, he should recite Shehecheyanu, but he should not recite “Who performed miracles” or the blessing Harav Es Riveinu, as Rabbi Yaakov Molcho writes. But the later authorities wrote that he should not recite even Shehecheyanu. This is the end of his words. I do not know in which later authorities he found this, aside from the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah who copied him, and from them there is no proof, as we wrote. This requires practical analysis. Only with the community. In the Beis Yosef, he wrote that Orchos Chayim wrote this in the name of the Yerushalmi. See Eliyah Rabbah, who proved from several authorities that one recites the blessing even alone. See the Biur HaGra, who wrote that from the Yerushalmi too there is no proof, for it refers only to the Torah blessing. Nevertheless, it appears that it is not worthwhile to recite the blessing afterward when alone, for in any case this blessing is not obligatory even with the community, and depends on custom, as stated in the Gemara. Who can say that the custom nowadays has spread to recite it alone? So too wrote the Pri Megadim, that in a doubt about blessings we are lenient.
