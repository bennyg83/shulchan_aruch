From plag haminchah upward — source from what we lenient above siman 233 regarding Maariv prayer; same Shema of Maariv per Rabbenu Tam — all this if he is careful always to pray Minchah before plag as explained there seif 1; Gra likewise. Peri Chadash challenged Terumat HaDeshen's main law since gemara derives from "and night" — night unlike day, night not called until tzet hakochavim; also most poskim dispute Rabbenu Tam on Shema; Beit Yosef 287 brought Orchot Chaim in Ravad: read somewhat before night to ease people and pregnant women from excessive fasting — found in Eshkol; Meiri similar for sick and birthing who needn't wait evening, may hurry reading though still within thirteen — Peri Chadash rejected this custom strongly; many acharonim defended citing rishonim (see Bigdei Yesha, Mardechai, Mor Uktzia); their view: though strictness brings blessing, for sick and suffering who lighten reading somewhat before night there is whom to rely on; possible where great need even congregation may lighten reading though night not complete.
