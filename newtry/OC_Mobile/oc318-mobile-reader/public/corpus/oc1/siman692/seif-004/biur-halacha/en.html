From plag haminchah and onward. Its source is from what we were lenient about above in siman 233 regarding Maariv, and the same applies to the evening Krias Shema according to Rabbeinu Tam's view. However, all this applies if he is always careful to daven Minchah before plag, as explained there in seif 1, and so wrote the Gra. The Pri Chadash challenged the Terumas HaDeshen on the main law, since in the Gemara we derive from the verse “and night, and not silence,” and it is not called night until the emergence of the stars. Also regarding Krias Shema, most authorities disagree with Rabbeinu Tam. However, the Beis Yosef in siman 687 copied in the name of Orchos Chayim in the name of the Raavad that the custom was to read somewhat before nightfall in order to be lenient for weak people and pregnant women, so that they should not fast too much. I found the same in the Eshkol. The Meiri also brought something similar regarding sick women and women after childbirth, that they do not need to wait so late and may hasten their reading even while it is still within the domain of the thirteenth. Although the Pri Chadash rejected this custom entirely, nevertheless several later authorities defended this custom, which was brought by several early authorities; see Bigdei Yesha, Maamar Mordechai, and Mor Uketziah. Their view is that although one who is stringent will receive blessing, nevertheless regarding a sick or distressed person who is lenient to read somewhat before nightfall, he has authorities on whom to rely. It is possible that in a place of great pressure, even with a community one may be lenient to read although it is not yet fully night.
