From plag haminchah. After he davens Maariv. But it does not appear to me to permit him to taste a little and wait until the reader comes, as we say in Berachos that a person should not say, “I will eat a little,” and afterward I will read Krias Shema. It appears that this is all the more so regarding Megillah reading, where we are concerned lest sleep overtake him and he miss its reading, since there is a considerable wait and its reading is a greater obligation, for all mitzvos are deferred before it, as the Terumas HaDeshen writes. See what I wrote in siman 687 in the name of the Re'em, who holds that because it is beloved, one will not come to negligence. For this reason they did not prohibit learning, as they did regarding bedikas chametz. This is the end of his words. If so, he holds that Megillah is more lenient than Krias Shema and chametz. Now, since regarding Krias Shema and chametz tasting is permitted, as written in siman 235 and siman 432, all the more so here. Furthermore, the primary Megillah reading is by day. It appears to me that for this reason the Rema changed his wording and wrote, “it is forbidden to eat,” and so on, implying that tasting is permitted. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient except for a great need. In the Tosefta of the first chapter of Shabbos it is written that Megillah is comparable to lulav, shofar, and Krias Shema. It implies from the Maharil that even a sick person should not eat before its reading unless there is danger. It is obvious that one who is under some compulsion may hear the daytime reading before sunrise after dawn has risen; see siman 687. There the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Orchos Chayim that the custom was to read somewhat before nightfall, in order to be lenient for weak people and pregnant women so that they should not fast too much.
