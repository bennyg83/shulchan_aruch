(12) And he must wait, and so on. This means until someone comes to read before him, for beforehand it is forbidden to eat, as regarding Krias Shema above in siman 235.
(13) From plag haminchah and onward. This is an hour and a quarter before night. The reason is that since we consider it night regarding Maariv, in that one may daven at that time, we consider it night for this matter as well.
(14) But it is forbidden to eat, and so on. See the Magen Avraham, who concludes that specifically eating is forbidden, but mere tasting is permitted. Therefore it is better to be lenient for this person that he taste before Megillah reading, rather than be lenient to read from plag haminchah and onward, for the Pri Chadash objected to this leniency. His view is that before the emergence of the stars one does not fulfill the obligation at all, even after the fact, and so the Gra's view also implies in his commentary. Even regarding tasting, one should be lenient only for a great need, meaning for someone somewhat ill or someone fasting for whom the fast is difficult. The measure of tasting is an egg-volume of bread or liquids in the measure of an egg. The Pri Megadim wrote that according to the Pri Chadash, even at twilight one should not read, and if one read at twilight after the fact, he is also uncertain there whether it helps. But in a case of compulsion and great necessity, there is room to lean toward leniency. The Chayei Adam wrote that even if he read part of it before the emergence of the stars, he has not fulfilled the obligation and must return and read, but without a blessing. See Shaarei Teshuvah in the name of the Birkei Yosef and the Biur Halachah, that nevertheless one who is lenient for a sick person or a woman after childbirth to hasten their reading somewhat while it is still day has authorities on whom to rely.
(15) Before he hears the Megillah reading. This is like the law of the evening Krias Shema, where we forbid eating because perhaps sleep will overtake him and he will miss its reading. If so, all the more so it is forbidden to sleep before Megillah reading, even if his intent is only to sleep a little, as there regarding Krias Shema. Megillah reading is a great obligation, for all mitzvos are deferred before it, as above in siman 687. See above in siman 235, seif 2, and in the Mishnah Berurah there for several details regarding Krias Shema; the same applies here. Know that just as at night it is forbidden to eat before its reading, the same applies by day: even if he already davened, it is forbidden to eat before its reading, as with shofar and lulav.
(16) Even if the fast is difficult for him. If he is a weak person, and the delay will harm his health and can lead to illness, and a small taste is not enough for him, it appears that it is permitted to eat. However, he should ask someone to remind him to read the Megillah after he eats. The Pri Megadim wrote that if there is doubt whether one read the Megillah, perhaps we do not say in this case that a doubt in a Rabbinic matter is treated leniently, because it is words of Kabbalah.
