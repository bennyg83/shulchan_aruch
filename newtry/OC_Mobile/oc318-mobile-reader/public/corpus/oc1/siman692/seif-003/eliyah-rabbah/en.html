[3] Levush: And afterward, and so on. This requires analysis from siman 284, seif 4, that initially another person should not read the haftarah, as the Malbushei Yom Tov writes. In my humble opinion, that case is different, for the reason they enacted that the maftir reads from the Torah is for the honor of the Torah. If someone else then reads the haftarah, there would be no honor of the Torah, and so it also implies in the Beis Yosef, siman 282. If the reader became unable to speak in the middle of the Megillah, it appears to me that the second begins from the place where the first stopped and does not bless. This resembles shofar, siman 581, seif 3, and is not comparable to a Torah reader at the beginning of siman 140, where he begins from the beginning and blesses. There the Levush wrote the reason, that the Torah blessing was instituted for itself, unlike here, where the blessing discharges others. So too one can distinguish between the haftarah in siman 284, seif 5, and here. Furthermore, there the reason is that a blessing is required before it and after it, but the blessing after the Megillah was not instituted over the Megillah. This excludes the Magen Avraham, who ruled that he should begin from the beginning without a blessing, for he did not enter into all of this.
