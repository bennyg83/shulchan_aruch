Seif katan 2. As written in siman 284, and so on, regarding haftarah, and similarly in siman 140 regarding Torah reading. The reason is explained in siman 140: since these matters require a blessing before them and after them, if he reads from the place where the first stopped, it emerges that the first verses were blessed before them and not after them, while the latter verses were blessed after them and not before them. Since he begins from the place where the first stopped, he will not recite the first blessing, for the first reader already blessed. Therefore he begins from the start and recites the first blessing, so the verses are blessed before them and after them. According to the Rambam, he fulfills the obligation with the blessing of the first reader; see the Beis Yosef in siman 140, who explains the Yerushalmi according to his view. In siman 585 regarding shofar, he does not begin from the start. It appears that this is not comparable to Torah, since shofar does not require a blessing afterward. Therefore he begins from the place where the first stopped, and then he does not bless, as written at the beginning. This is unlike Megillah, which requires a blessing before it and after it, so he begins from the start. The Magen Avraham concluded that although he begins from the start, nevertheless he does not need to bless first, because he fulfills the obligation, and so on. The Magen Avraham already explained this in siman 284, seif katan 4. According to what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 585, that the distinction between shofar and Torah is that regarding Torah it is written “the Torah of Hashem is complete,” one must say that the reason regarding Megillah is that it too is included in completeness, and similarly with haftarah. See what I wrote there regarding those words of the Magen Avraham.
