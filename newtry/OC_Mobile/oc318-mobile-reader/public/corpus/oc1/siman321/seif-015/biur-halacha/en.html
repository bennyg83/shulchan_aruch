He may mix it — See in Mishna Berurah what is written: even if they were not mixed, etc.; so too Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim according to this view; and so it is plain in Shulchan Aruch's explanation that if they were mixed from daytime — this is the "there are those who say" in seif 16; and see in Beit Yosef at length, that it is proven thus plainly; and it is implied they permitted even to make on Shabbat a thick batter in this manner, that he does not beat with force; and in truth requires great study in this — how did the Sages permit the essence of kneading, which is a d'oraisa prohibition, through this slight change — namely that he does not beat with force; and also from Rambam chapter 22 it appears that if he beats it is only a d'rabbanan prohibition, for he considered it there in that chapter dealing with matters of shevut [and perhaps this is Tur and the Mechaber's intent, that they wrote "only he mixes little by little" — and it is like what they permitted in roasted flour on yad al yad; but from Beit Yosef and the rest of the poskim it does not appear thus; also in the Gemara it is not mentioned that this is specifically on yad al yad]. And if we say it is something not subject to kneading — it is harder for the view of many early authorities; and Rosh himself in chapter 4 of Beitza holds that in something not subject to kneeding, putting water is its kneading; and here Tur wrote that Rosh agreed to the first view, that even if he did not put beverage erev Shabbat — also permitted to put in it on Shabbat; also very difficult — behold in the Gemara it states mustard that was kneaded — how can there be kneading without beverage? And what Peri Megadim resolved, that it deals with what was kneaded from its moisture — is a great strain. And were I not afraid, I would say plainly that it deals when the mustard was kneaded erev Shabbat; and likewise in what he said cress that they ground — also deals with what was kneaded erev Shabbat; and what he rubs now on Shabbat does not make an act of kneading at all, for it was already kneaded; and rubbing does not improve the kneading except through rubbing that softens the mustard so it will be fit to drink — and this is not called kneading at all; only when he beats well with the palm there is a prohibition similar to kneading. And all we wrote is proven from Rambam's words: mustard kneaded erev Shabbat — the next day he rubs and drinks, whether by hand or in a vessel; and it is implied that all his making on Shabbat is only to make it soft so it will be fit to drink; and therefore this is not in the category of kneading at all; and though in a beverage that is thin a change is required to put food first — there it is different, for he mixes flour with water and therefore even when thin it is similar to kneading d'rabbanan and requires a change; but here it is the reverse — through rubbing in water he separates the parts that stuck through kneading; and therefore no prohibition at all unless he beats with force, for then it is somewhat like kneading; and therefore Rambam wrote it in chapter 22 among the shevut. And behold some of our words are hinted in what Beit Yosef brought in the name of Sefer Shibolei HaLeket in the name of Rabbeinu Yeshaya, see there.
