In chapter Tolim a Tannaitic teaching: one puts the flour and one puts the water last — liable — the words of Rabbi; R' Yochanan bar Yehuda says he is not liable until he kneads; behold they disagree on the matter of liability for kneading — for according to Rabbi the liability is at putting the water even though he has not yet kneaded, and according to R' Yochanan bar Yehuda until he kneads. And behold the known view that here in this seif and in the next seif, where they permitted to put beverage on Shabbat, is the view of Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, who rule like R' Yochanan bar Yehuda; and therefore no change is required in putting the beverage, only in kneading that is the mixing; and therefore Terumat HaDeshen required further a change in the mixing that he not beat with force but mix little by little, and in the vessel — such as in the palm — but in his hand, or he holds the vessel itself and shakes it so what is inside mixes; and this is the second view that Rama brings. And that which he stated "only by hand" — the same applies in the vessel itself, as is written; only he stated one; and the second view that Shulchan Aruch brings in the next seif is according to Beit HaTalmud, who rules like Rabbi, that in putting the beverage there is the prohibition; if so, the change he makes in the mixing does not help; only requires study, for there in chapter Tolim they said according to Rabbi: whether thick or thin — meaning in thick a change does not help and in thin a change helps — namely he puts food first, etc.; and Rama copied the change alone and did not mention specifically in his thin batter; and Terumat HaDeshen wrote: in truth in this one requires in his thin batter; and Tur wrote: and in Beit HaTalmud it is written: all that is permitted to mix beverage in mustard is specifically what was put erev Shabbat; but on Shabbat forbidden to put beverage in mustard or in anything they ground erev Shabbat, end of his words; it appears a change does not help — for otherwise he should have written the permission through a change, as the permission of change is stated per the first view there; only certainly a change does not help, even in his thin batter; and its reason appears per the Yerushalmi that Beit Yosef brings: when he gives beverage — liable due to kneading — and the Bavli does not argue that a change helps in thin batter, that there is a distinction between something that is subject to kneading or not; and as Terumat HaDeshen wrote in the name of Tosafos beginning of Shabbat, that they proved a complete proof that in something not subject to kneading, putting water applies more — this is its kneading, end of his words; and so Beit Yosef siman 324 in the name of Tosafos, Ra'avad, and Acharonim; and further, there it is explained in the Rav HaMagid that in any case there is prohibition to knead in something not subject to kneading, see there in our words; and therefore the Yerushalmi forbade in every case to put beverage on Shabbat in mustard and cress, which are not subject to kneading, and a change does not help; and if so, difficult also on Terumat HaDeshen, that a change helps for him in this in his thin batter. And it appears to me that those who practice permission through the change of putting food first that Rama mentioned hold the halacha is like R' Yochanan bar Yehuda, and as the first known view — namely the majority, Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur; and the same in seif 8; and by law no change is required except in what he does not mix with force; but for abundance of the matter they require further a change in putting food first; and therefore they did not require here in his thin batter; but per the second view it appears one should also do in his thin batter; it follows that one who forgot to pour vinegar erev Shabbat into the cream must do on Shabbat a thin batter and put vinegar first and afterward the cream, not in the manner they do on weekdays, and he should not mix with force or hold the vessel itself and shake it; and if so, requires study even according to Rama's view, who requires at least a change in food first in what they practice on Shabbat — very fine radish with cucumbers that they call ogorkes very fine and afterward pour vinegar into it, and they make no change; and it appears here they were not stringent like regarding cream, since it is impossible to do this erev Shabbat, for the taste of the radish would spoil — therefore they did not require a change, since the main making is on Shabbat that they abandoned the main halacha that we rule like R' Yochanan bar Yehuda; and if so, he must at least not mix with force or by means of the vessel, as is written; and preferable that he put vinegar first in the vessel, as appears correct to me in these matters. And Maharach z"l brought in the name of Rashal that he heard to be lenient regarding mustard and cream that we make, for in their time they made it like dough, unlike in our country where its soaking is not its kneading; and Maharach wrote on this that one should not be lenient from estimation of the mind; and regarding the vegetable called salad one need not be precise in all this, since it does not enter this category, since they do not cut it very fine, as stated in these matters:
