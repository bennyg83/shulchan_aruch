(א) Radish. And the same applies to anything that requires salting, such as onions and garlic; and Rambam explicitly wrote radish and the like. And see Taz, who expanded and raised that the matter here is that there are two reasons for the prohibition of salting in this, in what is similar to tanning. The first reason is that he leaves it a long time so that its bitterness emerges after it sweats in its usual manner — and in this, even when he dips one by one and leaves it for some time — forbidden. Second, from the reason that he salts many together — and then even if he does not leave it long — forbidden, for in this too it is similar to tanning. But whenever he salts individually, each one of what is before him, and wishes to eat immediately — there is no prohibition at all; only it is impossible to be careful if he scatters salt on each one independently so that it not also come upon its fellow placed near him — therefore he must dip each one independently and eat immediately. And it is not specifically immediately literally, but a short time that it sweat a little, not a lot. And so is the world custom not to salt them at all but to dip each one in salt and eat; and still requires study in our custom that on Shabbat they cut radish very fine and place it in a bowl and salt it and pour vinegar over it and eat it — this is comparable to many pieces that are forbidden even if he eats immediately, and the permission is only by way of dipping each one independently. And it appears the reason is since they do not leave it at all to sweat but pour vinegar there and also other species — it is not similar to tanning; only I have seen some who do a vegetable called salad and salt the vegetable independently first and leave it thus and strain the water that emerges from it and afterward mix it with olive oil and vinegar — and this is a full prohibition and is more similar to tanning, since he waits until it accepts the salt well, end of Taz's words. And Magen Avraham likewise raised not to salt the radish at all but only to dip each one in salt and eat.
