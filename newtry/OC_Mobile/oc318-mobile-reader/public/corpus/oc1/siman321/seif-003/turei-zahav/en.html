(א) Forbidden to salt pieces of radish, etc. In order to clarify this law we will copy the sugya and explain it, with Hashem's good help upon us, in chapter 8 regarding Sheratzim: a Tannaitic teaching — R' Yehuda says one may not salt radish and egg on Shabbat. Rashi explained: 3 or 4 pieces together, for salt tans them and they become hard and it is a repair. Rav Chisda said: radish — forbidden; egg — permitted; and so we rule. R' Nachman said: initially I would salt radishes on Shabbat — I said I am ruining it, for Shmuel said: a winter radish is good; when I heard that which Ulla said in the West — salted ones are fit — fit; from salting I do not salt — only dipping I certainly dip — at the time of eating I dip and eat. And Terumat HaDeshen siman 95 wrote: it appears permitted to dip many pieces of radish in salt and leave them before him in order to eat one after another without leaving them so long — for thus is implied in Rashi's language and in the text; the essence of the concern is in what is salted 2 or 3 together; and that which is written "but one dips at the time of eating" — it may be said it means at the time of eating, to exclude if he wished to leave a long time, such as from the beginning of the meal until after the end of the meal; but to eat them immediately one after another — there is no concern. And so in Or Zarua: and permitted to dip radish a short time on Shabbat — and it does not mean specifically one piece, for Semag and Semak wrote: radish is forbidden to salt in order to leave it in salt more than one piece, but to dip once — permitted, like R' Nachman — it appears in one piece there is no concern even to leave a long time, end of his words. At first glance the Rav's words are very astonishing here, for in the prohibition of salting he wrote that it depends on salting 3 or 4 together — it appears in individual salting it is permitted even for a long time; and in the permission of dipping he wrote according to that it is for a short time — but for a long time within that meal forbidden even individually; and if so this statement of R' Nachman that he said "I do not salt but I dip" — is neither the beginning the end nor the end the beginning, for he should have distinguished between individual and many, or between a short time and a long time; and further, why distinguish between eating one after another and between the beginning of the meal and its end — what distinction is there in this from that which was stated above regarding unripe grapes, that all that is within that meal is called immediately? And further, let us mention what may be said in Rashi's words, who hung the prohibition on salting many together — from where? For since by default we forbid salting radish, and Rambam too did not mention except radish in general, and so in Or Zarua that Terumat HaDeshen brought; and further, why did Rashi first write 3 or 4 and afterward write two together? And from this difficulty Maharach z"l entered into distinctions between two and three, and the mind cannot bear to distinguish in this; and further, in Tur it states 3 or 4, not 4 or 5; and it appears correct to me to explain Rashi's words that they agree with Semag and Semak that Terumat HaDeshen brings, and he ruled well for the law that there is permission in the case he wrote, even though Or Zarua's view appears not so, as is written nearby; and the matter is thus: there are two reasons in the prohibition of salting in this, in what is similar to tanning. One, because he leaves it a long time so its bitterness emerges after it sweats in its usual manner — in this, even when he dips one by one and leaves for some time — forbidden, as stated. Second, because he salts many together — and then even if he does not leave it long — forbidden, for in this too it is similar to tanning; and in this, dipping one by one is permitted. Therefore first he wrote the reason for prohibition in what is written "one may not salt radish" — there the permission of dipping is not mentioned — therefore Rashi explained the reason because he leaves it — meaning when they salt many pieces, presumably they do so to prepare for eating that they will eat later; and in this, even by way of dipping — namely one by one — also forbidden, since there will be some time in this. And afterward, when he said "from salting I do not salt but dipping I dip" — here he comes to distinguish between the permission of salting and dipping; and this is when it deals at the time he eats — then one by one permitted, and more than one — forbidden; therefore Rashi here states two pieces together for prohibition — meaning the opposite of the permission of dipping, where there is only one and it is at the time of eating; and all who salt individually each of what is before him and wishes to eat immediately — no prohibition at all, only impossible to salt — meaning to scatter salt on each independently so it not also come on its fellow placed near him, but must dip each independently and eat immediately. And if so, Semak wrote well that forbidden to salt radish in order to leave it in salt more than one piece — but one piece, whether alone or that many are before him and he wishes to salt each independently — permitted if he eats immediately, only impossible to be careful in this if he salts by scattering as salters do; therefore he concluded "rather he should dip and eat immediately" as dippers do. And that which Or Zarua wrote permitted to salt radish in salt to leave a short time — meaning so he eats it for a short time — and this is with one piece; but many, even for a short time — forbidden, as stated; only certainly he does not hold like Semak, that with one even to salt and leave a long time — permitted; and for Or Zarua even one forbidden to salt in order to leave. And Tur's words are explained well in what was stated according to Rashi's view, who wrote forbidden to salt 4 or 5 pieces of radish together, but dip each one alone and eat; it is clear, as stated, that two reasons are required: one, each independently; second, that he eat immediately, and not specifically immediately literally but a short time that it sweat a little, not a lot that it sweat much and is similar to tanning. And that which Tur wrote "and eat it" — does not mean he eats only immediately after dipping, but means at the time of eating, as appears correct to me. For the law he ruled well, Terumat HaDeshen; only in explaining the words of the Gemara and poskim we explained somewhat differently; and still requires study in our custom that on Shabbat they cut radish very fine and place it in a bowl and salt it and pour vinegar over it and eat it — this is comparable to many pieces that are forbidden even if he eats immediately, and the permission is only by way of dipping each one independently; and it appears the reason is since they do not leave it at all to sweat but pour vinegar there and also other species — it is not similar to tanning; only I have seen some who do a vegetable called salad and salt the vegetable independently first and leave it thus and strain the water that emerges and afterward mix it with olive oil and vinegar — and this is a full prohibition and is more similar to tanning, since he waits until it accepts the salt well, as stated:
