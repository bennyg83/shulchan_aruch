[5] Four or five, etc. Bach writes that common custom is not to salt them together at all, but each person dips each piece in salt when he wishes to eat it. Maharil, Hilchot Yom Tov, writes similarly. This also appears to me to be the Gemara's meaning: even salting one piece in the prohibited fashion resembles tanning and is prohibited. Rambam and Raavan, siman 352, likewise imply this. Shiltei Gibborim writes that one may put salt on salad and afterward add oil and vinegar. Taz writes that he has seen people leave it salted until the water drains and only then add oil and vinegar, and calls this a complete prohibition because it resembles tanning. Perhaps it resembles the eggs of seif 4. Onions and fat have the law of radish. Magen Avraham writes that raw cucumbers have the law of radish unless oil is added immediately. See seif katan 3.
[6] Because it appears like pickling, etc. Therefore it is prohibited on Shabbos to pour wine or other liquids into a vessel containing vinegar in order that the new liquid also become vinegar, because this resembles pickling (Taz).
