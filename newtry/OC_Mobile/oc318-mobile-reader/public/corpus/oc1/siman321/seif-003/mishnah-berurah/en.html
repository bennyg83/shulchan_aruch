(א) Radish — and likewise everything that requires salting, such as onions, garlic, and raw cucumbers [Magen Avraham in s.k. 7]; and explicitly Rambam wrote radish and the like; nevertheless it appears that something whose manner is not to pickle is permitted [Peri Megadim]:
(ב) Four or five together — for when each piece lies alone there is no concern of pickling like pickled vegetables, and it also does not appear like tanning; but when he salts them together, even if his intent is to eat them immediately and not to leave them in their salt until their bitterness comes out through sweating as is their manner — likewise forbidden. And if he leaves them in their salt until they sweat as is their manner — even salting or dipping one piece of radish is forbidden by law; thus ruled Taz, and so R' Avraham wrote that salting radish is forbidden even one piece; and in Magen Avraham in s.k. 6 in the name of Bach he also wrote that the world is accustomed not to salt them at all but each one dips in salt and eats. And our practice is to cut radish fine on Shabbat and put it in a bowl and salt it and pour vinegar on it and eat it; and at first glance this is similar to many pieces that are forbidden by law even if he eats immediately, and the permission is only by way of dipping each one by itself; and it appears that since they do not leave it at all to sweat but pour vinegar there, and other types are also not similar to tanning — all the more if they pour oil there immediately, for oil weakens the strength of the salt; and in some places they make a vegetable called salad and salt the vegetable by itself first and leave it so and strain the water that comes from it and afterward mix it with olive oil and vinegar — and this is absolute prohibition and is more similar to tanning, since he waits until it accepts the salt well:
(ג) Like pickling pickled vegetables, etc. — this reason is from Rambam; and Rashi gave a reason that through salt they harden and it is a correction and similar to tanning. And Taz wrote that from Shulchan Aruch's reason one may learn that it is forbidden to pour wine and other liquids into a vessel that has vinegar in it so that this too will become vinegar, for it is similar to pickling pickled vegetables, since he intends that the little he pours there will be pickled and become vinegar. And Yad Aharon wrote it is forbidden because it appears like a weekday act on Shabbat; and if he does this only to weaken the strength of the vinegar alone — it is obvious it is permitted according to all. And to put raw cucumbers into pickles so they sour — likewise obvious it is forbidden according to all:
(ד) Because it is, etc. — even though certainly there is no liability since it is not a toldah of fire, nevertheless forbidden d'rabbanan:
(ה) And he eats it — immediately; and it does not mean literally immediately, but a short time that it sweats a little, not much [Taz]:
