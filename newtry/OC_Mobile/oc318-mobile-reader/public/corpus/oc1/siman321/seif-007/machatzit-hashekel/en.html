And Tosafos and Rosh in Chullin, etc., that we taught there: one who slaughters on Shabbat and Yom Kippur — although he is liable with his life his slaughter is valid; and the Gemara deduces: it teaches Shabbat similar to Yom Kippur — just as on Yom Kippur forbidden to eat that day, so on Shabbat that day forbidden; perforce it comes like R' Yehuda who said muktzeh; and Tosafos and Rosh proved from this that perforce permitted to eat raw meat without salting, for it is blood of limbs not separated; and permitted, for otherwise whence that it comes like R' Yehuda; tell us: otherwise impossible to eat it on Shabbat, for forbidden to salt on Shabbat; and so Ran, etc., as we say regarding pigla, etc.; and all the more one can forbid, etc., for it fixes it — meaning regarding pigla: initially R' Nachman thought to permit salting because it causes loss by ruining its sharpness until he heard in the west they salt it; perforce salting fixes the radish and he retracted and forbade salting. And even though he proved from the practice in the west that salt fixes radish — nevertheless it ruins the sharpness and thereby causes loss; perforce one must say the repair done to it outweighs the loss and therefore he forbade salting; all the more in salting meat where there is no loss at all but only fixing — fit to forbid; and all the more if you forbid him without salting, greater repair. Meaning also Ran comes to prove permitted to eat raw meat without salting, for otherwise without muktzeh prohibition impossible to eat on Shabbat, for it needs salting; and if one would salt on Shabbat — according to that view that raw meat is forbidden to eat without salting, certainly salting on Shabbat is forbidden, for now it is complete fixing, for without salting impossible to eat according to that view:
