[11] It is permitted, etc. Rambam, chapter 21, writes that one who needs to crush pepper or similar spices for food on Shabbos crushes them with the handle of a knife and in a bowl, but not in a mortar. Magen Avraham explains "or in a bowl" as crushing against the bottom of the bowl. Rambam's wording does not imply two alternatives; rather, both changes are required, using the knife handle and crushing inside a bowl. Beit Yosef explains likewise. When the Gemara mentions only the knife handle, one must say it assumes that in that manner he is not using a mortar. Semak, laws of grinding, and Ran, chapter Tolin, imply this. There is also proof from Kol Bo, p. 33, who writes that coarse salt may be scattered on the table and crushed with the knife handle, implying specifically on the table. He concludes there that salt and spices share the same rule. Thus Rambam's phrase "but not in a mortar" means that even crushing against a bowl bottom is a changed manner, while the requirement is to crush in an ordinary bowl or similar surface rather than a mortar. This is Agur's intent. Taz writes that one should not place pepper inside a cloth and pound it with a knife because that creates fragrance. See siman 511.
[12] It is permitted to cut it, etc. This supports Magen Avraham's ruling that pepper and all spices may not be finely cut with a knife, since only salt, which is already granular, is permitted. Crumbling by hand is plainly permitted. Magen Avraham somewhat implies that even with a knife it may be permitted when cutting for immediate eating. See seif 12. This requires study.
