[9] To leave it, etc. This implies that even leaving it from one meal to another is prohibited, whereas radish is prohibited even for the present meal (Magen Avraham). This is not compelled. See seif katan 3. Rabbenu Yerucham, p. 77, writes that meat fit to be eaten that day, whether cooked or raw if one wishes to eat it raw, may be salted. Thus it suffices that it be fit for that day, even if not for this specific meal. Shibbolei HaLeket, cited by Beit Yosef, implies that when meat will be lost if not salted, one may salt it by using the device of intending to eat it and then changing his mind. Taz understood that he must actually eat it on Shabbos, but my view is as stated. If the item is not fit for Shabbos, however, this device does not apply and it may not be salted even if it will spoil. Magen Avraham cites Tosafot, Rosh, Rashba, and Ran that meat may not be salted even when one wishes to eat it on Shabbos because salting repairs it. Rabbenu Yerucham and Shibbolei HaLeket clearly permit it. Perhaps all agree cooked meat is permitted. Magen Avraham cites Rashba, Bayit 3 Shaar 3, that one who salts meat for storage on a journey is liable for a sin offering. He questions this because the Gemara appears to rule like Rava that there is no tanning of food, and Rambam chapter 11 rules so. It appears possible that since Rav Ashi on 75a explains Rabbah's view, the law follows Rabbah. I found Maharshal's commentary to Semag stating that there is no tanning in food, but later that one who salts meat to take on a distant journey is liable for tanning. This is surprising, because Rava explicitly exempts even for a distant journey. It appears the later statement refers only to the amount of salt required, while for Shabbos liability Semag relies on his earlier ruling that there is no tanning in food. Rashba may be reconciled similarly.
