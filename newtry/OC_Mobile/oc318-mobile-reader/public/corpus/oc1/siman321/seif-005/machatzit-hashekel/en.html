(א) (s.k. 5) Eggs, etc.; and so in Terumat HaDeshen, etc., on fish on Yom Tov — meaning he wanted to forbid salted fish on Yom Tov if they split from erev Yom Tov, since salt improves them; and he brings proof from R' Nachman; and as will be explained it implies that if salting did not improve them — it would be permitted:

(ב) As Magen Avraham wrote regarding radish, etc., for we say in the Gemara: R' Yehuda ben Chachinai taught: one does not salt radish on Shabbat; R' Nachman said: initially I would salt pigla (namely radish); I said I ruin it, for excellent sharp radish — through salting its sharpness is ruined; when I heard that in the west they salt it — permitted (namely they hang many); apparently salting improves it — I do not salt, but I dip; behold according to what he thought that salting causes loss — he would have permitted salting radish; perforce where it causes loss there is no need that it be like pickling kavush or tanning; and even though Magen Avraham permits even where it does not cause loss but only does not fix — nevertheless from reasoning it is fit to say where it does not fix it is not like pickling kavush or tanning, where they fix; rather he thought that nevertheless it is fit to forbid on account of "do not distinguish" for this; he brings proof that R' Nachman thought to permit where it causes loss; perforce they did not forbid on account of "do not distinguish"; if so automatically where it does not fix it is not forbidden on account of "do not distinguish"; and the law of salting fish on Yom Tov will be explained later siman 500 s.k. 11:
