To leave it. It is implied even to leave it from meal to meal — forbidden; but radish, even for the need of that meal — forbidden (but in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he is uncertain about it). And Taz wrote: it appears there is no clear prohibition in this even according to their words, but from the aspect that he does not need this and why should he trouble himself for nothing on Shabbat — behold he can salt at another meal that he will eat on a weekday; it follows that if there is some aspect whereby it is better for him to salt now than to salt later, such as that it is now somewhat warm and will accept the salt more — there is no prohibition in the matter, end of his words. And see in responsum Pnei Me'orot siman 16 and in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 12. And Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me the same applies — it is forbidden to salt raw cucumbers, for they are accustomed to make pickles from them; and further, salting helps them and it is like radish; and the same applies to anything similar. And see in Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 12 that he inclined toward permission. However, when he puts oil immediately — permitted. Raw meat or fish — forbidden to salt on Shabbat so that it not spoil; see end of siman 500, for it is implied even where there is loss — forbidden — Beit Yosef in the name of Rashal. And Tosafos and Rosh in Chullin daf 14 wrote that even if he wishes to eat it thus raw — forbidden to salt it; and even though there is no tanning in foods, as is written at the end of siman 500 — nevertheless forbidden, for it appears like tanning, since salt makes the food fit and fixes it; and all the more so there is room to forbid salting raw meat, for he fixes it; and all the more so if you forbid it without salting — it is a greater repair. And it appears to me permitted to put the meat into vinegar in order to eat it, as is written in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 67. And it is clear from their words that permitted to rinse the meat in order to eat it, since the prohibition is not in the meat itself but that he rinses it due to blood in the eye — upon this it is permitted. And it appears forbidden to rinse meat on which the third day fell on Shabbat so that it not become forbidden for cooking — since he does not wish to eat it on that day, forbidden to rinse it, as is written siman 323 seif 6.
