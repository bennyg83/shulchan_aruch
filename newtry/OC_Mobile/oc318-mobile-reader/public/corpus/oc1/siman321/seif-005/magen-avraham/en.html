It implies to leave from meal to meal is forbidden; but radish even for that meal is forbidden; and it appears likewise forbidden to salt live cucumbers, for they regularly make kavush from them, and salting is effective for them like radish; and the same for all similar; however when he gives oil immediately — permitted like R' Yosi; and permitted to put salt on poultry or fish pieces and afterward give water or wine upon them (Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 22 in name of Semak). Raw meat or fish is forbidden to salt on Shabbat so it not spoil; see Yom Tov siman 500, which implies even where there is loss it is forbidden (Beit Yosef in name of Maharshal there). And Tosafos and Rosh in Chullin daf 14 wrote that even if one wants to eat it thus raw — forbidden to salt; although there is no tanning in foods as siman 500, nevertheless forbidden because it appears like tanning; and so Ran in Chullin, because salt prepares the food and fixes it as we say regarding pigul; all the more one can forbid salting raw meat, for it fixes it; all the more if you forbid him without salting it is greater repair [and see siman 314], end quote; and so Rashba responsa vol. 3, etc., and see there that they wrote one who salts meat to store for travel is liable for a chatas; and requires study, for in the Gemara it appears the halacha is like Rava who is exempt, for he is later; and so Rambam ch. 11. And it appears permitted to put the meat into vinegar in order to eat it as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 67; and it is clear from their words that it is permitted to rinse the meat in order to eat it; and Porat Yosef challenged from immersing vessels that is forbidden because one fixes the vessel as siman 323 seif 7; and the same should be forbidden here, and he strained in his answer and did not see what is written there seif 8 that it is permitted to rinse the cup; and the same here, since the prohibition is not in the body of the meat but he rinses it on account of blood visible upon it — permitted; and it appears forbidden to rinse meat when the third day falls on Shabbat so that it not become forbidden for cooking, since he does not want to eat it that day — forbidden to rinse it, as siman 323 seif 6 and siman 302 seif 3; and so in Pesachim ch. 7 that even on Pesach they did not permit rinsing the innards since it is not needed that day. And one gaon permitted as siman 311 regarding vegetables, since they are fit to eat that day — permitted to give them water as it is permitted to move them; and it appears forbidden, for it fixes the meat that it soften, as above in name of Ran; but regarding vegetables it does not fix at all; and further, according to what is written siman 308 seif 191 that meat is forbidden to move — if so likewise forbidden to put water on it; and further that even a preparatory act is forbidden for weekday need, such as to bring wine for Motzei Shabbat; and all the more in such a case; and one should not compare to vegetables that perhaps one eats their kinds that day, unlike meat where the matter is clear that it is for weekday need — therefore one may not be lenient; and see siman 397 seif 2; and even through a non-Jew one may not be lenient, for there is not so much loss, for he can eat it roasted; see siman 307 seif 5; and in Maharil laws of Pesach he wrote, end quote: and were it not that the great ones mumbled about the matter I would have said it is permitted to rinse them, for it is a slight need lest it sour and the vessel be lost; for even for muktzeh they make a repair such as placing a vessel upon it, etc.; but my heart was not full to be lenient; therefore it is good to rinse them through a non-Jew, end quote; and see siman 459; and nevertheless here there is no loss, for he can eat it roasted:
