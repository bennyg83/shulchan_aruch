(s.k. 4) A little, etc. — it implies that for another meal even a little is forbidden; even if he gives oil — forbidden; so it appears to me; and so Magen Avraham seif 5; and he wrote in Sefer Teshuvah, end quote: and this is when he gives oil afterward, for that does not help since he already made a prohibition, etc., before giving the oil, etc.; but if he gives oil before giving the salt or water — permitted, for according to R' Yosi there is no distinction between much and little, and nevertheless permitted according to him when he gives oil first; and from this we learn that also per the rabbis it is permitted in such a case even in large quantity; and so Hagahot Maimoniyot explicitly, etc., end quote; and that which we taught daf 108: one does not make much salt water (even if his intent is to put it into the cooked dish, since when it is much their manner is to put it into kavush — forbidden because it appears like pickling kavush); but permitted to make a little salt water and put it into the cooked dish; R' Yosi says whether much or little — forbidden unless he gives oil and salt or oil and water first; and we rule like the rabbis; and behold Magen Avraham's intent here is to forbid for another meal even a little, even if he gives oil; and per the explanation of Sefer Teshuvah — meaning when he gives oil afterward; but if he gives oil first — permitted; and they bring proof that when giving oil first even much is permitted from R' Yosi's words; and one must say he holds that just as giving oil first helps even for much, so it helps to permit even for another meal; but in my opinion it is not so, for if Magen Avraham's intent in what he wrote is that for another meal it is forbidden even if he gives oil — when he gives oil at the end it is plainly forbidden; and this is hilmi, namely the salt water mentioned in the Gemara that is forbidden; and also what Magen Avraham concluded "so it appears to me" — it appears he wrote thus from his own reasoning and it is an obvious matter; also why does he need proof from seif 5; also there is no proof from there; therefore it appears there is a scribal error in Magen Avraham; and it should read: it implies that for another meal it is forbidden even if he gives oil first; and now he teaches much: although in such a case even for R' Yosi it is permitted, and the same for the rabbis — permitted even much, as Sefer Teshuvah wrote as above; nevertheless from meal to meal even a little is forbidden; and on this he brings proof from seif 5 that forbids salting a cooked egg to leave it, although in an egg there is no pickling kavush concern and also it is not like tanning as Magen Avraham wrote in small seif 5; therefore permitted for that meal; even so for another meal — forbidden; and see there the reason; if so the same when giving oil first, although through this it no longer appears like pickling kavush or tanning — nevertheless from meal to meal it is forbidden; and what Sefer Teshuvah brought as proof from Hagahot Maimoniyot — that is specifically to permit even much, but for another meal we did not hear; and see Magen Avraham s.k. 7 where he brought that Hagahot Maimoniyot, and it is explained there;
