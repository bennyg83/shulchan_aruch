7) [Seif 2] One may not prepare brine, etc. This means water mixed with salt, or salt mixed with wine, vinegar, or another beverage. Semag and Semak; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
8) There: One may not prepare a large quantity of brine, etc. The wording implies that it is forbidden specifically for placing into foods to be pickled, but that one may make a large quantity for immediate consumption or to add to a cooked dish. The end of the ruling, however, which states 'but one may prepare,' implies the opposite, that a large quantity is forbidden even for a cooked dish. It appears to me that the Shulchan Aruch describes the usual case: people ordinarily make a large quantity for pickling and a small quantity for a cooked dish. In truth, a large quantity is forbidden even when made for a cooked dish, because the Sages did not distinguish. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, likewise writes that when one prepares a large quantity, it is forbidden even for a cooked dish and even for that meal, because it appears that he is performing the labor of preparing food. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah writes the same, contrary to Tosefes Shabbos; see there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, and Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraph 19, write likewise.
9) There: One may not prepare a large quantity of brine, etc. If one first put oil into the water and afterward added salt, or first put oil into the salt and afterward added water, Olas Shabbos writes there that one may make a large quantity. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 7, writes the same; see there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that even when oil was added first, one may not make a large quantity; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 2, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav there write likewise. See Eshel Avraham, paragraph 4, who writes that this also appears from the Shulchan Aruch; see there. It therefore appears that one should be stringent.
10) There: To place into foods being pickled. This means vegetables or other items pickled for preservation. It is forbidden even if he will eat some immediately, so that his effort is not solely for a weekday. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
11) There: But he may prepare a small amount, etc. This means the amount needed for dipping during that meal. Ran. This implies that even a small amount is forbidden for another meal. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4; Olas Shabbos there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there; Ben Ish Chai there. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3, however, writes that one may prepare everything needed for that Shabbos even if not for that meal. Ran mentions that meal only because this is the usual case: since one prepares it only for that meal, he makes a small amount. Eliyah Rabbah writes that Rambam, chapter 22, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, page 77, also imply this, and that it is explicit in Shibolei HaLeket, siman 4, as well as implied by Sefer HaTanya, contrary to Olas Shabbos and Magen Avraham; see there. Nehar Shalom also inclines this way. Pischei HaDevir, paragraph 2, likewise writes that a small amount does not fall within the category of labor and is therefore permitted even for another meal that day. This differs from selecting, which remains forbidden even for later that day; see there. Nevertheless, since the matter is disputed, it is proper to take care to prepare it shortly before the meal.
12) There: A small amount to place in a cooked dish. This implies that even a small amount may not be placed into foods intended for pickling. The beginning of the seif mentions not making a large quantity for pickling only because the usual manner is to prepare a large quantity for that purpose, as stated above in paragraph 8. In law, however, even a small amount is forbidden because pickling itself is forbidden on Shabbos, as stated below in seif 3; see there.
13) There: Into a cooked dish. The same may be poured upon pieces of poultry or roasted fish. It is certainly permitted when one first places salt upon them and afterward adds the liquid, since even Rabbi Yose permits this because fat sweetens the salt like oil. Tosafos there; Sefer HaTerumah; Semag; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4. See below, paragraph 32.
14) There: If he put two thirds salt into it. The remaining third consists of water or another liquid. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. See Yesod Yosef, who writes that one likewise may not prepare a mixture of two thirds sugar and one third water; see there.
15) There: One may not prepare even a small amount of it. Rambam, chapter 22, halachah 10, explains that it appears as though one is preparing fish brine. Beis Yosef writes that ordinary fish brine is made to last for an extended time and is used to preserve fish. It is therefore forbidden because it appears that one is pickling. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4, writes in the name of Maharash that one may add vinegar to fish brine after it has dried. The later authorities cite this ruling.
16) There: Even a small amount. It is forbidden even for dipping during that meal. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
