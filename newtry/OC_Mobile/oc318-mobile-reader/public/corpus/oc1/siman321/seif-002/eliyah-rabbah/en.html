[2] Salt water, etc. Meaning water mixed with salt, or wine and vinegar, or another beverage (Semag).
[3] A large quantity, etc. Then it is prohibited even when intended for the cooked food of this meal, because it appears that he is performing part of the food's preparation. A small quantity may be made whenever needed for that Shabbos, even if not for this particular meal. Ran's phrase "for this meal" is merely the usual case, because one who prepares only for the present meal ordinarily makes little. Rambam, chapter 22, and Rabbenu Yerucham, p. 77, likewise imply this. Shibbolei HaLeket, siman 4, explicitly writes that one may make a small amount of salt water on Shabbos for the needs of that day. Tanya likewise implies this, contrary to Olat Shabbat and Magen Avraham. Olat Shabbat writes that the Gemara clearly permits even a large amount if oil is first placed into the water or salt, and wonders why Shulchan Aruch omitted it. In my humble opinion Shulchan Aruch was correct, because the Sages appear to disagree with Rabbi Yose and prohibit even when oil is added first. Rambam, Rabbenu Yerucham, Mordechai, and Shiltei Gibborim likewise imply this.
[4] Into the cooked food, etc. The same permission applies to pouring it over pieces of chicken or roasted fish. All the more so, one may first put salt alone on them and afterward add liquid, for even Rabbi Yose permits this because fat tempers the salt like oil (Tosafot; Sefer HaTerumah; Semag). Maharam permitted adding vinegar to fish brine that had almost completely dried. Maharil prohibits placing a certain spice into vinegar for dipping in place of fish brine, and likewise preparing hard boiled eggs with parsley in vinegar because it resembles strong brine. Magen Avraham, seif katan 25, questions this because it resembles yinomlin, which is permitted, and a small amount is permitted according to all.
