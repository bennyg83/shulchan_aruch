(א) Brine — and likewise if he puts wine, vinegar, and other liquids in salt:
(ב) Pickled vegetables (kavshim) — a vegetable that they pickle in order to preserve it; since one puts brine in order to preserve it, it is similar to tanning hides that salt preserves; and even though we rule there is no tanning of foods d'oraisa, nevertheless d'rabbanan it is forbidden as the poskim wrote; and it is written in Tosafot Shabbos that specifically if his intent is to put it into the pickled vegetables, but if his intent is not for this [meaning his intent is to divide them into cooked dishes] even much is permitted; but R' Avraham forbids, and so is implied in Meiri who wrote that only in a small amount did they permit because it is recognizable that he does only for the need of the hour, unlike in much where they will think he does for weekday need on Shabbat:
(ג) Because it is similar to tanning — and even if he puts oil into brine to season it, likewise forbidden. And if he puts oil into the water before he puts salt into it, or into the salt before he puts water into it — some are lenient in this because the oil weakens the strength of the salt from being strong and the name of brine does not apply to it; and the reason they forbade if he puts oil at the end is that at the time he puts water and salt together he appears like one who tans:
(ד) The cooked dish — or to dip his bread in them; and likewise permitted to pour on roasted poultry and fish pieces, whether he does so for the need of this meal or for another need; but forbidden to do for weekday need:
