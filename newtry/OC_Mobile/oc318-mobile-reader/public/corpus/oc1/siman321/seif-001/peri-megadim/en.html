And a garment that absorbed — some say it is liable d'oraisa because of sechita even though not from its body; and if not so — in Magen Avraham it will be explained further from this. (ב) Forbidden — his words require explanation. Know that the distinction between salting and dipping will be explained in three ways: (1) salting — he scatters salt in throwing upon it — it appears like melacha of me'abed even though there is no me'abed in foods for liability — nevertheless rabbinically forbidden as Tosafos 75b s.v. ein wrote; and dipping — he dips in salt. (2) When he salts — it means dipping only for a long time; and dipping — he dips and eats immediately. (3) Salting implies many pieces; and dipping — one piece alone. For the way is to throw once on several pieces, etc. And behold Rashi 128b explained in the baraita: radish and egg — one does not salt four or five together — he labors them, etc.; and "I do not salt two" — they contradict each other; one may say above deals for a long time — until end of the meal (3: immediately; 2: there should be a little; 3: until end of meal); and the way of four or five is until end of meal; and salting four or five is only dipping — now they dispute: some say egg also he labors; some say egg at end of meal he does not labor; and so is the halacha; but to leave it until another meal — egg also forbidden. And this that "I do not salt two" immediately — also forbidden, for it is the way of me'abed through scattering two together — one alone through scattering permitted; and two eggs together through scattering forbidden because it resembles me'abed as it says; therefore Rashi was forced to explain above thus. And the Mechaber chose the way of Rambam here — now two eggs together permitted, for there is no way to pickle eggs (Magen Avraham sign 5, Levush). And the baraita disputes salting as its plain meaning — two together through scattering: some say it resembles melacha of me'abed and eggs also forbidden; some say the reason is it resembles pickling pickled vegetables and eggs permitted. And therefore in seif 2 he chose Tur's way — certainly in foods rabbinically there is me'abed; and in seif 6 to leave it — egg forbidden because there is me'abed rabbinically (sign 5, and I will explain).
