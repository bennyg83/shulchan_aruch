(א) One who cuts vegetables — detached; and from the Stam it is implied that even in vegetables fit to eat raw he also holds there is grinding in foods:
(ב) Before elders — who cannot chew unless he cuts them fine; and we do not follow the majority of the world as above in seif 9 regarding roasted meat, for since it is something that grows from the ground it is more stringent, for the essence of grinding is in things that grow from the ground:
(ג) To crumble bread — meaning even to prepare for them that they will eat after time, as below:
(ד) Since — and therefore to crumble potatoes and vegetables before them fine as is practiced before animals and large birds — forbidden unless he is careful that this matter is adjacent to their eating immediately, as below; nevertheless good and proper to be careful not to cut fine but somewhat large pieces; and especially before animals certainly one should be careful in this, as below in siman 324 seif 7 that one should not exert oneself in something fit to eat without this:
(ה) And all this does not deal — it refers also to cutting vegetables fine mentioned at the beginning of the seif:
(ו) To eat it immediately — and likewise one may cut for the need of others and they eat immediately, as we say above regarding borer [Beit Yosef]:
(ז) Everything, etc. — the reason that grinding does not apply when he cuts with a knife but in grinding to leave; but in order to eat immediately — certainly permitted, for they did not forbid a person to eat his food in large or small pieces; if so it is the manner of his eating thus; and likewise if he prepares vegetables fine before chickens that they eat immediately — likewise permitted, since their sustenance is upon you:
(ח) Permitted — and some question this permission; and also in Beit Yosef he wrote that even one who intends to eat immediately should also be careful to cut somewhat large pieces; and for this reason several Acharonim also wrote it is proper to conduct as Beit Yosef wrote; nevertheless those who practice to cut onions and radish fine — one should not protest against them, for they have upon whom to rely; and in any case forbidden to do until leaving the synagogue, for we require adjacent to the meal immediately, as is written in siman 319 regarding borer. And those who cut onions and radish fine an hour or two before the meal — it is close to say he is liable for a chatas and the onions are forbidden in eating; and also then forbidden on account of borer — see at the end of the siman in the gloss [so too in Chayei Adam]. And see in Be'ur Halacha that the permission mentioned in the gloss is when he cuts with a knife, but when he cuts with a vessel designated for this — it appears forbidden in every manner when he cuts fine, for also in borer itself if with a vessel designated for selecting in such a case he is liable, as above in seif 19; and therefore it is reasonable to be careful in what is practiced to cut onions fine with salted fish and the like that he not cut them in a vessel [called hackmesser] that possibly is considered a designated vessel — only with a knife alone; and also in any case forbidden on account of a weekday act, as above in seif 10. One who crumbles a piece of wood that rotted and makes powder from it, and one who crumbles a clod of earth — liable on account of grinding [Shabbos 74b]. And likewise one who saws wood to benefit from its sawdust, or planes a metal tongue [called file in German] — liable for whatever he planes. And one who cuts wood fine and makes them small — if he was particular from them enough to cook like an egg-sized dried fig — liable [Rambam and Magen Avraham in siman 314].
