(א) (s.k. 12) That they call, etc.; Rivash's responsum — there is no translation; I found only in Sefer Teshuvah that he brought his language to forbid grating cheese, end quote: like that which we say: one who slices celery is liable on account of grinding; and even if we say for immediate eating — and even in a vessel — that is specifically in a vessel like a knife that is not designated for grinding; but a grater, etc., that is designated for grinding — the same as a sieve and sifter for borer and a mortar for spices; and the Sages already forbade except with a knife handle that is a complete change; and further possible to forbid even on Yom Tov, since it is designated for this grinding and it is like pepper in their mill that is forbidden even on Yom Tov on account of weekday activity, etc.; except one can distinguish that pepper in a mill is different, for they regularly grind much together and it resembles weekday activity; but in this vessel there is no manner to grate except what is needed for the moment; and nevertheless it appears one needs a change similar to salt, because it does not spoil the taste, etc.; but on Shabbat it appears forbidden without doubt; and Ohel Yesharim challenged: here he ruled to forbid, and in Yom Tov laws siman 504 Rama wrote permitted to grate cheese through a change; and behold what the Mechaber forbade here — grating — perforce the reason is Rivash's first reason, grinding, for the Mechaber ruled meat is not called a plant and there is no grinding concern; perforce forbidden on account of weekday activity like Rivash's second reason; and therefore Sefer Teshuvah challenged: why does Rama permit through a change on Yom Tov, for according to this reason Rivash wrote there is no distinction between Shabbat and Yom Tov; also from Magen Avraham's words it appears he holds we rule like Rivash's second reason and therefore wrote forbidden even to eat immediately; and permitted to cut fine fine as in seif 9, namely cooked meat; and Sefer Teshuvah explained: specifically on Shabbat Rabbenu Yonah forbids, since there is an opinion that an animal is called a plant; although Rabbenu Yonah did not rule thus, nevertheless it helps for his need — weekday activity reason — and forbidden; unlike Yom Tov where there is no grinding prohibition but only weekday activity; and for this reason he did not want to forbid grating, since it is not done for many days:
