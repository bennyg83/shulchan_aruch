That it is forbidden to cut. What is written here in the name of the Tosefta is not correct, for the Tosefta teaches: one may not crush dried figs, etc., but one may crush them with the handle of a knife. If crushing meant cutting, it would be difficult, for how is it possible to cut with the handle of a knife? Therefore it appears to me that crushing means pounding with a beam, as written in siman 320, seif 2, which is forbidden, but one may crush it with the handle of a knife. So too the Tosefta teaches at the beginning regarding salt; see there, and as written in seif 8. Nevertheless, it appears to me that the law is true, that it is forbidden to cut a dried fig, since it grows from the ground, as written in seif 9. It implies that for someone who can chew it as it is, it is permitted.
To eat immediately. Or if the chickens will eat it immediately, it is permitted to cut it, as the Ran writes, for they did not forbid a person to eat his food in large or small pieces; if so, this is its manner of eating, as the Beit Yosef writes. In Shiltei Gibborim he questions this leniency, and the Beit Yosef also wrote that one should be careful to cut the pieces somewhat large; see there. But in our countries the practice is to cut radish very finely, and they have authorities on whom to rely. In any event, it is forbidden to do this until after leaving the synagogue, for it must be truly close to the meal, as written at the beginning of siman 319, and so it appears from the Ran at the end of chapter 15.
