(א) And he puts (sign 5). Said the writer: before I come to clarify the Mechaber's words in seifim 14, 15, and 16 and Taz's words — I will precede with a small introduction, and this is its description. And know that kneading is a primary melacha in flour and in dust — toldah (Rambam chapter 1 law 16); and this is even in honey and even in goose fat and the like; nevertheless kneading and it sticks. However for Rabbi — giving water is kneading; one may say in putting goose fat and hardened honey even for Rabbi there is no chatas liability, for kneading requires like dried figs in size; and in water it enters in flour — unlike in hardened honey and fat. And in Einayim HaGolah he challenged what Ramaz wrote: flax seed — liable (Zevachim 94b) — since they stick to each other, all the more flour that sticks — and why did he rule like R' Yehuda in the name of R' Yehoshua that giving water exempt; and apparently it is a strong question; and possibly flax seed stuck in them can be taken in the hands and made into a lump — liable for kneading; unlike flour in water — without kneading the adhesion is not good and cannot be taken by hand; deals siman 320 seif 12. And know further that bran and coarse meal have explanations (Magen Avraham siman 454); and Pri Chadash there: bran is the thick peel that falls in grinding; and coarse meal what remains in the sieve; and some say the reverse. And one may say the thin in it — binding applies; but the thick that falls in grinding — the sense testifies there is no binding as said; and in our chiddushim we wrote from this:

(ב) Now let us come to explain the Mechaber's, Mishna Berurah's, and Taz's words in seifim 14, 15, and 16 with Hashem's help. In Shabbos 155 and 156 and 118 — dispute of Rabbi and R' Yehuda in the name of R' Yehoshua whether liable when giving water to bran. Rif and Rambam and Rosh and Tur ruled like R' Yehuda in the name of R' Yehoshua: giving water — not liable until he kneads. And as the Mishnah 155b: one puts water into bran and does not knead. And what Rif wrote 155 — since we resolve according to R' Yehuda in the name of R' Yehoshua — infer the halacha is like him; and from the Mishnah there is no proof, for the Mishnah in the first chapter appears like Rabbi — see there; or that passage: since it says "how does one answer" on top of on top — infer the halacha is like him, meaning like one authority and not like the first tanna who said one does not knead; and we rule from this that roasted — thick permitted through a change a little at a time as in Einayim HaGolah: water he puts much immediately in chapter 1 — only the kneading is a little of what he eats that meal; and see Ran there and bran permitted much through a change — he puts flour first; and Rosh there omitted "how does one answer on top of on top" and wrote: infer halacha like him; perhaps there is a printing omission. And for all of them — for Rif and Rosh and Tur and Rashi — shatit is flour roasted, only this thick and that soft. And therefore one may say roasted is not subject to binding and rabbinically forbidden; and where it answers thick — we require a little at a time, for it is a great change; behold flour first is not similar to kneading; and bran — any change helps, flour first; or roasted is subject to binding and he mentions roasted because it is fit on Shabbat to eat — unlike flour with water not fit on Shabbat; and deals siman 324 Magen Avraham sign 1. But Rambam explained roasted — nevertheless not subject to binding and rabbinically forbidden; and through a change on top of on top permitted; behold flour thus for chickens forbidden through a change — whatever is its way nevertheless through a change rabbinically forbidden unless a great change or two changes as in siman 321 sign 7 and shatit is grain that did not bring a third — some say permitted because it is like bran and there is no kneading; and it challenges: but it teaches (letter 33: and it says) in another baraita shatit forbidden in thick in much (some texts: on top of on top like roasted); and bran permitted in much through a change — a little flour first; behold roasted — bran does not help, a little change only on top of on top specifically; and the Mechaber in seif 14 who omitted — to Tur's view to be lenient in roasted, bran through flour first, for there is no psik reisha as Taz sign 11 wrote; and matzah from above — likewise on top of on top permitted even for Rambam, for shatit is lighter than roasted and all the more what helps in thick shatit a little at a time. But the first view in seif 16 is for the one who rules like Rabbi that giving water is binding; and likewise he forbids in seif 14 flour roasted. And deals siman 324 sign 3; and one may give water to bran on Shabbat — this is the view of the one who rules like Rabbi who is liable when giving water even in flour first; since the latter says liable — examine Sefer HaTerumah 53 law 5: roasted at least rabbinically forbidden in thick a little — he gives water, this is its binding, there is no change; but soft through a change a little flour first permitted like shatit; and he challenged Mishna Berurah in seif 12: why did he not explain bran specifically, for thick on top of on top does not help — all the more flour first for the first tanna who is Rabbi; and in Magen Avraham sign 24 he corrected this — Mishna Berurah explained this like shatit, meaning soft through flour first:

(ג) Further he wrote: one may say even through a change bran does not help in garlic and leeks — since everywhere the view of the one who rules like Rabbi is that he did not mention bran permitted through a change; and the reason: roasted permitted bran because possible binding is lighter than garlic and leeks which are not subject to binding; or he holds like Rambam that shatit is grain that brought a third — behold leeks resemble roasted — bran does not help, flour first likewise leeks do not help, etc.:
