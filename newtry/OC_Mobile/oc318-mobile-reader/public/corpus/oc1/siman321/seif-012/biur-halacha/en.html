(א) One who cuts vegetables very fine — In the Gemara it states: R' Pappa said — one who spreads beets is liable due to grinding; and Rashi explained: spreads — cuts it very fine; and so Tosafos — it deals in small pieces like grinding; but in somewhat large pieces there is no manner of grinding; and so Rambam — very fine; but all this for liability; but for prohibition one must be very careful in this, for already Yere'im wrote to the Rambam that we do not know their measure of fineness; and so it appears in Beit Yosef, that for permission regarding ischelanda he required making pieces large in part and also immediately adjacent to the meal, see there:

(ב) Before elders — See in Magen Avraham, who wrote that it is implied permitted before one who can chew it thus; and in my humble opinion his words are not compelled, for he stated "before elders" due to etiquette, since they cannot eat unless he cuts them very fine; but in any case, in a thing that is produce of the ground, grinding applies in every manner; and so it appears from Biur HaGra in his explanation, who wrote the source for Rashba's words that immediately permitted from that which is stated: one may not crush neither chaff nor carobs before an animal due to trouble with food — it is implied that otherwise it would be permitted; and this is because it deals with eating immediately; therefore the prohibition of grinding does not apply, as they forbade regarding crushing carobs; and behold the Gemara there deals that the animal can eat thus — otherwise it would be making food permitted and permitted; and nevertheless grinding would apply if he prepared it for later; and therefore he holds as we wrote. And know that even according to Magen Avraham, who permits to cut before one who can chew it thus — this is specifically regarding dried figs and carobs that he has no need to cut; but what they cut vegetables very fine that they call mizrei'ah and the like — we require specifically for that meal, also according to him [Peri Megadim]:

(ג) To crumble bread — And even with a knife permitted if not in a vessel designated for crushing, such as to crush matzah in a mortar — forbidden, like cheese on a reib eizen, and as stated; and Sefer Tosefet Yerushalayim wrote that even to put water with the bread — also permitted, provided he does not knead them with the water, see there:

(ד) Comparable to borer — And at first glance, behold in borer they permitted only by hand and not by vessel — then it is considered borer in every manner; and if so, we should forbid in our matter when he cuts with a vessel, only when crumbling by hand; and see in Rivash siman 484, who raises this there as well; but from all the words of the poskim it is implied that in our matter, even when cutting with a knife — permitted; and Peri Megadim wrote the reason: as in borer, the manner of eating is when he selects by hand to eat immediately — so too in our matter, the manner of eating is even when he cuts with a knife to eat immediately, for the manner of eating is with a knife, unless he cuts with a vessel designated for grinding, like grating cheese on a reib eizen and the like [though they are uncertain about this because it is not produce of the ground] — then it is like a nafah and sieve, where borer applies even not immediately.
