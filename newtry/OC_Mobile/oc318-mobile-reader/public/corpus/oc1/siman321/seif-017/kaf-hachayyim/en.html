17) [Seif 3] One may not salt slices of radish, etc. Rambam, chapter 22, halachah 10, writes 'radish and the like, because it appears as though one is pickling.' This means that anything ordinarily pickled may not be salted on Shabbos because it appears that one is pickling. The rule is not limited to foods ordinarily pickled; it also includes anything upon which salt has an effect by removing its bitterness. Taz, paragraph 2, likewise writes that radish is not exclusive and that the same law applies to anything requiring salting, meaning that salting softens or hardens it or removes its bitterness, such as onions and garlic. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 5, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4, write the same. An item that is not ordinarily pickled and upon which salt has no effect is permitted, as stated below in paragraph 24. This applies specifically for that meal, as stated below in paragraph 25.
