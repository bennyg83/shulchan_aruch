(א) One may not knead, etc. — See what is written in Mishna Berurah that according to Beit HaTalmud he rules like Rabbi, that immediately when he put water in it he is liable; and this is the second view mentioned here in the gloss and in siman 324 seif 3. And know that it appears clear to me that even according to Beit HaTalmud, when he put water erev Shabbat — nevertheless if he kneaded afterward on Shabbat he is liable, and we do not say this is called kneading after kneading; and proof from what is stated in the Yerushalmi chapter Klal Gadol [brought in Rokeach and Or Zarua]: the arranger, the weaver, and the carder are liable due to kneading; and behold the melacha of arranging is after kneading — it is implied that even though the dough was already kneaded, nevertheless there is still liability of kneading through arranging, because through arranging the kneading is improved more; and all the more so here, where initially on a weekday there was only putting water, even though he holds this is its kneading — nevertheless if he kneads afterward he makes complete kneading on Shabbat and is liable; and one should not challenge this from what Rosh wrote in chapter 4 of Beitza siman 19 on what is stated there "and kitma is permitted" — it deals when he put water in it from erev Yom Tov, and his putting is its kneading, and it was already kneaded from erev Yom Tov; and he means that therefore permitted to knead afterward the kitma and smear on the oven — it is implied that according to Rabbi, from putting water he is no longer liable for kneading even if he kneads afterward; this is not so, for there it deals with ashes that are not subject to kneading in themselves, but we consider according to Rabbi putting water like kneading — therefore there is no room to obligate him further for kneading when he mixes the ashes with water afterward; also now there is no kneading at all, and the mixing he mixes now of ashes with water — behold according to Rabbi it is considered mixing from the time of beginning to put water; unlike in our matter, where he afterward makes complete kneading — certainly he is liable afterward for kneading even according to Rabbi; and one must distinguish thus so it not challenge Rosh from the Yerushalmi of arranger and carder mentioned:

(ב) Lest he come — Behold in the Gemara a Baraisa permits kneading through a change; therefore it is R' Yosei bar Yehuda, who does not obligate at putting water [for otherwise he would be liable from putting water, where change does not apply]. And Peri Megadim wrote: according to Tosafos Shabbat 18, who hold that in something not subject to kneeding all agree he is liable at putting water — therefore roasted flour is subject to kneading, and kneading was permitted through a change of little by little; and he wrote that therefore also in flour that is not roasted — permitted through a change of little by little for chickens, see there. And I saw in Sefer Nishmat Adam that he challenged him on this and wrote: according to what we rule, half a measure is forbidden by Torah law — certainly also little by little is forbidden by Torah law [one should not say because he has a large dough to knead and does it little by little it is considered a change, for we rule Shabbat 103b that even if he has a garment over the entire garment and weaves two threads he is liable, and it is not considered a change; and if so, the same applies to weaving one thread or kneading less than a dried fig — there is at least a Torah prohibition; and how can this prohibition of the first kneading be nullified from the later kneading — even if the chickens ate the first little he kneaded before he kneaded more little, for otherwise they combine and there is liability of a chatas; see Shabbat daf 80a]. And this was permitted only in roasted flour, which is not subject to kneading; and according to Tosafos and their supporters, who hold roasted flour is subject to kneeding — they explain what the Gemara said "little by little" — meaning through a change of k'lachar yad; and they were assisted in this from Rashi's words; but Heaven forbid to deviate in this from the words of the early authorities — namely Rif wrote explicitly that its explanation is little by little; and so in the explanation of R' Chananel; and so Aruch entry yad; and so it appears from Rambam; and so in the Gemara 19b and Koveitz: on yad yarbah — there it is proven its explanation is little by little; and so Rashi himself in Beitza 12 explains on yad al yad — little by little; and also here what he first wrote that this kneading is k'lachar yad — the intent is because it is through a change of little by little, as R' Chananel explains; therefore he called it k'lachar yad. But according to this his difficulty returns to its place, for we rule half a measure is forbidden by Torah law [and at first glance I wished to resolve that this statement in the Gemara of on yad al yad is in the name of R' Chananel, and we can say he follows his view on daf 74 that he permits to select less than the measure and eat; and as Tosafos wrote there; and the same in kneading roasted flour; but in truth this is not so, for according to what the Gemara concludes there to forbid — the Rif and Rambam should not have copied this matter for law]. And it appears to me that in truth the law is with Nishmat Adam, that flour in general has a Torah prohibition even in half a measure, and it was not permitted in roasted flour for this reason; and if so, R' Chananel should have said less than the measure, as he said above on daf 74 — why did he use the language on yad al yad? Only certainly that even if it were the size of a dried fig — also permitted in roasted flour, for a dried fig is also a little, and no posek did I see mention that specifically less than the measure; and so it appears in Sefer Netiv Chayyim that even the size of a dried fig — also permitted; and the reason for the permission appears to me according to Tosafos: because through what they roast the kernels in an oven and make them fit for eating — it is considered like something baked and cooked; and therefore even through putting water they put in afterward it becomes kneaded — this is not considered like the melacha of kneading but like repairing food in general, for the manner of eating is thus; and it is considered like gravy one puts in a dish, and this is not a Torah melacha; and therefore little by little was permitted, considered somewhat a change; or the intent of the Gemara little by little is also specifically at the time of eating, due to the manner of eating; and similar to what R' Chananel thought above on daf 74 in permitting less than the measure due to the manner of eating; and the same here; and even though the Gemara did not accept his conclusion there — his reasoning here in roasted flour is different, as we wrote, that it is like gravy in a dish [and what the Gemara said there "this is according to R' Yosei bar Yehuda" — because according to Rabbi there is at least prohibition due to putting water; and one must explain thus according to Rambam's explanation, who holds roasted flour is not subject to kneading]. But in plain flour there is no reason to permit kneading for chickens, even in less than the measure, because we rule half a measure is forbidden by Torah law in all melachot; and as we wrote in siman 340 in Biur Halacha, see there; and kneading for chickens was permitted only in the manner explained in Mor Uktziya in siman 324 seif 3, see there. And know that in the printed explanation of R' Chananel on Shabbat I saw a new matter, that he wrote that which they permit in a beverage and in roasted flour — specifically through stirring two and mixing, and not actual kneading; but from all the poskim it does not appear thus:

(ג) For behold it is like mortar — It is implied from this that on weekdays kneading does not apply; and Rambam follows his view, who wrote in chapter 8: there is no kneading in ashes nor in coarse flour, etc.; and Ra'avad disagrees and wrote: it is not so — they did not say ashes is not subject to kneading, but that it lacks kneading, and with putting water alone he is liable; and so is the view of the Acharonim [Magen Avraham in the name of Maggid Mishneh]:
