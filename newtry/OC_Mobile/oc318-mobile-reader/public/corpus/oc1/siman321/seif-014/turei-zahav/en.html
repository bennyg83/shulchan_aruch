Coarse grinding, for behold it is like mortar. This is Rambam's view; and Tur has another view according to Rashi's explanation, and the Rav HaMagid wrote that Ramban and Rashba agreed thus — that even initially, meaning roasted flour, there is a distinction between thick and thin, that in thick forbidden in quantity and in thin permitted in quantity through a change; and it appears Shulchan Aruch did not concern himself to bring this since it is not found among us; but it appears to me to learn one new law from Rambam's view — namely the flour they make from grinding baked matzah that they call matzah meal, and they are accustomed to mix it with wine or honey water that they call mead — that it is forbidden to make this on Shabbat even in a thin batter, for it appears the law of this flour is like the flour mentioned here — coarse grinding, etc.; and here, where thin batter is permitted, it is through a change that on weekdays the manner is to put vinegar first and on Shabbat he puts the flour first — the change is evident to all; but with matzah flour there is no known manner what one puts first on weekdays in order to know what the change is — therefore forbidden on Shabbat in every manner he wishes to mix in a knife or in the palm the matzah flour with the beverage, as appears correct to me:
