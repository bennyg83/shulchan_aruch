Flour that is not roasted. With this, even little by little is forbidden.
And it is permitted to knead, etc. For he maintains that one who puts water into flour is not liable until he kneads it. Therefore here, when he changes the manner of kneading, it is permitted, since it is not subject to ordinary kneading.
For it is like sand. The Rambam follows his own view, for he wrote at the end of chapter 8 that one who mixes ash is performing a toldah of kneading, but there is no kneading with earth, nor with coarse sand, nor with bran, nor similar materials. The Raavad disagrees and wrote that this is not so, for they did not say that ash is not subject to kneading, but rather that it does not lack kneading, and by merely adding water one is liable. This is also the view of the later authorities, as the Maggid Mishneh writes; see the end of siman 507 and siman 340, seif 12. It appears to me that it is forbidden to urinate on clay because of kneading; see siman 320, seif 14.
