(א) One may not knead — behold before we come to explain Shulchan Aruch's words we will explain somewhat the matter of kneading. Kneading is one of the 39 primary melachot and it is not specifically through water; the same applies in honey, goose fat, and every kind of liquid that is kneaded and sticks through them. Behold regarding kneading it is taught in a baraita in the Gemara that if one puts flour and one puts water, the last one is liable — the view of Rabbi; and R' Yossi son of R' Yehuda says he is not liable until he kneads; and most poskim ruled like R' Yossi son of R' Yehuda; but the view of Ba'al HaTerumot and his supporters is to rule like Rabbi that through putting water it is called kneading; and all this is in flour or dust, mud, and fine sand that is fit for kneading and everything similar; but in something that is not fit for kneading, such as ashes and coarse sand — Rambam's view and his supporters is that even if he kneaded he is exempt from chatas and there is only rabbinic prohibition; and the view of many Rishonim is the opposite, that in something not fit for kneading, even for R' Yossi son of R' Yehuda kneading is not required, and from when he puts water in it he is liable, for this is its kneading. And now we come to explain Shulchan Aruch's words:

(ב) Putting water — this expression is not precise, for the Mechaber deals here with complete kneading where one puts water and kneads:

(ג) Lest he come — all this seif is the language of Rambam, and he holds roasted flour is not fit for kneading; and something not fit for kneading — he is not liable even when he kneads, and it is forbidden only d'rabbanan lest he come to knead flour that is not roasted; therefore they permitted through a change, little by little; and even for other poskim who hold roasted flour is fit for kneading and he is liable from the Torah when he kneads — nevertheless they also agree that through a change, little by little, it is permitted in roasted flour. And know that the second view brought below in Taz — also in this matter forbidden to knead even little by little unless he put water on the flour from erev Shabbat:

(ד) Flour that is not roasted — and in this even little by little forbidden [Magen Avraham]:

(ה) Little by little — and not much, whether he makes a thin batter or a thick one; and the measure of little by little the poskim did not explain, and it appears even if there is the measure of an egg-sized dried fig — and see in Be'ur Halacha:

(ו) Coarse grinding — meaning it is not relevant to decree lest it be confused with kneading flour that is not roasted, for it is not in the category of flour; therefore they permitted in this blessing even much:

(ז) Thick forbidden — meaning much; but little by little permitted, like above in roasted flour:

(ח) And even blessing, etc. — see in Taz who wrote it is forbidden on Shabbat of Pesach to mix flour they make from grinding baked matzah to put them in wine or honey water; even though it is similar to coarse grinding where blessing is permitted there through a change — here in this flour it is not known what he puts first on a weekday in order to know what the change is; therefore forbidden if he does much. And R' Avraham wrote that if it is not known he should make the change explained in the Gemara that he puts the food first and afterward the liquid; and so Chok Yaakov in siman 473 sides to be lenient in this; and good to be stringent not to mix in the palm but shake the vessel until it mixes; and all this if he does much; but if he does little by little — permitted even thick and without change according to this view that the second view below in Taz forbids thick in every manner. Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me forbidden to urinate on mud on account of kneading; and his intent is even for most poskim who hold that in something fit for kneading he is not liable until he kneads — nevertheless there is rabbinic prohibition; and the same applies in fine dust and sand; and even though he does not intend kneading, nevertheless it is psik reisha; and a vessel in which one spits or rinses his mouth on Shabbat and beneath it is fine or coarse sand — one should examine if it is forbidden or permitted, for perhaps it is psik reisha de'lo nicha lei; and I found in Sefer Beit Me'ir who permits for this reason in a place of need even to urinate on mud, and it appears one may rely on this where the mud is not his, for then certainly he is not pleased with its kneading. Chayei Adam wrote: when one covers urine on a weekday with ashes, and when he puts a little it will mix in the urine and it is like kneading, and from putting water one is liable for many poskim; therefore he should put much ashes and sand and then the kneading is not recognizable at all:
