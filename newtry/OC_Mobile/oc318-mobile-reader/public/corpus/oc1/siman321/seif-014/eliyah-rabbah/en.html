[19] But thick, etc. Shiltei Gibborim asks why a thick mixture should always be prohibited, since shetit is less processed than kali and yet a thick shetit is permitted. Malbushei Yom Tov answers that kali too is prohibited when thick and the text relies on the previous rule. This is strained when Rambam is examined, and Bach straightforwardly explains that Rambam permits a small amount of thick kali. The Gemara distinguishes thick and thin specifically for shetit, not kali. According to the other authorities kali is plainly permitted. Since we always minimize disputes where possible, it appears better that Rambam permits a small amount of kali because he also permits a small amount of thick shetit, while the prohibition of thick mixture concerns a large amount. This answers Lechem Mishneh's question how, according to Rambam who treats shetit and kali as two different things, the Gemara could say that Levi's record allowed thick shetit because Rabbi Yose bar Yehudah permitted kali. According to my explanation the case is a small amount of thick shetit, which is no worse than kali.
