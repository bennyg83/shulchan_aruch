To set it aside. See Baer Heitev; and what Taz wrote on this — see in responsa of Peri Megadim that on the contrary, where it is better for him to salt now and it improves thereby more — there is reason to forbid on erev Shabbos; and see in Beit Yosef that even though he inclined to permit, he wrote to leave them for a short time — forbidden to salt; and so in salatin; and so if they salt in their shells the master [of the house?]; but if he puts a little oil first there is reason to permit. And what Baer Heitev wrote in the name of Magen Avraham that forbidden to rinse meat that became salted on the third day — see above siman 308; Eliyah Zuta wrote in the name of his ancestor to permit; and see in Ginas Veradim Ma'areches Shabbos siman 27: stuffed geese whose main purpose is for the fat, and in roasting the fat is entirely lost and there is loss — permitted to put water on the meat via a non-Jew; and needless to say ducks and geese fit for omtza; but even animal meat — and if impossible via a non-Jew, permitted to do via Israel; and especially if in any case he needs to wash hands on erev Shabbos; and so too in Dagul MeRevavah, and he concluded there "again I found in Eliyah Rabbah siman 308 who disputes Magen Avraham" on erev Shabbos; and in Shevut Yaakov part 3 siman 22 he wrote that Magen Avraham who is stringent applies specifically to an individual in his home, but a butcher who sells to others concedes that he can do via a non-Jew (and accordingly also in stuffed geese where there is loss of fat); and one who is stringent can tell a non-Jew to command a non-Jew, which is shvut d'shvut on erev Shabbos (and see Pri Megadim end of siman 508 regarding Yom Tov; and in Nedar Bakar siman 32 he wrote to permit on Yom Tov, and see there explicitly the words of Magen Avraham that he wrote on account of a remedy — meaning that it softens); and see in Chokhmas Adam siman 44 brought above siman 307, and in Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 44:
