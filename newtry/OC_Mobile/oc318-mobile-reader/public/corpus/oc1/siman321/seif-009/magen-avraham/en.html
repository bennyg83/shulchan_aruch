It is permitted to cut. For Tosafot and the Rosh wrote that grinding does not apply to foods. Even though some hold that grinding does apply, as written in seif 12, that is specifically with something that grows from the ground. Meat, which does not grow from the ground, is not subject to grinding. This applies specifically to roasted meat, since if one wanted, he could eat it whole and chew it with his teeth; therefore even one who cannot chew may cut it, for we follow the majority of people. But raw meat, since it is fit only to be swallowed in an unusual manner, may not be cut very finely before birds. Even though it is fit for dogs and wild animals, it is not designated for them, but for birds, because of its importance; therefore one should not permit it. Furthermore, there is room to touch upon the prohibition of making food fit for an animal, and also excessive exertion with food, as the Terumas HaDeshen writes. In Shiltei Gibborim he wrote that this is not compelling, for meat is considered something that grows from the ground, as Tosafot and the Ran wrote regarding extracting, which is a toldah of threshing.
And see siman 324, seif 7. What is written there proves that anything exceedingly hard may be cut, and so it appears from the Gemara, unlike the Olas Shabbos.
