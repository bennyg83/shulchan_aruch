(9) To leave it, etc. It implies even from meal to meal forbidden; but radish even for the need of that meal forbidden (Magen Avraham) — not necessarily compelled; and see s.k. 3; and further behold Rabbeinu Yeruham daf 87 — his language: permitted to salt meat that is fit to eat that day, such as cooked or raw if he wants to eat it thus — until here; behold that if only fit that day — permitted even if not for that meal; and it appears from Shibbolei HaLeket that Beit Yosef brought: whatever there is loss to the meat if he does not salt it — permitted to salt and to devise that he eat it and returns and regrets and does not eat on Shabbat; and Taz understood specifically when he eats on Shabbat; and what appears to me I wrote; however for a thing not fit to eat on Shabbat the devise does not apply and forbidden to salt even if it will spoil and be lost; and behold Magen Avraham brings in the name of Tosafos and Rosh and Rashba and Ran that forbidden to salt meat even if he wants to eat on Shabbat because it is me'abed; and from Rabbeinu Yeruham and Shibbolei HaLeket that I brought it is explained permitted; and it appears to me regarding cooked — possible that for everyone permitted. Magen Avraham wrote: Rashba wrote in Beit 3 portal 3 — one who salts meat to store for a journey is liable for chatas — requires study, for in the Gemara it appears the halacha is like Rava who is exempt because he is the latter authority; and so Rambam ruled chapter 11 — until here. And it appears to me it is possible: since Rav Ashi on daf 75 explains according to Rabbah the halacha is like him — requires study; and I found in Biurei Rashal on Semag: there is no me'abed in foods — he wrote later on daf 46: until one who salts meat on Shabbat to take on a distant journey is liable because of me'abed — until here; and this is astounding, for Rava on daf 75 says there is no me'abed in foods even for a distant journey he is exempt; and it appears to me on daf 46 they did not write except regarding needing much salt; but regarding liability on Shabbat he relied on what he wrote in Hilchot Shabbat that there is no me'abed in foods; and with this one may resolve Rashba's view above — requires study:
