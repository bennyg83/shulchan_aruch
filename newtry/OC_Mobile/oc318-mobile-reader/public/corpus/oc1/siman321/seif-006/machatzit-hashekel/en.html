(א) (s.k. 6) There is one who wrote, etc.; and one of the great ones ruled, etc. — meaning a greater leniency than what is written here in Shulchan Aruch; but one-by-one — permitted; and Terumat HaDeshen concluded: according to his view, in the manner of dipping in every case — permitted; meaning Terumat HaDeshen did not permit except as Shulchan Aruch wrote, to dip one-by-one; but that great one permitted even to dip many pieces at once; and forbidden only to scatter salt upon them, for one may not be lenient except to dip — meaning one-by-one; and also according to Terumat HaDeshen, even if one dips one-by-one and places them before him — each must not touch its fellow but must be separate from one another; and so Bach for Terumat HaDeshen's view; but according to that great one — even if they touch one another — permitted:

(ב) Like pickling kavush; and this reason Rambam gave; and even with two one should be stringent, for Bach challenged Rashi's words against themselves: he explained on that which is taught R' Yehuda ben Chachinai: one does not salt radish on Shabbat — one does not salt three or four pieces together, etc. — it implies but two are permitted; and in R' Nachman's words he explained: I do not salt — two together; I dip at the time of eating, I dip and I eat; behold he explained that for R' Nachman two together are forbidden; therefore he wrote that according to Rashi it follows R' Nachman's language who said "I dip," namely one-by-one, and eat immediately; and it is impossible that this is the law, for two are forbidden; perforce he explains the baraita of R' Yehuda ben Chachinai thus; and perforce R' Nachman's leniency comes to inform us that one alone is permitted and to eat immediately; and if so it is puzzling, for plainly one alone is permitted, for it is impossible to eat radish without salt; perforce two are permitted by law; and R' Nachman comes to inform a stringency that he was stringent upon himself and did not salt even two together, only one alone:

(ג) See in Shibolei HaLeket, etc.; Taz in s.k. 1 wrote that if he gives oil immediately after salting the lettuce — permitted, since he does not leave it to sweat — it does not resemble tanning; but when they leave the lettuce and likewise the cucumbers in salt a while until they sweat, and afterward pour off the sweat-water and afterward put oil and vinegar upon it — this is certainly forbidden; and see what is written in the law of cucumbers in the small seif after this:
