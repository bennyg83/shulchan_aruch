[16] They were detached before Shabbos or crushed, etc. This implies that detached honeycombs do not require prior crushing, whereas the Gemara at the beginning of chapter 22 requires crushing, as stated in siman 320, seif 2, regarding olives and grapes. Magen Avraham therefore explains that if they were detached before Shabbos one may remove the combs from the hive in order to eat the honey adhering around them or squeeze the honey into food; alternatively, if they were crushed, etc. This is strained. In my humble opinion his own later question provides the answer. Since the Gemara says honey is food from beginning to end, why should it be worse than fruits intended for eating, which may be squeezed because the expressed liquid has no separate beverage status? The answer is that the first question resolves the second: if the honeycombs were detached before Shabbos, crushing is not required and they may be squeezed in any manner because the honey remains food. Crushing is required only when the combs were not detached, because removal then resembles detaching. Olives and grapes differ because they begin as food and the expressed liquid becomes beverage, so even detached fruit may not be squeezed. This appears clear.
[17] It is permitted to remove it. Magen Avraham explains this as taking only the floating honey, while the combs themselves may not be removed, citing Tosafot 43b. This is surprising, because Rama and Levush imply that the combs too may be removed, as the preceding discussion also indicates. Tosafot there is no proof because it concerns uncrushed combs. Once crushed, removal is permitted. Beit Yosef likewise implies Tosafot discusses uncrushed combs, for he says that the permitted floating honey means honey that flowed before Shabbos, while honey flowing on Shabbos is prohibited unless the combs were crushed before Shabbos.
[18] [Levush] And grain, etc. Rambam writes so. The other authorities follow a different approach, as Beit Yosef explains (Malbushei Yom Tov). Taz likewise implies this. I add that Beit Yosef concludes Rabbenu Yerucham, part 9, explains like Rambam, but appears to understand his words as saying Egyptian zythum is the same as shetit, contrary to the Gemara. I am surprised because Beit Yosef there explicitly shows that Rabbenu Yerucham explains shetit as parched grain like Rashi and the other authorities, merely inserting the law of Egyptian zythum because it appears nearby in the Gemara and Rosh. He never intended to identify the two. Malbushei Yom Tov also overlooked that Maggid Mishneh, chapter 21, agrees with Rambam. Knesset HaGedolah rules like Rif and Rosh.
