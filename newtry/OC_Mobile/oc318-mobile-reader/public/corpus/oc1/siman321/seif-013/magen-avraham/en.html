They were detached from the previous day, or they were crushed, etc. This implies that if they are detached, crushing is not required. But in the Gemara at the beginning of chapter 22 it states that crushing is required, as written in siman 320, seif 2, regarding olives and grapes. The Rambam also wrote this in chapter 21, law 16; see there. Therefore it appears to me that this is the explanation: when the honeycombs were detached from the hive from the previous day, one may remove the honeycombs from the hive in order to eat the honey stuck around them, or to squeeze it into food. Or, if they were crushed, etc., it is permitted to remove it, meaning that it is permitted to take the floating honey, but it is forbidden to take the honeycombs. See Tosafot, Shabbos 43a. The Gemara says that honey is food at the outset and food at the end. This requires study, for if so, why should it be worse than fruits designated for eating, which may be squeezed since the name of liquid is not upon them? One must say that since the wax is not fit to eat, it is like selecting food from refuse, as written in siman 320, seif 5. But according to the one who permits there, this requires study to resolve.
