19) There: Four or five together, etc. Our custom requires examination. On Shabbos, people finely slice radish, place it in a bowl, salt it, pour vinegar upon it, and eat it. This resembles salting many pieces, which is forbidden even if eaten immediately, whereas the permission applies only to dipping each piece separately. It appears that since they do not leave it at all to release moisture but immediately pour vinegar and other ingredients upon it, it does not resemble processing. I have nevertheless seen some people prepare a vegetable called salad by first salting the vegetable alone, leaving it that way, straining off the water that emerges, and only afterward mixing it with olive oil and vinegar. This is absolutely forbidden and resembles processing more closely, since they wait until it absorbs the salt well. Taz at the end of paragraph 1. Shiltei Gibborim, chapter 8 of Sheratzim, writes that one may prepare salted vegetables on Shabbos by placing salt upon the vegetables, then oil, and then vinegar, and the same applies to similar cases. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, and Magen Avraham, paragraph 6, cite him. This must refer to adding oil and vinegar immediately after the salt. If one leaves the vegetables in salt until they release moisture and only then adds oil and vinegar, it is forbidden, as stated above in the name of Taz. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 4; Chayei Adam, klal 32, paragraph 11; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Bo, paragraph 17. See below, paragraph 67.
19) Therefore, when preparing salad on Shabbos from cucumbers, vegetables, or similar produce by cutting them into small pieces and salting them, one must add the vinegar immediately after adding the salt. One may also not squeeze them together with salt to soften them. Although siman 320, seif 7, permits squeezing pickled foods to soften them, that case differs because they were salted before Shabbos and squeezing has no further effect upon them. Here, since one is now salting them, squeezing them with the salt causes the salt to act upon them on Shabbos and resembles pickling. If one wishes to squeeze them to soften them, he should squeeze them without salt until they soften, then add salt and immediately add vinegar. It is preferable to prepare the salad before Shabbos whenever possible.
