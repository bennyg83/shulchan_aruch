(א) And if there is not, etc. — permitted to finish — Meaning even with hands; and as is proven from what he concluded "therefore," etc.; and see above in siman 252 in Taz s.k. 6, who wished to strain himself in explaining Tur's words that the intent is not to finish with hands; and in truth this is not so, as we wrote, for Tur also copied Rambam's language that permitted to finish grinding remedies afterward; I found that Eliyah Rabbah already challenged and rejected his words:

(ב) In food, etc. — I do not know what this teaches us, for initially it also deals with food of apple, as is stated in Mordechai; and it is possible Rama came to teach that one who is stringent should not be stringent except regarding food of apple [and the same regarding potatoes] and the like, whose manner is to sweeten and smooth food through spreading and it appears like smearing; but in other food one should not be stringent at all; and it is like what Magen Avraham was lenient regarding spreading on bread — though Magen Avraham was lenient for another reason, as will be written below:

(ג) A blessing will come upon him — See in Mishna Berurah; and see in Magen Avraham, who wrote in the name of Hagahat Semak that permitted to spread apples also onto the empty place; and Magen Avraham concluded the same applies on bread; and in order to explain Magen Avraham's words I will copy Hagahat Semak's language itself, which states: after he brought at first that there is erasing in foods d'rabbanan, he wrote there: permitted to spread cooked apples that are in the pastida, since he does only in order to place them in a place where there is none, end of his words; and he means that his intent in the spreading is not for the spreading itself, only to fill the empty place; and even though it is psik reisha — Magen Avraham agreed above in siman 314 that even in a d'rabbanan matter it is forbidden; here, since otherwise there is room to be lenient because it is possible to eat without this — he should not be stringent at all. And know that even though it appears to all that d'oraisa there is no erasing in foods — this is if he spreads the food itself; but if he seals the mouth of a barrel hole with it and spreads from above — erasing applies there, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote in siman 314 in the name of Ra'avan, that milk and fat are like wax:

(ד) To peel, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah; and it is from Beit Yosef in the name of the Yerushalmi that there is in this the melacha of borer; and that which Rama wrote forbidden to peel — not specifically; or he wished to inform us that immediately, even l'chatchila — permitted. And at first glance one should be precise: since peeling is included in borer, it should be forbidden even to eat immediately, for one who peels apples, garlic, and onions takes the refuse and leaves the food; and it was already explained above in siman 319 that refuse from food — even immediately — liable; and it appears that since it is impossible otherwise and the manner of eating is thus — it is not called refuse from food, for he only eats the inside; and all that is immediately — permitted; but to leave — forbidden, for it is not better than food from refuse [Mordechai]; and one should not challenge the words of the early authorities [namely HaTerumah, Semag, and Mordechai], who copied the words of this Yerushalmi that one who peels is liable due to borer for law, from that which is stated in Beitza 13b: Rav would peel for his wife kesi kesi — and this is not reasonable to distinguish between barley peel and garlic and onion peel; one can say there it deals with intent to eat immediately; but astonishing on Rambam, who omitted this law of the Yerushalmi entirely and did not mention it at all; and were it not for the holy words of the early authorities mentioned, I would have been inclined to reconcile the words of this Yerushalmi in a proper manner, and automatically Rambam's view would be resolved, who omitted it — and it is in advance of what is stated in the Gemara mentioned: one who peels barley peels one by one and eats [for it is casual eating and exempt from tithes]; and if he peeled and put into his hand — liable — R' Elazar; and so when stated, the first part was stated: one who winnows wheat winnowings winnows on yad al yad and eats, etc.; R' Abba bar Memel challenged and the first part for tithes yes, for Shabbat no; and is there such a thing, etc.; and it resolves there, in the conclusion its difficulty: as you resolve your difficulty — just as when one sets up a pile in his house he is exempt because melakha requires thought the Torah forbade it [meaning craft melacha] — so too regarding one who peels; and certainly he holds peeling is in the category of melacha like for tithes; and certainly one should not distinguish for this reason between immediately and later; and we can say this was the Yerushalmi's view, and the reason is due to borer and not like Rashi, who is due to grinding; but per the Bavli's conclusion that one who peels is exempt because melakha requires thought the Torah forbade it — there is no melacha at all, even with intent to eat later, similar to setting up a pile in the house that is exempt in every manner for this reason; and the same in every place in Shas that exempt due to melakha requires thought — certainly exempt in every manner, for it is not melacha at all; and if so, the same in our matter regarding peeling and borer in general — refuse from food is different, for it is called melakha requires thought, and what is permitted there immediately is because the manner of eating is thus, as appears correct to me, were it not for the words of the early authorities mentioned. And know that the words of Rabbeinu Peretz brought in Beit Yosef, who wrote that specifically little by little but a large gathering appears like borer — is very difficult from that sugya, where Rav peeled for his wife kesi kesi; and strained to say that kesi kesi is also included in little by little; and it is possible for this reason Rama omitted and did not copy for law:

(ה) Immediately — permitted. And so Magen Avraham above in s.k. 8, see there; and see in Chiddushei Ra'ah, who challenged accordingly on what Rashi explained on daf 74 in the passage "and let it also be counted as grinding" — and the difficulty remains; and in my humble opinion one can say plainly: the Gemara deals when he peels by hand, and there when he grinds in a mortar — which is with a vessel, it is considered like grinding; for until here we did not exempt in peeling except due to melakha requires thought for Shabbat as for the Mishkan, as the Gemara concludes there; and like what Rashi explained.
