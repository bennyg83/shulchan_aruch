(1) [Seif 1] These are the blessings in which we bow: in Avot at the beginning and end — and it opens and closes with Barukh because it is the first blessing, and all the others are a blessing adjacent to its fellow; therefore they do not open with Barukh. And even though there is no malchut in the opening "Elokei Avraham," it is like malchut — for Avraham enthroned Him over the world, that He made known His kingship and His divinity to all who come into the world. And some say HaEl HaGadol, HaGibbor, and HaNora are like malchut — for we do not specifically require the language of kingship, as we say in the second chapter of Rosh Hashana page 32b: Shema Yisrael is malchut — Tur and Levush; see Beit Yosef siman 214 and in the later authorities further reasons. And I, in my humility, do not wish to elaborate on these matters except what falls out for the matter of law. And Rambam wrote in his Iggerot siman 9 that even a convert says this text of the blessing of Avot and does not change whether he prays with the congregation or prays alone — Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Tashbetz, Shulchan Arukh siman 114.
