And if he comes to bow, etc. — Behold from the words of Eliyah Rabbah it is proven that the prohibition is specifically at "Baruch ata," but at the true end of a blessing one may bow; and the Peri Megadim raises doubts about this. See Rashi, Berachos 34a s.v. techilah va-sof, who explains Rashi in Modim "and to You it is fitting to give thanks"; and on this the braita concludes that at the end of all other blessings we teach him not to bow — this implies like the Peri Megadim. And perhaps Rashi did not mean it precisely; and also he holds like the Rambam that end of blessing means when one says "Baruch Ata... ha-Tov," etc.

At the end of every blessing, etc. — The Levush wrote in the chapter Ein Omdin that one should not bow in any blessing of the other blessings at beginning or end, even if it is not the [Amidah of] eighteen; and therefore I say that all those who bow in the blessing of the Torah at beginning and end err — see there. And see what I wrote in Mishna Berurah sec. 6 in the name of the Taz that it seemingly contradicts this; and perhaps also for the Taz it is forbidden in a blessing, for it appears as adding to the obligation of bowing that they enacted only in the blessing of Avot and Modim.
