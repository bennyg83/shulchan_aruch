We instruct him. See Taz and Tosafos, Berachos 34a. Beit Yosef explains that Tosafos offers two answers, and so does the Taz; see what I wrote in letter 2. Nechalas Tzvi, however, explains that it is one answer: the Sages instituted that an ordinary person should not bow like a Kohen Gadol at the end of every blessing, and doing so is arrogance. The whole concern is that he resembles a Kohen Gadol. The fact that one may bow in the middle without arrogance, despite the king's practice, can be explained as follows: once a king bows at the beginning, he does not straighten until the end, whereas an ordinary person who bows only in the middle does not resemble a king. See Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah, halachah 10, and Kesef Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh concerning the source that a Kohen Gadol bows at the beginning and end of every blessing. It must be so, for otherwise, if only at the beginning or only at the end, why would we instruct him to stop? At the end alone he would not resemble a Kohen Gadol. This proves the point, in accordance with Nechalas Tzvi.
