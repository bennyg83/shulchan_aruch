We instruct him, etc. Tosafos write: One might ask, let him bow, what harm is there? They answer that he should not uproot the enactment of the Sages, lest people say that each person may be stringent as he wishes. They further answer that we are concerned for arrogance, meaning that he presents himself as more righteous than the rest of the congregation. It appears to me that the first answer means as follows. Whatever a person adopts on his own as a personal stringency is not regarded as so weighty, for at times he may be lenient about it. This differs from an obligation imposed upon him by decree of the Sages. If he were to add bows at the beginning or end of other blessings, people would not know that the bows in Avos and thanksgiving are mandated by the Sages. They would think all of them are merely personal stringencies, and this could lead to leniency regarding the required bows. This appears correct.
However, in their middle, etc. According to the first reason of Tosafos cited above, this is readily understood, for there is no improper addition here, since the Sages never instituted bowing in the middle of any blessing. According to the second reason, however, that it appears arrogant, one may ask why bowing in the middle is different. It appears to me that we are concerned for arrogance specifically at the beginning and end, because a Kohen Gadol and a king bow at the beginning and end of every blessing, as stated at the end of the chapter Ein Omdin. One who does so therefore compares himself to a Kohen Gadol. This does not apply in the middle, where a Kohen Gadol does not bow. See siman 109, where I resolved what was challenged from here against the ruling there.
