They teach him, etc. Tosafos wrote, and a question: "and he would bow" — what of it? And one may say: so that there should not be uprooting of the Sages' enactment, lest they say everyone is strict as he wishes; and we are concerned for yohara, meaning he holds himself more proper than the rest of the congregation. It seems to me an explanation of the first answer: whatever a person does voluntarily as stringency from himself is not so weighty a matter — sometimes he is lenient with it; unlike an obligation from what the Sages decreed upon him. If he comes to add and bow in other blessings at beginning or end, they will not know the bowing is from the Sages' enactment in Avot and Modim and will think it is all his own stringency, and by this he will come to leniency, as explained.

But in their middles, etc. Per the first reason of Tosafos that we mentioned — one need not say here there is applicable addition, for the Sages did not enact anywhere in the middle; only per the second reason — it appears as yohara. I asked about the middle; it seems to me specifically at beginning and end we are concerned for yohara, since we find with the High Priest and king that they bow beginning and end in every blessing, as in the chapter "Alei He" — one finds this person likens himself to the High Priest; not so in the middle, where there is no bowing for the High Priest. See siman 109 where I wrote to resolve what they challenged from here on Tosafot.
