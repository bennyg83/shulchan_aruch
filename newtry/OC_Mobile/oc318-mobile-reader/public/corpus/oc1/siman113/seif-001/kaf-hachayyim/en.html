1) [Seif 1] These are the berachos in which one bows: in Avot at the beginning and at the end. It opens and closes with Baruch because it is the first beracha, and all the others are a beracha adjacent to its fellow; therefore they do not open with Baruch. And although there is no malchus in the opening, some say Elokei Avraham is like malchus, for Avraham caused Him to reign over the entire world, making known His kingship and His Divinity to all who enter the world. And some say HaEl HaGadol HaGibor veHaNora is like malchus, for we do not specifically require the language of malchus, as we say in the second chapter of Rosh Hashanah, daf 32b, that Shema Yisrael is malchus. Tur and Levush. See Beit Yosef, siman 214, and in the Acharonim further reasons; and I, in my poverty, do not wish to elaborate on these matters except what is relevant for practical law. And Rambam, of blessed memory, wrote in his letters, siman 9, that a convert too should say this text of Birkat Avot and should not change it, whether he prays with the congregation or prays alone. Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahot HaTur; Shalmei Tzibbur, page 114b.
2) There: in Avot at the beginning and the end, etc. You must know to intend the Name Havayah that raises to the Name Adnut in this manner: at the first bowing, intend that the yud of Havayah raises the alef of Adnut; at the second Baruch, intend that the first hei of Havayah raises the dalet of Adnut; at Modim, intend that the vav of Havayah raises the nun of Adnut; and at the end, intend that the final hei of Havayah raises the yud of Adnut. Of one who does not so intend, they said in the Tikkunim, Tikkun 18, that concerning him it is said, Hashem has delivered me into hands from which I cannot rise. Pri Etz Chayyim, Shaar HaAmidah, chapter 6; Ateret Zekeinim, ot 1; Kitzur Shlah in the laws of tefillah; Chesed LeAlafim, ot 1. If he did so intend, of him it is said, Then you shall call and Hashem will answer. The letters dalet-alef-zayin gimel-yud chet equal the numerical value of those two Names. Kaf HaChayyim, siman 15, ot 12. The meaning is: at the first bowing, intend that the yud of Havayah illuminates the alef of Adnut in order to raise it, and in the straightening, intend that the alef of Adnut ascends toward the yud of Havayah; and so he should intend for the rest.
