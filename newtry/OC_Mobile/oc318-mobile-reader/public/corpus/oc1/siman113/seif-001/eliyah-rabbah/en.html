[1] In Avot, etc. The Tur cites there "and I will make you a great nation" — this is what we say, "Elokei Avraham." Per the RaM's explanation, because the Holy One, blessed be He, does not associate His name with an individual except with the nation: God of Israel, God of the Hebrews, God of the nations. The Targum explained "I will make you" for Abraham himself without his seed into a great nation — and this is impossible except that He associated His name with him. Further in the Tur: "and I will bless you" — this is what we say, "Elokei Yitzchak." It seems to me in advance from the Talmud, Taanit, the chapter of the tree: what is "and I will bless you," etc.? If with sons — behold he had sons; if with wealth — behold he had wealth; if with Torah — behold he had Torah; rather, may it be His will that your descendants should be like you — to this point. And this is also difficult with Abraham, for we find he had all the blessings: wealth as Rashi explained the verse "and the people," etc.; Torah as stated in the midrash; sons — the simple meaning of the verse "and I will make you a great nation"; if so, in what "and I will bless you"? Therefore it says we say "Elokei Yitzchak" like Abraham, and that his descendants from his innards should be like him. Further in the Tur: even though there is no malchut in the opening "Elokei Avraham," it is like malchut — see there. One should not ask why he did not raise difficulty on the rest of the eighteen blessings — for one can say that a blessing adjacent to its fellow does not need malchut, as below siman 118; and below siman 214 the Levush elaborated on this, and the Kol Bo wrote in siman 11 that it has no malchut because when Abraham came his kingship was not yet known in the world. R. David Abudraham wrote: that we do not bless "who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning the mitzvah of prayer" — because prayer is a commandment that has no fixed measure from the Torah, but is in a person's hand to lessen or increase. R. David Abudraham wrote: these bowings are so that he remember before Whom he stands and that his end is to be dust; another matter — as a hint to draw blessing from above to below — to here. He wrote in Seder HaYom and other books: in the four bowings one should intend the four letters of Havayah, and in straightening up one should intend the four letters of Adonai; namely, in the opening of Avot one should intend to draw the yud to bring down influence to yesod "life of the worlds" where the connection is; and when he straightens up he should intend to raise the alef of Adonai to join with the yud — and so for all of them.
