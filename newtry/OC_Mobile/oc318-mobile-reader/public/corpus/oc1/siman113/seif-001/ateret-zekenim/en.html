These are the blessings in which one bows. One must intend when bowing in Avot at the beginning for the first letter of the name Havayah and the first letter of the name Adonai, namely yud-alef; and at the end one should intend heh-dalet. In Modim one should intend vav-nun, and at the end intend heh-yud. One who does not intend thus — about him it is said, "The the Master has given me into their hands; I cannot rise" (as Galanti wrote). And aside from these bowings it is forbidden for a person to sway his body at the time he prays (Rokeach; Ram"a in responsa siman 113). See what the Tur wrote, and Pardes, Shaar Erchei HaKinuyim, entry 14; and see in Zohar, parashat Terumah, and the Pardes brought it in Shaar HaTziruf ch. 7.

But in their middle one may bow. And one should not raise difficulty from what was written above in siman 109, that one should not begin to pray unless he arrives at the place where they bow — for when the shaliach tzibur reaches Modim it implies that in the middle one does not [bow]. And some say that although it is permitted to bow in the middle of a blessing, nevertheless since one is not obligated there to bow, he will forget in his prayer and not bow with them; but when he arrives at the place where he is obligated to bow, he bows with them by virtue of his prayer obligation (Yeshuot Malko). And there are those who write that when they mention Jerusalem one should prostrate toward the east (Maharil). Nevertheless it is not correct to bow when saying Boneh Yerushalayim, for it is forbidden to bow at the end of a blessing except in Avot and Modim.
