These are the blessings in which one bows. When bowing at the beginning of Avos, one must have in mind the first letter of the Name Havayah and the first letter of the Name Adnus, namely yud and alef; at the end, he should have in mind hei and dalet. At Modim he should have in mind vav and nun, and at its end hei and yud. Concerning one who does not have these intentions, the verse states, ‘Hashem has delivered me into their hands; I am unable to rise’ (as stated by Galanti). Apart from these bows, a person may not sway his body while praying (Rabbeinu Chananel and Rema MiPano, cited in Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 113). See the Tur and Pardes, Shaar Erchei HaKinuyim, entry 14; see also the Zohar, Parashas Terumah, cited by Pardes in Shaar HaTziruf, chapter 7.
However, one may bow in the middle of those blessings. One should not challenge this from what was written above in siman 109, that a person should not begin praying unless he will reach a place where one bows when the chazzan reaches Modim, which implies that bowing in the middle would not suffice. Some explain that although one may bow in the middle of a blessing, since he is not obligated to bow there, he may forget during his prayer and fail to bow together with them. But when he reaches a place where he is obligated to bow, he will bow together with them by virtue of his obligation in prayer. Some write that when Jerusalem is mentioned one should bow toward the east (Maharil). Nevertheless, it is improper to bow when saying Boneh Yerushalayim, since one may bow at the end of a blessing only in Avos and the blessings of thanksgiving.
