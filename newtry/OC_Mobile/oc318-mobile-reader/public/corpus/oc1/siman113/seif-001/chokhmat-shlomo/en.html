Se'if 1: These blessings, etc., at the beginning and end. Regarding the proper intent, I saw a precious teaching in Tikkunei Zohar, tikkun 18. It states: 'Whoever bows, bows at Baruch; whoever straightens, straightens at the Name. He bows toward Adonai through the tzaddik, of whom it is stated, And King Shlomo was blessed; and straightens toward the Name Havayah, to join the two together in the combination YaHDVNHY.' These words appear internally contradictory, because later in the same tikkun it says: 'They straighten with four straightenings corresponding to My four letters, which are Adonai, through the four that are Havayah.' This implies the reverse: The bowings correspond to the Name Havayah and the straightenings to Adonai. The second formulation is primary. Both passages imply that the intent is to combine the two Names in YaHDVNHY. The letters of Havayah must therefore come first, with Adonai incorporated within them, so judgment is included within mercy, not mercy within judgment. A hint to this interweaving appears in the fact that the world was created through ten utterances and the Torah was given through ten commandments. The world's construction began during the six days of Creation, while its enduring completion came through the giving of the Torah. The Sages explain that the words 'the sixth day' indicate that the world remained suspended until the sixth of Sivan, when it was perfected. The Sages further say that at first Hashem intended to create the world with the attribute of judgment; seeing that it could not endure, He joined mercy to it. He initially intended creation through the Name Adonai, which signifies judgment, and then joined the attribute of mercy, the Name Havayah. Adonai was thus incorporated within Havayah, producing the combined Name YaHDVNHY. This Name begins with yud and ends with yud. Through it the world was perfected: It was created at first through ten utterances and completed at the end through ten commandments, alluding to the combined Name through whose initial and final yuds the world was perfected. Although my soul is unworthy to understand exalted mysteries, I have not refrained from recording this; whether one does much or little, etc.
