To add. Why do we not at least say ‘King of the universe, the God of Abraham,’ and so forth? Because at the time He first became known as the God of Abraham, all inhabitants of the world had not yet accepted His kingship. Chizkuni, Parashas Chayei Sarah. The phrase ‘You are mighty forever’ means without any change or interruption, for there is no old age before Him. ‘You revive the dead,’ You Yourself and not through an agent. ‘He revives the dead with abundant mercy,’ as stated in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah concerning the three groups on the Day of Judgment, which Rashi and Tosafos explain as referring to the resurrection of the dead, through ‘and Rav chesed inclines toward kindness,’ and so forth. ‘He keeps His faith with those who sleep in the dust’ refers to the Patriarchs, who are described as sleeping and not dead. He promised them the gift of the Land and will fulfill His word at the resurrection. Alternatively, He keeps His promise to revive them although they did not truly die, by causing them to die for one hour immediately before the resurrection. See the chapter HaPoalim. ‘King Who causes death and restores life’: just as He causes death from above, so He restores them to life from above, without needing to lower dew upon them. See Midrash HaNeelam, Parashas Vayeira. ‘You are faithful to revive the dead’ means that He will first raise them with their blemishes, so that people should not say that they are different persons. See what I wrote in my responsa, Yoreh Deah, simanim 515 through 519.
