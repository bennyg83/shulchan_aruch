(א) In the thanksgiving of Hallel and in Birkat Hamazon — that is, in "Hodu laHashem ki tov" and in Birkat Hamazon in "Nodeh lekha" or in "Uvechal ana modim."

(ב) It is unseemly — for one may not bow except what the Sages enacted. And for this reason one does not bow at "Uvyerushalayim" or at "Boneh Yerushalayim," unlike Maharil [Dagul MeRevavah]; and Magen Avraham wrote that perhaps one may bow a little. All this is in the [Eighteen Blessings]; but outside the [Eighteen] one may bow and prostrate as one wishes. And therefore we find in the Jerusalem Talmud that a person leaves him in one corner and finds him in another corner from abundant bowings and prostrations. The poskim wrote that this was after he finished the eighteen blessings. What the Shulchan Aruch forbade in all this is because all these thanksgivings are in the manner of praise and celebration, and bowing and prostration do not apply to them. "And every upright one shall bow before You" — since he does not say it about himself but rather that the whole world says they shall prostrate before Him, may He be blessed — that too is in the manner of praise and celebration. But in "and we bow and prostrate," which he says about himself — he may bow [Taz; see there further].
