(א) On Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur when they say, etc. — and similarly those accustomed to pray the entire prayer on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur with bowing must straighten up at the end of each blessing and at the beginning of each blessing, so as not to add to the Sages' enactment; for they enacted only at the beginning of Avot and Modim and at their ends.

(ב) "Zokhreinu" and "Mi kamokha," etc. — the reason is not equal in them; for in "Mi kamokha" one must straighten up before the conclusion, since in the blessing of "Who gives life to the dead" there is no bowing there from the Sages' enactment, and if he bows it would be as though adding; but in "Zokhreinu" there is bowing there from the Sages' enactment when he says "Heaven, Shield of Avraham." Since this one bows throughout the entire prayer, the Sages' enactment will not be recognizable. Therefore there is a distinction between straightening up in "Zokhreinu" and straightening up in "Mi kamokha": in "Zokhreinu" one must straighten up before he says any words adjacent to "Blessed are You," so that he can bow afterward when he says "Blessed are You"; but in straightening up in "Mi kamokha" one need not straighten up until he says "Blessed are You." And similarly in the other blessings that have no bowing at their end [Taz].

(ג) Because of the obligation — for the first bowing was optional; and another reason that one must straighten up is in order to bow afterward at "barukh" and to straighten up at the Name, as below.
