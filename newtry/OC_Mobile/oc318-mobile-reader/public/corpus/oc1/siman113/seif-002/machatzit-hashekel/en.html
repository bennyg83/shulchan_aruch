(Seif kaf bet) It appears, etc., and to straighten up at the Name. That is, from the language of Shulchan Aruch it implies that the need for straightening up is only that it be recognizable that he returns and bows because of the obligation of the enactment of the Sages. And accordingly it implies that when he bows at Barukh he need not straighten up again and remains bowed. But the Agudah wrote that one must again straighten up at the Name. And the Taz wrote that one must also straighten up at the beginning of a blessing; and if so, at the beginning of Attah Gibbor one must be upright, and so at every beginning of a blessing. And the Taz wrote further that at the end of Mi Kamokha one need not straighten up except when he reaches Barukh Attah at the end of the blessing; but in Zokhreinu one must straighten up a few words before reaching the word Barukh — and so too in the laws of Rosh Hashana.
