Seif katan 2: It appears, etc. ‘And to straighten at the Name.’ Meaning, the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies that straightening is required only so that it be evident that he is bowing again because of the obligation enacted by the Sages. According to this, when he bows at Baruch, he would not need to straighten again but could remain bowed. The Agudah, however, writes that he must straighten again at the Name. The Taz writes that he must likewise stand upright at the beginning of the following blessing. Thus, at the beginning of Atah Gibor he must be upright, and likewise at the beginning of every blessing. The Taz further writes that at the end of Mi Chamocha he need not straighten until he reaches Baruch Atah at the end of the blessing. At Zochreinu, however, he must straighten several words before reaching Baruch. So he writes in the laws of Rosh Hashanah.
