So that it should appear, etc. Explanation of what is written: when they say "Zokhreinu" and "Mi kamokha" one must straighten up — the reason is not equal in them; for in "Mi kamokha" he must straighten up before the conclusion, since there is no bowing there from the Sages' enactment and it would be as adding, as above; but in "Zokhreinu" there is bowing there from the Sages' enactment when he says "Heaven, Shield of Avraham." Since this one bows throughout the entire prayer, the Sages' enactment will not be recognizable. Therefore there is a distinction between straightening up in "Zokhreinu" and straightening up in "Mi kamokha": in "Zokhreinu" one must straighten up before he says any words adjacent to "Blessed are You," so that he can bow afterward when he says "Blessed are You"; but in straightening up in "Mi kamokha" one need not straighten up until he says "Blessed are You." And similarly in the other blessings that have no bowing at their end, as seems to me. I wonder why they were not strict about the beginning of the other blessings that have no bowing — that he must straighten up there so as not to add to the Sages' enactment; perhaps the poskim relied on themselves in this, that we will understand from this that there too one must straighten up, and they cited the end of the blessing since the first blessing, which is Avot, has bowing at the beginning from the Sages' enactment — when saying "atah gibor" he must be in straightening at the beginning; and similarly at the beginning of the other blessings until Modim.
