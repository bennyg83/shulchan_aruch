And when he straightens up he straightens up at the Name — See Magen Avraham siman 127 who explains the view of Maharai, who holds that in bowing at the beginning of a blessing, if he wishes not to straighten up until near the closing, the matter is in his hands; but if he wishes to straighten up immediately he must straighten up at the Name. And likewise in bowing at the end of a blessing that he is obligated to straighten up immediately so as not to remain bowed at the beginning of the next blessing — then he must straighten up at the Name; for on account of "The the Master straightens the bent" — they required straightening up at the Name alone, not that one must straighten up from his bowing altogether; but if he straightens up anyway, then on account of "The the Master straightens the bent" he should straighten up at the Name. Therefore those who pray on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur with bowing need not straighten up except at the end and beginning of a blessing.
