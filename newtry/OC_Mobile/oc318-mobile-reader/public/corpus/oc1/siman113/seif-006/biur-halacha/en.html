And when he straightens, he straightens at the Name. See Magen Avraham, siman 127, who explains the view of Terumas HaDeshen. Regarding the bow at the beginning of a blessing, if one wishes not to straighten until close to the conclusion, he may do so. If he wishes to straighten immediately, he must straighten at the Name. Likewise, regarding the bow at the end of a blessing, where he must straighten immediately so that he not remain bent at the beginning of the following blessing, he must straighten at the Name. The phrase ‘Hashem straightens the bent’ alone does not require him to straighten from his bow at all. Rather, if he is in any event straightening, then because of ‘Hashem straightens the bent’ he should do so at the Name. Therefore, those who customarily pray on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur while bowed need straighten only at the end of a blessing and at its beginning.
