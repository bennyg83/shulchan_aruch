(Seif kaf heh) And when he straightens up, etc. Behold the priests on Yom Kippur — even though the reason is that one must straighten up at the Name, nevertheless the five [letters of the Name] straighten up bent. And it is possible specifically when one mentions the Name this reason applies; unlike the priests and the people on Yom Kippur, who did not mention the Name but heard from the mouth of the Kohen Gadol — and the Kohen Gadol in truth did not prostrate himself. Nevertheless it is difficult, for there are opposite views: when one mentions the Name it is fitting to straighten up — therefore this is the honor of God in straightening up; and if so, the listeners should be permitted to bow; nevertheless how is it said that the honor is specifically in bowing? For from this reason everyone bowed when they heard the Name. But accordingly it is not well settled in what Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Zohar. And it is possible per the secret we require thus: in a blessing one must be upright, and in hearing the Name without Barukh one must specifically bow; but in practice I have not stood in the secret of the Name. And see in the Taz what he wrote that they do not rebuke the people who strike their hearts when they say the confession of the Kohen Gadol.
