[5] He bows at Baruch, etc. When saying Baruch, he bends his knees; when saying Atah, he bends his head until the vertebrae protrude, and so forth. So rule the Piskei Tosafos, and so is written in the Zohar, Parashas Eikev. Maharshal writes in his commentary to the Semag that Baruch is related to berech, knee, teaching that one bends the knee at that word. This implies that at Modim one bends his body and head simultaneously. See Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 124.
