Even temporary sleep. Unlike the Tur and the Rosh who permit temporary sleep. The Beit Yosef raised a difficulty: why did the Rosh and the Tur not bring how much is the measure of temporary sleep that is in the Gemara Avodah Zarah — the tanna taught: Rami bar Yehezkel [said], for the time of walking a hundred amot; and the Beit Yosef answered that there is another baraita that taught: one who goes to sleep by day — if he wishes he removes [the tefillin], if he wishes he leaves [them], etc.; and this disagrees with the measure of a hundred amot; but he holds that it is permitted to sleep in them daytime sleep, which is temporary sleep, as we say in Sukkah. And it is very puzzling, for behold Rashi also explained in that other baraita: because by day it is not his way to sleep with his wife nor to sleep a fixed sleep, etc. — behold that there too it depends on temporary sleep; and what is temporary sleep here from the temporary sleep of the baraita of Rami bar Yehezkel? And furthermore, daytime sleep we say in Sukkah, chapter "The Elder," which is sixty breaths, as the Shulchan Aruch wrote above, siman 4, seif 16, and such was the practice of David. And who can say that both are not one measure — sixty breaths and walking a hundred amot? And it seems to my humble opinion that this measure of a hundred amot we cannot estimate at all, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ra'avad that from this one should be stringent to rule that one not sleep in them at all; but for the Rosh and the Tur it does not appear to be stringent in this, for the Torah was not given to ministering angels, or specifically to exceedingly wise people; and since the essence of this matter has no prohibition except as a rabbinic decree, we go in it to leniency, and one may sleep in them temporary sleep according to a person's way — and that is in the manner that the Tur wrote, siman 47, in the name of the Rosh, that whatever a person sleeps on the sides of his hands is temporary — such is the opinion of the Tur; but the Shulchan Aruch was stringent and ruled here like the Rambam: He wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yehuda, and you might ask: how is temporary sleep permitted, and behold it is forbidden to divert one's mind from them? And one may answer that distraction of mind is not called such except when standing with light-headedness and jest; but when he is dozing there is no distraction of mind, for he forgets the vanities of the world — thus far.
