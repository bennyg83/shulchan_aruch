Even. See Taz. It appears from Perishah that Tur follows his father Rosh exactly in explaining the baraisa in Sukkah 27a. If the tefillin are in his hand, it is forbidden in every case. This is what Tur means by, “My father Rosh did not write so.” If the tefillin are upon his head, an incidental sleep is permitted, provided he spreads a cloth between the pillow and cushion, as Rashi explains. Nevertheless, even when they are upon his head a cloth is required, because it is degrading to sleep in tefillin without one, as Levush explains this view. They are not concerned that he may fall fully asleep. Regarding Beit Yosef's challenge, see Beit Yosef at length. What Magen Avraham cites in the name of Beit Yosef, Rabbeinu Yonah, and Rosh is difficult, because Tur himself explicitly records it. See Magen Avraham, letter 2, and what I wrote there further.
The writer says: The sugya and the words of the poskim here are profound. I discussed them at length while studying with students, but here I shall merely allude to several points. See Bach regarding Rabbeinu Yonah and Rosh, who permit an incidental sleep and do not decree lest one come to a fixed sleep, because that would be a safeguard upon a safeguard: perhaps he will sleep permanently, and perhaps he will pass wind. This is difficult. Why, then, does Abaye ask what difference exists between sukkah and tefillin? Regarding sukkah there is only one concern, whereas regarding tefillin it would be a safeguard upon a safeguard. Even if one says that a fixed sleep would certainly lead to the concern, the difficulty remains: sukkah is a Torah obligation while tefillin here are governed by a Rabbinic safeguard.
Also, Tosafos there, s.v. “but,” asks from Shabbos 49a. According to Abaye, a clean body means that one must not pass wind, yet an incidental sleep presents no problem. Here, however, Abaye forbids it. Tosafos answers according to one approach. According to Rava, there is a different difficulty. There, a clean body means that one must not sleep, apparently including even an incidental sleep. Here, however, Rava is not concerned that one may fall fully asleep and therefore permits an incidental sleep. One may answer that Tosafos follows Rabbi Meir and understands that Rava does not disagree with the requirement to place one's head between his knees, but disagrees only regarding sukkah. According to Rashi, who explains that Rava is not concerned lest one fall fully asleep, one may explain like Tosafos on Shabbos 49a, s.v. “that he should not sleep,” that sleeping by day is forbidden because of distraction. Rava then holds that even forgetting worldly matters constitutes distraction. According to this, the baraisa permitting incidental but not fixed sleep must, according to Rava, refer to tefillin held in the hands while wrapped. The case of holding them in the hands is not one of incidental sleep according to Rambam's explanation. Fixed sleep is permitted only when a cloth is spread over the tefillin, following HaIttur as cited by Magen Avraham, letter 5. Alternatively, Rashi may hold that a cloth is always required, either as Bach explains or as Perishah explains, cited in Magen Avraham. This is why one must have a clean body: if a person is accustomed to incidental sleep, he should not put on tefillin, lest he sleep incidentally and forget while no cloth separates them. If so, however, Rashi should have explained that the cloth is spread over his head, but he did not. Nevertheless, fixed sleep remains forbidden. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillin, writes that Rambam therefore rules like Abaye in Shabbos 49a and not like Rava, because of the baraisa permitting incidental sleep. I did not understand this. According to Rambam, Rava is concerned lest one fall fully asleep, and even with his head between his knees and a cloth present it is forbidden. This requires study.
