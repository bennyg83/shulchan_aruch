(1) Temporary — for we decree lest he come to blow on them; and by placing the cloth over them he remembers that he has tefillin on him and will not come to blow:

(2) A woman with him — but if his wife is with him, it is forbidden by decree, lest he have relations with them:

(3) He sleeps in them — and distraction is not considered except when he is standing in jest and lightheadedness; but when he is engaged in his work and craft and his mind is not really on them, this is not called distraction [unless his mind is so disturbed by bodily needs that his heart turns away from the Omnipresent because of his worry]. And likewise when he sleeps he forgets the vanities of the world. Nevertheless, it is a mitzvah of the preferred way that his mind always be on the tefillin and that he not distract his mind from them to be drawn after and ponder evil thoughts; for this reason he is obligated to feel them at all times that he not distract his mind from them [Bach] — except during prayer and study, when he need not direct his mind to the tefillin:

(4) Temporary sleep — and there is no fixed measure for this; and some say for the time of walking a hundred amot, and that is approximately sixty-seven parts of an hour; and so ruled the Gra in his commentary:

(5) Between his knees — for otherwise we are concerned lest he come to sink into deep sleep:

(6) It is permitted to sleep — for we are not concerned here about blowing, since they are not on him:

(7) It is forbidden to sleep — for we are concerned lest they fall from his hand:

(8) In every case it is permitted — for even if they fall to the ground there is not so much concern; and see in the Gra's commentary that he wrote: specifically if the case holds a tefach, then it is considered an interposition to separate between him and the ground:
