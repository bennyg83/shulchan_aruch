And no woman was with him. If his wife is with him, however, it is forbidden, as a safeguard lest he have marital relations while wearing them. Gemara, chapter 2 of Sukkah.
One may sleep while wearing them. Distraction is considered present only when one engages in laughter or frivolity. Tur. Nevertheless, the preferred fulfillment is not to divert one's attention from them at all. Bach. See siman 252. According to what Tur writes, it appears to me that during prayer one need not touch them, because laughter and frivolity are forbidden there in any event. See siman 151.
An incidental sleep. There is no fixed measure for this. Some say it is the time required to walk one hundred amos, which is approximately one sixty-seventh of an hour.
It is forbidden to sleep. We are concerned that they may fall from his hand.
Inside their case. HaIttur writes only that when they are inside their case we are not concerned that he may pass wind. Rama adds that when they are inside their case we are likewise not concerned that they may fall. See what I wrote in siman 43, seif 7. Mishpetei Shmuel provides some support for the common custom to fast when tefillin fall to the ground. The same applies when a Sefer Torah falls. See there. This must refer to tefillin falling without their case. Regarding a Sefer Torah, however, one fasts even if it fell while inside its case. See siman 40.
