And no woman was with him — but if his wife is with him, it is forbidden by decree, lest he have relations with them [Gemara, chapter 2 of Sukkah].

He sleeps in them — and distraction is not considered except when he is standing in jest or lightheadedness [Tur]; nevertheless, it is a mitzvah of the preferred way not to distract his mind from them at all [Bach]. See siman 252, and it appears that according to what the Tur wrote — thus during prayer he need not be concerned with them, for otherwise it is forbidden through jest and lightheadedness. See siman 151.

Temporary sleep — and there is no fixed measure for this; and some say for the time of walking a hundred amot, and that is approximately sixty-seven parts of an hour.

It is forbidden to sleep — for we are concerned lest they fall from his hand.

In their case — the Ittur wrote only that we are not concerned about blowing when they are in their case; and the Rama innovated that for lest they fall too we are not concerned when they are in their case. And see above, siman 43, note 7; and in Mishpat Shmuel it is written somewhat supporting what people are accustomed to fast when tefillin fell on the ground; and the same applies when a sefer Torah fell — see there. And it must be said that it deals with [tefillin] without their case; but for a sefer Torah, even in its case — see siman 40.
