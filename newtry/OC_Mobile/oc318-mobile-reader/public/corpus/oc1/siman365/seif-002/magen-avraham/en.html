And the same applies when breached at its head itself — for example, when the alleyway is closed on three sides and partly on the fourth side, and the beam is placed from end to end, and a breach is made on the fourth side — it is permitted. But when the beam is placed on the post, if there are four handbreadths between the post and the wall it is forbidden, for a beam across the alleyway is required and there is none. And see what I wrote in siman 363, seif 14, seif kaf-hey, and seif lamed-gimel. For this the Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham intended. And that which the Gemara says it is permitted, as stated in siman 363, seif lamed-dalet — it deals with one rectified by a lechi at the side of the houses, as explained, to reconcile the view of the Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham. And if you ask: let it be that its head is breached at three like at its side — one may say that its head is different, since an alleyway that is not open at its head four handbreadths does not require any rectification at all, as stated in siman 363, seif kaf-chet.

That many people do not pass through it. Meaning: a filthy place or a place spoiled with mud, and the public does not pass through that partition (Rashi, Hagahot Asheri). And all the more so if ridges remain that obstruct passage. And we say in the Gemara on daf 5 that a courtyard whose breach is ten amos, as stated in seif 3. And it requires further study regarding our alleyways whether they have the law of a courtyard for this matter. And the same applies in a walled city that was breached with a breach of four handbreadths and many people pass through it — according to our ruling that it has the law of a courtyard, it requires further study whether it would be permitted, and one should be stringent. See what I wrote in siman 363, seif kaf-vav.
