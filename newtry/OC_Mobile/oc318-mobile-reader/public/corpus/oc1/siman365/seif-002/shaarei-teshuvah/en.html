And see in the responsa of Beit Efraim, siman 425, that the words of the Taz and Magen Avraham are correct. And see in the responsa of Pnei Me'orot, part 1, siman 425, who wrote regarding the community of Lvov: there was an alleyway open to the market of non-Jews, and at the end of the alleyway they erected a gate with tzurat hapetach as required, but the gate was set back from both sides of the alleyway more than four handbreadths and many people could enter through the breach on this side and that side. He wrote that no other rectification is needed, there. And what requires study in his words — see in that siman, and there is explained the law of a city not surrounded by a wall. And in order to permit carrying they rectified the alleyways with tzurat hapetach, and on Shabbos the tzurat hapetach in one place was damaged and the reeds were removed, and there was tzurat hapetach made to block passers-by because of the customs house which exists in many towns called "Schrank," and there is open space between the Schrank and the customs house beside it of about four handbreadths, and the townspeople pass there on foot. The questioner was concerned based on what Magen Avraham wrote regarding our alleyways — if many people pass through, a breach of four — to be lenient b'dieved: presumably at the place of tzurat hapetach the path is wide, so generally many people are not forced through the breach, for such a narrow passage; only if crowded from outside with wagons gathering there are they forced to the sides — then its usage is not comfortable. And for the reason that it is somewhat distant from the wall, one may say that even afterward he agrees when it is only on one side. Therefore, even l'chatchila one should not rely on this and the breach must be fenced. Nevertheless, since Peri Megadim permits l'chatchila, it is worth relying on it at least b'dieved in a time of need, there.
