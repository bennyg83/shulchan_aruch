That many people do not pass through — see Mishna Berurah, and he explains from Rashi. And see in Peri Megadim who explains that per the Levush, even if there is no mud and filth, whenever the public does not pass there it is called that many people do not pass through. And similarly I found in Rabbenu Yerucham who copied this statement and wrote: many enter it regularly — and it implies that whenever it was not in this manner it is called that many people do not pass through. And it is possible that Rashi's view is also thus — but Rashi included even where the breach faces a place where many walk, but the obstruction is due to mud and filth. Afterward I found in Pa'am Teshuvah in the name of She'elot U'Teshuvot who also wrote similar to what we suggested.

Must be rectified there — see Peri Megadim who sides that even when open to a karmelit one may say it nullifies the opening and requires rectification there, and it appears that rectification with lechi or beam there suffices, end quote. And in the Shulchan Aruch the Gra wrote that its law is like an open alleyway that requires tzurat hapetach on one side and lechi or beam on the other side. And it is known that tzurat hapetach on one side and on the other side lechi and beam in an open alleyway applies even when open to a field. And according to his words, if they made tzurat hapetach on the side of the alleyway opening, lechi and beam at the breach suffices. And so I saw in several later authorities who side thus. And the view of Even HaEzer: since there is tzurat hapetach on the side of the alleyway opening, no rectification is needed at the breach even of lechi and beam. However, in Beit Me'ir, according to what he copied in the name of the Ritva, it implies that rectification is always required at the breach specifically as tzurat hapetach — and it requires further study for practice.
