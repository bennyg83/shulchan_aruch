(6) Breached on its side — meaning: it was not breached adjacent to its head but inward from it. And if so, this is the law of seif 1 that a post of four handbreadths remained. And why did the Mechaber repeat and teach it? The later authorities answered: this seif is only a detail and explanation of the first law — that whenever a post of four remains at its head, it is considered breached not toward its head. And because of the latter part he cited it — and it is as if he said in seif 2: that which we say in seif 1 that if a post of four remains it is permitted up to ten amos — this applies even if nothing remains of the wall at its base. However, it is only permitted where many people do not pass through.

(7) Not toward its head — and the same applies when breached at its head itself — for example, when the alleyway is closed on three sides and partly on the fourth side, and the alleyway's rectification was by lechi or beam (that was placed from the place of closure to the opposite wall), and a breach was made at the place of closure — it is permitted up to ten amos, provided the standing portion exceeds the breach. And some say: here, when there is a breach on the fourth side, we require that the beam be placed specifically on the alleyway walls from end to end. But most later authorities agreed that what is placed on the standing portion is as if placed on the alleyway wall — this too applies to the alleyway. And what is breached in between is nullified relative to the standing portion. And the same applies if a lechi is made there — it suffices and a lechi adjacent to the house is not required.

(8) Even if nothing remained, etc. — meaning: and there is no obstruction to pass through that place — nevertheless we do not decree lest they abandon the large opening and enter through that place and the large opening is nullified.

(9) That many people do not pass through — meaning: it was a filthy place or a place spoiled with mud, and the public does not pass through that partition. And all the more so if three or four handbreadths height remained of the wall at its base across the entire breach — this certainly obstructs people's passage.

(10) But if many people pass through, etc. — for it is no longer called an opening but a public path (Levush). And behold, in seif 3 it is explained that regarding a courtyard the measure of its breach is always ten even if many pass through. And Magen Avraham is uncertain: according to what is explained in siman 363, seif kaf-vav in the gloss — that our alleyways have the law of courtyards — it is possible that also for this matter alleyways have the law of courtyards. And the same applies in a walled city breached in its wall with a breach of four handbreadths and many pass through — if also for this matter it has the law of a courtyard, it requires further study. And he concluded: one should be stringent. And see in Sefer Beit Me'ir who expanded on this and concluded that as the law we practice all these laws in our alleyways.

(11) Must be rectified there — meaning: they should reduce it from four handbreadths or make tzurat hapetach there.
