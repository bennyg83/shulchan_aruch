(Seif kaf-gimel) Breached (and know there is no distinction in the law between breached on its side toward its head or not toward its head), etc., from end to end — meaning: not on the end of the fourth partition but across the full width of the alleyway.

And for this the Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham intended. For it is stated on daf 16: R' Chanan bar Rava said in the name of Rav: an alleyway breached on its side — at ten; at its head — at four. And it asks: what is different about its side at ten that we say it is an opening — from its head too, etc. R' Huna, son of R' Yitzchak, answered: for example, when it was breached at a corner, for an opening at a corner they do not make. And the Tur wrote: if breached at its head, its breach is at four. And so Rabbenu Yerucham. And the Beit Yosef asked: he should have written specifically that it was breached at a corner. He further asked: for the Tur ruled in siman 363 like R' Yitzchak: an alleyway fifteen amos wide — he distances two amos and erects a post of three and a half amos — as stated in the Shulchan Aruch siman 363, seif 34 — behold, at its head even a breach of two amos does not disqualify. And on this Magen Avraham answered: the Tur and Rabbenu Yerucham deal with when the beam is placed on the post. And that which the Gemara says it is permitted, etc. — meaning: in that which we say that also at its head, if not at a corner, its breach is at ten. And also that which R' Yitzchak said: an alleyway fifteen amos wide distances two amos, etc. — with a lechi at the side of the houses. The same applies when the beam is placed from end to end — only the manner of speech is abbreviated. And from where does he know to ask to challenge: what is different about its side, etc., from its head too, etc. — lest it deals with one rectified with a beam and the beam is placed on the post. For if so, it should challenge: rather than distinguishing between its side and its head, let it distinguish within its head itself whether the beam is placed on the post or from end to end or rectified by a lechi standing at the houses — not so now that it answers: breached at a corner. It is not difficult to distinguish all of its head whether breached at a corner or not. One may say: in truth this is R' Chanan's intention — its side at ten includes all the partitions on the side, also the fourth side where the lechi or beam is. And "at its head" means at the head of the wall, i.e., at a corner. And that which the Tur did not copy the Gemara's law of how when it is at a corner — the Beit Yosef already wrote on the Rosh that he too did not copy this matter of a corner because it is already taught in several places that openings at a corner they do not make. And since in an alleyway that is not open at its head four handbreadths no rectification is required at all, as stated in siman 363, seif kaf-chet — even though there the view of one authority is specifically less than three handbreadths but three handbreadths requires rectification — nevertheless Magen Avraham's words are stated to reconcile the Tur's view. And the Tur holds like those who say: if it is not open four handbreadths, no rectification is required.
