And such as the alleyway walls entered, etc. — and it deals with when the alleyway walls protrude from the courtyard walls three handbreadths. But in less than three handbreadths we say lavud and behold it is as if they did not enter from inside (Prisha, Turei Zahav, and Peri Megadim). And see in Peri Megadim that the measure of the alleyway's entry into the courtyard is at most three handbreadths.

To one standing in the alleyway — see Magen Avraham: this wording is not precise. And see in the Prisha.

Because of lechi — and it refers to when it is not opposite the courtyard's breach that he wrote above — for otherwise behold it is in the category of open, and what would a lechi help him? And so it is proven in the Gemara in the conclusion.

And it is permitted — even if the courtyard residents are accustomed to pass through there, since the courtyard residents have another way to the public domain, we compel per the measure of Sodom that they should not pass through the alleyway (Magen Avraham in the name of Tosafot).

As if breached into a karmelit — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote regarding an alleyway terminating at a river whose bank beside the houses is not ten handbreadths deep, and on the other side the bank is ten deep — it is possible there is reason to forbid, etc. And he derived this law from the words of Tosafot (and even though per their view it is permitted in the conclusion in the Gemara even regarding a vineyard path terminating at a river — for we rely on the permissibility of the partitions on the far side of the river — he cited this for halachah according to Rashi's view and Hagahot Asheri in the name of Or Zarua, that per the conclusion it is forbidden regarding a path). And in truth there is much to question here — for even if we hold that regarding a path one should forbid, there is no proof here, neither according to Rashi's explanation nor according to Tosafos' explanation — for there is different, since the main permissibility comes through lechi (see Rashba that this is Rashi's reason who forbids regarding a path even in the conclusion). And they will err that the lechi permits even where the path is open and has no partition from the river — not so here where there is nothing to err about, and perforce they will understand that the permissibility is due to ridges on the far side of the river, and otherwise the Rashba and Ritva agree with Tosafos' view that in the conclusion the path is also permitted and we are not concerned at all that they will err — for we do not find this in the Gemara except where the permissibility comes through lechi made at the path — see there. And certainly one may rely on them to be lenient in a d'rabbanan matter.
