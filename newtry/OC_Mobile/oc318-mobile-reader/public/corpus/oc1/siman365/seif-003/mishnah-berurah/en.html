(12) Alleyway, etc. — because this seif has many details, I will first give a brief introduction. It is known from siman 364 that an alleyway open to a public domain is more stringent than one not open — it requires tzurat hapetach on one side. And it will be explained in this seif: if a courtyard separates between the end of the alleyway and the public domain, and the courtyard is breached on both sides about ten amos (and side-sections remain on this side and that side) — so that one standing in the alleyway sees the public domain opposite him, as the courtyard had no other wall except the alleyway wall entering into it — what is the law of the alleyway and courtyard regarding carrying? Perhaps the alleyway will be judged because of this as open to the public domain (and it will be explained whether there is a distinction if the courtyard's breach toward the public domain faces the courtyard's breach toward the alleyway — then it is judged as open; if not, not). And also the courtyard residents can restrict it if they did not make an eruv together. And how is the law regarding the courtyard — behold, it is breached to the public domain?

(13) Breached across its full width — excluding when side-sections remained in it — it is judged as lechi and the courtyard cannot restrict it even when they did not make an eruv together.

(14) If they did not make an eruv, etc. — he cited "did not make an eruv" to include: even in this case where in the alleyway carrying is forbidden as explained below, nevertheless in the courtyard it is permitted and the alleyway cannot restrict it.

(15) The courtyard is permitted — for the breaches in its walls toward the alleyway and toward the public domain — since they are not more than ten amos they are like openings, and the alleyway residents also cannot restrict it since the alleyway has no foot-traffic rights over the courtyard.

(16) Even if the breach faces, etc. — meaning: even though regarding the alleyway it is stated below that if its breach faced the courtyard's breach we consider it open to the public domain and it is forbidden in every case — nevertheless regarding the courtyard we are not stringent in this.

(17) And even if many pass through — even though above regarding an alleyway many passing through disqualifies, here regarding the courtyard we do not pay attention to many passing through.

(18) And provided, etc. — meaning: both the breach toward the public domain and the breach toward the alleyway — then they are considered openings. But if the breach was more than ten amos it is considered a full breach and behold it was breached to a forbidden place and is only permitted with tzurat hapetach, as above in siman 363, seif 2.

(19) Even if not opposite — meaning: even if the breach toward the public domain was not aligned opposite the courtyard's breach facing it — and if so we cannot forbid carrying in the alleyway because of openness to the public domain — nevertheless it is forbidden due to lack of eruv, as he explains further. And it is forbidden even if many do not pass from the public domain to the alleyway through the courtyard.

(20) The alleyway walls entered the courtyard — meaning: the ends of the length walls of the alleyway enter inward into the courtyard, and through this the courtyard's side-sections visible on this side and that side appear to be only the courtyard walls — therefore we cannot combine the side-sections for the alleyway. But if they did not enter inward, we count the side-sections also for the alleyway and they are considered as lechis on this side and that side, and the courtyard can no longer restrict it.

(21) Not on the courtyard's side — but in the middle of the courtyard. For if it were so that the alleyway wall equals the length of the courtyard wall, even on one side it appears like a long alleyway extending to the public domain opposite and open, and forbidden.

(22) On the other side opposite it — for through this the alleyway is judged as open and extends to the public domain opposite.

(23) Belonging to an individual is forbidden — lest he take counsel and build houses in the courtyard width exceeding the alleyway, and it becomes an alleyway breached on the side of the courtyard and is forbidden, as above.

(24) As if breached into a karmelit — and perforce even if the alleyway walls did not enter inward into the broad area and the side-sections are visible from outside — nevertheless carrying in the alleyway is forbidden, for now regarding the broad area itself the side-sections do not help to permit carrying in it, for it is karmelit — how would this help the alleyway? An alleyway whose fourth side opens toward a river — if the riverbank beside the houses is ten handbreadths deep, or at least is so sloped that within four amos of slope it reaches ten handbreadths — then this slope is considered a partition and no rectification is needed, for we do not nullify a partition. But if on the city side it is not so sloped, even though on the other side of the river it is ten deep — if it is wider than ten amos between the houses and this place, it is obvious it is forbidden, for behold there is a breach separating between the partition and the houses. And even in less than this measure it is also possible to forbid lest they think the permissibility is because of the bank on the far side of the river where ten handbreadths accumulate within four amos, and they will come to permit even when fully open without any rectification. And see in Biur Halachah what we explained that where necessary one may be lenient in this.
