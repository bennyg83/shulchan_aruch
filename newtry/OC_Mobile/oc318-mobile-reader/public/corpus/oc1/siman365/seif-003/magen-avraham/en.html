The courtyard is permitted. For a breach of ten does not forbid a courtyard, and the alleyway does not forbid it since it is wider than the alleyway and side-sections remain on this side and that side.

To one standing in the alleyway. This wording requires further study — for one standing in the alleyway, even if the alleyway walls did not enter the courtyard, does not see the side-sections; only one standing in the courtyard sees them, and it is visible from outside and equal from inside, as stated in siman 374, seif 3.

And it is permitted. Even though the courtyard residents are accustomed to pass through there, since the courtyard residents have another way to the public domain, we compel per the measure of Sodom that they should not pass through the alleyway (Tosafos). And see what I wrote in siman 364, seif 3.

On its side. For it appears like an open alleyway.

Belonging to an individual is forbidden. Lest he take counsel and build houses in the width exceeding the alleyway, and it becomes an alleyway breached on the side of the courtyard and is forbidden. And if one side of the broad area opens to a path that ascends to a riverbank and the bank's edge is ten high, making a partition — let him make a lechi at the mouth of the broad area at the end of the alleyway and carry from the alleyway to the broad area. The path itself is forbidden, and we do not make a lechi at the mouth of the path lest they say a lechi helps for an open path where there are no ridges (Ma'amar Mordechai, Hagahot Asheri, Beit Yosef, siman 364). And this lechi helps for the broad area to be enclosed for dwelling even though it requires a partition of ten, as stated in siman 358 — here, since the lechi helps for the alleyway, it also helps for the broad area. And the Tosafos wrote: if the river is ten deep on the side of the path, the path is permitted with a lechi. It only deals with when the ridges are beyond the river and the river is not wider than ten amos — nevertheless the path is forbidden to rectify, lest they think the ridges beyond the river help. Alternatively, it deals with when there are no residents on the path but only alleyway residents use it — then we are concerned they will say a lechi helps for a path where no one uses it, there. And if so, in a river encircling the city — if on the city side it is not ten deep at its bank, even though on the other side it is ten deep — if it is ten amos wide, it is obvious it is forbidden. And if it is not ten amos wide, there is reason to forbid lest they err, as the Tosafos wrote. And it is possible they decreed only regarding a path where people are not common.
