And the courtyard, Taz. The general principle of the matter: an alleyway is only permitted based on conditions: (1) they made an eruv with the courtyard — if not, they are forbidden. The alleyway breached across its full width into the courtyard — and we hold one eruv and not two. But from the reason that the courtyard has foot-traffic rights over the alleyway it is difficult: (1) one may say we compel, etc., as Magen Avraham wrote 7. And further: permitted foot in its place — siman 378, seif 65 — does not restrict when not in its place. And perforce the reason is: breached across its full width — Av Beit Chayim. And the Levush explained the reason: the courtyard residents' foot-traffic restricts the alleyway — requires further study. And in Turei Zahav he challenged the Levush and the Bach also thus. And the alleyway requires three conditions: (1) they made an eruv with the courtyard; (2) the alleyway's breach into the courtyard is not on the courtyard's side but in the middle of the courtyard — for one standing in the courtyard can turn right and left in the courtyard, for otherwise only the length of the alleyway wall extends with one courtyard wall and appears open; (3) the courtyard's breach on the second side is not opposite the alleyway's breach. Also condition (4): the courtyard belongs to many — for an individual will build on one side of the courtyard houses and the alleyway's breach will be on the courtyard's side itself and there is no room to turn at the side of the houses and it appears like an open alleyway, as stated in letter dalet. And "if not" in Magen Avraham will be further explained. And in letter zayin: an alleyway is forbidden due to permitted foot in its place — Magen Avraham siman 364, letter gimel. If so, the reason is foot-traffic rights. However, the other difficulties remain difficult.
