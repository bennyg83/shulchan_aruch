(37) In which they made a partnership — meaning: they made only alleyway partnerships and did not each make eruv chatzerot in their own courtyard. And even so it helps, as we hold below in siman 387 that we rely on partnership in place of eruv.
(38) Those that open into it — meaning: there is an opening or window through which they can carry and bring from one to the other not through the alleyway.
(39) That do not open, etc. — meaning: even those standing at the side of this courtyard — since they have no opening or window to that courtyard in which the eruv rests — they are forbidden, for the eruv does not apply to them and they cannot bring the eruv through the alleyway since the beam broke. And specifically when the alleyway's rectification broke on a weekday — but if it broke on Shabbos it is permitted to carry in all the courtyards since the eruv existed at twilight. And even from courtyard to courtyard it is permitted, both through openings between them and through holes — for since it was permitted for part of Shabbos it was permitted for all of Shabbos, as explained at the beginning of siman 374. Only through the alleyway carrying is forbidden.
(40) Forbidden — know that those courtyards forbidden to carry — even in one house it is forbidden from room to room if in each room a different person dwells alone and his fellow has no hold in them, as is proven in siman 370, seif 2 and 3. And one must warn about this in places that have no alleyway rectification in the city — even in one house two neighbors dwelling in two rooms may not carry if they did not make eruv chatzerot between them. And one must also warn in places where there is lechi or beam or tzurat hapetach and the rectification broke in that alleyway of the synagogue (where the eruv is placed, etc.) — if it broke on a weekday they may not carry again in all the alleyways even if they have their own separate rectification, unless they can bring the eruv to them from the synagogue. And know further: in a place where they do not make alleyway rectification in the city, one must be careful not to make eruv chatzerot unless there are at least in that courtyard two houses (and likewise two houses opposite each other with a courtyard between them, or two rooms opening to one house), or in the adjacent courtyard in a manner that they can bring the eruv to them through an opening or window on Shabbos. And the later authorities wrote: in this manner, when they do not rectify alleyways, they should not place the eruv in the synagogue as is customary, but in one of the houses. And if they did not do so, it is forbidden to carry even in the synagogue courtyard based on the eruv placed in the synagogue.
