Forbidden. Even if they stand at its side but have no opening or window to that courtyard in which the eruv is placed — they are forbidden, for the eruv does not apply to them and they cannot bring the eruv through the alleyway since the beam broke (there in Mordechai and in the responsum of Maharam, siman 422). And specifically when it broke on a weekday — but if it broke on Shabbos it is permitted to carry, as the Rama wrote in siman 374 that it is permitted to carry from courtyard to adjacent courtyard over the height of the wall, and in their courtyards it is obvious it is permitted to carry since the eruv existed at twilight. See siman 394. And if you ask: since they made one eruv and some courtyards were forbidden, all were forbidden — for we say a greater matter: on daf 48 they did not say residents should be stringent. See siman 378 and seif 3 there — we do not say eruv encourages violation. And know that those courtyards forbidden to carry — even in one house it is forbidden from room to room, as stated in siman 370, seif 2 and 3. And one must warn about this in places that have no lechi or beam. And according to our practice, since we place the eruv in the synagogue — if the rectification breaks in that alleyway, all the alleyways are forbidden even if they have their own separate rectification, unless they can bring the eruv to them. Therefore, in a place where there is no lechi or beam and they are forbidden to carry in the alleyway, and there are not two winter houses in one house — they should not make eruv chatzerot on Pesach, for it would be a blessing in vain.
