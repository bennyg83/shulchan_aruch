But when it opens to a karmelit, etc. Even though karmelit is lighter than a public domain, nevertheless for this matter we say: like finds like and is stirred — meaning: the karmelit between the lechis, even if it did not originally have the measure of karmelit, finds its kind that is outside it, which was originally nullified where there was not its kind. But when open to the public domain — neither this is its kind nor that is its kind — on this side and that side it is nullified. See what I wrote from this in siman 346 regarding peir levein — that it is obvious it is forbidden to carry there. And here the Beit Yosef brought the words of the Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Rashba: if the beam is four handbreadths wide and fit to receive plaster, even when open to a karmelit it appears that one may use beneath it, because the outer edge descends and closes, end quote. And this does not contradict what was written regarding peir levein, for there we do not say the edge descends and closes, because there is no partition around the ceiling, as the Rama wrote in siman 346 — not so here with a beam placed on the alleyway where there are three partitions, and we say the ceiling descends and closes the fourth side as long as there are four handbreadths there, as explained — simple. Further: I saw in the responsum of Maharil, siman 115, regarding a projection before the house — if it has three partitions and on the fourth the opening of the ceiling is four wide — he concludes one should be stringent even in this. And it requires further study whether peir levein helps, for it does not help unless the standing portion exceeds the breach, as explained in siman 362, seif 10.
