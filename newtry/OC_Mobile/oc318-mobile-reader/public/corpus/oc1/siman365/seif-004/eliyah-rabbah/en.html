[6] Whether under the beam, etc. The Shulchan Aruch writes this because the Beit Yosef emends the Tur and understands the Rosh to follow the Rambam, who prohibits even beneath a beam. Malbushei Yom Tov, however, writes that chapter 1, siman 8, of the Rosh implies permission under a beam. According to this, no emendation of the Tur is needed. This appears to me primary. I found the Agur, siman 545, quote the Tur's wording as halachah, understanding the Rosh to permit. I also found Rabbenu Yerucham, a disciple of the Rosh, page 94, and Tzeidah LaDerech rule this way. It is known that Tzeidah LaDerech generally follows the Tur, and Shiltei Giborim also implies this. The Rosh's phrase 'and so is reasonable' appears to refer only to rejecting the Raavad's first proof, not to the underlying law. Since major later authorities are lenient, we should rule this way, especially because the matter is rabbinic.
[7] [Levush] If the beam is four handbreadths wide and strong enough to support plaster, everyone agrees it is permitted, because the edge of the roof is viewed as descending and closing the opening.
[8] Whether it opens to a karmelit, etc. This implies that even an alleyway opening to a karmelit is prohibited. This remains so even nowadays when we have no true public domain, and even with our tzurat hapetach, because it is very high and does not provide sufficient visual recognition.
