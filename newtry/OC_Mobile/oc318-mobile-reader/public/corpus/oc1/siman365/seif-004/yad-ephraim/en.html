(Magen Avraham, seif 11.) As the Rosh wrote regarding a courtyard — meaning: from what is written "but at the entrance of the courtyard, etc." — he brought here in the Shulchan Aruch and its source is the Rosh's words. And that he cited the Rosh and it implies there is no proof from the Shulchan Aruch's words — one may say: regarding a courtyard specifically he needed to say that even when open to a karmelit it is permitted, lest one think that in karmelit, which is rabbinic, we are not so careful — there is more concern lest he roll to karmelit and bring to the courtyard entrance which is a public domain, as Tosafos wrote on Shabbos daf 9: a plain threshold of a house or courtyard is four wide and presumably its covering is also four wide — see Rashi there — in such a case it is a public domain as explained above siman 361. And if so he would carry from karmelit to public domain. Not so in an alleyway open to karmelit — beneath the beam is also karmelit as stated above seif 4, and it is permitted to carry there within four amos — beneath the beam with the karmelit inside is one domain. And that the Shulchan Aruch did not teach this permissibility for an alleyway open to karmelit — one may say it is implied from what is written "lest the object roll from his hand to the public domain, etc., since there is no distinction between him and the public domain" — it implies specifically when open to the public domain it is forbidden, but the Rosh there copied the Gemara's wording "lest he roll and come to bring" and did not mention the public domain at all — if it were that in karmelit it is permitted, he should have taught the permissibility of an alleyway to karmelit lest one err saying since he cited the courtyard's permissibility whether open to public domain or karmelit — the alleyway's prohibition is in every case. And from his omitting the alleyway's permissibility to karmelit and citing the courtyard's permissibility — from this it is true that in an alleyway in every case it is forbidden, and therefore he said that in a courtyard in every case it is permitted. And therefore Magen Avraham cited "as the Rosh wrote regarding a courtyard." And the reason it is forbidden in an alleyway to karmelit even though it is permitted to carry within four amos in karmelit and even from karmelit to karmelit — as explained above siman 346 — one may say: even though when open to karmelit we judge what is beneath the beam as karmelit — that is for stringency because like finds like and we combine it with the karmelit before it. But by law beneath the beam is also a public domain, for behold it is placed on the alleyway walls enclosed by three partitions — and if so for leniency that carrying from it to karmelit be permitted — we do not say so, and there is a prohibition of carrying from public domain to karmelit. And see what the poskim wrote on this in the name of the Bach who permits in an alleyway to karmelit. And he wrote in Tosafot Shabbos that according to his words one must explain regarding a courtyard such as with a beam four wide, etc., there — and we can explain that courtyard passage thus. But Magen Avraham's proof from his not writing permissibility in such a case for alleyway to karmelit — and if not — since karmelit is only rabbinic we do not decree that since in a courtyard he wrote permissibility when open to karmelit because of its recognizable distinction — from this we infer that even in karmelit, which is rabbinic, this decree applies. And if so he should have taught the permissibility that we do not decree in this — it would be like carrying in karmelit itself within four amos. Rather certainly this is the case: by law it was a public domain and it is like carrying from karmelit to public domain, as I explained.
