On its side toward its head. Meaning: one of the walls on the side of its length toward its head, adjacent to the lechi or beam. And beforehand: if a post four handbreadths wide remains at its head, the large opening is not nullified, even though the measure of the length of an alleyway is four amos — that is when an alleyway is first made. But here it was already an alleyway and merely became closed; four handbreadths in what remains from it suffices. And this seif is the wording of the Rambam. But the Tur follows the approach of the Rosh and distinguishes between whether many people pass through or not, as stated in seif 2. And the Beit Yosef asked: why did the Tur write at its head four handbreadths and not distinguish between a corner or not? And he further asked: for the Tosafos proved that specifically at a corner we say so — for otherwise we already said above in siman 364 that if one wishes he may distance two amos from the wall and make a post of three and a half amos — behold, the breach is two amos at the head of the alleyway and nevertheless it is permitted since it is not at a corner. And it appears to me to answer: since we conclude that regarding whether many people pass through it the matter depends on that — therefore at its head it is forbidden even when not at a corner, for many people pass through it there, meaning that it is in a clean place. And not in an unclean place but a filthy place — then it is called that many people do not pass through. And this is simple: at its head, which is adjacent to the opening, there is certainly no filthy place, for a person certainly does not make an opening in a place that is not clean — therefore it is as at its side where many people pass through and it is forbidden. And what the Tosafos proved from that case in siman 364 regarding one who distances two amos — I wondered, for this is not a proof, for here we come to disqualify the breach on the side because the opening that was originally made on that side is nullified, since now a breach was made in a place that was closed. Not so above, where he originally made it permissibly with a distance of two amos, and nullifying the opening is not applicable there, for so it was from the beginning. And without doubt this was the Tur's intention in this, in my humble opinion.
