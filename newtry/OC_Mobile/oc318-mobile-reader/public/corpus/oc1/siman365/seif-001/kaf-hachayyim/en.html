1) [Se'if 1] An alleyway in which a breach was made on its side. This means in one of the walls along its length. Rashi; Beis Yosef; Taz, subsection 1.
2) There. An alleyway in which a breach was made on its side. This means that it was breached into a public domain or a karmelis. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
3) There. Toward its entrance. This means close to the korah, on the inner side of it and forward. Rashi; Beis Yosef; Taz, there.
4) There. If a standing strip four handbreadths wide remains at its entrance, it is permitted. Since this is the minimum length required for a valid alleyway, the large entrance is not nullified. Although the required length of an alleyway is four amos, that applies when it is initially made into an alleyway. Here it was already an alleyway and was subsequently damaged, so a remnant four handbreadths wide suffices. Taz, there. If no standing portion remains, however, one must erect a strip four amos wide, because the initial minimum length of an alleyway is four amos, as stated in siman 363, se'if 13. Tosafos; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2. Tosefes Shabbos writes that this is contrary to Rashi's explanation, according to which even the initial creation of an alleyway requires only four handbreadths, as cited by Beis Yosef, and that the Levush's adoption of Rashi's explanation is incorrect. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, likewise questions Levush and cites several authorities who agree with Tosafos. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1, and Chayei Adam, rule 71, subsection 8, also rule accordingly.
5) There. If a standing strip four handbreadths wide remains at its entrance, it is permitted. It is permitted only where the public does not pass through the breach, as stated in se'if 2. Ma'amar Mordechai, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there; Chayei Adam, there; Pischei Olam, subsection 4.
6) There. Four handbreadths wide. These are expansive handbreadths, as stated in siman 363, se'if 26. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2. See below, subsection 8.
7) There. Provided that the breach is not wider than ten amos. Since it is not wider than ten amos, it has the status of an entrance, and an alleyway is not invalidated by having many entrances. Rashi, cited by Beis Yosef; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there; Chayei Adam, there.
8) There. Wider than precisely measured ten amos. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 3. This also follows from the statement in siman 363, se'if 26, for in these measurements we proceed stringently in each direction.
