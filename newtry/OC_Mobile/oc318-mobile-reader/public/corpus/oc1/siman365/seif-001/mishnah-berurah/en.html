(1) On its side toward its head — meaning: the alleyway was rectified with a lechi or beam and one of the walls on the side of its length was breached toward its head, adjacent to the lechi or beam.

(2) If something remains, etc., four handbreadths — that an important measure still remains in the alleyway; therefore the large opening is not nullified. And even though generally the measure of the length of an alleyway is not less than four amos, as explained above in siman 363, seif kaf-vav — that is when an alleyway is first made. But here it was already an alleyway and merely became closed; four handbreadths in what remains suffices.

(3) And provided, etc., ten — amos, for up to this measure it is within the category of an opening; beyond this it is considered a breach and must be closed or tzurat hapetach made at that place (Ritva).

(4) Forbidden unless, etc. — for even though less than ten amos is called an opening, here we are concerned lest the alleyway residents abandon the alleyway opening and go through this opening to shorten their way, and the rectification of lechi or beam — which must be at the alleyway opening — is nullified, and there is no alleyway opening upon it.

(5) Less than three handbreadths — considered as closed.
