On its side, Taz. What he wrote regarding what remains, Magen Avraham 1, Eruvin 5:1. And what he wrote: the Rambam's wording in chapter 17 of laws of Shabbos 17 — he does not distinguish regarding many people passing through; nevertheless, whatever breach remains a post of four handbreadths on its side at ten amos requires no rectification. But the Tur rules that many people passing through — even if there is a post of four handbreadths — requires rectification there. See Eruvin 6:1. And the Mechaber's wording is somewhat difficult — for he should have stated in seif 1 "and it does not deal with many people passing through" and no more. And the Levush changed from the Mechaber's wording and wrote similar to the Tur's wording: an alleyway breached on its side, etc. — see there. And the Mechaber and Rambam copied the Gemara's wording: on its side toward its head. And the Beit Yosef asked on what the Tur wrote — also a distinction: breached at its head at four handbreadths — and the Mechaber omitted it. And this is because in Eruvin 6:1 it is established at a corner, for an opening at a corner they do not make. At its head, not at a corner — no rectification at ten, like that case in siman 363, seif lamed-dalet: an alleyway twenty wide — he distances one amah from the wall and makes a post of three and a half amos (what he wrote "post of one and a half amos" appears to be a scribal error). See Tosafos 6:1, s.v. echad zeh, etc. And the Taz answered: at its head many people pass through. And so explained the Bach. And what the Tosafos wrote from Eruvin 10 in siman 363, seif lamed-dalet — there at the beginning it is lighter — so too explained the Bach, also in the second version. And the Gemara that establishes at a corner — because they had not yet concluded regarding many people passing through — Av Beit Chayim. And in siman 363, seif lamed-dalet — even though many people pass through, nevertheless at its beginning it is lighter than at its end for this matter, as the Taz wrote. And see Levush who wrote: many people of the public domain — it appears prima facie not like Rashi's filthy place; perhaps it is difficult for him from siman 363, seif lamed-dalet. And the Taz and Magen Avraham 3 explained like Rashi: many people passing through — the alleyway residents; even open to a karmelit one may say it nullifies the opening and requires rectification there, and it appears that rectification with lechi or beam there suffices.
