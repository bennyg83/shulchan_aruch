In the Mechaber, seif 1: "more than ten" — the letter hei was deleted and it should read "ten." And so in seif 2: "breached on its side" — [according to one version] "closed toward its head" — as we said, its measure should read "ten" — the hei was also deleted and it should read "ten." And it should read as I emended. And above in seif 1 it refers to not requiring ridges, but we require that many people not pass through. And so it is understood in the Levush. And if not, there is no understanding to the distinction between the two seifim — and what boundary is there between "toward its head" and "not toward its head"? And this law is also not found in the Talmud or Beit Yosef. Truth will show its way — and this is the approach of the Rosh and Tur, and not like the Rif and Rambam. And the Taz's words are very astonishing and not precise at all in the text. And also what was written in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 2, "Rif" — is a scribal error, for the Rif holds like the Rambam that no condition at all is required. Also in the Rif printed on daf 89 side 2, the word "ve'okimnan" until "bnei public domain" must be deleted, for it is explained in Rabbeinu Yehonatan there daf 90 side 1, and in Shiltai HaGibborim there, and in the Rosh and all the poskim that the Rif omitted this matter of ridges and many people passing through — understand this. And also Be'er HaGolah marked two types of references on the two seifim and there is no substance to them — the main principle as stated, and from it do not deviate.
