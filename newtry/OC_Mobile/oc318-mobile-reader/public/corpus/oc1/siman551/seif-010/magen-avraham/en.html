They are permitted in a cooked dish. Since his intent is to afflict himself, and he is in fact afflicted, and it resembles one who vows from meat, who is permitted in a cooked dish. It appears that meat fat has the status of meat itself, and one may not eat a dish containing it. Nowadays the practice is to forbid even a dish cooked with meat, and one may not change the custom.
And salted meat. Even though regarding the wayward son it is not considered meat.
To a child. This implies that one may give meat and wine to a child during this week, for the original custom was not to be stringent with a child, specifically a child who does not know how to mourn over Yerushalayim. It is not because of the mitzvah alone, for elsewhere it is implied that even for a mitzvah we do not feed a child a prohibition. Rather, there is no prohibition here for a child.
To drink the Havdalah wine. But Birkas HaMazon should be recited without a cup.
And a siyum of a tractate. See Rema MiPano and Yam Shel Shlomo. It is written that a meal is also made at the completion of a mitzvah.
And an engagement meal. This is difficult, since above in seif 2 he wrote that it is forbidden to make an engagement meal. Some precise copies deleted these words. The Bach writes that if he has no wife and children it is permitted, as above in the name of the Ran, though nowadays the practice is to forbid even then. Perhaps here it refers to a meal not at the time of the engagement or not in the bride's house, where there is not so much joy, and nevertheless it is a mitzvah meal.
Not to add. Maharil writes that this applies only to those who come because of kinship or friendship; those who come merely to drink are doing a transgression. Some take an exact minyan, and others take ten in addition to relatives who belong to the meal. Women who belong to the meal are also permitted meat and wine where women are customarily invited. One who is sent food to his home may not eat it. At a siyum, even one who did not learn with them may eat because of kinship or friendship.
