From Rosh Chodesh until the fast. In Bava Batra of Taanis they disagree — R' Meir and Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon: R' Meir holds the prohibition of laundering and haircut is until the fast; Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon holds the prohibition is the entire week — meaning that week; and Rava ruled the halacha is like R' Meir that it is not forbidden after the fast even in that week; and Rashal wrote in responsum siman 54 that there are people who practice not to eat meat those three weeks and add until Shabbat Nachamu as written in Minhagim — they do not act well since the reasons for which meat and wine were forbidden have passed; and he concluded: it also appears that even if one comes to be stringent until Shabbat like Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon — it is not proper conduct, as stated in first chapter of Berachos: R' Tarfon was on the road and inclined to read per Beit Shammai and endangered himself from bandits; they said to him: you should have obligated yourself since you transgressed the view of Beit Hillel; Tosafos explained: even though he was stringent on himself to act like Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel also permit to read like Beit Shammai to incline and read — nevertheless since he came to intend to transgress Beit Hillel's view, even though to be stringent he is liable for death; the same here where they established halacha like R' Meir and he comes to be stringent like Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon — he is called a sinner who afflicts himself, end quote; and the Rav's words are not clear here, for Tosafos wrote there in conclusion in name of R' Shim'ei: such is regarding Keriat Shema — even though by law he fulfilled b'dieved, since for Beit Hillel it is not correct as stated in the Mishnah — he should have obligated himself; if he acted like Beit Shammai for Beit Hillel he did nothing — end quote; if so R' Tarfon was not deserving of punishment except because he acted per Beit Shammai in what is not correct for Beit Hillel; unlike here in the dispute of R' Meir and Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon he did nothing against R' Meir by acting like Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon; also Maharachatz z"l disagrees with Rashal here for another reason — end quote; and certainly one who is stringent here has no sin, only it appears to me the stringent should not say he is concerned for Rav Shmuel bar Gadyon's view — for in that he appears to disagree with Rava's ruling, Heaven forbid — rather he should be stringent for himself beyond the letter of the law and say nothing:
