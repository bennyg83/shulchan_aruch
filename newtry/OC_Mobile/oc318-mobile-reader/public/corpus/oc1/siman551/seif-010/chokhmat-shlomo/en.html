Se'if 10. In the gloss: and at a seudas mitzvah, such as a milah and a pidyon haben and a siyum of a tractate, etc. Marginal note: see the Taz, s.k. 12, what he wrote regarding the meal of a milah, that it appears to me to be a complete permission, for milah overrides Shabbos, and Shabbos overrides building the Beis HaMikdash, etc. And his words are puzzling, for in the Gemara in Mo'ed Katan it derives there that a fortiori: if the Mishkan overrides Shabbos, all the more so the Beis HaMikdash; and it objects: if so, why were they afraid; and it answers: perhaps the needs of the Most High are different from the needs of an ordinary person, etc.; if so, it is proven that the needs of an ordinary person are different from the needs of the Most High; if so, it is explicit contrary to the words of the Taz, that in truth this a fortiori is not a [valid] a fortiori law; and even though afterwards the sin was forgiven them, there is no proof that it was not a sin, for one may say that it was a sin, only that through their regretting and doing teshuvah they are forgiven, as our Sages of blessed memory said: whoever transgresses a transgression and is ashamed of it, they immediately forgive him; and the words of the Taz require further study; and examine carefully.
