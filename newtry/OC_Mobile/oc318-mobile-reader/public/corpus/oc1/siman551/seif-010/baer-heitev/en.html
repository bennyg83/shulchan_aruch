In a cooked dish. It appears to me that meat fat has the same law as meat itself, and one may not eat a cooked dish containing fat. Nowadays the universal custom is to prohibit even a dish cooked with meat. Shach writes similarly in Yoreh Deah 216:19 regarding vows, and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe also prohibit it. Magen Avraham; see there. This applies specifically to a dish containing meat. A dish cooked in a meat pot is certainly permitted. Taz permits wine that became mixed into a cooked dish even when the taste of wine is perceptible. According to the foregoing custom, one should be stringent.
To a child. The cup used for Birkas HaMazon after a circumcision is likewise customarily given to a child, specifically a child who does not understand mourning for Yerushalayim. Magen Avraham. Magen Avraham implies that this is not limited to a mitzvah; throughout this week it is permitted for such a child. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees and permits it only for a mitzvah; see there.
Havdalah. For Birkas HaMazon, however, he recites it without a cup. Maharil.
Betrothal. This is difficult, for seif 2 states that a betrothal meal is forbidden. Bach writes that one who has no wife and children may make a betrothal meal, as explained above in the name of Ran; see seif katan 9. Nowadays, however, the custom is to prohibit it even for one who has no wife and children, as explained above. See Magen Avraham.
Who are connected. Maharil writes that this refers specifically to those attending because of family relationship or friendship. Those who attend merely to drink are committing a transgression. Levush rules in accordance with the customs that from Rosh Chodesh until Tishah B'Av one takes only a minimal minyan, while some take ten in addition to the relatives connected to those making the meal. Found written: Women connected to the meal may also eat meat and drink wine, specifically in a place where women are ordinarily invited to such a meal. One who is sent food at home may not eat it, as stated in siman 568:2 in the gloss. It appears to me that only relatives disqualified from testimony are considered relatives. At a siyum, even one who did not study together with them may eat with them because of kinship or friendship. Eliyah Rabbah proves that if the completion of a tractate did not arise in the regular order of one's study, he should neither hasten nor delay it for this purpose. If he would not have made a meal on other days, he should perhaps not make one now either; see there. It appears to me that one should not combine the leniencies of both opinions, but should follow either Rama with both his leniency and stringency, or Levush. In Yoreh Deah 246, seif katan 10, I wrote that a mourner within twelve months may eat at a siyum, but one observing a yahrzeit should not eat there; see there. When Tishah B'Av falls on Shabbos and is postponed, that week does not have the law of the week in which Tishah B'Av occurs regarding this matter, and all agree. Magen Avraham. See Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah.
On the eve. It appears to me that in any event it should be held before midday. Magen Avraham; see there.
