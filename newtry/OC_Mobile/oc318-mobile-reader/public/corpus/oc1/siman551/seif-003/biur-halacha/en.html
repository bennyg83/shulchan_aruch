And linen garments have in them no [issue] of pressing. The language of the Tur: because they are not concerned to press them, since they are close to the flesh; and the Ramban wrote the language: because they are not pressed, and sweat and filth do not come out of them; and that is, it speaks of linen garments that are close to the flesh.
For behold there is one who wrote, etc. According to this reason it is forbidden even regarding laundry of linen garments; for what they said, "linen garments have in them no [issue] of pressing," is specifically that they have no pressing, but laundry applies to them in Eretz Yisrael according to the view of Rabbeinu Yehudah bar Reuven; and since all the lands are equal to Eretz Yisrael except for Bavel, it emerges that laundry is forbidden in every place even regarding linen; so it is stated in the Ran explicitly. And what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, "and further there is one who explained this reason," is only to awaken us that our laundry is like pressing, and if so it is in any case forbidden by law regarding woolen garments even to leave [them]. And what he concluded further, "even regarding linen garments, at least to wear them": see in Beur HaGra, who is astonished that to wear them is certainly forbidden according to everyone by law; and all that the Mechaber wrote, "and they practiced," etc., is only to awaken us that even to leave [them] is forbidden. And in my humble opinion it is possible that one should read "and even," etc., meaning that even regarding linen garments, which according to this second reason we should not forbid, at least to wear them is certainly forbidden by law.
And we are accustomed to be stringent in all of this, etc. That is to say, in all that is stated above at the beginning of the se'if, except one who has only one undershirt: in this one should be lenient, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote [Chayei Adam]. And what he wrote in Pischei Olam, "from Rosh Chodesh until the fast it is permitted in this," is a printer's error, and it should read "until the week."
And likewise for the honor of Shabbos, etc. See in Mishnah Berurah that we wrote regarding haircutting that it is forbidden; and behold the Magen Avraham brought this in the name of Hagahos Asheri, and he brought this from Or Zarua; and I examined in Or Zarua in the laws of Tisha B'Av, and this is its language there: "And on Thursday they are permitted because of the honor of Shabbos"; meaning they are permitted to launder and to wear on Shabbos, but to cut hair on Thursday is forbidden, as it says in the Yerushalmi: "And on Thursday they are permitted because of the honor of Shabbos"; Rav Huna in the name of Rav Hamnuna said: regarding haircutting he is concerned in it; meaning that it is forbidden to cut hair; end of his words. And behold, one who examines the Yerushalmi itself: before us the reading is "regarding haircutting it was restored"; and even according to his reading it is also impossible to say that the intent of the Yerushalmi is to be stringent, for according to the context it is proven there to the contrary, that haircutting is permitted on Thursday before Shabbos; and this requires great further study. And see in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who also brought in the name of Tosafos that laundry and haircutting are both equal toward leniency.
