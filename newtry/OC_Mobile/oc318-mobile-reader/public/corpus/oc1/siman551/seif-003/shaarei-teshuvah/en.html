And a baal bris. See Baer Heitev; and see in Pri Megadim Avraham, part 3, siman 37, who challenged Baer Heitev and wrote that one should not infer from his not writing a permission to shave, and examine carefully the other side: since he did not write a prohibition, one may say that he relied on what he wrote to permit wearing whites, and the same applies to shaving, since they are accustomed to shave because it is his festival; and in Machtzik Beracha he wrote in the name of Responsa Zera Emes that his view is to forbid haircutting and wearing whites during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls in a teivah, and he challenged Pri Megadim Avraham and wrote that the Rama permitted only Shabbos garments, but not whites, etc.; but in the name of Bnei Yehuda he wrote to forbid haircutting and to permit whites; see there in Machtzik Beracha, letter 5. And see in Responsa Or Ne'elam, siman 10, who wrote: Tisha B'Av on Sunday and the milah on the Thursday before it: since there is no week in which Tisha B'Av falls, and there is no prohibition by the law of the Gemara, one should permit the mohel and the baal bris to cut hair for the honor of the milah. And see in Pri Megadim Avraham there, who challenged Baer Heitev for understanding what is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 394, "to shave," as referring to cutting the hair of the head: that is not so; it refers to cutting the nails; and likewise in Darkei Moshe he did not mention "to shave," and likewise in the Levush, etc.; and he challenged him for nothing, for Baer Heitev concluded there, and see what Baer HaGolah wrote; and in Baer HaGolah there in Yoreh De'ah he wrote there that in Darkei Moshe he did not mention "to shave," and perhaps it refers to the nails, etc.; and in Baer Heitev there he brought the words of the Levush and wrote that in Baer HaGolah he objected to this, that he should have said "and to shave them," etc.; see there.
(And see in Eliyah Rabbah and Eliyah Zuta, from which Baer Heitev copied, only that he omitted what is written there, that that of siman 393 in Yoreh De'ah refers to the nails, because he pointed to a place in Baer Heitev. And see there in Eliyah Rabbah and Zuta, that before the week in which Tisha B'Av falls one should permit haircutting as well; and if it falls on Shabbos and is postponed, he decided among the poskim that it does not have the law of the week in which Tisha B'Av falls; see there.) (And see there in Pri Megadim Avraham, who wrote to defend the custom that mourners are the escorts for bringing to the chuppah and eating there: for the Taz wrote that one should not permit eating there, and all the more so those who are lenient have on whom to rely, namely the words of the Rosh, chapter "HaCholutz," etc.; and even though other mourners practiced a prohibition, nevertheless escorts are considered for them a great mitzvah, etc.; therefore they practiced to be lenient; see there. And in our country they are accustomed to be stringent like the words of the Taz; only when they are relatives they serve during the meal like the hosts of the meal and afterward eat with them; and nevertheless it is not proper to be there to hear the playing of the musician with musical instruments, and all the more so a mourner for his father and for his mother, or a woman for her husband, even though she serves there, they should refrain from hearing the voice of singers.) And see in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 391 regarding cutting nails for immersion in the Taz and Shach there and Nechemas Kehein there. And what the Magen Avraham wrote, that the one who brings in and the one who takes out the infant is not included in this: the same applies, and all the more so, to the other honor-holders who pass the infant from one to another, and also those who place [him] on the chair of Eliyahu, of blessed memory, and likewise those who place [him] on the knees of the sandek, or who hold the infant at the time of saying "Asher Kidesh Yedid," etc. And it would somewhat appear to be lenient for the one who places [him] on the knees of the sandek, because the sandek's feet resemble an altar (and what is written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 265, that it is like one who burns incense: see in Responsa Noda BiYehuda, siman 26, who wrote to resolve that there should be no difficulty with what Maharil wrote, that one should not give to one sandek his two sons, for this reason: perhaps there is a stringency regarding the sandek and not via the mohel, for if the sandek's feet are like an altar of incense, then the mohel is like one who burns the incense, etc.; see there. And at first glance it is difficult against that very point, that a sandek who resembles an altar: what is the reason to say that one should not change him because he enriches, for we find this only regarding one who burns incense, and he derives it from what is written "they shall put incense," etc. And nevertheless there is support for this from what is written in Yoma folio 43: Rabbi Yehudah says there was no lottery for the coal-pan, etc.; would you think: since it is uncommon and enriches, they should institute for it a lottery of its own, etc.; hence even the coal-pan upon which they place the incense also enriches, like the one who places the incense). But I have not seen people practicing thus; and here in Brod they are accustomed that the father of the boy is the one who places the infant on the sandek's feet, and it is a fine custom; and the father's father of the boy and the father of the woman who gave birth also practice to be lenient and to wear Shabbos garments, they and their wives; and it seems that other relatives as well, whose way, if it were on other days, is to wear Shabbos garments, wear them also now; and this requires further study. And it appears that it is distress for the father of the boy and the woman who gave birth when these relatives do not join in their joy by wearing whites; and for other relatives one should be stringent. And see Machtzik Beracha in the name of Zera Emes, that a baal bris is forbidden to wear new garments during this week; and women who carry the infant to the synagogue, and who pass the infant to the men's synagogue, whom they call "kvatterschaft," also wear Shabbos garments, since they have no other mitzvah in the mitzvah of milah except this alone; and see in Peri Megadim.
