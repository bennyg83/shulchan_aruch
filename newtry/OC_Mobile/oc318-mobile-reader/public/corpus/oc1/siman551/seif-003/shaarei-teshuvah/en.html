And baal brit. See Ba'er Heitev; and see in Pri Megadim part 3 siman 37 who challenged Ba'er Heitev and wrote one should not infer from that he did not write permission to shave — examine the other side that he did not write prohibition — one may say he relied on what he wrote to permit wearing whites, likewise to shave since they are accustomed to shave for it is his Yom Tov; and in Machatzit HaShekel he wrote in name of responsum of Zera Emet that his view is to forbid haircut and wearing whites in the week that Tisha B'Av falls in the word; and he challenged Pri Megadim and wrote Rama did not permit only Shabbat garments but not whites, etc.; however in name of Bnei Yehuda he wrote to forbid haircut and permit whites — see there in Maharam Benet letter 5; and see in responsum of Or Ne'elam siman 10 who wrote Tisha B'Av on Sunday and milah on Thursday before it — since there is no week that Tisha B'Av falls and no prohibition from Gemara law, there is room to permit the mohel and baal brit to shave for honor of the milah; and see in Pri Megadim there who challenged Ba'er Heitev that he understood what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 390 regarding shaving refers to head haircut — meaning nail cutting; and similarly in Darkei Moshe he did not mention shaving and similarly in Levush, etc., and in vain he challenged him, for Ba'er Heitev concluded with it and see what Beer HaGolah and Bach wrote there in Yoreh De'ah that Darkei Moshe did not mention shaving — perhaps it refers to nails, etc.; and in Ba'er Heitev there he brought Levush's words and wrote in Bach he challenged this that it should have said "and to shave them," etc. — see there (and see in Or Zarua and Or Zaru'a that from there Ba'er Heitev copied except he omitted what is written there that this of siman 390 in Yoreh De'ah refers to nails because he showed place for Noda B'Yehuda; and see there in Or Zarua and Zuta that before the week that Tisha B'Av falls there is room to permit haircut too; and if it falls on Shabbat and is postponed the poskim ruled it does not have the law of a week that Tisha B'Av falls — see there) (and see in Pri Megadim who wrote to advocate for the custom that mourners are shushbinin to bring to the chuppah and eat there, for Taz wrote one may not permit to eat there, and all the more the lenient have whom to rely on — words of Rosh chapter HaCholeitz, etc., and even though other mourners practice prohibition, nevertheless shushbinin are considered a great mitzvah, etc., therefore they practiced to be lenient — see there; and in our country they practice to be stringent per Taz's words only when they are relatives serving inside the meal like meal hosts and eat afterward with them; nevertheless it is not proper that there be heard musical singing, and all the more a mourner for father or mother or a wife for her husband — even though she serves there they should refrain from hearing singers) — and see Yoreh De'ah end siman 391 regarding nail cutting for immersion on Tisha B'Av and Shach there and Noda B'Yehuda there and what Magen Avraham wrote that one who brings in and takes out the infant is not included in this — and all the more other honorees who pass the infant from one to another; and also those who place on Elijah's chair and those who place on the sandek's knees or hold the infant at "asher kidshanu," etc.; and somewhat it would appear lenient to seat them on the sandek's knees per custom that the sandek's feet are like the altar (and what is written Yoreh De'ah siman 265 that it is like offering incense — see in responsum of Noda B'Yehuda siman 26 who wrote to resolve that it should not be difficult on Maharil who does not give the sandek two sons from this reason lest there be grievance against the sandek and not because of the mohel — for if the sandek's feet are like the altar offering incense, then the mohel is like one offering incense, etc. — see there; and apparently it is difficult on this itself that the sandek is like the altar — how from Torah should he not change, for we do not find such except regarding the offerer and we learn from "they shall place incense," etc.; nevertheless there is support from what is written in Yoma 43: R' Yehuda said there was no lottery for the coal pan, etc. — it is taught since it is uncommon and they fixed a lottery for it separately, etc. — if so even the coal pan upon which they place incense is also by lottery like one who offers incense) — but I have not seen practiced thus; and in Prague the custom is the father of the son places the infant on the sandek's feet — a fine custom; and the father's father and the mother's father also practice to be lenient and wear Shabbat garments — they and their wives; and as I recall they practice to be lenient also for other relatives who would ordinarily wear Shabbat garments; requires study on this. And it appears the pain is for the father of the son and the mother when close relatives do not share in their joy by wearing whites; and for other relatives one should be stringent; and see Machatzit HaShekel in name of Zera Emet that baal brit is forbidden to wear new garments this week; and women who carry the infant to synagogue and pass the infant to men's synagogue called kvater — they also wear Shabbat garments since they have no other mitzvah in milah except this alone; and see in Pri Megadim:
