Seif 3, the week, etc.; even. This is the conclusion of the Gemara there.
And even if there is not, etc. There the Gemara says: always, etc.; and when he has no other garment, etc.
And likewise laundered ones, etc. There it says that a mourner may not wear them, implying even in such a case.
Whether, etc. Meaning, one should not say that only visible garments are forbidden. The discussion of pressing permits only items without the mark of pressing; otherwise even hidden items would be forbidden. From the fact that the launderers of the academy ceased work, we learn that everything is forbidden.
And even towels, etc. Meaning, although on Chol HaMoed these are permitted, here they are forbidden, as by a mourner, who is forbidden laundering. The Tosefta in Moed Katan teaches that those permitted to cut hair or launder on the festival may do so during the thirty days of mourning, and this is limited to specific cases; see Yoreh Deah siman 389, seif 1.
But our laundering is permitted. This follows from what is stated that our pressing, etc.; the practical difference is only for our laundering. See Rashi there and Toras HaAdam in the name of the Geonim.
But, etc., and linen garments, etc. This is stated there.
But it is forbidden, etc. Even our laundering of linen garments is forbidden. Since linen has no issue of pressing and is only laundering, yet wearing it is forbidden, laundering itself is forbidden. One cannot distinguish between strong and light laundering. So write Ramban explicitly, the Tur, and the Beit Yosef.
And the custom is, etc. The stringency is only regarding laundering to put away, but wearing is forbidden according to the law. The words of the Shulchan Aruch are difficult, for wearing is forbidden according to all opinions; their view is only that with other species, putting them away is forbidden according to the law.
That is not long, etc. As written in the fifteenth chapter of Shabbos and the first chapter of Yevamos; see Tosafot there and elsewhere.
And there is another, etc. So wrote the Nimukei Yosef in the third chapter of Moed Katan. The primary explanation, however, is that pressing is a hardening or finishing process, as in Kesubos; so wrote the Gaon.
And we practice, etc. See what is written in seif 7.
If not, etc. Just as laundering was permitted for the honor of Shabbos, as above.
But on Tisha B'Av, etc. As stated there, Abaye cursed this practice. The statement that our Yom Tov, etc., applies specifically to postponed Tisha B'Av. See above siman 559.
And likewise for the honor, etc. As above.
And it is forbidden to give, etc. Since the Gemara said only laundering and wearing, even through a non-Jew it is forbidden because of wearing. What is stated regarding putting away is also forbidden through a non-Jew.
But before, etc. As stated at the beginning of the second chapter of Moed Katan regarding Chol HaMoed.
