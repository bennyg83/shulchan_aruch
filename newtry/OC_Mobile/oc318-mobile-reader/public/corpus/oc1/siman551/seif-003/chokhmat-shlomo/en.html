Se'if 3. During the week in which Tisha B'Av falls they are forbidden to cut hair, etc. Marginal note: so it is in the Gemara, Ta'anis folio 30: Rava said the halacha is like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and Rava said the halacha is like Rabbi Meir, and both are toward leniency; see there. And behold, at first glance the matter is puzzling to me: since we say there above that all of them derive from one verse, the one who holds from Rosh Chodesh until the fast learns from "her festival," and the one who holds the entire month learns from "her month," and the one who holds the entire week learns from "her Shabbos," if so it is difficult regarding Rava, who holds that it is forbidden only during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls and until the fast: according to whom does he expound? If he expounds "her Shabbos," the entire week would be forbidden; and if he expounded "her festival," it would be forbidden from Rosh Chodesh; so from where does he have [license] to be lenient in both? And it appears to explain: for one must understand, according to the one who holds from "her festival," and Rashi explained that Rosh Chodesh is called a mo'ed, if so it is fine that the prohibition begins from Rosh Chodesh, but from where do we know that the prohibition ends at Tisha B'Av; perhaps [it lasts] the entire month. And it appears that his intent is: since it is explicit in the poskim, and below in the Shulchan Aruch, that Tisha B'Av is called a mo'ed, and if so, just as regarding the days of Pesach and the days of the festival all the intervening days are called by the language of "festival," even though there is only one day at the beginning and one day at the end, nevertheless all the days are called by the language of "festival" since its beginning and its end are Yom Tov; so too here regarding these nine days from Rosh Chodesh until the fast, since at their beginning there is Rosh Chodesh, which is called a mo'ed, and at their end it is called a mo'ed, therefore all the days are called by the language of "festival," and therefore he learns from "her festival" that all the days called by the language of "festival" are forbidden, namely from Rosh Chodesh until the fast. But Rava holds that both [derivations] are toward leniency; therefore the language "from her festival" serves to teach that it is forbidden only until the festival, namely Tisha B'Av, which is called a mo'ed; and the language "her Shabbos" serves to teach that it is forbidden not from Rosh Chodesh, but only that week; and these are two teachings toward leniency. So it appears to my humble opinion to be correct; and examine carefully.
