Of joy. This also refers to business dealings, such as wedding needs. Other business may be conducted as usual. Maharil wrote that brokers should not carry silver utensils around the town to broker them, for this causes joy to those who see them. Where the practice is to forbid all business dealings, release from the custom is needed; but where the practice is merely to reduce all business dealings, release does not help, since some authorities rule this way and their fathers accepted that opinion.
And for the need of a mitzvah. For example, one who has no wife and children may build a wedding house. The reason the practice is not to marry at all is that it is not considered a favorable sign. Therefore the custom is to make new garments for marriage needs, but one who has fulfilled the mitzvah of having children is forbidden, as below in seif 7.
And one does not marry. Even without a meal.
Lest another precede him. For this reason it is permitted to arrange a match. Nevertheless, it appears that one may not make a meal, even without dancing and circles, for this is unlike a bris meal, which has no joy at all, whereas here there is joy. It appears that even on Shabbos this is forbidden, for the Gemara states that from Rosh Chodesh until the fast it is forbidden, and that period necessarily includes Shabbos. However, the customary sweets eaten when the conditions are written are not considered a meal. It appears that dancing and circles are forbidden from the seventeenth of Tammuz and onward.
