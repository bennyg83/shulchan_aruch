(11) They reduce buying and selling: there are among the poskim who hold that that means he should not engage in buying and selling of joy, for example to buy silver vessels or needs of a chuppah and the like, but ordinary buying and selling one need not reduce at all; and there are who hold that one must reduce all buying and selling at this time and occupy himself only for his livelihood; and so it is implied in siman 554 at the end of se'if 22, that one must reduce all buying and selling. [And only if it is a fair, one may say that it is something that would be lost, and it is permitted in every case if he finds then more cheaply.] And in our time they practiced to be lenient in all of this because everything is now considered for our livelihood.
(12) And in a building of joy: and the same applies to any building that he does not need for his dwelling, and he only does so for mere comfort: it is forbidden; and if he hired a non-Jew before Rosh Chodesh under contract, it is permitted in every case, and even on Tisha B'Av itself, for a non-Jew acts for his own interest; and the same applies if he fixed a contract price with the non-Jew to paint his house: it is permitted to paint for this reason. And nevertheless, if he can appease him with a small matter that he wait until after Tisha B'Av, that is fine. And on Yom Kippur it is permitted, for it is a communal mitzvah.
(13) Leaning to fall: and even if it is in a manner that there is no danger in it [for example the wall stands in a courtyard, and it is possible to close the courtyard and no person will enter there], only that there is concern for monetary loss, for example that through its falling the wall will be broken and ruined more: nevertheless it is permitted to build even though it is a building of joy, for behold he is not doing it for joy, but only because of loss.
(14) And for the need of a mitzvah, etc.: for example, that he has no wife and children and he has no wedding house: it is permitted to build a wedding house, for by law even marriage is permitted in this; and that they are not accustomed to marry a wife at all is because the matter is not a good omen; and therefore it is permitted to make a wedding house or to make new garments for the need of a marriage that will be after Tisha B'Av; but one who has fulfilled peru u'revu: all of this is forbidden.
(15) And they do not marry: that is, even without a meal, for in marriage alone there is joy; and to return his divorced wife from nissuin, it is possible that it is permitted.
(16) But to betroth, etc.: for betrothal without a meal there is not so much joy, and all the more so it is permitted to bind with writing conditions; and nevertheless a meal is forbidden to make even in this manner, and even without dancing and circles, and even on Shabbos it is forbidden to make a meal for this. However, what they practice to eat kinds of preserves at the time of writing the conditions is not called a meal. And it is forbidden to make dancing and circles from the seventeenth of Tammuz onward, even without betrothal.
(17) Lest another precede him: that is to say, even though betrothal also has some joy, and we should have forbidden at least on Tisha B'Av: to this he said that it is permitted for the reason lest another precede him.
(18) And they practice to be stringent that they do not marry, etc.: that is, even one who has not fulfilled peru u'revu.
(19) That they do not marry, etc.: but regarding betrothal they do not practice to be stringent, and even a betrothal meal is permitted to make.
