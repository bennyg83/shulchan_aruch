Joy. This also refers to commerce, such as matters needed for a wedding. Other commerce is permitted in the ordinary manner. Beit Yosef; Bach. Maharil writes that brokers should not carry silver utensils through the city for brokerage, because this brings joy to those who see them. In a place where the custom is to prohibit all commerce, the custom requires annulment; see Yoreh Deah 214. In a place where the custom is merely to reduce all commerce, annulment does not help, because some poskim rule this way and their ancestors accepted that opinion. Siman 554, at the end of seif 22, also implies that all commerce should be reduced; see there. Beit Yosef cites Ran that any construction not needed except for mere expansion is forbidden. If one contracted with a non-Jew to paint his house, the non-Jew may paint it; but if he can appease him with a small payment to wait until after Tishah B'Av, this is praiseworthy. Maharil. It appears to me that construction of a synagogue is permitted, because it is a communal mitzvah. Magen Avraham.
Leaning. Even where there is no danger but there is concern for financial loss, construction is permitted. Only construction for expansion is forbidden. Taz.
A mitzvah. For example, one who has no wife and children may build a wedding house. Ran. The reason the custom is nevertheless not to marry at all is that it is considered an unfavorable sign. Beit Yosef. See Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 2, siman 35. Therefore, the custom is to make new garments for a wedding. One who has already fulfilled the mitzvah of procreation may not do so; see seif 7. Magen Avraham.
Women. Even without a meal.
To betroth. For this reason, making a shidduch is permitted. Nevertheless, it appears to me that making a meal is forbidden, even without dancing and music. It is unlike a circumcision meal, which contains no joy at all, whereas this does. It appears to me that it is forbidden even on Shabbos, for the Gemara teaches that from Rosh Chodesh until the fast it is forbidden, and this period necessarily includes Shabbos. Eating confections when the tena'im are written is not considered a meal. It appears to me that dancing and music are forbidden from the seventeenth of Tammuz onward. Magen Avraham.
