Seif 10: In the Hagahah, at a seudas mitzvah such as circumcision, pidyon ha-ben, siyum on a tractate, etc. NB: See Taz comment 12 what he wrote on the meal of circumcision: it appears to me complete permission, for circumcision overrides Shabbat and Shabbat overrides building the Temple, etc. His words are wondrous, for in the Gemara Moed Katan they derive the a fortiori: if the Mishkan overrides Shabbat, all the more so the Temple; and they challenge — if so why were they afraid — and answer perhaps the need of the Most High differs from the need of ordinary people, etc. — if so it is proven that ordinary needs differ from lofty needs; if so it is explicit opposite the Taz's words that this is not truly an a fortiori legal inference; and even though afterward their sin was forgiven, it is no proof it was not a sin — one can say it was a sin, only through repenting and doing teshuvah they are forgiven, as Chazal said: whoever transgresses and is ashamed immediately is forgiven. The Taz's words require further study — examine.
