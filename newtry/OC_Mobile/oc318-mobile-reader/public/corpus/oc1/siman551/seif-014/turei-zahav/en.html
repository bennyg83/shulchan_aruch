Mora Uchla zt"l wrote this is specifically in the week in which 9 Av falls; but before that it is permitted within the bounds of mourning — meaning one who completed his thirty days in those days when laundering and haircut are permitted. Tosafos wrote at the end of Taanis daf 29: although they sent in the name of R' Yehuda that linen garments may be laundered on the festival, nevertheless our teacher Rashi was stringent for us to give our sheets to launder in the week that had 9 Av before the fast; but where 9 Av falls on Thursday on Shabbat, from midday onward it is permitted to launder and haircut because of honor of Shabbat, for one cannot wait until erev Shabbat because of Shabbat trouble, end of his words. Beit Yosef challenged — a wondrous matter to permit haircut and laundering on 9 Av itself; and further, it is explicitly taught: if it falls on Thursday, before it is forbidden, after it is permitted — it is impossible to hear that on the day itself it is permitted; and further regarding falling on erev Shabbat it teaches: if he did not launder on Thursday it is permitted from Mincha and upward; and it concludes Abaye objects; and all the more when it falls on Thursday it is certainly forbidden that day even after Mincha; and he answered that what Tosafos permit from midday upward is on Wednesday which is eve of 9 Av, for it is taught regarding falling on Thursday: before it is forbidden; and further one should be more stringent after midday than before midday; therefore one should not rely on this ruling, end of his words. And Mora Uchla zt"l agreed to this answer that he recalled Beit Yosef; and that which is taught "before it is forbidden" means not for honor of Shabbat, and he did not distinguish, for it explicitly mentions there, in the braita where laundering is permitted it is because of honor of Shabbat — if so, where forbidden it is forbidden even for honor of Shabbat. And in my humble opinion Tosafos' words as their plain sense are correct: on 9 Av itself after midday is permitted, for after midday belongs to the next day, as we say that after midday they slaughter and prepare needs of the meal as stated below, and so in many matters; and with this Beit Yosef's difficulties are resolved — why they said permitted after it and did not say explicitly that even on 9 Av itself after midday is permitted, for this depends on it: since permitted on the morrow, automatically permitted on the day before after midday when the stringency is not so strong upon it, and they were lenient entirely for honor of Shabbat. And what Beit Yosef wrote that when 9 Av falls on erev Shabbat Abaye objects to one who laundered on 9 Av itself — there is no difficulty: there he could on Thursday as stated in the braita there: when it falls on erev Shabbat it is permitted to launder on Thursday, and if he did not launder on Thursday it is permitted to launder on erev Shabbat from Mincha upward — Abaye objects; it appears Abaye's main objection is since he could launder on Thursday and did not; but when 9 Av falls on Thursday he cannot launder on Wednesday, for there is no permission at all even for honor of Shabbat except on Thursday as stated in the braita — then he has no sin in laundering for honor of Shabbat after midday because he could not before; and similar to this we say regarding shaving on the festival: whoever could shave before the festival is forbidden to shave on the festival, and if he could not he is permitted to shave on the festival — and likewise here exactly; if so this ruling is correct and reasonable, except that for us one should not be lenient, since we are stringent even on the tenth day as below — therefore it is obvious there is no leniency on 9 Av itself after midday. And Mora Uchla zt"l permitted one traveling on a distant road in the week in which 9 Av falls to launder linen garments for honor of Shabbat even for many Shabbosim that he needs, since after 9 Av he will not find opportunity to launder them.
