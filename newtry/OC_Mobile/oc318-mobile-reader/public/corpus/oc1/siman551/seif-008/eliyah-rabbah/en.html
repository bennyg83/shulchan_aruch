[8] From Rosh Chodesh onward, etc. Even if he tells the non-Jew to launder after Tishah B'Av. Magen Avraham. In my humble opinion, it may be possible to be lenient, comparable to the end of siman 543 regarding Chol HaMoed, where one may give work to a non-Jew to perform after the festival. See seif 8. This does not divert one's attention from mourning to the same degree. Moreover, Bach writes that the essential restriction on laundering through a non-Jew is only a custom. If one has only one shirt for Shabbos, it is certainly permitted to give it to a non-Jew even during the week in which Tishah B'Av occurs. Bach permits even more, allowing one departing on a distant journey to have a non-Jew launder garments for several Shabbosos during that week. Although Bach permits even a Jew to do so, I do not consider such a broad leniency proper, as appears from Raavan, page 83. See Lechem Mishneh, chapter 5, s.v. Taanis, where the words of Raavan appear to have escaped his notice. Magen Avraham's question in seif katan 19 from the cancellation of Rabbi's launderers is not difficult, because departing on a distant journey is uncommon, as Terumas HaDeshen explicitly writes in siman 152.
