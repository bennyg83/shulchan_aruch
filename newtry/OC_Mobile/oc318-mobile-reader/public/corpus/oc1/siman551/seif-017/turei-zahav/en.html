And likewise fruit he will not find, etc. It appears from this reason one may permit all days between the straits lest a person die before fulfilling this mitzvah; I heard this reasoning in name of Rav Yosef Katz of Krakow; there is support from what Tosafot brought chapter Eizehu Mekoman Beit Yosef siman 549: permitted l'chatchila anything related to court on chol hamoed, for it is great loss lest the giver or witnesses or court die, etc.; if for bodily needs they were lenient with this reasoning for needs of Heaven to bless on a Torah scroll lest he hurry to fulfill — and in Sefer Chasidim siman 843 permitted Shehecheyanu on Shabbat. Reason for avoiding meat and wine from 17 Tammuz until 9 Av that individuals and some fast completely has three reasons: first Daniel fasted this measure; second from 17 when the city was breached until 9 when the Temple was destroyed one grief; third Temple sacrifice and wine libation ceased 17 Tammuz; R' Shlomo responsum 92 wrote for all three bathing prohibition does not apply; so below bathing on erev Shabbat and Rosh Chodesh in name of Mateh Moshe; per these reasons one should be stringent even on Shabboses between the straits, except Shabbat honor they did not be stringent; it appears the stringent should not say they fear Rashbag's view — Heaven forbid disagreeing with Rava — rather be stringent beyond the letter for themselves silently. One who vows meat and wine these three weeks — even without stipulation for Shabbat permission — Shabbat is not included, general rule Yoreh De'ah 218: in vows we follow the neder's intent; here he intended to be among ascetics practicing this between the straits; on Shabbat they do not practice this stringency — certainly the neder's intent likewise; only vows to strengthen himself lest he regret if he does not vow; although I wrote Yoreh De'ah 220: "I will not eat meat three weeks" includes Shabboses — unlike here he specified these between the straits, certainly intent as practiced between the straits for destruction as above, not applicable on Shabbat where no between-the-straits stringency; Mateh Moshe wrote: even one practicing general prohibition between the straits does not practice on Shabbat; one who vows explicitly — Shabbat included; see responsum Chacham Tzvi 116 to distinguish short and long time; Ya'arot Devash third derashah part 2 until Shabbat is short — see there; since on Shabbat more reason to bless than weekday, they permitted Shabbat and forbade weekday — correct, since until Shabbat no death concern and he will bless then; see Magen Avraham 568 s.k. 13 and Machatzit HaShekel there.
