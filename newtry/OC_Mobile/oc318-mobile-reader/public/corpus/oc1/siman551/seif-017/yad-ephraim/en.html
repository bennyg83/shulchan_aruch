On Taz, end of seif katan 5. The apparent explanation of the phrase 'old garments, etc.' is difficult in my eyes. He writes that it refers to linen garments and means that if they have been laundered, pressing them is forbidden. According to him, Ramban first discusses the prohibition of pressing them with the ancient form of pressing, and afterward writes that even our form of pressing is forbidden. This is surprising, for Ramban never first mentions such a ruling concerning linen garments that would justify the later expression 'therefore.' Moreover, he later writes: 'Nevertheless, our laundering is permitted, and linen garments are permitted everywhere even with their pressing.' Thus, the phrase 'our laundering' must refer to woolen garments, where only our laundering is permitted, while 'linen garments everywhere' means even with their laundering. The earlier passage therefore did not discuss linen garments at all. The phrase 'all linen garments,' meaning both white and colored, also implies that linen garments were not previously discussed. In my humble opinion, the correct explanation is as Bach writes, that it refers to woolen garments. The words 'and likewise old laundered garments' mean that not only is wearing old laundered garments forbidden, but even laundering them to leave until after the week is forbidden when using their laundering process on woolen garments. Although Ramban later states separately that wearing is forbidden, this teaches additionally that even linen garments may not be worn, whereas the law of woolen garments was already obvious. Bach's statement that their laundering is more stringent than our pressing, and even than their pressing regarding old garments not emerging from the press, is surprising. The lesser should not become more stringent than the greater, since their pressing is plainly more severe. Furthermore, the Gemara explicitly states that our pressing is like their laundering. One might answer that emerging from the press applies only to pressing, but if so Bach should have explained and proved this. On the contrary, Ramban in Toras HaAdam, page 61b, concerning a mourner, writes that it seems proper to prohibit old laundered garments emerging from the launderer, showing that this concept applies also to laundering. It therefore appears that Ramban means: old laundered garments are all forbidden, even when intended to be left until after the week, and our pressing is also forbidden for both new and old garments emerging from the press. The phrase 'emerging from the press' also applies where they are left until after the week. Were I not hesitant, I would suggest a scribal error and emend the words to 'old pressed garments,' meaning those emerging from the press. The language does not readily support this, however, so the explanation given above is preferable.
