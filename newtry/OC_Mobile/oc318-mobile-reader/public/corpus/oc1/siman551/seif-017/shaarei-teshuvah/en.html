To launder, see Baer Heitev. See Yad Ephraim on the words of the Magen Avraham here. See Birkei Yosef in the name of Nechpeh BaKesef, that even according to what Pri Chadash wrote in siman 468, that instructing a non-Jew is permitted in other rabbinic prohibitions, because of the severity of Tishah BeAv the custom is to prohibit even telling a laundress that she should take the garments and launder them. See Machazik Berachah in the name of Zera Emet, regarding giving linen garments to a laundress on erev Rosh Chodesh so that she will soak them that day in water and, after Av enters, finish whitening them with water, ash, and soap in the usual way, all in a Jew's house. Although according to the strict law one may permit it, nevertheless it is proper to be careful even in this, since it is in a Jew's house and there is an issue of marit ayin; see there. See below, seif katan 35.
(*) (Shulchan Aruch, seif 4:) After the fast. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 16, regarding those who customarily refrain from eating meat and drinking wine until Shabbat Nachamu. The Bach wrote that they require release from a vow, and without release even haircutting and laundering are prohibited until Thursday. Baer Heitev omitted this. See there in Magen Avraham, that if they customarily refrained from meat and wine but did customarily cut hair and launder, they are permitted, for we do not impose on them more than they accepted. The Magen Avraham also wrote that for those who keep the prohibition, even if the fifteenth of Av falls on Friday, they are prohibited in meat and wine. See Yad Ephraim, what I wrote there on the Magen Avraham, where I brought the words of Noda BiYehudah, Mahadura Tinyana, siman 105; see there. See Taz, seif katan 10, and Yad Ephraim there.
