To launder, see Baer Heitev; and see in Yad Ephraim on the words of Magen Avraham on this; and see in Birkei Yosef in the name of Nechpeh BaKesef, that even according to what is written in Peri Chadash, siman 468, that telling a non-Jew regarding other rabbinic prohibitions is permitted, because of the stringency of Tisha B'Av they practiced to forbid even telling a laundress that she should take the garments and launder them; and see in Machtzik Beracha in the name of Zera Emes, that to give a laundress linen garments on Erev Rosh Chodesh so that she soak them that very day in water, and after Av begins she finish whitening them with water and ash and boris as is the custom, and all in a Jewish house: even though by law one should permit, nevertheless it is proper to be careful also in this, since it is in a Jewish house and there is [concern] of appearance; see there; and see below, s.k. 35.
(*) (Shulchan Aruch, se'if 4) After the fast. See Magen Avraham, s.k. 16, regarding those who are accustomed not to eat meat and wine until Shabbos Nachamu: the Bach wrote that they need annulment [of the custom], and without annulment even cutting hair and laundering are forbidden until Thursday; and Baer Heitev omitted this; and see there in Magen Avraham, that if they practiced not to eat meat and wine, but they practiced to cut hair and launder, it is permitted for them, for one should not obligate them more than what they practiced; and Magen Avraham also wrote: and for those who practice a prohibition, even if the fifteenth of Av falls on that day, they are forbidden in meat and wine; and see in Yad Ephraim what he wrote on this regarding Magen Avraham, and I brought there the words of Noda BiYehuda, Mahadura Tinyana, siman 105; see there; and see in the Taz, s.k. 10, and in Yad Ephraim there.
