Shehecheyanu. On the intervening Shabbosos it is permitted to recite Shehecheyanu. Mateh Moshe and Levush. The writings of the Arizal prohibit it even on Shabbos. It appears to me that if the seventeenth of Tammuz falls on Shabbos and the fast is postponed, all authorities permit it. Magen Avraham.
That it should not. According to those who permit it on Shabbos, one must explain that the item will not be available on Shabbos, and if purchased during the week and kept for Shabbos it will spoil. Taz writes that for this reason it appears permitted on every day of Bein HaMetzarim, lest the person die before fulfilling this mitzvah. I heard this reasoning in the name of Mahari of Krakow; see there. Bach writes that those who customarily refrain from meat after Tishah B'Av require annulment. Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah writes that smoking tobacco on a private fast should not be prohibited, but on a communal fast it is forbidden, and certainly one should not be lenient on the four fasts. One who smokes on Tishah B'Av is placed under a ban, and one who cuts his hair after midday is fined. See Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah and Responsa Darkei Noam, siman 9. Regarding fragrance on Tishah B'Av or Yom Kippur, such as inhaling tobacco powder through the nostrils, Shiyarei Kenesses HaGedolah cites Rabbi Menachem de Lonzano that no prohibition against fragrance is found on Tishah B'Av or Yom Kippur. Rabbi Aharon Halevi, in Gan HaMelech, siman 145, writes that on Tishah B'Av it should be forbidden because of pleasure, whereas on Yom Kippur there is a mitzvah to honor it; see there and what Yad Aharon writes concerning it. Leket HaKemach is lenient and concludes: On Tishah B'Av I inhale plain tobacco powder without pleasant fragrance, while on Yom Kippur it should be prohibited publicly in the synagogue, because many unlearned people offer it to one another, which is forbidden even during the rest of the year; see there.
