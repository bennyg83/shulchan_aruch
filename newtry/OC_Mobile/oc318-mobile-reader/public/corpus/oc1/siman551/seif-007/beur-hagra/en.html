Seif 7, there are those who say, etc. This follows all the more so from laundering, which is forbidden even to put away.
And one should be stringent, etc. Although laundering is forbidden only during that week, and according to the Shulchan Aruch there is not even a custom otherwise, here there is reason to forbid from the Yerushalmi about not preparing warp threads from Av onward, because the foundation stone ceased. From this the custom spread, as in the gloss, to forbid laundering and other matters from Rosh Chodesh.
And the same applies, etc. As with laundering, where even putting them away is forbidden.
And likewise a craftsman, etc. As above in seif 5.
And the custom is, etc.; but, etc.; and likewise, etc. The Terumas HaDeshen permitted Jewish women to launder garments of non-Jews, because the prohibition is due to joy, which does not apply to garments of non-Jews; only appearance is a concern when it is not known that they are non-Jewish. The Beit Yosef disagreed based on the Yerushalmi. The Darkei Moshe allowed relying on the lenient view for preparing garments, but this is not enough to reject the words of the Gaon and the Tur, who derived a kal vachomer from warp threads. Moreover it is forbidden because of laundering, as above. Therefore, even without pay it is forbidden, and the custom to be lenient is not sufficient where the laundering rationale applies.
