And new shoes. The same applies that it is forbidden to wear them.
And one should be stringent. Regarding wearing, the Beit Yosef is lenient. The reason for forbidding making garments is that they practiced not to make warp threads, because the foundation stone ceased; all garment preparation is included, for the warp is the beginning of the work. It is implied that making for others or to sell in the market is also forbidden, but spinning threads is permitted, since it is not included. It appears that making wooden woven frames called crowns is permitted, for they are not a garment.
And the custom is to be lenient in this. This implies that even for a Jew the practice is to be lenient, and it refers to where he gave it before Rosh Chodesh. After Rosh Chodesh it is certainly forbidden to give it.
To non-Jewish craftsmen. Although with laundering it is forbidden, that is because laundering is explicitly mentioned in the Gemara. With new garments it is different, since they are not yet his and his name is not called upon them. The glosses of Minhagim write that even on Tisha B'Av itself it is permitted to have garments made by a non-Jew for marriage needs.
