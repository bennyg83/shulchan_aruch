(S.k. 7) Of joy. It also refers to buying and selling, etc. Rav Beis Yosef wrote thus: since we see that the world is not careful to reduce buying and selling, therefore he holds like the "some say" that Tosafos brought, who explain that buying and selling as well is forbidden only that of joy; but the Taz in s.k. 1 rejected this, for behold we see that even regarding buying and selling of joy, namely needs of a chuppah, they do not refrain; and further, that Tosafos wrote on that interpretation that it is not clear to him, and this is their language: "that only the meal itself is forbidden," end of their words; that is to say: if it were that buying and selling is forbidden only that of joy, then why do we forbid needs of a chuppah, for that too is not buying and selling of joy, only a wedding meal; therefore the Taz wrote that by law in truth all increase of buying and selling is forbidden except for his livelihood. But for us, who have upon us the tax of the king and the officers, everything enters within "for his livelihood"; and for this reason, even though by the law of the Gemara it is forbidden to lend to a non-Jew with interest except for his living needs, for we are concerned lest he learn from his deeds, and nevertheless Tosafos wrote that in this time everything is permitted for the aforementioned reason.
Requires annulment. So wrote Rav Beis Yosef; see there.
See Yoreh De'ah, siman 214.
That is to say, it is explained that a stringency they practiced because of a mistake does not need annulment. But something they know is permitted and they were stringent as a fence needs annulment. And in the Beis Yosef it implies that they practice thus because of a mistake, for they explain that just as Tosafos wrote "they reduce joy," meaning that they do not rejoice at all, so too "they reduce buying and selling," meaning that they do not buy and sell at all; and he wrote that this explanation is not true; if so their custom is a mistake and would not have needed annulment; and even so Rav Beis Yosef wrote that they need annulment. One must say: even though Rav Beis Yosef wrote to teach merit for them, that they explain that by law all buying and selling is forbidden by analogy to joy, nevertheless one may also say that they too knew the truth that the meaning is not thus, only that they were stringent upon themselves to forbid upon themselves all buying and selling; therefore out of doubt it needs annulment; unless we say that Rav Beis Yosef holds like the "one who says" that he brought there in Yoreh De'ah, that a mistaken custom needs annulment. And if so, according to what he brought there in Yoreh De'ah as the first view, which was always the main one for Rav Beis Yosef, as is known his way, that a mistaken custom does not need annulment, and the Rama wrote there that such is the custom, annulment would not have been needed here; and that is forced.
In all buying and selling. Except for his livelihood, as above.
