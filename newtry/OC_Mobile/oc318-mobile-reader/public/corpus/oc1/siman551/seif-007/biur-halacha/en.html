And they practiced to be lenient in this. And in Beur HaGra he casts doubt on this: that even though one should not learn fixing garments from that of the Yerushalmi as Darkei Moshe wrote, nevertheless for the reason that it at least resembles laundry one should not be lenient; and likewise what he wrote, "but if it is known and publicized," etc.: in all of this there is room to cast doubt for the reason of laundry; and likewise regarding what he wrote, "and so they practiced to give to non-Jewish craftsmen," etc.: on all of it the Gra marked, and it implies that his view is to be stringent in this; and it appears to me obvious that one should be stringent only during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, and not earlier, for regarding laundry itself it is only a custom, and since they practiced to be lenient in this, then one should not be stringent.
Of the non-Jew is permitted. It implies that if the work is of a Jew, even though it is known and publicized that he is not doing it for himself, and certainly there is no joy in it for the craftsman, for example to sell in the market after Tisha B'Av, it is forbidden; and so wrote the Magen Avraham; and one must say that the reason for the prohibition is that they will think that a Jew gave it to him to fix after Rosh Chodesh.
