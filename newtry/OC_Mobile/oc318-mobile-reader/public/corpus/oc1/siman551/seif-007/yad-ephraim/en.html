Ibid. 'Since all construction is forbidden, consequently this is also forbidden.' This requires study. Why must this follow? One may say that the Babylonian Talmud does not disagree with the Yerushalmi on this point. Just as the Yerushalmi permits even joyful construction where the structure is leaning toward collapse and there is financial loss, so too according to our Talmud all construction is forbidden only where there is no loss, but is permitted where it is leaning toward collapse. In truth, it appears to me that the authorities who write that our Talmud implies a prohibition on all construction understand that the Yerushalmi does not disagree. Rather, every construction intended merely for expansion is called joyful construction, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Ran. The first opinion must hold that since only joyful construction is forbidden, financial loss permits it, because the construction is then undertaken not for joy but to prevent loss. According to the other opinion, which prohibits construction even when not undertaken for joy, financial loss does not permit it where no danger is involved.
