And shoes. It is likewise forbidden to wear them. Agudah; Hagahos Maimoniyos.
From Rosh Chodesh. Regarding wearing, the Mechaber in Beit Yosef is lenient. The reason for prohibiting their manufacture, as stated in seif 8, is the custom not to set up the warp. This means not to make woolen warp threads, because the Foundation Stone ceased on Tishah B'Av, and all preparation of garments is included, since setting the warp is the beginning of the work. Beit Yosef. Darkei Moshe writes that for this reason it is forbidden even when making them for others. Terumas HaDeshen implies that it is forbidden even when making them for sale in the marketplace. Spinning thread, however, is permitted, since it is not included in this. Magen Avraham writes that one may make items called kronin that are woven from wood, since they are not garments.
To be lenient. This implies that the custom is to be lenient even for a Jew. The case is where he gave them to the worker before Rosh Chodesh, but after Rosh Chodesh it is certainly forbidden to give them. Magen Avraham.
The fast. Laundering must therefore be forbidden, as stated at the end of seif 3, because laundering is expressly mentioned in the Gemara as prohibited, unlike new garments. The reason is that the garments are not yet his and his name is not yet associated with them. Hagahos Maimoniyos writes that even on Tishah B'Av itself, garments needed for a wedding may be made through a non-Jew. Magen Avraham. It appears to me that this applies specifically after midday.
