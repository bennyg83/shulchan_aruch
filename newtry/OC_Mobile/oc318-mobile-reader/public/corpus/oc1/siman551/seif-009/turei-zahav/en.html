And permitted in vinegar of wine. This prohibition was like a vow, as Beit Yosef in name of an Ashkenazic responsum: one who vowed from wine is permitted vinegar of wine, as Yoreh De'ah siman 211 seif 11. At first glance it appears: since there it says one who vowed from wine and that wine became vinegar is forbidden in it — likewise here forbidden is vinegar made from wine at that time. But it does not appear so: there the wine itself was forbidden from vow prohibition resting on it; not so here, the prohibition is only due to joy — in vinegar there is no joy; the prohibition itself turned permitted. Also for meat the Talmud proves in Tur in the later siman that it is permitted after three days, and it is exactly like vinegar here; proof here it is permitted in meat stew as stated afterward; whereas one who vowed from meat and wine, certainly also stew that received taste is forbidden, Yoreh De'ah siman 217. Rather certainly as stated: here similar to one who vowed from meat and wine without specification — permitted in stew with wine taste; all the more permitted here wine mixed in stew even with wine taste — so appears to me plainly. And so in Kol Bo Beit Yosef brings this siman for the reason I wrote: clear in these days permitted meat gravy like vow from meat, Yoreh De'ah siman 217.
