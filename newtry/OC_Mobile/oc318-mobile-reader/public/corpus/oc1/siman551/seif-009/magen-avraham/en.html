Not to eat meat. It appears that once the community has prayed Maariv on Motzaei Shabbos, even if the individual has not yet prayed, meat and wine become forbidden. The same applies to Rosh Chodesh according to our practice. Some refrain already after sunset.
And vinegar is permitted. Since this is only a custom, anything people refrain from drinking because of its sourness is called vinegar, following common speech. One who explicitly accepted not to eat meat from the seventeenth of Tammuz until Tisha B'Av may nevertheless be permitted on Shabbos if he merely accepted the custom and did not use the language of a vow; a mitzvah meal is also permitted.
And they put away the knife. See Yoreh Deah siman 117 regarding whether one may kill animals by nailing. The custom is not to slaughter until the tenth of Av. If Tisha B'Av falls on Thursday, one may slaughter after midday in honor of Shabbos. In places where non-Jews do not buy meat on Friday or Shabbos, slaughtering may be done on Wednesday to avoid loss if the animal is found tereifah. One who cannot eat dairy foods may eat poultry, since birds were not offered as the regular joyous meat offering and joy is mainly with animal meat.
