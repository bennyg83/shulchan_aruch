(S.k. 13) On Tisha B'Av, etc. But in Maginei Eretz Beis Yosef, siman 5, it implies, etc.: Maginei Eretz Beis Yosef also permits bathing for the need of whitening through a slight change. For Rav Beis Yosef wrote that in Or Zarua he permitted to immerse on the night of Tisha B'Av, and likewise during the seven days of mourning. And even to bathe in order to whiten, Or Zarua wrote that it is permitted, only that she changes the bathing a bit; but a white undershirt she should certainly wear. And she should spread a white sheet so that she not come to a doubt; end of his words. And the Rama in Yoreh De'ah, siman 381: for us, who hold that immersion is forbidden, all the more so bathing for the need of whitening. And Maginei Eretz Beis Yosef disagreed with this: since the bathing is not for pleasure but for the need of a mitzvah, why should it be forbidden? Granted that we forbid immersion not like Or Zarua: that is because Or Zarua is consistent with his reason that immersion in its time is a mitzvah; but we, who hold that it is not a mitzvah, for what need should she immerse? For to her husband she is forbidden, for Tisha B'Av, and likewise a mourner, is forbidden in marital relations, and likewise on Yom Kippur. But bathing for whitening, since it is not for pleasure, why should she enter a doubt; and therefore it is permitted; end of his words. And the Taz in Yoreh De'ah, siman 381, brought his words and concluded, and this is his language: and nevertheless for practical law it appears to me that she should not bathe for wearing the whitening, rather she should wash her lower face and wear a clean undershirt, not actually white, etc.; so it appears to me clear and proper.
And one who is stringent in this to postpone wearing the whitening altogether is among the astonishing ones; end of his words; regarding mourning; and the same applies here regarding Tisha B'Av.
