Haircutting. See the Taz; and see Magen Avraham, letter 11, who permits for the need of a mitzvah; see there. What the Mechaber wrote regarding a beard: whatever impedes eating is permitted; see the Taz here and in Yoreh De'ah 390 in the Shach, letter 1. And regarding haircutting, a man and a woman are equal to forbid; see Yoreh De'ah 390 and in the Shach there; and a woman after seven, one may say like the Bach to permit shaving the temples; perhaps similarly here. A sandek or a mohel are forbidden to shave during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls within it, for by the law of the Gemara they are forbidden; one should not be lenient because of milah except what is from their custom, such as from Rosh Chodesh, and all the more so from the seventeenth of Tammuz for one who practices, or the week after it for one who practices; and as is written in siman 493 regarding Sefirah, see Magen Avraham there, letter 4; and see Eliyah Rabbah here, letter 28. And what is written in Yoreh De'ah 393: but a mohel is permitted to fix nails and to shave to shave them: one must say that means the nails; and in the Levush: to fix the nails and to cut them; but shaving the head is certainly something that by law has no permission for the sake of milah; and see Baer HaGolah there: certainly shaving the head is not a need of the milah, only for the honor of the milah; and the same applies to a mourner within thirty, if there is another mohel: Eliyah Rabbah forbids fixing his nails, for by law it is forbidden; and he brought what is written. In Yoreh De'ah 361, in [se'if] 15: all that is from custom is permitted; behold by law it is forbidden; and behold one may say that [the prohibition] is only of pleasure and beautification; but cutting his nails for the need of milah, one may say that specifically within seven he forbids there if there is another mohel, not because of his nails, only that there is some joy for him; and this requires further study.
