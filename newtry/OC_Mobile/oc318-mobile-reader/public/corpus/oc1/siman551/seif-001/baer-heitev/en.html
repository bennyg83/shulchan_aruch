With joy. Meaning, one does not rejoice at all, as Tosafot write at the beginning of Megillah 5b.
He should avoid it. Until Rosh Chodesh Elul. Rabbeinu Yerucham. This also appears from the Gemara. See Responsa Shevus Yaakov, part 3, siman 35.
And the baal bris. Magen Avraham writes that this appears to apply specifically to the one who holds the infant on his knees during the circumcision. The person who brings the infant in and carries him out is not included. This is certainly so when Tishah B'Av falls on Shabbos, when wearing white garments is forbidden by strict law, and permission was granted only to the one who holds the infant. See Yoreh Deah 391, seif katan 3, and what is written there. Since he did not also write that they may shave, this implies that shaving is forbidden. Eliyah Zuta writes that his grandfather ruled that a mourner within thirty days should not trim his nails when another mohel is available, and likewise should not shave within thirty days for a circumcision. Rama, however, writes in Yoreh Deah 393:3 that shaving is permitted. This requires study. See also what Baer HaGolah writes there concerning this.
The shirt. It is permitted even when Tishah B'Av falls on Shabbos. Levush. One who is stringent not to change it follows a foolish custom, since it is worn only because of perspiration. Mateh Moshe; Magen Avraham. Eliyah Zuta writes, however, that Sefer Tanya and Shibbolei HaLeket prohibit it because it is a private matter. Therefore, one should not protest against the Ashkenazic custom to prohibit it.
The paroches. The same applies to tablecloths and coverings. Maharil.
Garments. New garments, however, are forbidden. Found written; Maharil. Maharil made a spunholz for his son and wore a Shabbos coat in honor of his son the groom.
