(1) [Seif 1] When Av enters, we reduce joy. This means from Rosh Chodesh until the tenth of the month, inclusive, as written in Nechmad L'Mareh vol. 2 p. 207a, see there. One who is careful about this not to rejoice during these ten days will merit the ten things that God will renew in the future, as found in Midrash Rabbah Parshas Bo ch. 15. Yafeh L'Lev vol. 2 os 1, see there.
(2) Ibid. When Av enters, we reduce joy. This means that one does not rejoice at all, as written by Tosafos in the first chapter of Megillah 5b. Magen Avraham s.k. 1. Eliyah Rabbah os 1, Shulchan Gavoah os 2, Eishel Avraham os 1, Chayei Adam rule 133 os 9, Mishna Berurah os 1. The Mateh Yehuda wrote that "we reduce joy" means the types of joy that will be explained shortly in seif 2 regarding commerce, building, planting, and the like, the general principle is here and the specifics are there. He wrote that this is what the Ran wrote in the last chapter of Taanis, see there. However, from the words of the Ria"z there it is implied like the Magen Avraham who wrote: "When Av enters, we reduce joy, and the people reduce their business activities, etc.", implying that these are two separate matters: joy is one thing and commerce of joy is another, and both must be reduced; but the view of the Magen Avraham and the other later authorities is that one does not rejoice at all, as mentioned.
(3) Ibid. A Jew who has a lawsuit with a non-Jew should evade him, etc. This is the language of the Gemara in Taanis (29b), and it says there: "He should evade him during Av, for his mazal is bad." Tosafos there explained: because it says above that unfavorable matters are moved to an unfavorable day. This implies that one need only evade him until after Tisha B'Av, which are the days of misfortune. So too wrote Korban Nesanel in the last chapter of Taanis os 5. However, the Magen Avraham s.k. 2 wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yeruham: until Rosh Chodesh Elul, and wrote that this is implied by the Gemara. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah os 1, Eishel Avraham os 2, Machberes os 1. The Yafeh L'Lev vol. 2 os 3 wrote that one should only evade him until the fifteenth of Av, but from then on one should not evade, for the mazal is good for Jews, as we learned at the end of chapter 4 of Taanis: "There were no days as joyous for Israel as the fifteenth of Av, etc.," see there. The Chayei Adam rule 133 os 9 wrote that if possible one should evade the entire month, and in any case until after Tisha B'Av. So too wrote Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 7, Mishna Berurah os 2. The author of Ben Ish Chai Parshas Devarim os 1 wrote that this view, until after the tenth of Av, is the primary one, and one need not evade until Rosh Chodesh Elul, see there. Therefore it seems that if one cannot evade any longer, one may rely on this, as also written by the Chayei Adam and those who agree with him.
(4) He wrote in Amudei Chayyim, Amud Avodah siman 11 p. 101c, that it is not specifically from when Av enters, for the same applies from the seventeenth of Tammuz, see there. Yafeh L'Lev vol. 5 os 2. However, from the words of the authorities mentioned above this is not implied. The Eliyah Rabbah there wrote: it is possible that the Talmud implies that the same applies to the seventeenth of Tammuz and the tenth of Teves.
(5) Ibid. He should evade him, etc., this means from the molad [of Av], for his mazal is bad. But what is stated in seif 2 that we reduce in commerce of joy, that is specifically from Rosh Chodesh itself, and this is why the language differs. Eishel Avraham os 2, Tosfos Chayyim on Chayei Adam rule 133 os 20.
(6) In the Rama's gloss: The custom is that the mohel, the sandak, etc., specifically the one who holds the infant on his knees at the time of the circumcision, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 265 seif 11. But the one who brings the infant in and out is not included in this. Even though the women (i.e., the woman who carries the infant to the synagogue for the bris) also customarily wear Shabbos garments, that is because this is the primary form of their mitzvah. All the more so on Shabbos when Tisha B'Av falls, where it is by law forbidden to wear white garments, only the one holding the infant is permitted. Magen Avraham s.k. 3. Chayei Adam rule 133 os 21. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 15. However, the Eliyah Rabbah os 27 wrote regarding the words of the Magen Avraham that it is strained to distinguish between men and women, and furthermore the Beit Yosef in siman 559 wrote "the mohel and his sandakim, the baalei bris," and so wrote the Knesset HaGedolah on this siman; since he used the plural "sandakim," it implies that all of them are included, and it seems that this is the common practice. The Machatzis HaShekel s.k. 3 cited his words. This is also the view of the Beis Meir, that the one who brings in and takes out is also called a sandak, just like the one who sits and holds the infant, see there. See further on this in Sha'arei Teshuvah os 1.
(7) In the Rama's gloss: And the father of the child. And his mother. Chayei Adam ibid. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch ibid. Mishna Berurah os 4. The paternal grandfather and the maternal grandfather also have the practice to be lenient and wear Shabbos garments, they and their wives. Similarly, it is customary to be lenient that other relatives who would normally wear Shabbos garments [for a bris] at other times do so now as well. Sha'arei Teshuvah ibid. However, he wrote that for other relatives one should be strict, see there. It seems that everything stated regarding a bris milah applies equally to a pidyon haben, as is implied from what is written below in the Rama's gloss on seif 9 where he equated bris milah with pidyon haben, see there.
(8) In the Rama's gloss: They wear Shabbos garments. Even though the Shulchan Aruch rules below in siman 559 seif 8 that a baal bris wears other [non-Shabbos] garments on Tisha B'Av itself, which has the status of a festival, all the more so from Rosh Chodesh until Tisha B'Av. Nevertheless, here it teaches us that even actual white garments [are permitted], for we only forbid white garments on the day of Tisha B'Av itself. Furthermore, it teaches us that even according to the Ashkenazic custom of being strict on Shabbos Chazon not to change [garments], one might think that the festivity of a bris should not override Shabbos and he should not change his garments according to the custom, therefore it teaches us that the custom is to be lenient for a bris. Mateh Yehuda. The Machberes os 5 cited his words and wrote that this is unlike the Zera Avraham siman 76 who wrote the opposite in the name of this authority, see there.
(9) In the Rama's gloss: They wear Shabbos garments. But it is forbidden to wear new garments. Zera Avraham siman 76. Machberes os 3, Zechor L'Avraham os 9, Sha'arei Teshuvah os 1.
(10) In the Rama's gloss: They wear Shabbos garments. Regarding a haircut for a baal bris, it goes without saying that on Tisha B'Av itself it is forbidden, G-d forbid, to permit it; even from Rosh Chodesh until the fast it is forbidden. Beis Yehuda vol. 2 siman 85, and so he wrote in his work Mateh Yehuda on this siman. So too wrote Eliyah Rabbah os 28, except that he wrote that before the week in which Tisha B'Av falls it is perhaps possible to permit. So too wrote Mish'meres Zahav os 13, and so wrote the Zera Avraham ibid. The Machberes os 2 cited their words. So too wrote the Noda B'Yehuda, first edition, siman 28. R' Yaakov Emden in his responsa vol. 3 siman 2, 3 decisively ruled it forbidden, see there. However, in the responsa of the Chasam Sofer siman 158 he challenged the words of the Noda B'Yehuda and concluded that it is fully permitted even during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, and wrote that this is the common practice, see there. In the responsa Binyan Tziyon siman 31 he wrote that when Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos one may certainly rely on permission to take a haircut even on erev Shabbos, see there. So too wrote in the responsa Or Ne'elam siman 10 that when Tisha B'Av falls on Sunday and the bris is on the preceding Thursday, since there is no "week in which Tisha B'Av falls" and there is no prohibition by the strict law of the Gemara, it is permitted for the mohel and the baal bris to take a haircut in honor of the bris, see there; the Sha'arei Teshuvah os 1 cited his words. So too wrote Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 15 that before Shabbos Chazon it is permitted, but afterward it is forbidden. It seems that since there are many opinions on this matter, during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls one should be strict since it is the strict law of the Gemara. But from the seventeenth of Tammuz onward, and likewise when Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos or Sunday, which does not have the status of "the week in which Tisha B'Av falls" according to the unqualified ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, as stated below in seif 4, one may be lenient if necessary. All this applies to those who have the custom to be strict regarding haircuts from the seventeenth of Tammuz as stated below in seif 4 in the Rama's gloss, or those who have the custom to be strict from Rosh Chodesh. But for those who follow the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch that the prohibition only applies during the week in which Tisha B'Av falls, in any case it is permitted if it is not that week.
(11) In the Rama's gloss: Even on Shabbos Chazon one does not change, etc., even the tallis; rather, one wraps himself in a weekday one. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B'Av. Magen Avraham s.k. 3. Eliyah Rabbah os 38, Mishna Berurah os 5.
(12) In the Rama's gloss: Only the undershirt. The Hagahos Maimuniyos on Tractate Taanis, Laws of Tisha B'Av, explained that "undershirt" means the outer coat, not the undergarment; so too explained the Radak, and what Scripture calls "the tunic of the coat", sometimes it calls both as one. He further wrote in the Hagahos Asheri around the Mordechai printed in Kraków that so wrote Sefer Tzeidah L'Derech and Sefer HaTanya, but he himself wrote that he observed the practice of only changing the innermost undergarment. Knesset HaGedolah in the glosses. So too wrote Magen Avraham in siman 559 s.k. 10 in the name of Maharam Tiktin that the outer coat is forbidden because a conspicuous matter is more stringent on Tisha B'Av than during mourning, but the undergarment is permitted, see there. This appears to be the view of the later authorities as stated in the following paragraph.
(13) In the Rama's gloss: Only the undershirt. And it is permitted even when Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos. Levush seif 16. One who is strict about not changing it, that is a foolish custom, for one wears it only because of sweat. Responsa of Maharshal siman 27. Mateh Moshe siman 736. Magen Avraham s.k. 4. However, the Eliyah Rabbah os 39 wrote that those who have the practice not to change even the undergarment have what to rely upon, see there. All this follows the view of the Rama. However, Maran [the Mechaber] in Beit Yosef at the end of siman 552, after citing the words of the Hagahos Maimuniyos and the Mordechai, wrote that this is not the custom. So too the Radbaz in responsa vol. 2 siman 693 challenged this custom and wrote that the practice in Ashkenaz of not changing garments on Shabbos [Chazon], all the regions of Israel did not follow this custom, and rightly so, for it is against the law; and he elaborated further, see there. Similarly, the Gaon R' Tzvi, even though he was Ashkenazic, had the practice of not changing anything from other Shabbosos, even when Tisha B'Av fell on Shabbos. So wrote his son R' Yaakov Emden in his siddur p. 68, see there. The Machberes os 4 cited his words. So too wrote the Chayei Adam rule 133, end of os 21, that in the community of Vilna they practice according to the Gra to wear Shabbos garments, and some change one garment. So too wrote Or Chayyim on Chayei Adam ibid. that in many communities they practice wearing all Shabbos garments and do not change anything, even on Shabbos when Tisha B'Av falls, see there. Obviously, when he wrote "in many communities, etc." he meant Ashkenazic communities, but the Sephardic communities all practice not to change this Shabbos from other Shabbosos, as written by the Beit Yosef and the Radbaz. However, the Shulchan Gavoah os 4 wrote that the custom is not to wear white or distinguished garments like festival garments. So too wrote the Yafeh L'Lev vol. 3 os 2 that their custom is not to wear white as on other summer Shabbosos, but rather other colored garments as is done on winter Shabbosos. This is what we have written according to their custom, but some have the practice not to change at all and wear even white as on other Shabbosos, just not festival garments. See below siman 552 seif 10.
(14) The custom in Eretz Yisrael is not to say any kinah on Shabbos Chazon, as stated in the siddurim, and this is the custom of Egypt and the large cities and towns of Turkey. For even on Tisha B'Av that falls on Shabbos, one sets his table with fatty meat, the table of the final meal, to honor the day of Shabbos. Who placed a mouth to cry and mourn? He too brought [the kinah] as he lamented, and all the more so in public before the congregation on the holy day of Shabbos, it is certainly proper to nullify this custom in honor of Shabbos. Birke Yosef os 2. Likewise in the responsa Zaken Aharon siman 38 he discussed this at length and wrote that it is forbidden, see there. So too wrote the Radbaz in his responsa vol. 3 siman 645. The Machberes os 6 and the Sha'arei Teshuvah os 6 cited their words.
(15) In the Rama's gloss: But we spread the Shabbos paroches [curtain], etc., and the same applies to tablecloths and chair covers. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B'Av. Magen Avraham s.k. 5. Eliyah Rabbah os 38. Eishel Avraham os 5, Chayei Adam rule 133 os 21. Kitzur Shulchan Aruch siman 122 os 16. Mishna Berurah os 7. This means the tablecloths that are spread on the table in the synagogue and the study hall. Eishel Avraham ibid. And obviously it is permitted to lay white [cloths] on the table. Chayei Adam ibid.
(16) In the Rama's gloss: It is permitted to wear Shabbos garments, etc., but new garments are forbidden. As cited. Magen Avraham s.k. 6. Eliyah Rabbah os 40, Mishna Berurah os 9.
(17) In the Rama's gloss: It is permitted to wear Shabbos garments, etc. The Maharil made a shpinhultz [engagement celebration] for his son on Shabbos the sixth of Av and wore only his Shabbos coat in honor of his son, and the groom wore all his Shabbos garments. Derashos Maharil, Laws of Tisha B'Av. Darkei Moshe os 11. Magen Avraham ibid. Eliyah Rabbah ibid. Ateres Zahav. The Dagul Merevavah wrote that if Tisha B'Av falls on Shabbos, the groom is permitted to wear Shabbos garments, but for his father it is not permitted even to wear just the Shabbos coat alone, see there. So too wrote Mishna Berurah os 10. All this applies to those who follow the Rama's practice of not changing into Shabbos garments on Shabbos Chazon, but for those who practice changing [into Shabbos garments] as stated above in paragraph 13, it is permitted in any case.
(18) In the Rama's gloss: It is permitted to wear Shabbos garments, etc. But a haircut is forbidden. Dagul Merevavah. Pischei Teshuvah os 6.
