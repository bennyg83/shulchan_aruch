Seif 3: The week in which 9 Av falls — they are forbidden to launder, etc. NB: So it is in the Talmud Taanis daf 30: Rava said the halacha follows Rabban Gamliel; and another [statement of] Rava: the halacha follows R' Meir; and both are for leniency, see there. And behold, at first glance the matter is wondrous to me: since we say there above that all derive from one verse — one who holds from Rosh Chodesh until the fast derives from "its Festival"; one who holds "the entire month" derives from "its month"; one who holds "the entire week" derives from "its Shabbat" — if so, Rava's view that one is forbidden only the week in which 9 Av falls until the fast is difficult: if he expounds "its Shabbat," the entire week should be forbidden; if he expounds "its Festival," from Rosh Chodesh should be forbidden — and from where does he derive leniency in both? And it appears to explain: behold, one can understand the one who derives from "its Festival," and Rashi explains Rosh Chodesh is called a mo'ed — if so, Rosh Chodesh suffices for the prohibition to begin; but from where that the prohibition ends on 9 Av? Perhaps the entire month? And it appears his intent: since it is explicit in the poskim and below in Shulchan Aruch that 9 Av is called a mo'ed — if so, just as on Pesach days and festival days all the intermediate days are called "chag" although there is only one day at the beginning and one at the end, nevertheless all the days are called chag since beginning and end are Yom Tov — likewise these nine days from Rosh Chodesh until the fast: since at their beginning is Rosh Chodesh called mo'ed and at the end is called mo'ed, therefore all the days are called chag; therefore he derives from "its Festival" that it is forbidden all days called chag, namely from Rosh Chodesh until the fast. But Rava holds both are for leniency: therefore the term "its Festival" teaches that one is forbidden only until the chag, namely 9 Av called mo'ed; and the term "its Shabbat" teaches one is not forbidden from Rosh Chodesh but only that week — and there are two derivations for leniency. So it appears to me, correct — examine well.
