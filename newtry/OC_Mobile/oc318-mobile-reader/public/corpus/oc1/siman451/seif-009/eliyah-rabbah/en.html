[9] All rust, etc. The Taz explained: specifically what is called Rost, and there is substance there, and when one scrapes from there there is like dust; but when there is blackness or redness and one cannot remove it entirely, even after scraping there is no substance, only appearance alone: one hagals it after scraping all that is possible; only that in any case one should libun that place for a better measure; end of his words in brief. And this requires study: for he afterward wrote that with libun one need not remove the rust; if so, why scraping? And it is possible that that is specifically when one libuns the entire vessel, which is not so when one libuns one place. But it is difficult: for even with actual rust, libun helps (in a place of libun) in one place, as is written in se'if 5; and what difference is there between [cases with] no substance? And one may say that the Taz said for a better measure to libun, and not by law. But this requires study: for Darkhei Moshe wrote on Yoreh De'ah siman 121, this is his language: and if one cannot remove the rust, Issur VeHeter wrote that even if only a little remains, it has no remedy by hagalah; end quote; and so wrote Maharil. And it is possible that he deals with rust that has substance; and in my book on Yoreh De'ah there I raised a difficulty from this against the Bach there and the Perishah in the name of Maharshal in a gloss, who permit; and they wondered from their own reasoning that it is fitting to forbid, and they did not mention the aforementioned poskim who forbade explicitly. However, Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 14 of the laws of forbidden foods wrote to permit, and to this Maharshal intended; and so it appears to me, for it deals with the sort of case the Taz deals with, where the rust has no substance; and with this everything fits, with Heaven's help.
