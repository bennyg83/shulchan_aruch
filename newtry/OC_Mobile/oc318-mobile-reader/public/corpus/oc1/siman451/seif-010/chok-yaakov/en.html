Or to buy new ones. Meaning, if it is impossible to clean them out in order to perform hagalah because they have indentations. If one transgressed and used them without hagalah, it is permitted, because salting does not affect utensils, as the Magen Avraham wrote at the end of Yoreh Deah siman 105; see there, that salt is not called a sharp substance unless it is very salty. If it is known that chametz was used in it, see above siman 447, seif 4. See the Bach and Taz, who are stringent not to perform hagalah on baskets at all. The Taz wrote that this is the view of Maharil, and he wondered at the Shulchan Aruch for agreeing to be lenient, for apparently the words of Maharil are correct; end quote. He overlooked the words of the Rav nearby, seif 18 in the gloss, where the Rav explicitly ruled in accordance with Maharil, that baskets in which chametz is used have the law of a sieve, and hagalah is not effective. Here, however, since there was no known chametz in them, only a mere concern, he ruled that hagalah is effective. This is simple.
