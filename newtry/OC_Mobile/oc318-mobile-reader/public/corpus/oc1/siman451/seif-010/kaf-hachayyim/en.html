And one puts them away during Pesach, etc. A recent practice arose of placing tin chametz utensils high up in the winter room and claiming there is no concern that they will be used because of their height. People likewise leave Pesach utensils high up throughout the year. This is a complete error. They must be guarded in a place people do not frequent, lest someone mistakenly use them during Pesach. Bach; Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, subsection 1; Olas Shabbos, subsection 1. It is possible that the custom arose because the utensils had not been used that day, and many authorities permit after-the-fact use of such utensils, as stated in siman 447, se'if 10. This is certainly so for utensils used primarily as a second vessel or with cold food, such as pitchers, where there is no concern. One who is stringent will be blessed. Magen Avraham, subsection 2. Chok Yaakov, subsection 7, and Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1, however, permit this without qualification. See there. Peri Chadash writes that it is proper to take Bach's opinion into account. Birkei Yosef, subsection 2, writes likewise. Chok Yosef, subsection 2, agrees with Magen Avraham that one who is stringent will be blessed. Mekor Chaim, subsection 5, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4, write likewise.
