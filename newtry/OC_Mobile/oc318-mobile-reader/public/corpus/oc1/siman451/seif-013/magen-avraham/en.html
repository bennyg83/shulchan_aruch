The patch preceded. Meaning, the patch was made when the utensil was new, or he performed hagalah on the utensil before placing the patch on it.
He should place coals. This requires study, for the Rashba wrote this only where the patch preceded the absorption of prohibition, since we are concerned that there may still be actual prohibition there. The Bach wrote likewise, that if the absorption preceded the patch, coals do not help; rather, one must perform libbun on the whole utensil. This also appears in Issur VeHeter, sha'ar 58. Therefore it seems to me that when the absorption preceded the patch, he must place so many coals there that sparks fly from it, and this is what is meant by until the actual prohibition is burned. If the patch preceded the absorption, light libbun suffices, as written in seif 4 in the Rama. Old utensils that were plated with tin, some require hagalah afterward. The Re'em, part 1, siman 43, wrote that if so, hagalah should not help them, for it is like the absorption preceding the patch. He concludes that they do not require hagalah: first, because utensils whose law is hagalah suffice with this sort of light libbun, where one cannot touch the other side, as written in seif 4, and once the lead has melted, it is like light libbun. Second, since the tin separates, it is like new utensils. According to this, even if he plated them with old tin, it is permitted, for the tin itself undergoes libbun. So too is written in Hagahos Minhagim. See the beginning of siman 461, and see Yoreh Deah, siman 120, regarding immersion, and see below, seif 24.
And clean them out well. It is better to place coals on the place of the mark.
