A patch. Every patch in this seif means one fixed by nails. But one made by melting lead, iron, silver, or other types of metal, since it is made by melting in fire and becomes one body, is better than libun, for it is as though it was made that way from the beginning. This is the agreement of the later authorities. Regarding utensils plated with tin, such as copper utensils that they have in the land of Turkey, Re'em concludes in part 1, siman 43, that they do not need hagalah, since they are plated with tin. See Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah, who wrote at length to bring the opinions of those who disagree with this, and wrote that some of the great men of the land are stringent for themselves. In any case, it appears good in the eyes of God and man to immerse it after the tin plating without a blessing; end quote. In Pri Chadash, Orach Chaim, he ruled that it requires hagalah after tin plating, and so is the practice in Jerusalem; see there.
Or he should place coals on the place of the patch until the substance of the prohibition is burned. Then even if the absorption of the prohibited substance preceded the patch, it is permitted, since the place of the patch was subjected to libun, as implied by the wording of the Rashba in responsum siman 371, by the Mechaber, and so ruled the Levush. The Bach wrote about the Rashba that it appears to him that where the absorption of prohibition preceded the patch, libun of the place of the patch is not effective. I do not know from where the Bach derived this interpretation. On the contrary, the implication of the Rashba is that in every case, once the chametz underneath the patch was burned, it is enough. This appears to me to be the main ruling, unlike the Bach. In Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah and Magen Avraham I saw that they take the Bach's words as primary and wonder at the wording of the Shulchan Aruch here. All this happened because when they copied the words of the Bach, they also copied what the Bach concluded: and so wrote the Issur VeHeter, klal 58, law 19. They understood that this referred to this law, and therefore took his words as practical halachah. In truth, I examined the Issur VeHeter there well and saw no proof from his words for this law. The Bach wrote, and so wrote the Issur VeHeter, only regarding the end of his wording, where he concluded that the fire burns everything, and what now remains in it will never discharge; this is what the Issur VeHeter wrote explicitly there, see there. If it was subjected to libun in this way, or if he removed the patches one time until after he performed hagalah, then afterward it is again permitted to perform hagalah in this way with the patch each time, since it is no longer called a case where the absorption of prohibition preceded the patch. All this is where the patch is well attached, in a manner that there is no crack or indentation between the body of the utensil and the patch. If not, even where the patch preceded the absorption, it requires libun, or removal of the patch, or widening of the crack so that one can clean it. This is simple, and so is the agreement of the later authorities.
And clean them out well. If it is impossible to clean them out, he should place coals upon it, as above.
