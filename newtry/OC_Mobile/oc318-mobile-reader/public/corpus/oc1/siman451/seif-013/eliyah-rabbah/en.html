[13] And some are lenient, etc. Because permitted [food] is absorbed; and see siman 447, se'if 6, where the Rashba holds that it is called "it absorbed a prohibition"; if so, even after the fact one should forbid if one did not libun it thus: so wrote Magen Avraham. And it appears that in a case of major loss one may rely on the poskim who hold that it is called "it absorbed permitted [food]." And further, Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 17 of the laws of forbidden foods wrote that light libun suffices, upon the Rambam who is dealing with "it absorbed a prohibition"; and even though in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 1 of the laws of Yom Tov he wrote specifically regarding "it absorbed permitted [food]," meaning there he wrote in the name of the Semak, and here in the name of R' Avigdor. And further, in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 5 of the laws of chametz he wrote that with chametz, even though its use is by means of fire itself, hagalah suffices; and so wrote the Kol Bo, siman 48, and Rabbeinu Yeruham folio 40 in the name of the Rashba. And from this it is puzzling what Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Rashba to the contrary; and see s.k. 38. And even though we do not hold so, nevertheless in this one may certainly rely.
