To subject the mortar to libun. This is specifically regarding chametz on Pesach. By contrast, during the rest of the year, regarding a mortar used for prohibition and the like, according to everyone hagalah suffices. So too wrote Hagahos Ashri at the end of chapter HaSocher Es HaPoel in the name of Rabbenu Tam, and Responsa Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 447, and Kol Bo, Tanya, and Toras HaBayis. Yet this requires study, for from the words of all the poskim regarding knives and spits it implies that there is more basis to be lenient with hagalah regarding Pesach, while other prohibitions require libun. So too is explained there from the words of Rabbenu Tam himself. Perhaps specifically here, regarding a mortar, they were stringent because it is common to pound in it fat and onions with crumbs of bread, which is a sharp substance, like a leavening vessel according to Rashi, and requires libun, as explained in the Tur. This is unlike a mortar of prohibition, where the concern is only for fatty residue of prohibition, for it is not usual to pound in it together with sharp items; therefore hagalah suffices. So too is somewhat implied in Kol Bo; see there.
And the practice is to subject it to libun ab initio. The later authorities wrote that with our mortars, in which chametz is never pounded, only saffron and cloves, hagalah is therefore enough. Nevertheless, it is good to be stringent to pound everything before Yom Tov. Regarding after the fact, see above siman 447, s.k. 38. If one pounded in a chametz mortar and put it into a dish, the Magen Avraham ruled that one should not forbid after the fact; see there, where he left the matter requiring study.
