(ה) To whiten l'chatchila, however it suffices for him etc. — meaning that by law hagalah alone suffices like the first view; but the custom is to whiten it (until a straw etc. burns); therefore in a place where libun is impossible, such as in a wooden vessel, hagalah suffices if it is smooth and has no crevices. Acharonim wrote that our small mortars, in which they never grind chametz all year, only saffron and spices — hagalah suffices l'chatchila even for metal ones; nevertheless hagalah is required, lest perhaps before Pesach some chametz in any amount entered and was absorbed through sharpness of the spices ground afterward; and it is a mitzvah from the preferred to hagelah and grind everything before Yom Tov. B'dieved, even if he did not hagelah at all and ground spices in it on Yom Tov and put them in cooked food — Acharonim side to permit the cooked food, and especially if not ben yomo; certainly one should not be stringent b'dieved from a doubt lest a chametz crumb mixed in the spices ground in it, or that he cut ginger etc. with a chametz knife — for we do not establish a prohibition from doubt. All this is in a mortar where there is only concern for absorption of a little chametz; but in a hackmesser whose practice is to cut onions with a chametz knife and place it in meat and cut the meat very fine with the hackmesser — one may say that even b'dieved it is forbidden [Chayei Adam]; and indeed as practiced, they also put a piece of wheat bread with the onions and cut everything together very fine — then it is absorption of actual chametz together with sharpness of the onions; certainly one may not be lenient. Magen Avraham wrote: one should be careful not to cut ginger only with a new knife; and those who are not careful in this cause damage, for they cut ginger [which is a sharp substance] with a meat knife and grind it in the mortar, and the mortar becomes absorbed with meat, and afterward they grind spices in it and eat with milk.
