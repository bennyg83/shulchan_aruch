Their kashering follows their use. For the utensil releases in the manner in which it absorbed.
With which one stirs a pot. According to the opinion cited by Tur, Yoreh Deah 121, a utensil used in a first vessel while it stands upon the fire must likewise be kashered in a first vessel standing beside the fire. A spoon used to stir a pot while it is on the fire should therefore be kashered in this manner.
Their use is in a second vessel. Small spoons used only in bowls may be kashered in a second vessel. After the fact, if they were not kashered at all and were then used, see above, seif katan 11.
Their kashering is in a second vessel. See below, seif 6 in the gloss.
Which is used by pouring, etc. This applies even to a new utensil never previously used, if hot chametz was poured upon it once from a first vessel.
By kashering in a second vessel. Meaning, boiling water is poured into a vessel and the utensils are then inserted into it. Taste absorbed through pouring from a first vessel is expelled only by pouring in the same manner. Even after the fact, kashering in a second vessel is ineffective.
To pour upon it, etc. Care must be taken that the stream not be interrupted. Pouring is effective only for an object whose use was likewise through pouring. A utensil used in a first vessel cannot be kashered merely by pouring, but must be placed in an actual first vessel.
Which have cracks, etc. This is because of concern for actual chametz, for which hagalah is ineffective, as explained above in seif 3.
And it is inside the utensil. On the outside of a utensil, one need not presume that actual chametz lies in its indentations. If, however, the utensil is used to draw from a large cauldron and chametz commonly adheres to its outer surface as well, one must be careful even there, because hagalah water does not remove actual chametz.
And he cannot clean them, etc. The same applies to lids attached with hinges that cannot be cleaned, or bowls with ear-like hinges. Chametz enters those outer areas, as is known, and since they cannot be cleaned, they may not be kashered at all.
