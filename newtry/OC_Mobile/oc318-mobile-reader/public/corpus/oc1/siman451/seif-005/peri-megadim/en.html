(א) Knives [Taz] Pesachim 4b — and it appears in other issurim hagalah suffices even though sometimes they roast on a knife on fire — they follow the majority of their use; nitelaf such as this is permitted — unlike Pesach where they asked whether nitelaf is permitted or forbidden — he said for his view he was stringent new like BY siman 447 seif and below seif 6; see there. And in the piyyut "knives for meals" for a meal; and Magen Avraham wrote seif 5 note 5 — where possible to be concerned — there is no remedy even where impossible; and so all that have grooves in the blade — there is no remedy in hagalah; and b'dieved even if one kashered a vessel that has a groove and it is impossible to scrape — Acharonim note 16 — even b'dieved forbidden without libun — not like Bach; and so the halacha; and the reason: all vessels that have protrusions and rims and cracks or old pewter like talya'ir taken from gentiles and they are cut in the knife — many splits and it is impossible to scrape the grooves well without rust remaining — truly no remedy in hagalah. And a knife whose cauldron cannot be cleaned and hagalah and cutting food in the flame — not forbidden; see EH 121 seif 6.

(ב) Nachmanides wrote note 1 — a knife of metal: the blade and the cauldron and they used also in the cauldron in boiling water, and afterward inserted the cauldron in wood — hagalah does not help; this is true and established like Talui in seif 13 and Levush; and b'dieved if one inserts in the flame on Pesach via hagalah — it requires study; Magen Avraham note 24 — metal hot in part is not hot in whole to emit from side of the blade even if used in whole first in boiling water; and in Yoreh De'ah 121 in Shach note 16 — it does not appear so — for he used first at the fire itself and now used in the flame at the fire itself; even though afterward he libuned the blade and cauldron was in tin — he has no way to permit; see there note 15; and see Magen Avraham seif 4; see note 13 regarding knives in a horn and similarly; see note 6 regarding laws of immersing vessels in knives and similarly.

(ג) Or one may say Rabbina said to Rav Ashi — specifically a Pesach knife, for in a simple issur libun; and from meat to milk hagalah — only Pesach whether it is called permitted absorption or forbidden absorption; see note 8; and see Tosafot Pesachim 30b s.v. vehalakhta.

(ד) And know Rashi — even when removed from fire as the Mechaber wrote, and proof from Alfas that they passed through boiling water — meaning for a knife whose main use is in hot bread removed from fire, and the piece placed in talya'ir — Peri Megadim holds we do not follow the majority of use; one must insert on the fire and water raises boiling like Beit Yosef siman 462 seif 1; see Magen Avraham note 6 and note 7. And indeed a thing absorbed via fire requires libun — all that was removed from fire then has no law of libun; therefore Magen Avraham wrote note 6 to insert in a pan while still hot; Mahari Weil in the first chapter — a table requires hagalah specifically; see Magen Avraham note 37; see note 92 seif 7 in the gloss — they placed a hot pot on spilled milk in cold — the pot is forbidden and the food permitted — it requires study from Alfas that passed through boiling water — it implies even if he left in a cold place; and also Peri Megadim holds a lump forbids all the more — it requires study that law; and therefore those knives with which they cut hot bread removed from fire — hagalah suffices while still in the oven even if scraped and sealed; the oven is called via fire; see Magen Avraham note 21 from this. And Peri Chadash note 14 wrote additionally that knives have another leniency — namely a small knife.
