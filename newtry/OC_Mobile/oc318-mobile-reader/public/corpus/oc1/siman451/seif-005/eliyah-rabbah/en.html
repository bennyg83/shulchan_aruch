[5] And if one filled it, etc. This is the language of Beis Yosef: the Rashba in Torat HaBayit appears not to have had the reading in the Gemara, "and if it is filled with coals, it is fine," for he wrote: and if it is a vessel that is not normally heated from inside, like a pot, even if one heated it from inside it is forbidden, lest one be concerned; and if one returned it to the kiln, it is permitted; end quote. And the same applies to a bukhya that is not normally heated from inside; and it is possible that the Rashba agrees regarding a bukhya, since it is made of bricks and earth; end of the language of Beis Yosef. And in Chok Yaakov he lengthened to wonder: what does Beis Yosef wonder at the Rashba? Behold this distinction of a bukhya is proven at the end of the siman, where it says if it is filled with coals it is permitted; see there. And it is no difficulty at all: for nevertheless, since the Rashba did not bring there the law of filled with coals, it came to his mind that he does not have that reading. And further, in the Gemara one may say that even regarding pots it is permitted when filled with coals, as he wrote. And with apologies for his honor, he saw only Torat HaBayit folio 46, but folio 33 escaped him, where he rules explicitly that it is permitted when filled with coals; also in the long Torat HaBayit of the Rashba, folio 83, he did not see, where he copied the Gemara "and if filled with coals, fine," and has the reading like our Gemara. Also it is puzzling upon him, for it is explicit in the Rashba there that he permits even an earthenware bukhya when filled with coals; and so it is implied in the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, who wrote "and likewise a small oven that they call a padila," etc., and that is an earthenware vessel, as Beis Yosef wrote at the beginning of siman 461; and so I proved in my book on Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 97 from Tosafos of Pesachim folio 30, for if not so, what do they ask on that of a bukhya that is permitted when filled with coals: behold the Torah testified regarding an earthenware vessel that it never leaves the status of its wall; see there: for if a bukhya meant bricks and earth, then necessarily he holds that it means earthenware; and so the Rambam explained in chapter 5 of the laws of chametz. I also wondered from this at the Bach here, who wrote in the name of Tosafos to the contrary, that it means bricks and earth. And as for what we hold, that a thing heated from outside: heating does not help: one may say that is specifically pots, for which one is concerned; which is not so for a bukhya, for which one is not concerned even if of earthenware; and so it is explained in the Rokeach at the end of siman 484; and all this I wrote briefly in Eliyahu Zuta. One may further say that "filled with coals" which the Gemara permits also refers to pots: for specifically mere heating we say one is concerned; which is not so when filled with coals, for we see that one is not concerned, and it is similar to returning to a kiln; and so appears the view of Magen Avraham; and so it is implied in Tosafos there; and so it is explicit in Piskei Tosafos, chapter "Kol Shaah," siman 88; and all this is not like Shibbolei HaLeket and Tanya, who explained that "filled with coals is permitted" deals specifically with an oven heated from inside.
