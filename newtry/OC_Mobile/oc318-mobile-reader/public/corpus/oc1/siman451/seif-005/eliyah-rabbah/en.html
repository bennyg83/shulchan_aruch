[5] And if one fills them, etc. — this is the language of Beit Yosef from Rashba in Turat HaBayit; it appears he did not have in the Gemara "and if one fills coals it is fine" — for he wrote: if in a vessel whose way is not to heat from inside like a pot, even if they heated from inside it is forbidden lest one be concerned; and if one returns it to the kiln it is permitted — until here. And the same law applies to kuvia whose way is not to heat from inside; and possibly Rashba concedes regarding kuvia since it is made of bricks and clay — until here Beit Yosef's language. And in Chok Yaakov he elaborated to wonder: what did Beit Yosef wonder on Rashba — behold this distinction of kuvia is proven at the end of the siman where he says if one fills coals it is permitted; see there. And it is not difficult at all — for in any event since Rashba did not bring there the law of filling coals, it entered his mind he did not have that girsa; and furthermore in the Gemara one may say that even in pots it is permitted with filling coals, as he wrote; and in his honor he did not see except in Turat HaBayit page 46, but it escaped him on page 33 where he ruled explicitly that filling coals is permitted; also in the long Turat HaBayit of Rashba page 83 he did not see, where he copied the Gemara "and if one fills coals it is fine" and has the girsa like our Gemara; also one may wonder on him — for it is explicit to Rashba there that he permits even in a pottery kuvia with filling coals; and so it appears in Shulchan Aruch and Levush who wrote "and likewise a small oven called pidila," etc., and it is a pottery vessel as Beit Yosef wrote at the beginning of siman 461; and so I proved in my Yoreh De'ah at the beginning of siman 97 from Tosafot Pesachim page 30 — for if not, what do they challenge on that kuvia that it is permitted with filling coals — behold the Torah testified regarding pottery vessels that they never leave their mouths; see there — behold kuvia deals with bricks and clay; but perforce it is reasonable to him it deals with pottery; and so Rambam explained in chapter 5 of Laws of Chametz. Also I wondered on Bach here who wrote in the name of Tosafot to the contrary that it deals with bricks and clay. And that which is established for us that heating from outside does not help — one may say: specifically pots, for we are concerned; which is not so in kuvia, for we are not concerned even if of pottery; and so is explained in Rokeach end siman 484; and all this I wrote briefly in Eliyahu Zuta. Furthermore one may say: filling coals that the Gemara permits refers also to pots — for only regarding mere heating we say we are concerned, which is not so with filling coals, for we see we are not concerned; and it is comparable to returning to the kiln; and so appears the view of Magen Avraham; and so it appears in Tosafot there; and so is explicit in Piskei Tosafot chapter Kol Sha'ah siman 88; and all this is not like Shibolei Leket and the baraita who explained that filling coals permitted deals specifically with an oven they heated from inside.
