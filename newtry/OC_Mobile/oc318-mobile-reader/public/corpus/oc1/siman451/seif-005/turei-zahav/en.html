Rather, one must pour, etc. In any case, pouring helps for it, since its use is also by pouring. But a utensil whose use is by fire cannot be kashered by pouring; it requires an actual kli rishon.
And it is inside the utensil. The Taz explains the kashering of utensils for Pesach, according to their normal mode of use, with distinctions between hagalah, libbun, pouring from a kli rishon, soaking, cleaning away visible chametz, and after-the-fact cases. He clarifies the underlying sugyos and when the stringency is required by law or only as the preferred practice.
