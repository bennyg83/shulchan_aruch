It was heated from the outside, and it is forbidden, etc. This is the correct reading. Meaning, because it was heated from the outside, that heating does not remove what was absorbed within it.
And if he filled it with coals, etc. The reason is that an ordinary oven tile is not normally prone to crack, and therefore there is no concern that he will spare it and fail to heat it properly. Bricks fired in a kiln have the status of earthenware vessels. If a hot chametz substance was poured onto them, they can be kashered only through proper libun, as will be explained in siman 461. Nevertheless, libun is effective for them even inside our ovens, because one does not spare them as one might spare pots. Bricks dried only in the sun have the status of earthen vessels, and hagalah is effective for them. If food was baked or roasted upon them, their use was through fire and they require libun, as stated above.
