Glass utensils. Maharil wrote in the laws of Pesach that when buying glass utensils one must be careful to buy clear ones, for it is known that they were not used, since they are clear. See what I wrote above in s.k. 2.
However, some are stringent. The Magen Avraham wrote in s.k. 59 as follows: the Agudah wrote that utensils whose use is cold, such as pitchers and cups, it is better to kasher them by pouring than to perform hagalah on them together with utensils whose use is hot; end quote. The Tur and the Mechaber already wrote above in seif 9 and in siman 452 that one who performs hagalah before five hours has no practical difference in all this. Perhaps for this reason the custom spread in these countries first to perform hagalah on the utensils by means of a heated stone, and afterward they again perform hagalah on all of them together in a kettle. Nevertheless, one does not fulfill his obligation with the first hagalah, since they do it with water mixed with earth, as explained below in siman 452. They also do it while the utensils are still dirty, and hagalah is not effective when actual chametz is present.
