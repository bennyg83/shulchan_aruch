(98) And scrub it with water, etc.: that is to say, after cleaning it well; for if water comes upon it beforehand, even upon part of it, it again has no remedy to use it on Pesach according to all opinions.
(99) For all other kneading vessels: and regarding hagalah he did not mention it in this se'if, for he already explained above in se'if 17 that if one kneads in it all year it needs hagalah; and here he teaches us that one should not say that hagalah alone suffices; or alternatively, here he deals with a case where one kneads in it only occasionally, for which by law hagalah is not required.
(100) Not to use a sieve through hagalah: that is to say, even through scrubbing and hagalah, for the reason that it is very hard to clean the holes of the sieve; and after the fact, if one transgressed and used it on Pesach through thorough scrubbing alone, one may rely on the Mechaber's ruling that thorough scrubbing alone suffices.
(101) And one should not change: rather one must buy a new sieve; and if one cannot find a new one to buy, it is better not to sift the flour at all. However, if wheat grains are found in the flour, and if so if one finds them on Pesach in a cooked matzah they will forbid: it is possible that even l'chatchila one may rely to be lenient by first scrubbing it well and afterward hagaling it, for it is like blanching. And if it is impossible to clean it and one has no sieve, then one should take care not to eat the matzos except dry, and not cook them and not put them in gravy or anything moist. And know that if water came upon a sieve, it has no remedy to use it on Pesach even according to the Mechaber. And nevertheless, if one sifted flour on Pesach with a sieve upon which water came after Pesach of the previous year: it is permitted after the fact, for it is again hard chametz (chametz nuksheh) and is nullified in sixty [Chok Yaakov and Mekor Chaim].
(102) That they call cast iron: for sometimes one crumbles chametz bread upon it, and it is impossible that a mashehu crumb not remain in its holes; and also horseradish (called chrein) that one grated upon it all year and was cut with a chametz knife: the horseradish is a sharp thing and absorbs the taste of chametz, and afterward when one grates upon it, the taste of chametz is found in it. And in this even after the fact one should forbid if one used it on Pesach, even if one used it cold, for example if one crumbled matzah upon it [and all the more so horseradish, which is a sharp thing]; and even if one scraped it well first, because it is impossible that a crumb of chametz or a crumb of horseradish that absorbed from chametz not stick to it. But when one used it on eve of Pesach there are distinctions: if one used upon it only matzah on eve of Pesach, even if a crumb of chametz mixed into it, behold they were nullified in it in sixty, and it is permitted to eat it until nightfall [but when night arrives, the crumb reawakens and forbids in a mashehu, as is explained in siman 467]. And if one grated horseradish upon it on eve of Pesach, which is a sharp thing, one needs sixty against the entire cast-iron grater, which became entirely chametz through the absorption it absorbed from the horseradish grated upon it and cut with a chametz knife, as above. And if not its entirety, it is forbidden.
(103) And likewise baskets: of reeds that are full of holes, and one cannot scrape them; and see above in siman 442, se'if 11 in the gloss.
(104) But, etc.: and there regarding a sieve and a mill bag it is different: for flour of chametz always passes through the holes, and it is impossible that a mashehu not cling to it.
(105) Old sacks: not specifically; rather even with a new sack in which one once put chametz flour; and the same applies if one once put Pesach flour in it and wishes to wash it to put Pesach flour in it a second time: it also needs washing and opening the seams.
(106) Through washing: that is to say, with hot water and ashes and beating [later authorities].
(107) All the seams in them, etc.: see later in siman 452, se'if 6, and in Mishnah Berurah there.
(108) Before the washing: and one must also first scrape there well with a knife the chametz found there [Magen Avraham and other later authorities]. They further wrote that the sheet upon which one makes the matzos: one must change them between kneading and kneading, that is, after the measure of a mil, as below in siman 459; if one wishes to knead again on those same sheets, one needs complete washing as above, and rinsing alone does not help, for dough sticks to them; and all the more so turning them to the other side does not help, for the chametz seeps and comes out from side to side; and also the seams in the middle of the cloth one must untie. And after the fact, if one transgressed and made the matzos on the sheet without any washing: if it is during Pesach, even after the fact one should forbid; but if one turned the sheet over or washed it, even without untying the seams, one should not be stringent [Chok Yaakov and other later authorities].
