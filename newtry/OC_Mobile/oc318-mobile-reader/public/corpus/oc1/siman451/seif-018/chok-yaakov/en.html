Not to use a sieve. By means of hagalah, but rather one must buy new ones. If one transgressed and sifted with it after scrubbing it well and performing hagalah, one should not forbid even during Pesach, since according to the Kol Bo and the Mechaber, even ab initio this method suffices, and it is proper to rely on him after the fact regarding a prohibition of a minute amount. But ab initio one should not use it, rather one should buy new ones. Likewise, where water came upon the sieve before he scrubbed it well, even on part of it, it has no remedy to use it on Pesach. This is Maharil. The Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that even in a place where there is no new sieve, it is better that they not sift the flour; end quote. I do not know from where he derived this law, for our eyes see that flour that was not sifted contains many broken and whole kernels, and the matzah or dough becomes forbidden, as explained below in siman 467. This sieve is effective after the fact through thorough scrubbing and hagalah, and all the more so if he sifts before Pesach, when it is nullified and is not called intentionally nullifying a prohibition ab initio, since it is still the time of permissibility and only a mere doubt of a minute amount. Therefore it appears to me that in pressing circumstances it is proper to do so even ab initio, for every pressing circumstance is like after the fact. If one transgressed and kneaded the dough without the flour being sifted, it is possible to forbid the entire dough, for it is known that when flour was not sifted, broken and whole kernels are found in it, as has occurred in practice before us several times. And I was asked about one who said innocently that after Pesach he regularly washes the sieve well in order to keep it for the next year, and he sifted flour with it for Pesach. I permitted what he sifted after the fact, relying on thorough scrubbing according to the Mechaber's view. Moreover, even if we say that something stuck to it remained because he did not scrub it well, and it became mixed into the flour, see below simanim 453 and 466, that something separated into flour reawakens; nevertheless, even though he sifted the flour during Pesach, it was explained above in siman 447 that hardened chametz is nullified in sixty even during Pesach. Since there is only a concern for some crumb that remained stuck from the previous year, which is hardened chametz, it is nullified, as appears to me.
Old sacks. Not specifically old, but even a new sack in which one placed flour for Pesach one time, and he wants to launder it in order to put flour in it a second time, he must also undo the stitching; so wrote the later authorities.
All the stitching. That means even what is at the bottom of the sack, namely the hem. So too is implied there in Terumas HaDeshen from the proof in the chapter Dam HaNiddah; see there. The Magen Avraham wrote that so one should act with tablecloths on which matzos are made: if one wants to make matzos on them a second time, it is not effective to turn them over, because chametz seeps through and emerges from side to side; rather, one must undo the stitching and launder well; end quote. In this case it is possible that one need not undo the hem, because they do not make the matzos on the hem, only in the middle of the cloth. If one transgressed after the fact and made matzos on the sheet without laundering at all, if this was during Pesach, even after the fact one should forbid. But if he turned the sheet over or laundered it, even though he did not undo the stitching, one should not be stringent after the fact, as it appears to me. Similarly regarding sacks, if he laundered them but did not undo the stitching and placed flour in them, one should not forbid after the fact. See below siman 453, s.k. 21, what I wrote there.
