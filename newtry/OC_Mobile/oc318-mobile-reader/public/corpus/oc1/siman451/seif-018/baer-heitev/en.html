With a sieve. But one must buy new ones; and if one transgressed and sifted with it by rubbing well through hagalah, one should not forbid even during Pesach, since according to the Mechaber even l'chatchila such [treatment] suffices. And likewise where water came upon the sieve and one did not first scrub it well, even upon part of it, it has no remedy to use it on Pesach: Maharil. And Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that even where there is no new sieve, it is better not to sift the flour at all. And Chok Yaakov disagrees with him and wrote: for it is known that whenever the flour was not sifted, broken and whole wheat grains are found in it and will forbid the dough or the matzah; rather this sieve after the fact is helped by thorough scrubbing and hagalah; all the more so if one sifts before Pesach, for it was nullified; therefore in a time of need it is proper to do so even l'chatchila. A question: one said casually that his way was to wash the sieve well after Pesach in order to keep it for the next year, and he sifted flour for Pesach in it. And I permitted what he sifted in it after the fact, for even if we say that something stuck from the previous year remained in it, it is hard chametz (chametz nuksheh) and is nullified in sixty even during Pesach. Chok Yaakov; see there.
Cast iron. For sometimes one crumbles bread upon it; also horseradish cut with a chametz knife remains in it in kind. Even after the fact one should forbid on Pesach. But on eve of Pesach it is permitted after the fact, for it is nullified in sixty. Magen Avraham.
Old ones. Not specifically; rather even with a new sack in which one once put flour for Pesach and wishes to wash it to put [flour] in it a second time, one must also untie the seams. Later authorities.
The seams. That is, even also what is at the bottom of the sack, which is the hem. And Magen Avraham wrote: and so one should practice with cloths on which one makes the matzos, if one wishes to use them a second time: turning them over does not help, for chametz seeps and comes out from side to side; rather one must untie the seams to wash well; end of his words. And if one transgressed after the fact and made the matzos on the sheet without any washing: if it is during Pesach, even after the fact one should forbid; but if one turned the sheet over or washed it, even without untying the seams, one should not be stringent after the fact; and likewise regarding the sacks, if one washed them and did not untie the seams and put flour into them, one should not forbid after the fact. Chok Yaakov; see there.
