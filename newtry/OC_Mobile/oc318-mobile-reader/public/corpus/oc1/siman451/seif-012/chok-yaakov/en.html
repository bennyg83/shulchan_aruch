One should not forbid it after the fact. The reason is that we do not say that it carries its absorption throughout the whole utensil; and even if it does carry its absorption, nevertheless, as it absorbed, so it discharges. So too is explained in Yoreh Deah from the words of the Tur and Beis Yosef, and in Pesakim of Mahari, siman 151. Pri Chadash there, s.k. 15, also wrote at length to prove that even ab initio, throughout the year, it is permitted to kasher part of the utensil. Only regarding Pesach one should be stringent ab initio for the reason written by the Bach, that it is impossible that the handles did not also absorb the taste of chametz all year through a first vessel when they were washed to clean them from grime. So too Pri Chadash there, s.k. 16, wrote that this is the main ruling. But after the fact one should not forbid, even if he inserted the handles into the food, because we do not establish a presumption of prohibition, and as it absorbed, so it discharges, as the Rav ruled explicitly there in Yoreh Deah. Even in a case of doubt one should also permit after the fact, as the Issur VeHeter, klal 58, law 21, wrote explicitly in the name of the Tur in Yoreh Deah. All this is unlike the implication of the Taz and Magen Avraham; examine their words carefully.
