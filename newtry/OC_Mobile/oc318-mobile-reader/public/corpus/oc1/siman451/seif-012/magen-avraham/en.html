One should not forbid after the fact. For we do not say that it carries its absorption through all of it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 121, seif 6, cited by Darchei Moshe. This is the language of Issur VeHeter, sha'ar 58: one must perform hagalah on the handle of the frying pan, but it appears that after the fact there is not so much concern, and certainly with a wooden ladle that is ordinarily inserted entirely into the pot, one must perform hagalah on it. Even if it was certainly not inserted, we hold that a utensil that became prohibited in part requires hagalah in its entirety. If part of a spoon was inserted into prohibition, and he performed hagalah on part of it and then inserted it into a cooked dish, the cooked dish is permitted, but the spoon requires hagalah again, and he must insert all of it into the cauldron. It seems to me that this is specifically when he inserted into the cooked dish the part that had undergone hagalah. For even if you say that at first the prohibition spread through all of it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 92, nevertheless we do not say that it emits from the side that was not inserted, as written in siman 94. But if he inserted the side that was not kashered, it is forbidden. This also appears in Yoreh Deah, siman 92, seif 5, regarding a drop of milk that fell on a pot. What the Darchei Moshe wrote, that it does not carry its absorption, means that it does not emit from it, as seems to me. See Yoreh Deah there. In this way the Beis Yosef's questions in Yoreh Deah, siman 121, against the Tur are resolved: the Tur is discussing where he wants to use the part that he kashered, so it is enough to kasher part of it. But if he wants to use its other half, he must kasher that too. See there, and see the end of seif 26. It seems to me that what he wrote, that we do not forbid after the fact, means if he already used them. Initially, however, it is forbidden to use them, even during Pesach, when he cannot perform hagalah.
By pouring. For this is also similar to pouring, since the absorption passed from this side to that side.
