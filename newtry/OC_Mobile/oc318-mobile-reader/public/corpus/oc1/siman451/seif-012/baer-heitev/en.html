And Chok Yaakov wrote that after the fact one should not forbid even if one stuck the handles into the food, for we do not presume a prohibition, and [treat it] like [a doubt of] prohibition; see there. And Magen Avraham ruled that if one stuck in that side that was not hagaled, it is forbidden; see there. And the Taz ruled that regarding a knife one should be stringent even after the fact if one cut with a knife whose handle was not hagaled into a hot cooked dish on Pesach, since certainly one touched the handle while using the knife; which is not so for other vessels; see there. And Magen Avraham wrote: and it appears to me that what he wrote, "they do not forbid after the fact," means if one already used them; but l'chatchila it is forbidden to use them even during Pesach, when one cannot hagal them.
