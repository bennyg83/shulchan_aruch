All utensils, etc. When hot chametz is used in the body of a utensil, we say that the absorption travels through the entire utensil. That reason alone would apply only to metal utensils, because when part of metal is hot the whole becomes hot. Yet the Mechaber says “all utensils,” implying even earthenware and wood. Mordechai explicitly writes that the handle of a wooden ladle must be kashered. We must therefore also be concerned that the handle may at some time have received chametz from a first vessel when it was washed therein to remove grime, or, in the case of a frying pan, from splashes from the body of the utensil.
It should not be forbidden. This is so even for metal utensils, because just as chametz entered the handle through the body of the utensil, it now leaves through hagalah of the utensil’s body. After the fact, we do not maintain the additional concerns mentioned above. It therefore makes no difference whether on Pesach one used the body of the utensil or inserted its handles into the food; in either case the food is permitted, provided the body of the utensil underwent hagalah. If the body was not kashered, however, the food becomes forbidden merely through insertion of the handles. This applies where nothing specific is known. If it is known that the handles were used with hot chametz, kashering only part of the utensil is insufficient even after the fact. The Taz and other authorities therefore prohibit after the fact where a knife handle was not kashered and touched hot food on Pesach, since chametz frequently reaches the handle through hot bread and similar foods.
After the fact. Meaning, if they were already used. Initially, one may not use them before performing hagalah on the handles, even during Pesach when hagalah cannot be performed, as stated below in siman 509:5 in the gloss.
To kasher it by pouring. The handles are touched throughout the year with dirty hands, and pouring also expels what they absorbed through splashes, as explained above. If one knows that the handles were used with chametz in a first vessel, pouring is ineffective even after the fact, and they specifically require hagalah in a first vessel.
