(68) All the vessels, etc. - Because when chametz was used with hot food in the body of the vessel, we say that it carried the absorption throughout it. However, for this reason alone this law would apply only to metal vessels, because when part is hot the whole is hot; but the Mechaber says "all vessels," implying even earthenware and wooden vessels. So too Mordekhai explicitly wrote that a porridge spoon of wood requires hagalah for its handle. Therefore we must also be concerned that the handle perhaps once received chametz through a kli rishon when it was rinsed in it to clean it from filth, and with a pan through sparks that splashed onto it from the body of the vessel.
(69) One should not forbid - Even with metal vessels, because we say that just as the chametz was absorbed into the handle through the body of the vessel, so now it was expelled from it through hagalah of the body of the vessel. Regarding the other concerns we mentioned, we do not establish them after the fact. Therefore there is no difference whether he now used the body of the vessel on Pesach or used the handles, such as inserting them into the food on Pesach; in every case one should not forbid the food from being eaten, since he performed hagalah on the body of the vessel. But if he did not perform hagalah on the body of the vessel, the food is forbidden by insertion of the handles alone. All this is in an ordinary case; but where it is known that the handles were used with hot chametz, kashering part of it is not enough even after the fact. Therefore Taz and other Acharonim were stringent regarding the handle of a knife that was not kashered and touched hot food on Pesach, forbidding even after the fact, because most of the time chametz use reaches the knife handle as well, such as through hot bread and the like.
(70) After the fact - Meaning if one already used them; but initially it is forbidden to use them before performing hagalah on the handles, even during Pesach when he cannot perform hagalah, as below in siman 509 seif 5 in the gloss.
(71) To kasher it by pouring - For throughout the year people touch it with dirty hands, and also to expel the absorption that the handles absorbed through sparks splashing onto them, as above. If he knows that the handles were used with hot chametz in a kli rishon, pouring does not help even after the fact, and it specifically requires hagalah in a kli rishon as well.
