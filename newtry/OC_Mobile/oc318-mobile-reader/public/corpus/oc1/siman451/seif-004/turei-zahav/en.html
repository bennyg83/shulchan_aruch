Such as spits, etc. This is a baraita at the end of Avodah Zarah regarding other prohibitions; but the Rif brought it in the chapter "Kol Shabbat," which implies that for chametz as well one needs libun, since it was used by means of fire. And the Ran wrote: And even though we conclude that whenever it absorbed permitted [food], hagalah suffices for it, one may say that chametz, since its name remains upon it, is called "it absorbed a prohibition." And Beis Yosef wrote, and so it appears to be the view of the Rosh, for he too wrote this baraita in the chapter "Kol Shabbat." And it is difficult to me, for we hold in siman 447, se'if 4, regarding chametz that was nullified before Pesach, that it is permitted; and likewise regarding the time for hagalah in siman 452, we say that it absorbed permitted [food]. And further it is difficult to me, for regarding a spit the Mordechai wrote in the chapter "Kol Shabbat": a spit on which one roasted meat that was not guarded as to its salting before Pesach, and one roasted on it on Pesach, that it is permitted, for it was nullified before Pesach; and so it is written in the Shulchan Aruch in this siman, se'if 27. If so, at the very least hagalah should suffice for it l'chatchila. And further, even regarding a pan in which one fries the body of the chametz itself, it is permitted by hagalah, as is written in siman 451, se'if 11; so why should we require libun for a spit? One must say that this spit is dealing with a case where one roasted actual chametz on it, and therefore its law is like an oven in which one baked bread without liquid, which requires libun, as is written in siman 451, se'if 11, in the name of the Rosh. And "those who are lenient" whom the Rama brought are the Mordechai: he did not write thus regarding spits and grids, but regarding other things that absorbed from permitted [food], such as milk or meat, that they do not need heavy libun, and it is enough until yad soledes from both of their sides, or that one burn straw, for since it absorbed permitted [food], hagalah is enough for them; and even though their use is by means of fire, this [light] libun is not less than hagalah; end quote. And the Rama wrote this also here regarding chametz on Pesach, that it absorbed permitted [food]. But according to what I wrote, that here it means actual chametz was roasted on it, which is like an oven that is without juice, certainly the Mordechai too agrees that complete libun is required, since by law libun is required. And the Rama's approach is also difficult in what he wrote, "and we practice regarding every thing, etc.": why did he hang this on custom? For this is precisely the view of the lenient ones, who distinguish whether its law requires libun or not, as I wrote.
