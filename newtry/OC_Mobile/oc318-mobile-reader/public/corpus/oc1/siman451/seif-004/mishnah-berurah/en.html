(א) Through fire — meaning without water, such as a spit and skewer on which one roasts chametz directly [and even though when roasting on meat they coat it with fat or grease the face with fat, this slight moisture does not prevent the fire from fully dominating it, and it is not comparable to a pan explained below in seif 11]; and since the body of the chametz touches them during roasting and there is no liquid between them to carry chametz taste into the spit and skewer except through fire heat alone — chametz taste is absorbed in them; therefore it is not expelled from them through hagalah in boiling water either, except through fire heat, for as it absorbs so it expels. But if one knows he did not roast chametz on it, only that he roasted meat with unsifted salt — hagalah suffices, even l'chatchila.

(ב) Require libun — I will precede with a brief introduction and will need it in several places. Know that the Gemara teaches that specifically prohibited matter absorbed in a vessel through fire requires libun; but permitted matter absorbed through fire, even if later forbidden — such as kodshim meat roasted on a spit over fire, even if ultimately the absorption in the spit became notar — nevertheless kashering that spit requires only hagalah; and poskim dispute regarding chametz absorbed in a vessel: some say this too is called hittira belia, for when chametz of the roast was absorbed in the spit during the year it was permitted, and therefore hagalah suffices; according to them, all vessels whose use is through fire for Pesach need only hagalah. Others say: specifically notar at the time of absorption — there was no notar on the meat at all, therefore it is called hittira belia; but chametz — at the time the spit was used with chametz, chametz was on the absorption, and now too it is called chametz, only now the prohibition was retroactively aroused on account of this chametz — this is not called hittira belia, for chametz is absorbed within and it is still chametz and falls under issura belia, and libun is required. Most poskim agreed to this, and therefore the Mechaber ruled like them; nevertheless one should not infer from this law to other places, for sometimes where there are other grounds to be lenient we combine also the view of poskim who hold chametz is called hittira belia.

(ח) (34) A tripod requires libun — it is a vessel with three legs on which they place a pot or pan in the oven on the fire all year; if one wants to use it on Pesach he must libun it in fire because sometimes dough spills on it and chametz taste is absorbed through fire. This is only l'chatchila due to chametz stringency — in truth two pots touching each other do not transfer absorption as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 92 seif 7; and one may also say even if spilled it was already burned away since it is constantly on the fire — therefore certainly light libun suffices; and b'dieved even if used without any libun one need not forbid.
