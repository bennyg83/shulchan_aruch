And it is simple — behold we hold in siman 447 seif 4 it is not understood: regarding nullification what of it that its name is upon it and it is called by the name of prohibition — nevertheless we establish it on the law of nullification like all prohibitions; for even if it became mixed orally after midday it is certainly that the name of prohibition is upon it and it is nullified; and since it was nullified and its prohibition ceased, it is entirely permitted; but regarding permitted absorption one may say: since its name is upon it, the name of prohibition is upon it. And likewise regarding nat bar nat — Rashba holds it is not called nat bar nat of permitted absorption of chametz, for its name is upon it. And all this is that it is not called permitted absorption; but it is not more severe than absorption of other prohibitions. Also what Taz wrote "and furthermore I" regarding a pan — behold Taz himself in seif 16 wrote there it is through liquid. Also what he wrote "and it should read this spit deals," etc. — still the difficulty from siman 452 is not settled: regarding nat bar nat it is called permitted absorption; if so, even chametz roasted b'eyna suffices with hagalah. And all this requires study.
Seif 4 in the gloss — light libun such as digging — requires libun; it appears at first glance that even b'dieved it is forbidden; and in my humble opinion this is specifically if one used it b'eyna; but through a pot such as fish and the like — it is no worse than two pots; see Yoreh De'ah siman 97 and Taz; and so in Mishbetzot Zahav here; and therefore if it became absorbed in a gentile's house one may be lenient:
