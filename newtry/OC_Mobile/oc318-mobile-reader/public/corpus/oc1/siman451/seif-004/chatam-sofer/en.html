In my humble opinion see Shach siman 121 s.k. 8 and 88 and Pri To'ar — and none understood the words of Rama in siman 96 as he wrote there; and most things gentiles fry are fat and milk which is itself a prohibition — therefore libun is required. The same applies to our milk pan in which we fried meat — since the absorbing fat is also a prohibition together with milk, a milk pan requires libun; not so a sour pan where chametz was absorbed through fat that is not chametz and is a permissible medium — hagalah helps. These are his words of blessed memory; and from this it is understood that a pan in which we fried with permitted fat — hagalah helps if one wants to kasher it to cook milk in it, it does not require libun because the permitted was absorbed, as above s.k. 11; except we do not practice kashering a meat vessel for milk or vice versa, as below in Magen Avraham siman 509 s.k. 11. But essentially whenever permitted was absorbed, hagalah suffices — as regarding a spit of a chatas at the end of Avodah Zarah; except chametz is called prohibited absorption — therefore his reasoning above is required; and see Chavot Yair Yoreh De'ah siman 111.
