(s.k. 4) Lest, etc. Tosafos in chapter 2 of Pesachim, folio 30b, s.v. "the Torah testified," etc.; and in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, folio 34a, s.v. "that it does not leave," etc.
For in the Gemara it is stated explicitly, etc. For there in Pesachim it asks on that which Rav said: pots on Pesach should be broken; and it is taught: one does not plaster the oven with a fat-tail; and if one plastered, all the bread is forbidden, lest one come to eat it with milk, until one heats the oven; [implying] if heated, it is permitted; if so, pots too should be permitted by heating. And it answers in the answers: both this and that, both Rav and the baraita, deal with earthenware; only the baraita deals with heating from inside, therefore heating helps; but a pot, whose way is to heat it from outside, does not help. And it asks: then heat the pot from inside too and it will be permitted. And it answers: we are concerned lest one be concerned for it, lest it crack, etc.
And let us make an oven of the Mikdash, etc. For we say there: the oven of the Mikdash was of metal; and one might have thought the reason is because of a chatat that they cook in it, and if it were of earthenware one would need to break it each time. And on this it asks: still, make it of earthenware, and after cooking a chatat in it heat it; for heating helps, as is taught: one does not plaster the oven with a fat-tail, etc. And it answers: the reason they did not make it of earthenware is that one does not make service vessels of earthenware.
It refers regarding tumah, etc. Who asked on the view that the Torah testified regarding an earthenware vessel that it never leaves the status of its wall; and if you say: behold it leaves the status of its wall through kilns, etc.; and Rabbeinu Tam says that this is not considered leaving the status of its wall, for since one heated it, it becomes a new vessel, for such is the way, etc. And against Rabbeinu Tam one may ask: an oven that became tamei, why do they break it apart: since it is considered like another vessel, let one heat it. And it appears to the Rashba: regarding a pot that is not normally heated: when one returns it to the kiln, then new faces have come here; but an oven is heated inside every time, and is not like new faces, and needs breaking; end quote of Tosafos.
To make it like a new vessel: so one should read.
And what Tosafos of Avodah Zarah wrote regarding prohibition, etc. And this is the language of Tosafos there on what is written: an earthenware vessel does not leave the status of its wall. And it is puzzling: return them to kilns, etc. Said Rabbeinu Tam: this is not considered leaving; on the contrary, they are considered like another vessel, and new faces have come here. And if you say: since it is considered another vessel, if so an oven and stoves: "he shall tear it down," said the Merciful One: why? Let them return them to kilns. And Rabbeinu Shimshon of Sens said: certainly regarding pots that are not normally heated, etc.; but an oven and stoves, whose prohibition comes through heating, do not leave by returning them to the kiln, and are not called another vessel; end quote. Behold they opened with their difficulty: if so, an oven and stoves: "he shall tear it down," said the Merciful One, etc., which implies their difficulty is regarding tumah, as was asked in Pesachim. But from what they wrote in their answer, "but an oven and stoves whose prohibition comes through heating," it is proven that they came to resolve also regarding prohibition, that heating and kilns do not help. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote that they intended to resolve that which is taught in Pesachim folio 26b: an oven heated with skins of orlah: a new one should be torn down; and since it was finished, that is, through the first heating by means of a prohibition, therefore it has no remedy except tearing down; and it is also difficult: if we say that kilns are called new faces, they should have permitted by means of kilns and not required tearing down. And on this they also answered: since it was finished in prohibition, it has no remedy for that oven. And regarding an oven through heating it is not called new faces, and it remains the first oven; therefore it does not help. But to expel absorbed prohibition, even Tosafos of Avodah Zarah agree that heating helps for an oven.
And then it is not less than a pot, etc. For behold the Gemara asks that even regarding a pot, why did Rav say they should be broken: heat them from inside; and it answers: lest one be concerned for it, as mentioned above. Thus even regarding a pot, were it not for the concern lest one be concerned, heating from inside alone would suffice even without returning it to the kiln.
For in the Gemara we say, etc. Until it is heated, etc.: that is in the baraita that one does not plaster the oven with a fat-tail, etc., aforementioned.
And further it appears to me that a bukhya, etc. That is to say: that the Bach, even though he was stringent regarding an oven that even kilns do not help, nevertheless concluded: and nevertheless it appears that all this is specifically an oven and stoves that are earthenware vessels; but a padila made of bricks and earth: inside heating helps it. And that is a bukhya according to Tosafos.
Rather by libun, that is, heating from inside.
The law of earthen vessels. And I did not find the law of earthen vessels explained. And we do not hold so: stone vessels are explained in se'if 8 as having the law of metal vessels. And Chok Yaakov wrote in siman 461 s.k. 3: what we call Re'er: one should plaster upon it clay the thickness of a finger, both below and on the sides and above; and some practice to libun the Re'er by means of coals; end quote. And in my humble opinion it appears from Magen Avraham's words that a Re'er of new [material] certainly has the law of a pot, and filling with coals does not help, for one is concerned for it; but Peri Chadash at the beginning of siman 461 wrote that it is a dispute of the Rambam and Tur and Rashba; see there.
