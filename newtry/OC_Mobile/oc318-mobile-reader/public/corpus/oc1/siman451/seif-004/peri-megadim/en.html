And if. See Taz and Magen Avraham, letter 4. Regarding fired bricks, specifically libun is required, while a brick dried in the sun has the law of an earthen utensil and is permitted through hagalah. If it was heated only from the outside, even after the fact it is forbidden, because its kashering follows its use: it absorbed chametz on the inside even though it was always heated from the outside. This is also the Gemara's rule regarding a baker's tile, which is heated outside and is forbidden. With metal utensils, filling them with coals must likewise be done on the inside. Heating them from outside, even if that is always their manner of use and they become so hot that sparks fly from them, is insufficient even after the fact. So is the Tur. See below. The same applies to a metal utensil for which hagalah suffices: if he inserted it into the boiling-water vessel only up to its rim and afterward treated the remaining portion, but the water did not reach the inner surface properly, it is forbidden even after the fact. If hot beer was poured on the outside of an earthenware utensil and it absorbed the outermost layer, and he then returned it to our type of oven, even if we say, based on Pesachim 30b, that in such a case it is not repulsive because an outermost-layer absorption is easily expelled, and see Magen Avraham, letter 3, nevertheless it requires study after the fact, where this was done deliberately, whether food cooked in it on Pesach must be prohibited. Initially, Eliyah Rabbah, letter 3, already writes in the name of Issur VeHeter that the custom is not to rely even on returning it to the kiln. After the fact, or in a pressing situation when he has no other utensils, he may rely upon firing it again in a kiln.
