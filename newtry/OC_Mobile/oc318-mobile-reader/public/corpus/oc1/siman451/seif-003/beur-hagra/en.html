(א) Seif 3 — knives we kasher. — Gemara there; and in the first chapter of Chullin 8b a knife requires libun, and so in Tosefta Avodah Zarah: spits, skewers, and knives — one whitens them in fire; Rabbenu Tam explained in Tosafot Chullin there s.v. that was whitened, and at the end of Avodah Zarah 76b s.v. he said to distinguish between chametz which absorbs permitted and other prohibitions which absorb prohibited, as he distinguished; and there in Gemara regarding spits and skewers, see there; and Ran wrote that such is Raavad's view, and Magen Avraham wrote that such is Rambam's view — that in Laws of Chametz he wrote knives through hagalah, and in Pesachim 37. Magen Avraham wrote through libun, and therefore in Laws of Chametz he omitted that spits and skewers require libun, for according to his view they too are through hagalah, and it is not his way to write what is not explicit in Gemara; and further Rabbenu Tam explained there and there to distinguish between a small knife and a large knife on which one roasts meat and whose use is via fire, and he brings proof from Yerushalmi end of Avodah Zarah: a knife — thrust it three times in the ground and it suffices; R' Bun in the name of R' Yehudah — this that you might say regarding a small knife, but regarding a large knife it requires libun; and Tosafot rejected his proof from Yerushalmi — it deals with cutting cold with it, and cold — there is no distinction between their use via fire or via hot, rather he explained that a large knife is apt to have crevices, and therefore neither scraping nor hagalah helps; and R' Baruch reconciled the proof, see there; and accordingly one may say there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions, and so too Rif and Rosh in chapter Kol Sha'ah who brought the baraita of spits and skewers regarding chametz; and Ran wrote that such is Ramban's view — that chametz is not called permitted absorption since chametz is placed on it, unlike kodshim which is not called notar before its prohibition time, and one must distinguish between large and small; and this even for the first answer regarding a small knife — since its use is not via fire, libun is not required as written in the baraita; and so too Rashba — that in any event for all a knife needs only hagalah, and he deleted from the text of Avodah Zarah 'knives' since in Tosefta it is not found; and Ran and Tosafot; however Rabbenu Yitzchak ben Meir wrote that even a small one requires libun, since sometimes one fixes the wick of fat and turns meat over coals; and Rabbenu Tam rejected his view since its primary use is not via fire, and so too Ran; and proof — that in every vessel we follow the majority of its use, like cups and small jugs — even though sometimes they are used in hot, nevertheless we follow its primary use and rinse them; and Beit Yosef wrote that according to Tosafot's conclusion one must distinguish between that of Pesachim and that of Chullin and Tosefta — between a small one that has no crevices etc.; and the Mechaber's words appear to require study — for Yoreh De'ah siman 121 writes both large and small, and even if it has no crevices, libun is required to cut hot with it; and here he wrote regarding knives in general etc., and afterward wrote in seif 7 regarding vessels etc., such as spits and skewers require libun; and the main point is that he ruled like Rif and Rosh that there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions, for otherwise the entire siman would be hidden and libun would not apply at all to chametz; and even though Rabbenu Tam distinguishes between small and large, Yoreh De'ah is concerned to rule like Rabbenu Yitzchak ben Meir stringently in general, for here he ruled like Rabbenu Tam and Ran as written in seif 6: all vessels we follow etc.; and furthermore that even for Rabbenu Yitzchak ben Meir libun is not required except the tip of the knife as Rosh wrote in Avodah Zarah, except stringently he wrote thus; unlike a knife of chametz of a Jew — its manner of use is not via fire even in a large knife, and therefore he wrote here in general:
