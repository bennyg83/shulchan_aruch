Se'if 3. Knives: one hagals them. The Gemara there. And in the first chapter of Chullin 8b it requires libun for a knife; and likewise in the Tosefta of Avodah Zarah: the spits and the grids and the knives, one libuns them in the fire. Rabbeinu Tam answered in Tosafos of Chullin there s.v. "that he libuned it," and at the end of Avodah Zarah 76b s.v. "he said," to distinguish between chametz, which absorbed permitted [food], and other prohibitions, which absorbed a prohibition; and as he distinguished there in the Gemara regarding spits and grids; see there. And the Ran wrote that so is the view of the Ra'avad; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that so is the view of the Rambam, who in the laws of chametz wrote the knives by hagalah, and in chapter 16 of the laws of forbidden foods wrote by libun; and therefore in the laws of chametz he omitted that of spits and grids that need libun, for according to his view they too [are] by hagalah, and it is not his way to write what is not explicit in the Gemara. And Rabbeinu Tam further answered there and there to distinguish between a small knife and a large knife on which one roasts meat, whose use is by means of fire; and he brings proof from the Yerushalmi at the end of Avodah Zarah: a knife, one thrusts it three times into the ground and that suffices; R' Bun in the name of R' Yehuda: this was said regarding a small knife, but a large knife needs libun. And Tosafos rejected his proof, that the Yerushalmi deals with cutting cold, and regarding cold there is no distinction whether use is by fire or by hot water; rather this is the meaning: a large knife tends to have grooves, and therefore neither polishing nor hagalah helps. And R' Baruch resolves the proof; see there. And accordingly it is possible to say there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions; and so is the view of the Rif and Rosh in the chapter "Kol Shaah," who brought the baraita of spits and grids regarding chametz. And the Ran wrote that so is the view of the Ramban, that chametz is not called "permitted absorption," since the name chametz is upon it; which is not so regarding consecrated foods, which are not called leftover before the time of their prohibition; and one must distinguish between large and small; and this even according to the first answer regarding a small knife: since its use is not by fire, it does not need libun, as is written there in the baraita; and so wrote the Rashba, that in every case a knife needs only hagalah, and they erased from the reading of Avodah Zarah "the knives," for so they are not in the Tosefta; and see the Ran and Terumat HaDeshen. But Rivam wrote that even a small one needs libun, for sometimes one fixes a tallow wick and turns meat on coals; and Rabbeinu Tam rejected his reasoning, since its primary use is not by fire; and so wrote the Ran; and a proof: that for every vessel we follow its majority use, like cups and flasks: even though sometimes one may use them hot, nevertheless we follow the primary use and rinse them. And Beis Yosef wrote that according to the conclusion of Tosafos one should distinguish between that of Pesachim and that of Chullin and the Tosefta: between a small one that has no grooves, etc. And the words of the Shulchan Aruch at first glance require study: for in Yoreh De'ah siman 121 he wrote between large and small, and even if it has no grooves it needs libun to cut boiling with it; and here he wrote in general "knives," etc., and afterward in se'if 7: vessels, etc., such as spits and grids, need libun. And the main point is that he ruled like the Rif and Rosh, that there is no distinction between chametz and other prohibitions; for otherwise the whole siman collapses, and libun would not apply at all regarding chametz. And even though Rabbeinu Tam distinguishes between small and large, there in Yoreh De'ah he is concerned for Rivam's ruling as a mere stringency; for here he ruled like Rabbeinu Tam and the Ran, as is written in se'if 6: every vessel, we follow, etc. And further, even according to Rivam, only the edge of the knife needs libun, as the Rosh wrote in Avodah Zarah; only as a stringency he wrote thus; which is not so for a chametz knife of a Jew, whose way of use is not by fire even with a large knife; and therefore he wrote generally here.
And a kli rishon, etc. Shabbos 42: the pan and the pot, etc.; and there in the Gemara they held, etc., that in a kli rishon, etc., it cooked, etc.; and they concluded that even in a kli rishon it did not cook; and if it is on the fire, it is impossible. Ran.
