One hagals them. And specifically a Pesach knife, or from meat to milk or the reverse; which is not so for knives of prohibition, which need libun even if they have no grooves, wherever one wishes to use them hot, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, se'if 7. And see Peri Chadash there, who ruled that even for knives of prohibition, hagalah suffices even l'chatchila. And Chok Yaakov wrote: it appears to me that the main [ruling] l'chatchila is like the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch there; and so wrote Issur VeHeter; but after the fact one should not forbid if one hagaled and used it hot.
Boiling. And Magen Avraham wrote: and to stick the knife into it; but one should not hagal it by means of a libuned stone.
On a grindstone. And the same applies with any thing that removes the rust: it suffices (only that the Rambam wrote that sharpening on a millstone helps in place of libun; and see Issur VeHeter s.k. 16). The Bach and other later authorities wrote: since one must also hagal their handles, therefore if the handle is fixed with small nails, hagalah does not help, for there are many grooves at the place of the nails; and the same applies wherever there are two parts in their handles. And the same applies if part of the handle is of horn, or if the handle is attached to the iron with glue: one should not hagal it, for they are concerned lest they crack; and libun too does not apply to their handles, unless it is entirely iron. And since most knives are of this sort, therefore the paytan well established: if it is possible to make them new, they are praiseworthy; see Chok Yaakov and in the Taz.
Rust. Meaning that one need not sharpen except enough to remove the rust; but if one sharpens it well in a way that removes some of the iron, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, it is like libun; and see there in the gloss. And "rust" means what we call Rost in the vernacular. But a black or red stain in some place on the vessel, and especially on cauldrons and frying pans, is of no consequence, and one may hagal; and for a good measure, one should libun that place if scraping is impossible. Taz; see there.
Libun. And with libun one need not remove the rust, for the fire will burn everything.
A remedy. And if one opens the seams and scrapes them well, it is possible that it is permitted. Magen Avraham.
