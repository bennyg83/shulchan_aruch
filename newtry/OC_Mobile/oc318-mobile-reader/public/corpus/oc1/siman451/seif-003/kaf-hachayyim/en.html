In which chametz was used, etc. Regarding new utensils purchased in the marketplace, even earthenware utensils, one need not suspect that they were used, because people do not ordinarily use utensils sold as new. Metal utensils purchased in a non-Jew's home, however, must be kashered out of doubt with boiling water or fire, according to their use, unless one can identify them as certainly new. Issur VeHeter, rule 58, law 46; Chok Yaakov, subsection 2; Mekor Chaim, subsection 2. After kashering with boiling water or fire, they must be immersed in a mikveh even though they are old, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, siman 121, se'if 1. See there. See also siman 122, se'if 11. Nevertheless, clear glass utensils may be purchased from a non-Jew's home even if the non-Jew does not make an incidental statement that they are new and does not select one from among other utensils. Since they are transparent, it is certain that they have not yet been used. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 5.
