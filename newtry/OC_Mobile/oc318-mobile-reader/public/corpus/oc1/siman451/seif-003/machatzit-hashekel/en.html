(s.k. 3) Even, etc. And Darkhei Moshe wrote that according to the Tur's view, who wrote in the language of the Mechaber:
Not like Beis Yosef, who wrote that since the Rambam forbids even in a kli sheni, and one may say that the Tur too agrees with him; and one must say that what the Tur mentioned as pouring, according to Beis Yosef, is not precise.
And it appears to me that even the Rambam, etc. Not like Beis Yosef; and Darkhei Moshe too agrees with him in this: that in any case the Rambam forbids a kli sheni: that is not so.
If so, it necessarily deals with use through pouring; for we hold: as their use, so their kashering, as is written in se'if 5; and it is impossible that it deals with an actual kli sheni, for then kashering would also suffice in a kli sheni.
And regarding earthenware he also wrote, for example bowls; if so, that too means their use is through pouring, and not a kli sheni; even though he wrote, this is his language: every earthenware vessel used with chametz, etc., whether a kli sheni, for example bowls; end quote. He also calls pouring a kli sheni, as above.
And so is Beis Yosef's view, who omitted the law of a kli sheni; thus he retracted and holds specifically use through pouring. From this there is no proof from what the Rama wrote, "and some forbid," which implies that the Mechaber permits it. For it is known that such is the Rama's way: sometimes if it is not explicit in the Mechaber's words he writes upon it the language "and some say"; rather, the proof is that Beis Yosef did not bring the Rambam's view that forbids even a kli sheni. And even though in his book Beis Yosef he wrote on the Tur's language, which is the language of the Shulchan Aruch, that the Tur too holds that a kli sheni is forbidden: that is because according to Beis Yosef the Rambam forbids a kli sheni; if so, in order not to create a dispute between the Rambam and the Tur, we say that what the Tur wrote is not precise, and the same applies to a kli sheni. But in the Shulchan Aruch Beis Yosef should not have left it obscure and given room to err that pouring specifically is meant; rather, in truth he retracted and holds that the Tur mentioned pouring precisely, and the Rambam too holds so, as Magen Avraham wrote.
And see above in siman 447, se'if 3, in Magen Avraham s.k. 9, where Magen Avraham also wrote there that we have not found anyone who is stringent regarding a kli sheni on Pesach.
For behold the Rashba, etc. That is to say: even though we have not found anyone who is more stringent on Pesach than regarding other prohibitions to forbid a kli sheni, nevertheless behold the Rashba, etc. And what Chok Yaakov wrote: how can one say that Beis Yosef holds that a kli sheni does not forbid: behold he wrote in se'if 5: a vessel that was used, etc., and if their use is in a kli sheni, their kashering is in a kli sheni. And necessarily that means an actual kli sheni; where use is through pouring, he wrote its law afterward: a vessel that one uses through pouring, etc. Magen Avraham himself already resolved this at the end of s.k. 13, where he wrote on this: and all this is l'chatchila. And see what I wrote in se'if 1. That is to say: Beis Yosef holds that l'chatchila one should also hagal a vessel used through a kli sheni; but here, dealing with earthenware that has no remedy by hagalah, it is like after the fact; and therefore Magen Avraham holds that Beis Yosef relies on the main law that a kli sheni needs no kashering. And nevertheless Magen Avraham holds that one must reconsider the matter and be stringent according to the Rashba's words; and see Peri Chadash of Yoreh De'ah siman 68, who is stringent even all year regarding a kli sheni.
