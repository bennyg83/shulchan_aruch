Knives. Both large and small knives, even if used with hot chametz throughout the year, require only hagalah for Pesach. Although Shulchan Aruch rules in Yoreh Deah 121 that knives that absorbed other prohibitions require libun before being used with hot food, here hagalah alone is permitted initially. This is because some authorities hold that even utensils that absorbed chametz directly through fire require only hagalah, and several authorities maintain that knives are not considered utensils whose primary use is through fire. Even if a knife was once used over fire, such as to hold a piece of pastry over the flame, its usual use is not through fire, and we follow its majority use, as stated below in seif 6. Nevertheless, one who is able should ideally purchase new knives for Pesach. The later authorities further write that although knives that absorbed other prohibitions initially require libun, after the fact, if one performed hagalah and used them with hot food, the food is not forbidden. Knives used for meat may be converted to dairy use through hagalah, but the common custom is to prohibit this. Chasam Sofer, responsum 110, writes that when one performs hagalah for Pesach, he permits changing a knife from meat to dairy use or the reverse, since the kashering was not undertaken for that purpose.
While it is still boiling. The water must be actively bubbling, as stated below in siman 452; see there. Hagalah should be performed by inserting the knife into the boiling water. It should not be done by placing a glowing stone on the knife and pouring boiling water over it, as discussed below in seif 6 in the gloss, because inside a vessel the stone rests securely, unlike upon a knife.
With a whetstone. The same applies to any other means that removes the rust.
All rust. The passage concerning kashering the vessels of Midian states, “Only the gold,” meaning that hagalah works only when there is no rust and only the gold itself remains. Where rust is present, there is concern that a trace of chametz remains beneath it, and hagalah does not remove an actual substance. This applies only to rust with tangible substance, called rust, which produces particles when scraped and may conceal actual prohibited matter beneath it. If the rust is merely discoloration, such as black or red stains on the inside of a utensil, called spots, it does not matter. Likewise, the stains that form on tin vessels, called earth spots, do not impede hagalah because they have no substance and are merely discoloration.
If it has indentations. For this reason, the later authorities agree that hagalah is ineffective for a knife whose handle is fastened with small nails, because many indentations surround the nails. Certainly this is so if the handle consists of two joined pieces with a narrow gap between them into which the blade is inserted, since that area generally cannot be cleaned thoroughly. At times there is also a flaw between the blade and handle that cannot be cleaned unless the handle is removed. If the blade is attached to the handle with glue, hagalah is likewise ineffective, because the glue is damaged by boiling water and there is concern that he will spare it and fail to boil the water properly. The same applies to a horn handle, since horn is damaged by boiling water and one may spare it.
It requires libun. He should place burning coals upon that area. For libun, the rust need not be removed, because the fire consumes everything.
At the site of the indentations. This should be done before hagalah. If it was not done beforehand, it should be done afterward.
It cannot be kashered through hagalah. This is because the inside cannot be cleaned thoroughly unless the seams are opened and cleaned well; if that is done, even hagalah is unnecessary. A narrow-necked utensil containing rust that cannot be removed cannot be kashered through hagalah. The later authorities also write that wherever hagalah is required, shaving away the surface with a craftsman’s tool is ineffective, because absorbed taste travels through the wall from side to side. Such a tool helps only where mere peeling is required.
