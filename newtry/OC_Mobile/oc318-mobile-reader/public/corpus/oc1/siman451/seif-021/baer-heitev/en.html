The barrels. And one must remove the bottoms of the barrels in order to scrape them well: so wrote the Rokeach. But the later authorities agreed that wooden beer barrels should not be hagaled at all; and likewise wooden vessels in which grooves are common, and vessels that held hard liquor: hagalah does not help at all, for they are worse than a yeast-house and charoses. And the Taz wrote: nevertheless if one cooked them well in water with ashes until the smell was entirely removed, it is permitted to hagal them. However, a barrel used only with mead may be permitted by hagalah. See Magen Avraham.
And after the fact. The reason is that beer in wine is naten taam lifgam; and according to what we hold, that naten taam lifgam is forbidden on Pesach, if so one should not be lenient except to pour from it before Pesach into a Pesach barrel; and specifically with wine, for the beer gives it a detrimental taste; but with mead one should not be lenient unless the barrel is not ben yomo; but with a vessel of hard liquor, even if not ben yomo, it is forbidden, for it is a beneficial transfer of taste. Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov; see there; and see Taz.
