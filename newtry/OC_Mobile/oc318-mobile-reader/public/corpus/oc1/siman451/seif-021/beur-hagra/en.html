Se'if 21. Barrels, etc., are permitted by hagalah or, etc. As is written regarding wine of libation in Avodah Zarah 33a: the practical law: fill them, etc.; and there 75a: one who wishes to purify them immediately hagals them in boiling water; and they said there 34a and in the chapter "Kol Shaah" there what is written from wine of libation; and if you say chametz, etc.: there its use, etc. And if so, regarding chametz, a thing whose use is cold is equal to wine of libation. And even though hagalah does not help for earthenware specifically when its use is hot, as the Rosh wrote on the mishnah there 44b: thrust them and boil; regarding earthenware pouring without a peeling does not help; therefore he commanded to hagal them, for then a peeling is not needed; and as is written there 33a that even pouring helps for earthenware; and all the more so hagalah, as the Rosh wrote in chapter 3 of Shabbos, se'if 10: that hagalah is better than pouring, for pouring does not help for a thing whose use is hot even regarding other vessels. And in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 2 of Pesachim, R' Ammi ruled regarding those jars of kutach: fill them with water for three days, day after day. And all the more so hagalah, as above; and like Hagahot Asheri chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, se'if 25, s.v. "in its use," etc.
And after the fact. There in Hagahot Asheri; and see Magen Avraham in the name of the Mordechai. And what the Mordechai wrote, "since it absorbed permitted [food]," etc., means as is written in the chapter "Gi Hillel," siman 677: that we estimate against its entirety; and since sometimes it absorbs and sometimes expels, one cannot establish the matter, etc.; meaning that even if one knows how much comes out of it, nevertheless it returns and absorbs and the absorbed becomes a neveilah and returns and expels; which is not so regarding permitted [food], for which "a piece becomes a neveilah" is impossible, as Tosafos wrote in Avodah Zarah 76a s.v. "in." But regarding a vessel of permitted [food], etc. And Beis Yosef wrote: and it has already been explained that according to most poskim chametz is not called "permitted absorption," and therefore they omitted it in the Shulchan Aruch. And likewise naten taam lifgam: we hold in siman 447, se'if 10, that it is forbidden; and see Magen Avraham; and see Terumat HaDeshen, siman 201; see there.
