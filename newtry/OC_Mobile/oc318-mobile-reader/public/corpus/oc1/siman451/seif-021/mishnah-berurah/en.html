(116) That they put barley beer in them: that is to say, even if it was in them for several days [Taz and other later authorities].
(117) Permitted by hagalah: for what we say, that an earthenware vessel never leaves the status of its wall, is specifically when it absorbed by means of fire; but when cold, hagalah suffices for them. And according to this reason, even hot use is permitted in it after hagalah or pouring, even if it is ben yomo from the beer's absorption. And some say it is permitted only when it is not ben yomo, for then it is naten taam lifgam.
(118) Three days: that is, that one fill it with water, even cold, to all its edges, and leave it inside for twenty-four continuous hours or more; and afterward pour the water from it and fill it with other water and leave it inside for twenty-four continuous hours; and afterward pour those from it and fill it with other water and leave them inside for twenty-four continuous hours. And these three days after a day need not be continuous; rather even scattered, as is explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 135. And even when pitched, pouring helps, as is found in Yoreh De'ah. And know that the Shach's view in Yoreh De'ah siman 135 s.k. 33 is that regarding other prohibitions one should not be lenient through pouring for three days if the prohibition was pickled in a permitted vessel day after day; for we hold pickling is like cooking, and regarding cooking according to all opinions pouring does not help even for other vessels that are not earthenware; see there.
(119) The barrel: and one must remove the bottoms of the barrel if it is of wood, to scrape well inside in all the cracks; and also lees stuck to the bottoms of the barrel are common; and hagalah and pouring help only to expel what is absorbed inside, but not for chametz that is in kind. And if it is of earthenware, one must open the mouth of the barrel so wide that one can put one's hand into it and scrub it well inside on all sides. However, the later authorities agreed that one should be stringent not to use at all a wooden beer barrel in which grooves are common, and also it is impossible to scrape between the boards so that no mashehu of filth remain between board and board, and that is full chametz and remains in kind. And see Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the Perishah: if one used a beer barrel and did not remove the bottoms and scrape well, it is forbidden even after the fact, for hagalah does not help for what is in kind; but if one opened one of its bottoms and scraped well inside, hagalah helps after the fact; and so Chayei Adam agreed; see there where he lengthened in siman 125 on this. And all this is regarding a barrel of beer or other chametz liquid; but a barrel of mead may be permitted by hagalah even though the honey was cooked in a cauldron in which beer was cooked within a day.
(120) One should do it in this way, etc.: and the later authorities wrote that vessels that held hard liquor: hagalah does not help them at all, for smell and taste remain in them afterward too; but if one cooked them well in water with ashes until the smell was entirely removed, it is permitted to hagal them afterward; and specifically for vessels that are entirely open and one can put one's hand inside to scrape well and check in all the cracks that no substance cling to it; but if it is not of such a sort, it does not help even if one cooks with ashes several times. And also we require that the vessels be made of one piece, or that they are of tin vessels and other kinds of metal; for if they are made of many boards like our vessels, one should not be lenient to hagal them even if one cooked them with ashes several times, for it is impossible that filth not remain between board and board, as above.
(121) If they put in them: and left them there day after day, for it is pickling and the taste of the beer entered it; even so it is permitted, as below; for otherwise it is obvious that it is permitted.
(122) Wine or honey: see in Magen Avraham and Chok Yaakov that there is a distinction between wine and honey: regarding wine, even if the barrel is ben yomo from the beer's absorption, it is also permitted after the fact, for beer in wine is naten taam lifgam; and regarding mead it is not lifgam unless the vessel is not ben yomo from the beer's absorption; but if it was ben yomo and the mead stayed in it day after day, even after the fact it is forbidden to drink it on Pesach. But many later authorities agreed that even regarding mead it is permitted even when ben yomo, for the reason that pickling is not called until after staying day after day, and then the absorption in the vessel becomes lifgam and is permitted after the fact. And know that in every case this after-the-fact permission is only where one poured the wine and honey from the barrel before Pesach and put them into a Pesach vessel; for the wine and honey were already permitted before Pesach, and before Pesach naten taam lifgam is permitted; but if the wine and honey remained in the barrel into Pesach, it is forbidden, for in our lands we practice to forbid naten taam lifgam on Pesach, as above in siman 447, se'if 10 in the gloss. And the poskim wrote that if one put into it something sharp like vinegar, even if it was not ben yomo, it is forbidden, for through sharpness it becomes beneficial. The poskim further agreed that if the vessel held hard liquor, it is forbidden after the fact even if it is not ben yomo, for our eyes see that it gives a beneficial taste. And behold from the Levush it is implied that even if one hagaled it, what one put into it is forbidden, and even if it was not ben yomo, because our eyes see that taste and smell remain in it even after hagalah; but if no smell of hard liquor is sensed in it, hagalah helps after the fact not to forbid what one put into it; and nevertheless as soon as one remembers, one must pour the wine into another vessel.
(123) Only that one rinsed them well, etc.: and he deals with a case where there is no concern of chametz in kind: that the mouth of the earthenware barrel was wide above and one put one's hand into it and scraped well on its sides and bottoms so that no lees remained there; and regarding a wooden barrel, when one opened the bottoms and scraped well, as above.
