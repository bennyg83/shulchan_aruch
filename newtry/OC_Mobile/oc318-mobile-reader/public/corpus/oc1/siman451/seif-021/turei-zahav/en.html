The cited ruling. The Taz explains the kashering of utensils for Pesach, according to their normal mode of use, with distinctions between hagalah, libbun, pouring from a kli rishon, soaking, cleaning away visible chametz, and after-the-fact cases. He clarifies the underlying sugyos and when the stringency is required by law or only as the preferred practice. In particular, it addresses barrels used for beer, mead, wine, or spirits, earthenware and its special stringencies.

And after the fact if, etc. The reason for this permission is in the Mordechai, chapter Kol Sha'ah: the taste of beer was nullified in sixty before Pesach, and it does not reawaken on Pesach. One should not be stringent to measure against the whole barrel because we do not know how much emerges from it, for here it is different, since it absorbed a permitted item; therefore we measure according to what it emits, and it is nullified in sixty. Furthermore, anything absorbed in a utensil becomes impaired overnight, etc. R. Yitzchak explains that noten taam lifgam on Pesach is permitted. The Beis Yosef wrote that most poskim do not hold that this claim of permitted absorption helps regarding chametz. I am astonished, for I already proved in this siman, seif 4, that we rule like this view. What the Beis Yosef wrote in this siman regarding spits and grates, that since the Rosh brought the baraisa that they require libbun, this shows that he does not hold of this claim, in my humble opinion there is no proof, for there it discusses where actual chametz was used on the spit, as I wrote there.
