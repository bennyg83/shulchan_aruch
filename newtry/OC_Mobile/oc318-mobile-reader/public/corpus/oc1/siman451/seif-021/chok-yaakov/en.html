And the hagalah of barrels. One must remove the bottoms of the barrels in order to clean them well. So wrote the Rokeach, siman 262. However, the later authorities agreed that a wooden beer barrel should not be kashered by hagalah at all. So too wooden utensils in which indentations are common, and utensils that held liquor, hagalah is not effective at all, for it is worse than a leavening vessel and a charoses vessel. The Taz wrote that nevertheless, if one boiled them well in water with ash until the smell disappeared entirely, it is permitted to perform hagalah on them.
And after the fact, if they put wine in them. This is the wording of the Mordechai: the reason is that the taste of the beer is nullified in sixty before Pesach and does not reawaken on Pesach. One should not be stringent to measure against the entire barrel because we do not know how much comes out of it, for here it is different, since it absorbed permitted matter; therefore we measure only according to what it would discharge, and it is nullified in sixty. Moreover, all that is absorbed is considered spoiled taste, and beer in wine also gives a spoiled taste; end quote. In truth, the first reason is very puzzling. Even if we say that the main ruling follows the poskim who say that chametz is considered absorption of permitted matter, what of it? Since we do not know how much comes out of it, we certainly measure against the whole utensil. For regarding meat and milk, which is also considered absorption of permitted matter, nevertheless when a dairy spoon is inserted into a meat pot or the reverse, everyone agrees that we measure against the whole spoon because we do not know how much comes out of it. What difference is there between absorption of prohibition and absorption of permitted matter? Therefore it appears to me that the main reason for leniency is only the second reason. According to what we hold, that spoiled taste is forbidden on Pesach, one should therefore be lenient only to pour from it before Pesach into a Pesach barrel. So ruled the Magen Avraham, s.k. 40. This applies specifically to wine, in which beer gives a spoiled taste; but with mead one should not be lenient unless the barrel is not ben yomo. But with liquor utensils, even if not ben yomo, it is forbidden. This is the Magen Avraham; see there.
