(s.k. 21) Permitted, etc. What difference is fat to me, etc.? Even though a frying pan of other prohibitions: Beis Yosef ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, se'if 4, that it needs libun. The Shach wrote there that it is implied from Beis Yosef's words that regarding chametz, since it absorbed permitted [food], we are lenient; and accordingly the same applies to a meat frying pan when one comes to kasher it to use for milk or the reverse: for it too absorbed permitted [food], and hagalah likewise suffices, similar to chametz. And he wrote in the name of Responsa Maharam Azzulai, siman 96, who wrote to distinguish another way: since as its use, so its kashering: if its use is by means of hot water, if so a prohibition fell into it by means of fire and by means of hot water, then its kashering too is by means of hot water, namely hagalah, so that it will again expel also by means of fire and by means of hot water. But when its use is by means of fire without hot water, if so it absorbed by means of fire alone; therefore its kashering too is by libun, and it will expel by fire alone. Therefore a frying pan of chametz in which one fries with fat or with milk: whatever that thing may be, that thing itself is not chametz; even though there is absorption of chametz in it, nevertheless it is as if fried in water; even though the water received taste from the chametz thing now being fried: of what consequence? In any case the chametz was absorbed into the frying pan by means of fire and by means of the fat, which itself is permitted. And its law regarding what was absorbed in it by means of fire and by means of the fat is as if absorbed by means of fire and by means of water, for which hagalah suffices, as above. Which is not so for a non-Jew's frying pan in which the non-Jew fried: even if he fried with fat or milk or whatever: most things that non-Jews fry in are themselves prohibited; if so, that thing in which he fries, namely fat and the like, was absorbed into the frying pan by means of fire alone. For example, one fries meat by means of fat: the fat too is prohibited; and even if there was no meat in the frying pan, only prohibited fat alone, one would need libun; so why should it be lessened by there also being meat? Therefore a non-Jew's frying pan needs libun. And he wrote that accordingly the same applies to a dairy frying pan in which one fried dough in milk and comes to kasher it to use for meat, or a meat frying pan in which one fried with fat and comes to kasher it to use for dairy: even though it absorbed permitted [food], nevertheless it needs libun, for it resembles a non-Jew's frying pan; end of his words. And see in Magen Avraham siman 509 s.k. 11 the reason for the world's custom that they do not hagal dairy vessels to use for meat or the reverse.
