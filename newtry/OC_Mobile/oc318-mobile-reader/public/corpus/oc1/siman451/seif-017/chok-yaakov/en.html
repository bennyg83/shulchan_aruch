Not to use on Pesach. The Kol Bo wrote two opinions. One opinion is that one may use it with matzah even hot, by means of an intervening cloth. Another says that one may not use it at all; rather, it requires coating with clay. So wrote the Semak, siman 222, that this is correct, and the Rokeach, siman 250. So too the Rama ruled above at the end of siman 442; see there.
Even by means of hagalah. In Terumas HaDeshen, siman 131, it is explained that even if one wants to peel it with an artisan's tool and afterward perform hagalah on it, this too is not effective. So also is implied in Mahari Weil, siman 193. The reason is that it is impossible to clean it out properly. For this reason as well, we do not perform hagalah on utensils that constantly held flour, chametz liquids, or the like. The law of kashering barrels will be explained nearby. This applies specifically to wood; but for metal and tin utensils, the practice is to perform hagalah in every case. However, if before Pesach they held water in it for some time, it is permitted to perform hagalah on it. So is the agreement of the later authorities.
