(א) Require hagalah — meaning by essential law; but the Mechaber already wrote above in siman 442 that one should not rely on what they clean and wash them in hot water — and this is from the side of the good and proper [Bach above siman 442, and likewise Maamar Mordechai; he added that this is Rama's intent in the gloss]. Magen Avraham wrote there that it deals when the kneading trough is made of many boards and because it is impossible to clean between the cracks; but here Tur and Shulchan Aruch deal when made of one piece. Accordingly, from Rama's conclusion "and this was already explained above" it implies it deals in one manner; and therefore Bach's answer is required and from Eshel Avraham. Eshel Avraham agreed there to the law of Magen Avraham's answer that when made of one piece it is permitted to keep after scraping well; nevertheless regarding use on Pesach he is stringent l'chatchila even after hagalah — and according to him Rama's law here applies even when made of one piece. Likewise Magen Avraham who wrote the reason for the custom is because we practice like the view that hagalah does not help for a yeast container — it is obvious that even one piece, although one can scrape well, nevertheless absorption does not exit through hagalah.

(ב) And the custom not to use — see Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot who is uncertain perhaps one may permit through peeling with artisan tools and hagalah like a mortar above; at first glance I wondered — behold in Chok Yaakov he brings in the name of Terumat HaDeshen and Mahari Weil to be stringent even through peeling; but afterward I saw Terumat HaDeshen and Mahari Weil did not say this except regarding something whose use is through fire such as a shovel; and Levush implies he is stringent even through hagalah in all cases. In my humble opinion it appears one should decide: if it is one piece and has no cracks, one may permit through peeling and hagalah; if made of several pieces, one may not permit even through peeling.

(ג) On Pesach — see Mishna Berurah the reason because it is impossible to clean; and so several Acharonim; and from this metal vessels that can be cleaned are permitted through hagalah — and so truly Chayei Adam and Peri Chadash and Mekor Chayyim. However, it requires study — they should have been concerned for those who are stringent above seif 16 that Rama wrote there that the practice is l'chatchila to whiten; and perhaps a metal kneading trough that is wide and thin is hard to whiten because it is apt to break — therefore we are lenient with hagalah, similar to Rama's leniency there for wooden vessels because libun is impossible; but where it will not break, it is proper and good l'chatchila to be stringent; and so appears in Taz s.k. 27; and see Peri Megadim who also wrote that according to Magen Avraham one should be stringent even for metal.
