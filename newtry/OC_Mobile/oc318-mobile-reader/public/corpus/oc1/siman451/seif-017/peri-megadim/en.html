So too the Shach wrote there, os 18, that the Bach's question here in seif 11 on the Tur's words is not difficult, for initially even the handles must be kashered; see there. If so, against whom is the Rav's difficulty? He permits after the fact, while the Mechaber there is stringent even after the fact, because wherever part of it becomes hot it carries absorption through all of it but does not emit from all of it when part of it is heated. There too, as practical halacha, the Taz was stringent like the Mechaber and Rashba, that with metal utensils, if part becomes hot it carries absorption through all of it, and to kasher it, it does not emit from all of it; even after the fact one should forbid it. See there. One must say that there the Taz retracted from what he wrote here. Here he holds that even initially the Tur maintains that it is enough to kasher part of it as in its normal use; but here, initially, one must perform hagalah on all the handles of the utensils, even wooden ones, lest hot chametz spilled upon them, and therefore pouring suffices, as in their normal use. However, there it implies that he understands the Rav to permit initially to perform hagalah on part of it, and about this he wondered why he was lenient. But after the fact he agrees that kashering part of it is sufficient for metal utensils if he did not use all of it in its normal way. With Hashem's help, this will be explained further in Magen Avraham 24. By a knife he was stringent after the fact if he did not kasher the handle, as is clear in seif 3 and Pesachim 30b, that both the blade and the handle require hot water. Even a wooden handle inserted into the iron of the blade requires hagalah. With wood, certainly when part is hot the whole does not become hot. Since its use is frequent and it touches hot chametz many times, with bread and the like, if so even after the fact if he did not perform hagalah on the handle and it touched on Pesach, it is forbidden. But with other handles one should not be stringent in a doubt that perhaps chametz spilled on them.
