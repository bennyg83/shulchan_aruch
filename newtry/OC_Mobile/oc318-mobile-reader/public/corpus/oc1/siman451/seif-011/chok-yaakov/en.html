It is permitted by hagalah. The Taz wrote in the name of the Rosh that specifically an oven in which chametz was baked without liquid requires libun, but not a frying pan, for we are not concerned that perhaps the oil dried up and the chametz was baked without liquid, since some moisture of liquid still remains there. The same law applies to a metal pot in which one cooks; end quote. In Yoreh Deah siman 121, the Mechaber wrote that although regarding Pesach hagalah suffices, regarding other prohibitions it requires libun. His reason is that since there are many opinions regarding a frying pan that it is not considered use by fire and hagalah suffices, therefore we rely on them regarding chametz, for there are poskim who consider it absorption of permitted matter, as I wrote in s.k. 14 and 22. This is unlike Olas Shabbos, who left the words of the Mechaber requiring study. Also in Pri Chadash to Yoreh Deah siman 121, s.k. 8, he challenged the Shach and Taz, but according to what I wrote everything is settled. See what I wrote in the name of Responsa Maharam Azariah of Fano, siman 96, another distinction between a frying pan used all year and the law of Pesach; see there.
Nevertheless, hagalah suffices for it. So is the practice. The Magen Avraham wrote that those pans in which cakes are baked require libun.
