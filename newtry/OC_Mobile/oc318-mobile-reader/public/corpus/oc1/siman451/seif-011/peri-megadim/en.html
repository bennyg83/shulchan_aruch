To perform hagalah. The Peri Megadim analyzes the kashering of utensils for Pesach, according to their normal mode of use, with distinctions between hagalah, libbun, pouring from a kli rishon, soaking, cleaning away visible chametz, and after-the-fact cases. He also weighs the rules of absorbed taste, visible residue, sharp foods, earthenware, and whether the absorbed taste is treated as permitted before Pesach. In particular, it addresses glass or coated vessels, ovens, winter stoves, and hot surfaces, spoons, bowls, and pans.
Further. There is room to investigate, for people place kreplach and the like in bowls from a kli rishon, and also on plates. In Yoreh Deah 94, regarding a solid hot item, there are those who are stringent. If so, even a plate should specifically be kashered in a kli rishon. However, even if it is not boiling and has been removed from the fire, this differs from a bowl, which is sometimes on the fire; there we require it to be on the fire and boiling.
