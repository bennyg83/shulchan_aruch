By rinsing. And likewise after the fact, if one did not hagal the bowls in a kli rishon, they also do not forbid; and if it is known that within a day one used it in a kli rishon, even though its primary use is in a kli sheni, they need hagalah specifically in a kli rishon. (And if by means of fire, one needs to libun.) According to all opinions. And rinsing means: first scrub it well with water to remove everything stuck to it, and afterward rinse with water, as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 121.
Libuned. And so we practice regarding kashering tables and benches; and dry the table so that the water not cool. Later authorities. And see siman 452, se'if 2 in the gloss. And bowls that have ridges and protrusions like buttons and flowers, such that one cannot pass the stone over their entire face, have no remedy other than to put them into a cauldron; and likewise a knife one should not kasher with a stone. Magen Avraham.
