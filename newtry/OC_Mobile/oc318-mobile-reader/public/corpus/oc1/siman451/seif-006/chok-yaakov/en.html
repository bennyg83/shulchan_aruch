Although sometimes they use them in a first vessel. Olas Shabbos wrote that if it is known that within twenty-four hours it was used in a first vessel, even though its primary use is in a second vessel, it specifically requires hagalah in a first vessel according to all opinions. This is simple. See my Minchas Yaakov, klal 58, s.k. 25.
And after the fact washing suffices for them. Similarly, after the fact, if one did not perform hagalah on the bowls in a first vessel, they also do not forbid, as I wrote in my Minchas Yaakov there in klal 58 in the name of the Bach. The same applies regarding cups and utensils, even though there is a concern that liquid remained in them for twenty-four hours and was pickled in them, they do not forbid after the fact. It is explained in Yoreh Deah siman 121 that old earthenware vessels used cold, since hagalah is impossible for them, are considered an after the fact case, and cleaning and rinsing suffice. Here, regarding Pesach, the Rama omitted all this, it seems to me, because here it is not considered after the fact, since they can be kept until after Pesach, unlike there, regarding kashering forbidden utensils. This is unlike Olas Shabbos, who did not notice this.
By washing. That is, first to scrub it well with water in order to remove anything adhering to it, and afterward to rinse with water, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 121.
He should place on them a heated stone. So is the practice regarding kashering tables and benches. One should dry the table first so that the water not cool. This is from the later authorities. See below, siman 452, s.k. 14.
Over the entire utensil. Although in some place the stone does not touch, the boiling water reaches there. This is the Taz, and so wrote the Issur VeHeter, klal 58, law 67. The Magen Avraham wrote that one should be stringent with bowls that have rims and protrusions like buttons, since then one cannot pass the stone over all of them, and they have no remedy except to place them into a kettle.
