(s.k. 6) One hagals them, etc. It is implied in the Gemara, etc., that Ravina said to R' Ela: these knives on Pesach, how do we treat them? He said to him: for myself I make new ones. He said to him: fine for the master, for whom it is possible; for whom it is not possible, what? He said to him: like new ones I intended: their handles in clay (so they not burn in the fire at the time of libun) and their iron in the fire, and afterward we put their handles into boiling water. And the Gemara concludes: and the law is both this and that (that is to say: both the handle and the iron) in boiling water in a kli rishon. And according to the plain understanding of what R' Ela answered him, "like new ones I intended": that is to say, my original intent when I said "for myself, new ones" was intending like new ones: their handles in clay, etc.; and this is possible for every person; if so, according to R' Ela there is no distinction between one for whom it is possible and one for whom it is not; and in every case they do like new. And if so, for what we conclude "the law is," etc., it also refers both to one for whom it is possible and to one for whom it is not; and even one for whom it is possible: boiling water suffices.
He holds that the Gemara means thus, etc. For one for whom it is not possible, etc.; and such was R' Ela's intent: since I said "for myself, for whom it is possible, I make new," automatically for one for whom it is not possible, he should make like new; but where possible, actually new. And even though we conclude the law is boiling water: nevertheless one may say that that refers only to one for whom it is not possible; but one for whom it is possible should make new ones like R' Ela; for behold even according to R' Ela there is a distinction between possible and not possible; the same may be said according to what the law was ruled. And see the Taz.
And as is written in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 121: for this reason a knife taken from a non-Jew needs libun, because the non-Jew uses it on the fire; and regarding chametz we are lenient, for it is not a Jew's way to use a knife by fire, even though sometimes one uses it by fire, as Magen Avraham wrote; nevertheless, since according to the Mechaber we follow for vessels their majority use, as is written in se'if 6; and even though we are stringent as the Rama wrote in the gloss there, nevertheless we rely on the view that chametz is called "it absorbed permitted [food]" for this matter; and see Chok Yaakov. Also Peri Chadash there in Yoreh De'ah was lenient even regarding a knife of prohibition by hagalah; see there. Nevertheless new ones are still better for this reason.
