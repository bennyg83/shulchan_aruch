[6] One kashered, etc. — since permitted was absorbed, but prohibited absorption — I explained in Yoreh De'ah end siman 121 that libun is required; and Mateh Moshe wrote that also on Pesach one who is stringent to whiten — a blessing will come upon him; and so it appears in Magen Avraham, because sometimes they stick bread in a knife called pfeinitz near the fire, or stick pashitida or pankoken near the fire; and in practice, if possible with new ones it is praiseworthy. And where the sheath is of wood, Mahari Weil siman 153 wrote that by law hagalah does not help — we are concerned for it; and likewise of wood, since it sticks with adhesion we are concerned; and Bach wrote the same law if the sheath is fixed with nails — presumably there are crevices in them; and per this we do not find the law of hagalah in knives unless the sheath too is of the knife's kind, such as entirely metal; and it requires study — behold on Pesachim page 30 it says regarding their sheath of clay and iron in fire, and the halachah is both in boiling water; and Rashi explained their sheath of clay to protect them lest the fire burn them — until here; it implies it deals with a wooden sheath, and even so hagalah in boiling water helps.
