[6] One hagals, etc. Since it absorbed permitted [food]; but if it absorbed a prohibition, I explained in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 121 that libun is required. And Mateh Moshe wrote that even on Pesach, one who is stringent to libun it, may blessing come upon him; and so it is implied in Magen Avraham, because sometimes they stick in a knife bread called "penitz" by the fire, or stick in it a pastry or pancakes by the fire; and in the piyut: and if new is possible, it is praiseworthy. And where the handle is of wood, Mahariv siman 153 wrote that by law hagalah does not help, for one is concerned for it; and likewise regarding wood, since it is attached with glue, one is concerned for it. And the Bach wrote that the same applies if the handle is fixed with nails, for presumably there are grooves in them. And according to this we do not find a law of hagalah for knives unless the handle too is of the same material as the knife, for example if it is entirely metal. And this requires study: for in Pesachim folio 30 it says regarding their handles, [put them] in clay, and the iron in the fire; and the halacha is both this and that in boiling water; and Rashi explained: their handles in clay to protect them so that the fire not burn [them]; end quote. This implies that it deals with a wooden handle, and even so boiling water helps.
