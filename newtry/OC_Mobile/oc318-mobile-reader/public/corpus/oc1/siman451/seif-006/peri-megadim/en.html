All. See Taz and the Rosh in Pesachim 30b, and see Beis Yosef and Levush, who write that it does not emit, because rust separates, and they attached it to the verse, etc., in Parshas Matos. This implies that it is like a patch in seif 13, which separates, etc. According to this, if he removed the rust and cleaned well, then did not use it again and rust formed, he must again remove the rust. According to the Taz, it seems that hagalah helps, and it is not like a patch, because the water of hagalah acts within the rust and earthiness; the reason is only because of actual substance, for which it does not help. He will explain the word therefore as follows: since the reason is because of actual substance, if there are hollows in a knife that cannot be cleaned of the actual substance even without rust, etc. This supports his words. And regarding a black mark, once he removed the actual substance there is no concern, for otherwise he should have taught this greater novelty. However, libbun only in the place of the rust suffices and no more is needed, as in os 7 and as written in the Rama to seif 5. See Eliyah Rabbah, os 9. It is obvious that if he wants to perform libbun in this way he may do so. The case is only where he wanted to remove it, and Chok Adam then advised that he should perform good libbun. Therefore the Levush attached it to the verse; one may say that this is only regarding immersion, so that it should not be a chatzitzah, see Yoreh Deah 120, seif 13, and from logic, because hagalah does not act upon actual substance. Regarding the parenthetical note: from them neither unfinished nor plain utensils, I do not know. Unfinished utensils everywhere are pure and need no sprinkling, but their plain forms are impure; this requires study at present.
The cited ruling. The Peri Megadim analyzes the kashering of utensils for Pesach, according to their normal mode of use, with distinctions between hagalah, libbun, pouring from a kli rishon, soaking, cleaning away visible chametz, and after-the-fact cases. He also weighs the rules of absorbed taste, visible residue, sharp foods, earthenware, and whether the absorbed taste is treated as permitted before Pesach. In particular, it addresses glass or coated vessels, spoons, bowls, and pans.
