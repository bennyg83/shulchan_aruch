Magen Avraham s.k. 24. For we do not say that it carries its absorption. I did not merit to understand this: for that is when part of it is hot; but with a vessel in which one cooks, one sees with the senses that the handle too is hot, and one cannot grasp it except by means of a cloth. And the question whether, if one cooked in part of a vessel, the whole needs hagalah, is where it permeates upward not in the place of the gravy; but in the place of the gravy it certainly permeates through the entire thickness of the wall. And if so, the handle too is included in the thickness of the wall; unless we say that the handle is presumably not one mass with the vessel, but joined with pegs, and it is like two vessels, which do not permeate from one to the other; if so, it does not depend on the law of Yoreh De'ah siman 121; and may Hashem enlighten my eyes.
