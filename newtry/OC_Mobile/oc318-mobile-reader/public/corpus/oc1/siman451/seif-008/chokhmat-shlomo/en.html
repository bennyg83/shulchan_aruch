(There, se'if 8 in the gloss) And likewise bone vessels require hagalah. Marginal note: Behold, the source of this ruling is from the Mordechai, brought in Beis Yosef, and this is his language: It is written in the name of the Or Zarua: It appears to me that bone vessels do not need hagalah, for they do not absorb; and likewise the Ri of Paris explained that we do not find absorption that forbids regarding bone. And I act as I have written; but my heart is somewhat uneasy, for we say in the chapter of the vessels of Midian, "and all that is made of goats," etc., and Rashi explained: to include the horns and the hooves and the bones; and all the more so bone vessels and vessels of the horn of a large ox. Thus there is absorption in bone vessels, since it says "you shall purify"; end quote. And see what I wrote on this in my composition long ago, to wonder at him. And now I was asked by someone: what proof is there from there? Behold there it is dealing with tumah, and regarding the giul of Midian it was said afterward through Elazar; and that is truly puzzling. And to resolve this, it would seem as follows. First, one may say that a vessel that does not absorb is not a vessel even regarding tumah; and the proof is that we see that a glass vessel does not receive tumah according to the Torah, only the Sages decreed tumah upon them, and necessarily it is because it does not absorb that it is not considered a vessel regarding tumah; and if so, here too, if a bone vessel does not absorb, it would not be considered a vessel even for tumah. And a further proof from what we learn in Shabbos, first chapter, folio 16b, where it asks: metal vessels are from the Torah, as it is written, "only the gold and the silver"; and the difficulty there is the reverse: behold the verse is dealing with the giul of Midian, and from where do we know [this] regarding tumah? And if because it is written, "whatever will not come into fire, you shall pass through water," behold there it is dealing with immersion from a non-Jew by the law of immersion of vessels from the hands of a non-Jew, but not by the law of tumah; so from where do we know that metal vessels receive tumah according to the Torah? And necessarily, on the contrary, from the fact that it absorbs, it is proven that it is a vessel regarding tumah; and the same applies in reverse: this and that are one, and examine carefully. And further it would seem by way of pilpul, according to what we learn in tractate Zevachim, folio 96, where it asks that one should make the oven of the Mikdash of earthenware that was heated inside, and it answers: one does not make service vessels of earthenware. And it is difficult: if you would think that wooden vessels [editor's note: it appears this is a copyist's error, and it should say "bone," and so in all of these] do not absorb, then make [it] of wood; and necessarily bone vessels also absorb. But perhaps a wooden vessel is not a vessel at all, and one does not make service vessels from it; therefore he brought proof from what is written regarding Midian, "and all that is made of goats, you shall purify," that it is a vessel in any case; and since it is a vessel in any case, it is difficult: let them make the oven of wood; and necessarily it is proven that wood also absorbs; and it fits well for now. And as to why he does not bring proof from the mishnah that a bone vessel is tamei, one may say that that is only rabbinic, like glass vessels; therefore he was compelled to bring proof for this from the verse, etc. I afterward found in Tosafos of Chullin, folio 21, that they wrote thus; see there; and examine carefully.
