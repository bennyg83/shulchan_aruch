To pour. And Mahariv, siman 193, ruled that pouring does not help; rather kasher it by means of a libuned stone or a burning torch, and pour boiling water upon it. And the later authorities wrote in the name of Maharil that even after hagalah, if anything spilled upon it on Pesach, one should not lift it at all to eat; and even after hagalah one should not use upon them anything hot except by means of a separating cloth or boards or rags; and all these are excessive stringencies, for the stringency of hagalah itself suffices; and so we practice. Chok Yaakov. And the Taz wrote at the end of siman 452: women practice not to use on Pesach a pot into which one poured boiling water upon the stone with which they kasher, for the reason that the smell of chametz enters it; and there is neither smell nor taste, so there is no prohibition at all on Pesach; end of his words. And Chok Yaakov upholds the custom l'chatchila, but after the fact it certainly does not forbid.
