Because sometimes soup spills. Without this reason, even though hot chametz is stored in it, there would be no concern, since it is placed there with the utensils that contain the chametz, and it does not cause any absorption, because the utensil interposes. Therefore the reason was needed, that soup spills upon it. This is the intent of the Hagahos Maimoniyos brought by the Beis Yosef, and he retracted here from what he brought in the Beis Yosef to explain the words of the gloss in another way. The Bach also agrees with this in practice. Mahari Weil, siman 193, ruled that pouring is not effective, because sometimes they place a pastry on it; rather, one should kasher it by means of a heated stone and pouring. The later authorities also wrote in the name of Maharil that even after hagalah, if anything spills upon it on Pesach, he should not lift it at all, and even after hagalah he should not use any hot item on them except by means of an intervening rag. All these are excessive stringencies, for the stringency of hagalah is enough, and so is the practice. The Taz at the end of siman 452 wrote: women have the practice not to use on Pesach a pot into which boiling water was poured over the stone used for kashering, because the smell of chametz enters it, but this has neither smell nor taste, for there is no prohibition in it at all on Pesach; end quote. See what I wrote in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 23, s.k. 12. It appears that there are two reasons to prohibit ab initio by the law of the Gemara, and the custom of Israel is Torah. However, after the fact it certainly does not forbid.
