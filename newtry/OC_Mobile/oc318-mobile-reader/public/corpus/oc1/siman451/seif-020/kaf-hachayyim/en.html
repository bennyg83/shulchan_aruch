Magen Avraham, subsection 3, writes that by omitting the law of a second vessel, the Shulchan Aruch retracted what was written in Beis Yosef. Chok Yaakov there challenges this. Yad Aharon in Hagahos Beis Yosef likewise writes that Magen Avraham's ruling requires consideration. Nehar Shalom, subsection 2, challenges Magen Avraham's entire discussion. See there. In Yoreh De'ah, siman 105, se'if 2, the Shulchan Aruch permits after the fact food heated in a second vessel, whereas here it maintains that heating does not kasher such a utensil. Yad Aharon there suggests that the Shulchan Aruch may have wished to be stringent regarding chametz. See there. Regarding the practical law, Chok Yaakov writes there that in a case of substantial loss and loss of Yom Tov rejoicing, one may be lenient after the fact if a utensil not used that day was used as a second vessel, even if it is earthenware. See there. Yad Aharon, there; Mekor Chaim, subsection 6; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 33; Chayei Adam, rule 125, subsection 3, write likewise. For example, a spoon used primarily for eating is ordinarily inserted into a bowl after food was poured into it from a first vessel. If the spoon was not kashered and during Pesach was inserted into a first vessel, the food is permitted after the fact when the spoon had not been used that day and a substantial loss or loss of Yom Tov rejoicing is involved. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, there. Obviously, this applies only if the spoon was clean. Otherwise, chametz during Pesach prohibits even in the slightest amount, as is known.
