(113) That one stores foods in them: hot foods; but ordinary chests need no hagalah.
(114) To pour upon them, etc.: from a kli rishon. And Mahariv, siman 193, ruled that pouring does not help, because sometimes one places a hot pastry upon it and it is as if used in a kli rishon; rather kasher them by means of a libuned stone and pour boiling water upon it and roll it to another place and pour upon it so that the boiling water go throughout. [And in this it suffices even if the boiling water is from a kli sheni, for behold they are boiled upon the board and it becomes like a kli rishon.] And then it is permitted to place even hot matzah upon them; and after the fact pouring alone is permitted; and see above in se'if 6 in the gloss and in Mishnah Berurah there.
(115) Boiling water: and some wrote that one must also place upon them a cloth or another thing that separates, lest still some chametz in kind cling to it. And see Eliyah Rabbah, who concluded that so it is proper to practice regarding chests and cabinets, for it is almost impossible to purify them so that nothing remain between the joints; but regarding plain tables one should not be stringent; and see later in siman 452, se'if 1 and 2, in Mishnah Berurah there.
