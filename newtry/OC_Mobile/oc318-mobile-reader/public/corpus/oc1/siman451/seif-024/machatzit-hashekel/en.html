(s.k. 24) We do not, etc. For we do not say that it carries its absorption, etc. And this is the language of Issur VeHeter, etc.: the plain understanding of what is written, that it does not carry the absorption through its entirety, means that originally, at the time the prohibition was absorbed into the vessel, the prohibition did not spread except to the place used in the vessel; but Magen Avraham will clarify afterward that this is not the meaning.
As is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 92, se'if 5, and brought in the Shach there s.k. 18 in the name of the Semak: a drop that fell on the wall of the vessel: we are in doubt in the chapter of the blood of a chatat whether it permeates through the entire vessel or not, etc.; end quote. Thus there is a doubt whether the prohibition spread through the entire vessel; and from a doubt we go there to stringency; the same applies here. And as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 92, se'if 1: that one view there is primary: that if one stuck a dairy spoon into a meat vessel or the reverse, one need not have sixty except against that part that was stuck in; and therefore one must say that it does not expel from that side that was not stuck in.
And as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 92: this is the proof he wrote above, and he repeated his words.
And thus is resolved, etc. Upon the Tur, who wrote according to the Rashba, who wrote: a vessel that needs libun needs its entirety libuned; and this is his language: and it appears to me specifically where one used its entirety; but if one knows that one used only part of it, kashering of part of it too helps: as it absorbed, so it expels; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote that from the Rosh's words it is implied that he holds even if one used only part of a vessel, the whole vessel needs to be libuned: for he wrote regarding kashering a non-Jew's knife that since the non-Jew's way is to use it by means of fire, namely to fix a tallow wick while lit and turn meat on coals, the Rosh wrote, this is his language: and it appears that it suffices if one libuns the edge of the knife, since non-Jews regularly use it (that is to say: its edge) by fire. And even though if part is hot the whole is hot and the prohibition spread throughout, nevertheless since a Jew does not use by means of fire, this suffices; end quote of the Rosh. (And in truth the rest of the knife needs at least hagalah; and see there in the Taz.) And on this Beis Yosef wrote, this is his language: behold he permitted libun only in the place used by means of fire alone because a Jew does not use a knife by means of fire: this implies that to use it by means of fire is forbidden, etc.; end quote. And if so it requires explanation: why does the Tur disagree with his father the Rosh, and also did not bring the Rosh's view at all?
But if one wants to use its other half, one needs to libun its entirety, even though one used with prohibition only in its other half. And therefore the Rosh needed to say that a Jew does not use by means of fire: meaning he does not use the whole vessel except for the place of the edge by means of fire; for if a Jew used there by means of fire, the Tur would agree that its entirety needs to be libuned.
And the Bach and the Taz gave another reason to kasher the handles: for it is the way to touch the handles of vessels with dirty hands; and for this reason the Turei Zahav was stringent regarding knife handles even after the fact: that if one did not hagal the handles; and see Chok Yaakov.
