The Taz wrote that a Pesach peel, if one keeps it for the next year, needs no kashering at all for the next year, and one who is stringent to perform hagalah on it has not acted well, for this may lead people to err and perform hagalah on a chametz peel. Rather, one should clean it well; end quote. His words appear correct, unlike what the Magen Avraham wrote in the name of the Darkei Moshe in the name of Mahari Bruna, that it requires hagalah. The Magen Avraham also wrote that it is not effective if he peels the peel and performs hagalah on it. And if one removed matzah with a peel that is used all year, if it is clean, peeling off the surface of the matzah suffices. If it is not clean, since cakes with fat are used on it all year, the whole matzah becomes forbidden. If they removed a leavened matzah with it and afterward removed other matzos with it, all require peeling. The work Ape Ravrevei wrote in Shaar 70 that if one removed a leavened matzah with a peel and afterward removed others with it, and the first ones became mixed with the later ones, they are nullified; end quote. This is a rejected reasoning, for he holds that absorbed prohibition does not leave a utensil without liquid, and the only prohibition is the first loaf, because there was a little substance present; if it became mixed, it is nullified. But we rule that a utensil discharges even without liquid; end quote, these are the words of the Magen Avraham. My brother-in-law, Rabbi Aharon, may his light shine, answered that Ape Ravrevei did not intend those matzos that were removed afterward, that the first ones removed immediately after the leavened matzah should be nullified, as the Magen Avraham understood. Rather, by first ones he meant those removed before the leavened matzah was removed, which then became mixed with what was removed afterward. He understood this well. Although it appears to me that the wording of Ape Ravrevei pressed the Magen Avraham in that direction, since he wrote that the first ones became mixed with the later ones, implying that the later ones were the majority of permitted food, as the Gemara in chapter Elu HaNisrafin infers regarding those sentenced to stoning among those sentenced to burning, nevertheless this is not such a decisive inference in the words of poskim as to reject their words on that basis. See further in the Magen Avraham, who wrote at length about this nullification. In my humble opinion, all these matters were said only according to the one who holds that dry matter mixed with dry matter is nullified one in two regarding Pesach. But according to what was explained above in siman 447, that dry with dry is not nullified on Pesach, and even from six hours onward it reawakens during Pesach so long as it was not mixed wet with wet, and it is forbidden to eat them during Pesach because the prohibition is present in them, as explained in the words of the Magen Avraham himself in siman 458; see there. If so, the law of nullification is not relevant here at all, except to feed them on erev Pesach to a child or the like. The Magen Avraham further concluded: it appears to me that all this speaks of matzah that became full chametz, but a folded or swollen matzah, since it itself is only a stringency, as written in siman 461, is sufficient to forbid the peel ab initio, but not to forbid the other matzos; end quote. I saw an even greater ruling by my grandfather, the gaon Rabbi Shimon, that he did not forbid the peel when he was asked about swollen or folded matzah. The reason, it appears to me, is that this is considered like after the fact, for in most bakings it is almost impossible that one of them not be forbidden because of swelling or folding, and each time one would need a new peel, and the oven too would be forbidden and would need heating again. In such cases the Torah has pity on the money of Israel. Moreover, the Kol Bo, brought in the Beis Yosef in this siman, ruled that if one placed dry matzah, even hot, on a chametz utensil, or even on hot chametz bread, it does not forbid any part of it at all, and he brought this as practical halachah. He wrote further there regarding a chametz peel: perhaps if he scours it very much and heats it inside the oven, it is permitted, for as it absorbed, so it discharges. Although we do not rule like him, nevertheless regarding swollen and folded matzah, which itself is only a stringency, let us not add to it, and we do not forbid the peel even ab initio. So practiced after him my father-in-law, the gaon Rabbi Wolf, may his light shine. Regarding the statement that one must buy a new one, the author of Imrei Noam wrote in a responsum, siman 5, of an incident where matzos were baked before Pesach with a peel and they were uncertain whether it was new or old, on which chametz had been used; all faces are equal that there is no concern of chametz prohibition in those matzos; end quote.
