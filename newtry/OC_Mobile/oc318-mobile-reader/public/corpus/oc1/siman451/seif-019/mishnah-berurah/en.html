(א) (cat) is a revolt that brings cakes into the oven and extracts it from the oven:

(ב) That it does not help — because it absorbs from chametz through fire without any liquid; and to whiten it is impossible, for it is of wood; and see in Ateret Zekenim who wrote that all this is l'chatchila, but b'dieved if one used a chametz peel for matzah after hagalah — it is permitted.

(ג) Hagalah — and even peeling with artisan tools beforehand also does not help. B'dieved, if one removed hot matzah from the oven with a chametz peel — if the peel was not clean but had visible chametz filth, it forbids on the underside of the matzah kedei netilah, which is the width of a thumb (and according to what we practice to bake thin matzot — the whole is forbidden); and if they baked chametz cakes with fat on this peel — the whole matzah is forbidden, for through the fat the chametz spreads throughout [and specifically during Pesach; but before Pesach until night that little b'eyna on the peel is nullified in sixty]. But if they rinsed the peel beforehand and there is no chametz b'eyna on it, only what was absorbed in it — it forbids only kedei kelipah, whether they used it beforehand with fat or without fat, for prohibition absorbed in a vessel cannot forbid more than kedei kelipah. If one removed chametz matzah with a Pesach peel and afterward threshed other matzot in it and they were forbidden through the peel, and afterward these mixed with many other kosher ones — all are forbidden, for it was already explained in siman 447 that on Pesach dry is not nullified in dry; and even if mixed on erev Pesach, nevertheless it returns and is reawakened during Pesach and forbidden to eat on Pesach [and only on erev Pesach it is permitted to feed them to a child]; and some say that since it was forbidden only from absorbed matter it does not return and is reawakened on Pesach; and all this is with full chametz. But in a peel from which they removed doubled or puffed matzah — it suffices to forbid the peel l'chatchila, but b'dieved it does not forbid; and more than this Kaf HaChayyim wrote: if one cannot easily find another peel, it is permitted l'chatchila to remove matzot with it, for otherwise one would always need a new peel and it would be like b'dieved; and if they were in doubt about the peel whether it was chametz or new — all the matzot are permitted.
