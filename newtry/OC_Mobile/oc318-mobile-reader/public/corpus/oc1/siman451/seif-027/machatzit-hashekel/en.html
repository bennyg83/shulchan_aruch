(s.k. 27) One places, etc. This requires study, etc. The intent is that the Tur wrote, this is his language: a frying pan or cauldron that has a patch is not permitted by hagalah, because what was absorbed under the patches is not expelled by hagalah; and it is explained there specifically where absorption preceded the patch. But if the patch preceded absorption, hagalah helps: as it absorbed, so it expels. And Beis Yosef wrote on this, this is his language: and the Rashba wrote in a responsum that hagalah expels from here as from here; only that we are concerned lest under those patch pieces the body of the prohibition and its substance entered, and the substance of the body of the prohibition is not kashered by hagalah; therefore in such cases we are accustomed to place coals on the place of the cracks so that the body of the prohibition will burn if it is there, and afterward hagal it; and if it is of wood, etc., widen, etc., as is written in the Shulchan Aruch. And therefore Beis Yosef's words in the Shulchan Aruch have their source from the aforementioned Rashba. And since he wrote in the Shulchan Aruch that where absorption preceded the patch one must place coals on the place of the patch, and where the patch preceded absorption he does not require coals: therefore it deals with a case where it is very well attached, such that now after placing the patch upon it it is impossible that chametz enter under the patch, as the Taz wrote in s.k. 18. And he understood from the Rashba's words that he disagrees with what the Tur and the Rosh and Ravyah wrote, and holds that even where absorption preceded the patch, hagalah helps and the patch does not interrupt, and nevertheless the absorbed under the patch comes out. (Or that the absorbed comes out from the other side that has no patch; and so Peri Chadash understood from the Rashba's words; see there.) Only that one should be concerned that there is in kind under the patch what entered there before the patch was placed upon it; and what is in kind: hagalah does not help for it; therefore he requires placing coals on the place of the patch to burn the chametz that was under the patch beforehand, as above. But Magen Avraham holds not to create a dispute between the Rashba and the Tur and those who stand in his approach; and therefore he explains that the Rashba deals only where the patch preceded absorption and is not so well attached, and there is concern that after placing the patch chametz entered under it; and even though in such a case where the patch preceded, according to all opinions hagalah helps, nevertheless for the place of the patch where there is concern of chametz in kind, hagalah does not help; therefore one must place coals there. (And those coals are not by the law of libun; for if so he should have explained explicitly that many coals are needed until sparks fly from it; rather he means that a few coals suffice, and it is like the light libun mentioned in the Rama above in se'if 4; and they suffice here only to burn the chametz under the patches.) But where absorption preceded the patch: of this the Rashba did not speak, and presumably one may say he agrees with the Tur and his company that hagalah does not help with those few coals; for the patch interrupts, and it is impossible to extract from a vessel by hagalah in the place under the patch.
And so wrote the Bach, etc. That the Rashba deals specifically where the patch preceded. But where absorption preceded, the Rashba agrees that hagalah does not help. And on this the Bach wrote from his own reasoning that the whole vessel needs libun: for we hold as its use, so its kashering; and since one used it without a patch, one cannot hagal it when the patch is upon it.
And so it is implied in Issur VeHeter, etc. Chemdat Moshe understood that he brings proof from Issur VeHeter that where the patch preceded, libun is required. And on this he lengthened and wrote that the Bach and Magen Avraham erred in Issur VeHeter's intent; see there. And in my humble opinion their intent is to bring proof to the contrary: that in any case libun helps; for one might have thought we specifically need as its use so its kashering; and since one used by means of boiling water, libun does not help; even though in truth libun is better than it, nevertheless there is room to err. And on this they brought proof from Issur VeHeter that in any case libun helps; and this is proven there from Issur VeHeter; see there. And the same applies that they could have brought proof for this from the Tur's words, who wrote: and likewise a metal pot, if one wishes to heat it and fill it with coals, it is permitted; end quote. And perhaps it is possible that in Issur VeHeter he wrote thus specifically regarding a patch law, which may be worse; therefore they brought proof from Issur VeHeter.
Therefore it appears to me where it preceded, etc. Magen Avraham disagrees with Beis Yosef in two points; but he also disagrees with the Bach: for the Bach holds that where absorption preceded, the whole vessel needs to be libuned; and Magen Avraham holds that it suffices if one libuns the place of the patch. Nevertheless regarding the main law he agrees with the Bach in two points.
And that which he wrote, until the body of the prohibition is burned; and if it preceded, etc. It appears that it was printed in error, and one must reverse the words, and so one should read from it: and if the patch preceded absorption, light libun suffices; and that which he wrote, until the body of the prohibition is burned, etc.: that is to say, according to Magen Avraham's approach that the Rashba deals with such a case where the patch preceded absorption, and even so he requires coals, namely light libun. And therefore the Rashba wrote the language "until it burns the body of the prohibition": that it does not help in place of libun except to burn what is in kind under the cracks of the patch.
Vessels, etc. And further, since the tin interrupts, etc. And on this reason Peri Chadash disagrees and agrees with the first reason. And accordingly the later authorities wrote that the patch mentioned in the Shulchan Aruch deals with attaching the patch by means of nails, in such a case. But if one melted tin and the like and sealed the hole, hagalah helps even if absorption preceded.
And accordingly even, etc. That is to say: whether according to the first reason or the second reason: in any case the first reason applies to old tin.
And see Yoreh De'ah siman 120 regarding immersion: that is to say, that he wrote: vessels coated with lead inside or outside: immerse without a blessing (but if coated outside and inside, Peri Chadash ruled there to immerse and bless). If so we learn: even though the vessels themselves need no immersion, for they are earthenware, nevertheless through a lead coating they need immersion; if so the same applies to old vessels: even though they were already immersed, nevertheless if one coated them with lead or tin they need immersion again because of the coating. And if the lead belonged to a Jew, see there for its law.
