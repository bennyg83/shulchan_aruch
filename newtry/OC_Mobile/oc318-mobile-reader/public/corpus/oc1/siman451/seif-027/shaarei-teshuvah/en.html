In hagalah. See Baer Heitev; and he wrote in Har HaKarmel siman 5: there is a way when they want to test yayin nesech how much water is in it — they pour from the yayin nesech onto a tin vessel and ignite the yayin nesech to see how much water remains; the tin vessel suffices with hagalah because it becomes immediately flame; and if we say the yayin nesech near the fire and still fire is not yet upon it — it absorbs in the vessel — meaning again it is as absorbed through liquid; see there he wrote proof from a pan in which they fry — Rosh wrote it is not called through fire due to the moisture of the liquid; see there — and there is no proof, for there the fire is outside around the pot, and here the fire is inside and it is absorbed through it; therefore if one used many times in this matter to test yayin nesech — one should be stringent not to permit through hagalah; and nevertheless it appears to permit b'dieved if used through hagalah — for it is close that the nature of yayin nesech is to be after the fire and to be ignited from it; therefore it is not absorbed in the vessel — for immediately when the fire's heat reaches it, it is drawn after the flame and ignites; and all the time one does not feel the fire's heat, there is none who absorbs it; and we find such a case regarding roasting — we say fire draws and absorbs; and even though there is no proof — for there they establish that fire's nature is to draw to itself and not to absorb, which is not so here — who will tell us it is drawn after it, only its heat burns and absorbs also, like milk and similar that ignites and absorbs also; and nevertheless it is somewhat reasonable regarding yayin nesech whose nature is to ignite immediately when fire touches it — fire does not do two actions together; only if the way is to test with a large measure of yayin nesech until it ignites such that the vessel heats from the fire such that yad soledes bo — it requires study lest since the vessel was hot from the fire itself when absorbing from the prohibition not through liquid — hagalah does not help except libun; but it appears in such a case its use is not called through fire — such as a pot or bowl that stood on fire until it was heated greatly yad soledes bo and a prohibited thing was placed in it without liquid — nevertheless its law is not as absorbed through fire literally; and hagalah suffices; for if not, in metal pots and similar if some mishap occurred and the food was forbidden and the pot — they would require libun for the pot even though most times they pour the gravy from the meat and the pot is still boiling yad soledes bo from the fire's heat — and nevertheless they do not require libun for it; if so, since after pouring the gravy they remove it from the fire — what boils by itself from boiling due to the fire does not do the fire's action literally. But one may distinguish as Rosh wrote regarding a pan — one need not require libun even though sometimes the liquid burns until it chars at the bottom of the pot — we have no concern; nevertheless there is moisture of liquid there; see there; and with this one may say all agree regarding a pot from which they pour gravy — nevertheless there is moist moisture; and one may also say: since at the time of the pot's heat through fire there was liquid inside, fire does not control so much to consider it as the fire's heat literally, even though now there is no liquid and it stands in its heat yad soledes bo; and somewhat one may learn this matter — whether products of fire are like fire literally — from Pesachim 74a: an oven they fired and scraped and roasted the Pesach in it — this is not fire-roasting, etc.; see there it depends on two resolutions: if we say the Merciful One revealed fire-roasting twice and we learn from it — if so one may say also here "every thing that comes in fire" means the fire's power literally, but products of fire not; and for that resolution that said there if the Merciful One had not revealed we would say the Merciful One was particular only about fire and even if scraped it is fire-roasting; and Rashi explained: the oven's heat is a product of fire and if you put a garment in it, it burns and becomes charcoal and fire; see there — if so here too it depends on two resolutions; and if we say we learn from it — if so likewise "every thing that comes in fire" means specifically what was used in fire literally, but what received from products of fire such as an oven that was scraped — not; and at first glance from the Gemara's language there page 76 — it asks and the Pesach was heated from the pottery's heat and the Merciful One said fire-roasting and not roasting due to something else — it implies the first resolution: since the Merciful One revealed fire-roasting twice, automatically its implication is not roasting due to something else; for the second resolution it should have said and the Merciful One said fire-roasting twice — for generally where it is not written twice, even due to something else it is called fire-roasting; and from stam it implies it grasps primarily the first resolution; see there page 74 — they say one does not roast the Pesach on a metal spit because part of it is hot, all of it is hot, and it is heated due to the spit and the Merciful One said fire-roasting and not roasting due to something else, etc.; see there. However from what Tosafot page 75 s.v. ha lo amar etc. wrote — that which needed to answer there to distinguish between wood coals and did not answer the Merciful One revealed in a burn and we learn from it — this is because of the last language above; see there; and if so, since the Gemara discusses according to this last language, it appears somewhat this language is primary; and per this one should study regarding a spit or pan and similar that was heated in fire greatly and is boiling yad soledes bo and forbidden meat or another prohibition b'eyna was placed in it without liquid — whether it requires libun or hagalah, since absorption inside is not through fire literally, only due to products of fire alone; and from Pesachim page 30 — an oven they plastered in its upper story — one does not bake in it until the oven is fired; and for one resolution in metal immediately; and if so it implies even if they plastered in the oven after scraping the fire — nevertheless firing is required and hagalah does not help even though it did not absorb through fire literally, only due to products of fire; and possibly an oven according to its size cannot be inserted into a pot to kasher it — therefore it cites until the oven is fired; and if hagalah were possible, hagalah would suffice; but from the Gemara's language there it implies specifically complete firing; and one may also say: since in any event it did not absorb through liquid — hagalah does not help for it, for the rule "as it absorbs so it discharges" does not apply when absorption was not in this manner; therefore it appears libun is required; except light libun suffices and one need not until sparks fly from it; however if at the time the prohibition was absorbed in it the vessel or spit was whitened so much that sparks fly from it — there is no law requiring libun, for this was as absorbed through fire literally and good libun is required until sparks fly from it — so it appears to me. And behold this law is not its place here, only in Yoreh De'ah siman 122; but since incidentally in my girsa it occurred to me here and these matters were renewed to me, I fixed them here; and when I reach their place they will be refined and whitened further, there's help.
