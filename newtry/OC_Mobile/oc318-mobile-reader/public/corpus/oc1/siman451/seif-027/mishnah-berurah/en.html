(160) That previously, etc. Meaning, during the year they roasted salted meat on it with salt that had not been checked for chametz crumbs. Had it been known that chametz was present in the salt, the food would now be forbidden even though the roasting occurred before Pesach. Since no such fact is known, however, we do not establish a prohibition merely out of doubt. If stuffed pastry or other chametz was roasted on it, such as bread dried upon it even in a tiny amount, and it was not subjected to libun afterward, then even if it was kashered through hag'alah and meat was later roasted on it during Pesach, the meat is forbidden b'dieved. In a case of major loss, or when the utensil was not ben yomo, one may be lenient after hag'alah if the meat contains sixty times the absorbed taste.
(161) The spigots. Because they are handled with hands to which chametz adheres.
(162) It is customary to wash them. It is preferable to obtain new spigots, lest the old spigot have previously been inserted into a beer barrel.
(163) Drinking utensils. This refers to vessels used for beer and distilled liquor, specifically the portion inserted into the vessel and not the handle, as stated by Eliyah Rabbah. If a spigot from a beer barrel was inserted into mead on Pesach and remained there for twenty-four hours, the mead is forbidden for drinking and benefit. If the spigot was not ben yomo, one may be lenient. If it was a spigot used for distilled liquor, it is forbidden even when not ben yomo, and even benefit is forbidden, as stated by the Peri Megadim. He further cites Knesset HaGedolah that porcelain vessels known as porcelain have the law of earthenware and not of glass vessels.
