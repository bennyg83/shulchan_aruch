Se'if 22. Every vessel. This entire se'if is all the language of the Tur, and like the reading of the Rosh: every vessel, etc., one uses them for matzah; and he does not have the reading "cold," for even hot is permitted.
Even of earthenware. That is to say: even according to the Rif's reading, which reads "every earthenware vessel," etc., one uses them for matzah cold: "cold" is not precise, but because of the latter clause of a yeast-house: even cold it is forbidden; and as the Mordechai wrote according to his reading.
Except a yeast-house, etc., forbidden. Like the Rosh's reading: every vessel, etc.; and he does not have the reading "cold," rather it deals with hot.
But it is forbidden to knead. See the Taz; and the Rosh wrote that it is possible like the reading "one uses matzah cold," and because of the latter clause of a yeast-house even cold is forbidden; and for example to knead in them.
And all this, etc. Like the explanation of the Arukh and all the poskim, as above in se'if 16.
But for those of, etc. As is written in the Gemara there; and as above in se'if 1. And that is according to the Tur's view, who disagrees with what is written in se'if 21: that a thing whose use is cold, even earthenware, hagalah helps, as the Tur wrote there. And from the words of Rav Hai it appears that hagalah does not help them for earthenware even if used only cold, etc.; see there. But according to what the Shulchan Aruch ruled in se'if 21 that hagalah helps, and likewise regarding wine of libation even though it is very sharp and strong, the same applies here; and so Milchamot wrote explicitly there that a yeast-house and a charoses-house have the law like wine of libation; and see there and in the Ran, who lengthened there.
And even if not, etc. Like the Rif's reading, as above.
Even cold. But the Bach and Peri Chadash emended in the Tur and wrote that one should read "rather cold," to exclude from R' Chananel and the Rif and Rambam; the Tur wrote that earthenware is equal in this to other vessels. But their words require study: for if so, what is "and even if one did not use," etc.: what is novel? Behold regarding other vessels too the law is so. And above where he wrote "but cold," etc., he should have said "and even earthenware," as he began "every vessel, even," etc.
And nowadays, etc. There in the Rif; that is to say: even other earthenware, even if used only cold, by the law of a yeast-house of earthenware.
And an oven, etc. And heating the oven from outside does not help, as above in se'if 2.
And forbidden. That is to say: a thing of matzah in kind; but in a pot there is no prohibition after the fact, as is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 92, se'if 8.
