(א) House of yeast and charoset — see responsum of Chavot Yair siman 120 who discussed kashering yeast containers in which they burn yayin nesech; fortunate is one who can make new ones, but they would not heed to forbid entirely; I commanded before hagalah to cook in the container and reeds and the hat to cook in them water with ash called lye, also much ash so they are very strong — and they impair the taste; and great supervision is needed to clean the container at its bottom and remove the reeds through an artisan, or place coals on the place of the patch; and he concluded there that after all this one who guards his soul should distance from drinking yayin nesech on Pesach — but one should not forbid them after these repairs; and see Chiddushei R' Akiva Eiger on "and it appears" etc.

(ב) But for pottery libun does not help — see Biur HaGra who wrote this is only according to Rav Hai Gaon brought in Tur that his view is hagalah does not help for pottery even if used only cold; but according to what he ruled in seif 21 that hagalah helps, the same applies here; see there. It requires study regarding other poskim — for from their view it appears that although there we hold hagalah helps, here it is worse.

(ג) Winter-house oven — this is what is made of kakalin to heat the winter house. See Mishna Berurah that the prohibition here is because heating fired from inside does not help to expel what was absorbed from its back — so too Magen Avraham and Gra; but regarding the face itself it simply appears heating helps like other ovens. Some Acharonim entirely rejected heating a winter-house oven to kasher it (see Eshel Avraham and Chayei Adam). Chayei Adam wrote: it appears what the Acharonim were stringent about regarding a winter-house oven — that libun does not help — deals like what they do in Poland where the oven is made only of kakalin and it is literally a pottery vessel; but in this country it is known they build around the kakalin a partition of bricks and its law is literally like an oven in which one bakes — regarding this we are not concerned lest they crack; therefore it appears all agree libun or coating with clay helps; and the same for ovens called in our country "oven" or "ofen" that have a cover — libun helps; therefore it appears in a place where one has no stove to cook, one may permit whitening winter-house ovens in Lithuania and the like where ovens are made thus. In essential law, even one made only of kakalin, the Acharonim agreed libun helps — since they heat from inside, the concern lest they crack does not apply; and Levush too wrote on this that it is extra stringency. In any event in our ovens certainly one should not be stringent as Chayei Adam wrote. What Chayei Adam wrote about ovens in our country etc. that have a cover — I do not know why he requires specifically a cover, for behold regarding kuvia in seif 2, if filled with coals from inside it helps even without a cover; and likewise regarding pots in Gemara it was permitted with filling coals only because of concern lest they crack though there is no cover — if so, there is no distinction here.
