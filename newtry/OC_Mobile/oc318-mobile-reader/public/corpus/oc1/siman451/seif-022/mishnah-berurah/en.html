(א) That they used them for chametz cold — meaning they used them for dry chametz, or even for something moist but did not stand there above, and it was not a sharp substance; for if it stood inside above, it becomes like pickled inside, even in other vessels, and requires hagalah or pouring, as below s.k. 131.

(ב) (c) Even in the mind, a demon who did not use them, but in the order, did not swallow anything and enough for them to pass what was taken in the eye to the hands of the vessel:

(ג) Except from the house of light, we were a vessel in which the woman drew his light in the air, and sometimes that the light was much in the same instrument, and that he was hardened into the same instrument

(ד) We would have given him some bad things with a vine and not even a potion:

(ה) Baked matzah — cold; and the same applies to other cold things, even moist, if they rinsed first; and specifically by temporary use and not fixed use, as below in seif 61 in the gloss.

(ו) It is permissible, even if it is not a refugee from the fire inside the vessel, which is in it above:

(ז) (c) It is forbidden to be slain in them - the eloquent odor in the vessel causes a buffer to the spraying in it:

(ח) But through hagalah etc. — permitted, both to knead in it and to use them with hot food. And behold, all this is from essential law; but according to what Rama wrote above in seif 17 that the custom is not to use kneading vessels because one cannot scrape — certainly one should be stringent also in a sourdough starter container. And if it is of metal, Rama already wrote above in seif 90 regarding a mortar that poskim equated it to a sourdough starter container — they practice l'chatchila to whiten it; and how much more so in an actual sourdough starter container.

(ט) One should not use them for matzah even cold — one should not use them for matzah except cold: such is the girsa of Bach and Peri Chadash; and some read "even cold." That which is written at the beginning of the seif that cold is permitted — applies to sourdough starter and charoset, and does not deal with pottery.

(י) (Celig) Do not wear arms – even in the order, and even in other tools that are not a lighthouse, and we will use them only in the order:

(כ) (Clad) and the oven of the winter house (we were a hot stove in which the winter house) which is used to put on the back of Koddida and the rest of the year's entire year, and there was a buffering taste in the heat of light while the oven is heated and does not come out of the mouths of the oven, which is made in the oven

(ל) Its law is like pottery etc. — meaning pottery never expels absorption from within its wall except through heating (where concern lest it crack does not apply, as above in seif 62 regarding a small fire); and here heating that they heat from inside [even if they kasher as required] does not help to expel absorption absorbed from above, and it is comparable to what is explained there in seif 62 that something heated from outside — heating does not help to expel what was absorbed from the other side.

(מ) To place anything on the oven — meaning when the oven is hot it is forbidden even if the matzah is cold; and likewise the reverse, when the matzah is hot even if the oven is cold. And when both are cold — by fixed use it is forbidden; by temporary use there is no prohibition if he scrubbed and cleaned the oven so no chametz b'eyna is found there, as explained above at the end of seif 61 s.k. 16. Regarding b'dieved: if he placed matzah when the oven was hot, or when the matzah was hot, or both were hot — it forbids on the underside of the matzah kedei kelipah. All this is when he placed the matzah without interruption beneath it; but if he placed pots with cooked food, even if both are hot — it does not forbid b'dieved, for absorbed prohibition does not exit from the oven wall to the pot wall; and l'chatchila pots are also forbidden to place there in all cases (unless both are cold). And when one places an interruption such as iron etc. beneath it, or plastered with clay finger-thickness — permitted to use everything from above; and all these laws apply also to kakalin made in ovens from the side, except there iron interruption or clay plaster below alone does not help unless he plastered with clay also on the sides and from above; however plaster from above does not hinder b'dieved. And when one places a cover on the pots placed there — permitted l'chatchila even when there is no plaster from above. And now we will explain regarding inside the oven: if they did not kasher beforehand, if one placed matzah inside — its law is like on the oven, as stated. Regarding baking matzot there — its law is as explained in siman 461 — see there. Regarding cooking inside — even though l'chatchila one must kasher it also for this like for baking [or plaster its floor with clay finger-thickness] — nevertheless b'dieved one does not forbid the cooked food even if they did not kasher.
