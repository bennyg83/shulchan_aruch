Se'if 1. Even those, etc. Tur; and see siman 444, se'if 3.
And it is permitted, etc. The Gemara there.
And it is good to close them, etc. Sefer HaPardes of the Rosh, and the Tur, as is written there on folio 6a: the chametz of a non-Jew, etc.; and even if he did not accept responsibility, that he need not destroy it, as is written in siman 440, se'if 2; and the same applies here; and see the Bach.
And not to place, etc., even, etc. There and there; and as is written in Zevachim 95b: Our Rabbis taught, "which," etc.
And some forbid. The Rambam at the end of chapter 5, according to his reason: since regarding other vessels hagalah is required, as the Rif and Rambam and all the poskim wrote, and they learned from what is written at the end of Avodah Zarah: things that were used with hot water, etc.; and all the more so according to those who hold that a kli sheni absorbs, from that of Chullin 8b, as is written in siman 447, se'if 3 in the gloss. And we say in Zevachim there: absorbed without cooking, etc.; and in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Maaser and third chapter of Shabbos: there an earthenware vessel absorbs; and so wrote the Rif and Rosh; and as is written in se'if 5: and if their use, etc. But that is not so: for they wrote only regarding bowls whose use is through pouring, to kasher also through pouring, as Tosafos wrote in Shabbos; for those certainly need kashering, as appears to me; and that which the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote, "except that one poured," etc.; and so wrote the Ri Azariah at the beginning of the chapter of Pesachim, distinguishing bowls into which one poured boiling water (into them) from those into which boiling water was poured. But the words of the Shulchan Aruch in se'if 5 are puzzling, and are only according to the Rashba; and see siman 447, se'if 3 in the gloss; and see Magen Avraham.
And even if. Pesachim there.
However. Zevachim there.
But regarding ovens, etc. There: rather, pots of the Mikdash, etc.; and if it were so, let one return them to the ovens, for that is possible even in Jerusalem.
Every vessel. Pesachim 30b: all vessels used with chametz cold may be used with matzah cold, except a yeast-house, etc.; so is the reading of the early authorities. And the Mordechai wrote: not that with hot [use] it is forbidden, but because of the latter clause of a yeast-house: even if one used cold, since its leavening is hard, it is as if one used hot, and it is forbidden to use even cold; and the same applies to every thing whose use is hot, lest one come to use it hot; and so too the Ran wrote from that of a yeast-house, as appears to me; only that he explained that in the first clause it is forbidden to use hot; and so wrote the Rambam; only that the Ran wrote specifically outside [a yeast-house] because it is sharp like wine of libation; and the Maggid Mishneh wrote that "one uses cold" was stated not that hot is forbidden, but to exclude that if one used hot, even cold use is forbidden; and the Ran wrote that therefore they said regarding pots on Pesach they should be broken; and in Chullin 111b, that of that plate, etc.; only that the Ran and Maggid Mishneh wrote specifically an earthenware vessel, like the reading of the Rif and Rambam and the Geonim: every earthenware vessel, etc.; and the reason is that since it has no remedy by kashering, one comes to keep it, and thereby comes to use it hot. And the Rosh does not have the reading "cold," and permits even hot; and see above se'if 22. And here he ruled according to the Mordechai's reading, who reads "every vessel," etc., and according to his explanation; and so is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, se'if 5 in the gloss; only that he wrote specifically for fixed [use], but temporary use is permitted, as is written at the end of Avodah Zarah: and thrust, etc., and to cut cold with it; but if one used cold, it is permitted even hot according to all readings, and even according to the Rambam and Ran; and see Milchamot. And even regarding earthenware, of which the Ran wrote specifically regarding chametz which is sharp like wine of libation: if one puts it away for keeping, and even if one does not put it away for keeping, only that it is earthenware and its first use was with wine of libation, it is forbidden, as is written in Avodah Zarah 33; see there. But regarding other prohibitions, no; and so he ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 121, se'if 5 in the gloss; only that he was stringent regarding other vessels if there is concern that sometimes they were used hot, since kashering is possible. And all this is with rinsing; but without rinsing it is certainly forbidden, as is written at the end of Avodah Zarah 73: things used cold, rinse them, etc.; and even to use them cold, as is written in Chullin: cold with cold it is; granted, etc.; and there 111b: a knife that slaughtered, etc.; and we hold like the view that rinsing is required, and it refers to a knife, as the Rosh wrote at the beginning of Chullin, se'if 10; see there. And even though it did not absorb at all, as is written there 8b: permitted [food] too, etc.; and likewise with a treifah knife to slaughter with it, even according to the view that a yeast-house cold needs rinsing; and specifically a kosher knife to slaughter is permitted; and see Tosafos there s.v. "a knife." And all this is regarding something moist; but regarding something dry it is permitted, as is written there siman 91, se'if 2 in the gloss; and see Tosafos of Avodah Zarah 32a, s.v. "and this," and the like.
