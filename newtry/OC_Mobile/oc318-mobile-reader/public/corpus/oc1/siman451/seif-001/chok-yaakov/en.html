Earthenware pots. This is not specifically pots, for the same law applies to bowls whose use is by pouring from a first vessel or by a second vessel. This too is the law, and so is implied by the Rambam in chapter 5 of the laws of chametz, law 25; see there.
Earthenware that were used. The Issur VeHeter writes in klal 28, law 43, that new utensils bought in the market, even earthenware utensils, need not be treated doubtfully lest they were used, for it is not at all usual to use new utensils. But metal utensils bought from a non-Jew in his home, when one does not recognize with certainty that they are new, require, out of doubt, hagalah or libun according to the manner of their use.
All year. The same applies if chametz was used in it during Pesach, as explained above at the beginning of siman 442; see what I wrote there in s.k. 4.
He scrubs them well. If he did not scrub them before Pesach, he scrubs them during Pesach or after Pesach, like the law of one who did not remove chametz before Pesach, which was explained above in siman 435. The same applies to other utensils that are not earthenware: if he does not want to kasher them by hagalah or libun, he may also use this remedy, to scrub them and keep them until after Pesach. The text merely speaks in the usual case, since with other utensils, because they have a remedy, one does not usually do this, but rather performs hagalah or libun, as will be explained before us.
And puts them away on Pesach. Maharam of Rothenburg wrote in a responsum, siman 441, that what the liturgical poet composed, regarding earthenware pots by breaking them, and afterward, that they permitted them with a partition in order to hide them away, is not difficult, for such is the way of poets, to arrange both the words of Rav and the words of Shmuel; end quote. In truth, in our piyyut I did not find this, but in the piyyut in the Musaf of Shabbos HaGadol of the communities of Ashkenaz I did find it. According to what I wrote above in siman 447, s.k. 4, in the name of the Rokeach, this piyyut may be resolved another way. One may also say that this is its meaning: earthenware pots by breaking them, meaning that although they have no remedy to expel anything except breaking, nevertheless on Pesach they permitted them by means of partitions, to hide them away. The explanations of their piyyutim require study, for I do not have them available to examine.
So that he not come to use them on Pesach. This implies that even with utensils that are not ben yomo we are also concerned about this. This is somewhat difficult, for according to the Mechaber above, siman 447, seif 10, a spoiled taste is permitted on Pesach, and it has already been explained many times in the words of the poskim that whatever is permitted after the fact, we do not decree against ab initio lest he forget. All the more so with those utensils, where there is only a concern for a minute amount of chametz. Perhaps one may say that regarding chametz, since people are not separate from it all year, we decree more strictly. In practice there is no difference, since we rule in accordance with the glosses of the Rav above in siman 447, to forbid even a minute amount and even a spoiled taste on Pesach.
And it is good to close them in a room. This is the wording of the Tur, and it is from the responsa of the Rosh, klal 14, siman 1, not like the Beis Yosef. See the Bach, who was stringent and protested against those who place tin chametz utensils above, in a place that a person's hand cannot reach. See my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 17, s.k. 11, where I wrote in the name of my father-in-law, the gaon Rabbi Wolf, may his light shine, a major objection to the Bach in this matter, in order to uphold the custom. This is also the agreement of the later authorities, and so is the practice.
Nor for any earthenware vessel that was used with hot food. This is the wording of the Tur. The Beis Yosef explained that his intent is that even if it was used only as a second vessel, heating it does not help. Similarly, the Rambam in chapter 5 of the laws of chametz equated an earthenware vessel used as a second vessel with one used as a first vessel, that it has no remedy; end quote. If so, it appears to me that when the Mechaber copied here the wording of the Tur, his intent was to forbid even a second vessel, in accordance with the opinion of the Rav in the gloss. However, the Rav in Darkei Moshe wrote that according to the Tur it absorbs only through a first vessel or by pouring, but not through a second vessel, unlike the Beis Yosef; end quote. Therefore he needed to gloss here upon the wording of the Mechaber, which is the wording of the Tur, that some say even a second vessel. This is clear. But certainly the Mechaber himself also holds that it absorbs and becomes forbidden, as is explicit from his words nearby, seif 5: if their use is in a second vessel, their kashering is in a second vessel. It is explicit from his words that there is absorption through a second vessel, and so too is the wording of the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 121. All this is simple, not like the Magen Avraham, s.k. 3, who wrote that the Beis Yosef omitted the law of a second vessel, which shows that he retracted and that one need not perform hagalah. This is not so; rather, the matter is clear as I wrote. Nevertheless, in practice, one who examines the Rif, the Rambam, the Rosh in chapter Kol Shaah, and all the other poskim, will not find it at all explicit in their words that a vessel becomes forbidden through a second vessel, except for bowls and similar items whose use is by pouring, which require hagalah by pouring. This they called in their words a second vessel, as the Magen Avraham wrote. This is also explicit in Kol Bo in the laws of Pesach, and so the Issur VeHeter ruled. See siman 467, s.k. 33. On this basis what I asked in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 33, s.k. 3, is well resolved. However, in the Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 121, and in Shibbolei HaLeket, klal 7, siman 58, they wrote in the name of Baal HaDibros as the Mechaber wrote nearby in seif 5, that if their use is in a second vessel, their kashering is in a second vessel, and a vessel used by pouring, etc. This implies that it speaks even of an actual second vessel, as the Shach wrote in siman 105. The Taz also rules stringently there regarding a second vessel, as I also wrote in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 33, and in Pri Chadash, Yoreh Deah siman 68, he was very stringent regarding a second vessel. Nevertheless, in a case of great loss and prevention of Yom Tov joy, particularly with a utensil that is not ben yomo, it appears to me to be lenient after the fact if one used a utensil that had been used as a second vessel, even if it was earthenware, since most early and later authorities agree that a second vessel does not cause absorption at all, as explained in Beis Yosef, Yoreh Deah siman 68. See my Minchas Yaakov, klal 33, and what I wrote there. In addition, many are lenient regarding spoiled taste in the case of chametz.
Lest he have pity on them because they may crack. This is because all year they were heated from the outside. This is unlike ovens, which are heated from the inside, where we are not concerned that perhaps they may crack, and heating is effective. So too I concluded in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 60, s.k. 2, from the Gemara and early poskim, and see seif 2, unlike the Bach, who erred in this. See there. I saw that the later authorities here and in siman 461 all intended to say what I wrote there. Examine it carefully.
Any utensil that requires libun. The Issur VeHeter writes in klal 58, law 35: utensils that require libun, if one performed hagalah on them without libun in fire and then used them with hot food, the dish is forbidden even after the fact, since the utensil does not discharge everything through hagalah, and whenever one uses it with hot food it discharges little by little; end quote. See Yoreh Deah siman 121 in the gloss, and so wrote the Rashba in Toras HaBayis.
It is forbidden to use it even cold. That is in a regular manner of use, lest he come to use it hot; therefore, even if he washed it first, it is forbidden. But for incidental use, it is permitted by means of washing. All this I concluded in my work Minchas Yaakov, klal 11, s.k. 6, unlike Olas Shabbos, who erred in this. In a regular manner, even on erev Pesach after the time of its prohibition, it is forbidden to use it, as I wrote there. So also appears to be the agreement of the Taz, and this is the main ruling, unlike Pri Chadash in Yoreh Deah siman 98, who has another approach in these laws. According to what I wrote there, everything is settled. Examine it carefully, and see Soles LeMinchah.
