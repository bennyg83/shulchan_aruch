(א) And permitted to leave them. Some say for chametz that became mixed it is forbidden to leave it, for there it appears though not recognizable; but absorption in a vessel is not visible and common (Ra'avad); so too Rabbeinu Yeruham; and so in the Ran. Some want to bring proof from Tosafot that specifically if they used them a little chametz is permitted, but if they used them much — forbidden; they did not examine well, for Tosafot [speaks] so per Rav, but we hold like R' Shimon — even if they used them much, permitted to leave them since not in existence; and therefore permitted afterward to cook in them l'chatchila.

(ב) And hide them, etc. Those who place vessels atop wall heights for decoration — even ten cubits high — forbidden (Bach); possibly they practiced thus because not ben yomo; and even if used, the majority permit as siman 447 seif 10; all the more so those whose main use is kli sheini or cold, like flasks — no concern; one who is stringent — see Radbaz vol. 1 siman 35.

(ג) Even in kli sheini. Meaning even if he did not pour from the pot into it, only poured from pot to kli sheini and then placed this vessel into it. Darkei Moshe wrote: per Tur it absorbs only via kli rishon or pouring; but kli sheini not — not like Beit Yosef. It appears even Rambam forbade only pouring regarding metal vessels used in kli sheini like bowls and cups — pour boiling water on them; bowls' use is via pouring. Furthermore from "pour boiling water" it implies hagalah must be via pouring; therefore it deals with use via pouring; and what is called kli sheini is because after pouring into it it is kli sheini. So too Rif; and Rabbeinu Yosef Karo omitted kli sheini law — he retracted. See siman 447 seif 3; already practiced to kasher everything; but b'dieved if used in a vessel known never used in kli rishon — requires study, for Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen is stringent all year in kli sheini and ruled peeling required — therefore at least on Pesach practice like him.

(ד) Lest it crack. Bach wrote it implies in pottery oven heating helps, not concerned for cracking; however in Tosafot Pesachim ch. 2 and Avodah Zarah ch. 2 — pottery oven even returned to kilns does not help; therefore even internal heating does not help; so we rule. He was not precise, for Gemara explicitly states this and that in pottery oven — heating inside, etc.; we hold like this answer; so too in Zevachim 95-96 — make Temple oven of pottery, etc. Tosafot who wrote heating does not help speaks of tumah — if became tamei, heating does not make it like new vessel; but for issur, once issur expelled — suffices. So too the Gaon in Chadashim. Tosafot Avodah Zarah regarding issur deals with oven heated with orlah peels; so too Tosafot Zevachim explicitly — see there. However Maharil wrote: not called kilns unless heated 2-3 days — see s.k. 22. Levush wrote to be lenient with heating and slight plastering; in any case heat well until whitened — then no worse than pot full of coals; here no concern for cracking; also Gemara explicitly says until oven is heated — does not require kilns; we establish in pottery. Further: bricks burned in kiln have all law of pottery — thicker; if boiling spilled — no remedy except libun; sun-dried brick has law of earthenware. See Tur Yoreh De'ah — earthen and stone vessels like pottery; we do not hold thus.
