And good [Taz]. Bach forbids placing in great height both chametz vessels on Pesach and Pesach vessels the rest of the year — they are collateral vessels from the reason of lest one forget. But Pesach vessels in great height is a remote concern lest one forget and use chametz; and on Pesach lest one use matzah — but even if so, we hold nitelaf is permitted on Pesach, as in siman 447. Rosh forbids all year and Magen Avraham forbids even without a partition. Bach was stringent even above a place a person's hand does not reach; Magen Avraham upheld the custom; the Acharonim agreed. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote that one may permit with a partition similar to burying — see Rokeach on siman 447 note 4. Or one may say paytanim mean: even pottery that has no remedy except breaking — nevertheless they permitted on Pesach with sufficient partitions to bury them similar; and it requires study in the explanations of their piyyutim. Maaseh Rokeach wrote not to leave year-round without a partition — we are also concerned thus even in vessels that are not ben yomo, since we are strict even for a minute amount of chametz; and per what we hold in the Rav's gloss on siman 447 to forbid even in any amount and nitelaf on Pesach. And it is good to lock them in a room — such is Tur's language from Rosh responsum general rule 14 siman 1 — not like Beit Yosef; Bach quarreled with those who place tin chametz vessels above an unreachable place; Minchat Yehudah disagreed; such is practiced. "Not any pottery vessel used hot" — Beit Yosef explained: even if one did not use it except in kli sheini — heating does not help; Rambam equates pottery kli sheini to kli rishon. The Mechaber appears to have copied Tur's language — his intent to forbid even in kli sheini, like the Rama's view in the gloss; but Rabbeinu Moshe in Darkei Moshe wrote per Tur it does not absorb except through kli rishon or via pouring — not like Beit Yosef. The Mechaber's own view too is that it absorbs and becomes forbidden; explicit from seif 5: and if their use is in kli sheini — kasher them in kli sheini. For the law, one who examines Rif, Rambam, Rosh and poskim — it is not explained that the vessel is forbidden through kli sheini; only in bowls whose use is via pouring. Nevertheless in a case of great loss — especially in a vessel that is not ben yomo — it appears to me to be lenient b'dieved if one used a vessel used in kli sheini, even pottery, since the majority of Rishonim agreed kli sheini does not absorb; nat bar nat for detriment — many are lenient regarding chametz. Lest one be concerned lest they crack — since they were fired all year from outside — not so with ovens fired from inside; Minchat Yehudah general rule 60 note 2; not like Bach; see siman 461. Forbidden to use it even cold in a fixed manner — Minchat Yehudah general rule 11 note 16; not like Olat Shabbat; fixed manner — even orally after its prohibition time; Taz agrees; not like Peri Chadash Yoreh De'ah siman 98; see Solut leMinchah.
