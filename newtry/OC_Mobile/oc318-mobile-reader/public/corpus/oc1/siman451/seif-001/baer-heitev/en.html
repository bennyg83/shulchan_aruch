Pots. Not specifically pots; the same applies to bowls and a kli sheni (and the same applies to those used through pouring): the law is so as well.
That one used. Issur VeHeter wrote in klal 58, din 43, that new vessels purchased from the market, even earthenware, one need not suspect that they were used, for it is not the way at all to use new vessels; but metal vessels purchased from a non-Jew in his house, and one does not recognize in them that they are certainly new, need from doubt to be hagaled or libuned according to their use. Chok Yaakov.
One scrubs them. And if one did not scrub them before Pesach, one scrubs them during Pesach or after Pesach, by the law of one who did not destroy before Pesach, as is written above in siman 435.
Modest. And those who set vessels atop the walls for ornament, even ten stories high, are forbidden: Bach. And Taz and Magen Avraham wrote: and it is possible that they practiced so because they are not ben yomo; and even if they were used, the majority permit, as is written in siman 447, se'if 10 (and the Taz added: since they are regularly there all year, one will not come to err); and all the more so those whose primary use is in a kli sheni or cold, such as jugs: one need not be concerned; and one who is stringent, may blessing come upon him; and see the Radbaz, part 1, siman 35. And see Yad Aharon.
In a kli sheni. (That is, one poured from a kli rishon into a second, and afterward placed this vessel into it.) And where there is major loss and prevention of Yom Tov joy, one may be lenient regarding a kli sheni if it is not ben yomo. Chok Yaakov; and see Magen Avraham.
To the kiln. I was asked about earthenware vessels that remained after burning: that is to say, whether it is permitted to use them on Pesach, for it is known that this is more than returning [them] to the kiln; and I permitted it; and nevertheless everything is according to the matter.
Cold. That is, in a fixed way, lest one come to use it hot; and if so, even if one rinsed first, it is forbidden; but in a temporary way it is permitted to use it by rinsing; and in a fixed way, even on eve of Pesach after the time of its prohibition, it is forbidden to use it. See Chok Yaakov.
