[4] [Levush] And even if they still, etc. It appears that this also refers to the first clause. I found the same stated in the name of the Or Zarua, as noted by Malbushei Yom Tov. The Rokeach, siman 196, writes of an incident in which a siege was laid against the city of Worms, and they permitted Jews to go out and return with their weapons. The reason was that if the Jews did not help the townspeople, the townspeople themselves might kill them.
[5] [Levush] Easily, etc. This requires study, because Rashi in Eruvin 45 writes that perhaps the enemy will capture that city, and from there the entire land will become easier for them to conquer. I later found the same reasoning in the Raavan, siman 363.
[6] [Levush] Even if they did not come, etc. See the preceding seif, which permits action even when they merely intended to come, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov.
