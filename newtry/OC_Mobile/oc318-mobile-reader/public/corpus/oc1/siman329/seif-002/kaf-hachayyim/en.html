5) [Seif 2] One of them separated into another courtyard, etc. It is obvious that when they remain in the same courtyard and a collapse falls upon them there, the rubble is cleared because the group is established in place, and anything established in place is treated as evenly divided. The ruling teaches that even if one person separated into another courtyard and the structure fell upon him there, the rubble is cleared. Tur.
6) There: One of them separated, etc. This is not limited to one person. Even if most separated and only one person remained at the original fixed location, and a structure fell upon one of those in another courtyard, the rubble is cleared for him because the original fixed group remains in its place. Bach; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2.
7) There: One of them separated, etc. Although regarding other prohibitions we say that anything separated is presumed to come from the majority, as stated in siman 439, the law is lenient regarding danger to life. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2. This is also stated in the Gemara. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2.
8) See Even HaEzer, siman 4, seif 34, which states that Shabbos is not desecrated for an infant found in a city whose majority is non-Jewish. Since all the inhabitants leave their fixed locations each day, the majority is followed. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 15 of the Laws of Forbidden Relations; Beis Yosef there; Magen Avraham there. Rema, however, writes there in the gloss that some say the rubble is cleared on Shabbos even when most inhabitants are non-Jews. Beis Shmuel explains there that the residents of a city are not considered mobile. It is therefore as though one person separated from a fixed location, in which case the majority is not followed; see there. Tosefes Shabbos there cites this and writes that we rule leniently regarding danger to life. He questions why Magen Avraham did not cite this; see there. All this concerns an infant found in a city. If the infant is found in a house, that is a fixed location, and all opinions agree that the rubble is cleared. Beis Shmuel there.
9) If an infant is found on a road, one can say that if most travelers are non-Jews, all opinions agree that the rubble is not cleared for him. This resembles a case in which none of the original fixed group remains, and in such a case the majority is followed. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2.
