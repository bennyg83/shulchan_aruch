(א) (s.k. 2) And one departed etc. And see in Even HaEzer siman 4 seif 34 that an infant found in a city whose majority is gentiles — one does not desecrate Shabbat for him, since every day they all separate from their place of establishment we follow the majority; and Rama there brought one view that we desecrate Shabbat for him since not every day they all separate — therefore Keshet HaShabbos challenged, for in pikuach nefesh we go to leniency, it should be for Magen Avraham to bring Rama's view:

(ב) What is written in Shulchan Aruch seif 3: one upon whom fell... doubtful he is there... Sefer Torah VeHaShabbos wrote: the Acharonim mean that perhaps they already took him from there, but if the doubt is whether initially a collapse fell upon him or not — they do not dig out, end of his words. And one must say his reason is because we establish him on his chezkah, and this is not compelling, for we rule that rov is preferable to chezkah as Rashba explains in last chapter of The Woman. And since in pikuach nefesh we do not follow rov — all the more we do not follow chezkah; and in truth he too concludes requires study for the law, end of his words Sefer Torah VeHaShabbos:

(ג) And in my humble opinion, the doubt of the Acharonim is from another angle — for it appears that in pikuach nefesh we go to leniency even against rov, and if so all the more against chezkah; but combined together — rov and chezkah — perhaps we do not go against them to leniency in pikuach nefesh. And according to Rashba's responsum siman 401 brought by Shach in brief Yoreh De'ah siman 114 in general rules end seif 27, and Peri Chadash there brought at length — the reason we go to leniency in a doubtful case is that through the first doubt it was half and half; and when the second doubt was added the side of permission increased and permission is the majority and prohibition is the minority; and he wrote it is possible preferable to the majority of the Acharonim; and if so when there is a doubt whether a collapse fell upon him — there is chezkah and here there is also a doubtful case — there is rov and chezkah — perhaps in such a case we do not go to leniency in pikuach nefesh; but according to this we derive where there is no doubtful case except doubt whether a collapse fell upon him — the Acharonim admit they dig him out, and requires study for the law:
