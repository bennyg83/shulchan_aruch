Seif 1, all pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat. NB: Behold there is to be in doubt in the law of pikuach nefesh that overrides Shabbat, or doubtful pikuach nefesh that overrides Shabbat — if through his negligence he brought himself to this danger, whether it afterward overrides Shabbat or not. If we say as we rule a positive commandment pushes aside a negative commandment, nevertheless a positive commandment that comes through negligence does not push aside — similarly in pikuach nefesh it would be so, that pikuach nefesh pushes aside, nevertheless if the danger came through negligence it does not push aside, or not; rather we say pikuach nefesh is stringent and in every case one is obligated to save him and push aside Shabbat. And it appears to me to resolve this doubt from what we learn in chapter 2 of Shabbat daf 45: they asked Rav what is the law to move a Chanukah lamp etc. until he said to them R' Shimon is worthy to rely upon in time of distress; and Rashi explained danger on Shabbat at length in the sugya of Shabbat. And I strongly question: if there is danger — why specifically "R' Shimon is worthy"; behold without this there is nothing that stands before pikuach nefesh, and even doubtful pikuach nefesh overrides Shabbat — why need to say "R' Shimon is worthy." And perforce it is proven that where danger came through negligence, such pikuach nefesh does not push aside Shabbat; and since Tosafos challenged there "behold we say that in time of danger one leaves it on his table and it suffices," and they answered that it deals with if it occurred that he did not leave it on erev Shabbat — if so, since he was negligent in this, such pikuach nefesh does not override Shabbat; and this is a novel law; also for Tosafos' second answer there our question remains in place — requires study; however one may say Tosafos' second answer intends thus: there it deals with that they decreed only not to kindle a Chanukah lamp, but other lamps are permitted — therefore there is a remedy to leave it on his table; but here it deals with that colleagues decreed not to kindle any lamp — if so, what difference whether on his table or not; nevertheless since by law he was exempt from kindling since it is time of danger, therefore if he kindled it is negligence and Shabbat is not given to push aside, were it not for the reason that R' Shimon is worthy to rely upon — examine well; and this is a great novelty in my humble opinion. Again it was told to me that in Sefer Tosafos Shabbat siman 248 he noted this question and explained that not specifically danger but close to danger; and in my view this does not appear so, for if so why did they challenge from there that one leaves it on his table — perhaps there it deals with actual danger but here it deals with not actual danger — therefore obligated to leave it in its place; and perforce they do not hold so — examine well. And behold it appears another proof for this law from Tosafos' words: why did they challenge from that which we say there "leave on his table" — perhaps there it deals with that they did not decree intending to make them transgress their religion — therefore he may transgress and not be killed, therefore permitted on his table; but here it deals with that they decreed intending to make them transgress their religion, and in this even on the king's carpet he should be killed and not transgress — therefore obligated to leave it on the threshold outside as its law; however to take the lamp afterward in this he is not obligated to surrender his life — why did Tosafos not answer thus; and perforce it was difficult for them — if so he was obligated to leave it on the threshold outside, and if so it is not negligence — why need to say R' Shimon is worthy to rely upon; and perforce he was not obligated to leave it on the threshold outside; and if it deals according to Tosafos in Shabbat — still difficult, for he should answer that here their intent is to make them transgress the religion — therefore he was obligated to leave it outside; and nevertheless needed to say R' Shimon is worthy to rely upon since it is not actual danger — and perforce they explain actual danger — our law is compelled — examine well:
