8) See Even HaEzer, siman 4, seif 34, which states that Shabbos is not desecrated for an infant found in a city whose majority is non-Jewish. Since all the inhabitants leave their fixed locations each day, the majority is followed. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 15 of the Laws of Forbidden Relations; Beis Yosef there; Magen Avraham there. Rema, however, writes there in the gloss that some say the rubble is cleared on Shabbos even when most inhabitants are non-Jews. Beis Shmuel explains there that the residents of a city are not considered mobile. It is therefore as though one person separated from a fixed location, in which case the majority is not followed; see there. Tosefes Shabbos there cites this and writes that we rule leniently regarding danger to life. He questions why Magen Avraham did not cite this; see there. All this concerns an infant found in a city. If the infant is found in a house, that is a fixed location, and all opinions agree that the rubble is cleared. Beis Shmuel there.
