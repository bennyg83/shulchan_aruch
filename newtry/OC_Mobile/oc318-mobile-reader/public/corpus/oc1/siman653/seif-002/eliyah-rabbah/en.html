[2] Not to smell, etc. The Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that this is specifically at the time of taking, but before or afterward, he does recite a berachah, for it is like fruit that stands for eating and he took it to smell; and so it is implied from the language of Semak. End quote. In my humble opinion, it is not comparable to fruit, which stands for eating and for smelling; but an esrog is only for the mitzvah, and its fragrance is completely negated from it. His proof from the Semag is astonishing, for the Semag permits reciting a berachah, only that he was concerned lest one forget; therefore he wrote not to smell it at the time of taking. But according to those who prohibit reciting a berachah, it appears that it is prohibited all day. At the end of siman 216 I wrote more about this.
