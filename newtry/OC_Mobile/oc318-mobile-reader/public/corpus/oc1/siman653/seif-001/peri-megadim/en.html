Hadas. See the Taz. All seven implies even the seventh day, when there is no issue except Shabbos; see siman 645 and Magen Avraham here, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. Regarding an esrog, all seven days one should not smell it because of the berachah, for it does not stand for fragrance during the time of the mitzvah. Even on Shabbos alone, when he has another fruit to smell, he recites a berachah and smells the esrog as well, as in siman 651:4 according to his approach there; see there. In Rabbeinu Yerucham 4 and 81, according to their approach and the Taz, whenever the status of eating is removed, the status of fragrance is also removed, and even not at the time of taking one does not recite a berachah over it; even on Shabbos, only over another fruit does he recite a berachah and then smell the esrog as well, even on a weekday, and on Shabbos it is fit for moving. He brought the words of Semak, who holds that one recites a berachah to smell it, only that it is a foolish custom to smell it at the time of taking, for they think it is a mitzvah and there is no need; on the contrary, sometimes one does not remember the fragrance berachah at the time of taking. But not at the time of taking, according to him, he smells and recites a berachah, like Avi HaEzri; see Beis Yosef. This is not so according to us, according to the Taz's approach: even not at the time of taking, it is proper to refrain because of doubt regarding the berachah, and it is prohibited to benefit, etc. What the Mechaber wrote, 'one should refrain,' is not good. In Rabbeinu Yerucham, seif 14, he wrote simply like the first opinion, that one recites a berachah, and that it is proper not to smell. With difficulty, one may say that here it speaks at the time of taking, and there not at the time of taking, and it is not necessary; understand this. With Hashem's help, it will be explained further in the Magen Avraham.
