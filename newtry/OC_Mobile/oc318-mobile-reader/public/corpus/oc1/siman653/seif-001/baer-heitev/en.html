To smell. Since its main purpose is only to smell it, from this it was set aside for all seven days, like sukkah wood. But the esrog's main purpose is eating, and it was set aside only from that, not from smelling. Rather, they disagree about the berachah, for some say that since it was not made for fragrance, the berachah 'Who gives a good fragrance in fruits' does not apply to it; therefore one should not smell the esrog all seven days. The Semak wrote: those who smell it when reciting the berachah on taking it have a foolish custom. End quote from the Taz. The Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that a hadas is prohibited to smell even on Shabbos, for it was set aside for all seven days, and so it appears in siman 665. But an esrog is permitted to smell on Shabbos, as written in the gloss in siman 658:2, for at least now it is not being used for a mitzvah. It further appears to me that specifically when he smells it at the time of taking it to fulfill the obligation, some say he does not recite a berachah; but if he takes it in the middle of the day to smell it, everyone agrees he recites a berachah, like fruit that stands for eating and he took it to smell; see siman 216:5.
