It appears to me that a hadas, etc. For it was set aside for all seven, etc. But an esrog, etc. Meaning: it is understandable with a hadas, where the reason for the prohibition to smell is because it stands only for fragrance, and since he set it aside for the mitzvah, he set it aside from fragrance, as is stated in the Talmud. If so, it was set aside for all seven days, and therefore even on Shabbos, when we do not take the lulav, it is nevertheless prohibited to smell it. But an esrog stands for eating, and when they set it aside for the mitzvah, they set it aside only from eating and not from smelling, as stated in the Talmud. Therefore, by law it is permitted to smell it, but because of doubt about the berachah one should not smell it. Some say that one does not recite a berachah over it, because it does not stand for fragrance but for the mitzvah. Since on Shabbos, when we do not take the lulav, it does not stand for the mitzvah, according to everyone one recites a berachah over its fragrance. So too it is implied from the language of Semak in the Beis Yosef. Meaning, the Rav Beis Yosef brought the language of Semak. The Semak holds like the one who says that one recites a berachah over it, and wrote as follows: it is good to refrain from smelling it at the time of taking, for most times one does not remember this berachah, and it is prohibited to benefit from this world without a berachah. Those who smell it when reciting the berachah on taking it have a foolish custom. End quote from Semak. The Magen Avraham inferred from what the Semak wrote, 'it is good to refrain, etc., at the time of taking,' that not at the time of taking, it is permitted to smell it. If the concern is that he may forget to recite the berachah, that concern applies all day. Therefore he holds that what the Semak wrote, that most times one does not remember this berachah, is specifically at the time of taking; for not at the time of taking, where everyone agrees one recites a berachah over it, this is known to everyone according to the Semak, and he will certainly recite the berachah, since it is prohibited to benefit, etc. This is not so at the time of taking, where there are differing opinions and some say one does not recite a berachah. Although according to the Semak one must recite a berachah even at the time of taking, nevertheless it is not clear to everyone that one recites a berachah; and even those who know one must recite a berachah, since the berachah then is not agreed upon by everyone, forgetfulness will more likely occur, and we are concerned lest he forget to recite the berachah. From this it is proven that not at the time of taking, everyone agrees one recites a berachah. But in my humble opinion, this is not a complete inference, for one may say that for this reason he wrote, 'it is good to refrain, etc., at the time of taking,' to exclude the common custom of smelling it at the time of taking, as he wrote afterward. See Magen Avraham, siman 216, seif katan 21, where he wrote as follows: I found written in the name of Rashal that specifically at the time of taking it for the mitzvah, but before then and afterward, he recites a berachah. End quote there.
