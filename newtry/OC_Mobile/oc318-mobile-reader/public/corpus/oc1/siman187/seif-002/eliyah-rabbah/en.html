[2] And some say that he must, etc. Bach agrees, but only as a substitute for Bircas HaMazon. Other blessings must be recited in their regular form. Bach in siman 192 records a longer abridged formula with which one fulfills the obligation, and Mateh Moshe cites it. I am astonished, for one certainly fulfills the obligation with the shorter formula, as is explicit in Berachos 40b and in all the poskim. Bach also writes that children younger than eight should be taught the longer text. In my humble opinion, since the shorter formula is valid, they should be taught that formula. I am also astonished that he changes the order to “The Merciful One, Master, King,” in order to place the Divine name before kingship. The word “Merciful One” itself is a Divine name, as stated in the Gemara there. It is therefore entirely proper to say, “The Merciful One, King, Master,” as appears in all the poskim and above in siman 167, seif 10. Bach in siman 192 further cites the abridged text from Bedek HaBayis: recite the entire first blessing in its proper form, and afterward say, “We thank You, Hashem our God, for having granted,” and so forth. It appears there that one who is under duress and unable to recite the full text may use this formula. It also appears to me that children younger than eight should be taught this version, except that in place of the full first blessing they should be taught the formula beginning, “Blessed is the Merciful One,” which we mentioned.
