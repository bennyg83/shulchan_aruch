Covenant and Torah—even if he omitted only one, etc.—it is in Talmud: must say therein thanks at beginning and end; one who omits must not omit less than one; all who omit from one [meaning who said nothing as Rosh]—this is repugnant, etc.; and whoever does not say covenant and Torah in blessing of land and kingdom of house of David in Boneh Yerushalayim—did not fulfill his obligation; and it implies plainly from this language that at the beginning if he did not say thanks—though he acted improperly and it is repugnant—nevertheless b'dieved he fulfilled; and so Sefer Maaseh Rokeach and Shulchan Aruch HaGra; and Magen Avraham's words written opposite require study; and though one can support from Tur's language that if he did not mention what Sages required to mention he did not fulfill—end—that he refers only to good broad land; and to covenant and Torah written at beginning of matter.

They return him—see Chayei Adam who wrote if uncertain whether he said covenant and Torah must return; and see there in name of some their reason that mention of covenant and Torah in birkat hamazon is d'oraisa from Tosafot Berakhot 20b see there; and this matter is not clear—for there are Rishonim who hold it is d'rabbanan; see novellae Rabbenu Aharon HaLevi printed anew on Rif Berakhot who wrote covenant and Torah in blessing of land and kingdom of house of David in Boneh Yerushalayim are d'rabbanan; and so in Shitah Mekubetzet there; also from Beit Yosef's language it is proven he holds mention is d'rabbanan [see Peri Megadim]; if so when uncertain he need not return; and know further that for us whose birkat hamazon formula is accustomed in everyone's mouth—this is not common at all that if he doubts whether he said—we rely certainly he said—for his lips are agent; and as found above siman 64 s.4.
