Seif katan 3, "and women," etc. The wording begins with 2 categories, saying that women and servants should not say "covenant" and "Torah." This implies that women should say neither covenant nor Torah, and servants likewise should say neither. Yet it concludes with only 1 reason, that women are not members of the covenant, which would imply that women may say Torah and that servants may say covenant. Tosafos at the beginning of Perek Mi SheMeito, Berachos 20b, explain the question whether women's obligation in Birkat HaMazon is rabbinic on the ground that they are not included equally in covenant and Torah; this was cited above in siman 185. In Berachos 49a, Rabbi Zeira said to Rav Chisda, "Let the master come and teach." Rav Chisda replied that he had not mastered Birkat HaMazon. The Gemara's discussion indicates that omission of covenant and Torah is significant where the wording must be suitable for all those being discharged. The difficulty remains concerning the wording for women and servants. If the reason regarding Torah were simply that women do not study the entire Torah, one must still account for the fact that women recite Birkat HaTorah because they are obligated to learn the laws of the mitzvot that apply to them, as explained in siman 47 and Yoreh De'ah siman 246, seif 6. The verse "Thus shall you say to the house of Yaakov" is interpreted as referring to the women. Why, then, should they not say "Your Torah that You taught us"? Likewise, Avodah Zarah 27a discusses whether a woman has the halachic status associated with brit milah. According to the derivation from "he shall surely perform the milah," a woman is regarded as included in that status, whereas according to the derivation from "and you shall keep My covenant," women are not members of that covenant. It is therefore possible that women may say the wording, "Your covenant that You sealed in our flesh," according to the first approach, because they are treated as belonging to the covenantal category. When Rav Chisda omitted covenant and Torah in a zimun that discharged women, the point was that those phrases do not apply to women in precisely the same manner as to men. Men bear the actual sign of brit milah and are commanded in study of the entire Torah. This explains the view that if covenant and Torah were omitted from Birkat HaMazon one nevertheless fulfilled the obligation. Regarding pronunciation: the wording should be read carefully in "lo chiser lanu" and "al yechsar lanu," with the vocalization recorded by the authorities. "She'atah zan" likewise has its proper vocalization, and "b'fi kol chai" is read as a construct phrase; see siman 51. Shelah writes that one should say "chaser yechsar," and explains that the numerical value represented by the vowels corresponds to "lechem."
