"If he did not mention." If he did not say an expression of thanksgiving at both the beginning and the end, he is required to repeat. This appears from the Tur. One should say "at every time and at every hour," because "times" include periods of cold and heat, and one who changes the wording is mistaken. [Darchei Moshe; Mateh Moshe] Amen is not answered here, because this is not the end of the blessing. The only reason people commonly pause here is because of Al HaNissim. See siman 284. "And women," etc. This requires study, because the passage begins with 2 matters and ends with only 1. Tosafos at the beginning of Perek Mi SheMeito explicitly state that women should not say covenant and Torah, and Tosafos and Rashi in Arachin write likewise. Berachos 48b states: covenant does not apply to women; Torah and kingship do not apply to women or servants. This requires study, unlike Olat Tamid and Levush, and it also requires study what basis women now have for reciting these phrases. Lechem Chamudot strained to provide a reason. Bedek HaBayit writes that women are like men because a man is not called a complete person until he has a wife, and the 2 are therefore considered 1 body. This too is a strained defense of the custom. In my view there is another difficulty. We rule in siman 47 that women recite Birkat HaTorah because they are obligated to learn the mitzvot that apply to them, and the same is ruled in Yoreh De'ah siman 246, seif 6. We also expound, "Thus shall you say to the house of Yaakov," as referring to the women. Why, then, should women not say, "Your Torah that You taught us"? Furthermore, Avodah Zarah 27a states that according to the view derived from "he shall surely perform the milah," a woman is considered within the category of those associated with milah, whereas according to the view derived from "and you shall keep My covenant," women are not members of the covenant. It is therefore possible that women can say, "Your covenant that You sealed in our flesh," because they are treated as belonging to that category. The statement that Rav Chisda did not say covenant and Torah was in a case in which he led zimun and discharged women. He therefore omitted covenant and Torah because these matters are not equal in women and men. Men actually bear the sign of brit milah and study the entire Torah. This is why he held that if these phrases were omitted, one nevertheless fulfilled the obligation. The pronunciation of "lo chiser lanu" and "al yechsar lanu" should follow the vocalization recorded by the authorities. "She'atah zan" has a kamatz under the shin. "B'fi kol chai" is read as a construct phrase; see siman 51. Shelah writes to say "chaser yechsar," because the numerical value of the vowels equals that of "lechem."
