[1] [Levush] Fresh and clean, etc. And since I saw many variants in the texts I exerted myself and examined the rishonim to correct the nusach of Birkat Hamazon as proper — and this emerged first: "u'v'rachamav noten lechem" — so is R' David Abudraham and not in the nusach "noten." "Lo chasar lanu v'al yechsar lanu mazon" — some practice to say the word chasar with chet kamatz and samech patach; some with chet chirik and samech patach — they must also say yechsar with yod in sheva and chet patach and samech tzere so both are from one root, as Mateh Moshe wrote; Shelah wrote I chose the first way. "Ki hu zan u'mefarnes lakol" — so in Mordechai and R' David Abudraham and Mateh Moshe, and not as Orach Chayim who wrote ki hu Kel zan — and Mordechai explained zan u'mefarnes: zan in eating and mefarnes in other needs. "Lakol u'metiv lakol" — the two lakol are pointed kamatz under kaf because they are separated and not adjacent; but "l'chol beriyosav" which is adjacent has kamatz under shin of she'atah. "Zan" is pointed kamatz to broaden the alef; and so in Shemoneh Esreh Modim anachnu she'atah — so Radbaz and Mateh Moshe. "B'chol eit u'v'chol sha'ah" — for eit is six hours and four times change each day: morning until midday, midday until night, etc. — Maharam and Mateh Moshe. "Anachnu modim lach" and not anu — for anu is language of curse as "va'anu hadayagim" with yod not peh; peh is recognizable — Radbaz and Mateh Moshe. Also our teacher wrote in sheva: chaser vav — not ro'einu with cholam; "zokeinu" in melafum — meaning praying before Him that He feed us, not cholam; likewise one may explain parnaseinu v'chalkelenu. Acharonim conclude to say both on Shabbat and weekday. "Boneh Yerushalayim b'rachamav" — Yerushalayim rishon patach, sheni kamatz, for the end is a verse; wait a little then answer amen in a whisper. Beit Yosef in name of R' David Abudraham: do not say ha'El for already said Elokeinu; Rav Amram in blessing after megillah — but in Rambam's nusach it says ha'El Avinu Malkeinu — end; and so Tur, Mordechai end Berachos, teshuvat Rashal siman 64, Mateh Moshe — therefore not proper to change custom but say ha'El Avinu; reason needed. Rashal and Mateh Moshe brought from Mahari he would not say boreinu but adireinu go'aleinu yotzreinu — see there at length. I wonder: in Rambam and Abudraham I saw boreinu not yotzreinu — therefore do not depart from our custom saying both boreinu yotzreinu. "Hatov" — heh patach, do not say only sheb'chol yom etc. implying two authorities chas v'shalom — bless the Omnipresent who daily benefits us; say El b'chol yom without shin — Bach siman 189 in name of Kolbo and Shelah; Taz siman 189 wrote not to say ha'El at all; in my humble opinion do not change custom since Kolbo and other poskim wrote. Tur: Ra'ash would say hu heitiv lanu hu meitiv lanu hu yeitiv lanu hu gemalanu hu gomleinu hu yigmol lanu b'adinus — so ruled Shulchan Aruch, Bach siman 189, and such is custom; so I practiced and it is correct, unlike those who say hu heitiv meitiv yeitiv — rather in order of benefits I found in Kolbo: hu meitiv hu heitiv lanu hu yeitiv lanu hu gomleinu hu gemalanu hu yigmelanu — first present then future, like HaMelech HaMelech HaMelech Yimloch (the King, the King reigns, the King shall reign) — end; Mordechai in name Pardes and Rabbenu Yonah, Abudraham in name Ra'ash; perhaps scribal error in Tur — should also say in this order. From above poskim: do not say hu yigmol b'adinus but hu yigmelanu l'ad — Mateh Moshe. Or Chadash: both kol tov and mi'kol tov in melafum — end; but I saw in Teshuvot siman 317: kol tov cholam. On this table even if pages move table to table as at large meals — not called two tables unless two actual tables each with host separately (requires study in Beit Meir, Maharam on this) — only when they invite together he blesses on these tables we ate upon — Beit Meir; apparently resha and sifa contradict — perhaps orcha demilta; same when two actual tables at large meal or one host eating with household on actual tables — bless on these tables. Harachaman: yevarech avi mori not abba; do not bless ba'al habayit unless eating at his table from his bread — Yerushalmi: you blessed the masters of the house. As our fathers Avraham Yitzchak Yaakov were blessed b'chol michol kol — all dagesh in Birkat Hamazon not adjacent to the Divine Name. So may He bless us all together Bnei Brit when Kutim or apostate Kutim in house. Magen Avraham siman 189 in name of Taz to say specifically otanu Bnei Brit kulanu yachad — no concern since said within kedei dibbur; rather his words are like repetition — Kolbo. Or Chadash [36b] implies no difference woman to woman, unlike those who err at wedding saying chatan, kallah, acheinu, raboteinu, all sitting here — Beit Meir — end; I saw in Kolbo p. 24 they say Harachaman yitmoch ve'yisa'od ve'yichalkel ve'yachmoleh marom marom hachatan v'hakallah hane'imah v'chol rabotai hamesubin kemo she'nisbarechu etc. — Mateh Moshe: they say amen and we do not say as end of Birkat Hamazon; Magen Avraham siman 189: cannot decree amen — unlike oseh shalom which is the Blessed One's and did not cite Mateh Moshe. Some say me'am the Divine Name not me'at — correct not to change verse language, say me'at the Divine Name, articulate alef, tzidkatecha me'Elohei yisheinu, etc. Harachaman yizkeinu l'yemot hamashiach ul'chayei olam haba — Mateh Moshe and Orach Chayim; Abudraham: yizkeinu v'yechayenu v'yikarevenu l'yemot hamashiach — I do not know where they find to change Abudraham's nusach for he brings a verse for each. Abudraham: on Shabbat when he is a great king against weekday they say magdil with vav and cholam — hu Melech gadol; weekday magdil chaser vav chirik without yod — Melech katan; also magdil in Tehillim before David was king, magdol in Nevi'im after he was king — end; acharonim: Yom Tov and Rosh Chodesh like Shabbat — siman chodesh v'shabbat korei mikra. Kolbo: answer amen after Harachaman and after every supplication even without name and berachah — Shelah; Mateh Moshe: say hu ya'aseh not v'hu; acharonim: after ki l'olam chasdo the verses na'ar hayiti, tzadik ne'ezav, baruch hagever, the Omnipresent oz, the Omnipresent yevarech et amo bashalom; when blessing on cup say before cup of salvation: esah uv'shem the Divine Name ekra — esah in gematria Ehyeh asher Ehyeh:
