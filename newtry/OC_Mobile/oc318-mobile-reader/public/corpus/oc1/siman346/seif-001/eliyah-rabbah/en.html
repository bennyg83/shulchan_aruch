[1] [Levush] That he walks, etc. For this reason the Bach and Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah permit initially taking an object from a private domain, walking with it through a public domain, and placing it in a makom petur. The Taz prohibits doing so initially because it degrades Shabbos, and likewise prohibits carrying from one private domain to another through a public domain.
[2] And some permit. Olat Shabbos writes that this appears correct because otherwise it would be a decree upon a decree. This requires study, because many decrees upon decrees exist. See the end of siman 349 and siman 308, seif 40, and similarly the Magen Avraham in siman 348. See siman 355, seif 3, in the gloss. Rabbenu Yerucham, page 96, likewise appears to prohibit: he first cites the Rif as permitting, then concludes that the Raavad, Baal HaMaor, Tosafot, and Rosh prohibit. The Beit Yosef also writes that the Maggid Mishneh, Rashba, and Tur prohibit, and so rule Shiltei Giborim and Riaz in chapter 18. I further saw Olat Shabbos in siman 372 express astonishment that the Shulchan Aruch records a dispute concerning three pillars, asserting that the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh permit. His astonishment is itself surprising, because the Rosh prohibits, as he writes in the name of Rabbenu Yitzchak, and the Beit Yosef and Olat Shabbos himself say so in this siman.
[3] [Levush] They permitted taking out, etc. The Beit Yosef concludes this because the Maggid Mishneh writes in the name of the Rashba that this is the view toward which he inclines. Knesset HaGedolah writes that the Beit Yosef was imprecise, because the Maggid Mishneh supposedly says the Rashba inclines to the Rambam, who prohibits, and that the Maggid Mishneh never mentioned Rabbenu Tam's lenient position. This is an astonishing error. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 16, law 2, explicitly writes that the Rambam prohibits on dry land but Tosafot permits, including from here to a garden, and that the Rashba inclines to this view. Thus he explicitly mentions the lenient Tosafot position and states that the Rashba inclines to it. I later examined a Mishneh Torah edition with glosses where the phrase 'but Tosafot permit' is indeed missing. Evidently Knesset HaGedolah used that text rather than the version printed with Kesef Mishneh, which was before the Beit Yosef. I further say that the version with the missing phrase clearly contains a scribal omission. Otherwise, how could the Maggid Mishneh continue, 'and they permitted from here to a garden,' when according to the Rambam that is prohibited on dry land? That permission belongs only to Tosafot. The missing words must therefore be restored as printed in Kesef Mishneh. I also found clear proof in the Rashba's Avodat HaKodesh, page 17b, where he explicitly writes that he inclines to the lenient view. This makes Knesset HaGedolah's error even more surprising. I also saw responsa Mabit, part 3, siman 48, stumble in the same way by saying the Rashba inclines to the Rambam and prohibits. That is incorrect, and he left room for me to clarify the matter.
