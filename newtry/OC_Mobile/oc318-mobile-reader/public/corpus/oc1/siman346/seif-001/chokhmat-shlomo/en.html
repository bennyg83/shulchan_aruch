Seif 1 — from the Torah one is not liable etc. Note: behold a new book reached me called responsa of Maharam Mintz by the great gaon R' Moshe Mintz may he rest in Eden, av beit din of Oben Yashan; and I saw there in responsum 2 that a certain sage challenged him on the Mishnah in Kerisos "there is one who eats one eating" — R' Meir says even if it were Shabbat and he removed it in his mouth he is liable; they said to him it is not from the Name; and that sage challenged from what is written in Shabbat chapter HaMitzniy'ach that the Gemara says there "all fit to store except idolatry wood" and Rashi explained because it is forbidden in benefit; and it is difficult — if so neveilah is also forbidden in benefit — why is he liable for removing it? Perforce it is proven there it is different because he ate it and it was accounted to him — if so it comes to him from the eating power — why is it not from the Name? Thus far; and if this question can be dismissed simply as the editor wrote there, in the gloss — nevertheless in my humble opinion it appears no need even per the editor's words, for neveilah has no similarity at all to idolatry — for regarding idolatry since it is among the buried ones he can burn it with anything; if so on Shabbat too he can do so; and even though Rashi wrote Beitza 24a "the Merciful One accounted its burning" — we already wrote in a responsum that this is specifically regarding neveilah which is burning; and then if he burns it with something else this is in place of burning and therefore "the Merciful One accounted" applies; but the buried ones — since the Torah did not specify with what to burn them "the Merciful One accounted" does not apply; and if so he can burn them also on Shabbat; and even if he cannot burn it himself he can tell a gentile to burn it; and even though there is no agency for a gentile — here we do not require agency since it is in his power in any case — if so telling the gentile is in his power and it is like feeding to dogs; and to a gentile certainly permitted to throw it to the sea which is shevut upon shevut and permitted for mitzvah need; and if so here where he can burn it also on Shabbat and fulfill the mitzvah — why should he store it? Therefore he is not fit to store; but neveilah which is specifically burning — if so on Shabbat he cannot burn it; and via gentile also he cannot — not to mention d'rabbanan which is shevut — only also from the Torah it does not help since there is no agency for a gentile; if so he cannot fulfill the burning mitzvah and telling a gentile is not like burning; if so he must store to fulfill the burning mitzvah on a weekday — therefore on Shabbat he is fit to store to fulfill the mitzvah with it and therefore liable even without eating; and proof from what is written there in tractate Shabbat to exclude idolatry or menstrual blood — why did it not say kodashim and neveilah?
