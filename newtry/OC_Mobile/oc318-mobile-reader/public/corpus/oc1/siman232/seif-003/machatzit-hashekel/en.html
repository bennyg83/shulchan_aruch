(Note 3) To get a haircut etc. — I was forced to bring gemara's words so Magen Avraham's words be understood; and although Taz brought them, nevertheless to be lenient. It states in Shabbat 9b: a man should not sit before the barber close to Mincha until he prays, nor enter bathhouse, tannery, judgment, or eat; and if they began they do not stop. They stop to read Shema and challenge: this 'close to Mincha' — if close to Mincha Gedolah why not, behold there is much time? Rather close to Mincha Ketanah — why teach 'if they began they do not stop,' and R' Yehoshua ben Levi said: once Mincha prayer time arrives it is forbidden to taste anything before praying Mincha. (And it appears to me R' Yehoshua ben Levi forbade two things — even casual tasting; and also if he began he stops — and Tosafos wrote the stopping too is heard from forbidding tasting; and Bach wrote he derives stopping from the word 'anything' — see there.) And they answer: the plain gemara always means close to Mincha Gedolah and deals with a large meal and haircut — meaning Ben El'asah's which required extra craftsmanship — all as Magen Avraham note 13 wrote, therefore even though there is time in the day it is forbidden; and Rav Acha bar Yaakov said always close to Mincha Gedolah, even a small meal and our haircut and all of them — nevertheless forbidden as Shulchan Aruch wrote the reason here. And in this there are four views among poskim: View 1 — Rif and Rambam and their followers, Rabbeinu Yonah ruled and they ruled like Rav Acha bar Yaakov since the gemara arranged his answer last — therefore he wrote even a small meal close to Mincha Gedolah is forbidden, and if they began they do not stop — the mishnah deals with both; but if he began close to Mincha Ketanah, even a small meal must stop, for the questioner's reasoning remains who challenged: if close to Mincha Ketanah why teach 'if they began' etc. and R' Yehoshua ben Levi — the questioner has not yet concluded it deals with a large meal, nevertheless he derives from R' Yehoshua ben Levi one must stop — such is R' Yehoshua ben Levi's language; and the other three views will be explained one by one. And it is possible if one says in both cases all razors may break — nevertheless Magen Avraham wrote in language 'it is possible' one can say even so it is forbidden because the Sages did not differentiate; and so truly ruled in Sefer Avnei Nezer to forbid in every case because of not differentiating.
