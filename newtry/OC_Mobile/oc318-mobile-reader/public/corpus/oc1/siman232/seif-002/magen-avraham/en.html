To get a haircut — if he has a spare razor and a different return, he is permitted — Perishah.

Close to Mincha — we were half an hour before.

To the bathhouse — even to sweat casually, a decree lest he faint.

To the tannery — even to inspect casually, lest he see the hides were ruined and be distressed and be troubled in his sorrow and not pray; and it is implied the same law applies to any work or accounting that may prolong or cause distraction.

And not for judgment — even if he already heard their claims, it is forbidden to conclude the judgment lest he see merit and overturn the judgment.

A small meal — lest they prolong; Maharil wrote in Laws of Pesach, and it appears the same applies to one who did not pray Maariv with the congregation — forbidden in work and study until praying, as first chapter of Berachos; and eats a little etc. But at Shacharit since the congregation prays we are not concerned he will be negligent, and therefore they were accustomed to eat after Mincha Ketanah; and also the sun reminds and calls to synagogue; but where there is no minyan forbidden to study at Shacharit before praying — until here; and likewise above siman 89 seif 6, unlike Bach's explanation in siman 231 of Maharil — see there. And that which they say in Berachos etc. — deals with before its time; but when time arrives forbidden to study unless congregation — see siman 89 seif 6.

And if he began — even after Mincha Gedolah one does not stop, even when Mincha Ketanah time arrives; but if they began close to Mincha Ketanah they stop even if time remains; see Choshen Mishpat siman 5; and whoever is exempt and stops is called a commoner (Levush Yerushalmi); and he challenges there: but a groom is exempt from Kerias Shema and if he wishes to read he reads; and they answer there like Rabban Gamliel who said 'I do not listen to you to cancel the yoke of heaven even one hour' — see siman 70 seif 63.

If there is no time remaining — even if he began when permitted he stops.

To stop immediately — even while the day is still long, whenever he knows his meal will continue until dark he must stop immediately — and so Rashi in Sukkah 38 — see there.

From washing his hands — and for one accustomed to loosen his belt when eating, from loosening his belt even before netilat yadayim.

That a small meal is permitted — and according to them our haircut is also permitted; and bathhouse specifically the whole matter of bathhouse, but to sweat casually is permitted; and tannery specifically large tanning; and judgment specifically beginning judgment is forbidden; nevertheless they agree if they began close to Mincha Ketanah they stop even for a small meal.

And some say even — and according to them close to Mincha Ketanah is forbidden even for a small meal; and if they began they do not stop even from a large meal.

Close to Mincha Ketanah — nevertheless when Mincha Ketanah arrives he admits it is forbidden; however in Mordechai he is lenient also in this and wrote prohibition is only in a large meal close to Mincha Ketanah — meaning the tenth hour for all is forbidden and one should not be lenient — and so Bach; and also Rosh in Rabbah admits this, unlike what Bach wrote; and so Agudah — one should not rely on a large meal on calling to synagogue; nevertheless for beginning judgment and other matters one relies on calling to synagogue since they do not prolong so much; and also they say in gemara drunkenness is not common — if so obviously in a meal where drunkenness is common it is forbidden to begin, for when drunk calling to synagogue does not help.

And the hour is passing — Magen Avraham wrote: even while the day is still long one must stop if he knows his meal will attract until dark. Bach wrote: when there is a wedding on Shabbat and they did not leave synagogue until after chatzot they pray Mincha with congregation and afterward eat, for wedding feast is a large meal; but Shabbat meal at home is not called a large meal.
