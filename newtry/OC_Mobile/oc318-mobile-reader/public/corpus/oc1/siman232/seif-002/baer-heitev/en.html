For Mincha — meaning half an hour before. And one should not hold a child in his lap lest he soil his clothes, and while he returns for water Mincha time may pass or he may be late coming to synagogue. Chayei Adam siman 18.

A small meal — lest they prolong. Maharil wrote in Laws of Pesach, and it appears to me the same applies to one who did not pray Maariv with the congregation — he is forbidden in work and study until he prays, as they say in the first chapter of Berachos; and he eats a little etc. But at Shacharit, since the congregation prays, we are not concerned he will be negligent, and therefore they were accustomed to eat after Mincha Ketanah time; and also the sun reminds and calls to synagogue. But in a place where there is no minyan it is forbidden to study at Shacharit before praying — until here. And likewise above siman 89 seif 6, unlike what Bach explained in siman 231 regarding Maharil's words — that specifically one who prays in synagogue and once did not pray Maariv in synagogue is forbidden to study, but one who always prays at home is permitted to study — your eyes see it does not exist; and because Bach did not copy the end of his words another explanation was given — see there. And that which they say in Berachos that a man should not say 'I will eat and drink first and afterward read Shema' but rather enter the beit midrash and read and review and afterward read Shema — deals with before its time, meaning half an hour before the time when eating is forbidden and study permitted so he not sit idle half an hour; but when the time arrives study is forbidden unless there is a congregation — see above siman 89 seif 6. Magen Avraham.

He began — even after Mincha Gedolah one does not stop, even when Mincha Ketanah time arrives; but if they began close to Mincha Ketanah they stop even if there is time remaining. And anyone who is exempt and stops is called a commoner — see Magen Avraham.

To stop immediately — even while the day is still long, whenever he knows his meal will continue until dark he must stop immediately. And even if he began when permitted he stops. Magen Avraham.

His hands — and for one accustomed to loosen his belt when eating, loosening the belt is considered beginning even before netilat yadayim.

Is passing — see above note 5 that even while the day is still long one must stop if he knows his meal will continue until dark. Bach wrote: when there is a wedding on Shabbat and they did not leave synagogue until after chatzot they pray Mincha with the congregation and afterward eat, for a wedding feast is a large meal; but Shabbat meal at home is not called a large meal.
