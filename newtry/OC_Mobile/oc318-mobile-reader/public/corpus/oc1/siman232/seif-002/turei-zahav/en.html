A man should not sit before the barber etc. — all this is mishnah first chapter of Shabbat; and they challenged: this close to Mincha — if close to Mincha Gedolah, behold there is much time — meaning Mincha Gedolah is from six and a half and up, and close is half hour at beginning of seventh hour; rather close to Mincha Ketanah — if they began do not stop? Let it refute R' Yehoshua ben Levi who said once Mincha time arrives forbidden to taste anything before Mincha — always close to Mincha Gedolah; and Ben El'asah haircut which was like twenty-three and not bathhouse whole matter, not judgment for beginning judgment — found accordingly only Ben El'asah haircut and whole bathhouse and large tanning and beginning judgment forbidden close to Mincha Gedolah, and if they began do not stop; but small meal — Rav Acha bar Yaakov says always in our haircut, and l'chatchila forbidden lest razor break; and not bathhouse even casual sweating, and l'chatchila not lest he faint; and not tannery even casual inspection, and l'chatchila not lest he see loss and be troubled; and not eat even small meal, and l'chatchila not lest they prolong; and not judgment even concluding judgment, and l'chatchila not lest he see merit and overturn judgment; and Rif and Rambam ruled like latter version — Rav Acha bar Yaakov, and this is the view Shulchan Aruch brings — stringently; and Tosafos in Rabbeinu Tam's name ruled leniently like first answer — and this is first view Rama brings.

And if he began in one etc. — meaning even Ben El'asah haircut, for now we do not find stringency in Ben El'asah more than other haircuts — and from where do we say stringency here in mishnah? Mishnah deals with all haircuts and on all says if they began do not stop; and same for large meal and beginning judgment and whole bathhouse; but it appears large meal even if began he stops for another reason mishnah does not deal with it — drunkenness reason in gemara first chapter Shabbat 10 regarding Maariv for one who holds obligatory — they toil for him and challenge: but Mincha for all obligatory and we learned if they began do not stop; and answer there drunkenness not common here common drunkenness; and Rosh wrote from this proof for Rif's ruling — if mishnah deals with small meal properly drunkenness not common; but if large meal like Rabbeinu Tam common and common drunkenness — we see according to Rif forbidden in large meal because of drunkenness even if began he stops; but mishnah does not deal with this but with all these small matters — meal and haircut and judgment — nevertheless should not begin because of decrees, and if began do not stop; but large matters mishnah does not deal with; and it appears from another side there is distinction: in great haircut and beginning judgment and whole bathhouse matters if began do not stop even in them since we find no stringency; but in them in large meal we find stringency because of drunkenness — therefore he stops even if began when Mincha Ketanah time arrives even if much time afterward to pray. And from Rosh's words one can say Rabbeinu Tam ruled halachah like first answer in small meal or beginning judgment one does not begin — apparently mishnah deals with beginning judgment and it is not proof he derives beginning judgment because if they began do not stop as beginning bathhouse and tanning explained — until here Perishah's explanation; and we can well rule like Rif — if so according to Rif also in large meal if began do not stop like beginning judgment, for large meal is like beginning judgment — no difficulty at all; certainly according to first answer mishnah deals with large matters — then proven in mishnah large meal permitted b'dieved since mishnah permits b'dieved; but according to latter version Rif's ruling mishnah deals with small matters and permitted b'dieved in small, and large matters not dealt with at all — except we distinguish externally: in large meal drunkenness concern and decree even b'dieved like Maariv for one holding obligatory; unlike other large matters where b'dieved also permitted like small; and not like first answer permitting mishnah even large b'dieved — and even in large meal difficult because of drunkenness concern; and what compared to Maariv one can say gemara has further distinction between Maariv and Mincha — in Mincha since time fixed one fears and will not be negligent unlike Maariv — we can say first answer holds this distinction of fixed time between Mincha and Maariv; but gemara saying distinction is because of drunkenness holds like latter version — can well divide in this distinction since mishnah does not deal with this; it appears clear according to this view Rif and Rambam rule to forbid even b'dieved in large meal when Mincha Ketanah arrives; but other large matters such as Ben El'asah haircut and whole bathhouse and beginning judgment if began do not stop — found accordingly first answer has further leniency in large meal b'dieved, and latter has stringency even b'dieved in large meal; and Mahari Veil wrote in one place and it appears to me I wrote.

And some disagree — this is Rabbeinu Tam's view who ruled like first answer of mishnah — forbids l'chatchila only in large matters, and b'dieved do not stop even in large meal and at Mincha Ketanah; and Tur wrote accordingly even in small meal and in Ri's name wrote accordingly in small meal even close to Mincha Ketanah permitted to begin — and this is third view Rama brings; and second view Rama holds mishnah deals from Mincha Ketanah and nevertheless b'dieved do not stop; but in Mincha Gedolah not dealt with, and even in small meal permitted; and that gemara establishes in Mincha Gedolah is so it not be difficult from R' Yehoshua ben Levi who said once prayer time etc. — and we do not rule like R' Yehoshua ben Levi as said elsewhere — automatically mishnah established even at Mincha Ketanah if they began do not stop; but Tosafos rejected this explanation and said that which we rule elsewhere not like R' Yehoshua ben Levi is regarding what he said forbidden to taste anything — even fruits; but regarding forbidden to eat meal we rule like him — automatically we rule like R' Yehoshua ben Levi; and so Tur and Shulchan Aruch in next seif 3.

And the hour passes — Tur wrote even when Mincha Ketanah arrived from nine and a half hours provided he has time to pray after finishing meal, until here; and if Rama intended to say he will not have time after meal — why write forbidden from 'one should be stringent' side, for in this all forbid? It appears this 'and hour passes' means though truly there is still time afterward, nevertheless one should be concerned seeing meal will still continue until measure time passes — be stringent in this.
