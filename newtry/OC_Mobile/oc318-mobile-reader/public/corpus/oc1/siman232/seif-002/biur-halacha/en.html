To the tannery — see Mishnah Berurah what is written regarding other labors — it is from Magen Avraham; and I found in Meiri who wrote on this matter, and these are his words: Some commentators wrote that labor was not forbidden on weekdays from the time close to Mincha Gedolah except for labors not usually done halfway, such as haircutting or tanning — for after you worry about loss of hides how will you stop [and go pray], and also his way is to wear soiled clothes that hour until he finishes and washes his feet and puts on his usual clothes — how will he stop in order to return immediately to his work; and likewise for judgment it is hard for him to move while his claim is in hand and his witnesses are ready for him, and so for all similar matters. But sewing, writing, and other things a person is accustomed to leave halfway — he may begin, and when prayer time arrives he stops — until here. And Meiri's intent in 'some commentators' is Rashba brought in Rosh Levanah in Beit Yosef; and possibly this is also Magen Avraham's view, therefore he wrote 'and the like'; and so Peri Megadim. However from Mahariu's language that Magen Avraham brought in note 8 it implies he holds all labor is forbidden, and in a place of need one may rely to be lenient.

And if he began etc. he does not stop — and whoever need not stop and stops is called a commoner (Magen Avraham in the name of Yerushalmi); and in Aderet Eliyahu first chapter of Berachos mishnah 3 disagrees on this — see there what he wrote on this Yerushalmi; and it appears simple that even according to Magen Avraham where by law one must stop — for example he began at Mincha Ketanah time and we only rely on the sun's call to be stringent — certainly he is not called a commoner. And know that wherever they said he does not stop — even if through this he will lose communal prayer he is not obligated by law, for the Sages did not burden him b'dieved since he has time to pray afterward.

And some disagree and hold a small meal is permitted etc. — see Mishnah Berurah that close to Mincha Ketanah even according to this view a small meal is forbidden; and see Biur HaGra who wrote the essence is like this view — therefore it appears even one who does not wish to rely on the custom to be lenient as explained in the gloss below should not be stringent except like this middle view, but not like the first view.

And even if he began before etc. — see Mishnah Berurah; and in Derech Chaim I saw he rules that if he began before Mincha Ketanah time arrived [meaning before three seasonal hours before night] even a large meal one does not stop — and it is implied there from his words that even if he knows his meal will last until night; only he stops a short time before prayer time ends, even where they do not call to synagogue — see there. And seemingly this is not Rama's view; and it appears Derech Chaim's reason is that Tur rules if there is no time to pray that day after finishing his meal he stops immediately, brought thus above in Shulchan Aruch — when he began in prohibition; but when he began when permitted he need not stop immediately, only a short time before day ends so he can pray; and we rely on Ba'al HaMaor that until Mincha Ketanah time even a large meal may be begun — if so then he began when permitted; but from Rama it implies even if he began during permitted time, when Mincha Ketanah arrives if he knows his meal will last until night he must rise immediately — requires study in practice.
