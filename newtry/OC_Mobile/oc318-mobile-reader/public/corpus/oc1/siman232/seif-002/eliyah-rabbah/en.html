[Levush] Rather, in the prayers, etc. Prayer at this time has a special quality precisely because a person is occupied with his affairs, as is clear from the Tur and Levush in their discussion of Shacharit and Minchah, and so writes Perishah. The writings of the Arizal say that he was exceedingly careful to pay a hired worker before Minchah, and sometimes borrowed money in order to pay him.
[Levush] Before the barber, etc. This applies even to our ordinary haircuts. According to Rif and Rambam all these activities are prohibited even shortly before Minchah Gedolah, lest the scissors break and he have to seek another pair. Magen Avraham writes that perhaps if he has two or three pairs it is permitted. It appears, however, that the Sages made no distinction. Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah writes that the concern is that some mishap may occur to one of the barber's tools. According to their view, once one began he need not interrupt even for the elaborate haircut of Ben Elasa or for the beginning of a legal proceeding. Taz argues at length that for a large meal one must interrupt, but Tosafot there do not imply this, and Responsa Rav Avraham Chaim likewise writes that even during a large meal one does not interrupt.
