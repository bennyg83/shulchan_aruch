A person should not sit for a haircut close to Mincha until he prays, nor enter a bathhouse, nor a tannery (a place where hides are processed), nor a court, nor eat even a small meal close to Mincha Gedolah; and if he already began any of these he need not stop, even though he began in a prohibited state — provided there is enough time in the day to pray after he finishes his meal or work. But if there is not enough time to pray afterward he must stop immediately. From when is beginning a haircut? From when the barber's cloth is placed on his knees. Beginning a bathhouse? From when he removes his outer garment. Beginning tanning? From when he ties a cloth between his shoulders in tanners' manner. Beginning judgment? If they were already engaged — from when litigants begin to argue; if not yet engaged — from when judges wrap themselves; and according to our practice from when they sit with intent to judge. Beginning a meal? From when he washes his hands.(Some disagree and hold a small meal is permitted — only a wedding feast or circumcision feast is forbidden (Tur in Rabbeinu Tam's name). Some say even a large meal is permitted close to Mincha Gedolah (Ba'al HaMaor and Hagahot Mordechai first chapter of Shabbat). Some say even a small meal is permitted close to Mincha Ketanah (Tur in Ri's name). The custom is to be lenient according to both views — a large meal close to Mincha Gedolah and a small meal close to Mincha Ketanah. The possible reason is that nowadays they call to synagogue, so we are not concerned one will be negligent and not pray (Agudah first chapter of Shabbat) (see above siman 89).
