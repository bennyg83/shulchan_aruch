A person should not sit down for a haircut close to Mincha until he has prayed, nor enter a bathhouse, nor a tannery, nor a court of law, nor eat even a small meal close to Mincha Gedolah. If he already began any of these he need not stop, even though he began in a prohibited state — provided there is enough time in the day to pray after he finishes his meal or work. But if there is not enough time to pray afterward he must stop immediately. From when is the beginning of a haircut? From when the barber's cloth is placed on his knees. The beginning of a bathhouse? From when he removes his outer garment. The beginning of a tannery? From when he ties a cloth between his shoulders in the manner of tanners. The beginning of a court case? If they were already engaged in it — from when the litigants begin to argue; if they were not yet engaged — from when the judges wrap themselves, and according to our practice from when they sit with the intention to judge. The beginning of a meal? From when he washes his hands.(Rema: Some disagree and hold that a small meal is permitted — only a wedding feast or circumcision feast is forbidden. (Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam) Some say even a large meal is permitted close to Mincha Gedolah. (Ba'al HaMaor and Hagahot Mordechai ch. 1 of Shabbat) Some say even a small meal is permitted close to Mincha Ketanah. (Tur in the name of Ri) The custom is to be lenient according to both opinions — meaning a large meal close to Mincha Gedolah and a small meal close to Mincha Ketanah. The possible reason is that nowadays the synagogue announces the time, so we are not concerned one will be negligent and not pray. (Agudah, ch. 1 of Shabbat) (See above Siman 89) However for a large meal one should be stringent even close to Mincha Gedolah, and even if one began before that time — when Mincha Ketanah arrives and the time is passing one must rise and pray.
