1) [Seif 1] If time is pressing, etc. One must be extremely careful regarding Minchah, for R' Chelbo said in the name of R' Huna: A person should always be careful with the Minchah prayer, because Eliyahu was answered only during Minchah, as it is stated, 'At the time of the afternoon offering, Eliyahu approached,' etc. Berachos 6b. The Tur explains that the time of the morning prayer is known: upon rising from bed in the morning, one prays immediately before becoming occupied with his affairs. Likewise, the time of the evening prayer is known: at night, when he returns home and is free of his affairs. Minchah, however, occurs in the middle of the day while he is occupied with his affairs. He must therefore take it to heart, withdraw from all his affairs, and pray. If he does so, his reward is exceedingly great. These are the Tur's words.
2) The essence of prayer is intent, because through it abundance and blessing are drawn to him. The Zohar, parashas Pekudei, 238, states: Regarding anyone who prays and binds the unification, they examine whether his prayer and bond are proper. If that prayer and that bond are proper, he is then blessed first from the place from which all blessings emerge. This is the meaning of, 'Behold, they had performed it,' etc., immediately followed by, 'Moshe blessed them.' See further the Zohar, parashas Vayechi, 230a, which states: A person must direct his mind during the Minchah prayer. During every prayer a person must direct his mind, but during this prayer more than all the others, because judgment rests upon the world. Therefore, Yitzchak instituted the Minchah prayer.
3) The Arizal's practice was to give tzedakah before beginning Minchah as well. He would give three small coins, as stated in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 52a; see there. One should give it before entering the synagogue so that it counts as two acts, one before entering the synagogue and another before prayer, along the lines of what we wrote above in siman 51, paragraph 44; see there. If he finds neither poor people nor a collector at that time, he should set the money aside and retain it, in accordance with what is stated in that siman, paragraph 45. See there further regarding the intention to direct while giving tzedakah, and siman 132, end of paragraph 6; see there.
