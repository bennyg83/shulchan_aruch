He should pray, etc. Responsa Menachem Azaryah, siman 39, strongly warns to wear tefillin at Minchah. Knesset HaGedolah writes that one should wear Rabbenu Tam tefillin. Menachem Azaryah, siman 22, explains that the word Minchah derives from the Aramaic translation of "the spirit of the day" as the resting of the day, when the sun inclines toward its resting place and, as it were, bows to its Creator in the west. The body of the sun fixed in its sphere, when it encounters the western point, is itself like a minchah offering to its Creator, and serves as a model that we offer ourselves and our souls to Heaven. Responsa Emunot Shmuel, siman 20, explains that the morning tamid atones for sins of the night, and the evening burning of limbs and fats for sins of the day. The slaughter and offering of the afternoon tamid, however, seems unnecessary because it occurs in the middle of the day, so it is truly called a gift, minchah. Shelah, page 281a, gives another reason. Rabbi David Abudarham writes that Adam HaRishon sinned in the tenth hour of the day, corresponding to the verse "toward the spirit of the day," translated as the resting of the day. Sefer Chasidim, siman 18, writes that near the time of evening prayer one should not take a child onto his lap, lest the child soil his clothes. Even if he can wash them, they may not become clean as before; while searching for water he may miss Minchah, Kaddish may be said before he can answer, or the child may cry when put down and he may have compassion on the child rather than on the honor of his Creator and fail to go to the synagogue. "Near" means about half an hour before Minchah, as in seif 2 and siman 235 seif 3. Sefer Chasidim further writes that one should not wonder how people with speech impediments who confuse certain Hebrew letters can pray and read, even where their pronunciation seems to turn a sacred verse into an offensive phrase. The Creator examines hearts and asks only that a person's heart be wholehearted with Him; since the person does not know how to pronounce correctly, it is credited as though he pronounced properly. Likewise, those who recite Pesukei DeZimrah in a pleasant loud voice but do not know the verses and make mistakes are accepted like a pleasing fragrance. Hashem rejoices in them greatly, saying, as it were, how beautifully he sings before Me according to his understanding. This is alluded to by "his banner over me is love." An incident occurred with a kohen who mispronounced the priestly blessing; a sage removed him, and Heaven showed the sage that if he did not restore him he would be punished.
