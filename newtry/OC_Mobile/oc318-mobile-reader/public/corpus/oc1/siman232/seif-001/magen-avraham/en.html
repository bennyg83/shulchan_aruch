One may ask why this prayer is called Mincha. If it is because a minchah offering was brought then, a minchah offering was also brought in the morning. Tosafos answer that the principal time of Divine favor is when the afternoon minchah is brought. This indicates that the primary time is Mincha Ketanah, as stated in siman 233, for then the minchah offering was brought after the Tamid. At six and a half hours, however, was the time for slaughtering the Tamid. See what I wrote there. The Yerushalmi, chapter 3 of Berachos, states that Mincha was instituted at the time the ketores was offered, as it says, “Let my prayer be established as incense before You,” etc. They should pray quietly. The chazzan may not pray aloud in order to discharge them, because the mitzvah of prayer is recited quietly, and neither an individual nor a congregation fulfills the obligation of the Amidah except through a quiet recitation. Beit Yosef; Shiltei HaGibborim. This requires study, for siman 124, seif 2, implies that one may fulfill the obligation through the chazzan's audible prayer. One must say that there it is impossible otherwise.
The chazzan together with the congregation. Nevertheless, on a communal fast they should not initially do this. Since the chazzan says Aneinu as a separate blessing while the congregation says it in the usual place, there will not be ten people listening to the chazzan. If time is extremely pressing, however, he should immediately pray aloud. The chazzan should recite Aneinu as a separate blessing, while the congregation should recite it in Shomea Tefillah. Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, siman 15.
