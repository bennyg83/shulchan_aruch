1) [Se'if 1] An alleyway with a non-Jew on one side, etc. Although this alleyway contains only one courtyard belonging to a Jewish person, it can be rendered permitted by a lechi or korah because another courtyard opens onto it. Although a non-Jew resides there, the non-Jew's residence is considered a residence for purposes of leniency. Beis Yosef in this siman, citing the Tur in siman 363. The Shulchan Aruch already records this there in se'if 26. See there, and see our words there in subsection 175.
2) There. An alleyway with a non-Jew on one side, etc. If a non-Jew's courtyard is situated between courtyards belonging to Jewish people, meaning adjacent to two Jewish courtyards, then if the non-Jew's courtyard does not open into this alleyway and has no entrance or window except one more than ten handbreadths high, it is permitted to carry from the non-Jew's courtyard into a Jewish person's courtyard. Since the non-Jew does not use the passage directly, rental is unnecessary, for the non-Jew does not prohibit carrying for them. If, however, the non-Jew's courtyard opens into the alleyway or into a Jewish person's courtyard at a height below ten handbreadths, it is entirely forbidden to share use with the non-Jew, whether in the alleyway or the courtyard, because the non-Jew prohibits carrying for them. Bach in siman 382 in the name of Mordechai. Beis Yosef there, however, maintains that the non-Jew prohibits specifically by way of the alleyway, but does not prohibit carrying from one courtyard to another, even if the window is within ten handbreadths. See there, se'if 3. Even two Jewish courtyards with a non-Jew's courtyard between them, meaning that it separates them, may establish an eruv together through entrances and carry by way of the non-Jew's courtyard. The non-Jew's courtyard is not considered an interruption, since the non-Jew does not prohibit carrying. Nevertheless, this conclusion is not entirely conclusive. Maharshal; Magen Avraham, subsection 1.
