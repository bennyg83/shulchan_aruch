Then it is obligated in tzitzis — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote on "the adult who wears it," meaning that the Shulchan Aruch deals with this; and regarding the four corners of a child who has reached chinuch, whose father is obligated to place tzitzis on him, as explained below in siman 17—we gauge by the head and majority of the child as he is; and if he is bar shith we gauge by his head and majority, and his father is obligated rabbinically to place tzitzis on him—Peri Megadim in Mishnatzav. And what he further wrote in Mishna Berurah: if it is the custom of the people of the place that adults, etc.—even though Bach mentions plainly "and the adult goes out in it regularly," and this is brought in Magen Avraham—still it appears simple that Bach's intent is thus, for otherwise his intent would be nullified with every person; and also the Gemara's wording "and the adult goes out in it," from which Bach infers his law specifically for casual wear and not for regular wear, proves this; and thereby the objection of Orach Mishor against Bach and Knesset HaGedolah is removed. And it appears that this was Magen Avraham's intent in what he concluded at the end of his words, stating plainly that if he goes out in a tallit that others do not go out in, his intent is nullified—and he did not specify specifically for casual wear, implying even for regular wear; and he wrote us no hint that he is against Bach's words, which teaches us that he does not hold as Eliyah Rabbah explained Bach's words, that we do not say his intent is nullified where he walks in it regularly—for that does not exist. And furthermore it appears simply that even a tallit in which the child covers his head and majority—if no adult is accustomed to walk in it in the market even casually, even though he goes out in it casually as well, his intent is nullified; and according to Magen Avraham, who ruled like the gloss, one should be stringent not to bless on it—and this is also Magen Avraham's rule in his brief words, as you know, for what he adjoined refers to a tallit in which the child covers his head and majority [see in Peri Megadim, who strains greatly to explain Magen Avraham's words and explained that the end of his words is as a supporting proof for Bach's words, and refers to a tallit that does not have the measure of head and majority—and his words are not clear; also in Machatzit HaShekel it does not imply thus, but it is a separate matter, and what he adjoined refers—and likewise it is proven in Sefer Eretz HaChayyim]. And therefore it appears that even if he walks in it regularly his intent is nullified; but even if you say that since he walks in it regularly to the market we do not say his intent is nullified with every person, since the garment is considered a garment for the child who has enough to cover his head and majority—still, in this case where there is not enough for the head and majority of the child, his walking regularly does not help, as I have clarified, with God's help. And know further that what we wrote below in Mishna Berurah about what Ashkenazim practice, etc.—one does not fulfill [the mitzvah] with this at all according to all opinions, as written in Sefer Eretz HaChayyim: even according to Bach, who holds that since the adult walks in it regularly it is considered a garment—specifically if he goes out in it regularly outside as a garment without being ashamed of it; not so those who hide the corners of their garments without outward appearance, for they would truly be ashamed to go out in these tallisim that resemble amulets on their clothes—there is no permission in this; and see Bach, Taz, and all the later authorities, who all cry out like peddlers about this.

To the market — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote, and the later authorities agreed to be stringent, etc. Apparently there is room to question: in our time how do we bless on our tallit katan even if it has a long measure—is there no adult accustomed to walk in it in the market? But in truth this is not so, for "adult" in our context means a youth of thirteen years, as implied from Bach's and Eretz HaChayyim's words; and "to the market" that the Shulchan Aruch wrote is not precise, for the same applies at the doorway of his house in the street, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote; and surely a youth of thirteen will not be ashamed to walk in it on hot days outside at his doorway. Furthermore it appears simple that in any case for this reason the name of garment is not removed from it, and they said only if he is ashamed to walk in it because of its shortness—for otherwise, even if the tallit katan has enough for the head and majority of an adult, and even if it reaches his ankles, likewise he would be exempt from tzitzis; and if so, in places where the adult is ashamed to go out in tallit gadol in the market, likewise tallit gadol would be exempt from tzitzis. Rather certainly the Gemara's intent is specifically if the people of the region are ashamed to walk in it casually to the market because of its shortness—then it is not considered a garment; unlike in our context, where the shame is because that garment is customarily worn under one's clothes and not over one's clothes—for this reason the name of garment is not removed from it. And see in Mishna Berurah further what he wrote regarding the measure of the garment, that it suffices in three-quarters of an amah; and it appears that this measure helps specifically if the tallit is woven or sewn like a square and in the middle there is a neckline; but if one makes one side of the tallit katan from the front and one from the back and in the middle it is connected on one side in part and on the other side connected temporarily when worn by hooks—there is room to examine whether this measure suffices; and perhaps we combine one side with the other also for the measure, as we combine for the matter that it be called a four-cornered garment by name; but if so we would be forced to say we combine the length and width of the garment reciprocally for the obligation of tallit katan in tzitzis—and in truth this law is not clear, for some say we require enough for head and majority both in length and in width—see in Eretz HaChayyim. Still it appears to me that if the tallit katan holds on each side the measure of an amah by an amah, we can certainly rely and bless on it; for even if you say we do not combine one side with the other for this matter, many later authorities hold that the measure of an amah by an amah alone is the measure of a garment obligated in tzitzis. And the measure of an amah is six tefachim, and we do not require our [larger] amah—Peri Megadim wrote: if the garment has enough to cover head and majority but it is disgraceful dress and one does not go out in it to the market casually, and plainly most people go out in it to the bathhouse—for they hold both are required, as the Tur ruled [and as Magen Avraham wrote at the end of his words to be stringent]—perhaps this is not so in our context, for it is not fitting to bless on such a tallit, for we follow his intent—and it requires study. And apparently it is astonishing, for certainly the Torah did not give measures for each person according to his mind; also they did not say in the beraisa "and he goes out in it casually to the market" but "and the adult goes out in it"; and perhaps Bach's wording in this siman brought them to this doubt—but in truth Bach did not intend this; and it appears more to me that what brought them to doubt this is because he wrote in Orach Chayyim for Bach: if he goes out in it regularly even though it does not have head and majority of an adult, and no person goes out in it—still he is obligated, for we follow him and do not say his intent is nullified; therefore he also doubts the reverse—unlike according to what we clarified initially, there is no doubt in this that he is obligated.
