(1) Of a child, etc.—and this means nine years old. In the responsa of the Rema, siman 110, he analyzes how one fulfills [the mitzvah] with four corners of the small [tallis] from this measure, and the gist of his words is that it is permitted to go in them rather than go all day without tzitzis. And in Darchei Moshe, siman 9, he wrote to teach the people to bless in the morning on a tallit gadol and thereby exempt the small one and go in it all day. Still, if a person can make a large one that is the [proper] measure, how good and pleasant that is—end quote. And one should make the shoulder straps of the tallit katan wide so that they are recognizable and have the status of a garment and not merely straps.

(2) Then it is obligated in tzitzis—the adult who wears it; to exclude if it does not have this measure of head and majority—then according to all opinions it is exempt, even if the adult goes out in it casually to the market; but if it is the custom of the people of the place that adults walk in it regularly to the market and are not ashamed, it is implied from Bach's words that it is obligated in tzitzis; and see in Biur Halacha.

(3) When the adult—a youth of thirteen years.

(4) To the market—or at the doorway of his house in the street; but if the adult is ashamed to go out in it even casually to the market, it is not considered a garment even if he wears it in his house; and some disagree about this and hold we require only that it have enough to cover the head and majority of a child; and the later authorities agreed to be stringent not to bless on tallit katan even if it has enough for head and majority of an adult if the adult is ashamed to go out in it casually to the market—for even though he goes out in it, his intent is nullified with every person. Regarding the measure of head and majority, it is written in Sefer Pri HaAretz in the name of Sefer Derech Chachmah that its measure is three-quarters of an amah in length and half an amah in width; and Machatzit HaShekel and Eretz HaChayyim wrote on this that there is no support for this from the Talmud, especially what he wrote of half an amah in width. The custom of practical people is to be precise that there be an amah in front and an amah in back, and the neckline hole does not count toward the total; and with such a tallit katan one emerges from the doubts and can bless on it; and in any case one should not have less than three-quarters of an amah in length on each side—for otherwise there is concern of a blessing in vain, for the later authorities wrote they measured and found that the measure of head and majority of a child is an amah and a half; and see in Biur Halacha. And what Ashkenazim practice, taking one small piece of cloth in front and one in back and sewing straps on them and making tzitzis on them—one does not fulfill the obligation of tzitzis with this at all; and besides nullifying the mitzvah of tzitzis, they recite a blessing in vain every day and transgress "do not bear [false witness]"; it is also forbidden to go out in it on Shabbos, as explained in simanim 10 and 13—see there. And in any case those who do so should not bless on them, but should bless in the morning on tallit gadol and go in it all day, since they are at least designated thus not to nullify the mitzvah of tzitzis all day; but every God-fearing person should not rely on this at all, but should make a real garment per the law before his custom. And in some places the custom is that the garment called kamizel or vest is open from behind and they place tzitzis on its four corners—fortunate are they, only they must be careful that most of it be open; and likewise with tallit katan made in our countries they should be careful about this; and see above, siman 10, seifim 7–8, what we wrote there. The Arizal wrote that a tallit katan is better without armholes. If one wore the tallit and it folded somewhat as people wear garments, and after folding there is not the measure to cover head and majority—Beit Yaakov wrote in his responsum, siman 106, that since he is about to spread it out it is considered as spread even though he has not yet spread it, and behold there is the measure—see there; and see in Bnei Chayyeh. A tallit katan that has enough to cover head and majority, and through sewing there is not the measure—it is exempt from tzitzis—there, siman 66; and likewise in Leket HaKemach.
