(1) Of a minor, etc. This means a nine-year-old. Responsa Rama, siman 110, discusses how people fulfill the mitzvah with four-cornered garments smaller than this measure. The essence of his answer is that it is better to wear them than to go all day without tzitzis. In Darkei Moshe, siman 9, he writes that the people should be taught to recite the blessing in the morning over a tallis gadol, thereby exempting the tallis katan, and then wear it all day. Nevertheless, if a person can make it large enough to contain the required measure, how good and pleasant that is. The shoulder sections of the tallis katan should be made wide so that they are clearly recognizable and have the status of fabric rather than straps.
(2) He is then obligated in tzitzis. This refers to the adult who wears it. If it does not contain enough material to cover the head and most of a minor's body, then according to all opinions it is exempt, even if an adult occasionally wears it in the marketplace. If, however, the local practice is for adults to wear such a garment regularly in the marketplace without embarrassment, Bach implies that it is obligated in tzitzis. See Biur Halachah.
(3) When the adult. Meaning, one who is thirteen years old.
(4) To the marketplace. Or to the entrance of his home facing the street. If an adult would be embarrassed to go out wearing it even occasionally in the marketplace, it is not considered clothing, even if he wears it inside his home. Some disagree and maintain that the only requirement is that it contain enough material to cover the head and most of a minor's body. The later authorities agree that one should be stringent and not recite a blessing over a tallis katan, even if it can cover the head and most of a minor's body, where an adult would be embarrassed to wear it even occasionally in the marketplace. Even if this individual does wear it, his personal practice is disregarded in relation to everyone else. Regarding the measure of the head and most of the body, Peri HaAretz writes in the name of Derech Chochmah that it is three quarters of an amah in length and half an amah in width. Machatzis HaShekel and Artzos HaChayim write that this has no basis in the Gemara, especially the requirement of half an amah in width. The practice of scrupulous individuals is to ensure that it contains one amah in front and one amah behind, excluding the neck opening from the measurement. With such a tallis katan one avoids doubt and may recite a blessing. In any event, each side should not be shorter than three quarters of an amah. Otherwise there is concern that the blessing is recited in vain, because later authorities measured and found that the head and most of a minor's body equal one and a half amos. See Biur Halachah. The practice among some Ashkenazim of taking one small piece of fabric for the front and another for the back, sewing straps to them, and attaching tzitzis does not fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis at all. Besides nullifying the mitzvah of tzitzis, they recite a blessing in vain each day and violate the prohibition against taking Hashem's Name in vain. It is also forbidden to go out wearing it on Shabbos, as explained in simanim 10 and 13. At the very least, those who do this should not recite a blessing over such garments. They should recite the blessing in the morning over a tallis gadol and wear the tallis katan all day, since they at least intend not to neglect the mitzvah of tzitzis throughout the day. Every God-fearing person, however, should not rely upon this at all, but should make an actual garment in accordance with the local manner of clothing. In some places, the garment called a kamizel or vest is open at the back, and tzitzis are attached to its four corners. Fortunate are those who do this. They must only be careful that most of the garment remains open. The same care is required regarding the talleisim ketanim made in our countries. See above, siman 10, seifim 7 and 8. The Arizal writes that it is preferable for a tallis katan not to have sleeves. If one wore the tallis and it folded somewhat in the ordinary manner of wearing clothing, so that after being folded it no longer contains enough material to cover the head and most of the body, responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 106, writes that since it is destined to spread out, it is treated as though already spread out. Even though it has not yet spread, it is considered to contain the required measure. See there and Bnei Chayei. A tallis katan that contains enough material to cover the head and most of the body, but because of sewing no longer contains the required measure, is exempt from tzitzis. Ibid., siman 66. Leket HaKemach writes the same.
