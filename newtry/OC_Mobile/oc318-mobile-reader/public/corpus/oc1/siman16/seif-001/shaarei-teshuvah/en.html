And his majority. Of a child. Baer Heitev; and what he wrote that the shoulders should be wide, etc.—see in She'elot Yaavetz, part 1, siman 4, who wrote there is no insistence that the shoulders be wide. And what they feared because air enters, etc.—this does not apply except to air considered a partition through lavud; but not for an adult, for we do not require combining air with it—ordinary air does not enter and nullify it; and see in the responsa of Chacham Tzvi, siman 59—see there. And see in Be'er Mayim Chayyim that there are differing opinions on the measure of tallit katan, and one should make it encircle the head and the whole body of a person to the chest, front and back—see ibid., in the name of Maharatz Tzemach; and what is written in Baer Heitev in the name of the Arizal, of blessed memory, that there were no armholes for tallit katan—that is, long armholes like those made for an outer garment; but still one may make like a hole through which the hands enter; and see in the writings of the Arizal that he wore it directly on his shirt and did not put his head into it. Still, at the time of the blessing he made a wrap, and afterward let it down to the neck and would bless to wrap himself—see there. And what he wrote in the name of Beit Yaakov—see there—it is clear that even if he wants to wear it thus when folded and it does not have the measure of a tallit, still it is forbidden to wear it without tzitzis, for behold he is about to spread it out.
