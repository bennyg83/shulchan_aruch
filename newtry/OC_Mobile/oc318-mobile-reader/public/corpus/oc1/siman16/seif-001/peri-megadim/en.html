The measure. See Taz. The Gemara on Menachos 41a states this measure. The phrase, “and an adult goes out wearing it occasionally,” is a novelty. Meaning, even though an adult does not wear it regularly but only occasionally, it is nevertheless obligated. See Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Abuhav and Mahari Chaviv, as cited in Magen Avraham. However, even an important person who would not wear it occasionally is obligated. Tur writes, “and an adult is not embarrassed,” following Rashi's wording in the Gemara. This implies that the phrase states a condition rather than a novelty. Mahari Abuhav's explanation is likewise difficult, because he writes of a measure, as Magen Avraham notes. The reason the latter clause does not repeat the condition that an adult must wear it occasionally is that this follows all the more so. Taz writes, “One cannot say: why do I need both?” It appears that he refers back to Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. According to them, why is more than one measure needed? The statement that an adult goes out wearing it is therefore a novelty. Magen Avraham raises a similar question and answers that otherwise we would not know which age of minor the baraisa means. It therefore gives this additional measure: an unspecified minor and adult surely refer to a nine-year-old minor, as Raavad writes in chapter 3 of Hilchos Tzitzis, “They did not say this regarding every minor, for if so, no adult would wear it even occasionally.” Taz explains Rama's gloss as agreeing with the Mechaber, unlike Magen Avraham. Darkei Moshe copies Beit Yosef's wording and agrees with it. Here the interpretation follows Mahari Abuhav: the baraisa does not discuss the minor's obligation, because the minor himself is under no obligation. This requires consideration. A minor who has reached the age of education is certainly discussed below in siman 17, where his father is obligated to make tzitzis for his tallis. The measure is surely based upon the head and most of that particular minor's body. If he is six years old, the measure is his own head and most of his body, and his father is obligated D'Rabbanan to attach tzitzis to it. If a garment can cover the head and most of the body but is considered degrading clothing by this person, who does not wear it even occasionally in the marketplace, while ordinary people do wear such a garment, then according to Bach, who requires both conditions, and according to Tur as Taz understands him, perhaps one should indeed not recite a blessing over such a tallis because we follow his personal practice. This requires study.
