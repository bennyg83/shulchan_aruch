Measure of tallit, etc. The beraisa's wording is thus: our Rabbis taught—a tallit in which a child covers his head and majority and an adult goes out in it casually to the market is obligated; if a child does not cover his head and majority, even though an adult goes out in it casually—it is exempt. Rambam and after him Shulchan Aruch hold the main [factor] is the measure of the child's covering; their reason: since it says in the conclusion "even though the adult," etc., and it does not ask the reverse—if the adult does not go out, even though the child covers, it is exempt—therefore everything depends on the child's covering. And that which the Tanna cited first "and the adult goes out" is for amplification: if an adult goes out in it regularly, obviously it is obligated; and then one cannot say any measure in a small child—for this is obvious: even if the child covers in it, since the adult goes out regularly; rather even if the adult goes out only casually, still it is called "your garment" since the child covers. And the Tur wrote the whole beraisa, only that for "adult goes out" he wrote "and the adult is not ashamed to go out," etc.—it appears he holds both are required. And that which he did not cite in the conclusion only "if the child does not cover" and not the reverse—it is learned by kal vachomer, for the main obligation is on the adult, only we gauge by the child; and therefore the Tanna teaches amplification: even though for the adult it is at most casual garment, still it is exempt—and all the more so when it is not a garment at all for the adult, for we do not care what the child covers. And one could ask: why do I need two measures, for in truth they are one measure? One can say: not all children are equal; and in the Tur he cited a child of nine years—and also in this there is a difference between one nine-year-old and another; and also regarding the adult not being ashamed, sometimes one is more ashamed than another—therefore he combined both together. And what the Tur wrote "nine years"—it appears they learned from what is stated in Nezikin in the mishnah: they taught regarding minor children—the young ones, meaning nine and ten years old; and they say there each according to his cleverness; and the child here too is in the beraisa—therefore we interpret in it all we can, meaning that he goes out and comes among people to buy and sell, meaning from nine years and above, as stated. And Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Chaviv wrote that the Tur's words are not with precision; and Darchei Moshe also brought them. And what the Rema wrote here, "specifically when the adult wears it," etc.—he did not cite this for the measure but for the obligation of tzitzis, meaning there is no obligation of tzitzis on the child, only the obligation is on the adult who sometimes wears it; but the main measure is by the child, as he wrote in the name of Mahari Chaviv. But in my humble opinion the Tur's intent is as stated, and already many great ones cried out against the masses who make four corners from a small garment; and they also mentioned in Mordechai; and he concludes: therefore Rabbi calls out as a peddler against those who make them, whether with tzitzis or without tzitzis, for he is lax in his hand due to doubt to leave a four-cornered tallit without tzitzis—end quote; therefore it is incumbent on every God-fearing person to warn not to bless on such a small garment.
