And most of a minor. This means when he is nine years old. Responsa Rama discusses at length how people fulfill the mitzvah with four-cornered garments smaller than this measure. He answers that it is better to wear them than to go all day without tzitzis. In Darkei Moshe, siman 9, he writes that the people should be taught to recite the blessing in the morning over a tallis gadol, thereby exempting the tallis katan, and then wear it all day. Nevertheless, if a person can make it large enough to contain the required measure, how good and pleasant that is. Mateh Moshe writes in the name of Maharil that the shoulder sections should be made wide, so that the open space on either side does not combine and nullify them. Magen Avraham. Sefer Peri HaAretz writes in the name of Derech Chochmah that its measure is three quarters of an amah in length and half an amah in width. The measure of a tallis gadol is that it cover one's head and most of his body when wrapped, and reach to the chest; see there. The Arizal writes that it is preferable for a tallis katan not to have sleeves. If one wore the tallis and it folded somewhat in the ordinary manner of wearing clothing, so that after being folded it no longer contains enough material to cover one's head and most of his body, responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 106, writes that since it is destined to spread out, it is treated as though already spread out. Even though it has not yet spread, it is considered to contain the required measure; see there and Bnei Chayei. A tallis katan that contains enough material to cover one's head and most of his body, but because of sewing it no longer contains the required measure, is exempt from tzitzis. Ibid., siman 66. Leket HaKemach writes the same.
Occasionally. If an adult would be embarrassed to go out wearing it even occasionally in the marketplace, one should not recite a blessing over it. If he goes out wearing a tallis that other people do not wear, his personal practice is disregarded. Magen Avraham. See Taz, Shiurei Knesses HaGedolah, Peri HaAretz, siman 1, and Olas Tamid.
