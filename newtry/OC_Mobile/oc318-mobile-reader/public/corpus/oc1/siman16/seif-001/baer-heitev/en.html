And the majority of a child. That is, when he is nine years old. In the responsum of the Rema he analyzes how one fulfills [the mitzvah] with four corners of the small [tallis] from this measure, and the gist of his words is that it is better to go in them than to go all day without tzitzis. And in Darchei Moshe, siman 9, he wrote to teach the people to bless in the morning on a tallit gadol and thereby exempt the small one and go in it all day. Still, if a person can make a large one that is the [proper] measure, how good and pleasant that is—end quote. And Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of Maharil that one should make the shoulder straps wide so that air from this side and that side does not enter and nullify it—Magen Avraham. And in Sefer Pri HaAretz he wrote in the name of Sefer Derech Chachmah that its measure is three-quarters of an amah in length and half an amah in width; and the measure of a tallit gadol is that it cover his head and most [of his body] by wrapping and reach to the chest—see there. The Arizal wrote that a tallit katan is better if it has no armholes. If one wore the tallit and it folded somewhat as people wear garments, and after it folded there is not the measure to cover head and most—Beit Yaakov wrote in his responsum, siman 106, that since he is about to spread it out it is considered as if spread even though he has not yet spread it, and behold there is the measure in it—see there; and see in Bnei Chayyeh. A tallit katan that has enough to cover head and most, and through the sewing there is not the measure—it is exempt from tzitzis—there, siman 66; and likewise in Leket HaKemach.

Casual wear. And if an adult is ashamed to go out in it casually to the market, one does not recite a blessing over it. And if he goes out in a tallit that others do not go out in, his intent is nullified—Magen Avraham; and see Taz; see in Knesset HaGedolah and in Sefer Pri HaAretz, siman 1, and in Orach Mishor.
