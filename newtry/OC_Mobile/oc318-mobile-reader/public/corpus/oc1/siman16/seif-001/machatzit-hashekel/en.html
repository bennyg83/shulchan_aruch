Magen Avraham wrote, and this is his language in the responsum of the Rema, etc.: that it is better to go, etc.—it is implied from his wording that in any case one should not bless on it; and likewise it is implied from Darchei Moshe's words that Magen Avraham brought. And likewise Mordechai, Bach, and Taz cry out against these small tallisim. And in Shulchan Aruch I saw well that he wrote, and this is his language: and in Sefer Pri HaAretz he wrote in the name of Sefer Derech Chachmah that its measure is three-quarters of an amah in length and half an amah in width—end quote. But I did not know the source of this measure. It is also not explained in which amah it is measured. I do not have these books at hand. I saw in Sefer Tzon Kodashim on Menachos, page 41b, on that which was taught: and likewise that one should not bring even an amah by an amah from another tallit that has tzitzis and hang it on it—and this is the law mentioned above, siman 15, seif 2. Rashi wrote the reason: for it is written "on the corners of their garments," and this corner was not from this garment at the time of making, as above, siman 15, seif 2. And in Sefer Tzon Kodashim he wrote, and this is his language: and the Nosei Keilim wrote because of tolaim. And it is possible Rashi holds that since it was an amah by an amah it is the measure of a tallit with which the child covers his head and majority; and one can make four tzitzis on that piece, for it is not tolaim—therefore Rashi wrote "on the corners of their garments," etc.—the aforementioned Sefer Tzon Kodashim. And if so the measure is an amah by an amah: according to Sefer Tzon Kodashim, in a dispute stated according to Rashi's view, the measure of tallit katan would be an amah by an amah; and according to Nosei Keilim, who wrote the reason is because of tolaim—therefore an amah by an amah does not have the measure with which the child covers his head and majority. And the amah of the Talmud of six tefachim is three-quarters of an amah of Aharon Rokeach. And if so, if the length of the tallit katan is three-quarters of an amah of Aharon Rokeach and also the width three-quarters of an amah of Aharon Rokeach, there is room to rely on Rashi's view as explained in the above Sefer Tzon Kodashim. But according to what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 15, s.k. 43, there is no proof from Rashi that Rashi also intended the reason of tolaim—see there. If so, on the contrary it is implied that also for Rashi an amah by an amah does not have the measure of a tallit.

That air from this side and that side should not enter, etc.—this is a borrowed expression. And the main reason is that they should be recognizable and that on the shoulders there should also be the status of a garment and not merely straps; but "air from this side and that side," etc., does not apply here, according to what Tosafos wrote in Eruvin 10b, brought in Magen Avraham below, siman 362, s.k. 16, and this is his language: we do not say that ordinary air comes and nullifies a partition, but only with air that I consider as sealed—end quote; see there.

(s.k. 1) Child, etc.—not as Bach wrote, that the Tur disagrees with the Semak, for in the Semak he wrote a child who has reached chinuch; and Bach holds this means six and seven years old, each according to his cleverness—and if so he disagrees with the Tur, who wrote nine years. Magen Avraham holds that "reached chinuch" that the Semak wrote means nine years old, and this is the Tur's view. And as Tosafos wrote in Eruvin 2b, s.v. shehigi'a lachinuch, etc.; still Tosafos there concluded "and it is not clear"—see there.
