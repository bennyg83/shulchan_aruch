In Shulchan Aruch Baer Heitev I saw the following: “Peri HaAretz writes in the name of Derech Chochmah that its measure is three quarters of an amah in length and half an amah in width.” I do not know the source of this measure, nor is it explained which type of amah is intended. I do not have these works available. I saw in Tzon Kodashim on Menachos 41b, concerning the baraisa, “All agree that one may not bring even a piece measuring one amah by one amah from another tallis with tzitzis upon it and attach it,” which is the law mentioned above in siman 15, seif 2. Rashi explains that the reason is that the verse says, “upon the corners of their garments,” and this corner was not part of this garment at the time the tzitzis were made, as explained above in siman 15, seif 2. Tzon Kodashim writes: “Nimukei Yosef explains that the reason is ‘you shall make,’ and not something already made. Perhaps Rashi maintains that since the piece is one amah by one amah, it contains the measure of a tallis with which a minor can cover his head and most of his body. He could therefore attach four tzitzis to that piece itself, and it would not be ‘you shall make,’ and not something already made. Rashi therefore gives the reason of ‘upon the corners,’ etc.” According to Tzon Kodashim, then, the measure of one amah by one amah is disputed. According to Rashi it is the measure of a tallis katan. According to Nimukei Yosef, who explains the invalidity as “you shall make,” and not something already made, one amah by one amah necessarily does not contain enough to cover the head and most of a minor's body. The amah of the Gemara, which is six tefachim, equals three quarters of the local amah used in Moravia. Accordingly, if a tallis katan measures three quarters of a Moravian amah in both length and width, one may rely upon Rashi's view according to Tzon Kodashim. Yet according to what Magen Avraham writes in siman 15, seif katan 3, there is no proof from Rashi, because Rashi too intended the reason of “you shall make,” and not something already made. On the contrary, this implies that even according to Rashi, one amah by one amah does not contain the required measure of a tallis.
So that the open space on either side should not, etc. This is borrowed terminology. The essential reason is that the shoulder sections should be clearly recognizable and should themselves have the status of fabric rather than merely that of straps. The principle that open space on either side combines to nullify something does not apply here. Tosafos on Eruvin 10b, cited by Magen Avraham below in siman 362, seif katan 16, writes that ordinary open space is not said to combine and nullify a partition; that principle applies only to open space that would otherwise be treated as closed. (Seif katan 1) A minor, etc. This is unlike Bach, who writes that Tur disagrees with Semak. Semak writes, “a minor who has reached the age of education,” and Bach understands this to mean six or seven years old, each according to his maturity. According to Bach, this disagrees with Tur, who writes nine years old. Magen Avraham maintains that Semak's phrase “reached the age of education” likewise means nine years old, in agreement with Tur. Tosafos on Eruvin 2b, s.v. “who has reached the age of education,” writes similarly, although Tosafos concludes there that this explanation is not convincing; see there.
