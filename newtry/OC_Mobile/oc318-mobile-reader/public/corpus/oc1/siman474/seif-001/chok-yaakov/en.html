To recite the first blessing over each and every cup. And Magen Avraham wrote, and these are his words: And even though the Haggadah does not constitute an interruption, as written in siman 178, nevertheless, since each and every one is a mitzvah on its own, we recite the blessing over each one; end quote. And according to this it is difficult regarding what the Tur and the Mechaber ruled at the end of siman 475, concerning one who extends his meal until nightfall: he spreads a cloth and recites Kiddush and does not recite borei peri hagafen; yet that is certainly a mitzvah on its own, so why do we not recite borei peri hagafen over it? And in truth, according to the reason explained in the Rosh in chapter Arvei Pesachim for those who require that one recite the blessing over each and every cup, namely because they interrupt with the Haggadah and Hallel and Birkas HaMazon, if so there is nothing difficult here, for there there is no interruption at all, since the blessing over wine always precedes the day's Kiddush; therefore there is no need to recite the blessing a second time. But according to what Magen Avraham wrote it is difficult. And it is possible to resolve his words with difficulty: that even though it is not a complete interruption, as stated in siman 171, nevertheless here it is like an interruption by force of combining the reason that each and every one is a mitzvah on its own. And see what is written in the Taz.
And so is the view of most of the Geonim. Meaning, for this reason we recite the blessing over each and every cup; but regarding the fact that we do not recite an after-blessing except over the last cup, even if that were not the view of most of the poskim, one should still not recite the blessing, because of the doubt. And I wrote all this to exclude what Maharil wrote in Hilchos Haggadah, and these are his words: And we recite borei peri hagafen over the second cup, even though the Rosh wrote not to recite the blessing; for the Tosafists it is obvious to them that we recite the blessing, and [as for] the Rosh, since it is forbidden to derive benefit without a blessing, and a doubtful prohibition is treated stringently; end quote. And in truth we hold that a doubtful blessing is treated leniently, and one should not recite it; and so Maharil himself wrote in his responsa, siman 79; and his words are puzzling. Also the Rosh himself in a responsum, Klal 14, siman 5, wrote: One who minimizes blessings does not lose, for the blessings are not indispensable; but one who multiplies them loses, for it is a blessing in vain; end quote. And see above at the end of siman 210. And this is not in the category of the prohibition against deriving benefit without a blessing, since one restrains oneself because of the doubt of a blessing in vain; rather, ideally one should not put oneself into a doubtful blessing in a matter that one can avoid. And here regarding the cups, the Ashkenazic custom is to recite the blessing over each and every cup, since that is the view of most of the Geonim (and see the Taz); and this is obvious.
