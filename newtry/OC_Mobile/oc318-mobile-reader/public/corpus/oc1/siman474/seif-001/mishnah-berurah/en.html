(1) Not a first blessing. And even though the four cups that the Sages enacted, each one is a mitzvah on its own, nevertheless, since there is no diversion of attention from drinking, for when he recited the blessing over the first cup he knew that he would drink another cup, therefore he need not recite a blessing over each and every cup.
(2) And over the cup of Birkas HaMazon. And the fourth cup is exempted by the blessing of the third cup.
(3) Only after the fourth cup. And even though Birkas HaMazon is a conclusion and departure and is considered like a complete interruption with respect to the first blessing of the third cup, nevertheless with respect to the after-blessing nothing is lost, for diversion of attention does not require that one immediately recite the after-blessing, and one may wait until he has drunk all the cups, and then with the after-blessing he will exempt all four cups together.
(4) Over every cup. For once he has begun the Haggadah it is forbidden to drink, and therefore it constitutes an interruption requiring a blessing on the second cup; and even if we say that this does not interrupt with respect to the blessing (as in that of siman 178, se'if 6), nevertheless, since each and every one is a mitzvah on its own, one should recite a separate blessing over every cup.
(5) But an after-blessing. For diversion of attention does not establish [a requirement of] a blessing after it, as above.
