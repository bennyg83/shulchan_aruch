(9) [Seif 3] One does not knead mursan, etc. — but one puts water in it, etc.; and this is Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, who ruled like R' Yosi b'R Yehuda; and some forbid — what Shulchan Aruch wrote afterward is Semag and Semak's view; and Terumat HaDeshen ruled like Rabbi, as explained in Beit Yosef; see there. And likewise above siman 321 letter 15; see there. And the reason of those who permit — Maggid Mishneh wrote (end of chapter 8) because mursan is not a matter of kneading; but Beit Yosef wrote that kneading also applies in mursan, only the reason according to R' Yosi b'R Yehuda is that in putting water alone he is not liable until he kneads, but nevertheless there is a prohibition; and this is in flour that has no need, but in mursan that has need that day to feed an animal and a bird they did not decree, as explained in Beit Yosef. And Taz s.k. 1 wrote that likewise in flour it is permitted to put water on Shabbat for this view if it is for the day's need to feed an animal and a bird; see there. However Tosefet Shabbat letter 4 challenged Beit Yosef and Taz on this and wrote that from mursan there is no Torah liability in its kneading but only rabbinic prohibition, and therefore permitted; but in flour where there is Torah liability in kneading — forbidden to put water on it on Shabbat even for need; see there; and so Chayei Mishpat letter 1; and so Birkhei Yosef in Shiyurei Berakhah letter 1 in the name of Maharil and Eliyahu; and he wrote: accordingly in our mursan, which always retains flour in it, kneading from the Torah applies, and putting water is forbidden rabbinically; and so Kaf HaChayim letter 1; and so Ben Ish Chai Parashat Mishpatim letter 14:
