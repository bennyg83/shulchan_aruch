(א) (s.k. 12) Before a dog — it implies in the gemara as stated at end of Shabbat: Who knows the judgment of the poor? The Holy One knows the judgment of the poor of the world — this is a dog whose sustenance is scant; therefore He embedded in its nature that it holds its food in its bowels three days and three nights. And further there: we learn proper conduct — to give a small piece of meat the length of an ear to a dog; and Rashi wrote thus: we learn from what He said that the Holy One was particular about its sustenance, end of his words. If so, also for another's dog there is a mitzvah; and even though the Talmud said proper conduct — it implies only it is not merely proper conduct; nevertheless Magen Avraham calls it a mitzvah, for behold it is included in "and you shall walk in His ways" — as He is, etc. Only in Choshen Mishpat and siman 328 he challenged him, for we say there "this is in the field," but in town not, lest he come to exert himself after it and lose in his house; and on Shabbat necessarily discusses in town, see there:

(ב) (s.k. 13) And see siman 328 s.k. 29 — meaning from there it is proven even before another's dog permitted to give on Shabbat, for Rama wrote there: and accordingly permitted to move wood, etc., if fit for dogs, for dogs are common, end of his words; and necessarily the intent is not that dogs are common to each one — for this is not so; rather meaning they are common in the city, and impossible that forbidden to give before another's dog — why would moving be permitted; for relative to the homeowner they are fit for nothing, for he has no need for dogs; and in muktzeh everything follows the homeowner's intent, as stated above end of siman 328; nevertheless permitted to give sustenance before another's dogs; if so even if he has no need for dogs they are fit for him, as I wrote:

(ג) (s.k. 14) And Abaye — not like the words of Eliyah Rabbah. For on daf 155b we learned: from gathering for chickens — and all the more gathering; and for doves — and all the more not gathering; and R' Yuda explained gathering — feeding by hand, and gathering — throwing before them and they eat by themselves; and he explained the reason permitted for chickens — since their sustenance is upon you; but for doves their sustenance is not upon you (and Rashi: for they are common in the field) — therefore forbidden to exert for them; and as taught: one gives sustenance before a dog and one does not give sustenance before a swine — what is the difference between this and that? Rather this one's sustenance is upon you, etc.; Rav Ashi said the mishnah also is precise, etc., water before bees but one gives water before geese, etc. — why not on account that these one's sustenance is upon you, etc.; and he challenged: and according to you why specifically water, even wheat and barley also not; rather (so our version, unlike Ramban who lacks "rather") water is different for it is common in marshes, end of the gemara. And it appears from the conclusion it does not depend on whether its sustenance is upon you or not, only where it is common in marshes it is forbidden, but if not common in marshes permitted to give even before one whose sustenance is not upon you; and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote since all poskim did not write thus, rather necessarily according to our version we read "rather this water that is common in marshes is from the words of the one who says," and rejects the inference of R' Elazar who relied on whether its sustenance is upon you; rather one may say since he cited water that depends whether common in marshes, but R' Elazar holds it depends whether its sustenance is upon you and was not concerned that he cited water and not wheat and barley — if so they rejected the inference of R' Elazar; nevertheless we hold like R' Elazar, and even wheat and barley also forbidden where its sustenance is not upon you even though not common in marshes, end of Rabbeinu Yerucham's words; and Eliyah Rabbah wrote for us that which forbidden to give sustenance before doves is specifically in summer when wheat and barley are common in the field, but in winter when nothing is in the field — even though its sustenance is not upon you, permitted to give them sustenance; and he wrote in Ramban it appears his words are true, Ramban's view inclines to his words; but Rabbeinu Yerucham already wrote Ramban likewise only according to his version that lacks "rather"; but according to our version that we read "rather R' Elazar did not retract," etc., and we rule accordingly — and there is no distinction between common or not common, only whether its sustenance is upon you or not:
