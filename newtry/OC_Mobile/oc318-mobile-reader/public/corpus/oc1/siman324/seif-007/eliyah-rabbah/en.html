[8] Whether it was healthy, etc. This is the approach of the Beit Yosef, who distinguishes between Shabbos and Yom Tov. I proved in Lechem Chamudot at the end that not all authorities hold this way, and the Rosh does not hold so. Since we follow the Rosh, one should therefore not be lenient regarding a healthy animal, as stated in Malbushei Yom Tov. This requires study.
[9] Hard, such that it is impossible, etc. Even if it can be eaten with difficulty without cutting, it is permitted, as stated by the Beit Yosef. Certainly, then, this is permitted when it is entirely hard.
[10] [Levush] Because all this is exertion, etc. The Rambam, chapter 21, however, gives the reason that one may come to crushing legumes or kneading flour. He further writes that one may not feed a camel on Shabbos enough food for three or four days, nor force-feed a calf or the like by opening its mouth and placing vetch and water into it together. Likewise, one may not place food into the mouths of pigeons or chickens in a place from which they cannot return it. One may, however, feed an animal while it is standing and give it drink while standing, or place water alone into its mouth and vetch alone into its mouth, in a place from which it can return them. End of his words. This implies that with force-feeding, vetch and water together are permitted, and likewise with force-feeding either vetch or water is permitted where the animal can return it. This is unlike the implication of the Levush. Also, in the Gemara, Shabbos 155, it states that one feeds it while standing and places vetch separately. It appears to me that it means either one or the other, and this requires study. The Rama writes in responsum 79 that it is permitted to tell a non-Jew to force-feed geese once daily because of cruelty to animals, since they cannot eat without force-feeding. The Magen Avraham, siman 301, seif katan 12, writes that this implies that if no non-Jew is available, a Jew may do so because of cruelty to animals, though it is preferable through a minor. I say, however, that the responsum itself implies stringency when done by a Jew. I also have a difficulty with the Rama, who derives this from the Gemara on page 156, which says that if it does not pick up food with its tongue, one may feed it, and he explains this as force-feeding. This is difficult, for earlier it says that one may not stuff it, which Rashi explains as force-feeding. If so, why did it not also say that one may stuff it? Moreover, the expressions for feeding used by the Gemara on page 156 are concluded there to mean placing food where it can return it. As for the Rama's question that the Mishnah there permits this even where it picks up the food with its tongue, provided it can return it, it appears to me that this can be resolved by saying that the case is where the animal does not belong to him, or involves dovecote pigeons whose sustenance is not his responsibility. Even so, if it does not pick up food with its tongue, one may feed it. Examine this carefully. Therefore one should not be lenient to force-feed through a Jew.
