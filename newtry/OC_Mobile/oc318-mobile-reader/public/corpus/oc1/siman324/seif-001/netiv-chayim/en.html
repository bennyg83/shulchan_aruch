(Taz s.k. 4) And Rama's words are very difficult, etc. NB and it appears to me that Terumat HaDeshen also ruled like R' Yehuda, only regarding equating food — such as to cut fine fine, which is excessive exertion in this matter R' Yehuda concedes it is forbidden. And if you ask — if so, what does he challenge on Shabbos daf 155 against Rav Huna: we learned "one does not crush," etc., apparently we do not exert ourselves with foods — say that in such a case Rav Huna concedes, since there is excessive exertion, to forbid exerting with foods. One may explain that he challenged him from the mishnah — since the end of the mishnah forbids on account of exerting with foods, it implies that the reason of the first part is also on account of exerting with foods; and this is Rashi's intent s.v. "one does not crush" — the end of the mishnah, and one reason. And so it appears in Sefer Mageinei Shlomo to resolve Tosafos' difficulty on Rashi's explanation s.v. "R' Yehuda" for this reason, see there. And this is what Rama emended in simanim 321 and 324 — that he is concerned for Terumat HaDeshen's view since there is excessive exertion; and not like Taz who wrote that Rama's words are astonishing and deficient, for his words are straight and ancient.
