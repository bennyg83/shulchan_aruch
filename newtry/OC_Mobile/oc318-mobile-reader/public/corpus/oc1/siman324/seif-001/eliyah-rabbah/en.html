[1] One may not sift, etc. Because of meraked, sifting (Yerushalmi). Rambam, chapter 21, likewise writes this. Rashi on 140a makes clear that this straw is ordinarily sifted with a sieve. This creates a difficulty with Levush in siman 319, seif 8, where he says there is no reason to decree in such a case. That rationale does not apply here, yet taking the sieve is prohibited. In truth Levush added that argument himself. I found no such rationale in the authorities, and it appears that no such general decree was ever made.
[2] [Levush] With the kav itself, etc. This excludes putting the feed into the measuring vessel by hand.
[3] And he estimates, etc. This requires study, because Tur makes clear that this is Rabbenu Yerucham's view and that he disagrees with Rashi, the first view. How then did Levush combine the two? Shulchan Aruch and Bach rule like Rabbenu Yerucham. Yam Shel Shlomo at the end of chapter Ein Tzadin rules leniently according to both because the issue is rabbinic: one may fill the measuring vessel itself rather than by hand, or may estimate using another vessel. This can reconcile Levush's formulation.
[4] And one passes it through, etc. Even several times (Bach). One may place before each animal, etc., even two vessels for a single animal, although on weekdays one does not provide so much at once (Beit Yosef; Nachalat Tzvi). Nevertheless, more than is needed for that day is prohibited (Olat Shabbat; Magen Avraham).
