One does not cut shachat. Rambam's language in chapter 21: one who cuts a vegetable fine fine in order to cook it — this is a derivative of grinding; therefore one does not pound neither shachat nor carobs before an animal, whether fine or coarse, because it appears like grinding; but one cuts gourds before an animal and a carcass before dogs, for there is no grinding in fruits. And Kesef Mishneh wrote that they asked Rashba why Rambam gave the reason that it appears like grinding, for in the gemara they gave the reason on account of tircha in food; and Rashba conceded it was a difficulty. And Beit Yosef strained there; and it appears to me to resolve in this way: Rambam wrote here that one who cuts a vegetable fine fine is a derivative of grinding — not for the essence of the law did he come here, for they already wrote in chapter 8; and further, here he wrote in order to cook it, and in chapter 8 they did not mention it; and it appears to me his intent is that whatever a person does in order to eat it, there is no grinding except if he cuts in order to cook — automatically they will set it aside until tomorrow, for on Shabbat he will not cook; therefore it is a derivative of grinding; and therefore one does not pound shachat, for we say in the gemara on account of tircha, and Rambam explained the reason: what prohibition is there in tircha in food? Rather it is because since they can eat without it, only that he does it for extra repair — automatically it is as if it were not for eating on Shabbat, for there is no need for eating; therefore it appears like grinding. Actual grinding it would not be, for in any case it is repair for eating; rather since it is only from the side of extra repair, it is forbidden rabbinically that it appears like grinding. And what Rambam concluded that there is no grinding in fruits — Maggid Mishneh wrote there is another textual reading: there is grinding only in fruits; and he wrote I did not understand either version; and Beit Yosef strained himself to uphold the reading that there is no grinding in fruits, and the reason is to exempt pounding shachat and carobs from liability, etc. — and it is very strained; and it is more plausible in my humble opinion to explain in this way: there is no grinding in fruits eaten on Shabbat; and therefore it is forbidden only in pounding shachat because it appears like grinding, since it is possible to eat without it — which is not so in cutting gourds and a carcass, for it is impossible for the animal to eat without it; it is the animal's need for eating, and there is no grinding at all, as is plausible to me — very correct:
