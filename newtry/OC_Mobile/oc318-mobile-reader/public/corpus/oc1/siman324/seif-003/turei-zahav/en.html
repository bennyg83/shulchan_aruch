(א) But one puts water in it, etc. — the man of this view is Rif and Rambam and Rosh and Tur, whom we cited in siman 321 seif 15, who rule like R' Yosi b'R Yehuda that one is not liable for putting water until he kneads; and therefore it is entirely permitted to put water, for he does nothing; and that which R' Yosi b'R Yehuda said he is not liable until he kneads implies there is in any case a prohibition in putting water — this is not a matter, for in the mishnah it is permitted l'chatchila to put water in mursan, and it follows R' Yosi b'R Yehuda; rather since the Rabbi said there one puts flour and one puts water, the latter is liable — therefore he stated and said he is not liable; but in truth it is entirely permitted. And Beit Yosef wrote that nevertheless there is also a prohibition in putting water, and this is in flour that is not needed for Shabbat — therefore there is nevertheless a prohibition; but in mursan, which is needed to feed an animal and a bird, they did not decree. And I wonder at the holy mouth that would say thus — is it that in flour there was no need for birds and animals? And certainly the one who puts water in it does so for this need. And further, in the gemara they challenged: perhaps R' Yosi b'R Yehuda spoke only of flour, a matter of kneading, etc. — behold we see that for permission according to R' Yosi we require that the permission be explicit, and it does not suffice if we say one may say it follows R' Yosi, for otherwise what is the challenge, and one invents a distinction; but it is preferable for us to say there is no distinction between a matter of kneading or mursan, and we can establish the mishnah like R' Yosi, for according to Rabbi at any rate it only follows when it is certain; and certainly the gemara must say that it certainly follows R' Yosi and there is no distinction at all; otherwise it should have challenged at least that R' Yosi only said he is not not liable but the mishnah where there is permission l'chatchila — no; rather certainly that wherever we do not find a prohibition explicitly, we certainly do not infer a prohibition from ourselves; and such is the truth according to R' Yosi b'R Yehuda; and even though in the words of the fifth [Maggid] we find a distinction between prohibition and liability for one who holds that a matter that is not a matter of kneading is lighter — as he wrote, and this is his language: and the reason is because it is not a matter of kneading, and therefore even if he kneads there will not be liability but only a rabbinic prohibition from his words, and from this putting water was permitted; and if its kneading were forbidden from the Torah, putting water would be forbidden rabbinically from their words, etc. — end of his words — there is no proof from this that he wrote for that one who holds that a matter that is not a matter of kneading is preferable according to R' Yosi; and if so, why did R' Yosi say there, in the baraita in chapter Mi sheHechshich they put water in mursan — he should have said even kneading where there is no liability, for it is not a matter of kneading; or that there is a prohibition in kneading even in a matter that is not a matter of kneading on account of a decree on account of a matter of kneading — and if so, likewise in a matter of kneading such as flour it is obvious there is a prohibition even in putting water, a decree on account of kneading, for putting water in a matter of kneading is not preferable to kneading itself in what is not a matter of kneading; but we rule like the view of Tosafos and Ra'avad that in what is not a matter of kneading it is stricter; and nevertheless R' Yosi b'R Yehuda said he is not liable until he kneads, and there is not even a prohibition, for he said explicitly they put water into the mursan; if so, all the more in a matter of kneading there is no prohibition in putting water according to R' Yosi b'R Yehuda, as stated:
