(א) One does not knead mursan, etc. — behold regarding putting water in mursan Rabbi and R' Yosi b'R Yehuda disagree: Rabbi is stringent [and according to him there is liability for a sin-offering in this, for he holds putting water is its kneading], and R' Yosi b'R Yehuda is lenient l'chatchila; and there is a stam mishnah in the last chapter of Shabbos like R' Yosi b'R Yehuda, and in the first chapter like Rabbi; and Rif, Rambam, and Rosh rule to decide like the later stam to be lenient, even though generally we rule the halacha follows Rabbi from his colleague — here the later stam is preferable; and some rule [namely Ra'em, Beit HaTefutzot, Semag, Semak, and their words are brought in Hagahot Maimoniyot and also in Mordechai; and see above siman 321 in Biur Halacha] that since the stam mishnayot contradict each other, we return to the general rule that the halacha follows Rabbi from his colleague; and the Mechaber's view in the essence of the law is to be lenient in putting water, and he adopted the latter view only in the name of one authority, because Rif, Rosh, and Rambam stand in one view here; and in truth such is also the view of R' Chananel, Razah, Ramban in Milchamot, Ran, Rokeach, Or Zarua, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Meiri — all ruled like R' Yosi b'R Yehuda. And likewise Rabbi and R' Yosi b'R Yehuda disagree in putting water into flour, dust, and clay: Rabbi makes liable and R' Yosi b'R Yehuda exempts until he kneads; nevertheless there is a rabbinic prohibition even according to R' Yosi b'R Yehuda in putting water into flour, dust, and clay lest he come to knead; and that which he permitted in mursan l'chatchila — Rambam's view is because even when he kneads it there is no d'oraisa prohibition, as below; and therefore they did not decree on putting water; and some say the reason for permission is because it is needed to feed an animal and a bird, and therefore the rabbis did not decree; and according to this reason, putting water in flour would also be permitted when it is needed to feed birds, as in mursan, through shinui [Magen Tzedek and Peri Megadim]. And know further that b'dieved if he kneaded mursan — Rambam's view is that he is exempt because it is not a matter of kneading; and likewise in coarse dust or sand; and such is Rif and Rashi in chapter 4 of Beitzah that in dust that is not a matter of kneading, even when he kneads he is exempt; but many Rishonim disagree on this [they are Tosafos in Shabbos 18, Ra'avad in chapter 8 of Hilchot Shabbat, Rosh in chapter 4 of Beitzah, Ran, and Rashba there], and they hold the reverse like Abbaye, that in a matter that is not a matter of kneading, as soon as he puts water in it he is liable; and therefore in dust or coarse sand he is liable as soon as he puts water in it; but mursan they call a matter of kneading, and therefore he is exempt at the time of putting water and liable when he kneads it like flour. And all this in mursan; but in plain chaff that is not a matter of kneading according to all, and according to these Rishonim there is liability for a sin-offering when he puts water in it on Shabbat:

(ב) Mursan — see in Mishna Berurah what he wrote: whether much or little, and it was not permitted little by little except in a vessel; and even though Magen Avraham brought in the name of Ramban in Milchamot that little by little is permitted even without shinui — examine well in Milchamot at the end of the tractate: you will see there is no proof at all, for what he wrote "all are permitted" means whether thick or thin in a vessel; and so explicitly in his novellae; and the reason they did not permit this except in human food and not to trouble for animal food — or that mursan for an animal is eaten even if he does not knead it entirely [namely only through shinui of passing back and forth] — therefore they did not wish to be lenient in this through permitting little by little; see there in his novellae. Afterward I found in Korban Netanel that he too agreed not like Magen Avraham, and wrote there is no proof from Milchamot, etc., and the truth accords with Ramban's words as we wrote:
