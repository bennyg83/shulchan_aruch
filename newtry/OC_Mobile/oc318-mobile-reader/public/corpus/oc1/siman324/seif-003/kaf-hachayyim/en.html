9) [Seif 3] One may not knead bran, etc. But one may place water into it, etc. This is the view of Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, who rule like Rabbi Yose son of Rabbi Yehudah. The prohibiting view subsequently cited in Shulchan Aruch is that of Semag, Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah, who rule like Rebbe, as explained in Beis Yosef; see there. We wrote the same above in siman 321, paragraph 90; see there. Maggid Mishneh, at the end of chapter 8, explains that the permissive view holds that bran is not capable of kneading. Beis Yosef, however, writes that kneading does apply to bran. The reason is that Rabbi Yose son of Rabbi Yehudah holds that one is not liable merely for adding water until he actually kneads. There is nevertheless a rabbinic prohibition. This prohibition applies to flour, which is not needed that day, but the Sages did not decree concerning bran needed that day to feed an animal or bird; see Beis Yosef there. Taz, paragraph 1, writes that according to this view, one may likewise place water into flour on Shabbos when it is needed that day to feed an animal or bird; see there. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 4, however, challenges Beis Yosef and Taz. He writes that kneading bran does not incur Torah liability but is only rabbinically forbidden and therefore is permitted, whereas kneading flour incurs Torah liability, so water may not be added on Shabbos even when needed; see there. Chemed Moshe, paragraph 1, writes the same. Birkei Yosef, Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1, writes in the name of Mahari Molcho that according to this, kneading applies by Torah law to our bran, in which flour always remains, and adding water is therefore rabbinically forbidden. Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, likewise forbids adding water to our bran because it contains flour. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Mishpatim, paragraph 14, writes the same.
10) There: One may not knead bran, etc. Ramban writes in Milchamos that according to the first view, namely the unqualified ruling of Shulchan Aruch, if one kneads a little at a time it is permitted without changing the method, as stated in siman 321, seif 14; see there. The later authorities cite him. Erekh HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, questions the preceding words of Magen Avraham. Ramban writes specifically that for parched grain and a drink intended for people, kneading a little at a time is permitted even when thoroughly mixed. For an animal, thorough mixing is always forbidden; it is permitted only to pass the implement lengthwise and crosswise and then pour the mixture into another utensil; see there.
11) There: He passes a ladle through it, etc. Rashi explains that one passes the stick once lengthwise and once crosswise. Beis Yosef writes that Rashi appears to permit passing the stick only once in each direction. Rif and Rambam, however, appear to permit passing the ladle lengthwise and crosswise even many times, since one does not mix it with his hand or rotate the implement; see there. Bach writes the same and considers this reasonable. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 4, likewise permits doing so many times. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. With one's hand, however, even one pass lengthwise and crosswise is forbidden. Olas Shabbos there.
12) There: And he places it before each animal, etc. He may place even two utensils before one animal, although during the week he does not give it so much. Beis Yosef; and it appears to me, Eliyah Rabbah there.
13) There: Even a kor or even two kor. This applies specifically when he needs a kor or two. If he does not need this quantity, it is forbidden. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 5. Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, similarly writes that one may not knead more than is needed that day. Eliyah Rabbah there; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 6; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav there.
14) There: Some forbid, etc. This is the view of Semag, Semak, and Sefer HaTerumah, as stated above in paragraph 9. Rema likewise adopts this view in Darkei Moshe, paragraph 1. Bach writes that the stringent view of Sefer HaTerumah should be followed. One who follows it must also take care not to pour the mixture into another utensil, but should shake it thoroughly within the first utensil. These are his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there likewise writes that one should be stringent like this view and that this is the custom. Chayei Adam, klal 19, paragraph 1, writes that we rule stringently because the matter concerns Torah law. Only in pressing circumstances may one permit it through a non-Jew, with the non-Jew adding water on Shabbos and mixing it unusually; see there. We wrote above in siman 13, paragraph 7, that whenever Maran first issues an unqualified permissive ruling and then writes that some forbid, he accounts for the stringent view initially when possible; see there. Independently, we wrote at the end of paragraph 9 that because flour always remains in our bran, kneading applies to it by Torah law and water may not be added on Shabbos even according to the first view; see there.
15) Dry tobacco powder may not have water added and be kneaded by hand, because it resembles bran. Although Maran rules without qualification like those who permit, this permits only adding water; all opinions forbid kneading by hand. In Baghdad, may Heaven protect it, the custom on Shabbos is to place drops of water into the small tobacco container, close it, and strike the container against the palm several times so that the powder turns within and mixes by itself, distributing the drops throughout. This resembles the permission to pour bran from one utensil into another. Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Mishpatim, paragraph 15. This permission consequently appears limited to the unqualified view of Shulchan Aruch. According to those who forbid adding water to bran on Shabbos, it is also forbidden to add water to tobacco. We already wrote in the preceding paragraph that Maran himself accounts for the prohibiting view when possible. Here it is possible to inhale the tobacco without water, so adding water on Shabbos is forbidden even when one does not knead it by hand. Where this custom is already established, one should nevertheless not protest. Every person who fears Heaven should be stringent personally and thereby satisfy all opinions. See above, siman 321, paragraph 69.
