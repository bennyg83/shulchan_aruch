(א) (s.k. 3) And he places, etc. — and it is difficult, for we say in the gemara, etc., thus the gemara daf 156a: Rav Menasya said one measure before one, two before two — fine (Rashi: one measure that they are accustomed to give before an animal — permitted to give before it on Shabbat; and likewise two before two that eat in a trough); three before two — forbidden (Rashi: since on a weekday he would not give them thus, it is unnecessary exertion). Rav Yosef said a kav, even two kavin; Ula said even a kor, even two korim, end of the gemara. And the poskim ruled like Ula, and so ruled Rambam; and Shulchan Aruch quoted Rambam's language. And Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote on Rambam's language that it is like Shulchan Aruch's language — it appears from his words he does not permit kneading more than the animals need; and he wrote his words are reasonable, for even though it is through shinui, nevertheless why permit unnecessary exertion (unlike Ramban). And on this Rabbeinu Yerucham challenged — impossible that Ula who said even a kor and even two korim only permits when a kor or two korim are needed; if so in what does he disagree with Rav Menasya, for Rav Menasya only forbade three before two, but three before three — also for Rav Menasya permitted, and likewise more according to need; therefore Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote Rambam had a different version in the Talmud, see there. And if so this is what R' Yehuda means by "even a kav or two kavin," etc. — behold even four kavin are permitted according to need, and certainly permitted:

(ג) (s.k. 5) Therefore it appears, etc. — rather Rav Menasya, etc., meaning certainly four kavin that are a cow's food for a day are not given at once but divided into several times; and by way of parable, if they give at once one kav — Rav Menasya only permitted to give and knead one kav for one cow; and R' Yehuda added even two kavin even though enough for it in one kav at once — nevertheless they permitted two kavin, but not more, even though it eats four kavin every day; and Ula added to permit even two korim — meaning if there are so many animals that need two korim on Shabbat, permitted to give before them at once all the korim:
