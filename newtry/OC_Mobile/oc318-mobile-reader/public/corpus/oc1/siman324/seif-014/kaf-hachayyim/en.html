Bach writes that the stringent view of Sefer HaTerumah should be followed. One who follows it must also take care not to pour the mixture into another utensil, but should shake it thoroughly within the first utensil. These are his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav there likewise writes that one should be stringent like this view and that this is the custom. Chayei Adam, klal 19, paragraph 1, writes that we rule stringently because the matter concerns Torah law. Only in pressing circumstances may one permit it through a non-Jew, with the non-Jew adding water on Shabbos and mixing it unusually; see there. We wrote above in siman 13, paragraph 7, that whenever Maran first issues an unqualified permissive ruling and then writes that some forbid, he accounts for the stringent view initially when possible; see there. Independently, we wrote at the end of paragraph 9 that because flour always remains in our bran, kneading applies to it by Torah law and water may not be added on Shabbos even according to the first view; see there.
