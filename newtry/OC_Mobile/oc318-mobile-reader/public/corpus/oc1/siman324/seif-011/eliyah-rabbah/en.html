[11] [Levush] And likewise for a dog in one's house, etc. The Gemara implies that even a dog that one does not raise in one's house may be fed, because there is a mitzvah to give it food, as stated by the Magen Avraham. The Levush does not imply this, and the Gemara there says only that it is proper conduct, not that it is a mitzvah. This applies in the field, but not in a city.
[12] Nor food, etc. The Olat Shabbos expressed surprise and concluded as a matter of law that one may place before them food that is not commonly found in the field. This is imprecise, for the Ran already raised this question, as cited by the Beit Yosef, and the Shiltei Giborim also addresses it. There is no distinction. So too rule the Bach and Nachalat Tzvi. The custom of placing wheat before birds on Shabbos Shirah is improper, because their sustenance is not your responsibility, as stated by the Magen Avraham.
[13] Before a pig, etc. This implies that even if one raises it in the house it is prohibited, unless it is a circumstance in which raising it is permitted, as discussed in siman 409.
