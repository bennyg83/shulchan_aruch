[10] [Levush] For all this is tircha, etc. — but in Rambam chapter 21 he wrote the reason lest he come to pounding legumes or kneading flour; further he wrote: a camel may not eat on Shabbat food for three or four days, and one may not make a calf stand and open its mouth and put vetch and water in at once; and likewise one may not give to doves and chickens to a place they cannot return; but he feeds the animal standing and gives it drink standing, or puts water in its mouth by itself and vetch by itself in a place it can return — until here; it implies that in making stand and vetch and water at once — permitted; and likewise making stand and vetch or water — permitted where it can return; and this is not like Levush implies; also in the Talmud [Shabbos] daf 155: he feeds it standing and they put vetch by itself — it appears to me it is "or" and requires study. Rama wrote in responsum siman 79: permitted to tell a gentile to force-feed geese once a day on account of tza'ar ba'alei chayyim, for they cannot eat without forcing — until here; and Magen Avraham wrote siman 301 s.k. 12 that it implies if there is no gentile it is permitted through a Jew on account of tza'ar ba'alei chayyim, and better to do through a minor — until here; and I say it implies there to be stringent through a Jew; also difficult to me is Rama who learned thus from gemara daf 156 that they do not gather in their language — they gather for it through forcing; and difficult: behold he said before "and one may not force" — Rashi explained one may not force; if so why did he not also say they force for it; also the language melaktin and mehalketin the gemara concludes daf 156 that it is to a place it can return; and what Rama challenged — that even if he gathers in his language, the mishnah there permits to a place it can return — it appears to me to resolve: it deals when it is not his, or with coop doves whose sustenance is not upon him; for even so if he does not gather in his language they gather; and understand; therefore one should not be lenient to force through a Jew:
