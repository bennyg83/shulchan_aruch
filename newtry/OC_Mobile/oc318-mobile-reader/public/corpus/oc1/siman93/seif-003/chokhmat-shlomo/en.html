Seif 3 — One does not stand, etc., except out of a settled halachah, etc. Note: I saw in responsum of Terumat HaDeshen (siman 41) that he noted: why did the Sages forbid standing for prayer only out of a settled halachah, yet they did not forbid likewise for going to the bathhouse except out of a settled halachah? And what he wrote to answer: in the bathhouse, since there is no sanctity in it itself, he will not come to ponder — and he brought proof from what is written in the Talmud in the first chapter of Pesachim, that we say: it itself turns upon him to burn it — "from it he eats" — see there. And behold, in Mahakhat Tzedek this proof is astonishing and not comparable — for there it pursues this very thing to burn it, and we are not concerned it will do the opposite of its intent; but here, shall we say he goes to the bathhouse in order not to study? This is certainly not so — he goes there for his pleasure, not so that he should not study; on the contrary, if it were permitted there too he would study. And also, are we dealing with an ignoramus? A Torah scholar knows it is forbidden to ponder during prayer, and more than all thoughts he turns away during prayer than when walking to the bathhouse. But in truth there is no beginning to this question: granted, in prayer — even if some pondering comes to him by compulsion, nevertheless he is nullified from prayer, and what help is there in a claim of compulsion? But in the bathhouse it is explicit in the Talmud in Zevachim and in Shulchan Aruch that on account of compulsion it is permitted; if so, whether way you turn — willingly he certainly will not ponder; and if by compulsion there is no prohibition. And further: in prayer, since he has no recognition that he is standing in prayer at all, therefore we are concerned he will come to ponder and forget he is standing in prayer; but in the bathhouse the bathhouse is before him and he stands naked — if so he does not forget he is in the bathhouse; and if he knows he is in the bathhouse he will certainly remove Torah pondering and banish it from his heart. So it is explained, and it is plain — examine well:
