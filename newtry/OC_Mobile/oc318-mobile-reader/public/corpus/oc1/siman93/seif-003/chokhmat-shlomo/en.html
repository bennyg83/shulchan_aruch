Seif 3: One may not stand, etc., except out of a matter of the heart that is settled halacha, etc. Note: I saw in the responsa of Terumat HaDeshen (siman 41) that he wondered why in prayer the Sages forbade standing except out of settled halacha, yet we do not forbid likewise for the bathhouse that one should go only out of settled halacha. And what he wrote to answer: in the bathhouse, since there is no sanctity in it, he himself will not come to contemplate; and he brought proof from what is stated in the Gemara, Pesachim chapter 1, that we say he himself regrets [eating from the sacrifice] — is he eating from it? See there. And behold, in my humble opinion this proof is astonishing and is not comparable: eating is different, for there he regrets this very thing to burn it, and we are not concerned that he will do the opposite of his intention; but here, does one go to the bathhouse in order not to study? Certainly not — he goes there for pleasure, and not so that he would not reflect; on the contrary, if it were permitted there too he would reflect. And also, are we dealing with an ignoramus? Behold, a Torah scholar knows it is forbidden to reflect during prayer, and he turns away more from all his thoughts during prayer than when walking to the bathhouse. But in truth there is no beginning to this question: granted, in prayer, even if some reflection comes to him by force, nevertheless it is nullified from the prayer — and what help is there in this a forced claim; but in the bathhouse it is explicit in the Gemara Zevachim and in the Shulchan Aruch that due to force it is permitted — if so, necessarily when willing he certainly will not reflect; and if by force there is no prohibition in the matter. And further, in prayer, since he has no awareness that he is standing in prayer at all, therefore we are concerned he will come to reflect and forget that he is standing in prayer; but in the bathhouse, behold the bath is before him and he stands naked — if so, he has not forgotten he is in the bath; and if he knows he is in the bath he will certainly remove the reflection by Torah law and remove it from his heart. And this is sufficient, and it is plain — examine well.
