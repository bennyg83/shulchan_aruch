(There) Seif 4: One engaged in communal needs is considered as one engaged in Torah, etc. Note: From the words of Tosafos in Sanhedrin [17a] on the passage "and they are worn out," that they wrote there on what we say in the verse "Adonai Moshe, k'lalom" — that they said to him, "Adonai Moshe, cast upon them the communal needs and they are worn out by themselves"; and Tosafos wrote there: the meaning of "their prophecy ended" — for the Divine Presence does not dwell amid sadness but amid joy — see there. If so, it is explicit from them that involvement in communal needs is considered sadness and not joy; and necessarily he did not hold like the poskim's explanation that it is joy for him, but held like the second explanation per the view of R' Yehuda Isserles that the Shulchan Aruch brought on this — examine. See in Magen Avraham note 5 what he agreed with the Derishah, not like the Taz that even oness is considered exempt; and see in my composition L'Orach Chayim from the year 5699, Laws of Rosh Hashanah siman 585, what I proved from the words of the Rambam, Laws of Disqualified Offerings chapter 3 halacha 25 — there I proved that the Rambam holds that oness is not considered exempt but remains in his obligation, like the view of the Taz — see there; and see in my composition L'Torah from the year 5511, parashat Va'era, what I also wrote thus — see there, and examine well.
