There, seif 4. One who is occupied with communal needs is considered as one occupied with Torah, etc. Note: From the words of Tosafos in Sanhedrin 17a, s.v. vehem kalim, concerning the verse, “My master Moshe, restrain them,” where they explain that Moshe was told to impose communal responsibilities upon them and they would cease of themselves, and Tosafos explain that their prophecy would cease because the Shechinah rests only amid joy and not amid sadness, it follows explicitly that involvement in communal needs is regarded as sadness rather than joy. Necessarily, they do not accept the explanation of the poskim that such involvement brings him joy; rather, they accept the second explanation, according to the view of the “others say” cited by the Shulchan Aruch here. Examine this carefully. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 5, who agrees with the Derishah, contrary to the Taz, that even one prevented by circumstances beyond his control is considered exempt. See also my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5609, Hilchos Rosh Hashanah, siman 586, where I proved from the Rambam, Hilchos Pesulei HaMukdashin 3:25, that the Rambam holds that one prevented by circumstances beyond his control is not considered exempt, but remains obligated, in accordance with the Taz. See there. See also my Torah commentary from the year 5610, Parshas Vaera, where I wrote the same. Examine this carefully.
