He should wait one hour, etc. The Tur, however, writes that he should wait a short while. One must therefore say that only the early pious men would wait a full hour, whereas for us a short while suffices. So write the Kesef Mishneh in chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, the Lechem Chamudos, folio 40, and the Lechem Mishneh. The same law applies after prayer. The Lechem Mishneh there infers from the Tur and Rambam that specifically sitting for a short while is required; see there. In my humble opinion, however, the Rambam in chapter 11 of Hilchos Tefillah and the Tur in siman 151 indicate that the term “sitting” means remaining, and even remaining while standing is included. The Bach writes likewise. It is nevertheless possible to distinguish based on the reason: Here, actual sitting is required because sitting settles his mind more and enables him to direct his thoughts. After prayer, sitting demonstrates that prayer is not a burden upon him, unlike merely remaining while standing. There, however, the reason is that he should perform a mitzvah in the synagogue, and remaining while standing also constitutes a mitzvah. The Seder HaYom writes that if he can remain until fewer than ten people are left in the synagogue, it is good for him to do so, so that he will not be the one who causes the Shechinah to depart. He has thereby remained until there is no longer a place for the Shechinah to rest. Furthermore, sitting in the synagogue is itself a mitzvah.
