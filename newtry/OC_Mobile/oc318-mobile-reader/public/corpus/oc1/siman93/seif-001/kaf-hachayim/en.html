(1) [Seif 1] One should wait one hour, etc. — Thus it is in tractate Berachos, beginning of chapter Ein Omadin, page 30b: the early pious ones would wait one hour and pray; and there on page 32b they said one who prays must wait one hour after his prayer, etc. — see there. And so too the Rambam z"l, chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah, law 16, except that he wrote there that before this one must sit a little before prayer to direct his heart, etc., and a little after prayer. And Maran z"l wrote in Kesef Mishnah on the Rambam's words cited: although it is impossible to wait one hour like the early pious ones, nevertheless one must sit a little — so far. And so too Lechem Mishneh there, that for us it suffices that we learn from them for a short time — see there. And likewise the Perishah explained letter 1 on the Tur's words, and wrote that the measure of "a little" is the measure of two doorways, which is the time to walk eight handbreadths, as written in siman 90 seif 20 — see there. And so too Magen Avraham note 1; Eliyah Rabbah letter 1; Rabbeinu Zalman letter 1; Chayei Adam, general rules 22, letter 2, 38; chapter referenced letter 37; and see there what he wondered about the Shulchan Aruch that it cited one hour, and further wrote that the waiting should be seated and not standing — see there. And so too Eliyah Rabbah letter 1; Nishmas Shabbat letter 1; Yafeh Lelev letter 1; and he wrote there in HaNe'im Shimcha: "Indeed, from the plain sense of Maran the Shulchan Aruch z"l it does not imply thus, but he holds that one needs an actual hour and makes no distinction between pious and other people"; and Semag and Terumat HaDeshen too did not distinguish between pious and other people. And their reason appears to me from what we say in the gemara there, page 32b: R' Yehoshua ben Levi said, one who prays must wait, etc.; and it is also stated there in a baraita: one who prays must wait one hour before his prayer and one hour after his prayer, etc. — and there it was not mentioned that the pious did so, but they said "must" generically, implying that every person must. And so too is the view of the Rif z"l and the Rosh z"l, who brought this statement of R' Yehoshua ben Levi who said "must" — after that of the early pious ones — to say that every person must; and likewise the Tur's words plainly in the abridged rulings of the Rosh, siman 11 — so far. And further see there what was written to reject the words of Lechem Chamudos on the Rambam chapter 4 of Hilchos Tefillah who wrote one hour — that the Shulchan Aruch did not mean literally but meant a little. And he wrote: nevertheless, from the words of Birkei Yosef letter 1, who wrote: "For most of the world it suffices to wait a little," and likewise Maharam Rabbi Yishai — although Maran's view z"l is brought in Merkavat HaMishnah — so far — see there. And see Rabbeinu Zalman there; and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch on the laws of prayer conduct, and they brought Yafeh Lelev there. Nevertheless, since there are those who interpret Maran's words z"l that he literally means one hour for every person, everyone may be stringent where possible — that is, one who can should come to study in the place where he prays, like one hour before Minchah and Maariv prayer; and through this he gains doubly by setting times for Torah and also waiting an hour before he prays. And at Shacharit, in locales where they have before prayer the order of korbanot and pesukei dezimra and Yotzer, which one waits in them like one hour; and after prayer too they say Ashrei, etc.; and likewise Rishon LeTzion wrote that this helps for one hour before, etc.; and they brought Eliyah Rabbah in Maadanei Yom Tov letter 1.
