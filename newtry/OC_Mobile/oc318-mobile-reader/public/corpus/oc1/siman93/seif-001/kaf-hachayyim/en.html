(a) [Seif 1] He waits one hour, etc. — so it is in tractate Berachot at the beginning of chapter Ein Omedin, page 30b: the early pious ones would wait one hour and pray; and there on page 32b they said: one who prays must wait one hour after his prayer, etc. — see there. And likewise Rambam z"l chapter 4 of Laws of Prayer halachah 16 — except that he wrote there before this one must sit a little before prayer in order to direct his heart, etc., and a little after prayer. And Maran z"l wrote in Kesef Mishneh on Rambam's words: although one cannot wait a full hour like the early pious ones, nevertheless one must sit a little — end quote. And likewise in Lehem Chamudos there: for us it suffices to learn from them for a short time — see there. And Perishah ot 1 explained the Tur's words and wrote that "short time" means the measure of two doorways, which is the time to walk eight tefachim, as written in siman 90 seif 20 — see there. And likewise Magen Avraham s.k. 1, Eshel Avraham ot 1, Rav Zundel ot 1, Chayei Adam klal 22 ot 2, Lev Chaim in the aforementioned chapter ot 37; and see there what he wondered on Shulchan Aruch that it cited one hour, and wrote further that the waiting should be in sitting and not in standing — see there. And likewise Ateret Tzvi ot 1, Nitzotzot ot 1, Yafeh Lelev ot 1; and Nitzotzot wrote: however, from the plain meaning of Maran Shulchan Aruch z"l it does not imply thus, but he holds one needs a full hour and makes no distinction between pious ones and others; and Semag and Terumat HaDeshen also did not distinguish between pious ones and others, and their reason appears from what we say in the gemara there page 32b: Ribi Levi said one who prays must wait, etc.; and it is also taught there: one who prays must wait one hour before his prayer and one hour after his prayer, etc., and it was not mentioned there that the pious ones did so, but they said "one must" — implying every person must. And so too appears to be the view of Rif z"l and Rosh z"l, who brought this statement of Ribi Levi who said "one must" after that of the early pious ones, to say every person must; and so are Rosh's words in brief in Piskei HaRosh siman 11 — end quote. And see further there what he wrote to reject the words of Lev Chaim on Rambam chapter 4 of Laws of Prayer that wrote one hour — that Shulchan Aruch did not mean literally but "a little"; and he wrote that Kesef Mishneh from Birkei Yosef ot 1 wrote: for most of the world it suffices to wait a little, and likewise Mahar Yeshaya Almoli — Maran's view is brought in Merkavat HaMishnah — end quote — see there. And see Rav Zundel there. And Kaf HaChayyim in laws of prayer conduct brought it. And nevertheless, since there are interpreters of Maran's words that he means a full hour, and everyone may be stringent where possible — namely, one who can should come to study in the place he prays about one hour before Minchah and Maariv prayer, and thereby gains doubly: he establishes times for Torah and also waits an hour before praying. And in Shacharit on weekdays, before prayer there is the order of korbanot and Pesukei DeZimra and Yotzer, in which one waits about one hour; and after prayer too they say Ashrei, etc. And likewise Rishon LeTzion: this helps for one hour before, etc., and Eliyah Rabbah brought it in Mishna Berurah ot 1:
