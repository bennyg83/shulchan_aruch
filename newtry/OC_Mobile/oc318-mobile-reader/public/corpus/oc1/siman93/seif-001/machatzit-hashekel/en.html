(note 1) One hour. Terumat HaDeshen proved from the gemara, etc., for we say (Berachos 32b): The early pious ones would wait one hour and pray one hour and return and wait one hour; and it challenges: Since they wait nine hours a day in prayer, how is their Torah preserved and how is their work done? And it answers: Rather, because they are pious, their Torah is preserved and their work is blessed — so far. And it is impossible that one hour is not literal; there is no place for the challenger's difficulty. And the challenger proved that therefore the hour is literal, just as what is taught, "and pray one hour," is literal; so too the hour before prayer and that after prayer are also literal.

And for the rest of the people a short time suffices, since the rest of the people are not pious; if they were to wait nine hours in their prayer, how would their Torah be preserved and how would their work be done? For it is impossible for them to rely on their deeds that a miracle will be done for them.

As written in siman 90, end of note 20, so it should read — and the meaning is that there, "there are those who say" in the gemara that one should enter before prayer two doorways — meaning that he should not pray immediately upon his entry, but rather wait before prayer the measure of two doorways; behold, a short time before prayer suffices; and the same applies after prayer.

And even though "there are those who say" two doorways in another matter, as written there — nevertheless, regarding the measure of waiting, which does not require a literal hour, no new dispute was introduced between them.

And see Levush, siman 71, seif 37, that the waiting before prayer and after prayer should be seated. Before prayer — through sitting one focuses one's mind more to direct toward the Omnipresent; and after prayer — through sitting it appears more that the prayer is not a burden upon him. And in Sefer Eliyah Rabbah he disagrees.

As written in the Zohar, etc. — see above in Magen Avraham, remez 46.
