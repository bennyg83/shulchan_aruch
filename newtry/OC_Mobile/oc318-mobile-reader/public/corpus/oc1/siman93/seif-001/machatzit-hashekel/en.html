Seif katan 1: One hour. Rabbeinu Yonah proved this from the Gemara, etc. The Gemara on 32b states: The early pious men would wait one hour, pray for one hour, and then remain for another hour. It asks: Since they spent nine hours each day in prayer, how was their Torah retained and how was their work accomplished? It answers: Because they were pious, their Torah was retained and their work was blessed. If “one hour” were not precise, there would be no basis for the question. The questioner proved that it must mean an actual hour, just as the statement that they prayed for one hour is precise. The same therefore applies to the hour before prayer and the hour after prayer.
For ordinary people, a short while suffices. Since ordinary people are not pious, if they were to spend nine hours in prayer, how would their Torah be retained and how would their work be accomplished? They cannot rely upon their deeds to merit a miracle.
The correct text is: “as written at the end of seif katan 20 of siman 90.” Meaning, one explanation there of the Gemara’s statement that a person should enter two doorways before prayer is that he should not pray immediately upon entering, but should wait before prayer for the amount of time needed to traverse two doorways. This shows that a short time before prayer suffices, and the same applies after prayer.
Although others explain “two doorways” differently, as stated there, we should nevertheless not create a dispute between those explanations regarding the required waiting time by asserting that an actual hour is necessary.
See Lechem Chamudos, siman 71, seif 37, who writes that the waiting before and after prayer should be while seated. Before prayer, sitting settles his mind and enables him to direct his thoughts toward Hashem. After prayer, sitting makes it more evident that prayer is not a burden upon him. Eliyah Rabbah disagrees.
