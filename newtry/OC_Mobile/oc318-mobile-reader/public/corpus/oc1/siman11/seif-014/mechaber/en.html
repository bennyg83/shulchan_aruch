One takes four strands from this side and four from that side and ties double knots one atop the other; then one winds the long strand around the seven [others] some windings and ties double knots again; repeat until five double knots and four spaces between filled with windings — no set measure for windings except that all winding and knots span four thumbbreadths width and the free “sprouts” [anaf] eight thumbbreadths. Rema: If the tzitzis is long, see that one-third is gdil and two-thirds anaf (Rambam ch. 1). The custom is to wind in the first space seven windings, in the second nine, in the third eleven, in the fourth thirteen — totaling forty, like “Hashem Echad”; twenty-nine [windings] plus the Divine Name make forty. At each strand’s end they tie a knot so it holds in the twist.
