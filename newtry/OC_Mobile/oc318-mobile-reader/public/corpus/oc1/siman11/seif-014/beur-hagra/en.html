Seif 14, "he should take four threads," etc. The Rosh and Mordechai: in this way all the threads endure better, unlike Rabbenu Tam and Ri, who would make the knots with the wrapping thread. So they proved there in Tosafos 38b, gloss beginning "kedei," etc.

"And he should tie." To account for the second explanation that Tosafos wrote in Rashi's name, 39a, gloss beginning "kesher."

"Twice." For one knot is not a lasting knot and is not a knot. Mordechai. See siman 316.

"The long thread." There: the wrapping thread, etc.

"And he ties," etc. 38b: learn from this that it requires, etc. Although it rejects it, that is only a rejection, and we do not reject Rabbah's statement because of this. This is what he wrote: perhaps, etc., as the commentators wrote.

"And so he should do," etc. There, 39a: the tanna taught, one who reduces, etc.; the tanna taught, when he, etc. Tosafos there, gloss beginning "lo," wrote that for every two coils, one white and one techeles, there was one knot, and at the end one knot, making five knots. The Baal HaIttur wrote that each coil had a knot, but we make the knots of white alone.

"There is no measure," etc. Only regarding techeles did they say one who reduces, etc., and the measure of a coil there. But with white one need not be precise about the number of coils, for their count corresponds to the heavens, as stated there, because techeles resembles the firmament; this does not apply to white. Tosafos there and other decisors. Nevertheless we do it as a remembrance, as with techeles. Tosafos and Rambam.

"Only," etc. The Rambam's language: he should intend that the wrapped section be one third and the loose strands two thirds. Here he wrote according to Tosafos' explanation in seif 4, and it is from the words of the Rosh and Tur.

"And the knots." For they are included in the count of the gedil; Mordechai in the name of Ri, siman 940, and the Rosh.

"And if he lengthened," etc. From the Rambam's above words, and as stated in the Gemara: the beauty of techeles, etc.

"They are accustomed," etc. Tosafos there wrote: some explain "one who reduces," etc., as referring to the number of wrappings; therefore in the first section there are seven, and they increase by two until the last has thirteen. But the later authorities wrote that all together there are thirty-nine, corresponding to the numerical value of the Name; for some explain "one who reduces," etc., as referring to the number of coils.
