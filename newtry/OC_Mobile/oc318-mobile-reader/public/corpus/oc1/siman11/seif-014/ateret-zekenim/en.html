The custom is to make seven windings in the first space. Gloss: In the tzitzit of Rabbi Shlomo Molcho there were five windings in the first space, six in the second, five in the third, and ten in the fourth, corresponding to the numerical value of the Divine Name. Kanfei Yonah writes likewise. Two holes should be made specifically in a tallit katan, but not in a tallit gadol, so that one should not be among those whose conduct appears strange. Under one's garments, however, one should make two holes, since it is covered. The two holes should be adjacent to one another, like the vowel tzerei, and not one above the other, like the vowel sheva. Rabbi Yitzchak Luria, according to the kabbalistic truth. In the second space one should make eight windings, as it is written, 'Your dew is a dew of lights.' The numerical value of orot equals that of tzitzit together with the eight threads and five knots; meaning a total of 39 windings for the tzitzit, as explained above.
