A non-Jew. Regarding the spinning of a deaf-mute, an insane person, or a minor, whether it is effective when an adult Jew stands over them and teaches them to make it for its required purpose, see siman 460 in the Beit Yosef, where there is a dispute about this. See Biur Halachah, where we explained the consensus of the later authorities in this matter.
Standing over him. See Biur Halachah, where we wrote that this applies specifically if the spinning does not continue for so long. But if the spinning continues for a long time, then it appears that even according to the Rosh it does not help that he instructed the non-Jew at the beginning of the spinning to do it for its required purpose. All the more so if he paused in the spinning and afterward, at a later time, began again to spin, where certainly all agree that he acts with his own intention and it is invalid.
Invalid. He holds that a non-Jew acts with his own intention and does not obey what the Jew commands him to do for its required purpose.
According to the Rosh, it is valid. In pressing circumstances, when he has no Jew to spin it for its required purpose, one may rely on this. But otherwise one should not, because in the commentary of the Vilna Gaon he wrote that the opinion of Tosafos and the Mordechai is also like the Rambam.
And the custom is that he assists, etc. All this is according to the Rosh, but according to the Rambam none of this helps. See the later authorities, who conclude that this custom is for the best practice, but after the fact it is valid even without any assistance at all according to the Rosh, since the Jew stands over him and instructs him to make it for its required purpose. If the Jew is not standing over him to command him, the assistance does not help according to all views, for we hold that assisting has no substance.
And they require. This refers to ordinary tzitzis strings, not to those spun by a non-Jew, for there, according to those who invalidate, it does not help even if a Jew later twisted them for their required purpose.
Twisting. This means that after the spinning he should double them into two and twist them. If he doubled each string into eight and twisted it, we have no objection. The reason for twisting is that it was taught in the Sifrei: and they shall make for themselves tzitzis. I might understand that he makes tzitzis as it is; therefore the verse states, and they shall place on the tzitzis of the corner a thread of techeiles, meaning spun and twisted, as in the Mishkan, which was twisted, or alternatively because an ordinary thread is twisted. I have this only for techeiles; from where do I know white? The Torah said, place techeiles and place white; just as techeiles is spun and twisted, so too white is spun and twisted. See Biur Halachah.
For their required purpose. From spinning onward, everything is included in making, for we hold that it must be for its required purpose. Magen Avraham wrote that even after the fact it is indispensable if they were not twisted for their required purpose, and this is also implied by the commentary of the Vilna Gaon. Some are lenient after the fact even without twisting. See Biur Halachah, where we explained that one should not rely on this. However, if the twisting was done without explicit specification, one may be lenient after the fact, since the spinning was for its required purpose and anyone who acts does so according to his original intention.
