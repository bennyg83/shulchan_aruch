A non-Jew — and spinning of cheresh, shoteh, and katan, whether it helps when an adult Jew stands over them and teaches them to do it lishmah — see in Siman 460 in Beit Yosef that there is a dispute in this; and see Biur Halacha where we explained the agreement of the later authorities on this.

(9) Stands over him — see in Biur Halacha where we wrote that this applies specifically when the spinning does not continue for so long; but if the spinning continued for a long time, then it appears that even per the Rosh it does not help that which he taught the non-Jew at the beginning of the spinning to do it for its sake. And certainly if he stopped in the spinning and afterward, at another time, began again to spin — for certain everyone agrees that he acted for his own intent and it is invalid.

(10) Invalid — he holds that the non-Jew acts for his own intent and does not heed what the Jew commands him, that he should do it for its sake.

(11) Per the Rosh it is valid — and in a time of constraint, when he has no Jew to spin it for its sake, one may rely on this; but otherwise not, for in the Biur HaGra he wrote that the view of Tosafos and the Mordechai is also like the Rambam.

(12) And the custom is that he should assist, etc. — and all this is per the Rosh; but per the Rambam none of this helps. And see in the later authorities who concluded that this custom is on the side of the better way, but after the fact it is valid even without any assistance at all per the Rosh, since the Jew stands over him and instructs him to do it for its sake. And if the Jew is not standing over him to command him, the assistance does not help for all opinions, for we hold that one who assists has no substance.

(13) And they require — this refers to tzitzit strings in general, and not to spinning by a non-Jew, for there it does not help to invalidate according to those who invalidate even if a Jew afterward interwove them for their sake.

(14) Interweaving — namely, that one doubles them after spinning into two and interweaves them; and if one doubled each strand into eight and interwove them as well, we have no issue with it. And the reason for interweaving is that it was taught in the Sifri: "and they shall make for themselves fringes" — I would understand that they should make fringes as they are; therefore the verse teaches, "and they shall place on the fringe of the corner a cord of tekhelet, spun and interwoven" — meaning like the Mishkan, which was interwoven, or alternatively that an unspecified cord is interwoven. I have only tekhelet; from where do we know white? The Torah said: give tekhelet, give white — just as tekhelet requires spinning and interweaving, so too white requires spinning and interweaving. And see in Biur Halacha.

(15) For their sake — for from spinning onward it is all in the category of making, and we hold that it requires being done for its sake. And Magen Avraham wrote that even after the fact it prevents if they were not interwoven for their sake, and so it is implied from the Biur HaGra. And there are those who are lenient after the fact even without interweaving; and see in Biur Halacha where we explained that one should not rely on this. But if the interweaving was done without specification, one may be lenient after the fact, since the spinning was for its sake, and whoever does an act does it per the first act.
