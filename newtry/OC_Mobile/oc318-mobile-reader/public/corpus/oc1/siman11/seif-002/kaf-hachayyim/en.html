5) [Seif 2] A non-Jew spun them while a Jew supervised him, etc. If a non-Jew spun them and no Jew supervised him, however, they are invalid, and twisting them for the sake of the mitzvah does not validate them. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of the Rosh, unlike Baal HaIttur.
6) There. “According to Rambam it is invalid, and according to the Rosh it is valid.” Shulchan Gavoah, entry 4, writes: “Under the general rule that when one opinion is followed by a second opinion, the halachah follows the latter, as stated in the rules of the Shulchan Aruch, here Rabbeinu's view inclines toward the Rosh's ruling that it is valid”; this concludes his words. See below, siman 13, entry 7, and the following entry.
7) There, in Rama's gloss. “The custom is for the Jew to assist slightly,” etc. If the Jew did not assist but merely told the non-Jew to perform the work for the sake of the mitzvah, whether it can be validated after the fact is discussed in my sacred work Kol Yaakov, siman 271, entry 10, regarding tanning, where a dispute is recorded. Maran derived the present law from tanning; see Beit Yosef. Maran's position there in Beit Yosef, Yoreh De'ah, siman 271, is that if the Jew did not assist, it is invalid. Shulchan Gavoah there, entry 11, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav here, entry 4, rule likewise. See Eliyah Rabbah, entry 1. Shulchan Aruch HaRav writes in Kuntres Acharon, entry 1, that even according to Bach, who holds there that in the Rosh's view it is valid even if the Jew did not assist at all, spinning tzitzis is different. Since all the spinning must be for the sake of the mitzvah, it resembles writing a get, regarding which the Rosh concedes that a non-Jew acts according to his own intent. Bach therefore rules here in siman 14 that without the Jew's assistance it is ineffective even after the fact; see there. This also resolves Eliyah Rabbah's difficulty there against Bach and Dagul Merevavah's difficulty against Maran.
8) There, in Rama's gloss. “The custom is for the Jew to assist slightly,” etc. Chayei Adam, rule 11, entry 13, writes: “In pressing circumstances, when no one is available to spin the threads for the sake of the mitzvah, he should tell a non-Jew to spin threads for him for the sake of tzitzis. The Jew should also assist slightly in the spinning, stand beside the spinning, and intend that it be for the sake of tzitzis”; this concludes his words. I write likewise in my sacred work Kol Yaakov there, entry 11, concerning tanning: one should do this only when no Jewish tanners are available; see there.
9) If a deaf-mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor spun them while an adult supervised: Magen Avraham, se'if katan 2, writes that this depends upon the dispute recorded by Beit Yosef in siman 460. Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 1, however, writes that there is greater room for leniency here. There, Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification that tanning by a non-Jew under Jewish supervision is invalid, whereas here he records two opinions. Rama's view also appears to be so; this concludes his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 4, likewise validates it; see Kuntres Acharon, entry 2. It appears that it should be validated only in pressing circumstances, when an adult supervises and assists, as Chayei Adam writes regarding a non-Jew, since Maran there compares the two cases.
10) There. “And they require twisting.” Be'er Esek, siman 51, cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, entry 2, writes that one should not protest against a person who makes the tzitzis threads as a thick cord; first twisting two strands, and afterward making a thick cord from four, so that each resulting thread contains eight fine strands and each corner contains sixty-four. Rokeach, however, writes that the preferred fulfillment is to make each thread twisted from eight strands. Amudei HaShulchan on Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 70, entry 17, cites him. Rema MiPano writes likewise, as Kitzur Shelah cites in Hilkhot Tzitzis. Kitzur Shelah writes that according to Kabbalah it is also proper to do so. Shemesh Tzedakah, Orach Chayyim, siman 22, writes likewise, as does Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, entry 4, in the name of one holy authority. Lev Chayyim, volume 1, siman 86, likewise writes: “The preferred fulfillment is for every tzitzis thread first to be spun as one strand, then for two strands to be twisted together, then the two doubled into four and the four into eight. At the very least, one should take four spun strands, twist them into two, and thereby produce eight; there is no concern whatever with this. Nevertheless, if one found only threads twisted from two or four strands, he fulfills his obligation, and this is clear”; this concludes his words. Ben Ish Chai, parashat Noach, entry 10, writes likewise.
11) There. “They must be twisted for the sake of the mitzvah.” If they were not twisted for its sake, they are invalid even after the fact. See Magen Avraham, se'if katan 3. Olat Tamid, entry 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, entry 3; Chayei Adam, rule 11, entry 14; and Chesed LaAlafim, entry 2, imply likewise. Mor U'Ketziah, however, writes that in pressing circumstances they are valid and one may bless over them even if they were not twisted at all; see there. Nachalat Tzvi rules likewise, as cited by Eliyah Rabbah, entry 2, and Eliyah Rabbah there agrees. Machazik Berachah, entry 2, writes that one may rely upon them when no others are available and it is a pressing circumstance; Shaarei Teshuvah, entry 2, cites him. Lev Chayyim, volume 1, siman 85, however, discusses this at length and concludes: “It therefore appears to me as a matter of practical halachah that if the threads were not twisted, one should not wear such a tallis. It is invalid according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak and the consensus of the later authorities, including Olat Tamid, Magen Avraham, and Peri Tzaddik and those who agree with them. He would consequently violate a positive Torah commandment. If he does not wear it, by contrast, he violates nothing, because we rule that tzitzis is a personal obligation: if one wears a four-cornered garment, it must have tzitzis in accordance with halachah. This is unlike Machazik Berachah's statement that one may rely upon the lenient authorities in pressing circumstances when no other tallis is available; see there. After due respect, it does not appear proper to be lenient, for the reason I have explained. The same applies if they were not twisted for the sake of the mitzvah, in accordance with Magen Avraham, Olat Tamid, and Peri Tzaddik, as stated”; this concludes his words.
