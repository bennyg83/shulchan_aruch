"Gentiles spin," etc. — and regarding spinning by a deaf-mute, imbecile, or minor — see siman 460 regarding making matzah, where there is a dispute among the poskim; so too in our matter, and it emerges there for the law per the agreement of the later authorities [namely Taz, Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and Chayei Adam] that if it is impossible in another matter, it is permitted with one standing over him, even through a gentile — and all the more through a deaf-mute, imbecile, or minor. But lekatchilah one should be stringent not to do it through a gentile or deaf-mute, imbecile, or minor, even with one standing over him [a deaf person is one who does not hear and does not speak; an imbecile — see Yoreh De'ah siman 1; a minor is until he becomes bar mitzvah, and a minor girl until age twelve (Eliyah Rabbah, Chayei Adam, and Peri Megadim there, unlike Taz)]. Without one standing over him it is invalid in all these cases, even after the fact. See responsum Meshkenot Yaakov [siman 21], who proved from the words of Tosafos that standing over him is not called "saying to him" once before the act, but one must warn him at all times not to divert his attention at all; but whoever does not stand over him and constantly warn him — according to all he has no intent at all. From all this we learn that those accustomed to give little girls to spin tzitzis and rely on this that they tell them to spin for its sake — it helped nothing, and the tzitzis are invalid biblically according to this law — see there — and so it appears from the words of Chayei Adam — see there. And know further: regarding spinning for its sake, since it is a matter of Torah, it is proved from the words of Peri Megadim there in siman 460 that one should not give to spin even to a boy of thirteen or a girl of twelve if we do not know that they brought two hairs. But after the fact it seems to me one may be lenient, for we rely on Rava's presumption [see siman 39 in responsa that he was lenient in the name of Noda B'Yehuda — see there in Biur Halacha]. And when an adult stands over them and teaches them to do for its sake — it seems one may be lenient in this lekatchilah.**** END BLOCK ****

"And an Israelite stands over him," etc. — see Beis Yosef, who learned this from the law of tanning, which the Rosh validated with an Israelite standing over him. See Dagul MeRevavah and the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger and other later authorities, who wondered at Beis Yosef: behold the Rosh's reason for distinguishing between a bill of divorce and circumcision and tanning is that in a get we require the entire essential text to be for its sake, which a gentile will not do; but in tanning we require only at the beginning of tanning, when he places the hides in the lime, that he then say "I am doing this for the sake of a Sefer Torah," and the gentile will do for the Israelite's sake when told "do thus." So too circumcision, since in a moment he acts — he will surely act with the Israelite's intent as told. So too the view of Baal HaIttur that a gentile suffices if he tans for their sake — so wrote the Rosh in Laws of Sefer Torah. If so, this leniency does not apply to spinning tzitzis, for each act of spinning is a separate matter. And it is even more astonishing to me regarding Beis Yosef: behold the Rosh brought proof from Baal HaIttur, who holds like him — yet the Ittur itself rules regarding spinning that it does not help through a gentile even if an Israelite stands over him, and he learned this from a get — see there in his words in Laws of Tzitzis, where he wrote so explicitly; thus he disagrees with Beis Yosef, who equates spinning to tanning. However, from the words of the Gra in his explanation, who showed the source of Shulchan Aruch that wrote that per the Rosh it is valid — in his citation: "and the Rosh wrote in Laws of Sefer Torah, and the reason is that it is not comparable to a get, where there we require the entire essential text for its sake, unlike circumcision, Sefer Torah, tzitzis, and the like, which require only the beginning, since in a moment he acts with the Israelite's intent" — end quote. In truth the Rosh is not mentioned at all regarding tzitzis; it implies he holds that what he wrote regarding circumcision, "since in a moment," is not precise, but rather in a matter that does not last long, like a get, he acts with the Israelite's intent — thus spinning tzitzis for a tallis, which lasts only moments, also helps with one standing over him [and what the Gra wrote "only at the beginning" means: since spinning does not last long, the intent he thought at the beginning of spinning suffices, for he has not yet diverted from that intent — unlike a get]. If so, if spinning lasts a long time, it seems according to all it does not help with one standing over him. And one could say he should tell the gentile again to do for its sake — and again. If so, even a get would become valid in this way, and the Mishnah states anonymously — and therefore he did not heed him in this way — so too with spinning. The result: Shulchan Aruch did not leniently rule like the Rosh regarding standing over him except when spinning does not last long, as he wrote above "spin me tzitzis for a tallit" — though there that wording is not precise, for the same applies if she spins for him all day. Nevertheless we see Shulchan Aruch does not deal with spinning that lasts all day, as in our time in factories. Therefore one may somewhat resolve the great question above from Baal HaIttur — perhaps he deals with what lasts a long time.**** END BLOCK ****

"And they require twisting" — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote that one should double them into two — so it is proved from the conclusion of the Ran's language at the beginning of Beitzah, and so from the Gemara's language in Eruvin 96b. And perhaps with the intent of the border of the cloak he doubled them — meaning he doubled them, as is known. See Taz in small paragraph 2, who explained on what the Sifrei teaches: "a thread of techeles, spun and twisted, like a kind of thread" [and this is from Hagahos Maimoniyot]. Apparently one could question this from Yoma 72a in the Gemara: it teaches "thread, threads" — behold here are four — see in Rashi there and Tosafos that it is through cutting. And if it were through doubling and twisting into two, there would be eight threads — unless we say he holds twisting suffices even without doubling the thread, and that too is included in twisting — and this is a novel point. However, according to what is implied in the Ra'avad there, that they had the version in the Sifrei that we derive techeles from the Mishkan where there was twisting — everything is clear: an ordinary thread is not twisted. In any case, for all poskim it suffices that it be twisted into two. See Aruch HaShulchan, who brought an opinion that for the mitzvah in the optimal way it is good that every thread be doubled to eight. However, I truly wonder about the meticulous who buy specifically doubled to eight, which in any case is only for the mitzvah in the optimal way according to all — is it not more fitting for them lekatchilah to be meticulous with the tzitzis to know if they are spun and twisted for its sake per the law through an Israelite? For there are places that rely on spinning tzitzis through a gentile at an Israelite's command — and such is a leniency. See in this siman in Mishna Berurah, small paragraphs 9 and 10. I was uncertain: if they were twisted into two not for its sake and afterward twisted them into eight for its sake, or vice versa — do we follow the later twisting or the earlier? "For its sake" — see Mishna Berurah; this is from the words of the Gra — if so it implies it is biblical; and Peri Megadim sides that it is biblical. Bach, Nekudas HaKesef, and Eliyah Rabbah are lenient after the fact even without twisting, but one should not rely on this, for the majority disagree, for behold Maharan, Nimukei Yosef, and Shulchan Aruch seif 3 prove explicitly that twisting is indispensable; so wrote Eretz Tzvi explicitly in seif 3; so it is proved from Chayei Adam, Da'as HaKadosh, Sha'arei Shalom, and Aruch HaShulchan [and what Eliyah Rabbah wrote to reject Eretz Tzvi's proof — one who investigates Nimukei Yosef and Beis Yosef's words will see explicitly that the law is with Eretz Tzvi — see there; Peri Megadim already challenged him with several valid proofs; but what Peri Megadim proved from the Levush that he also holds like Nekudas HaKesef — one who investigates Beis Yosef and Levush will see the opposite, for the agreed view wrote anonymously and the rejected view in the name of an individual, as is known]. If they were twisted but not for its sake — one should investigate the matter, for although from Eretz Tzvi, Magen Avraham, and the Gra it implies it is indispensable, and Chayei Adam and Shulchan Shlomo copied Magen Avraham's words into the halacha — nevertheless one may rely in pressing circumstances on the above lenient authorities regarding lishmah. So Da'as HaKadosh and Aruch HaShulchan copied Eliyah Rabbah's above words regarding lishmah; so too in responsa in the name of Mor VeKatzia; and Peri Megadim is also uncertain regarding lishmah — see there. And know further: it seems simple to me that even per the stringent regarding twisting for lishmah — this is specifically when explicitly not for its sake; but when done anonymously, if the one who spun twisted them himself, one could say here that "whoever does — the first way he does it he does," even though it is two actions. Proof: Tosafos in Zevachim 2b, gloss beginning "ha," etc., who explained the Gemara deals with two acts — slaughter and receiving — and nevertheless the Gemara concludes there that even if we say ordinary sacrifices are not for its sake, nevertheless the offering is valid if he intended at slaughter for the sake of Pesach and at receiving anonymously, because "whoever does — the first way he does it he does." So too in our matter: although ordinary threads are not made for tzitzis, where he spun from the outset explicitly for its sake, then anonymous twisting is also for its sake — for here and there it is one matter. There we derive in the Gemara that four acts require lishmah from the verse "you shall make Pesach" — that all his acts be for Pesach; here too we derive from "gedilim you shall make for yourself" for your obligation, between spinning and twisting — all is included in making, as the Gra wrote [and even according to what Tosafos rejected that explanation there because we must ask in Pesachim whether we learn one act, etc. — in any case they did not reject because "whoever does" does not apply here, only for another reason]. And although Magen Avraham wrote if they were not twisted for its sake it is invalid, and did not write if twisted not for its sake — the implication is that even anonymously it is invalid; it is possible this is not precise, or when the twister was not himself the spinner, for then "whoever does — the first way" does not apply, as written in Mishna Avraham siman 19, small paragraph 23, regarding writing a Sefer Torah.**** END BLOCK ****
