(s.k. 2) A non-Jew, etc. In siman 460 in the Beit Yosef, the opinion of the Rashba is that although one should invalidate when a non-Jew performs it while a Jew stands over him, because the non-Jew acts with his own intention and does not obey what the Jew commands him to do for its required purpose, nevertheless regarding a deaf-mute and a minor we are more lenient. A deaf-mute means one who neither hears nor speaks, an insane person means one who loses what is given to him, and a minor means one less than thirteen years old, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 1, seif 5. For a deaf-mute and a minor have at least a weak degree of understanding, and standing over them helps; but for an insane person, who has no understanding at all, standing over him does not help. It helps only by a get, where the matter proves that he has some understanding and that he hears and intends to fulfill what he was commanded, for he is commanded to write it for its required purpose and he writes the names of the husband and the wife. But by shechitah and the making of matzah for the mitzvah, where there is no proof, standing over him does not help. If so, the same applies here regarding spinning; since there is no proof, standing over an insane person does not help. But the opinion of Rabbeinu Yonah is that for something that can be done through an agent, standing over him helps, for through standing over him it is as though he is his agent. According to this, although an insane person is not subject to agency, nevertheless this case, where one stands over him, is stronger than ordinary agency. If so, also for shechitah, matzah for the mitzvah, and likewise spinning, standing over an insane person helps, unlike chalitzah, which cannot be done through an agent, where standing over an insane person does not help. See there in Pri Chadash.
