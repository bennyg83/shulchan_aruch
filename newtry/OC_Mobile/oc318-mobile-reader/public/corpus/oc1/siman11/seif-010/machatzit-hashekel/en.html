And perhaps he holds — i.e. for our view that requires it to be after abandonment — for he holds the threads are called reverting to their original state by undoing their twist and beating them again until they become wool again; therefore it did not acquire. But if there are two — abandonment and change that reverts to its original state — it acquires, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 353.

And see there siman 360; and now he comes to reconcile Rama's view, that when unspecified it implies even before abandonment; and therefore in siman 360 that it is a complete change — i.e. that spinning is a change that reverts to its original state; nevertheless before spinning one must whiten it, and libun is a complete change. So too it is explained there, and so too Taz here; and see there that Rama did not require abandonment — but this is satisfactory according to what is before us there in Shulchan Aruch. But per Shach's words there he proved that therefore there is an error in Shulchan Aruch, that whitening is not a change except whitening through sulfur; Magen Avraham's difficulty returns to its place — why did Rama state unspecified and not require abandonment? For before spinning one must whiten it — i.e. ordinary libun — and this is not a change. But ordinary wool is not whitened before spinning with sulfur, and Rama should still have required it.

And if the robber sold it, etc. — i.e. after abandonment the buyer may recite a blessing over it, for regarding him it is not a commandment of the Omnipresent.
