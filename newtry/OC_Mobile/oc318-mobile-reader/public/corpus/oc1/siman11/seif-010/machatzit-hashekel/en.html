(s.k. 10) That they are valid, etc. An act, and the Taz wrote a change of name. Both are true. But in the Nimukei Yosef, from whom this law originates, perhaps he holds, meaning the Nimukei Yosef holds, that it must be after despair, because he holds that the strings are considered able to return to their original state by undoing their spinning, then beating them again, and they again become wool; therefore he does not acquire. But if there are both factors, despair and a change that can return to its original state, he acquires, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 353.
See there in siman 360. He now comes to reconcile the opinion of Rama, who wrote it without qualification, implying even before despair. On this he wrote, see siman 360, for it is a complete change. That is to say, although the spinning is a change that can return to its original state, nevertheless before spinning it must be whitened, and whitening is a complete change. This is also explained there, and so the Taz wrote here. See there, for Rama did not require despair. But this is satisfactory only according to the text before us there in Shulchan Aruch. According to what the Shach wrote there, proving that there must be a scribal error in Shulchan Aruch, for whitening does not effect a change unless he whitened it with sulfur, the difficulty of Magen Avraham returns to its place: why did Rama write without qualification and not require despair? For although before spinning it must be whitened, that means ordinary whitening, and this does not effect a change. Ordinary wool is not whitened with sulfur before spinning, and Rama should still have specified it.
And if the robber sold, etc. This refers to after despair; the purchaser may recite a blessing over it, for with respect to him it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression.
Even if he stole strings and sold them, the purchaser acquired them through despair and change of possession. See there.
