(48) And they were torn away, etc. The same applies if it tore at the hole until, because of this, the required measure did not remain, even if this happened immediately after the first winding section together with the knot, as stated nearby.
(49) From the woof threads. Consequently the length of the garment was reduced through this, and according to what will be explained nearby, the same applies if the garment was reduced in its width.
(50) At the time. The verse says, and they shall make for themselves tzitzis on the corners of their garments. The Torah was particular only that at the time of making they should be on the corner, but not afterward. This applies specifically to those tzitzis that were hung validly. But if he wants to insert other tzitzis into it, which is a new act of making, it is invalid until he first sews it, as below in siman 15; see there.
(51) Less than the measure. Although after the fact it is valid, nevertheless it is good to do this so that onlookers will not come to say that it is invalid, for not everyone is learned in the law. See Beis Yosef.
(52) Some say that within, etc. For they hold that the primary name corner applies to the lower edge of the length of the garment, not to the width of the garment. The width of the garment is what a person wears over his stature from his head to his feet, and the length of the garment is what he wraps himself in, for the manner of wrapping is along the length.
(53) It has no measure. This means downward, for he may make it even less than a thumb joint, but above three fingerbreadths it is invalid. See Beis Yosef.
