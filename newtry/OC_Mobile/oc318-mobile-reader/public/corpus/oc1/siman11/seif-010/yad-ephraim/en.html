(In Shulchan Aruch seif 15) That one must be careful to hang the tzitzis, etc. — in the Taz's view, the term "hanging" is interpreted after making them on the garment and they protrude at the edge of the garment and were hanging on its edge along the length of the tallis toward the ground, as toward the ground, as stated in seif katan 17; and according to Magen Avraham above seif katan 17, "hanging" is interpreted at the time of making the tzitzis, as we find in the Gemara "he hung them" and afterward cut the tips of the strands, etc., and the like — that is, he should make the tzitzis toward the length of the tallis, namely the sides of the wrapping, not its width — namely that he should not make the tzitzis toward the edge that faces the ground, for there is the width of the tallis; and as written above, it appears that for the front tzitzis too he should make them along the length of the tallis, because when he lifts the tzitzis onto his hand they hang on the corner. Nevertheless it appears that since the mitzvah of wrapping is on the body, as explained in siman 8, and when he stands thus wrapped without lifting his hand — if he makes them along the length of the tallis they do not hang on the corner — therefore it is better to make the upper ones along the width of the tallis, even though the lower ones must be along the length, we are not particular about this; and see in Beit Yosef in the name of Maharam; and one could say this depends on the explanation of the length of the garment, over which the Taz and Magen Avraham disagree. In the Taz's view, who explains the length of the garment after he wears it and places two tzitzis behind him — the length of the garment is called from his head to his feet; those between before him and behind him — what protrudes from the garment in its width should hang along the length of the tallis, and we do not follow the lifting of the hand, since without lifting the hand it hangs on the corner along the length of the tallis. Not so according to Magen Avraham's explanation: the length of the garment is before wrapping, and its meaning is the actual length of the tallis; and after he wraps, its width is to his stature — then the edge he wraps in is the edge of the length, and the lower edge that faces length is the edge of the width; if so, one cannot say he should make the upper ones along the width so they hang on the corner when he is wrapped without lifting the hand — for if so, why did it state he should hang along the length of the tallis? For what did it need to state regarding the lower tzitzis behind him — it should have stated regarding the tzitzis before him that he should make them along the width; rather it must be that before him too he makes them along the length of the tallis, for through lifting the hand they hang on the corner. Nevertheless I heard from my uncle the gaon Rabbi Alexander z"l that he would also make the tzitzis before him along the width of the tallis — namely that the beginning of making them should be toward the edge he wraps in, and they hang on the corner when he is wrapped in it without lifting the tallis by hand; and even though from the plain sense of Tur and Beit Yosef it appears according to Magen Avraham, and if so it is fitting to make them all along the length of the tallis as I wrote — nevertheless Beit Yosef already wrote in the name of Maharam that everything depends on how one wraps in it: sometimes they must be along the length and sometimes along the width; and even if some are along the length and some along the width — we have no concern.
