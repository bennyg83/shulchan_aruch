There, in the continuation. “See the beginning of siman 649.” This is difficult for me. There the discussion concerns the second day of Yom Tov, when the requirement of lachem (ownership) does not apply, and a stolen item is invalid only because it is a mitzvah habaah ba'aveirah. Regarding this, some say that when a subsequent person did not steal it, there is no disqualification of mitzvah habaah ba'aveirah. Here, however, it is invalid because it is not his. If the thief sold it after ye'ush, the purchaser acquired it through ye'ush together with a change of possession, and it is not a mitzvah habaah ba'aveirah, because it was acquired before the mitzvah, just as a physical change renders it valid here. Perhaps one can say that Magen Avraham means that it was sold after ye'ush and is discussing the blessing: according to the authorities in siman 649 who hold that for a subsequent person it is not a mitzvah habaah ba'aveirah, there is no disgrace and he may recite the blessing. This still requires further study.
