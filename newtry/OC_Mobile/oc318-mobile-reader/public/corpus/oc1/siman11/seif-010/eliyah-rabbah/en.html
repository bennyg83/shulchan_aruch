[10] Its wool is invalid, etc. — for we are uncertain whether it is like bowing to something attached, which is not forbidden to a layman — and the same would apply to the altar; or etc. (Levush). I wondered in Eliyahu Zuta: yet in the Gemara, Avodah Zarah page 46, it was resolved that there is worship of the attached for the altar, as Rambam ruled at the end of chapter 4 [Laws of Forbidden Offerings on the Altar]; and as Kesef Mishnah wrote explicitly — only that it is a question whether attached is forbidden for the altar; nevertheless it is a question whether we say the same law applies for a mitzvah, or only for sacrifices. And so Rashi's explanation proves on page 46, s.v. its branch — what of it for a mitzvah, etc. Also the beginning of the question regarding attached for the altar is whether it is like an animal that is permitted to a layman and forbidden for the altar, as Rashi explained there. If so, even attached would be permitted for the altar; nevertheless an animal is certainly forbidden. The same applies to wool attached to an animal — it is no better than the animal itself. Rather this question is clear: whether it is also forbidden for a mitzvah — and it does not depend at all on "attached." My father the rabbi, may his light shine, answered the first wonder: the Levush also holds that attached is forbidden for the altar. What he wrote "and the same applies for the altar" means a matter of mitzvah in which there is also altar need; but for the altar itself it is certainly forbidden. Olat Tamid wrote: why what was explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 360 — regarding stolen wool dyed in a dye that cannot be removed, acquisition is through change — the same law applies here for tzitzit; in practice this requires study — until here. To me it appears from Rambam chapter 4 of Laws of Forbidden Offerings and the Gemara there that even if it cannot be removed it is invalid for tzitzit, for here we require a change that is repugnant to the altar. Also they invalidated the wool even for tekhelet. At the beginning of the chapter HaGozel Kama it implies that tekhelet is like kala ilan that does not revert; and so it appears in Menachos page 43.
