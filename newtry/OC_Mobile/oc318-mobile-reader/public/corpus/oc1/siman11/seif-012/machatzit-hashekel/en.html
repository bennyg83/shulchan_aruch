(s.k. 12) Invalid, and even though it changed, etc. That is to say, both laws, the law of wool and the law of linen, are from the words of the Rambam. Just as he validated the linen because it changed, he should also have validated the wool that changed.
Its appearance is still upon it and it appears to be wool. The phrase its appearance is upon it is not precise; even if its color changed, as long as it still appears to be wool, as is proven below. With this, the difficulty of Tosafos is resolved, etc.
There are four questions there. First, Rami bar Chama asked: if one bowed to a mountain, what is the law of its stones for the altar? Although something worshipped while attached is permitted for ordinary use, he was uncertain whether it becomes forbidden for use on high. And if you say that something worshipped while attached is forbidden for use on high, are items that enable a korban, namely the altar, which is not itself a korban but enables a korban, treated like a korban or not? Second, Rami bar Chama asked: if one bowed to a standing crop of wheat, what is the law of its flour for menachos? Although something worshipped while attached is forbidden, and menachos are themselves a korban, nevertheless since it changed, originally wheat and now flour, do we say there is change regarding something worshipped for use on high or not? Third, Reish Lakish asked: if one bowed to a palm tree in a manner that it remains permitted for ordinary use, and one took a lulav from it for the mitzvah, what is the law? Is it repulsive for a mitzvah or not? Fourth, Rav Pappa asked: if one bowed to an animal, what is the law of its wool for techeiles? The Shas asked: techeiles for what? If techeiles for kohanim, namely for the garments of the kehunah, that is the question of Rami bar Chama. Rashi explained: the two questions of Rami bar Chama, whether items that enable a korban are like a korban, and whether there is change regarding something worshipped, for techeiles for the garments of the kehunah enables the korban and has also changed. If it means techeiles for tzitzis, whether it is repulsive even though it changed, that is the question of Reish Lakish regarding the palm tree, and it was not resolved. Therefore the Rambam ruled stringently that it is invalid for tzitzis. Tosafos asked: why, when it said, rather, techeiles for tzitzis, did it say that this is the above question of Reish Lakish? It should also have said that this is the question of Rami bar Chama regarding one who bowed to a standing crop of wheat. They answered that since it resembles the palm tree, for the lulav is a mitzvah and tzitzis are also a mitzvah and not a korban, it mentioned Reish Lakish and not Rami bar Chama. According to the Rambam this is satisfactory, for from Rami bar Chama’s question regarding a standing crop of wheat made into flour, one cannot resolve techeiles for tzitzis, because wheat and flour are a complete change, since the name wheat has gone and it is called flour. This is not so with techeiles; although its color changed, the name wool remains upon it and it is not a complete change. Therefore it appears to me that this is the question of Reish Lakish, for a lulav from a palm tree still has its original name and is recognizable as coming from a palm tree. See there in Maharam Lublin. As for what it initially said, if techeiles for kohanim, this is the question of Rami bar Chama, even though in the case of Rami bar Chama there is a complete change, unlike techeiles: the Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 4 of Hilchos Issurei Mizbeach, law 7, that the Rambam explained that this refers to the question of Rami bar Chama, whether items that enable a korban are like a korban. See there. One must say that it is still possible that he means techeiles for kohanim, namely that even if you say items that enable a korban are like a korban, there remains the question whether it is called a change, which was not included in Rami bar Chama’s question. One must therefore say that this is truly the intent of the Shas: and if techeiles for tzitzis, meaning a doubt relevant also to tzitzis, namely whether there is change. He asks it both regarding kohanim and regarding tzitzis, and this is the question of Reish Lakish. See siman 587. There Magen Avraham wrote that what he wrote here, its wool is invalid, means only initially, but after the fact one fulfills the obligation.
