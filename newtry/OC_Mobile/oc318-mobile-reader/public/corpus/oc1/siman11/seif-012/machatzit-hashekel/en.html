"Its appearance is still upon it" and "it appears to be wool" — the term "its appearance is upon it" is not precise: even if only its color changed, as long as it still appears to be wool, as proven below. And thereby Tosafot's difficulty is resolved, etc.

For there are four uncertainties there. First: Rami bar Chama bowed to a mountain — are its stones for the altar? Even though something worshipped while attached is permitted to a commoner, nevertheless he is uncertain whether it is forbidden for sacred use; and if you say there is something worshipped while attached for sacred use (and so we rule) — accessories of a sacrifice (i.e. the altar, which is not a sacrifice but accessories of a sacrifice) — are they like a sacrifice or not? Second uncertainty: Rami bar Chama bowed to a stack of wheat — what is its status for meal-offerings? Even though there is something worshipped while attached and meal-offerings are themselves a sacrifice, nevertheless since it changed — at first wheat and now flour — is there change in something worshipped for sacred use or not? Third uncertainty: R. Levi bowed to a palm branch in a matter permitted to a commoner and took a lulav from it for a mitzvah — what is its status: is it repugnant for a mitzvah or not? Fourth uncertainty: R. Pinchas bowed to an animal — what is its wool for tekhelet? The Gemara asked: tekhelet of what? If tekhelet for priests — i.e. for priestly garments, which is the uncertainty of Rami bar Chama (and Rashi explained two uncertainties of Rami bar Chama: whether accessories of a sacrifice are like a sacrifice, and whether there is change in something worshipped — for tekhelet for priestly garments are accessories of a sacrifice and also changed); and if tekhelet for tzitzit — whether it is repugnant even though it changed — that is the uncertainty of R. Levi with the palm, and it was not resolved; therefore Rambam ruled stringently that it is invalid for tzitzit. Tosafot asked: why when he said "rather tekhelet for tzitzit" did he say that is the above uncertainty of R. Levi — he should also have said that is the uncertainty of Rami bar Chama bowing to a stack of wheat? They answered: since it is similar to a palm — for lulav is a mitzvah and tzitzit too are a mitzvah and not a sacrifice — he cited R. Levi and not Rami bar Chama. Per Rambam it is satisfactory: from the uncertainty of Rami bar Chama from wheat stack made flour, there is no resolution for tekhelet for tzitzit — for in wheat and flour there is complete change, for there wheat is gone and it is called flour; unlike tekhelet — even if its color changed, nevertheless the name wool is still upon it and it is not complete change; therefore it appears that is the uncertainty of R. Levi, that a lulav from a palm still has its original name upon it and it is recognizable that it came from a palm (and see Maharam of Lublin). And that which he said first "if tekhelet for priests" — that is the uncertainty of Rami bar Chama, even though in Rami bar Chama's case there is complete change, unlike tekhelet. Kesef Mishnah wrote chapter 4 of Hilchot Issurei Mizbeach law 7 that Rambam would explain that is the uncertainty of Rami bar Chama whether accessories of a sacrifice are like a sacrifice; see there. And one must say: still perhaps "tekhelet for priests" he speaks — if you say accessories of a sacrifice are like a sacrifice, nevertheless if it is called change that is not in the category of Rami bar Chama's uncertainty. And one must say: in truth this is the Gemara's intent — and "tekhelet for tzitzit" means doubt that applies also to tzitzit — i.e. whether there is change. And from what it asks regarding both priests and tzitzit — that is the uncertainty of R. Levi; and in siman 387. There Magen Avraham wrote that what he wrote here "wool invalid" is specifically lekhatchilah, but b'dieved it is valid.
