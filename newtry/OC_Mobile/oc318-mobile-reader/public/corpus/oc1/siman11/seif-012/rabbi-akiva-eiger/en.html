Magen Avraham, se'if katan 13. “For the Gemara says: infer from this that the upper knot,” etc. A scholar posed the following difficulty to me concerning the Gemara's proof that the upper knot is D'Oraisa. Perhaps the verse is needed for a case in which the tzitzis became knotted to the garment by themselves and it is impossible to untie the knot on Shabbos. The verse would then permit wearing it in that state, because kilayim is permitted in tzitzis. I answered him as follows. Quite apart from this, we must understand: since kilayim is permitted in tzitzis, what difference is there whether the tzitzis together with the garment constitute kilayim or the garment itself is kilayim? Let us say that one may place tzitzis on a garment that is itself kilayim and wear it to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis, because a positive commandment overrides a prohibition. It appears, however, that in principle the positive commandment of tzitzis should not override the prohibition of kilayim. In an ordinary case, the positive commandment is obligatory and incumbent upon the person to perform. The positive commandment of tzitzis, by contrast, does not obligate one to wear a four-cornered tallis and attach tzitzis to it. Why, then, should it override the prohibition of kilayim? If he simply does not wear the garment, he will not violate the positive commandment at all. The answer follows from Mordechai's statement that there is no prohibition resting upon the tallis that forbids wearing it without tzitzis. Rather, when one wears it, the mitzvah to attach tzitzis takes effect. For this reason, if no tzitzis are available in the city, or on Shabbos when one cannot attach tzitzis, it is permitted by Torah law to wear the garment without tzitzis. We can therefore properly say that the positive commandment of tzitzis overrides the prohibition of kilayim. If the law were that it did not override the prohibition, he would not attach tzitzis, yet he would still be permitted to wear the garment without tzitzis and would thereby nullify the positive commandment. Had we forbidden him to wear it in the absence of an override, there would in truth be no override, because without wearing it there is no nullification of a positive commandment. Since he is in fact permitted at least by Torah law to wear it without tzitzis, however, the positive commandment of tzitzis consequently takes effect and overrides the prohibition of kilayim. All this applies when the garment itself is not kilayim, but the tzitzis together with the garment create kilayim. If the positive commandment did not override the prohibition, he would nevertheless wear the garment without tzitzis. If the garment itself is kilayim, however, one cannot say that the positive commandment should override the prohibition. If it does not override it, he cannot wear the garment at all, since the garment itself is kilayim, and the positive commandment therefore never takes effect upon him. According to this, the foregoing difficulty is resolved. When the tzitzis became knotted to the garment by themselves and on Shabbos it is impossible to untie them, the positive commandment should not override the prohibition. Since he cannot untie them, he cannot wear the garment because of kilayim, and the positive commandment consequently never takes effect upon him. In truth, however, since the upper knot is D'Oraisa and the override properly applies, even if they became knotted by themselves on Shabbos it is permitted, because during the week too the mitzvah is performed by joining them in a manner that creates kilayim. If, however, the upper knot were not D'Oraisa, and the sole possible case of override were one in which the threads became knotted by themselves on Shabbos, no override should apply at all, as explained above. Examine this carefully. In this same matter, the words of Tosafos in Yevamos 5a, s.v. Mai LeLav DeHakafah, etc., are astonishing to me. Tosafos holds that the law is not derived from gedilim. If so, according to his view, from where do we know that the upper knot is D'Oraisa? Independently of this, the same difficulty applies to the statement there, “Rather, from his head.” Regarding that Tanna, we presently hold that he does not derive from adjacent verses that kilayim is permitted in tzitzis. If so, from where does he know that the upper knot is D'Oraisa? This requires further study.
