Magen Avraham, subsection 13 — for we say in the Gemara, “From here [we derive] the upper knot,” etc. A certain scholar challenged me on the Talmud's proof that the upper knot is by Torah law, lest a verse be needed for a case where the tzitzit tied themselves to the garment and it is impossible to untie the knot on Shabbat; in that case the verse comes to permit wearing it so, for shaatnez in tzitzit is permitted. And I answered him: otherwise we would need to understand — since shaatnez in tzitzit is permitted, what difference if the tzitzit are shaatnez with the garment, or the garment itself is shaatnez? And let us say the garment of shaatnez may have tzitzit placed on it and one may wear it to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzit, that a positive commandment overrides a negative one. Therefore it appears that in truth the positive commandment of tzitzit should not override the prohibition of shaatnez, for it is different in general, being a positive commandment of obligation placed upon him to do it; but the positive commandment of tzitzit — there is no obligation upon him to wear a four-cornered tallit and place tzitzit. If so, why does the positive commandment override the prohibition of shaatnez? For if he does not wear it, he does not violate the positive commandment at all. Therefore it appears according to what Mordechai wrote that there is no prohibition at all on the tallit not to wear it without tzitzit; only when he wears it does the mitzvah apply to place tzitzit. And for this reason, if tzitzit were not found in the city, and likewise on Shabbat when one cannot place tzitzit on it — it is permitted by Torah law to wear it without tzitzit. If so, we say well that the positive commandment of tzitzit overrides the prohibition of shaatnez. For if the law were that it does not override, he would not place tzitzit on it, and nevertheless it would be permitted to wear it without tzitzit, and again the positive commandment would be nullified; for without override we would be blocking him from wearing it. In truth there is no override here, for without wearing there is no nullification of a positive commandment; but since in truth in any case it is permitted to wear it without tzitzit, automatically the positive commandment of tzitzit applies and overrides the prohibition of shaatnez. All this if the garment in itself is not shaatnez but the tzitzit are shaatnez with the garment — for if the positive commandment would not override the prohibition, nevertheless he would wear it without tzitzit. But if the garment in itself is shaatnez, there is no reason to say the positive commandment overrides the prohibition — for if it does not override, it is impossible to wear it at all, since the garment is shaatnez in itself; automatically the positive commandment does not apply at all. And therefore the above question is not difficult — for regarding this case, that the tzitzit tied themselves to the garment on Shabbat when one cannot untie it: in this there is no reason for a positive commandment to override a prohibition, since one cannot untie it, and he will not be able to wear it because of shaatnez, and the positive commandment will not apply at all. Rather, since in truth the upper knot is by Torah law and override is applicable — automatically even if they tied themselves on Shabbat it is permitted, for such is the mitzvah also on a weekday, to join them in the manner of shaatnez. But if the upper knot is not by Torah law, the main override would be only in the case where they tied themselves on Shabbat — in this it is not fit to override at all, as above. And understand. And in this matter I wonder at Tosafos Yevamos 5a, s.v. “What is the negative commandment of wrapping,” etc., 60, that “gedilim” we do not derive. I question: if so, in my view, whence do we know the upper knot is by Torah law? And otherwise it will be difficult likewise in what we say there, “Rather from its head” — this tanna; if so, our proofs now that we do not derive “adjacent” to permit shaatnez in tzitzit — whence do we know the upper knot is by Torah law? This requires study.
