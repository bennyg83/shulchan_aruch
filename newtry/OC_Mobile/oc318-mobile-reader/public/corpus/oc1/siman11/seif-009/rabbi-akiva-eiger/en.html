Magen Avraham, se'if katan 10. “But only together with ye'ush.” According to this, one can say that what Nezir Shimshon calls a physical change means a change of name, as Taz writes, for that acquires together with ye'ush, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 353. Its source is Tosafos at the beginning of the third chapter of Sukkah concerning the merchants. A physical change that can revert to its original state, however, does not acquire even together with ye'ush. This is proven by the passage there, which answers that it is a change that can revert to its original state, although ye'ush was present there. It is true that I saw that Perishah in Choshen Mishpat, in the foregoing siman, writes as obvious that ye'ush likewise effects acquisition when combined with a physical change that can revert to its original state; see there. In my humble opinion, however, this is astonishing, as explained above. Shaar HaMelech later came into my possession, and I saw that in the first chapter of Hilkhot Gerushin he challenges Perishah in the same manner.
