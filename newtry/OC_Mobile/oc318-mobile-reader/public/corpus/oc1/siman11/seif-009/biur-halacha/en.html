He should make a hole, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah regarding two holes. These are the words of the Peri Megadim: one makes only one hole, and one who makes two holes should make them straight, not diagonally. One measures the three fingers from the first hole in the garment, not from the second, on the right side and on the left side. See Eretz HaChaim. Also the two holes should be in such a way that the tzitzis hang to the sides of the garment, one to the right and one to the left, before him and behind him, and not hanging toward the ground. All this is in the Peri Megadim there.
This means thumbs. See the Taz, who wrote that it means at the width of the thumb, and see the Peri Megadim, who wrote that we rule this way as halacha. Chayei Adam also quoted this. Nevertheless, according to what we wrote earlier, that one should be careful to make the hole specifically within the third finger before its completion, if he wants, he may measure the three fingers with the middle, ring, and small fingers adjacent to one another, as I wrote in the name of the Arizal, and then make the hole; these are both one measure. Something similar is also written in Shaarei Teshuvah; see there. However, he wrote that the reason of the Arizal is perhaps because he holds like the doubt of Tosafot, that the thumb is measured at its narrow place. Our reason is that perhaps specifically by tzitzis the Arizal was concerned for the view of the Rokeach, Rabbi Yosef ben Chaviv, and Mahari Abuhav, who hold specifically within three. To satisfy all concerns, namely the concern of Tosafot and the view of Rabbi Yosef ben Chaviv, it is better to make the hole within these three fingers. Afterward I also found in Eretz HaChaim that it is good to measure the three fingers with these three adjacent fingers. However, if one borrowed a tallis from another to fulfill tzitzis with it, and the tzitzis hole is within three thumbs, it appears that he may recite a blessing over it, for as practical halacha we rule like the Mordechai, that the measurement is at the wide place, as the Peri Megadim wrote.
And not below the required measure, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the place of the hole should be above the measure of a thumb-joint. So it is implied from the language of the Tur and Mahari ben Chaviv, who is brought in the Beis Yosef. But from the language of the Rokeach brought in the Beis Yosef, s.v. the Rokeach wrote, it appears, etc., it seems at first glance that he holds that if the tzitzis were exactly at the completion of the thumb-joint measure, even though they are not above it, it is valid, for the tzitzis hole may be within the measure, provided it is not below the measure. What the Rokeach wrote, in the width, must mean the width and a little more. The words of the Bach also imply somewhat that he too holds this way as practical law; see his words carefully, s.v. he should make a hole in the length of the tallis, etc., and in the continuation. So too it is clear from the language of the Rambam, who wrote: he distances from it not more than three fingers and not less than a thumb-joint, and inserts there four threads, etc. According to the Bach, then, the Rambam's words, and inserts there, refer to precisely within these measures. But from the Darchei Moshe it implies that he explains the words of the Rokeach in another way; see there, and this is very forced in his wording. Also from the words of the Beis Yosef it implies that he does not explain like the Darchei Moshe, for otherwise the Beis Yosef should have quoted the Rokeach below as support for the Tur. On the contrary, he wrote there the opposite, that he did not find this law explicit anywhere. Rather, it is clear that the Beis Yosef does not explain like him. Initially, however, one must certainly be careful in this, that the threads be placed above the measure, for the measure of a thumb-joint is implied by the poskim to be according to the Torah and indispensable, as is explicit in Eliyah Rabbah. But regarding after the fact, this requires study.
