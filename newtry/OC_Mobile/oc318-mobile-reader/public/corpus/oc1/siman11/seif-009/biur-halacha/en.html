"He shall make a hole," etc. — see Mishna Berurah regarding two holes. Thus Peri Megadim: one makes only one hole; one who makes two holes should make them straight, not diagonally; and one measures three fingers from the first hole in the garment, not from the second, on the right side and left side [see Aruch HaShulchan]. Also both holes should be such that the tzitzis hang to the sides of the garment — one to the right and one to the left before him and behind him — not hanging toward the ground. All this in Peri Megadim there.**** END BLOCK ****

"That is thumb-breadths" — see Taz, who wrote in the place of the width of the thumb; see Peri Megadim, who wrote we rule thus for the law; Chayei Adam also copied thus. Nevertheless, according to what we wrote before, one must be careful to make the hole specifically within the third finger before it is completed — therefore if he wishes he may measure the three fingers with the bent middle finger and the adjacent pinky, as written in the name of the Ari z"l, and afterward make the hole — and both are one measure. Something similar wrote Sha'arei Teshuvah — see there — except he wrote the Ari z"l's reason may be because he holds like Tosafos' doubt that measurement of thumb is in the short place; our reason is that perhaps specifically for tzitzis, because the Ari z"l was concerned for the view of Rokeach, R' Yehuda bar Chava, and Mahari Abuhav, who hold specifically within the third. To satisfy all concerns (Tosafos' concern and R' Yehuda bar Chava's view) it is better to make the hole within these three fingers. I later found in Aruch HaShulchan it is good to measure the three fingers in these three adjacent to each other. However, if he borrowed a tallit afterward to fulfill tzitzis and the tzitzis hole is within three thumb-breadths — it seems he may bless on it, for the law rules like the Mordechai that measurement is in the broad place, as Peri Megadim wrote.**** END BLOCK ****

"And not below the measure," etc. — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote that the place of the hole should be above the thumb-knot — so it is implied from the Tur's wording and Mahari ben Chabib, cited in Beis Yosef. But from Rokeach's wording, cited in Beis Yosef, gloss beginning "the Rokeach wrote, it appears," etc. — it appears he holds that if the tzitzis were completed with the thumb-knot in close measure, even if they are not above it, it is valid: the tzitzis hole may be within the measure, only not below the measure. And what Rokeach wrote "in width" must mean width. Somewhat implied in Bach's words that he also rules thus, as we wrote — see there well, gloss beginning "and he shall make a hole along the length of the tallis," etc., in Eshel Avraham. So explained in Rambam's wording: "and he distances from it not more than three fingers and not less than a thumb-knot, and inserts there four threads," etc. — behold for Bach what Rambam wrote "and inserts there" refers within this close measure. But from Darkei Moshe it appears he explains Rokeach's words for another matter — see there; this is a great strain in his wording. Also from Beis Yosef it appears he does not explain like Darkei Moshe, for otherwise Beis Yosef should have copied Rokeach's words below to support the Tur — yet he wrote the opposite there, that he did not find this law explained anywhere. Certainly Beis Yosef does not explain like his words. But lekatchilah one must certainly be careful that the threads be placed above the measure, for the measure of a thumb-knot implies from the poskim that it is biblical and indispensable, as explained in Eliyah Rabbah. But regarding after the fact — requires investigation.**** END BLOCK ****
