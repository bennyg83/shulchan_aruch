[9] Valid, etc. — it is acquired through despair (ye'ush) and change of action; and some say that even b'dieved it is invalid because it is a change that reverts to its original state (Levush). The Taz agreed with these "some say," but not for their reason — rather because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. It is truly difficult for me regarding the Levush's reason, for in Choshen Mishpat there, seif 2, he ruled that change of name — even if it reverts to its original state — acquires; and here too there is change of name. Now what puzzled Olat Tamid and the purchaser from the remnants of Knesset HaGedolah — who wondered at Rama that he ruled like "some say" that it is valid when spun, etc., that a reverted change does not acquire — until here — is not difficult at all, for here too there is change of name; nevertheless for practical law everything is forbidden, as I wrote in the name of the Taz. But it is difficult on the Taz: below, siman 649 seif 2, it is explained that where there is despair and change it is not a mitzvah through transgression. One may answer: since the change is by making threads, and we hold that spinning must be lishmah — if so the change is with assistance of the mitzvah, and it is comparable to one who consecrates his animal, as Tosafos wrote in the chapter Merubah page 67. But one could question: the Taz challenged Beit Yosef, who explained Rambam's words that here it is a change that reverts; and the Taz wrote — this is his language: "I wonder at the holy R. Yosef Karo, for he omitted the sugya in the chapter HaGozel Kama, where it is explained that bleaching (libun) is a change that does not revert; and one who stole wool and made threads from it — perforce he already bleached it, for without bleaching first one cannot make threads from it" — until here his words briefly; therefore he explained Rambam's words as mitzvah through transgression. In truth Rashi there explained that he bleached first and then spun; if so one could question that the change occurred before spinning. I also wonder at the Taz: why did he wonder at Beit Yosef — perhaps Rambam deals with one who stole white wool and then spun and made threads from it, as the Gemara establishes the mishnah "one who stole wool and made a garment from it" — that it deals with stolen spun wool; see there. It is somewhat difficult why the Gemara did not emend that it deals with stolen white wool that he spun and made a garment from it; there is room to settle this — examine. In any case in Rambam's words one may surely explain thus. The Taz also wondered at Beit Yosef, who explained that Rambam deals before despair — if so, why do I need the verse "and you shall make"? In the chapter Merubah it asks: if before despair, why do I need a verse? — until here. My father the rabbi, may his light shine, answered: here there is a question because there is change. It is somewhat difficult, for the Gemara deals with a sacrifice and there is also change of name, yet it still asks why a verse is needed. In my humble opinion it appears that originally it is not difficult — one may say we need the verse for stolen threads that are invalid even after despair; and perforce one must say this for Yosef Karo's reasons, who holds that stolen wool made into threads is valid — if so, perforce he establishes the verse regarding stolen threads. One may also say, as Tosafos wrote in Sukkah page 30, that only regarding consecration does it ask why a verse is needed, for it is non-sacred; see below siman 649 in Eliyahu Zuta and in the introduction of Beit Yaakov on this.
