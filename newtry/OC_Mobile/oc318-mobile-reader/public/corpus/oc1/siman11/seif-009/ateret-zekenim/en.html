One should make a hole along the length of the tallit, no more than three fingerbreadths from the edge, because beyond that point it is not called a corner. Here too, the thumbbreadth must be measured at the broad part of the thumb rather than at its narrow tip, as stated above. Shulchan Aruch.
And not lower than the measure of a full thumb joint, etc. If it appears from the width of the hole containing the threads that the threads are not a full thumb joint from the edge, there is no concern. Beit Yosef; Rokeach. Here, regarding this law, Beit Yosef cites the words of the great Rabbi Mahari Abuhav: 'The reason it must be within three fingerbreadths yet a full thumb joint from the edge is that less than three fingerbreadths is not called a garment, and it must therefore be within three. It must be a full thumb joint from the edge because only at that measure is it called a corner; if there is less than that measure below it, there is no longer a corner.' Beit Yosef writes concerning this: 'I did not understand his words. Even though an area of less than three fingerbreadths is not called a garment, it is called the corner of a garment. The verse says, "upon the corners of their garments," not "upon their garments," etc.' See there. If I were not afraid to do so, I would say that Beit Yosef was quite correct in stating that he did not understand the words of the great Rabbi Mahari Abuhav, for in truth he did not understand them. Beit Yosef understood Mahari Abuhav's statement that an area of less than three fingerbreadths is not called a garment as a reason to disqualify that area for placing tzitzit. If so, how, according to that understanding, could Mahari Abuhav conclude, 'It must therefore be within three'? In truth, however, the words of Mahari Abuhav are as clear as the sun and visible to all. They accord with Rashi and with the distinctions that Nimukei Yosef himself explained above in Rashi's words: The very end of the garment, less than a full thumb joint from the edge, is called the point; from a full thumb joint until three fingerbreadths is called the corner; and above three fingerbreadths is called the garment. Mahari Abuhav's words proceed along this same path. The reason the hole must be within three fingerbreadths yet a full thumb joint from the edge is that an area of less than three fingerbreadths is not called the garment and is therefore fit for placing tzitzit. Were it called the garment, it would not be fit, because the Torah says, 'upon the corners of their garments,' and not upon their garments. Likewise, an area less than a full thumb joint from the edge is not called the corner, but the point. Thus Mahari Abuhav's words do not deviate right or left from Rashi's words. His statement that an area less than three fingerbreadths is not called a garment provides a positive reason to validate that place for attaching tzitzit, unlike Beit Yosef's understanding that it was a negative reason to disqualify it. Beit Yosef therefore brandished his spear against the rabbi for no reason, for all the words of Mahari Abuhav are just and accord with the halakhic explanation of Rashi and Semag. This appears correct to me.
