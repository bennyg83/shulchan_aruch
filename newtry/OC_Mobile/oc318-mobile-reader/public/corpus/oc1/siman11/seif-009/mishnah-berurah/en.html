(39) He should make a hole. The Beis Yosef wrote that although some say he should make two holes like a tzerei, insert the tzitzis through them, and bring them out to one side, one need not be concerned for this. One who comes to be stringent upon himself in such a matter is not among the stringent, but among those who cause wonder, because it appears like arrogance, end quote. The Bach wrote that with a small tallis one should make two holes, for in that case it does not appear like arrogance, since it is not visible to all. It is likewise written in the Kavanos that the Arizal practiced this way. However, in Birkei Yosef he wrote that Maharam Shmuel testified that his father, Rav Chaim Vital, did not practice this way. So the custom spread in the country of Poland. However, in Hungary and Germany they do not practice this way, and each river follows its own course.
(40) Along the length. See below in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 52, what we wrote there.
(41) Not higher than three. For it is not called the corner of the garment, but the garment itself, and the Torah wrote, on the corners of their garments. See the Beis Yosef, who wrote that exactly three fingerbreadths is permitted, and one may then make the hole there. Many poskim disagree with this and hold that once it is three fingerbreadths it is called the garment and not the corner. Therefore one must be careful to make the hole begin within three fingerbreadths. The Magen Avraham wrote that if he made the hole higher than three, even though when he tied the tzitzis he made the strings on the garment short and the corners folded, so that it is now lower than three, it is invalid, for in any event the corner contains the measure of a garment. Likewise, if he made the hole higher than the measure of a thumb joint, and through the tying the garment folded until it is less than a thumb joint, it is valid, since in any event there is the required measure in the corner.
(42) That is, thumbbreadths. See above s.k. 19. See Eretz HaChaim, who concludes that initially it is better to measure the three fingers with the middle finger, ring finger, and small finger adjacent to one another. See Biur Halachah.
(43) For it is not called a corner. This is indispensable even after the fact. Even if, after he made tzitzis in it, and even if he made only one winding section, he cut at the hole so that the tzitzis would hang lower, nevertheless it is invalid because of the rule, make it, and not from what is already made. The same applies in the reverse case, when he hung them within a thumb joint and then cut above in the garment, raising the tzitzis above the measure of a thumb joint, and sewed below, or if he hung a small piece of cloth on the corner. This too is considered make it, and not from what is already made. If a wool tallis had proper tzitzis, and linen threads were found in the silk border made at the edge of the tallis, and they needed to remove the border from the tallis, it is proper to be stringent, to untie the tzitzis and tie them again.
(44) And not lower, etc. This is indispensable even after the fact. It appears to me that the place of the hole must also be higher than this measure, and perhaps this too is indispensable even after the fact. See Biur Halachah.
(45) Than the measure, etc. It is obvious that this too is measured by an average person.
(46) Until the nail. The place that is level with the flesh.
(47) Straight. This means that the three fingerbreadths are measured straight from the edge of the garment, and likewise the measure of a thumb joint, whether along the length or the width, as below at the end of seif 10. The measures are not taken diagonally, for through the diagonal the measurement is reduced by about two fifths. See Beis Yosef.
