(39) He shall make a hole — Beit Yosef wrote that even though some say one should make two holes like a tzeirei [two dots], insert the tzitzis in them, and draw them out to one side — we need not be concerned for this. One who comes to be stringent with himself in such matters is not among the stringent but among the astonished, for it appears as showiness — end of his words. Bach wrote that for a tallit katan one should make two holes, for there it does not appear as showiness since it is not seen by everyone. And so is written in Kavanot that the Ari, of blessed memory, practiced thus [However in Birkei Yosef he wrote that Maharsha testified that his father R. Chaim Vital did not practice thus]. And this custom spread in Poland; however in Hungary and Ashkenaz they do not practice thus — each place and its custom.

(40) In its length — See below in Mishna Berurah, seif katan 52, what we wrote there.

(41) Not above three [finger-widths] — For beyond that is not called the corner of the garment but the garment; and the Torah wrote "on the corners of their garments" (Numbers 15:38). See Beit Yosef, who wrote that for exactly three finger-widths it is permitted to distance [the hole] and make the hole there afterward; many poskim disagree and hold that once there are three finger-widths, beyond that is called garment and not corner. Therefore one should be careful to begin the hole within three finger-widths. Magen Avraham wrote that if one made the hole above three finger-widths, even though when tying tzitzis the strings on the garment became shorter and the corners folded so it is now below three — it is invalid, for in any case the corner contains the measure of garment [area]. And similarly if one made the hole above a thumb-knot length and through tying the garment folded until less than a thumb-knot remains — it is valid, since in any case the [required] measure remains in the corner.

(42) Meaning thumbs — See above, seif katan 29. See Eretz HaChaim, who concludes that lechatchilah it is better to measure the three finger-widths with the middle finger, ring finger, and pinky that are adjacent to one another. See Biur Halacha.

(43) That it is not called a corner — This prevents even bedieved. Even if afterward he made tzitzis, even only one segment, and cut the hole so the tzitzis hang lower — nevertheless invalid because of te'aseh velo min ha'asuy (you shall make, and not from that which was [already] made). The same in the opposite case: when he hung them within a thumb-knot and cut upward in the garment and raised the tzitzis above a thumb-knot and sewed below or hung a patch on the corner — this too is te'aseh velo min ha'asuy. A wool tallis that had tzitzis affixed properly, and they found in a silk gadil made on the edge of the tallis linen threads [shatnez], and they had to remove the gadil from the tallis — it is proper to be stringent to untie the tzitzis and tie them again.

(44) And not below, etc. — This prevents even bedieved. It appears to me the place of the hole should also be above this measure; and perhaps this too prevents even bedieved. See Biur Halacha.

(45) From the measure, etc. — It is obvious this too is measured on an average person.

(46) Until the fingernail — The place level with the flesh.

(47) Straight — Meaning from the edge of the garment one measures straight three finger-widths; and similarly the measure of a thumb-knot, whether in length or width, as below at the end of this siman. One does not measure diagonally, for a diagonal reduces the measure by about two-fifths [Beit Yosef].
