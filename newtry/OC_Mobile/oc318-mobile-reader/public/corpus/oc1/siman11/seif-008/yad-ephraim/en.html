Se'if katan 16: 'The length of the garment is what one wraps around himself.' Siman 8, seif 3, states that one places the width of the tallis along the person's height; see Tur there. The edge that wraps around him is therefore the tallis's lengthwise edge, while the edge facing the ground is the garment's widthwise edge. According to the 'some say,' no minimum distance is required from the latter edge: even if he places the tzitzis close to the edge facing the ground there is no concern, provided it is one thumb-joint from the edge that wraps around him. Taz holds that although the usual manner of wrapping places the tallis's width along the person's height, the terms 'length' and 'width' here are defined by the garment as worn. What runs along the person's height is then called its length, while what wraps around him is its width. The principal point requiring care is that the tzitzis not be close to the lower edge facing the ground, which is now called the garment's widthwise edge. One must raise it a thumb-joint from the edge nearest the ground, because the required measure must lie within the garment's length. Regarding the garment's lengthwise edge, however, one might have thought he must move inward from that edge toward the garment's width (the direction wrapped around him) but that is unnecessary. Taz's words thus state the opposite of Magen Avraham. See below regarding Taz, se'if katan 17.
