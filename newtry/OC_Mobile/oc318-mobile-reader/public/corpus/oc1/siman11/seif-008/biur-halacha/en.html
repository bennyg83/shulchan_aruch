One who bows to an animal, etc. It seems obvious to me that the same applies to an animal that was violated, that its wool is also forbidden for tzitzis, for the Gemara in Avodah Zarah 47a places them in one category; see there. If so, it is obvious that the same applies regarding a mitzvah. The Peri Megadim wrote that one who is forbidden by vow to benefit from wool is forbidden to use wool tzitzis, for we require, you shall make for yourself, from your own. But if he is forbidden by vow to benefit from his fellow, perhaps he may take his fellow's tallis, since mitzvos were not given for benefit. This is not clear, for he is benefiting from the garment itself, for otherwise kilayim would be permitted in the tallis itself. Perhaps his intent is to take tzitzis from the one from whom benefit is forbidden, and what he wrote, his tallis, was imprecise language.
To planted flax. See the Mishnah Berurah. This is specifically if the detached flax is his, for if it belongs to his fellow, even if there is no change in it at all, it does not become forbidden, for a person cannot forbid something that is not his. Even regarding the altar, most poskim hold that he cannot forbid it. See chapter 4 of Hilchos Issurei Mizbeach, halacha 5, in the Lechem Mishneh there, and in Yoreh Deah, siman 145, seif 8, in the Gra's commentary there. The Peri Megadim, in Eshel Avraham, letter 12, overlooked this.
