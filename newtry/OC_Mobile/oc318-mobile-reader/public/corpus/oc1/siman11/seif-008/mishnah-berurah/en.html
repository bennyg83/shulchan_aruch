(35) Its wool, etc. — although a worked object is forbidden only for a sacrifice but permitted for a common person, nevertheless it is forbidden for the mitzvah of tzitzit, since it is for an elevated purpose it is repugnant above, since it did not change so much, for its appearance is still upon it and it appears to be wool; not so with flax that was planted, which is like a tree — once one makes it into strands its original appearance has passed and gone.

(36) Invalid — see in siman 586 in Magen Avraham subsection 6, where he concludes that per the Mechaber there this invalidity is only lechatchilah because it is repugnant, but after the fact he fulfilled his obligation; and so wrote the Gra in this siman that after the fact it is a matter of dispute.

(37) For tzitzis — Olas Tamid wrote that wool that was on the animal at the time of bowing to it [as idolatry] is forbidden, and all the more so what grew afterward. And in Sefer Bnei Chayei he wrote the opposite, that what grew after it was worshiped is permitted [and in Be'er Heitev he wrote that the Taz also holds thus later in siman 649, seif katan 5; see there in Eliyah Rabbah and Pri Megadim].

(38) Planted — But flax that was uprooted is forbidden even for common use, like the laws of idol worship and its libations. And changing [its form] does not help, for whatever one makes from it remains as it is.
