(35) Its wool, etc. Although something worshipped is forbidden only for an offering, but is permitted for ordinary use, nevertheless it is forbidden for the mitzvah of tzitzis, since it is a need of Heaven and is repulsive before Heaven, because it has not changed so much, for its appearance is still upon it and it still looks like wool. This is unlike one who bows to planted flax, which is like a tree, and once he makes it into strings its original appearance has passed and gone.
(36) Invalid. See siman 586 in Magen Avraham s.k. 6, where he concludes that according to the Mechaber's view there, this invalidation is only initially, because it is repulsive, but after the fact one has fulfilled the mitzvah. The Gra likewise wrote in this siman that regarding after the fact it is subject to dispute.
(37) For tzitzis. The Olas Tamid wrote that wool that was on it at the time of the bowing becomes forbidden, and all the more so what grew afterward. In Sefer Bnei Chiya he wrote the opposite, that what grew after it was worshipped is permitted. In Baer Heitev he wrote that the Taz also holds this below in siman 649 s.k. 5; see there in Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim.
(38) Planted. But with flax that has been uprooted, it then becomes forbidden even for ordinary use, like the law of idolatry and its offering, and a change does not help it, for whatever you bring into being from it is like it.
