[13] Valid, etc. — it implies that if he made it from the outset not according to law it is invalid; and so the Bach wrote: whether one makes it with less than a thumb-knot's distance or more than three fingerbreadths — invalid. So it appears to me from the Gemara Menachos page 42: Rav Sama saw the corner of Ravina's cloak that was closed like a full thumb-knot; he said to him, "Does the Master not hold that the knot must be distant from the thumb-knot?" He said to him, "That was stated at the time of making" — until here. And if b'dieved it is valid, what did Rav Sama challenge? This is b'dieved! Ravina should also have answered him, "That applies l'chatchila" — rather it is invalid even b'dieved. I wrote this to exclude Knesset HaGedolah, who wrote that Mahari Faiga leaned to say b'dieved it is valid; therefore he did not mention the words of the Bach and Levush — and I proved the above. Magen Avraham wrote: if he made the hole more than three [fingerbreadths away], even though when tying the tzitzit he made the threads on the garment short and the corners folded so that it is below three — invalid. The same in reverse regarding a valid thumb-knot distance: if after making tzitzit in it he cut the hole so the tzitzit would hang below — invalid because of "you shall make" and not from what was [already] made.
