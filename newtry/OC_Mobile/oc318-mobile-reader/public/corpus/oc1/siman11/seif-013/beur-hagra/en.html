Seif 13, "eight." So Tosafos explained the phrase "and twists them," etc.

"For if," etc. In Sukkah there: but Shmuel taught, etc. The Semag and Mordechai wrote that if he does not cut them until after tying and wrapping, the tzitzis will have made themselves and are invalid because of "make, and not from what is already made." His reason is that we say on 39b: and Rav holds, etc., that both are required indispensably, as written there: techeles where he wrapped most of it is valid, and even if not, etc.; from which it follows that otherwise it is invalid. We rule like Rav, and so is the Sifrei, cited by Tosafos there, gloss beginning "or," etc. So wrote the Mordechai at the end of siman 940. We say there that the upper knot is biblical. Rashi explains: the knot after the gedil; so wrote the Nimukei Yosef there. Even if he did not wrap, etc., this is where he made a knot after the coil according to law, for a coil without a knot does not endure, and there is no gedil. The upper knot is biblical, namely the one at the end of the gedil, like Rashi's explanation, etc. Therefore what the Semag wrote, that if he wrapped, etc., refers to such a case, since with this it becomes valid. Rashi in Sukkah there, gloss beginning "tela'an," who wrote "until he made all the gedilim and their knots," was not precise as to all of them, but wrote it to emphasize Rav's point. Beis Yosef. Their reason for validating as long as it did not yet become valid through their making is that Shmuel disagrees with Rav there only because he holds cutting them is not an act; but whenever an act is done to validate, it is certainly called an act. See there, 15a: according to Beis Hillel it is understandable, etc.; if, etc.
