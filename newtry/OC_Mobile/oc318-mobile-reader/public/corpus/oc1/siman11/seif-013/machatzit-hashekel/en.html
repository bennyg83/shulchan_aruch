Beit Yosef wrote, etc., that some say one should make, etc., explaining that which we say in Menachot "on the corner invalid" — i.e. as we do, only one hole; and for the reason written in Sifrei that it is like eight tzitzit — for on every side one turns the tallit the tzitzit are visible and one can use them; therefore if one makes two holes and the tzitzit come out to one side, the tzitzit are visible only on one side and it is only four tzitzit, as they say in the Gemara Menachot folio 39.

Kilayim in tzitzit, etc. — how shall we read this? For R. Levi said: wherever you find a positive commandment and a prohibition and you can fulfill both, the positive commandment does not override the prohibition. And it is impossible that by Torah law one need not tie tzitzit — i.e. if he has only a linen tallit and must have tekhelet tzitzit in it, which is wool, nevertheless he should not tie tzitzit and there would be no kilayim, for we rule one who attaches one attachment is not a connection for kilayim.

It should be two attachments, as stated at the end of Kilayim: we learned one who attaches one attachment is not a connection and there is no kilayim in it; and one who removes on Shabbat — exempt. If he made two heads to one side (i.e. two heads of the sewing threads he brought out to one side of the garment, and it is like what Beit Hillel require for tzitzit) — connection, and there is kilayim in it; and one who removes on Shabbat — liable; so in all.

And even per Tur. I.e. not only per Rambam who holds two attachments alone, even without tying, are a connection for kilayim — certainly difficult for Beit Hillel, for the Gemara's proof of upper knot d'oraita is hidden. Rather even per Tur, that even with two attachments it is not a connection until one ties — thus the Gemara's proof that upper knot is d'oraita; if even per Beit Hillel that there are two attachments, nevertheless if he did not tie it is not a connection — still difficult for Beit Hillel in the Gemara's wording.

For we rule one who attaches one attachment, etc. — for in tzitzit there are two attachments.

And a question on Tur's view, etc. — this is not relevant to Beit Hillel's words; nevertheless in the end this will produce a difficulty for those who practice like Beit Hillel.

If he does not pass the needle except once even though he ties, etc. — and this is like how we make tzitzit with only one hole. For he rules to make one hole, unlike Beit Hillel; thus even upper knot d'oraita, nevertheless there is no kilayim in it. And see Taz siman 10 subsection 4, what he wrote to reconcile.

And one must say that when he ties two knots, etc. — thus properly there is kilayim in tzitzit per Tur, even for him who makes only one hole — for in tzitzit one must tie two knots one upon the other.

And so from the proof Rosh brings in tractate Niddah in the laws of kilayim.

From Shabbat, etc. — i.e. from this it is proven that with two attachments, even with one knot, it suffices and there is kilayim. And thus by reasoning one could say that with one attachment he agrees when there are two knots one upon the other that there is kilayim.

Unless he ties, etc. — for we say in Shabbat 74b on this that the Mishnah counts among the 39 melachot "one who sews two stitches" and challenges: but they do not endure. Rashi explained: it is not a melacha. It answers: Rav Bibi bar R. Yehuda said — when he tied them. Thus for Shabbat one is not liable for two stitches unless he tied them; and the same for undoing stitches — their law is equal. Since in the Mishnah in Kilayim Shabbat and kilayim are combined: it teaches "if he made two heads to one side — connection, and there is kilayim; and one who removes on Shabbat — liable" — it implies their laws are equal. Thus for kilayim too it is not a connection until the two attachments are tied together. Ran wrote there on this that R. Yehuda answers "when he tied them" — necessarily it deals with when he tied them only with one knot. And this is his language: and from here R. Eim learned that tying one knot does not make one liable, for it does not endure — for if liable, since one sews two stitches you do not find it without a knot, you must say he is liable for tying; or as we say — and here he did not tie except one knot is the case, end quote. And since Rosh's proof for kilayim requiring knot with two attachments is only from what we find there in Shabbat — and since in Shabbat necessarily two attachments suffice with one knot — so too for kilayim their laws are equal with one knot; see there. And we derive from this, etc., as should be. And Tosafot Yom Tov objects, etc., until see there — it should be in this subsection. And this is the explanation: per what he proved from Tur's words, that therefore he holds with two attachments one knot suffices — and this is what Magen Avraham said: we derive that for all, with two holes — i.e. two attachments — one needs only one knot. For Rambam no knot at all; for Tur who requires a knot, one knot suffices as above. Thus those who make two holes and two knots contradict each other — for they did not know at all that a knot is required for tzitzit except that otherwise you do not find kilayim in tzitzit — since they make two holes, which are two attachments, even with one knot even per Tur there is kilayim. And from where do they require two knots one upon the other? For one who makes only one hole it is well to say two knots are required, for with one knot there is no kilayim in one attachment, as Tur wrote as above. But for one who makes two holes, which are two attachments — even per Tur with one knot there is kilayim. And from where to require more?

Nevertheless one could say: even though for Tur, etc. — i.e. in any event they did not do a prohibition.

Permitted; but in Tosefet Yom Tov (chapter 7 of Shabbat Mishnah 2 s.v. avot melachot) he wrote it is proven in the Gemara per Bartenura's explanation that only two knots make one liable; see there as should be — and here is the end of this subsection. And one must say Tosefet Yom Tov proved there unlike Ran — necessarily with two attachments specifically two knots are required, for there 72b: Abaye said — this one who made a hive on Shabbat is liable for eleven chatat offerings. And if he sewed the mouths (Rashi: to make a rim for it; he added here sewing and tying that one must tie after sewing) — liable for thirteen. And it is impossible that sewing two stitches with one knot he should be liable only for twelve. And if it deals with tying two knots, how did he use a definitive expression? And if he sewed the mouth — liable for thirteen. Here you find he is liable only for twelve; or you do not find liable for sewing except when tying two knots (and regarding Ran's difficulty who holds for tying — one could say in truth every time one is liable for sewing he is liable twice, for sewing and for tying). And since for Shabbat one is not liable for two attachments except with two knots — thus for them the same law in kilayim per Rosh who holds equal to Shabbat — also not a connection except two attachments and two knots (but Tur certainly does not hold thus — for if not, Magen Avraham's difficulty returns to its place on Tur — you do not find kilayim in tzitzit per Tur). Thus those who make two holes and tie two knots — on whom should they rely? I.e. Bartenura and Tosefet Yom Tov.
