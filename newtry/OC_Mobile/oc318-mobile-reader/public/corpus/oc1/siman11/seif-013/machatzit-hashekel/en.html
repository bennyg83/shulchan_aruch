(s.k. 13) Hole. The Beit Yosef wrote, etc., that there are those who say that he should make, etc. They explain what we say in Menachos, that on the corner is invalid, as referring to the way we make only one hole, and for the reason written in the Sifrei, that it is like eight tzitzis, since whichever way one turns the tallis the tzitzis are visible and can be used. Therefore, if one makes two holes and the tzitzis emerge to one side, the tzitzis are visible only on one side, and there are only four tzitzis, as we say in the Gemara, Menachos, folio 39.
Shaatnez in tzitzis, etc. How can you say this, for Reish Lakish said: wherever you find a positive commandment and a negative commandment, and you can fulfill both, the positive commandment does not override the negative commandment. If according to the Torah there is no need to tie the tzitzis, although if one has only a linen tallis and must put techeiles tzitzis in it, which are wool, nevertheless he should not tie the tzitzis and there will be no shaatnez, for we hold that one who sews one stitch does not create a connection regarding shaatnez.
It is like two stitches, as stated at the end of Kilayim, for we learned in a Mishnah: one who sews one stitch, it is not a connection and there is no shaatnez in it, and if one removes it on Shabbos he is exempt. If he made two ends to one side, meaning he brought out the two ends of the sewing thread to one side of the garment, which resembles what the Baal HaIttur requires to be done with tzitzis, it is a connection, there is shaatnez in it, and if one removes it on Shabbos he is liable. End quote.
And even according to the Tur. That is to say, not only according to the Rambam, who holds that two stitches alone, even without tying, constitute a connection regarding shaatnez, is there certainly a difficulty, for according to the Baal HaIttur the proof of the Shas that the upper knot is according to the Torah is undermined. Rather, even according to the Tur, that even with two stitches it is not a connection until he ties, and if so the proof of the Shas that the upper knot is necessarily according to the Torah fits even according to the Baal HaIttur, since although it is two stitches, if he did not tie it is not a connection, nevertheless the wording of the Shas remains difficult for the Baal HaIttur.
For we hold that one who sews one stitch, etc. But with tzitzis there are two stitches.
And there is another difficulty according to the Tur, etc. This is not directly related to the words of the Baal HaIttur, but in the end he will derive from it a difficulty for those who practice like the Baal HaIttur.
If he passes the needle only one time, even though he ties, etc. This resembles the way we make tzitzis, with only one hole, for he rules that one makes one hole, unlike the Baal HaIttur. If so, even if the upper knot is according to the Torah, nevertheless there is no shaatnez in it. See the Taz, siman 300, s.k. 4, for what he wrote to resolve this.
And one must say that when he ties two knots, etc. If so, tzitzis indeed constitute shaatnez according to the Tur, even according to him, who makes only one hole, because with tzitzis one must tie two knots one on top of the other.
This is also implied by the proof that the Rosh brings in tractate Niddah in Hilchos Kilayim.
From Shabbos, etc. That is to say, from this it is proven that with two stitches, even one knot suffices and it constitutes shaatnez. If so, by reasoning one can say that with one stitch he concedes that when there are two knots one on top of the other, it constitutes shaatnez.
Unless he ties, etc. For in Shabbos, folio 74b, regarding what the Mishnah lists among the thirty-nine labors, one who sews two stitches, the Gemara asks: but they do not endure. Rashi explains: and it is not a labor. It answers: Rabbah bar bar Chanah said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, this is when he tied them. Thus, regarding Shabbos one is not liable for two stitches unless he tied them, and the same applies to undoing the stitches, for their law is the same. Since the Mishnah in Kilayim grouped Shabbos and kilayim together, teaching that if he made two ends to one side it is a connection, there is shaatnez in it, and if one removes it on Shabbos he is liable, it implies that their laws are equal. If so, regarding shaatnez as well it is not a connection until the two stitches are tied together. The Ran wrote there, on the answer of Rabbi Yochanan that it is when he tied them, that it must refer to tying only one knot. These are his words: from here the Re’em learned that one is not liable for tying one knot, since it does not endure; for if one were liable, then in sewing two stitches you could not find a case without a knot, and why say he is liable for sewing? Rather, necessarily as we said, here we are dealing with a case where he tied only one knot. End quote. Since the proof of the Rosh regarding shaatnez, that two stitches require a knot, is only from what we found there regarding Shabbos, and since regarding Shabbos it must be that two stitches suffice with one knot, the same applies to shaatnez, for their laws are equal, with one knot. See there. And what follows should read: and it emerges for us from this, etc. From the words, but in Tosafos Yom Tov, etc., until see there, should be at the end of this seif katan. This is its meaning: according to what he proved from the words of the Tur, it must be that the Tur holds that two stitches suffice with one knot. This is what Magen Avraham wrote: and it emerges for us from this that according to all views, with two holes, meaning two stitches, only one knot is needed. According to the Rambam no knot is needed at all, and according to the Tur, who requires a knot, it must be that one knot suffices, as above. If so, those who make two holes and two knots contradict themselves, for we know that a knot is required in tzitzis only because otherwise one could not find shaatnez in tzitzis. Since they make two holes, which are like two stitches, even with one knot it is shaatnez, even according to the Tur. From where do they know that two knots one on top of the other are required? Granted, for one who makes only one hole, one can properly say that two knots are required, for with one knot it is not shaatnez in one stitch, as the Tur wrote and as above. But for one who makes two holes, which are like two stitches, even according to the Tur one knot makes it shaatnez, so from where is there a requirement for more?
Nevertheless, one can say that even though one knot is sufficient, etc.; meaning that in any event they have not committed a prohibition.
It is permitted. But in Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 7 of Shabbos, Mishnah 2, under the heading The Primary Labors, he wrote that it is proven in the Gemara like the explanation of the Bartenura, that one is liable only with two knots. See there. This is how it should read, and here is the end of the seif katan. One must say that the Tosafos Yom Tov proved there not like the Ran, for necessarily with two stitches we specifically require two knots. For there on folio 74b, Abaye said: one who makes a beehive on Shabbos is liable for eleven sin-offerings, and if he sewed its mouth, Rashi explains, to make an edge for it, he added here sewing and tying, for he must tie after the sewing, he is liable for thirteen. If two stitches with one knot sufficed, he would be liable for only twelve. And if it refers to tying two knots, how could he state it as an absolute matter, that if he sewed its mouth he is liable for thirteen? One can find a case where he is liable for only twelve. Rather, necessarily one cannot be liable for sewing unless he ties two knots. As for the difficulty of the Ran, who holds that one would be liable for tying, one can say that indeed, whenever he is liable for sewing, he is liable for two, one for sewing and one for tying. Since regarding Shabbos one is not liable for two stitches unless there are two knots, if so according to them the same applies to shaatnez according to the Rosh, who holds that it is equal to Shabbos; it is not a connection unless there are two stitches and two knots. But the Tur certainly does not hold so, for if not, the difficulty of Magen Avraham against the Tur returns to its place, that according to the Tur one could not find shaatnez in tzitzis. If so, those who make two holes and tie two knots have whom to rely upon, namely the Bartenura and Tosafos Yom Tov.
