In the Beis Yosef in the name of the Nimukei Yosef, it implies that despair together with a change in the object applies from wool to strings. If so, why specifically from wool to strings? Even from strings that he made twisted, both can return to their original state, and according to us twisting is indispensable. Rather, it must be that even despair with a complete change in the object does not acquire in theft so long as the name of the stolen item has not changed, for the Rav says this in Choshen Mishpat siman 360, seif 9. See Sema note 14 regarding large beams that he made into small ones, and Sifsei Kohen there. Here the reason is because of change of name, wool and strings, and despair together with an inferior change of name effects acquisition, as explained in Choshen Mishpat 361, seifim 1 and 2. This applies specifically to wool. But if he had strings and twisted them, there is no change of name, for at first they were strings and now they are strings. Despair with a change in the object, even one that cannot revert, does not acquire so long as the stolen item has not changed, as the Rav wrote in siman 360, seif 6. And against. Against our master the Beis Yosef, the Taz asked on two answers. On the first answer he asked from the flow of the Rambam's language, of one who stole wool and made it into spun thread, for he wrote similarly, one may not make from thorn wool, etc., and for this reason the Beis Yosef brought the Rambam. According to the Beis Yosef's words, why did he take the case of wool before despair, which is somewhat obvious even without a verse? And even if we say that a verse is needed, why did he not teach us the essential law and a greater novelty, namely that if he stole strings after despair they are invalid, which we would not derive from wool before despair? See Eliyah Rabbah 9. Chemed Moshe was angered by the Taz, but according to what I have written, all is well, and it will be explained further below, with Hashem's help. Certainly a verse is in any event needed for strings, but as I wrote. On the second answer he asked, first, that despair with an inferior change acquires, as is explained in Choshen Mishpat 361, seif 2, and so the Derishah wondered. He asked even more strongly, that it presumably speaks where he whitened it, and if so this is a change that does not revert to its original state. Even though we rule in Yoreh Deah 333 regarding the first shearing that if he whitened it but did not dye it, he is liable and did not acquire, like the Rabbis against Rabbi Shimon at the beginning of HaGozel, that is without despair. But with despair, and a change of name together with whitening, he certainly acquired, for it no longer returns to its original state, and all the more so an inferior change together with despair also acquires. He concluded that the reason is because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Although in siman 649 it is explained that whenever he did not assist in the mitzvah there is no mitzvah that comes through a transgression, one may answer, first, as Eliyah Rabbah wrote, that spinning for its required purpose is itself the mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Furthermore, when did he acquire? At the spinning. Before the spinning it was not his, so it was not called spinning for its required purpose, for it was not his and was not in his domain. According to this, the opposite would apparently follow regarding one who stole strings and twisted them after despair, that he acquired and there is no mitzvah that comes through a transgression, if we say that twisting not for their required purpose does not invalidate after the fact. See Magen Avraham s.k. 3. However, if so it is despair alone without a change of name, for at first they were strings and now they are strings. A change in the object, so long as the name of the stolen item has not changed, like large and small beams in Choshen Mishpat siman 360, seif 6, does not acquire. This is what we wished to explain.
