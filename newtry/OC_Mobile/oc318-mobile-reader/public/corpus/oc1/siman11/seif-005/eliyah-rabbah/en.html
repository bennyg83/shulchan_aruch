It can be explained that initially he wove the threads for the sake of tzitzit and afterward wove a garment from them. See Rosh, who gives the reason that attachment must be performed for its sake. This implies that if he detached the threads from there and made tzitzit from them, they are valid, provided that they had been spun for the sake of tzitzit. Rabbeinu Yeruham, page 169, writes that the reason is that spinning must be for its sake. This is puzzling; examine carefully, for I have abbreviated. According to Rambam, Shulchan Aruch, and Levush, however, it appears that even if he detached them from there they remain invalid. [8] Because of degradation of the mitzvah, etc. At first glance this would imply that after the fact they are valid, and this is also the apparent wording of Shulchan Aruch and Levush. Taz and Nachalat Tzvi, however, interpret Shulchan Aruch to mean that even after the fact they are forbidden, in accordance with Rambam. I also found that Baal HaMaor gives another reason: the corner must be of the same material as the garment. These materials are unfit for making a garment because they are wool waste. According to this reason, they are certainly invalid even after the fact.
