In Magen Avraham, se'if katan 13: 'Even one knot constitutes a connection, etc.' At least one knot is required because otherwise it will not endure, as the Gemara asks in Shabbos, chapter Kelal Gadol. It would be like one stitch, which is not a connection because it does not endure in that form. The Gemara's question in Menachos is therefore understandable: we rule that one stitch is not a connection because it does not endure unless he knots it. It follows all the more so that at least one knot is required by Torah law. The Gemara could not simply have asked, as Magen Avraham wrote earlier, that we rule there is no connection until he knots it. No Mishnah explicitly states that two stitches require a knot. We infer it logically: since one stitch is not a connection because it does not endure, the same applies to two stitches without any knot. Tosafos Yom Tov at the beginning of Kelal Gadol, however, proves from the Gemara in accordance with Bartenura that one who sews two stitches and must knot them is liable on Shabbos also for tying. This proves that even two stitches require a complete knot. The Mishnah in Kilayim, which distinguishes between one stitch and two, is then understandable according to Rosh only if one stitch does not create a kilayim prohibition even when he ties two knots. The Gemara's statement, 'The practical difference is whether the upper knot, etc.,' is consequently difficult. We rule that one stitch is not a connection, and according to the wording 'one stitch,' even a knot does not help. It must mean that if the upper knot is required by Torah law, the rule is understandable because he passes the threads through two holes (two stitches) and the knot is effective. Why, then, did the Gemara mention at all that we rule regarding one stitch? It should have said that even two stitches do not create a kilayim connection until he ties them. One stitch is irrelevant, since according to Tosafos Yom Tov, even if the upper knot is required by Torah law it does not help a single stitch. We must therefore say that Tosafos Yom Tov does not derive the law of kilayim from Shabbos at all. Although on Shabbos two stitches require a knot, regarding kilayim no knot is needed, following Rambam; or at least one knot suffices, following Rosh as a practical ruling but not for Rosh's reason, because Rosh derives the rule from Shabbos while Tosafos Yom Tov does not accept that derivation. In any event, all agree that two stitches require no more than one knot. This is what Magen Avraham means by, 'It follows for us that according to all opinions, with two holes, etc.' It is also possible that parentheses should be inserted from the words 'But Tosafos Yom Tov' through 'It follows for us.' The phrase 'It follows for us that according to all opinions, with two holes, etc.' then means both according to Rambam and according to Rosh; Tosafos Yom Tov may also agree with Rambam that kilayim is not derived from Shabbos. See Taz, Yoreh De'ah, siman 300, se'if katan 3, who writes that according to Rambam one must distinguish kilayim from Shabbos. See also se'if katan 4, where he distinguishes according to Rosh: when one ties two garments together, tying alone is ineffective, unlike tying threads into a garment. See there. He did not, however, mention the Gemara that Magen Avraham cites concerning this.
