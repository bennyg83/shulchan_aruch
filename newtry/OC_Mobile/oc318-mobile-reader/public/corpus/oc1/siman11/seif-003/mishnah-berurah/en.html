(16) And they became sixteen — meaning that all eight strands unraveled, and for the greater novelty of the first clause he taught it; but the same applies if two strands unraveled and became four — we also require that enough remain to tie a bow, for otherwise it is invalid, as if two strands were missing from his tzitzit, which is invalid unless he was careful at the time of making that there would always be four heads on one side, as below at the beginning of siman 12.

(17) Enough to tie a bow — and even though there are those who invalidate if all of them broke, even if enough remained to tie a bow, as written in siman 12; nevertheless, regarding interweaving we rely on the first view, for the halacha is in accordance with it, as he ruled there [Magen Avraham and Pri Megadim, see there]. And know further that the Shulchan Aruch deals specifically with where each strand was interwoven into two; but if it was doubled and interwoven into four or eight, as is common nowadays, and they unraveled into sixteen, even if enough did not remain to tie a bow it is valid, for at any rate the strands remain interwoven into two. And regarding "do not add," see in Biur Halacha.

(18) It is good to tie — and there are those who say the opposite, that it is better not to tie; and it is proper to be stringent not to tie if they are interwoven well, for then it is not common that they unravel [Magen Avraham in subsection 23 and Pri Megadim, see there].
