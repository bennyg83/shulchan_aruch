(16) And they became sixteen. This means that all eight strings separated. He stated it this way as the novelty of the first clause, but the same is true if two strings separated and became four, we also require that enough remain to tie a bow. Otherwise it is invalid, for it is as though two strings are missing from his tzitzis, which is invalid unless, at the time of making them, he was careful that there should always be four ends on one side, as below at the beginning of siman 12.
(17) Enough to tie a bow. Even though some invalidate if all of them snapped, even if enough remains to tie a bow, as written in siman 12, nevertheless with respect to the twisting we rely on the first view, for the halachah follows it, as he ruled there. See Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim there. Know further that the Shulchan Aruch speaks specifically where each string was twisted into two, but if it was folded and twisted into four or eight, as is common in our times, and they separated into sixteen, even if enough did not remain to tie a bow it is valid, for in any case the strings remained twisted into two. Regarding the prohibition of adding, see Biur Halachah.
(18) It is good to tie. Some say the opposite, that it is better not to tie them. It is proper to be stringent if they are twisted well, for it is uncommon that they will unravel. This is from Magen Avraham in s.k. 23 and Peri Megadim; see there.
