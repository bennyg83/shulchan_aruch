In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 15: 'One must take care to suspend the tzitzis, etc.' According to Taz, the word 'suspend' describes their position after they have been attached to the garment: they emerge from the garment's edge and hang along its edge, lengthwise down the tallis toward the ground, as he writes in se'if katan 17. According to Magen Avraham above, se'if katan 17, 'suspended' describes the time when the tzitzis are made, as in the Gemara's expressions, 'he suspended them and afterward cut the strand ends,' and the like. That is, he should make the tzitzis toward the length of the tallis (the sides used for wrapping) and not toward its width; he should not place them toward the edge facing the ground, which is the tallis's width, as explained above. At first glance it appears that even the front tzitzis should be directed along the tallis's length, because when one raises them upon his hand they then flow over the corner. Nevertheless, since the mitzvah of wrapping applies to the body, as explained in siman 8, when he stands wrapped normally without raising his hand, tzitzis directed along the tallis's length do not flow over the corner. It is therefore preferable to direct the upper, front tzitzis along the tallis's width. Although the lower, rear tzitzis must be directed lengthwise, there is no concern that the sets differ. See Beit Yosef in Maharam's name. One can say that this depends upon the dispute between Taz and Magen Avraham over the meaning of 'the garment's length.' According to Taz, this means the garment's orientation after it is worn and two tzitzis are cast behind him: the direction from his head to his feet is called the garment's length. Both in front and behind, the tzitzis should emerge from the garment's widthwise edge and hang lengthwise down the tallis. We do not follow the interruption created by his hand, because without his hand intervening they flow over the corner along the tallis's length. According to Magen Avraham, however, 'the garment's length' is determined before wrapping and means the tallis's actual length. After he wraps with its width along his height, the edge that wraps around him is the lengthwise edge, while the lower edge that runs in the direction of its length is the widthwise edge. One then cannot say that he should direct the front tzitzis along the tallis's width so that, without his hand intervening, they flow over the corner while he is wrapped. If that were so, why does the source say to suspend them along the tallis's length? Why state a categorical rule based upon the rear tzitzis? It should instead have instructed that the front tzitzis be directed widthwise. We must conclude that the front tzitzis too are directed along the tallis's length, and his hand's intervention causes them to flow over the corner. Nevertheless, I heard from my teacher and uncle, the gaon R' Sender, of blessed memory, that he too would direct the front tzitzis along the tallis's width: when first made they faced the edge that wraps around him, so they flowed over the corner while he was wrapped without his hand moving the tallis. Although the plain wording of Tur and Beit Yosef implies Magen Avraham's view, so that all the tzitzis should be directed along the tallis's length as I wrote, Beit Yosef already cites Maharam that everything depends upon the manner in which one wraps the garment: sometimes the tzitzis must be directed lengthwise and sometimes widthwise. There is no concern if some are lengthwise and some widthwise.
