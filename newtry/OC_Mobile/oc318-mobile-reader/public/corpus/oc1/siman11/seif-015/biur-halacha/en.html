"Along the length of the tallis" — see Magen Avraham, who explains that the length of the tallis is what the person wraps himself in, and the width is his height from head to feet. The later authorities questioned this: behold with the upper tzitzis, if he makes them along the length of the tallis they will not hang on the keren so as to hang toward the ground. They answered this. Nevertheless in practice they agreed that it is better that the two upper ones be made along the width of the tallis and not its length — but all this is lekatchilah; after the fact there is no stringency, as I explained in Mishna Berurah — see there.**** END BLOCK ****

"That one should not place," etc. — see Aruch HaChayyim, who explains the reason: because we require the kind of the kanaf. So too the Gra translated the reason. It appears he does not wish to state the reason as "because of a patch on top," as the Mordechai wrote in the name of Maharam — so that we not be puzzled that the patch itself, since it is sewn, is itself the garment and becomes kanaf. Therefore he says: do we not require that the tzitzis be of the kind of the kanaf? And the Mordechai himself — his main ruling also deals specifically if the patch is not of its kind, as is proved there — see there well. If so, if this is of the kind of the kanaf, or the tzitzis is wool and linen which exempt in all cases — it is permitted [but what he wrote in his wording "any garment" is strained according to this]. And the novelty is against the later authorities who did not comment on this. And know further: what is written in Shulchan Aruch in the holes of the tallis — the intent is not specifically inside the hole, but a patch placed under the kanaf around is also included — so it is proved in Beis Yosef and Biur HaGra.**** END BLOCK ****
