“Four thumbbreadths.” We measure at the wide part of the thumb (Mordechai, Darkei Moshe). Beis Yosef wrote that Tosafos, Parashas HaTekheles, was uncertain: the measure of an average person’s thumb is about seven hairs’ width side by side, snugly, and in length two hairs with space (Rambam, Hilchos Tzitzis ch. 9).
“They have no maximum.” R. Yehudah used to make them long because of executioners’ [custom] (Mordechai).
“If it will be longer.” It appears to me: if one string is not long enough to wrap everything, he may wrap partly from one string and partly from the second, for in the era of tekheles they would make some wraps from tekheles and some from white.
“After it is tied.” Beis Yosef wrote: for tying one needs an extra thumbbreadth—thirteen beyond what lies on the garment.
