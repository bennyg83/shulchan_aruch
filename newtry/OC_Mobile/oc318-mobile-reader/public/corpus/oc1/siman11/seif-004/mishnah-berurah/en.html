(19) Four thumbbreadths. The measure of a thumbbreadth in every place is that of an average person. It is measured at the upper joint, at the broad place in its middle. See Shaarei Teshuvah. See Magen Avraham and Shach 272, who cite the Rambam that the measure of a thumbbreadth is the width of seven barley grains placed side by side tightly, and they are equal to the length of two barley grains placed loosely.
(20) And so is the custom. The Levush also ruled this way. If so, the sellers who make the length of the strings exact and narrow do not act properly, and it is close to causing a blessing in vain. For although according to the Torah any amount suffices, D'Rabbanan we require that there be twelve thumbbreadths after the tying, aside from what rests on the corner of the garment. They should be warned about this. See Peri Megadim, and see Biur Halachah.
(21) The measure. Rabbeinu Yitzchak would make it longer so that if part of it snapped, the required measure would still remain.
(22) Should be, etc. If one string is not long enough to wrap all of it with that string, he should make some windings with one string and some windings with the other string, for in the time of techeiles they would make some windings from the techeiles and some from the white. One must see that the length of all the strings remains twelve thumbbreadths after the winding and tying, as the Rama wrote, and even this long string, after it is wound, must remain twelve thumbbreadths.
(23) The tied section. It is called a tied section with reference to what it will become in the future, for it becomes a tied section after it is wound around. This is what the Rama wrote afterward, namely the smooth part, etc., that is to say, initially it is smooth, without a tied section or weaving, and through this the string becomes a tied section. Yad Ephraim.
(24) After it is tied. Because of the tying done in the tied section it becomes shorter, and an additional fingerbreadth is required, namely thirteen thumbbreadths, aside from what rests on the garment. Even after the fact this is indispensable, yet people are lenient about this, and it is proper to protest against them.
