(19) Four thumbbreadths — and the measure of a thumbbreadth everywhere is that of an average person. We measure it from the upper joint at the place where it is widest in the middle [and see in Shaarei Teshuva]. And see in Magen Avraham here, and in Shach in siman 272, who bring in the name of the Rambam that the measure of a thumbbreadth is like the width of seven barley-corns placed side by side tightly, which is the length of two barley-corns with space.

(20) And so is the custom — and so ruled the Levush; and if so, the sellers do not act properly when they are stingy with the measure of their length, and it is close to causing a blessing in vain, for although by Torah law any amount suffices, by rabbinic law they require twelve thumbbreadths after the tying, apart from what rests on the corner of the garment; and they should be warned about this [Pri Megadim]. And see in Biur Halacha.

(21) The measure — the Ri was accustomed to make them longer so that if a little broke off, enough measure would still remain.

(22) It should be, etc. — and if one strand is not long enough to wrap everything, he should wrap some wrappings from one strand and some from the second, for in the time of tekhelet they would make some wrappings from the tekhelet and some from the white. And one must see that on every strand twelve thumbbreadths remain after the wrappings and tying, as Rama wrote; and even that long strand, after he wraps, twelve thumbbreadths should remain.

(23) The gedil — they called it gedil for the future, for it becomes a gedil after it is wrapped upon it; and this is what Rama wrote afterward, "namely the smooth part," etc. — meaning that initially it is smooth without gedil and weaving, and through this strand it becomes a gedil. Yad Ephraim.

(24) After they are tied — and because of the tying they make on the gedil it shortens, and it requires an extra thumbbreadth, namely thirteen thumbbreadths, apart from what rests on the garment; and even after the fact it prevents, and people are lenient about this, and it is proper to protest against them.
