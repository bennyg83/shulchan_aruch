There is no less, etc. See the Levush, who wrote that according to the Torah there is no measure for the length of the tzitzis, except that it must have a tied section and loose thread. The Sages, however, gave us a measure because of the beauty of tzitzis, according to one view four and according to one view twelve. So Eliyah Rabbah explained his words. See Chayei Adam, who inclines to say that at least enough to tie a bow is according to the Torah; see there.
And so the practice is. See the Mishnah Berurah, where we wrote in the name of the Peri Megadim. It is proven from this that he holds that the measure of twelve thumbbreadths according to this opinion is indispensable, and he wrote so explicitly in letter 8 in Eshel Avraham. So too it is implied below in siman 12, seif 1. In the commentary of the Gaon Rabbi Meir Binet on the Mordechai in the laws of tzitzis, he is uncertain about this; see there. In the Chinuch, parashas Shelach, it also implies somewhat to be lenient in this after the fact, and the practical law requires study.

Longer. After the fact, at first glance one should not be stringent in this if, after the wrapping of the first section and its tying, it had the required measure. This is no worse than if that thread had then been completely severed, which is valid according to everyone, for these are remnants of tzitzis, as below in siman 12. According to this, what the Rama wrote, that this measure must be after it is tied, meaning all the knots as implied in the Beis Yosef, is only initially. After the fact, it is enough if after tying the first section the threads had the measure of twelve thumbbreadths, even though they were later shortened by the amount taken by the knots, for it is no worse than remnants, regarding which we rule below in siman 12 like the first opinion. See the Peri Megadim whose words I brought in Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 24, and from the plain meaning of his words it implies the opposite of my words. Perhaps his reasoning is that as long as he is still occupied in the mitzvah to make one third tied section, even though this is only initial, the mitzvah is still called unfinished, and we cannot yet apply the name remnants to the tzitzis. This is worse than if he had made only one section and afterward one thread was cut. A semblance of proof to this is from what we say in Shabbos 133b, that as long as he is occupied with circumcision on Shabbos he may return even for shreds that do not prevent validity; the explanation is that for shreds that prevent validity he may return, but for shreds that do not prevent validity he may not return. There it is with respect to leniency, and all the more so here with respect to stringency. The practical law requires study. Know further that if one is on the road and has no other tzitzis except ones shorter than twelve thumbbreadths, he may rely on the view of the Mechaber, who is lenient with four thumbbreadths, to attach them to the garment and wear it. This is especially so according to what the Levush wrote, that according to the Torah there is no measure for the length of the tzitzis, so in any case he does not transgress the positive commandment of tzitzis. All the more so according to the poskim who hold that where tzitzis are not available, one does not transgress the positive commandment of tzitzis. But regarding the blessing one should be stringent, according to our practice like the second opinion, which requires twelve thumbbreadths. So it is proven from the Peri Megadim, letter 3 in Mishbetzos Zahav, and from the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger at the end of siman 12; see there.
