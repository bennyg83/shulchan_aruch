"There is no less," etc. — see Levush, who wrote that biblically there is no measure for the length of tzitzis, only that it have gedil and anaf; only the Sages gave us a measure for beauty of tzitzis — according to one view four thumb-breadths, according to another twelve. So Eliyah Rabbah explained his words. See Chayei Adam, who sides that in any case enough for a knot is biblical — see there.**** END BLOCK ****

"And so they practice" — see Mishna Berurah in what we wrote in the name of Peri Megadim: it is proved from this that he holds the measure of twelve thumb-breadths for this view is indispensable; so he wrote explicitly in letter 8 of Eshel Avraham; so it appears below in siman 12, seif 1. In the explanation of the Gaon Maharam Bennett on the Mordechai in Laws of Tzitzis he is uncertain in this — see there. In Chinuch, parashas Shelach, it also implies somewhat to be lenient after the fact — requires investigation for the law.**** END BLOCK ****

"He may shorten it" — even if the anaf before cutting was much more than two-thirds of the gedil, nevertheless this is not called "from what is made" invalidly, since this is not indispensable after the fact, as stated below in seif 14 in Mishna Berurah in the name of the poskim. Aruch HaShulchan.**** END BLOCK ****

"Longer" — and after the fact it appears one should not be stringent if after wrapping the first coil and tying it had the measure — it does not detract that it was then cut entirely, for that thread is valid according to all as remnants of tzitzis, as below in siman 12. And therefore what Rama wrote — this measure must be after tying, meaning all the knots as implied in Beis Yosef — is only lekatchilah; after the fact it suffices if only after tying the first coil the threads had the measure of twelve thumb-breadths, even if later shortened by the measure of the knots — it does not detract from remnants, as we rule below in siman 12 as the first view. See Peri Megadim, whose words I brought in Mishna Berurah, small paragraph 24: the conclusion of his words implies the opposite of my words. Perhaps his reasoning: as long as he is still engaged in the mitzvah to make one-third gedil — even though only lekatchilah — it is not yet called that the mitzvah ended and we cannot now call the tzitzis remnants; and this detracts from if he had made only one coil and afterward one thread was cut. And as a form of proof: we say in Shabbat 133b — as long as he is engaged in circumcision on Shabbat he returns even for tzitzin that are not indispensable; he interpreted: for indispensable tzitzin he returns, for non-indispensable tzitzin he does not return. There it is to be lenient — all the more here to be stringent — requires investigation in practice. And know further: if he is on the road and has no other tzitzis but ones shorter than twelve thumb-breadths, he may rely on the Mechaber's leniency of four thumb-breadths to place them on the garment and wear it — especially per the Levush, that biblically there is no measure for length of tzitzis; thus he does not violate the positive commandment of tzitzis in any case — and all the more according to poskim that where tzitzis are unavailable one does not violate the positive commandment. But regarding blessing one should be stringent, as the custom follows the second view requiring twelve thumb-breadths — so it is proved from Peri Megadim, letter 3 in Mishbetzos Zahav, and the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger at the end of siman 12 — see there; and see what I wrote above in the name of the Gaon Maharam Bennett z"l.**** END BLOCK ****
