(11) There: and they must be twisted for their sake. If they were not twisted for their sake, even b'dieved they are invalid — Magen Avraham subsection 3; and so Ohr Torah sign 3, Rabbi Zvi sign 3, Chayei Adam general rule 11 sign 14, Chasdei David sign 2. However Machazik Berakhah wrote that even if they were not twisted at all, in a time of pressure they are valid and one blesses on them; see there. So too Neta Sorek; they brought Eliyah Rabbah sign 2, and Eliyah Rabbah agreed there. The author of the Machberet sign 2 wrote that one may rely on them when he finds no others and it is a time of pressure; they brought Shaarei Teshuva sign 2. But Lev Chaim part 1 siman 85 elaborated on this and concluded: "Therefore it appears to my humble opinion for law and practice that if they were not twisted he should not wear such a tallit, since it is invalid as the Ran holds, and per the agreement of the later authorities — the rabbi, Rabbi Yehonatan, Magen Avraham, Peri Tzaddik, and their colleagues; if so he transgresses a positive commandment by negative inference. Not so if he does not wear it — he does not transgress at all, for we hold tzitzit is an obligation on the person — meaning if one wears a four-cornered garment he must have it properly tzitzit — unlike what the Machberet wrote, that one may rely on the lenient in a time of pressure when he finds no other; see there. After the Maharal it does not appear to be lenient here, as he said a word in its reason; and the same if they were not twisted for their sake, as Magen Avraham, Ohr Torah, and Peri Tzaddik wrote — as stated — until here."
