That is within. See Eshel Avraham on Taz. At first glance he explains the length of the tallis as the length of a person's stature, and the width of the garment as that with which he wraps himself, the reverse of what the Magen Avraham wrote in note 10. The reason would be that in the width of the garment, which is the length of the tallis corresponding to a person's stature, the end of the tallis at the edge near the ground is called a corner and requires a thumb joint, whereas in the length of the garment, which is across the person's width, it is not called a corner at all, and even less than a thumb joint is valid. But one who examines the Tur and Beis Yosef cannot explain except as the Magen Avraham wrote in note 16. One must say that the word etc. refers to the final clause, and there are those who say, for the reason is that the primary corner refers to the width, while in the length it is not so fully called a corner. So it appears to me to correct the text. If so, all the more so the width requires a thumb joint. However, more than three fingerbreadths is certainly indispensable both in length and width; see Beis Yosef and Levush.
