(54) Which are called orileiza. This means what they leave as some warp threads without woof, and at the end they weave them. Some wrote that this is what we call in our language kreikes. There is no difference whether the border is along the length of the garment or along its width.
(55) It is wide. Meaning that it is more than a full thumb joint, for otherwise it would in any event be invalid, as above.
(56) Three fingerbreadths. Meaning that one should not distance the hole from the edge of the border and beyond by more than three fingerbreadths.
(57) Without the border. If the border is two or three fingerbreadths wide, he should cut off part of it. The same applies if warp threads protrude without woof, or woof without warp. There is a doubt whether they count toward the measure, and therefore he should cut them at the corners. Therefore the edge of our tallis, called shlak, which does not contain threads woven as warp and woof, should be cut before inserting the tzitzis at the corners.
