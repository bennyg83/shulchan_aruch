Which they call oreillezza — meaning what they leave of some warp threads without woof, and at the end they weave; and some wrote that this is what we call in our language krikes. And there is no difference whether the gadil is along the length of the garment or its width.

It was wide — that it was more than a full thumb-knot; for otherwise, without that, it is invalid, as above.

Three finger-widths — meaning that one should not distance the hole from the edge of the gadil and beyond more than three finger-widths.

Without the gadil — and if the gadil was two or three finger-widths wide, he should cut part of it; and similarly if warp threads protrude without woof, or woof without warp, there is a doubt whether they count toward the measure, and therefore he should cut them at the corners. And therefore the edge of our tallit, which we call sheliak, in which there are no threads woven of warp and woof, one should cut them before affixing tzitzit at the corners.
