On the Rambam — Eshel Avraham on Taz. Below in siman 32 the Mechaber wrote in seif 9 regarding the Rosh that it is valid when an Israelite stands over him and assists — implying without assistance it is not valid. If so, the Rav's wording that the custom is a Jew assists slightly should have said "and the custom follows the Rosh"; and see the reasoning of R. Akiva Eiger in Shach 5: if he did not assist at all, bedieved it is valid if he said it should not be done for its sake — and the same for tzitzis, and Eliyahu Rabbah there disagrees. And behold matters here are worth mentioning. (1) Spinning for its sake is Torah law — see Sukkah 9 and Menachot, and Levush here: "You shall make twisted cords for yourself" for your mitzvah; Magen Avraham siman 20, letter 1, on sending by a non-Jew; twisting for its sake — whether it is also Torah law or not, and it appears Torah law as well; and what he wrote that one must say explicitly, not [mere] thought — likewise tefillin 32, seif katan 92, that one must say; and in beth din R. Akiva Eiger ruled 1:39 that "he must say" implies hesitation; and Eshel Avraham YD 3 — it is doubtful whether thought suffices or specifically speech. However at the beginning of spinning it suffices, and one need not say each time, even for another day, for their silence still stands for its sake, as in Zevachim (see Mordechai, laws of tzitzis). But when he spun a little and said it does not help — what was already spun has no remedy — see Taz YD, R. Akiva Eiger, letter 3. What the Mechaber wrote to tell a woman "spin tzitzis for me" — if the woman herself said so it helps. Women are valid for spinning — Taz YD 14:1, and Levush there; Eliyahu Rabbah in the name of Maharit — it is obvious, and I do not understand the obviousness: according to sons and not daughters it fits well; unlike Rabbeinu Tam, for anything not in wearing — if so "you shall not wear shatnez," "make twisted cords for yourself for its sake" — shatnez refers to spinning and twisting (and some say combing too for its sake); here it is written "make for yourself on four corners of your garment" — why not exclude women? Anything not in wearing they cannot spin — requires further study. A minor likewise — in such a case there are two concerns (Magen Avraham 2): an apostate for appetite who does not trouble himself to make tzitzis on his garment — permitted to spin and believed regarding its sake; one who angers [God] is a heretic — thus the whole matter; see his reasoning, siman 62.

If at the beginning of spinning he said for its sake and afterward explicitly said not for its sake — invalid, and this is obvious. Even for a tallis of tzitzis in such a case, if explicitly not for its sake — explained with God's help in siman 14; and see siman 1 of shechita, Maharshal — invalid in such a case. See what I wrote in letter 2 thereof.
