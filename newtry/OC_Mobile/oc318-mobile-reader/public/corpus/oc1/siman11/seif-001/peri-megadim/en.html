According to the Rambam. See Eshel Avraham on Taz. Below in siman 32, the Mechaber wrote in seif 9 that according to the Rosh it is valid when a Jew stands over a non-Jew and assists him, which implies that without assisting it is not valid. If so, the language of the Rav, that the custom is that a Jew assists a little, should have said, and the custom follows the Rosh. See Yoreh Deah 271 in Shach s.k. 5, that if he did not assist him at all, after the fact it is valid if he told him to prepare it for its required purpose. The same applies to tzitzis. See the Beis Yosef there, who disagrees. Now. There are matters here fit to mention in this place. First, spinning for their required purpose is according to the Torah; see Sukkah 9 and Menachos, and the Levush here, who derives from, you shall make tied sections for yourself, meaning for the sake of your obligation. See Magen Avraham siman 20 s.k. 1 regarding sending through a non-Jew. Whether twisting them for their required purpose is also according to the Torah or not, I do not know, but it appears that it too is according to the Torah. As for what he wrote, that he should say explicitly and not rely on thought, this is also written in tefillin, siman 32 s.k. 17, that he should say, and in Yoreh Deah 271, Sifsei Daas s.k. 1, the wording that he should say implies that it is indispensable. See Taz s.k. 3, that it is a doubt whether thought suffices or specifically speech is required. However, at the beginning of the spinning it is enough, and he need not say it every time, even on another day, for thereafter its default status remains for its required purpose, as by offerings. See Mordechai, Hilchos Tzitzis. But if he spun a little and then said it, that does not help, for what has already been spun has no remedy; see Taz, Yoreh Deah 271 s.k. 3. As for what the Mechaber wrote, that he should say to a woman, spin tzitzis for me, it is reasonable that if the woman herself said this, it is effective. Women are valid for spinning; see Taz siman 14 s.k. 1, and so wrote the Levush there, and the Eliyah Rabbah in the name of the Yom Tov wrote that it is obvious. I do not know why it is obvious, for according to the reason of sons and not daughters this is well, but according to Rabbeinu Tam, that whoever is not included in wearing is excluded, then the juxtaposition, you shall not wear shatnez, you shall make tied sections for yourself, indicates that the required purpose applies to shatnez, spinning, and twisting, and some say even combing for its required purpose. Regarding this it is written, you shall make for yourself on the four corners of your garment. Why should women not be excluded on the rule that whoever is not included in wearing cannot spin it? This requires study. A minor too has two concerns in this kind of case. See Magen Avraham s.k. 2. An apostate for appetite, who does not trouble himself to make tzitzis for his garment, is permitted to spin, and he is believed that it was for its required purpose. But one who sins provocatively is a min for the entire Torah; see Yoreh Deah 2.
If. If at the beginning of the spinning he said that it was for their required purpose, and afterward he explicitly said that it was not for their required purpose, it is invalid, and this is simple. Even with a tallis and tzitzis in such a case, if he explicitly made it not for its required purpose, this will be explained, with Hashem's help, in siman 14. See Yoreh Deah siman 1 regarding slaughter, where according to Maharshal it is invalid in such a case. See what I wrote in note 2 about this.
