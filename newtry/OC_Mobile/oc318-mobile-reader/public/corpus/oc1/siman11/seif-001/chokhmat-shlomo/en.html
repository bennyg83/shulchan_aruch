In the gloss Beis Yosef brought the view of R' Yehuda: wherever at some time it is possible to fulfill both, then even if it happens that it is not possible to fulfill both, a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment. And there appears proof for this from the Talmud in Makos [8a], where Rava said: since if he found a felled tree he does not chop it, now too it is not a mitzvah — see there. In the entire sugya it is proved from this: whatever can be a mitzvah without a negative commandment — even if now it is impossible without the negative commandment — it is not a mitzvah and therefore does not override a negative commandment. So too regarding preparatory acts of a mitzvah: whether they override a negative commandment if considered in their time — proof also appears from the above Talmud: where if it happened it could be without these preparatory acts, then it is not considered a mitzvah at all and does not override a negative commandment for it; but whatever cannot be without these preparatory acts is considered a mitzvah, exists in its time, and overrides. So it is proved from the above Talmud; from there is clear proof for these laws, with God's help. See in my writings on the Torah, parashas Shemini [year 5533], what I wrote as proof for this, with God's help, and examine well. And behold, what everyone wondered about the above Beis Yosef from the Talmud in Shabbat 133 — what it answers "and if there is another," etc., "there is no other" — see in this in my responsa to the community of Semyatitsch in Laws of Milah; also see in my responsa on Tractate Menachos 72, what I wrote as proof for this and for what is in the above responsum — it is in my Orach Chayyim writings from year 5599; also in Laws of Shofar siman 586 we wrote from this in the sugya of the Rosh Hashana shofar "we do not pass," etc. — see there; it is in my writings of that year — see there and examine well. Here one asked me to explain the words of the Ramban in Milchamos, chapter 2 of Shabbat, who wrote the reason of Beit Shammai, who hold kilayim is forbidden in tzitzis and the adjacent verses do not puzzle him — one could say the verse comes to reverse, to say a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment. And we asked: behold, that it does not override — no verse is needed, for by reasoning it does not override, as the Talmud asks "positive overrides negative — whence do we know?" implying that by reasoning it does not override; so Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Yevamos. I answered that it fits well: to override lekatchilah a positive commandment over a negative commandment — no verse is needed, since by reasoning we do not know which of them is preferable; perhaps the negative is preferable, or both are equally good and balanced — therefore by reasoning it does not override lekatchilah; therefore for lekatchilah override a verse is needed, and it asks "whence do we know." But to reverse — regarding lashes, if he transgressed the negative and fulfilled the positive — by reasoning we would say the reverse, that they are balanced; if so, what advantage does one have over the other? Therefore after the fact he is not lashed, and there should not be lashes from doubt either; therefore a verse is needed for Beit Shammai that even after the fact he is lashed, for the negative is stricter than the positive — and this is the Ramban's intent, that a verse is needed that a positive commandment does not override a negative commandment even after the fact and he is lashed — examine well. With this one may resolve the words of the Rambam and Ramban that Tosafos brought on Yom Kippur in the sugya "they feed him the lighter of the lighter," who hold that on Shevi'is and nevelah they feed him nevelah, for the positive is stricter. In truth it is astonishing from the Talmud in Yevamos [7a], which says "positive overrides negative — no, the negative is stricter than it" — if so, explicitly reversed [and see in this in Laws of Sukkah siman 637, as the sugya of "since it is a wall for the sukkah," and in my Orach Chayyim writings from year 5561]. And now it appears thus: the Ramban, according to his method, proved this from the Talmud from his above question and answers; therefore the Rambam will hold here like the Ramban, and the difficulties of Tosafos and Ramban were hard for them — why need the adjacent verses at all? Perforce the Ramban's answer teaches that it does not override; perforce the intent is as we explained, that it teaches regarding after the fact that he is lashed — if so, it is proved that in any case the positive is equal to the negative, or perhaps the positive is stricter than the negative; therefore it is a reasonable supposition that in any case after the fact he is exempt from lashes; therefore in any case it is better to feed him nevelah from either possibility: if the positive is stricter, certainly better to feed nevelah; if they are balanced, one may feed whichever he wishes. But if we feed him Shevi'is, perhaps the positive is stricter than the negative — and Rambam and Ramban hold the Talmud on 7a says as the baraisa's explanation that the negative is stricter — R' Shimi bar Ashi says this explanation; he will hold unlike Rav Yosef on 6, but hold "whence do we know" — even one who does not derive adjacent verses in the Torah of Moses derives them; if so, one could say Beit Shammai did not derive adjacent verses; therefore one could say the negative is stricter than the positive, only that by scriptural decree it overrides; and for Beit Shammai there is no verse at all. But for us, who rule like Rav Yosef, who said even for one who does not derive adjacent verses in the Torah of Moses — he derives them; and since he said so, it is proved all hold thus — if so, Tosafos' and Ramban's question on Beit Shammai is difficult: why rely on "gedilim you shall make for yourself" for shaatnez? Perforce the Ramban's answer teaches it does not override — and it is difficult: behold we know this by reasoning without a verse; perforce it teaches regarding after the fact that he is lashed — if not for that we would say in any case after the fact he is not lashed; and it is proved from this that in any case the positive is equal to the negative and in any case does not detract from the negative; and perhaps preferable — if it detracts from the negative, by reasoning he would be lashed; since a verse was needed, it is proved they are in any case balanced, and perhaps preferable; and in this we find no dispute with Beit Shammai, for in this they do not disagree — therefore it teaches like Rav Yosef; and thereby they feed him nevelah from either possibility — examine; so it seems very correct to me in settling the method of the Rambam and Ramban — examine well. Here, since the source of "positive overrides negative" is from kilayim in tzitzis, I will set here one thought that arose in my study of Tractate Shevuos chapter 2 [15b]: "We build on Yom Tov and sanctify on Yom Tov — building the Temple does not override Yom Tov." Rashi: as it is written "you shall keep My Sabbaths" — from this they learned building the Temple does not override Shabbat; the same for Yom Tov, for Shabbat is called, etc. I was greatly puzzled by what Tosafos wrote in Yevamos [5b]: it all comes from shaatnez; and he wrote: one could say it is different there, for their mitzvah is not nullified thereby and he can burn it after Yom Tov; nevertheless, if not for the second morning I would not have divided; and that which overrides, etc. — see there. If so, the Talmud's question remains in its place: "we build on Yom Tov" — meaning building the Temple does not override Shabbat and Yom Tov refers to other Shabbat and Yom Tov, since they can be compensated afterward and the mitzvah is not nullified thereby; but on the Yom Tov of Shavuot, since we say it must be sanctified thereby specifically with chametz — then it is impossible otherwise and the mitzvah is nullified thereby — one could say well that it overrides and is entirely permitted on Yom Tov. And what does it ask? Requires investigation. Also puzzling: what it says there in Yevamos [6a] — the reason Scripture wrote "you shall keep My Sabbaths" — otherwise one would think it overrides; it answers with the prohibition of plowing, etc. According to Rashi here it should have answered that the intent is because of Yom Tov, which is called Shabbat, as written regarding the omer "the day after Shabbat," and they learned from this for the day after Yom Tov — if so, we could say the verse comes for Yom Tov, which is only a negative commandment — why need it to override the prohibition of plowing? This too requires investigation. Incidentally I will mention: puzzling what is said in the Talmud there — even per the one who says in one of these the two are one mitzvah — what does it ask? Perhaps in truth one suffices of them; and what we require two is since the mitzvah must be eaten, and in one it is impossible it be eaten lest it be disqualified by leaving over — therefore there are two, and the first is sanctified and the second eaten. In truth one suffices — this too requires investigation, examine.
