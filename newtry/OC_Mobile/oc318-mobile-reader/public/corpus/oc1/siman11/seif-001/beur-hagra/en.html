Seif 1, "the threads," etc.; "and if," etc. Like Shmuel and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for the sugya in Gittin 54 follows them, as does the sugya in Sukkah 9. There too we conclude that dyeing must be for its own sake. Rava in Sanhedrin 48 also holds like them, according to Tosafos' explanation there and there.

"And some are stringent," etc.; "and the custom," etc. Since they said only spinning. But the stringent view holds that since Shmuel requires all acts to be for their own sake, as written in Sukkah there, the same applies to combing; it mentions spinning only to exclude Rav's view.

"That he should say," etc. Although for separating terumah thought suffices, as stated in Kiddushin 41b, and derive from there, and likewise misappropriation, even though it is written "for every matter of trespass"; see Tosafos, Bava Metzia 43b, gloss beginning "hachoshev," who do not write so, and Rashi there, gloss beginning "hachoshev," etc.; and see Tosafos, Pesachim 63a, gloss beginning "Rabbi Meir," etc. Nevertheless regarding piggul we find that although it is written "it shall not be considered," speech is required. See Gittin 54b, the Rosh in Laws of Sefer Torah in the name of Rabbi Baruch, and as written below siman 32, seif 19, and Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 271 and beginning of siman 274.

"At the beginning," etc. See what I wrote in seif 2, and see Tosafos, Zevachim 2b, gloss beginning "ha," etc.; and as written in Gittin there: whoever does, etc. Although according to the conclusion this is not needed, nevertheless this is how we rule, as written in Shabbos 91a. Therefore specifically at the beginning, for we are uncertain whether we say the end proves the beginning, etc., at the end of Chullin chapter 2.

"That it is," etc. For ordinary spinning of threads is not for tzitzis. This is what he wrote: "or that he should say," etc.; then by default it is for tzitzis. See the Gemara in Zevachim there and the Mordechai there.

Seif 6, "according to the Rambam it is invalid." As stated in Gittin 23a: if so, etc.

"And according to the Rosh it is valid." As stated in Avodah Zarah 27a: although according to Rav Yehudah circumcision must be for its own sake, it is nevertheless valid when done by a gentile. The Rambam and Rosh follow their own approaches, as written below in seif 5 and see seif 14, seif 2. The Rosh wrote in Laws of Sefer Torah that the reason is that it is not comparable to a bill of divorce, where the whole essential text must be for its own sake; see Tosafos, Gittin 5b, gloss beginning "even," etc. This is unlike circumcision, Sefer Torah, tzitzis, and the like, which require only the beginning; since he performs it for a moment on the Jew's intention. But Tosafos in Gittin there, Avodah Zarah there, and Zevachim there answered that circumcision by default is for its own sake, unlike a bill of divorce, as written in Zevachim there. The same applies to Sefer Torah, since it says in HaNizakin that one wrote them not for their own sake; and also since we say there, 45b, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel requires tanning for its own sake, etc. See Tosafos, Avodah Zarah there. The same applies to tzitzis, as above. So wrote the Mordechai, Laws of Tzitzis, siman 949, and the gloss there in the name of Or Zarua Katan.

"And the custom is," etc. So wrote the Beis Yosef, that according to the Rosh the Jew must also assist, as written there. Rabbi Baruch wrote that if a gentile tans them and a Jew stands over him and assists him, this assistance is substantial, for the gentile acts with the Jew's intention, etc., as written below siman 32, seif 9. But from the Rosh's conclusion there and the words of the Baal HaIttur there it appears that this is unnecessary, and so it appears from Rabbi Baruch's own words, for he made it depend on the gentile acting with the Jew's intention. So wrote the Bach and Shach, Yoreh Deah siman 271, that "assists him" means only advice, for assistance has no substance; and this is what he wrote, "this assistance is substantial," etc., meaning the main point is that the Jew stands over him. So wrote Magen Avraham, siman 32, seif 9. But according to the Rambam and Tosafos, none of this helps.

"And they require twisting." Eruvin 96b and Sifrei, parashas Shelach: "they shall make for themselves tzitzis"; I might think he makes it as it is, therefore Scripture says, "on the tzitzis of the corner, a thread of techeles," spun and twisted. I know only that the techeles thread is spun and twisted; whence for white? You reason: since the Torah said give techeles and give white, just as techeles is spun and twisted, so white is spun and twisted. This is unlike the Rambam. See Tosafos, Yevamos 2, gloss beginning "and he said," etc., and Tosafos, Niddah 61b, gloss beginning "shua," etc.; therefore it was necessary, etc.

"And when they are," etc. As stated in Sukkah: making for its own sake; from spinning onward certainly Shmuel requires it for its own sake.
