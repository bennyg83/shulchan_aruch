"That he should say at the beginning of the spinning" — see Mishna Berurah: the reason is because of "whoever does a thing," etc. And all the more if he says that all the spinning he will spin shall be for the sake of tzitzis — it is certainly effective, and we need not further invoke the reason of "whoever does," etc. [so it is proved in Zevachim 2b, Tosafos there, gloss beginning "ha," etc.]**** END BLOCK ****

"That he should say to a woman, 'Spin for me,'" — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote: R' Levi — at the beginning of the spinning, just as for himself he needs the declaration at the beginning of the act, so too when telling a woman. One must also have intent by the woman for its sake, for otherwise of what avail is others' speech, since thereby it is not called for its sake if she truly has no intent for its sake, etc. And this and more — it is unnecessary, for the Mordechai wrote R' Levi that an Israelite performing the mitzvah needs neither further speech nor intent, but a woman in any case needs intent at her beginning for its sake [from the explanation of Maharam Bennett on the Mordechai].**** END BLOCK ****
