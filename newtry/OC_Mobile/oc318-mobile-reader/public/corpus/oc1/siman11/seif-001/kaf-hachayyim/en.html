1) [Seif 1] The threads must be spun for the sake of the mitzvah. {Rama: Some are stringent to require even combing them for the sake of the mitzvah, but the custom is to be lenient concerning combing.} Agur writes that conscientious individuals in Ashkenaz follow the stringent opinion. Darkei Moshe, entry 1, cites him.
2) There. “He should say at the beginning of the spinning,” etc. If, while spinning, he did not say aloud that he was spinning for the sake of the mitzvah of tzitzis but intended it in his heart, it may be validated after the fact, as I wrote in my sacred work Kol Yaakov concerning the tanning of hides, siman 271, entry 5; see there. The same applies here, because the Gemara, Menachos 42b, compares the two; see there. See the aforementioned work, siman 274, entry 1.
3) Concerning when a situation is considered after the fact: some say that once the particular act has been performed, it is considered after the fact. Thus, here, once the thread has been spun, it is an after-the-fact case and the thread may be attached to the garment. Others say that it is not considered after the fact until the entire matter has been completed, which here means that the tzitzis have already been attached to the garment. This is discussed in the aforementioned work regarding a parchment sheet of two columns, siman 272, entry 7; see there. The same applies here. Therefore, one who guards his soul should distance himself from this and should use such tzitzis only in pressing circumstances.
4) There. “If they were not spun for the sake of the mitzvah, they are invalid.” Chesed LaAlafim, entry 2, writes that one must take great care not to purchase tzitzis from just anyone, but only from people whom he knows are proficient in making them for the sake of the mitzvah, lest his blessing be in vain and he fail to fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis.
