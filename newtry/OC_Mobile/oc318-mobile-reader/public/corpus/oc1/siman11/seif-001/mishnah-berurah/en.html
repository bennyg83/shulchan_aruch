The strands — the Rambam's wording: one who finds tzitzis strings in the marketplace, even if cut to size and twisted, they are invalid, for a person will trouble himself to make strings like tzitzis, and if so perhaps they were not made for the sake of tzitzis. The Maggid Mishneh disagrees, and Kesef Mishneh also wrote that the Rambam in a responsum later retracted — these are the words of Magen Avraham. The Gra in his commentary in siman 301 wrote that the law follows the Rambam as he wrote initially — see there.

Spun for the sake — and this is from the Torah, as it is written “You shall make for yourself tassels” for the sake of your obligation.

To be lenient regarding combing — for what is written “you shall make for yourself” means from spinning onward, for then is the main making of the gdilim; and see in the Prisha in the name of the Maharal of Prague who agrees that lechatchilah one should be stringent to comb for the sake.

Should say - Explicitly [and] not [just have it] in his mind. And even after the fact (ie. if he spun them without stating out loud that he is doing it for the sake of the mitzvah), it requires further analysis if having it mind was effective. And see further in Siman 32, Se'if 9 and 19 and in Chiddushei Rabbi Akivah Eiger there.

At the beginning — and this suffices even for another day, for presumptively the later [spinning] is still for the sake, for whoever does acts per the first [intention] unless he afterward said explicitly that he is doing it not for the sake.

The spinning — and if he spun a little and afterward said [for the sake], it does not help for what he already spun, for we are in doubt whether we say the end proves the beginning. And if at the beginning he also thought it would be for the sake but did not say explicitly, it seems to me one can lean to leniency here, for it is a sfek sfeka.

Say to a woman — meaning only at the beginning of spinning. And likewise if the woman herself says she spun for the sake of tzitzis it helps, and she is believed on what she said, unlike a non-Jew and a deaf-mute, insane person, or minor who are not believed. A habitual sinner out of appetite who does not trouble himself to make tzitzis on his garment may spin and is believed when he says he spun for the sake, and all the more if he transgresses only other sins out of appetite; but one who sins to provoke is an apostate regarding the entire Torah and certainly acts for his own intent per all opinions.
