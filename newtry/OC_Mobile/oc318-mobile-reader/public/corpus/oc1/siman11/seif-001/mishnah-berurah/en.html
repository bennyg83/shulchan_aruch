The strings. The wording of the Rambam is: one who finds tzitzis strings in the marketplace, even if they are cut and twisted, they are invalid, because a person troubles himself to make strings resembling tzitzis, and if so perhaps they were not made for the sake of tzitzis. The Maggid Mishneh disagrees, and the Kesef Mishneh also wrote that the Rambam later retracted this in a responsum. End quote from the Magen Avraham. The Vilna Gaon, in his commentary in siman 301, wrote that the law follows the Rambam as he wrote originally. See there.
Spun for their sake. This is according to the Torah, as it is written, you shall make tassels for yourself, meaning for the sake of your obligation.
To be lenient regarding combing. For what is written, you shall make for yourself, applies from the spinning onward, for then the essential making of the tassels begins. See the Perishah in the name of the Maharal of Prague, who agrees that initially one should be stringent to comb it for its required purpose.
That he should say. Explicitly, not merely in thought. Even after the fact, it requires study whether thought is effective. See siman 32, seif 9 and seif 19, and the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger there.
At the beginning. This is sufficient even for another day, for presumably it continues as being for its required purpose, since anyone who acts does so according to his original intention, unless afterward he explicitly said that he is doing it not for its required purpose.
The spinning. If he spun a little and afterward said it, it does not help for what was already spun, for we are uncertain whether we say that the end proves the beginning. If at the beginning as well he thought that it would be for its required purpose, but did not say so explicitly, it appears to me that there is room to be lenient in this, for it is a double doubt.
That he should say to a woman. This means only at the beginning of the spinning. The same applies if the woman herself says that she is spinning for the sake of tzitzis, for that is effective, and she is also believed that she said so. This is not so regarding a non-Jew, a deaf-mute, an insane person, or a minor, who are not believed. A mumar out of appetite, who does not trouble himself to put tzitzis in his garment, may spin, and he is believed when he says that he spun for their required purpose; all the more so if he violates only other transgressions out of appetite. But a mumar to provoke is an apikores regarding the entire Torah, and certainly acts with his own intention according to all views.
