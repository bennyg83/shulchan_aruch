(s.k. 1) The strings, the wording of the Rambam, etc. See siman 301, seif 42, where he wrote as follows: One who finds tefillin on Shabbos in a disgraceful place, etc.; if they have straps, by which it is recognizable that they are tefillin and not amulets, and they are tied so that he can wear them, he brings them in pair by pair in the manner of wearing. This too is the wording of the Rambam in chapter 19 of Hilchos Shabbos, and its source is in Eruvin, folio 96b: Rabbi Elazar said, one who finds techeiles in the marketplace, if they are strings, they are valid. The Shas asks, perhaps they were spun with a garment in mind, and thus not for their required purpose. It answers that they were twisted, since it is not usual to weave a garment with twisted threads. It asks further, perhaps he twisted them for the edge of a garment, for at the edge of a garment one does weave with twisted threads. It answers that they were cut into strings in the manner of tzitzis strings, for people certainly do not trouble themselves to that extent. Rava said: does a person trouble himself to make an amulet resembling tefillin, and nevertheless we are concerned for this? The same applies to tzitzis strings, for we learned in a Mishnah: one who finds tefillin brings them in pair by pair. When is this said? With old ones, but with new ones he is exempt. According to the Rambam, one must say that the conclusion is that new means that they do not have straps, or are not tied, unlike what Tosafos wrote there. Thus, regarding new ones we are concerned lest one troubled himself to make an amulet resembling tefillin. Rava said, if we are concerned that a person troubles himself, etc., it is a dispute of Tannaim, for it was taught: one who finds tefillin brings them in pair by pair, whether new or old; these are the words of Rabbi Meir, showing that he does not say that one troubles himself, etc. Rabbi Yehudah forbids new ones and permits old ones, showing that Rabbi Yehudah is concerned that people trouble themselves, and the Mishnah follows Rabbi Yehudah, while Rabbi Elazar holds like Rabbi Meir. Since regarding Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah we rule that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah, and also the anonymous Mishnah is like Rabbi Yehudah, it follows that we say a person troubles himself, etc. Therefore he ruled regarding Shabbos that new ones are forbidden, because we are concerned for an amulet, and likewise regarding tzitzis he ruled not like Rabbi Elazar, that even if they are twisted and cut we are concerned that he troubled himself, etc., and they are invalid. The Raavad challenged him, and the sages of Lunel also asked him about this, since that is not the conclusion of the Shas there. For it says there: now that the father of Shmuel bar Yehudah taught, which are old? Any that have straps and are tied. New means that they have straps but are not tied. According to all views, a person does not trouble himself; rather, Rabbi Yehudah follows his own reasoning, that a bow is considered a knot and one is liable for it on Shabbos, and therefore he forbids new ones, since one cannot bring them in by wearing them. Rabbi Meir holds that a bow is not a knot, and therefore one can bring them in by wearing them through a bow. If so, the ruling of Rabbi Elazar concerning techeiles is according to all views, that when they are twisted and cut they are valid. He responded to them: I erred, and correct your books as follows: cut ones are invalid; twisted ones are valid. So wrote the Kesef Mishneh in chapter 2 of Hilchos Tzitzis. See the Beit Yosef, siman 20, who wrote that the Rif and the Rosh omitted this law of Rabbi Elazar, one who finds techeiles, and it appears that their reason is because they hold it was stated regarding techeiles, but regarding white strings we do not have it; and perhaps the Rambam also holds so. The intent of the Beit Yosef is to say that regarding white strings they were more stringent, and according to all views they are invalid, for only techeiles is expensive, and presumably was made for the sake of tzitzis and certainly fell from an expert, as Tosafos wrote there, since a non-expert is not accustomed to deal with them because he knows people will not buy from him; therefore we assume they are certainly valid. This is not so with white strings. However, one can argue the opposite, that regarding white strings all agree they are valid, for specifically by techeiles, where there is concern that the spinning was not for its required purpose and also concern that the dyeing was not for its required purpose, they were more stringent than with white strings.
