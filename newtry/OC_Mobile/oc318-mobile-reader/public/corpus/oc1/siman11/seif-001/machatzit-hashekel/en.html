(subsection 1) "The threads" — Rambam's wording, etc.; and in siman 301 seif 42 he wrote there, and this is his language: "One who finds tefillin on Shabbat in disgrace," etc. — if they have straps by which it is recognizable that they are tefillin and not amulets, and they are tied so one can wear them, he brings them in pair by pair by way of wearing, end quote. These are also Rambam's words in chapter 19 of Hilchot Shabbat, and its source is Eruvin 96b: R. Eliezer — one who finds tekhelet in the market, threads are valid. The Gemara challenges: perhaps he spun them for a tallit, and they are not for their purpose. It answers: in twisted threads, for it is not customary to weave a tallit with twisted threads. It challenges again: perhaps for the edge of the tallit he twisted them, for at the edge of the garment one weaves with twisted threads. It answers: in broken threads, like tzitzit threads — for thus people certainly do not trouble themselves. Rava said: would a person trouble himself to make an amulet like tefillin? — yet we are concerned. The same applies: one should be concerned about tzitzit threads. For we learned: one who finds tefillin brings them in pair by pair — in what case? With old ones, but with new ones he is exempt. Per Rambam one must say that he concluded that new ones are called those without straps or that are not tied, unlike Tosafot there — thus new ones we are concerned lest one troubled to make an amulet like tefillin. Rava said: if we are concerned that a person troubled, etc. — it is a tannaitic dispute, for it teaches: one who finds tefillin brings them in pair by pair — one new and one old, the words of R. Meir (thus we do not say "troubled," etc.). R. Yehuda forbids with new and permits with old — thus R. Yehuda is concerned that people trouble, and the baraita follows R. Yehuda, and R. Eliezer follows R. Meir. Since R. Meir and R. Yehuda — we rule halachah follows R. Yehuda, and also an unattributed Mishnah follows R. Yehuda — thus we say a person troubles, etc. Therefore he ruled for Shabbat that new ones are forbidden, for we are concerned about an amulet; and likewise for tzitzit he ruled unlike R. Eliezer, for even twisted and broken threads we are concerned that one troubled, etc., and they are invalid. Raavad challenged, and also the scholars of Lunil asked about this, for the Gemara's conclusion there is not so — and see there. Now that the father of Shmuel taught in the name of R. Yehuda: these are old — any that have straps and are tied; new — that have straps but are not tied — for all hold a person does not trouble, except R. Yehuda for his reason that wrapping is a knot and one is liable for it on Shabbat; therefore he forbids new ones, for one cannot bring them in by way of wearing. R. Meir holds wrapping is not a knot, therefore one can bring them in by way of wearing through wrapping. Thus R. Eliezer's ruling for tekhelet is according to all, that twisted and broken are valid; and they answered him: I erred — correct your books: thus broken threads are invalid, twisted are valid. So too Kesef Mishnah chapter 2 of Hilchot Tzitzit, and in Beit Yosef siman 20 he wrote, and this is his language: and Rif and Rosh omitted this law (of R. Eliezer who finds tekhelet), and it appears their reason is that they hold it was stated regarding tekhelet but for white we have nothing; and perhaps Rambam also holds thus, end quote. The intent of Rabbeinu Yosef — i.e. for white they were more stringent, and for all invalid, for only tekhelet whose price is dear and presumptively made for tzitzit, and certainly fell from an expert — as Tosafot wrote there: one who is not an expert is not accustomed to deal with them since he knows they do not buy from him; therefore he relied that certainly they are valid, unlike white. However one could say reasoning to the contrary: for white all are valid, for only tekhelet where there is concern of spinning not for its purpose and also concern of dyeing not for its purpose they were more stringent than white.
