Seif 6, "and precisely," etc. In the Yerushalmi, cited by the Maggid Mishneh: this applies where one stole a lulav or shofar, etc., and as written below at the end of siman 454 and beginning of siman 649.
"However," etc. The Nimukei Yosef wrote that it is because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. But in Sukkah chapter 3 it implies that in such a case there is no mitzvah-through-transgression problem even initially, as written regarding those merchants: let them acquire it through change of name, etc.; thus even initially it is permitted, as Tosafos wrote there. Rather, because of the blessing it is prohibited initially, as written below, and regarding blessing, as with terumah for a mute, naked person, or one who cannot hear. Nevertheless the above question remains difficult. I also found that Magen Avraham in siman 649 left it unresolved. It seems that there, since it is only a concern regarding being called a blasphemer, and he already acquired it, we are not concerned, as Magen Avraham wrote there, small paragraph 5; unlike a definite case. For fulfilling the mitzvah we are concerned even for doubt, unlike a blessing on a stolen item, which is only an asmachta, and all the more where he has already acquired it, even to fulfill the mitzvah.
