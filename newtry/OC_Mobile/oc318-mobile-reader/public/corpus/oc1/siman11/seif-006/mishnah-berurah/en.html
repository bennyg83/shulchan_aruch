(27) For themselves, from their own — it appears straightforward that the same applies if one bought tzitzit on credit and the seller comes and demands payment and this one does not want to pay him, for by law they are not his, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 190, see there, and all similar cases; if so, he does not fulfill his obligation with these tzitzit. And see in Biur Halacha.

(28) And specifically, etc. — and then, even if the owners despaired, it is invalid, for despair does not acquire. And even if the strands were not interwoven and he interwove them, nevertheless this is not considered a change, for its name did not change thereby [Pri Megadim].

(29) Where he stole the strands, etc. — see in Magen Avraham subsection 10; and per his words this gloss argues on the Mechaber, for he holds that the verse limited even if he made the strands from stolen wool.

(30) They are valid — Taz explains that Rama deals with a case where there was also despair, and he acquired through despair and change of name, for initially it was wool and now it is strands. But Magen Avraham wrote that from Rama's ambiguity it appears that he holds that even before despair it is valid, for he acquired through change of action; and so it is implied from the Gra in his explanation, that the reason is because of change of action, see there. And if he stole strands and sold them to another before despair, the second one does not fulfill his obligation with these tzitzit, for change of domain without despair does not acquire, and we do not call it "and they shall make for themselves" [Pri Megadim]. And if after it came into the second one's hands the owners despaired, see in Choshen Mishpat siman 353, seif 3, for there are varying opinions about this. And if they despaired and afterward he sold them, the second one acquired the tzitzit; and even regarding a blessing it appears from Magen Avraham in siman 649, subsection 2, that he may bless on it and it is not a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since he did not steal; and so ruled Machatzit HaShekel. However, Pri Megadim cites the Levush as stringent on this, and Taz in siman 25 and the Gra there are stringent as well.

(31) To make them — meaning, to affix these tzitzit on his garment, because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression; and see in Biur Halacha.

(32) Siman 649 — for he ruled there that one may not bless on it; and see in Shaarei Teshuva and Biur Halacha.
