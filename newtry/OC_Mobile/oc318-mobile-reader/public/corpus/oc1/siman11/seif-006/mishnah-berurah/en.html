(27) For them from their own. It appears obvious that the same law applies if one bought the tzitzis on credit, and the seller goes in and out demanding the money, and this buyer does not wish to pay him. According to the law they are not his, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 190; see there. In any such case, then, he has not fulfilled the law with the tzitzis. See Biur Halachah.
(28) And specifically, etc. Then, even if the owners despaired of recovery, it is invalid, for despair does not effect acquisition. Even if the strings were not twisted and he twisted them, nevertheless this is not considered a change through which acquisition is effected, for their name has not changed through it. See Peri Megadim.
(29) Who stole the strings, etc. See Magen Avraham s.k. 10. According to his words, this gloss disputes the Mechaber, who holds that the verse excludes even if he made the strings from stolen wool.
(30) They are valid. The Taz explains that the Rama is speaking where there was also despair, and he acquires through despair together with a change of name, for at first it was wool and now it is strings. But the Magen Avraham wrote that since the Rama wrote without qualification, it implies that he holds that even before despair it is valid, for he acquires through a change in the object. This also appears from the Gra in his commentary, that the reason is due to a change in the object; see there. If one stole strings and sold them to another before despair, the second person has not fulfilled the mitzvah with the tzitzis, for transfer to another domain without despair does not effect acquisition, and we do not apply to it the verse, and they shall make for themselves. See Peri Megadim. If the owners despaired after it came into the second person's possession, see Choshen Mishpat siman 353, seif 3, where there are differing views about this. If they despaired and afterward he sold them, the second person acquires the tzitzis, and even regarding the blessing it appears from Magen Avraham in siman 649 s.k. 2 that he may recite a blessing over them and there is no concern of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression, since he did not steal them. So ruled the Machatzis HaShekel. However, the Peri Megadim there, in the name of the Levush, is stringent about this, and the Taz in siman 25 and the Gra there are also stringent about this.
(31) To make them. That is to say, to insert these tzitzis into his garment, because this is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. See Biur Halachah.
(32) Siman 649. For he ruled there that one may not recite a blessing over it. See Shaarei Teshuvah and Biur Halachah.
