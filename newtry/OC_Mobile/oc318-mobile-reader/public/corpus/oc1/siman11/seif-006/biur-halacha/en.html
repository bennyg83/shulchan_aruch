If he made them, etc. See the Peri Megadim, who wrote that the same applies if one stole a tallis and attached his own tzitzis to it: he also did not fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis by wearing it, and consequently his blessing is in vain, for the verse says your garment. See what I wrote in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 27, regarding borrowing tzitzis; the same applies regarding a tallis.
From stolen wool. I was uncertain about one who stole tzitzis threads from another and, after attaching them to his garment, paid him for them or received them from him as a gift: is he required to untie them again? Shall we say that since it is written, they shall make for themselves, we require that at the time of making they be his, or is the matter revealed retroactively? This requires great study. Afterward I found a similar case in siman 658 regarding lulav, in Shaarei Teshuvah, s.k. 6; see there. See also what he wrote there at the beginning of the siman in the name of the book Machaneh Ephraim. From there we also learn for our matter, since we require yours according to the Torah, that one who is meticulous in his actions should be careful to pay for the tzitzis before attaching them to the garment, so that a Torah-level acquisition be in his possession, and he should not rely on meshichah alone; see there.
And specifically when he stole the threads. See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote even if the owners despaired, and this is from the Beis Yosef. At first glance this is difficult, for here there is also a change of name, since originally they were threads, and now, after he attached them to the garment, the lower wrappings cannot be called threads at that place, but only a tied section, as is proven in Menachos, folio 39a, where it says that the beauty of techeiles is one third tied section, etc., and below there, either strand or tied section. Even Rav also admits to the actual term tied section in general, as is clear to one who examines there. If so, even if the change can revert to its original state when he unties them, combined with despair he should acquire them. Perhaps one can say that it is still called a tied section of threads, similar to what they said in Bava Kama 96a that palm fragments are called palm-fragments. Or perhaps, since it is written, they shall make for themselves, from their own, we require that they be his at the beginning of the making, namely the hanging on the garment, as stated below at the beginning of siman 14; see Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim there.
But, etc., the threads are valid. See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote in the name of the Magen Avraham that he holds it is a change through an act. The intent of the Magen Avraham is because of the whitening, as he brought from siman 360, seif 6, which speaks of whitening. In truth there is no proof at all from there, according to what the Shach wrote there, that ordinary whitening does not acquire; see there. The later authorities have already challenged the Magen Avraham and the Taz on this. But in my humble opinion there is a very great difficulty on the Beis Yosef and the Nimukei Yosef: why did they not write that it is a change through an act because of the spinning? One cannot answer as they wrote, that it is a change that can revert to its original state, for this is not so to one who examines the sugya in Bava Kama 93b, where it discusses one who sheared each one separately, dyed each one separately, spun it, etc. In the conclusion there, one will see explicitly that even Rabbi Shimon, who holds that dyeing is considered a change that can revert, admits regarding spinning. Also, from the fact that at the beginning of the sugya it answers, with spun wool, etc., and does not answer, with whitened wool, it implies that he would acquire it through spinning. And that which it said at the beginning, if he whitened them, no, and it raised a contradiction, etc., it asked in a stronger way, that even through whitening, which is the beginning of everything, he should acquire. Afterward, with Hashem's help, I found in the book Nahar Shalom that he noted all this. So too I found in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kama there, explicitly, that he rules regarding whitening that he does not acquire, but if one stole wool and spun it he acquires through the force of the above sugya, even though with a reversible change he first ruled there that one does not acquire at all; see there.
To make them. See the Mishnah Berurah, where I wrote that the reason is because it is a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. Although he had already acquired them beforehand through despair and change, and the transgression does not come from the mitzvah itself, nevertheless since here it comes through the spinning, and spinning is part of the mitzvah, for we require spinning for its required purpose, it follows that the acquisition comes through the mitzvah. Eliyah Rabbah. In the commentary of the Gra it implies that he does not distinguish in this, and nevertheless as practical law he also agreed that it is forbidden initially to attach the tzitzis to the garment, but for another reason, namely that afterward he will not be able to bless. For regarding a blessing, which contains mention of Hashem's Name, the matter is more severe, as the Beis Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran in siman 649. It is no better than one who hears but does not speak, who is not permitted to separate terumah because of the blessing.
And regarding the blessing, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah. See Shaarei Teshuvah, who wrote that according to the words of the Magen Avraham there in his answer regarding the case of avocado merchants, if there was despair together with a change of name, it is permitted to bless. The same applies to despair and a change through an act, or despair and a change of domain, as is proven from the Magen Avraham there. However, from the words of Chok Yaakov in siman 454, and from the words of the Gra in this siman in what they answered regarding that case, it is proven that even after despair and change of name, or change through an act, it is forbidden to bless. Although from the Gra in our matter it is not explicit except regarding despair and change of name, it appears that the same applies to despair and change through an act, for what difference is there? Afterward I found in siman 25 in the Taz, s.k. 14, that it is also explicitly proven from him that he holds that even after despair and change of name and change through an act, like the case in our matter regarding threads according to the reasoning of the Taz at the end of s.k. 5, or through despair and change of domain, it is forbidden to bless. In the Gra I also found there that he explicitly wrote that the same applies through despair and change of domain, that it is forbidden to bless.
