"If he made them," etc. — see Peri Megadim, who wrote that the same applies if he stole a tallis and placed his own tzitzis on it — he also does not fulfill the mitzvah of tzitzis by wearing this (and his blessing is thereby void), for "your garment" is written in Scripture. See what I wrote in Mishna Berurah, small paragraph 27, regarding wrapping tzitzis — the same applies to a tallis.**** END BLOCK ****

"Stolen wool" — I was uncertain regarding one who stole tzitzis threads from his fellow and after placing them on his garment paid him for them or gave them as a gift — is he obligated to return and untie them? Shall we say: since it is written "and they shall make for themselves," we require that all seven acts of making be his? Or will the matter be revealed retroactively? Doubtful — requires investigation. Later I found something similar in siman 658 regarding lulav in responsa, small paragraph 206 — see there. See also what he wrote there at the beginning of the siman in the name of Machaneh Ephraim — from there we learn for our matter too that biblically "for you" requires that one meticulous in his actions see to pay for the tzitzis before placing them on the garment, so there be Torah acquisition under his hand, and he not rely on pulling alone — see there.**** END BLOCK ****

"And specifically that he stole the threads" — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote: even if the owners despaired — and this is from Beis Yosef. Apparently it is difficult, for there is also change of name: initially threads, and now after placing them on the garment with wrappings below we cannot call that place threads but gedil, as proved in Menachos 39a, where it says "the beauty of techeles — one-third gedil," etc., and below there "or thread or gedil," and even Rav admits regarding the essence of the name gedil in general, as proved for one who investigates there. If so, even though the change reverts when untied, with despair combined it acquires. Perhaps one could say gedil of threads is so called — like what they said in Bava Kamma 96a regarding palm trunks. Or perhaps since it is written "and they shall make for themselves," we require they be theirs at the beginning of the making, which is placing on the garment — as stated below at the beginning of seif 14 — see there in Magen Avraham and Peri Megadim.**** END BLOCK ****

"But," etc. — "threads are valid" — see Mishna Berurah in what he wrote in the name of Magen Avraham, who holds it is change through action; Magen Avraham's intent is because of bleaching, as he brought from siman 340, seif 6, which deals with bleaching. In truth this is no proof at all from there, according to what Shach wrote there that ordinary bleaching does not acquire — see there. The later authorities already challenged Magen Avraham and Taz on this. But in my humble opinion it is very difficult on Beis Yosef and Nimukei Yosef: why did they not write it is change through action because of spinning? And one could say, as they wrote, that it is a reverted change — this requires investigation in the sugya in Bava Kamma 93b: "shear first and dye first and spin," etc. In the conclusion there it appears explicitly that even R' Shimon, who holds dyeing is considered a reverted change, admits regarding spinning; and from what they answered there at the beginning of the sugya with spun wool, etc., and did not answer with bleached wool — it implies he would acquire through spinning. And what he said at first "let us bleach them" — they challenged, etc., for emphasis: it is difficult even through bleaching, which is the beginning of everything, that he should acquire (later I found in Az Nafshi in Nahar Shalom he commented on this). So I found in Yeshuos Shalom on Bava Kamma there explicitly: he rules regarding bleaching that one does not acquire, and stolen wool that he spun — he acquires from the above sugya, even though regarding reverted change he ruled there at first that one does not acquire at all — see there.**** END BLOCK ****

"To make them" — see Mishna Berurah, who wrote because of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. And although he already acquired them initially through despair and change, and the transgression does not come from the mitzvah itself — nevertheless, since here it comes through spinning, and spinning is from the mitzvah, for we require spinning for its sake — thereby acquisition comes through the mitzvah [Eliyah Rabbah]. In Biur HaGra it appears he does not distinguish in this. Nevertheless for the law he also agreed it is forbidden lekatchilah to place tzitzis on the garment — but for another reason, for he will not be able to bless afterward [for regarding blessing, which contains God's name, it is stricter, as Beis Yosef wrote in the name of Ran in siman 649]. And it is not better as it sounds, and it is not that one may not tithe because of the blessing.**** END BLOCK ****

"And regarding blessing," etc. — see Mishna Berurah; see Sha'arei Teshuvah, who wrote according to Magen Avraham there in his answer on those merchants: if there was despair with change of name, it is permitted to bless (and the same for despair with change of action or despair with change of domain, as proved in Magen Avraham there). However, from Chayei Adam in siman 494 and from the Gra in this siman in what they answered on those merchants — it is proved that even after despair and change of name or change of action it is forbidden to bless. Although in the Gra in our matter it is not proved explicitly except despair and change of name — it appears the same for despair and change of domain: what difference is there? Later I found in siman 25, Taz, small paragraph 14, proved from him explicitly that he holds even after despair and change of name and change of action — as in our matter regarding threads, according to Taz's view at the end of small paragraph 5 — or through despair and change of domain it is forbidden to bless. In the Gra I found there he also wrote explicitly that the same applies through despair and change of domain — it is forbidden to bless.**** END BLOCK ****
