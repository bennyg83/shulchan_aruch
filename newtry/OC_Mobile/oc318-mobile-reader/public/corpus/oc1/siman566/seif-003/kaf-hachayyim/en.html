3) Ibid. He concludes: "Blessed are You, Hashem, Who answers His people Yisrael in a time of distress." This is the text in the Tur and Levush, and this is the text in the Sephardic siddurim. However, the text in Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchos Taaniyos, law 14, is: "Who answers in a time of distress." This is also the text in Rambam's order of prayer, in Avudraham, 94b, and in the Ashkenazic siddurim, as written by the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, Eliyah Rabbah, os 1, and Mishna Berurah, os 2. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 126, distinguished between cases and ruled that in the blessing of Aneinu recited by the shaliach tzibbur on fasts such as the four fasts commemorating the destruction and the like, he should say, "Who answers His people Yisrael in a time of distress." When there is a distress that encompasses both the nations of the world and Yisrael, he should say, "Who answers in a time of distress"; see there.
