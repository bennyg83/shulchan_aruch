(7) However, if they established the fast, etc. - that is, if the congregation decreed a fast; to exclude the fasts written [in Scripture]: even if they fall on Monday or Thursday they push aside the weekly portion and read Vayechal according to all opinions.
(8) They do not push aside the weekly portion - for it is frequent, and a fast that did not spread throughout Israel is not important enough to push aside the weekly portion. And see Eliyah Rabbah, who brought in the name of Malbushei Yom Tov that his opinion as law is to rule like the Tur and like the opinion of Rav Sar Shalom, that they push aside the weekly portion and read Vayechal; but nevertheless, since they practiced so, one should not change the custom. And see Biur HaGra that his opinion too as law is like Malbushei Yom Tov, and he wondered at the Rama; see there. However, in Eliyah Rabbah he leans to rule like the Rama, for so it is found in the Agudah in the name of his teachers; see there. Nevertheless after the fact, if they began to read Parashas Vayechal, one should not go back, as it appears to my humble opinion.
(9) Rather at Shacharis they read in the [weekly] portion - Nevertheless the chazzan says Aneinu between Goel and Rofei at Shacharis and Minchah.
(10) Only BeHaB that they fast after Pesach, etc. - because these fasts were established in all the lands, and those who do not fast are the minority; which is not so for other decrees of a fast on a particular province, then they do not push aside the weekly portion on account of Vayechal. And the same law applies to the fast of the 20th of Sivan: one should also read Vayechal even at Shacharis, even when it falls on Monday or Thursday, for this fast is fixed and spread more in Israel than Monday and Thursday after Pesach and Sukkos (Taz and Lechem Chamudos). And it appears to me that today the fast of the 20th of Sivan is not so fixed that only individuals fast on it; and likewise the BeHaB fast after Pesach and Sukkos has the law of other fasting, and they do not push aside the weekly portion at Shacharis, and toward evening they read Vayechal. And if the 20th of Sivan falls on erev Shabbos, when they practice not to complete the fast except until the hour they leave the synagogue, this requires further study regarding Aneinu how he should act, and also regarding the communal Torah reading at Minchah.
(11) Some have the custom to treat it as a communal fast regarding Aneinu, etc. - that is, that the chazzan should say Aneinu between Goel and Rofei at Shacharis and at Minchah, and likewise Vayechal; and if it falls on Monday or Thursday, according to the opinion of the gloss above they do not push aside the weekly portion.
(12) And some, etc. - And accordingly, erev Rosh Chodesh on which they read a "small Yom Kippur," when in some places they practice to fast for the sake of repentance: regarding the blessing of Aneinu and regarding reading Vayechal it depends on the custom of the places. And there are places that practice to read Vayechal on it, and likewise to say Aneinu between Goel and Rofei at Minchah and not at Shacharis, because at Minchah they elaborate with Selichos and confessions; and one should not protest against those who practice so, for they have upon what to rely. However, where there is no custom, one should not read Vayechal even at Minchah, and likewise the chazzan should say Aneinu only in Shomea Tefillah. The custom is to give charity at Minchah of a fast, for the reward of a fast is charity; and some have the custom to estimate what one would have eaten on the fast day and give it to the poor in the evening (Eliyah Rabbah); and likewise some congregations practice to announce giving "kofer nefesh." Nevertheless one should see to do so not during the chazzan's repetition of the prayer, for they distract and one must listen to the chazzan (Peri Megadim).
