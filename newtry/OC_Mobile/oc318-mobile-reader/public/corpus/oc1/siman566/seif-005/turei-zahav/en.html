A chazzan who is not fasting, etc. So wrote the Tur in the name of R' Nasan, and he wrote upon him: and I do not know why, for he does not say "on the day of my fast" but "on this fast day," and it is a fast for others; and certainly if it is possible that there be a chazzan who is fasting, that is better than another, but if it is impossible, it appears to me that he may pray. Nevertheless here he ruled like R' Nasan, that even if there is no one there who is fasting, even so one who did not fast should not pray [as chazzan]. And it appears to me that all the more so regarding the Torah reading, where there is concern of a blessing in vain; and even the Tur would concede in this, for he disagreed only where one says merely "the fast" and not "my fast," so that there is then no falsehood in his mouth; which is not so here, where there is a blessing in vain, for one must recite a blessing over the reading even though he already blessed at Shacharis; rather, it must be that because of the communal reading he blesses a second time, and this applies only to one who is fasting, but for one who is not fasting it is a blessing in vain. Therefore even if there is no one there who can bless, or there is no other kohen there, even so they should not call one who is not fasting. And therefore it appears that even if they already called him he should not ascend, for it would be a blessing in vain. And one should not say that there is concern they will say he is disqualified, for if so it is difficult from what is written in siman 66, se'if 8, that one who is reading Shema and they called him to the Torah should not interrupt; and why did they not concern themselves with this? Rather, certainly where there is a transgression we are not concerned about suspicion; all the more so here, where it is a blessing in vain. And that concern is enough that the kohen must leave the synagogue, as written in siman 135, se'if 5, but it does not avail to commit a prohibition for this. And so it appears from what Beis Yosef wrote, and this is his language: Maharik wrote in shoresh 9 that daily practice is that when the kohen is not fasting, the kohen leaves the synagogue and a Yisrael who is fasting stands to be called to the Torah; it appears from his words that one who is not fasting does not stand to be called to the Torah; end quote. This implies that he is not called at all, even if they already called him, for otherwise he should have written that he should not ascend to read, or that they should not call him, as it appears to my humble opinion. And my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that if they erred and called him to the Torah he should ascend, for there is no prohibition here, etc. And what appears to my humble opinion I have written.
