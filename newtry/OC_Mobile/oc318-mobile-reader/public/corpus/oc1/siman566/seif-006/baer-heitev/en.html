He fasted. It appears to me that at Shacharis it is permitted to ascend [for an aliyah] even though they push aside the weekly portion and read Vayechal. Nevertheless, in any case they read from the Torah even without the fast, unless the fast is in the middle of the week. And the Bach wrote that if they called him to ascend, even at Minchah he should ascend, for there is no prohibition in this, only a mere custom. But the Taz wrote that even if there is no one there who can recite the blessing, or there is no one else here, even so they should not call one who is not fasting, and even if they already called him he should not ascend, for it would be a blessing in vain; see there. And so wrote Beis Yosef, siman 135; see there. And Magen Avraham wrote that there is no prohibition if he ascends; see there. And in Sefer Bnei Chiyya he ruled that not only if they called him should he ascend, but even ideally they may call him; and see Responsa Emunas Shmuel, siman 46, and see above, siman 135. And know that this dispute applies also to a resident of Eretz Yisrael who came abroad, whether he may ascend for the Torah on the second day of Yom Tov of the Diaspora; and see Devar Shmuel, siman 324, and Halachos Ketanos.
He went out. So that they not say that he is blemished; nevertheless if he is in the synagogue there is no concern, for there is no blemish, since all know that there are people who are not accustomed to fast (Beis Yosef, siman 135). And if there is another kohen in the synagogue, it is obvious that he should enter the synagogue and hear Kaddish, and if he is concerned that they will call him to ascend, he should tell the chazzan not to call him. Magen Avraham.
