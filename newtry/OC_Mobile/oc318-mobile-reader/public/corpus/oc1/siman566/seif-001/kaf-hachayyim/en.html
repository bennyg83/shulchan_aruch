1) [Seif 1] On a communal fast, the shaliach tzibbur recites Aneinu between Goel Yisrael and Refa'einu. For it is written, "Hashem, my Rock and my Redeemer," and immediately following it, "May Hashem answer you on a day of distress." Beit Yosef in the name of Orchos Chaim. Levush:
2) Ibid. The shaliach tzibbur recites Aneinu, etc. Even when the congregation prays together with the shaliach tzibbur, in accordance with our present practice when there is insufficient time to complete the full communal prayer, ten assemble and conduct another prayer. When they reach the 18 blessings, the congregation does not first pray silently and the shaliach tzibbur then repeat the prayer. Rather, the shaliach tzibbur and congregation pray together, and the shaliach tzibbur recites the first three blessings and the final three blessings aloud so that the congregation may answer Kedushah and bow at Modim. It is preferable for the shaliach tzibbur to recite Aneinu as an independent blessing between Goel Yisrael and Refa'einu rather than recite it in Shomea Tefillah like an individual. Mahari ben Lev, siman 15. Knesset HaGedolah cited his words in Hagahos HaTur and wrote that this was followed in practice. So too wrote Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, os 1; see there. See above, siman 124, os 9, and examine carefully:
3) Ibid. He concludes: "Blessed are You, Hashem, Who answers His people Yisrael in a time of distress." This is the text in the Tur and Levush, and this is the text in the Sephardic siddurim. However, the text in Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchos Taaniyos, law 14, is: "Who answers in a time of distress." This is also the text in Rambam's order of prayer, in Avudraham, 94b, and in the Ashkenazic siddurim, as written by the Magen Avraham, s.k. 1, Eliyah Rabbah, os 1, and Mishna Berurah, os 2. Shalmei Tzibbur, page 126, distinguished between cases and ruled that in the blessing of Aneinu recited by the shaliach tzibbur on fasts such as the four fasts commemorating the destruction and the like, he should say, "Who answers His people Yisrael in a time of distress." When there is a distress that encompasses both the nations of the world and Yisrael, he should say, "Who answers in a time of distress"; see there. 6) Ibid. They read "Vayechal," etc. The congregation says, "Relent from Your burning anger, etc.," and the shaliach tzibbur repeats it because the congregation says it as a supplication. Sefer Chassidim, siman 250. Terumas HaDeshen explains that it is because they recited it by heart. The same applies to Kol Nidrei, which the Ashkenazim customarily recite; see there. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. Care should be taken that the shaliach tzibbur wait until everyone has finished, and only then begin reading, so that everyone will hear it from the Torah. Eshel Avraham, os 2:
