Se'if 1: and he concludes, etc. Yerushalmi, chapter 2, halachah 2.
His people Israel. So it is written in the Tur, and it is an error, and it is not in the Yerushalmi there, nor in the Mishnah of chapter 2, nor in the Rambam.
And they read Vayechal. In Maseches Sofrim, chapter 17, halachah 7: on a fast of Tishah B'Av and the last seven [fasts] for rain withholding, they read blessings and curses; but on other fasts they read "Vayechal Moshe" and recite the haftarah Dirshu. And the Rosh wrote at the end of Megillah that this does not disagree with the Mishnah, for the Mishnah too deals with the last seven.
At Shacharis and Minchah, whether, etc. The language of the Tur: some say that any fast that falls on Monday or Thursday, at Shacharis they read the portion of the day and at Minchah Vayechal, for the frequent comes first, etc.; and R' Akiva wrote that specifically on the fasts written in the verse they read Vayechal; and Rav Sar Shalom wrote that on every communal fast and every fast decreed for rain, and the like that they need, they read Vayechal at Shacharis and Minchah, whether on Monday and Thursday or on other days; and so he wrote here in the Shulchan Aruch, and in the gloss of se'if 2 he ruled like R' Akiva except for BeHaB. And the Ran in the first chapter wrote from what is said there, "from midday toward evening, a quarter of the day," etc., it implies that they would not read at Shacharis the portion of the fast; and so wrote Rav Platia: on a Monday or Thursday fast, in the morning we read the Shabbos portion and at Minchah the portion of the fast; and one may wonder at our custom that they also read Vayechal at Shacharis; end quote. And see Beis Yosef, siman 575. And one may wonder: is it not explicit at the end of Megillah like the words of Rav Sar Shalom, and our custom, for we say there "and on fasts, why do we need a break? Let them read from the morning," etc., which supports, etc.? And one cannot say that the Ran interprets like Rashi's interpretation there, for if so why did he say they read the weekly portion? According to Rashi they do not read at all at Shacharis. And even without that, Rashi's words are forced: if as his words that they would not read at Shacharis at all, it should not have said "why do we need... let them read from the morning," etc.; he should have said "why do they not read at Shacharis?" And also the difficulty does not belong there at all; certainly they were not in doubt about their custom. And it is a great wonder at the commentators who would have [them] read at Shacharis the Shabbos portion; is there not an explicit Mishnah for all to interrupt, etc.? And so it is implied in the Yerushalmi that they read of the fasts at Shacharis, and even on Rosh Chodesh as I wrote below. And what is written "a quarter of the day," etc., is to exclude that they do not recite the haftarah at Shacharis, as Tosafos wrote there on 21a, s.v. HaKore; and that is what is in the gloss: and they recite the haftarah, etc., and at Shacharis, etc.
Dirshu. Maseches Sofrim as above; and Rashi in Taanis there; and Tosafos in Megillah there.
Except for Tishah B'Av. As written at the end of Megillah: "on Tishah B'Av itself," etc.; and that is at Shacharis.
And they read, etc. See what I wrote above in siman 550, se'if 3.
And if they fast, etc. In the Yerushalmi he concludes to the contrary; for it is stated there at the end of chapter 2: And with what do they read? That is, on Rosh Chodesh. R' Yosa said: blessings and curses, etc. R' Yudan of Cappadocia said before R' Yosa in the name of R' Yehudah ben Pazi: they read [the portion] of Rosh Chodesh. R' Yosa arose with R' Yehudah ben Pazi; he said to him: Did you hear this matter from your father? He said to him: Father did not say so, but rather like Tishah B'Av; because they know that it is Rosh Chodesh they read [the portion] of Rosh Chodesh, and other places read blessings and curses. And from this Yerushalmi there is support for what I wrote above, that they read even at Shacharis of the fast. And the Ran rejected the Yerushalmi from the halachah according to his view, for he wrote that from our Gemara it is implied that they do not read at Shacharis of the fast; and even though they practiced to read also at Shacharis, nevertheless on the strength of custom it is not enough to push aside that of Rosh Chodesh; see there.
