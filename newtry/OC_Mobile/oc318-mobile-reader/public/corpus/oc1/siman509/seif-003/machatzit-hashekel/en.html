(s.k. 3) It is permitted, etc. And as implied at the end of siman 502; so it should read. Meaning that Beis Yosef there brought in the name of Maggid Mishneh, who wrote as follows: a knife is certainly permitted to sharpen in its usual way (meaning on Chol HaMoed), even by professional artisan work; and to make it anew l'chatchila when it broke on the Moed, possibly that is forbidden, because it is something that can be borrowed, and there is room to permit, end of Maggid Mishneh's words. Behold: even on Chol HaMoed one might initially think to forbid since one can borrow; and although he concludes to permit, and thus we hold, nevertheless on Yom Tov we do not find anyone who permits explicitly; one should forbid where one can borrow.
