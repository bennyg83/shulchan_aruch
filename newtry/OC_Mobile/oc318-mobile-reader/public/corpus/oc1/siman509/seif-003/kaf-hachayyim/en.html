3) There. A spit that became distorted, etc. This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 4, law 8. Maggid Mishneh writes there that according to Rambam, every repair of a spit is forbidden, even if it became completely unusable and even if it became damaged on Yom Tov, because Rambam holds that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah regarding preparatory work. See there. Beis Yosef likewise writes that according to Rambam, even if the spit became only slightly bent it is forbidden to repair it, and it is certainly forbidden if it became severely damaged. Rambam holds that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah, who permits preparatory work that could not have been done before Yom Tov. See there. Since Shulchan Aruch here quotes Rambam's wording rather than Tur's wording, which permits repairing the spit if it is unfit for roasting, this implies that Shulchan Aruch holds that repairing it is forbidden in every case, as Beis Yosef explains Rambam's view. Although in siman 495 Shulchan Aruch rules according to Rabbi Yehudah that preparatory work which could not have been done before Yom Tov is permitted, one may answer that some maintain that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah in every case, but that in certain places the ruling is stringent, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 514. See there. Therefore, here too Maran appears to have ruled stringently in accordance with Rambam, since one can use another spit, borrow one from someone else, or roast directly upon the coals.
