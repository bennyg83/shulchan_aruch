A spit that was severed, etc. — this is the language of the Rambam chapter 4 law 8, and the Maggid Mishneh wrote there that per the Rambam any repair of a spit is forbidden, even if it broke completely and even if it broke on Yom Tov, because he holds that the halachah is not like Rabbi Yehuda regarding machshirin — see there. And similarly Beit Yosef per the Rambam, that even if it bent slightly it is forbidden to fix it, and certainly if it broke greatly it is forbidden, since he holds that the halachah is not like Rabbi Yehuda who permits machshirin that cannot be done from before Yom Tov — see there. If so, from that which he copied here in the Shulchan Aruch the language of the Rambam and not the language of the Tur, which permits to fix it if it is not fit to roast with it, it is implied that he holds that in every case it is forbidden to fix, as Beit Nadan wrote per the Rambam. And although in siman 495 the Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rabbi Yehuda that machshirin that cannot be done from before Yom Tov are permitted — one can say that since it is said that we do not rule like Rabbi Yehuda everywhere, but there are places where they ruled to be strict, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 514 — see there; and therefore here also it appears to Maran to be strict per the words of the Rambam, because it is possible with another spit, or to borrow from others, or to roast on coals.
