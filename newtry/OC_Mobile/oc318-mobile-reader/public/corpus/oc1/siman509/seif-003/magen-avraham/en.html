And he should set it in a needle. For if he sets it in the needle on Yom Tov, we decree lest he cut the thread (Rabbeinu Yeruham; Yerushalmi). It is implied there that if they did not set it in the needle from erev Yom Tov, it is forbidden to put it in the needle on Yom Tov; and see seif 1 in the glosses of the Asheri.
And likewise they were accustomed to burn, etc. This is the wording of Darkei Moshe: and according to what is written in seif 2 that it is forbidden to burn the spit, likewise it is forbidden to burn the thread; but they were accustomed to be lenient in this. And it appears that even according to the Mordechai who forbids burning, that is before it is filled: it is forbidden to burn the thread for the need of sewing; but after it has been sewn and one wishes to remove from there the needle or the remaining thread, it is obvious that it is permitted even to cut, for one could not do it from erev Yom Tov; only that they were accustomed to do this by burning the thread, end quote. The general rule: whatever is forbidden to cut is also forbidden to burn, because of repairing a utensil, as stated in the Gemara, folio 32; and whatever is permitted to burn is permitted to cut, only that they were accustomed to burn it; and see siman 314, seif 9.
