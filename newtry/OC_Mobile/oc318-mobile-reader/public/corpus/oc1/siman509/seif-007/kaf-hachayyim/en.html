7) There, in the gloss. And it broke on Yom Tov. The same law applies if it broke before Yom Tov but he did not know of the break or had no time to repair it. If, however, he forgot to repair it before Yom Tov, repairing it is forbidden. Likewise, if it could have been repaired on the first day, it may not be repaired on the second day of Yom Tov. For this purpose, the first day is considered a weekday relative to the second, although we are expert in determining the new month. Mahari Weil; Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 94, subsections 2 and 3; Mikraei Kodesh, subsections 5 and 6. Bigdei Yesha, however, writes that if the spit was not needed on the first day of Yom Tov, according to Rama it may be repaired on the second day when it is needed, unlike Mahari Weil. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger likewise questions the aforementioned ruling of Mahari Weil. See there. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 5, cites this.
