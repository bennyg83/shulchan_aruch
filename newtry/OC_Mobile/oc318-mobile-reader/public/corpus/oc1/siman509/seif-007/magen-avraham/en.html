And it is permitted to move muktzeh. For we heat upon ash that was heated on Yom Tov, and we move it for the need of ochel nefesh to cover the pastry (Mordechai). And one should wonder why they did not permit slaughtering l'chatchila so as to cover with ash that was heated on Yom Tov; and this requires further study (Beis Yosef, siman 518). And Bach answered that covering is not a need of ochel nefesh, for one can eat without covering; and this requires further study, for on this account it is forbidden to slaughter, and if so one cannot eat it. And there is a further difficulty: on the contrary, we hold that if one slaughtered, it is permitted to cover with it, as written in siman 498, even though it was possible to eat without covering. And in Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 3, siman 21, he wrote: specifically what one needs greatly is permitted because of simchas Yom Tov, but not for every ochel nefesh; for otherwise, eggs and fish and fruits detached on Yom Tov, why are they forbidden? End quote. And this requires further study: is a pastry needed more than fish? And there is a further difficulty: for they wrote that it is permitted to move stones and dirt lying before fruits in order to eat the fruits, even though one does not need them so much. Therefore it appears to me that it is permitted to move muktzeh for the sake of ochel nefesh, but forbidden to eat it or to make use of it; and so it appears from Tosafos at the end of folio 38, who wrote that moving muktzeh they permitted because of ochel nefesh; and so it appears from siman 501, seif 3, and siman 507, seif 2, that it is forbidden to cook with muktzeh wood; and so it appears from siman 507, seif 2, that it is forbidden to heat with wood that fell, even if one has no other wood. And Tosafos in Avodah Zarah, folio 62b, wrote that bread baked by a gentile with wood detached on Shabbos is permitted, for we do not say that there is the benefit of the wood in the bread except regarding issurei hana'ah, and muktzeh is permitted for benefit, end quote; one must say that only after the fact it is permitted to benefit from it, but l'chatchila it is forbidden to make use of it. And as for their permitting to move a board to cut meat upon it, that is because it is a utensil, as written at the end of siman 499. And what the Mordechai wrote, that it is permitted to cover the pastry with ash, that is after it has been heated, when it is no longer muktzeh, as written in siman 507, seif 6; rather he brings a proof from the fact that they move it at first while it is still cold. And in Hagahot Maimoniyot and in Tosafos the text does not read "to cover the pastry," but only "to bake the pastry." And this is the wording of the Rambam, chapter 1: Whatever is forbidden to move on Shabbos is forbidden to move on Yom Tov except for the need of eating and the like; and whatever is permitted on Shabbos is permitted on Yom Tov; and there is on Yom Tov what there is not on Shabbos, namely the prohibition of muktzeh, etc. How so? A hen kept to raise eggs, and an ox kept for plowing, and dovecote pigeons, and fruits kept for commerce: all these and the like, it is forbidden to eat of them until one prepares them from the evening, end quote. Behold it is explicit for you that it is permitted to move muktzeh for the need of eating, but forbidden to eat it; as above is primary regarding these matters; and see siman 518, seif 3.
