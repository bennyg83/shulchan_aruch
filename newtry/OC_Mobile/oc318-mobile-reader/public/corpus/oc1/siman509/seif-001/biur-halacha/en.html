That one. Know that a spit on which they roast meat, which is among the preparations for ochel nefesh, that became damaged on Yom Tov (for if it had been from erev Yom Tov he should have fixed it while it was still day, and according to all opinions it is forbidden) is a dispute between R' Yehuda and the Sages whether one may fix it; and the same applies to all preparations for ochel nefesh that one could not do while it was still day, whether one may do them on Yom Tov, as I wrote in Mishnah Berurah. Now the Mechaber copied in this seif the wording of the Rambam (and likewise in the following seif), and his view, as Beis Yosef explained, is that the Rambam holds like the Rabbis; and accordingly, even if it became greatly bent on Yom Tov, or broke and one cannot roast with it at all without repair, it is nevertheless forbidden to repair it. But the view of the gloss, which is the view of the Rosh, the Rashba, the Tur, and those who follow them, is to rule like R' Yehuda; and therefore he wrote that only when one can use it without repair is it forbidden to repair it, because of excessive toil; but if it broke on Yom Tov so that one cannot use it that way, and likewise if it became greatly bent, it is permitted to repair it on Yom Tov. And if so, it is apparently puzzling why the Rav did not write in the language of "and some say"; and perhaps it is because the Mechaber stated his words briefly in the Shulchan Aruch and did not explicitly clarify his view that in every case it is forbidden, while the Rav's own view on the essence of the law was to be lenient like the Rosh and those who follow him, and therefore he cast his words in the manner of an explanation. Afterward I found all the essentials of my words in the Sefer Olas Shabbos. And for the practical law, several later authorities agreed (see Eliyah Rabbah, the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, and other later authorities) that one should not be lenient to act even for oneself regarding what the Rama wrote; for one must be concerned for the view of the Baal HaMaor and the Ran, who were stringent when the spit broke or became so greatly bent that one cannot roast without repair, for they hold that if one repairs it this is a complete repair and it is like making a new utensil on Yom Tov for the need of ochel nefesh, which is forbidden according to all opinions, even according to R' Yehuda. And see Beur HaGra, whose view is not like Beis Yosef but like Yam Shel Shlomo, that the Rambam too holds like R' Yehuda that preparations for ochel nefesh that one could not do while it was still day may be done on Yom Tov, for so he wrote explicitly in chapter 1, halacha 8; and therefore according to him, what they said that a spit that became bent is forbidden to repair means when it broke or became greatly warped, because one has made it into a utensil, as the Gra copied in s.k. 5 according to the view of the Baal HaMaor; and so wrote Eliyah Rabbah.
And the same law applies to all the preparations that one could not, etc. See Beur HaGra: according to the Rambam, it is specifically regarding a knife that became nicked that we do not rule thus [publicly], lest one come to sharpen it on its whetstone; but regarding other preparations for ochel nefesh that one could not do while day remained, the law is such and we do rule thus [publicly]; and therefore the Mechaber did not mention in siman 495, seif 1, that we do not rule thus, and that is according to the view of the Rama.
