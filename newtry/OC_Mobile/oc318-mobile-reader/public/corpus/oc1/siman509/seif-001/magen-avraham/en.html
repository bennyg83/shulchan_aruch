That became bent. Meaning that it became warped.
And it broke on Yom Tov. And the same applies if it broke on erev Yom Tov and he did not know of it, or he had no time to repair it, as written in siman 507, seif 4, and Beis Yosef in the name of Rashi; but if he forgot to repair it from erev Yom Tov, it is forbidden; and likewise if it was possible to repair it on the first day, it is forbidden to repair it on the second day of Yom Tov, for the first day of Yom Tov is considered a weekday relative to the second for this matter, even though we are expert in the fixing of the month (Maharil); and see what I wrote in siman 499, seif 3.
It is permitted to repair it. It appears to me that if one can borrow another for him, it is forbidden to repair it; and so it is implied at the end of siman 540.
