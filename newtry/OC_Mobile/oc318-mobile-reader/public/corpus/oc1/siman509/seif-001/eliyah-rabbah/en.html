[1] [Levush] "For you," to include all, etc. It is puzzling: if so, how did the Tur rule in siman 512 that the needs of gentiles are forbidden? For in the Gemara (Beitzah), folio 28, the first Tanna who forbids regarding makhshirim learned "for you" and not for a gentile; but according to R' Yehuda, who learned regarding makhshirim that they are permitted, [what of] the needs of gentiles (Olas Shabbos)? And his difficulty is itself puzzling, for on folio 21 it is clear that all forbid the needs of gentiles, and likewise from all the incidents brought there, and likewise on folio 20 Beis Hillel said so; therefore it appears to me that R' Yehuda holds that two things are learned from "for you," and in this manner Tosafos wrote in Pesachim, folio 47, s.v. "for you." Later I found in Tamim Deim, folio 25, column 4, that he resolves this difficulty: two "for you" are written; one is "it shall be a day of assembly for you," which comes to exclude gentiles; and the other is "he alone shall do for you," which comes to include all their needs; and according to the Rabbis, one excludes gentiles and one excludes dogs; and R' Yehuda holds like R' Akiva, who said "nefesh" even includes the nefesh of an animal, and for this reason we hold like R' Yehuda, until here. It further appears to me that R' Yehuda holds that the needs of gentiles we learn from "akh," as Rashi explained in Parashas Bo, and that is from the Mechilta; and the Ra'am and all the commentators on Rashi wondered at Rashi that he did not bring the derasha of the Gemara mentioned above from "for you"; and to my humble opinion there is no difficulty at all, for the Gemara said that according to the first Tanna, but according to R' Yehuda, who needs "for you" for makhshirim, therefore he learned the needs of a gentile from "akh"; and Rashi there is according to R' Yehuda, for he explained that makhshirim are permitted.
