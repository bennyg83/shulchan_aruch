1) [Se'if 1] A spit that became distorted, etc. Know that Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages disagree about preparing implements needed for food when the preparation could not have been done before Yom Tov. The Sages forbid it and Rabbi Yehudah permits it, as stated in Beitzah 28b. Rav Chisda rules there according to one version that the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah. See there. Tur rules likewise here and in siman 495, and Shulchan Aruch rules accordingly there in siman 495. There is, however, a dispute about whether Rif and Rambam follow Rabbi Yehudah or the Sages, as explained in Beis Yosef. See there. Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages disagree only regarding repairing a utensil when the repair could not have been performed before Yom Tov. Rabbi Yehudah agrees that making a utensil for food preparation is forbidden, as explained in Beis Yosef. Know further that even according to the authorities who follow Rabbi Yehudah, some matters were permitted only for a person to perform for himself, but not for one to instruct others to do, lest they become excessively lenient, as explained in Beis Yosef and here in Shulchan Aruch. See also Bach, Taz, subsection 1, and Beis Yosef, siman 514, which show that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah in every circumstance. See there. We have written this so that no apparent contradiction will arise between one place and another.
2) There. A spit that became distorted. This means that it became crooked and bent. Taz there; Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
3) There. A spit that became distorted, etc. This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 4, law 8. Maggid Mishneh writes there that according to Rambam, every repair of a spit is forbidden, even if it became completely unusable and even if it became damaged on Yom Tov, because Rambam holds that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah regarding preparatory work. See there. Beis Yosef likewise writes that according to Rambam, even if the spit became only slightly bent it is forbidden to repair it, and it is certainly forbidden if it became severely damaged. Rambam holds that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah, who permits preparatory work that could not have been done before Yom Tov. See there. Since Shulchan Aruch here quotes Rambam's wording rather than Tur's wording, which permits repairing the spit if it is unfit for roasting, this implies that Shulchan Aruch holds that repairing it is forbidden in every case, as Beis Yosef explains Rambam's view. Although in siman 495 Shulchan Aruch rules according to Rabbi Yehudah that preparatory work which could not have been done before Yom Tov is permitted, one may answer that some maintain that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yehudah in every case, but that in certain places the ruling is stringent, as Beis Yosef writes in siman 514. See there. Therefore, here too Maran appears to have ruled stringently in accordance with Rambam, since one can use another spit, borrow one from someone else, or roast directly upon the coals.
4) There. It may not be repaired. The same applies to a needle that became bent: even if it bent only slightly, it may not be straightened. Sefer HaZichronos, page 56; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4; Mishnah Berurah, subsection 7.
5) There, in the gloss. Specifically when he can roast with it without repair, etc. We have already written in the preceding subsection that Beis Yosef holds that even if one cannot roast with it without repairing it, it is forbidden to repair it on Yom Tov. Accordingly, Rama should have presented this as an alternative opinion. It is, however, Rama's manner in his glosses that when the wording of Shulchan Aruch does not conclusively reject a position, although Shulchan Aruch does not actually agree with it, Rama writes that position without qualification as though Shulchan Aruch concedes it. Yad Malachi, Rules of Shulchan Aruch, siman 19, writes this in the name of several authorities. See there. Bigdei Yesha writes likewise.
6) There, in the gloss. And it broke on Yom Tov. This means that its point broke. Tur writes this, and Rashi explains it likewise. Rabbeinu Yerucham, section 7, also writes this, and Beis Yosef cites him. Rashba writes likewise in Avodas HaKodesh, gate 2, subsection 8. Nevertheless, one authority ruled that when it broke or became entirely bent, it is forbidden even if this occurred on Yom Tov, because utensils may not be made on Yom Tov. Permission was granted only when it became slightly bent. Avodas HaKodesh there. Ran agrees. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, cites this and writes that it appears proper to be stringent. See there.
7) There, in the gloss. And it broke on Yom Tov. The same law applies if it broke before Yom Tov but he did not know of the break or had no time to repair it. If, however, he forgot to repair it before Yom Tov, repairing it is forbidden. Likewise, if it could have been repaired on the first day, it may not be repaired on the second day of Yom Tov. For this purpose, the first day is considered a weekday relative to the second, although we are expert in determining the new month. Mahari Weil; Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3; Chemed Moshe, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, rule 94, subsections 2 and 3; Mikraei Kodesh, subsections 5 and 6. Bigdei Yesha, however, writes that if the spit was not needed on the first day of Yom Tov, according to Rama it may be repaired on the second day when it is needed, unlike Mahari Weil. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger likewise questions the aforementioned ruling of Mahari Weil. See there. Mishnah Berurah, subsection 5, cites this.
