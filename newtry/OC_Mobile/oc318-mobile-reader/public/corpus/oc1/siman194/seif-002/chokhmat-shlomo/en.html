Seif 2. Three who ate together, and one of them went out to the marketplace, call him, etc. Note: This proves that if he does not join and answer with them, they cannot recite zimun, even though they originally ate together and became obligated in zimun. They cannot recite it without him. This differs from prayer, where we rule that if one began Avos with ten present, he completes the prayer, and similarly in many other cases, once one began with ten he completes. Here, beginning the meal with three does not suffice; three must be present for the zimun itself. I have difficulty from Sanhedrin 8a, where the Baraisa states, “Zimun requires three.” The Gemara asks what “zimun” means. If it means the blessing of zimun, was it not taught, “Zimun and the blessing of zimun require three”? And if one says the latter Baraisa explains the former, was it not separately taught, “Zimun requires three and the blessing of zimun requires three”? The difficulty is unclear. Why not explain that the repetition teaches two separate laws? Had only one phrase been taught, we might have thought it merely teaches that the institution of zimun requires three from the outset, excluding a case in which they were never three. The repetition could then teach that it is not enough that they originally ate as three; rather, three must also be present when the zimun is actually recited. What, then, is the Gemara’s difficulty? This requires study. I also have difficulty with Magen Avraham, siman 191, who explains the Tur’s statement that the Sages were particular regarding zimun even for the fourth blessing as referring to one person discharging another in Bircas HaMazon, which is possible specifically through zimun. Why, then, does the Gemara not explain that “zimun” refers to one person discharging another in Bircas HaMazon itself, while “the blessing of zimun” refers to the formula Nivarech Sheachalnu, etc.? Had only one been stated, we might have thought that only Nivarech requires three, but that one person may discharge another in Bircas HaMazon without three. The two expressions would therefore teach both laws, and one would clarify the other. “Zimun” would then have the same meaning as in the Tur’s statement concerning the fourth blessing. What, then, is the Gemara’s question? This requires study. Examine this carefully.
