And he answers with them, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that this is indeed implied by the Gemara and Rosh, but below in siman 200 the law appears otherwise based on the Rashba. According to that, the Shulchan Aruch would also seem to contradict itself. It therefore appears to me there is no dispute between the Rosh and Rashba. Here the person must be near enough that he is able to answer with them, and initially he should in fact answer. If he chooses not to answer, however, they may still include him in the zimun, as stated below. The Levush's words can possibly be understood this way. It is strained to say the present case is worse because he is not physically standing with them and therefore must actually answer.
Who does not come, etc. The case is that the house opens onto the street and he sits opposite the doorway. They call to him, he hears their voices, and they make zimun including him. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba. The Bach writes that he too fulfills his obligation of zimun. Kol Bo writes that when he finishes his work he should return to the house where he ate and recite Birkat HaMazon. However, if he remained there until they completed Birkat HaMazon and concentrated on their words, he fulfills everything and need not bless again.
