(s.k. 3) One, etc. — the third; and see siman 193 — meaning that in any case one who does not know nevertheless understands the Holy Tongue; and see there in Magen Avraham; and requires further study, etc. — we find that four can, etc.; and so in the Gemara daf 46, where R' Nachman and R' Shimon disagree until where is the berachah of zimun: R' Nachman said until n'varech and R' Shimon said until ha-zan; and we say: let us establish it like the tannaim, for one taught in birkat hamazon: two and three (and Rif and Tosafot explained: sometimes it works with two people and sometimes with three, such as when one knows only one berachah as written here in Shulchan Aruch), and another taught: three and four. They thought that for all the good and the best is not d'oraisa (and he did not count it). What — do they not disagree here: one who taught three holds until ha-zan (and one should read: only three berachot), and one who taught four holds until n'varech (if so, with berachat zimun there are four)?

And it is possible that he need not inform us, etc. — meaning that it is common that one knows the berachah of zimun and does not know any berachah from birkat hamazon; and if there were a novelty that one can say the berachah of zimun even though it is not common, nevertheless he should have informed us; but in truth there is no novelty, since he already informed us that the three berachot of birkat hamazon — each one can bless one berachah — likewise a fourth person can bless the berachah of zimun alone, for what he wrote that berachat zimun is a separate berachah; only on the braita he properly challenges: one who holds berachat zimun is until n'varech should have taught four, and the novelty would be that we learn from this that berachat zimun is until n'varech like R' Nachman and not like R' Shimon; but Rav Yosef Karo, since he already wrote for himself in siman 200 that berachat zimun is until n'varech — there is no longer a novelty here and he need not inform us.

And it is implied in the Gemara and Rif, etc. — the proof, see what I wrote in this s.k.

That it impedes, etc. — and the reason is possible: since all the berachot are derived from one verse, the Gemara derives it on daf 48b.

And see siman 193 as explained there in Magen Avraham — the implication of the Gemara that all year in the eighteen-berachah Amidah one berachah does not impede its fellow except the berachot of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur that impede one another; if so in birkat hamazon one could say the law is like the berachot of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur of Yovel.

And it is proven, etc. — from the Torah, etc., unlike what Rav Yosef Karo wrote in siman 151; Magen Avraham brought it there, see there.

Regarding shofar blasts — that from the Torah nine sounds suffice, but due to doubt one must blow three sets tashrat, tashat, tarat; and if one knows one set he blows that set lest he fulfill thereby. All the more so here where he certainly fulfills the berachah d'oraisa.

Regarding to where he returns, etc. — on daf 46b it is taught: to where does he return? R' Zevid in the name of Abbaye said: he returns to the beginning; and the Rabbis said: to the place where he stopped; and the halachah is: to the place where he stopped. On this Ramban wrote that he came to explain one who went to the market in the middle of birkat hamazon — when he returns, to which place does he return? Since he tarried and interrupted in between, there is no reason for this dispute here, for the Gemara there does not deal with one who went to the market; see there in Ramban.

That even if we say the berachot impede one another, etc. — for certainly one must hold the berachot do not impede one another; why should it deal with one who went out in the middle of birkat hamazon? If so it would be difficult for R' Zevid in the name of Abbaye who holds he returns to the beginning — if so, even if he went out in the middle of the second berachah or between the first and second, nevertheless he must return to the beginning of birkat hamazon and loses even the first berachah since he interrupted — what of it that he interrupted? For even if he did not bless the second berachah at all he fulfilled through the first berachah, for they do not impede one another. If so, why should it be worse when he blessed the second berachah but interrupted between second and first (for it appears strained that it deals specifically with one who went out in the middle of the first berachah, for the stam deals with that, and it would also be difficult why the Rabbis)? Rather, even if we say the berachot impede one another and he tarried, etc., and do not hold like one who returns to the beginning.

As Rif wrote: to where does he return, etc. — it refers to the blesser in birkat hamazon who said n'varech she'achalnu mishlo and they answer Baruch she'achalnu mishlo, etc. — whether the blesser must return and say n'varech; since he tarried until they answered Baruch she'achalnu, etc., and he holds berachat zimun is until n'varech, and he holds that if he tarried enough to finish one berachah he returns; and behold until n'varech is one berachah, and since he taried until they answered Baruch, etc., and that is longer than n'varech — it is tarrying enough to say one berachah and he must return and say n'varech, etc.; and see above siman 183 what is written on this in the name of Terumat Yisrael.

It implies that he holds thus per force — since he wrote "even if we say the berachot impede one another," it implies it is not reasonable to him to say so, but he wrote thus that even if one would give that commentator that they impede one another, nevertheless he was not given that explanation, and in Berachot one should be stringent. And in particular, etc., they have no ruling; and even if we say the berachot impede, etc., there is no full ruling that Ramban for himself holds they do not impede; and I saw in Even HaEzer who wrote on this and all his words are wondrous in my eyes; on the contrary, Rif proves the opposite, and I will abbreviate his words. One challenge: Rif's language is not as Magen Avraham wrote, but rather Rif brought the Yerushalmi to teach from it the explanation of our Gemara's words. For in the Yerushalmi they object to R' Nachman who holds berachat zimun is until n'varech — from what was taught: three who ate are obligated in birkat hamazon (and this is when each one knows only one berachah — each blesses the berachah he knows and birkat hamazon is completed from the three of them), and one must say berachat zimun until n'varech would be saying four. And they answer: there is a tanna who taught truly four, and I say like him; and if you say this second ha-tov veha-metiv — for R' Huna said ha-tov veha-metiv in Yavneh they enacted it; and Rif explained he meant: if you ask, this one who taught four is on account of ha-tov veha-metiv that is taught here — one can say to him: a fourth man, you cannot say thus, for ha-tov veha-metiv is d'rabbanan and the tanna does not count it with d'oraisa berachot, end of Rif's words. Therefore Even HaEzer challenged that the intention is not because ha-tov veha-metiv does not impede, but because it is not counted — only d'oraisa berachot are counted; and so explained Terumat Yisrael. For if one would think as Magen Avraham wrote, because it does not impede — it should have said: since it is d'rabbanan and does not impede it is not counted. Further he challenged: if one would think ha-tov veha-metiv is not counted because it does not impede — if so, how is berachat zimun counted? For even though it is d'oraisa, nevertheless if they blessed birkat hamazon and forgot to invite zimun they fulfilled birkat hamazon — behold in the written zimun it does not impede. Further he challenged: what does he challenge R' Nachman — it should have said four, meaning a fourth for berachat zimun; perhaps because ha-tov veha-metiv is not counted since it does not impede. Further he wondered at what Magen Avraham wrote that they go to be stringent in doubt, and if one knows only one he should not bless at all — behold it is explained in siman 184 that doubt in birkat hamazon which is d'oraisa must bless from doubt, see there. And in my humble opinion what he wrote against Magen Avraham — Rif's language is not so — rather against Rav Yosef Karo who wrote after citing Rif's language above: and it is implied from his words that berachat ha-tov veha-metiv does not impede, for even though no one knows it they can invite zimun, etc., end of Rav Yosef Karo's words; and so ruled here in Shulchan Aruch — behold Rav Yosef Karo too understood what Rif and the Yerushalmi wrote: since ha-tov veha-metiv is d'rabbanan it is not counted — meaning because it does not impede; and what compelled Rav Yosef Karo to this — it should have said: because it is d'rabbanan, even if it impeded it would not be counted with d'oraisa, as Even HaEzer wrote. If so it is difficult for R' Nachman whom they objected from a braita that teaches three, etc. — necessarily berachat zimun is until ha-zan; and they answer that there is a braita that argues on this braita and holds berachat zimun is until n'varech, and holds like that braita, and brings a braita that teaches four; and if on account of ha-tov veha-metiv — if so the braitot do not disagree, since it is d'rabbanan it is not counted. Whence for R' Nachman that d'rabbanan berachot are not counted, and why did the braita that teaches four prove they argue on the other braita and hold berachat zimun is until n'varech? It is not worthwhile to make a dispute between the braitot; and perhaps both hold berachat zimun is until ha-zan, and this one that teaches four counts ha-tov veha-metiv even though it is d'rabbanan. Perforce one must say: since it is d'rabbanan it does not impede; and if so why need it count ha-tov veha-metiv — say a fourth man; if so what does it teach us here, that if one says a fourth man ha-tov veha-metiv they fulfill birkat hamazon? It is obvious — who is worse than these who did not say the fourth berachah at all, namely ha-tov veha-metiv, that they fulfilled, for ha-tov veha-metiv does not impede. And further, since they do not impede, it has no connection to the other three berachot — if so it is obvious another person can say it. And from this R' Nachman proved ha-tov veha-metiv is not counted. And from this Magen Avraham proved the other three berachot impede one another — for one must say they too do not impede, and it is also obvious each can bless one berachah, since they have no connection to one another, for they do not impede one another — what does the braita teach us? Rather they impede one another. And with this his challenge from berachat zimun is also resolved — for it too does not impede, yet it counts it — per Even HaEzer's understanding that what does not impede is counted without reason; but per Magen Avraham's reason why it is not counted — what does not impede, since it has no connection to other berachot it is obvious another can say it; not so berachat zimun — even though it does not impede, nevertheless it teaches us berachot of zimun are until n'varech like R' Nachman, to exclude R' Shimon; therefore it works out in birkat hamazon from four people. And what he challenged on Magen Avraham's words "and in doubt regarding berachot one should be stringent" — on the contrary, in doubt in birkat hamazon one must bless since it is d'oraisa; one could say Magen Avraham holds since it is compelled from the Gemara Yerushalmi and Rif that three berachot impede one another, how can Ramban disagree? Or one could say Ramban had proof for this from some place in the Gemara; in any case how is it obvious to him they do not impede, when the opposite appears and is compelled from Yerushalmi and Rif (and this is if we analyze what Ramban wrote: "even if we say the berachot impede," etc. — for Ramban himself did not hold thus); or one could say from the Torah Ramban agrees they impede, and if one knows only one from the Torah he need not bless anything; but nevertheless from the enactment of the Sages he blesses the berachah he knows, as they enacted a new berachah for the slain of Beitar — likewise they enacted if he knows only one berachah he blesses it; and it can be said there is proof for this. Therefore Magen Avraham wrote: and in doubt regarding berachot one should be stringent — in this case birkat hamazon has the law of other berachot that from doubt one does not bless, for the implication of Rif is they impede; and even if we consider Ramban's view, his obligation is only d'rabbanan and it is like other d'rabbanan berachot that from doubt one does not bless.
