One knows a berachah — and the same applies if they are two and one knows two berachot and one knows the third; and see siman 193. And requires further study, for Rav Yosef Karo ruled siman 200 as one who holds berachat zimun is until n'varech — if so we find that four can bless birkat hamazon, for one says thus n'varech she'achalnu mishlo, Baruch she'achalnu mishlo, etc.; and so explicitly in the Gemara and in Terumat Yisrael; and it is possible he need not inform us of this; nevertheless per what Rama wrote there, one person must say until ha-zan — it is implied in the Gemara and Rif that if there is no one who knows except one berachah he should not bless anything, for they impede one another; and see siman 193. And it is proven from here that the count of three berachot is d'oraisa — for if one would think from the Torah only ha-zan, let him say ha-zan alone; and because he does not know, should he not say what is d'oraisa on account of what is d'rabbanan? And similarly in siman 193 regarding shofar blasts and in wars he wrote regarding to where he returns, etc. — he came to explain one who heard part of birkat hamazon and went to the market and returned — he returns to the place where he stopped; he errs, etc., for there is no reason for this dispute here, even if we say the berachot impede one another and he taried until he finished all of them they disagree — this place is not relevant here; and the correct view is as Rif wrote: to where does he return, etc.; and R' Zevid said he returns to the beginning since he interrupted in the middle of zimun — if because he holds that tarrying until finishing all of them he returns to the beginning, and it is reasonable to him that berachat zimun is a matter in its own right and does not require finishing all the berachot; if because the Sages enacted thus that he return to say them in order; and the Rabbis said to the place where he stopped, end of his words. It implies he holds they do not impede one another; nevertheless it appears to me to be stringent, for Rif wrote that if there is no one who can say ha-tov veha-metiv we have no concern since it is d'rabbanan — it implies if they do not know one of the other berachot they cannot bless, for they impede one another; and in doubt regarding berachot it is fit to be stringent, and especially since Ramban's words have no decisive ruling; and see siman 187 and siman 200.
