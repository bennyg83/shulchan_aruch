It is forbidden to begin eating half an hour, etc. This he learned from the words of Rabbenu Yonah that Beit Yosef brings who wrote as follows: both the Sages and R' Gamliel agree that l'chatchila one must read at the emergence of the stars — know that we said below lest a person come from the field in the evening and say 'I will eat a little', etc., and he is found transgressing the words of the Sages; and he meant even when he comes from the field before its time he should not say since the time of Keriat Shema has not yet arrived I will eat and drink first but he should read or learn, etc. — end quote; and therefore he wrote here that it is forbidden to eat even half an hour before like Minchah prayer. And it appears to me that this law applies only to the time when Keriat Shema is essential — in what he reads obligatorily to fulfill — that is, that he reads with its blessings; but what he reads for spreading the matter — that is, what is written nearby seif 1 'and when the time arrives he reads Keriat Shema without blessings' — in this there is no prohibition to eat; for they said in this lest he not transgress the words of the Sages and whoever transgresses the words of the Sages is liable death — and in this he certainly does not transgress the words of the Sages, for he already read and prayed with the congregation — Heaven forbid that there be concern to say on the congregation that they are liable death in what they do every day l'chatchila; rather this comes to be extra cautious (afterward I saw 267) — nevertheless when the emergence of the stars arrives he can do so for spreading the matter and then he should not eat first; and per the way I wrote to read two paragraphs in Keriat Shema on the bed and intend to fulfill for spreading the matter — this is more correct even though he eats and drinks first since he has limited time to read before sleep when every person is accustomed to read — and therefore one should say thus; for otherwise you will challenge Rashi who fulfills with Keriat Shema on the bed — let it emerge to you that he eats first — and how does he transgress the words of the Sages who forbade eating first — except as I explained. And that Keriat Shema is stricter than prayer — Keriat Shema is d'oraisa: in the Gemara we say lest a person come from the field in the evening and say 'I will eat a little and drink a little and sleep a little and afterward I will read Keriat Shema and pray' — and if sleep seizes him he is found sleeping the entire night, etc., lest he not transgress the words of the Sages and whoever transgresses the words of the Sages is liable death. At first glance it implies he transgresses only if he does all three — eating and drinking and sleeping — but it is not so; for the main concern is because sleep may seize him and he is found sleeping — and if so there is prohibition in sleep alone; and if so eating and drinking each alone is forbidden lest sleep seize him through this; and nevertheless it is obvious that tasting fruits is permitted like Minchah above; and that which is stated regarding the sons of R' Gamliel who came from the banquet, etc. — and it is difficult how they transgressed the words of the Sages; and in the Gemara it implies they were Torah scholars — one could say they began in permissibility before the time of Keriat Shema the eating at the banquet; and the Mordechai wrote in the first chapter of Shabbat that if he began eating from when it darkens before Ma'ariv prayer he must interrupt for Keriat Shema; and if he began in permissibility he need not interrupt; and Beit Yosef brings this — and I wonder why he wrote here that half an hour before close to the time of Keriat Shema there is prohibition — for even from when it darkens it is called time of permissibility in the Mordechai; and R' Yitzchak mentioned only before its time; and so the Rashba that Beit Yosef brings wrote close to it a little before it and did not write simply 'close' as regarding Minchah prayer 'close to Minchah' — but certainly here since its time is long there is no prohibition in 'close' except a small measure before the time; and all the more that Tosafot that Beit Yosef brings wrote from when the time arrived, etc. — therefore one should not be stringent with half an hour; as it appears to me.
