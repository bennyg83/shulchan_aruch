Magen Avraham, seif katan 4, is more lenient and holds that one who recited Krias Shema in the synagogue while it was still day may eat a little even after the proper time arrives, and is not considered one who transgresses the words of the Sages. Regarding Taz’s proof from Berachos 4b, see Rabbeinu Yonah there, who explains the phrase “if he is accustomed to read, he reads.” This must refer to the half hour before the time. If it referred to night itself, the mitzvah begins immediately and the preferred observance is to recite it at once. Thus, even during the preceding half hour eating is forbidden. Rashba, however, holds that ideally the time extends throughout the entire night, yet even according to him eating is forbidden during the preceding half hour, by a kal vachomer from Minchah, which is Rabbinic, to Krias Shema, which is Torah-level. See Berachos 2 and 9, and our extended discussion in our novellae. According to Rashba, therefore, the half-hour restriction is comparable to Minchah. The inference from Mordechai is that if one began after dark he interrupts, because a Torah-level obligation requires interruption when he began in violation. If he began during bein hashmashos before darkness, however, he began permissibly and does not interrupt even after tzeis hakochavim arrives. Thus, even “after dark” may sometimes be called a permitted time, meaning that bein hashmashos can sometimes be included. How then can the Mechaber rule that beginning during the preceding half hour is considered beginning in violation and requires immediate interruption? According to Rashba this may be understood, but according to Rabbeinu Yonah one can distinguish: Minchah has a short time window and therefore the preceding half hour is prohibited, whereas Maariv does not. The phrase “if he is accustomed to read,” etc., would then refer only to a short time beforehand, as the Mechaber writes in seif 3. Still, the statement that Rashba wrote “close to the time” appears to derive the rule from Minchah. We discuss this at length in our novellae. See Tosafos, Berachos 4b, s.v. vekorei, and Beit Yosef.
