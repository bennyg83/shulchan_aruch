Forbidden — Taz; in Magen Avraham letter 4 he is more lenient — that one who reads in the synagogue ab initio even though its time arrives afterward can eat a little and he is not called one who transgresses the words of the Sages; and what the Taz wrote from the Gemara Berachot 4b — see Rashi there who said: accustomed to read — reads — therefore within half an hour before; for if at night its mitzvah is immediately and the mitzvah from the choicest is to read it immediately — therefore half an hour before — nevertheless eating is forbidden; but the Rashba's view is that its time is the entire night l'chatchila and nevertheless half an hour before is forbidden — a fortiori from Minchah prayer which is d'rabbanan, all the more Keriat Shema which is d'oraisa — see there in Berachot daf 2 and 9; and in our novellae we expanded; and if so for the Rashba it implies half an hour before is forbidden like at Minchah. And what he wrote in Mordechai — meaning since it wrote if he began from when it darkens he interrupts (d'oraisa he interrupts to begin in prohibition) it implies that if he began while it is still day before darkening it is called he began in permissibility and does not stop even if afterward the time of the emergence of the stars arrived; and he said even from when it darkens it is sometimes called time of permissibility — meaning while it is still day likewise sometimes it is called thus; and how did the Mechaber write half an hour before is beginning in prohibition — he stops immediately; and granted for the Rashba one could say thus; but for Rabbenu Yonah one could say Minchah prayer's time is short and therefore half an hour before is forbidden — unlike Ma'ariv; and only 'accustomed to read', etc. — meaning a little before — is what it deals with, as the Mechaber wrote in seif 3; nevertheless what he wrote and the Rashba wrote close to it — behold from the Rashba it implies he learned it from the law of Minchah prayer; and in our novellae we expanded on this. And Tosafot Berachot 4b s.v. 'and reads' ab initio — see there.
