And if the congregation precedes, etc. In Teshuvot HaRashba siman 1 he wrote that in most communities on long days they read the evening Keriat Shema and pray Ma'ariv three or four hours before night — they rely on Rashi that one fulfills with Keriat Shema on the bed; and the custom spread through the Teshuvot Koach that descended to the world and those desiring to eat while it is still day; and he concludes it appears that even a Torah scholar if he is in a congregation that precedes to pray and read Keriat Shema on long days — if he cannot separate from them he need not separate but prays and reads with them and fulfills thereby; but if he was accustomed to other separations he prays and reads at the time the Sages enacted according to all views — end quote; also the Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabeinu Elyakare that one who is stringent not to rely on Rabbenu Tam appears as presumptuous — except that Beit Yosef wrote on this: since per the view of several great ones he does not fulfill with reading in the synagogue it does not appear presumptuous — end quote. And therefore he wrote here for halachah that when the time arrives he reads Keriat Shema without its blessings — and this way does not appear presumptuous; but I am astonished at the way of our Rabbi the author of the Shulchan Aruch in these matters who wrote 'and when the time arrives he reads Keriat Shema without blessings' — for we do not find this way in Beit Yosef and we find only the view of Rashi that one fulfills with Keriat Shema on the bed; and what people are accustomed to read Keriat Shema ab initio is in order to stand in prayer from d'oraisa. And Tosafot challenged on this: one — behold then he reads without its blessings; and further — if so we should read three paragraphs; and further — if so we do as R' Yehoshua ben Levi that prayers in the middle they enacted; and further — Keriat Shema on the bed was enacted only because of harmful spirits. And they wrote in the name of Rabbenu Tam that one fulfills even ab initio — for we rely on a tannaitic view that holds the time of Keriat Shema is before the emergence of the stars; even though for Minchah he holds it is still day that they pray Minchah then — and it would be two leniencies that contradict each other — we pay no attention to this regarding prayer for they were lenient therein as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba and Rabbenu Yonah; Rabbenu Yonah wrote that one who wishes to fulfill should read Keriat Shema with its blessings and pray and not intend to fulfill Keriat Shema with that reading but to stand in prayer from d'oraisa and he fulfills the reading of the blessings; and after the emergence of the stars he reads it and intends to fulfill; and if he does not wish to read except the two paragraphs it suffices for him thus — since he mentioned Yetzi'at Mitzrayim when reading it in the synagogue we do not require him to mention it again; and so that he not read it without a blessing he wrote that he says the blessing Ahavat Olam alone — end quote. And in the name of Rabbenu Hai he wrote another way — see there; but the way written here in Shulchan Aruch has no place; for if he wishes to rule like the way of R' Yitzchak he should have mentioned here that he intend in the synagogue not to fulfill with what he reads there and prays; and as it appears the Rav in Shulchan Aruch retracted from all the ways mentioned in Beit Yosef and chose this way — that in the synagogue he reads and prays intending to fulfill; and for spreading the matter he reads a second time without blessing at the emergence of the stars — and this is very astonishing to me; for if so why did he depart from Rashi's way to fulfill with Keriat Shema on the bed — and we should have enacted to say that he truly read three paragraphs before the bed; and if so there would be no difficulty from what Tosafot challenged on Rashi — only the difficulty that they read without blessings and the difficulty that they do as R' Yehoshua ben Levi — and this is not resolved even for the Shulchan Aruch's way; and one could say that in the way I mentioned there is concern lest he eat and drink and sleep and pass Keriat Shema as we say 'I will eat a little', etc. — this is not so; for if so Tosafot should have challenged thus on Rashi's explanation too; but certainly in this there is no concern, as Baer Heitev wrote; and therefore it appears to me that in truth whoever is careful to act for the sake of the good in this should read Keriat Shema on his bed and intend to fulfill with this — not because of harmful spirits — and need not read only two paragraphs since he already read and fulfilled in the synagogue as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of R' Yitzchak according to his way — and this is more correct than the Shulchan Aruch's way; for in the Shulchan Aruch's way it is close to certainty that at the emergence of the stars he may forget to read, especially since he already read and fulfilled — only that he must return because of spreading the matter as written — unlike at the time of lying down when there is then arousal to read because of harmful spirits — and in this a person is not apt to forget; as it appears to me it is correct for anyone who wishes to fulfill for spreading the matter. Again I found in Sefer Mateh Moshe that Rashal read on his bed all three paragraphs.
