"And if the congregation prays early," etc. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 1, writes that in most communities the custom on long summer days is to recite the evening Shema and pray Maariv 3 or 4 hours before nightfall. They rely on Rashi, who holds that they fulfill the obligation through the Shema recited at bedtime. This custom spread because weakness descended upon the world and people desire to eat while it is still day. He concludes that even a Torah scholar, if he is in a congregation that prays and recites Shema early on long days and cannot separate himself from them, need not withdraw. He prays and recites with them and may rely on this. But if he is accustomed to other acts of piety, he should pray and recite Shema at the time established by the Sages according to all opinions. Mordechai likewise writes in the name of Ra'avyah that one who acts stringently and refuses to rely upon Rabbeinu Tam may appear presumptuous. Beit Yosef, however, writes that since according to several great authorities one does not fulfill the obligation through the synagogue reading, such stringency is not presumptuous. In my view, the proper way for one who wishes to satisfy the matter is to rely upon Shema at bedtime, but to recite it then with intent to fulfill the mitzvah, not merely because of harmful forces. There is no need to recite all 3 paragraphs for the basic obligation if he already recited them in the synagogue, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of R' Yitzchak according to his approach. This is preferable to the approach of the Shulchan Aruch that one should repeat Shema after nightfall, for according to that approach it is very likely that one will forget to repeat it when the stars emerge, especially since he already recited Shema and believes that only a later repetition remains for publicizing the proper time. By contrast, at bedtime there is a natural prompt to recite Shema because of the established bedtime recitation, and a person is less likely to forget. One might object that if he waits until bedtime he may eat, drink, and fall asleep before reciting Shema, like the concern expressed by the Sages when a person says, "I will eat a little," etc. But this cannot be decisive, because Tosafos did not raise such an objection against Rashi's view. It therefore appears that there is no special concern here, as Baer Heitev writes. Accordingly, one who wishes to act properly should recite Shema at bedtime with intent to fulfill the mitzvah. I later found in Mateh Moshe that Maharshal recited all 3 paragraphs at bedtime.
