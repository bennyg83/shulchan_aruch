(16) It is forbidden to begin, etc. The reason is that he may prolong his meal and sometimes fall asleep as a result, thereby forgetting to recite Krias Shema. Even eating a small amount is forbidden. Mere tasting, however, such as eating fruit or even bread up to the volume of an egg, is permitted.
(17) To eat. It is likewise forbidden to sleep, even if he intends to sleep only briefly. Gemara. The same applies to all the activities listed above in siman 232, seif 2, which may lead to negligence. Certainly, once the time for Krias Shema has arrived, one may not begin them. Levush; Peri Megadim in siman 232; Derech HaChayim. Eliyah Rabbah inclines to permit them before the time for Krias Shema arrives. Learning, however, is permitted according to all opinions shortly beforehand. On the contrary, it is a mitzvah, as the Gemara states: a person comes from the field in the evening, enters the beis midrash, and if he is accustomed to read he reads, if accustomed to study he studies, and then recites Krias Shema and prays. Once the time for Krias Shema has arrived, however, even learning is forbidden when he prays privately at home. If he asks a friend who is not learning to remind him to pray, it is permitted. Later authorities.
(18) Half an hour. Taz is lenient and requires only a short interval. The later authorities agree with the Shulchan Aruch that whenever only half an hour remains before the time, one may not begin eating. If he asked another person to remind him to pray, however, there is no prohibition against beginning to eat even after the time for Krias Shema has already arrived.
(19) Close to the time for Krias Shema. Meaning, close to tzeis hakochavim. Magen Avraham implies that even on Motzaei Shabbos one must be careful not to begin the meal then. See Mishnah Berurah 299:1 regarding this. The later authorities write that those who recite Krias Shema and pray while it is still day, although they must repeat Krias Shema after tzeis hakochavim as explained in seif 1, may nevertheless eat before repeating it, since they have at least already recited it once. Even so, once tzeis hakochavim actually arrives, it is proper to be careful in this regard as well.
(20) He began eating after, etc. Beginning within the half hour before tzeis hakochavim is treated the same as beginning after the time arrived.
(21) He interrupts. He must interrupt immediately, since Krias Shema is a Torah-level obligation and he began in violation of the prohibition. If he began permissibly, namely before the half hour preceding tzeis hakochavim, he need not interrupt even for Krias Shema, provided that enough time will remain to recite it after completing his meal, as explained above in siman 70, Mishnah Berurah seifim katanim 23 and 24.
(22) Without its blessings. Since the blessings are only Rabbinic, Chazal did not require him to interrupt for their sake.
(23) But he need not, etc. As the Mechaber writes, he interrupts only to recite Krias Shema itself, without its blessings and without the Amidah, since those are only Rabbinic. Rama repeats the point here only in order to conclude with the words, “but,” etc.
(24) Even though he washed his hands. Even if there is still ample time to pray, we require him to interrupt. This differs from beginning a meal close to Minchah, where after the fact he need not interrupt once he has washed his hands, as explained above in siman 232. Since the time for Minchah is short, a person is apprehensive and will not be negligent. Maariv, however, may be prayed after the fact throughout the night, and we are concerned that he may rely on the length of the night and become negligent. Therefore, so long as he has not actually begun eating, we require him to interrupt even though he has washed his hands. This applies only if he has not yet recited the blessing over washing. If he already recited that blessing, it is proper not to interrupt. Rather, he should recite Hamotzi, eat an olive-volume, and then interrupt his meal. Although one who has begun eating generally need not interrupt, here he began only to avoid an improper interruption between washing and Hamotzi, as explained above in siman 166.
(25) And if there is insufficient time. Meaning, whether he is close to dawn, or even at the beginning of the night but the meal will continue until dawn, as may occur with large meals during the short summer nights, he must interrupt immediately at tzeis hakochavim. This applies even if he began permissibly while it was still day.
