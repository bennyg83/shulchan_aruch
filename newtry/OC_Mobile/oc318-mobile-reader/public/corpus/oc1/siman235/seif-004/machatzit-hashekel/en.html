(Note 4) To begin etc. — eating a little etc. — implies a taste:

And siman 692 — Terumat HaDeshen derives from this also regarding Megillah:

For Tosafos in Sukkah rejected etc. on that which we taught there — one who was on the way and has no lulav in hand — when he comes home he takes it on his table; and it challenges — from that which is taught he takes it on his table — apparently he must interrupt; but in Minchah tefillah we taught if they began they do not interrupt. And one could say lulav is different for it is d'oraisa — if so necessarily on the second day of Yom Tov it deals since it teaches one who was on the way; and on the second day of Yom Tov also regarding lulav d'rabbanan; and they answer R' Shimon bar Yochai — this where there is time that where there is no time; and Tosafos challenged — Minchah likewise as we say on Shabbos that regarding Maariv even if he loosened his belt or washed hands nevertheless he must interrupt; even though in Minchah in such a case he need not interrupt — nevertheless in Maariv since the entire night is its time we are concerned lest he come to sin as Magen Avraham wrote in note 7; and if so likewise lulav since the entire day is its time he must interrupt; (and it should read that Tosafos hold like R' Yehuda brought in Beit Yosef — that for us who accepted Maariv as obligatory — if he washed hands only; but even if he began to eat he interrupts for Maariv); and Tosafos wrote and one could say because there night is time of sleep and we are more concerned — if so the reason should have said there (meaning the Gemara did not cite in Maariv the reason except because the entire night is its time — for in this alone it depends; for if one thought he must also join the reason that night is time of sleep the Gemara should have mentioned it and said Maariv is different for the entire night is its time and also it is time of sleep; perforce in this alone that the entire night is its time suffices); and Tosafos' explanation: and one may say tefillah requires time and one is lazier in it more than taking lulav — for once he lifts it he fulfills — end quote. Behold they rejected this reason and one could say lest the reason is because the entire night is its time — why even a taste is forbidden lest he come to sin; one may say Magen Avraham holds as Tosafos wrote that this reason is insufficient since lulav does not require time and the time is short — likewise tefillah that requires time nevertheless since taste does not require time the reason also does not suffice since the entire night is its time; perforce because it is d'oraisa they are stringent:
