(1) At night, from the time, etc. For it is written, “when you lie down,” and before night is not the time of lying down. Night begins when three medium stars appear in the sky. Since not everyone is expert in distinguishing them, however, and people might err by relying on large stars, which are certainly not a sign of night because they sometimes appear even during the day, Chazal were stringent regarding Krias Shema and required one to wait until three small stars are visible.
(2) Three stars. Even if they are scattered.
(3) He should wait, etc. Since this is a doubt concerning a Torah-level obligation. Biur HaGra.
(4) From his mind. If he knows when the sun set, he should wait the time required to walk four mil, namely seventy-two minutes. See below, siman 293, seif 2, in Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah, for the entire discussion there applies here as well.
(5) Before then. Meaning, before tzeis hakochavim, or on a cloudy day before the doubt has been resolved in his mind.
(6) He repeats and recites it. See Maamar Mordechai and Nehar Shalom, who write that this is the essential law and not merely a stringency. This is also the view of Biur HaGra, seif katan 1.
(7) Without its blessings. Since he already recited them. Although he did not fulfill his obligation of Krias Shema with the reading before its proper time, the blessings were nevertheless not recited in vain. Although they are juxtaposed to Krias Shema, they were not instituted specifically for Krias Shema. Therefore, after the fact, if he recited them before the time for Krias Shema, he fulfilled his obligation regarding the blessings.
(8) And if the congregation, etc. They acted this way because of pressing circumstances. Many times, if they had waited to pray Maariv until tzeis hakochavim, each person would have gone home and they would not have prayed with a congregation, since it would have been burdensome to assemble again. There were also many unlearned people who, if they did not pray with the congregation, would not pray at all. They therefore relied on the view of Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that from plag haminchah onward it is considered night with respect to praying Maariv, as explained above in siman 233, where this is permitted in pressing circumstances. Since it was considered night with respect to prayer, their custom was also to recite Krias Shema then, although according to most poskim this was not in accordance with the halachah.
(9) He should recite Krias Shema with them. He should not intend then to fulfill his obligation of Krias Shema, but only with the second recitation that he will recite afterward at its proper time.
(10) And its blessings, and he should pray with them. In order to juxtapose redemption to prayer and also to pray with the congregation.
(11) And when the time arrives, he recites, etc. It is sufficient to recite the first two sections, since he already mentioned the Exodus from Egypt in the synagogue. Although he did not fulfill his obligation of Krias Shema at that time, because it was not yet the time of lying down, he did fulfill the obligation to mention the Exodus at night. Magen Avraham in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. Shaagas Aryeh, however, discusses this at length and concludes that a God-fearing person should be careful to recite all three sections of Krias Shema after tzeis hakochavim.
(12) Krias Shema without its blessings. One should not rely on the Krias Shema recited before sleep, even if his custom is to recite all three sections. He must intend to fulfill the positive mitzvah of Krias Shema, as explained above in siman 60, whereas the Krias Shema before sleep is generally recited not for that mitzvah but to ward off harmful forces. Furthermore, even if intention to fulfill the mitzvah were not required, one must at least consciously accept the yoke of Heaven with awe, and one does not ordinarily have that intention in the bedtime recitation. Nowadays most people recite Krias Shema and pray after tzeis hakochavim, as required. In some synagogues, however, the old custom remains to pray immediately after Minchah while it is still somewhat daytime. One who prays in such a place should repeat Krias Shema when its time arrives. At minimum, he should recite all three sections before sleep and intend thereby to fulfill the positive mitzvah of Krias Shema. In some places there is a custom that meticulous people do not recite Krias Shema with the congregation. Instead, they remain silent until the Amidah, pray with the congregation, and afterward wait until tzeis hakochavim, when they recite Krias Shema and its blessings. They are not concerned with juxtaposing redemption to prayer. This custom is also mentioned by the early authorities. See Beit Yosef, and what we wrote above in siman 233, seif 1, in Mishnah Berurah. Fortunate is one who prays Maariv at its proper time with a congregation. See also what we wrote in Biur Halachah in the name of Maaseh Rav.
(13) Nevertheless, he should not repeat, etc. See Biur Halachah.
(14) They recite it early. The consensus of the poskim is that one may not recite it earlier than plag haminchah. If he did so, he fulfilled neither Krias Shema and its blessings nor the Amidah according to any opinion, and he must repeat Krias Shema with its blessings and pray the Amidah again. Even from plag haminchah onward, this allowance applies only to one who prayed Minchah before plag. To pray both Minchah and Maariv after plag creates contradictory practices.
(15) For then it does not appear arrogant. Repeating Krias Shema does not appear arrogant, since nearly all poskim agree that its time begins at tzeis hakochavim. The later authorities write that one who normally recites Krias Shema and prays with the congregation at the proper time, but found himself with a congregation that recited Krias Shema and prayed while it was still day and mistakenly began the blessings of Krias Shema, should complete the blessings together with Krias Shema until the Amidah so that the blessing not be in vain. He should not, however, pray the Amidah with them. When the time for Krias Shema arrives, he should recite it without its blessings and pray with the congregation. Although he will not then juxtapose redemption to prayer, prayer at its proper time is preferable to juxtaposing redemption to prayer.
