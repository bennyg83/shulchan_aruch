(1) At night from the time etc. — for it is written uvshachbecha and before night is not time of lying down; and night is called from when three medium stars appear in the sky; however because not all are expert and they will err with large stars [and large stars are certainly not a sign of night for sometimes they appear even by day] they were stringent regarding Keriat Shema that he not read until three small ones are seen:

(2) Three stars — and even scattered:

(3) He should wait etc. — for it is safek d'oraisa [Gra]:

(4) From his heart — and if he knows when the sun set he should wait the measure of four mil which is 72 minutes; and see below siman 293 seif 2 in Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah — for the entire matter there applies also here:

(5) Before then — meaning before tzeit hakochavim or on a cloudy day before the doubt clears from his heart:

(6) He returns and reads it — see in Ma'amar Mordechai and Nehar Shalom who wrote that fundamentally it is the law and not because of stringency; and so is Gra's opinion note 1:

(7) Without blessings — for he already blessed them; and even though he did not fulfill with Keriat Shema he read before the time — nevertheless the blessings are not in vain — for even though we connect them to Keriat Shema — nevertheless their essence was not enacted specifically for Keriat Shema; and therefore b'dieved that he read them before the time of Keriat Shema he fulfills:

(8) And if the congregation etc. — and they did so because of pressure — for sometimes these waited for Maariv tefillah until tzeit hakochavim — afterward each one went home and they did not pray in congregation for it was trouble for them to gather again; and also there are many amei ha'aretz and if they do not pray in congregation they would not pray at all; and perforce they rely on the opinion of R' Yehuda who holds from plag haminchah and above is considered Maariv to pray Maariv tefillah as stated above siman 233 that it is permitted to do so in time of pressure; and since it is considered for tefillah as night — their custom was that they then also read Keriat Shema even though it is not according to halachah for most poskim:

(9) He should read Keriat Shema with them — and not intend then to fulfill the obligation of Keriat Shema — only in the second reading that he reads afterward at its time:

(10) And its blessings and he prays with them — in order to connect geulah to tefillah and also to pray with the congregation:

(11) And when its time arrives he reads etc. — and it suffices that he read the first two sections since he mentioned yetzi'at Mitzrayim in the synagogue — for although regarding Keriat Shema he does not fulfill at this time because it is still not time of lying down — regarding yetzi'at Mitzrayim which we are obligated to mention at night he fulfills at this time [Magen Avraham in the name of Tur]; but in Sha'agat Aryeh he extensively concludes that a God-fearing person should be careful to read all three sections of Keriat Shema after tzeit hakochavim:

(12) Keriat Shema without blessings — and it is not worthwhile to rely on the reading he reads on his bed even if his custom is to read all three sections — for he must intend to fulfill the positive commandment of Keriat Shema as above siman 60; and Keriat Shema on his bed we do not designate for the mitzvah — only to drive away harmful spirits; and further even if we say kavanah is not required to fulfill the positive commandment — he requires at minimum kavanah of the heart to accept upon himself the yoke of the kingdom of Heaven with awe; and in that reading on his bed he does not intend for this. And behold in our time most of the world practices to read Keriat Shema and pray after tzeit hakochavim according to law; however in some places in synagogues there is still the old custom that they pray immediately after Minchah even though it is a bit while still day — and the one praying in those places should see to return and read Keriat Shema when the time arrives; and at minimum he should see to read all the sections on his bed according to law and intend to fulfill thereby the positive commandment of Keriat Shema; and in some places there is a custom that the ancients do not read Keriat Shema with the congregation but are silent until Shema and pray with them and wait after tefillah until tzeit hakochavim and read Keriat Shema and its blessings and they are not concerned for connecting geulah to tefillah; and this custom too is mentioned in the words of the early ones — see Beit Yosef; and see what we wrote above siman 233 seif 1 in Mishnah Berurah; and fortunate is one who prays Maariv at his time in congregation; and see what we wrote in Biur Halachah in the name of Maaseh Rav:

(13) However he should not return etc. — see Biur Halachah:

(14) They advance — the agreement of poskim is that at minimum he should not advance more than plag haminchah; and if he advanced he does not fulfill neither in Keriat Shema and its blessings nor in tefillah according to all; and he must return and read Keriat Shema and its blessings and pray Shemoneh Esreh. And even from plag haminchah and above is only for one who prayed Minchah before plag — but to pray Minchah and Maariv after plag is two contradictory things:

(15) For then it does not appear arrogant — but what he returns and reads Keriat Shema does not appear arrogant — for regarding Keriat Shema almost all poskim agree its time is tzeit hakochavim. The later authorities wrote — one accustomed to read Keriat Shema and pray with the congregation at its time — and when he was in a congregation that read and prayed while still day and erred and began the blessing of Keriat Shema — he should finish the blessings with Keriat Shema until Shema so as not to have a blessing in vain; but Shemoneh Esreh he should not pray with them; and when the time of Keriat Shema arrives he should read Keriat Shema without blessings and pray with the congregation; and even though now he does not connect geulah to tefillah — tefillah at its time is preferable to connecting geulah to tefillah:
