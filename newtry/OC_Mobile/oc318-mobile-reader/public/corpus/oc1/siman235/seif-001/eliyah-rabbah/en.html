[Levush] Maariv is optional, etc. See Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon, siman 70, in his analysis of Berachot 26 and 27. Regarding small stars, Beit Yosef cites Rabbenu Yonah that although medium stars certainly indicate night, since we are not expert in distinguishing medium from small stars one must wait until small stars are visible. The word "small" in the phrase describing what we cannot distinguish appears to be a scribal error and should read "medium from large," because large stars are visible by day. Yet below, siman 562, regarding a fast, Rama and Levush rule that medium stars certainly constitute night. Taz, printed later, writes that for a fast they did not burden people so greatly, but he cites Rabbenu Yonah's wording unchanged and did not note that he thereby makes fasting more lenient. Beit Yosef also quotes Ohel Moed that for Keriat Shema, when two stars are visible it is called night, and says he does not know the source. There is no difficulty. Seeing two stars is bein hashmashot, as in Shabbos chapter Bameh Madlikin. Ohel Moed may follow the Tur's view that for Keriat Shema and prayer we rule like Rabbi Eliezer, who says from the time the day becomes sanctified on Erev Shabbos, namely bein hashmashot, as Rashi explains at the beginning of Berachot. Nevertheless, in practical law we require three stars, apparently even scattered. Below in siman 292, where on Shabbos we require them close together, the reason is to add from weekday onto sanctity, as Beit Yosef explains there. Olat Tamid's objection to Shulchan Aruch there is therefore mistaken.
He repeats and recites, etc. He should begin "Kel Melech Ne'eman, Shema, etc.," as Lechem Chamudot writes. See above, siman 61.
And when he reaches, etc. The reason is that the Levush considers primary the poskim who hold that one fulfills the nighttime Keriat Shema obligation from plag haminchah onward, but nevertheless takes account of those who require tzeit hakochavim. Therefore one should recite Keriat Shema without its blessings after tzeit hakochavim. Taz asks why Shulchan Aruch did not advise reciting all three paragraphs of Keriat Shema at bedtime and relying on that. He overlooked Talmidei Rabbenu Yonah at the beginning of Berachot, who write that initially Keriat Shema must be recited before eating. In addition, one may recite it at bedtime merely for protection and not intend to fulfill the mitzvah. Nevertheless, one who does not otherwise repeat Keriat Shema after tzeit hakochavim should at least recite two paragraphs at bedtime and intend to fulfill his obligation. This resolves Shiltei Giborim's surprise, page 4, that Rabbenu Yeshayah appears contradictory; there is a distinction whether he already recited after tzeit hakochavim. Magen Avraham says Olat Tamid's statement that otherwise the Sages' question would not arise is unintelligible, because that itself is their question: the Sages hold both Keriat Shema and prayer must be at night. In my humble opinion it is quite intelligible. Bach explains Tosafot on Berachot 26 that the Sages' view that prayer is at night is derived from Keriat Shema being at night, because geulah must be placed immediately before tefillah. If one recites Keriat Shema with its blessings by day in order to connect geulah to tefillah, perhaps one may also pray by day and repeat only Keriat Shema at night.
[Levush] The blessings are not, etc. This is difficult: why does the Levush need this reason when in siman 2 he himself follows Rashba that the blessings of Keriat Shema are not blessings directly over the mitzvah of Keriat Shema? Beit Yosef gives that reason here as well, and Malbushei Yom Tov wonders at the Levush. I later examined Beit Yosef in siman 60 and found that he cites Rashba himself saying that the blessings are not indispensable. This is indeed because they are not directly over Keriat Shema, as appears from Chiddushei Rashba at the beginning of Berachot. Alternatively, Rashba adds the broader reason that blessings do not prevent fulfillment, which would be true even if they were direct mitzvah blessings, like other blessings that do not prevent fulfillment. The reason that they are not direct blessings over Keriat Shema is needed to show that reciting them publicly before the proper time of Keriat Shema is not a blessing in vain. See above, siman 58, the end of siman 67, and siman 62, where he says the blessings are not indispensable because they are not d'oraisa; the correct explanation is as I have given.
