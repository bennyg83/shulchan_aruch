Three stars. See Taz. Eliyah Rabbah emends the text to read “between large and medium stars.” It may be explained as follows. Shabbos 35b states that we do not rely on large stars, which are visible by day, nor on small stars, which are visible only at night, but on medium stars. This implies that even two or three small stars are visible only at night. Why, then, are three small stars required? As a safeguard: were three medium stars sufficient, people might mistakenly rely on two and assume that they were small. Since this concerns a Torah-level doubt, Chazal required specifically three small stars. This also explains why they did not impose the same burden regarding a fast. If the concern were merely that people cannot distinguish large from medium stars, one should be stringent regarding a Rabbinic fast as well, because a doubt caused by lack of knowledge is not treated leniently. Rather, people can identify medium stars, and the additional requirement of small stars is only a safeguard in Torah matters. When the authorities say that regarding a fast they did not burden people, this may apply even to Yom Kippur, which is Torah-level, because once three medium stars are visible in close proximity and appear as one grouping, there is already an addition to the sacred day. See siman 624. Magen Avraham in siman 293 likewise cites Rabbeinu Yonah that people are not expert in distinguishing small stars, although Beit Yosef there reads “between large and small.” Presumably no one disputes Ran, who requires adjacent stars for the addition to Shabbos. It follows that three adjacent medium stars constitute an addition. See Levush in siman 293, who writes that then it is certainly night, meaning even for one who cannot distinguish them, and it is treated as an addition. See Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Shabbos, halachah 4, who specifies three medium stars, while in chapter 7 of Hilchos Terumos he does not specify medium stars. I do not know why. It cannot be that because Shabbos carries kares and stoning he was stringent, while regarding terumah, where the penalty is death at the hand of Heaven, a Torah doubt would be permitted and only Rabbinically restricted. That is erroneous for several reasons. See also chapter 9 of Hilchos Tumas Meis, halachah 12. Peri Chadash and the other commentators did not accept the version cited by Kesef Mishneh regarding an asham talui, and the matter of an established prohibition is not relevant here. With Hashem’s help, it will be explained elsewhere. See Magen Avraham 572:1, where the words “but small ones do not suffice” are a scribal error and should read “but large ones do not suffice.” One could force an explanation that fewer than three small stars do not suffice, but it is plainly a scribal error. See Magen Avraham here, seif katan 1, and with Hashem’s help the matter will be explained there.
