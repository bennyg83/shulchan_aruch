6) [Se'if 2] Likewise over plague. Nowadays one does not fast at all during a plague, for experience shows that lack of food and drink makes one vulnerable, G-d forbid, to the altered air. Magen Avraham 2; Eliyah Rabbah 1. This was also transmitted in the name of the Arizal, and has support in Ta'anis 22. Maharam ibn Chaviv, manuscript responsum 16; Birkei Yosef 4; Shaarei Teshuvah 2. See the end of Yoreh De'ah 374. Regarding Tishah B'Av, see above siman 524, paragraph 35.
7) There: Likewise over plague. During a plague one should not go to a cemetery. Shemiras HaNefesh 61 in the name of Sefer Refuos; Zivchei Tzedek on Yoreh De'ah 116, paragraph 107.
8) During a plague, one should not walk in the middle of the roads, because the Angel of Death has permission and walks openly. When there is peace in the city, one should not walk along the sides of the roads, because the Angel lacks permission and hides himself. Gemara, Bava Kamma, chapter HaKones. The rule during peace applies specifically at night, when harmful forces are common; by day they are not. The plague rule applies both day and night. Taz on Yoreh De'ah 116:5 in the name of Maharshal; Knesses HaGedolah there, Hagahos Beis Yosef 35; Beis Lechem Yehudah 12; Zivchei Tzedek 40.
9) When there is a plague in the city, the deceased are examined, lest one of them have swallowed the shroud, which is dangerous, or have hands not extended, as it says, 'After him all men will be drawn.' Anything that involves possible danger to life may be done to a corpse. Sefer Chasidim 451. Zivchei Tzedek there, paragraph 42, records that in Baghdad in 5533, during an earlier plague, people mistakenly opened the grave of a woman who died at its outset and saw her consuming her shrouds, may the Merciful One protect us. The av beis din ordered that she be slaughtered; when this was done, the plague immediately ceased. See there.
10) Pitum HaKetores is a wondrous practice to recite during a plague. It is also good to fulfill 'You shall meditate upon it day and night'; mystically, the numerical value of 'lekach tov' equals that of 'mageifah.' One must also avoid falsehood; this is a great principle, for one who speaks falsely during a plague is in danger, may the Merciful One protect us. Beis David IV:57 in the name of a manuscript of the Arizal. Zohar, Vayeira 101, Midrash HaNe'elam, says that forty worthy men from the city should be divided among its four corners, ten at each, recite Pitum HaKetores with great concentration, and then the verses 'Moshe said to Aharon: Take the firepan, etc.,' 'Aharon took it as Moshe had spoken, etc.,' and 'He stood between the dead, etc.' They did this three times and the plague ceased. See also Shaar HaKavanos, discourse on Pitum HaKetores, for additional passages to recite during a plague, may the Merciful One protect us.
11) When there is a plague in the city, it is fitting to cover one's forehead so that the Angel of Death will not recognize the sins engraved there. Beis David there in the name of Asis Rimonim, page 18a. See further in Yoreh De'ah 116:5, gloss, and the later authorities there.
12) There: A city containing five hundred footmen, etc. The five hundred include both Jews and gentiles. Birkei Yosef 2.
13) There: And three deaths occurred in it, etc. Maharikash writes in his glosses that since it says simply 'three deaths' rather than 'three of them,' plague status applies even if the dead were women, children, or elders; they need not be counted among the footmen. This is common practice. The Shulchan Aruch's later statement that women and children are not included among the city's people, however, implies they are also excluded in counting the dead. Levush explicitly writes that women, children, and elders who ceased working are not included among the deaths because they are physically weak and may have died without plague. Birkei Yosef 3 challenges Maharikash and writes that Maran holds the opposite view; Maamar Mordechai 2 agrees.
14) There: And three deaths occurred in three days, etc. One death on each day. Rashi, Ta'anis 21a. The three must have been healthy rather than infirm, and young rather than old. Shiltei HaGibborim; Pis'chei Olam 2.
15) There: They occurred on one day. Meaning that all three occurred on the first day, while none occurred on the second and third days.
16) There: This is not a plague. Because it is not an established pattern, but merely an incidental occurrence. Beis Yosef; Levush.
