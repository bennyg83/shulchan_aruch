1) [Se'if 1] Just as we fast and sound the alarm over rain, etc. Rambam writes at the beginning of chapter 1 of Hilchos Ta'anis that it is a positive Torah commandment to cry out and sound trumpets over every distress that comes upon the community, as it says: 'Against the adversary who oppresses you, you shall sound the trumpets.' That is, cry out and sound the alarm over anything that causes distress, such as drought or plague. This is among the paths of repentance: when distress comes and people cry out and sound the alarm, everyone realizes that their evil deeds brought the harm, as written, 'Your sins diverted these,' and this will cause the distress to be removed. Regarding Rambam's trumpets, Maggid Mishneh writes that Rashba's view is clearest: in the Temple both trumpets and shofar were used, while outside the Temple only one; trumpets or shofar. Magen Avraham at this siman's beginning asks why we do not sound them in distress. Nehar Shalom 1 answers that the practice relies on Semag, who did not mention this commandment even by allusion. Mishbetzos Zahav 575:2 suggests the verse says 'in your land,' perhaps excluding the Diaspora, and perhaps even in Eretz Yisrael when it is not under our control. See the later authorities' additional answers.
2) There: 'Or to take a tax from them' means a new tax. The same applies if a ruler or government official persecutes them with accusations to establish a new permanent expense: a fast may be decreed. But not for a single, temporary, incidental accusation. Maharam ibn Chaviv, manuscript responsum 16; Birkei Yosef 1.
3) There: 'Even if they came only to pass through their land, etc.' This applies even to a peaceful army. No army was more peaceful than Pharaoh Necho's, yet Yoshiyahu was harmed by it. Ta'anis 22a; Beis Yosef.
4) There: Even if they came only to pass through, etc. Although such a case does not appear to be a complete distress, one nevertheless fasts over it. Maamar Mordechai 1.
5) See Maggid Meisharim to Maran, Parashas Emor, page 35d. It states that when they circled Rabbi Elazar with the Four Species, which appease regarding water, the waters were aroused and came. Had they circled again, extremely abundant rain would have come, as in the days of Choni HaMe'agel. Therefore continuous rains of blessing came so that they would not circle them again. Keep this principle: whenever the world greatly needs rain, go, circle the aforementioned righteous men, and respond; and for any distress that should not befall the community, circle them four times and respond. The Four Species appear necessary only for rain, because they appease regarding water; for other distresses it suffices to circle seven times. 'The aforementioned righteous men' seemingly includes other righteous men as well; the incident merely happened with these.
