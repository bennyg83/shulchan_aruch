As much as fills the mouth of the oven, etc. Meaning grain fit to make bread as much as fills the mouth of an oven, which is a minimal amount, etc.; meaning not a true mere trifle, but since the work of [filling] the mouth of an oven is a small thing, it is called a mere amount; so wrote Beis Yosef and Maggid Mishneh. And Lechem Chamudos wonders why the Rambam omitted the doubt in the Gemara: whether it means the cover of the oven, or the row of loaves stuck at the mouth of the oven one beside another, or alternatively one above the other. And it seems to me that he ruled stringently, and therefore spoke without qualification that for the smaller measure they Fast. And Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, wonders at what Rashi explained on Taanis folio 22, that the incident was thus; but as a matter of law, any amount at all suffices; if so, what is the question, for whatever was, was? And it seems to me that Rashi explained thus only according to R' Akiva there, and he holds that the anonymous Mishnah comes even like R' Akiva; but according to the first Tanna we establish the Mishnah as "as much as fills the mouth of an oven" as law, and the question stands for the first Tanna. But this requires study, for the Rif, the Rosh, and the Remazim ruled as any [literal] amount at all.
