(19) Even the distant ones: And this requires study regarding the Rama, that he did not gloss here that it be only in that province, as he glossed in se'if 8; and likewise why he did not dispute the words of the Shulchan Aruch in se'if 1 and se'if 12, where he wrote "and all the cities round about them" etc., to explain that this likewise means the distant ones that were in that province.
(20) It was seen in the field by day: if it saw, etc.: This implies that in the field at night, where its way is to be there, standing still is no proof that it was sent forth to harm; but in the city at night, where there is also one side of suspicion, it is possible that this too is included in being sent forth.
(21) Two people: As it is said, "And the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast of the earth"; and this one that stood against them and did not flee is included in being sent forth.
(22) A field adjacent to a marsh, etc.: Meaning in this case, if it stood still so long as it did not pursue them, it is not [deemed] sent forth; for since it is adjacent to a marsh, that is its habitat, and the reason it did not flee is that it reasoned: if they come after me, I will flee to the marsh at once.
(23) In a marsh: It is full of reeds and small trees; and the same applies to a large forest.
(24) Even if it pursued them, it is not [deemed] sent forth: For since that is its place, it relied on that awareness and ran after them.
(25) And it ate one of them: There is a novelty here: that even though it ate one of them, it is [deemed] sent forth, since it tore the second and did not eat it; and all the more so if it did not eat at all, it is certainly sent forth, since it tore them (Tosafos). And in the commentary of Rabbeinu Chananel the opposite is stated; see there.
