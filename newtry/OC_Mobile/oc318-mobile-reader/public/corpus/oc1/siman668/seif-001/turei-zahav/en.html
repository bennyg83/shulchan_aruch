And our custom is that one does not say 'festival,' etc. In Yoreh Deah, siman 220, se'if 20, I brought from chapter Eilu Ne'emarim, folio 41, that Shemini Atzeres is called a festival, from the fact that we say: if the Merciful One had written 'on the festival of Sukkos,' I would have said even on the last Yom Tov; therefore the Merciful One wrote 'when all Yisrael come.' This proves that were it not for 'when they come,' etc., I would have said that the last Yom Tov, which is Shemini Atzeres, is called a festival. Afterward they challenged me and said that this 'last Yom Tov' mentioned by the Talmud does not mean Shemini Atzeres, since 'festival of Sukkos' is written about it, but rather refers to the last day of Chol HaMoed. I answered this: it is impossible to say so. First, we do not find anywhere in the Gemara that the days of Chol HaMoed are called Yom Tov, for at the beginning of HeChalil we say that it does not override Yom Tov, which proves that Chol HaMoed is not Yom Tov. There on folio 56 the Talmud mentions the last Yom Tov of the festival many times, and likewise in Megillah, chapter Bnei HaIr, and all of it refers to Shemini Atzeres. Why should we explain this expression here unlike the manner in many places? Furthermore, what is the meaning of the word 'even' that the Gemara says? There is no greater status in one day of Chol HaMoed over another; rather, it should have said, I would have said that there is no difference between the first Yom Tov and the last Yom Tov. Furthermore, why mention the language of Yom Tov here at all? It should have said only 'last day.' Rather, it is clear that it refers to Shemini Atzeres, as the implication of the verse, which called it a festival, and it states a novelty: even the last Yom Tov is included, for it is also related to Sukkos, since it is Atzeres from it, as appears in Rashi on Chumash, that the Holy One, blessed be He, said: your separation is difficult for Me. Everywhere, the last day of Sukkos is called the last Yom Tov. Since the Talmud needed to say that it is divided from it regarding peh, zayin, reish, kuf, shin, beis, this proves that without that it is one festival and related to it, even though on that day we do not sit in sukkos. A comparable matter is the festival of matzos all seven days: the obligation to eat matzah is only on the first night, and on the other days there is only the obligation to avoid chametz. Why then are all the days called the festival of matzos? They should have been called the prohibition of chametz. Rather, everything is drawn after the first night, and the other days come because of it. So too here regarding the eighth day of the festival. Therefore, one who forbade meat to himself during 'the festival' without specification, meaning the festival of Sukkos, forbade it even on the last Yom Tov. I would derive this from the fact that the Merciful One tied it to the language 'festival of Sukkos,' which includes even the last Yom Tov, which is also called a festival. They further challenged me: how could I have thought that it refers to Shemini Atzeres, since it is impossible then to fix the bimah, as Rashi wrote afterward in the conclusion on that which is written, 'the Merciful One wrote: when all Yisrael come,' from the beginning of the moed. However, on Yom Tov, no, for fixing the bimah overrides neither Shabbos nor Yom Tov; the previous day as well we do not make it, because it would crowd the Azarah. I say that this is not difficult at all, for one can ask about what the Mishnah wrote, 'they make him a bimah,' etc., which implies that it is a halachah leMoshe miSinai that a bimah is required. Why indeed should it not override Yom Tov, since the verse says 'when all Yisrael come,' which is as though it were written 'on the first day'? Regarding milah it overrides Shabbos because it is written 'on the eighth day'; here too let us say so. If this matter of the bimah were a halachah leMoshe miSinai, why would it be delayed until after Yom Tov? Rather, the matter is this: in the verse we have an inclusion and an exclusion. The language 'on the festival of Sukkos' means all the days of the festival, even the last Yom Tov, and the bimah is made before it. But 'when all Yisrael come' excludes, that it is not all the days of the festival but the beginning of the festival. We therefore must uphold both: not literally at the beginning and not literally at the end, but an intermediate matter. We place it where it is reasonable, namely at the beginning of Chol HaMoed, at a time when there is permission to make a bimah. Although also on the first Yom Tov there is permission for a bimah, meaning if it is made on erev Yom Tov, reason dictates not to do so, since it would crowd the Azarah; it is more preferable to make the bimah on that very day. If so, the verse taught us with this inclusion and exclusion that it is made on an intermediate day, which is more reasonable and has no deficiency. This appears to me correct in Rashi's view, and the difficulty of Tosafos there is not difficult against him; see there. According to these words, that Shemini Atzeres is included within the festival and drawn after it, we should say, 'this day of Shemini Atzeres, this festival.' Certainly one should not say 'this festival of Shemini Atzeres,' for we need to hint that it is not a festival in its own right, but only because of the festival of Sukkos; rather, it is called Atzeres of the festival, as if saying that it is subordinate to the festival. So too it appears in the Maariv piyyut of Simchas Torah: 'on this Atzeres, festival of the ingathering,' etc. I later saw in Responsa Maharshal that he agreed to this formulation, and not to say 'this eighth day, festival of Atzeres,' because one should not interrupt between 'eighth' and 'Atzeres,' since in the verse they are adjacent. It is therefore proper for us to say in this language, against which there is no difficulty. It is written in Hagahos Minhagim: that which some have the custom to eat half the meal in the sukkah and half the meal in the house, this custom is not mentioned anywhere. But it appears to me that there is a clear proof from Midrash Tanchuma, parashas Pinchas: 'Our teacher should teach us,' etc., until: Why did the Sages permit one to exempt himself from the sukkah on the final Yom Tov of the festival? Rather, all seven days of the festival they pray for dew, and on the final Yom Tov they pray for rains; therefore they are exempt from the sukkah, in order to pray for rains with a full heart. End quote. The Maharshal wrote in Responsa, siman 68, that he saw a gloss in the name of Maharar Tevil, that he would not eat at the entry of the day of Shemini Atzeres until night, for if so, he would need to recite the blessing of leishev basukkah, since it is still day, etc. The Maharshal wrote a proof for this from the blessing of leishev basukkah: if he will not say it, this is not proper, for why should an obligatory blessing be diminished? Granted that blessings do not prevent fulfillment, that is after the fact. But to obligate himself in a blessing, meaning to eat in the sukkah while it is still day, and not say it, is a great sin. One cannot say that since he adds from weekday to holy, the day has passed. This is not so. Granted, we add in prayer, as we find that Rav prayed the Shabbos prayer on erev Shabbos, but not to make it night, for he would say the evening Shema at its time. For this reason, it is not proper to count the Omer on Friday night after Kiddush until night, as I wrote in Responsa, siman 13. To say it also is not proper, for they contradict each other: if he says leishev basukkah, it is weekday, and why does Kiddush come? If it is Yom Tov, why does leishev basukkah come? A great proof is from the words of the Rif and the Rosh at the end of Lulav VaAravah, and this is their language: Regarding sitting in the sukkah on Shemini Atzeres, the halachah is that we sit, but we do not recite the blessing, since it is Shemini Atzeres and it is impossible to recite a blessing over the sukkah, for the two contradict each other. Because we are uncertain about both, we act stringently: we sit, and we practice it as Yom Tov, which is Shemini Atzeres, stringently. End quote. Thus, were it not that we act stringently because of doubt, we would not know what to do, how to sit and not recite the blessing. If so, where it is still day and there is no doubt, how can one sit and not recite the blessing? And to recite the blessing is impossible, because the two contradict each other. I then examined the Ran, that one should not shorten an obligatory blessing even if he makes Yom Tov into weekday, for he wrote as follows: The explanation of 'we sit' is that even though we know the fixed calendar, it is because they sent from there: be careful with the custom of your fathers. But we do not recite the blessing so as not to degrade Yom Tov; so ruled the Rif. And although on the second Yom Tov of Pesach we keep Yom Tov and also count the Omer, there it is different, because the Omer and Kiddush do not come together; rather, each one is done by itself. But here the sukkah blessing is on one cup together with Kiddush, and if one recites the sukkah blessing on Shemini Atzeres, he comes to degrade Yom Tov. It does not appear to me that this is necessary, for counting cannot be pushed aside because of degradation, since we know the fixed calendar and he is obligated in counting according to the Torah. But sukkah on the eighth, which is a doubtful seventh, is rabbinic. End quote. Thus, if he is obligated according to the Torah, it is not proper that the blessing be uprooted. Here, if it is day, he is obligated according to the Torah not to eat outside the sukkah, and if he eats, he must recite the blessing, so it is not proper to uproot it. Nevertheless, after the fact it does not appear that he should recite the blessing, for the Ran does not disagree with the first reason; he only wrote, 'it does not appear to me that this is necessary,' etc. According to the first reason, since it is one matter, it is impossible to degrade Yom Tov. Therefore it is a great prohibition for one to recite Kiddush before night, etc. End quote. These are the words of the Rav, the Maharshal. Although one does not answer the lion after his death, and all the more so a fox like me, nevertheless I will express my view: the words of the Rav are very surprising, and the mind of my poor understanding cannot bear them. Why did he decree a fast for no reason from the entry of Shemini Atzeres, which is while it is still day, and this is necessary because adding from weekday to holy is according to the Torah, so that perforce one must fast until night? This is certainly impossible. First, the Sages certainly would not have been silent about this. We see that they labored to find an arrangement for the day of Shemini Atzeres, to sit and not recite a blessing; why did they not find an arrangement for its entry until night, to save a person from a great prohibition, that he not come to eat before night? At least they should have warned us about this, especially since most people do not know. We should not add a prohibition beyond what is found in the words of the Gemara and poskim. Furthermore, according to his concern, it would be forbidden to enter the sukkah at that time, for the Rif, Rosh, and Tur ruled that one must recite a blessing every time he enters it, as written in siman 639, except that Rabbeinu Tam explained that the main fixed dwelling is eating, so he recites a blessing over eating and exempts all other matters. Since this person is forbidden to eat in the sukkah, with what will he exempt what he is obligated for by entering, according to the essential law? If you will say that what he recited during the day exempts him, this is not so, for he has already turned away from that fixed dwelling and interrupted it with his Shemini Atzeres prayer. When he enters afterward while it is still day, he is obligated for his entry, and no one exempts him. Furthermore, according to his words, on every Yom Tov that falls on erev Shabbos it should be forbidden to eat until night, because that day obligates him to recite in Birkas HaMazon Yaaleh Veyavo for the food he eats, since it is still day and the sanctity of Yom Tov is upon him; yet because he accepted Shabbos upon himself, the Yom Tov departed from him. How then will he handle Yaaleh Veyavo? He should need not to eat until night. One cannot say that corresponding to this he says Retzei, for here too, corresponding to leishev basukkah, he says Kiddush. In truth, the reasoning on which the Rav built is not sustainable at all. Certainly one who adds from weekday to holy acts according to the command of our Torah; what obligation of the day that was upon him before this has already passed and gone from him, and it is like actual night and tomorrow. What the Rav wrote to refute this from Rav praying the Shabbos prayer on erev Shabbos and waiting with Shema until night, and likewise from the Omer, astonishes me regarding a great man whose light shines with him. What did he think in this? The cases are not comparable at all. Shema and the Omer have their time at night, so why should he advance them while it is still day and do them not according to their law because he accepted Shabbos upon himself? What would he lack by waiting with these matters and not relying on his acceptance of Shabbos while it is still day? Is it not enough without this? This is unlike the obligation of sukkah before us, both from the standpoint of entry and from the standpoint of eating, which is necessarily prevented from him at the time he accepts the sanctity of the Yom Tov of Shemini Atzeres, by which he contradicts the mitzvah of sukkah. He has the ability to do so. It is obvious that he immediately enters the category of: we sit, but we do not recite a blessing. It is like night, for the holy mouth, may He be exalted, blessed is He, that commanded him in the mitzvah of sukkah on that day is the same holy mouth that permitted him after he accepted Shemini Atzeres upon himself. It turns out that everything the Maharshal wrote from the Rif and the Ran is not a proof, for there is no distinction at all between after accepting Shemini Atzeres while it is still day and the following day. Who would give us of the finest of the fine, that we could eat at that time in the sukkah, only without reciting leishev basukkah; this appears clear to me.
