Night. It is forbidden to establish a meal from Minchah time and onward, unlike those who eat, drink, and become intoxicated until they cannot recite Kiddush at night; Magen Avraham.
The festival of Atzeres. The Maharshal wrote in a responsum to say, 'this day of Shemini Atzeres, this festival,' and the Taz agreed; see there. See Responsa Shevus Yaakov, simanim 78 and 142, regarding what he challenged against him; see there. The Levush wrote as the Beis Yosef wrote.
We eat. But we do not sleep in the sukkah, for with eating there is a recognition, since he does not recite the blessing, unlike sleeping, where there is no recognition; Mordechai. For this reason they hurry to leave after the morning meal; Maharil and Levush. There are those who do not sit in it at night but do sit in it by day, and this is not a proper custom; Tur. The Magen Avraham wrote that in our country there are some people who practice this way because they saw their fathers do so; see there. It is implied in all the poskim that one must eat all meals of that day in the sukkah, and even if a meal happens for him during bein hashmashos, he must eat it there, for this is the reason that we forbid sukkah decorations on the ninth day. What Hagahos Minhagim wrote to somewhat justify the common practice to eat only half the meal in the sukkah so that they pray for rain with a full heart, etc., see there, is not a proof. He himself also wrote that if it is Shabbos, one must eat seudah shelishis in the sukkah, and so wrote the Bach in the name of the Mordechai; this is the custom of the meticulous; Magen Avraham.
And we do not recite a blessing. Therefore one should not eat at night until it is dark; Hagahos Minhagim. So too wrote the Maharshal in Responsa, siman 68, the Bach, and the Levush. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if he ate after the Maariv prayer while it was still day, he should not recite the blessing of leishev basukkah, for he has already accepted Shemini Atzeres in his prayer and in Kiddush; Magen Avraham. The Taz disagrees with them and concludes: who would give us of the finest of the fine, that we could eat in the sukkah at that time, only without reciting the blessing of leishev basukkah; this appears clear to me.
