[1] Eighth, festival, etc. So too the Mateh Moshe agreed, for then it implies that it is subordinate to the festival. Even according to the one who says that it is a festival in its own right, that is regarding peh, zayin, reish, kuf, shin, beis, but regarding compensation it belongs to the first. The Maharshal wrote to say, 'this day of Shemini Atzeres, this festival,' but the world does not practice like him. End quote. I already wrote in Eliyahu Zuta that it is proper to practice like the Levush, for this is the main view among the early and later poskim. I will now explain: I examined and found that the Tur, Rashi, Tosafos, Sefer Tanya, Avudraham, the Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Agudah, Shibolei HaLeket, Kol Bo, Maharil, Maaglei Tzedek, and Rokeach all ruled this way. If so, who would think to stand against the early and later authorities mentioned above? Also, what the Rama wrote in the name of the Minhagim, that one should not say 'festival' because we do not find anywhere that it is called a festival, is nullified in relation to them. Likewise, at the end of Sefer Ahavah, the Rambam wrote to say 'festival,' and this escaped the Levush. Now, in the Taz to Yoreh Deah, siman 220, he brings proof from Sotah 41, where it says: If the Merciful One had written only 'on the festival of Sukkos,' I would have said even on the last Yom Tov; therefore the Merciful One wrote, 'when all Yisrael come,' from the beginning of the moed. This implies that were it not for 'when they come,' I would have said that Shemini Atzeres is called a festival. Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 78, objected that on the contrary, this is why 'when all Yisrael come' is written, to tell us not to err that Shemini Atzeres is called a festival; see there at length. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for the derivation from 'when they come' is needed also to exclude the six days of Sukkos itself. If so, how can we learn from this that Shemini Atzeres is not called a festival? I further saw in Responsa Beis Yaakov, siman 142, that he objected to what the Taz wrote here, that we do not find anywhere in the Talmud that Chol HaMoed is called Yom Tov, for in Sukkah 38 it calls the first day of Chol HaMoed 'second Yom Tov' for Bnei Eretz Yisrael, who have only the first Yom Tov; see there at length. In my humble opinion this is not difficult at all, for one can say that specifically the first Chol HaMoed is called Yom Tov, since for Bnei Chutz LaAretz it is a complete Yom Tov, unlike the other days, as it says here, 'even the last Yom Tov.' Know that the Taz himself wrote in siman 652 that it is called Yom Tov. Furthermore, there is more proof to bring from Sukkah 29: there is no difference between the first Yom Tov and the second Yom Tov; and there it refers to Bnei Eretz Yisrael, as above at the end of siman 649. So too on folio 36: here, on the second Yom Tov.
