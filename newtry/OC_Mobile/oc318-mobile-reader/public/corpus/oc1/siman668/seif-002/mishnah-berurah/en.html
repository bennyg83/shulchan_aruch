(9) And on the first they read, etc. - Here he did not say that five are called, as below, because in Eretz Yisrael, where they observe only one day of Yom Tov, that day itself is Simchat Torah; therefore they read Vezot Haberachah on it, and on Simchat Torah the custom is to add to the number of aliyot, as above in siman 282.
(10) And on the second, Bereshit, etc. - Therefore the practice is to begin Bereshit immediately, so that the Satan not have an opening to accuse and say that they have already finished it and do not wish to read it anymore, as the Tur writes.
(11) To do - They place the third sefer Torah beside it and say half Kaddish.
(12) Kol habekhor - We begin with Aser te'aser even if it falls on a weekday, because it is the time of tithes and gifts to the poor. However, after the fact, if he already blessed intending to read Kol habekhor, he does not need to repeat, as Derech HaChayim writes in the laws of Torah reading.
(13) Seven - If he completed the parashah with five, he should not say Kaddish, but should go back and call two more aliyot, or at least one aliyah besides maftir. If he did say Kaddish, as long as he has not removed the sefer Torah he should go back, read, and say Kaddish a second time. If he removed the first scroll, he should call one aliyah to the second scroll and complete with him the parashah of Aser te'aser, and afterward call the maftir for the obligation of the day, as Shaarei Ephraim writes.
(14) Te'aser - They place the second sefer Torah beside it and say half Kaddish.
(15) Atzeret - They mention souls and say Av Harachamim, as Levush writes.
(16) And he announces Mashiv haruach, etc. - This is explained above in siman 114; see there. The practice in some places, after Minchah of the first day of Yom Tov, to sit down to drink until evening, sometimes continuing an hour or more into the night, is improper for several reasons. First, we hold in siman 99 that if one drank a revi'it of wine, he should not pray even if he can still speak before the king; the same applies if he drank other intoxicating beverages, as explained there in seif katan 1. Even if he drinks other beverages that do not intoxicate, once doubtful nightfall arrives the obligation of Kiddush rests on him and he may not taste until he recites Kiddush. Even after Kiddush, he is still prevented from eating because of the evening Shema and is permitted only a mere taste, such as fruit or bread up to a kebeitzah and no more. Yet one may search for merit for them, since many remind one another and they will not come to forget Maariv. In any event, one must be careful then not to drink an intoxicating beverage; all this is explained above in siman 99 seif katans 1 and 2.
