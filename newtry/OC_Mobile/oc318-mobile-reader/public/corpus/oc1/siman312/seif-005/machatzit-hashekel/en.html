(א) (s.k. 5) Because, etc. — and so in the gemara daf 82a: one may not wipe with reed peel, and Rashi explained because sparks fly from it and pierce the intestines:

(ב) It is implied that other grasses, etc. — for it is taught there: five things with reed peel one may not slaughter with it, etc., and one may not wipe with it; and Rashi explained in all of them the reason is because of sorcery when they press it — sparks fly from it and there is danger in circumcision lest the cord be pierced, in slaughter lest the signs be pierced, and likewise in wiping as stated; and they challenge: but it is taught "with all one slaughters," etc., "whether with reed peel" — Rav Papa said: with signs of the marsh; Rashi explained: marsh grass when dry is sharp and cuts, and sparks do not separate from it; behold even reed peel — some do not cut; and the same applies to other grasses; and how did Shulchan Aruch forbid to wipe with all dry grasses because they cut:

(ג) But nevertheless he wrote, etc. — for on this that they say daf 82: a clod and grasses — wipe with grasses; and they challenge: but we say one who wipes with a thing over which fire dominates — his lower teeth fall out; and they answer: here with moist ones, here with dry ones; and Rashi explained: dry ones over which fire dominates — one may not wipe; and that which is said here "a clod and grasses — wipe with grasses" deals with moist ones over which fire does not dominate; and so wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot: some have the reverse version — with moist ones one may not wipe, etc.; and so wrote Magen Avraham: requires further study how he said that that which we say "one who wipes with a thing over which fire dominates" deals with moist ones, and dry ones fire does not dominate — on the contrary, here with moist ones fire does not dominate; and further, a difficulty for Hagahot Maimoniyot: what did they challenge in Chullin, and answer that one who wipes with a thing, etc. — what is the difficulty; perhaps they mentioned the reason because of sparks to forbid even with moist ones where there is no danger of lower teeth; granted according to Rashi's explanation that with dry ones there is concern of lower teeth and not with moist ones — why need they mention the reason because of sparks to forbid even with moist ones; here with moist ones there are also no sparks; and presumably it deals with dry ones, for otherwise they are not cut and not fit for slaughter and circumcision mentioned in the baraita; but for Hagahot Maimoniyot it is difficult:

(ד) But in Tur Choshen Mishpat, etc. — for they say in Bava Kamma: ten conditions Joshua stipulated with Israel, and one of them is relieving behind his fellow's fence; and R' Acha bar Yaakov said: it was needed only to take a clod from it; R' Chisda said: even on Shabbat; and Yeshuot Malko's words are according to our version that reads "to take from it" with a vav at the end of the word — it refers to the fence; but the Tur who wrote "to take from it" with a heh at the end — it must refer to the field; and it must be that he had this version also in the gemara:

(ה) They already wrote in the laws of Shabbat that it is permitted to take a clod to wipe with it:

(ו) In the incident of Mar Zutra the pious — as should be — meaning from there it is implied per the version before us that it refers to a fence; for Mar Zutra the pious would take on a weekday and say to his attendant: go plaster it and join it well, end of his words; and this language is only regarding a fence:
