And if recognizable on the stone. Shlah wrote, and it is difficult: what is stated regarding chips that if one sat on them intentionally it is permitted even though they are not recognizable on them, as is written siman 308 seif 20; and it is possible to say that a large stone does not stand for wiping, whereas other stones that one wiped with intentionally — one need not that the mark be recognizable on them, but rather he mentioned "their mark is recognizable" — then it is permitted to move the stones adjacent to him, for he testifies about the others that they too were prepared for this; and specifically when he did not designate them explicitly for this; but if he designated them — permitted, like one who designates a stone to crack nuts with it; and the same applies to one who designates smooth paper to wipe with it — permitted, end of his words; and see siman 308 and siman 322 that some dispute regarding designation, and in my humble opinion the difficulty from the outset does not exist: for granted regarding chips, since one sat on them intentionally and left them in their place in order to sit on them again — they are not muktzeh; but a stone with which he wiped and threw away again — his mind was not upon it for use; therefore we require that the wiping be recognizable on it; therefore one may not be lenient to take the others; and so is clear in the gemara and in Shulchan Aruch:
