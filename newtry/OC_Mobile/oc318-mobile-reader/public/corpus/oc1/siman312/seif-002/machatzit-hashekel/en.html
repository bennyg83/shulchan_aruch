(א) (s.k. 2) And if recognizable, etc. — and he said that a large stone is not for wiping. Meaning — specifically chips that presumably stand for sitting and were forbidden only through his intent that he cut them for burning; therefore it suffices with intent or sitting to remove them from the initial thought; and as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 39 and s.k. 40:

(ב) And other stones that one wiped with, etc. — meaning — still a difficulty remains with stones one wiped with: that presumably they stand for wiping — why was it required on them that their mark be recognizable, unlike chips; and to this he answered that "their mark is recognizable" helps regarding the adjacent stones that are permitted even though he did not wipe with them and did not designate them:

(ג) And see siman 308 in Magen Avraham s.k. 10 regarding paper, and seif 22 that some require designation; but even in a usual object we require specifically that an action be done with it:

(ד) But a stone with which he wiped and threw it away, etc. — therefore it requires that it be recognizable, etc. — for then even though he threw it away, nevertheless it stands for wiping:

(ה) Therefore one may not be lenient to take the others, etc. — meaning — what Shlah wrote to permit if one of them has a recognizable mark — the adjacent ones are permitted even though he did not wipe with them; and according to Magen Avraham one may not be lenient in this, for Shlah himself did not explain that they permitted except from the necessity of his difficulty; and since Magen Avraham answered Shlah's difficulty — from where do we learn to permit such a case:

(ו) And so is clear in the gemara and in Shulchan Aruch. In the gemara it is on this that if the mark is recognizable it is permitted to bring in even many more than a full hand; and the gemara challenges: what does a recognizable mark help — for since he already wiped with it, forbidden to wipe with it again, as it is taught: one who wipes with a clod with which his fellow wiped brings to loosening of his lower teeth; and they answer there; and for Shlah — that very clod with which he wiped and its mark is recognizable testifies about the adjacent ones: even though he did not wipe with them — nevertheless they were prepared from erev Shabbat and permitted; and with them there is no concern of loosening of lower teeth, for no person wiped with them; and in Shulchan Aruch it is implied so, and from the conclusion "since he wiped with them for many people" — it is implied that we require that he wipe with all of them:
