(א) (s.k. 1) A designated place, etc. — it appears to me that this applies, etc., came to exclude the view of Bach, and as Magen Avraham wrote in this s.k. 1, not like Bach; for Bach explains that what is written "if he has a designated place" means that it is designated for him alone and others do not enter — therefore it was permitted to him a full hand, for if some remain for him he will wipe with them himself in the morning; unlike when others also go there — if some remain for him others will come and take them to wipe — it was not permitted to him except the measure of a spoonful; and see how he was challenged from the Tur's words; but Magen Avraham explains "designated place" means designated for him — that he goes every time to that place, even though others also go there — it makes no difference. And so is clear in R' Shimon of Shantz in the gloss: even though selecting on Shabbat is forbidden, nevertheless what one selects for the need of that meal is permitted, even if others also eat with him — permitted, and behold he selects for the need of others — nevertheless permitted. And some say regarding siman 333 that he wrote it is permitted to clear a storehouse first for guests or for students — permitted to clear four or five crates for each one; and we rule there that each one must clear for himself and not that one clears for all of them:

(ב) For it is one trouble — and with this the law here was settled: since he does not bring except a full hand it is well one trouble whether he brings a full hand or only the measure of a spoonful; but still requires settlement of that which R' Shimon of Shantz wrote regarding selecting: for certainly the trouble in selecting, even on behalf of others, is more trouble than if he had not selected except for himself; therefore he was precise and wrote "and if he has no designated place," etc. — he meant: since he opened with "if he has a designated place," etc., he should also have concluded "and if he has no designated place" but changed and concluded "and if he has no designated place" — to inform us that the explanation is not like Bach, and there is no distinction whether others also enter there, but rather it depends on this: if he has a designated place — meaning that he goes sometimes to another place:

(ג) And so is clear from Tosafos at the end of s.v. "all of it" — he meant in Shabbos daf 81b: Rabbah bar Shmuel asked R' Chiya: what about bringing up stones to a bathroom on a roof adjacent to him; and Tosafos explained that he was in doubt: perhaps since the bathroom is adjacent to him they did not permit to bring on Shabbat, for this is preparing from erev Shabbat and to bring it up to the roof; and he resolved that it is permitted because great is human dignity that overrides a lav of the Rabbis; and Ravina challenged: but the Rabbis of R' Eliezer hold it is forbidden to take a splinter from before him to pick between his teeth, and here too there is human dignity — for sometimes flesh stuck between the teeth is seen outside and it is disgraceful to him; and nevertheless the Rabbis forbade; therefore their reason is that one must prepare from erev Shabbat; and they answer: granted there a person designates a place for a meal and it is well said that one must prepare from erev Shabbat; but here a person does not designate a place for a bathroom and there is no need to prepare from erev Shabbat; and Tosafos wrote: that which he did not challenge on the entire subject matter that they permitted to move stones for the need of a bathroom — therefore on account of human dignity, and we do not say one must prepare from erev Shabbat; and it should be difficult from the Rabbis who forbade taking a splinter to pick his teeth, since he did not raise to distinguish that at a meal a person designates a place, etc.; and Tosafos answered: since their custom was to relieve in a field that is distant — they did not trouble him the Rabbis to prepare from erev Shabbat; and further, if he prepares them there another will take them, end of Tosafos' words; and according to Bach who did not permit a full hand except when he has a designated place where others do not enter — therefore the second answer of Tosafos is difficult, for they wrote "and further, if he prepares them there another will take them" — still, according to what was permitted to him a full hand, others certainly do not enter there, and he must prepare from erev Shabbat; and in Sukkah 36 they wrote: and that which Rabbi Yanai said there — if he has a designated place for a bathroom, a full hand — there it deals with a field, for the place is distant and trouble to prepare from erev Shabbat; but for us who have a designated place in the house — forbidden unless he prepared; and perhaps since there are many household members who take them — it is not possible, and permitted, end of their words; behold even with many household members who go there — nevertheless a full hand is permitted, not like Bach:

(ד) And if he has a bathroom adjacent, etc. — they followed the reasoning of Tosafos that the place is close and there is no trouble if he prepares them from erev Shabbat, and also no need for concern another will take them, for no other enters there:
