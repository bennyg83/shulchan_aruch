[1] [Levush] Because a person does not consider, etc. It appears Levush understands the Gemara on 81a, which asks incredulously whether a person establishes a fixed place for relieving himself, as meaning that one ordinarily does not think in this manner, unlike Rashi's explanation. This resolves Bach's difficulty. According to this, even if he has a designated place at home where no other person can enter, it is permitted. Tur likewise writes that he has a designated place for himself alone. The phrase "for himself alone" implies that another person could otherwise enter there. Although Hagahot Ashiri does not appear this way, we follow the lenient reading. Tosafot on 81a also proves it, writing that even if he designates stones, another person may precede him. This can be understood as applying throughout the discussion. Thus the Gemara's difficulty concerns a place another person cannot reach. Nachalat Tzvi rules accordingly.
[2] A handful, etc. The known hand means the left hand, with which one wipes (Bach). Tur's wording "his hands" contradicts the Gemara. Taz suggests perhaps Tur had that reading in the Gemara, but this cannot be, since the Remezim also say "his hand," as do Rif, Rosh, and all the authorities. It therefore appears that Tur contains a scribal error and should read "his hand."
[3] [Levush] He will wipe with them afterward, etc. Even if another person can enter that place, since he regularly goes to this particular place it is permitted. This differs from one who has no fixed place and sometimes goes here and sometimes elsewhere (Tosafot there).
[4] With which he wiped, etc. Meaning he wiped with it before Shabbos, even if he did not formally designate it. The same applies when it is known that it was used for wiping even if no trace is visible. Likewise, if he explicitly designated it for wiping, it is permitted even if it was never used. The same applies to one who designates blank paper for wiping. Even without designation it is permitted like a pestle, as Shiltei Gibborim and Nachalat Tzvi write. Magen Avraham says that once one wiped with a stone and threw it away, he no longer intends it for use, and therefore the trace of wiping must be visible. This is incorrect, for if throwing it away removed its prepared status, even a visible trace would not help. Regarding taking other stones lying near the used stone, there is no basis for leniency.
[5] And some say that even, etc. This is primary. Nevertheless, when no other person can go there, one should not be lenient to carry from a karmelit into a private domain.
