1) [Seif 1] Because of human dignity, etc. Human dignity overrides a negative commandment in the Torah, namely the prohibition of 'you may not hide yourself.' The verse states, 'and you shall hide yourself from them,' from which the Sages expounded that there are occasions when one may hide himself, such as when he is an elder, and so forth. Here too, rabbinic moving, which the Sages linked to the prohibition of 'do not deviate,' is overridden in the interest of human dignity. Levush; Olas Shabbos, paragraph 1.
2) There: Because of human dignity, etc. Responsa Chavos Yair writes that one nevertheless requires atonement for this. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. This does not appear to follow from the words of the halachic authorities. Nevertheless, it is obvious that one should take every possible precaution not to move muktzeh when another method is available.
3) There: They permitted moving stones, etc. The same applies to pebbles or any similar item that is ordinarily not subject to being moved. Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 1. See Korban Ishah, siman 11, which states that one traveling in a wilderness who needs to relieve himself may move a washing vessel of water in increments of less than four amos in order to clean himself with it; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 1. See below, paragraph 17.
4) There: One who has a designated place, etc. The reason is that if stones remain in the evening, he may use them to clean himself in the morning. Taz, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Although others also enter there and might take the stones to clean themselves, he may nevertheless bring in a handful. This is not needless moving, because what difference does it make whether he or another person uses them? This is also implied at the beginning of siman 319 in the gloss. Magen Avraham there; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. This is contrary to Bach, who forbids bringing in a handful when others enter there; see there. If he has no fixed place, however, even if the place is fixed for others and one might argue that others will take them to clean themselves, so his moving was not needless, it is nevertheless forbidden because we are concerned that others may not take them. Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1.
5) There: A handful. Tur reads 'both his hands full.' Taz writes that perhaps this was his version of the Gemara, but he did not consult Rosh and the remazim, where the reading is 'a handful.' The wording in Tur must therefore be a scribal error. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2; Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2.
6) There: A handful. It says 'the hand full,' rather than merely 'a hand full,' meaning the left hand, with which one cleans himself. Bach; Eliyah Rabbah there. This makes a practical difference if the left hand is smaller than the right, or regarding whether a left-handed person may take only a left-handful. Mishbetzos Zahav there. See above, siman 3, paragraph 20. See also Eshel Avraham from Hagahos Kodesh, who inclines to permit cleaning oneself with a finger because smoothing does not apply to those hairs, nor does squeezing, and rabbinic prohibitions were permitted because of human dignity; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 2. One must nevertheless take care not to wipe forcefully, lest hairs be detached.
7) There: If he has no fixed place, etc. This means that he sometimes goes to one place and sometimes elsewhere. If he later goes elsewhere, his earlier moving will prove needless. He therefore brings in only the volume of a small pestle. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2.
8) If he has a privy near his home that no other person enters, he may not bring stones there on Shabbos, even the volume of a small pestle, because he should have designated them the previous day. Magen Avraham there in the name of Hagahos Ashri, Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 26, and Tosafos; Tosefes Shabbos there; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, inclines to be lenient and writes that Netzach Yosef rules this way; see there. This also appears to be Beis Yosef's view at the end of this siman.
9) There: The volume of a small pestle. This means the size of the foot of a small pestle used for spices. Beis Yosef, according to Rabbeinu Tam's understanding of Rabbeinu Chananel's version, which is also explicit in Yerushalmi; see there. This is an amount fit for cleaning oneself once. Levush. Rif and Rosh, however, read 'the size of the head of a small pestle,' and Tur likewise has this reading.
10) There: If a mark is recognizable on the stone, etc. The reason is that it has already become designated through this use, namely when one cleaned himself with it before Shabbos. It is possible that even when there is doubt, one may permit it because of human dignity, since the matter is rabbinic. Olas Shabbos, paragraph 2.
