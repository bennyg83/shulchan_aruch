(א) (s.k. 3) Permitted, etc. — and from the fact that it is unstated, etc. — for they say there: R' Levi said: a clod upon which grasses grew — permitted to wipe with it; and one who detaches from it on Shabbat is liable for a sin-offering; Abaye said: a perforated pot that was placed on the ground and he placed it on stakes — liable for detaching; if it was on stakes and he placed it on the ground — liable for planting; and Rashi explained on this that he is liable for detaching because he no longer benefits from the smell and moisture of the ground; nevertheless he wrote that presumably this "liable" means lashes of rebellion and rabbinically, for it resembles detaching; for if you would think he is liable for a sin-offering, that it is actual detaching — if so, also a clod upon which grasses grew, if he lifts it from the ground, he is likewise liable from the Torah; and why would they permit to wipe with it; and would the Rabbis permit a Torah prohibition on account of human dignity; but from Rambam's words he proved Hagahot HaRav — that presumably he holds he is liable for a sin-offering; if so, that which R' Levi permitted to wipe with a clod upon which grasses grew — presumably it deals when the clod rests on stakes; but if it rests on the ground — a Torah prohibition was not permitted on account of human dignity; therefore Magen Avraham wrote: from the fact that it is unstated in Shulchan Aruch and he did not stipulate that we require the clod to rest on stakes — it must be that he ruled like Rashi and those who stand in his line:

(ב) And so is clear siman 336 seif 8. For there he wrote: a pot, even if it is not perforated — one should be careful not to take it from upon the ground, etc.; behold he did not write language of prohibition, only language of caution; and he wrote "even if it is not perforated" — it is implied that also in a perforated one the law is so, for so is the sugya; and one can say more: impossible — that in a perforated one there is liability for a sin-offering; at any rate in a non-perforated one there is a full rabbinic prohibition, since he wrote that in a non-perforated one there is only mere caution; from which it is implied that in a perforated one there is only a rabbinic prohibition; but nevertheless in all this there is no full implication for my humble opinion:

(ג) And one who detaches, etc. — according to all. Meaning — in this Rashi also agrees: specifically in a perforated pot that one places on stakes, for he is not actually detaching but ceasing to draw moisture from the ground — Rashi wrote that there is only a rabbinic prohibition; but in detaching from the clod it is actual detaching — Rashi agrees he is liable for a sin-offering, as R' Levi stated explicitly: one who detaches from it is liable for a sin-offering; unlike Abaye who did not say liable for a sin-offering but only "liable" — so is the view of Hagahot HaRav, not like Hagahot HaRav who wrote that Rashi holds that also in detaching grasses from the clod there is no Torah prohibition; therefore Rashi did not have the version in R' Levi's words "liable for a sin-offering," there in Beit Yosef:
