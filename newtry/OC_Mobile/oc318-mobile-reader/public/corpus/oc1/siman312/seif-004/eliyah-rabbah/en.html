[8] That is not fit, etc. Since it is not fit for wiping, it is prohibited to move it (Tur).
[9] [Levush] Because of danger from sorcery, etc. It appears Levush thereby resolves Bach and the later authorities' difficulty that Tur contradicts himself: in siman 3 Tur gives the reason of sorcery, whereas here he says danger. Levush means that Tur's "danger" here is danger from sorcery, and that this is Tur's final understanding of the Gemara. Certainly a person can see whether a shard is so sharp that it may cut flesh, and if so he will not use it.
[10] And there is no danger, etc. Meaning no concern of sorcery, as above. He holds that the sorcery concern applies only to a shard that is not otherwise movable. This supports what I explained in siman 3, seif katan 10. Bach, however, understands that the rim of a shard also carries a sorcery concern, but on Shabbos it was permitted because it is preferable to a muktzeh clod. He therefore objects in siman 3 to Rabbenu Yerucham, who permits wiping with the rim of a shard even on a weekday. According to my explanation there is no difficulty. I did, however, find in the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter HaMotzi: "not only a shard, but even the rim of a barrel." This implies that the rim too carries a sorcery concern and is prohibited on a weekday, but on Shabbos is permitted in preference to a clod. This requires study.
