If he erred or is uncertain, etc. It appears that here it does not help to recite the phrase 90 times, as in siman 114, seif 9, because here he may not say, "Blessed are You, Hashem," since that would be a blessing in vain. If he says it without the Name, then during prayer, when he says it with the Name, he will revert to his accustomed wording, "Blessed are You, Hashem, the holy God." He therefore has no remedy. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. The Pri Chadash writes that it does help. Birkei Yosef, os 3, likewise writes that the words of the Magen Avraham are not compelling, for if he says, "Blessed are You, Hashem," pronouncing the Name where it is written, it helps, see there. However, the Shaliach Tzibbur in Shalmei Tzibbur, p. 306, disagrees with them and concurs with the Magen Avraham, see there. His words are cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, os 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, os 4, also cites the Magen Avraham. So ruled the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 3; Chayei Adam, klal 24, os 10; Mishna Berurah, os 3. Mateh Ephraim, os 7, writes that if one said 90 times, "Blessed are You, Hashem, the holy King," and is now uncertain, he should pray again as a voluntary prayer. Therefore, since the poskim dispute this matter, it appears that one should not say it 90 times, lest he later enter into a dispute if he becomes uncertain. However, regarding what Chayei Adam writes there in the name of R. Avraham of Posen, that one who erred on the night of Rosh Hashanah and did not say "the holy King" need not repeat the prayer, as with Yaaleh Veyavo, see there, the later authorities have already challenged him: Responsa Mayim Chaim, siman 22; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 129, in Peas HaShulchan; Mishna Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, os 4, see there.
