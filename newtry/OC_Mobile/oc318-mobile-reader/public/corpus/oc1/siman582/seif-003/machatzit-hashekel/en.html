(s.k. 3) To stand erect, etc. Before he says "Blessed," for the reason of standing erect is that we hold it is forbidden to bow at the end of every blessing (but in the middle of a blessing it is permitted), as above in siman 113. And the reason is not to add upon the enactments of the Sages, who enacted to bow in Avos at the beginning and end and in Hoda'ah at the beginning and end; and the Sages enacted to bow when he says "Blessed," as written in siman 113: he bows at "Blessed" and stands erect at the Name. Therefore in another blessing aside from Avos and Hoda'ah he must stand erect before he says "Blessed," for there the enactments of the Sages are to bow in Avos and Hoda'ah. And the Taz wrote in siman 113 that in all the blessings it is enough if he stands erect before he says "Blessed"; but in Zochreinu and the like, where he must bow by virtue of the enactments of the Sages in that blessing, he must stand erect a few words before "Blessed," so that afterward when he bows at the end of the blessing it be recognizable that it is by virtue of the enactments of the Sages; and so wrote the Taz here. And Magen Avraham above wrote in the name of Agudah that in a place where one must bow by the enactment, he must stand erect beforehand as above, and afterward when he says "Blessed" he should bow, and when he mentions the Name he should stand erect, and afterward he should bow again.
And know that what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, that on Shabbos one must say in the blessing me'ein sheva "HaMelekh HaKadosh." The Peri Chadash wrote: if he erred and said "ha'El HaKadosh," if it is possible to return him within toch kedei dibbur to say "HaMelekh HaKadosh," that is good; and if not, he need not return; see there.
