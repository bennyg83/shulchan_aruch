And the view of the Taz implies that one should say it on the first day after midday as well, for perhaps the second day is the main one, and he prays for his fellow that he be written tomorrow for good life; and on the night of the second day of Rosh Hashana it is obvious that he should say so, as in siman 600, that if they came from Mincha onward the second day is the main one; and on the second day, when they leave according to the majority after midday, he should not say "may you be written," etc.
The wording of Abudirham, of blessed memory: "great dread from fear"; he said "eimah" upon the important creatures, man, heaven and earth, regarding whom "creation" is said, and "pachad" upon Your lesser works, the works of Creation, regarding whom "making" is said; see there. And in Exodus 15, "eimasah vafachad," and in Rashi and the Rema, of blessed memory: at first it appears that eimah is less than pachad, for this is upon the distant and pachad upon the closer who fear more; see Targum Yonasan and the Yerushalmi; and at first "eimasah" is doubled with the feminine ending, possibly emphasizing-vav to diminish the matter; and see the Mikhlol of Maharash Meloch, of blessed memory; and this still requires study for now. Later there came to my hand Tzahar HaTeivah in the count of the Names: 15, one with segol-kamatz is unique, two patachs is any at all, and the ending of a small question with two patachs, "echad ha'am" in construct is also unique; see there; and "umisafah achas" with two patachs is somewhat difficult; see what I wrote in letter 2 regarding this.
