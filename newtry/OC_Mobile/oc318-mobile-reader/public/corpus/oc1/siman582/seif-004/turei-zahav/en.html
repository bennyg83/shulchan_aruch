And they must stand erect, etc. The Tur wrote that the meticulous ones adopted this practice, for the reason that we say in Ein Omdin: these are the blessings in which one bows at the beginning and at the end; and if one comes to bow at the end of every blessing, they teach him not to bow; which implies that by law one need not be concerned for this, for only the meticulous do so. And one must say that the reason one should not bow at the end of every blessing is so that if a person bows on his own in a place where Chazal did not enact, he will come to stumble regarding the enactments of Chazal, as written in siman 113; and if so, there is a prohibition only in a place where there is an enactment of the Sages in Avos and Hoda'ah, namely at the end of each of those blessings; therefore if one comes to bow there, that is the prohibition; which is not so here, where one bows even throughout the entire blessing, and there there is no prohibition at all, as written in siman 113; therefore there is no prohibition even in concluding at the end; only that the meticulous are stringent about this as well. And their reason appears to be because of those who enter, who may not know that one says everything while bowing, and in Avos and Hoda'ah they must by law stand erect first before saying "Blessed are You," so that all will see that he bows afterward when he concludes "Blessed are You." And in the Shulchan Aruch he rules that everyone should be meticulous about this. And in the Levush he challenged that one need not stand erect at the end of the blessing since he says everything while bowing, and he elaborated with reasoning that this is a manner of honor and that is not a manner of honor; and according to what I wrote, there is nothing to this challenge. And above in siman 113 I wrote to distinguish between the erect stance of Zochreinu and the erect stance of Mi kamokha: in Zochreinu one must stand erect before saying a few words adjacent to "Blessed are You," so that he can bow afterward when he says "Blessed are You"; but for the erect stance of Mi kamokha one need not stand erect until he says "Blessed are You, Hashem"; and likewise regarding other blessings that have no bowing at their end; see there, a matter with its reason. The Tur wrote in the name of Maharam that he would say "lechaim" with a sheva and not with a patach, which implies "not life," as it is in Nedarim: "lecholim" with a patach is like "not chullin." And R' Elazar of Prague wondered about this, for Scripture says "all who are written for life in Jerusalem" with a patach; and Maharshal answered that Maharam follows his own view that wherever there is room to err we do not rely on the verse, as he wrote regarding Birkas HaMazon in the matter of "nodeh lecha," as above in siman 187, end quote. And the Tur in siman 187 disputes Maharam on the strength of the verse, and here there is likewise a verse and he does not dispute him; and one must say that here it is different, for there is room to err that it would be a curse, and "do not open a mouth to the Satan"; therefore one must be more careful; which is not so regarding "nodeh lecha," where the error is only to say "nodeh lecha" to someone else. And further one may say that in the Days of Judgment it is more severe; and with this is resolved why he did not mention the same regarding "veha'amideinu malkenu lechaim" that we say on weekdays; and so wrote my father-in-law, of blessed memory. Further the Tur wrote that one should not say "lechaim tovim" until the end in Hoda'ah, for one must first request a small matter, etc.; which implies that in Hoda'ah he should say "and write for good life," etc.; and so wrote the Levush, not like those who are particular not to say "tovim" so that there should be five words corresponding to the five books of the Torah, which is foolishness. And they say "shehasultan" with a kamatz under the shin, meaning dominion, and not with a chirik, which would imply the man who rules. And "ha-risha" should be said with a chirik under the shin. In Musaf of Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur it is written "umeilumadecha hem melumadim," and it has no explanation according to the matter of the verse he brings, "and I will favor whom I favor," etc.; therefore one should emend in this place to "umerachamicha hem meruchamim," the entire verse "and I will have mercy on whom I have mercy"; and so it is at the end of the introduction of Beis Yosef to Orach Chaim.
