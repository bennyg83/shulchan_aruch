"Lechaim": the lamed with a sheva, and not with a patach, which would imply "not life," as it is in Nedarim regarding "lecholim" with a patach, which is like "not chullin"; see there. And specifically on Rosh Hashana we are particular; but throughout the year we say in Hashkiveinu "lechaim" with a patach under the lamed, according to the grammar: wherever the he of knowledge that should have been written after the letters beis, kaf, lamed, and mem is missing, the patach from that he of knowledge comes under that letter of beis-kaf-lamed-mem, as in "shamayim," which should have been "ba-shamayim," except that the he of knowledge is missing, so the patach comes under the beis to indicate the he of knowledge; and so in all such cases. And see Rashi on parashas Beshalach, s.v. "lenachosam" etc.; and see what Magen Avraham wrote on this in the name of the Bach and Mateh Moshe. And "ha-risha" with a chirik under the shin. "Shehasultan" with a kamatz under the second shin. One should not say "ve'az tzaddikim," but rather "uvechen"; Zohar. And one should not say "be'ahava mikra kodesh," but rather "yom teruah mikra kodesh." And one should not say "umeilumadecha hem melumadim," but rather "umerachamicha hemah meruchamim" (Taz). And they double "le'eila" in all the Kaddishes. Minhagim.
