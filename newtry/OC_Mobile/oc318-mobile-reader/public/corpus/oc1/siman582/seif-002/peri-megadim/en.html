In the Tur he wrote that the meticulous ones customarily stand erect; thus one who is not so meticulous in his deeds need not stand erect in the other blessings, since he bows from beginning to end; the prohibition is specifically when he bows only at the beginning or only at the end, which is forbidden in the other blessings, for that uproots the enactment of Chazal that they enacted in Hoda'ah and Avos. But in Avos and Hoda'ah everyone must stand erect before the blessing so that it appear that he fulfills the enactment of Chazal to bow at the end in Avos and Hoda'ah. And the Mechaber ruled that every person should stand erect at the beginning and at the end; and see siman 113, letters 1 and 2; and in the Levush he elaborated; see there.

It appears at first that specifically in Zochreinu and what is unique to the prayer of Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur one should say it with a sheva; but regarding Hashkiveinu one may say that he should say it as he says all year, "Hashkiveinu lechaim" with a patach; and nevertheless one may say that on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur in Hashkiveinu as well he should say it with a sheva. And see Shaarei Tefillah, that regarding "lecholim" the lamed with a kamatz is in Aramaic "not chullin"; see there; and nevertheless one may say it with a sheva per the Bach. And "vechol ha-risha": the resh with a chirik (a printer's error regarding the shin); on Chanukah "ha-risha" with a sheva on the resh is said as an attribute-name, and here one says "risha" with a chirik on the resh as a noun, that wickedness itself should be uprooted from the world in the future and there remain only good. And the wording of the Peri Chadash, of blessed memory, is "veha-risha kulah be'ashan tikhleh" with a beis, the language of the verse "kalu be'ashan yamai," "kalu be'ashan kalu"; and some say with a kaf; and I stand in my place. And behold, Tehillim 37 has "be'ashan" with the beis in a segol, and Tehillim 104 has the beis with a sheva; I think according to "hasseir hechai" with two patachs, "hasseir hechai" with the ches kamatzed and the hei with a segol, "from her family and her father's house," and this is a hint in my view; and "vechisahu be'afar"; see Yoreh Deah 28 in the Derishah that preparation is required; the segol under a patach of knowledge is like "lenachosam"; see Tzahar HaTeivah, "kalu be'ashan kalu," which refers to fat rams of fat on the altar that are entirely consumed in smoke, known regarding the arrangement, according to the fruit of the festival; which is not so of "kalu be'ashan yamai," that he had many troubles, pointed with a sheva on the serving letters kaf-lamed-mem, etc. And here one may say "be'ashan" with a segol, or possibly with a sheva. And according to those who say with a kaf, one may say with a sheva. And some versions have "vechol ha-risha kulah," etc.; one may say as the Taz wrote in letter 3 regarding "melokh al kol ha'olam kulo," that it does not merely mean the majority, etc. And in Siddur Shaarei Tefillah the wording is "vechol ha-risha kalah" with two kamatzim; and in my humble opinion it is as I wrote. "Agudah achas": some read alef with a segol and ches with a kamatz, and some read with two patachs; see Eliyah Rabbah 12 in the name of Maharil and Kenesses HaGedolah. And behold, II Samuel 2:25, "And the children of Binyamin gathered themselves together and became one band (agudah achas)": I found it pointed alef with a segol and ches with a kamatz; perhaps it is an error. And behold, "echad" with two patachs appears to indicate importance and clear lineage of "one of the nation," the king; see there. And regarding Binyamin, if it is pointed with a patach, one may say it is not important, for they turned aside from David the king; and "Noach safah achas" with two patachs is for the Holy Tongue, a unique and clear language, and holiness. However, the Rema, of blessed memory, explained from the verse brought upon him that "echad" implies any one at all, and "achas" with a segol is possibly unique; and see Tzahar HaTeivah whether if one says "keves" why say "echad"; there is not now the Targum of Tzahar HaTeivah, and God willing it will be explained further.
