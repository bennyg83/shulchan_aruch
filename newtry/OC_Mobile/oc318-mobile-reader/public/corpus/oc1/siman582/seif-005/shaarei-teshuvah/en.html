And see Peri Chadash: if he said on a weekday "zikkaron teruah," he need not return; and even on Shabbos if he said "yom teruah," he need not return, since a fixed and ordained beis din in Eretz Yisrael sounds the shofar, or another reason, etc.; and see below siman 591 regarding the blessing of "Who hears the sound of the teruah," etc. (•) (In the Shulchan Aruch, se'if 6) He says in the prayer, etc. And if he erred and did not mention the character of Yom Tov, they make him return, as written in Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah; and even though Maariv is optional, the prayer of Shabbos and Yom Tov is obligatory; so wrote Mor u'Ketziah; and Machatzis HaShekel wrote that this is not so, but rather that anyway nowadays every Maariv has been treated as obligatory; see there and in siman 265. And he further wrote regarding one who said Maariv properly and concluded "Who sanctifies Israel and the times": even though Rosh Hashana is included in "times," nevertheless he did not intend Rosh Hashana, and he was drawn by the habit of the festival and came thus; and as on Yom Tov if he concluded "Who sanctifies the Shabbos" he has not fulfilled, even though Yom Tov is called Shabbos; and further, that this one did not know from the outset that Rosh Hashana is included in "times," etc.; see there. And see Machatzis HaShekel, siman 268, who brought in the name of Maamar HaMordechai that if Yom Tov or Rosh Hashana falls on Shabbos and he forgot in Maariv and did not mention Yom Tov, he returns, and he cannot rely on hearing the blessing from the chazzan, since they do not mention Yom Tov in it; and Machatzis HaShekel wrote that he fulfills with the blessing me'ein sheva; and in Shaarei Chochmah, folio 306, he disputes Machatzis HaShekel, who compared it to eating fruits and grain foods, etc., which does not fit well; rather he does not fulfill with me'ein sheva; and so it appears to me; and see there in Shaarei Chochmah that if it falls on Motzaei Shabbos and he forgot to say "Vatodi'enu," he need not return, for he will yet make Havdalah over the cup. And see Dagul Merevavah: that the Mechaber omitted the word "be'ahava" is according to his view in siman 87, which is the wording of the Rambam; and according to our custom that we say it on every Yom Tov, also on Rosh Hashana one may say it, and not like Eliyah Rabbah; see there. And in Shaarei Chochmah he wrote that if he forgot to say "melokh al kol ha'aretz" in Shacharis he has fulfilled; and even if he did not say "Our God and God of our fathers, reign over all," but after Yaaleh VeYavo he concluded "Blessed are You, Hashem, Who sanctifies Israel and the Day of Remembrance," he has fulfilled; but in Musaf prayer, which is Malchuyos, it is possible that he must conclude "King over all the earth," analogous to Zichronos and Shofaros; but according to what Abudirham and Kol Bo wrote, that in the verses of Zichronos and Shofaros one says adjacent to the conclusion the last verse of Torah, because in Malchuyos he does not conclude regarding kingship but regarding the sanctity of the day, etc., it is implied from this that the word "melekh" was not fixed as a strict requirement, for otherwise he would need to say a verse of kingship adjacent to the conclusion so that what is adjacent to the conclusion be of like kind to the conclusion; see there. And if it falls on Shabbos and he erred in the conclusion and did not mention Shabbos, the view of Kenesses HaGedolah, siman 487, is that he has fulfilled since he mentioned it in the middle of the blessing, and the Peri Chadash disputes, and likewise several great authorities; see above siman 487 in Baer Heitev s.k. 5; and in Birkei Yosef, Machatzis HaShekel also agreed with the Peri Chadash, and wrote that so it appears from the Ritva, chapter 2 of Beitzah; see there. And see there where he elaborated that our wording "melokh al kol ha'olam kulo" is fine, as the Taz wrote; and Machatzis HaShekel brings proof from the words of the Gemara several times; and see Binyan Ariel on parashas Korach, "the entire congregation, all of them are holy," etc.; see there; and see Taz above, siman 380, and here in the Taz who wrote "and there is a proof for this above," where the printed text is missing the words "siman 380," as written in Yad Ephraim.
(*) (In Shulchan Aruch, seif 6:) He says in the prayer, etc. If he erred and did not mention the theme of the festival, he is brought back, as written in Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. Although the evening prayer is optional, the prayer of Shabbat and Yom Tov is obligatory; so wrote Moed Katan. Machazik Berachah wrote that this is not so, but in any event nowadays all evening prayers have been made obligatory; see there and siman 265. He further wrote regarding one who recited the evening prayer properly and concluded "Who sanctifies Israel and the seasons": although Rosh Hashanah is included among the seasons, nevertheless he did not intend Rosh Hashanah and merely followed the accustomed formula of the festivals, just as on Yom Tov if one concluded "Who sanctifies Shabbat" he has not fulfilled his obligation, even though Yom Tov is called Shabbat. Moreover, this person did not know from the outset that Rosh Hashanah is included among the seasons, etc.; see there. See Machazik Berachah, siman 268, who brought in the name of Maamar Mordechai that if Yom Tov or Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat and he forgot in the evening prayer and did not mention Yom Tov, he repeats and cannot rely on hearing the chazzan's blessing, since Yom Tov is not mentioned in it. Machazik Berachah wrote that he fulfills his obligation with the blessing resembling the seven, but in Shulchan Aruch, p. 306, he disagreed with Machazik Berachah, comparing it to eating fruit and mezonot, etc., and that comparison does not work; rather he does not fulfill his obligation with the blessing resembling the seven. So it appears to my humble mind. See there in Shaarei Teshuvah, that if it falls on Motzaei Shabbat and he forgot to say Vatodi'enu, he need not repeat, since he will later make havdalah over a cup. See Dagul Merevavah, that the Mechaber omitted the word "with love" according to his own view in siman 87, which follows the Rambam's version; but according to our custom, in which we say it on every Yom Tov, also on Rosh Hashanah, one should say it, unlike Eliyah Rabbah; see there. He wrote in Shulchan Aruch HaRav that if one forgot to say "Reign over all the earth" in Shacharit, he has fulfilled his obligation, and even if he did not say "Our God and God of our fathers, reign over all," but after Yaaleh VeYavo concluded "Blessed are You, Lord, Who sanctifies Israel and the day of remembrance," he fulfilled his obligation. But in Tefillat Mussaf, which is Malchuyot, perhaps he must conclude "King over all the earth," similar to Zichronot and Shofarot. Yet according to what the Abudarham and Kol Bo wrote, that in the verses of Zichronot and Shofarot one says near the conclusion the final Torah verse because in Malchuyot he does not conclude with the matter of kingship but with the sanctity of the day, etc., this implies that the word "King" was not fixed as an absolute requirement; otherwise one would have needed to say a verse of kingship near the conclusion so that the conclusion would be thematically adjacent to it; see there. If Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat and he erred in the conclusion and did not mention Shabbat, Kenesset HaGedolah in siman 487 holds that he fulfills his obligation since he mentioned it in the middle of the blessing, while Pri Chadash disagrees, as do several great authorities; see above siman 487 in Baer Heitev, seif katan 5. In Birkei Yosef, Machazik Berachah also agreed with Pri Chadash and wrote that this also appears from Ritva, Perek 2 of Beitzah; see there. See there, where he discussed at length that our version "Reign over the entire world" is proper, as the Taz wrote, and Machazik Berachah brings proof from several statements in the Talmud. See Binyan Ariel on Parashat Korach, "the entire congregation, all of them are holy," etc.; see there.
