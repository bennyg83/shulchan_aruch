For a good year may you be written. In the Levush, siman 556, he wrote that on the first day after the prayer one should not say "for a good year," etc., for in the first three hours is the writing for the righteous, and it is not fitting that one say to his fellow a wording that indicates he is not a tzaddik but a beinoni; granted that each person himself should regard himself as a beinoni, therefore they pray "and write us for life," namely on Yom Kippur. And it appears to me that on the second day one also says "zeman," for the reason that the main day is sometimes the second day, as written in siman 600; the same applies here regarding praying for his fellow "for a good year may you be written"; therefore one should say it also on the night of the second day of Rosh Hashana.
