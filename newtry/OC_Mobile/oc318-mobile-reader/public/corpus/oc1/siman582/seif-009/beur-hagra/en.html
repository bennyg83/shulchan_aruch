Se'if 9, even though, etc. End of Rosh Hashana 35a, because they grow loud; and according to the explanation of the Arukh and Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 8 of Hilchos Tefillah, in the name of Tosafos, the wording is "making the voice heard"; and as written in chapter 2 of Berachos, and chapter 4 of Kesubos, and chapter 7 of Bava Metzia, and chapter 3 of Chullin; and the reason is that they are not accustomed to them, therefore they raise their voices. But the Rambam and the Rif and the other poskim explain like Rashi's explanation there, as written in chapter 4 of Eruvin and the beginning of Nedarim; and that is the main ruling.
And to incline them, etc. As written in chapter 3 of Berachos 24b; and according to the aforementioned explanation of the Arukh, therefore one fulfills his obligation on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, because they raise their voices and cause one another to err, and they do not fulfill their obligation with silent prayer; only that now they do not rely on the chazzan, for one must hear from the mouth of the chazzan word by word, as Tosafos of Berachos 29b wrote, s.v. "ta'ah," etc., and the other poskim; and as written in siman 124, se'if 10; and as written in the Yerushalmi at the end of Rosh Hashana: R' Ada of Caesarea in the name of R' Yochanan: and that is provided that he be there from the beginning of the prayer.
And they have the custom, etc., "may you be written." For what is written there, "three books," etc., that refers to life of the World to Come, as Tosafos wrote there, and not like the Ran who wrote regarding this world, and righteous and wicked in their judgment; for we say "and a person is judged on Rosh Hashana and the decree," etc.; thus it is clear that the sealing is only on Yom Kippur, and the Mishnah speaks of every person; and likewise what is written there regarding produce concerning which an incident occurred, etc., a person, etc.: the Gemara decided it regarding every person; only that life of this world is equal for every person; only that even though it was written, if he returns during the Ten Days of Repentance the decree is torn, and he is written and sealed on Yom Kippur for good; and this is the benefit that he is not sealed until Yom Kippur; and that is what is written, "sit in the meantime," etc.; and this is what they request during the Ten Days of Repentance, "and write us," etc., that the decree be torn and He write us for life; and therefore one should not say "and may you be sealed," but rather "may you be written."
