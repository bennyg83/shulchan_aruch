1) [Seif 1] During the Ten Days of Repentance one says "the King," etc. See Zohar, Parashas Noach, 69b, which states that that day was the festival of Rosh Hashanah, and the Holy One, blessed is He, was judging the world. Elisha therefore said to her, "Is there anything to be spoken for you to the king?" This refers to the Holy One, blessed is He, for at that time He is called King, "the holy King," and "the King of judgment," see there. The Levush likewise explains that we say "the holy King" because these are days of judgment, when the Holy One, blessed is He, sits and judges the entire world, displaying His kingship and dominion, which rule over all.
2) Ibid. One says "the holy King" and "the King of judgment," and if he erred, etc. All of this was already explained above, siman 118, regarding one who erred and said "the King Who loves charity and judgment," one who said "King of judgment" without the definite article, and one who, during the rest of the year, mistakenly said "the holy King," "the King of judgment," or "the God of judgment," see there.
3) Ibid. If he erred or is uncertain, etc. It appears that here it does not help to recite the phrase 90 times, as in siman 114, seif 9, because here he may not say, "Blessed are You, Hashem," since that would be a blessing in vain. If he says it without the Name, then during prayer, when he says it with the Name, he will revert to his accustomed wording, "Blessed are You, Hashem, the holy God." He therefore has no remedy. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. The Pri Chadash writes that it does help. Birkei Yosef, os 3, likewise writes that the words of the Magen Avraham are not compelling, for if he says, "Blessed are You, Hashem," pronouncing the Name where it is written, it helps, see there. However, the Shaliach Tzibbur in Shalmei Tzibbur, p. 306, disagrees with them and concurs with the Magen Avraham, see there. His words are cited by Shaarei Teshuvah, os 1, and Eliyah Rabbah, os 4, also cites the Magen Avraham. So ruled the Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 3; Chayei Adam, klal 24, os 10; Mishna Berurah, os 3. Mateh Ephraim, os 7, writes that if one said 90 times, "Blessed are You, Hashem, the holy King," and is now uncertain, he should pray again as a voluntary prayer. Therefore, since the poskim dispute this matter, it appears that one should not say it 90 times, lest he later enter into a dispute if he becomes uncertain. However, regarding what Chayei Adam writes there in the name of R. Avraham of Posen, that one who erred on the night of Rosh Hashanah and did not say "the holy King" need not repeat the prayer, as with Yaaleh Veyavo, see there, the later authorities have already challenged him: Responsa Mayim Chaim, siman 22; Kitzur Shulchan Aruch, siman 129, in Peas HaShulchan; Mishna Berurah in Shaar HaTziyun, os 4, see there. See further below, os 6 and os 29.
4) Ibid. If he erred or is uncertain, etc. He returns to the beginning, because there is a presumption that he mentioned the wording to which he is accustomed. Beit Yosef; Levush; Mishna Berurah, os 3.
5) Ibid. He returns to the beginning. This is because the first 3 blessings are considered one unit. Levush. So too is stated above, siman 114, seif 6, in the gloss, see there. If a shaliach tzibbur erred in "the holy King" and returns to the beginning, he repeats Kedushah as well. Zekan Aharon, siman 206, at the end of the responsum; Shalmei Tzibbur, p. 306c; Zechor LeAvraham, os shin; Shaarei Teshuvah, os 1; Mateh Ephraim, os 8; Ben Ish Chai, Parashas Nitzavim, os 18.
6) Ibid. He returns to the beginning. If, on Rosh Hashanah or Yom Kippur, he realizes that he prayed the entire prescribed order, including "And so, place Your fear," etc., but is uncertain whether he concluded the blessing with "the holy King," it is possible that he need not repeat the prayer. Perhaps the rule of following one's customary wording throughout the year does not apply here, because of the additions recited before the conclusion of the blessing. Responsa Mayim Chaim, Orach Chaim, siman 22; Chayei Adam, klal 28, os 8; Mishna Berurah, os 4. Furthermore, one presumably prays from a siddur and says "the holy King" as written there. See also Zechor LeAvraham, vol. 1, os shin, and vol. 3, maareches resh, os 138, who cites 4 great poskim, namely Ravyah, Agur in the name of Riaz, Baal HaMaor, and Raavad, who hold that, according to the primary law, if one said "the holy God" or "the King Who loves charity and judgment," he need not repeat the prayer, see there. Although the custom does not follow this view, where there is an additional doubt one can say that he need not repeat the prayer because of the rule that we are lenient in cases of doubt regarding blessings.
7) Ibid. If he did not remember until he moved his feet, etc. See above, siman 117 and siman 422, where it is explained that this applies specifically when one is accustomed to recite supplications after the prayer. If he is not accustomed to do so, once he has completed his prayer, even if he has not yet moved his feet, it is considered as though he moved them. According to our custom to recite "My God, guard my tongue," etc., after the prayer, followed by "May the words of my mouth," etc., the prayer is not considered finished until he recites the second "May the words of my mouth," etc., which precedes "He Who makes peace." However, once he has completed the second "May the words of my mouth," etc., even if he has not yet said "He Who makes peace in His heights," the prayer is considered finished, as written above, siman 117, os 40, see there.
8) Ibid., gloss. See above, siman 118. Meaning, it is written there that if one erred and said "the King Who loves charity and judgment," he need not repeat the prayer, see there. See our words there, where we wrote that this is indeed the opinion of the later authorities, that he need not repeat it, because we rule that in cases of doubt regarding blessings one is lenient, see there.
