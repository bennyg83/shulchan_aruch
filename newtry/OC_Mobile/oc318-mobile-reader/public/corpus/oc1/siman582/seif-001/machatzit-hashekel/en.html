(s.k. 1) Uncertain, etc. In siman 114. It is explained there that in Musaf of the last day of Yom Tov of the festival they begin to say "mashiv haruach." And if he is uncertain whether he said it within thirty days adjacent to the festival, the presumption is that he did not say it, and he must return and pray. And after thirty days he has already become accustomed to saying it, and the presumption is that he said it, for a person follows the habit of his tongue. And he wrote there at the end of the siman that if on the last day of Yom Tov he said ninety times "Atah gibor" until "morid hageshem," from then on the presumption is that he said it; for in thirty days there are ninety prayers; if so, if he said it ninety times he becomes accustomed in one instance as if they said it for thirty days.
In the name of Abudirham. Who disagrees with the aforementioned law. And the reason is that it is impossible that he begin from the beginning of the Amidah of eighteen and say it ninety times until after "mashiv haruach," because of a blessing in vain. And if he begins only before "mashiv haruach" and says it ninety times, then afterward when he begins to pray from the beginning of the Amidah he will follow his habit and not say "mashiv haruach." And granted that we do not hold like Abudirham in that matter, since there is no change in his saying it ninety times (whether he prays from the beginning of the Amidah or from the beginning of "Atah gibor," there is no change adjacent to "mashiv haruach"). But here everyone agrees that it does not help, since there is a change immediately adjacent to "HaMelekh HaKadosh," which he mentions in prayer with "Blessed are You, Hashem."
