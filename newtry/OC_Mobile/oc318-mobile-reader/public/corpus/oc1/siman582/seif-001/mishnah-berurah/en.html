(1) During the Ten Days of Repentance. In Maharil he wrote that there are places that customarily say Ma'aravos on the two nights of Rosh Hashana, and they do not have that custom in our place, because they are songs and praises [Darkei Moshe]. He further wrote in the name of Minhagim that the congregation has the custom to say the verse "Tik'u bachodesh shofar," etc. And it is not an interruption, for it is like an extended Geulah.
(2) HaMelekh HaKadosh. Because now they are days of judgment, when the Holy One, blessed be He, sits and judges the entire world and shows His kingship and dominion, that He rules in everything.
(3) Or he is uncertain. For the presumption is that what he is accustomed to, that is what he mentions. And here it does not help to say it ninety times as in siman 114, se'if 8, for here one is not permitted to say "Blessed are You, Hashem," for that would be a blessing in vain; and if he says it without the Name, then in prayer when he says it with the Name he will revert to his habit of saying "Blessed are You, Hashem, the holy God"; therefore he has no remedy.
(4) He returns to the beginning. For the first three blessings are considered as one. And if on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur it became known to him that he prayed in order to say "Uvchen ten pachdecha," etc., only that he is uncertain at the conclusion of the blessing whether he concluded "HaMelekh HaKadosh," it is possible that he need not return; for perhaps one should not say regarding this that it is like the habit of his tongue throughout the year, because of the additions that he adds before the end of the blessing.
(5) See above at the end of siman 117, se'if 5; and see there in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 18.
(6) See above, siman 118. For there the Rama ruled as the halacha that if one said "Melekh ohev tzedakah u'mishpat," since he mentioned "melekh" he need not return; and see there in Mishnah Berurah s.k. 3 what we wrote there in the name of the later authorities on this.
