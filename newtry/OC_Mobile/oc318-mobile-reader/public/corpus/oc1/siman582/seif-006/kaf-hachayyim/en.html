If, on Rosh Hashanah or Yom Kippur, he realizes that he prayed the entire prescribed order, including "And so, place Your fear," etc., but is uncertain whether he concluded the blessing with "the holy King," it is possible that he need not repeat the prayer. Perhaps the rule of following one's customary wording throughout the year does not apply here, because of the additions recited before the conclusion of the blessing. Responsa Mayim Chaim, Orach Chaim, siman 22; Chayei Adam, klal 28, os 8; Mishna Berurah, os 4. Furthermore, one presumably prays from a siddur and says "the holy King" as written there. See also Zechor LeAvraham, vol. 1, os shin, and vol. 3, maareches resh, os 138, who cites 4 great poskim, namely Ravyah, Agur in the name of Riaz, Baal HaMaor, and Raavad, who hold that, according to the primary law, if one said "the holy God" or "the King Who loves charity and judgment," he need not repeat the prayer, see there. Although the custom does not follow this view, where there is an additional doubt one can say that he need not repeat the prayer because of the rule that we are lenient in cases of doubt regarding blessings.
