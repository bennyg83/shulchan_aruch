And therefore what is written here is fine, that one should not say it after three hours, since the judgment and the writing for a tzaddik have already been completed.
"From His place He will turn," etc. That is, in "Ne'aritzcha" of Musaf, for a plain Musaf prayer is after the first three hours. "In the second ones" means the second three hours of the day.
That is, according to the first answer, etc. For in Avodah Zarah folio 4 it is stated: R' Yosef said, a person should not pray Musaf in the first three hours of Rosh Hashana except with the congregation, whose prayer is not pushed aside; but not as an individual, lest his judgment be visited and they examine his deeds and his prayer be pushed aside. And one might have thought that this is because in the first three hours He sits and judges; and it objects: but we say that in the first three hours He sits and engages in Torah, and in the second three hours He sits and judges; and it answers: reverse it: in the first three hours He judges and in the second He engages in Torah; and if you wish, say: never reverse it, and in the first three hours He engages in Torah, even so one should not then pray Musaf as an individual, because regarding Torah it is written "truth," namely according to the strict line of the law; therefore the Holy One, blessed be He, does not enter beyond the line of the law. Thus far the words of the Gemara. And Tosafos on folio 3b wrote that some say that for this reason they say in Musaf "From His place He will turn," etc., because we hold that in the second hours He sits and judges. And upon this they wrote: and it does not appear so, for according to what we conclude, that in the first three hours He sits and judges; and this would not fit. And necessarily the fixed wording of the prayer is thus, end quote. But we hold like the latter answer, for according to the latter answer one need not say "reverse it," and that is preferable.
