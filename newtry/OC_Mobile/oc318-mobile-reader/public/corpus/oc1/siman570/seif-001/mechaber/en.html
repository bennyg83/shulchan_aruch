Hanukkah and Purim and days on which Tachanun is not said — if they occur within the days of his vowed fasts. It contains 3 seifim:
One who accepted upon himself a fast for such-and-such number of days, and Shabbatot, Yom Tov days, or Rosh Chodesh of Hanukkah and Purim, or erev Yom Kippur occurred among them — if he accepted with general language of accepting a fast he does not need annulment; if he accepted with language "behold upon me" which is vow language — he needs annulment by a sage who opens with regret saying if you had placed it on your heart that these days would occur you would not have vowed, and he permits him; but Rambam: once he accepted with vow language and Shabbatot and Yom Tov days or erev Yom Kippur or Rosh Chodesh occurred — he is obligated to fast them unless they annul for him; but if Hanukkah and Purim occurred — his vow is void and he does not fast them because they are rabbinic and need reinforcement:The custom follows the first view. One who vowed to fast a set number of days and said except Yom Tov — Hanukkah and Purim are not included; if he said his intent was upon them we follow him (Responsa Rashba siman 259); but erev Yom Kippur is included and so is practiced, unlike Yoreh Deah siman 215 seif 2.':
