Acceptance of a fast. It should read: that they accepted it at Mincha; see siman 563 and siman 568, se'if 2.
Behold it is upon me. Meaning that he said, "Behold, eating on such-and-such a day is konam upon me"; see Yoreh De'ah, siman 215. But if he said, "Behold I vow to fast one day," it is not called a neder. Therefore they practiced that one who said, "Behold I vow to fast Mondays and Thursdays all the days of the year," need not fast on Rosh Chodesh and needs no annulment.
He needs annulment by a sage. And if they did not annul it for him, he must fast according to all opinions; and it appears to me that he must sit a [follow-up] fast for his fast, as is written in siman 568, se'if 5.
Chanukah and Purim are not included. For these days are not clearly [understood] in people's speech as being called Yom Tov or festivals (Rashba there).
We follow him. Meaning he is not obligated to fast; [compare] that which we learned: unspecified nedarim are [to be interpreted] stringently, and their explanation [when explained] leniently (there). And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 208, in the Shulchan Aruch, in the Taz and the Shach: that sometimes he is not believed. And it appears to me that Rosh Chodesh is equal to Chanukah and Purim in this matter; but Chol HaMoed is called Yom Tov.
Erev Yom Kippur is included. For it is called Yom Tov; and so wrote Matteh Moshe in the name of the Maharil.
And so is the practice, etc. This refers to what was written above, that the custom follows the first view.
