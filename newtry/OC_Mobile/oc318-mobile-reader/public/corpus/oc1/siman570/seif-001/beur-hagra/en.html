Seif 1: An individual, etc.; if, etc. The first chapter of Taanis discusses an individual who accepted a fast, etc., and says, ‘If our decree, etc.’ The authorities asked from the statement in Nedarim 66a, ‘One may find an opening,’ etc. Ramban and Shitah Mekubetzes answer that ‘accepted’ here uses the language of acceptance, not a vow, as the Gemara states on 12b, ‘Is it a vow?’ etc. Even according to the latter version, when it says that it is only a vow, it means that it is not literally a vow. The Gemara there also states that one interrupts for Rosh Chodesh and Yom Tov only when he accepted the fast upon himself; necessarily, that refers to acceptance, for otherwise it would not constitute a fast at all. The Tosefta, chapter 2, states: What is the difference between a communal fast and an individual fast? A communal fast is not interrupted for the festivals recorded in Megillas Taanis, unlike an individual fast. Rambam distinguishes between a Torah festival, which needs no reinforcement, and the rabbinic days under discussion, as stated in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah and second chapter of Taanis regarding the days preceding them. This is the meaning of ‘according to Rambam,’ etc. Chanukah and Purim are mentioned because Megillas Taanis was annulled except for Chanukah and Purim. When one accepted the fast in the language of a vow, Rambam too agrees, as explained by the statement that one interrupts for Rosh Chodesh, etc. This also appears from Rambam’s Hilchos Taanis: an individual who accepts a specified number of fast days does not fast on Rosh Chodesh; see there. The authorities were therefore forced to interpret his use of ‘vowed’ as an actual vow. Ramban, however, explains it as the language of acceptance, and this is also implied by the Jerusalem Talmud: If one vowed to fast and Shabbosos or festivals occurred, he incurs lashes. Rabbi Chizkiyah and Rabbi Yudan both cited Rabbi Yochanan in the name of Rabbi Chiya bar Abba; one said that he fasts but does not complete the fast, and one said that he incurs lashes and does not require a sage’s release. Thus, regarding Shabbos and Yom Tov, all agree that he does not require a sage’s release. It must therefore refer to the language of acceptance.
One who vowed, etc. As stated in the first chapter of Megillah: ‘They did not accept it upon themselves,’ and all the more so regarding Chanukah. Moreover, regarding vows one follows ordinary human usage.
If he said, etc. As stated in Nedarim 18b: Unspecified vows are interpreted stringently, but their explanations are interpreted leniently. This is not comparable to the statement on 20a, ‘He vowed by cherem,’ etc., because there his wording is unusual and he intended to deceive others through language by which people would be misled; it is therefore interpreted stringently. This differs from the first clause and similar cases.
