(1) An individual who accepted upon himself a fast, etc. For if they decreed a fast upon the community in Eretz Yisrael and they began to fast and these days occurred among them, their decree takes effect after the fact, as above in siman 418; which is not so for an individual.
(2) He needs no annulment. For the force of his acceptance does not take effect at all upon this day; and see in siman 418 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 7, what he wrote there.
(3) With the language "behold it is upon me." Meaning that he said, "Behold, eating on such-and-such a day is konam upon me"; because a neder takes effect even upon a matter of mitzvah, for a neder forbids the object upon him, and there is no commandment upon the object; but with a shevuah, when he swore to fast on these days, it does not take effect, as is explained in se'if 3, because a shevuah forbids himself regarding the object, and he is already commanded not to do so.
(4) Which is the language of a neder. The Magen Avraham wrote that if he said, "Behold I vow to fast one day," it is not called a neder; therefore they practiced that one who said, "Behold I vow to fast Mondays and Thursdays all the days of the year," need not fast on Rosh Chodesh and needs no annulment; and see in the Peri Megadim. And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 206, se'if 5.
(5) He needs annulment by a sage. And he should be careful to have it annulled so that he not be obligated to fast [and if he did not have it annulled and he fasted, he must afterward sit a [follow-up] fast for his fast, so that it be atoned for him for having fasted on these days, as above in siman 568, se'if 5]. And when he annuls it for him, automatically all the days are released [Yoreh De'ah, siman 215].
(6) And Purim. For these days are not called Yom Tov; and the same applies to Rosh Chodesh; but Chol HaMoed is called Yom Tov.
(7) We follow him. Meaning he is not obligated to fast.
(8) Erev Yom Kippur is included. For regarding nedarim we follow common speech, and in common speech it is called Yom Tov. And see below in siman 604 in the Mishnah Berurah, s.k. 2.
(9) And so is the practice. That is to say, like the first view above, and not like the opinion of the Rambam.
(10) Siman 215, se'if 4. So it should read. And see in the Shach there what he wrote on this.
