Since. In the responsa Pri HaAretz, part 3, it was asked concerning one who forbade himself by neder [from] lighting the Chanukah lamp or reading the Megillah, [that] his neder does not take effect. And the rav wonders at the questioner, for is this not a well-established law? And see Yoreh De'ah, siman 239, regarding a shevuah, and all the more so a neder, that it takes effect. And it is possible that he erred from what is written there in [siman] 215, that a shevuah takes effect on matters of the Scribes, and the implication there is that a neder is weaker still; but when we examine carefully, we see how the Mechaber relied on the Beis Yosef, who agreed with the Ramban that acceptances in the language of a neder take effect, and not like Maharam of blessed memory, who holds that regarding a matter of the Scribes it does not take effect, because their words need strengthening; see there.
