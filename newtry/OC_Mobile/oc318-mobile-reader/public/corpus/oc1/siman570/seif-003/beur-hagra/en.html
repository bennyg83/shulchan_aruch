Seif 3: If he took an oath, etc. An oath expressed through a comprehensive formulation takes effect even regarding a Torah obligation, as stated in the Jerusalem Talmud and cited by Rabbi Yitzchak Alfasi and Rabbeinu Asher at the end of Pesachim. The third chapter of Shevuos likewise discusses an oath that one will not eat matzah, etc. See Tosafos, Shevuos 24a, s.v. ela.
But, etc., for there is no, etc. See Nedarim there, Shevuos there, the Jerusalem Talmud cited above, and elsewhere. The Tosefta, chapter 2 of Taanis, states in the name of Rabbi Yose HaGelili that one who swears to fast on Friday takes a false oath. Although we do not rule accordingly, because the law is that one fasts and completes the fast, the rule relevant to the cases here may nevertheless be learned from it. Megillas Taanis likewise states that one who swears to fast on Shabbos has taken a false oath.
Rather, he incurs lashes. Shevuos 29a discusses one who swears to nullify, etc.
And if he swore, etc. See Nazir 4a and the version of Tosafos there, as well as Shevuos 24a, ‘Granted, according to Reish Lakish,’ etc. See also Yoma 74a.
Except that from Rambam’s words, etc. As stated in seif 1, ‘and according to Rambam,’ etc. His reason is that Rosh Chodesh is treated as Torah law, as indicated by the verse ‘and on your new moons,’ etc., and as stated in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah and second chapter of Taanis: ‘Rosh Chodesh is from the Torah,’ etc. The first opinion maintains that it is not literally Torah law, but has support from a Torah verse. See Tosafos, Taanis 12a, s.v. ve’im, and Berachos 49b, s.v. אי, who write that Rosh Chodesh is included in Megillas Taanis. This is also implied by the Mishnah’s wording: ‘One does not decree,’ etc., ‘and if they began,’ etc. It does not ask about Shabbosos and festivals, indicating that regarding those, even if the fasts began, they are interrupted because these days are from the Torah. The question therefore concerns only Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, and Purim. The Tosefta, chapter 2, similarly states: What is the difference between a communal fast and an individual fast? A communal fast is not interrupted for the festivals written in Megillas Taanis, etc.
