If one swore to fast such-and-such days, and these days [on which one may not fast] occurred among them, the oath takes effect upon them by virtue of [the principle of] an all-inclusive prohibition [issur kollel]. But if he swore to fast on Shabbos or Yom Tov explicitly, the oath does not take effect upon him, for an oath does not take effect upon a matter of mitzvah; rather, he is lashed for having sworn in vain, and he eats. And if he swore to fast on Chanukah and Purim, the oath takes effect upon him, since they are only Rabbinic {and one should have his oath annulled, and he does not fast}. And it appears to me that the same applies to Rosh Chodesh and Erev Yom Kippur, which are only Rabbinic — although from the words of the Rambam it appears that they are of Torah [origin].
