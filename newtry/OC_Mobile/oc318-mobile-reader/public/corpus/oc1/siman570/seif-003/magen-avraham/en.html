Only by rabbinic [law]. And according to the Rambam the shevuah does not take effect; see what is written in se'if 1.
For the same applies to Rosh Chodesh, etc. Meaning that what is forbidden to fast on them is by rabbinic [law], and the shevuah takes effect; even the Rambam concedes this, since in any case they themselves are of the Torah and need no strengthening, as is written in se'if 1. And it appears to me that [the prohibition of fasting on] Rosh Chodesh is rabbinic; for in siman 572 he ruled that if they began, they do not interrupt, and it is a complete Mishnah; and if it were according to the Torah, they would have to interrupt, as on Shabbos and Yom Tov. And also in siman 580 he ruled to fast on Rosh Chodesh; and even though the Rema ruled there not to complete [the fast], nevertheless it is stated at the end of chapter 3 of Eruvin: Yom Tov is different, for their words [apply], since one fasts on it for hours, etc.; this implies that on a Torah-level Yom Tov it is forbidden to fast on it even for hours; rather, Rosh Chodesh is rabbinic, such that it is forbidden to fast on it; but Erev Yom Kippur is proven to be according to the Torah, for we learn from the verse "and you shall afflict," that there is a mitzvah to eat. And what the Beis Yosef wrote, that it is a mere asmachta, is puzzling; for we say in Yoma, "And the Tanna [asks]: this 'and you shall afflict,' what does he do with it? He needs it for..., etc."; if so, it is proven that it is a genuine derashah, for otherwise the question remains in place: what does he do with "and you shall afflict"? And similarly the Tosafos proved in Moed Katan, folio 8, regarding "and you shall rejoice on your festival"; see there. And so it appears in Rosh Hashana, that we hold like R' Akiva, who holds this derashah to be a genuine derashah; see there. And so it appears in the Tur, siman 604, that it is according to the Torah.
