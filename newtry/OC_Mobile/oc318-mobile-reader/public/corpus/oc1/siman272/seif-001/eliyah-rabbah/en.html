[Levush] It has a somewhat foul odor, etc. Because it rests in a repulsive vessel, as Rashbam explains in Bava Batra 97.
[Levush] All the more so if it tastes like vinegar, etc. At first glance this is difficult: what novelty is being taught for kiddush if one would not even recite Borei Pri HaGefen over it, as above in siman 204 seif 4? Regarding the Tur one can answer as Taz does: after saying that even if its smell is vinegar kiddush may be made over it, he clarifies that if its taste is vinegar kiddush may not be made. But the Levush and Rama still require explanation. Perhaps they teach Bach's view that even where Borei Pri HaGefen is still recited, namely before a white film has formed, one may nevertheless not make kiddush once it tastes like vinegar. Taz attacks this and says that when its taste is vinegar it is certainly worse than wine with a film. But he did not note that Bach also holds that a mere film still permits Borei Pri HaGefen; only when both a film and vinegar taste are present does the blessing change. Taz further says it is not realistic for wine to taste like vinegar without such a film, apparently understanding "film" merely as particles. Bach explicitly means a white skin growing over the wine, which can certainly be absent even when the taste has soured. Yet I have a difficulty from Bava Batra 97: Rav says kiddush is recited only on wine fit for the altar. The Gemara asks what this excludes; if souring wine, that is the dispute of Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. Rashbam explains that Rabbi Yochanan considers it wine even for kiddush, while Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi considers it vinegar such that even Borei Pri HaGefen is not recited. The Gemara could have answered that Rav excludes wine that tastes like vinegar but has no film.
Nowadays, etc. Perhaps if wine stood uncovered only a short time there is no concern. Even fastidious and distinguished people are not particular after the fact unless its smell or taste has deteriorated, as Magen Avraham writes.
