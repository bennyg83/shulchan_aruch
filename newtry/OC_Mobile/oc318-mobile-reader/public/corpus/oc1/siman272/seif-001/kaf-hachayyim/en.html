1) [Seif 1] Kiddush is not recited over wine with a foul odor, etc. Kiddush is recited only over wine fit to be poured upon the mizbe'ach. This excludes wine with a foul odor, meaning that it is somewhat malodorous, and uncovered wine. Tur. Perishah, paragraph 1, writes that this principle is stated specifically to exclude the cases enumerated here. One should not derive from it that Kiddush may not be recited over everything unfit for libation upon the mizbe'ach, because Tur concludes that Kiddush may be recited over raisin wine and cooked wine; see there. This applies specifically according to Tur and the Shulchan Aruch. Rambam, chapter 29, halachah 14, however, rules that it excludes everything unfit for libation upon the mizbe'ach; see there. We maintain the view of Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, which excludes only these cases.
2) There: Kiddush is not recited over wine with a foul odor, etc. Rashbam explains that although the blessing Borei Peri HaGafen is recited over it, because its aroma and taste are those of wine, it is somewhat malodorous because it was stored in a repulsive vessel. It is nevertheless invalid for Kiddush. Since it is invalid for libation upon the mizbe'ach because of the principle, 'Present it, if you please, to your governor,' it is likewise invalid for Kiddush for the same reason. These are his words, cited by Bach.
3) If, on Friday, a person is in a place where wine is available but knows that he will have no wine on Shabbos, such as when he cannot bring it home, he should recite Kiddush over the wine while it is still daytime and drink it. He may not perform labor afterward. Shibbolei HaLeket in the name of a Gaon; Magen Avraham at the beginning of this siman. This appears to discuss reciting Kiddush before Maariv, which is permitted, as stated above in siman 271, paragraph 22. We already wrote there, however, that according to the Arizal one must pray Maariv first and recite Kiddush afterward; see there. According to the Arizal, it therefore appears preferable to pray Maariv and then recite Kiddush over bread, rather than recite Kiddush over wine before prayer. If he prayed Maariv from plag haminchah onward and then wishes to recite Kiddush while it is still daytime, however, there is no concern even according to the Arizal.
4) There: Kiddush is not recited over wine with a foul odor, etc. Even if no other wine is available and only this wine with a somewhat foul odor can be obtained, Kiddush is not recited over it. Responsa Shaagas Aryeh, siman 18; Beis Menuchah, paragraph 1. If it was mixed with other wine and thereby corrected so that its foul odor disappeared, however, Kiddush may be recited over it. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 1.
5) Similarly, Kiddush is not recited over wine that has acquired a slight foul odor from the barrel. Responsa Shaagas Aryeh there; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 2; Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2. If one inadvertently recited Kiddush over wine with a foul odor, it can be said that he fulfilled his obligation and need not repeat Kiddush, because we maintain that the requirement to recite Kiddush over wine is rabbinic.
6) Regarding whether Havdalah may be recited over wine with a foul odor, see Shaagas Aryeh there, who considers both sides. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, regards it as obvious that Havdalah may not be recited over it and writes that this is contrary to Shaagas Aryeh; see there. See below, paragraph 8.
7) There: Nor over uncovered wine, etc. It is possible that there is no objection if it remained uncovered only briefly. Even governors and officials would not object after the fact unless its aroma and taste deteriorated. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 3; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 6, paragraph 7; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1. Beis Yehudah, siman 43, similarly writes that it becomes invalid only if it remained uncovered long enough for its aroma and taste to deteriorate, or if it remained uncovered overnight; see there. This implies that if it remained uncovered overnight, it is invalid for Kiddush even if its aroma and taste did not deteriorate. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Bereishis, paragraph 25, writes the same. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1, however, writes that one should object even if it remained uncovered briefly; see there. Therefore, ab initio one should take care not to leave it uncovered even briefly. After the fact, it should be disqualified only if its aroma and taste deteriorated or it remained uncovered overnight.
8) Beis Yehudah writes there that it is obvious there is no distinction between Kiddush and Havdalah in this regard. This is clear not only according to Rambam, who maintains at the beginning of chapter 29 that Havdalah is also a Torah obligation, but even according to the opinion that it is a tradition from the Sages. Whenever the Sages instituted a law, they patterned it after Torah law; see there. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, cites this.
9) There: Nor over uncovered wine, etc. It appears that if the wine vessel is inside a cabinet, chest, or cupboard, then even if the vessel itself is uncovered, it does not become invalid when the outer enclosure is closed, unless its aroma and taste deteriorate.
10) There: Nor over uncovered wine, etc. If one recited Kiddush over uncovered wine after the fact, see Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1, who considers both sides. It appears that in his opinion one must repeat Kiddush; see there. Machaneh Yehudah, siman 230, similarly concludes that he did not fulfill his obligation and must repeat Kiddush; see there. Petach HaDevir, paragraph 3, cites this. In my humble opinion, however, it is difficult to instruct him to repeat Kiddush, since we maintain that the requirement to recite Kiddush over wine is rabbinic. This is especially so nowadays, when we are not concerned about uncovered liquids and most people drink such wine. It can therefore be said that since the act was already done and he recited Kiddush, he fulfilled his obligation after the fact and need not repeat it.
