(1) One may not recite Kiddush. Even if he has no other wine. Even after the fact, if he recited Kiddush over it, the Ramban's words cited by the Beis Yosef imply that he did not fulfill his obligation; see Biur Halachah. Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger questions whether one may recite Havdalah over such wine. It appears plainly forbidden, since the reason is, 'Offer it, please, to your governor.' I later found that Birkei Yosef rules likewise in the name of Beis Yehudah. Plainly the same applies to beer with a foul odor: one may not recite Kiddush over it.
(2) Whose odor is foul. It has become somewhat foul from being kept in a repulsive vessel. Although one recites Borei Pri HaGefen over it, it may not be used for Kiddush because of, 'Offer it, please, to your governor.'
(3) For we are not particular about uncovered wine. Since snakes are not common among us. Nevertheless, it is forbidden because of, 'Offer it, please, to your governor.' If it stood uncovered only briefly, however, there is no present concern so long as its taste and odor have not deteriorated. Chayei Adam writes that this applies specifically where wine is costly and people are not particular about such brief exposure; one using beer for Kiddush should be careful. It appears that after the fact, when he has no other beverage, one need not be particular even with beer, just as with wine.
(4) And its taste is that of vinegar. We follow taste, so it is considered vinegar. Therefore, in se'if 3 below, when it tastes like wine one may recite Kiddush over it although it smells like vinegar. Wine is called vinegar-tasting when people refrain from drinking it because of its acidity, as stated in siman 204:4. The later authorities write that the Rema copied this ruling unnecessarily, for one does not even recite Borei Pri HaGefen over it, as stated there, and certainly may not recite Kiddush over it.
