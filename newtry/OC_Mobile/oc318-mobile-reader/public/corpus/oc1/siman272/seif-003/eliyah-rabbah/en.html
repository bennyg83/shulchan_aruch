That has particles, etc. Mordechai in chapter HaMocher Peirot writes that wine containing particles, since it is unfit for libations as shown in Zevachim, may not be used for kiddush. This requires study because Rambam and Bartenura there explain that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Yose, and Bava Batra 97 also appears to show this. One cannot simply say Mordechai speaks where it was not strained, because then how would Rambam and Bartenura know to rule against Rabbi Yose? Perhaps Rabbi Yose himself also speaks only of unstrained wine. One might infer from his wording "particles rose in it" rather than "there are particles in it," but there remains a difficulty: when the Gemara in Bava Batra asks what Rav's rule excludes, why not answer wine not strained from its particles? I can explain only that this is included in the answer excluding foul-smelling wine: the reason is repulsiveness even though it is genuine wine, and the same applies when particles remain in it. This still requires study.
Its smell is vinegar, etc. Nevertheless, if a white skin has formed on top, called kahn, one may not make kiddush over it, according to Bach. Once the film is removed, it appears to me there is no need for stringency. See below, siman 204 seif katan 8.
