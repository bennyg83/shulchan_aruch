Seif 7: Wine lees, etc. See above, siman 204, seifim 5 and 6. Bava Basra 96b states: “The Sages taught, etc.; Rava said, etc.; they disagree only, etc.” The law applies specifically where one poured three measures of water and obtained four. If he obtained less than four, the liquid is not wine. See Rashbam there, in the passage beginning “there remains for them, etc.” Although Yoreh Deah, siman 123, seif 8, and siman 134, seif 5, require specifically six parts, and some say sixty, those laws concern actual wine. They are derived from Avodah Zarah 73b concerning two cups, etc. The first opinion understands that case as involving sixty, while the final opinion assumes the cups were equal and therefore derives a ratio of one to six. Beit Yosef explains this in siman 123, in the passage beginning “diluted wine, etc.” Ran writes that some say, etc., but rejects the proof. Rashba writes similarly in Toras HaBayis. Grape skins that were not pressed in a beam do not have the law of lees. Even if the amount of liquid obtained equals the amount of water placed upon them, or is less, the water has absorbed the taste of the skins and is forbidden like actual wine, unless the water is six times the volume of all the grape skins. The same applies to actual wine. This is the meaning of the gloss above in siman 204, seif 5, “provided that, etc.” It refers to actual wine, as stated in Agur and cited by Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, regarding lees and grape skins of idolatrous wine, the same rule of three measures producing less than four would permit the liquid. Rosh writes this in Bava Basra in the name of Raavad. In the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, however, he does not write so. Rabbeinu Yonah compares permitted grape skins and lees, regarding a non-Jew’s contact, to lees of first tithe. The first infusion is forbidden, in accordance with Rashbam’s explanation that the entire baraisa concerns a case where the resulting quantity equals the original amount of water. See Rashbam in the passage beginning “the first, etc.” The second infusion is permitted unless one poured three measures and obtained four. In that case it is always forbidden. When the grape skins were not pressed in a beam, even the second infusion is forbidden although only the original measure was recovered, as explained above. Rabbeinu Yonah holds this specifically regarding idolatrous wine, but not regarding the blessing. We, however, rule that the same standard applies even regarding the blessing, as explained above. Wine used for idolatry that became mixed is compared to consecrated property and is always forbidden, since it was prohibited from the outset. The statement that one who soaks and adds a measured amount of water is exempt even the first time follows Rashbam’s explanation that the entire baraisa was stated as a stringency. According to Rabbeinu Yonah, regarding soaking for the laws of untithed produce, the Gemara applies Torah law, because one cannot impose a stringency there lest it cause separation from exempt produce, as explained in chapter 4 of Pesachim. The same applies regarding blessings. See Tosafos there, Bava Basra 96b, in the passage beginning “and more, etc.,” and Chullin there. This is the meaning of Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 123, seif 9: “water, etc.” Since the grape skins were not pressed in a beam, the same law applies even regarding the blessing. The following words, “if they were pressed with a wheel, etc.,” refer to the second infusion. The first infusion is forbidden even when the amount obtained equals the water added, as explained. This is also what is stated there in seif 11 concerning lees. The phrase there, “and the second time it is permitted,” means only where less than four was obtained. If four was obtained, it is forbidden. The wording there is extremely obscure. What Shach writes there in seif katan 18, that “he found four” is not exact, is an error in understanding Rashba. Rashba speaks only of lees that were already from idolatrous wine, as Beit Yosef explains there in the passage beginning “Toras HaBayis writes concerning lees of idolatrous wine, etc.” He writes that according to those who require sixty in actual wine, the same applies there. See also Beit Yosef in the passage beginning “diluted wine,” in the name of Ran and Rashba. Regarding ordinary lees, all agree that the criterion is specifically obtaining four from three, as stated explicitly in the Gemara in the dispute between Others and the Sages. Rashba’s approach on 97a is that the baraisa concerns a case where more liquid was obtained than the amount of water added. Once the grapes were pressed with a wheel and beam, all their natural moisture had already been extracted. The case therefore concerns surplus liquid beyond the original amount, which has the law of actual wine. According to one authority it requires sixty, and according to another six. In such a case the Sages were stringent regarding tithe, terumah, consecrated property, and actual idolatrous wine. Regarding permitted wine, however, Rashba applies the same law as untithed produce and blessings. See Beit Yosef there in the cited passage and Toras HaBayis.
