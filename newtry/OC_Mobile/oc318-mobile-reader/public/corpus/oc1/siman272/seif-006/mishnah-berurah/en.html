(15) Raisin wine. This is wine made from grapes dried either by the sun or by a derivative of fire. Although such grapes are not initially used for libations, their wine is valid for Kiddush. It is made by crushing the raisins, adding water, and allowing it to ferment; after remaining three days it is called wine.
(16) One may recite Kiddush over it. Bechor Shor writes that since siman 204:5, gloss, establishes that one part wine in six parts water is nullified and no Borei Pri HaGefen is recited, here too one must put enough raisins into the water that they comprise slightly more than one-sixth of the water; otherwise the blessing is in vain, since this is no better than if all the raisins were wine. He writes that the raisins are measured as they stand after swelling from the water, not when first placed in it. Yad Ephraim there questions this, while Derech HaChaim plainly disagrees and measures them at the time they are put into the water, since their later swelling comes only from absorbed water. Some poskim hold that whenever it tastes like wine it is considered wine [see Yoreh De'ah 123:8, gloss]. Nevertheless, every God-fearing person should be careful, since Yoreh De'ah 134:5 and siman 204:5, gloss, rule that it is nullified in six parts water. Ordinary wine certainly contains the required proportion and may be used for Kiddush. See Biur Halachah for what we wrote in the name of Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov.
(17) Some moisture. Meaning that if no moisture emerges even when the raisins are trodden by foot or pressed in a beam, and it emerges only through soaking, one may not recite Kiddush over it and does not even recite Borei Pri HaGefen.
