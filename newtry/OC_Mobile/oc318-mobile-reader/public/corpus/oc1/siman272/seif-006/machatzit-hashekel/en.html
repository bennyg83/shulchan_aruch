(Seif katan 6) That one recites kiddush, etc. And there are those who say that wine does not grow, etc. The Magen Avraham wrote above, in siman 182, in the name of the Bach, that these authorities are lenient and hold that even if there is much wine by the shopkeeper, as long as wine does not grow, etc., beer is considered the beverage of the locale. He further wrote there that even if a little wine grows near the city, this is of no consequence.
And so it appears from the Gemara, for Ameimar, etc. This is in Pesachim, folio 107a: Ameimar came to a certain place, and on Motzaei Shabbos they brought him beer for Havdalah. He did not want to recite Havdalah over it, and he passed the night fasting, meaning he slept in a fast, because it is forbidden to eat before Havdalah. The next day they exerted themselves and brought him wine, and he recited Havdalah and tasted. Another year he again came to that place, and again they brought him beer for Havdalah. He said, if so, this is the beverage of the locale, and he recited Havdalah over it and tasted; thus far. Presumably, in the first year, when they saw that he did not taste anything, they certainly exerted themselves with all their strength to obtain wine for him and did not find any. Ameimar also presumably realized that wine was not available. If so, even in the first year it was the beverage of the locale, and why did he not recite Havdalah over it? Rather, it must be that it is not called the beverage of the locale unless throughout the whole year there is no wine there. Therefore, in the first year Ameimar erred, thinking that throughout the whole year they had wine, only that on that day, by chance, they had no wine, and therefore he did not want to recite Havdalah over beer.
And it seems to me that one does not recite kiddush over liquor, etc. See siman 271, seif 13. That is to say, there is another reason one does not recite kiddush over liquor, etc., for it is explained in siman 271 that the one reciting kiddush must drink a cheekful, meaning the majority of a reviis, and it is not the way to drink so much liquor at one time. Although there are those who say there that the drinking of all those reclining joins to make up a cheekful, that is after the fact; initially the one reciting kiddush himself must drink a cheekful. So too wrote the responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 57. Although the Turei Zahav wrote above, in siman 210, that for liquor one does not require the measure of a reviis for the after-blessing, and whatever is the measure of drinking for most people suffices, and if so the same should apply regarding kiddush, nevertheless the Magen Avraham follows his own reasoning, for he wrote in siman 190, seif katan 4, that liquor too has the measure of a reviis. So too wrote the above responsa Beit Yaakov, and there he rejected the proof of the Taz. Rather, he wrote there that if one can drink the required measure without a large interruption, meaning within the time needed to drink a reviis or within the time needed to eat a pras, each authority according to his view, as written in siman 612, it combines. However, in the responsa Nahar Shalom, siman 35, he wrote that even in a place where liquor is not the beverage of the locale, it is permitted to recite kiddush over it in the morning, since the morning kiddush is only for recognition, and we do not require it to be the beverage of the locale. This is the opinion of the Tur in siman 289, cited in the Magen Avraham there. But this is not the opinion of the Beit Yosef there. On the contrary, it is more preferable to recite kiddush over it than to recite the morning kiddush over beer, for on an empty stomach liquor is certainly more beloved to him than beer, and it is proper to bless over what is beloved. But in this, both the author of the above responsa Beit Yaakov and the above responsa Nahar Shalom agree that it is not mere vapor, but a full beverage; see there. In the sefer Yedei Moshe on Midrash Rabbah, he wrote a proof in the name of one great man that liquor must not be called a beverage, for they said: why were Yisrael compared to oil? Just as oil rises above all liquids, meaning that if you place oil upon all liquids, the oil floats above, so Yisrael will rise above all the nations of the world. But with liquor, if it is placed into liquids, the liquor sinks downward. If so, according to the words of the midrash it is necessarily not included among liquids. This is also brought in the sefer Tosafos Shabbos, but he concluded that we do not derive halacha from aggadah.
