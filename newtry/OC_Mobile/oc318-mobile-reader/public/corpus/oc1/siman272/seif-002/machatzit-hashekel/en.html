(Seif katan 2) And its reason, etc. As written in siman 216, one does not bless Borei peri hagafen over such wine.
For it relies on what preceded, that is to say, on what is written in siman 206, that one does not bless Borei peri hagafen over it; all the more so one may not recite kiddush over it.
And see the Bach, etc. He challenged what the Tur wrote: if its smell is vinegar but its taste is wine, it is wine; but if its smell is wine and its taste is vinegar, it is vinegar, for we follow its taste, end quote. The Bach objected: if its smell is wine and its taste is vinegar, it is obvious that one may not recite kiddush over it, for it was explained above that one does not even bless Borei peri hagafen over it. Therefore, he answered that the rule that one does not bless Borei peri hagafen over it speaks specifically when a film formed upon it, meaning a kind of film grew on it; but if no film formed upon it, even though its taste is vinegar, one blesses Borei peri hagafen over it. Here it teaches us that nevertheless one may not recite kiddush over it. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote that his words are not correct; rather, even if no film formed upon it, one does not bless Borei peri hagafen over it. As to the Bach's question, one must say as the Taz wrote, that since the Tur wrote that if its smell is vinegar and its taste is wine one may recite kiddush over it, he also wrote the reverse, that if its smell is wine and its taste is vinegar one may not recite kiddush over it.
