That Kiddush may be recited over beer. This applies specifically where beer is the chamar medinah; that is, no wine is available anywhere in the city. Some say this means that no wine grows within a day’s journey around the city. Tur. The statement that no wine is available in the entire city means that throughout the year wine is not available there. This is also implied by the Gemara’s account of Ameimar, who remained overnight fasting. Mordechai writes that if wine failed that particular year and is unavailable, beer is considered chamar medinah. If Jewish wine is unavailable, the availability of non-Jewish wine is of no consequence. Even if wine is expensive, it is nevertheless considered available. Beis Yosef and Hagahos Maimoniyos. It appears to me that Kiddush may not be recited over liquor unless it is in a country where the principal beverage consumed by the populace each day is liquor, as stated in siman 182. This is also implied by Rambam and Agur, siman 383. See siman 271, seif 13.

And one who does not drink, etc. These are the words of Darkei Moshe: If wine is not acceptable and beloved to him, he should recite Kiddush over bread. So write Beis Yosef and Rambam. According to this, one who does not drink wine need not seek wine for Kiddush, because bread is certainly more beloved to him. Hagahos Maimoniyos, however, writes that if wine is available in the city, Kiddush may not be recited over bread. Beis Yosef records that the custom is that even one who does not drink wine seeks wine for Kiddush. Or Zarua explicitly writes that one who does not drink wine should recite Kiddush only over bread. Responsa Maimoniyos, Sefer Haflaah, siman 4, likewise rules that he should recite Kiddush over bread or hear Kiddush from others. It is possible that Beis Yosef refers only to one eating with others, since the others drink and this was the custom; when he recites Kiddush for himself, however, he should use bread. This explanation is difficult. It appears that the cited authority discusses a case in which wine is not beloved to him, as he writes immediately afterward: Although the Gemara says that when bread is beloved to him he recites Kiddush over it, nevertheless, if he wishes to recite Kiddush over wine, he may do so. See siman 271, seif 12. Responsa Maimoniyos, by contrast, discusses one who does not drink wine because of a vow; there he recites Kiddush over bread. It appears to me that he may not recite Kiddush over wine for others to drink, since those others know how to recite Borei Peri HaGafen themselves, as stated in siman 273, seif 4; rather, they should recite Kiddush. Rema’s ruling therefore requires examination. Bach expresses surprise at the great sages who preceded us, such as Maharshal and Rabbi Shachna, who, although they had wine in their homes, recited Kiddush over beer on Shabbos morning; this became the general custom. In my opinion, their reason is that in our country beer is chamar medinah. Accordingly, even if wine is available in the city, one may recite Kiddush over beer, though one opinion maintains that Kiddush may not be recited over it. There is likewise an opinion that when bread is beloved one recites Kiddush over bread, but according to Rabbeinu Tam Kiddush may not be recited over bread. Rema therefore writes that if wine is available in the city, one should not recite Kiddush over bread. In the morning, however, one recites Kiddush over both beer and bread, since he also recites HaMotzi, and therefore fulfills the obligation according to either view. At night this cannot be done. Moreover, in the morning bread is presumably beloved to him because he desires to eat, so he may recite Kiddush over bread, and there is a mitzvah to recite the blessing over the item he prefers. He nevertheless recites the blessing over beer as well so that the Kiddush is recognizable. This explains their custom. Bach also writes that Mahariv cited this in the name of Agudah, but he did not examine Agudah itself, which discusses only a place where beer is chamar medinah. Bach further writes that the sages recited Kiddush over beer to exempt the beer drunk during the meal, as stated in siman 174. I do not understand why they did not use wine, since wine exempts all types of beverages. In any event, the ideal mitzvah is always to recite Kiddush over wine.
