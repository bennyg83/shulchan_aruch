(Seif katan 9) And one who is not, etc. This is the wording of the Darchei Moshe, etc. The Magen Avraham's intent is to challenge the Rama's distinction, as he writes afterward. Since in the Darchei Moshe the Rama proved his distinction, he first brings the wording of the Darchei Moshe and its proof, and then rejects his proof. The Beit Yosef speaks where wine is not beloved to him. That is to say, he did not vow off wine, and if he wishes he can drink wine, only it is not beloved to him. Although in the Gemara they say: when bread is beloved, etc., this is the statement of Rav Brona, who said: if he washed his hands he should not recite kiddush, for since he washed his hands, he revealed his mind that bread is beloved to him, and it is proper to recite kiddush over what is beloved. However, Rav Brona holds that one does not recite kiddush over bread, according to the Rif's understanding. Rabbi Yitzchak said to him: many times I stood before Rav and bread was beloved to him, and he recited kiddush over bread. Thus one sees that one may recite kiddush over bread. This is according to the Rif's view. See above, siman 271, in the Magen Avraham, seif katan 26, where I brought the words of the Talmud and its commentators. It follows that where bread is beloved to him, he should recite kiddush over bread and not over wine.
Nevertheless, if he wishes, etc. And it should not be difficult from the statement of Rav Brona, based on what is written above in siman 271. And it seems to me that it is forbidden, etc. That is to say, now that he has rejected the Rama's proof, then by reasoning one should not do as the Rama wrote, that he should recite kiddush and others should drink.
Since others know, etc. That is to say, we rule in siman 271 that initially the one reciting kiddush must drink a cheekful. Although after the fact he fulfills the obligation if the others reclining tasted, nevertheless initially one should not do so. But do not say: it is written in siman 273, seif 4, that a person may recite kiddush for others, for for them it is the place of the meal, and it concludes there: if he has not yet recited kiddush for himself, he should be careful not to taste with them, etc. Thus it is permitted initially to recite kiddush for others while they taste and not he. One must say that since his primary intent is to discharge others, it is as though they themselves recited kiddush; if they drink, it is like the one who recited kiddush tasted. If so, here too perhaps he can do so, intending to discharge those reclining, and it would suffice that the others whom he discharged taste. Nevertheless, here he too may eat through this kiddush, for for him too it is kiddush in the place of a meal. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote that this applies specifically when they do not know how to recite kiddush; but if they know, they should recite kiddush themselves. Even more, one may say that the Magen Avraham's intent is to raise the difficulty from siman 271, that initially the one reciting kiddush must taste, as above. If we say that the Rama's intent, when one vowed, is that he should recite kiddush over the wine and others should drink, speaks specifically of the daytime kiddush, then his intent in reciting kiddush over wine is to discharge the others, while he in truth fulfills his own obligation only by blessing HaMotzi over bread. Still, he can recite kiddush over wine; but when others are not with him, he cannot at all bless Borei peri hagafen over wine. This the Magen Avraham rejects, because one cannot discharge others if he himself is not fulfilling his obligation, unless they do not know how to recite kiddush. Since in the daytime the kiddush consists only of Borei peri hagafen, and presumably they know that blessing, see the end of the responsum of the Chacham Tzvi, siman 168.
And the Bach wrote, etc. That although they had wine in their house, etc. The word 'in their house' is a scribal error, and it should read 'in their city.' So it is in the Bach, and so wrote the sefer Tosafos Shabbos, that it is a scribal error. This is also compelled by the Magen Avraham's answer, for if there were wine in their house, it would not suffice to say that it is the beverage of the locale. But regarding the common practice the Bach wrote: the common practice is to recite kiddush over beer, even though they have wine in their house; see there.
And if so, even if there is wine in the city, etc. That is to say, he holds like the poskim brought by the Magen Avraham in seif katan 7, that if wine does not grow within a day's journey around the city, even though wine is available in the city, nevertheless beer is the beverage of the locale. Those who say that one may not recite kiddush over it are the poskim who hold that it is not called the beverage of the locale, even though it does not grow near the city, unless wine is not available in the city. There are also views among the Rishonim who hold that one may not recite kiddush over beer even if it is the beverage of the locale, as the Tur and the Beit Yosef wrote; see there.
For one recites kiddush over bread, and in the morning, in any event, he must bless HaMotzi, and through this he fulfills the obligation of kiddush.
If it is beloved, even if he has wine, see what the Magen Avraham wrote at the beginning of this seif katan in the name of the Beit Yosef.
He fulfills the obligation whichever way, etc. The wording is not exact. Rather, it means that there are two sides by which he fulfills through bread: according to those who say that one may recite kiddush over bread, and also that he can fulfill through beer according to the one who says that one fulfills with it. We will not be so stringent as to be concerned for the view of Rabbeinu Tam that one may not recite kiddush over bread, and also be concerned for the view of those who say that one does not fulfill with beer, and take two stringencies together. See above, in the Magen Avraham, siman 184, seif katan 3. The sefer Tosafos Shabbos asked that even with one stringency he is still acting improperly, namely according to the poskim who say that one does not fulfill with beer. Although he nevertheless fulfilled kiddush with bread, still, what he drank from the beer before reciting kiddush over bread was improper; see there. One may answer that here too there is another side for leniency, to rely on the Raavad, who holds that in the morning it is permitted to taste before kiddush; see above, siman 269. It emerges that here too there are two sides: one, that he fulfilled with beer; and if you will say that he did not fulfill, one may rely on the Raavad, and say like the view that one may recite kiddush over bread. Perhaps the sefer Tosafos Shabbos intended this.
But in the evening one cannot do so. Specifically in the morning, when the kiddush has no additional blessing except the blessing over wine, beer, or bread, one can properly say that he fulfills the obligation whichever way. This is not so in the evening, when the Sages instituted a special blessing for kiddush in addition to the blessings of benefit. If he recites kiddush over beer and says the additional blessing after Shehakol, perhaps he did not fulfill with beer. One cannot say that he fulfills with bread, for he cannot go back and say a second time the special blessing of kiddush, since one of them would be in vain. What the Bach wrote, that the Geonim, etc., is as written in siman 174. That is to say, there is there a dispute among the poskim whether one must bless over drinks other than wine during a meal, or whether we say that bread exempts them because they are needs of the meal. Therefore, the Beit Yosef wrote there that in order to avoid the doubt, one should sit before washing in the place of his meal, drink less than the required measure, bless over it, and intend to exempt also the drinks during the meal. The Bach concluded: if they would recite kiddush over wine, they would not avoid the doubt, end quote. Regarding this the Magen Avraham wrote: I do not know, etc., for wine exempts, etc. In the sefer Eliyah Rabbah and the sefer Tosafos Shabbos, both intended the same point: that the Geonim were concerned for the view of the Rokeach, brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 174, who holds that wine exempts other drinks only from the after-blessing, not from the first blessing. The Rama alluded to this at the beginning of siman 174, where the Mechaber wrote that wine exempts all kinds of beverages, and the Rama wrote: even from the first blessing, end quote. He used the language 'even' regarding the first blessing because the Rokeach agrees only regarding the after-blessing. These are their words. In my humble opinion one may say that in any event there is a difficulty with the Bach's answer: they wanted to avoid the dispute among the poskim about whether one must bless over drinks, but they entered into the dispute about whether one may recite kiddush over beer. Also, according to all the poskim, the main rule is that if there is wine in the city one should not recite kiddush over beer. What did they gain by reciting kiddush over beer? The Bach resolves this with his precise language, for he wrote as follows: Do not say that the Geonim were accustomed, etc., to bless each time over other drinks in the place of a meal with intent to drink during the meal; therefore even on Shabbos morning they would recite kiddush, etc., end quote. That is to say, since they had already established this good practice, they did not want to change their practice, and that practice was for them like a vow that could not be changed, even though by this they entered themselves into the dispute among the poskim whether one may recite kiddush over beer. Therefore the Magen Avraham asked that with wine too they would exempt the drinks during the meal. And if they were concerned for the view of the Rokeach, as above, this creates the difficulty stated above: is it worthwhile to satisfy the Rokeach, who is a lone opinion in the matter, and enter into a dispute about whether one may recite kiddush over beer, when many poskim hold that even where it is the beverage of the locale one may not recite kiddush over it, all the more so where wine is available in the city? Regarding this one cannot answer as above that they had already practiced to satisfy the Rokeach, for practice does not apply to this, since it is not the way to drink wine on weekdays. On the contrary, every Friday night they acted not according to the Rokeach, for at night they recited kiddush over wine. With what did they exempt the drinks during the meal if not through the wine? One cannot say that they blessed over beer; when would they do so? Before kiddush over wine it is forbidden to drink beer. According to the Bach, one must say with difficulty that since their holy practice was to remove themselves from dispute among the poskim concerning the blessing over drinks during the meal, therefore wherever it was possible for them to satisfy the Rokeach, they would do so, since in any event their practice in the essential matter was to satisfy the dispute regarding the blessing. But this is forced.
