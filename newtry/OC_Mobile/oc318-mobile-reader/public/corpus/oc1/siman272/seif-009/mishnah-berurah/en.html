(24) That one may recite Kiddush over beer. Specifically where it is chamar medinah: no wine is available throughout the city that year, and their principal drink is beer or other beverages. If wine exists there but is expensive, it is considered available. If Jewish wine is unavailable, gentile wine being available does not render wine 'available' for this purpose.
(25) And other beverages. Milk and oil are not included, since they are not chamar medinah and are not ordinarily drunk as beverages. 'Except water' means that even if everyone in that place drinks only water, it can never be called chamar medinah. Regarding distilled spirits, see below.
(26) Except water. See below, siman 296:2, and what the Mishnah Berurah writes there.
(27) And according to the Rosh, etc. The Rosh also agrees with the first alternative, but since bread is needed for the Shabbos meal it is more significant than beer for nighttime Kiddush. In practice, one should initially be careful not to recite nighttime Kiddush over any beverage other than wine, or bread when no wine is available in the city, as explained below. Many great Rishonim are stringent and hold that one does not fulfill Kiddush with such a beverage, and the Mechaber likewise did not decide the law.
(28) Over bread. He places his hands upon it until completing Kiddush, as stated in siman 483. Just as the Kiddush cup must be held in his hand, bread must be held when Kiddush is recited over it.
(29) It is good to recite Kiddush. Meaning that by day beer is preferable to bread, as the stated reason explains; but where wine is available, he should certainly bless over wine even by day, as proven in siman 289:2. Nevertheless, in our lands, where wine is expensive and the country's principal drink is other beverages, even prominent people have not customarily sought wine for daytime Kiddush, which according to all opinions is rabbinic. They rely on the lenient authorities. One who also blesses over wine by day certainly performs the mitzvah in its preferred manner.
(30) Over beer. If distilled spirits are dear to him, in our countries where they are chamar medinah he may initially use them for daytime Kiddush. He must take a cup holding a revi'is and drink melo lugmav, which is most of a revi'is. After the fact, or under pressure when he cannot drink melo lugmav and has neither wine nor another beverage, the drinking of all those assembled may combine to melo lugmav, as above in siman 271:14. Regarding mead and other beverages, see what we wrote below in siman 296 in the Mishnah Berurah.
(31) Shehakol. This additional blessing before the meal makes it evident that it is for the honor of Shabbos. If he blessed over bread, there would be no such indication, since no Kiddush formula is recited in the morning.
(32) He should not recite Kiddush over bread. This takes account of Rabbeinu Tam's opinion that Kiddush may never be recited over bread [Magen Avraham]. Know that even the Rosh and the other poskim whose view we follow, permitting Kiddush over bread, agree that where wine is available in the city one should not use bread, since the primary mitzvah is over wine. In their view, however, if bread is dearer to him than wine he may use bread even when he has wine; Derech HaChaim rules so. Perhaps the common lenient practice relies on this.
(33) He may recite Kiddush over it, etc. Magen Avraham decides that he may not recite Kiddush intending that others drink, since they know how to recite Borei Pri HaGefen themselves, as proven below in siman 273:4. Rather, they should recite Kiddush themselves, and he should recite Kiddush over bread.
(34) Or hear Kiddush from others. Meaning that he should go to other diners who are reciting Kiddush for themselves.
