[Levush] We are not particular, etc. In my humble opinion, a God-fearing person should follow Ran in chapter HaMocher Peirot and not make kiddush over white wine except under pressing circumstances when no red wine is available. This applies specifically to wine that is excessively white, as Tosafot Bava Batra 94 write.
[Levush] Even if it is strong, etc. The Tur cites Baal Halachot Gedolot. I merited seeing Baal Halachot Gedolot itself and found on page 13 that he derives the rule from Pesachim 108, where the Gemara says regarding the four cups that if one drank them undiluted he fulfilled the obligation. Thus dilution is only a mitzvah preference, following the Sages in chapter Sheloshah SheAchlu. Tosafot, Mordechai, and Agudah derive it similarly. I note this against Beit Yosef and Bach, who had not seen his words and therefore offered strained explanations.
[Levush] Even if it is strong, etc. The Tur cites Baal Halachot Gedolot. Beit Yosef explains that he may have derived it from the baraita in which Rabbi Eliezer says no blessing is recited over wine until water is added, while the Sages say one does bless, though even they agree that for a cup of blessing it is a mitzvah preference to add water. Beit Yosef assumes this concerns wine so strong that it is entirely undrinkable undiluted, whereas wine capable of bearing one part wine to three parts water can still be drunk. This is very surprising, because Semag, positive commandment 29, explicitly writes that Baal Halachot Gedolot rules like the Sages that one blesses over wine that can bear three parts water to one part wine. Thus Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages dispute precisely such wine. Beit Yosef was likely troubled because Baal Halachot Gedolot permits it initially while the Sages only waive dilution except as a mitzvah preference. In my humble opinion there is no difficulty: since according to the Sages dilution is only hiddur mitzvah, one may call undiluted use initially permitted for one not fulfilling the hiddur. Baal Halachot Gedolot himself concludes that it is nevertheless better to dilute, meaning as a mitzvah preference. Bach also surprisingly challenges Beit Yosef without noticing this explicit Semag. Bach argues at length that Baal Halachot Gedolot learned from Bava Batra 97, where the Gemara asks that dilution improves wine and does not ask that undiluted wine is invalid. This is imprecise: perhaps Rav there follows the Sages; moreover, completely undiluted wine could be invalid while partially diluted wine is valid. All these difficulties arose because Bach did not examine Baal Halachot Gedolot and Semag, which explicitly derive the rule from Pesachim 108, "if he drank them undiluted, he fulfilled."
