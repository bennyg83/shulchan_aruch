(19) Over cooked wine, etc. The first view holds that none of these processes changes the wine for the worse. The alternative view holds that it does change for the worse, and one does not even recite Borei Pri HaGefen over these wines, but Shehakol.
(20) That contains honey. Such as konditon, made from wine, honey, and pepper, even if its taste and odor changed thereby.
(21) One may not recite Kiddush over them. Even if he added only a minute amount of honey [Rambam]. Those who add honey-water to wine to sweeten it, or add sugar, should be stringent in accordance with his words [Pri Megadim].
(22) To recite Kiddush over it. Accordingly, one may cook raisins, strain the wine, and recite Kiddush over it [Chayei Adam]. Some are stringent that it is not called wine until it ferments for three days after the raisins were cooked [Sefer HaChaim].
(23) That is not as good, etc. If they are equal, one should heed the alternative view, which is stringent and forbids Kiddush over it. Regarding the blessing, siman 202:1 has already ruled plainly that one recites Borei Pri HaGefen over it.
