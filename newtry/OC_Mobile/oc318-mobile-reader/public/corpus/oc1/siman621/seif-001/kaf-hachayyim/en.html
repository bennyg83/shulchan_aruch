1) [Seif 1] For the first reading, six are called. This is because its sanctity is greater than that of other festivals, since all melacha is forbidden on it. Levush.
2) Ibid. From parashas Acharei Mos, etc. The reason Acharei Mos is read is that it contains the service of the day. The reason for the Haftarah is that it contains the subjects of repentance and fasting, and it is written there, "And honor the sacred of Hashem," which we expound as referring to Yom Kippur. Beit Yosef; Levush. In the holy Zohar, parashas Acharei Mos, 56b, it is stated further: We read the passage concerning the sons of Aharon on Yom Kippur so that it should serve as atonement for the sins of Israel. The Holy One, blessed is He, said: Occupy yourselves with the deaths of these righteous men, and it will be considered for you as though you offered sacrifices on this day to atone for yourselves, etc., see there.
3) Ibid. From parashas Acharei Mos, etc. So that the people will hear and grieve over the loss of the righteous, and their sins will be atoned. Regarding anyone who grieves over the loss of the righteous or sheds tears over them, the Holy One, blessed is He, proclaims concerning him and says, "Your iniquity has departed and your sin will be atoned." Moreover, his children will not die during his lifetime, and concerning him it is written, "He shall see offspring and prolong his days." Zohar there, 57b. The Magen Avraham cited his words at the beginning of the siman. The essential point in this is that through it he should take to heart the need to repent from the sins in his possession, and the living should take it to heart: If a flame has seized the cedars, what shall the hyssops of the wall do? Mateh Ephraim, siman 619, sec. 51; Mishna Berurah, sec. 2.
4) Ibid. From parashas Acharei Mos, etc. The shaliach tzibbur should be especially meticulous not to omit a word from the service of the day. If this was said regarding the other days, all the more so on this sacred day. L'David Emes in Toras HaShelamim, siman 13, sec. 1. It is possible that since the reason for the reading depends upon the service of the day, namely because of, "Let our lips substitute for bulls," if he omitted or altered somewhat in the description of the service, he must repeat it. However, it appears that the proper course is for the shaliach tzibbur to repeat aloud what he omitted, without a blessing. Shalmei Tzibbur, 274c. It appears that this applies specifically if they already completed the reading. If they have not yet completed it, they should reread with the second person called up whatever was omitted or altered.
5) It is obvious that if he did not complete the parashah with the final person called up, and that person recited the concluding blessing and Kaddish was said, it is unnecessary to return and read the remainder of the parashah, as Gever the Beit Din ruled in siman 60 concerning parashas Mishchu on the first day of Pesach. However, if he remembered before Kaddish, it is proper that another person be called up and read the remainder, since our custom is to add to the required number of those called up on Shabbosos and festivals. Shalmei Tzibbur, ibid.
6) Ibid. If it falls on Shabbos, seven are called. As on other Shabbosos. If the custom on other Shabbosos is to call more than seven, they should not call fewer on Yom Kippur. Levush. I heard that some great authorities ruled not to add on Yom Kippur, even when it falls on Shabbos, because the beginnings of the passages are precisely arranged and assist in matters of atonement; therefore, it is proper not to alter them. Magen Avraham, siman 282, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah in this siman, sec. 2. Mateh Ephraim, siman 619, sec. 50, wrote that even in a place where the many people called up result in increased charity contributions, the custom is not to add, and they conclude the reading of the sixth or seventh person with the verse, "This shall be," etc., see there. So too he wrote in his work Shaarei Ephraim, gate 7, sec. 17. However, Eshel Avraham at the beginning of the siman wrote that in a place where they give a great deal of money to charity, one who is lenient to add has not lost. So too wrote the Mishna Berurah, sec. 3. See above siman 282, secs. 10 and 12, and examine carefully.
