(Se'if katan 4) The order, etc. What the Olas Shabbos wrote, etc., to resolve the question of the Razah against the paytanim, who mentioned in their piyyutim the four lots even on Yom Kippur. But on Yom Kippur there was no lot, for all the service of the day had to be specifically through the Kohen Gadol. The Olas Shabbos resolved their words, and so did the Tosafos Yom Tov. The Tosafos Yom Tov explained further in the name of the Ramban that even on Yom Kippur there were four lots: one lot for the removal of the ash, which was not done by the Kohen Gadol; a second for clearing the inner altar and the menorah; a third for the incense of the golden altar, namely who would merit the pan, but regarding the incense of the Holy of Holies we learned, 'He took the pan in his right hand and the spoon in his left'; and a fourth lot, who would pass the limbs to the Kohen Gadol, and the Kohen Gadol would throw them onto the arrangement. These are his words; see there.
And we say 'Ana Hashem,' etc. Meaning, he begins 'Ana Hashem, I have sinned' (or 'they have sinned' in the third confession), 'I have transgressed,' etc.; 'Ana vashem, atone,' etc. Because the Kohen Gadol, meaning, for this reason we do not say the Name with its substitute pronunciation, namely Adonai, as we do all year, because the Kohen Gadol said the Name as written. Therefore we allude that the Kohen Gadol too did not say it with the substitute pronunciation. As for why he says first 'HaShem' and afterward 'vashem,' see the reason in the Taz and Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 6, mishnah 2.
'Before Hashem you shall be purified,' etc. And likewise, 'for Hashem as a sin-offering.' Meaning, although the Kohen Gadol also said those two Names with the explicit Name.
It is a verse that we read. Meaning, specifically when we say the words of the Kohen Gadol, we must alter and say 'HaShem,' to indicate that the Kohen Gadol would not mention the Name with the substitute pronunciation, namely Adonai. But 'before Hashem you shall be purified,' and likewise 'for Hashem as a sin-offering,' although the Kohen Gadol also said them and mentioned the explicit Name, nevertheless, since they are the language of Scripture, we read in our ordinary way, reading the Name with its substitute pronunciation when reading the Name in verses.
Several requests for ourselves. The Taz wrote in siman 113 that one should not mock people who beat their hearts when saying the confession of the Kohen Gadol. Although it is only a narration of the Kohen Gadol's words, nevertheless we imagine ourselves as though we are those members of the House of Yisrael, and we regret our sins. This is like the bowing when we say, 'and the kohanim and the people,' etc., 'would bow,' etc.; see there.
