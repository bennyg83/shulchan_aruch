(There, se'if 4, in the gloss) The custom is to fall on one's face when saying 'and the kohanim and the people,' etc. Note: See Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 7, who asks why the paytan did not institute, regarding 'for Hashem as a sin-offering' as well, that 'the kohanim and the people' should be said. This is because the paytan wanted to end with the chazzan saying to them, 'you shall be purified,' whereas regarding 'for Hashem as a sin-offering' the Kohen Gadol did not say 'you shall be purified'; see there. I add to his words that one may say the kohanim made four prostrations on Yom Kippur, and one may say that they corresponded to the four letters of the Name, blessed be He. Since they bowed because of the Name, and the Name Havayah has four letters, therefore each letter of the Name corresponded to one prostration. This is fine in the time of the Temple, when there was no other prostration, and therefore there were these four prostrations. But we, who bow first in Aleinu, already have one prostration; therefore they instituted three more, to combine into four prostrations. So it seems correct in my humble opinion; examine it carefully. We learned in the Mishnah and in the piyyutim of the Avodah that the entire night they would not allow him to sleep, lest a seminal emission occur to him. Seemingly this is difficult: since it is explicit in Avos chapter 5 that ten miracles were performed for our fathers in the Temple, including that a seminal emission did not occur to the Kohen Gadol on Yom Kippur, why would they not let him sleep? However, this by itself is not difficult, for one may say that we do not rely on miracles. But it is difficult according to the one who says in Pesachim chapter 5, regarding 'the gates of the courtyard were locked,' where Abaye and Rava disagree, that according to the one who taught 'they were locked,' we do rely on a miracle. How does he explain this Mishnah in Yoma? Why do we not rely on the miracle? Perhaps one may say that regarding the courtyard, where the miracle is for the sake of the courtyard and not for the sake of the person, we rely on the miracle. But regarding the Kohen Gadol, that a seminal emission should not occur to him, this belongs to the person, the Kohen Gadol, and in this we do not rely on a miracle, lest he is not worthy of it; and if he is worthy, it will be deducted from his merits. However, from this it is difficult: why is it explicit in the Mishnah of Tamid that they would place the innards on marble tables so that they should not spoil? It is explicit in the Mishnah that sacrificial meat never spoiled. If so, according to the one who taught 'they were locked,' why did they not rely on the miracle here? Perhaps the miracle in this case, since the offering is for atonement for Yisrael, is considered for the sake of a person. But regarding the Pesach offering, where there is no invalidation in the body of the Pesach itself, only that there is a mitzvah that the Pesach be slaughtered in three groups, this relates to the Temple, and is a miracle of the Temple; in this we rely on the miracle. This still requires analysis; examine it carefully.
