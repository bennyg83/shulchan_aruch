(א) But, etc. — and it is not similar to what is stated in seif 1 that if a gentile lit the candle for his need, a Jew may use its light, and there it is certainly permitted to use even after the gentile finished his need, as the Acharonim wrote — for there the prohibition was not created in the kindling, unlike here where all permission is because he did not benefit through the gentile's act, and therefore it is forbidden afterward when he benefits through it. And so too with a bonfire — it is forbidden for this reason when the gentile added wood on Shabbat after the first wood was extinguished, as written in Mishna Berurah. And know that the Taz's view is that the lenient decisors regarding a bonfire permit even after the first wood is extinguished, and so too with candles even after the first candle is extinguished — we do not rule like them; and in a situation of hardship depending on this oneg Shabbat, the lenient have whom to rely on, and I did not copy his words in Mishna Berurah because Bach and Magen Avraham disagree with him and hold there is no one who permits with a bonfire, and so with candles after the first candle kindled permissibly is extinguished; and what he left — the allowance remains — that with a bonfire one cannot know when the first wood finished — they do not hold like him, and so in Sefer Mateh Yehuda. And in truth, in my humble opinion, I was in doubt about the Taz's words, for he brought proof from Hagahot Maimoniot for his words regarding a bonfire — they permitted students of Maharam to warm themselves in what the servants added on Shabbat, and it appears there that even after the first wood finished entirely — thus they follow the beginning and it is no proof at all, and almost a proof to the contrary, for the reason for permission there, for the discerning reader, is because they were all ill with cold and the Sages did not decree in this through a gentile's speech even l'chatchila.
