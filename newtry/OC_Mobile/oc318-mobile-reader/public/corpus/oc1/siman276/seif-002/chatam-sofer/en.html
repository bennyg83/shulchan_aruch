Regarding seif 2 in the gloss, which states that on Shabbat one must protest accordingly, see Responsa Ketav Sofer, Orach Chayim 116. See also Ramban on Parashat Emor concerning the expressions “a day of rest” and “a remembrance of the shofar blast.” The term “a day of rest” regarding Rosh Hashanah teaches a positive commandment applicable to all festivals. In Sukkot, however, where Scripture says “on the first day, a day of rest” and “on the eighth day, a day of rest,” these phrases exclude Chol HaMoed from the prohibition of labor based on the positive commandment. Accordingly, the first and seventh days of Pesach exclude Chol HaMoed from the negative prohibition. See there. These points escaped the notice of the author of Maayan HaChochmah, page 72a, in the stanza beginning “It shall surely blossom”; see there.
