2) There: For a Jewish person, etc. It appears obvious that if he kindled it for an apostate Jewish person, all Jewish people are likewise forbidden to benefit, because although he sinned, he remains a Jewish person. The same law applies if he kindled it for the son of an apostate Jewish woman and a non-Jew, because her child has her status, as stated in Even HaEzer, siman 43. If he kindled it for a Karaite, however, the matter is uncertain. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Zichron Yitzchak, siman 19, cites these words of Tosefes Shabbos and concludes that we lack authority to be lenient regarding Shabbos prohibitions in a matter where no explicit leniency is found. He writes that the same applies if half the people present were non-Jews and half were Karaites, and a lamp was kindled in their location: a Jewish person is forbidden to benefit from its light; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 2, cites this. See Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 6, who inclines toward leniency when it was kindled for an apostate Jewish person; see there. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halachah, it appears that one should be stringent like Tosefes Shabbos.
