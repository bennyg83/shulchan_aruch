(ד) And if there is proof — see in Mishna Berurah where we copied in the name of Magen Avraham that if he did it for both of them it is forbidden, and Magen Avraham noted this as written in siman 515, seif 6 — meaning as stated in the gloss there that if he gathered for both of them it is forbidden. And at first glance why did Magen Avraham not bring earlier from siman 325, seif 6, where it is explicit that if he gathered and intended for both Jew and gentile he must wait, etc.? One may say that there it is because there is no proof that the primary intent is for the gentile — we would say it is for the Jew; but here we deal with one who kindled for himself and for the Jew — we would say the primary intent is for himself, and this reasoning is mentioned in Rashi [Shabbat 122]. Therefore Magen Avraham brought proof so that there, Rama ruled that if it is known he gathered for both it is forbidden — meaning even with a majority of gentiles [as is clear to the discerning reader] — even though generally we say one who acts acts per the majority, as in the Gemara; and therefore if there is proof it is permitted, meaning because the primary intent is his own and we do not follow the others even if they were the majority — namely we do not know he did it for them; but if we know he also did it for them, it is forbidden in every case. [Gloss: and behold in the Prisha he challenged why proof helps — but behold in the Yerushalmi R' Yonah concludes that for his need and for a Jew's need it is forbidden; per Magen Avraham the Yerushalmi's view is that initially when Shmuel turned his face it was because he knew he kindled for his need, and afterward when he saw the gentile read his document by the candle's light he understood he also kindled for his own need — therefore R' Yonah thought that since he knew he kindled for his need, what does it help that he also kindled for his own need? Unlike in our case where we do not know at all that he kindled for the other Jews — only from the reasoning that he acts per the majority we say he kindled according to others; therefore we say that since there is proof he kindled for himself, we do not say at all that he kindled according to others, etc.]. And behold in Nishmat Adam he rejected Magen Avraham's proof and, per our reasoning above, because the primary intent is his own — this reasoning is preferable to what we say generally that one acts per the majority, and Magen Avraham's proof is strong even with a majority of Jews, and as the Mechaber ruled; therefore even if he explicitly also did it for the others, it is permitted. However, in truth there is much room to examine the fundamental law, for even from Rama's words copied from Semag it appears he rules like R' Yonah in the Yerushalmi, that for one's own need and a Jew's need it is forbidden — and in the Rashba's novellae on Shabbat, chapter Kol Kitvei, it is clear explicitly that R' Yonah's words are rejected because of the difficulty the Yerushalmi raised, and so the simple meaning of the sugya at the end of chapter Kol Kitvei in the incident of Shmuel appears per the Yerushalmi's view for permission [namely when he kindled for his need and a Jew's need like that Yerushalmi incident, and so appears from Rashi there that Shmuel and the gentile were present, and see Tosafot there] and not like R' Yonah's rejection there, unless we strain and say it is a different incident. And so ruled in Sefer Chemed Moshe to be lenient when a gentile kindled for his need and a Jew's need; but what shall we do, for from the Rambam's words in his Mishnah commentary and in his laws it appears he forbids also in this, and what Chemed Moshe wished to reject — his words are strained. And nevertheless it appears that one who relies to be lenient when kindling for his need and a Jew's need, per the Rashba and the simple meaning of our sugya — one should not protest. And incidentally, regarding that Rama gloss: Rama wrote there that if he gathered for both it is forbidden — even with a majority of gentiles, as is clear there, and Semag, Beit Yosef are found on this; and Semag deals only when he gathered for two gentiles and a Jew, or for many people half and half — otherwise why did Beit Yosef write that Semag does not deal with a majority of gentiles?
