(א) (ב) A butcher, etc. — meaning contracting. Meaning: in such a case in other melachot it is permitted; nevertheless here it is forbidden, as Rama wrote, since the Jew benefits, etc. — meaning as Magen Avraham wrote above, siman 252, note 11, in the name of the Ran, who holds that in contracting it is permitted because the gentile acts for himself; this is only where the gentile's intent is only to complete his work, unlike here with a candle, where the gentile did it to receive his wages — nevertheless the gentile's intent is also that the Jew will use it on Shabbat, see there in Magen Avraham. However, necessarily what Magen Avraham wrote — contracting means he hired him without specification to light a candle when needed — is not understood as plainly as giving the gentile a sum for the year so the gentile will light whenever the Jew needs; for in such a case in general it is permitted, for in such a case there is no need for the gentile to act for himself; rather certainly he acts for the Jew's intent, for even if the gentile does not light on Shabbat and the Jew does not need him, he still takes all his wages. And even in siman 274, seif 5, if he hired him for specific work — namely to weave for him or write when needed — it is permitted per the Rambam that he may write on Shabbat; only the Raavad forbids there. Nevertheless in our case, even per the Rambam it is forbidden — specifically there if the gentile wishes he does not work on Shabbat but on a weekday, and if he works on Shabbat he acts for himself, for the Jew does not care whether he works on Shabbat or a weekday; unlike here where the Jew needs him on Shabbat and the gentile must do it on Shabbat to fulfill the Jew's will — forbidden. And therefore what Rama wrote — since the Jew benefits, etc. — for in such a case in sending a letter in siman 247, hired in contracting and tells him to go on Shabbat, or limits him to a known day when on that day the letter must be there and it cannot arrive unless he goes on Shabbat — also forbidden, see there. Therefore it appears to me that the intent in what Magen Avraham wrote — he hired him without specification to light when needed and set a fixed sum for all lighting — in this the gentile acts for himself, for if the gentile wishes not to light on Shabbat he must still pay for other weekday lightings, and so if he hired him for days giving wages each day such and such — the intent is that each day he lights he pays that day; in general it is permitted, but here forbidden since the Jew benefits, etc., and so per the Ran, and so it appears to me.
