The law when a Non-Jew lights a candle on Shabbos. Containing 5 paragraphs: If a Gentile lit a candle for a Jew, it is forbidden for everyone, even for people it was not intended for. However, if a
Gentile lit for his own use or for a Jew who is ill, even if he is not life endangered, , every Jew may make use of the candlelight. The same applies when a Gentile makes a bonfire for himself or for someone who is sick. Yet there are those that prohibit benefiting from a bonfire lest the Gentile add (to the fire) for him.RAMA: It is immaterial whether a price was prearranged with him i.e. whether he was hired per lighting or per day, or was not hired, for since the Jew is benefiting from the Melacha (forbidden work) itself, it is forbidden under all circumstances. [Ha'gahos Ashiri, Beis Yosef in the name of the Smag] RAMA: or for the sake of children, who have a status of an ill person not endangered [Mordechai] Rema: Nevertheless if a Gentile did it in a Jew’s house on his own accord, the Jew does not need to leave his house, even though he is benefiting from the candle or fire.
