1) [Seif 1] If a non-Jew kindled a lamp for a Jewish person, it is forbidden, etc. This differs from an item brought from outside the techum for a Jewish person, which another Jewish person may use, because the techum prohibition is rabbinic. Furthermore, even regarding a rabbinic prohibition, it is possible that the leniency applies only to the techum prohibition, which does not apply equally to everyone: a place outside one person's techum may be within another person's techum. It does not apply to other rabbinic prohibitions. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham rejects this reason and concludes that another Jewish person may benefit after any rabbinic prohibition; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, writes the same. See Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, who cites a dispute among the early authorities in this regard and therefore distinguishes as follows: when a non-Jew commits a rabbinic prohibition for a Jewish person, others may benefit, because in siman 325 we follow most halachic authorities and permit others to use water that a non-Jew drew for a Jewish person's animal from a private domain into a karmelis. When a Jewish person commits a rabbinic prohibition, however, others are also forbidden to benefit; see there.
2) There: For a Jewish person, etc. It appears obvious that if he kindled it for an apostate Jewish person, all Jewish people are likewise forbidden to benefit, because although he sinned, he remains a Jewish person. The same law applies if he kindled it for the son of an apostate Jewish woman and a non-Jew, because her child has her status, as stated in Even HaEzer, siman 43. If he kindled it for a Karaite, however, the matter is uncertain. Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. Zichron Yitzchak, siman 19, cites these words of Tosefes Shabbos and concludes that we lack authority to be lenient regarding Shabbos prohibitions in a matter where no explicit leniency is found. He writes that the same applies if half the people present were non-Jews and half were Karaites, and a lamp was kindled in their location: a Jewish person is forbidden to benefit from its light; see there. Orach Chaim, paragraph 2, cites this. See Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 6, who inclines toward leniency when it was kindled for an apostate Jewish person; see there. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical halachah, it appears that one should be stringent like Tosefes Shabbos.
3) There: It is forbidden to everyone, etc. The Shulchan Aruch does not state here regarding kindling a lamp that after Shabbos even another Jewish person must wait the time required for the act to be performed, because it relies upon what is written below in siman 325 regarding a non-Jew who made a coffin, etc.; see there. The same law applies when a non-Jew drew water for a Jewish person or made a ramp by which a Jewish person could descend: after Shabbos they are forbidden for the time required for the act to be performed. This is demonstrated by Rambam's words at the beginning of chapter 6, and is obvious. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1.
4) There: It is forbidden to everyone, etc. If a non-Jew kindled a lamp for his companion, but in truth his companion intended it for a Jewish person's benefit, it can be said that one may be lenient, because the non-Jew who kindled it does not appear to be the Jewish person's agent. Mishbetzos Zahav there.
5) There: It is forbidden to everyone, etc. The prohibition is rabbinic, because if benefit were permitted, the Jewish person might tell the non-Jew to kindle it. Chiddushei Rashba. Ran explains that the concern is that the Jewish person might accustom the non-Jew to doing so on another Shabbos. Mishbetzos Zahav there cites both explanations and writes that they appear to express the same reason as Chiddushei Rashba; see there.
