1) [Seif 1] If a non-Jew kindled a lamp for a Jewish person, it is forbidden, etc. This differs from an item brought from outside the techum for a Jewish person, which another Jewish person may use, because the techum prohibition is rabbinic. Furthermore, even regarding a rabbinic prohibition, it is possible that the leniency applies only to the techum prohibition, which does not apply equally to everyone: a place outside one person's techum may be within another person's techum. It does not apply to other rabbinic prohibitions. Beis Yosef; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, however, writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham rejects this reason and concludes that another Jewish person may benefit after any rabbinic prohibition; see there. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, writes the same. See Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, however, who cites a dispute among the early authorities in this regard and therefore distinguishes as follows: when a non-Jew commits a rabbinic prohibition for a Jewish person, others may benefit, because in siman 325 we follow most halachic authorities and permit others to use water that a non-Jew drew for a Jewish person's animal from a private domain into a karmelis. When a Jewish person commits a rabbinic prohibition, however, others are also forbidden to benefit; see there.
