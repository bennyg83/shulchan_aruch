(א) [Seif 1] A gentile who lit the candle for a Jew — forbidden, etc., and it is not similar to what is brought for him outside the techum that it is permitted after techumim, which are rabbinic. And furthermore, even in a rabbinic prohibition it is possible we are lenient only in the prohibition of techumim, which is not equal for all — what is outside this one's techum is within that one's techum; but in other rabbinic prohibitions, no. Beit Yosef. Magen Avraham, note 1. But Eshel Avraham, letter 1, wrote that R' Yonah rejected this reason and agreed that in every rabbinic prohibition a Jew may benefit afterward, see there. And so Mateh Yehuda, letter 1; however see Erech HaShulchan, letter 1, who brought a dispute in this among Rishonim — therefore one must distinguish: every rabbinic reduction done on Shabbat for a Jew by a gentile is permitted for others, as we learn in siman 325 from most poskim that a gentile who filled water from a well in the public domain for an animal to a karmelit is permitted for others; but a rabbinic prohibition done by a Jew is forbidden also for others, see there.
