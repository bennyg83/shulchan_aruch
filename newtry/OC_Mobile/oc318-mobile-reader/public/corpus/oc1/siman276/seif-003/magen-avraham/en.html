(ב) Only moving the candle — since the gentile also needs it. And see siman 279, seif 64 [written in the margin]. His intent is that there it permits only through a condition, and here it is permitted because the gentile also needs it — see there; but in Hagahot Maimoniot it is written as follows: Rabbenu Tam permitted telling a gentile: bring a lit candle, for moving the candle is forbidden only because it is muktzeh, and it is permitted in tiltul min hatzad; and since if the Jew wishes he may move it permissibly, if the gentile takes it through prohibition we have no concern. But it is forbidden for the gentile to kindle it, for this thing a Jew cannot do, and also in this matter there is a side of permission, for if the Jew wishes he can bring it without a candle — nevertheless one should not be lenient so much, and one who is stringent will be blessed. Thus I found and heard that R' Shmuel of Neuburg — his teacher Rabbenu Meir commanded the maidservant to go with the youths to remove their shoes on Shabbat night so she would take the candle to light for herself and they would also use its light — thus it appears Rabbenu Tam permits in every matter; and so Mordechai, Agudah, and R' Yonah in the name of Rabbenu Tam, and Maharam, and see siman 325 and siman 334, seif 2; and it requires study on Rashi who explained daf 44 that it is forbidden to move the candle because of a decree lest it be extinguished — and so Raavad; and this was the questioner's difficulty, but in the Gemara daf 44 this reason is rejected because even if extinguished it is davar she'eino mitkaven, and the reason is because it becomes a base for a forbidden object — and so Semag, and as written in siman 277, seif 61; and Eshel Avraham, siman 279, was not precise in this.
