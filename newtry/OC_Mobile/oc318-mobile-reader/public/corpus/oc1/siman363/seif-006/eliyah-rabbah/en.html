[9] [Levush] Therefore, etc. The Taz disputes the Rama because Terumat HaDeshen appears to permit the use of a painted mark specifically for a tzurat hapetach, where one needs only one post on each side and each may be of any width, but not for a lechi. There is no difficulty, because according to the Beit Yosef even a lechi may be of any width, as above. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that although the Beit Yosef generally holds any width suffices for a lechi, he is uncertain regarding a mere streak of lime. According to the Rama, however, the matter is obvious from the law of broken fragments. Shiyarei Knesset HaGedolah writes that the practice is lenient even for a lechi, and indeed there is more reason to be lenient with a lime mark for a lechi than for a tzurat hapetach. The practice is lenient in both. Obviously the lime must have some thickness, like a garment thread. Stones projecting from a wall one above another, each within three handbreadths of the next, form a lechi provided their combined height is ten handbreadths, as stated by Avodat HaKodesh.
