7) [Se'if 2] A courtyard that was breached across its full width, etc. This means into a place to which carrying from it is forbidden. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yehonasan; Levush; Olas Shabbos, subsection 2. Namely, it was breached into a public domain or a karmelis, as stated above in subsection 3.
8) There. A courtyard that was breached across its full width, etc. For a square courtyard breached on its fourth side, a different rectification is required from that used for an alleyway that is longer than it is wide and open on its fourth side. A courtyard requires a four-handbreadth strip on one side, or two strips of any width, one on each side. An alleyway longer than it is wide and open on its fourth side, however, was permitted with a lechi of any width on one side, as stated below in se'if 3. Bach. The same applies to a courtyard that is longer than it is wide, whose law is that of an alleyway, as stated below in se'if 27. The reason for the distinction between a courtyard and an alleyway is that anything used with greater privacy requires a more substantial partition, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rashba. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3, writes likewise. See below, subsection 164.
8) There. Across its full width. This means the entire wall.
9) There. Up to ten amos, it is rendered permitted by a strip, etc. Although an opening of up to ten amos is regarded as an entrance, as stated above in siman 362, se'if 9, this case is different because a breach across the full width is more severe.
10) There. It is rendered permitted by a strip, etc. It is certainly rendered permitted by a tzuras hapesach, as stated at the end of the se'if. Only if it is narrower than four handbreadths should it not be repaired by a tzuras hapesach, as stated above in siman 362, at the end of subsection 94. See there.
11) There. It is rendered permitted by a strip, etc. If the strip is made from the wood of an asheirah, its law is like that of a korah made from the wood of an asheirah, which is invalid, as stated in se'if 16. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 2. See below, subsection 100.
12) There. Two strips of any width suffice. The Shulchan Aruch distinguishes as follows: if one makes the strips deliberately, two strips of any width suffice; if portions of the wall remained, a handbreadth must remain on each side, as he states afterward. Bach writes that one should not be lenient and that even when making them deliberately, one should initially make a handbreadth on each side. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, however, challenges Bach, and this is also the opinion of the later authorities. See below, subsection 14.
13) There. In one place. This means at the end of the wall, for otherwise the open space on this side and the open space on that side combine to nullify it, as stated below in se'if 34. Even if that remaining portion is four amos wide, it does not lose its status as a strip. Tosafos on Eruvin 5b, beginning with the word 'It is not'; Rabbi Akiva Eiger.
14) There. A handbreadth on this side and a handbreadth on that side. Beis Yosef writes this in the name of Rabbeinu Yehonasan, but comments that a handbreadth is not literal, since any amount on each side suffices. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 4, writes that this is explicit in Rabbeinu Yehonasan's words cited by Beis Yosef in siman 372. Since the Shulchan Aruch does not write so here, one must say that he reconsidered and ruled stringently in accordance with Tosafos. See there. Darkei Moshe, subsection 1, likewise challenges Beis Yosef and writes that when part of the wall remains, it specifically must be one handbreadth wide. See there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 2, rules likewise. However, Rambam writes in chapter 15, halachah 12, that even when remnants remain, any amount on each side suffices. Ritva on Eruvin 10a and Rabbeinu Chananel there write likewise, as does Levush. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, cites him and writes that this also appears from Rashba's Avodas HaKodesh, folio 6, and that Rashba's resolution may be relied upon. See there. Chayei Adam, rule 71, subsection 3, and Nishmas Adam there, subsection 1, write that initially one should be stringent and prohibit carrying unless a handbreadth remains, but in pressing circumstances one may rely upon the lenient authorities, who hold that any remnant suffices. See there. It appears that this should be the practical ruling.
15) There. All this applies at a height of ten handbreadths. This means that they need not extend the full height of the wall; a height of ten handbreadths suffices. They may not be lower, for then they do not have the status of a partition.
16) There. Even if the breach is not four handbreadths wide. It is possible that this discusses a courtyard that is wide in its middle but narrow at its end, so that its full width at the breach is less than four handbreadths. If the entire courtyard is less than four handbreadths wide, however, it requires no rectification, as stated in se'if 28. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. Shulchan Shlomo comments that this was not written because of a difficulty from se'if 28, for there is certainly room to distinguish between an alleyway and a courtyard, as is clear from the Gemara. Rather, it means that the Gemara's statement that even a breach narrower than four handbreadths requires rectification in a courtyard may refer specifically to a courtyard wide in its middle. Ritva on folio 12 writes similarly. See there. See Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 3.
17) There. Since it is breached across its full width, it requires rectification. The same applies if it is not breached across its full width but the breached portion exceeds the standing portion, as stated in siman 362, se'if 9.
18) There. Unless it is less than three handbreadths wide. Then, even if it extends across the full width, it requires no rectification because of the principle of lavud.
19) There. Even if it is not breached across its full width, etc. This appears to mean that it is obvious that when it is breached across its full width and the breach exceeds ten amos, it must be repaired with a tzuras hapesach. Even when it is not breached across its full width, however, since the breach exceeds ten amos it must be repaired with a tzuras hapesach. Although according to Rambam a tzuras hapesach is ineffective for a breach exceeding ten amos unless the standing portion exceeds the breached portion, as stated above in siman 362, se'if 10, we already wrote there in subsection 89 that on the third side it is effective even according to Rambam. See there.
20) There. Even if it is not breached across its full width, etc. Although approximately ten amos of the wall remain standing, if the breach exceeds ten amos the remaining wall is entirely ineffective. It is as though the entire wall fell, and carrying within the courtyard is forbidden until one makes a tzuras hapesach for that courtyard. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yehonasan.
21) There. Even if it is not breached across its full width. Rather, remnants remain or projections were erected on both sides. Even if the projections are wider than the breach, it must be repaired with a tzuras hapesach, as stated in siman 362 regarding both a courtyard and an alleyway. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 4.
