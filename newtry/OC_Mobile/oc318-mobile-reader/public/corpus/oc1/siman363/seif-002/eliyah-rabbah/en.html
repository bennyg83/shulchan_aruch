[1] [Levush] It is not a public domain, etc. Regarding one who throws from a private domain into a public domain, the Taz distinguishes a case where it opens into the public domain and the public presses through it, in which case he says there is no liability. See there. The Bach and Magen Avraham imply liability in every case. See the beginning of siman 345.
[2] [Levush] Any amount on this side, etc. The Shulchan Aruch requires a handbreadth of remaining wall on each side, because a remnant of a wall is less effective than a separate side-post of any width. This follows Tosafot's second answer in Eruvin 10. The Levush appears to follow Tosafot's first answer, that even a wall remnant of any width suffices. So too it appears from Avodat HaKodesh, page 6, and the Rashba's decision is worthy of reliance. This is unlike the Bach, who requires a full handbreadth even for separate side-posts based on Rabbi Yonatan. In truth, Rabbi Yonatan says that only regarding a wall, and even there perhaps he did not mean precisely a handbreadth, as the Beit Yosef writes that any amount suffices.
[3] If the breach is not four handbreadths, etc. The Magen Avraham suggests that the courtyard is wide in the middle but narrows at its end so that the opening itself is less than four handbreadths. If the entire courtyard is less than four wide, he says it needs no correction, as in seif 21. This is not persuasive. There the case is an alleyway, but a courtyard always requires a correction, as is clear from Eruvin 12 and implied by the Mordechai, Agudah, and Hagahot Alfasi there. Regarding a tzurat hapetach, see siman 362, seif 10.
[4] Any amount. Even less than the width of a finger, as stated by the Beit Yosef.
