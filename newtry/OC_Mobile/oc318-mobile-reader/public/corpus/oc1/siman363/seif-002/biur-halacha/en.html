(א) With two pasin of two mashehuin — Even though it is literally like lechi they called it pas by reason that he said first a pas 4 tefachim wide:

(ב) A tefach from here, etc. — See in Mishna Berurah that not specifically a tefach; and although in Darkei Moshe he wrote that in what remained it is worse than placing pasin l'chatchila, and as Tosafos 10 wrote in the second answer — nevertheless for the law it appears as Beit Yosef wrote and as the first answer of Tosafos on 10 s.v. b'shnei tefachim that the Gemara did not mean specifically tefachim and mashehuin suffice, and so in Rambam chapter 15 Hilchot Shabbat it is explicit in what remained that two mashehuin suffice, and so Ritva on 10, and so I found in R' Chananel's commentary there on 10 in the sugya where the Gemara reads b'shnei mashehuin, and so Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh — certainly we do not reject all these great authorities because of Tosafos' words, especially since Tosafos themselves in the first answer agreed for the law with all these great authorities, and so Eliyah Rabbah ruled for the law as we wrote. [Afterward I found in Nishmat Adam that he also agreed thus from the essence of the law, and he brought that Rashi's view is also thus explicitly on 10a s.v. le'ashme'inan, etc., that a large one is permitted with a mashehu wrapping; but l'chatchila the view of Chayei Adam is to be strict in this matter like Shulchan Aruch unless in a time of need]:

(ג) Since it is in its full — Magen Avraham wrote: it is possible he speaks of a courtyard wide in the middle and at its end it is narrow so that in its full width there is not 4 tefachim; but if the entire courtyard is less than 4 tefachim no repair is needed, end of his words; and in my humble opinion his words are correct for the law in this, for thus wrote Rashba in his chiddushim to Eruvin 13a: Ra'avad wrote: it appears to me they disagree in the tannaim on Rav Achlai [whether a mavo less than 4 tefachim needs repair] — such as the mavo is wide inside and narrows until the opening is less than 4 tefachim; but if its wall was shorter than 4 tefachim it is a exempt place and needs nothing, end of Rashba's words, and so Ritva there, in the name of Ra'avad agreed to his words; and if so according to this all the novelty of Rav Achlai who is lenient in a mavo less than 4 tefachim is only in a mavo wide throughout but narrows near the opening, and we teach that even in this the Sages did not require repair; and when we challenge in the Gemara on Shmuel from Rav Achlai and answer Rav Achlai speaks of a mavo but in a courtyard we are strict even in less than 4 — it is reasonable we are strict in a courtyard only in the case Rav Achlai speaks of in a mavo, namely where throughout it is wide but at the end narrows, and in this we are strict in a courtyard since in any case throughout there is the measure of 4; but where throughout there is no place of 4 there is no reason to be strict, as Ra'avad wrote regarding a mavo that none disagree to be strict in such a case that whatever lacks 4 is an exempt place — so too one can say regarding a courtyard that it is impossible to be strict in such a case that it is an exempt place; and what Magen Avraham wrote as in seif 28 — that is what he wrote there himself in s.k. 29: he speaks where at its opening it is narrow but inside wide, and his intent there is the words of Rashba and Ra'avad, that if the inner width with its opening are equal even width of 3 tefachim nothing is needed even for the first view; and the later authorities did not contemplate well Magen Avraham's words and therefore challenged him, for a arranged sugya escaped them; and I found in Tosafot Shabbos and Machatzit HaShekel and Peri Megadim that they wrote explicitly as our words, except they did not see Rashba's and Ritva's words, and therefore did not fix nails on this;
