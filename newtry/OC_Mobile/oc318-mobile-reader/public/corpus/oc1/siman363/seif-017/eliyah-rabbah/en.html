[17] [Levush] If it is four amot wide, etc. The same applies if he lined the area with multiple side-posts, each within three handbreadths of the next, for a length of four amot. This is specifically when the outer lechi does not project at all beyond the alleyway. If it projects even slightly outside, it is permitted. If the posts are three handbreadths apart, he may use the area until the outer edge of the outermost lechi. If each lechi stands separately, etc., as stated in Avodat HaKodesh, page 6.
[18] If he inserted, etc. This applies specifically where he inserted them into the thickness of the wall and they project outward. If they stand beside the wall, the arrangement is permitted by virtue of the lechi itself, as stated by Olat Shabbos.
[19] Sufficient to hold, etc. Meaning, if the alleyway is ten amot wide, the beam must be able to hold eighteen half-bricks, which amount to nine amot, as stated in seif 25, according to Avodat HaKodesh. The Beit Yosef and Bach write that if the beam is made from straw or reeds, even if it happens to be strong enough to hold a half-brick it is invalid, because most straw-and-reed beams are not strong. If an alleyway is open at both ends and was corrected on one side with a tzurat hapetach, while on the other side its two walls are unequal, one shorter and one longer, a lechi suffices there, as stated by Ralbach, responsum 66.
