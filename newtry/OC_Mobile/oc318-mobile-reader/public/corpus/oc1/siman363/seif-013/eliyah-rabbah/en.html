[15] And it is flush on the inside, etc. Tosafot on page 9 and the Rosh conclude that in such a case it is not a lechi, because 'visible from the outside' means visible to those standing behind the alleyway, not merely to those within. The Beit Yosef does not explain Tosafot and the Rosh this way, but Chochmat Shlomo does, and this appears correct, as stated by Malbushei Yom Tov.
[16] [Levush] For this is the law of every lechi, etc. This is difficult from siman 365, seif 4. One must say that the present case opens into a karmelit. This requires study. Regarding the Shulchan Aruch, the Magen Avraham writes that here the other side is shorter, whereas the Gemara discusses a case where the other side is also long. At first glance every lechi has the other side shorter, and nevertheless it is valid. This requires study.
