(s.k. 13) With its end — for on the other side it is shorter than this, etc. So it appears to me to resolve Eliyah Rabbah's question — Eliyah Rabbah challenged: but we say daf 10 side 1 — Rav Huna: lechi extending with the side of the mavo less than four amot is judged as lechi and one uses with its inner end four amot — judged as mavo; and we infer from this between lechayim it is forbidden, meaning we say one does not use the mavo except from the inner end of the lechi onward; and since we rule below siman 365 seif 4 that it is permitted to use opposite the lechi — why does Shulchan Aruch write here and one uses only with its inner end; and he answered that it deals open to karmelit, for in such a case it is explained below that it is forbidden to use opposite the lechi, for we say found its kind and it awoke; otherwise Shulchan Aruch should have explained and not been terse; and further if so why does the Talmud say we infer between lechayim forbidden — perhaps Rav Huna also deals open to karmelit; perforce the Talmud's explanation is strained since Rav Huna stated his words generally — and how do we interpret Shulchan Aruch's terse words; therefore Tosafot Yeshanim's explanation — and this is Magen Avraham's explanation; and what Eliyah Rabbah questioned on Rashi in what challenges the Talmud's question that it brought in s.k. 12 — it challenges Rashbag why need another lechi, behold it is visible from outside and equal from inside; and Rashi explained that the way of placing lechi is to extend the lechi somewhat outward, etc., as above; and Tosafos challenged: behold it implies in the entire sugya that for the Rabbis one must permit until the outer end of the lechi — and how is it permitted, etc. — for how will you permit reshus ha-rabbim, for a standard mavo the length of its walls is equal, end of Tosafos' words; and it is explained as Magen Avraham's words; and one should not ask why Shulchan Aruch was terse since a standard mavo the length of both its walls is equal; if so he placed the lechi opposite the thickness of the wall — on the side opposite the lechi there is a mavo wall, and therefore it is forbidden to carry even for us opposite the lechi — as Tosafos' question above; and what Magen Avraham wrote that in the Gemara it deals when its other side is also long — this is because perforce it must be according to Tosafos there who hold a standard mavo both walls' length is equal, and they challenged from this on Rashi's explanation as above; perforce it must be said that Tosafos hold that the Talmud was explaining what Rav Huna said — lechi extending with the side of the mavo — meaning with the side opposite the lechi, that it deals where the wall opposite the lechi is longer than the other wall opposite it; and now that he placed the lechi on the side of the shorter wall, the shorter side's length is extended by the lechi with the long side of the mavo opposite it and they become equal; if so it is fine that we infer between lechayim forbidden — unlike Shulchan Aruch which added lechi extending, etc., that he placed its end opposite the thickness of the wall — it implies this explains what extending with the side of the mavo means, and the side of the mavo means that side on which the lechi stands at its end; if so it deals as a standard mavo where both walls are equal, and therefore forbidden opposite the lechi since it has no wall opposite it; or one may say a plain way for Tosafos there — that is how they explain why the Talmud says we infer between lechayim forbidden — for Tosafos hold perforce Rav Huna does not deal with a standard mavo where both walls are equal, for if so an obvious matter that one does not use except from its inner end — for it has no wall opposite it; if so Rav Huna need not say and one uses with its inner end; he should only have said it is judged as lechi; perforce it comes to teach even where the wall opposite it is longer, nevertheless one is not permitted except with its inner end — for it holds between lechayim forbidden;
