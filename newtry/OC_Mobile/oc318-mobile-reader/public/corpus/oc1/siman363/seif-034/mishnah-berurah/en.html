(א) And he makes a pas three amot wide — for thereby standing becomes greater than the breach at its side and it is considered closed; and although generally we hold that breach like standing is also permitted, as explained in siman 362 seif 9 — here it is different, for it is also breached from the other side, and in such a case where they are equal we say the air of this side and that side comes and nullifies it:
(ב) And an opening ten amot wide remains — and nevertheless lechi or korah is required to permit the mavo, for it is not better than other ordinary mavo'ot; and one places the korah only on the area of ten amot [and likewise when making lechi, he places it at the head of the ten amot], for from the pasin onward until the walls it is permitted otherwise because of standing greater than breach:
(ג) And likewise every similar case — meaning that he distances four amot from the wall and makes a pas of six amot [Rashi]:
(ד) And one must beware, etc. — but from the default we are not concerned lest they walk through the small one and leave the large one, for the presumption is that a person does not leave a large opening and enter through a small one:
(ה) That if he did so, etc. — meaning that thereby the opening status of the large one is nullified, and the lechi or korah corrected in it is nullified, and this mavo is found without correction:
(ו) For the small one — see Chiddushei R' Akiva Eiger who wonders what helps in making tzurat hapetach for the small one after opening status was nullified from the large one; and the correct view is as written in Sefer Tiferet Shmuel that it is a scribal error and should read "for the large one," and now it is well explained that after tzurat hapetach was made, opening status was not nullified from it even if they do not walk through it:
