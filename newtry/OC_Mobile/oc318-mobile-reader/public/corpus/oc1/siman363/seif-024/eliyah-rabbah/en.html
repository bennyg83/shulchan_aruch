[26] A wide beam, etc. This means a beam ten handbreadths wide. Since it hangs down ten handbreadths, one might think it loses the status of a beam, just as with a mat, and becomes a partition beneath which goats can pass, which the Rashba invalidates. The law teaches that it remains valid because the beam itself is visible and not concealed by a mat, so the name 'beam' remains upon it. The statement that even if it extends to within less than ten handbreadths of the ground, etc., means that one might have thought that once it descends below ten from the ground it no longer has the status of a beam. The Taz's question against the Rashba from seif 12 appears to me no difficulty, because a partition requires a stronger visual indication than a lechi.
