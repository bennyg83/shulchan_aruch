**26.** The ruling that an alleyway is permitted by a lechi or beam applies only when its height is not less than ten full handbreadths, its width is no more than ten precise cubits, and its length is at least four full cubits. If any of these conditions is lacking, it can only be rectified with a doorframe shape. If the inner height exceeds twenty precise cubits, it cannot be rectified by a beam, but can be rectified by a lechi. If one wishes to rectify it with a beam, a decorative marking or design must be made on it — one marking on the wall in plaster or on the face of the beam — so that everyone looks at it. If the height exceeds twenty cubits and a structure is built beneath the beam to reduce it below twenty, a structure one handbreadth wide along the width of the beam suffices. But if the height is less than ten handbreadths and one carves into the ground to complete ten, one must carve four cubits into the alleyway across its full width. An alleyway is not permitted by a lechi or beam unless two courtyards open into it and each courtyard has two houses. Some disagree with this. Each entrance must be at least four handbreadths wide. There must be residents eating in each house, since the location of the bread determines the eruv. Even if one house belongs to a father and one to a son, and the son receives his meals from his father's table yet eats in his own house, they are considered as two. Even if one side has a non-Jew and one a Jew, this is sufficient. Furthermore, the length of the alleyway must exceed its width. If any of these conditions is lacking, it can only be rectified with a four-handbreadth post, two minimal posts on either side, or a doorframe shape. {Rama: (Even if all the courtyards open into each other.) Some say the custom today is to rectify all alleyways with a doorframe shape, since all our alleyways have the status of courtyards. The widespread custom in these lands is to rectify them with a rope tied across the width of the alleyway. This rope does not have the status of a beam since it is not a handbreadth wide, but it works as a doorframe shape, which is valid even with reed at the top, as explained above at the end of Siman 362. Therefore one should be careful to place two poles ten handbreadths high directly beneath the rope, which then works even for a passage open on both ends (*mavoi mefulash*) as a doorframe shape, or even in a courtyard or wherever a doorframe shape works, as explained at the end of Siman 362.}
