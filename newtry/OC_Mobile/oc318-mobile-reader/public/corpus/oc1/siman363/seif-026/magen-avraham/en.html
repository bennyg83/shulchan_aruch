(א) Spaced. The measure of spacing is more than constricted — half a finger per amah (Maggid Mishneh, Peri Megadim):

(ב) Except with tzurat hapetach. And Rambam wrote that less than 4 amot its law is as a courtyard, and it is possible he did not mean tzurat hapetach specifically:

(ג) In a wide building. And the same with earth, and one must be careful when trampled by feet that it not diminish from the measure:

(ד) Two courtyards, etc. For whenever there is not abundance of residents they use it discreetly and require more partition (Beit Yosef, Rashba), and if so it is possible to be lenient in our mavo'ot since there is abundance of residents, and it is possible they did not distinguish in a mavo:

(ה) And there are those who disagree. Since they can make eruv together through the openings they are considered as one courtyard, and the same regarding houses open to each other:

(ו) Law of courtyards. Since most towns are surrounded by a wall in Yeshiv and afterward were enclosed — one should say the law of a courtyard and must correct the mavo'ot (between Jews and gentiles) as the law of one who comes to divide one courtyard into three and four domains if there were small paths in the courtyard between house and house — tzurat hapetach or two pasin of mashehuyin are required, as stated in Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of Maharam, end of his words Terumat HaDeshen; and if so if the mavo is not ten amot wide, two pasin of mashehuyin suffice; and so Mahariv in his laws siman 18; and thus in Hagahot Maimoniyot explaining Maharam — our mavo'ot require only two lechayim, for they are only removal of residents, and tzurat hapetach is not required; nevertheless it appears our mavo'ot are not in their law as pass-through to require tzurat hapetach in its crookedness like the law of a crooked mavo, for there it deals pass-through to reshus ha-rabbim, end of his words; and it is obvious it deals with walled enclosure, for he wrote it is only removal of residents; but in unwalled cities tzurat hapetach is required, as Rashi wrote siman 364 in openings to karmelit — see there seif 63 — even in its crookedness correction is required, for there is no difference between reshus ha-rabbim and karmelit; and so Beit Yosef there, in the name of responsum of Ran; and so Mishna Berurah siman 92; except he moved Mahariv's words to another view — see there; requires study, for I did not see practice thus; therefore it appears to me that since Rama explicitly wrote all our mavo'ot have the law of courtyards — it implies even without a wall, since houses and courtyards are not open into it, they have courtyard law, and in a courtyard there is no difference between crooked and straight — only one correction at its head is required; nevertheless regarding this that mavo'ot have courtyard law upon them — if it has no wall, tzurat hapetach is required since it is close to reshus ha-rabbim, as Semak wrote; but that its crookedness is not close to reshus ha-rabbim — correction in the bend is not required, end of gloss; and see siman 364 that on the other side lechi suffices; and siman 361:

(ז) Two reeds. On the ground, but the walls of the mavo are not counted in place of the reeds — for otherwise why was a korah a tefach wide needed, for in all that it is there is tzurat hapetach; rather reeds must be placed, and one can say what he wrote in Sukkah that one places a reed on the walls and need not a reed from here — see siman 631 seif 3; and it is possible this is a mere stringency to make tzurat hapetach, and it appears to me if there is a protruding pillar in the wall it is judged as a reed, for it is no worse than lechi in seif 11:
