(א) When it is not low — meaning that if the ground in the mavo is not low from inside opposite the walls by the measure of ten tefachim, it is considered as if the mavo has no partitions, for there is no partition less than ten:
(ב) Spaced — and in the latter clause precise; for we go stringently everywhere for this matter. And the measure of spaced over precise is half a fingerbreadth per amah:
(ג) More than ten — for if it is more than ten it has no name of opening, and lechi and korah do not help for this:
(ד) Precise — meaning that for each amah its six tefachim are measured precise; and likewise in the latter clause for mavo length, for we require four amot spaced, it is likewise that its tefachim are measured spaced; if so, two fingerbreadths are added to the four amot, as explained:
(ה) Its length four amot, etc. — for in less than this measure it is not considered a mavo but as a courtyard:
(ו) Only by tzurat hapetach — meaning that even if it was low less than ten tefachim, tzurat hapetach also helps; and likewise if the width is ten amot, or if its length was not four amot; but many Acharonim agreed with the ruling that if the hollow of the mavo was low less than ten tefachim, tzurat hapetach does not help, for even a partition does not help when it is not ten high, and all the more so mere tzurat hapetach. However, if the hollow of the mavo was ten tefachim high and only at the mavo opening it was low less than ten, in this tzurat hapetach helps, provided it was made as required — meaning the reeds were ten tefachim high [Gra]. And know further that if its length was not four amot, the view of several poskim is that its law is like a courtyard, and therefore it is also permitted by a pas of four on one side, or two pasin of mashehuyin on two sides; and likewise the Mechaber himself below in seif 362; and the Mechaber who wrote "only by tzurat hapetach" is not precise:
(ז) In the height of its hollow, etc. — but if the height of the hollow of the mavo up to the korah is only twenty precise, even though the korah is above twenty, it is valid:
(ח) Is not permitted by korah — for korah is because of recognition, and above twenty there is no recognition, for the eye does not rule over it; but a lechi, which is close to the ground and suffices at height nineteen — what harm is there if it is very high?:
(ט) To reduce it from twenty — and the same, he can lower the korah itself and place it within the hollow of the mavo on pegs protruding from the walls below twenty:
(י) In a structure one tefach wide — and the same for earth of this measure; and one must beware when trodden by feet that it not diminish from the measure in its width; and the same regarding its height, that it not diminish and become more than twenty:
(כ) In the width of the korah — for its measure is one tefach, as in seif 17; and the reason one tefach width helps is that we hold it is permitted to use beneath the korah, and one standing in that place can see the korah, for at that place it is not higher than twenty, and there is recognition because of this:
(ל) Must hollow four amot — and that which above one tefach suffices is different here, for in less than ten tefachim it is not called a side at all, and one must make it a side; therefore specifically the measure along the mavo length, which is four amot, is required, as at the beginning of this seif; but there it is called a side, only that it is higher than twenty amot, and therefore one tefach suffices as the measure of wall width (Rashi):
(מ) Into the mavo — and if he hollowed less than this, he requires specifically a pas of four, or two pasin of two mashehuyin, as the law of a courtyard, as explained at the end of the word "only":
(נ) Across its entire width — for if a gap remains between the hollowing and the walls, the walls cannot join to it, and it is as if there are no walls for the mavo. However, if the gap between the hollowing and the wall is only less than three tefachim, some say it helps [Peri Megadim]:
(ס) Two courtyards — the reason for this is that what they permitted in a mavo by lechi and korah is because it has many residents and they do not use it so much for privacy; therefore such a correction suffices; unlike a courtyard, where there are not so many residents, they use it more for privacy, therefore stricter qualification is required — two pasin or a pas of four; and the same for a mavo that does not have two courtyards open into it — few residents and they do private uses in it, therefore lechi and korah do not suffice:
(ע) And some disagree — for since they can join through the openings, they are considered as one courtyard; and the same for houses open to one another; and Acharonim wrote that since it is a rabbinic matter, we follow the lenient view:
(פ) And eats in his house — to exclude if his manner of eating is in his father's house:
(צ) That its length, etc. — for such is the way of a mavo to be:
(ק) More than its width — even any amount [and its length is called the way of its entry into the mavo, and what is not the way of entry is called width]; for if its length equals its width, it is as a courtyard, as below:
(ר) One of these — refers to all explained here from "a mavo is not permitted," etc.; and the same law when the mavo length was not four amot:
(ש) Is not permitted, etc. — for its law is like a courtyard, and therefore for this a pas of four tefachim, etc. is required, as above in seif 2:
(ת) Or tzurat hapetach — and this helps even when the width is ten, as above in seif 2:
(110) All mavo'ot, etc. — whether closed on one side, whether open on both sides, whether wide up to ten amot, whether more than ten amot:
(111) For all our mavo'ot, etc. — this is because in our mavo'ot there are no houses and courtyards open into them, and therefore they are not permitted by lechi and korah as the mavo permission law; and although in a courtyard two lechayim on two sides suffice without tzurat hapetach, nevertheless because sometimes the mavo is open on both sides, then certainly one must be stringent that it requires tzurat hapetach even without houses and courtyards open into it, because its head is open to reshus ha-rabbim or karmelit, or because sometimes it is wider than ten, for in this even in a courtyard tzurat hapetach is required by law, as in seif 2; therefore the practice was always to make tzurat hapetach. And behold, on the other side of a mavo, if the width is not more than ten amot, there are views among Acharonim — the view of many of them is that there in any case two lechayim on two sides suffice and tzurat hapetach is not required; their reason is that either way, if its law is like an open mavo, we also hold below in siman 364 that tzurat hapetach is not required except on one side, and on the other side one lechi or korah suffices; and if its law is like a courtyard, as explained, behold in any case two lechayim suffice; and some Acharonim side that from the practice one should make tzurat hapetach on every side. And certainly l'chatchila one must be careful in this; but in a place of need, such as if the rope over the lechayim broke on Shabbat or any similar case, it appears one may rely on them to be lenient where the width is not more than ten amot, even if it is fully breached. And behold, in our mavo'ot mentioned above that have no houses and courtyards open into them, if it is a crooked mavo, there are views among Acharonim whether correction is required in its bend as explained below in siman 364 seif 3; some say that since from the outset its law is like a courtyard, no correction is needed there, and what they do at its two heads suffices; and some are stringent in this, and the practice is to be lenient. And know further that what is explained that in our towns tzurat hapetach is required — this is in towns not surrounded by a wall, or surrounded by a wall and the wall was breached to reshus ha-rabbim or karmelit more than ten amot; but when surrounded by a wall and the wall was not breached, only that one must correct and divide between where Jews live and where gentiles live so they should not forbid Jews to carry — there one need not make tzurat hapetach, but a pas four tefachim wide or two pasin of mashehuyin suffices when breached there fully and the place is not wider than ten amot where one wishes to make the correction (even if the mavo in the middle is wider than ten amot), for this city has complete courtyard law. And when no correction was made between Jew and gentile, the law here is also like a courtyard, that it is permitted to carry vessels that rested within it, but not to a house and not from house to courtyard:
(112) Beneath the rope, etc. — and since the reeds below are ten tefachim high, no further reeds are needed above to tie the rope on them; rather he can tie the rope opposite them even on a roof above this and that, as we hold above at the end of siman 362 that the upper reed need not touch the lower reeds. Also that which is explained above at the end of siman 362, that one should not tie the upper reed or rope from the side of the reeds but only above upon them — here, when tying the rope above on the roof on some reed, it is not relevant to be precise about the manner of tying whether on top or from the side, provided the lower reeds are aligned beneath the rope. If there is a roof interrupting between the height of the rope and the lower reeds, it is not tzurat hapetach in this, since the roof interrupts between the rope and the reeds; and one does not make tzurat hapetach in such a matter, but must open a large hole in the roof above opposite the reeds so the reeds are aligned beneath the rope without interruption:
(113) Two reeds — on the ground, but the mavo walls are not counted in place of the reeds. And if there is a protruding pillar in the wall, it is judged as a reed if they relied on it from erev Shabbat, for it is no worse than lechi in seif 11. And the same when projections come out from the wall, less and less than three tefachim up to less than three tefachim near the ground — one may rely on them from erev Shabbat for reeds of tzurat hapetach, provided they are exposed above beneath the rope; and some are stringent with such projections that are not made in the manner of doorposts of an opening, and it is not considered an opening. If one places the reeds inside the house or inside the fence surrounding it on four sides, it is nothing; and even if he placed one reed there, it also does not help:
(114) Aligned beneath the rope — and if something protrudes from the side, it is invalid:
