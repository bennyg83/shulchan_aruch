[27] Generously measured, etc. In the latter clause the measurement is exact, because in a doubt we rule stringently.
[28] Only with a tzurat hapetach, etc. The Rambam says that an alleyway less than four amot long has the law of a courtyard. The Magen Avraham therefore suggests that the mention of a tzurat hapetach is not precise. It appears to me that when the opening is wider than ten amot it likewise has the law of a courtyard. The Shulchan Aruch and Levush rely on what they write at the end of the seif, that it may be corrected by a board or a tzurat hapetach. So too it appears from Avodat HaKodesh, page 4. The Magen Avraham also overlooked what the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 32.
[29] [Levush] Because the eye does not perceive it, etc. If the beam is four handbreadths wide and strong enough to hold plaster, it is permitted not because it serves as a recognizable beam but because we say the edge of the roof descends and closes the opening, provided the alleyway opening is not wider than ten amot. It also appears to me that if the beam is seven handbreadths wide, even if not strong enough to hold plaster, it is valid, because whenever it is four wide the eye notices it. So writes Avodat HaKodesh, page 4.
[30] A structure, etc. The same applies to earth. One must take care that trampling by people's feet does not reduce it below the required measure.
[31] Until there are openings, etc. The Levush omitted the Rama's gloss: 'even if all the courtyards open into one another; some disagree.' Perhaps this is because the Beit Yosef rules like the dissenters and the unqualified wording means the courtyards do not open into one another, as suggested by Malbushei Yom Tov. It is astonishing that so great an authority did not notice that at the end of this very seif the Levush quotes the Rama's words exactly. As practical law, the first view is primary, as the Bach writes, including when houses open into one another. Avodat HaKodesh, page 4, states that even if an eruv was made, each house must still contain four amot by four amot or more.
[32] That its length be greater, etc. Even by the smallest amount, as stated in Avodat HaKodesh. The length of an alleyway is the direction of its entrance. The dimension not running in the direction of entry is called its width even if that width is actually greater than its length. The Rama's gloss here is quoted by the Levush at the end of the siman. Malbushei Yom Tov erred in thinking the Levush omitted it and therefore elaborated unnecessarily.
