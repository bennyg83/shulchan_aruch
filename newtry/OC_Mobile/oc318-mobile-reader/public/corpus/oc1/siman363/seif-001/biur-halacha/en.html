The Sages forbade, etc. — And regarding whether it is called reshut hayachid from the Torah to obligate one who throws from it to reshut harabbim — there is a dispute among the early authorities: for Rambam chapter 14 it is not called reshut hayachid from the Torah except with four partitions or at least three complete partitions and a lechi in a 4[-tefach] wall that is considered as a partition, and so is proven from R' Chananel in his commentary on daf 12 — see there; however according to most poskim [namely Rashi on Eruvin 12b and Tosafos in several places and Ra'avad in his hasagot and Rosh first chapter of Sukkah and Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh and in his chiddushim and Ritva] with three partitions or with two partitions and a lechi it is complete reshut hayachid from the Torah. And know there is no practical difference for this view whether the two partitions were like a gimmel or the two partitions were opposite each other — for even in this it is called reshut hayachid from the Torah since it has another lechi (so is proven from Eruvin 12b from the Gemara's difficulty there from the baraita of R' Yehuda see there); however if reshut harabbim passes between the partitions it is proven in Shabbat 6b that for all opinions this lechi does not help to make it reshut hayachid since many pass between the partitions; and see in Eruvin 12b in Tosafos s.v. itiveih and 22a s.v. drabbanan drabbanan, etc., that it appears at first glance contrary to our words; but the Gra already wondered on them from that sugya in Shabbat, and so I found in Ritva 22a in the name of Tosafos themselves that the language they do not combine reshut harabbim in this implies even from the Torah it does not help. And know further there are some early authorities who make a distinction between the views and it is brought in the Taz: where the mavo or courtyard is open to reshut harabbim, since sometimes many push into it and enter inside, there is not upon them from the Torah the name reshut hayachid, and this resolves the sugya of all roofs 94a where it appears explicitly there that whenever one wall of his courtyard adjacent to reshut harabbim was breached — if one threw from it to reshut harabbim he is exempt — see there in Ritva and in Rambam chapter 17 of Hilchot Shabbat and in Kesef Mishnah there, in the name of Ramban:
