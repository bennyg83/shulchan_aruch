[33] That does not contain four, etc. This means that its entrance is less than four handbreadths wide even if the interior is four wide, as stated by the Beit Yosef. So too it appears from Rabbenu Yerucham, page 92. Rabbenu Yerucham, page 88, writes that this leniency applies specifically to an alleyway; a courtyard always requires a correction. See seif katan 3.
[34] [Levush] One of its sides, etc. This means that the riverbank is ten handbreadths high, and specifically when it is immediately adjacent to the head of the alleyway. If the river is ten amot away from the houses, a correction is required because that intervening area is open. Likewise, a refuse heap must be ten high. The Beit Yosef implies that even if one side is the sea, one side a refuse heap, one side closed, and one side open to the public domain, a lechi or beam at the public-domain side suffices according to this view. The Levush mentions only one side apparently because the dissenting opinion is a greater novelty, requiring a correction even then. Emunat Shmuel, responsum 40, writes that a city surrounded by a ditch ten handbreadths deep and four wide is considered surrounded by a partition.
