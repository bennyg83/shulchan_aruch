(א) (s.k. 27) Courtyard law — since Rema's gloss in the gloss derives from Terumat HaDeshen siman 74 — therefore Magen Avraham cited Terumat HaDeshen's language; for at first glance from his words it appears that not in all mavo'ot is tzurat hapetach required, as explained:

(ב) Sat and at the end enclosed — meaning and if so it is a proper partition, for it was enclosed for dwelling:

(ג) Law of courtyard — meaning the law of a mavo does not have even if houses and courtyards are open into it — nevertheless behold it is enclosed with four partitions on four sides, only that correction is required to divide between the place where Jews dwell and where gentiles dwell, for otherwise the gentiles who have no share in the eruv forbid the Jews to carry:

(ד) Like one who comes to divide one courtyard, etc. — meaning if some courtyard residents wish to join eruv alone and some of the other courtyard wish to join alone — so that these do not forbid those, for each courtyard portion is fully breached to a forbidden place for them:

(ה) Tzurat hapetach or two pasin — meaning if it is not wider than ten amah, two pasin suffice as courtyard law; and more than ten amah requires tzurat hapetach; and so Mahari Weil — tzurat hapetach is not required if not wider than ten amah:

(ו) Removal of residents — meaning to remove gentile residents so they do not forbid the Jews:

(ז) Therefore it appears mavo'ot as it should read:

(ח) To require, etc. — like crooked mavo law as below siman 364 seif 3:

(ט) And it is obvious it deals, etc. — he comes to exclude the view of Mishna Berurah that he brought afterward who understood Mahariv's intent to be lenient in our mavo'ot — tzurat hapetach is not required in its crookedness since our mavo'ot are not pass-through to reshus ha-rabbim, for we have no reshus ha-rabbim nowadays; and therefore Mishna Berurah challenged in that responsum — this law depends on the dispute whether there is reshus ha-rabbim nowadays since the width is sixteen amah or we require specifically sixty myriads passing in it, as above siman 345 seif 7, and see Magen Avraham s.k. 7; and he brought Mishna Berurah's words and also Shulchan Aruch Mechaber already agreed above siman 345 with poskim that we have no reshus ha-rabbim nowadays, for we require sixty myriads passing in it; if so there is no challenge of Mishna Berurah on Mahariv; nevertheless Mahariv's explanation is not as Mishna Berurah understood; and there is practical difference for law — what Mahariv wrote there deals pass-through to reshus ha-rabbim, meaning it has no partitions; unlike Mahariv who deals in most towns that are enclosed for dwelling, only gentiles forbid the Jews; if so the Jews' mavo'ot are pass-through to the gentiles' mavo which are enclosed by a wall — only their law is as pass-through to a courtyard not combined in an eruv, for he wrote it is only removal of residents, meaning gentile residents; and it cannot be it is not enclosed by a wall — perforce without it, even though there is no reshus ha-rabbim nowadays, at least it is karmelit:

(י) And correction is required since open to karmelit; but in unwalled towns open to karmelit — and perforce the reason is not as Mishnah Berurah wrote that it is because we have no reshus ha-rabbim nowadays — nevertheless it is karmelit; and also when open on two sides to karmelit we hold below siman 364 that tzurat hapetach is required on one side:

(כ) And see there seif 3 that even in its crookedness, etc. — from this it is also proved Mahariv's explanation is not as Mishna Berurah wrote, for nowadays it is karmelit — nevertheless correction was required in its crookedness, for regarding a crooked mavo there is no difference between pass-through to reshus ha-rabbim or to karmelit; and how did Mahariv write that for this reason tzurat hapetach is not required in its crookedness since it is not pass-through to reshus ha-rabbim; and so Taz challenged below siman 364 on Mahariv — he also understood like Mishna Berurah; and why does Taz require for law correction in its crookedness also for us — see there; Eliyah Rabbah, etc. Mahariv's intent in what he wrote that it is not pass-through to reshus ha-rabbim — meaning the city is surrounded by a wall; if so in unwalled cities indeed correction is required in its crookedness also for us, for we have no reshus ha-rabbim; and so Mishna Berurah returns above — for us if it is not wider than ten amot, two pasin suffice:

(ל) Rather he leaned, etc. — as above, he understood Mahari Weil's reason that nowadays there is no reshus ha-rabbim:

(מ) And requires study that I have not seen practiced thus — meaning to make correction in unwalled towns in its bend:

(נ) Nevertheless for this there mavo — meaning and not two pasin suffice if not wider than ten amah as courtyard law; since close to reshus ha-rabbim as Semak wrote, etc. — Rabbeinu Yosef Karo in the name of Hagahot in the name of Rosh, and so it appears in our mavo'ot: if there are no houses and courtyards open into them — two lechayim are required because they are close to reshus ha-rabbim, even though we have no reshus ha-rabbim — we do not distinguish; end. And these are Rema's gloss words seif 27, explained more in Hagahot Rosh first chapter of Eruvin — see there; behold "since they are close to reshus ha-rabbim" they placed on it the stringency of courtyard — if there are no houses and courtyards open into it — two pasin are required, even though in the courtyard itself if its length exceeds its width two pasin are not required even if no houses and courtyards open to it — so Rema was stringent to add because they are close to reshus ha-rabbim the stringencies of mavo open to require tzurat hapetach:

(ס) But its bend, etc. — tzurat hapetach is not required; one may say courtyard law — there is no difference between crooked and straight:

(ע) And see siman 364 that on the other side lechi suffices — meaning in a mavo open on two sides — on one side tzurat hapetach is required; nevertheless on the other side lechi or korah suffices; and if so the same here, since they gave it open-mavo law — therefore tzurat hapetach is required; nevertheless on the other side lechi or korah suffices; but in Terumat HaDeshen's book he questioned: since they were stringent to give it open-mavo law requiring tzurat hapetach since close to reshus ha-rabbim — nevertheless in truth it has courtyard law — and how are we lenient on the other side with lechi or korah, it should require two pasin as courtyard law; and from his words it appears to explain Magen Avraham's words that in truth such is his intent — since tzurat hapetach is not required and two lechayim do not suffice as courtyard law because they were stringent and gave it open-mavo law — if so on the other side tzurat hapetach is not required in any case, for from the mavo side on the other side lechi or korah suffices — perforce we need not give it courtyard stringencies; and if so at least two pasin suffice; however Magen Avraham's language per this explanation is strained and very abbreviated; and perhaps one may say in my humble opinion that Magen Avraham holds that even for leniency they gave it mavo law and on the other side lechi or korah suffices, since Rashba wrote that in a courtyard two pasin are required and lechi does not suffice since there is not abundance of residents using it discreetly, unlike a mavo where there is abundance of residents and no discreet use — therefore lechi suffices; if so since our mavo'ot are close to reshus ha-rabbim — thereby there is no discreet use even if no houses and courtyards open; and in the second version they made tzurat hapetach and on side two lechi or korah suffices as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 25 — see there:

(פ) And Maharil — but we have only Shulchan Aruch's words, etc. — one may say courtyard law; and if so even if they made no correction — nevertheless forbidden to carry vessels that rested in a house within the mavo; nevertheless vessels that rested in the mavo permitted to carry in the mavo, as below siman 372 regarding a courtyard not joined in eruv — in any case permitted to carry in the courtyard vessels that rested in the courtyard:
