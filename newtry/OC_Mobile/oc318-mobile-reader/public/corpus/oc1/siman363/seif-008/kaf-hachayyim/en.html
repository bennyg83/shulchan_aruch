A courtyard that was breached across its full width, etc. For a square courtyard breached on its fourth side, a different rectification is required from that used for an alleyway that is longer than it is wide and open on its fourth side. A courtyard requires a four-handbreadth strip on one side, or two strips of any width, one on each side. An alleyway longer than it is wide and open on its fourth side, however, was permitted with a lechi of any width on one side, as stated below in se'if 3. Bach. The same applies to a courtyard that is longer than it is wide, whose law is that of an alleyway, as stated below in se'if 27. The reason for the distinction between a courtyard and an alleyway is that anything used with greater privacy requires a more substantial partition, as Beis Yosef writes in the name of Rashba. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 3, writes likewise. See below, subsection 164.
