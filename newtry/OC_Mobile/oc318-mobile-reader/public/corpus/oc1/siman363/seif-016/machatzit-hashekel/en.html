(s.k. 16) Enough, etc. — it is nine amot, for each brick is half a brick which is three tefachim length and three tefachim width, and the brick that is its half is one tefach width and half and length three tefachim; thus brick length opposite korah length — eighteen bricks are nine amah; from each side of the korah half an amah remains which is three tefachim; and not precise that in any case something is lacking, for it is impossible that there not be slight space between brick and brick; and since on each side there is only less than three tefachim — even that bit of korah does not exist at all — it is fine, for we say lavud from two directions, as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 17:
