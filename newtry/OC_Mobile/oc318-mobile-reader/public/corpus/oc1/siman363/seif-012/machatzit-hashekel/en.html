(א) (s.k. 12) The extending lechi. Avodat HaGolah wrote, etc. R' Yehuda wrote that this law stems from what is stated daf 9 — R' Yehuda: a mavo they paved with lechayim less less than four tefachim over four amot — meaning they placed lechayim four within the mavo over four amot and between one lechi and its fellow is not four tefachim — we come to the dispute of Rashbag and the Rabbis; for Rashbag who holds that for anything less than four tefachim we say lavud — if so these lechayim are considered as one and there is four amot in its length and it leaves the category of lechi and another lechi is required; but for the Rabbis who hold specifically less than three tefachim we say lavud — for them another lechi is not required; and from this the Avodat HaGolah infers for us who rule less than three tefachim lavud — if so less than three tefachim from one lechi to its fellow, another lechi is required; except R' Yehuda challenged him — for even for Rashbag why need another lechi, let it be permitted through visible from outside and equal from inside; and Rashi explained that the way of those who place lechi is to extend somewhat outward so it is seen inside and outside that it was fixed for lechi (Tosafos disagree and explain another way) and they answer — this is the version of the sugya of R' Yehuda, and R' Yehuda holds visible from outside and equal from inside is not judged as lechi; if so for us who rule visible from outside and equal from inside is judged as lechi — if so the Avodat HaGolah should be challenged why another lechi is required; and R' Yehuda concluded and it is possible the Avodat HaGolah's intent is where the outer lechi was not extended outward at all — except it appears to R' Yehuda to explain that if so the conclusion for practical law is as Magen Avraham; and know that although Shulchan Aruch wrote the extending lechi does not help — visible from outside and equal from inside — and a mavo they paved with lechayim helps visible from outside and equal from inside — the reason is specifically for the extending lechi where one placed the entire lechi at the end of the mavo wall, meaning outside the mavo — if so the entire lechi is visible from outside and equal from inside; Eliyah Rabbah to permit through visible from outside and equal from inside — for this lechi that is visible from outside and equal from inside leaves the category of lechi and has the law of a wall; if so in a mavo they paved with lechayim where all lechayim stand inside, only part of the outer lechi is extended outward — R' Yehuda's explanation is to permit through visible from outside and equal from inside of the outer lechi:
