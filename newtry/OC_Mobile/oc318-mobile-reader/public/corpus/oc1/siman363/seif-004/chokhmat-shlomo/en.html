(Seif 29) A mavo whose one side ends at the sea, etc. See in the Taz what is written regarding frozen water that partitions are nullified and it is worse than garbage heap and is not in the category of a partition at all, as the Rambam wrote explicitly in his commentary chapter 8 of Ohalot that they melt quickly, see what is written there and examine: (there, in the gloss) And there are those who disagree and hold that we are concerned lest the sea rise as a sandbar and there is no partition here. Behold the Taz expanded here in a responsum to forbid in all waters whose way is to freeze, similar to what we are concerned lest the sea rise as a sandbar — and the later rabbis in responsum of Emunat Shmuel and Chacham Tzvi and Knesset Yechezkel disagree and permit; and behold I have no time now to study this through analysis; but in brief it appears it is not similar to the concern lest the sea rise as a sandbar — for there it has no fixed time, therefore it is applicable to forbid on all Sabbaths of the year and on every Shabbat there is concern in this; but regarding freezing of water this is not common except in winter such as Tevet and Shevat and the like, but on other days and especially in summer days their way is not and it is impossible at all that they be frozen — therefore certainly we should not decree on summer days because of winter days since in summer days it is not common that they be frozen; and one cannot say we decree on summer Sabbaths because of winter — from where do we know to decree thus; and behold in truth the essence of the matter is rabbinic and it would be a decree on a decree — therefore we have only what the Sages said lest the sea rise as a sandbar — and that is where on this Shabbat itself there is this concern; but where on those Sabbaths it is not common we need not be concerned thus; one cannot say we decree on those Sabbaths because of others — from where do we have to say thus since we did not find this decree in the Talmud; and also even in winter days whose way is to be frozen and even when already frozen it appears the Taz's proofs are not proof — for all his proofs are from things that are not removed by themselves but only by a person, therefore one may say partitions are nullified; but frozen water behold is destined to melt by themselves and whatever stands by itself — therefore it is as if already done thus, therefore certainly they do not nullify partitions since it stands to melt by itself; and even though regarding irrigation we rule that frozen irrigation water is not considered irrigation water — see above laws of Netilat Yadayim siman 158 — nevertheless this is regarding it in itself, but in any case to be considered ground adjacent to be nullified on ground — in this even something that is not irrigation water if destined to be removed it does not nullify partitions; and if so here too — whatever its end is to melt by itself does not nullify a partition — thus it appears in my humble opinion to be lenient and rely on the view of those who permit, examine well; and see in my Chiburei on Orach Chayim from the year 5601 siman 409 in a responsum to the holy community of Barditchev what is written on this matter further with Hashem's help, examine well; and further I was asked by the great sharp elder R' Yissachar av beit din of Shanstichov in the land of Poland regarding the bridges there, and thus he wrote: I ask one question regarding the bridges that great and mighty lords place and fish are beneath them, also launderers are found there and sometimes people pass beneath the bridge, end of his question. Behold according to me there is no doubt this is not considered a partition — and what helps in this that fish are beneath it and likewise people — this does not help except to nullify a partition through which goats penetrate, but to make a partition it does not help — for in the end since people also pass above, the public comes and nullifies the partition; and further I wonder at what he wrote regarding the ditch belonging to the country — who nullifies it — it is astonishing, as if this nullification requires that it be theirs — the intent is only whatever is breached and the public passes there the partition is nullified by itself and their intent is not needed; and behold in a partition through which goats penetrate — who nullifies the partition, and is there power in hand to nullify the partition — only by itself it is nullified; and also what he wrote regarding the ditch for garbage — I wonder at him how he did not sense, for behold regarding the sea we say lest the sea rise as a sandbar — it is proved that if the sea rises as a sandbar it nullifies the partition even though it is by itself and is not set for garbage — nevertheless if the public can pass there the partition is nullified; and likewise the Taz forbids where it can freeze in winter and the Magen Avraham who disagrees in any case forbids if the sea froze even though it belongs to the country and is not set for garbage — if so here too what difference whether it froze above or a bridge was made above and the public passes on it — whatever the public comes and nullifies the partition; and what difference whether it applies to an individual or the public or the entire country — by itself since the public penetrates it they nullify the partition. And also what he wrote regarding the sides of the bridge being coated with tar and an amaltara placed on them — he brought what is stated in Eruvin daf 11 that amaltara does not help in width — behold this too according to me is not so: one, that what is coated with tar does not have the law of amaltara — amaltara is when there are many designs and flowers; but what is coated with tar does not have the law of amaltara; and further amaltara only helps where there are partitions and lechi or korah only where it is wide more than ten in a manner that it is not like an opening — here regarding this the Talmud is uncertain whether amaltara helps in its width; but here in a bridge since there are no partitions and no korah and lechi — what relevance is amaltara here, especially per the truth amaltara does not help in its width; and also what he wrote regarding amot — whether we require precise or imprecise — already see in the first chapter of Eruvin daf 3 that for eruvin to be stringent we require imprecise; and what the Rema wrote that it is a matter that is not applicable — he challenged from daf 5 — and one may distinguish: in my opinion there is no need for distinction, for are precise or imprecise from this uncertainty — it is not from uncertainty but thus the Sages enacted that at the beginning of the enactment they enacted everywhere to be stringent — this is its measure in imprecise and in precise; but where there is uncertainty then it is an eruv uncertainty and valid, so what I answered:
