Seif 29: In a mavoi that ends on one side at the sea, etc. NB: See the Taz, who wrote regarding waters that freeze that the partition is nullified and that this is worse than straw that stands to be removed, for it is not considered a mechitzah. He relied on the Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah in Ohalot 8, that frozen water is not considered a partition because it quickly melts.
There, in the gloss. "And some disagree and hold that we are concerned lest the sea raise a sandbank, and there is no partition here." NB: The Taz wrote that where the waters freeze it is assur, for the same reason that we are concerned lest the sea raise a sandbank. But I saw that later rabbis, in Emunat Shmuel, Chacham Tzvi, and Knesset Yechezkel, disagreed with him and permitted. I will write briefly what appears to me. The concern that the sea may raise a sandbank has no fixed time; it can occur every Shabbat, and therefore the Sages made that concern. Freezing, however, occurs only in winter and not in summer. We do not decree summer because of winter, particularly where the entire matter is rabbinic and would be a decree upon a decree. Even in winter, and even when the water has actually frozen, the proofs of the Taz are not decisive. Something that stands to be removed by human action is different from frozen water, which will thaw of itself; anything destined to change by itself is considered as though it is already changed, and therefore it does not nullify the mechitzah. See what I wrote at length in my responsum on Orach Chayim from the year 5601 to Barditchev. I later received another responsum from the rabbi R. Yissachar of Shentsikhov in Poland regarding bridges over water, with fish beneath them and people passing underneath. In my view there is no doubt that this is not a mechitzah. People passing through can nullify a mechitzah, but they cannot create one, and a public passage nullifies it. What he wrote regarding the ditch belonging to the government and regarding the possibility that it may be filled is not a reason to permit. Nor is there proof from the sea that freezes, for as written above that case is different, since it will change by itself. What he wrote about an amlatra on the sides of the bridge also does not help for this. Likewise, regarding whether ten tefachim must be exact or may be generous, in matters of eruvin one must be stringent and require the exact measure. And the principle of davar sheyesh lo matirin is not relevant here, for this is not a doubtful issur that will later become permitted, but a fixed measure. This is what appears to me. Examine carefully.
