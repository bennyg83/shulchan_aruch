22) [Se'if 3] An alleyway that has three partitions, etc. If it has only two partitions, its law is explained below at the beginning of siman 364.
23) There. They permitted it with a lechi, etc. This means that although a courtyard requires a strip four handbreadths wide or two strips of any width, an alleyway requires only a lechi of any width. Olas Shabbos, subsection 3. Regarding the reason for the distinction between a courtyard and an alleyway, see above at the end of subsection 8.
24) There. They permitted it with a lechi, etc. There is, Heaven forbid, no concern of transgression in arranging rectifications for alleyways. On the contrary, one who acts promptly is praiseworthy, and there is reason to be astonished at one who can arrange a rectification but does not do so. Were it not for the demands of Torah study, it would be the way of a Torah scholar to arrange it. One whose conscience troubles him about this is either an utter ignoramus or has been infected by heresy. Tashbetz, responsa, volume 2, siman 37; Birkei Yosef, subsection 2. See below, siman 366, subsection 123.
25) There. Of any width. Even less than the width of a finger. Beis Yosef; Bach; Magen Avraham, subsection 2; Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 4; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 6.
26) There. He should position it at the entrance to the alleyway. It should be beside one of the walls. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
27) There. He should position it at the entrance to the alleyway. If he positioned it in the middle of the alleyway, see below, se'if 31.
28) There. Its height is ten handbreadths. These are expansive handbreadths, not precisely measured ones, as stated below in se'if 26.
29) There. Its height is ten handbreadths. Even if the alleyway is more than twenty amos high, it is rendered permitted by a lechi ten handbreadths high. The requirement that it not be higher than twenty amos was stated only regarding a korah, as explained below in se'if 26. See there.
30) There. Gloss: Even if one tied a person there, etc. This discusses a case in which the person was not informed that he had been positioned there as a lechi. He will therefore certainly leave and will not remain there all day, so he must be tied. Magen Avraham, subsection 3. This implies that if he was informed, tying is unnecessary. This is strained, for if so, why undertake this effort when it would be preferable simply to inform him? It appears more likely that informing him is ineffective here, lest he forget and leave, or incline his body so that he is no longer ten handbreadths high. Siman 362, se'if 5, discusses a temporary arrangement, in which case we are not concerned about this. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 7. Eshel Avraham, subsection 3, cites his words. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 5, however, writes that if the person was informed that he was positioned as a lechi, we are not concerned that he will leave. In that case, however, he must be positioned before Shabbos, for a lechi is treated as a partition, and it is forbidden to make a partition from a person on Shabbos unless the person is unaware, as stated in siman 362, se'if 5. See there in se'if 3 and below in se'if 11.
