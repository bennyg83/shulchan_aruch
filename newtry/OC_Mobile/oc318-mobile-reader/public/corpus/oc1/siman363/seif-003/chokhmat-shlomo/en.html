There, seif 26: That a mavoi is permitted by a lechi or a korah, etc., and its width must not be more than ten exact amot. NB: This is the dispute at the end of the first chapter of Eruvin: Abaye holds that the amot of a mavoi and the amot of a sukkah are amot of five tefachim, while Rava holds they are amot of six, but exact. The Rosh wrote there that the halacha follows Rava, because Abaye's statement is established like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, while Rava's statement accords with everyone. I wondered why he needed this proof; in any case the Gemara states explicitly that wherever Abaye and Rava disagree, the halacha follows Rava except for Ya'al Kagam. One cannot say that this rule applies only to Torah law and not rabbinic law. First, here Rava is lenient, so all the more in rabbinic law the halacha should follow Rava leniently. Moreover, within Ya'al Kagam is a lechi that stands by itself, proving that the rule applies even in rabbinic matters. I also saw in the Rosh there, letter 4, that he analyzes whether the halacha follows Rav Yosef, who was Abaye's teacher, or Abaye, which proves that he holds that the rule that the halacha follows the later authorities from Abaye and Rava onward does not include Abaye and Rava themselves. See my work Nedarim Zerizin, siman 217 seif 18, where I brought the opposite in the name of the Bach, and in my work Sefer HaChayim, siman 358. It is astonishing that these words of the early authorities, brought here by the Rosh, escaped them, and this requires study. Later I saw that the Korban Netanel noticed the question I asked on the Rosh, why he needed the reason that Abaye follows only Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and Rava follows everyone, when in any event the halacha follows Rava against Abaye except for Ya'al Kagam, and he was pressed in answering it. In my humble opinion one may defend the Rosh more properly. Rava said, "these are atzavot," and Rashi explained atzavot as exact. According to this, one might say that the ayin in Ya'al Kagam alludes to atzavot, or the mem in Kagam alludes to metzumtzamot, exact, and that in this case the halacha would follow Abaye. It may be that the Gemara had a tradition that the halacha follows Abaye in Ya'al Kagam, but was itself uncertain whether the ayin alludes to atzavot, in which case the halacha would not follow Abaye in the case of an ed zomem, or the reverse, that the ayin alludes to ed zomem while the mem alludes to metzumtzamot and not to a mumar. Since it was uncertain, we rule stringently like Abaye in both of those places, for Abaye is stringent there in both. Thus one might have said that atzavot here is one of the signs of Ya'al Kagam, especially since it is stringent. Therefore the Rosh wrote that here it is reasonable that the halacha does not follow Abaye, since his view is like one tannaitic opinion, and therefore this is not Ya'al Kagam. This resolves it, and it is a better defense of the Rosh than the strained answers, for one who examines them. Examine carefully. Now while on this matter, I find difficult what both Abaye and Rava say, that regarding sukkah, since it is a stringency, the measure is five or is exact. But in Sukkah chapter 3, regarding Rabbi Yehudah's statement that a sukkah is valid only with the four species of the lulav by kal vachomer, the Rabbis said: any derivation whose beginning is stringent and whose end is lenient is not a valid derivation, for if he did not find the four species he would sit idle. This proves that such a stringency is a leniency. So too here, if he has no sukkah except one with a bent wall of exactly four amot, or one exactly twenty amot high, and we say it is invalid and he has no other, he will sit idle. This stringency therefore leads to leniency. How can the Gemara call it a stringency? And if one says that there, since it speaks of Torah law, if by Torah law a sukkah is valid only with the four species, then if he has no other he is exempt from sitting in that sukkah, and therefore it is considered a stringency that leads to leniency; but here it is only rabbinic, so if he has no other he should in truth sit in it, and therefore it is not a stringency that leads to leniency, then it is difficult why the Gemara asks that it is lenient regarding the span of four amot. According to one answer, it does not hold that it refers to most amot; it should have asked more strongly that with height and bent wall too there is a leniency, where if he has no other, whether he must sit in this one or not, we measure by an amah of six. Perforce we must say it refers to most amot. If so, it remains very difficult according to the first version, which does not hold that it refers to most amot. Examine carefully. See also my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5601, in Hilchot Chanukah, siman 671, where I wrote that regarding a korah, as long as part of it is within twenty or close to twenty amot, so that the open space is only twenty, even if, were the upper part removed, the lower part would not suffice and it would fall, it is nevertheless valid, since as it now stands it is like iron rods. But regarding sukkah-covering it is not so; if, were the upper part removed, the lower part would not have more shade than sun, it is invalid, unlike the Ran brought by the Taz in siman 633. See there for the reason, for it is good, with God's help. Examine carefully.
There: If it was higher than twenty and he built a structure under the korah to reduce it below twenty, a structure one tefach wide across the width of the korah suffices, etc. NB: What it says, "higher than twenty," must mean the open space, that the open space was higher than twenty, not the korah, for the Shulchan Aruch ruled above that we learned the open space of a mavoi, as it says explicitly in this seif, "and if its open space was higher than twenty, etc." Since here it speaks where the open space is more than twenty, why does it then write, "and he built a structure under the korah to reduce it below twenty"? It would suffice to reduce it from more than twenty until twenty, where it is valid. From this it is plainly seen that his intent is as the Darkei Moshe wrote in s.k. 7, that it appears from the words of the Rif and Rabbeinu Yehonatan at the beginning of Eruvin that if it was initially above twenty and one comes to reduce it, he must leave the korah also included within the twenty; but if only the korah itself is above twenty, he need not reduce it for that reason. This is why the Shulchan Aruch wrote precisely: first, "if it was higher than twenty," meaning the open space was more than twenty, for otherwise if the open space was not more than twenty, even though the korah lay above twenty, no reduction would be needed. But since the open space is above twenty and must be reduced, it must be reduced below twenty, so that even the open space of twenty is not present, but the korah is within twenty. Nevertheless, it appears that the reduction need not be so great that the entire thickness of the korah is within twenty; it is enough to reduce slightly below twenty so that some part of the korah lies within twenty. The reason is that if the law required the korah itself to be included in the twenty, then the whole korah would need to be within the twenty, for otherwise if you remove the upper part it is like a korah taken away by the wind and would not help. But since we hold that the measure is the open space of the mavoi, by strict law the whole korah could be above twenty. The reason we initially require the korah to be within twenty is only lest one not be exact in measuring and perhaps the open space will be slightly more than twenty, making the korah invalid. Therefore it is proper initially to be precise that the korah itself be within twenty so one not err, and it suffices if some part of the korah lies within twenty; then one cannot err and leave the open space more than twenty. The fact that the thickness of the korah is above twenty does not matter, for in truth the whole korah may be above twenty, since the open space of the mavoi is what we learned, and the concept of removing the upper part does not apply. Thus the Shulchan Aruch was precise and wrote that he built a structure to reduce it below twenty, without writing how much to reduce according to the thickness of the korah; perforce, a minimal reduction suffices, as I have written, that if a small part of the korah is within twenty it is permitted even initially. This resolves what my sharp rabbinic friend R. Yisrael Bick asked against the Darkei Moshe from Eruvin 3b, where the Gemara asks against the view that we learned the open space of the mavoi from the teaching, "How would he do it? He places the korah from the edge of twenty and below." What is the question? Initially we require the korah to be within twenty, and he said that the measure is the open space only with regard to after the fact. According to what I wrote, it is very well resolved. Rashi explained there, "from the edge of twenty and below," that the thickness of the korah should be within twenty. The Gemara therefore asks properly: if the korah itself is included in the twenty, then the entire thickness of the korah must be included; but if we learned the open space of the mavoi and the requirement is only initial, it should suffice that a small part of the korah be within twenty, not the whole thickness. Why require the whole thickness within twenty? When the Gemara answers, "from the edge of twenty and above," it does not mean up to but not including twenty, so that one may initially place the whole korah above twenty. Rather, it means up to and including twenty: the korah begins exactly at the edge of twenty and extends upward, so that part of it lies at the edge of twenty and the rest of its thickness is above twenty. This is exactly our view. Examine carefully. However, I later examined the language of Rabbeinu Yehonatan at the beginning of Eruvin, which is the source of this law of the Darkei Moshe, and he writes explicitly that the entire thickness of the korah must enter to complete twenty amot. Therefore the question of that rabbinic scholar remains in place. But the language of the Rif there also does not bear the Darkei Moshe's understanding, for the Rif writes: "How does he reduce it? He places a korah upon it from twenty and below, so that its open space will be twenty." This wording has no clear explanation at all; even according to the Darkei Moshe it is difficult, for at first it implies that if one reduces it he must initially reduce it below twenty, and afterward it says, "so that its open space will be twenty," implying that he may make the reduction so its open space is twenty. His words contradict themselves immediately. Therefore it appears plainly that a scribal error fell in the Rif, and it should read "from twenty amot and above, so that its open space will be twenty." Rashi too, s.v. kamah de-tzarikh lei, on fol. 4b, writes explicitly that initially it is enough to reduce it so that an open space of twenty remains, unlike the Darkei Moshe. To defend Rabbeinu Yehonatan, and also to resolve the additional question of R. Yisrael Bick, who asked why on fol. 4 the Gemara asks, "If it was higher than twenty amot and he comes to reduce it, how much must he reduce it?" and then asks, "as much as necessary," why not say that the question was how much he must reduce, whether until twenty or more? It appears that according to Rava himself, who holds that we learned the open space of a mavoi, it is permitted even initially to leave twenty as the open space. But since Rabbah holds that the thickness of the korah is also included in the twenty, one may say that Rav Yosef and Abaye, who answer afterward, were themselves the ones who asked this question, as Tosafot write similarly in Bava Metzia chapter 2 regarding "and how much? R. Yitzchak said, a kav in four amot," and in Beitzah 38 and Nedarim. So too here, Rav Yosef and Abaye themselves asked it, and they may hold like Rabbah, that the measure with the korah is twenty. Since they and Rabbah hold so, and even according to Rava the baraita from which the Gemara asks is difficult and requires emendation, therefore although the halacha follows Rava because he is later, and also because korah is rabbinic and the halacha follows the lenient view, that is specifically after the fact. Initially one should be concerned for Rabbah and his support, and see that even the thickness of the korah is within the open space of twenty, to satisfy all opinions. But according to Rava himself, even initially we do not require the korah to be within twenty, and the Gemara properly asks against Rava. This is what appears to me in explaining Rabbeinu Yehonatan's view. Yet since according to Rashi the opposite is proven, and from the Rif too this is proven once the scribal error is corrected, and since this matter is rabbinic, it appears to me that one need not be concerned for it even initially. Examine carefully.
I said to copy here several questions that are necessary for practical halacha, which I answered briefly. I was asked by the great rabbi R. Yoel Deutsch, av beit din of Monasterzyska, regarding the eruvin of that holy community: the tzurat hapetach extends over several roofs of houses in such a way that, in the gaps between the middle houses, one cannot see the lechi beneath the tzurat hapetach from either side, but can see only the rope. Is the eruv valid? He asked because he saw in Tosafot to Eruvin and in the Rosh there, in the passage of Bei Rav, that the people of the mavoi must see the tzurat hapetach, and in this case the people in the middle do not see the whole tzurat hapetach. I answered as follows: there is no concern here. It is certainly enough that they see the upper part of the tzurat hapetach, and since they do not see where it ends, they know that there is a lechi on this side and a lechi on that side. As for the proof he brought from Responsa Panim Meirot siman 144 and from Mekor Chayim that a tzurat hapetach does not help if it stands inside a house, that is not because it is not visible; the reason is that a house is considered as if it is filled, as Mekor Chayim states explicitly. I already resolved the difficulty from the Gemara against that in my earlier novellae. Even if the reason there were because it is not visible, that is specifically where it is not visible at all from outside. Here, since it is at least visible to some people, it is no worse than visible from the inside and flush from the outside, or the reverse, which is judged as a lechi. It is not comparable to a bent mavoi where the tzurat hapetach is not visible at all. Moreover, we do not make any correction at the bend and rely on the fact that our mavo'ot have the status of courtyards, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 363. If so, here too there is no concern at all. He also asked about a case where one lechi does not stand next to the wall but near a porch. If the porch is not ten tefachim high, it appears that it does not help, for the main reason of the Tevuot Shor is that it is a partition through which young goats can pass. If so, in the end the goats can force their way through there and the partition is nullified. Therefore it appears that it should be placed on the porch next to the wall, and then there is no concern at all. This is what I answered. I was also asked about a tzurat hapetach where the pole with the ropes was set up on Shabbat: may one carry even though they had not relied on it before Shabbat? I answered that in my opinion the matter is simple and clear: it is permitted to carry. Reliance before Shabbat is required only for a lechi, where there is no new recognizable feature and no new act now, for it is as it was yesterday; therefore it must have been relied upon before Shabbat. But with a tzurat hapetach, which is recognizable as an entrance, certainly if he set up the lechi and the ropes on it now it is permitted, for one cannot say that they did not rely on it before Shabbat when there is a great recognizable sign in setting it up today. Even if the lechi stood from yesterday and only now they tied the ropes on it, it is also permitted, since we see that it is an entrance and no prior reliance is required. What the Taz wrote at the end of this siman is different: there the rope is not placed on the posts but above them, and it is not an actual tzurat hapetach; we combine it conceptually in place of a lechi. Since it stood there before Shabbat and also is not placed upon it but above it, prior reliance is required. But where he made it anew on Shabbat, even if the rope is above, or where it existed previously and he placed the rope on it in a manner that makes it an actual entrance, certainly prior reliance before Shabbat is not required. This is clear and correct in my humble opinion. Although they wrote that the Peri Megadim was uncertain about this, in my opinion there is no concern or doubt at all; it is clear to me that once he made it like an actual entrance, prior reliance before Shabbat is not required. Examine carefully. I was also asked by the dayan R. Yosef Kostenbrohn of the holy community of Kolomyya regarding new people who recently came and fixed the city's eruvin in this way: they make at the two ends of the thread and cord a wooden ring tied to each end, and each ring rests around the top of the lechi; the top of the lechi protrudes above those rings, and the protrusion is visible to all. The head of each lechi is grooved around its outside, away from the thread, so that the ring can rest there and not fall down. This was the questioner's wording. I answered that he spoke well: this is a tzurat hapetach made from the side, which does not help. In his case, since the groove is on the outside and on the side of the cord there is no groove, the whole thread and cord stand at the side of the lechi and not on top of it. Even if the groove were on the side of the cord, or all around it, since the thread and cord are not on the lechi but on the ring, and the ring is what rests on the lechi, in the end the thread begins only from the side of the lechi. This is a tzurat hapetach made from the side. What difference is there between this side facing the street and this side facing the opposite lechi? Even between the lechis it does not help if it is from the side and not on top. Therefore this is not a repair but a spoilage; whoever adds, diminishes. The main practice is like the custom of the earlier authorities and our custom. This is what I answered. Examine very carefully.
