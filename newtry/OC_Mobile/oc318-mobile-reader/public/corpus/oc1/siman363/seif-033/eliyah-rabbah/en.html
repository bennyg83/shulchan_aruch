[35] And some say, etc. This is the primary ruling, and the Bach rules likewise. The Magen Avraham writes that the concern that a sandbank may form applies specifically to the sea, not ordinary rivers, unless the river commonly dries substantially in summer. If the water freezes in winter, the partition is nullified and carrying is prohibited. The Taz argues at length that since such waters ordinarily freeze, they should always be prohibited, while the Gemara discusses a sea that does not ordinarily freeze. See Magen Avraham, siman 365, seif katan 9.
[36] [Levush] And they will carry from the alleyway, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov writes that the Beit Yosef says this in the name of the Maggid Mishneh, whereas Rashi explains that the concern is carrying within the alleyway itself, and the Beit Yosef and Levush overlooked Rashi. Malbushei Yom Tov suggests that the Maggid Mishneh does not discuss a case where the entire side is sea, nor one wider than ten amot, and therefore was not concerned with carrying within the alleyway. It appears to me, however, that according to the Maggid Mishneh, even if a sloped dry bank remains and itself serves as a partition, as stated at the end of this siman, it is nevertheless prohibited because one may carry between the alleyway and the karmelit. So too the Magen Avraham implies. As for his statement that according to the Maggid Mishneh neither a tzurat hapetach nor lechi or beam helps, only a complete partition, in my humble opinion those corrections do help, because once there is a recognizable marker one will not come to carry improperly.
