(א) That one side is long — and its wall extends to reshus ha-rabbim more than the other wall:
(ב) Even if the side, etc. — four amot — and the same even if less than this, he places only opposite the short side specifically and not diagonally; and the reason is that korah is made for recognition so they should not come to confuse with reshus ha-rabbim; and diagonally there is no recognition — meaning, if he places it diagonally and uses on the long side opposite the long, where there is no side opposite it, the viewer will say it is permitted to use reshus ha-rabbim, because that excess does not appear to be from the mavo; and this is the reason he later permitted in Shulchan Aruch to place the korah inside the mavo diagonally, for there they will not come through this to err and permit use in reshus ha-rabbim:
(ג) He should make tzurat hapetach there diagonally — and use on the short opposite the short and on the long opposite the long; and the reason is that tzurat hapetach is like a complete partition, and a complete partition certainly helps even diagonally, and even if the mavo opening width were more than ten amot, it is also permitted through tzurat hapetach, as at the beginning of siman 366; but if he places one lechi at the long side and one lechi at the short side, they do not help to use diagonally, and even if the diagonal were not more than ten amot, for although lechi is not only because of recognition but because of partition, nevertheless a lechi on the long side does not help to permit use next to it, since there is no partition opposite it, and it is not permitted to use except opposite the lechi standing on the short side, which permits only directly opposite it. However, this is specifically in a mavo permitted by one lechi; but in a courtyard requiring two pasin, or in our mavo'ot that have courtyard law, even up to the short place one is also not permitted to use, since we cannot join to it the other lechi that does not stand opposite it; therefore one must be careful where placing two lechayim to place them level opposite one another and not diagonally [Chayei Adam]; and tzurat hapetach helps even diagonally, as explained.
