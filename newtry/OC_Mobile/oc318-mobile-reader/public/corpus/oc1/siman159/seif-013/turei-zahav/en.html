And one must return it, etc. According to the Rosh and Tur, the reason is that human force is required for every pouring, like Rabbi Yosei, who invalidates a tilted barrel. They compare this to Yoreh Deah, siman 7, regarding slaughter through a wheel. According to the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, who rule like the first Tanna, it is difficult why here each pouring needs renewed force and the initial action is not enough as in seif 10. The Taz answers that when one tilts a barrel, the act of tilting moves all the water in the barrel even if it does not spill at once. By contrast, when removing a spigot, only the water near the hole is moved. The Bach made a different distinction between a vessel that wobbles and rolls while pouring and one that pours in one place, but the Taz does not accept it. A hole with a spigot is valid because it was made originally to be used that way, as the Rosh wrote concerning a vessel full of holes.
