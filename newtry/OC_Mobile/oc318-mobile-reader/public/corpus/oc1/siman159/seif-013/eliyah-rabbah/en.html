Tilted on its side, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov explains that it was already tilted before the water was placed in it; otherwise it would be the first clause, where it is valid. Eliyah Rabbah questions this, for in the first clause he keeps it on his knees, whereas here it flows by itself after the initial tilt and his force has ceased. The words of the Shulchan Aruch can be explained as a barrel that became tilted on its own, not by a person. The Bach's forced explanation that it keeps wobbling and pouring from the person's action is not implied by the rishonim or acharonim. The Rama in Darkei Moshe writes that if he places his hands to stop the flow and then lets the water flow onto his hands, doing this for each pouring, it is permitted even with a tilted barrel, for that is human force.
