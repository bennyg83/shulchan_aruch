And if it was. See the Taz. The Mishnah in Yadayim states: he places a barrel between his knees and washes; he tilts it on its side and washes; a monkey washes the hands; Rabbi Yosei invalidates the last two because human force is required. There are three approaches: the Rambam holds that even the first Tanna requires at least force of giving, such as a monkey or a barrel set flowing by a person; Rabbi Yosei requires actual human force. The Rosh and Tur rule like Rabbi Yosei. The Rashba rules like the first Tanna and follows Halachos Gedolos that even force of giving is not required; the irrigation channel is invalid because it is not a vessel or because the water was already drawn. These approaches explain the apparent contradictions between the seifim.
And now. The Mechaber seems at first to rely on Halachos Gedolos in seif 8 and yet in seif 9 and 10 invalidates a self-tilted barrel. One must say that placing the hands inside a vessel is stronger than a barrel tilted on the ground, or that the Mechaber distinguishes the cases as the Magen Avraham hints. The Taz writes that the Shulchan Aruch requires actual human force, while the case of a monkey is brought as a dispute. This follows Rabbeinu Shimshon's first explanation, not the Bach.
