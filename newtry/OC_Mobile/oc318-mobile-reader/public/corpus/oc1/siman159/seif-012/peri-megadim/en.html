And here there are three views: (1) Rambam in chapter 6 of Berachot laws 13–14 and chapter 1 of Mikvaot law 1, and Tur and Rosh hold that even the first Tanna requires force of pouring at least, like kof and certainly tilting the barrel itself and placing it all day is also considered human force since through one act all the water pours out; and R' Yehudah disputes — no, in the body we require human force, even tilting is not human force. For halachic ruling Rambam ruled like the first Tanna who are the rabbis, and force of pouring at least is required; therefore the arita of dala'i mentioned in seif 7 (Chullin 107a, Rav Papa etc.) — one does not wash, for it is not human force, but rather there is no force of pouring; and certainly to rub one's hands inside the vessel within the vessel is not netilah, for there is no force of pouring at all — not like the case brought in the beginning of the siman, and so Rambam wrote in chapter 1 Mikvaot law 1, and as Beit Yosef wrote in his words, and Rosh and Tur ruled like R' Yehudah; and it is possible they hold the first Tanna does not require force of pouring, and the sugya of arita is like R' Yehudah, and therefore they ruled so. Rashba in Taharat HaBayit house six gate four ruled like the first Tanna that even force of pouring we do not require at all, and ruled like Beit HaGadol in the third and fourth gates that washing inside the vessel is proper netilah, and from this proof we do not require force of pouring at all, see there; and arita of dala'i is not a vessel, and ruled force of the vessel and human force — no, rather ruled force of the bucket which is a vessel.
