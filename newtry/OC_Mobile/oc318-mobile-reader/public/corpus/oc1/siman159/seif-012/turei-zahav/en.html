And if it was tilted etc. — In the mishnah in chapter 3 of Yadayim it states thus: one places a barrel on his knees and washes; and tilts a barrel on its side and washes; and kof washes his hands — R' Yehudah disqualifies these two; he explained: tilting a barrel and kof, because human force is required. Rambam ruled like the first Tanna; Raavad questioned — for Rosh disqualifies arita of dala'i, which is the law mentioned here in seif 7 because human force is required. Beit Yosef answered: like all water — also per the first Tanna human force is required at least initially, only that one does not take water that pours by itself even initially; and this is that the first Tanna validates tilting on its side because initially the pouring was from human force — we do not care that its end is not by a person; but per R' Yehudah all the pouring must be from human force; and therefore he ruled here like the first Tanna: if it was tilted on the ground and by itself pours water — one does not wash from it since there is no human force at all. And in the next seif: if he tilted the barrel and there is at least human force at the beginning of the pouring — it is valid. And in arita of dala'i that Rav Papa disqualified — because there when pouring finished from the bucket to the pipe, then human force ended and regarding the pipe it is as if automatic, since the person takes from the pipe; and this is like the first explanation of Rosh as Beit Yosef wrote. But Tur and Rosh ruled like R' Yehudah — we require specifically human force in all the pouring, and human force initially does not suffice; and accordingly it is difficult why Tur wrote "was tilted on the ground" etc. that it is invalid — it implies if a person tilted it initially it is valid; and per R' Yehudah who disqualifies even in this — for the mishnah deals with this and nevertheless R' Yehudah disqualifies. And it appears to me he mentioned this because of the greatness of the end that he wrote "but if there was a spigot" etc. that it is valid even if initially there was no human force to pour; and one could say he should have written the greatness regarding this matter that even if a person tilted initially it is invalid — we should not err in this since he already wrote at the beginning "a barrel that has water in it, he places on his knees" etc. — it implies only if he holds it the entire time of the washing is it valid, and holding initially alone does not suffice.**** END BLOCK ****
