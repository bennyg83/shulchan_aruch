And if it was tilted, etc. The Mishnah in the third chapter of Yadayim states that one may place a barrel on his knees and wash, tilt a barrel on its side and wash, and have a monkey wash the hands; Rabbi Yosei invalidates the latter two because human force is required. The Rambam rules like the first Tanna. The Raavad challenged him from the irrigation channel, which Rav Pappa invalidates because it lacks human force. Beis Yosef and Kesef Mishneh answer that even the first Tanna requires some initial human force, but not that every moment of the flow be human force; Rabbi Yosei requires human force for the entire pouring. Thus a barrel tilted by a person is valid according to the first Tanna, while one already lying and pouring on its own is invalid. The Tur and Rosh rule like Rabbi Yosei, so actual human force is required throughout. The Taz explains that the Tur mentioned a barrel already tilted in order to teach the contrast with a spigot, where it can be valid even without initial human force if the spigot is operated.
