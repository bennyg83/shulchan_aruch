(51) If he inserted his hands, etc. I will preface an explanation. Most poskim forbid immersing one's hands in a vessel in place of washing because they require human force, meaning the water must reach the hands through a person pouring it. The lenient authorities hold that handwashing does not require the force of a person pouring upon the hands, only that the water be taken from a vessel. They therefore regard immersion in the vessel as equivalent to washing from it.
(52) The washing was ineffective. Even according to those who regard immersion in a vessel as washing, attaching the vessel to the ground is worse, because it then appears one seeks to validate it as immersion in a mikveh, and immersion in drawn water is invalid. See Biur Halachah.
(53) Some say it was effective. Only a reviis of water is required, because according to them it is treated exactly as washing, and they hold that handwashing does not require the force of a pourer.
(54) Others say, etc. This applies even if it contains forty seah, because water must be applied to the hands through the force of a pourer. It is also compared to sanctification of the hands in the Mikdash, where immersing the hands in the laver is invalid.
(55) Under pressing circumstances. Such as when he has no other vessel and this vessel cannot be moved.
(56) He may rely. Taz cites Maharshal, who disagrees and holds that even under pressing circumstances one may not rely on this, because all the poskim disagree. The Gra also implies this. Even one who relies on the Shulchan Aruch under pressing circumstances should not recite a blessing over this washing. Gra'z and Chayei Adam agree, and write that he should eat with his hands wrapped in a cloth.
(57) He washes without a blessing. Perhaps the halachah follows the lenient authorities and he already fulfilled the obligation by swishing his hands in the water for the purpose of washing.
