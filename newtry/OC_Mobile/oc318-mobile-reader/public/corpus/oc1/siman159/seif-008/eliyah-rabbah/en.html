Attached to the ground, etc. This implies even if there are forty seah, and so the Bach explains the Shulchan Aruch. He disagrees and writes that if one already immersed there he should wash again without a blessing, because according to Hagahos Ashri it is immersion, and if there were forty seah it certainly worked according to Halachos Gedolos. Eliyah Rabbah found in Halachos Gedolos that it is called washing and not immersion, and the Taz writes that the text in Hagahos Ashri requiring forty seah is a scribal error and should read even without forty seah. Olas Tamid writes that even a detached vessel later attached is not a vessel for this purpose; it helps only for washing from it, not for swishing one's hands inside it, since that looks like immersion, and immersion in drawn water is ineffective.
And in a pressing circumstance, etc. This implies even without forty seah, as the Taz writes and as appears from Halachos Gedolos. Maharshal ruled that because Rabbeinu Peretz explicitly requires human force, and so do Semag, Rabbeinu Yonah in the name of the Raavad, and the later authorities, even in a pressing circumstance one may not wash inside the vessel; if he did, he must wash again and recite a blessing. Eliyah Rabbah challenges this, for Maharshal himself elsewhere rules that in disputes like Rabbi Yosei against the first Tanna one should wash again but not bless. Eliyah Rabbah therefore explains the concern through the Raah and Bedek HaBayis: even the first Tanna may require at least force of giving from a vessel, so washing inside the vessel is not valid. Still, according to the Shulchan Aruch one may rely on the lenient view in pressing circumstances, and the Magen Avraham implies that then he recites the blessing.
