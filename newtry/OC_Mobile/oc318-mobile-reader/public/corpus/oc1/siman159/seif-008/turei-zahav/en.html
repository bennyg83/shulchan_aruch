One should not wash his hands from those stones. I have room to investigate this: in Agudah chapter HaMocher the house learned this from what Rav Yosef requires regarding fitness of seeds from rainwater that he intended to rinse in them the istrobel — and it is required istrobel that is nullified by the ground regarding sale: per the rabbis who grind — if it were completely nullified like a wall and also attached regarding fitness of seeds, one could say for fitness of seeds it is not considered attached like a wall but specifically in sale because the seller sells with a good eye, and it concludes teiku. And accordingly it appears one should not take water for eating from those stones that have a receiving chamber and are fixed in the wall and have spigots or copper faucets with which one draws (see chapter 5 Parah), end quote; and Rama ruled here to distinguish whether he attached first or not, and it appears he learned thus from what Agudah wrote — see chapter 5 "permitted" meaning if they hollowed first, for there the mishnah wrote the trough in rock deals specifically with attaching first. And it is difficult to me initially why Agudah ruled stringently for netilat yadayim, since it remains teiku in the Gemara whether it is like attached or not, and every doubt in netilat yadayim we are lenient as in mishnah chapter 2 of Yadayim: because they said doubtful hands — to defile and to purify, pure; and similarly below in this siman in Tur and Shulchan Aruch; and the reason is it is a rabbinic doubt, and Beit Yosef brought in the name of Terumat HaDeshen that the same applies in a dispute of rabbis, and similarly Rashal chapter 25 regarding an unresolved question — if so why did he seize it here stringently?
