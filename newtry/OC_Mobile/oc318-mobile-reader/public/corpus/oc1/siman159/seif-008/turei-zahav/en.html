One may not wash his hands from those stones. The Taz raises difficulty with the Agudah's derivation from Bava Basra regarding stones fixed into a wall with receptacles and spouts. Since the Gemara leaves the status unresolved, and doubts in netilas yadayim are generally lenient because the law is D’Rabbanan, why rule stringently? He further asks that the distinction between carving before fixing and fixing before carving depends on the dispute between Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages, and if for mikveh, a D’Rabbanan concern of drawn water, we rule leniently, all the more so by netilas yadayim we should be lenient and consider it a vessel. He leaves the matter requiring study.
