If the vessel is attached to the ground, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah for the reason, which is from Olas Tamid and Perishah, cited by Eliyah Rabbah and Peri Megadim. Olas Tamid writes that Levush implies this applies only when the vessel was attached before it was hollowed out, since it never acquired the status of a vessel. Therefore immersion in it cannot substitute for washing, while immersion itself is ineffective, perhaps because the water initially entered this place through a vessel and is therefore drawn, or because it lacks forty se'ah. If it was hollowed out first and then attached, however, just as one may wash from it through the faucet, as explained above in seif 7, because it is a vessel, one may likewise immerse within it. Although Olas Tamid rejects Levush, I found Biur HaGra explicitly ruling this way. In truth, although the Mechaber's wording somewhat implies the later authorities' explanation, the words of Ramban and Rashba, who are the source of the ruling, are better understood according to Biur HaGra. They did not create a new decree because it appears like immersion. Rather, since an object hollowed out while attached to the ground does not acquire the status of a vessel for washing, its validity would have to come from immersion, and immersion is ineffective because the water is drawn, having been brought by bucket into this vessel. They compare it to bodily immersion, which is ineffective in drawn water. Ritva states this explicitly; see there.
He washes without a blessing - See Mishnah Berurah. Although Taz, citing Maharshal, completely rejects the Mechaber's view and requires him to wash again with a blessing, one should not depart from the Mechaber's ruling. The entire basis of those who disagree is that his view appears contrary to the passage in Chullin, whose plain meaning invalidates washing without human force, as Maharshal writes. According to the explanation above, however, several Rishonim reconcile his view and state that agitating one's hands in the water is itself considered human force. Their citation of his ruling appears to show that they agree with him. Although Eliyah Rabbah cites many Rishonim who disagree, after the fact one certainly should not recite another blessing.
