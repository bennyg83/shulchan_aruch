One may not. See the Taz. The Rambam rules like the Sages that something fixed while detached remains a vessel according to the Torah even after attachment, as by a baker's board in a wall. Regarding mikveh, however, since drawn water is D’Rabbanan, the Sages were lenient if it was fixed and then carved, but not if it was carved and then fixed. The Agudah derived from the unresolved question in Bava Basra that one should not wash from stones fixed in a wall and having receptacles. The Taz asks why we should be stringent in a doubt concerning netilas yadayim, which is D’Rabbanan. Peri Megadim answers that one should not create a doubt initially when other water is available, and the Sages may have given an attached item the status of ground both leniently and stringently. If one carved a receptacle in a mountain, washing from it is certainly not washing at all, though immersion there helps because it is ground. If a detached stone was fixed and afterward carved, immersion may help for netilas yadayim, but if he washed from it he should wash again without a blessing.
