Our law is like a spring — Taz in Beit Yosef in the name of R' Yitzchak of Corbeil: forty se'ah in the body and in vessels; even for impurity d'oraisa forty se'ah are required to cover their bodies, and the rabbis nullified the revi'it of a mikveh; but for netilat yadayim, which is rabbinic, they did not nullify it. In Levush the wording is somewhat strained, for he wrote forty se'ah cover the whole body at once and for hands it is not required — but in vessels too forty se'ah is rabbinic since their impurity is d'oraisa and for netilat yadayim the rabbis were lenient. And immersion of vessels in Yoreh De'ah 120:11 — forty se'ah even for glass is rabbinic forty se'ah that need not be said; and forty se'ah like menstrual blood we require — but what is enough for me in a spring that we require forty se'ah in vessels, here there is room to investigate as I will say. And a hard vision was told to me: what Levush wrote in Yoreh De'ah 201:61 — forty se'ah in a mikveh for both man and vessels with less than forty se'ah, and rabbinically we require forty se'ah, and a spring too is rabbinic, see there — requires much study, for the spring in Beit Yosef Yoreh De'ah 201 and Niddah Taharat HaBayit house seven gate 4 (Ketubot 108a side b) — a mikveh even for a tiny amount requires forty se'ah, and vessels only with a revi'it — this is Torah law in a mikveh, and the rabbis nullified revi'ot for vessels; but a spring purifies with any amount (if his whole body ascends in them) even for a person, and this is the plain meaning of the baraita in Tosefta Kifshuta: a spring in any case and a mikveh in forty se'ah — meaning even if his body ascends in less. And this is Rambam's method in chapter 9 of Mikvaot and Raavad in Sefer Ba'alei HaNefesh (in gate of waters chapter 2 and chapter six), and it is explained there that forty se'ah in a mikveh for a person is forty se'ah, but Rabbeinu Tam, Tur, and Rosh hold that in a spring forty se'ah are required for a person and for vessels all the more so; and the baraita in Tosefta Kifshuta is an asmakhta for vessels — in a mikveh revi'it is nullified and a spring for vessels all the more so; but for a person all agree that in a mikveh forty se'ah are required and a spring is disputed. And the mishnah of an impure person who went down to immerse — doubt regarding measure is impure with impurity d'oraisa; behold forty se'ah in a mikveh at least are d'oraisa — how did Levush write in a mikveh it is rabbinic for a person? And for immersion of vessels of gentiles they are more stringent, and therefore we require forty se'ah in a mikveh in niddah water like niddah, and in a spring per the disputed version. And now per what Levush wrote here in Orach Chayim regarding netilat yadayim in a spring — all the more so rabbinically to be lenient — it implies the view of Rambam and R' Eliezer with Rabbeinu Tam: it is a doubt; and in truth in Yoreh De'ah 201 Levush did not bring at all the view of Rambam and R' Eliezer — it implies there is no doubt (and Rambam conceded to him, and after their ruling nothing remains, as Peri Chadash wrote in Yoreh De'ah regarding custom of Israel). And in netilat yadayim it is not a rabbinic doubt, but for all it is valid per Rambam — one immerses his whole body, and per R' Yehudah even in a mikveh he is lenient. Levush in Yoreh De'ah 120:11 brought: there are those who dispute regarding a spring — that is Tur, and one should be stringent; and now in glass which is rabbinic, per Levush it is a doubt to be lenient; and one could also say that per Rambam and R' Eliezer, niddah in a spring — all the more so — therefore the exposition "like niddah water" applies, and a spring all the more so — therefore rabbinic doubt to be lenient; and it appears that if he immerses afterwards without a berachah, for doubtful berachot we are lenient.
