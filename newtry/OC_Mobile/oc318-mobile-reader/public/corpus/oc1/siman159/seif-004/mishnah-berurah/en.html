(18) He repaired them and made, etc. Meaning, he repaired them by making, etc. Taz asks: leather bottles are made to hold liquids, and this is their original form, like a vessel originally fashioned that way in seif 5. Why is repair required? He answers that without forming a base they do not have a receptacle, because when empty they contain no cavity and merely lie folded upon themselves.
(19) Because they are not made. Meaning, they were not originally made at all to receive liquids.
(20) A felt hat. Meaning what we call kapelushin and yarmulkes, even when made of leather.
(21) Under pressing circumstances they are permitted. This applies when they are stiff and water does not seep through them. Maharshal disagrees and holds that a hat was not made to receive at all and is no better than a sack or basket. Chayei Adam writes that in a very pressing circumstance, such as while traveling with no other vessel, one may wash from it without a blessing, wrapping his hand in a cloth. When the need is not extreme one should be stringent, since travelers nowadays also do not drink from such items. Peri Megadim likewise rules that no blessing should be recited over this washing.
