The Shelah wrote, etc. Through the spout, etc. Although there it speaks of one that protrudes upward, see there, letter kuf, nevertheless, as a matter of law, Magen Avraham holds that since it was not made to receive, it is invalid. See Eliyah Rabbah.
Like a tube. That is to say, it is complete on all sides, and also at the hole through which the water exits it has a spigot. In such a case it is valid, as the Rama wrote in the following se'if. Know that even where it is made in such a manner like a tube, this is only when the spigot is inserted in the mouth of the tube, and at the time of washing he removes the spigot from the mouth of the tube and the water exits onto his hand from the mouth of the tube; if so, the water comes from a vessel. Nevertheless, the spigot must be tight. If it is not tight and drips there, then it is no better than if there were no spigot there. If it is punctured to the point that it admits liquid, it is invalid. Although according to Beis Yosef it is permitted to wash through the hole, nevertheless according to Magen Avraham, who agreed with the Rashba, if it is punctured with a hole that purifies it, even through the hole is forbidden, unless they are of metal, where in such a case the hole does not purify it, since it still performs something like its original function, as written above. Or, the spigot is inserted in the middle of the tube, and when he washes his hands he removes the spigot completely, and the water exits onto his hand through the place where the spigot had been inserted; this too is considered water coming from a vessel. But those in which the spigot is inserted in the middle of the tube, and the spigot also has a hole opposite the hole of the tube, except that all day the solid side of the spigot is opposite the hole of the tube and the water does not exit, while at the time of washing he turns the spigot and the hole of the spigot comes opposite the hole of the tube, so that the water exits through the punctured spigot into the piece of tube above the spigot and then exits onto his hands from the mouth of the tube, in such a case one may not wash his hands from this vessel. That piece of tube above the spigot has no receptacle, and the water does not exit onto his hand from the vessel at all. See further below, se'if 9, and siman 162, se'if 4, in Magen Avraham, s.k. 10.
But when it is made like a pipe, etc. Even if a spigot is inserted into it, nevertheless when he removes the spigot the water first passes over the pipe and from there comes onto his hand. If so, it did not come to his hand from a vessel, for the pipe is not a vessel. This is in the name of Tosafot and the Rosh, that a pipe is not considered a vessel. See s.k. 8 and what is written there.
