(77) In spring water. Meaning, in all naturally flowing waters, even if they run a great distance.
(78) Even if it does not contain, etc. If it contains forty seah, it is obvious that immersion is effective even in other waters that do not flow naturally.
(79) Whenever they can be covered. As with immersion of utensils, where the established halachah is that a spring purifies with any quantity of water, provided the utensil can be entirely covered by it. All the more so does this apply to the Rabbinic requirement of washing hands. Moreover, according to several poskim, even immersion of the body in a spring does not require specifically forty seah, but only enough water to cover the entire body of the person according to his size. Although we do not rule this way, one may at least be lenient regarding washing hands.
(80) At one time. Although it is valid to immerse the hands one at a time, as in seif 18, the quantity of water must be sufficient for both hands to be immersed at one time.
(81) In mikveh water. Meaning, water that does not flow naturally, but collected in one place from rainwater or melted snow. If it is extremely murky, see below, siman 160, seif 9.
(82) Whose law is like that of a spring. For D'Oraisa, even collected water containing a reviis suffices for immersion of utensils, if they are small enough to be entirely covered by it. The Sages required forty seah only as they did for immersion of the body. Regarding Rabbinic washing of hands, they did not impose this stringency. Rabbeinu Yonah.
(83) That it requires forty seah. They hold that once the Sages invalidated immersion in less than forty seah of collected water, it no longer has the status of valid immersion for any purpose. Tosafos.
(84) In accordance with the lenient opinion. Because this is a matter of Rabbinic law.
(85) Initially. After the fact, or in a pressing circumstance, one may be lenient. Chayei Adam. See Biur Halachah.
