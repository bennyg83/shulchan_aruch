Seif 14, if he immersed, etc.; and if, etc.; and some say that it requires, etc. As written in Chullin 106a: now if his whole body, etc.; and in the second chapter of Chagigah regarding holy things they immerse, which shows immersion helps. The Tosefta also speaks of one who immerses his hands, as in seif 19. The opinion that it requires a mikveh follows Rashi there, who holds that it requires the law of a mikveh like the whole body. This is also implied by Keilim, first chapter, Mishnah 7, and by the Rambam, who writes explicitly that if the mikveh does not contain forty seah one may not immerse the hands in it. The opposing view requires study. It appears their reason is that Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah explain the case as fully separated from the mikveh, even without forty seah, like vessels for which a reviis suffices according to the Torah; although the Sages later abolished that leniency for vessels, they did not abolish it for netilas yadayim, which is D’Rabbanan. Proof is brought from the irrigation channel of the buckets, which ordinarily lacks forty seah, and if not for being drawn water, immersion would work there. Even if it is connected to a mikveh, the fact that we are lenient about a slope and about a stream indicates a leniency for hands. The argument from now if his whole body, etc., refers to bitterness of the water and not to the measure. The Tosefta is no proof against this, for it also mentions vessels. A spring, however, suffices with any amount even for a person according to many sources; all the more so for hands. The Beis Yosef writes that since netilas yadayim is D’Rabbanan we follow the lenient view, especially as the Rashba and Rabbeinu Yonah validate even a mikveh with a reviis. The Gra concludes that here all would agree regarding hands, which are no stricter than vessels, and with a spring the original law for vessels was not abolished according to all.
