The Bach, however, writes that when a vessel breaks or splits, the entire vessel is considered a broken vessel. Even if liquid does not enter through the break and it only lets out liquid, it is entirely invalid for washing, even if it holds a revi'is below the split. Since this break purifies it from its impurity, it is a broken vessel and completely invalid. This is the ruling we accept, unlike what appears from the Shulchan Aruch. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, paragraph 14; Olas Tamid, paragraph 3; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 2; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2, cite him. Although the Taz, paragraph 1, writes that the Bach's ruling has no source, and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 12, rules like the Taz, Eliyah Rabbah there partially reconciles the Bach's position; see there. Therefore, a cracked vessel should be permitted like a perforated vessel only in pressing circumstances. So rule Chayei Adam, klal 37, paragraph 7, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Taharos, paragraph 5. One should not recite a blessing over this washing, as written above in paragraph 9. Ben Ish Chai writes the same there. With regard to a glass vessel, even if the crack is extremely fine and does not even let out liquid, one may not wash from it if it can no longer hold hot liquid as it holds cold liquid. Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 11. However, Mishnah Berurah's distinction there between a crack in a copper or metal vessel and one in other vessels does not appear from the unqualified words of the poskim. Their words imply that they discuss every type of vessel. Therefore, one should be stringent even with a metal vessel, permitting it only when no alternative is possible, and should not recite a blessing, as explained above. Examine this carefully.
