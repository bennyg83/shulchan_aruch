(s.k. 14) Some say it counted, and some say specifically when the vessel contains forty se'ah. Hagahos Ashri and the Taz, s.k. 10, wrote that this is a textual error in the gloss, and it should read: even if they do not contain forty se'ah. For the view of Baal Halachos Gedolos, which is the opinion that it counted for him as washing, brings proof from the fact that a verse was needed regarding sanctifying hands and feet from the laver: and they shall wash from it, from it and not inside it. This implies that generally, even if one inserted his hand into a vessel, it is permitted. From this it follows that even with less than forty se'ah it is permitted, for the laver did not contain forty se'ah, and even so a verse was needed, from it and not inside it. This implies that generally, even where there are not forty se'ah, it is permitted according to Baal Halachos Gedolos. From the language of the Magen Avraham it certainly implies that according to Baal Halachos Gedolos it is permitted even without forty se'ah. But Hagahos Ashri is a third opinion and does not derive from the laver, but holds so by reasoning: although a vessel is required, we do not require that the water come by force of the vessel; rather, whether one pours from a vessel onto his hand or inserts his hand into the vessel, it is permitted. Nevertheless, he requires forty se'ah, so that it is somewhat similar to immersion. See Bach. If so, the Taz's question is not difficult at all.
