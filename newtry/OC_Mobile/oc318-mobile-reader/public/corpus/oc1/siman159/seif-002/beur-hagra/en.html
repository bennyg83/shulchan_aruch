In the Tosefta, first chapter, broken earthenware vessels that can hold a reviis may be used for netilas yadayim; and there, a skin flask and a basket, even though defective, may be used. Rabbeinu Shimshon explains that this is provided they hold a reviis, as stated in Chullin there. See also the fifth chapter of Parah, Mishnah 7, regarding a hole below or on the side, and Rabbeinu Shimshon there. Certainly a rag is not a connection to make it into a vessel, as in chapter 8 of Keilim and chapter 9 of Ohalos regarding a broken and plugged beehive. See the Taz at the end of s.k. 1.
But if, etc. This is like the case of a spigot in Terumas HaDeshen. But according to what is written in the gloss to seif 5, and all the more so a vessel, etc., there is no proof from a spigot; so I also found in the Bach. See Rabbeinu Shimshon at the end of the first chapter of Yadayim: if one opened a boiler and washed from it, or washed from a pipe that holds a reviis, his hands are tahor, while Rabbi Yosei says they are tamei. Rabbi Yosei agrees that if one placed a barrel between his knees or under his arms and washed, his hands are tahor. The opening of the boiler means that he made a crack and the water went out, namely after the first force of his opening. The sugya there follows Rabbi Yosei, and we rule like Rabbi Yosei, as written below. This is the meaning of seif 9, that he must return it, etc. Still, we learn that the issue is specifically human force; without that, it is valid, for the first Tanna and Rabbi Yosei dispute human force, as with a pipe. Semag and Hagahos Maimoniyos hold that even through the hole it is forbidden when the hole is large enough to admit liquid; when it is only large enough to let liquid out, it is permitted through the hole. Sefer Mitzvos Katan writes that if it was designated for olives, only a hole large enough to let out an olive matters, but with less than that one may wash through the hole if it holds a reviis below that point. See Beis Yosef. This is also the Rambam, that one may not wash from a vessel broken with a break that purifies it from tumah. So it also appears in Terumas HaDeshen, the Taz, and Magen Avraham. So too from Tosafot brought by Rabbeinu Shimshon in Yadayim, first chapter, Mishnah 2: the bottoms of vessels, etc., may not be used; but if one trimmed or smoothed them and made them into vessels, one may wash from them if they hold a reviis. Although the Beis Yosef strained to explain that trimming means that they can sit unsupported to hold a reviis, the wording does not imply this, and the Magen Avraham already challenged him from the Mishnah in chapter 30 of Keilim.
