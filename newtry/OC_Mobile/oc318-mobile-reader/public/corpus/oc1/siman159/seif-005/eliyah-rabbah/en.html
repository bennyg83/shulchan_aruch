One may wash, etc. Maharshal forbids this, for it is not called a vessel but only a novelty. The Taz likewise writes that a wooden vessel not made from one piece, whose bottom is attached with pitch, may not be used. The Shelah writes that a vessel called a giskan, whose upper part has a protruding spout higher than the body through which the water flows, should not be used for washing through that spout, for that elevated part has no receptacle. The Magen Avraham questions this from the laver, which had spouts, and answers that those were tube-like spouts that received water, but an open half-rounded channel is not a vessel. Eliyah Rabbah adds that on the laver the protrusion was not higher than the vessel, but protruded from the sides at the same level. The Taz writes that if such a vessel has a large hole near its mouth, even if made so initially, one should not wash through its mouth, only through that hole.
In a pressing circumstance, etc. Maharshal is stringent even in a pressing circumstance, and Olas Tamid writes that nevertheless he should not recite another blessing. The Pesikta Zutrasa states that one may not wash from hand to hand even from a river, but should immerse both hands together in the river. The Bach explains that even for words of Torah, if he merely rubs water from one hand to the other he must wash again with a blessing; pouring from one hand to the other helps only when the first hand had already been washed and the water spilled, and he then poured into it again. But if he first received the water into the palm and poured it onto the other hand, it becomes tamei, as the Taz wrote.
For they are, etc. Even according to the opinion at the end of the siman that inserting one's hand into a vessel counts as washing, here attachment to the ground is worse, as explained. The Bach concludes that even if there are forty seah it is invalid. Olas Tamid writes that water drawn from a distance through hollow pipes may not be used directly from the pipes, for it does not come by human force; but one may immerse in the water standing before the pipes, for it is as if connected to the river through the flowing stream. If the flow from the pipes stopped, immersion there is ineffective. Those who disagree may admit this when the pipes are very wide.
