(א) (a) That its initial repair was thus — meaning it was initially made to use it by leaning.

(ב) (b) Full of holes — here from the Mechaber's closing language it implies there is no difference between small holes and large ones, since when one places his finger on it the water does not exit, it is called properly a vessel and valid for netilat yadayim; and accordingly the same applies to our faucet that has only one wide hole below at the water outlet, and when one removes his finger the water comes out abundantly — it is also permitted. But from the Rambam's wording [brought in the Taz] it implies that specifically when the holes from which the water exits are very thin so that even when one removes his finger the water does not exit because of that but only a little with great delay; but when the holes are large it is forbidden, and certainly our faucet is forbidden — see in Beiur Halachah.

(ג) (g) It is permitted to take from it — meaning one removes his finger and the water exits from below. The Acharonim wrote one should not wash his hands from the vessel (called gisakan) via the spout [what they make as half a round], and specifically when the spout is higher than the rim of the vessel and because the vessel does not hold the liquid except up to the rim, therefore from the rim upward there is no vessel on it; but if the spout is level with the rim of the vessel it is permitted to wash also from the spout; and if the spout is lower than the rim of the vessel one washes only via the spout and not from the rim of the vessel, and because the vessel receives water only up to the spout, above that there is no vessel on it, and some are lenient in this. All this is specifically when the spout is like half a round; but if the spout is like a tube (called schnautz) even though it protrudes above the rim of the vessel, it appears from Magen Avraham that it is permitted to wash even via the spout, and some forbid via the spout.

(ד) (d) A vessel that has a spigot — meaning what they call tzepin or what they call handpash, which has a rod from below, and when one wishes to wash from it one pushes the rod upward and the water pours out — only one must be careful to return it after each pouring, as will be explained at the end of seif 69. See in Magen Avraham later siman 162 that when washing from the handpash one must pour a revi'it at once on the hand.

(ה) (h) Below — meaning even on its base nevertheless the name of vessel was not nullified from it by this hole, and it is permitted to wash whether via the spigot or above via the mouth of the barrel.

(ו) (v) Since it was initially made etc. — and the same if a hole was made in a vessel that nullified its status as a vessel and he repaired it there with a spigot for use — via the spigot it is also considered as if it was initially made for this, since now he dedicated the spigot it becomes a vessel thereby [Magen Avraham].

(ז) (z) To receive thereby — meaning via the spigot that one inserts there it holds the water so it will not pour out through the hole; and it is proven from the Acharonim that in an earthenware vessel the spigot must be tight so it will not drip through there, for if it drips a drop it is no better than if there were no spigot at all and even to wash through the hole is forbidden even if it holds a revi'it from the hole downward; and in all other vessels it is permitted to wash through the hole if it holds a revi'it from it downward. And accordingly in our handpash where the spigot is at the edge of the vessel from below, if it is not tight and the water drips through there, it is nullified from being a vessel until it is repaired, and it is forbidden to wash from it.
