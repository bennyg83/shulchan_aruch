And see. The Derishah explains the Tur and Rava as referring to a hole large enough to admit liquid in a vessel designated for foods, which is not purified when used through its mouth but may be used through the hole; however, a vessel designated for liquids that becomes purified even through the hole may not be used even if a reviis remains. Tosafot in Chullin and Niddah distinguish the measures for shards and vessels, and the matter depends on whether the designation is for food, liquids, olives, or ordinary use.
And an earthenware pot. If an earthenware pot was perforated enough to admit liquid and he washed from it, and afterward washed again from a valid vessel, whether he blesses depends on the dispute of Rabbeinu Shimshon, the Rambam, and the Mechaber in siman 160, seif 11. If according to Sefer Mitzvos Katan the first washing was valid, another blessing would be unnecessary; according to the Tur and others, if the vessel was invalid he must wash again. Because of the dispute, the blessing follows the usual rules of doubtful berachos.
Say from here. There are three types of vessels for netilas yadayim: glass, earthenware, and wooden vessels. A broken glass vessel may be invalid even if it holds a reviis, because it scratches the hand and is not considered a usable vessel. Earthenware and wooden vessels depend on their measures and whether they remain fit for reception. A vessel with a hole that invalidates it for its normal use may not be used for washing above that hole; if the remaining part below still holds a reviis and retains the status of a vessel, it may be valid according to some views.
