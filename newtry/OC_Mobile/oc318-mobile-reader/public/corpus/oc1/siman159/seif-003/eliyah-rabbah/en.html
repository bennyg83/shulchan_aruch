A jar stopper, etc. Tosafot in the chapter Kol HaBasar write this. Nachalat Tzvi asks why it differs from a sack or basket, which cannot be repaired for this purpose. If the case is that the stopper was initially fashioned to hold water, why is it different from any vessel whose initial formation was for that use? He suggests that Tosafot may mean that it was initially made to hold water without support, such as by holding it in one's hand while pouring. But the Levush writes that a stopper was not made to hold liquid, which leaves the question why it differs from a sack or basket. In my humble opinion his first question is not difficult. In the case discussed by the Tur, the reason is that this is the vessel's principal use. A stopper, even if it is also capable of holding water, is principally made to cover the mouth of a barrel. The Bach, Taz, and Magen Avraham imply this, and the Levush's words can likewise be understood accordingly. I am also surprised at Nachalat Tzvi's final question on the Levush, because the Semag and Rabbenu Shimshon in chapter 1 of Yadayim write exactly as the Levush does. Rashi explains likewise, and we do not find Tosafot disputing him on this point. It also seems to me that a sack or basket differs because it is never ordinarily used to hold liquid, whereas a stopper is sometimes fashioned so that liquid can be received in it. Torat HaBayit HaAruch, folio 159, proves this. This also appears to be the Tur's intent when he writes that it is not ordinarily made to receive liquid. Nachalat Tzvi's interpretation of Tosafot is also very strained, because if it was originally made to hold water without support it would be like a vessel full of holes.
And to receive, etc. Olas Tamid understood that this repair alone is sufficient. The Bach, however, wrote in the name of Maharshal that initially one should not wash from a barrel cover until it holds a reviis, has a broad base, and is designated for receiving from now on; he concludes that one should be stringent where possible. Eliyah Rabbah objects that Maharshal ruled like the Shulchan Aruch and Levush, and the Bach did not cite this from Maharshal but wrote it on his own. His reasoning is also not compelling, for Rabbeinu Shimshon explained that a cover is invalid because it is not made for use inside but as a barrel cover, but where it is carved to hold a reviis it is valid. Sefer HaTerumah, Mordechai, and Tosafot also imply that if it held a reviis and had a broad base it is valid even without a new designation. Therefore there is no need to be stringent.
