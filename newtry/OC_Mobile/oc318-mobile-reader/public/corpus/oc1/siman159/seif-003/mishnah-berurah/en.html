(12) A vessel that holds, etc. The Tur and Beit Yosef state that the same applies to a fragment of a vessel that holds a reviis without support, such as a barrel split lengthwise that can hold a reviis on its side without support. Some say that even according to them this is permitted only if it was designated for use. Magen Avraham discusses this at length and concludes that one should never be lenient, since a broken vessel is not regarded as a vessel at all. This is also the Gra's view. This applies specifically to the walls of a vessel. If a vessel was broken at the top but can stand on its base without support and hold a reviis, one may use it when no other vessel is available, provided he pours from the broken, lower edge. Several later authorities are stringent regarding the higher edge, because the vessel does not retain water at that height and the water spills through the broken side. If such a vessel later develops even a small hole, merely large enough to release liquid, one should be stringent and no longer use it at all.
(13) A reviis when supported. This is not precise. Even if it holds much more than a reviis only through support, it is invalid only if, without support, the water would spill until less than a reviis remained. If a reviis remains after spilling, it has not lost the status of a vessel.
(14) It is not a vessel. This applies when it was not originally fashioned this way. If it was originally made for use in this manner, it is considered a vessel, as explained below in seif 5. Rather, the case is where part of its base later broke so it cannot stand without support, or where it is like a stopper, which is not considered a vessel even if originally made that way, as explained next.
(15) A stopper, etc. The same applies to pointed vessel covers that cannot hold a reviis without support. They may not be used unless repaired. If they were broad from the outset and hold a reviis, they are valid, because they are sometimes used to receive liquids. Some are stringent unless they were explicitly designated for receiving.
(16) It is pointed. It is pointed below where it sits in the mouth of the barrel, while above, on its back, it has a cavity capable of receiving.
(17) One may not wash from it. Although it was originally made pointed, it is not treated like a vessel originally fashioned for use in that way, which seif 5 permits, because it was not made to receive liquid; its primary purpose is as a cover. Nor is it comparable to the sack and basket in seif 4, where repair does not help even if they can stand without support, because this cover is at least sometimes used to receive liquid.
