A non-Jew and a niddah. Although the Tosefta cited by Rabbeinu Shimshon says that all are valid to give water to the hands, even those who are tamei, and even one who had relations with a niddah, meaning anyone who does not convey tumah by carrying is valid, from which it would follow that a niddah and a non-Jew, who do convey tumah by carrying, are forbidden; Tosafot explain that this applies to terumah or to chullin prepared with the purity standards of terumah. Terumas HaBayis and other poskim write likewise.
And there is one who says, etc. Because the minor lacks understanding, as in the fifth chapter of Gittin regarding young children, and this follows the view that intent is required, as Rabbeinu Shimshon writes in the first chapter, Mishnah 5. In the Tosefta Rabbi Yosei requires the intent of the giver and the washer. Since here it implies that he agrees regarding a deaf-mute, shoteh, and minor, they must be capable of intent; from this was learned that it refers specifically to a child who reached the age of business transactions and has some understanding. This requires study from a shoteh, whose transactions are entirely ineffective even regarding movables. In any case, that is according to Rabbi Yosei; we rule like the first Tanna, for whom one person's intent suffices, as in seif 13, and all the more so according to the other poskim who do not require intent at all.
