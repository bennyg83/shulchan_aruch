(68) A deaf-mute, an imbecile, and a minor. For they are not mentally competent.
(69) An idolater and a niddah. Although for washing hands for terumah it is forbidden to have them pour, because they render the water tamei by carrying it, a niddah D'Oraisa and the others through a Rabbinic decree of tumah, no such decree was made regarding washing for ordinary food. Some explain that nowadays all water is in any event tamei, since we are all tamei through contact with the dead, and therefore this concern was disregarded. The later authorities write that nevertheless, initially it is proper to avoid having them pour the water for washing.
(70) His law is like that of a monkey. This refers to the case explained below, in which some invalidate the washing because such a young child has no understanding at all. Biur HaGra implies that one need not be stringent about this. It is unlike a monkey, from which one may not receive the water because it is not a human being and there is no human force involved. A child, by contrast, is no worse than an imbecile even though he lacks understanding.
