And Rabbeinu Tam permits in this case - Meaning, in the second case, not the first. I nevertheless found a dispute on this point. Levush and Olas Tamid hold that in the first case even Rabbeinu Tam prohibits, even if the other person's hands had previously been purified, meaning washed from a vessel, because for this person there was never any washing from a vessel. Biur HaGra holds that according to Rabbeinu Tam, if the other person's hands had previously been purified, this also helps for the present person, so that the other may pour onto his hand from his cupped hands. Shibolei HaLeket likewise implies this; see there.
A revi'is at one time - See Biur HaGra, who challenges this and holds that according to Rabbeinu Tam it is permitted in every case. Practically, however, there is little difference, since in any event the primary ruling follows those who prohibit, as Biur HaGra himself writes.
