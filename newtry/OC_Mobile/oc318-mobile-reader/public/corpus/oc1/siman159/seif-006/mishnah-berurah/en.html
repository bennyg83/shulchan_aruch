(א) (a) In his cupped hands — and even if his hands were pure.

(ב) (b) And poured from it — meaning after netilah was already finished on one hand, he poured from the water remaining within his handful onto the other hand — therefore it is nothing because the netilah did not come from a vessel. And if he held the hands one under the other and poured onto the upper hand and from it automatically also poured onto the lower hand beneath it, it becomes pure thereby, as will be explained in siman 162 seif 5 that it is one netilah.

(ג) (g) And provided that — for it is explained later in siman 162 that the first waters that one pours on his hand for netilat yadayim themselves become impure by contact with his hands; and if so, if he pours from one hand onto his other hand they will not purify it since the water itself is impure; therefore he wrote that his first hand was pure — then the water he poured on it did not become impure.

(ד) (d) Pure — the Acharonim wrote that Rabbeinu Tam permitted only to pour from this hand to its fellow but not to rub and touch, for if he rubs and touches it even though it is pure, nevertheless it will become impure by contact with its fellow.

(ה) (h) A revi'it at once — and in such a case even though it was impure, his hand becomes pure with one pouring, and the water itself also did not become impure, for a revi'it at once makes it like a mikveh that purifies, and the water too is pure, as will be explained in siman 162; therefore he can pour from this very water that gathered in his handful onto the other hand. However this is specifically if he poured the revi'it on his hand properly, i.e. per the law of netilah on the entire hand inside and outside; for if he poured it all only into his handful it is not proper netilah and his first hand itself is impure and the water also became impure through it.

(ו) (v) It is the essential — and so is the opinion of Shach, not like Rabbeinu Tam [Gra].

(ז) (z) L'chatchila — and Rashal was stringent even b'dieved; and also per Rama it is specifically in a time of pressure, but if water became available to him he should wash again and without a berachah.
