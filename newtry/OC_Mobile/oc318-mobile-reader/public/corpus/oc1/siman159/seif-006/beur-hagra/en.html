And the same applies, etc. This follows from the above. In the Tosefta, chapter 2: four or five may wash side by side and there is no concern for the four matters, namely, perhaps the water became tamei from the hands of the first one, since when all wash at once it is like two hands of one person washed together; perhaps work was done with the water; perhaps it was not taken from a vessel; and perhaps it was taken from less than a reviis. But one who washes and gives to another in his cupped hands has tamei hands, because at the outset he did not wash from a vessel.
And Rabbeinu Tam, etc. From the Tosefta, which says that at the outset he did not, etc., it implies that if he first washed from a vessel, it is acceptable. See Piskei Tosafot at the beginning of Sotah, siman 5. He explains that the latter clause refers to one who first washed not from a vessel. The phrase side by side in the first clause is not precise, for the same applies one after another; but the first opinion holds that it is specifically side by side, for then it is considered like one hand. See siman 162, seif 5, and siman 160, seif 13.
And it is, etc. This is in Bedek HaBayis, for otherwise the water has already become tamei. But according to what was written above, Rabbeinu Tam appears not to distinguish. As in the first clause of the Tosefta, there is no concern that the water became tamei from the hands of the first person. According to Bedek HaBayis, one who gives to another in his cupped hands is likewise acceptable in such a case, as he inferred from the Tosefta. So it also appears from Terumas HaDeshen in the name of Tosafot at the beginning of Sotah and from Piskei Tosafot there.
