One may wash, etc. Maharshal forbids this, for it is not called a vessel but only a novelty. The Taz likewise writes that a wooden vessel not made from one piece, whose bottom is attached with pitch, may not be used. The Shelah writes that a vessel called a giskan, whose upper part has a protruding spout higher than the body through which the water flows, should not be used for washing through that spout, for that elevated part has no receptacle. The Magen Avraham questions this from the laver, which had spouts, and answers that those were tube-like spouts that received water, but an open half-rounded channel is not a vessel. Eliyah Rabbah adds that on the laver the protrusion was not higher than the vessel, but protruded from the sides at the same level. The Taz writes that if such a vessel has a large hole near its mouth, even if made so initially, one should not wash through its mouth, only through that hole.
