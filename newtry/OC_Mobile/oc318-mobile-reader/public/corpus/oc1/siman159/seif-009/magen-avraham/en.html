He places it on his knees. And he moves it with his knees each time the water comes out.
Tilted on the ground. This means that it rolled over by itself. See seif 18.
Through the hole. Meaning that the spigot fell out.
A spigot in the hole. There is proof from the laver, as cited by the Agudah.
Each pouring. Meaning each separate stream, for only the first stream is considered human force. So write Agudah and Mordechai; see Yoreh Deah, siman 7. Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that the entire pouring that comes onto his hands is called human force, and only another person must return the spigot. Magen Avraham says he may not have seen Agudah and Mordechai then, and the law follows them, as in Yoreh Deah, siman 7, an explicit Talmudic passage in Chullin. See siman 162, seif 3.
