Seif 9. “It is tilted on the ground and the water flows.” This requires study. Why did the Mechaber rule without qualification? This applies only according to Rosh, who requires human force, and according to Rambam, who at least requires the force of the one pouring. According to Baal HaTerumos, which is the first dissenting view in seif 8, only the force of a vessel is required. It is obvious that even when the vessel is tilted, the washing is valid if he places his hands near the place from which the water pours. Rashba explicitly writes in Toras HaBayis HaAruch, where he rules like Behag, that a tilted vessel is valid. How, then, could the Mechaber rule here without qualification that the washing is ineffective, while in seif 8 he rules that in pressing circumstances one may rely on Behag? This requires study.
