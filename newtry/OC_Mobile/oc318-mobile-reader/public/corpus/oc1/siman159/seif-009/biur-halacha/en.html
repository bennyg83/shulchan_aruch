And if it was lying on the ground - Rabbi Akiva Eiger leaves the Mechaber's unqualified prohibition here unresolved, for according to the opinion in seif 8 that human force is not required, this case too should be valid, and above the Mechaber ruled that in a pressing circumstance one may rely on that lenient opinion. Rashba, who is the source of that opinion, explicitly permits this case as well. Peri Megadim raises the same difficulty, and Biur HaGra likewise notes that according to that opinion it is permitted. Practically, however, the Mechaber's ruling here appears primary. Mordechai explains that Behag validates immersion within a vessel because agitating one's hands in the vessel is itself considered human force. Ritva writes the same, and I found this also in Eshkol and Rokeach. According to them, Behag does not disagree at all with the other poskim who require human force, in keeping with the plain meaning of Rava's statement in Chullin 107. In the present case, where the water merely flows over his hands, all agree it is prohibited. Rashba is therefore a lone opinion in permitting it. This may also be the Mechaber's reason for ruling stringently without qualification. Although Beit Yosef did not cite Mordechai's position, the Mechaber may have reasoned that agitation within a vessel is superior and wished to be lenient only where Behag explicitly did so. Peri Megadim inclines this way. One should therefore not be lenient, in accordance with the Mechaber's ruling. This is especially so because Maharshal and Biur HaGra are stringent even regarding immersion in a vessel in a pressing circumstance, as explained above, all the more so here. The later authorities likewise rule without qualification.
