(58) He places it on his knees. Meaning, he wishes to wash both hands together and has no one else to pour for him.
(59) And washes from it. He moves it with his knees each time the water is to emerge. Initially this is required, because in this manner it is valid according to all opinions, since each pouring comes directly through his force. Nevertheless, if he tilted it only once at the outset, it is valid after the fact according to the Mechaber's ruling below in seif 10.
(60) Tilted on the ground. Meaning it rolled over by itself, not through a person. If the flow began through a person tilting it, the Mechaber validates it below in seif 10, because it is considered human force.
(61) Through its hole. The hole was made for a spigot, the spigot fell out, and the water flows by itself.
(62) It was ineffective. The water must reach his hands through some giver, not by itself.
(63) It is properly considered, etc. Since the water flows because he removes the stopper.
(64) With each pouring. Meaning, with each separate flow. Only the first flow is considered to come through his force, so he must replace and remove the stopper for every pouring. The later authorities write that one who washes from a vessel fitted with a metal valve, called a rooster or crane, must close and open it several times for each hand, because the stream is narrow and the first flow is insufficient to wash the hand properly.
