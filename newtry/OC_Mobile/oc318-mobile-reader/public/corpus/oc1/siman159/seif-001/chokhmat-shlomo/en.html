Seif 1. "One washes hands only with a vessel, etc." NB: This law is simple, that netilat yadayim requires no less than a revi'it. I was uncertain about a vessel divided into two parts, with a wall inside dividing it, where one side contained half a revi'it and the other side contained half a revi'it; or two vessels joined together in this way, half a revi'it in one and half a revi'it in the other, but pierced with an opening like a shfoferet hanod joining the water together. Does such joining help for netilat yadayim or not? Should we say that if such joining helps for a Torah mikveh, all the more for rabbinic netilat yadayim; or should we say that since Zevachim 22 states explicitly that anything that completes a mikveh completes the kiyor, but does not complete a revi'it, and the Gemara concludes this refers to placing a se'ah and removing a se'ah, where for a mikveh it is valid up to the majority but for netilat yadayim it does not help, and Rashi explains that a revi'it is not important enough to nullify them, perhaps the same applies here: a mikveh is an important measure and joining through shfoferet hanod helps, but a revi'it is not an important measure, so it must be together and not joined through shfoferet hanod. True, it is astonishing that the poskim omitted this law, that placing a se'ah and removing a se'ah does not help for netilat yadayim; this requires study for now. To resolve the above doubt, it appears one can prove from the Gemara in Zevachim itself. The Gemara there analyzes how to explain the statement that anything completing a mikveh completes the kiyor, but does not complete a revi'it. What does it exclude? Why not answer that it means joining through shfoferet hanod? Especially since earlier on that folio it says regarding the kiyor, "where he bored into it," etc., proving that joining helps for the kiyor. The Gemara did not say the practical difference is joining through shfoferet hanod. Perforce, even for netilat yadayim joining through shfoferet hanod helps. However, the Gemara itself remains difficult: from where does it know that regarding placing a se'ah and removing a se'ah, netilat yadayim is more stringent than mikveh, while regarding joining it is not more stringent? This requires study. While on this matter, I saw the Gemara there says that any kiyor that does not contain enough to sanctify four kohanim at once, etc., as it says, "Moshe, Aharon, and his sons shall wash from it." This is seemingly astonishing, since this was said during the seven days of miluim, and there were also Nadav and Avihu, so there were six; how does it say four? Perhaps the main derivation is from the generic phrase "his sons," and the plain meaning of "his sons" is two, so it implies that four kohanim suffice. This still requires study. Examine carefully. The Taz brought a dispute among the poskim about a vessel broken with a hole that purifies it from tumah, whether one may wash from it through the hole. In my humble opinion there is a clear proof for those who permit washing through the hole, that it still has the status of a vessel. This is from an explicit Mishnah in Zevachim 11:5: an earthenware vessel that went outside the curtains is brought back and broken in a sacred place; if it became tamei outside the curtains, he pierces it and brings it in and breaks it in a sacred place. The Rav of Bartenura explains there that "pierces it" means a break that purifies it from its tumah, the size of a small root; it is still a vessel and one fulfills breaking it in the Mikdash. But if it had a large hole that removed its vessel-status, he may not bring it in and break it, for the Torah said, "and the earthenware vessel in which it was cooked shall be broken," meaning that at the time of breaking it must be a vessel, and this one is not a vessel at the time of breaking. This proves explicitly that even after a hole that purifies it from tumah, it still has the name vessel. If so, why may one not wash from it through the hole? If even through the hole one may not wash, perforce it is not a vessel at all; if so, how can he pierce it and then bring it in and break it, since it must still be a vessel? Perforce it is still considered a vessel and one may wash from it through the hole. This is a clear proof in my humble opinion. See my work on Orach Chayim from the year 5589, fol. 64a, where I also noted this proof and elaborated somewhat; see there. Regarding this law, that one washes hands only with a vessel, I will copy here what I wrote, with God's help, in my novellae to the Rambam, Hilchot Bi'at Mikdash 4:6, where the Rambam writes that the tzitz atones for tumat yadayim. The Mishneh LaMelech wrote that in Yerushalmi Yoma chapter 2 there is a question whether the tzitz atones for tumat yadayim, and he did not understand it, since there is no tumat yadayim in the Mikdash, as the Rambam wrote in Hilchot She'ar Avot HaTumah 8. And if the meaning is that he touched sacred foods outside the Mikdash, the tzitz does not atone for tumah of foods. These are his words. In my poverty I could not understand his words at all. First, what is difficult? Although the Rambam rules like our Talmud that there is no tumat yadayim in the Mikdash, perhaps the Yerushalmi disagrees and holds there is tumat yadayim in the Mikdash. And although there is no shevut in the Mikdash, perhaps the Yerushalmi holds that this applies only to Shabbat and not to other issurim; and even if it applies to other issurim, perhaps only to issurim and not to tumah, for we do not derive issur from tumah, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 83, and likewise not tumah from issur. Also, what he wrote, that one cannot say it speaks where he touched foods outside the Mikdash because the tzitz does not atone for tumah of foods, is not decisive. For rabbinic tumah one may say it atones even for foods; this itself is the Yerushalmi's question, whether it is like Torah tumah and the tzitz does not atone for foods, or perhaps rabbinic tumah is lighter and it atones even for foods. But the main intent of the Yerushalmi appears to be that it can occur where he touched wine or flour for menachot outside, and afterward brought them inside for libations or menachot. If so, this is tumah of things brought on the altar, and for that the tzitz atones. The Yerushalmi asks whether the Sages did not strengthen their words more than Torah law, and therefore its law is only like Torah tumah and it atones, or perhaps the Sages strengthened their words more than Torah law and it does not atone for tumat yadayim. The Rambam omitted this question because it is proven in several places that we hold the Sages did not strengthen their words more than Torah law; see Even HaEzer, Beit Shmuel siman 66. Thus there is no doubt: the tzitz atones. This resolves it. The Rambam appears to explain like this second explanation. If he explained the first way, regarding foods, where one may say rabbinic tumah is lighter, he should have brought it. Since he omitted it, it is proven that the Rambam explains like the second interpretation above. Examine carefully; this resolves it.

In the same seif: "And if it was pierced with a hole that admits liquid, etc." NB: See the Taz regarding the dispute of the poskim: some hold that the Gemara's measure of koness mashkeh speaks of an earthenware vessel, while for other vessels the measure is the hole that purifies them from tumah; the Taz concluded that for all vessels the measure is koness mashkeh. In my opinion there is a clear proof for the Taz from Zevachim, chapter Dam Chattat, 95a. The Gemara asks there that regarding an earthenware vessel that went out, the Torah said "vessel," and this is not a vessel; it answers that it was pierced with a small root-sized hole. Rashi emends there that it was pierced with a hole that lets liquid out, etc. This implies that even where it was broken with a break that purifies it from tumah by Torah law, as is proven there in the Gemara, it is nevertheless called a vessel in Torah language regarding sacred vessels, even for an earthenware vessel. If so, since what we require for netilat yadayim to be a vessel is by comparison to the kiyor, and regarding the kiyor it is considered a vessel even if pierced with a break of koness mashkeh, even if it were earthenware it would be a vessel; since it still has the name vessel even when pierced with koness mashkeh, and for kodashim it is considered a vessel, why should it be invalid for netilat yadayim? Perforce, the Gemara's reason that an earthenware vessel is invalid for netilat yadayim is not because this break purifies it from tumah, but because it is not fit for that use for netilat yadayim; therefore it does not have the name vessel for this matter. But for other uses, as a generic vessel, it still has the status of a vessel. If so, the same applies to copper and other vessels: although such a hole does not purify them from tumah, nevertheless they do not have the name vessel for this use. Thus even if we say the reason the Gemara mentions koness mashkeh is because it is a break that purifies from tumah, it remains difficult, for the Gemara explicitly says that even with a purifying break of koness mashkeh, since a small-root hole is certainly koness mashkeh and is proven to purify from tumah, nevertheless it is still called a vessel where a vessel is required. If so, regarding the kiyor it is called a vessel with such a hole; why should it be invalid for netilat yadayim? Perforce, the matter does not depend on the hole that purifies from tumah, but on whether for that use it is considered a vessel. Therefore the same applies to copper and other vessels. This is a clear proof for the Taz. Examine carefully. This is not the place to elaborate; perhaps God will grant me another time to explain this further.
There. Seif 18: "One hand by washing, etc." NB: So it appears in Mishnah Yadayim and at the beginning of Gittin chapter 2. It requires study from Ohalot 18:5: how does one purify a beit haperas? One removes three tefachim from it. If he removed from one half three tefachim and placed it on the other half three tefachim, it is pure. Rabbi Shimon says: even if he removed a tefach and a half from it and placed a tefach and a half on it, it is also pure. The Tosafot Yom Tov explains that Rabbi Shimon holds half by removal and half by placement helps; see there. It is proven that according to the first tanna this does not help, and so we rule, as the Rav of Bartenura and Tosafot Yom Tov wrote there. If so, here too, a person is one body, and there, placing a tefach and a half and removing a tefach and a half is like here where one hand is by pouring and one hand by washing. It is not comparable to the first clause, where he removed from one half and placed on the other half, for there each half is judged independently; it is possible for only one half to be a beit haperas and not the other, so each is judged by itself. But in one person, with one hand, netilah is not applicable that way. It is therefore comparable to placing a tefach and a half and removing a tefach and a half, which does not help. There is an even stronger difficulty from Gittin, where the Gemara asks whether a mound of five tefachim and a partition of five tefachim combine. Why did it not resolve from there? It is a kal vachomer: there, in a rabbinic matter, half removal and half placement does not help; all the more in a Torah matter it should not help. This requires study. Examine carefully.
