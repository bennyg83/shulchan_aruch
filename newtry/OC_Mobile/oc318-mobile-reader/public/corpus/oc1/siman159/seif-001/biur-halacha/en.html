And it must hold a revi'is - One might ask: Is this not obvious, since one must wash his hands with a revi'is? The answer is that the case is where the water comes from remnants of purification, in which case a revi'is is not required, as explained below in siman 160, seif 13. Even so, the vessel itself must be capable of holding a revi'is. Perishah; see there further.
And it is large, etc. - See Mishnah Berurah, which explains that if the hole is only large enough to let liquid out, one may wash through the mouth of the vessel. This is the conclusion of the later authorities, unlike what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag and Semak, who hold that one may wash only through the hole. Their essential position is very difficult to understand. A hole of this size merely lets liquid out and does not produce even a drop-by-drop flow. How could they permit washing this way initially, in light of Magen Avraham below, siman 162, seif katan 5, and Eliyah Rabbah there, who write that when the stream is not poured all at once it resembles washing the hands in separate stages? One should therefore permit this only through the vessel's mouth.
It loses the status of a vessel - This is not limited to an earthenware vessel designated for liquids, regarding which several poskim hold that even for tumah it loses its status through a hole large enough to admit liquid, since it is no longer fit for its intended use. It applies even to other vessels, which according to all opinions do not become tahor through such a hole, because they are valuable and are retained for food uses suited to them, and likewise to earthenware vessels designated for food, whose measure regarding tumah is a hole large enough to let out an olive. Nevertheless, regarding netilas yadayim, they lose the status of a vessel for washing through their mouth once there is a hole large enough to admit liquid. The reason is that at this measure the vessel no longer retains its water at all, since it leaks drop by drop, and is therefore not regarded as a vessel for this purpose. Perishah; see there. Alternatively, since it is now being used with water for washing, it is always judged according to the measure of an earthenware vessel designated for liquids, for it is unfit for water use, as Mateh Yehudah writes. Tosafos, cited by Beit Yosef, write similarly that the same law applies even to a non-earthenware vessel, and it is obvious that the same applies to an earthenware vessel designated for food. This is also implied by the Rif, as the Taz writes. Maharshal, cited by Taz, seif katan 7, likewise rules that in all of them the measure is a hole large enough to admit liquid. Although Semak, cited by the Tur, holds that for a vessel designated for olives the measure is a hole large enough to let out an olive, and Beit Yosef writes that Rambam also holds this way, while Biur HaGra appears to agree with this ruling and holds that a smaller hole is not significant, it is nevertheless obvious that one should not be more lenient than Semak himself. Thus, one may wash only through the hole, provided the vessel holds a revi'is below the hole, but not through its mouth. Biur HaGra also appears to cite Semak only for this point; see there.
