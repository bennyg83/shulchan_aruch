At a hole that admits liquid, etc. If one puts water into the vessel and it drips out drop by drop, that demonstrates that the opening is of the size that admits liquid. Magen Avraham, seif katan 6, writes that if one inserts another object into the hole and thereby seals it, the vessel is considered whole. If he designates a stopper to use there regularly, this is considered part of the vessel's initial repair and is permitted. The Taz, however, writes that sealing helps only for an earthenware vessel and specifically with pitch; see there.
Through the hole it is permitted, etc. This applies specifically to a hole. If the vessel broke and split, then even if liquid does not enter through the break but only exits from it, and even if the vessel holds a reviit below the split and one pours through the broken opening, it may not be used for netilat yadayim. Since such a break removes the vessel from susceptibility to tumah, it has the status of a broken vessel. Bach. The Taz writes that there is no source for this, because it appears that even regarding tumah a split does not invalidate the vessel as long as liquid does not enter through it. It seems that the Bach compares it to a shard of pottery, which is tahor and invalid once liquid leaks out, as the Beit Yosef writes. Although the Taz argues that netilat yadayim is more lenient and a vessel may be valid even if liquid leaks out through a break, he himself concludes that in practice one should be stringent.
