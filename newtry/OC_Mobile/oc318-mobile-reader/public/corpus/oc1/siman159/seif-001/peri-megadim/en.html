And if. See the Taz; in Magen Avraham s.k. 3 I discussed this at length, and here I will explain briefly. Rava in Chullin 106a, the Rambam in Hilchos Berachos 6:11, and Shabbos 95 discuss the measure of vessels for tumah and taharah. For earthenware, wood, bone, leather, and metal vessels, see the Rambam in Hilchos Keilim. The Beis Yosef's discussion of a gistra, a vessel designated to receive drippings, requires analysis: a whole vessel designated for drippings is not thereby purified, whereas a broken vessel may be. The main rule is that one does not bring a shard to support a shard; a whole vessel, even if designated for drippings and perforated only enough to emit liquid, remains tamei and valid for netilas yadayim. The Beis Yosef's two answers can both be true: designation alone may not help for netilas yadayim, but vessels of wood and bone may retain their tumah measure until a pomegranate-sized hole, and the same may apply to washing. Still, according to the Rif's version in Chullin, even a wooden bucket perforated enough to admit liquid is invalid for washing, and the matter remains difficult.
Now. The Taz disputes the Beis Yosef. The Beis Yosef inferred from the Rambam's wording, a vessel that broke, that if a broken piece remains capable of holding a reviis below the hole it may be used for washing, and he included the case of a gistra. The Taz challenges this: if a broken piece is treated as a vessel for washing because it is tamei, then the smaller tumah measures for earthenware shards should also suffice; yet for washing one needs at least a reviis. The Taz therefore distinguishes between a vessel that still retains its form, such as a shortened skin flask or earthenware vessel standing on its bottom, which is valid if it holds a reviis, and detached walls or shards, which even if tamei are not vessels for netilas yadayim. He concludes against the Beis Yosef that for all vessels, wood, bone, metal, or earthenware, a hole large enough to admit liquid invalidates for netilas yadayim, as Rava and Semag imply, while a gistra that is tamei with a smaller measure may still be valid for washing if it is designated for that use.
And regarding what. Regarding the Beis Yosef's requirement that there be a reviis below the hole, the Taz asks how the Rambam could include a gistra, for a shard is not tamei unless designated to receive drippings or another use. If it was designated only for netilas yadayim and not for drippings, a hole large enough to emit liquid would not invalidate it; even if it was designated merely to draw water, as long as it was not designated for drippings, its law must be clarified. The essence is that the tumah status of shards depends on designation and on the type of use, and one cannot automatically derive the law of netilas yadayim from tumah.
And know. The Rambam, in his commentary to Keilim and in Hilchos Keilim, explains the measure for anointing a small person differently. According to his approach, the version in Chullin regarding up to a log must be read differently. A gistra split again is tahor, because they did not say remnants of remnants remain significant. Accordingly, one cannot easily derive from a twice-broken shard to the law of netilas yadayim.
And regarding what. The Beis Yosef's reading of Toras Kohanim and Toras HaAdam is difficult. The Magen Avraham follows the Beis Yosef, but the Taz understands that Toras HaAdam does not pose a difficulty to Sefer HaTerumah. According to the Taz, with earthenware, a hole large enough to admit liquid invalidates even if a reviis remains, whereas if the hole only lets liquid out and a reviis remains below it, one may wash from it. The dispute turns on whether the laws of tumah for shards are being applied directly to netilas yadayim.
And regarding. For a vessel whose side was perforated, see Keilim and Parah. A rag stopper does not make it into a vessel, except for pitch and the like. If a vessel was perforated above but still holds a reviis below, the law depends on whether the hole is at the side or bottom, whether it was stopped in a valid manner, and whether the remaining receptacle is considered a vessel. These are the points discussed by the Taz, the Beis Yosef, and the earlier authorities.
