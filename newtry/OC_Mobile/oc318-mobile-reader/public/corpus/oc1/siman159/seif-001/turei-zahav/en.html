And if it was perforated large enough to admit liquid, etc. The Gemara says that a vessel perforated enough to admit liquid may not be used for netilas yadayim. The Rambam instead wrote that vessels broken with a break that purifies them from tumah may not be used because they are shards. The Beis Yosef explains why the Rambam did not use Rava's language, and infers that the decisive matter is whether the break removes the status of vessel. The Taz challenges the Beis Yosef at length. He argues that a shard's tumah law cannot automatically define its fitness for washing, because washing requires an important vessel that can hold a reviis for that use. He analyzes the measures of earthenware, wood, bone, metal, and glass vessels, the status of a gistra made to catch drippings, and whether designation for olives or other uses helps. His conclusion is that a vessel punctured by a hole large enough to admit liquid is invalid for netilas yadayim, even if for some tumah purposes it might still retain significance; however, if the lower portion itself remains a usable receptacle of a reviis and was made or repaired as a vessel, there is room to validate it according to the Tur and Rosh. This entire discussion explains the disagreement among the Beis Yosef, Tur, Rambam, Semag, and Sefer Mitzvos Katan.
