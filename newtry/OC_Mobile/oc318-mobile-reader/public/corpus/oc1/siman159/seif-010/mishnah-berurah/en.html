(65) If he tilted, etc. Even where another person tilted the barrel and had no intention at all that this should constitute washing that prepares one for eating, it is nevertheless valid, since the person who subsequently washed his hands intended it as washing, as explained below in seif 13, that the intention of either the pourer or the one washing suffices.
(66) And he went and sat down, etc. This is unlike the ruling in seif 9, where only the first stream is considered to come through human force, and for this reason the stopper must be returned for each pouring. There, he performed no act upon the water itself, but merely removed the stopper that prevented the water from flowing. Therefore, only the first stream that emerges immediately upon opening is considered to come through his force. Here, however, he performed an act upon the water itself by setting it in motion, and because of his tilting it continues to pour. So long as it flows from the barrel in a stream, it is always considered to come through his force.
(67) The washing is effective for him. See Biur Halachah, which states that it is preferable to be stringent about this initially.
