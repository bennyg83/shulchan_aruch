His washing is valid. The Mechaber rules this without qualification and cites no dissenting opinion, and the later authorities agree, including Magen Avraham, Eliyah Rabbah, and the other authorities. See what I wrote explaining the Mechaber’s reasoning, for he holds that all opinions agree in this case. The Gra, however, proves that this matter is disputed, for those who prohibit the case of a monkey in seif 12 likewise prohibit this case, since in their view this is not considered human force. These two laws are taught in tractate Yadayim as a dispute between the Sages and Rabbi Yose, as explained in Beit Yosef. Accordingly, those who are stringent there should also be stringent here. Rokeach explicitly rules as the Gra explains, and so does Maharshal. This also appears from the plain meaning of the Mishnah and from Semag and the other Rishonim who prohibit it. It therefore appears proper to be stringent initially.
