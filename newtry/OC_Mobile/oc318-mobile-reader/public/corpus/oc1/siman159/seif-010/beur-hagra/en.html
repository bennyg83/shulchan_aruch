Seif 10, if he tilted. This is the Mishnah there and follows the first Tanna according to the Rambam. See Magen Avraham, who distinguishes between this case and seif 9, where one must return the spigot, saying that even according to the Rambam that is required because he did not act on the body of the water. This does not seem correct, for the first Tanna validates even such a case in the Tosefta. Perhaps the Shulchan Aruch does not rule like the first Tanna of the Tosefta, but like Rabbi Yosei there, since the sugya in Chullin follows him regarding a pipe; this answers Terumas HaDeshen's challenge on the Rambam. There are three views: Halachos Gedolos does not require human force at all, and invalidates the irrigation channel because it is not a vessel, or because it was fixed and then carved. Tosafot and most poskim rule like Rabbi Yosei, so tilting a barrel here, a monkey, and a pipe are invalid unless there is continuing human force, and by a spigot one must restore it. The Rambam disagrees: he rules like the first Tanna with a tilted barrel, a monkey, and a spigot as with the boiler, but agrees regarding the pipe in seif 7 that at least the force of a giver is required.
