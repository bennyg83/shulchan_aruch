The pouring spout is higher and longer than the opening through which the water is put in. It does not have the law of a perforated vessel, and one may use it to wash his hands for a meal. Ketonet Yosef, siman 8. Maharikash likewise writes in his glosses that one may wash from a vessel with two openings in whichever manner he wishes. He says that he wrote this only because he saw people question the vessel called an ibrik in Arabic; see there. Yad Aharon cites him in Hagahot HaTur and writes: this is unlike the Taz, paragraph 7, who forbids washing from an ibrik and brings a proof from a titros, for there is no proof from there, as one who examines the matter will see. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, writes that if the spout of the ibrik is shaped like a half-circle, like an open channel, it is invalid, but if it is enclosed it is valid, in accordance with Maharikash. Halachos Ketanos, volume 2, siman 158, agrees. This also appears to be the view of Mateh Yehudah at the end of this siman. Machazik Berachah, paragraph 4, writes that the established custom in Turkey, Eretz Yisrael, and Egypt is to wash from the copper vessel called an ibrik; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 3, likewise writes that the later authorities agree that a copper vessel with a projecting spout is valid and one may pour from whichever opening he wishes. Beit David, end of siman 72, did not see what the Rambam writes at the end of chapter 4 of Hilchos Beis HaBechirah, that the kiyor had twelve spouts so that all the kohanim engaged in the daily offering could sanctify their hands and feet at once; see there. Beis Menuchah, paragraph 12, writes the same. However, Divrei Menachem in Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 3, writes in the name of his grandfather that an ibrik has two openings, one wide and one narrow, and the narrow opening projects above the wide one. It appears that washing from it is ineffective and the projecting portion must be cut off. Otherwise, one washes from a portion that is not a vessel, for that part can never hold water. Once it is filled to the wide opening, it can hold no more water that would enter the projecting portion. Divrei Menachem agrees with him; see there. Derech HaChaim, paragraph 2, similarly writes that if the spout is higher than the walls of the vessel, it is invalid to wash through the spout; see there. Since the matter is disputed, although Machazik Berachah writes that the custom is lenient, one who fears Heaven should satisfy all opinions. If the spout is higher than the rim, he should pour from the wide opening, which is lower, or cut the spout down until it is lower than the rim and then pour specifically from the spout. If the spout is lower than the rim, he should pour specifically from the spout, because the wide opening above the level of the spout cannot hold water, and he would be washing from a portion that is not a vessel, as explained above.
