If he tilted. Although in seif 9 only the first stream is considered human force, there he did not act on the water itself but merely removed the spigot preventing it from going out. Here, however, he acted on the water itself by tilting the vessel, and because of his tilting it pours; this is called human force. This is not like seif 7, where his force already ceased when the water ran into the pipe. Levush and Lechem Chamudos write this, unlike the forced explanation of the Bach. So it is implied in Chullin 16, where with a pottery wheel even the second rotation is considered human force.
