(s.k. 7) Pure, and Terumas HaDeshen wrote, etc. That is to say, Rabbeinu Tam, who permits where one washed one hand from a vessel and poured from it onto the other hand, holds this so that it should not be difficult from what we rule below in siman 162, se'ifim 4 and 5, that if one washed one hand and rubbed it against the other, the first hand that he washed also becomes impure. Therefore they wrote that specifically the first hand must have been washed from a revi'is, so that the water is pure. Nevertheless, see the Taz, who says that one must pour the revi'is over the entire hand, so that the first hand becomes purified; but if he took the water in his first hand, even if there was a revi'is, nevertheless, since the back of the hand became impure, the water also became impure. But Terumas HaDeshen wrote that even if he placed in the first hand less than a revi'is, nevertheless Rabbeinu Tam holds that one may wash from it onto the second hand. This is not comparable to siman 162, where if he washed one hand it is forbidden to rub it against the other before washing the second, for in siman 162 it is different, since he did not intend with the first pouring to wash anything but one hand. This is unlike the law of Rabbeinu Tam, where by that pouring he intended to wash both hands, namely to place the water in his palms onto his second hand.
That an erring student wrote it, etc., for there is no reason to distinguish.
