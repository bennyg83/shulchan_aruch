(36) One draws water, etc. Meaning, a person draws water from a river in a bucket and pours it into a channel that carries it to fields. Another places his hands in the channel, far from the place of pouring, so the water will flow over them, thinking this counts as washing.
(37) They come through his force. Although washing requires a vessel, because his hands are close to the place where the bucket is poured, the flow is also considered to come from the vessel. Obviously, if the water was poured into the channel from an invalid vessel, such as a perforated one, it is invalid even close to the place of pouring, because it did not come from a vessel, except in the manner explained below.
(38) If he immersed his hands. Meaning, he inserted them into water collected in the channel and seeks to validate them through immersion, as explained below in seif 14, that immersion also helps for hands when one immerses them in a spring or mikveh.
(39) Because they are drawn water. Immersion validates only in water fit for bodily immersion. See below in seif 16, where Raavad validates forty seah of drawn water collected in the ground, though many disagree, as explained there in Biur Halachah.
(40) Outside the channel and draws it along. The minimum continuation needed to remove the status of drawn water is three tefachim. The channel itself does not render it drawn water because the case is where it does not have the status of a vessel, such as where it was not made with four walls to receive water. If the channel itself were a vessel, continuation would not help, because the water is drawn by that very channel and immersion has no relevance.
(41) They drew it along. Although regarding bodily immersion we are stringent when all the water was drawn, even after continuation, for Rabbinic handwashing they relied on the authorities who permit it there.
(42) It is valid. Specifically when it contains forty seah. Even according to the lenient view below in seif 14 that less may suffice, continuation itself is already a leniency, and we do not combine leniencies to that extent. The water must also be collected and not flowing, meaning the end of the channel must be blocked so the water gathers.
(43) Into the groove. Meaning, into the groove of the channel.
(44) Large enough to admit liquid. Above, seif 1, it was explained that when water emerges drop by drop the hole is considered large enough to admit liquid. Some maintain that here it must be somewhat larger, so a visible stream appears, for only then is it connected to the river water. According to the Beit Yosef's citation from Maharik at the beginning of the siman, the required hole is therefore the size of a lentil.
(45) It connects the vessel to the river. The same applies to one in a boat who cannot reach the sea to immerse his hands and has no whole vessel, only one perforated with a hole admitting liquid. He may draw water from the sea and immerse his hands in the vessel, because its water remains connected to the sea through the water dripping from the hole.
(46) Regarding immersion. A narrow descending stream is not considered a connection there at all. Even where mixing can connect bodies of water, a substantial measure is required, not such a thin stream.
(47) Some disagree. Maharshal agrees with this stringent opinion. Nevertheless, under pressing circumstances one may rely on the lenient view, which is held by most poskim. If one immersed his hands relying on the connection of the stream and later had an opportunity to wash properly, he should not bless over the second washing, since according to most poskim he already fulfilled the obligation. Hollow pipes conveying river water do not constitute washing when one places his hands beneath the flow. Even according to those in seif 8 who do not require human force, it is invalid because there is no vessel; the pipes are nothing, since they have no receptacle. Nor may one immerse his hands in the surrounding reservoir after the flow from the pipes has stopped. While the flow continues, however, one may immerse his hands in the reservoir, for such a broad stream is a connection according to all views and it is as though he immerses in the river. The later authorities write that one may not wash directly from a pump because it is not a vessel. This applies when placing the hands near the outlet of the protruding pipe. One may, however, place one hand near the ground and operate the pump with the other to bring water upon it, then switch hands, or have another person operate it. This works because the stream connects the water and it is as though he immerses in the spring itself. If he holds his hands high above the ground, however, it does not help, because it is like immersing in a wave suspended in the air.
(48) Fixed in place. Handwashing requires a vessel. Since these are fixed in the wall, they are considered part of the wall and not vessels, even if made with a receptacle. Peri Megadim suggests that this applies only to washing from the spigot, but if they contain forty seah one may immerse in them, since immersion is permitted in forty seah as explained below in seif 14, and this is not immersion in a vessel because the installation is considered attached. Nevertheless, one should be lenient only where the water was not drawn by means of a vessel.
(49) Initially a vessel, etc. Since it had the status of a vessel while detached, washing from it is considered washing from a vessel.
(50) One may wash from it. Meaning, through a spigot that he removes for each pouring, so that each flow is produced by human force, as explained at the end of seif 9.
