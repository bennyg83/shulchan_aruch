What was written in the name of Maharshal. Maharshal's statement refers to seif 5, where a vessel full of holes is declared valid. He invalidates it by comparison to a wooden vessel, whose tumah measure may be pomegranate-sized and yet for netilas yadayim a hole large enough to admit liquid invalidates. The Taz writes that Maharshal would agree that a vessel made from the outset to stand with support is valid, since it is a complete vessel. In practice, regarding felt hats or leather caps, if the item is whole, can stand without support, and holds a reviis with difficulty, one should wash without the blessing over netilas yadayim; one who wishes to be more stringent should eat with gloves, as in siman 163, seif 8. A giskan with a large decorative hole above may not be used through its mouth.
For example. See the Taz and siman 162, seifim 4 and 5. The custom following Rabbeinu Tam applies where one already washed one hand with a reviis and then pours water from a vessel onto that hand and from it to the other. If another person took water in his cupped hands without washing, he may return the water to the vessel and the other may wash with it, because water not during washing did not become tamei. Whenever we say that one should not do this initially, after the fact, if he has other water, he should wash again without a blessing. Even if he poured a reviis at once onto his hand and water fell below into a vessel, one should not use that water for other hands.
