Seif 7, it is necessary, etc. This is from the Rambam. Although according to him human force is not required, nevertheless the force of a giver is required, as he writes there in halacha 14 regarding a trough, and at the end of Hilchos Mikvaos regarding which giving is by the force of a giver. At the end of the first chapter of Yadayim it is taught that all are valid, etc., and Rabbi Yosei invalidates. Tosafot rule like Rabbi Yosei because the sugya in Chullin regarding an irrigation channel follows him, and the commentators explain the two cases as the monkey and the barrel. The Raah initially explained that Rabbi Yosei refers only to the monkey and the deaf-mute, minor, and shoteh, but then retracted in Bedek HaBayis. Most poskim rule like Rabbi Yosei. Halachos Gedolos, however, ruled that one may wash his hands inside the vessel, since human force is not required, following the first Tanna, as in seif 8. Tosafot explain that the irrigation channel was invalid not because of lack of human force but because it is not a vessel. The Rashba writes similarly and rules like the first Tanna, both because the halacha follows the many against an individual and because the Gemara says, are you particular about a vessel, and does not say, are you particular about human force. The Rambam also rules like the first Tanna, but holds that washing must be from the force of a giver, meaning even a monkey. Beis Yosef explains the tilted barrel accordingly. The Rashba challenges this, arguing that a monkey’s act is not significant like a minor’s act. The Raah defends the Rambam and brings proofs that even the first Tanna requires the force of a giver, though these proofs can be answered; see there.
Therefore a pipe, etc. This is the Gemara in Chullin there.
Even though it is not, etc. Nevertheless the water is still from his force, as stated there. See Turei Zahav 16a and Rabbeinu Yonah.
And if he immersed, etc. Because they are drawn water. This is the version of the Rif there: one may not immerse the hands because the water is drawn. See seif 8.
These words apply, etc., because it is drawn, etc. Although regarding a mikveh we rule like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov in Temurah 18, regarding netilas yadayim we rely on the Sages there, since netilas yadayim is D’Rabbanan and drawn water is also D’Rabbanan, as stated in Bava Basra 66.
Although a stream of liquid, etc. As stated at the end of chapter 8 of Taharos and in chapter 6 of Mikvaos regarding joining mikvaos. See Tosafot in the first chapter of Yevamos, the second chapter of Gittin, and the third chapter of Chagigah, and see Tosafot to Chullin there, s.v. and if, etc., and Niddah 49a, s.v. if, etc.
And some disagree, etc. According to the version of the Rif and Rambam, if the bucket has a hole large enough to admit liquid, one may not wash from it, for it is not a vessel. Accordingly, one may also not immerse in it, like a mikveh. This is the Rosh.
One may not, etc. This is the Tosefta cited by Rabbeinu Shimshon in the first chapter of Yadayim: if one carved out a water channel and made it into a receptacle, even if water later detached it and reattached it, one may not fill from it, may not sanctify with it, and it does not invalidate a mikveh. If he detached it and reattached it and then intended it for use after detachment, one may fill from it, etc., and it invalidates a mikveh. This is like Bava Basra 65b regarding a pipe, and Parah chapter 5 regarding a trough. The same applies to netilas yadayim, for their laws are equated in the first chapter of Yadayim. We likewise rule that something detached and afterward attached is treated as detached for all matters in the first chapter of Chullin.
