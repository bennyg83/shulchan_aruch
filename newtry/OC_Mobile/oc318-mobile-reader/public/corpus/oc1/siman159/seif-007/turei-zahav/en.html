It is considered — and Rashal chapter 25 wrote on this: and it appears to me that although it is considered a vessel for impurity, nevertheless for netilat yadayim we require a proper vessel, for a hole like all water disqualifies even in a wood vessel although it is still a vessel, because the importance of the vessel is nullified — all the more so here where there is no vessel on it for complete use but a mere novelty, end quote. Also in the language of Raavad's hasagah chapter 18 Kelim he called this vessel that is not made perforated but for pleasure; nevertheless if they make it initially to drink from it so, specifically by leaning, it appears Rashal concedes it is valid for netilat yadayim since in any case it is a complete vessel. And know: Rambam chapter 18 Kelim brought this vessel — tigris: although it is perforated and gives out coins it becomes impure, since the water gathers on the sides and they are made to receive, end quote. And he explained there in several places: because the holes are thin and the water does not exit except little by little with great delay, the water gathers on the sides — for if the holes were large the water would not gather and it would be found it was not made for receiving, end quote. I brought this to learn from it regarding those tin vessels placed at a meal to wash hands from them, called gisakan; in some of them above, near the rim on its side, is one hole, and they say they make it for decoration; in any case it is much larger than minimum measure, and I heard say this hole does not disqualify since they initially made it so — and there is no substance in these words, for here too in tigris it was also initially made perforated; rather the reason is because the holes are thin as explained.**** END BLOCK ****
