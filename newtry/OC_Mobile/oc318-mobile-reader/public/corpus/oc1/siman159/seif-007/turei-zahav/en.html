Granted, it is considered a utensil regarding impurity, nevertheless for netilat yadayim we require a significant utensil, for a hole of any size disqualifies even an earthenware vessel, even though it is still a utensil, because the significance of the utensil is nullified; all the more so here, where it does not have the name of a utensil for full use but only for an unusual novelty; end quote. Also in the language of the Raavad's objection in chapter 18 of Kelim he called this vessel one that was not made punctured except for pleasure. Nevertheless, if from the outset they make it to drink from it this way, specifically with support, it appears that Maharshal agrees it is valid for netilat yadayim, since in any case it is a complete utensil. Know that the Rambam in chapter 18 of Kelim brought this utensil as follows: a titros, although it is punctured and lets out small coins, becomes impure, for the water gathers at the sides and they are made to receive; end quote. He explained there in several places: because the holes are thin and the water exits only little by little with great delay, the water gathers at the sides; if the holes were large the water would not gather, and it would not be made for receiving; end quote. I brought this to learn from it regarding those tin vessels placed at meals for washing hands, called a gies-kan. Some of them have one hole above near the mouth on the side, and they say it is made for beauty, but in any case it is much larger than a hole of any size. I heard people say that this hole does not disqualify because they made it so from the outset. There is no substance to these words, for here with the titros the holes were also made from the outset only for pleasure, and nevertheless we require that they be very thin; otherwise it is not called made to receive. If so, this vessel is certainly invalid, for the upper portion where the hole is located is not called made to receive, since there is a large hole there; only below it is made to receive. How, then, can one wash through its mouth, unless he washes through that hole? In this the Rosh and Tur agree that it is invalid for washing, as appears simple to my humble mind. What is written here to be lenient with a felt hat in pressing circumstances, Maharshal wrote: it is not clear to permit it even in pressing circumstances, for it is no better than an upright sack, since a hat is not made to receive at all, etc.; end quote.
(7) Such as where he poured a reviit over it, etc. It appears obvious that this speaks where the water is not gathered inside the palm, but he pours over the whole hand and the water spills away. But if it is gathered inside the hand, it is obvious that it became impure even though it is a reviit, for the back of the hand is impure and renders the inside of the hand impure, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, that the hand should not touch the other in the meantime, etc. All the more so here, where the hand itself was not purified on its back. For this reason too the Rama wrote well that one should be stringent initially, for the masses err and pour water into the palm, and those waters then return and he rubs his other hand with them; this is prohibited according to all views. He must specifically dry before washing his hands a second time properly, for these waters became impure and have no remedy even if he pours more water over them, as below.
