There, "fit to place." Meaning: regarding ritual impurity, the reason a broken earthenware shard can contract impurity is that it is fit to be placed beneath dripping liquid. Therefore its minimum useful size is determined by a hole through which liquid can escape, because one does not place one broken shard beneath another broken shard. Washing the hands is different. The reason a broken vessel is valid for washing is not that it can be placed beneath drips, but because the Sages did not require specifically an intact vessel for washing. They required only that it be capable of holding a revi'it, even if it is a broken vessel. Thus, for this law an intact vessel and a broken vessel are equivalent, and the disqualifying size of the hole is one through which liquid can enter.
