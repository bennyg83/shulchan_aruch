1) [Se'if 1] Only with a flax rope. Although nothing is more suitable for measuring than iron chains, our Sages nevertheless received a tradition that measurement is performed with a flax rope, as it is written in Yechezkel 40, 'with a flax cord in his hand and a measuring rod.' Yerushalmi, Eruvin, chapter 5. Rambam, chapter 28, halachah 11, writes that the reason for using a flax rope is so that it will not stretch excessively.
2) There. Fifty amos long, etc. This is derived from the verse, 'The length of the courtyard was one hundred amos, and the width fifty by fifty.' The apparently extra letter in the expression 'by fifty' teaches that one should measure with a rope fifty amos long. Gemara. This is only a Scriptural support, for techumin are Rabbinic. Even according to the authority who maintains that twelve mil is of Torah origin, measuring with a fifty-amah rope is nevertheless Rabbinic. Mishbetzos Zahav, subsection 1. See Levush and our words at the beginning of siman 397.
3) There. Because it does not stretch properly. Its weight makes it sag and contract in the middle, thereby shortening the measurement of the techum. Gemara and Rashi's explanation.
