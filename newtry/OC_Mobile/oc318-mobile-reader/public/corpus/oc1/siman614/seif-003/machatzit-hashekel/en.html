(Subparagraph 3) And it is permitted, etc. For sometimes, etc. From the language of the Shulchan Aruch, and also from Terumas ha-Deshen responsum 149, it implies that this is permitted for every person. The Terumas ha-Deshen also cites the words of the Mordechai mentioned above. It must be that the Mordechai's intent is also to permit it for everyone, because some people have pain, and for some people the cold is difficult by their nature. Therefore it is permitted to everyone, for presumably one who stands on cushions needs this and does not intend pleasure.
And at the time, etc. On anything. This means even cushions of cloth.
See the beginning of siman 90, that it is forbidden to pray on a place that is three handbreadths high because he appears arrogant. But when he stands on cushions, even if they are not three handbreadths high, he appears arrogant.
