Se'if 4. And if rain fell, etc., and he is delicate, etc. This is from the Yerushalmi in Yoma there and Ta'anis there: Rabbi Yitzchak bar Nachmani went up to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi on the night of the great fast. He went out to him wearing his soles. He said to him: What is this? He said to him: I am delicate, etc.; see there. From here there is all the more reason for one who has a wound. And even if we say that wearing shoes is forbidden even when it is not for pleasure, as with anointing, nevertheless in such a case it is permitted. The Rashba wrote in Yevamos there that this is specifically because of danger, for a delicate person, as is written in the Yerushalmi there: one of Rabbi Mana's students ruled for one of the household of the Nasi's relatives to wear soles. He said to him: From where did you get this? He said to him: From Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. He said to him: Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi was delicate. And in the Tosefta, last chapter of Yoma: A person may not wear a sandal and stroll in the house, even from bed to bed. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel permits it. And so Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel would say: if his feet were soiled with mud or excrement, he may rinse them with water so that they should not soil his clothes. That is to say, for this reason he also permits wearing shoes in such a case. The Rashba wrote there that what it says there, "delicate," means one who is chilled and in danger because of the cold, just as Rabbi Eliezer permitted it for a woman after childbirth. See Magen Avraham, that this is unlike the Levush. And he wrote there that that student initially thought to permit because it is a shoe without a heel, and he said to him that this is not so, and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi went out because of danger. See the Rashba there and Magen Avraham subparagraph 5. And what he wrote, "the explanation is a person," etc., and "if they became soiled," etc., all of this the Rashba wrote there explicitly in his explanation of the Yerushalmi and the Tosefta, as above. And what he wrote, that not every person, etc., see the Rosh at the end of the second chapter of Berachos and in Yoreh De'ah siman 381, se'if 3.
