Delicate - This means a person who is susceptible to cold. But one who is not delicate should go barefoot and take care not to place his feet in rainwater. If his feet became dirty, he may afterward rinse them (Mahari Weil, Maggid Mishneh, Nimukei Yosef, Perek Mitzvas Chalitzah, Bach). The Levush explained that it means one who would suffer if he did not wear shoes. It requires study on what people now rely to be lenient, for not everyone can say, "I am delicate," as is written in siman 640, se'if 4, and in Choshen Mishpat siman 155, se'if 41. This is especially so since they wear cloth socks, and therefore they do not have so much suffering from walking in mud and mire. Therefore one should protest if he knows they will accept it.
His shoes - In the Beis Yosef it implies that specifically a sandal without a heel is permitted, but not a shoe. In the Shelah he wrote that one should reverse the right to the left and the left to the right.
To his place - And he should send them home, or leave them in a concealed place in the synagogue (Darkei Moshe, Mahari Weil, Shelah). They say in the Gemara that it is permitted to pass through water when one goes to his teacher on Shabbos, for there is the concern of shoes. If so, learn from this that on Yom Kippur it is forbidden to go out wearing shoes even in order to come by way; see siman 554, se'if 17. And possibly even among non-Jews it is forbidden, although there is concern that they will mock him, since some say it is a Torah prohibition. And there, aside from this, there are authorities who forbid it. Therefore one should be stringent.
