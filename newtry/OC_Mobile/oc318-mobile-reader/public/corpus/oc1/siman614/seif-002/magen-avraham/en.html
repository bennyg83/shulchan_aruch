It is forbidden to wear shoes - One removes his shoes before daytime ends and goes barefoot to the synagogue (Shibbolei ha-Leket, Mahari Weil in Darkei Moshe, and Sefer Chasidim siman 762). The Bach wrote: I saw my teachers go completely barefoot, and this is correct. Even one who is delicate and goes in cloth socks should not walk in an alley that has no eruv, for they are a burden; see there. But in the Ran on Yoma he wrote to be lenient; see there. And this is the practice.
And it is permitted to stand - For sometimes a person has pain in his foot, and there are people for whom cold is difficult (Mordechai there). But while praying the Amidah it is forbidden to stand on anything (Mateh Moshe and Bach in the name of Mahara Ashkenazi). The reason is that one appears arrogant when standing on cushions (Levush, siman 619). See the beginning of siman 90.
Nevertheless, one who is stringent - But Radbaz, siman 177, wrote that there is no concern here at all, even with straw.
