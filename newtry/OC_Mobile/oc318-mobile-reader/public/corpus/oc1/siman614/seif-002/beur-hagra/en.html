Se'if 2. A sandal. This is the Mishnah there.
Or, etc. This is the Gemara there: "and they agree, etc.," for according to everyone, etc. But in Yevamos, it is specifically leather, or wood covered with leather, like Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri at the end of chapter 6 of Shabbos. This is what he wrote: "or, etc., even," meaning, if it is of leather or of wood covered with leather, as he wrote below: "even of, etc." See siman 301, se'if 16, and what is written there.
Even of wood, etc. The view of Rashi is that Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosi disagree regarding the amputee's wooden leg, whether it is a shoe or not a shoe, as is written in the sugya in Shabbos and the sugya in Yevamos. And although in Yoma Rava said that according to everyone it is a shoe, the Baal ha-Maor wrote that that passage disagrees with the others. Although Rava himself said in Yevamos, "from the first clause, Rabbi Meir," he said that according to the reasoning of the earlier amoraim. The Baal ha-Maor wrote that since it is a dispute, we adopt stringency in all respects: for chalitzah, that it is not a shoe, and for Yom Kippur, that it is a shoe, unlike Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, who went out with a sandal of cork. But this is difficult, for Rava went out with diblei. Rashi there was careful about this and wrote in the comment beginning "a shoe, etc.": "but, etc." The view of Tosafos is that the sugyos do not disagree, and their dispute is whether we decree lest it slip off, as written in Yoma. And what it says in Shabbos there, that Shmuel said, "it is Rabbi Meir," also depends on this: if it falls off, it is not fit for chalitzah, as they said, "an adult who cannot walk, etc." And what is stated in Yevamos there about covered with leather, and the comment beginning "this is the reason," is because when covered with leather it is tightened. But there is a difficulty from what is stated in Yevamos there regarding a cloth anpilya: "we have reached the Rabbis." Surely it is tightened. They answered that certainly the questioner thought they disagreed about whether it is a shoe or not a shoe, for if not, the difficulty would be: to whom will you assign an anpilya? But after Rava answered that even Rabbi Meir agrees regarding an anpilya, this is no longer necessary. Even so, in Tosafos in Yoma and in the Rashba they answered that that question and that answer in Yevamos are all according to Abaye, who said in Yoma that they disagree about this. Therefore Rava came and answered, "from the first clause, Rabbi Meir," since it is impossible to say that an anpilya follows only the Rabbis, as above. The view of the Ramban is that Shmuel and Rav Huna in Shabbos hold like Abaye, that they disagree whether it is a shoe, but Rava, who said there "who is not, etc.," follows his own view in Yoma. And what is stated in Yoma, "according to everyone," means Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yosi, but the Rabbis disagree with them, namely Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri's Rabbis, who did not agree with him; and we rule like them. Therefore Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi and all these amoraim went out, and Rava himself in Yoma there as well. So too the sugya in Yevamos says that leather specifically is required, and "I shod you with tachash," and they ask there about straps of hair, etc. And what is stated in Yevamos, "from the first clause, Rabbi Meir," is not precise, for the same is true of Rabbi Yosi; it is only to exclude the Rabbis, as is stated there, "the Sifra is the Rabbis," meaning the Rabbis mentioned above. Since Rabbi Meir is mentioned explicitly there, it stated Rabbi Meir. And so what is stated there about covered with leather, and the comment beginning "this is Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri," and we rule like him, as above. The view of the Rashba, from the fact that Rava in Shabbos spoke on Rav Huna's statement and not on Shmuel's statement, is that even Rabbi Yosi is included. One must therefore say as Tosafos said, that Rabbi Yosi forbids chalitzah because it falls off unless it is covered with leather, as above. But with a lime-burners' sandal, since it is of straw, it is well tightened; and even according to that explanation that it is of wood, nevertheless it has an inside and does not fall off. Therefore he needed to establish it like Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as the Ramban said and as above. With this it is resolved how they went out with them according to Rav Huna, for one might ask that it slips off, and we rule like Rabbi Yosi, and we do not find that Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri disagrees with him about this. But according to what I wrote it is fine, because Rabbi Yosi forbade only a sandal, specifically one of wood. Similarly, Abaye, who established their dispute as whether it is a shoe, also holds that the reason is that we decree lest it fall off, except that with a garment we do not decree, for if so we would decree about every garment. Rather, according to everyone we hold that we decree, but they disagree whether it is a shoe. Yet with something that is tightened, even though it is not a shoe, it is permitted. Therefore Abaye went out with dehutzei. Rava holds that they disagree even about a shoe. One should not ask: in any event, what does the Gemara ask there from "and they agree, etc.," forcing Abaye there into a difficult answer? According to Abaye, whether according to Rabbi Meir or according to Rabbi Yosi, it is forbidden to go out into the public domain on Yom Kippur. One can say that since it said "and they agree," it implies for one and the same reason. Therefore Abaye said it is because of pads, and Rava said it is because of a shoe. With this it is understandable that in Yevamos there it says that the amputee's wooden leg is specifically Rabbi Meir and not Rabbi Yosi, but one of cork or bast is even according to Rabbi Yosi, and an anpilya is only according to the Rabbis, namely Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, as above. We rule like him. The practical difference is that with cork and the like it is permitted to go out into the public domain on Shabbos and on Yom Kippur. Nevertheless, the entire proof of the Ramban and the Rashba from Rava's explanation in Shabbos is not compelling, for in the Yerushalmi in Yevamos there they said: Rabbi Elazar in the name of Rabbi Yosi said, even if it is entirely of wood, it is valid. The Mishnah supports Rabbi Yosi: a sandal of straw is susceptible to midras impurity and a woman performs chalitzah with it, the words of Rabbi Akiva; but the Sages did not agree with him regarding midras. Therefore Rava in Shabbos turned aside from Rav Huna's answer. According to this, for everyone it is a shoe, and as for Rava going out with them, one can say as Rashi wrote, as above. See Milchamos there.
But of reed-grass, etc. There: Rabbi Yehoshua went out with rahitni. The Rosh wrote that it is a type of reed-grass; and some explain it as straw, etc.
Or of cloth. There: Rabbah bar Rav Huna wrapped a cloth, etc. And in Yevamos 102b: Does this mean that an anpilya is not a garment? But raise a contradiction: "whether a shoe, a sandal, or an anpilya, etc." Rabbi said: it is not difficult; here, etc. The same is also implied, etc. And in the Yerushalmi there and there: there is a tanna who teaches that one may go out with an anpilya on Yom Kippur, and there is a tanna who teaches that one may not go out. Rabbi Chanina said: the one who says one may go out speaks of a cloth anpilya; the one who says one may not go out speaks of a leather anpilya.
Or of other, etc. This is from Yoma there.
Even to go out, etc. That is, although in Yevamos there they said, "but he may stroll with an anpilya inside his house," this is not precise. Rather, it was stated because of the first clause, which says, "he may not stroll," and it used the ordinary case: one who wishes to stroll inside his house and is concerned that his feet not become soiled, such as to the privy and the like. For if not, the Gemara in Yoma there should have answered that what it says "and they agree, etc." refers to the public domain. And similarly, what it says there, "he went out with it, etc.," implies even into the public domain. So write the Rosh there and the Rashba in chapter 12 of Yevamos; see there. And although in the Tosefta at the beginning of the last chapter of Yoma it teaches, "even with a cloth anpilya," that Tosefta disagrees and follows Rabbi Meir; and the sugya in Yevamos there follows Abaye, that according to Rabbi Meir even a cloth anpilya is forbidden. The Rashba wrote there that that Tosefta of Yoma mentioned above is corrupt, since it was not brought either in the Gemara or in the Yerushalmi; see there.
And it is permitted, etc. The Terumas ha-Deshen learned this from what is written in the Or Zarua, that it is permitted to go out with a torn shoe. Presumably this means torn above, and all the more so in this case. What the Or Zarua wrote is in the Yerushalmi, chapter 3 of Megillah, halachah 2: this shows that Antoninus converted, etc.; and there are statements that say Antoninus did not convert, for they saw him go out with a deficient shoe on Yom Kippur. What do you hear from this? Even God-fearing people go out in such a thing.
Nevertheless There he was uncertain about the language of the Mordechai and wanted to infer from his wording that it is forbidden. See Magen Avraham.
