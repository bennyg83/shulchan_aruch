(Subparagraph 2) It is forbidden, etc. And they go to the synagogue barefoot. The Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Maharil the reason, in these words: He should remove his shoes at home and not in the synagogue, because it is disrespectful to the synagogue to leave shoes there, end quote. See Magen Avraham subparagraph 7.
And the Bach wrote: I saw my teachers go completely barefoot. That is because they were concerned to be stringent like the authorities who hold that even a cloth shoe is considered a shoe with regard to Yom Kippur, since in any event it protects. And regarding cork or straw they were stringent like the view of the Baal ha-Maor, who holds that even one of cork or straw is considered a shoe. There is also concern for Rashi's view: although Rashi permits one made of cloth and one made of cork, nevertheless regarding one made of wood he indicates that it is forbidden. In the glosses to the Rif he wrote that one made of cork is permitted specifically when it is moist, but when it is dry it is forbidden, for it is like wood, which according to Rashi is forbidden. See the Bach there. For it is a burden. This is the view of the Ra'ah, cited by the Nimukei Yosef in Yevamos, Perek Mitzvas Chalitzah. This is also the view of the Ramban, cited by the Ran in Yoma: those who wear cloth socks on Yom Kippur must necessarily hold that it is not a shoe. And it is proved in the Gemara that anything that is not a shoe is a burden, and it is forbidden to go out with it into the public domain or into a courtyard that has no eruv, for we rule that there is an eruv and carrying on Yom Kippur. But the Ran disagrees and writes that specifically on Shabbos, when wearing leather shoes is permitted, it is not the practice to wear cloth socks, cork shoes, and the like, and therefore they are a burden. But on Yom Kippur, when it is forbidden to wear shoes and people's practice is to go out with those types, they are not a burden; rather, on that day they are like a shoe, end quote of the Ran. In any event, from the words of all of them we have learned that on an ordinary Shabbos it is forbidden to go out into a courtyard that has no eruv with a cloth shoe, cork shoe, or anything that is not a shoe. I did not find this law explicit in the authorities in the laws of Shabbos. However, I found in the Eliyah Rabbah, who wrote in siman 301, subparagraph 35: Rabbenu Yerucham wrote that a sandal of cork is permitted according to all views, since shepherds go out with it, end quote. And in the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 3 of the Laws of Resting on the Tenth, law 7, I found that he wrote as follows: It is permitted to go out with a sandal of cork, etc.; there is one who forbids going out with them into the public domain, both on Shabbos and on Yom Kippur, because of the prohibition of carrying, since they are not shoes. But this is not the main view; rather, it is permitted to go out with them, end quote. Since he wrote generally that it is permitted to go out with them, it implies somewhat that this applies to both of them, both Shabbos and Yom Kippur, though he did not write the reason.
