1) [Seif 1] It is forbidden to anoint even part of one's body. For part of the body is considered like the entire body. Baraisa, Yoma 77b; Tur; Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, sec. 1.
2) Ibid. Even if it is only to remove grime. Anointing is not like washing, because its pleasure is greater. Even if his intent is not for pleasure, he nevertheless derives pleasure from it. Levush; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, ibid. Regarding whether washing grime with water is permitted, see above siman 613, sec. 2.
3) Ibid. But if he is ill, etc. For he does not do so for pleasure. Levush. Although we wrote in the preceding section that even when his intent is not for pleasure he nevertheless derives pleasure, one can say that this applies specifically to a healthy person who anoints his skin. This is not so for a sick person or when applying it upon scabs, for he does not derive pleasure from it, but only alleviates his suffering. One can further say that for a sick person or for scabs, since he does not do it for pleasure, the Sages did not decree against it even if he incidentally derives pleasure from it. See the following section.
4) Ibid. Or if he has scabs on his head, it is permitted. This applies specifically in a place where even a healthy person commonly anoints himself on weekdays. But in a place where a healthy person does not commonly anoint himself on weekdays, it is forbidden to anoint over scabs, whether on a festival or on Shabbos, because it is evident that he does so for healing, as stated above siman 327, seif 1 in the gloss. Derech HaChaim, sec. 1; Zechor L'Avraham, letter yud; Pischei Olam, sec. 1; Mishna Berurah, sec. 2. See our words there from sec. 10 through sec. 14.
