In Shulchan Aruch, seif 1. Birkei Yosef writes in the name of a responsum of Rabbi Moshe Zacuto, of blessed memory, that in some places Selichos are recited at Maariv. This practice should be abolished, because the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy should be mentioned only at a time of Divine favor. One who is present when they are being recited should sit silently or recite Tehillim. He may, however, recite the confession. On Motzaei Shabbos even confession may not be recited until after midnight, because of the sanctity of Shabbos. He further writes in his name that one who rises while the congregation is reciting Selichos, but must also recite Tikkun Chatzos, should preferably recite Tikkun Chatzos according to the order of the Arizal. It is of greater primary importance than Selichos. He cites Rabbi Shmuel Shaar Aryeh, who writes that Selichos must be recited calmly, deliberately, and with concentration. It is forbidden to mention the Thirteen Attributes without concentration. Tur Barekes writes that during these days it is preferable to increase Selichos and supplications together with the congregation rather than engage in study. Birkei Yosef writes that he saw some rabbis who regularly occupied themselves with the main bodies of halachah and with writing works, but during Elul they set aside part of their normal schedule and studied only a basic fixed amount, devoting themselves instead to supplications. He further writes that in some places, beginning from Rosh Chodesh Elul, the shamash announces after prayer, “Return, wayward children.” This is a fine custom. Machazik Berachah cites Shach on the Torah, parashas Matos, who writes that some are accustomed to perform hataras nedarim during Elul. The allusion is in the words “he shall not profane his word,” whose final letters spell Elul. See there, and see below at the end of seif katan 7.
