The laws of the days of supplication and the eve of Rosh Hashana. It contains 4 seifim: The custom is to rise during the night watch to recite Selichot and supplications from Rosh Chodesh Elul onward until Yom Kippur. {Rama: The custom of the people of Ashkenaz is not so. Rather, from Rosh Chodesh onward they begin to blow the shofar after Shacharit, and in some places they also blow it at Maariv. They rise during the night watch to recite Selichot on the Sunday before Rosh Hashana. If Rosh Hashana falls on Monday or Tuesday, they begin on the Sunday of the preceding week. [Minhagim] A mourner may not leave his house to enter the synagogue to hear Selichot, except on the eve of Rosh Hashana, when Selichot are greatly increased, in which case the mourner may enter the synagogue. [Piskei Mahari'a, siman 133] They should be meticulous to seek the most suitable prayer leader available, one who is greatest in Torah and good deeds, to lead Selichot and the Days of Awe; he should be 30 years old and also married. [Kol Bo] Nevertheless, all Jews are fit, provided that he is acceptable to the congregation. But if he forces himself upon them, they do not answer Amen after him. He must also intend in his prayer to discharge the obligation of every person. If he has an enemy and intends not to discharge that person's obligation, even his friends do not fulfill their obligation through his prayer. [Hagahot Minhagim Yeshanim] In some places the custom is that whoever leads Selichot also leads all the prayers of that day. [Kol Bo]}
