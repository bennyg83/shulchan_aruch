They are accustomed to rise in the watch [of the night]: for at the end of the night the Holy One, Blessed be He, roams in this world, and it is a favorable time; and that they became accustomed from Rosh Chodesh is because then Moshe ascended Mount Sinai to receive the latter Tablets, and they sounded the shofar in the camp [announcing that] Moshe ascended the mountain, so that they not err again after idolatry; and it is a favorable time. And there is a support from the verse "I am my Beloved's and my Beloved is mine": the initial letters are Elul, and the final letters equal forty, corresponding to the forty days from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur; for in these forty days repentance is accepted, that his heart be close to his Beloved in repentance, and then his Beloved is close to him to accept his repentance from love. And further support from the verse "And Hashem your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your seed": the initial letters are Elul.
(1) Elul onward: but on Rosh Chodesh itself one does not say Selichos and Tachanunim; and likewise those accustomed to say Tehillim all of Elul with Tachanunim of "May it be Your will" should not begin on Rosh Chodesh (Peri Megadim).
(2) Until Yom Kippur: and in our country they are accustomed from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur to say every day after finishing the prayer "A psalm of David: Hashem is my light," etc., morning and evening, and after it they say Kaddish; and we are accustomed to say it until Shemini Atzeres inclusive. And on a day that has Musaf, after finishing the Shacharis prayer before "Ein Kamocha," and in the evening after the Minchah prayer. And in places where they say it after finishing the prayer on Rosh Chodesh, one should prioritize the psalm "Bless Hashem, O my soul"; and likewise in places where they say it after the Shacharis prayer and are also accustomed to say the Song of the Day after the Shacharis prayer, one should prioritize the Song of the Day (later authorities).
(3) From Rosh Chodesh onward, etc.: some begin from the first day of Rosh Chodesh and some begin from the second day of Rosh Chodesh; and so wrote in the sefer Derech HaChaim. And one should sound [the shofar] every morning after the prayer. They are accustomed every weekday from Rosh Chodesh Elul onward, after the prayer, to say in a congregation ten psalms [and in the "May it be Your will" before saying it one should skip the words "in them seventy years"]; and since one does not say a complete book, he should say "by the merit of the Tehillim chapters that we read before You," etc.; and after the "May it be Your will" they say Kaddish. And if there are not ten at the time of saying Tehillim and they wait for one to complete the minyan, one should leave over one psalm and say it after they complete ten, and say Kaddish. And on the Days of Repentance between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur they say more than ten psalms in order to finish Tehillim a third time before Yom Kippur (Mateh Ephraim).
(4) To say Selichos: and even an individual may say those that do not contain the Thirteen Attributes. And in a selichah in which the Thirteen Attributes are mentioned he should skip those words. And likewise those petitions that are in the Aramaic tongue, such as "Machi u'Masi," etc., and "Maran di biShmaya," etc., he should not say when there is no minyan of ten (Eliyah Rabbah). When they begin Selichos they say "Our God and God of our fathers," except for a selichah that begins with one of the Names of the Holy One, Blessed be He. Abudraham wrote: "And he called in the name of Hashem": one should pause slightly between "in the name" and "Hashem." If there is no minyan at the time of saying Ashrei, he should say Selichos, and when a minyan arrives they should say Kaddish in the middle of Selichos; and in Eliyah Rabbah he wrote that afterward they should say three verses when there is a minyan. And if there was a minyan at the beginning of saying Selichos and at the end some of them left, one may say that nevertheless he says Kaddish after Selichos, like what was explained in siman 55 (Peri Megadim).
(5) On Sunday before Rosh Hashana: that is to say, the first day of the week that falls before Rosh Hashana.
(6) From the first day of the week before it: because many are accustomed to fast ten days including Yom Kippur, and always four days from Rosh Hashana until Yom Kippur are missing on which they cannot fast, namely the two days of Rosh Hashana, and Shabbos Shuvah, and erev Yom Kippur; therefore one must complete four days before Rosh Hashana; and so that there be a set day for the beginning, they enacted Sunday each time. And a further reason that they fixed four days: for we find regarding offerings that they require inspection for a blemish four days before the offering; and regarding all offerings in Parashas Pinchas it is written "and you shall offer an olah," and regarding Rosh Hashana it is written "and you shall make an olah," to teach that on Rosh Hashana a person should make himself as if he offers himself; and therefore they fixed four days to inspect all the blemishes of his sin and to repent for them. The prayer leader wraps himself in a tallis when they say the Thirteen Attributes even though it is before daybreak; but then he does not bless over it even though it is a garment designated for the day, since he wears it at night, and there is an opinion that he is then exempt from tzitzis, as above in siman 18 (Levush). And the Taz disputed him: why should he enter into a matter that has a doubt? Therefore he should not then take his own tallis nor a communal tallis, which is also like his own; rather he should take his fellow's tallis, for in this according to all opinions he is exempt from tzitzis, and he should intend that he takes it only for honor and not to acquire it.
(7) And a mourner is forbidden, etc.: but within twelve months for his father and mother [or within thirty for other relatives] it is forbidden to pass before the ark on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, for their law is like festivals for all matters; but on the Selichos days, even on erev Rosh Hashana and the Ten Days of Repentance, it is permitted [and where there is no other chazzan it is permitted even on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur]. And likewise on days when Tachanun is not said it is permitted to lead Shacharis if another will say Hallel, but Musaf he should not lead. And the Chayei Adam wrote that it seems the Gra would not allow leading even Shacharis on Rosh Chodesh; however, where there is no other, according to all opinions it is permitted to lead even Musaf.
(8) On erev Rosh Hashana: and the same applies on erev Yom Kippur in a place where they multiply Selichos.
(9) And they should be particular: they were accustomed that trustees and leaders who know the suffering of the generation pass before the ark; and that is specifically when they are faithful and shield the generation and are welcomed by the people; and for our many sins in this generation, etc.; and to repair this breach it is proper that the fixed prayer leader lead the prayer.
(10) The most proper: and all who assist and aid a prayer leader who is not proper are as if they rob good from the congregation, and they are destined to give judgment (Sefer Chassidim). And one who knows himself that he is not very expert and does not know how to guard even from inadvertent error should not enter himself into the service of Hashem. And if he is not fit and proper and expert, they do not wait for him as with other punishments; rather they collect immediately (Eliyah Rabbah).
(11) In Torah and in deeds: and it is proper that the prayer leader and the one who sounds [the shofar] be complete penitents; also it is proper that they learn the intents of the prayers and the soundings, and this is from the Zohar. And they were accustomed that one who began to lead the prayer or to sound, even once, should not give the mitzvah to another; and if he fell ill, it is in the hands of the congregation to appoint another; and nevertheless when he returns to health the mitzvah returns to the first. One who engaged in illicit relations is not fit to be the prayer leader on Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur unless he has done repentance. If he sees that there is strife because of the prayer, he should not lead even though one who is not proper will lead.
(12) That he be thirty years old: for then a Levi is fit for the service, and prayer corresponds to the service; and also then his heart is broken and crushed.
(13) That he be married: similar to the Kohen Gadol, for whom they would prepare another wife, and that he have a wife to guard him from sin. And it is obvious that if two present themselves, one who is a Torah scholar and fears sin but lacks these particulars, and the second is a simple man who is married and more than thirty years old, the Torah scholar takes precedence.
(14) The entire day: Shacharis and Minchah [for one who begins a mitzvah, they say to him, Finish; and some say even the Maariv beforehand, but the Maariv of the following day is not relevant to him]. And the Magen Avraham wrote that for this reason he takes precedence over a mourner and a mohel and one observing a yahrzeit, for prayer depends on the will of the congregation; and the Eliyah Rabbah is doubtful about this, and his view is that one observing a yahrzeit should lead if he is fasting, and from "Lamnatzeach" onward one should allow even a mourner to lead; and his words were cited in the Peri Megadim.
