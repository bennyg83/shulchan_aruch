The custom is to rise at dawn: At the end of the night the Holy One, blessed be He, moves through this world, and it is a time of favor. The custom from Rosh Chodesh is because then Moshe ascended Mount Sinai to receive the second tablets, and they sounded the shofar in the camp that Moshe had ascended the mountain so they would not again err after idolatry; it was a time of favor. There is a scriptural support from "I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine," whose initial letters spell Elul, and whose final letters total forty, corresponding to the forty days from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur. During these forty days repentance is accepted, so that one's heart is close to his Beloved in repentance, and then his Beloved is close to him to accept his repentance with love. There is another support from the verse "and the Lord your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your offspring," whose initial letters spell Elul.
(1) Elul and onward: But on Rosh Chodesh itself we do not say selichot and supplications. Likewise, those who customarily say Tehillim throughout Elul with supplicatory "Yehi ratzon" should not begin on Rosh Chodesh (Pri Megadim).
(2) Until Yom Kippur: In our country the custom is from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur to say every day after completing the prayer, morning and evening, the psalm "Of David, the Lord is my light," and afterward they say kaddish. Our custom is to say it until Shemini Atzeret, inclusive. On a day with Mussaf, it is said after completing Shacharit before "Ein kamokha," and in the evening after Minchah. In places where they say it after completing the prayer on Rosh Chodesh, they should first say "Barkhi nafshi." Likewise, in places where they say it after Shacharit and also customarily say the psalm of the day after Shacharit, they should first say the psalm of the day (later authorities).
(3) From Rosh Chodesh and onward, etc.: Some begin on the first day of Rosh Chodesh and some begin on the second day of Rosh Chodesh, and so is written in Siddur Derekh HaChaim. One should blow the shofar every morning after prayer. The custom on every weekday from Rosh Chodesh Elul onward is that after prayer the congregation says ten psalms, and in the Yehi Ratzon before saying them one should omit the words "in them seventy years." Since one does not say a complete book, he should say "in the merit of the psalms of Tehillim that we have read before You," etc. After the Yehi Ratzon they say kaddish. If there are not ten present when saying Tehillim, and they wait for one to complete the minyan, they should leave one psalm and say it after ten are complete, and then say kaddish. During the days of repentance between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, they say more than ten psalms in order to complete Tehillim a third time before Yom Kippur (Mateh Ephraim).
(4) To say selichot: Even an individual may say those selichot that do not contain the Thirteen Attributes. In a selichah where the Thirteen Attributes are mentioned, he should skip those words. Likewise, those requests that are in Aramaic, such as "Machei u-masei," etc., and "Maran di bi-shemaya," etc., he should not say when there is no minyan of ten (Eliyah Rabbah). When beginning selichot they say "Our God and God of our fathers," except before a selichah that begins with one of the names of the Holy One, blessed be He. Abudarham wrote that in "and he called in the name of the Lord," one should pause slightly between "in the name" and "the Lord." If there is no minyan when Ashrei is said, they should say selichot, and when a minyan arrives they should say kaddish in the middle of selichot. Eliyah Rabbah wrote that afterward they should say three verses once there is a minyan. If there was a minyan at the beginning of selichot and at the end some left, one may say that nevertheless they say kaddish after selichot, similar to what is explained in siman 55 (Pri Megadim).
(5) On the Sunday before Rosh Hashanah: Meaning the first day of the week that falls before Rosh Hashanah.
(6) From the Sunday of the preceding week: Because many customarily fast ten days together with Yom Kippur, and there will always be four days missing from Rosh Hashanah until Yom Kippur on which they cannot fast, namely the two days of Rosh Hashanah, Shabbat Shuvah, and erev Yom Kippur. Therefore they must make up four days before Rosh Hashanah. In order that there be a fixed day to begin, they instituted Sunday each time. Another reason they fixed four days is that we find with offerings, which require inspection for blemishes four days before offering. With all the offerings in Parashat Pinchas it says "you shall offer an olah," while on Rosh Hashanah it says "you shall make an olah," to teach that on Rosh Hashanah a person should make himself as though he were offering himself. Therefore they fixed four days to inspect all the blemishes of his sin and repent for them. The chazzan wraps himself in a tallit when saying the Thirteen Attributes, even though it is before daylight, but then he does not bless over it, even though it is a garment designated for day, because he wears it at night and there is an opinion that then it is exempt from tzitzit, as above in siman 18 (Levush). The Taz challenged him: why should he enter himself into a matter of doubt? Therefore he should not take his own tallit then, nor the congregation's tallit, which is also like his, but should take another person's tallit, for in that case according to all views it is exempt from tzitzit, and he should intend that he takes it only for honor and not to acquire it.
(7) And a mourner is forbidden, etc.: A mourner within twelve months for his father or mother, or within thirty days for other relatives, may not lead the prayers on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, for they have the status of festivals in all respects. But during the days of selichot, even on erev Rosh Hashanah and during the Ten Days of Repentance, he is permitted. Where there is no other chazzan, it is permitted even on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Likewise, on days when Tachanun is not said he may pray Shacharit if someone else will say Hallel, but he should not pray Mussaf. The Chayei Adam wrote that it seems the Gra would not allow him to pray even Shacharit on Rosh Chodesh; however, where there is no one else, according to all views he may pray even Mussaf.
(8) On erev Rosh Hashanah: The same applies on erev Yom Kippur in a place where they increase selichot.
(9) They should be particular: The custom was for communal leaders and officers who know the generation's pain to lead the prayers. This is specifically when they are trustworthy, protect the generation, and are acceptable to the people; but because of our many sins, in this generation, etc. To repair this breach, it is proper that the regular chazzan lead the prayer.
(10) The most fitting: All those who help and assist an unfit chazzan are as though they rob the congregation of good, and they will have to give judgment (Sefer Chasidim). One who knows of himself that he is not very expert and cannot guard even from error should not enter himself into the service of God. If he is not proper, fitting, and expert, they do not wait for him as with other punishments, but collect from him immediately (Eliyah Rabbah).
(11) In Torah and deeds: It is proper that the chazzan and the shofar blower be complete baalei teshuvah. It is also proper that they study the intentions of the prayers and the shofar blasts; this is from the Zohar. The custom is that one who began to pray or blow shofar, even once, should not have the mitzvah given to another. If he became ill, it is in the congregation's power to appoint another; nevertheless, when he returns to health, the mitzvah returns to the first. One who came through gentile courts is not fit to be chazzan on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur unless he repented. If he sees that there will be dispute because of the prayer, he should not pray, even if one who is not fit will pray.
(12) That he be thirty years old: For then a Levi is fit for service, and prayer corresponds to service; also then his heart is broken and contrite.
(13) That he be married: Similar to the Kohen Gadol, for whom they would prepare another wife, and so that he have a wife to guard him from sin. It is obvious that if two candidates present themselves, one a Torah scholar and one who fears sin but lacking these particulars, and the second a simple man who is married and over thirty, the Torah scholar takes precedence.
(14) All day: Shacharit and Minchah, for one who begins a mitzvah is told, "complete it." Some say even the preceding evening prayer, but the evening prayer of the following day does not belong to him. The Magen Avraham wrote that for this reason he takes precedence over a mourner, a mohel, and a yahrzeit, because prayer depends on the congregation's will. Eliyah Rabbah questions this, and his view is that a yahrzeit mourner should pray if he is fasting, and from "LaMenatzeach" onward even a mourner should be allowed to pray. His words are brought in Pri Megadim.
