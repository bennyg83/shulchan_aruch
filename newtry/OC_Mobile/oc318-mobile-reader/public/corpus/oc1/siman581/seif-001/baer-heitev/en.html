In the watch [of the night]. For at the end of the night the Holy One, Blessed be He, roams in this world, and it is a favorable time (Gan Melech Naaman); and see what I wrote in se'if 11.
From Rosh Chodesh. But on Rosh Chodesh itself they do not say Selichos and Tachanunim (Magen Avraham).
To sound [the shofar]. Some begin from the first day of Rosh Chodesh, and some begin from the second day of Rosh Chodesh, and one should not change any custom; see Magen Avraham.
Selichos. When they begin a selichah they say "Our God and God of our fathers," except for a selichah that begins with the Divine Name.
Before it. So that they can fast four days before Rosh Hashana corresponding to the two days of Rosh Hashana, and Shabbos, and erev Yom Kippur, on which they do not fast. And it appears to me that one who fasts on Rosh Hashana need fast only two days before Rosh Hashana, even though he already practiced fasting, for his intention was only for makeup [days], and now that he fasts on Rosh Hashana he needs no makeup; and see siman 568, s.k. 8 and s.k. 21, what is written there. And the reason for these four days is that regarding Rosh Hashana it is said "and you shall make," that he should make himself an offering, and an offering needs four days of inspection because of blemishes (see Taz). The Levush wrote that the prayer leader wraps himself in a tallis (and see above siman 48 what is written there) and does not recite the blessing. And the Taz wrote that to satisfy the doubt he should borrow a tallis from his fellow, for one need not bless over a borrowed tallis. The Levush further wrote that he should say the blessing over the Torah before Selichos and skip it afterward. And the Taz wrote that he need not skip it afterward, for otherwise he will find no prayer leader who will say it after Selichos, since it is incumbent upon each one to bless before Selichos; see there.
On erev Rosh Hashana. And the same applies to erev Yom Kippur; and specifically in a place where they multiply Selichos (Magen Avraham). And see Beis Yosef and Taz, siman 131, that confession and Tachanunim are not said before a mourner.
Proper. The Shelah wrote that the prayer leader and the one who sounds [the shofar] must be complete penitents; and see Responsa Beis Ephraim, siman 107, that it is not fitting that the one who sounds be unmarried and without the dignity of a bearded face, and there is no claim of established right for an unmarried man who held the sounding; he should also first learn the prayers and the Tachanunim and the intents of the soundings especially. The Bach wrote that leaders who are fit to lead the prayer means when they are faithful and shield the generation and are welcomed by the people; and for our many sins in this generation, etc.; and to repair this breach it is proper that the fixed prayer leader pray, and especially in small communities it is proper to abolish this custom; and see Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 186, regarding a shofar-sounder who demands much pay, how they should conduct themselves. The Rama wrote in Yoreh Deah, siman 376, that mourners were not accustomed to lead prayer on Shabbos and Yom Tov, and likewise on the High Holy Days it is forbidden; and where there is none better than he, the Mahariv wrote that it is permitted to lead the prayer; and on other days when Tachanun is not said it is permitted for a mourner to lead the prayer, even on Rosh Chodesh at Minchah and Maariv; see siman 671 in the Taz, s.k. 7. And they were accustomed that one who began to lead the prayer or to sound [the shofar] should not give the mitzvah to another, as is written at the end of siman 53. And if he fell ill, it is in the hands of the congregation to appoint another; and see siman 53 what is written there.
The entire day. Even Maariv, for one who begins a mitzvah, they say to him, Finish (Magen Avraham); and for this reason he takes precedence over a mourner and a mohel and one observing a yahrzeit.
