In the watch [of the night]. Because the Holy One, Blessed be He, roams among eighteen thousand worlds, and at the end of the night He roams in this world, and it is a favorable time (Gan Melech Naaman); and see what I wrote in siman 1.
From Rosh Chodesh Elul onward. But on Rosh Chodesh itself one does not say Selichos and Tachanunim. The Chochmas Shlomo and Levush and Maginei Eretz and Shelah wrote that one should begin to sound [the shofar] from the first day of Rosh Chodesh Elul, for then it is forty days before Yom Kippur, as Moshe ascended the mountain, as is stated in the Tur; and so wrote Mateh Moshe in the name of the Maharil; and he wrote that those who are accustomed to begin on the second day, their reason is to sound thirty days including the two days of Rosh Hashana. And it appears to me that the reason is as Tosafos wrote in Bava Kamma, folio 82, in the name of the Tanchuma, that Moshe ascended on the second day of Rosh Chodesh Elul, only that Elul was intercalated that year; and if so one should not budge from the custom to begin on the second day.
The week before it. So that they can fast four days before Rosh Hashana corresponding to the two days of Rosh Hashana, and Shabbos, and erev Yom Kippur, on which they do not fast. And it appears to me that one who fasts on Rosh Hashana need fast only two days before Rosh Hashana, even though he already practiced fasting, for his intention was only for makeup [days], and now that he fasts on Rosh Hashana he needs no makeup; see what I wrote in siman 568, se'if 2 and se'if 11. One must wrap himself [in a tallis] when they say the Thirteen Attributes (Levush); see siman 18 and siman 46 and siman 565.
And a mourner is forbidden, etc. But within twelve months it is forbidden to lead the prayers on the High Holy Days, for their law is like festivals for all matters, and also regarding the melody of their Kaddish; however, where there is no other chazzan it is permitted (Maharil in Responsa, siman 136; Darkei Moshe; Mateh Moshe; Gan Melech Naaman; laws of Yom Kippur; and see Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 376).
The mourner may. And the same applies on erev Yom Kippur (so it is); and it appears to me that this is specifically in a place where they multiply Selichos.
And they should be particular. They were accustomed that trustees and leaders who know the suffering of the generation pass before the ark (Darkei Moshe; Mordechai; Rokeach). They were accustomed that one who begins to lead the prayer or to sound [the shofar] should not give the mitzvah to another, as is written at the end of siman 153; and see in the Zohar.
The entire day. Even Maariv, for one who begins a mitzvah, they say to him, Finish (Mateh Moshe); and it appears to me that for this reason he takes precedence over a mourner and a mohel and one observing a yahrzeit; and there is some proof for this from what is written in siman 174, see there, that even if there is one better than he, he takes precedence; see there; and see at the end of siman 53, se'if 20.
