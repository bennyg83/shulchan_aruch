In the Tur; see Taz. On the first day, Shabbos is not relevant to the accounting of sins, as with the lulav, as I wrote below. And Mahari De Leon, of blessed memory, answered that in Parashas Emor Sukkah and Chagigah are written "on the fifteenth of the month," for the verse's way is to speak by the count of the month regarding time-bound mitzvos; so why did it change regarding lulav and say regarding lulav "And you shall take for yourselves on the first day a fruit," etc., and not say "And you shall take on the fifteenth a fruit," etc.? And the Taz, of blessed memory, answered that the word "first" is not relevant here at all, for there are two kinds of number: one is a number joined to the described [thing], first, second, third; and an abstract number apart from the described [thing], one, two, three, etc. And here the lulav in the provinces is an obligation only one day; if so it should have written "And you shall take on one day a fruit," etc., and "you shall rejoice seven days before Hashem" in the Temple. Rather it is a derashah: first for the accounting of sins, for there is a second after it regarding the accounting of sins. And against the answer of Mahari De Leon, of blessed memory, it is difficult: if it had been written "on the fifteenth of the month, and you shall take a fruit," etc., and "you shall rejoice seven days," we would not have known that in the provinces it is one day; see the beginning of [the chapter] Lulav and Aravah. And according to the Taz it is fine; and therefore he also cited the words of Mahari De Leon, of blessed memory. And regarding what he wrote "and there is a connection," etc.: see Bach, of blessed memory, who explained that the four species appease regarding water, and rains need forgiveness of sins; see there. And the Taz explained according to his own approach.
And what is written in the explanation of the Midrash and the Tur, that on erev Rosh Hashana He remits a third, and on erev Yom Kippur a third, and on Yom Kippur a third: that is, the first two thirds are only a passing-over and a suspension so as not to chastise and [not] death, Heaven forfend, like an asham talui; and on Yom Kippur He remits all three thirds, a complete pardon. And it is hinted in the verse "for on this day [He will atone for] all your sins": even the first two thirds are completely forgiven. A parable: a mortal king sends his troops to collect his debt from a province that rebelled, and when they appease him somewhat he then removes the troops and the collectors from them; nevertheless they still remain distant from seeing the face of the king until they appease the king completely and he draws them near. So a person who transgresses: the Creator [sends] a destroyer, Heaven forfend, to collect his debt and chastise him and diminish his livelihood; and if he does repentance He removes the destroyer from before him, yet still his own iniquity is not erased and he is distant from Hashem and cannot come in sackcloth garments and dirty clothes; and on Yom Kippur "before Hashem you shall be purified," for until now they are purified in the eyes of the troops and the heavenly hosts, but still distant from the Holy One, Blessed be He, until Yom Kippur. And it is proven that there is atonement before Yom Kippur by way of passing-over on erev Rosh Hashana and erev Yom Kippur; for otherwise, if only on Yom Kippur everything is forgiven at once, what does the verse teach us by "on the first day," and not writing "one" or "fifteenth," to say first for the accounting of sins? It is obvious, since it is written "on this day He will atone." Rather, do not read "He will atone" as a passing-over like on erev Rosh Hashana and erev Yom Kippur; therefore it teaches us "on the first day": that until now it was completely forgiven on Yom Kippur; and from that which is written "before Hashem you shall be purified," etc. And the difficulty of the Levush is resolved, for it is proven that there is prior atonement as stated; and the Tur also needed to explain from this Midrash that on erev Rosh Hashana, etc., there is atonement as stated.
