They are accustomed, etc. On the first day of Selichos most of the congregation were accustomed to fast (Tur; Mateh Moshe).
Erev Rosh Hashana. Even young children fast (Maharil, siman 3). The Beis Yosef wrote: even though it is stated in the Midrash that on erev Rosh Hashana the great ones fast, they all became accustomed to fast, because regarding a matter of suffering, one who wishes to make himself great does so, as is stated in the third chapter of Taanis; end of his words. And this requires further study, for he himself did not rule so at the beginning of siman 575; see there. And it appears to me that there, where one fasts for the distress of the community, we say you are not important enough to seek mercy upon them, as is written at the beginning of siman 579; which is not so here, where each one fasts for his sins, for there are those who fast more than this; if so there is no arrogance. And that they fast specifically on this day is because it is a day of favor, as we find in the Midrash that the great ones fast, as above. And even though those [Ten] Days are days of favor even more, for even the middling ones fast, and if we consider ourselves great ones, all the more so for middling ones; nevertheless they cannot stand to have everyone fast, and they also at least fast on Tzom Gedaliah; if so, since they fasted on erev Rosh Hashana there is no need; see what I wrote in siman 576. It is written in the Shelah that the prayer leader and the one who sounds [the shofar] must separate themselves three days beforehand from anything that brings to impurity, and they should learn the intents of the prayers and the soundings; and this is from the Zohar.
He need not complete [the fast]. Even on erev Rosh Hashana, because they must add from the weekday to the holy (Levush). And even one who does not fast all the Ten Days need not complete; and he does not say "Our God and God of our fathers," etc., because it is not a communal fast (Darkei Moshe); see siman 128 and siman 129.
They may eat. Those attached to the bris, and whoever wishes (there); meaning that there is no obligation to eat, only whoever wishes on erev Rosh Hashana; but those attached to the bris, it is a mitzvah to eat, and to eat in his house is forbidden until the circumcision, except for the baal bris; and it appears to me that one should perform it before the tenth hour; see siman 249.
Because of the practices of the idolaters, etc. And the Levush wrote because it is forbidden to fast on erev Yom Tov; and so wrote Gan Melech Naaman; and see what I wrote in siman 249. And it is difficult: if so, the firstborns also should have eaten on erev Pesach; and perhaps since they are not the majority of the world, the matter is not publicized so much; however, according to the Levush's reason this requires further study. It is written in the Shelah: they do not allow a mourner on the day of burial to fast the fast before Rosh Hashana; end of his words. And we hold in Yoreh Deah, siman 375, that it is permitted to fast; and nevertheless one should learn from this to be lenient regarding these fasts where there is some illness, and no annulment is needed; see siman 686; except for one who fasts more than what the congregation practiced.
