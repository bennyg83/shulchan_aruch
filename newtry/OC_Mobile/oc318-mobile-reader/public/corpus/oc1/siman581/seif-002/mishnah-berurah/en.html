(15) They are accustomed, etc.: and on the first day of Selichos most of the congregation were accustomed to fast; and an individual says Aneinu at Minchah in Shomea Tefillah, but the prayer leader does not say Aneinu in the repetition of the Amidah.
(16) Erev Rosh Hashana: and even the young children fast (Magen Avraham in the name of the Maharil); and "young children" is not precise; rather it means youths who have reached thirteen for a male and twelve years for a female fast. And he needs no acceptance and no completing [until night], even if he does not fast the Ten Days of Repentance.
(17) And they do not read in them "Vayechal," etc.: and that is even those who fast and read "Vayechal" on every erev Rosh Chodesh (Pitchei Teshuvah); and they do not say "Our God and God of our fathers, bless us with the blessing," etc., because it is not a communal fast.
(18) And if a bris milah falls, etc.: see above in siman 568, se'if 2 in the gloss, and in the Mishnah Berurah there this matter is explained at some length.
(19) They may eat: that is, one whom the householder invites; but those attached to the bris, it is a mitzvah to eat: the father of the son, and the sandak, and the mohel are all called baalei bris, and it is their festival. The Magen Avraham wrote that where there is some illness one should be lenient regarding all these fasts, and no annulment is needed, except for one who fasts more than what the congregation practiced; and the Shach in Yoreh Deah, siman 214, wrote that because he is never healthy he always needs annulment (Chayei Adam).
(20) Without a condition: that is to say, even if he slept a fixed sleep, and it was explained above that it is forbidden to eat after sleep unless he stipulated before he slept that he does not accept the fast upon himself before dawn; here it is permitted.
(21) After they so practiced: and nowadays the world is accustomed not to eat, only that he drink coffee or tea before going to the synagogue; for in our time the saying of Selichos continues until daybreak, and it is obvious that once dawn has risen it is forbidden to eat and drink (Shaarei Teshuvah).
