And those who are particular. Pesikta on Parashas Emor, on the verse "And you shall take for yourselves on the first day": but is it not the fifteenth, and yet you say on the first day? Rabbi Yeshivav and Rabbi Yehoshua of Sakhnin say in the name of Rabbi Levi: a parable of a province that owed a tax to the king, and the king went to collect within ten mil; the great ones of the province went out and praised him, and he remitted for them a third of their tax; then the city notables went out and praised him, and he remitted for them another third of their tax; once he entered the province, men, women, and children went out and praised him; the king said to them: whatever has gone, has gone; from here on, the accounting. So on erev Rosh Hashana the great ones of the generation fast, and the Holy One, Blessed be He, remits for them a third of their sins; and from Rosh Hashana until Yom Kippur the individuals fast, and He remits for them one third; on Yom Kippur everyone fasts, men, women, and children, and the Holy One, Blessed be He, says to them: whatever has gone, has gone; and from here on, the accounting. And from Yom Kippur until Sukkos all Israel engage in mitzvos, etc. And the Mordechai cited it at the beginning of Rosh Hashana; and so it is in the Tanchuma, and the Tur cited it.
