Erev Rosh Hashana. Even young children fast (Maharil). And on the first day of Selichos most of the congregation were accustomed to fast (Tur).
To complete [the fast]. Even on erev Rosh Hashana, and even if he does not fast all the Ten Days. And he does not say "Our God and God of our fathers," etc., because it is not a communal fast (Darkei Moshe); and see siman 562, se'if 2. The Bach wrote: a fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, at the hour when he begins to practice thus, or afterward if it was his intention to practice thus forever, requires annulment even if he practiced so only once; and if he did not practice so even once, but only intended to and afterward regretted, he needs no annulment. And regarding those who fast forty days, the Shelah wrote to begin from the seventeenth of Tammuz on scattered [days], and days when he fasts anyway, such as Tishah B'Av and a yahrzeit and erev Rosh Chodesh, do not count toward completing [the forty], but within Elul itself they count (Maharil); and see siman 568, s.k. 21, what is written there. Once Elul has entered, when a person writes a letter to his fellow he must hint at the beginning that he seeks for him a good year. And one who fasts two or three days consecutively, how he should conduct himself regarding "Aneinu," see siman 562, se'if 7. And how he should conduct himself regarding Havdalah, see siman 556 and siman 299, se'if 6. And one who accepted to fast two or three consecutive days, his intention is to fast until night even on erev Shabbos; and one who guards his soul should stipulate not to complete [until night] because of the honor of Shabbos.
To eat. Those attached to the mitzvah of bris, and to eat in his house is forbidden until the circumcision, except for the baal bris; and one should perform it before the tenth hour (Magen Avraham); and see siman 568, s.k. 8.
Beforehand. And one who studies in the Zohar and in the writings of the Arizal regarding the great sin, Heaven forfend, of eating before daybreak will not do so; and see siman 89 and siman 564. It is written in the Shelah that a mourner should not fast on the day of burial when it is erev Rosh Hashana; and the Magen Avraham wrote that we hold in Yoreh Deah, siman 375, that it is permitted to fast; and nevertheless one should learn from him to be lenient where there is some illness, and no annulment is needed except for one who fasts more than what the congregation practiced; see there.
