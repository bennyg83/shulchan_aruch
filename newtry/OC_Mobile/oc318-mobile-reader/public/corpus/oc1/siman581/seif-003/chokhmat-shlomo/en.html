(There, se'if 3.) And they do not sound [the shofar] on erev Rosh Chodesh, etc. Note: it appears there is support for this in the Yerushalmi, first chapter of Yoma, where we learn there in halachah 4, and these are its words: They pass before him rams and sheep; and why did it not say a goat? Said Rabbi Ba of Kartigna: even without this he need not be aroused. He found a teaching: and a goat; end of his words. And see in Korban HaEdah, who explained that the intent of what he said "even without this he need not be aroused" is as a question: without this would he not need to be aroused regarding it, for the essence of atonement is with the goat? End of the words of Korban HaEdah. And his explanation is puzzling: for are bulls and rams, etc., regarding which the essence of atonement is not with them, such that without this he need not be aroused regarding them? Yet all the atonements of Yom Kippur impede one another; also the essence of the atonement of the community depends on him, for we need "and he shall atone for himself and for his household," and let the innocent come and atone for the guilty; and if so the essence of atonement depends on him. Also what of it whether the essence of atonement depends or not? In the end he must do it and he must be expert in its law. Therefore it appears one should say the intent is as follows: at first glance it would seem that the words of Rabbi Ba are by way of a difficulty, and he comes to intensify the difficulty, based on what we learn in our Talmud, which also asks thus: why did it not teach "and goats," and answers: since it is for the atonement of the community his mind is weakened; see there. Therefore Rabbi Ba comes to strengthen the difficulty: why did it not teach "and a goat," and one cannot say like the Talmud's answer that his mind is weakened, for is it so that without this he need not be aroused, as a question, according to the explanation of Korban HaEdah? And if so, since without this he must put his mind also to the act of the goat, then his mind is not weakened by passing it if he does not pass it; therefore it is more difficult why it did not teach "and a goat"; therefore he says that he found that it taught a goat as well. So it would seem. But it appears more that the intent is this: that it is by way of an answer, and from this there will be support for the law above that they do not sound on erev Rosh Hashana so that the Satan not know when Rosh Hashana is. So too here: behold, the Satan on Yom Kippur has no permission to prosecute; but if so, if he knows that on Yom Kippur he will have no permission to prosecute, he will accuse on erev Yom Kippur against Yom Kippur; therefore one must see that he not know when erev Yom Kippur is. Therefore if they also pass the goat, on which the essence of the atonement of the community depends, through this he will sense that it is erev Yom Kippur and will accuse; therefore they do not pass the goat, and he will not sense that it is erev Yom Kippur. And therefore this is said calmly and refers to the Satan, and he says that without this, meaning without passing the goat, he need not be aroused: the Satan is not aroused; but if they also pass the goat the Satan will be aroused to accuse then; therefore they conceal the goat; and this is said calmly. And in my humble opinion this is a true explanation, and there is support for the law above. And thereby it is fine what the Torah wrote the eating of erev Yom Kippur in the language of affliction: for if it had been written explicitly "eating," the Satan would sense that it is erev Yom Kippur; but now that it wrote in the language of affliction and they eat, the Satan does not sense erev Yom Kippur; and it is fine, and examine carefully. Further it appears in my humble opinion a proper reason why they do not sound on erev Rosh Chodesh: because on Rosh Hashana they recite Shehecheyanu over the shofar, and if they sounded every day then it is not a new matter, for yesterday they also sounded; and even though there it is by way of permission and here for the sake of a Torah mitzvah, nevertheless upon intent alone without an act it is not fitting to bless Shehecheyanu; but if they do not sound on erev Rosh Hashana it is similar to what is explained in the laws of Shehecheyanu, that a fruit that renews twice a year, they bless Shehecheyanu; so too here, although they sounded beforehand, since he interrupted one day it is fitting to bless Shehecheyanu; and this is a proper reason in my humble opinion, and examine carefully with the help of Hashem. Further it appears to me a new reason: since the Sages said that from Heaven they ask when is Rosh Hashana, and the Holy One, Blessed be He, answers, I and you shall go to the great beis din in Jerusalem; and if so it is proven that the fixing of Rosh Hashana depends on the hand of Israel, and they must fix the day of judgment. And according to this, in the days of the Temple when they sanctified the month it was possible to do so, for they would sanctify on its day; but now that there is no beis din, with what is Rosh Hashana fixed? Therefore it appears that it was enacted to sound the shofar throughout the month, and on erev Rosh Hashana they interrupt; and if so, by this interruption it is recognizable that tomorrow will be Rosh Hashana; but if they did not sound at all in Elul, or they sounded even on erev Rosh Hashana, then it would not be known when Rosh Hashana is; only through the soundings of the month and interrupting on erev Rosh Hashana is it recognizable that tomorrow will be Rosh Hashana, and this is a fixing from us. And this appears to be the explanation of the verse: "Sound the shofar on the New Moon, on the covering for the day of our festival, for it is a statute for Israel, a judgment for the God of Yaakov"; and it appears his intent is that he said it is proper to sound the shofar throughout the month, and that is because it is "on the covering for the day of our festival," and it is impossible to explain it openly; therefore one must sound throughout the month to be by way of a hidden hint, and it is recognizable when Rosh Hashana is; and the reason in this is "for it is a statute for Israel," that this is a statute for Israel that they fix the judgment for the God of Yaakov; and since we need that they fix the judgment, therefore it must be in this manner; and it is fine, and examine carefully.
