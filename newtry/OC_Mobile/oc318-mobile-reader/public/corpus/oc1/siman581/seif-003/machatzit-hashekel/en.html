(s.k. 3) A week, etc. Four days before Rosh Hashana; therefore when Rosh Hashana falls on Monday or Tuesday there are not in that week four days before Rosh Hashana; and the Rama did not mention if Rosh Hashana falls on Wednesday, when likewise there are not four days, because [the calendar rule is that] Rosh Hashana cannot fall on Sunday, Wednesday, or Friday, as is written in siman 428.
One who fasts on Rosh Hashana, as is written below in siman 597.
Who already practiced fasting. That is to say, before he began to practice fasting on Rosh Hashana because of a dream, he already practiced fasting the Ten Days of Repentance, and each time he fasted four days before Rosh Hashana. Do not say that the vow to fast four days before Rosh Hashana already took effect upon him.
See what I wrote in siman 568, se'if 2: if a bris milah falls on the Selichos days before Rosh Hashana, or between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, although it is a mitzvah to eat at that meal as written there, nevertheless if he knew before Rosh Hashana that he would need to eat at that meal, he must make up in place of that day another day on which to fast. And specifically if it is possible to complete it during the Selichos days; but if it is impossible, for example if Rosh Hashana falls on Thursday, when they rise early for Selichos on Sunday and before Rosh Hashana there are only four Selichos days, he need not complete it in the week before the Selichos days, for from the outset they accepted upon themselves to fast only specifically during the Selichos days. And se'if 11, where the Magen Avraham wrote: if he practiced fasting the Ten Days of Repentance, and afterward his father or mother died, and the yahrzeit falls on those days: if it falls between Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur, or before Rosh Hashana on the first day of Selichos or on erev Rosh Hashana, since these days are fixed for the fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, they also count for him as a yahrzeit, just as Tzom Gedaliah is an obligatory fast and counts; the same applies to a yahrzeit, even though it is an obligation, it counts. But when a yahrzeit falls on other Selichos days, since they are not fixed for the fast of the Ten Days of Repentance, for they shift from day to day according to when Rosh Hashana falls, therefore if it is possible to make up in place of the yahrzeit another day among the Selichos days, he should complete it. But if it is impossible, for example where there are only four Selichos days before Rosh Hashana, that is when Rosh Hashana falls on Thursday, he need not complete it in the week before the Selichos days, for the reason stated above.
One must, etc. See siman 18 in the Magen Avraham, s.k. 2, that whoever passes before the ark must wrap himself [in a tallis]. And he wrote there that if he wore it before daybreak, since there is a dispute between the Rosh and the Rambam whether one who wears at night a garment designated for the day is obligated in tzitzis, therefore from doubt he should not recite the blessing then at the time of wearing. And the Taz wrote here that so as not to enter the dispute of the Rambam and the Rosh, it is good that he borrow a tallis from his fellow, and the like, for he wears it only for modesty; according to all opinions he does not bless over a borrowed tallis. But according to what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 14, s.k. 6, that according to the Rosh as well in such a case one must bless over a borrowed tallis, if so we have still not left the dispute. And siman 46: that one must say the blessing over the Torah before he begins Selichos and skip them afterward at the time of prayer, for they say several verses in Selichos; and even though they say them by way of request, nevertheless according to our custom one must bless; see there.
And siman 565: that an individual should not say the Thirteen Attributes, but Selichos he may say, only that he should not say them at the end of the Amidah; and see there regarding the law of Aneinu.
