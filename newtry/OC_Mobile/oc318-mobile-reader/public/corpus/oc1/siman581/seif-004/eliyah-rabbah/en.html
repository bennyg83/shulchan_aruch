[ד] [Levush] It is in order to interrupt, etc. It implies that even on the night before one should not blow; Magen Avraham — and one can reject this, for the d'oraisa tekiah is in siman 588 specifically by day; if so there is a great interruption when one does not blow by day. However since Levush wrote above until 28 Elul it implies somewhat so; and so it appears to me from the reason I wrote: "Blow in the month of the shofar" — and if they do not blow in the evening it would be thirty-one days, for the night follows the day after it. And further, in many places they do not blow at all in the evening. Maharil and Mateh Moshe wrote the reason one does not blow on the eve of Rosh Hashanah: then Satan would think the day of judgment has already passed. And it appears that even for this reason one may permit blowing to learn on the eve of Rosh Hashanah, for in any case since they do not blow in the synagogue — Satan would think the day of judgment passed, unlike Magen Avraham who forbids. I again found in Sefer Imrei Kol written on parchment, and this is its language: one who wishes to blow in order to learn blows in a mikveh or in a closed room so as not to accustom Satan — end quote.
