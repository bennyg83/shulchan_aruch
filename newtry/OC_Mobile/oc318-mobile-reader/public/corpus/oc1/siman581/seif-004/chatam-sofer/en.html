There, s.k. 9: In my opinion, granted, etc. Note: Perhaps one can say that since rain is withheld because of the arrogant, as explained in the first chapter of Taanis, it is therefore improper for anyone fasting on account of rain to appear arrogant; otherwise the prosecutor would become the defender. Regarding the fast on Erev Rosh Hashanah, however, we are not so stringent about the appearance of arrogance, because the mitzvah of fasting carries greater weight. This is easy to understand. It further appears to me that ordinary people fast, as on every Erev Rosh Chodesh, whereas the great people, who would thereby be prevented from the service of Heaven, do not fast on the other days preceding Rosh Chodesh. They fast only on Erev Rosh Hashanah, to greet the blessed King. We likewise find in the third chapter of Taanis that the men of the maamad and mishmar would not fast on Wednesday because of the Temple service; see there. The same applies to the great people on Erev Rosh Chodesh during the rest of the year, and the matter is well understood.
