All that he needs. The Bach wrote that the custom is to place food before him and say to him, 'It is Yom Kippur; if you eat, permission is in your hand.' The same applies to a woman after childbirth, after three days and within seven. The Taz wrote: It seems to me that one must say to him: if you know that there is danger to you if you do not eat the full measure, eat; and if not, first eat less than the measure. For if one says to him only, 'Today is Yom Kippur,' he will think that according to the law it is forbidden for him to eat, and he will bring himself into danger; see there. Regarding women after childbirth, we believe women, for they are expert in these matters. Magen Avraham. A nursing woman who has a sick and endangered child, and the child refuses to nurse except from her, and if she fasts there is danger to the child, does not fast even on Yom Kippur. Responsa Devar Shmuel, siman 107.
