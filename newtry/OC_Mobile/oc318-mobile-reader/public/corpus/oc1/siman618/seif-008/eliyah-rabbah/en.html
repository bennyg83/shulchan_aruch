[8] If the sick person and the physician, etc. Although in se'if 1 we rely even on the sick person alone against one hundred physicians, nevertheless here, where one physician supports him, one might have thought this is worse, as he concludes, because he believes the physician. The Taz wrote at length to challenge from the Talmud in Yoma 83, which establishes the text as deficient, etc., and he answers that we follow the majority of opinions even to prohibit, and we do not join the opinion of the sick person who says he needs. These are his words in brief. This reasoning is astonishing, that his view should not be joined to be lenient against the majority of opinions. Furthermore, one can say that the Talmud holds that in danger to life we do not follow the majority of opinions. In any event there is no difficulty at all to one who examines it. What the Taz distinguishes, that our relying on the sick person against one hundred applies when he asks to eat, but if he does not ask at all and only when asked says, 'I need,' he is not believed more than one physician, is difficult. If so, what does the Gemara ask there, 'but experts, no?' Perhaps there it is speaking when they ask him.
