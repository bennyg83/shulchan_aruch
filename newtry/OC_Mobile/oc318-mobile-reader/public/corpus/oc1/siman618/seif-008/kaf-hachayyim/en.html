8) A frail elderly person who was fasting, most of the day had passed, and he had already become so weak that there was concern of danger, is fed even on Yom Kippur; he is not left to die. This follows by a kal vachomer from the case of a pregnant woman who smelled food, etc. It applies all the more to a distinguished elderly person whose death or endangerment through this fast would be a loss to the public. Responsa HaGeonim printed at the end of Neharos Damesek, called Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 325; see there. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1; Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, paragraph 1.
