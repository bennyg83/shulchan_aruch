A sick person who ate. And he breaks bread over two loaves [Kenesses HaGedolah]. It seems to me that this is unnecessary, because they did not institute this on Yom Kippur, and certainly not for a dangerously ill person whose mind is unsettled. He also does not need to recite kiddush. The Shibbolei HaLeket wrote in the name of Rabbi Eliezer Katz: Since the day caused the prohibition for him, and only saving life caused permission for him, it is like an ordinary weekday for him; there is no kiddush, no cup, and no mention in Birkas HaMazon. Even if he wants to mention it, he should not mention it, for we have not found mention in Birkas HaMazon except where there is a mitzvah in eating. End quote. This seems correct to me, for even on Chanukah and Purim the Gemara says he is not obligated to mention it. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient, since the Tur is stringent; see the end of siman 108. In any case he need not recite kiddush, because there is concern for a blessing in vain.
