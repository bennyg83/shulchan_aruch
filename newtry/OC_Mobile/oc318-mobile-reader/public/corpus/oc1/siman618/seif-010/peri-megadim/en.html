Who says. See the Taz, who concludes that this requires analysis. Regarding not saying Ya'aleh VeYavo, behold he recites Birkas HaMazon; the same applies on Tishah B'Av, that he recites the blessing without Nachem. From Magen Avraham, letter 10, it implies that his view is to be stringent and to say Ya'aleh VeYavo, because it contains no concern of a blessing in vain, but he does not recite kiddush; see there, and with Hashem's help it will be explained there. See siman 196 and 204 in Magen Avraham, letter 21. It requires analysis whether, if the whole amount is only a kezayis or a kebeitzah, so that the blessing is only rabbinic, he recites the blessing on Yom Kippur in a case of danger like this; see there. If one physician says he needs and one says he does not need, where the doubt is treated stringently and he eats, it appears that he also recites Birkas HaMazon, and we do not say, 'go this way stringently and that way stringently.' Whether one may mix bitter things into the food, see the Rambam, Hilchos Ma'achalos Asuros, chapter 14, halachah 10. This requires analysis on Yom Kippur in such a case, for the Torah did not formulate it as 'eating,' but as 'do not afflict yourself.' One can say that even eating in an abnormal manner is liable from the Torah, and there is no difference regarding first choosing the lighter prohibition in such a case. See Yoma 85a: let it write 'do not eat'; eating is with a kezayis and there would be no need for 'do not afflict.' See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 5 of Yesodei HaTorah, whether in other prohibitions eating in an abnormal manner is rabbinic or whether a Torah prohibition still exists, only without lashes. It is reasonable that for a sick person who is not in danger, even things from which one is exempt but which are rabbinically forbidden should not be given to him to eat or drink; see siman 612.
