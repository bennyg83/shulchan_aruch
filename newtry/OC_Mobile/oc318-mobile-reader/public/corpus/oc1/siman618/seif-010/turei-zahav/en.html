That he says Ya'aleh VeYavo, etc. So wrote the Rosh, for since he ate permissibly, Yom Kippur is for him like any other Yom Tov for us. End quote in the Beis Yosef. This is astonishing to me from what is stated in Bameh Madlikin, page 24: the halachah is that when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos, one who prays Ne'ilah must mention Shabbos, because it is a day that obligated four prayers. The Gemara challenges from Rava's statement that when Yom Tov falls on Shabbos, the shaliach tzibbur who descends before the ark at Ma'ariv need not mention Yom Tov, because were it not Shabbos, the shaliach tzibbur would not descend before the ark at Ma'ariv on Yom Tov. It answers: there, according to the law, even on Shabbos he would not be required, and the Sages instituted it because of danger; but here, it is the day that obligated four prayers. Thus we see that only something fixed for this day is mentioned as belonging to the day, like Ne'ilah, where the day causes the obligation to say that prayer, just like the other prayers. Therefore all the prayers are equal. This is not so with the Ma'ariv repetition, where the day does not cause the obligation but only the rabbinic enactment because of danger; it is not comparable to the other prayers that are obligations of this day. If so, all the more so here he should not mention the day in Birkas HaMazon, for there at least there is an obligation from the day to say Ma'ariv by enactment, yet he does not mention it because it is not a full obligation of this day like the other prayers. All the more so with this person's Birkas HaMazon, where the day does not cause it. On the contrary, the order of the day is not to recite Birkas HaMazon, because there is no eating; only the incident of compulsion that happened to him brings him to Birkas HaMazon. How can we say here that the day causes it? If you say that the day causes it if he comes to such a case, then regarding Ma'ariv too let us say so, even though there is no full obligation upon him. Rather, certainly this is not so. This is unlike other Yamim Tovim, where he has an obligation from the day to eat and it is forbidden to be without eating. Here it is the opposite; the compulsion causes the eating, and what place does the sanctity of Yom Kippur have here? Another proof is from siman 284, regarding Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos: we do not mention Rosh Chodesh in the haftarah, since there is no reading from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh. All the more so here, where there is no Birkas HaMazon on Yom Kippur. It seems to me that sitting and refraining from action is preferable, not to mention Ya'aleh VeYavo in this case. Birkas HaMazon itself has a dispute whether he recites it over prohibited food in a case of danger, as mentioned in siman 196; see our words there. Let us not add to it by saying Ya'aleh VeYavo and mentioning Yom Kippur in it. Regarding Tishah B'Av too it appears so.
