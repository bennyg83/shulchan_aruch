10) There: If the ill person says, ‘I need it,’ even if one hundred doctors, etc. This applies provided that he is first reminded that today is Yom Kippur, lest he have forgotten. Once he is informed that today is Yom Kippur and still asks to eat, no further investigation is required, because the heart knows the bitterness of its own soul and we do not presume a person wicked. Levush; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 5. The same applies if he knows that today is Yom Kippur and asks to eat: he is fed. Bach; Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1; Ateres Zekeinim; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Mateh Efraim there. Beis David, siman 424, sought to infer from the Tur a distinction between one who says ‘I need it’ and one who simply asks to eat. See the challenges of Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, and Shemen HaMaor; see there. Orchos Chaim, klal 60, siman 1, states explicitly that there is no distinction; see there.
