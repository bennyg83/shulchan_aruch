(Se'if katan 10) A sick person, etc. For they did not institute this on Yom Kippur, since it is only a rabbinic enactment and is not such an absolute obligation, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 254, se'if katan 23, unlike the Taz at the end of siman 677, who seems to hold that it is from the Torah. Since it is not such an obligation, one can say that on Yom Kippur they did not institute it. There is a reason for this: the reason for two loaves is as a remembrance of the manna, as stated in Shabbos 117b, from the verse 'they gathered double bread.' On Yom Kippur, although no manna fell on it, as Tosafos wrote at the beginning of Beitzah, nevertheless by reasoning, on Erev Yom Kippur a double portion did not fall as on Erev Shabbos, since they did not need manna on Yom Kippur, for they had to fast. Perhaps because of children and sick people, double bread did fall on Erev Yom Kippur. Nevertheless the Sages did not institute two loaves for Yom Kippur, because eating on Yom Kippur is only incidental.
However, one should not be lenient, etc.; see the end of siman 108.
Meaning, do not say that this stringency is actually a leniency according to the one who says he need not mention it, because the mention would be an interruption. Therefore he wrote to see the end of siman 108, where if it happened that one mentioned an occasion of other days within Shemoneh Esrei, it is not an interruption; the same applies here.
And see there in the Taz. Here too the Taz holds that one should not mention it.
