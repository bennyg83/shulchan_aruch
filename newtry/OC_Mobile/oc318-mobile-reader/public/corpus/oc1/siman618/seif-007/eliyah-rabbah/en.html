[7] [Levush:] And the two say he needs, etc. This implies that if only one says he needs, we rely on the expert who says he does not need; and the same applies with two against two or three against three. This is also clear from the fact that the Rema's gloss in se'if 2 omitted this; see there and examine carefully. The Bach likewise disagrees with the Rema, who ruled like the Tur, for all the authorities disagree with him. I say even more than this: the Rema and Bach understood that what the Tur wrote, 'rather, as I explained,' refers to what he wrote initially, the laws in general, meaning that there is no distinction at all regarding an expert. In my humble opinion, they were not precise, for the Tur refers to what he wrote, 'and it does not appear so to Ramban,' etc., 'nevertheless, with one who is outstanding,' etc. If so, the Tur's view is like all the authorities; examine carefully. I also see a proof for this from the Agur, siman 940, and Shiltei Gibborim, page 324, who did not bring the beginning of the Tur's words at all, but only, 'there is one who says,' etc., 'and it does not appear so to Ramban,' etc., 'rather, as I explained.' End quote. If the Tur referred to the beginning of his words, they certainly should have brought it. Rather, it is as we said. It is also possible that the Rosh agrees to this. A proof is from Rabbeinu Yerucham, who is very lenient whenever he finds a lenient view because of danger to life, yet in this law he ruled plainly like Ramban and did not bring the view of the Rosh, his teacher. Therefore it seems to me in practical halachah that when they are equal, one may rely on the expert, even if he says the person does not need to eat. However, if three say he needs to eat, even though two say he does not need to eat, one should be lenient and follow the majority of opinions. In this I have great astonishment at the Sheyarei Kenesses HaGedolah, who wrote regarding the Ramban in the case of one against two that so wrote the Rema, but the Bach wrote, etc. End quote. But the Bach too holds like Ramban in this case; rather, the Rema disagrees with Ramban when they are equal, and the Bach agrees with Ramban. I have shortened, since I have already established the practical law.
