(13) And the physician is uncertain - Meaning, he recognizes the illness but is uncertain whether the person will be endangered; otherwise he is like an ordinary person.
(14) We feed him - Because the words of the sick person have force only when he says, 'I need,' etc. Consequently, we feed him because of the physician's words, for the physician is uncertain, and his uncertainty is a doubt involving life, regarding which we rule leniently.
(15) We do not feed him - For the sick person's 'I do not know' does not come from expertise like the words of a physician, because most sick people do not know and are not expert in their illness. Where the physician is uncertain and says, 'I do not know,' even if another physician says he does not need, we feed him. The Taz holds that if the sick person himself is a physician and is uncertain and says, 'I do not know,' we also feed him, even if another physician says he does not need. Eliyah Rabbah questions this, for perhaps he does not understand regarding himself at the time of his illness, even though he is a physician; see there. It appears that this depends on the nature of the illness. If it is an illness of fever, where his mind is not clear, there is certainly room for the words of the Eliyah Rabbah.
(16) We feed him - Even without physicians, for it is a doubt involving life.
