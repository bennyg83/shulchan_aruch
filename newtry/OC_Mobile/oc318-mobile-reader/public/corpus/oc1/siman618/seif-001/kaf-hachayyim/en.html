1) [Seif 1] An ill person who needs to eat. This specifically concerns a case involving possible danger. Turei Zahav, s.k. 1. That is, if he does not eat, there is concern that he may come into danger.
2) There: Even if the doctor is a non-Jew, etc. Although Shevus Yaakov, volume 3, siman 20, writes that one may not rely upon the statements of medical experts for purposes of halachic rulings, as Yafeh Mareh writes in Berachos, chapter 9, page 71b, and in Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 219; see there; where there is possible danger, one relies upon them. Yafeh LaLev, volume 5, paragraph 1.
3) There: Even if the doctor is a non-Jew, etc. Rabbeinu Asher writes in the name of Rashi (the text should read Rashi) that a non-Jew or a woman is believed to contradict a Jew. Magen Avraham, s.k. 1. In any event, that woman or non-Jew must be a medical practitioner. Machatzis HaShekel; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 6; Mateh Efraim, paragraph 1; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 6. See above in siman 328, seif 10 in the gloss, and our comments there, with the help of Heaven.
4) There: Even if the doctor is a non-Jew, etc. Regarding Shulchan Aruch’s statement, ‘even if he is a non-Jew,’ Tiferes Yisrael, in his commentary to the Mishnah, Yoma, chapter 8, paragraph 26, writes that nowadays the matter requires careful consideration. In my experience, medical practitioners routinely say even regarding a mild illness that fasting will endanger the patient. Responsa Ramatz, Orach Chaim, siman 39, paragraph 10, writes similarly. Ruach Chaim, siman 551, paragraph 4, likewise questions whether one may rely upon Jewish doctors when many are suspected of violating the religion and desecrating Shabbos, and they themselves do not fast because of disbelief; see there. In truth, the matter depends upon the halachic authority’s evaluation of the circumstances. Mateh Efraim, paragraph 2, likewise writes that it depends upon the authority’s judgment and the doctors’ expertise in the particular case; see there. Mishna Berurah in Biur Halachah. See above, siman 551, paragraph 148, where we cited the words of Ruach Chaim. Nevertheless, in our locality there are G-d-fearing Jewish doctors. Everything therefore depends on the place, time, circumstances, and the authority’s assessment.
5) Know that regarding a woman in childbirth, women are included among the experts, because they are knowledgeable about the matter. Darkei Moshe, paragraph 3; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Magen Avraham, s.k. 8; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 1.
6) There: And he will be endangered, etc. Even if the doctor does not say that he will be endangered, but only that the illness may become more severe, he is fed, because we are concerned that he may thereby come into danger. Magen Avraham, s.k. 2; Eliyah Rabbah there; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 2; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 2; Mateh Efraim, paragraph 1; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 2.
7) If the doctors say that if he does not eat he will certainly die, whereas if he eats he may live, he is fed. One may not derive otherwise from the incident involving Rabbi Akiva, for it is possible that he knew he would certainly die and there is no place for pious conduct in such a case. Radbaz, volume 3, siman 444; the Mechaber, paragraph 1; Ruach Chaim, paragraph 2; Mateh Efraim, paragraph 7.
8) A frail elderly person who was fasting, most of the day had passed, and he had already become so weak that there was concern of danger, is fed even on Yom Kippur; he is not left to die. This follows by a kal vachomer from the case of a pregnant woman who smelled food, etc. It applies all the more to a distinguished elderly person whose death or endangerment through this fast would be a loss to the public. Responsa HaGeonim printed at the end of Neharos Damesek, called Shaarei Teshuvah, siman 325; see there. Ruach Chaim, paragraph 1; Yafeh LaLev, volume 2, paragraph 1.
9) There: Even if the ill person says, ‘I do not need it,’ one listens to the doctor. Perhaps the patient is confused and, because of his great weakness, does not perceive his illness; in a case of possible danger one is lenient. Levush. This implies that even if the patient himself is an expert doctor, one nevertheless does not listen to him, because his illness has confused him and he does not know his condition. Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 2; Mateh Efraim, paragraph 1; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 3.
10) There: If the ill person says, ‘I need it,’ even if one hundred doctors, etc. This applies provided that he is first reminded that today is Yom Kippur, lest he have forgotten. Once he is informed that today is Yom Kippur and still asks to eat, no further investigation is required, because the heart knows the bitterness of its own soul and we do not presume a person wicked. Levush; Mishna Berurah, paragraph 5. The same applies if he knows that today is Yom Kippur and asks to eat: he is fed. Bach; Sheyarei Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 1; Ateres Zekeinim; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Mateh Efraim there. Beis David, siman 424, sought to infer from the Tur a distinction between one who says ‘I need it’ and one who simply asks to eat. See the challenges of Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1, and Shemen HaMaor; see there. Orchos Chaim, klal 60, siman 1, states explicitly that there is no distinction; see there.
