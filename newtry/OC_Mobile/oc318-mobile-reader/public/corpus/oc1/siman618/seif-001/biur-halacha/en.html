A sick person who needs to eat, etc.; it is possible that the illness will become heavier for him, etc. See Pri Megadim and Chayei Adam, that this means even if at present he is a sick person who is not in danger, but they assess that if he does not eat, the illness may strengthen upon him and it may be that he will come into danger. Regarding what the Shulchan Aruch wrote, 'even if he is a non-Jew,' the Tiferes Yisrael wrote that nowadays one must consider the matter carefully, for with my own eyes I have seen that they have abandoned their trustworthiness, and for every light illness they always say that if he fasts he will be endangered. Similar to his words wrote Responsa Ramat Tzvi, Orach Chayim, siman 39, letter 10, and Responsa Ruach Chayim, siman 551, letter 4, also regarding Jewish physicians, for many of them are suspected of violating the words of the Torah and desecrating Shabbos, and they too do not fast because of lawlessness; there is great need for analysis whether one may rely on them; see there. In truth, the matter depends on how the decisor sees the issue, and as the Mateh Ephraim wrote in siman 618, letter 2: the matter depends on the decisor's visual assessment and on their expertise in examining this matter; see there. Regarding if, Heaven forbid, there is an epidemic, meaning what is called cholera, may Hashem protect us, in the city, see what we wrote in siman 576 in Bi'ur Halachah in the name of Pischei Olam regarding the four fasts. Regarding Yom Kippur, see Chasam Sofer, part 6, siman 15, which is now printed in the Lemberg Shulchan Aruch in his glosses. However, the book Reishis Bikkurim, page 33, disagrees with him; see there. See Sdei Chemed, Ma'areches Yom Kippur, siman 3, regarding what he wrote on this.
And if the sick person says, 'I need,' even one hundred, etc. See the Taz, who inclines to say that this is specifically when the sick person begins and asks to eat. But if he asks nothing, and only when they ask him he says, 'I need,' then specifically one physician must say like him, and then it helps even against one hundred. I did not copy his words in the Mishnah Berurah, because many later authorities, the Eliyah Rabbah, Nahar Shalom, Mateh Ephraim, and Ma'amar Mordechai, disagree with him.
