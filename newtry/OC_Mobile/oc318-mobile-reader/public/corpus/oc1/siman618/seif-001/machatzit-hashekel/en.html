(Se'if katan 1) Even, etc. The text should read: the Rosh in the name of Rashi. This is in Rashi, page 84b, on that which was taught, that these things are not done for a sick person on the basis of women or non-Jews, but they join with another opinion. Rashi explained: for example, two say he needs, and three say he does not need, and a woman or non-Jew says he needs; they join to make it half and half, and in a doubt involving life we rule leniently. End quote. In any case, that woman or non-Jew must be a physician. Regarding a woman and a woman after childbirth, see Magen Avraham, se'if katan 8. Nevertheless, there is a distinction between a Jew and a non-Jew. According to what was written above in siman 328, se'if 10, some say that an expert is not required, for all people are considered somewhat expert and in a doubt involving life we rule leniently. The Magen Avraham there wrote that he must at least recognize that illness, and in any case he is not believed to contradict an expert even to be lenient. The Rema wrote about this: some say this applies specifically to Jews, but ordinary non-Jews who are not physicians are not presumed expert.
