4) There: Even if the doctor is a non-Jew, etc. Regarding Shulchan Aruch’s statement, ‘even if he is a non-Jew,’ Tiferes Yisrael, in his commentary to the Mishnah, Yoma, chapter 8, paragraph 26, writes that nowadays the matter requires careful consideration. In my experience, medical practitioners routinely say even regarding a mild illness that fasting will endanger the patient. Responsa Ramatz, Orach Chaim, siman 39, paragraph 10, writes similarly. Ruach Chaim, siman 551, paragraph 4, likewise questions whether one may rely upon Jewish doctors when many are suspected of violating the religion and desecrating Shabbos, and they themselves do not fast because of disbelief; see there. In truth, the matter depends upon the halachic authority’s evaluation of the circumstances. Mateh Efraim, paragraph 2, likewise writes that it depends upon the authority’s judgment and the doctors’ expertise in the particular case; see there. Mishna Berurah in Biur Halachah. See above, siman 551, paragraph 148, where we cited the words of Ruach Chaim. Nevertheless, in our locality there are God-fearing Jewish doctors. Everything therefore depends on the place, time, circumstances, and the authority’s assessment.
