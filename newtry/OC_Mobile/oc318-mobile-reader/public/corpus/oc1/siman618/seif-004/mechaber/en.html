If two (doctors) say that he (the sick person) needs (food) and even if one hundred (doctors) say that he does not need (food) even if the sick person agrees with them (who say) he does not need (the food) we feed him since two say that he does need (the food). 126 According to Judaism, two witnesses in any situation are legally just as weighty as one-hundred witnesses, and therefore two witnesses decide the issue as is the case in this instance. In this situation when two say yes, feed him and one hundred say no, do not feed him, the one hundred are legally considered the same as the two. Two against two in this case decides the issue for leniency in that the man should be fed so as not to endanger the health of the sick man. (See B .
