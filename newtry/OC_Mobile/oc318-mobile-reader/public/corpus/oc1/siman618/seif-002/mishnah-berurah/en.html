(6) One physician says he needs - Even if he is a non-Jew or a woman, they are believed to contradict the second Jewish physician who says he does not need, because in a doubt involving life we rule leniently. This is specifically if the non-Jew is expert; if he is not expert, he is not believed to contradict a Jew.
(7) And one physician says he does not need - And the sick person is silent or says that he does not know. For if the sick person also says that he does not need, we would not listen to the physician who says he needs, as will be explained below in se'if 3.
(8) Even if some are more expert than others - Meaning that the two who say he does not need are more expert and more outstanding in this wisdom; even so, we do not follow them to be stringent in a doubt involving life, since the others who say he needs to eat are also expert in this wisdom. See the Magen Avraham and Eliyah Rabbah, whose view is to rule like the authorities who hold that when the numbers are equal, we follow those who are more expert and more outstanding in this wisdom, unlike the Rema. However, if those who say he needs are more numerous, we follow them to be lenient even if they are not as wise and expert.
