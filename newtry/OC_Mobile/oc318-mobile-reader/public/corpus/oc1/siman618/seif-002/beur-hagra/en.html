Se'if 2, A physician, etc. There, according to the version of the Rif and the Rosh: It is obvious, danger to life, etc.
And the same applies, etc. There the Mishnah teaches, etc.; it is obvious, a doubt, etc.
And even some of them, etc. There, according to the version of the Rif and the Rosh, where it asks: the sick person says he needs, etc., is this not obvious? It answers: lest you say, etc., that he is frightened, etc., it teaches us that the heart, etc. It does not say, according to one opinion, that this one understands better, as in another version. But the Ran wrote that from the phrase 'on the basis of experts' we learn that we follow the one who is more expert. He wrote, therefore, that if the more expert one disagrees with two, we do not follow the two, as stated in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah: one who asked a sage, etc., greater in wisdom and number, etc. This shows that both are required, wisdom and number. And in the first chapter of Yevamos, when we say to follow the majority, etc., sharper, etc.; see there. See the Magen Avraham, se'if katan 4 and 5. This is the view of the Ramban, and it contradicts the Ran's reasoning. See the Tur and Beis Yosef.
