(1) They bow, etc. In the Tur: if one did not bow, his spine becomes a snake after seventy years, etc.; in our Talmud, Bava Kamma folio 15, the version is after seven years, and it is possible to reconcile this because the snake is born for seven years, as stated in the Talmud Bechoros; and it is stated in the Midrash, "everything is healed except the snake." Sefer Chassidim wrote: one should not bow in Modim if there is saliva in his mouth until the saliva leaves his mouth. HaPekudot Lecha — so it is in all the old versions, and likewise in Abudraham, and not as some change the text to "that You" with kamatz on shin, which is not proper; and the Levush seems to me that he omitted it intentionally because he holds it is correct to say it, for so wrote Kol Bo, Rashbatz, Maharil, and customs, and likewise below at the end of siman 127 it is explained thus, and so wrote Derisha, who expounded at length in a responsum to say it, and so ruled Mateh Moshe, and so in Pesakim of Tosafos they disagree with Rama on this. "Barcheinu" — in the second blessing, first word in the opening. Knesset HaGedolah wrote siman 128 that one does not say "our Heaven," etc., in a house of mourning, and the law of an individual fast at Mincha will be explained below at the end of siman 124. Kol Bo wrote that Solomon instituted saying Birkat Kohanim in prayer. Shelah and Beit Yosef wrote briefly: when he says "May Heaven bless you" — His face should be toward the Sanctuary, and "and keep you" toward His right; "May Heaven make His face shine" toward the Sanctuary toward you, and "and be gracious to you" toward His left; "May Heaven lift His face" toward the Sanctuary toward you, toward the right, and "and give you peace" toward His left, toward the right — until here. And this somewhat contradicts what Magen Avraham wrote in siman 127 in the name of Zohar Chadash that when the cantor says Birkat Kohanim the individual should say "For the leader, with instrumental music, a psalm, a song," etc., until the ends of the earth, look at the form of the menorah, and it is good to say it even on Rosh Hashana. In "grant peace" Shelah wrote: say "all of us as one (masculine)" and not "as one (feminine)," and what is "as one" — this is Abraham.
