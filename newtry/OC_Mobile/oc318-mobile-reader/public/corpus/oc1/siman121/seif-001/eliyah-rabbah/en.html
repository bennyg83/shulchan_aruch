[1] They bow, etc. The Tur states that one who does not bow will have his spine transformed into a snake after seventy years. In our Gemara, Bava Kamma 16a, the reading is after seven years. This may be understood because a snake is born after seven years, as stated in Bechoros, and the Midrash states that all creatures can be healed except the snake. Sefer Chassidim writes that one should not bow at Modim while saliva is in his mouth, until he expels it. The phrase “the deposits entrusted to You” appears this way in all old texts and in Abudraham, unlike those who alter it. The word she’Atah has a kamatz under the shin, but this is not evident. It appears to me that Levush omitted the phrase intentionally because he holds that it is proper to say it, as Kol Bo, Tashbetz, Maharil, and the minhagim write. This is also evident below at the end of siman 127. Derishah likewise wrote at length in a responsum that it should be said, and Mateh Moshe ruled accordingly; Piskei Tosafos also disagrees with the Rama on this point. In Barecheinu, the first beis has a patach. Knesses HaGedolah, siman 128, writes that Elokeinu, etc., is not recited in a mourner’s house. The law of an individual fast at Minchah will be explained below at the end of siman 124. Kol Bo writes that Shlomo instituted the recitation of Bircas Kohanim in the Amidah. Kitzur Shelah and Beit Yosef write that when saying Yevarechecha Hashem, one faces the heichal; at Veyishmerecha, toward his right; Yaer Hashem Panav, toward the heichal; Eilecha Vichuneka, toward his left; Yisa Hashem Panav, toward the heichal; Eilecha, toward the right; Veyasem Lecha, toward the left; and Shalom, toward the right. This somewhat contradicts what Magen Avraham writes in siman 127 in the name of Zohar Chadash, that when the chazzan says Bircas Kohanim an individual should recite Lamnatzeach Binginos Mizmor Shir through Afsei Aretz, contemplate the form of the menorah, and that it is beneficial to recite it even on Rosh Hashanah. Regarding Sim Shalom, Shelah writes that one should say kulanu ke’echad, not ke’achas. What is “as one”? This refers to Avraham.
