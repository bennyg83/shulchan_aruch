One who is careful. See Baer Heitev. Regarding embroidering verses, see Yoreh De'ah, siman 283, which says this should not be done. Ginas Veradim, klal 1, siman 25, writes that if the passage of tzitzis was already embroidered on a tallis, one may recite the blessing upon it. When sitting on a bench, he should lift the tallis from the sides and not sit on the garment itself where words of Torah are embroidered. See Shach there in Yoreh De'ah, who records a lenient custom regarding embroidery. Ginas Veradim, siman 110, writes that when asked whether verses may be embroidered on cloth coverings, one should not permit it initially; certainly not on a tallis. He cites Radbaz. Leket HaKemach likewise cites Radbaz and writes that in a responsum he agreed that after the fact one may recite the blessing. Rambam's ruling cited in Yoreh De'ah does not concern our talleisim, which we treat with sanctity by not entering a privy while wearing them and the like. See Eliyah Rabbah in the name of Mekor Chaim: Although mitzvos generally do not protect a person from the evil inclination, the mitzvah of tzitzis does. He apparently derives this from the incident in Menachos involving a certain man, etc. The verse likewise says: 'You shall see it and remember all Hashem's commandments, etc., and not stray after them, so that you shall remember, etc., and be holy.' May our words conclude in holiness.
