[4] A hint to the Divine Name, etc. Perishah writes that the verse “and you shall see it” hints that through seeing the tzitzis you will perceive and discern the Divine Name. Sefer HaChinuch in parashas Shelach Lecha writes: “My heart tells me that it contains a hint and reminder that body and soul both belong to Hashem, blessed be He. White alludes to the body, which is from the earth that was created from snow, which is white. The strings allude to the body, in keeping with the teaching that the beginning of the body's formation resembles threads, as the Sages said that its two thighs are like two threads of crimson, etc. Techeiles, whose appearance resembles the sky, alludes to the soul, which comes from the upper realms. This is why they said that techeiles resembles the sky, the sky resembles the Throne of Glory, as it says, ‘They saw the God of Israel, etc.,’ and, ‘Like the appearance of sapphire stone was the likeness of a throne.’ Beneath the Throne of Glory is the place where the souls of the righteous are stored. Therefore, the techeiles is wound around the white, for the soul is from above while the body is below.” Bach writes: “I found that since the Sages said, ‘And Hashem passed by, having wrapped Himself in His tallis,’ and the verse says, ‘His garment was white as snow,’ we derive that the tallis should be white.” The Gemara states that anyone who wears tefillin, has a mezuzah at his doorway, and wears tzitzis upon his garment will not sin, as it says, “A threefold cord, etc.,” and it also says, “The angel of Hashem encamps around those who fear Him.” One might ask why two verses are needed. From “a threefold cord” one might have thought that specifically the combination of three mitzvos protects him. Therefore, the second verse says, “The angel of Hashem encamps around,” and Rashi explains that mitzvos are likewise called angels of Hashem because they are the emissaries of the Omnipresent to Israel. Conversely, from “the angel of Hashem encamps around” one might have thought that tzitzis alone protects from sin because it surrounds a person, as Levush also implies. This creates a difficulty for what I found in Sefer Mekor Chaim, siman 16, that although mitzvos generally do not protect from the evil inclination, tzitzis does protect because it is equivalent to all the mitzvos, and also because the Torah placed this mitzvah opposite the heart and the eyes. There, however, it is explained that it protects specifically from sexual immorality, as in the well-known story of the student.
