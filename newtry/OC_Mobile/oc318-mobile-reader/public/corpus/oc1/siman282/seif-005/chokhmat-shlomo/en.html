Seif 5. This one who wishes to read maftir must return and read, etc. NB: see in Magen Avraham and Taz who wrote that since they said Kaddish there is concern people enter and do not know he already read; it appears to me this is only when the Torah scroll was not yet rolled and also the seventh reader or this one wishing maftir already left the bimah — then there is concern people enter, etc.; but if the seventh is still standing there and has not left, there is no concern people enter, since they see one standing above to read maftir and know he reads in the Torah first; and also even if he left the bimah, if they already lifted the Torah scroll they will roll it — then it is b'dieved to return and set it down and open it, and one may say they did not decree because of those entering only l'chatchila and not b'dieved, as Taz wrote similarly in Yoreh Deah siman 13 regarding marit ayin that it applies only l'chatchila and b'dieved is valid — so too here since they already rolled the Torah it is b'dieved and the seventh may read maftir thus without reading. And especially Magen Avraham's words are not binding — one may say Shulchan Aruch deals with another case not the seventh reader — here one may distinguish whether Kaddish was said or not; but if the last one wishes maftir, one may say even after Kaddish he may read maftir without reading. And so this distinction appears somewhat in Ateret Zekenim above siman 137; therefore in any case if they already rolled the Torah it appears to do so that the seventh reads maftir — and it requires study for practical law; and likewise the law if they forgot and did not read for maftir yet and rolled the Torah depends on the above law as mentioned — and it requires study in all this; examine well.
