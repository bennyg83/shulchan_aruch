3) There: Seven people read from it. The requirement of seven readers is only a rabbinic enactment. Moshe Rabbeinu's enactment did not specify a number and could be fulfilled even by one person. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2. Shibbolei HaLeket writes in the name of the Geonim that seven people read from the Torah so that if circumstances beyond a person's control prevented him from attending the synagogue throughout all seven days of the week and he did not hear Barechu, he can listen to the seven readers recite Barechu seven times and fulfill his obligation. Beis Yosef cites this. This is not the principal reason, but an additional one, because in the era of the Mishnah's Sages, only the first and final readers recited blessings. A blessing for every person called to the Torah was instituted only in the era of the Talmudic Sages, as stated at the beginning of the chapter HaKorei. Mateh Yehudah there. The Arizal, however, gives an esoteric reason in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 73d. The seven people called to the Torah on Shabbos correspond to the seven sefiros: the Kohen to Chesed, Levi to Gevurah, the third Israel to Tiferes, the fourth to Netzach, the fifth to Hod, the sixth to Yesod, and the seventh to Malchus. Complete revelation occurs only in the lower two thirds of Tiferes and all of Yesod. Yesod is therefore the most elevated of them all, because it is completely revealed. Rabbi Keruspedai consequently ascended to the Sefer Torah only as the sixth reader, corresponding to the revealed Yesod, which receives all the light in complete revelation. Next in rank is the third reader, corresponding to Tiferes, most of whose light is revealed in its lower two thirds. The lights corresponding to all the other readers are concealed and therefore rank below these two. They nevertheless have an internal order: Chesed is greater than Gevurah, followed by Gevurah, Netzach, and Hod. The lowest of all is the seventh, corresponding to the crown of Yesod, where only an extremely small illumination is present. This explains the Gemara's statement that everyone may be counted among the seven, even a woman, slave, or minor, as the halachic authorities record. The reason is explained by the foregoing: the seventh is lowest of all, representing the crown, and the designations woman, slave, and minor are therefore fitting there. These are his words.
