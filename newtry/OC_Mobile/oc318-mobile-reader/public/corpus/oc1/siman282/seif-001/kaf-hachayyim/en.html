1) [Seif 1] A Sefer Torah is taken out, etc. Every Shabbos morning, when the Sefer Torah was taken from the ark, the Arizal took care to recite the text written in the sacred Zohar, parashas Vayakhel, namely, Berich Shemei DeMarei Alma, etc. On Shavuos he would ascend and personally read the Ten Commandments aloud, as the sages of Sephardic communities customarily do. Similarly, on the Shabbos of parashas Bechukosai, he would ascend and personally read aloud the curses in that parashah, like the sages of Sephardic communities. On every Shabbos he would receive only the sixth aliyah, as stated in the sacred Zohar, parashas Shelach Lecha, regarding Rabbi Keruspedai, beloved of his Master's heart: on Shabbos he would ascend to the Sefer Torah only as the sixth reader, corresponding to the tzaddik, concerning whom it is stated, 'The Name of Hashem is a tower of strength; through it the righteous man runs and is elevated.' Shaar HaKavanos, folio 73c. We already wrote above in siman 134, paragraph 10, the intentions for opening the ark and the Sefer Torah. Take them from there.
2) If one did not recite Berich Shemei when the Sefer Torah was taken out, he should recite it when the scroll is opened before the people, as stated above in siman 134, paragraph 12.
3) There: Seven people read from it. The requirement of seven readers is only a rabbinic enactment. Moshe Rabbeinu's enactment did not specify a number and could be fulfilled even by one person. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 2. Shibbolei HaLeket writes in the name of the Geonim that seven people read from the Torah so that if circumstances beyond a person's control prevented him from attending the synagogue throughout all seven days of the week and he did not hear Barechu, he can listen to the seven readers recite Barechu seven times and fulfill his obligation. Beis Yosef cites this. This is not the principal reason, but an additional one, because in the era of the Mishnah's Sages, only the first and final readers recited blessings. A blessing for every person called to the Torah was instituted only in the era of the Talmudic Sages, as stated at the beginning of the chapter HaKorei. Mateh Yehudah there. The Arizal, however, gives an esoteric reason in Shaar HaKavanos, folio 73d. The seven people called to the Torah on Shabbos correspond to the seven sefiros: the Kohen to Chesed, Levi to Gevurah, the third Israel to Tiferes, the fourth to Netzach, the fifth to Hod, the sixth to Yesod, and the seventh to Malchus. Complete revelation occurs only in the lower two thirds of Tiferes and all of Yesod. Yesod is therefore the most elevated of them all, because it is completely revealed. Rabbi Keruspedai consequently ascended to the Sefer Torah only as the sixth reader, corresponding to the revealed Yesod, which receives all the light in complete revelation. Next in rank is the third reader, corresponding to Tiferes, most of whose light is revealed in its lower two thirds. The lights corresponding to all the other readers are concealed and therefore rank below these two. They nevertheless have an internal order: Chesed is greater than Gevurah, followed by Gevurah, Netzach, and Hod. The lowest of all is the seventh, corresponding to the crown of Yesod, where only an extremely small illumination is present. This explains the Gemara's statement that everyone may be counted among the seven, even a woman, slave, or minor, as the halachic authorities record. The reason is explained by the foregoing: the seventh is lowest of all, representing the crown, and the designations woman, slave, and minor are therefore fitting there. These are his words. See further what we wrote above in siman 135, paragraphs 19 and 20, and siman 136, paragraph 4; see there.
4) There: Seven people read from it. Why did the Sages not decree lest one go to an expert to learn and thereby carry the Sefer Torah four amos in the public domain? One can answer that Torah reading occurs only with a congregation, and all opinions agree that no such decree is necessary. This differs from lulav, shofar, and Megillah, which are handled by each individual. Maharlanch, siman 32, writes this explicitly; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 1. Regarding the custom of bringing books to the synagogue without applying the decree made for shofar, Tashbetz, volume 2, siman 197, writes that the greatness of the mitzvah requires the decree regarding shofar, since a person is preoccupied with it and might carry it. With other books, whose use does not itself fulfill a mitzvah, he will not forget; see there. Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 2, cites this.
5) There: Seven people read from it. Whenever the required number of readers read all the material obligatory for that day, the congregation fulfills its obligation. This applies whether the first reader read most of the parashah and the seven readers read a small remainder, or one reader completed the parashah and the second then repeated it from beginning to end, with each subsequent reader doing the same. Maharam di Trani, volume 1, siman 224; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur. See above, siman 137, seif 6, and our words there, and note this well.
6) Our custom on a Shabbos with a bridegroom or a bris milah is to add more than seven readers. Sometimes the chazzan reads for the second person what he already read for the first, and the same is done on Simchas Torah. Tikkun Yissachar, folio 56, writes that this procedure is valid even ab initio. Knesses HaGedolah there.
7) It is a widespread custom in many places on Simchas Torah for four or five children to read from one Sefer Torah, while one child recites a blessing for all of them and the others stand with him. This is all done for the joy of the Torah. I also saw this done in Jerusalem in the presence of my teacher Maharlanch. Tikkun Yissachar there; Knesses HaGedolah there.
