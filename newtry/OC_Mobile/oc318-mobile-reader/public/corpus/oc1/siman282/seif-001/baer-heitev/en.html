(א) They take out a Torah scroll. One should say "Barich shemeih" both on Shabbat and on weekdays — the Ari, may his memory be blessed; and one who gives the Torah scroll and one who receives it must be with the right hand. Tractate Soferim:

(ב) Seven. The reason is that if a person has an unavoidable circumstance and did not come to the synagogue all seven days and did not hear Barchu, he should listen with his ear to hear seven times Barchu and he fulfills his obligation. And see Knesset HaGedolah what he wrote in the name of Mahariv:

(ג) He adds. And Rashbatz wrote that nowadays when everyone recites blessings, one should not add because of an unnecessary blessing, and so it is fit to conduct. And the Gaon Maharash enacted in Prague not to read more than ten besides the maftir because of burden on the congregation. Or Zarua. See in responsa Ganat Veradim siman 53 in Halachot Ketanot part 2 siman 153, and see Yad Aharon:

(ד) To add. And when Yom Tov falls on Shabbat, according to all opinions it is permitted to add an aliyah. When Yom Kippur falls on Shabbat one may not add, because the section breaks are directed to end with matters of atonement; therefore it is good not to change them. Magen Avraham:
