(א) He wrote — Magen Avraham on Shabbat day, etc., one should say Barich shemeih, etc.; and I saw in Ba'er Heitev in the name of the Ari, may his memory be blessed, that also on weekday one says Barich shemeih when they take out a Torah scroll:

(ב) He wrote — the Zohar, etc., to stop, etc., that Moshe stopped his parashah, etc.; it appears to me his intent is that if he comes to end the sedra, etc., do not say his intent was not to stop a parashah — meaning:

(ג) For all seven men, etc. Meaning, just as one may not end the sedra except where Moshe ended, so too one may not end with the one going up except where Moshe ended — namely an open parashah:

(ד) For in the time, etc. — if so, the middle one read without the blessing before and after, and the first did not bless after him and the last did not bless before him; rather perforce they are considered as one and one blesses for all:

(ה) And parashat Balak has no parashah at all — how can seven men read and each end at a parashah break? But in parashat Balak there is one parashah at the end of the sedra; and they showed me that in parashat Miketz, etc.:

(ו) And so too in Berachot end of chapter 1, etc. — this refers to the body of the words of the Zohar who holds one may not divide a parashah in two; and the second version is that our Talmud divides; and so too in Megillah 22a, all the poskim hold one may not divide:

(ז) And also requires study in Megillah, etc. — this refers to the end of the words of the Zohar who wrote "and he should not interrupt words of another Shabbat," etc.:

(ח) And see what is written siman 32 — meaning Magen Avraham brought Gemara Kiddushin that we are not expert in verses where they end; if so, certainly in the middle of a verse one may not interrupt; if so, one may not end with the one going up except at a parashah break:
