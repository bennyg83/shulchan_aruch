(א) (18) The custom is — that which they practice to sell the sixth aliyah separately has some support from the Zohar Shelach Lecha [Magen Avraham]; and the last they sell separately, for the last is beloved as they complete with him [Peri Megadim]:
(ב) (19) To read seven, etc. — for from the law we rule the maftir counts in the minyan of seven, and he could complete the parashah — meaning the sedra — with the maftir who is the seventh; but the custom is not to do so:
(ג) (20) And to complete the parashah with them — and similarly he can add beyond the seven; but the main point is to inform us that the custom is not to complete the parashah with the maftir, and afterward to say Kaddish and read again with the maftir what the seventh read:
(ד) (21) And similarly on Yom Tov — meaning that also on Yom Tov, even though by law the maftir could be from the minyan of five, nevertheless they also practice that the maftir is not from the minyan:
(ה) (22) Or three, etc. — such as Rosh Chodesh Tevet that falls on Shabbat when they must read the weekly parashah, Rosh Chodesh parashah, and Chanukah matter; or on Simchat Torah when they must read parashah V'Zot HaBerachah and Bereshit:
(ו) (23) Or in four parashiyot — and even though parashat Zachor is a d'oraisa obligation that every Jewish person must hear, and a minor who is not obligated in the matter cannot discharge them — nevertheless, since he counts even for a minyan of seven, we practice to read him maftir:
(ז) (24) Even though there are disputants — meaning, the disputants hold specifically on Shabbat when the maftir reads what the seventh already read, then we can call a minor; unlike all these when they read a separate parashah for the maftir:
(ח) (25) And they say Kaddish — he did not innovate anything on the Mechaber, only to inform us what he concluded at the end — there is no distinction, etc.; rather in every case they say Kaddish before the maftir:
(ט) (26) Before the maftir goes up — that is on Shabbat; but on weekday when the maftir is from the minyan of readers, they say Kaddish after the maftir [meaning after they return the Torah scroll to the heichal]:
