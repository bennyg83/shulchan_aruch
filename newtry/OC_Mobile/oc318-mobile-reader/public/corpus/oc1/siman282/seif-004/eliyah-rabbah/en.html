[1] [Levush] "And on Yom Tov," etc. On Yom Tov there is a prohibition of melacha; on Yom Kippur the punishment is kareit; and on Shabbos the punishment is stoning. This gradation underlies the increase in the number of aliyot. Mateh Moshe gives an allusion: the relevant words contain letter-counts of 3, 5, 4, 6, and 7, and together they equal 25, corresponding to the verse, "Thus shall you bless." [2] [Levush] "And on Shabbos," etc. The number of aliyot fixed by the Sages corresponds to the sanctity of the day, with 7 on Shabbos, 6 on Yom Kippur, and 5 on Yom Tov. Although additional aliyot may be added on Shabbos, the fixed minimum should not be reduced. The authorities discuss whether additions should be made freely on Yom Tov as well, and the practical custom follows the established communal order. Where there is a need connected with the honor of Torah, a groom, a brit milah, or other recognized communal obligations, the laws of adding readers are governed by the distinctions stated in siman 282. One should not multiply aliyot in a way that causes unnecessary burdens upon the congregation or disrupts the fixed reading.
