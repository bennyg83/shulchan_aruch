Seif 2 — one who sees the sun at its tekufah and it is every 28 to 28 years. Behold I saw in later authorities who brought in the name of responsa Peri Megadim who ruled to bless even on a cloudy day when the sun is not seen etc.; and I am exceedingly astonished — for it is explicit in the Talmud who says one who sees the sun — it is clear in any case that we require sight; if so why do I need 'one who sees' that it cited — it should have said on the day of the tekufah in the morning one blesses. And further what he wrote regarding the moon — we also know it is renewed each month, so why not bless without sight. And further in the baraita both are cited together: one who sees the sun at its tekufah and the moon at its renewal etc.; and if so, just as regarding the moon one does not bless except with sight, so too regarding the sun. And what Sefer Yad Ephraim wrote as proof from what is said in one responsum that one blesses on the king on a ship — this has no similarity, for blessings on luminaries are different, being like blessings on enjoyment, and we require that one benefit from its light; and furthermore it is not similar at all — for there one need not see the king himself, nevertheless honor obligates seeing, and otherwise one does not bless; and there the blessing is not on the king's body but on the honor, and one sees the honor in any case. And know that otherwise — is it sensible that a person sitting in his house who knows the king passes somewhere would bless? Certainly not; and if so, on the contrary from there is proof: just as there one blesses when seeing the honor and otherwise does not bless, so too here — since this is a blessing on the sun, if he does not see it he does not bless; and so is proven from the Talmud in Berachos — the gentile Min asked Rav Sheshet: 'Half the river is mine and what is this question — behold he goes to bless' — rather necessarily we require sight, only Rav Sheshet answered him that since he sensed in the matter it is like sight, and therefore he was reluctant to mention blessing on a cloudy day without sight. And also it appears for practical halacha that the essence is like Levush and Magen Avraham that after three hours of the day one does not bless, and not like those who permit blessing until noon — for even if so it would be a doubt, behold all these blessings are rabbinic, and it is a doubt in rabbinic matters; and therefore it is a doubt of prohibition to mention Heaven's name in vain. And further since Aruch explains another explanation here in the language 'chamah betokfatah' — if so, after three hours there is a double doubt not to bless. And so I saw in Abudraham in the name of Aruch that otherwise one does not bless at all on the sun every 28 years because of doubt in blessings; and he wrote that thus is the practice — see there; and it is that we do not practice thus — in any case after three hours which is a double doubt one does not bless; and one who blesses there's name and kingship makes a blessing in vain; and so I instructed practically this year 5601 when the time of blessing was on the second day of Pesach and the sun was not seen until an hour before noon — and I blessed without Heaven's name and kingship; and so is the essence in my opinion, there's help — examine well.
