By His word, etc. Raavan, siman 200, gives the text: "Blessed are You, Hashem our God, King of the universe, the faithful God in His word and enduring in His covenant. Blessed are You, Hashem, Who remembers the covenant." This answers the claim of Perishah and Bach in the name of Maharshal that according to all poskim this blessing has no concluding formula, because those who do conclude have an authority on whom to rely. Beit Yosef's statement that according to all opinions there is no conclusion refers only to Rif and Tosafot, whom he had just cited. Nevertheless, in practical law our text as given by the Levush is primary. The Gemara in Chagigah says that one who gazes at a rainbow will have his eyes dimmed. Sefer Chasidim, siman 810, relates that someone left the synagogue after the chazzan said HaKel HaKadosh in order to bless over a rainbow. Although he was engaged in the mitzvah of answering Amen after the chazzan, the rainbow blessing took precedence because the opportunity passes.
[Levush] Three hours, etc. Lechem Chamudot writes that he does not know the source. See below in the laws of Rosh Chodesh in the calculation of the seasons, where the Levush writes that the luminaries were suspended on Wednesday three hours into the day. Malbushei Yom Tov nevertheless writes that one may recite the blessing the entire day, or at least until midday. This differs from Magen Avraham, who says not to bless after three hours. Tzeidah LaDerech, page 61, also appears to support Malbushei Yom Tov. Nevertheless, initially the congregation should bless when leaving the synagogue in the morning, when they are gathered together.
