They buried the deceased, etc., if they are able, etc. Although seif 2 states that a deceased person is not taken out unless there is time to bury him beforehand, here they initially estimated that there would be enough time, but afterward there was not. This is stated in the Yerushalmi.
And all the people walk after them. This requires analysis, for according to all opinions the first verse may not be recited while walking, as explained above in siman 63, seif 3. How, then, can there be time to recite it before reaching the condolence row? Even if the row is some distance away, recitation while walking is forbidden. This would be understandable were the case one in which there would be no time to recite afterward, as will be explained below, but according to the gloss it is difficult. I later found this question in Maamar Mordechai. He suggests that the meaning is that, if the row is somewhat distant, they can stand still for the first verse before the mourner reaches it, yet still arrive together with the mourner at the row, such as when they estimate that, because the mourners walk more slowly, they will catch up before the mourners reach the row.
If there is enough time, etc. See Mishnah Berurah regarding the reason, 'since, etc.' Magen Avraham writes likewise. Peri Megadim infers from this that one engaged even in a Rabbinic mitzvah need not interrupt even for a Torah-level mitzvah. He concludes that the basis for this requires study.
During the day, to recite afterward. See the glosses of Maharlach regarding this ruling. Maamar Mordechai also questions the gloss, writing that there is no proof from the Rambam, and that Rabbeinu Yonah does not appear to agree. Rabbeinu Yonah writes that they should not begin because comforting mourners is Torah-level, being included in acts of kindness. Therefore, once one is engaged in that mitzvah, he is exempt from the other mitzvah, even though the deceased has already been buried. The rule that one engaged in a mitzvah is exempt applies even when there will not be time later. See above, siman 70, seif 4, regarding one engaged in communal needs, where it concludes that the practical ruling requires study. The Rambam, in Hilchos Aveilus, rules that comforting mourners is a positive mitzvah instituted by the Sages. According to this, the gloss is correct. See Peri Megadim, seif katan 4.
