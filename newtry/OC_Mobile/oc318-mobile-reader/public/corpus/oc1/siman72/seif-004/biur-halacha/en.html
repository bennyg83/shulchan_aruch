"They buried the deceased...," etc., "if they are able...," etc.—even though in seif 2 it states that one does not bring out a corpse unless there is time to bury him beforehand, this refers to a case where they thought at first that there would be time and afterward there is not time; and so it is in the Yerushalmi.

"and all the people go after them"—one must analyze carefully: for the first verse, according to all opinions, it is forbidden to recite it while walking, as explained above siman 63 seif 3; if so, how do we find a case where there is time to recite it before they reach the row—for even if he is a little distant, nevertheless it is forbidden to recite while walking. And it works out well if we say that it deals with a case where there is no time to recite afterward, as written shortly below; but according to what is written in the gloss, it is difficult. Later I found this difficulty in Maamar Mordechai, and he wrote regarding it: perhaps one can say that it means if he is at a slight distance—before the mourner arrives there—they can stand for the first verse and be with the mourner for the row together, such as when they estimate from the mourners' pace that they will reach them before they themselves reach the row—end of his words.

"if there is sufficient time...," etc.—see Mishna Berurah, what he wrote "since...," etc.; so too Magen Avraham. And see Peri Megadim, who wrote that from this it is implied that one engaged in a mitzvah—even a rabbinic mitzvah—may no longer interrupt even for a Torah mitzvah; and this requires study—from where does he derive this—end of his words.

"in the day to recite afterward"—see the glosses of Maharalach on what he wrote about this gloss; and also in Maamar Mordechai he questions this gloss and wrote that from the Rambam's words there is no proof, and also from Rabbeinu Yonah's words it does not imply so, for he wrote "they do not begin"—the reason of the matter is that comforting mourners is by Torah law, as it is included in "and you shall love your fellow as yourself"; therefore they said that since one is engaged in a mitzvah, he is exempt from the mitzvah—even though the deceased is already buried he does not begin—end of his words. And the law of one engaged in a mitzvah applies even when there is no sufficient time; see above siman 70 seif 4 regarding one engaged in communal needs, and he concludes there that this law requires study in practice. And the Rambam in Hilchos Aveilus ruled that comforting mourners is a positive commandment by rabbinic enactment; therefore the law accords with the gloss; see Peri Megadim seif kaf-dalet.
