(4) We do not bring out, etc. — and if the time for Keri'as Shema has already arrived, they may delay [burial for] a time such that they can estimate that most of the congregation has already prayed; and also the bier-carriers have already read and prayed.

(5) The deceased — even an important person; for regarding this matter we do not have in our time an important person to be lenient on this account.

(6) That the time will arrive, etc. — from the closing phrase of his words it is implied that the same law applies to evening Keri'as Shema; and for practical law the later authorities agreed to be lenient regarding evening, because if we do not bury him today he transgresses "you shall not leave"; and even though at night he could bury him after he reads Keri'as Shema and prays, nevertheless he transgresses a positive commandment, for "burying you shall bury him on that day" if we do not bury him before sunset. And if it happened that the burial was delayed until it grew dark, they wait from bringing it out until the congregation prays Maariv.

(7) They began to bring it out — even after the time for Keri'as Shema arrived.

(8) We do not interrupt — meaning the people needed to carry the bier, even if thereby the time for Keri'as Shema passes, for burial of the dead is Torah law; but those who are not needed for the bier are obligated to read immediately even if there is still further time.
