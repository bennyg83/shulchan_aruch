(4) They do not take out, etc. If the time for Krias Shema has already arrived, they should delay until they can estimate that most of the congregation has already prayed, and that the bearers have also recited Krias Shema and prayed.
(5) The deceased. Even an important person, because in our times no person has the status of importance that would permit leniency in this regard.
(6) Before the time arrives, etc. The unqualified wording implies that the same rule applies to the evening Krias Shema. As a practical ruling, the later authorities agree that one may be lenient at night, because if he is not buried that day they will violate the prohibition of leaving a corpse overnight. Although they could bury him at night after reciting Krias Shema and praying, they would at least violate the positive commandment, 'you shall surely bury him on that day,' if they do not bury him before sunset. If the funeral was delayed until after nightfall, they should wait to take him out until the congregation has prayed Maariv.
(7) They began to take him out. Even after the time for Krias Shema arrived.
(8) They do not interrupt. Meaning, those needed to carry the bier do not interrupt, even if the time for Krias Shema will pass, because burial of the dead is a Torah-level obligation. Those whom the bier does not need must recite immediately, even though there is still additional time.
