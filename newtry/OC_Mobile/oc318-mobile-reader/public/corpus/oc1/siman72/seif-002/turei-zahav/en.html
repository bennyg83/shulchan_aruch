They do not take out, etc. The Gemara asks that Rabbi Yose was taken out close to the time of Krias Shema, and answers that an important person is different. Accordingly, the Rambam distinguishes between an important person and another person. The Tur writes in the name of the Rosh that he did not distinguish, and neither did the Rif. Beit Yosef explains that perhaps their reason is that in our times it is uncommon to find a person important enough to justify neglecting Krias Shema for him. I would state a broader rule. Whenever the Gemara distinguishes an important person in order to impose a stringency, every person may regard himself as important for the purpose of being stringent, as the Tur writes below in siman 554 regarding Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's statement that one should always consider himself a Torah scholar and fast, without concern for arrogance. But where importance produces a leniency, whatever the context, no one in our generations is important enough to justify leniency. A parallel is that nowadays one does not impose the fine of a litra of gold for insulting a Torah scholar, as stated in Agudah, and likewise regarding permitting vows before a single expert and similar matters. The Rif and Rosh therefore acted correctly in not distinguishing an important person in our generations.
