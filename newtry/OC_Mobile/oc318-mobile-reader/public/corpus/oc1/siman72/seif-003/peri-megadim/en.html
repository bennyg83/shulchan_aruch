The inner ones — the Taz: for in the Mishnah they said the inner ones are obligated and the outer ones exempt; and in the Gemara 19b: a row that sees inward is exempt and one that does not see inward is obligated. And the Bach explained that they stand rows in a circle like an onion with two circles exempt, for the mourner is in the middle with a row surrounding him; and afterwards a second row — two rows are exempt, for perhaps through the shoulders of row one they see row two and see the mourner — it counts. And the Taz challenged on this: if so, rows upon rows — from where [do we derive] that two rows exempt? Therefore he explained that the mourner stands in a row facing inward without a circle, and afterwards they surround with rows; and the first surrounding row is exempt because those in front of it see the face of the mourner and also comfort him — even though that row is also behind the mourner in the circle, all that circle is exempt. And this is what is meant by "row" — namely one circle; one that sees inward — the row facing inward; and one that does not see the face of the mourner from behind is exempt. And "circle" — meaning those standing in a square — for if so it is difficult, for there are four rows; and why did he say "row"? Therefore he said "circle" in a round form and it is one row. See Rambam's Mishnah commentary that they are exempt only when there is no break between the mourner and them; and the Bach — Rashi disagrees with the Rambam, and for the Taz there is no dispute here.
