Seif 3: And they pray, etc. Although the latter clause teaches, 'they recite and pray,' while the first clause mentions only recitation, this is not precise. The latter clause comes to teach how they should act regarding Krias Shema and regarding prayer. Proof is found in the Tosefta, chapter 2, and in the Yerushalmi there: One delivering a eulogy and all those occupied with the eulogy interrupt for Krias Shema but do not interrupt for prayer; yet an incident occurred in which our teachers interrupted for both Krias Shema and prayer, and practical precedent is authoritative. Rabbeinu Yonah. This requires study, for the Yerushalmi concludes there: 'Did we not learn that if they can begin and finish?' That Mishnah concerns the first day, whereas the teaching concerns the second day. It therefore appears to refer to the latter clause, where the deceased is not lying before them. See the Tur, Beit Yosef, and Taz.
