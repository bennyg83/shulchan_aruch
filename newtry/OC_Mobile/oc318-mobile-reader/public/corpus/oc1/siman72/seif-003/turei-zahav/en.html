The people engaged in the eulogy. Tur concluded with this — specifically the people who come to hear the eulogy; but the cantors who eulogize are exempt — specifically on the first day; but from then on even the cantors are obligated. It is implied from his words that the law of cantors — even in a place where those engaged in the eulogy interrupt — the cantors need not stop their eulogy; and therefore Beit Yosef was challenged: why did they say in the Gemara that the people engaged in the eulogy slip away, etc., and the mourner sits silent and the judgment is justified for him and he says Master of the worlds, etc. — why should he say now more Master of the worlds, since the cantors do not stop their eulogy? And further, how can the cantor eulogize while the congregation reads — will they not nullify the cantor's intent? And he strained to answer that the cantors also are silent when the people read, only that even so they are exempt from Keriat Shema since they are anxious to reflect on eulogy matters, etc.; and to this Moharach z"l agreed — except that he strained in explaining the words of the Jerusalem Talmud to explain that it says two things; and he also brought the challenger's words by another path. And in my humble opinion it appears the words stand as they are plainly; and it is that there is somewhat missing in the Jerusalem Talmud that Rosh brought; also an error in what he wrote exempt from Keriat Shema and do not interrupt for prayer — what difference between exempt and do not interrupt; and in our version the text is thus: it teaches the eulogy and all engaged in the eulogy interrupt for Keriat Shema and do not interrupt for prayer; an incident occurred and our rabbis interrupted both for Keriat Shema and for prayer; and we taught if they can begin and finish, etc.; mishnah on the first day and what it taught it taught on the second day; and the explanation: the eulogy means the cantors who make the eulogy, and the people engaged in the eulogy means the listeners — all are obligated in Keriat Shema because it is Torah law, but not in prayer which was rabbinic; an incident occurred and our rabbis interrupted also for prayer; and he challenges: but we taught if they can begin and finish they should begin, and if not they should not begin — from this we see they are exempt from Keriat Shema; and if you explain it on those who are not engaged but escort for honor of the dead — meaning like listeners at the eulogy — it is obvious there should be difficulty on what he wrote that even the cantors are obligated. And the resolution of all is per the conclusion: those who came for honor are obligated and he exempted only those actually engaged like the cantors; and this matter stands per the conclusion; nevertheless there is a difficulty of cantors against each other; and they answer to distinguish between cantors of the first day and cantors of the second day. It turns out there is no leniency for cantors over listeners except on the first day; and the explanation that cantors would eulogize even while the listeners read Keriat Shema and then they would be exempt due to anxiety — this cannot be entertained; rather their leniency is if the listeners already read Keriat Shema and the cantors have not yet read, they do not interrupt for them but eulogize; but if also the listeners have not yet read, certainly the cantors also read with them — and this itself is the explanation of Tur's words without any need for explanation; and certainly also for the Jerusalem version as Rosh brought in Beit Yosef, these matters are justified; and the challenger's difficulty will be on what he wrote an incident occurred and they interrupted for Keriat Shema, etc.; but per our version it is more justified that per all opinions one is obligated in Keriat Shema, also the cantor on the second day. Also the hints that Beit Yosef brings — no difficulty; and these are his words: all engaged in the eulogy are exempt from Keriat Shema, and cantors eulogizing on the first day are exempt; from then on they are obligated. Beit Yosef raised many difficulties on this; and in truth everything is resolved: the first part teaches that even the people engaged in the eulogy are exempt — meaning those who do not slip away but remain here, they are exempt — and these are the inner row as Beit Yosef wrote; and from this all the more the cantors eulogizing; and the latter part teaches that even cantors are not exempt except on the first day, and all the more the other engaged and comforters.
