The people occupied with the eulogy — the Tur concluded with this: specifically the people who come to hear the eulogy; but the chazzanim who eulogize are exempt — and specifically on the first day; but from then onward even the chazzanim are obligated. It is implied from his words that the law of the chazzanim: even in a place where those occupied with the eulogy interrupt, the chazzanim need not interrupt their eulogy. And therefore it was difficult for the Beit Yosef: why did they say in the Gemara that the people occupied with the eulogy slip away, etc., and the onen sits and is silent; and the law vindicates him and he says "Master of the worlds," etc. — why should he say now more "Master," etc., since the chazzanim do not interrupt from their eulogy? And further, how can the chazzan eulogize while the congregation reads — will not his intent be nullified? And he strained himself to answer that the chazzanim also are silent when the people read — but even so they are exempt from Keri'as Shema because they are occupied reflecting on matters of the eulogy, etc. And for this the Mabit agreed as well; but he strained himself in explaining the words of the Yerushalmi to explain that it states two [things], and he also brought the words of the questioner by another path. And in my humble opinion it seems that things are as they are and their plain meaning; and there is a slight omission in the Yerushalmi that the Rosh brought; and there is also a textual error in what is written "exempt from Keri'as Shema and do not interrupt for tefillah" — what difference is there between exempt and do not interrupt? And in the version before us the text reads thus: "The eulogy and all occupied with the eulogy interrupt for Keri'as Shema and do not interrupt for tefillah." An incident occurred and our teachers interrupted both for Keri'as Shema and for tefillah. And we taught: if they can begin and finish, etc. — the Mishnah on the first day; and what was taught was taught on the second day. And the explanation: "the eulogy" means the chazzanim who make the eulogy; and "the people occupied with the eulogy" means the listeners — all are obligated in Keri'as Shema, which is Torah law, but not in tefillah, which was rabbinic. An incident occurred and our teachers interrupted also for tefillah; and it challenged: but we taught, if they can begin and finish they begin, and if not they do not begin — from this we see they are exempt from Keri'as Shema. And if you explain it on those who are not occupied but only escort in honor of the deceased — that is, like listeners at the eulogy — it is obvious that it would be difficult on what is written that even the chazzanim are obligated; but in any case this is not difficult on the listeners, who are truly obligated, as written at the head of the chapter that those who came for honor are obligated; and he exempted only those actually occupied, like the chazzanim. And this matter stands per the conclusion. Nevertheless there is a difficulty of chazzanim against chazzanim; and it answers: to distinguish between chazzanim of the first day and chazzanim of the second day. It turns out that the chazzanim have no leniency over the listeners except on the first day. And it is not the explanation that the chazzanim would eulogize even at the hour when the listeners read — and then they would be exempt because of occupation — this cannot enter the mind; rather their leniency is: if the listeners already read Keri'as Shema and the chazzanim have not yet read, they do not interrupt on their account but eulogize; but if the listeners too have not yet read, surely the chazzanim also read with them. And this itself is the explanation of the Tur's words without any need for explanation. And certainly also for the Yerushalmi version, as the Asheri wrote that the Beit Yosef brings, these matters are also justified; and the questioner's difficulty will be on what is written "an incident occurred and they interrupted for Keri'as Shema," etc. But according to our version it is more justified that according to all opinions the chazzan too is obligated in Keri'as Shema on the second day. Also the words of the hints that the Beit Yosef brings do not help; and this is its language: "All occupied with the eulogy are exempt from Keri'as Shema, and the chazzanim who eulogize on the first day are exempt; from here onward they are obligated." And the Beit Yosef raised many difficulties on this. And in truth everything is satisfactory: the first clause teaches that even the people occupied with the eulogy are exempt — that is, those who do not slip away but remain here are exempt, and these are the inner row, as the Beit Yosef wrote; from this, all the more so the chazzanim who eulogize. And the latter clause teaches that even the chazzanim are not exempt except on the first day, and all the more so the other occupied ones and comforters. It turns out everything is satisfactory, with Heaven's help.
