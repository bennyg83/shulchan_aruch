The inner ones who see the mourner's face. The Gemara states, “the row that sees the inner area.” Rashi explains: “That sees the inner open space of the enclosure, behind the innermost row, where the mourner sits.” These words require explanation. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, explains that Rashi also includes the row behind the innermost row within the exemption, because it can see the mourner through the spaces between the shoulders of those standing in the innermost row; only the third surrounding row is obligated. This is difficult. If so, why does the Gemara say “row” in the singular? It should say “rows.” Furthermore, why did Rashi need to mention the open space of the enclosure? It appears to me that Rashi resolves two difficulties. First, why did the Gemara not simply say “the row that sees the mourner”? Second, if the rows form a square around the mourner, four rows see the mourner's face; why then does the Gemara use the singular? Rashi therefore explains that one row sits in the interior, with the mourner among them, while the other rows form a circular enclosure around that row. This is what Rashi means by “the enclosure behind the row”: others encircle the row, and the entire area enclosing that row is called one row, even where it extends behind them. So it appears to me in explanation of Rashi's view.
