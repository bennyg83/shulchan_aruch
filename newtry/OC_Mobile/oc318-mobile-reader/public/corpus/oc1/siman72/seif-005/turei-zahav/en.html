The inner ones who see the face of the mourner — In the Gemara it states: a row that sees the inner [space]; Rashi explained: that sees the inner space of the circle behind the inner row, and the mourner sits there — end quote. This wording needs explanation. And the Mabit explained that even the row after the inner one Rashi included in the exemption, that it can see the mourner through the air between the two shoulders of those standing in the inner row; and only the third circle is obligated. It is astonishing: if so, why did they say "row" in singular — it should have said "rows"; and further, why did Rashi need to mention the space of the circle? And it seems to me that Rashi corrected here two things: one — why did he not say again "a row that sees the mourner"? If the row is through a square around the mourner, there are four rows seeing the face of the mourner; and why singular language? Therefore he explained that facing inward sits one row and the mourner between them, and the other rows surround in a circle around that row. And this is what Rashi wrote "circle behind the row" — meaning that they surround the row also from behind, and all the area of that circle is called one row, even though it is also from behind; as I explained to settle Rashi's view.
