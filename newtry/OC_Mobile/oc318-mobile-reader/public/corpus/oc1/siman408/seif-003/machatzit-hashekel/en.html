(s.k. 3) Nevertheless, etc. — so wrote D.M., etc.; requires study what difference, etc.; for per the law if he rested in a place not surrounded by partitions he has four cubits and from those four cubits he has two thousand in every direction in the width of four cubits — he has two strips like two sides of a rectangle four thousand by four thousand and four cubits; but the width of each strip is only four cubits like the width of his shevitah place, then corners are filled in that rectangle — he has a square tablet four thousand and four cubits long by four thousand and four cubits wide; not so when he rested in a city where the whole city is four cubits even if its length and width are a thousand cubits — when measuring outside it two thousand from each side he has two strips like a rectangle each five thousand cubits, but each strip's width is only a thousand cubits like the city's width and length, then corner-fill of the strips yields a square tablet five thousand by five thousand; therefore Rabbeinu Yonatan wrote if his measure ended in half the city, since he has only half the city and the whole city length is not four cubits for him, likewise the width of half the city he has is not four cubits — therefore he may not carry in that half except within four cubits width per the strip from his shevitah place which is in a place without partitions as above; and it was thought D.M. discusses when the city is not broader than four thousand and four cubits — therefore it was difficult to him what difference, etc.; for how is it worse via city partitions — even in a field, though they give only two strips like a rectangle of four-cubit width, nevertheless after corner-fill he has four thousand and four cubits long by four thousand and four cubits wide — so too when his measure ended in half the city, though the city is not four cubits and he has only city-width strip of four cubits, nevertheless when corners are filled as above he may walk the entire half-city width; therefore Rabbeinu Yonatan's intent is truly that he has only a four-cubit-wide strip and after corner-fill may walk the entire city width; and since so, when his measure ended at the end of the city it implies he has more; therefore we must say Rabbeinu Yonatan holds when his measure ended at the end of the city, since the whole city is four cubits for its length, likewise it is four cubits for its width regarding techum measure, and they give him from the side north and south (when he placed eruv west of the city one thousand cubits from the city, as this case is explained afterward) two thousand cubits from the city; and when the city is four thousand cubits wide he has opposite the city north-south a strip four thousand wide and long (if the city length is four thousand) of two thousand each, even though in those thousand cubits between city and eruv he has only a strip four thousand and four cubits wide north-south as above — such is D.M.'s intent per Magen Avraham's understanding.

And I was astonished, etc. — he has no walking outside except the city gate toward north, etc., in general when the city is four thousand and four cubits wide — then within the city for its width his strip measure of four cubits with corner-fill ended.

To the place he gave his eruv it extends — meaning: he did not rest within the city to be given a strip north and south from the city side except what is given him north and south of the city because he is still within his techum from his shevitah place outside the city; therefore what is given north and south of the city extends after the place he gave his eruv and he has only four cubits with corner-fill.

That even westward he has no thousand cubits, etc. — discusses when he placed eruv west of the city one thousand from the city as above; even those thousand he does not have per city width, though he had permission initially — meaning: initially before placing eruv, if he were resting in the city he would have westward (where he now placed eruv) two thousand length and width opposite city width, then corners would be filled as above; now that he placed eruv outside the city and uprooted shevitah from the city he has only a four-cubit-wide strip with corner-fill; and what is written when his measure ended in the middle of the city, etc. — that is D.M.'s difficulty: since even if his measure ended at the end of the city they do not give him north and south outside the city as above — on this Magen Avraham wrote it discusses when the city is broader than four thousand and four cubits: when his measure ended in half the city he has only a four-cubit strip with corner-fill — if the city is eight thousand wide he has in the middle of the city (if he placed eruv opposite middle of city) four thousand and four cubits and is forbidden to walk even within the city north and south two thousand less four cubits north near the city wall and likewise south; not so when his measure ended at end of city — since whole city is four cubits for its length, likewise for width only four cubits — therefore he walks all its width which is eight thousand, but outside the city even one cubit he may not go north and south even if the city is only four thousand and four cubits wide per his four-cubit strip width with corner-fill; and to permit him entire city width within when very much broader than his strip measure and his measure ended at end of city — he is in doubt, all the more outside the city certainly forbidden.
