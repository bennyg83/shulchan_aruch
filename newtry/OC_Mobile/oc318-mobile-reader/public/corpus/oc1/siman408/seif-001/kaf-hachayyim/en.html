1) [Se'if 1] Gloss: It is permitted to establish an eruv, etc. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies only after the fact. The gloss therefore adds, 'It is permitted to establish an eruv,' to teach that it is permitted even initially. This initial permission applies specifically for a mitzvah purpose. For an optional purpose it is effective only after the fact, as explained below in siman 416. See there.
2) There, in the gloss. It is permitted to establish an eruv, etc. Since techumin are Rabbinic, the Sages ruled leniently. They permitted one who needs to travel four thousand amos in one direction to place an eruv at the end of two thousand amos and then walk another two thousand amos beyond the eruv. Nevertheless, he loses the corresponding two thousand amos in the opposite direction, because a person has only a total span of four thousand amos. Since he placed his eruv two thousand amos from the city, we regard him as though he resides at the eruv's location, and from there he has two thousand amos in every direction. Tur. Although some authorities maintain that techumin are of Torah origin, a distance within twelve mil is nevertheless Rabbinic, as stated above at the beginning of siman 397. See there.
3) There, in the gloss. Eruvei techumin. It is called this because it combines the techum in which one could not initially walk with the techum in which one could walk. Maggid Mishneh, beginning of chapter 6 of Hilchos Yom Tov, explaining the opinion of Raavad; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
4) There. One who left the city on Friday, etc. Alternatively, if he wishes, he may send the eruv through an agent, as stated below in siman 409, se'if 8. See there.
