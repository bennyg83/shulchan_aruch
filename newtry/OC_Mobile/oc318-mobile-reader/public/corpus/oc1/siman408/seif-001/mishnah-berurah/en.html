(1) Permitted, etc. — that is, even though a person may not walk on Shabbat from his place of shevitah except two thousand cubits, as explained above siman 397, they permitted him to walk four thousand on one side by placing food for two meals at the end of two thousand from where he stands on erev Shabbat; thereby he is considered as if his shevitah were there and he may walk two thousand in every direction from that place. And because the Mechaber wrote in language of b'dieved, the Rama added that even l'chatchila one may make eruv; and this is specifically for a mitzvah matter l'chatchila, but for a discretionary matter only b'dieved, as explained below siman 415 — see there.

(2) Who left, etc. — the same applies if he sent via another to place his eruv there, as below in the siman after this one.

(3) Distant from the city within the techum — even if from his house it is more than two thousand we do not care, since it is within the city's techum; as opposed to if he placed his eruv distant from the city more than two thousand — his eruv is not valid, for he cannot go there at bein hashemashot since it is outside the city's techum.

(4) And established his shevitah there — that is, he placed it there to acquire shevitah.

(5) Even though he returned to the city — that is, willingly; but if he was delayed there until after bein hashemashot he need not leave food there at all, as explained in Tur seif 7.

(6) And this is called eruv techumin — it is called thus because it mixes a techum he could not initially walk with the techum he could walk.

(7) Therefore, etc. — meaning: his primary shevitah is made thereby at the eruv place; therefore if he wishes to return to his city he may walk only until his two-thousand measure ends from the eruv place; and even if he has not yet left the house where he slept, nevertheless if by measure from the eruv to his house it is two thousand, he is forbidden to walk from his house in the other direction even within the city even one cubit; and even if he reconsidered his shevitah it does not help.

(8) Until the end of the thousand — and if his measure ended in the middle of the city he may walk only until the end of his measure, as above.

(9) Less, etc. — not specifically less; the same if his thousand-cubit measure ended exactly at the end of the city — the whole city is considered four cubits. And some say the Shulchan Aruch's words are precise because this measure of intermediate thousand cubits is transmitted to every person and cannot be shortened; therefore Poskim were stringent that the measure must end specifically outside the city; in time of need one may be lenient even when his measure ends flush with the city.

(10) From his house — why it says from his house and not distant from the city two thousand: when distant from his house more than two thousand, even though he may walk from his house to his eruv place as above in sk 3, to return from there after dark and sleep at home is forbidden — he must sleep in one of the city houses near within two thousand of his eruv place.

(11) May walk the entire city — for they hold that even though he placed his eruv two thousand from his house in the city, since he slept within the city the whole city is considered four cubits regarding permission to walk all of it and certainly to return home; but regarding techum measure, even per this view we count the city in the two-thousand measure since his measure ended in half the city — therefore forbidden to walk outside it at all. All this when he slept in that city; but if he slept at his shevitah place and his two thousand ended in the middle of the city, or slept in this city and his measure ended in half another city — he may walk only until where his measure ended, all agree. By throwing he may carry throughout the city if it were private domain, as above siman 403 — see there (acharonim).

(12) One may be lenient — so too Bach and other acharonim.

(13) To be stringent — meaning: we would say it is as if his measure ended in the middle of the city because there is still extension for city residents — we do not say city extension counts as city except to be lenient on techum measure, not to be stringent; and it appears that in this at least we do not count extension with the city as four cubits; however if his measure ended at the end of the extension, plainly all is considered four cubits and his measure begins from the end of the extension and beyond.

(14) North and south — the same for every direction; and because of the conclusion of his words at the end of this Hagah he mentioned north and south.

(15) Opposite the width of the place — meaning: if he was in a house or city, even if larger, they give him two thousand in every direction opposite the width of that place, then add corners to complete a square tablet (what the Rama wrote with their corners and as above applies also to this); but no more.

(16) When the eruv is not in private domain — meaning: he placed his eruv in a field.

(17) Within the width of his four cubits — that is, the very breadth his techum occupies: on each side two thousand cubits opposite the place of man's shevitah which is four cubits; already explained above siman 399 seif 10 that corners are filled — each corner in a square tablet of two thousand; this is what the Rama wrote with their corners: after filling corners his techum measure becomes a square tablet of four thousand by four cubits on four thousand, and in all that area he may walk. If his shevitah place were large, such as a city holding a thousand cubits in width and length, his techum measure is a tablet of five thousand by five thousand, and so in every such case.

(18) Nevertheless if his measure ended, etc. — meaning: even though we wrote he has only width of four cubits opposite his shevitah place, nevertheless if a city met his techum and his measure ended at its end, its entire width counts as four cubits even if it holds more than four thousand cubits.

(19) End of the city — but if his measure ended in the middle of the city, even per the first view above that if he slept in the city he may walk the entire city even if very broad, nevertheless outside the city, even north and south, he may walk only opposite the width of his techum measure at his shevitah place (and until completion of his techum length); and if the city is four thousand and four cubits wide like his techum width, he has nothing north and south opposite the city at all. Per the Mechaber above, even within the city if broader than his techum width he does not walk the entire city width even in that half-city area — only until his techum width with corners, since his measure ended in the middle of the city.

(20) And he has two thousand, etc. — several acharonim disagree with the Rama here, because Rabbeinu Yonatan from whom this law is sourced implies the opposite: he has nothing north and south of the city, only if the city is not as broad as his techum width — after filling corners his techum width is four thousand and four cubits as above — then he also has outside the city north and south up to his techum width and no more; and if the city is larger than his techum width, even though within the city he may walk all of it even as large as Antioch since his length measure ended outside the city, outside north and south he has nothing; see Biur Halacha on what we wrote.

(21) North and south — to exclude the thousand cubits length he has west of the city, for there all agree we count only the width of his shevitah, like the first thousand toward the east of the city which are four cubits width with corner-fill making four thousand and four cubits width and no more; so too these.

(22) Even though he does not have so much width, etc. — meaning: if we considered width only opposite the eruv acquisition place it would not reach so much; now that we count city width plus the thousand cubits from both sides, nevertheless it is permitted.
