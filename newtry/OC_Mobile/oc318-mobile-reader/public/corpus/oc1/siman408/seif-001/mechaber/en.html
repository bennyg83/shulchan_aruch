Laws of placing eruv techumin and acquiring shevitah. Contains 4 seifim. (One may make eruv techumin and acquire shevitah at the end of the techum; therefore:) One who left the city on erev Shabbat and placed food for two meals distant from the city within the techum and established his shevitah there — even if he returned to the city and slept in his house — he is considered as if he rested Shabbat at the place where he placed the two meals. This is called eruv techumin. He may walk from his eruv place the next day two thousand cubits in every direction. Therefore when he walks from his eruv place two thousand cubits toward the city, he walks only within the city until the end of his measure. If the city is swallowed within his measure, the entire city counts as four cubits and he completes his measure beyond it. How so? If he placed his eruv one thousand cubits east of his house in the city, he may walk two thousand cubits east from his eruv, and two thousand cubits west from his eruv — one thousand from the eruv to his house and one thousand cubits from his house within the city — and he walks within the city only until the end of that thousand. If from his house to the end of the city is less than one thousand — even one cubit — so his measure ends beyond the city, the entire city counts as four cubits and he may walk 996 cubits beyond it to complete the two thousand. Therefore if he placed his eruv two thousand cubits from his house in the city, he loses the entire city and may walk from his house to the eruv two thousand cubits, but may not walk from his house into the city toward the west even one cubit.
(Some say that even if his measure ends in the middle of the city he may walk the entire city but not beyond it, and one may be lenient (Tur and Hagahot Maimoniot ch. 27 Laws of Shabbat in name of Semak and Rashi). And if his measure ends at the edge of the city, even though there is still an extension beyond the city, the entire city counts as four cubits — we do not say the city's extension counts as the city, to be stringent (Maggid ch. 27 Laws of Shabbat). Wherever one places eruv techumin and has two thousand in each direction, he is forbidden to walk north and south except opposite the width of the place where he acquired shevitah. If it is only four cubits — such as when the eruv is not in a private domain — he may walk in each direction two thousand cubits only within the width of his four cubits and their corners, as above siman 398 seif 2.
