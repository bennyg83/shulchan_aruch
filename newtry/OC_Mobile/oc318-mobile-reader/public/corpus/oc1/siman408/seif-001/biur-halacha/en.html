Distant from the city within the techum — see Mishna Berurah: even if from his house more than two thousand, as long as within two thousand to the city he may place his eruv and acquire shevitah there; so Magen Avraham, Tosafot Shantz, and Shulchan Aruch Atzei Shittim — not as Ohr Sameach wrote the opposite, that per that view he may place eruv only two thousand from his house, and if the city is large so from house to city end is more than two thousand he cannot place eruv at all. Magen Avraham proved this: when placed outside techum it is not eruv, Rambam wrote, only because he cannot reach there at bein hashemashot — so here within city techum where he can reach at bein hashemashot his eruv is valid, and we necessarily do not consider his house for this (I saw in Avnei Nezer he brought from Raavan proof for Ohr Sameach — see there; nevertheless for practical law it appears we follow Magen Avraham to be lenient, even if we are stringent per the first view, as Acharonim and Beit Meir ruled: one brings compelling proof from Rambam and halacha follows the lenient in eruv; also from Raavan there is not so strong a proof — possibly he discusses when his house was at city end; and truly it is a novel matter that in a large city he cannot place eruv outside the city because his house's two thousand are consumed at city end). However even per Magen Avraham, if he slept at home and walked on Shabbat to his eruv place and wishes to return, he cannot return per this view except until his two thousand end from the eruv place, as Magen Avraham explains; yet regarding placing eruv l'chatchila we cannot prevent him as long as within city techum, since he can go there at bein hashemashot and acquire shevitah there, and once he acquires shevitah there he is permitted to walk there on Shabbat — otherwise of what use is his eruv; such is Magen Avraham's explanation for one who examines plainly, and so from Tosafot Shantz, though seemingly difficult: if permitted to go there, likewise to return; and since forbidden to return home because farther than two thousand from his shevitah place, he should be forbidden to go there — but necessarily so per Magen Avraham; reason may be said: to go there, since he can acquire shevitah there after within techum as above, he is necessarily permitted to go and is like before eruv acquisition; but after he came there and they measure two thousand in every direction from there, the direction toward the city is not better than other directions — he has only two thousand from his shevitah place; so his reasoning should be explained. However in acharonim I saw they explained Magen Avraham differently: per him when his house is in the city and he wishes to place eruv two thousand from the city he must sleep at night in a house near the wall or at least be there at bein hashemashot at eruv acquisition (see Maamer Mordechai, Beit Meir, Machatzit Hashekel) — from Magen Avraham's language it seems the opposite, and in this Ohr Sameach would also agree to be lenient — in what does he argue (also from Avnei Nezer it seems he does not explain as they say, for otherwise his proof from Raavan against Magen Avraham is no proof); and truly it is novel that in a large city he cannot place eruv because his house's two thousand are consumed within the city, as I wondered above on Ohr Sameach; and if we require him at bein hashemashot near city wall that too is novel — such things Poskim should explain; I saw in Bigdei Yesha he wondered on this. All we expanded is only per the first view; per the second view all agree to be lenient to place eruv at end of two thousand from the city, and Ohr Sameach agrees; therefore for practical law, though Avnei Nezer wrote to be stringent like the Mechaber because it is majority of rishonim and Beit Meir agreed, nevertheless regarding requiring him specifically at bein hashemashot at city end it appears we need not be stringent; in this one may rely on Rama who is lenient as those who say, for possibly Rambam also agrees, as seems from Magen Avraham's plain words as we wrote.

Within the width of his four cubits — see Mishna Berurah: after filling corners his techum measure is a square tablet of four thousand and four cubits on four thousand and four cubits; and as written, until his techum width measure which is four thousand he may walk in all that area, even south to north, all agree — so in all acharonim, plain in Poskim and Shulchan Aruch siman 399 that corners are filled as a square tablet and in all that measure he may walk length and width; though Rabbeinu Yonatan seemingly does not imply so — Acharonim found support for his words from Radbaz — see there; but in all Poskim it is plain as written, and other acharonim explained also Rabbeinu Yonatan's view; and so from Rambam beginning ch. 27: the whole square tablet is his techum measure and he may walk in it like within the city itself even if very large; and there is support from Gemara per those who hold techum of two thousand is d'oraisa, learned from Levite cities — plainly the whole area belongs to the city, not a narrow path of four cubits; Radbaz's view truly requires study to reconcile well and it is a singular view.

Nevertheless if his measure ended, etc. — see Mishna Berurah: the whole city is considered four cubits even if it holds more than four thousand and four cubits; so Magen Avraham; likewise Perishah; so Chayei Adam. Even though in Even HaEzer he is stringent and holds we do not say the whole city is four cubits unless its width is also swallowed within the techum — so Bigdei Yesha — we did not cite the many lenient acharonim as above; also Chemdas Moshe, though initially he wanted to say likewise, nevertheless wrote Rabbeinu Yonatan does not imply so and agreed to be lenient as Magen Avraham (and necessarily this discusses when swallowed in his techum — otherwise not fit to doubt from the start); and especially when he slept in the city, per Rama one may be lenient in all aspects.

And he has two thousand, etc. — see Mishna Berurah: several acharonim wondered on this. Know that some acharonim (Avnei Nezer, cited by Tosafot Shantz, Chemdas Moshe, Bigdei Yesha) established Rama's words for practical law, only writing this specifically if the city is not four thousand and four cubits wide like his techum width — since the city is swallowed within his techum width they again give him opposite the city two thousand from both sides since the city counts only as four cubits; but if the city is broader than his techum measure (so Avnei Nezer, Bigdei Yesha, Chemdas Moshe — even if equal to his techum measure), though within the city he may walk all of it, nevertheless also for its width since his length techum measure ended at city end, from the sides he has nothing then — see there. And though the main difficulty Magen Avraham and supporters raised from Rabbeinu Yonatan is explained the opposite, it is not so difficult — one may say Rama wrote thus from his own reasoning; and though from his words in D.M. it seems he wrote this per Rabbeinu Yonatan, necessarily he wrote for practical law not his view, and there he wrote in language of possibility, and here ruled thus for practical law (what is written in Rama Rabbeinu Yonatan is known to be only from his notes, not Rama himself, as one who looks at first Poskim will see); nevertheless for practical law, since Rabbeinu Yonatan wrote explicitly to be stringent, difficult to be lenient here as Magen Avraham and Avnei Nezer; also Gra pointed to see Magen Avraham; so Chayei Adam ruled and rejected Avnei Nezer who elaborated to reconcile Rama's view and what Bigdei Yesha and Chemdas Moshe wrote to justify Rama who holds there is no difference width from length — as in length of city they give him a thousand toward west because city counts only four cubits, so too in width since city is four cubits he has two thousand from both sides; no need to reject Magen Avraham and supporters who wrote per plain implication of R' Yonatan that there is difference between length and width; reason is simple: for his measure westward, since he placed eruv east of city one thousand cubits and his techum measure goes through the city — after we count whole city as four cubits he still has another thousand westward; but for sides where he need not walk through the city — shall a city or village swallowed in his techum expand his techum measure? On the contrary it is reasonable his techum measure extends equally from the sides from his shevitah place even opposite the city, and only for walking within the city itself we are lenient that it counts as four cubits even if broader than his techum measure, but for the sides his techum does not expand at all; similar wrote Chayei Adam; nevertheless since it emerged from Rama to be lenient and Avnei Nezer, Bigdei Yesha, and Chemdas Moshe — the lenient in time of need has whom to rely on, as it appears to me.
