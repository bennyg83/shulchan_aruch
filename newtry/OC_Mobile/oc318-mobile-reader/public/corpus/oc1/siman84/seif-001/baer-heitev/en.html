And in an old one. Even though there is no person there now. Talmud and Beit Yosef. And see above siman 45, seif katan 3, where I wrote, in Magen Avraham's name, that in a mikveh it is permitted when there is no person there; see there. And Taz wrote here that this immersion room truly has the law of the middle room of a bathhouse, and ab initio one should not bless there any berachah except the blessing on immersion, which is necessary because it pertains to immersion; but in the water all berachot are permitted, as written regarding Kerias Shema in siman 74 — that it is permitted in murky or clear water if one's heart is not within the water; and see Yad Aharon and Peri Chadash siman 85. (And see in Eliyah Rabbah a lengthy analysis of this.)

Forbidden. For shalom is a name of the Holy One, blessed be He. And the same applies in filthy alleyways — inquiring after shalom is forbidden. And regarding a man whose name is Shalom — Taz permits mentioning his name in a bathhouse and in a beit hakisei, for one does not intend except that person. Bach and Magen Avraham hold that it is forbidden. But in a vernacular language it is permitted to call him. And Peri Chadash in this siman disagrees with Bach and holds that the name Shalom is forbidden to call even in a vernacular language; see there. But one whose name is Shimshon is permitted; see there in siman 85. If a man asked him a halachic matter, it is forbidden to tell him: we do not respond in a bathhouse. But in the middle room it is permitted to say so. One who wishes to drink in a bathhouse should bless outside with intent to drink inside.
