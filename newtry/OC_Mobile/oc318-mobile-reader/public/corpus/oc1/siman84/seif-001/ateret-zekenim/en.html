A new bathhouse, etc.; and in the middle room, where some stand there dressed and some naked, etc. — even though there is no person there now, since there was once naked and dressed people there, it is forbidden to recite Kerias Shema and prayer; and so too the inner room, for regarding a bathhouse they said: even though there is no person there (Shabbos 2a).

And it is permitted to contemplate words of Torah, etc.; and everywhere that contemplation of words of Torah is permitted, it is permitted to rule there regarding permission or prohibition, but one should not state the reason for the matter, as written in the gloss in siman 85.

And in the inner room, even inquiring after shalom is forbidden. The reason is because it is written: And He called him shalom; and Rashi explained: And He called Gideon to the Holy One, blessed be He, "the Omnipresent is his shalom" (in the first chapter of Shabbos) — and Rashi did not explain so in Judges chapter 6; see there. And it is not specifically inquiring after shalom; rather, it is even forbidden to call — even in the inner bathhouse — a man whose name is Shalom; and so too in a beit haknesset. But in the Ashkenaz language or in a vernacular language there is no prohibition; and even though names that are not erased may not be mentioned in a beit haknesset and in a bathhouse, even in Ashkenaz or vernacular — for matters of sanctity it is forbidden to say them in secular language, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 3 of Laws of Kerias Shema — for there they are set apart for holiness; but in mentioning shalom it is not set apart for holiness, and there is no prohibition except when one mentions it specifically in the holy tongue and not in secular language (my teacher in Bach). And one who wishes to drink in a bathhouse should bless in the outer room with intent to drink in the inner room (Agudah). And the prohibition of a bathhouse is only because of filth; therefore these mikvaot whose water is cold have no filth and are permitted (Kesef Mishnah in the name of R' Zerachiah) — and this is straightforward.
