(1) They did not bathe — meaning: even once; for if they bathed in it, it is like an old bathhouse for all its laws, since there was designation and action in it.

(2) It is permitted to recite — and even though in a beit haknesset we rule above in siman 83 seif 2 that it is forbidden through mere designation, a beit haknesset is different, for it is more repugnant.

(3) And some naked — some say that even if now all are dressed, or there is no person there at all, nevertheless, since the room is designated for naked and dressed people to be there, the law of a bathhouse applies to it somewhat. And there are those who are lenient in this; and according to their words, in a room where the bath attendant regularly is, and people stand there naked too, it is permitted to say words of Torah and Kiddush and prayer when there is no person there — and all this regarding the middle room. But the inner room, where all stand there naked and bathe there, is more repugnant; and according to all, its law is like a beit hakisei, and it is forbidden even when there is no person there. And even if that bathhouse's custom was to bathe in it only in summer days, and in winter days it is closed — nevertheless then too all laws of a bathhouse apply to it, for its name has not yet been uprooted from it. But if they made a physical change in it — such as they removed the oven there and the like, and made it a dwelling house — it is permitted to place a mezuzah at its entrance and to bless upon it and to recite and pray there; and the same law applies regarding a beit haknesset.

(4) But not, etc. — and so too all berachot and words of Torah are forbidden there. And it is implied from Taz's words that after the fact, if one blessed there, he does not return and bless; and so too in Kerias Shema he does not return and recite, even if a naked person was present at that time in the middle room — but in a manner that he did not stand opposite him at the time of reading, or such as he turned his face and body from him; for if not so, he returns and recites because he read opposite ervah (Peri Megadim). And regarding an immersion room, Taz concludes that all its law is like the middle room of a bathhouse, and one should not bless there any berachah except the blessing on immersion, which is necessary there — after the fact it is considered valid. And all this if the one blessing is not naked, and also that he not stand opposite naked people at the time of the berachah — for then it is forbidden because of ervah, unless he turned his face and body from them. And the same applies in all this to a woman blessing in an immersion room; and Peri Megadim wrote that it is better that she bless in the water (and see above in siman 74 seif katan 16, who wrote that it is good that she muddy it with her feet so her limbs not be visible in the water). The reason is that there, in the place of the water, it is more reasonable that it is not considered like the middle room of a bathhouse. And it is straightforward that there too she needs at the time of the berachah to turn her face and body from the naked women standing in that immersion room, as proven above in siman 79 seif 2; see there. And behold, in Taz's view, even when there is no naked person there, one should not bless there another berachah — per the stringent view above regarding the middle room; but from Magen Avraham above in siman 45 it is implied to be lenient in this, that there is no filth; and so wrote Birkhei Yosef. And behold, in a mikveh into which hot water is poured, one may say that there is steam in it; nevertheless it is possible to say that there is not so much filth and steam as in a bathhouse. And these mikvaot that stand in the bathhouse itself — in the bathhouse itself it is certainly forbidden to bless there the blessing on immersion; and even in the water it requires study — for one may say the bathhouse steam is abundant, and in the water too it is forbidden (Peri Megadim; and see what he wrote further on this in siman 45 in Eshel Avraham).

(5) To contemplate there — and even opposite naked people; for contemplation opposite ervah has no prohibition.

(6) Even shalom is forbidden — for shalom is a name of the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is said: And He called him shalom. And the same applies: it is forbidden to give shalom to one's fellow in filthy alleyways, for all its law is like the inner room. And regarding a man whose name is Shalom — some forbid in a bathhouse to call him by his name, except in a vernacular language; and some permit, since he does not intend the matter of shalom but only mentioning that man's name. And in matters of scribes one follows the lenient view, and so is the custom; and one who fears Heaven should be stringent, since many of the later authorities forbid. And one should swallow the mem and say only "Shalo" without mem, or "Sholon" with nun. And also in a bill of authorization it is proper not to write shalom with a vav, but only shalem, which is found thrown in refuse heaps.

(7) To answer Amen — upon one who said outside or in any room some berachah; and it appears that this is specifically in the inner room, which is like a beit haknesset; but in the middle room it is permitted to answer. Also, if a man asked him a halachic matter in the inner room, it is forbidden to tell him: we do not respond in a bathhouse. And the same applies when one man gave him shalom in a bathhouse — it is forbidden to say: we do not respond in a bathhouse; but he may say in any case: it is a bathhouse — for this is not even contemplation. But in the middle room it is permitted to tell him: we do not respond in a bathhouse. And some are stringent also in the middle room in this — only if there is reason of ways of peace may one say in this language. One should not drink in a bathhouse, for behold it is forbidden to bless there, and without a berachah one should not drink. And one who wishes to drink — Peri Megadim concludes that he should bless in the outer room or in the bath attendant's room when there are no naked people there (or that he enter the immersion room in water up to above his nakedness), and also cover his head and bless and drink there a little with intent to drink in the bathhouse — and this is when the rooms are in one house within the bathhouse; behold, to bless entirely outside the bathhouse is forbidden. And it is straightforward that to bless and not drink at all — only in the bathhouse — is certainly forbidden to interrupt between the berachah and drinking; and see above at the end of siman 62 in Mishna Berurah and Biur Halachah. Mezuzah in a bathhouse — in the inner and middle rooms one is exempt; and the same applies to a bathhouse that has a courtyard before it, and in the courtyard some stand there naked — exempt too. And the bath attendant's dwelling is obligated in mezuzah, for it is a complete room; but because naked people are commonly there, one must cover the mezuzah. A summer house that they sometimes make into a bathhouse once or twice a year, or a winter house — is obligated in mezuzah; and some say one must cover the mezuzah. And so too it is permitted to pray inside it. A house in which bathtubs stand for bathing — it is implied from the later authorities that its law is like the inner room, for steam and filth are abundant there.
