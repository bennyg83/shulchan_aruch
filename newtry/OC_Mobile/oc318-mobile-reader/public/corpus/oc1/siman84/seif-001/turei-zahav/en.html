They did not bathe in it, etc. — for thereby a bathhouse is more lenient than a beit haknesset, for a beit haknesset is more repugnant than when one merely designated a beit haknesset.

And in an old one in the outer room, etc. Kesef Mishnah wrote, and so he wrote: R' Menachem wrote, and I say: since the prohibition of a bathhouse is because people stand there naked, the same applies to these mikvaot in which women immerse — it is forbidden to bless or recite therein. And it is not reasonable that the essence of the bathhouse prohibition is only because of filth and steam that is in it through its use with hot water; but these mikvaot whose water is cold have no filth and are permitted. And it requires study — for regarding mezuzah, an immersion room is like a bathhouse; so far. And let it enter your mind from what is written in the blessing on immersion in Yoreh De'ah siman 200 — Rosh wrote: when she removes her garment while standing in her robe, she blesses and removes the robe and immerses; so far — it implies that blessing applies in an immersion room. And it seems to me that this immersion room truly has the law of the middle room of a bathhouse, where some are naked, etc.; and ab initio one should not bless there any berachah except the blessing on immersion, which is necessary there because it pertains to immersion — it is like the middle room of a bathhouse, where one does not remove tefillin if he already put them on, as written above siman 45. So too, it is considered after the fact, since it is impossible otherwise; but in the water all berachot are permitted, as written regarding Kerias Shema in siman 74 — that it is permitted in murky or clear water if one's heart is not within the water.

Even inquiring after shalom is forbidden. So in the first chapter of Shabbos: it is forbidden for a person to give shalom to his fellow in a bathhouse, as it is written: And He called him shalom. But from then on, emunah too is forbidden to say in a beit haknesset, as it is written: HaEl haNe'eman; and Rav said it is permitted to say emunah in a beit haknesset — there the name itself is not called so, for we translate HaEl haNe'eman; here the name itself is called shalom. From this it implies that the prohibition is specifically regarding one who intends in saying shalom upon shalom itself, in the manner of those who give shalom to wayfarers from the road; but if there is a man whose name is Shalom, certainly there is no prohibition to mention his name in a bathhouse or beit haknesset, for he does not intend the matter of shalom but only mentioning that man's name. Therefore they did not say in the Gemara: it is forbidden to say shalom in a bathhouse, but rather: it is forbidden to give shalom; and so too afterward: but from now on emunah too, etc. — he did not say "faithful" but emunah, which is a matter of faithfulness — not so in mentioning the word as a mere sign; this did not arise even on the view of the questioner to forbid. And so too Tur and Shulchan Aruch mentioned only inquiring after shalom and not saying shalom. And further proof: the Rambam wrote that it is permitted to say in a bathhouse "Compassionate"; and Raavad on this wrote: we do not find the name Compassionate except regarding the Creator, and it is forbidden to say it in a beit haknesset; so far. And Kesef Mishnah brought that R' Zerachiah disagrees on this — for behold it is written: gracious, compassionate, and righteous are before You — that wherever a person has the same designation as the Holy One, blessed be He, it is permitted to say it; all the more so calling a name alone, where one does not intend except as a sign for calling that man — which is straightforward that it is permitted. And Maharshal wrote that it is forbidden to call a man in a bathhouse if his name is Shalom and he does not intend otherwise — in my humble opinion.
