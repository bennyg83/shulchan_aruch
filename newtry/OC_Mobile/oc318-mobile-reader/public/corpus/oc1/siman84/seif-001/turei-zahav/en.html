And in an old one in the outer room, etc. In Maggid Mishneh he wrote thus: Rabbeinu Manoach wrote, and some say: since the bathhouse prohibition is because people stand there naked, the same applies to these mikva'ot in which women immerse — forbidden to bless or recite inside. And it is not reasonable that the essence of the bathhouse prohibition is only filth and steam through use in hot water; but these mikva'ot whose water is cold have no filth and are permitted — and study is needed, for regarding mezuzah an immersion room is like a bathhouse — so far. And it is clear from what is written in the blessing of immersion in Yoreh De'ah siman 200: Rosh wrote — when she removes her garment while standing in a robe she blesses, removes the robe, and immerses — so far; it implies blessing is relevant in the immersion room. And it seems to me this immersion room truly has the law of the middle room of the bathhouse — some naked, etc.; and ab initio one should not bless there any blessing except the immersion blessing, which is necessary there and pertains to immersion — it is like the middle room of a bathhouse, for one does not remove tefillin if he already donned them, as written above siman 45. Likewise after the fact it is considered valid, since there is no alternative; but in water all blessings are permitted, as written regarding Keriat Shema in siman 74 — permitted in murky or clear if his heart is not in the water:
Even greeting with Shalom is forbidden. So in the first chapter of Shabbat: it is forbidden for a person to greet his fellow in the bathhouse — for it says: and he called to him the Lord Shalom; only from now on Ne'emanut is also forbidden to say in a privy — for it is written: the faithful God; and Rav permitted saying Ne'emanut in a privy — there the name itself is not called thus, for it is translated "faithful God"; here the name itself is called Shalom. It implies specifically when he intends in saying Shalom actual Shalom, in the manner of those who greet travelers from the road; but if there is a person whose name is Shalom, certainly there is no prohibition to mention his name in the bathhouse or privy, for he does not intend the matter of Shalom but mentioning that person's name — perforce the gemara did not say it is forbidden to say Shalom in the bathhouse, but forbidden to greet with Shalom; and likewise afterward "only from now on Ne'emanut" — it did not say Ne'eman but Ne'emanut, which is the matter of faithfulness; not so in mentioning the word merely as a sign — it would not come up even according to the questioner to forbid. And likewise Tur and Shulchan Aruch mentioned only greeting with Shalom and not saying Shalom. Further proof: Rambam wrote it is permitted to say in the bathhouse Rachum; and Ra'avad wrote on this: we do not find the name Rachum except for the Creator, and it is forbidden to say it in a privy — so far; and they brought it in Maggid Mishneh — Rav Hai Gaon disagrees, for behold it is written: gracious and merciful and righteous before You — behold, whatever attribute a person has, as the Holy One has, it is permitted to say; all the more mentioning a name alone, where one intends only as a sign of calling the person — plainly permitted.
