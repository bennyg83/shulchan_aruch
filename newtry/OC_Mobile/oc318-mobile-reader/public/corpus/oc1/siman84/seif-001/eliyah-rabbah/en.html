[1] And some naked, etc. Kesef Mishneh wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Manoach — this is the language of Or Zaru'a: since the prohibition of a bathhouse is because people stand there naked, the same applies to these mikva'ot in which women immerse — it is forbidden to bless or recite inside them. And it is not reasonable that the essence of the bathhouse prohibition is only on account of filth and steam that comes through use in hot water; but these mikva'ot whose water is cold have no filth and are permitted — and study is needed, for regarding mezuzah an immersion room is like a bathhouse — so far his words. And Nachalat Tzvi wrote in general that it is permitted in mikva'ot, and Rama ruled thus in Yoreh De'ah siman 200; and it appears he resolves that mezuzah is different, for we require a dwelling made for honor, as in Yoreh De'ah siman 286 — so it seems to me. But I wonder: what is different from the middle room, where there is no filth — for people sweat only in the inner room, and the middle room is only where they enter naked and dress immediately, as explained in Shabbat daf 44 in the gemara and poskim; and even so it is forbidden to recite inside — and it is a great strain to say that since they exit from the inner to the middle naked there is filth and steam. Therefore it seems to me Rabbeinu Manoach, who permits in mikva'ot, permits specifically when there is no person there, and it is reasonable to him that in the middle room it is permitted when there is no person there — examine carefully. And see Magen Avraham siman 45 that he did not go down to this. And from this there is a difficulty on Bach, who ruled in general that also in the middle room it is forbidden when there is no person there; and Olah Tamid wrote on him that his words are correct. And in my humble opinion it is permitted when there is no person there, and so is implied from Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch and Levush, who wrote that the inner room is more repulsive, etc. — it implies the middle room is permitted when there is no person there; and so it seems clear to me from Rashi at the end of chapter Mi sheMeto, who explained on that which says "bathhouse — they said" even when there is no person there — so is his language: it is forbidden to think in it — so far; behold it deals specifically with the inner room — for if in the middle room, behold it is permitted to think there. But it is difficult: what does the gemara in Shabbat daf 10 challenge on Rav Ada, who said it is permitted to pray in the bathhouse — from that which says bathhouse even when there is no person there? Perhaps Rav Ada deals only with the middle room. And one may say the plain bathhouse deals with the inner room — for in chapter Kirah we say that in the bathhouse it is forbidden to think, and we establish it is forbidden specifically in the inner room. And further: if so, Rav Ada should have explained that it deals with the middle room, lest we err to be lenient also in the inner room. And know: if not so, what would the gemara challenge from "they designated it for a bathhouse" — that it should be forbidden like a privy? Perhaps the middle room is different, for there is no prohibition of designation, only it is not comparable to a privy; rather perforce it is plain to the gemara that Rav Ada deals even with the inner room. And now it is settled what the gemara challenges in Bava Kamma daf 86: the bathhouse carries [liability for] humiliation, for people stand naked — and they do not establish it in the middle room.
