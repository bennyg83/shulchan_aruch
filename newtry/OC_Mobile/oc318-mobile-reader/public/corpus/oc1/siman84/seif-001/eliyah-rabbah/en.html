(1) And some naked, etc. Kesef Mishnah wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Menachem — this is the language of Or Zarua: since the prohibition of a bathhouse is because people stand there naked, the same applies to mikvaot in which women immerse — it is forbidden to bless or recite therein. And it is not reasonable that the essence of the bathhouse prohibition is only because of filth and steam that comes in it through its use with hot water; but these mikvaot whose water is cold have no filth and are permitted. And it requires study — for regarding mezuzah, an immersion room is like a bathhouse; so far his language. Nachalat Tzvi wrote without qualification that it is permitted in mikvaot; and so ruled Rama in Yoreh De'ah siman 200. And it appears that he is inclined to answer that mezuzah is different, for we require a dwelling made for honor, as stated in Yoreh De'ah siman 286 — so it seems to me. But I am puzzled: what is different about the middle room, where there is no filth — for people sweat only in the inner room, and the middle room is only where one enters naked and dresses immediately, as explained in Shabbos 44a in the Talmud and among the poskim; and even so it is forbidden to recite therein. And it is a great stretch to say that since they come out from the inner to the middle naked, there would be filth and steam. Therefore it seems to me that Rabbeinu Menachem, who permits in mikvaot, permits specifically when there is no person there; and it is reasonable to him that in the middle room it is permitted when there is no person there — examine closely. And see Magen Avraham siman 45, who did not address this. From this there is a difficulty on Bach, who ruled without qualification that even in the middle room it is forbidden when there is no person there; and Olas Tamid wrote on him that his words are correct. In my humble opinion it is permitted when there is no person there; and so it is implied from Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch and Levush, who wrote that the inner room is more repugnant, etc. — it implies that the middle room is permitted when there is no person there. And so it seems to me proven from Rashi at the end of the chapter "One whose relative died," who explained on what they say: a bathhouse — even though there is no person there — his language: it is forbidden to contemplate in it; so far. Behold, we deal specifically with the inner room — for if in the middle room, behold, contemplation is permitted there. But it is difficult: what does the Talmud challenge in Shabbos 10a on Rav Ada, who said it is permitted to pray in a bathhouse, from that which they said regarding a bathhouse: even though there is no person there — perhaps Rav Ada deals specifically with the middle room? And one may say that an unspecified bathhouse deals with the inner room — for in chapter Kirah we say that in a bathhouse contemplation is forbidden, and we establish that specifically in the inner room it is forbidden. And further, if so Rav Ada should have explained that we deal with the middle room, so he would not be mistaken to be lenient also in the inner room. And you should know that if not so, what they challenge from "they invited him to a bathhouse" would be difficult — that it should be forbidden like a beit hakisei; perhaps the middle room is different, for there is no prohibition of invitation there, but rather it is not comparable to a beit hakisei. Rather, perforce it is straightforward to the Talmud that Rav Ada deals even with the inner room. And now what the Talmud challenges in Bava Kamma 86b is resolved — a bathhouse is subject to humiliation because people stand naked, and they do not establish it in the middle room; and furthermore there is room to distinguish. Taz wrote that in the water all berachot are permitted, as above in siman 74.
