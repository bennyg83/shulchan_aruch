And in an old one. See Be'er Heitev and Responsa Beit Yehudah, siman 10. See Machazik Berachah in the name of Mor U'Ketziah, who questions the custom of women reciting the blessing in a mikveh where they bathe in hot water in the immersion room. They are forbidden to do so; rather, a woman should recite the blessing before entering and should not interrupt with speech. Machazik Berachah writes that because most women would interrupt during such a period, which is not brief, they relied on Turei Zahav, who considers it an after-the-fact situation; see there. See Panim Me'irot, part 1, siman 87, who writes that if a physical alteration was made to the bathhouse (such as removing the stove or the like) and it was converted into a house, it is permitted to live there, recite blessings there, and affix a mezuzah to its entrance; see there.

One must therefore be careful not to call a person named Shalom by that name in a bathhouse or privy, but only by translating its meaning into a foreign language. See there what he writes about the Gemara's discussion in Sotah concerning Shimshon. See what I wrote in Responsa Beit Ephraim, Even HaEzer, siman 86, page 162b, explaining Tosafos in Shabbat, page 10, regarding the statement that the divine name itself is called Shalom; the words of Kesef Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh concerning Rambam's omission of the law that one may not extend a greeting of Shalom in a bathhouse; and the words of Nekudot HaKesef in Yoreh De'ah, siman 285. See there. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 266, regarding not writing the full word Shalom. Birkei Yosef, siman 85, writes that most people are not careful about this. See there, where he cites manuscript novellae of Ritva on Shabbat in the name of Tosafos, that Shalom is among the divine names that may be erased, and people are therefore not careful about writing it in ordinary documents. Rosh writes likewise in Responsa, klal 3. In manuscript novellae on Shabbat, Ran writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yechiel that it is forbidden to write Shalom in an ordinary letter, which is liable to be thrown into a refuse heap; accordingly, our practice is not to write the full word Shalom. These are his words. See Tashbetz, part 1, siman 172; Responsa Rashbash, siman 267; Rashbash, part 2, siman 220; Responsa Zekan Aharon, siman 72 and siman 188.
