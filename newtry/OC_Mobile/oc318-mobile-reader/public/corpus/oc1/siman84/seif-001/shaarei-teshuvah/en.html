And in an old one — see Ba'er Hetev. And see in responsa Beit Yehudah siman 10; and see in Magen Gibborim in the name of Moreh veKitziah to protest the custom that women bless in the mikveh where they bathe — in the hot immersion room it is forbidden except to bless before she enters and not interrupt in speech. And Magen Gibborim wrote that since most will interrupt in such a time that is not short, they relied on Taz that it is considered after the fact; see there. See in Penei Meiros volume 1 siman 87 that if they made a physical change in the body of the bathhouse and removed the oven and the like and made it a house — it is permitted to dwell and bless and place a mezuzah at its entrance; see there.

Forbidden — see Ba'er Hetev. And see in Bechor Shor in his novellae on Sanhedrin 65, who wrote in explanation of Bach's words and brought proof for his words, and concludes that the main view is as Bach's words; and one must beware of calling a man whose name is Shalom in a bathhouse and in a beit haknesset, except as explained in a vernacular language; see there what he wrote on the Talmud's words in Sotah regarding Shimshon. And see what is written in responsa Beit Ephraim, part Even haEzer siman 86, page 162 side b, in explanation of Tosafos Shabbos 10a on this — that the name itself is called shalom; and on Kesef Mishnah's words; and Lechem Mishneh on the Rambam, who omitted the law that there is no inquiring after shalom in a bathhouse; and in the words of Nachalat Tzvi in Yoreh De'ah siman 285; see there. And see Yoreh De'ah siman 266 not to write shalom shalem; and in Be'er Heitev siman 85 he wrote that most of the world are not careful; see there that he brought from Ritva's novellae on Shabbos manuscript in the name of Tosafos that shalom is among names that are erased, and they are not careful to write it in bills of authorization; and so too Rosh in responsa kelal 3; and Ran in novellae on Shabbos manuscript wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yechiel that it is forbidden to write shalom in a bill of authorization, which is commonly thrown in refuse heaps; and so we practice not to write shalom shalem; so far.
