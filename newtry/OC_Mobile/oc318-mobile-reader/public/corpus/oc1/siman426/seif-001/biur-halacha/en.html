(ב) And benefit from its light — see Mishna Berurah what he wrote regarding if he estimates the moon will be covered in clouds, etc. It is from responsa Radbaz and Magen Avraham and Sha'arei Teshuvah and the Gaon R' Chaim of Sanz in his glosses wrote it appears to him that even if he had not yet begun to bless until the moon was covered — as long as not more than kedei dibbur passed since the moon shone, he may l'chatchila begin and bless even if a cloud covered it; and its source is from above siman 227 seif 3 — see there; and one may reject somewhat, for there regarding thunder and lightning the blessing was fixed only on the world's custom, but here we require benefiting from its light, and therefore he does not bless after the benefit ended — as regarding Birkat Hamazon one does not bless after finishing his meal, and as above siman 167 seif 8 — see there in Mishna Berurah; and it implies even if he remembered within kedei dibbur. And regarding a blind person see Mishna Berurah — it is from Rashal in his book siman 77, and Maharikash disagrees; but Magen Avraham and Avraham Chaviv and Peri Chadash and Sha'arei Teshuvah brought Rashal's words for halacha; they further brought Radbaz's responsum that if there is a screen between him and the moon — if the screen is thick until the moon is not visible to him, he does not bless and it is a blessing in vain, as explained explicitly in his responsum part 1 siman 341; and nevertheless it appears Rashal too agrees to this because then the whole world does not benefit from its light — as Rashal wrote there; and from this it is not difficult also from Borei me'orei ha'esh that Rabeinu Yechiel ruled in chapter 2 of Megillah a blind person does not bless because the mitzvah is not dependent on benefit alone but specifically on seeing with benefit as taught there "one does not bless until he sees the flame and uses its light" — and specifically Birkat me'orei ha'esh; but Birkat HaLevana it appears he may bless well even though they also said in it "until they benefit from its light" — nevertheless they did not say he must see its light but that the world benefits from its light, for this blessing is on the creation of His world and its renewal, and it is similar to a blind person who may bless Yotzer hame'orot — end of Rashal's words; behold he too holds we require the world benefit from its light, and automatically if covered in clouds they do not benefit from its light — Rashal agrees to Radbaz's law; but Radbaz appears at first glance he does not agree to Rashal regarding a blind person and holds like Maharikash who wrote in his responsum: we learned one does not bless on light until they benefit from its light, and Ulla said until he recognizes, etc.; and regarding Birkat HaLevana too if the screen is thin, etc. — and the same regarding a cloud, etc.; and one may ask: here it is on the world's custom he blesses even when he will not benefit from its light — what of it? This is not so, for R' Yehudah said "one who sees the moon in its renewal," etc.; and we also learn in Tractate Soferim one does not bless on the moon except, etc., and fixes his eyes toward it, etc. — behold explicitly one must look at it — end of his words — see there; from all this it appears at first glance he argues on Rashal regarding a blind person and holds like Maharikash; and therefore regarding a blind person it seems better he not bless himself but ask another to take him out with the blessing Who at His word created heavens [and the other utterances said at kiddush levana he may say himself].
