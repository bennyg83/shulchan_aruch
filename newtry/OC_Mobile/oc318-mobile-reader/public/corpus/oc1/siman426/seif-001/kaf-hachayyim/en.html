1) [Se'if 1] One who sees the moon in its renewal, etc. Women are exempt because this is a time-bound positive mitzvah. Although women fulfill other positive mitzvos, such as sukkah and the like, they do not fulfill this mitzvah because they caused the moon's defect. Shelah. Sanhedrin 42a states regarding Birchas HaLevanah, 'Our women also recite the blessing,' which somewhat implies that women do recite it. It is possible that this does not literally refer to women, but is merely a common expression. Magen Avraham, beginning of the siman. Since the Gemara implies that they recite a blessing, it appears that women should hear the blessing from others and intend to fulfill their obligation, or recite only, 'Blessed is He Who renews the months,' as mentioned in the Gemara, which is the blessing for women. It is possible that Shelah would also agree to this, and objects only to women reciting the longer text used by men, while there is no objection to this shorter text.
2) There. One who sees the moon, etc. A blind person is obligated in Birchas HaLevanah. Responsa Maharshal, siman 77; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, there. Maharikash, however, writes that since the wording says, 'One who sees the moon,' the obligation depends upon sight, and a blind person should not recite the blessing. Birkei Yosef, siman 224, subsection 1, cites his words. He likewise writes in Machazik Berachah, siman 229, subsection 6, that although Maharshal in Responsa, siman 77, and those who agree with him maintain that a blind person recites Birchas HaLevanah, the matter nevertheless constitutes a doubt concerning blessings. Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 231c, likewise writes that the proper course is not to recite the blessing but to hear it from another person who is reciting it, thereby satisfying all opinions. Chesed LaAlafim, subsection 2, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayikra, subsection 23, write likewise. A blind person may personally recite the other passages said during Kiddush Levanah.
3) One who sees by means of eyeglasses may recite the blessing even if unable to see without eyeglasses. Shaarei Teshuvah, subsection 1; Ben Ish Chai, there.
3) A congregation that came to recite Birchas HaLevanah but found no one who knew the blessing except one blind person, who then stood and recited it, may have fulfilled their obligation even according to Maharikash, who maintains that the blind person is not personally obligated. He may nevertheless discharge the obligation of others, similar to the ruling in siman 297 that one who cannot smell may recite the blessing on behalf of others. Sdei HaAretz, volume 3, siman 9; Ikarei HaDat, end of siman 16.
4) There. One who sees the moon in its renewal, etc. Those who travel by sea do not set sail until five days after the moon's renewal. They are careful not to undergo bloodletting near its renewal. Flax found soaking or in a cauldron for cooking at the moon's renewal is damaged, and various forms of work require care not to be performed when the moon renews. Sefer HaChinuch, siman 403; Birkei Yosef, subsection 2; Shaarei Teshuvah, there. See above, siman 417, subsection 22. 5) There. One recites, 'Who, by His word, created the heavens,' etc. 'So that they do not alter their assignment' means the mitzvah that He commanded them to conduct themselves according to the movement of their cycles. 'Joyful and glad' is as written, 'It rejoices like a mighty man.' 'Workers of truth' means that they do not alter their order. 'And to the moon the Holy One, blessed is He, said that it should renew itself each month.' 'A crown of glory it is for those borne from the womb' means that it is a sign for the Jewish people, who count by the moon, that they too are destined to be renewed from their exile like it and to glorify their Creator. Bach's text reads, 'in their greatness.' Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 2, likewise writes that this does not refer to the destruction of the Beis HaMikdash but to the renewal of souls. Rashi on Sanhedrin 42a. Tosafos there write that some versions read the singular, 'Worker of truth,' referring to the Holy One, blessed is He, Who diminished the moon in truth and justice. The blessing therefore continues, 'And to the moon He said that it should renew itself.' Rabbeinu Yonah likewise writes that 'Worker of truth, Whose work is truth' refers to the Creator. He is truth, and the work itself is also true because it endures, as it is written, 'He established them forever and ever.' If the wording referred to the heavenly hosts, it would have sufficed to say, 'Workers of truth.' Why would it also need to say that the work they perform is truth? Beis Yosef. Bach likewise writes that this blessing should be recited according to the version of Tosafos. See there. Birkei Yosef, subsection 3, also writes that the correct wording is, 'Worker of truth, Whose work is truth,' and that this is the version of the Arizal's disciples, who explain it according to the hidden dimension of Torah. Rabbeinu Bachya in parashas Ha'azinu writes likewise. Ruach Chaim cites this, as does Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 232d. This is also the version in Mevo She'arim by the Arizal, folio 60c; Peri Etz Chaim, gate 19, chapter 3; and the siddur of Rashash. See there for the explanation of the entire blessing according to the hidden dimension of Torah. One should pause between the words 'that it should renew itself' and 'a crown.' Eshel Avraham, subsection 9.
