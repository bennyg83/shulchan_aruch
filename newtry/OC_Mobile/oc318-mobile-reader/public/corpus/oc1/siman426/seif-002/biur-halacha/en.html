(א) But, etc. — and know that by law one may sanctify even alone as Peri Chadash wrote, and all the more he does not require ten — only l'chatchila it is a mitzvah to do it with a multitude, for in a multitude is the King's glory; therefore if he knows he will have opportunity to bless with ten after some days until the tenth night, he must wait, and not more — as written in the gloss regarding motza'ei Shabbat; so too in Eshel Avraham; and per what Chayei Adam proved in general seif 8 that with three too it is called a multitude — one need not wait for ten until the tenth night, and especially in winter on cloudy days.

(ב) On motza'ei Shabbat — and Bach's view is not to let the mitzvah sour until motza'ei Shabbat, but after three days passed from the molad they sanctify; and so in Ma'aseh Rav [based on the practice of the Gra] — and see at the end of the siman.

(ג) Before Tisha b'Av — and Magen Avraham wrote the same for a mourner that he should not sanctify during the seven days of mourning, and concludes: if he will not complete mourning by the 10th of the month he should not wait until the night of the 11th but sanctify during his mourning days — end of his words; and his words are very difficult to permit sanctifying during mourning days because of a mere concern lest five nights be covered in clouds — behold on motza'ei Tisha b'Av we do not sanctify and wait until the night after, which is the night of the 11th, and we are not concerned for five cloudy nights; and especially per what we wrote in Mishna Berurah in the name of Darkei Moshe: if Tisha b'Av falls on Thursday we wait until motza'ei Shabbat which is the night of the 12th — behold for some matter whatever it is, like kiddush on motza'ei Tisha b'Av there is no concern since mourning already ended, only he is not so in joy — we are not concerned lest he cannot sanctify afterward; and all the more for a mourner forbidden in joy — certainly one need not fear lest he cannot sanctify afterward; and perhaps Magen Avraham wrote this per what he brought afterward in small seif 6 that also on motza'ei Tisha b'Av one may sanctify — see there; but this too is difficult: one, Mishna Berurah did not decide thus there but wrote that for this reason one may at least be lenient for an individual fasting to join the congregation — see there; and further, in truth this matter does not apply there, for even per the Acharonim' conclusion there to decide one must sanctify on motza'ei Tisha b'Av and not wait for days afterward — nevertheless they agree for a mourner to wait until mourning days end, for that case is different since there is no concern, for the mourner already ended; but during mourning days, and especially private mourning which is stricter — certainly he should not sanctify during mourning as long as there remains time until half the month; and so it implies from Maharil's wording on Tisha b'Av: he wrote on motza'ei Shabbat Chazon Maharil did not sanctify the moon and delayed until motza'ei Shabbat Nachamu and gave the reason: since for us receiving the Divine Presence is considered, and we receive it only in joy, and nowadays we are forbidden in joy — end of his words; and it is plain private mourning is no less, and on the contrary it is reasonable that even per Darkei Moshe's writing — all this if Tisha b'Av was on Thursday, but if on Tuesday he should not wait until motza'ei Shabbat — see there — all that there since mourning already ended; but in our case Darkei Moshe too agrees that even if only one night remains at the end of mourning days, he should wait until mourning ends and we are not concerned lest he cannot sanctify on the last night; and so is explained truly in Sha'ar Efraim siman 85 in the question's wording there: if there is time he is not required to wait, for he is required when there is no time and he is in doubt — perhaps it is like one who is not expert explained above siman 128 seif 43 that he is not expert; and nevertheless he concludes it is closer to say he is obligated to sanctify — see there; and see in Baer Heitev and responsa Da'at Shabbetai veYom Tov on this like a decision; and in my humble opinion the law is with the author of Sha'arei Efraim.

(ד) And not before Yom Kippur — and Levush's view is on the contrary he should fulfill the mitzvah on such days that hang and stand, lest one merit tip the scale; and so tends Beit Meir's view.

(5) And one blesses Ma'amad — in the Gemara there Tanna Debei Rabbi Yishmael: were it not for Ma'amad one who enters to sit would recite Shema immediately; see there at length.

(ו) And we practice, etc., David king, etc. — see in Peri Chadash who wrote there is support from chapter 2 of Rosh Hashanah where R' said to R' Chiya: go to Ein Tav and sanctify the moon for me and send me a sign "David king of Israel," etc. — and see in Rashi's explanation.
