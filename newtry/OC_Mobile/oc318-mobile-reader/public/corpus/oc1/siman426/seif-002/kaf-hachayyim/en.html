2) There. One who sees the moon, etc. A blind person is obligated in Birchas HaLevanah. Responsa Maharshal, siman 77; Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur; Magen Avraham, there. Maharikash, however, writes that since the wording says, 'One who sees the moon,' the obligation depends upon sight, and a blind person should not recite the blessing. Birkei Yosef, siman 224, subsection 1, cites his words. He likewise writes in Machazik Berachah, siman 229, subsection 6, that although Maharshal in Responsa, siman 77, and those who agree with him maintain that a blind person recites Birchas HaLevanah, the matter nevertheless constitutes a doubt concerning blessings. Shalmei Tzibbur, folio 231c, likewise writes that the proper course is not to recite the blessing but to hear it from another person who is reciting it, thereby satisfying all opinions. Chesed LaAlafim, subsection 2, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Vayikra, subsection 23, write likewise. A blind person may personally recite the other passages said during Kiddush Levanah.
