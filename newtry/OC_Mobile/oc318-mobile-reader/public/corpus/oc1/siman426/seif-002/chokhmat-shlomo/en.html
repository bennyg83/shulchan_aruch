Seif 2. "One recites the beracha over the moon only on motzaei Shabbat, etc." In my Torah novellae, in an essay for Shavuot of the year 5590, I concluded there that even according to those who hold that one need not wait until seven full days have passed and may sanctify the moon earlier, nevertheless on Shavuot it is proper to wait with kiddush levanah until after Yom Tov, as on Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av. The reason is that it is proper to wait until after Matan Torah, when the luminaries became ours through the power of the Torah; also then the luminaries were strengthened, for until Matan Torah they were suspended weakly, as the Sages said that this is why it is written "the sixth day," because everything was suspended until the sixth of Sivan. Likewise the Sages said, "the earth feared and then was still": at first it feared, and afterward it was still. And, "Were it not for My covenant day and night, I would not have established the statutes of heaven and earth." Therefore the luminaries were suspended weakly until the sixth day of Sivan; then they were strengthened, fortified, and renewed, and were considered as though created anew. Therefore one should wait with kiddush levanah until after Yom Tov. This appears correct to me. Examine carefully.

Seif 2, in the gloss: "And the custom is to say, David king of Israel lives and endures, etc." NB: Rashi in Rosh Hashanah, regarding "and he sent him a sign: David king of Israel lives and endures," explains that David is compared to the moon, as it is written, "his throne shall be as the sun before Me; like the moon it shall be established forever." At first glance the intent is unclear; on the contrary, from this it implies that he is compared also to the sun. Why compare him more to the moon than to the sun, after both are mentioned in the verse? It appears resolved, and through this we will also understand why the phrase "David king of Israel lives and endures" is relevant to kiddush levanah. We must understand the verse itself: "his throne shall be as the sun before Me; like the moon it shall be established forever." The author of the Akedah wrote that just as the moon has fifteen stages of ascent and fifteen stages of descent, so too there were fifteen stages of ascent from the Exodus until the days of Shlomo, and afterward fifteen stages of descent; see there. According to this, below, the kingdom of the house of David sometimes rises and sometimes falls, and one might mistakenly think that above it is also so, sometimes ascending and sometimes descending. Therefore the Holy One, blessed be He, says that this is not so. Just as the sun is always in one state and one level in its greatness, so too the kingdom of David: "his throne is as the sun before Me," meaning before Me, above, it is always equally bright. But "like the moon it shall be established forever," meaning with respect to the world below it is like the moon, sometimes great and sometimes small; but above it is always great. The Sages said that anyone who leaves a son like himself is as though he did not die. So too with David: according to what our eyes see, after the kingdom of Shlomo, David's descendants continually diminished; if so, he did not leave a son like himself and would be considered, God forbid, as dead. But here, since we are certain that although it is now diminishing, in the end it will return and rise to its greatness, he is considered alive, for he left sons like himself. "David king of Israel lives and endures" means he is considered alive and enduring forever. Just as the moon, though it wanes, returns and is renewed, so too is the kingdom of David. Since it is destined to be renewed, even now he is alive and enduring. Especially if above it were also, God forbid, diminishing, he would not be called one who left a son like himself and would not be considered alive. But since the verse says, "his throne is as the sun before Me," meaning above it always stands in its greatness, and only below it has diminished, either way he is considered alive and enduring. This is what the verse says: "his throne is as the sun before Me," for before Me it is always like the sun; "like the moon it shall be established forever," for only below it is like the moon; "and the witness in the sky is faithful forever," meaning the Holy One, blessed be He, who dwells in the heavens, is faithful to testify that above it is like the sun. Therefore he is called alive and enduring always, because he left a son like himself. This resolves it. Examine carefully. There. Seif 3: "Until when may one recite the beracha over it, etc." NB: I was asked by one scholar concerning the Tur and those who agree with him, who hold that we calculate from the molad. His honor asked from Sanhedrin 13b, where the Gemara asks, "let them make Adar full." What would it help to make Adar full? In the end there would be more than fourteen days from the molad. One cannot say there is a distinction between the law of the tekufah and the beracha over the moon, for if so what did Tosafot ask there against Rashi from the view of Rabbi Yochanan that one recites the beracha until the fifteenth? This was his question in brief. I answered as follows. His question is not difficult at all. Certainly the beracha over the moon differs from the law of the tekufah. Regarding the beracha over the moon, we follow the present state: if now its defect has already been filled and it is no longer in its renewal, it is not relevant to bless over it. What does it matter that sometimes at such a time it could still be in its renewal? In the end, now it has aged and the beracha is not relevant. But regarding the tekufah, logically the tekufah has no connection to the moon; were the verse not to reveal that it must fall within the tekufah during the renewal of the moon, we would not connect them at all. Therefore, although the verse reveals that it must be within the renewal of the moon, we say: you have only its novelty. In this, the matter depends on the days of the month, not on the days from the molad. The Torah merely gave a measure: that the tekufah must fall within the beginning of the month for the measure of days fit for the moon's renewal. "The moon's renewal" is said only as a measure: the tekufah must fall at the beginning of the month within the measure of days that are days of the moon's renewal. Therefore the measure is fourteen days of the month, for the duration of the moon's renewal is fourteen days; just as this measure of the moon is from the molad, the measure of the tekufah is within the month. Therefore Tosafot properly asked against Rashi: since Rabbi Yochanan said there that the time of the moon's renewal is fifteen days, the tekufah should likewise extend fifteen days into the month according to the measure of the moon's renewal. And the Gemara properly asks, "let them make Adar full," because for the tekufah the matter certainly depends on the days of the month, as I wrote, that you have only its novelty, meaning the days of the month during which it is in the month's renewal; only the measure is that it extends into the month according to the days that are the moon's renewal. This is also implied to the Gemara by the view of Acherim, that the matter depends on the days of the month and not on the molad, for the baraita's statement, "Acherim say: its minority," refers to what preceded it. Earlier it said that we intercalate the year only if the tekufah is lacking most of the month; and how much is most? Sixteen days, etc. The first tanna certainly means actual days of the month, not days from the molad, for follow the reason: his reason is so that the festival fall in the new tekufah, and in this we certainly follow the days of the month and not the molad. Upon that Acherim said "its minority," implying that they refer to the same matter discussed by the first tanna. Thus it is proven that they too hold the matter depends on days of the month, not days from the molad; only the measure is according to the measure of the moon's renewal. Therefore the Gemara properly asks, "let them make Adar full," and Tosafot properly asked against Rashi. This is clear and correct in my humble opinion. Examine very carefully. There, in the gloss. "One should sanctify it only until half of 29 days, 12 hours, and 793 chalakim from the molad." NB: Regarding the law if the moon was not visible until after 29 days, 12 hours, and 793 chalakim, but fifteen full days from hour to hour had not yet passed, there is doubt whether to recite the beracha. At first glance there is a double doubt not to recite: perhaps the halacha follows the Rama, that the measure is only half of 29-12-793; and if the halacha follows the Mechaber, perhaps the intent is not full days from hour to hour, as the Magen Avraham wrote that one may explain so, that full days are not required. If so, there is a double doubt not to recite the beracha. Yet it appears that since this involves cherishing the mitzvah and an exigent circumstance, one should recite the beracha, only not everyone should recite it. Rather, one person should recite the beracha with the Divine Name and kingship and intend to discharge the obligation of the many, and they should intend to fulfill through his beracha, while all of them recite it without the Divine Name and kingship. Why should all of them enter a possible beracha levatalah? Let one of them enter the possible beracha levatalah, and not all. Also, when one discharges the many, the concern of transgression is not so great, since he intends for the sake of a mitzvah; even if there is a transgression, for a public mitzvah we are not particular about mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah, as stated in Berachot and Gittin regarding R. Eliezer who freed his slave, etc. The Magen Avraham wrote in siman 445 that it is called a public mitzvah only when one discharges the obligation of the many, not when many perform a mitzvah together. If so, here too one will discharge the many and it will be a public mitzvah, with no concern of mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah. True, in my novellae we wrote that even with a public mitzvah we are concerned for mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah wherever the mitzvah can be done individually, and only where it cannot be done individually is it considered a public mitzvah so as not to invalidate it because of mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah. Nevertheless, here, since in any case there are views that require full days from hour to hour, it is proper to combine this consideration: one person discharges the many, thereby lessening the transgression. Since we see that a public mitzvah is an advantage regarding mitzvah haba'ah ba'aveirah, it is fitting here too to make it into a public mitzvah as much as possible, to lessen the transgression and magnify the mitzvah. Therefore it is good and proper that one person recite aloud, and they hear the beginning and end of the beracha from him and intend to fulfill through him, while they recite it themselves without the Divine Name and kingship. This appears to me as the proper practice. So I ruled in practice in the year 5591 here in the holy community of Brody, when the moon was not visible in Kislev until Wednesday night, the fifteenth of Kislev, and half of 29-12-793 had already passed earlier, but several hours still remained within fifteen full days if we calculate full days. I ruled to act as above. However, afterward the hour became disturbed and the moon was not seen at all, and they did not sanctify it that month. May it be His will that it be a sign for good, and that they be for signs, appointed times, and joyous festivals. Amen.
