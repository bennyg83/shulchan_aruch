Seif 2, "the blessing over the moon is recited only on Motzaei Shabbos," etc. In my Torah novellae for Shavuot of the year 5590 I wrote that even according to those who do not wait 7 complete days before reciting Kiddush Levanah, on Shavuot it is proper to wait until after Yom Tov, just as one waits until after Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av. The reason is that it is fitting to wait until after Matan Torah, for the luminaries were strengthened through the power of Torah. Until Matan Torah the continued existence of creation remained contingent, as the Sages expounded from "the sixth day," referring to the sixth of Sivan, and from "the earth feared and became tranquil": first it feared, and afterward it became tranquil. Likewise, "If not for My covenant day and night, I would not have established the laws of heaven and earth." The luminaries therefore remained in a weakened, contingent state until the sixth of Sivan, when they were strengthened and renewed. They can thus be regarded as newly created at that point, and it is proper to wait until after Yom Tov for Kiddush Levanah. There, seif 2 in the Rama, "and the custom is to say David, King of Israel, lives and endures," etc. Rashi in Rosh Hashana explains that David is compared to the moon, based on the verse, "His throne shall be as the sun before Me, as the moon it shall be established forever." At first glance this is difficult, for the verse compares David's throne to the sun as well, so why is the moon the particular comparison? The matter can be explained by understanding the verse itself. Baal HaAkeidah writes that just as the moon has 15 stages of increase and 15 stages of decrease, so too the kingdom of Israel had 15 stages of ascent from the Exodus until the days of Shlomo and afterward 15 stages of decline. Thus, in this lower world the Davidic monarchy sometimes increases and sometimes decreases. One could mistakenly think that this fluctuation exists above as well. The verse therefore teaches otherwise: "His throne shall be as the sun before Me," meaning that before God, in the upper realm, David's throne remains constant like the sun; "as the moon it shall be established forever," meaning that only in relation to the lower world does it resemble the moon, sometimes full and sometimes diminished. The Sages say that one who leaves a son like himself is considered as though he did not die. If, according to what our eyes see after Shlomo's reign, David's descendants only continued to diminish, one could have said that David did not leave descendants fully like himself and would therefore not be considered enduring. But because we are certain that although the kingdom now diminishes, it will ultimately return and rise to its greatness, David is considered living, for his descendants will again become like him. This is the sense of "David, King of Israel, lives and endures." Just as the moon, after diminishing, renews itself, so the Davidic kingdom will be renewed. Above, however, it never diminished at all, for "his throne is as the sun before Me." In the lower world alone it is "as the moon." God, who dwells in the heavens, is the faithful witness to this, and therefore David is always called living and enduring. There, seif 3, "until when may one recite the blessing over it?" I was asked about the Tur and those who agree with him, who calculate the final time from the molad. The questioner challenged this from Sanhedrin 13b, where the Gemara asks that Adar should be made into a full month. What would that accomplish if, in any event, more than 14 days would have elapsed from the molad? One cannot answer that the law of the solar season differs from the law of Kiddush Levanah, because Tosafos there challenge Rashi from R' Yochanan's view that the blessing may be recited until the 15th day. The answer is that the 2 laws are indeed different in their operative calculation. Regarding Kiddush Levanah we follow the moon's actual present state. Once its blemish has been filled and it is no longer in the stage of renewal, the blessing of renewal no longer applies, even though in another month the astronomical configuration might differ. The law of the tekufah, however, is not intrinsically dependent upon the moon. Only because Scripture revealed that the tekufah must fall within the period called the moon's renewal do we apply a measure borrowed from the lunar renewal. We therefore say that the Torah fixed the measure by the days of the month: the tekufah must fall within the beginning of the month for a number of days equal to the period during which the moon is called new. Thus, if the lunar renewal period is 14 days, the tekufah must fall within the first 14 calendar days of the month; if according to R' Yochanan the lunar renewal lasts 15 days, the corresponding measure for the tekufah is 15 days. This explains both Tosafos' question and the Gemara's proposal to make Adar full, because for the law of the tekufah the calendar days of the month are controlling, not the elapsed time from the molad. Accordingly, the baraita's language concerning whether the tekufah is missing most of the month is naturally read in terms of calendar days. The first Tanna plainly speaks of the actual days of the month because his purpose is that the festival should fall in the new solar season. The dissenting opinion that speaks of the minority of the month continues within that same calendar framework. The reference to the moon's renewal supplies the numerical measure, not a requirement to count elapsed hours from the astronomical molad. Where the final night for Kiddush Levanah arrives and there is still room according to those who count 15 full days from hour to hour, but the earlier calendrical calculation has already passed, there is an additional consideration to minimize doubtful blessings. It is proper for 1 person to recite the blessing aloud on behalf of the congregation, so that the many can fulfill through him and the number of possible blessings in doubt is reduced. The others should hear the beginning and end of the blessing with intent to fulfill through him, while any accompanying wording they recite themselves should avoid an independent Divine Name and kingship. I ruled this way in practice in 5591 in the holy community of Brody, when the moon had not been seen in Kislev until Wednesday night, the 15th of Kislev. The midpoint of the lunar month had already passed according to 29 days, 12 hours, and 793 parts, but several hours remained according to the calculation of 15 complete days. I ruled to proceed in the manner stated above. In the end, however, the sky became disturbed and the moon was not seen, and Kiddush Levanah was not recited that month. May this be a sign for good, and may the luminaries serve for signs, appointed times, and festivals of joy. Amen.
