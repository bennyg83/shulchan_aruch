Labor. For when a person does something with water that he needs, it becomes like waste water standing to be poured out, and it is invalid for washing.
That he soaked in them. For this is considered labor, and the water became waste water. Even if someone else soaked in them, who was not the owner of the water, it is the same. So too with other labors, there is no difference between himself and others. However, in Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger he is in doubt regarding all this.
Even if he intended. Since, in any event, he intended to soak his bread in the water. But if he did not intend at all to soak it and it fell into the water by itself, it is valid, for he did not render it waste water. The same applies if a vessel fell into water and was rinsed unintentionally. If a garment fell into the water and he removed it from there, the water is not invalidated, since he did not intend it, as with bread; but the water that he squeezed from the garment is invalid, according to the later authorities.
And if he cooled, etc. For this is considered labor, since through the water it was cooled. If the wine was cold and he placed it into the water only so that it should not become warm from the heat of the day, the water is valid. The same applies if he placed live fish into it so they should not die, for all this is not called labor, since the water does not act upon them but only preserves them. Even if the fish later died, the water is not invalidated; Eliyah Rabbah.
And if he rinsed. From something adhering to them, or so that the smell would leave them.
Vessels. The same applies if one rinsed vegetables in them, according to Rabbeinu Yerucham. It appears at first glance that if they had already been rinsed and were clean, the water is valid, for this is not called labor at all, similar to vessels. Nevertheless, if he soaked vegetables in them so they should not wither, the view of the Magen Avraham and several later authorities is that this is called labor and they are invalid. Some are lenient after the fact if they are moist and clean of dirt and he placed them only so that their moisture should remain. If water later becomes available to him, it is proper to wash a second time without a blessing.
Invalid. The same applies to other labors, such as if he placed in them vessels that split during the summer so that they should contract, or if he measured measures with them. Likewise, if the water stood in vessels that had marks for weighing, meaning grooves marking a litra and two litra, so that when one places inside the item he wants to weigh, the water rises to the grooves, and he weighed some item in them, this too is considered labor and they are invalid; Magen Avraham and other authorities. If he placed something fragrant into the water, the Shelah wrote to forbid it, but the later authorities agreed that there is no prohibition at all, for he does not perform labor with the water, but comes only to improve it.
Or new. If they had dirt on them, then there is a need to rinse them and the water is invalid. If he tested a vessel in the water to see whether it was punctured and the water would flow out or not, one can judge that it is not called labor performed with them, for the water performs no action and only reveals the matter. So wrote Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger. Nevertheless, he concludes there that this requires study in practice, and Birkei Yosef in the name of Beis David is stringent in this.
If he immersed, etc. For immersing hands alone is not labor, and the water also does not become disgusting through immersing hands; therefore it is not like waste water. Even if the hands had not been washed, the water did not become impure through their touching it, as below in seif 11; Magen Avraham.
The cakes. Types of cakes that after being formed are commonly smeared on their surface with water. Sometimes they are dipped into the water, and sometimes one dips his hands and smears their surface; Rash.
Or if he scooped from them, etc. This is stated as an ascending formulation: this, and needless to say this; Peri Megadim.
The remaining water. In the vessel. But the water in his handfuls, if he smeared the surface of the cake with it, had labor performed with it.
Which the baker rinses. For ordinary hands that are working with dough are clean and have no filth, so it is like rinsing already-rinsed vessels above; Beit Yosef. Regarding the dough, one must say that it is not considered labor for them, perhaps because he could remove it even without rinsing. The Taz disagrees with this law and proved that the Rambam holds that this is considered full labor, for he rinses his hands from the dough. So ruled the Maharshal, and so agreed the other later authorities.
