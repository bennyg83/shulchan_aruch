9) [Seif 2] If work was performed with the water, etc. R' Shimshon, in chapter 8 of Yadayim, explains that it is because of the change that removes its status as water. The Rambam, however, explains that when work is performed with water, it becomes included among wastewater, and therefore one may not wash his hands with it. The Beit Yosef cites both views; Perishah, paragraph 4. The Levush gives the reason of R' Shimshon, that the water inevitably changes through the work. Ralbach, siman 123, however, gives the Rambam's reason, that it becomes wastewater designated for disposal. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3, cites him. The practical difference between the reasons is that according to R' Shimshon, the water is not invalid unless the work caused some change in it. According to the Rambam, it is invalid even if it did not change at all. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, paragraph 1, writes the same. Maran, of blessed memory, writes in seif 3, 'even though it did not change,' implying that he agrees with the Rambam, of blessed memory, that water becomes invalid for netilas yadayim through work even when it did not change at all. This is the ruling we accept.
10) There: Even if he intended to soak, etc. The Bach writes that the water is invalid specifically when he intended to soak the bread, but if he did not intend to do so and it fell from his hand, the water remains valid. If he intended to soak it, the water is invalid even if he intended to soak it in this vessel and it fell into a second vessel. He writes that this is the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, cites him. The Taz, paragraph 2, however, writes that when the Shulchan Aruch states here that even if he intended to soak it in this vessel and it fell into another vessel, the water is invalid, the Shulchan Aruch does not mean that intention is always required and that only then does a fall into another vessel disqualify the water. Rather, even if he had no intention at all, such as where the bread fell by itself and soaked there, the water is invalid. The Shulchan Aruch used the wording of the first Tanna in the Mishnah, unlike Shimon HaTimni; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 4, agrees and writes that one should not rely upon the Bach's explanation. He also writes that Eliyah Rabbah's words in paragraph 4 are inconclusive; see there. Since the matter is disputed, one should not wash with water in which bread soaked, even when it fell by itself without any intention. If he already washed, he should wash again with valid water without a blessing. R' Chazon Nachum writes the same on chapter 1 of Yadayim, and Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 3, cites him. Chayei Adam, klal 39, paragraph 2, likewise writes that he should wash again without a blessing. Chayei Adam writes there that even if he intended to soak the bread in another vessel and it fell into this one, although he must wash his hands again, he should not recite a blessing. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Kedoshim, paragraph 3, similarly writes that initially one may not wash with this water even when he did not intend to soak anything and the bread fell into the water by itself. If no other water is available, he should wash but recite the blessing without Hashem's Name and Kingship, because a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently; see there. It appears to me that this applies only if the bread remained in the water briefly and soaked. If he removed it immediately after it fell and it did not remain there at all, there is no concern regarding the water.
11) There: Even if he intended to soak, etc. If Reuven soaked his bread in Shimon's water, according to the opinion that a person cannot prohibit something that does not belong to him, the water remains valid. According to the opinion that a person can prohibit something that does not belong to him, one should be stringent. R' Avraham Nachum in a handwritten responsum; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 4; Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 4. Shaarei Teshuvah writes there that linking this question to the dispute whether one can prohibit something that is not his is astonishing. The Mordechai in Chullin distinguishes between the case of a vineyard partition, etc., and one who throws forbidden fat into another person's pot; see there. His intent is that our case resembles one who throws forbidden fat into another person's pot, and all opinions would forbid it. Sefer Yad Yosef on Bava Metzia, folio 30, reaches this conclusion; see there. Orchos Chaim, paragraph 3, cites him.
12) There: If one cooled wine in the water, etc. Water in a barrel is sometimes used while producing beer or rosewater that flows through a tube from the boiler. The water in the barrel that surrounds the tube cools the tube, thereby causing the water or beer to flow. It appears obvious that one may not wash his hands with this water, for work was performed with it, just as water used to cool wine is invalid. Shach, folio 18c; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2; Ben Ish Chai there, paragraph 10.
13) There: If one cooled wine in the water, etc. This applies specifically when he cooled the wine. If the wine stood in the water only so that it would not become warm, and the wine was already cold, the water is not invalid for netilas yadayim. Olas Tamid, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 6; Siddur Beis Oved, paragraph 3; Eshel Avraham, paragraph 6; Ben Ish Chai there, paragraph 7, unlike Beis Menuchah, paragraph 4. The same applies if one put live fish into the water; it is not invalid. Olas Tamid there; Magen Avraham, paragraph 5; Yad Aharon in Hagahot HaTur; Siddur Beis Oved there; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 2. Eliyah Rabbah writes there that it is permitted even if the fish died in the water; see there. Chayei Adam, klal 39, paragraph 3, and Mishnah Berurah, paragraph 9, cite him. The words of the aforementioned poskim, however, appear to apply specifically while the fish remain alive in the water. If they died, the water becomes invalid. Even according to Eliyah Rabbah, it appears that he refers only to a case where the dead fish did not remain in the water. Otherwise, the water putrefies and becomes wastewater. If the fish was removed immediately upon dying, it appears that even according to Olas Tamid and Magen Avraham the water is not invalid.
14) There: If one cooled wine in the water, etc. The same applies if one cooled water in water, such as during the summer; the outer water is invalid. Maaseh Rokeach on the Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos. He writes that this is unlike R' Arama, who was uncertain and concluded that it is permitted; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 5, likewise rejects that view and concludes that it is forbidden; see there. Zechor L'Avraham, paragraph 50, writes that this is evident from Maran's ruling concerning cooling wine in water and from Beit David, siman 530; see there. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 3, cites him.
15) There: If one cooled wine in the water, etc. The same applies if one tested vessels in the water to determine whether they were whole or broken; the water is invalid. Beit David, siman 530; Birkei Yosef, paragraph 5; Zechor L'Avraham there; Shaarei Teshuvah there; Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 2; Ben Ish Chai there, paragraph 11.
