His bread. If he placed something fragrant in the water, such as spikenard, it is valid for soaking his bread in it. Shelah. Magen Avraham writes that we do not rule this way. See there.
He intended. If bread fell into the water on its own, Bach permits it, while Taz prohibits it.
In the water. This applies specifically when he cooled wine in it. If the wine stood there merely so that it would not become warm, and the wine was already cold, the water is not invalid for netilas yadayim. The same applies if he placed live fish in the water so they would not die. The water is not invalidated, because this is not considered labor. The water does not act upon them at all, but merely saves them from death. Magen Avraham, Olas Tamid, and Knesses HaGedolah in the name of Dammesek Eliezer.
They are invalid. The same applies if he rinsed vegetables in them; they are invalid. Rabbeinu Yerucham. This implies that vegetables are analogous to utensils: if they were already rinsed and clean, the water is valid, because this is not considered labor. Nevertheless, if he soaked vegetables in the water so they would not wither, it is considered labor and the water is invalid, for this is no better than cooling wine in water. The same applies if, during the summer, he placed cracked utensils in water so they would contract; the water is invalid. Magen Avraham; see there.
