One performed work with the water. The water has thereby lost its status as water. Rabbi Levi Chaviv, siman 103, writes: When a person uses water for something he needs, it becomes like waste water designated for disposal. Rambam writes likewise.
He intended to soak. If he did not intend to soak the bread and it merely fell into the water, the water remains valid, because he did not render it waste water. This is also implied by the language of the Tur and the poskim. Bach.
Water used to rinse vessels is invalid. The same applies if vegetables were washed in it. Rabbeinu Yerucham. At first glance, this implies that vegetables are analogous to vessels: if they had already been washed and were clean, the water remains valid, because this is not considered work at all. Nevertheless, if one soaked vegetables in the water so that they would not wilt, it is considered work, no less than cooling wine in water. The same applies if one placed in the water, during the summer, vessels that had split in order that they should contract. But if one placed live fish in the water so they should not die, this is not considered work, because the water does not act upon them at all, but merely saves them from death; nor does the water become waste water. Gittin 53a states that if water is in a graduated vessel and one places meat into it, causing the water to rise to the markings so that he can determine the meat’s weight, this is considered performing work with the water. Mordechai writes that if one measured containers with the water, it is invalid. In our version of the Mishnah the reading is, “or if he rinsed the measuring vessels with it,” which denotes washing. Rambam explains that he swished the measuring vessels in the water until none of the odor of the measured substance remained. The water is invalid because he performed work with it. This implies that if one placed an aromatic substance into the water, the water is not invalid. Shelah writes that he received a tradition from his teachers that if one placed spikenard into the water to give it a pleasant fragrance, the water is invalid, for this is comparable to soaking bread in it. This requires study. If so, why was it necessary to invalidate water into which ink fell because its appearance changed? It should be invalid because work was performed with it. Furthermore, when bread is soaked, it is understandable that the water becomes waste water, as explained above. Here, however, his intention is to improve the water. An even stronger case appears in seif 12, where it is written that in pressing circumstances one may wash with beer and mead. Even according to the one who invalidates them, it is because they are other liquids; but water would be valid according to everyone. It is possible that Shelah’s teachers follow Rashi’s opinion, but we do not rule that way, as explained in seif 4. See there.
