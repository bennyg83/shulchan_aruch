His bread, etc. Those who place cakes in water on Erev Shabbos to soak them should be protested against, so that they should not wash from that water. The Shelah, page 81, wrote: I have received that those who place something fragrant into water, such as spikenard and the like, are like one who soaked his bread in them. The Magen Avraham wondered about him, for if so, why did it need to invalidate when ink fell into them because of change of appearance? It should invalidate because of labor. In my humble opinion this is not difficult, for it refers even to where it fell unintentionally, in which case it is permitted with respect to labor, as below. One may also say that because of change of appearance it is invalid even for a mikveh and spring, as stated in Yoreh Deah, siman 201, seif 28. He also asked: granted, soaking his bread made them like waste water, but here he intends to improve them. According to the reason of the Rash which I wrote, certainly here too it is forbidden. There is also no proof from the Rambam, who permits water with which one rinsed measures so that no odor remain in them, for in my humble opinion there there is no tangible substance. He further wrote that if one soaked vegetables so they would not wither, this is called labor, no less than cooling wine in water. In my humble opinion it is permitted, for it is comparable to wine that was already cooled, as in seif katan 10. I later found in the Perishah, who wrote: one should be careful regarding some things that women commonly soak in water, such as vegetables or herbs, and sometimes they wash greens, radishes, and horseradish and the like in water, for they invalidate it, since they are worse than water with which one cooled wine. However, it appears that if they are clean of all dirt and he placed them in water so that they remain moist and do not dry out, although initially one should be careful, after the fact one may compare it to rinsed vessels, and greater than this they permitted a kneader to rinse his hands. However, if he placed in the water a vessel that split in the summer, it is possible to forbid it.
Whether even if he intended to soak, etc. The Taz wrote that the same law applies when he had no intention at all, for example if it fell in by itself, that it is invalid. I am astonished that he did not mention the words of the Bach, who explicitly permits it, and the Magen Avraham cites him and writes that this is also implied by the Tur and the poskim, and so it is clear in Tosafot Yom Tov, first chapter of Yadayim, in the name of Maharam. Furthermore, according to one version cited by the Rash there, and likewise in the Agudah, even if he intended with this and it fell into the second, it is valid. Although we do not rule that way, nevertheless where he had no intention at all, it is certainly valid according to everyone. See Damesek Eliezer, page 330.

He cooled wine, etc. In Damesek Eliezer he wrote that according to the Rash it is valid in this case, for through cooling its appearance did not change. According to what I explained above, it is forbidden even according to the Rash. In Nachalas Tzvi he wrote that the world is lenient with cooling wine when the vessels are rinsed and new, and it is possible that his reason is also that they understood the words of the Rash this way. The Nachalas Tzvi also concludes that one should be careful in this. Nevertheless, it appears to me that if he already washed, he should wash again without a blessing. The Olas Tamid wrote in the name of Damesek Eliezer that if the wine was already cold and it stands there only so that it should not become warm, the water is not invalid for netilas yadayim; the same applies if one placed live fish into the water. In my humble opinion, even if the fish died there it is permitted. I examined Damesek Eliezer himself, and this is his language: so I practice on Yom Tov in order to eat the fish alive because of simchas Yom Tov. This implies that he mentioned alive only because of the reason of simchas Yom Tov. Likewise, in Shiyarei Knesses HaGedolah he did not copy live fish, but simply fish. So too it appears to me from fish grease in seif 11. If the water is in a vessel that has measurement marks, and he places meat into it and the water rises to the marks, and through this he knows its weight, this is called performing labor with them; Gittin 53.
The same applies if the baker, etc. The Maharshal, siman 14, invalidates in this case, and so ruled the Taz.
Levush: because of doubt, etc. But the Nachalas Tzvi wrote: I say that it is not comparable to a blacksmith, since he only immerses them to soak the hairs, similar to one who immerses his hands and smears the cake before him, which is valid. According to this, even when it is known that he immersed his hands, it is valid, and it requires study. I later found in Toras HaBayis HaKatzar, page 59, explicitly like the words of the Levush, and the Nachalas Tzvi certainly missed it. So too it is implied in the Tosefta cited by the Rash in the first chapter of Yadayim.
