On account of themselves, etc. So wrote the Tur. The Bach wrote: I do not know from where he derived this, for perhaps they are invalid only when their natural state changed because of their place in the ground, or because something fell into them, but not when they changed by themselves after they were drawn. There are versions that do not include the words ‘whether on account of themselves,’ and this is primary, though one should be stringent. The Perishah wrote: how can they change naturally on account of themselves? One can say that when water stands for a long time in hot air, it becomes green. However, this is difficult to me, for in Chullin 104 the Gemara says: are you particular about appearance? He said to him: yes. Tosafot wrote that what we learned in tractate Yadayim, that if ink or kankantom fell into them and their appearance changed they are invalid, perhaps there it refers to terumah; and so wrote the Rosh and Mordechai. What is the difficulty? One could say they are particular about appearance even when it changed by itself. Therefore I say that one should be lenient in this, and so wrote the Taz and Magen Avraham. It further appears to me that there is proof from the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchos Bi'as HaMikdash, who wrote that the water of the kiyor must not have changed appearance, but must be water valid for immersion; while in chapter 7 of Hilchos Mikvaos he wrote that a mikveh whose appearance changed by itself is valid. The law of netilas yadayim is learned from the kiyor. However, it is difficult, for regarding mikveh water it implies that even if it changed because of its place it is valid.
