(66) That there be a revi'it in the water - For so the Sages instituted for netilat yadayim. He pours it twice over his hands, for the first time purifies the hands and the second purifies the water upon them, as just below. If he poured the entire revi'it at once over both hands, his hands are also purified, even though he poured only once, as explained in siman 162. All this is in a place where water is not available, but in a place where water is available, it is good to wash generously, as above in siman 158 seif 10.
(67) For one person - Whether he washes both hands at once, such as through another person who washes them, or whether he washes one hand at a time, one revi'it always suffices for both hands. However, even if he wants to wash only one hand, such as where one hand was already pure from before, he should also not use less than the measure of a revi'it [Tosefta, brought in Beit Yosef]. It appears this is because the vessel always requires a revi'it, for with less there is no measure of purity, as just below in seif 14; but upon one hand he need not pour all of it. Below in siman 162 seif 4 I copied the words of the Acharonim, who wrote that one should practice not to use less than the measure of a revi'it of water for each hand, for by doing so he will not need to be careful about many things that require care.
(68) Who came to wash together - The Acharonim wrote that this means that from the beginning they intended to wash together. Therefore, even if the second extended his hand after the first had already begun pouring on himself, the washing counts for him too, for it is like washing both together, since they do not render one another tamei. But if they did not intend from the beginning, and the first poured only for himself, and afterward the second extended his hands, even before the stream stopped, it is like washing with the water of his fellow's washing, and his washing does not count; he must dry his hands and wash again, as below in siman 162 seif 4.
(69) The last one does not need - Specifically when the water reaches his hands around the entire required washing area of his hands.
(70) Even one after the other - Meaning, not only when they extended their hands at once, one beneath the other, where the washing helps for both, but even if the second extended his hands afterward, when he had already begun to pour on the first, his washing counts, since from the beginning they intended to wash together, as above.
(71) That the stream not stop - For by this we consider it as though the second also washed from a full revi'it; and because the water comes from the remnants of purity, they were lenient to consider this like washing from a revi'it through the connection of the poured stream [Beit Yosef and Rashba].
(72) Close, etc. - For then we consider them like one hand; but when they are distant, it is like washing with the water of his fellow's washing.
(73) And some permit even, etc. - Meaning that they wash entirely by themselves, each one separately. According to this view too, it applies specifically when they came to wash at the same time, but the second waited until the first washed; but when the second came after some time, this does not help to reduce the measure below a revi'it [Tur].
(74) For this second one too it counts, etc. - But according to this view, the first must pour over his hands two times and be exact in his washing so that enough also remains for the second to wash his hands. This is unlike the first view, according to which the first must pour the entire revi'it of water over his hands. In pressing circumstances one may rely leniently on this view, for it is the view of many poskim, as I wrote in Biur Halacha.
(75) And in this manner, etc. - Meaning that it is permitted to wash one after another, even with several people, for the reason mentioned above.
(76) Half a log for three - If you ask: since a revi'it suffices for two, then a revi'it and a half should suffice for three. One may answer that the Sages were concerned that since there are many people, they will not be exact to wash well up to the joint, because each one will restrict the water so that it remains for his fellow; therefore they forbade with a revi'it and a half. After the fact, if one washed with half a revi'it, the last two may wash with the remaining revi'it.
(77) Three times - In the Tur he wrote two times, for the essential washing for purity is two times; only if there is dirt upon them that interposes must he first wash once to remove the dirt, as below in siman 162.
(78) Side by side - They do not render one another tamei by touching, for all of them are considered like one hand, as below in siman 162 seif 5. For this too a revi'it suffices for two, and half a log for three and four, with the manner of pouring being that the attendant goes and pours water on all of them. It is reasonable that the first view is also lenient in this.
(79) Or one atop the other - We do not say that the water became tamei from the hand of the first, for all of them are considered like one hand, as above.
(80) That they loosen, etc. - Meaning that they should not press against one another.
