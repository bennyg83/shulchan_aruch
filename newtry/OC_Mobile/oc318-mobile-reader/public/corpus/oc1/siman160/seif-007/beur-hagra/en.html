Seif 7: Hot water, etc. There, according to the reading of the Rif and the other poskim: Chizkiyah said one may not take from them for the hands, while Rabbi Yochanan said, if one’s entire body may immerse in them, all the more so his hands. Rav Pappa said, etc. The Rash writes in the first chapter of Yadayim that this is the reading of Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Gershom, and proves that it must be so. Otherwise, Rabbi Yochanan would oppose the anonymous Mishnah in the first chapter of Yadayim, “the water,” etc., whereas Rabbi Yochanan holds that the halachah follows an anonymous Mishnah everywhere. The Rif rules like Chizkiyah because he was Rabbi Yochanan’s teacher. Although Chizkiyah follows a minority view, we follow the Amoraim. See Terumas HaDeshen. The Rambam, however, requires study, for at the beginning of chapter 6 of Hilchos Shabbos he rules like Rav and Shmuel. See Kesef Mishneh there, and what I wrote in seif 9. See Rashi there, s.v. de’afsekinehu, etc., “and one may not,” etc., “but,” etc. This is what the Shulchan Aruch means by, “and if he drew,” etc., “if there is,” etc., “and if,” etc., “and if,” etc. Toras HaBayis likewise writes that if the waters are not connected, one may not wash from them in any event. Since they do not contain the measure required for immersion, netilas yadayim is required, and that requires a vessel. Tosafos there, s.v. de’afsekinehu, etc., likewise writes, alternatively, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah explains that it was necessary to say that they do not contain forty seah, for otherwise the original difficulty returns: “if his entire body may immerse in them,” etc. It was also necessary to say that they remain connected; otherwise, how could Rabbi Yochanan say, “his entire body,” etc.? Rather, they disagree in such a case. Chizkiyah decrees, and the statement, “now,” etc., does not apply. Regarding his body, there is no concern lest he confuse it with a vessel, unlike here, where if he sees that immersing his hands is permitted, he may think this is not because of their mixture with other water, but because there is no prohibition at all regarding the waters of Tiberias. Rabbeinu Yonah, however, writes that “they were entirely separated” is implied, following the first explanation of Tosafos there. Chizkiyah then decrees lest one see that there are neither forty seah nor any mixture and nevertheless they are permitted in a vessel. Rabbi Yochanan’s statement, “his entire body,” etc., does not mean in those waters literally, but in that type of water when there are forty seah. See what I wrote in siman 159, seif 14.
