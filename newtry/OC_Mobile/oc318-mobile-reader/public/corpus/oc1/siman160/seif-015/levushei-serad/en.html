Taz, seif katan 10 “Why did he not rule like the Sages?” Maharshal did not see the longer version of Toras HaBayis, which writes, “according to both opinions,” etc. He saw only the abbreviated version cited here in Shulchan Aruch. Maharshal therefore assumed that Rashba understood the Sages’ statement, “one may not wash,” to mean that they invalidate the washing even after the fact, whereas Rabbi Eliezer validates the washing and prohibits it only initially because of wasting wine, and that Rashba ruled like Rabbi Eliezer. Maharshal therefore asked why Rashba did not rule like the Sages, and answered that the Sages too prohibit it because of waste. Taz objects that this still does not resolve the issue: regarding waste, why did Rashba rule like Rabbi Eliezer, who prohibits it initially only when water was added? Taz answers that since Maharshal saw only the abbreviated Toras HaBayis, he assumed that its statement that wine has a distinct blessing applies even before water was added, because wine still has a distinct blessing. According to Maharshal’s explanation, the abbreviated Toras HaBayis therefore properly rules like the Sages regarding waste. In truth, however, Rashba writes that this applies specifically when water was added, and the question remains why he did not rule like the Sages.
