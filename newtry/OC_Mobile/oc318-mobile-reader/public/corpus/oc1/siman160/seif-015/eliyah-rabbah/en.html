(15) [Levush:] Some invalidate, etc. So wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi and Rashba, who explained the dispute of Chizkiya and R' Yochanan regarding its waters when connected. I wonder at what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba in siman 159, s.v. "One who immerses in it" — his wording: "And if it is difficult for you: if there are less than forty seah, how can immersion be valid for him, even for his hands? — One should say: for his hands, since it suffices for him," etc., up to here. And this refers to the dispute mentioned above. What is the difficulty? It deals with [waters] connected to a spring; it requires great study. Regarding practical halacha, Bach ruled like Rashi and Rashba, and he inferred from what the gemara says (Chullin 106) "in their place, everyone agrees it is permitted," and it does not state a novelty that even in a cistern and when connected to a mikveh it is permitted, up to here. In my humble opinion this is not proof, for since they are connected they are "in their place." Also, what he wrote that Beit Yosef wrote that thus it appears from the Tur's words — I wonder, for the simple meaning of Beit Yosef's words is that from what Rashi writes "and it has no mikveh measure," it is permitted when there are forty seah; it also appears so from the Tur, who also wrote "and it has no mikveh measure," and this does not refer at all to when they are connected. Also what he wrote that from the Tur's silence that it is forbidden even when connected is strained; on the contrary, from his silence it implies permission, as one may immerse the whole body; and just as Rashi had to write explicitly, so too the Tur should have written it, as he himself wrote earlier — see there. Whoever explains from the Magen Avraham in siman 159 that the Tur holds like R' Yehudah — it is not so, for the Tur wrote explicitly that one may not take from them for the hands, and the rest of the waters are not in a mikveh measure, and they are permitted for immersion of the whole body. This is the case of the Rambam and Rif, who wrote explicitly that one may immerse the whole body in them, and the Tur wrote like them.**** END BLOCK ****
