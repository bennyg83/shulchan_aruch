Whose appearance changed. We derive this from the water of the kiyor, which was for sanctifying the hands in the Mikdash, for it is invalid when its appearance changes; Beit Yosef in the name of the Ra'ah.
On account of themselves. For example, it stood in a vessel for a long time until it became green because of the hot air. The Mechaber copied this from the language of the Tur, but in the Rambam it is not mentioned as invalid except when it changed because of something else. The later authorities proved this and agreed in practice that it is permitted when it changed on account of itself.
Because of something that fell in. Such as ink and other types of dyes and colors. Even if the substance of the dye did not dissolve into them, but their appearance changed through soaking, the same applies if it changed because of smoke or any other matter, for only when it changed on account of itself is it valid. However, what changed because of earth and mud that mixed into it is not considered a change of appearance, as below in seif 9, because this is the manner in which water grows, to be mixed with earth and mud, and also because in the end, when they remain standing, they usually become clear. See below in seif 9.
Because of their place. Whether they changed while standing in the ground or afterward, when he drew them into a vessel and they changed because of the vessel. Know that water becoming invalid through any change of appearance applies specifically when one pours it from a vessel onto his hands. But if he immerses his hands in forty se'ah, it is not invalidated by any change of appearance, just as with immersion of the body, where a mikveh is not invalidated by change of appearance unless the substance of the color fell into it and dissolved into it, or a liquid such as red wine and the like from other fruit juices, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 201, seif 25 and 26. In a spring, change of appearance does not invalidate at all, as explained there.
They are invalid. If they returned to their original state, they are valid; Magen Avraham and other authorities.
