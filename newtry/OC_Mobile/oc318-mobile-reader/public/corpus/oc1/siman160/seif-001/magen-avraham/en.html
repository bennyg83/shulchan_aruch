Whether on its own. This requires study. Rash, in chapter 9 of Parah, cites a Tosefta: Water of purification whose appearance changed on its own is valid; if it changed because of smoke or another substance, it is invalid. Rambam likewise rules this way in chapters 6 and 9 of Hilchos Parah, and so is evident from the Mishnah at the end of chapter 8 of Parah. Since such water is valid as water of purification, all the more so it should be valid for netilas yadayim. Rambam was also precise here, writing that water is invalid whether it changed because of its location or because of another substance, which implies that if it changed on its own it is valid. This is likewise implied by the Mishnah, which teaches that if ink or vitriol fell into the water and changed its appearance, it is invalid. It did not simply state that water whose appearance changed is invalid, implying that it is invalid specifically when the change resulted from another substance. See Yoreh Deah 221:25-26. This also appears from the Rosh, who asks why the Gemara raises an objection from appearance, since this is already taught in the Mishnah. He does not answer that a liquid is invalid when it changes on its own. Necessarily, a change occurring on its own is valid. The poskim’s question why wine should not be invalid because of a change in appearance, although that change is intrinsic to the wine, may be answered as follows: Since water becomes invalid when wine falls into it and changes its appearance, all the more so wine itself should be invalid, just as ink itself is invalid. See seif 12. This requires study. It therefore appears to me that one may be lenient when the water changed on its own, and Bach rules likewise.
