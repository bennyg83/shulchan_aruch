1) [Seif 1] Water whose appearance changed, etc. The reason is that netilas yadayim requires water whose form has not changed, like the sanctification of the hands and feet from the kiyor. It is reasonable that there we derive this from the extra mention of water in the verses, 'You shall put water there,' etc., and 'They shall wash with water,' etc. Beit Yosef in the name of the Ra'ah; Levush; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1. See above, at the beginning of siman 159.
2) There: Whose appearance changed, etc. This applies whether the water is in vessels or in the ground. Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchos Berachos, halachah 7; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. Regarding the Rambam's further ruling that exposed water is invalid for netilas yadayim, the Tur writes in this siman that nowadays, when we are not concerned about water left exposed, it is valid even for washing. Maran, of blessed memory, agrees with him in the Beit Yosef; see there. So rules the Levush, seif 5. It appears that when possible, one should initially take care with it just as one takes care regarding drinking. See Yoreh De'ah, at the beginning of siman 116, and the later authorities there. If we saw that a snake drank from it, it is obviously invalid because of danger. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 11.
3) There: Whose appearance changed, etc. If the water returned to its original state, it is valid. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Eliyah Rabbah there; Yad Aharon in Hagahot HaTur; Peri Megadim, Mishbetzos Zahav, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam, klal 39, paragraph 1; Derech HaChaim, paragraph 1; Ben Ish Chai, parashas Kedoshim, paragraph 1. Machatzis HaShekel at the end of paragraph 1, however, writes that if valid water later becomes available, one should wash again without a blessing; see there. Since all the aforementioned later authorities agree with Magen Avraham, there is no need to be concerned for Machatzis HaShekel's position except initially, if other water can easily be found. If no other water is available, one should wash and recite the blessing. If he already washed, he need not wash again.
4) There: Whose appearance changed, etc. If a sponge is full of water and one squeezes it and washes his hands from it, the washing is invalid, because the water's appearance inevitably changes because of the sponge. Yafeh LaLev, paragraph 1. See above, siman 159, paragraph 3.
5) There: Whether by itself. The Levush writes the same. The Taz, paragraph 1, and Magen Avraham, paragraph 2, however, are lenient when the water changed by itself, because this case is not stated in the Mishnah or the Rambam; see there. The Bach writes that one should be stringent in accordance with the Shulchan Aruch's ruling. Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 1, and Yad Aharon in Hagahot HaTur cite him. See Birkei Yosef, paragraph 2, who discusses this at length and concludes: 'Regarding the practical halachah, although the later authorities are lenient when the water changed by itself, one should be stringent in accordance with the Tur, Maran, Maharshal, and the Bach.' This also appears to be the view of Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 1. See there, where he resolves the questions of the Taz and Magen Avraham. So rule Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, and Ben Ish Chai, parashas Kedoshim, paragraph 1. This is the primary ruling. We already wrote in the preceding siman that although netilas yadayim is rabbinic, one should be stringent because of the doubtful blessing. This is all the more so here, since this is also the view of Maran, of blessed memory, whose rulings we have accepted. This also appears to be the view of the Rama, of blessed memory, since he does not disagree. Only when no other water is available should one wash with this water and omit Hashem's Name and Kingship from the blessing. Ben Ish Chai writes the same there.
6) There: Whether by itself. This means that when water stands for a long time in the hot air, it turns green. Perishah, paragraph 2; Chayei Adam there; Ben Ish Chai there.
7) There: Whether because something fell into it. For example, ink or other coloring substances fell into it. Mishnah, chapter 1 of Yadayim. Likewise, if its appearance changed because of smoke or another substance, it is invalid. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 1. But if its appearance changed because dirt or pebbles fell into it, it is valid, as written below in seif 9.
8) There: It is invalid. A change of appearance disqualifies water only when one washes his hands from a vessel. If he immerses his hands in a mikveh containing forty se'ah, a change of appearance does not disqualify it. The water is valid even for immersing his entire body, and all the more so for immersing only his hands. Bach; Shiyurei Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, paragraph 1; Olas Tamid, paragraph 1; Chayei Adam there, paragraph 10. The rule that a change of appearance does not disqualify a mikveh containing forty se'ah applies specifically when one rinsed vessels in it, or soaked spices or foods in it, and its appearance changed. But if wine or grape juice fell into it and changed its former appearance, it is invalid, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 201, seif 25. See the Taz there for the reason. In a spring, however, no change of appearance disqualifies it, as written there in Yoreh De'ah, seif 28. So too write Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 9, and Ben Ish Chai there, paragraph 4.
