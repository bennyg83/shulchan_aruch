Between. I have thoughts here and I set them forth there's help. Netilat yadayim they learned from the kiyor, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Ra'ah here; and there "extra water" is written to teach that it was not changed (this is per Rambam, chapter 3 of Sotah, halacha 9; not so Rashi, Sotah 15b, regarding sotah water — although "extra water" is not written for Rabbanan, it implies that in their existence they are like spring water — understand). Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilchot Bi'at Mikdash, halacha 12: kiyor water is like water fit for immersion. Eshel Avraham, note 2, brought this. Truth: in a mikveh, colored water that changed by itself is valid — one reason, as Kaf HaChayim wrote in chapter 7 of Mikvaot; and we learn from the law of rinsing olive baskets, Mikvaot 7:3; the reason: whatever has no substance there, mikveh [validity regarding] change of appearance; if so, likewise changed because of place, whatever has no substance in it is valid; if so, we should say likewise regarding netilat yadayim that colored water and [water changed because of] place are valid in a mikveh, and it is a fortiori d'oraisa, etc. But if we say netilat yadayim relies on mei chatat, as in siman 159, Magen Avraham note 1 — there, regarding mei chatat, Rambam ruled in chapter 4 of Parah, halacha 14, that because of themselves it is valid, and likewise in the mishnah, Parah, chapter 8, mishnah 1: colored water from place invalidates — so here colored water is invalid, but from themselves valid; this is why Magen Avraham, note 2, distinguished from mei chatat as stated. Also what Kaf HaChayim wrote in chapter 7 of Mikvaot: a mikveh that changed because of itself is valid — we learn from olive baskets; one could say from Parah, chapter 8, mishnah 1, that even for chatat it is valid. Also what is written in Yoreh De'ah siman 201, seif 28, regarding a spring: change of appearance does not invalidate — one must say the [rule in] Parah, chapter 8, mishnah 1, is because chatat is stricter. I will further explain to you what Magen Avraham, note 1, wrote: if they changed and returned, they are valid — Sotah 15b; behold, broad learning appears from Mikvaot, chapter 7, halacha 1, Ra'avad and Ba'alei Nefesh and other poskim, that change of appearance helps when it returns to its original state; and if we say they rely on mei chatat, there too return of appearance helps, as in mishnah 11, chapter 8 of Parah, which explains: if pottery or earth fell in, wait until it settles — thus it helps; and R. Akiva says one need not wait, and Rambam, chapter 6 of Parah, rules like R. Akiva, since it is not mixed much together one need not wait; therefore people properly are not careful and take for hands water whose appearance changed from dust and stones that fell in — and a fortiori from kiddush, where one need not wait; and specifically komos and kankantum, which are mixed and cannot be separated, not so this, which in time will settle and is not connected, as if it had settled.
