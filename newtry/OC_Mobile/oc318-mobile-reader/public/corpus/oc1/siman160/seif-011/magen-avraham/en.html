Or he is uncertain. Meaning, he washed his hands and is uncertain whether the water contained the required measure, as explained above, unlike Olas Tamid. Rash writes that even if all these uncertainties are present, he is pure. In every uncertainty involving a dispute among the poskim, we rule leniently. Terumas HaDeshen; Beit Yosef. Yam Shel Shlomo, siman 77, writes: If one washed properly and is later uncertain whether his hands became impure, he need not wash again even to remove the doubt. This requires study, for diversion of attention invalidates the washing, as stated at the end of siman 173. See also siman 164, seif 2.
One who did not wash his hands. Therefore, one who leaves the privy may scoop water from a barrel with his hands in order to wash them. Yam Shel Shlomo. See Tosafos, Sotah 15a.
It was not invalidated. The case of the baker in seif 2 plainly does not involve hands that had already been washed, yet the water remains valid. Although in siman 162, seif 4, if one washed one hand and rubbed it against the other, the first hand becomes impure, a distinction may be drawn. The water upon the hands during washing is readily rendered impure because of the laws of impurity that apply during the washing process, whereas water in a vessel does not become impure in this manner. Terumas HaDeshen.
