In Taz, note 10: l'chatchila it is forbidden to take with wine — as cited. His words are puzzling: what Rashba and Shulchan Aruch in the second opinion [hold] like R' Eliezer — meaning, on the contrary they ruled like the Sages; therefore, even though he did not give water, it is forbidden l'chatchila, for the Sages fix the blessing of hamotzi for himself until he gives water. What he wrote in the name of Rashba in Magen Avraham regarding the spring — it appears his intent is that he challenged Ra'ah, s.v., who wrote the reason is because of loss of food; if so, there was no place to distinguish between one who gave water and one who did not — meaning, in R' Eliezer's words (and in truth Ra'ah's text in both [cases] in R' Eliezer's words [says] they take hands, and Rashba appears not to have had that text in his words). On this he wrote that thus he should have said: the reason is degradation of food; therefore R' Eliezer properly distinguishes between one who gave water, who is forbidden to degrade it since he fixes a berachah, etc. — see there. But certainly Rashba rules like the Sages, and the reason of degradation applies in both, whether before he gave water or after; therefore it is forbidden l'chatchila. What Taz wrote at the end, to wonder why there should not be concern for loss of food before he gave water, etc. — I wonder, for this is Rashba's question on Ra'ah in Magen Avraham, and he wrote that he should have said from the reason of degradation, and the distinction comes out well. Also what Taz wrote in the name of Rashal, and Ma'ayan challenged him there — it appears he did not see Terumat HaDeshen, who wrote that on both sides the reason is because he degrades the beverage; rather he thought Rabbanan and R' Eliezer argue b'dieved regarding validity of netilah only — but the essence is as above.**** END BLOCK ****
