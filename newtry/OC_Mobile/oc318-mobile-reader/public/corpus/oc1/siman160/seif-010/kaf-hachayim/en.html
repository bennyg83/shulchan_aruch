(10) There, "even if he intended to soak," etc. Bach wrote that specifically when he intended to soak; if he did not intend but it fell from his hand, it is valid; and if he intended to soak, even if he intended to soak in this [vessel] and it fell into a second, it is invalid. He wrote that such is the Tur's and Shulchan Aruch's opinion — see there; and they brought Magen Avraham note 4. But Taz note 2 wrote that what Shulchan Aruch says here, "even if he intended to soak in this vessel and it fell into another vessel it is invalid" — the author of Shulchan Aruch does not mean that we require intent at least, and then it is invalid if it fell into another vessel; rather, even without any intent, such as when it fell by itself and soaked there, it is invalid; and Shulchan Aruch cites the mishnah's wording per the first tanna, unlike Shimon HaTeimani — see there. And Ma'amar Mordechai agreed, note 4, and wrote that one should not rely on Bach's explanation nor on what Eshel Avraham wrote, note 4, that there is no decision — see there. Therefore, since there is a dispute, one should not take water in which bread soaked, even if it fell by itself without any intent; and if he already took, he should take again in valid water without a berachah. And likewise Rav Chazon Nachum, chapter 1 of Yadayim, and they brought Aruch HaShulchan note 3; and likewise Chayei Adam, general rule 41, note 2, that he should return and take without a berachah. Chayei Adam wrote there that even if he intended to soak in another vessel and it fell into this one, although he must take netilat yadayim again, nevertheless he does not recite a berachah. And likewise Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Kedoshim, note 3: l'chatchila one should not take from this water even if he did not intend to soak at all, only that the bread fell by itself into the water; and if he has no other [water], he takes and does not bless except without Name and Kingship, because of doubt — see there. It appears that this is specifically if the bread remained in the water a while and soaked; but if immediately when it fell they removed it and it did not remain there at all, one need not be concerned about this water at all.**** END BLOCK ****
