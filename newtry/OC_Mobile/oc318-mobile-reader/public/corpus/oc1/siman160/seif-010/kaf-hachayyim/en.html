10) There: Even if he intended to soak, etc. The Bach writes that the water is invalid specifically when he intended to soak the bread, but if he did not intend to do so and it fell from his hand, the water remains valid. If he intended to soak it, the water is invalid even if he intended to soak it in this vessel and it fell into a second vessel. He writes that this is the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch; see there. Magen Avraham, paragraph 4, cites him. The Taz, paragraph 2, however, writes that when the Shulchan Aruch states here that even if he intended to soak it in this vessel and it fell into another vessel, the water is invalid, the Shulchan Aruch does not mean that intention is always required and that only then does a fall into another vessel disqualify the water. Rather, even if he had no intention at all, such as where the bread fell by itself and soaked there, the water is invalid. The Shulchan Aruch used the wording of the first Tanna in the Mishnah, unlike Shimon HaTimni; see there. Maamar Mordechai, paragraph 4, agrees and writes that one should not rely upon the Bach's explanation. He also writes that Eliyah Rabbah's words in paragraph 4 are inconclusive; see there. Since the matter is disputed, one should not wash with water in which bread soaked, even when it fell by itself without any intention. If he already washed, he should wash again with valid water without a blessing. R' Chazon Nachum writes the same on chapter 1 of Yadayim, and Erech HaShulchan, paragraph 3, cites him. Chayei Adam, klal 39, paragraph 2, likewise writes that he should wash again without a blessing. Chayei Adam writes there that even if he intended to soak the bread in another vessel and it fell into this one, although he must wash his hands again, he should not recite a blessing. Ben Ish Chai, parashas Kedoshim, paragraph 3, similarly writes that initially one may not wash with this water even when he did not intend to soak anything and the bread fell into the water by itself. If no other water is available, he should wash but recite the blessing without Hashem's Name and Kingship, because a doubt concerning blessings is treated leniently; see there. It appears to me that this applies only if the bread remained in the water briefly and soaked. If he removed it immediately after it fell and it did not remain there at all, there is no concern regarding the water.
