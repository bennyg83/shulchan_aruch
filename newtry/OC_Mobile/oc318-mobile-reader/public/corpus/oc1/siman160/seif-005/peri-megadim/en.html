There are. See Taz. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 208, states that invalid water mixed with valid water is nullified in a minority. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 7, which implies that the invalid water is nullified by any majority, however slight. Certainly that suffices, and a ratio of one to two is unnecessary, as with water that did not remain overnight in Taanis Esther. All the more so, sixty is unnecessary, for anything whose status does not depend on its taste is nullified one in two, even when mixed with a different type, if it can be conceived that the substances are not distinguishable.
It appears. Water that absorbed a prohibited substance through fire, such as water cooked in a utensil used that day for neveilah, is valid for washing the hands so long as its appearance did not change. Although it is forbidden for drinking, it remains fit for an animal. Even according to Bach in seif 5, who explains exposed water as invalid because it is unfit for drinking, it appears that this water is valid. It is certainly valid according to Beit Yosef, who explains the issue there as danger. See there. Water that absorbed a substance prohibited for benefit is likewise permitted for benefit apart from the monetary value of the prohibited substance itself, as I concluded in my Peri commentary to Yoreh Deah. See Yoreh Deah 87:6 in the gloss. Even water completely prohibited for benefit because of idolatry may, if no other water is available, be used for washing, since even Rabbinic mitzvos were not given for benefit, as I wrote in the general introduction. Nevertheless, one should not do so initially, because washing one’s hands presumably gives some incidental benefit. It is analogous to one who says, “Sitting in the sukkah is forbidden to me by a vow.” See Shach, siman 215; with Hashem’s help this will be explained there. Here too, he benefits through the washing. After the fact, however, the washing is valid, as stated here. Whether such water must have the required measure depends on siman 649:3 concerning an object whose required measure is viewed as crushed. With Hashem’s help, this will be explained there. See what I wrote on Magen Avraham, seif katan 12, concerning this matter.
