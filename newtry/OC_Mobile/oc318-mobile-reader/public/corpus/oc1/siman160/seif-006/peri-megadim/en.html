It is not. See Taz. There is a distinction: water disqualified by a change in appearance is invalid even in a valid mikveh. If ink or the like fell into it, one may not immerse in it. See Yoreh Deah 201:25 and 28 concerning a mikveh, and there concerning a spring. Water invalid because it is unfit for drinking may nevertheless be used for bodily immersion in a mikveh, and certainly for immersing the hands, as in seif 9. If it is in a utensil, however, even if it became invalid while still attached to the ground, it is invalid in the utensil. This is also implied by Rambam, who refers only to change of appearance. See Bach, seif 1, who writes that every change of appearance invalidates water in a utensil but not in a mikveh. His use of the word “every” requires study, for a visible change of appearance caused by something that fell into the water certainly invalidates a mikveh; only a change originating in the water itself does not invalidate it. See Taz, seif katan 1, and what I wrote there. Taz’s a fortiori argument there regarding washing the hands is difficult, for water rendered unfit even for an animal, as in seif 9, disproves it. The a fortiori applies only as follows: anything valid for bodily immersion is certainly valid for immersing the hands, but not necessarily for pouring water over the hands. This requires study. See what I wrote on Magen Avraham, seif katan 2, regarding this.
