And the hail. Rain that hardened as it descended, and it is like hail.
And the salt. It is not in every manner that salt water is permitted, for with washing from a vessel over his hands, the Mechaber already invalidated even salty water above in seif 9. Rather, immersion in it is permitted when it has the measure of a mikveh, because it is valid even for immersion of the body. Since all these were taught together regarding a mikveh, he included them here as well, even though their laws are not entirely the same.
If he crushed them, etc. But without this, one may not wash with them from a vessel. However, to immerse the hands in them, if they have the measure of a mikveh, it is permitted in pressing circumstances when he has no water, even without crushing them. Even if the snow is not gathered together at once, but lies on the ground and much of it has melted, as long as there are forty se'ah connected together, one may immerse in it. Nevertheless, it appears that there must at least be enough to cover his hands in the place where he inserts them.
There is one who says, etc. This does not refer to the beginning of the seif, for those are included in water. Rather, it comes only to exclude fruit juice, for they are not considered water, and certainly wine is not. The reason is that netilas yadayim was supported on the verse, ‘and he did not wash his hands with water,’ and therefore water specifically is required; Beit Yosef.
And there are those who say that wine is valid. All the more so other fruit juices and other liquids, for they hold that water specifically was not required for washing. There is no disgrace of food here, since he needs it for this, and it is not in a manner of destruction.
But it is forbidden, etc. This means whether he placed water into it or did not, for in all cases it is called significant wine and establishes a blessing for itself, as below in siman 272, seif 5.
For itself. The blessing Borei peri hagafen. But with other liquids and fruit juices, which were not elevated with a blessing of their own, one may wash with them even initially according to this opinion.
But not red. For they changed from the appearance of water. The first ‘some say’ holds that it is not considered a change of appearance, since it remains in the appearance it had from the beginning; Rosh. If it was white and he made it red through dye, as is sometimes done with our wines, it appears that according to everyone it is invalid.
And there is one who says that all fruit juices, etc. This is a third opinion, which distinguishes between wine and fruit juices: regarding wine, it is invalid according to the law even after the fact, like the first opinion, but with fruit juices one may wash in pressing circumstances, for he holds that all fruit juices are called water except wine. In practice, one should rule like this opinion, to be stringent regarding wine and lenient regarding fruit juices in pressing circumstances.
And all the more so it is permitted, etc. They are not invalidated because of change of appearance, for he holds that where it changed for improvement, it is not considered a change; Magen Avraham and other authorities. See Biur HaGra, where it implies that he does not accept this reasoning.
