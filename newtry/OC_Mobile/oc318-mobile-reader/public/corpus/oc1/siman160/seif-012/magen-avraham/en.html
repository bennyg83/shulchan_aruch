Snow. Beit Yosef, Yoreh Deah 201:6, writes that one should be stringent not to immerse one’s body in snow unless it has melted. In pressing circumstances, however, one may immerse one’s hands in snow even if it has not melted, since netilas yadayim is D’Rabbanan. These are Beit Yosef’s words. In Shulchan Aruch there, seif 30, he rules without qualification that one may immerse in snow, implying even if it has not melted. Here, however, regarding netilas yadayim, he rules that the snow must first be crushed. The opposite would seem more appropriate. Therefore, one may rely upon the lenient view and immerse one’s hands in snow even if it has not been crushed, provided that there are forty seah. It may be answered that the statement, “if it was crushed,” applies only to washing from the snow, meaning that when one takes it in a vessel and pours it upon his hand, it must first be crushed, because netilas yadayim requires water. This is also implied by the gloss in seif 10. But one may immerse his hands in forty seah of snow lying upon the ground even if it was not crushed, as stated in Yoreh Deah there. Rambam’s phrase “and he crushed it” means that he compressed its empty spaces, as Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh explain. It appears to me that when snow lies upon the ground, if forty seah are connected together, immersion is permitted even if the layer is very shallow. See Niddah 10b, where it is considered joined for purposes of purity. Shach in Yoreh Deah, however, rules that it must specifically be crushed. See there.
In water alone. This excludes fruit liquids.
But not red wine. This is because its appearance has changed. The first opinion holds that fruit liquids are not invalidated by a change in appearance, and Bach rules accordingly. See seif 1 and what is written there.
For all fruit liquids. This requires study. In my humble opinion, whoever validates wine should likewise validate other fruit liquids. Why, then, did the Shulchan Aruch formulate three separate opinions? It is possible that he refers to honey, as written in Beit Yosef. In truth, however, the term means honey-water, as Rama writes, and similarly in Yoreh Deah 103:4. The reason is that its primary component is water. This is also implied by Tosafos, Eruvin 29b, who write that it has the law of water to invalidate a mikveh with three lugin and is not treated as fruit liquid. See there. Although its appearance changed, it must be that because the change improved it, the liquid is not invalidated on that account.
