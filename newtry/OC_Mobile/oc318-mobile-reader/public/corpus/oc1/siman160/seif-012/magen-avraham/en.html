Snow. Beit Yosef in Yoreh De'ah siman 201, seif 66 wrote that one should be stringent not to immerse one's body in snow if it is not melted; at a time of pressure it is permitted to immerse one's hands in snow even if not melted, since netilat yadayim is d'rabbanan — end quote in Beit Yosef. And in Shulchan Aruch there, seif 30, he wrote anonymously that one immerses in snow — implying even if not melted; and here regarding netilat yadayim he ruled specifically when crushed. The opposite should be required; therefore one may rely to immerse hands in snow even if not crushed if there are forty seah in it. And one could say that what he wrote "if crushed," etc., refers only to when taking from them — meaning, when taking in a vessel and pouring on the hand, crushing is required, for netilat yadayim is only in water; and such appears from what is written in the gloss, seif 10. But to immerse hands in forty seah in the ground is permitted even without crushing, as written in Yoreh De'ah there. What Rambam wrote "and crushed" means to compress the hollow — as Beit Yosef and Kaf HaChayim wrote. It appears to me that snow lying on the ground, whenever forty seah are connected together, even very low, it is permitted; see Niddah 10b, that for purity it is considered connection.
