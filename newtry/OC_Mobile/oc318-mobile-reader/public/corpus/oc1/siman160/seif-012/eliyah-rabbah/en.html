[Levush] One may not wash, etc. This means that even according to the opinion that is lenient above in siman 159, seif 14, here it is forbidden because of a decree, since these waters are bitter. It is clear from the poskim that even after they become cold they remain forbidden.
[Levush] Some invalidate, etc. So the Beit Yosef writes in the name of Rashi and the Rashba, who interpret the dispute between Chizkiyah and Rabbi Yochanan as concerning hot spring water still connected to its source. I am puzzled by what the Beit Yosef writes in the Rashba's name in siman 159, under the heading concerning immersion: 'If you ask, if there are fewer than forty seah, how can immersion be effective even for his hands? The answer is that for his hands, since it suffices, etc.' This refers to the dispute just mentioned. What is the question if the water is connected to a spring? This requires serious study. As a matter of practical law, the Bach rules like Rashi and the Rashba. He infers this from Chullin 106, where the Gemara says that when the waters are in their place everyone agrees they are permitted. The Gemara does not state the greater novelty that even if they are in an artificial pool but connected to a mikveh they are permitted. In my humble opinion this is not a proof, because being connected may itself be considered 'in their place.' The Bach also writes that the Beit Yosef says the Tur appears to agree. I am surprised, because the plain meaning of the Beit Yosef is that since Rashi writes 'and there is not the measure of a mikveh,' it follows that if there are forty seah it is permitted, and he says the same appears from the Tur, which likewise writes that there is not the measure of a mikveh. He is not discussing the question of connection at all. The Bach's inference that the Tur's silence means it is forbidden even when connected is strained. On the contrary, silence implies permission, just as one may immerse the entire body there. Just as Rashi needed to state explicitly that it is forbidden when connected, the Tur would likewise have needed to say so, especially as the Bach himself writes earlier. Nevertheless, I find a clear proof that the Tur agrees with Rashi. In siman 159, seif 14, the Tur rules like the Rambam and Rashi that even for other kinds of water forty seah are required. It follows that Tiberias water with less than a mikveh measure is certainly forbidden. The case must therefore be one where it remains connected to its source. Note this well. Accordingly Rabbenu Yonah is a lone opinion against the Rambam, Rashi, and Tur. Tosafot on folio 106 also cites Rashi's view.
