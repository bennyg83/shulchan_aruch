Seif 12: Snow, etc. As stated in Zevachim there: anything that completes, etc. The exclusion applies only where he added a seah, etc. Likewise, as stated in chapter 25: now, regarding his entire body, etc.; and at the beginning of chapter 7 of Mikvaos: there is, etc., and they testified, etc.
If he crushed them, etc. See Mordechai at the end of chapter 4 of Shabbos, who explains the Mishnah as referring to lukewarm water. However, in the Tosefta cited by the Rash there, it states: rounded masses of snow submerged in a mikveh raise its measure. Beit Yosef likewise rules this way in Yoreh Deah there, at least regarding netilas yadayim; and in Shulchan Aruch there he rules that it is valid even for a mikveh. See the gloss there and Magen Avraham.
Some say, etc. Berachos 50b states: The Sages taught, wine, etc. Raavad explains that this does not refer to netilas yadayim for eating, for washing is valid only with water and not with any other liquid. The Sages modeled it after the purification of a mikveh for terumah, relying on the verse, “and his hands,” etc. Rather, the Gemara refers only to ordinary cleansing. And some say, etc. This is the view of Rashba, who writes in Toras HaBayis: This does not appear correct, for if so, it should have said, “one may not wash the hands.” The term netilah derives from natla, meaning that a vessel is required. He further writes that it is reasonable that, according to both Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages, the prohibition is only because of disrespect, since wine stands in a position of distinction and is fit for the blessing Borei Peri HaGafen. After the fact, however, the washing is always valid. This is the meaning of “rather, it is forbidden,” etc., “until it establishes,” etc., excluding other beverages, which are permitted even initially and involve no disrespect, since they do not have an independent blessing category.
And some say, specifically, etc. As stated in chapter 25: Were you particular about appearance, etc.? The first opinion follows the Rosh, who holds that this applies specifically to water whose appearance changed, as in the case, “something fell into it,” etc. This differs from fruit juice. Proof may be brought from the fact that fruit juice does not invalidate a mikveh and is valid when a seah was added. See Mishmeres HaBayis.
And there is one who says that all fruit juices, etc. Meaning, wine is invalid for netilas yadayim, but fruit juices are valid. This is Rashi’s view; see his commentary there, s.v. venotlin, etc., and “when one added,” etc. Although Zevachim 22a states that the verse excludes a case where one added a seah, etc., this does not refer to fruit juice, but to liquids unfit for washing. For this reason the Gemara does not say, “to exclude fruit juice, such as where one added a seah,” etc. It parallels the opening case, where it specifies the substance, namely mud and mosquito larvae.
All the more so that it is permitted, etc. They are certainly water, as stated at the beginning of chapter 3 of Eruvin that beer invalidates a mikveh with three log. See Tosafos there 29b, s.v. hacha, etc. However, this requires study, for it should be invalid because of its appearance. The Gemara there likewise compares it to dyed water. Fruit juice itself is not difficult, as Tosafos and the Rosh explain, because it is not water and was so from the outset. This requires study.
