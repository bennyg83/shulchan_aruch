Magen Avraham s.k. 7 — since there is a ledge of any size there is recognition and it is permitted. And I wonder — what does the sugya challenge R' Huna: sometimes there are not ten and he carries from karmelit to reshus hayachid — for R' Huna who holds from the ground is washed automatically it is through a makom patur and a ledge of any size helps like for us when the sides of the ship are ten above water — and more than that, for always it is a makom patur since from the ground is washed; and requires further study; and similarly I question Rama's ruling that two ships ten above water even when not tied require eruv together — for this reasoning is hidden from R' Huna from the ground is washed and always the sides of ships are ten high — if so how do we find what is taught: if severed they become forbidden — requires further study.

seif 3 — in the gloss: standing between two houses forbidden to relieve oneself there. It appears to me it deals when the seat-place of the bathroom belongs to him alone and it is taking out from his domain to the partners' place; for otherwise also the seat-place for the partners — even though excrement is called one's Shabbos domain in the house as Magen Avraham wrote siman 376 — nevertheless until the seat-place of the bathroom carrying is permitted; then the prohibition of carrying in the partners' courtyard is only within four amos; but less than that is permitted, for one who goes from his house in his clothes to the partners' courtyard and removes clothes there — it clearly appears permitted to carry less than four amos; and as proven from that which we say in Eruvin daf 87b: behold R' Yehuda — even in d'rabbanan domains forbidden; nevertheless when lowering from wall to courtyard and he cannot avoid carrying in his fellow's courtyard — all the more; and what if they decided a makom patur — since through permitted carrying it is also forbidden like a garment and scarf — if so because they decided a makom patur the carrying was not in prohibition — it is not forbidden to carry in a fellow's courtyard except outside four amos; and afterward I saw so too is the opinion of Even HaEzer later siman 386 s.v. then it was clarified; and if so why forbidden here to relieve — behold he does not carry the excrement four amos; if so it deals here when the seat-place of the bathroom belongs to him alone and it is taking out from domain to domain; and so agreed the Gaon Baal Beit Meir. Nevertheless we need Magen Avraham's words siman 346, for the law of the bathroom is also if it is between two courtyards as explained in Darkei Moshe siman 355 seif 3 — what of it that he takes out from his domain to both domains — we rule courtyards are one domain for them; if so it is like vessels whose Shabbos rest is within the house.
