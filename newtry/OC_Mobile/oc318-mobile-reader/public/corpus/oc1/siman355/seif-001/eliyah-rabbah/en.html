[1] [Levush] Below the balcony, etc. Most authorities explain 'below' as near the water, opposite the hole in the balcony, so that we view the partitions as though they rise upward and reach the balcony. It appears that both forms are valid halachah.
[2] To pour. Even if the opening is near shallow water that has the status of a public domain, since he does not intend to discharge into the public domain it is permitted.
[3] [Levush] And surrounds it with partitions, etc. The Shulchan Aruch did not present this as the primary view, only as 'some say,' following its approach in the Beit Yosef, where this view was attributed only to Rashi and the Rosh, while the other view is held by the Rambam, Rav Hai, and the Ran. But I saw Tosafot in Eruvin 84, the Agudah in Perek HaZorek, the Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh, page 27, and Rabbenu Yerucham all agree with this view. It therefore appears that the Levush was correct, and this is the primary ruling.
[4] He makes a hole in it, etc. The Raavan in Eruvin writes that one may draw water even not directly opposite the hole, and the Magen Avraham says this is primary. He was imprecise. The Raavan is discussing even the smallest projection when it is ten handbreadths high, as stated below. In the present case everyone agrees that one must draw opposite the hole, because otherwise he transfers from a karmelit to a private domain, namely the balcony. If the balcony itself is a karmelit, he transfers from it into the boat, which is a private domain. Perhaps there is a scribal error in the Magen Avraham and his comment belongs to the later case.
[5] Within ten handbreadths of the water, etc. The boat's walls must nevertheless be ten handbreadths high, because otherwise the boat itself is a karmelit like the sea.
