(א) gezuztra. Meaning a panel extending from the wall above the water with a hole in its middle and a window open to it; and when one draws water through the hole he carries from the water which is karmelit to reshus hayachid — therefore one must make a partition all around, for now everything opposite it until below is reshus hayachid, for we say extend the partition downward.

(ב) four by four. For any partition made for less than a four-wide space is not a partition — so Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi and Tosafos.

(ג) permitted also to pour from it. Unlike one opinion that holds pouring forbidden because the river carries water outside the partition of the gezuztra.

(ד) they were lenient on a ship. Meaning in the gezuztra mentioned at the beginning one specifically needs partitions and the leniency is we say extend the partition downward; but on a ship there is further leniency that partitions are not needed, but we say bend the sides as if there were partitions and afterward we say extend the partition downward.

(ה) a ledge of any size and makes a hole in it, etc. This matter is astonishing and has no answer; and we uproot the sugya: it was stated — a ship: Rav Huna said one puts out from it a ledge of any size and draws (Rashi: draws from the sea and brings into the ship) — he holds karmelit from the ground is washed; Rashi: the measure of karmelit prohibition is until ten — they wash from the ground of the sea and the air which is above ten — even if they are water it is a makom patur; therefore in the permitted case he takes l'chatchila; and by law it should be that a ledge too should not be required except for recognition; and R' Chiya and Rabba bar Rav Huna said: make a four-wide place — they hold karmelit from the lip of water is washed — water, ground, support; and if he does not make a four-wide place he draws from karmelit to reshus hayachid; and we rule like R' Chiya and Rabba — except if the sides of the ship are ten high above the water we rule like Rav Huna that a ledge of any size suffices; and the reason is then he carries the bucket on the sides of the ship — that is above ten which is a makom patur, for the side is ten and the bucket above it. And Maggid Mishneh chapter 15 wrote: and the commentators asked — what does this four-wide place help, for if it is within ten of the lip of the sea its law is like karmelit and the ship reshus hayachid; and our Rabbi explained: behold drawing through the ledge means the ledge is hollow in its middle and he lowers his vessels through there and draws; and since it is a significant place we say bend the sides and extend as if they reach the lip of the water and all is reshus hayachid — end of his words; it is clear his intent that specifically where four is needed, drawing through the hole is required because it is difficult — behold he takes out to reshus hayachid from karmelit, for since there are not ten from the water the four-wide place he makes is karmelit from the water to the four-wide place he makes — what does this four-wide place help? Perforce there is a hole and the place is significant — it is as if there are partitions around the hole — that place with partitions joins to the side of the ship and is reshus hayachid like the ship; and if so it is explained that when there is ten in the height of the side of the ship he takes out from a makom patur to karmelit and it is permitted with a ledge of any size for mere recognition — he need not have a hole in its middle nor draw through there, for here we rule like Rav Huna who suffices with a ledge of any size; and there the Gemara explicitly says by law a ledge too should not be required except for recognition; and for recognition one need not make a hole in its middle; and if so partitions are not needed nor say bend; and further a partition there neither raises nor lowers, for any partition not made for a four-wide hollow space does not help at all as stated at the beginning of this seif in the name of Rashi and Tosafos; and here there was only any size — therefore what Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of Rav Hai that drawing through the ledge means in a ledge that has four by four — and so Tur and Rambam wrote; but if above ten he puts out a ledge of any size and draws, for behold he draws through a makom patur, etc. — behold they did not mention making a hole in the ledge as at the beginning within ten — rather certainly the intent is he draws directly through the air of a makom patur — that is the air above the side of the ship; and in Tur it is written explicitly: through air of a makom patur — all this appears clear and simple; except Beit Yosef and Maggid Mishneh understood Rambam's intent that also the ledge of any size needs a hole and one draws through there; and Rav Hai's language that Maggid Mishneh cites misled him, for he thought what Maggid Mishneh wrote that drawing through the ledge means in a ledge of any size — he did not examine well, for the simple meaning does not go this way; rather also a four-wide place is called a ledge; and further Maggid Mishneh wrote: and our Rabbi already explained this when he said for behold he draws through a makom patur — it appears from this that through the ledge the bucket enters — end of his words; and his Mishnah of our Rabbi the Baal Beit Yosef overpowered him; and Maggid Mishneh in his own reasoning explicitly wrote that if you say there is a hole, automatically you say there are partitions — it becomes reshus hayachid as Maggid Mishneh wrote that all is reshus hayachid; and if because it lacks four it is not reshus hayachid you cannot join it to the ship — if so it is karmelit, for the side of the ship is ten — automatically the panel he makes even if made adjacent directly to the lip of the side, nevertheless the air beneath the panel to the water is karmelit and the panel rests on karmelit and this is not a makom patur name but he brings through air of karmelit beneath the panel to the ship which is reshus hayachid; rather plainly this makom patur does not refer to through the hole for there is no hole at all, but the air above the side of the ship — as written explicitly: through air of a makom patur; and Mahari Chai wrote that it appears from all poskim that we do not require a hole in a ledge of any size; except from Maggid Mishneh's words in the name of Rav Hai he learned thus — the truth is with him that all poskim imply thus except what Maggid Mishneh wrote appears also he erred like Beit Yosef in this, for it did not accord with Maggid Mishneh's opinion at all: and for halachah Mahari Chai directed well and this matter written here "and makes a hole in it," etc., is imprecise — it was not taken thus; rather one draws directly from the water to the ship; and as Rashi explicitly explained Rav Huna's words — so too for R' Chiya and Rabba when ten high — and this is clear to all who examine properly.

(ו) and one may pour his water onto, etc. This refers when the sides of the ship are not ten high, for one needs a four-wide place; but to pour one does not need a ledge at all but pours directly on the side of the ship since there is only its force; unlike if he pours somewhat far from the side — it is throwing from reshus hayachid to karmelit literally; and if it is ten high and he does not want to pour on the side, e.g. they are foul and smell would enter the ship and he wants to pour far to the wall — he also needs recognition of a ledge of any size — so it appears to me.
