1) [Se'if 1] A balcony, etc. This means a board that projects from the wall above the water, with an opening in its center and a window of the house opening onto it. When one draws water through the opening, one carries from the water, which is a karmelis, into a private domain. It is therefore necessary to make a partition around the entire balcony. Everything directly beneath it, extending down to the water, is then a private domain, for we apply the principle that the partition is viewed as descending. Taz, subsection 1.
2) There. And a window of the house opens onto it. Through the window of the house that opens onto it, the balcony becomes a private domain like the house, as stated above in siman 345, se'if 16. Consequently, when one draws water from the sea or river, which is a karmelis, and raises it onto the balcony, one transfers from a karmelis into a private domain, which is forbidden. This is permitted only if one made a partition, for through the partition the area down to the water becomes a private domain, and one is then transferring from one private domain to another, which is permitted. An eruv is unnecessary because there are no residents there, as stated above in siman 354, subsection 14.
3) There. And a window of the house opens onto it. This applies when the window is four by four handbreadths wide and is within three handbreadths of the balcony. The balcony is then comparable to the holes of a private domain, whose status is that of a private domain, as stated above in siman 345, subsection 94. See there.
4) There. And a window opens onto it, etc. For otherwise the balcony is a karmelis, and it is permitted to carry from one karmelis to another karmelis. Magen Avraham, subsection 1. This means from the water, which is a karmelis, to the balcony, which is also a karmelis because it does not open onto a window, as stated above in siman 346, se'if 2. See there. It is, however, forbidden to carry from the house to the balcony or to the water, for that would constitute carrying from a private domain to a karmelis. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1, writes that even if the balcony is more than ten handbreadths high, it is a karmelis, and it is permitted to carry from the water to it. See there. Machatzis HaShekel writes likewise. However, we already wrote above in siman 345, subsection 93, in the name of several authorities, that although it is four handbreadths wide, since it is more than ten handbreadths high it is an exempt area. See there. According to their opinion, it is permitted to carry from it to the water or to the house, and one may rely on this in pressing circumstances.
5) There. And a window opens onto it, etc. If no window opens onto the balcony and the balcony is also not four by four handbreadths wide, it is an exempt area, as stated above in siman 345, se'ifim 11 and 19, and it is permitted to carry from it to the water or to the house. Regarding whether it is permitted to carry water from the sea to the balcony, which is an exempt area, and then bring it into the house, see above in siman 346, at the end of se'if 1. The Shulchan Aruch cites a dispute in this regard, and we wrote there in subsection 17 that if the object comes to rest in the exempt area and one subsequently carries it into another domain, one may be lenient. See there. All this applies when the balcony is more than ten handbreadths high. If it is less than ten handbreadths high, see above in siman 345, se'if 19, in the gloss, where it is stated that if it is in a karmelis, its status is that of a karmelis. We wrote there in subsection 31 that one should rule stringently in this manner, notwithstanding the dispute in this regard. See there. See also Olas Shabbos, subsection 120, and examine the matter carefully.
