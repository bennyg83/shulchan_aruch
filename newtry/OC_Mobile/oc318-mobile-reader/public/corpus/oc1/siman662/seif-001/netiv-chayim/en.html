(Magen Avraham seif katan 1) And it is astonishing. Note: He overlooked what R. Yitzchak ibn Giat wrote, brought in the glosses of the Rosh in chapter Bechol Me'arvin, that the Shehecheyanu of the night of Rosh Hashanah is rabbinic, but the Shehecheyanu of the night of the Festival of Sukkos is from the Torah. This is the explanation: Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are therefore not called festivals from the Torah, and the Sages obligated reciting Shehecheyanu, as concluded in chapter Bechol Me'arvin. The Shehecheyanu of shofar is said over a Torah mitzvah, and a Shehecheyanu over something rabbinic does not exempt a Shehecheyanu over a mitzvah that is from the Torah. This is not so with the Shehecheyanu of the Festival of Sukkos, which is a festival from the Torah; it exempts the Shehecheyanu of lulav.
