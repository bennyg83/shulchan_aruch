(Seif katan 1) He does not recite Shehecheyanu. See what I wrote at the end of siman 600. This means to raise a difficulty on the words of the Rema: in siman 600, the Magen Avraham wrote that some say that even if the first day of Rosh Hashanah occurs on a weekday and one recited Shehecheyanu over the shofar on the first day, nevertheless he recites Shehecheyanu over the shofar also on the second day, and he wrote that this is the custom. Here the Rema did not gloss otherwise, implying that regarding lulav, everyone agrees that one does not recite Shehecheyanu on the second day if the first day occurred on a weekday. What difference is there between lulav and shofar? In Eliyah Rabbah he wrote that the Magen Avraham missed the words of the Levush, who wrote a reason to distinguish them, as the Magen Avraham himself wrote at the end.
And see Minhagim, etc. This means that he wrote to resolve and distinguish between lulav and shofar. This is his wording in Hilchos Sukkah: We do not recite Shehecheyanu over the lulav on the second day. And if you ask: why do we recite Shehecheyanu over the shofar on both days? One may answer that the Shehecheyanu of the night of Rosh Hashanah is rabbinic, and therefore it does not exempt the shofar from reciting Shehecheyanu. But the Shehecheyanu of the night of the Festival of Sukkos is from the Torah, and therefore it exempts the lulav from reciting Shehecheyanu. And if you ask: if so, on the first day one should not recite Shehecheyanu over the lulav. One may answer that the first night requires Shehecheyanu because of sukkah, apart from Yom Tov, and therefore it does not exempt. End quote. I did not understand this; perhaps he means that one Shehecheyanu blessing does not exempt three things: the Shehecheyanu of the festival, of sukkah, and of lulav, but this is forced. It is an astonishing matter. This means that we do not find any berachah from the Torah except Bircas HaMazon according to everyone, and according to one opinion also the abbreviated blessing resembling three blessings is from the Torah, as explained in the Tur at the end of siman 209, for they raised this question in the Gemara, Eruvin 40b. This is not so with shofar. Meaning, over shofar we do not recite a berachah at the time of making it, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 641; see there for the reason. If so, one fulfills the Shehecheyanu for shofar only at the time of blowing, which is the time of fulfilling the mitzvah. Therefore, because of the doubt that perhaps the first day was weekday and he did not fulfill his obligation yesterday with Shehecheyanu, he must recite it on the second day. Thus the Shehecheyanu of shofar is like the Shehecheyanu of the festival.
