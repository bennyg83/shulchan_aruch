(53) One who lodges in a courtyard — even if the dwelling was by rental.
(54) In a house by itself — where he eats and sleeps; and he specified "by itself" — for if he were together with the homeowner, even permanently he does not restrict when he did not eruv.
(55) Except for thirty days — and more than thirty, even if it was only by borrowing alone — he is called permanent and leaves the category of guest and restricts. And it is reasonable that if he established himself from the outset for more than thirty he restricts immediately. Taz wrote that regarding more than thirty we forbid specifically when lodging in a place without a community; but in a place with a community that makes eruv verbally as is our custom — then he is exempt even in this, for the court's heart stipulates on all residents who will be added, as explained in siman 366, seif 9 — see there. Nevertheless it is good in a place where there are fairs and guests regularly come and stay more than thirty days that the community explicitly state when making eruv that it is for whoever will be added in this city.
(56) He does not restrict, etc. — some say even if he regularly comes there several times to lodge; and there are those who are stringent on this, as regarding a non-Jew in siman 384, seif 1 — see there.
(57) And even if the guests are many, etc. — they too are nullified relative to the homeowner and become as his household.
(58) But guests together — meaning there were only guests in the courtyard; and it deals when each has a separate room for eating as stated in seif 3.
(59) They restrict, etc. — for since there is no permanent one here to be attached to him they are as other homeowners; and it deals when that courtyard was a solitary courtyard; but when they lodged in a city and there are other courtyards of Israelites and the city is arranged with alleyway correction as required — these guests are nullified relative to the other permanent homeowners in the city, for a city arranged with alleyway correction as required is considered entirely as one courtyard, as stated.
(60) One another — and if it is in a non-Jew's place they need rent his permission too [Magen Avraham].
(61) Immediately — meaning even if they Shabbos only one Shabbos [later authorities].
