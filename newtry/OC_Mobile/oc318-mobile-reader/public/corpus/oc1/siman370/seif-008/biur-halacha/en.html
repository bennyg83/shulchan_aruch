Except for thirty days — and in Beit Yosef itself there is a contradiction here, for at first he wrote more than thirty, and afterward he wrote that specifically less than thirty days is considered a guest but thirty is called permanent; and Rabbi Akiva Eiger already noted this — see there; and so in Darkei Moshe siman 382 he wrote that at thirty it is called permanent, and so is the wording in Levush siman 384 and in Magen Avraham siman 391, seif kaf-bet — see there; and in truth we did not find anywhere a measure of more than thirty, and on the contrary everywhere we say that at thirty one is called a resident — see Bava Batra daf 8; and that which is in the Yerushalmi and brought in siman 384 does not restrict after thirty either — its meaning is to exclude before thirty; and so one may resolve the wording in Teshuvot HaGeonim, and as we say in the Gemara in many places "a son of thirteen years and one day" — its meaning is thirteen complete years — so too here, and it requires further study for practice.

He does not restrict the courtyard residents — see in Even HaEzer who disputes the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, and according to him a guest restricts when he has his own room where he eats; nevertheless for practice one should not deviate from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch, and all his main proofs were already rejected in Sefer Beit Me'ir who agreed to the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch — see there; and so I found in the novellae of the Rashba on daf 73b in the passage "that which the homeowner asked Rambach" that if he designated rooms for them for a time it is reasonable they do not restrict — see there; and this "for a time" means it was not more than thirty days, as he concludes a proof from the Yerushalmi on this — see there; and so in Avodat HaKodesh, fourth gate, letter bet.

And he and they are permitted, etc. — see in siman 391 in Magen Avraham seif kaf-bet who agrees to the ruling of the Mordechai that even though the guest does not restrict the courtyard residents, the courtyard residents properly restrict the guest from taking from his house to the courtyard if he did not eruv with them [and this is when he has a separate room]; however several later authorities were astonished at his words, and Beit Me'ir wrote one should not deviate from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch.

Permanent — see in Teshuvot HaGeonim and it is proven there that even guests lodging in houses not belonging to this homeowner — permanent ones who have a place there but belong to another person who does not dwell there, whether non-zero or Israel — nevertheless we say the guests are attached to the homeowner who dwells permanently in the courtyard and he does not restrict him; and so the Gra. And know that just as we say when he dwells in one house in the courtyard he is attached to the rest of the houses and does not restrict them to carry in the courtyard — so too if the guest Shabbos in some courtyard [even if in that courtyard no homeowner dwells] and the city is arranged with alleyway correction as required — he is attached to other courtyards where there are permanent homeowners and does not restrict carrying in the city streets, for a city arranged with alleyway correction as required is considered entirely as one courtyard [as appears in Teshuvot HaGeonim siman 76, and brought below in siman 391, seif 2].

They restrict one another immediately — this law the Rama brought in Darkei Moshe siman 382 from the sugya daf 65b in the incident of R' Eliezer, etc., they arrived at that inn, etc., and according to what Tosafos wrote on daf 66 inited "that they arrived" — see there; and accordingly it appears that even without rental but merely as guests alone and they can leave — like that incident where the homeowner was not at all in his house [and also none of his household as Tosafos wrote there] to rent from him — and nevertheless they restrict one another; however in Nishmat Adam he did not write thus, and it requires further study on him from this sugya; and see in Beur HaGra who wrote the source for this law from siman 366, seif 2, from the law of a ship and tents — and accordingly it is possible to uphold his law as we rule.
