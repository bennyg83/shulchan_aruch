In Magen Avraham, seif kaf-yod-bet: and if it is in a place of non-Jews, etc. — meaning that a non-Jew also dwells there. But if the homeowner is a non-Jew, he is properly called permanent for this matter that the guests should not restrict one another, and also not the others dwelling in the courtyard — all who rented from the non-Jew, as explained in siman 382 in the Beit Yosef and in the Shulchan Aruch in the gloss, seif 16, who wrote: and a non-Jew's tenant, etc. And it deals there even though each dwells independently, as stated there.
