1) [Se'if 1] One who resides in a gatehouse. A gatehouse is a situation in which one house is situated inside another and the resident of the inner house has no way to enter the courtyard or public domain except through the outer house. Chayei Adam, rule 73, subsection 1. Although it has four partitions, since people pass through it, it is not considered a residence. Magen Avraham, subsection 1; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1. This remains true even if it is roofed. Eshel Avraham, subsection 1; Chayei Adam, there.
2) There. One who resides in a gatehouse. There is no distinction between a public gatehouse and a private gatehouse, or even the gatehouse of a house. This is clearly the opinion of Maran, of blessed memory, in Beis Yosef, who writes that this is the ruling we adopt. Tosafos, however, distinguish between them. Specifically one who resides in the gatehouse of a courtyard does not prohibit carrying, since it is unfit for residence, but one who resides in the gatehouse of a house does prohibit carrying. Beis Yosef cites their words. See there. Bach rules likewise. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, subsection 1, cites him. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1. Chayei Adam, there, subsection 2, likewise writes that a private gatehouse, meaning one with only a single householder residing farther inside, is not regarded so fully as a gatehouse and its resident prohibits carrying. See there. It therefore appears that one should be stringent. This applies only to requiring its resident to contribute a share to the eruv, but the eruv itself should not be placed within it. See above, siman 366, subsection 27, and below, subsection 26.
3) There. One who resides in a gatehouse. Even if he also eats there on this Shabbos, it is not called a residence. Beis Yosef; Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1.
4) There. And a balcony. This is explained below at the beginning of siman 375.
5) There. Does not prohibit, etc. This means if he did not contribute a share with them to the eruv.
6) There. For they are not considered residences. The same applies to a sukkah during Sukkos. Yoma 10a; Magen Avraham, subsection 2. This is so even if it has four complete walls and a roof. Chayei Adam, there. Similarly, one who resides in a house that is not four by four amos does not prohibit carrying, for a residential house is required. Eshel Avraham, subsection 2. See above, siman 366, se'if 3, and our words there in subsections 29 and 30.
7) There. But one who resides in a straw shed, etc. It is made from tree branches and reeds, meaning that it is not a permanent house, yet it is nevertheless called a house for every purpose. Beis Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yehonasan. This applies if he regularly establishes his meal of bread there. If he does not regularly establish his meal of bread there, one who resides there does not prohibit carrying, as stated below in se'if 5. Similarly, if the householder retains rights of use in that place, one who resides there does not prohibit carrying, as stated below in se'if 2 and explained in Beis Yosef.
8) There. Prohibits. He prohibits carrying until he contributes a share to the eruv with the residents of the courtyard.
