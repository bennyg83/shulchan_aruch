(7) And he lent them, etc. — and specifically when he lent them for a long time; but if he lent them for a short time he does not restrict him in any matter [Avodat HaKodesh].
(8) Due to their weight — even though they do not become muktzeh through the weight — nevertheless since there is trouble to remove them there is no way to take them on Shabbos.
(9) That it is forbidden to carry them even, etc. — for if it were permitted to carry them for the sake of their place and location, the homeowner might need to use them on Shabbos or take them out of his house for some reason and he would not retain a hold there.
(10) That they all become like guests — since he did not remove himself from these houses entirely; and specifically when his objects are placed there — but as long as nothing is placed there, even though he has permission to place there it does not help.
(11) With him — and it deals with when he has permission to place there; and even if he does not have permission to place in the entire house but only in one corner — but if he has no permission for this at all, only that he placed there incidentally and they do not object to this — they do not thereby become like guests with him and they restrict him.
(12) And if the houses are not his, etc. — meaning that the law above deals with when the houses are essentially his or he rented or borrowed them and he rented or lent them to another — for in this we say he becomes like a guest with him; but if the house does not belong to him at all but both are neighbors in a courtyard and each has his own house and one has permission in his fellow's house to place his objects there and he placed there — he does not become the homeowner over him and the counterpart is like a guest with him.
(13) For all this, etc. — meaning: when does the homeowner's hold help when there are no other residents in the courtyard except him and those men he lent and rented to [or even if there are other residents in the courtyard but those residents bring their eruv and place it in this homeowner's house or in one of the borrowed houses where the homeowner has a hold — then they need not give any bread at all for the eruv, for all these houses are as one house and a house in which the eruv rests need not give bread]; but when there are other residents in the courtyard and they place the eruv not in this homeowner's house but in the houses of the other residents — then the homeowner in any case needs to give a share in the eruv; the hold does not exempt the renters from giving a share in the eruv, for since the other residents restrict, those dwelling in his house also restrict him until they give a share in the eruv.
(14) One of the rooms — meaning one room of the hall.
(15) With this intent — meaning at the time he rented from the non-Jew he did not intend to rent on behalf of himself and his fellow.
(16) But, etc., with this intent — meaning from the beginning of the rental he intended to rent also on behalf of his fellow and the non-Jew agreed that they dwell together — it turns out this renter from the non-Jew acts as agent for his fellow and both are equal in this house.
(17) And it does not help — and likewise in the Mechaber's words: if the houses are not his, etc.
