One who dwells in a gatehouse — and there is no distinction between a public gatehouse or an individual's, and even a house's gatehouse — such is Maran's view in the Beit Yosef, and he wrote that so we rule. But Tosafos' view is to distinguish: specifically one who dwells in a courtyard's gatehouse does not restrict, for it is not fit for dwelling, but one who dwells in a house's gatehouse restricts. Beit David, Beit Yosef — see there. And so the Bach. And they brought it in Knesset HaGedolah, letter alef — see there, letter alef, Avnei Nezer, letter alef. And so Chayei Adam there, letter bet — an individual's gatehouse, meaning when no one dwells inside except one homeowner, is not so significant and restricts — see there. And perforce one should be stringent and give a share in the eruv but not place the eruv inside it. And see above siman 366, letter 27, and below letter 26.
