A homeowner who has many houses in a courtyard and lent or rented them to others — if he has in each of them items that cannot be moved on Shabbat due to their weight or due to prohibition (meaning things forbidden to move even for the sake of their place) — the residents of those houses do not restrict him, since they are all considered like guests in his home. Therefore they too are permitted to carry from their houses to the courtyard even without giving an eruv. But if the houses do not belong to him — neither owned nor rented — even if he has items in them that are forbidden to move, they restrict one another. {Rama: Rema: Some say all this applies when there are no other residents in the courtyard besides them, but when there are other residents who bring their eruv to them, each one must make an eruv. One who rented a large building from a non-Jew and sublet one of its units to another — if he did not originally rent it with that intention, it is as if the entire building is his and he merely rented one of the houses to another. But if he originally rented it with that intention, it is as if there are two houses and it does not help, even if one of them has a hold in the other's house.}
