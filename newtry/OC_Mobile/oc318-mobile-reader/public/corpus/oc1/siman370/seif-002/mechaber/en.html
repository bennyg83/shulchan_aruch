A homeowner who has many houses in a courtyard and lent or rented them to others — if he has in each of them items that cannot be moved on Shabbos' due' to their weight or due to prohibition, meaning things forbidden to move even for the sake of their place — the residents of those houses do not restrict him, since they all become like guests with him. Therefore they too are permitted to take out from their houses to the courtyard even though they did not give an eruv. But if the houses are not his — neither owned nor rented — even if he has in them things forbidden to move, they restrict one another.And some say all this applies when there are no other residents in the courtyard except them. But when there are other residents and they bring their eruv to them, each one must make an eruv (Rabbenu Yehonatan, chapter Keitzad Mishtatfin). One who rented a house from a non-Jew and sublet one of the large building's units to another — if he did not originally rent it with this intention, it is as if the entire building is his and he rented one of the houses to another. But if he originally rented it with this intention, it is as if there are two houses and it does not help even if one of them has a hold in his fellow's house (Maharik, root 41).
