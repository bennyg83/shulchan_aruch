2) There. One who resides in a gatehouse. There is no distinction between a public gatehouse and a private gatehouse, or even the gatehouse of a house. This is clearly the opinion of Maran, of blessed memory, in Beis Yosef, who writes that this is the ruling we adopt. Tosafos, however, distinguish between them. Specifically one who resides in the gatehouse of a courtyard does not prohibit carrying, since it is unfit for residence, but one who resides in the gatehouse of a house does prohibit carrying. Beis Yosef cites their words. See there. Bach rules likewise. Shiyurei Knesses HaGedolah, subsection 1, cites him. See there. Eliyah Rabbah, subsection 1; Eshel Avraham, subsection 1. Chayei Adam, there, subsection 2, likewise writes that a private gatehouse, meaning one with only a single householder residing farther inside, is not regarded so fully as a gatehouse and its resident prohibits carrying. See there. It therefore appears that one should be stringent. This applies only to requiring its resident to contribute a share to the eruv, but the eruv itself should not be placed within it.
