Except, Taz. His words, seif 62. And in letter 9 too, similarly two inner ones — one eruv. And he challenged the Bach. And in Avnei Nezer, letter 4, he wrote to reconcile the Bach's words — see there. And behold the Bach holds: in the beginning, when all have one opening to one another; in the latter part, when each has an opening independently — there is a distinction between partitions reaching the ceiling within three handbreadths or not reaching. And this is: if they reach the ceiling and there are other courtyard residents besides the hall and the eruv is with others — since the others restrict, those in the hall also restrict. Then the two inner ones each give independently a complete eruv by themselves to the other courtyard residents, and the outer ones give nothing to the eruv even in partnership, for one who dwells in a gatehouse does not restrict at all. And if partitions do not reach the ceiling, then the two innermost give together one complete eruv, as explained in siman 368, seif 3, and the rest are completely exempt, for one who dwells in a gatehouse, etc. Not so in the latter part where each has independently — then even the outer ones are all included in the eruv. And there is a distinction between reaching the ceiling or not. And that he did not distinguish in the beginning at one opening for all between reaching the ceiling or not — he relied on what is stated in the latter part and in seif 6: ten houses, etc. — the dispute of Shmuel and R' Yochanan. Eruvin 75 too — according to R' Yochanan, since the halachah is like him, the two innermost each give an eruv independently, since their opinions were divided from the outset they are considered as dwelling in their own domains even though there are no other courtyard residents, and even if the eruv comes to them the law is thus. But the Taz disagrees and holds: whenever they have only one opening and the inner one cannot exit except through the outer one — they are considered as one winter-house room. And if they are only two brothers, no eruv is needed at all since they have one opening and the inner cannot pass to the inner except through the outer. And that which is in seif 7: when there are courtyard residents — then the two innermost give in partnership one eruv to the other courtyard residents. And if the eruv comes to them, then the two innermost give nothing, for the house in which the eruv rests gives nothing. And therefore he asked in the beginning when there is only one opening — there is no distinction at all between reaching the ceiling or not reaching the ceiling, for certainly when there is only one opening the entire hall is like one winter house (according to the Taz). And when there are no courtyard residents the hall is exempt from eruv. And when there are courtyard residents, the two innermost give in partnership one eruv even when partitions reach the ceiling, since there is only one opening. Only in the latter part is there a distinction between reaching the ceiling or not, since each has an opening independently. And nevertheless his words still need explanation regarding what he wrote in the latter part when partitions do not reach — in any case one eruv is required, for we hold these restrict — somewhat difficult. And what he further wrote in the latter part that each has an opening — there is a distinction regarding the two innermost — prima facie it requires further study. One may say: here there are three distinctions: (1) when there is only one opening — no distinction at all between reaching the ceiling or not, and the entire hall is completely exempt from eruv even in partnership — only the two innermost give one eruv in partnership to the other courtyard residents and the eruv is with the courtyard residents. And if each also has an opening independently and nevertheless they also have permission to pass through one another — for sometimes they so stipulated — then the Bach's law applies: the outer ones are completely exempt as gatehouse and the two innermost — if partitions reach the ceiling each gives an eruv independently to the other courtyard residents; if partitions do not reach, the two innermost give one eruv in partnership to the other courtyard residents with the courtyard residents. Not so when each has an opening independently and there is no permission to pass through one another at all and they prevent passing — then it is the third law mentioned in the Shulchan Aruch here. And see there. And for what I wrote "if not" and in Magen Avraham letters zayin and chet — there is practical difference between the Bach and Taz as stated. And nevertheless it requires further study, for it is not found explicitly to say that whenever there is one opening — one eruv in partnership. And "if not" in letter 9 will be further explained.
