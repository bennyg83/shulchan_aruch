Five groups who spent Shabbos in a large hall and divided it with partitions — if they all pass through one another's sections, since there is only one opening to the courtyard through the outermost section and all pass through it — they need give only two innermost groups to the eruv, and all the others are considered a gatehouse for them.Or they do not pass through one another but each has an opening to the gatehouse before them and the gatehouse opens to the courtyard (Beit Yosef).} If the partitions reach the ceiling within three handbreadths, so that each is a room by itself — even if they are of curtains — if the courtyard residents place the eruv in one of the other courtyard houses, each and every group in the hall must give an eruv. But if they placed the eruv in this hall, none need give an eruv at all, since they all dwell in this house, and the house in which the eruv is placed need not give an eruv. And similarly if there are no other residents in the courtyard they need no eruv. If they divided it with partitions that do not reach the ceiling — even if the courtyard residents place their eruv in one of the other courtyard houses — one eruv suffices for all five groups. {Rama: If some made partitions and some did not — those who made them are separate, and those who did not are considered joint (Magid, chapter 4).} And if there are residents in actual upper stories, even if the courtyard residents placed the eruv in the hall, each group must give an eruv.
