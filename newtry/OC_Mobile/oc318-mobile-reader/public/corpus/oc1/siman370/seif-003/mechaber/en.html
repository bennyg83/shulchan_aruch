Five groups that spent Shabbos in a large hall and divided it with partitions: if they all pass through one another, because only the outermost section has an opening to the courtyard and all of them pass through it, only the two innermost groups must contribute to the eruv, while all the others are considered a gatehouse for them. If each group has an opening to the courtyard, {Rama: or if they do not pass through one another, but each has an opening to the gatehouse before it and the gatehouse opens to the courtyard [Beit Yosef],} then if the partitions reach to within 3 tefachim of the ceiling, so that each section is a room unto itself, even if the partitions are curtains, and the residents of the courtyard place their eruv in one of the other houses of the courtyard, each group in the hall must contribute to the eruv. But if they placed the eruv in this hall, none of them need contribute at all, because they all live in this house, and the house in which the eruv is placed need not contribute. Likewise, if there are no other residents in the courtyard, they do not require an eruv. If they divided the hall with partitions that do not reach the ceiling, even if the courtyard residents place their eruv in one of the other houses, one eruv suffices for all 5 groups. {Rama: If some made partitions and some did not, those who made partitions are considered separate, while those who did not are considered jointly situated. [Maggid Mishneh, chapter 4]} If they actually reside in upper stories, even if the residents of the courtyard placed the eruv in the hall, each group must contribute to the eruv. But one who has a tutor or a scribe in his house, and likewise students who study before a rabbi and live in his house, each in his own room, even if each has an opening to the courtyard and eats and sleeps in his room, do not prohibit carrying for the others.
