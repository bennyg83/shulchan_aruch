The two inner ones, etc. Meaning, they jointly make one eruv, whether the partitions reach the ceiling or do not reach it. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, exempts the inner one when the partitions do not reach the ceiling, but he was not precise, as the Taz notes. In my humble opinion, the Bach means that if they reach the ceiling, each makes an eruv separately, while if they do not reach the ceiling, they make one jointly. This is also implied by Eruvin 75 and the poskim. Likewise, what is implied by the Taz, that if there are no residents in the courtyard they are entirely exempt from an eruv, is incorrect. The Beit Yosef makes clear that this is only according to Shmuel, who holds that only the innermost group makes an eruv. According to Rabbi Yochanan, who holds that both inner groups make an eruv, they must do so even if there are no residents in the courtyard. It also appears that each makes its own eruv, as stated above.
Curtains, etc. This applies specifically when they are fastened with nails so that the wind cannot move them, as stated by Olat Shabbos. See seif 4.
That does not touch, etc. It requires study whether this applies when the construction is permanent, for perhaps the discussion concerns only temporary partitions, as Olat Shabbos writes. In my humble opinion, however, the Mordechai and Agudah that I cite below indicate that even with permanent construction an eruv suffices, as the Bach writes, since the partitions do not reach the ceiling.
Actual upper stories, etc. The Mordechai in Perek HaDar writes: But with actual rooms and upper stories divided by complete partitions, even if the eruv is brought to them, each one requires its own eruv. The Agudah there writes: This is unlike partitions that reach the ceiling, for that case concerns temporary partitions such as curtains, whereas here there is permanent construction. End quote.
The Levush writes: Furthermore, one does not lend, etc. This second reason is the principal one, and one should not rely on the first reason to permit where the second reason does not apply, as stated by the Beit Yosef and later authorities. At first glance it appears that even for this reason one should not permit, and this is somewhat implied by Responsa Rama, siman 120. I also find a proof from seif 2, where it states that even in a case of lending, tefisat habayit is required, and we do not say that one does not lend, etc., and that he can remove them. Yet according to this, this reason alone should indeed permit because he can remove them. The case above in seif 2 must therefore concern one where he cannot remove them. See siman 382, seif 18.
