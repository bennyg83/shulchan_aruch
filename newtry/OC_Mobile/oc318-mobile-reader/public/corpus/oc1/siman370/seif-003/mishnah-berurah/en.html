(18) That they Shabbos in a hall — meaning their dwelling was in a hall.
(19) Except the two inner ones — for even though the second is also a gatehouse for the first, since the first passes through it — nevertheless it is not called a gatehouse to exempt from eruv, for an individual's gatehouse [meaning only one person passes through it] is not called a gatehouse as below seif 7; and perforce each one is obligated in eruv. And it deals with when the partitions between them reach the ceiling; and if the partitions do not reach the ceiling they are considered as two like one house and need give only one eruv between them if there are other residents in the courtyard with them.
(20) And if each had — meaning they do not pass through one another but each has from his room an opening open to the courtyard.
(21) Or they do not pass, etc. — meaning the Mechaber depicted they do not pass in the manner where each had from his room an opening to the courtyard, and the Rama adds that even if all the openings were not open to the courtyard but each was open independently to the gatehouse that was opposite all the rooms, and in the gatehouse there was one opening from which they exit to the courtyard — and he informs us: do not say since they all exit to the courtyard through one opening — meaning the gatehouse opening — they are called joined and as if passing through one another; he teaches us we do not pay attention to this since they do not pass through one another. And accordingly in our houses where several neighbors dwell in one house, each in separate rooms — even though they pass outside through the house before them which is their gatehouse — nevertheless all are obligated in eruv; and therefore if the city's eruv was damaged they are forbidden to carry each from his room to his fellow's room [unless the homeowner has a hold in their rooms — such as objects not carried, etc., as above in seif 2 — then they are considered as if the entire house and rooms are his and they are only guests with him and eruv is not needed]; and regarding carrying from room to house see below seif 7, and see in Be'er Heitev and Biur Halachah what we wrote on this.
(22) Within three — for it is lavud and considered sealed above.
(23) Of curtains — however in a manner tied well below so the wind will not move them, as above in siman 362, seif 1 [later authorities].
(24) Each one requires, etc. — for since those residents in the courtyard restrict if they did not eruv, those in the hall also restrict since the partitions in them are divided even though they are weak partitions.
(25) They need not, etc. — and it deals with temporary partitions like curtain partitions; but if they divided with complete partitions such as of boards and the like — certainly there is no difference between the eruv coming to them, etc., and whether there are other residents in the courtyard or not — in all cases they need give a share in the eruv, as the Mechaber concludes at the end: if there were residents in actual attics, etc.
(26) That they all dwell in this house — meaning even though the eruv rests in one room it is as if the eruv rested with all of them, for the ceiling joins them to one house since beneath it are weak partitions.
(27) In partitions that do not reach the ceiling — even if they are permanent partitions of boards and stones and even if higher than ten.
(28) One eruv suffices, etc. — for they are as one house.
(29) They made partitions — reaching the ceiling.
(30) They are as partners — meaning: then all give one eruv.
(31) Actual attics — we already wrote that the same applies when the companies were divided by permanent partitions such as of boards and the like since they reach the ceiling.
(32) Each company requires, etc. — and even when there are no other residents in the courtyard they need make eruv chatzerot.
(33) But one who has, etc. — the reason: since they all use the homeowner's for baking and cooking and everything — they are all considered as if they eat and sleep in one place; and even though they have an opening toward the courtyard they are called in the homeowner's name; and furthermore he did not lend them permission to restrict him. And all this when it was lent to them; but if the place of the rooms was purchased or rented to them, or even borrowed but each uses independently in his room — they restrict; for specifically with both reasons combined do we be lenient. And all this when the homeowner had no hold with them — otherwise it is permitted in every case, as above in seif 2.
(34) Opening open to the courtyard — and one might say he can restrict the courtyard residents when they did not eruv.
(35) They do not restrict — the courtyard residents and not one another.
