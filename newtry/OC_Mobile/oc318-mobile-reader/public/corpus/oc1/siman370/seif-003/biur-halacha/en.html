Or they do not pass, etc. — see in Magen Avraham what he wrote that from room to house is forbidden if they are two [and I say even if the room has no other opening to exit except through the house — because a single person's gatehouse is not called a gatehouse as below seif 7]; and it is proven from his words that just as the Sages forbade carrying from house to courtyard without eruv, so too they forbade taking from house to house — and likewise he himself at the beginning of siman 366, and all later authorities there. And see in Be'er Heitev who wrote in the name of Sefer Divrei Shmuel that he was uncertain about this law, and already they challenged him in Sefer Maamar Mordechai and Birkhei Yosef that it is not so — on the contrary, in that sefer he explicitly agreed to the prohibition. However, there are some later authorities who wavered on this due to the wording of the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchot Eruvin, that it appears from him that the main decree was from house to courtyard so they should not come to confuse private domain with public domain. But the Tosefta already wrote at the beginning of chapter Halon that he too agrees to the prohibition — only that the decree began at house to courtyard and afterward they forbade also from house to house. And even though in Shoshanim LeDavid he expanded to dispute the Tosefta — nevertheless for the law, the main view is like Magen Avraham and other later authorities who agreed to the prohibition. And so it is explained explicitly in Avodat HaKodesh to the Rashba, third gate, seif 9, and thus: the houses — even though their use is one — it is forbidden to carry from this one's house to that one's house until they make eruv; and not only that, but even from his house to the house shared between him and his fellow, and from house to the courtyard shared with his fellow — forbidden until they eruv both, end quote — behold for him the main decree was from house to house and afterward they added that even from house to courtyard is forbidden; and if so, at least from house to house is no worse. And why compare the dispute between him and the Rambam? And so it appears in Eruvin 17a in Rashi on the mishna "and from eruv"; also in Semag in the name of Beit HaGera at the beginning of Hilchot Eruvin; and in Orchot Chayim at the beginning of eruv — it appears plainly that the language of the Sages' decree was also from house to house — see there.

Each company requires, etc. — specifically when the companies were made permanent here — meaning more than thirty days; less than this they are in the category of guests and are nullified relative to the permanent homeowner and need not give a share in the eruv, as below at the end of the siman; and so in Sha'arei Teshuvah there — see there.

And if they dwelled, etc. each company, etc. — besides that company beside which they place the eruv — they need not give a share in the eruv.

They do not restrict — see Mishna Berurah seif kaf-lamed-gimel, and it is from Taz and Magen Avraham, and as the commentators explained like the words of Magen Avraham that he too holds that two reasons are specifically required. [And prima facie it is astonishing on Taz and Magen Avraham that they did not mention in the second reason except because he did not lend permission to restrict him — and they did not conclude because he can evict them, as mentioned in Tosafos — however according to what Tosafos' view was brought in the novellae of the Rashba and Ritva and in Orchot Chayim it is satisfactory that they did not conclude in Tosafos' view because he can evict them — and so it is proven further there, and also in his Avodat HaKodesh that regarding "he can evict them" even the Rashba who disputes Tosafos' view agrees to be lenient, as will be written below.] And so it appears to be the main view for practice, for behold the Rashba in Avodat HaKodesh and in his novellae and Ritva dispute on the fundamental law of a company — see there; and if so, even if we adopt Tosafos' view to be lenient regarding lending because he did not lend with intent to restrict him — even when he lent for a long time, as appears in the novellae of the Rashba according to Tosafos' view as stated — meaning at least where there is also the first reason of baking and cooking [and baking alone certainly does not suffice, as Beit Yosef wrote, and furthermore behold the Rashba and Ritva who brought this view of Tosafos to permit did not copy this reason at all — only the second reason because he did not lend to restrict — see there] — and it appears that only until here did they require two reasons in a place where he lent for a time that he cannot evict them at any hour — and therefore if not for the first reason of baking and cooking in one place he could certainly restrict, as explained by the Mechaber in seif 2, and so it is explained in Chayei Adam that where there are two reasons, even if he lent for a long time it is permitted; but where he can evict them at any hour — such as when he did not lend for a specified time — he cannot restrict him even if he bakes and cooks independently, and so I found in Rabbi Akiva Eiger in the second answer who sides thus that where he can evict them at any hour we do not require the first reason at all — and so it appears from the Gra — see there — and somewhat so in Beur HaGra [who did not bring only the second reason mentioned in Tosafos, and perforce "he can evict them" — otherwise behold he contradicts the Mechaber's words in seif 2]; and so I found explicitly in the novellae of the Rashba and in Avodat HaKodesh that specifically when he lent for a long time, but for a short time he is like a guest and does not restrict — and it is understood accordingly that the same and all the more so when he can evict at any time is no better than a short time. And know that the Gra's view depends only on whether he can evict them — and where he cannot evict them it appears from him that eruv is required even if they bake and cook together, and it is exactly like the view of the Rashba and Ritva as stated, and somewhat so in Beur HaGra, and the Mechaber was silent on this. And know further that even though we conclude that through baking and cooking together alone it does not help to permit — nevertheless where they all pass through the house to the courtyard or public domain and each does not have a special opening to exit — they are all considered as one house, as proven in Beit Yosef at the end of the passage beginning "the Rambam wrote" — see there; and even though Beit Yosef himself in this siman brought in the name of Semak and the Rama copied it in the first gloss that what they all exit through one gatehouse does not help to join them since each room has its own opening — nevertheless here, where the house through which they pass is also the place of baking and cooking for all — it is considered as one house, and still requires further study.
