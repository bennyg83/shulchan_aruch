Courtyard residents who all eat at one table — even if each has their' own house — need no eruv since they are like members of one household. If they need to make an eruv with residents of another courtyard, they bring only one eruv and one loaf to the place with which they are making the eruv. If the eruv comes to them, they need not contribute an eruv, just as the house in which the eruv is placed need not contribute, since all these houses are considered as one.Rema: Similarly, if many homeowners eat in one room each at their own table, even if each sleeps in their own room, one eruv suffices for all, since there is no partition separating their eating area — this is like five who spent Shabbat in a hall, etc. Even if they sometimes draw a curtain before them for privacy, it is not considered a partition since it is not there permanently.
