Courtyard residents who all eat at one table — even if each has their' own house — need no eruv, since they are like members of one household. If they need to make an eruv with residents of another courtyard, they bring one eruv for all of them and only one loaf to the place with which they are making the eruv. If the eruv comes to them, they need not give an eruv, like the law of the house in which the eruv is placed, since all these houses are considered as one.And similarly if many homeowners eat in one room each at their own table, even if each sleeps in their own room — one eruv for all, since no partition separates their eating place — it is like five who spent Shabbos in a hall, etc. And even if they sometimes spread a curtain before them for privacy, it is not considered a partition since it is not there permanently (Rabbeinu Yerucham, part 16).
