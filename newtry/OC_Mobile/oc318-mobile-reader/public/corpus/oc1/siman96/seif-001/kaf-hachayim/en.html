(1) [Seif 1] When one is praying, one should not hold in one's hand tefillin, etc.—Rashi, may his memory be blessed, explained: tefillin and a Sefer Torah, because one is preoccupied in thought lest they fall from his hand and be disgraced, and one's mind is not settled on one's prayer; and a knife—one fears lest it fall and stab him in the foot; and a full bowl and money lest they scatter; and a loaf—if it falls it will be repulsive. Therefore it implies that other things in which there is no loss if they fall from his hand are permitted to pray while still in his hand. But R' Yonah wrote in the chapter Mi Shemeito that some explain that a knife, bowl, and all these that he listed are not specifically required but he mentioned them as the usual manner of speech; and likewise one should not take anything in his hand during prayer except a lulav alone, for because of love of the mitzvah one does not diminish thereby one's concentration in prayer—so he wrote. And Beit David, Beit Yosef, and Prishah, note 2; and see Taz, note 2, who wrote to bring proof for the words of R' Yonah, and it appears that he agrees with his words—see there. And likewise Eliyah Rabbah, note 2, that in the Semak, siman 11, it is written as the words of R' Yonah, and the Taz likewise agreed—see there. And likewise Chayei Adam, general rule 22, note 7; and although Maamer Mordechai, note 2, and Nishmat Shabbat and Nehar Shabbos, note 1, wrote regarding the Taz's proof that it is not proof, and they also wrote there that Maamer Mordechai and Nishmat Shabbat hold that Maran's view, may his memory be blessed, is like Rashi—nevertheless one should be stringent and not hold anything in his hand during prayer. And likewise Shulchan Aruch HaRav, Derekh Chayyim 1:1, and the Chida in Kaf HaChayyim 12, note 35, that one should not hold anything in his hand when praying except the machzor. And further see below, note 6.
