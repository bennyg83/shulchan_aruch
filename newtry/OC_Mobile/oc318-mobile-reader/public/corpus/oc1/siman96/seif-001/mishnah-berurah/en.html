(1) While he is praying - Peri Megadim writes that the same applies during Krias Shema and Pesukei DeZimrah.
(2) He should not hold, etc. - After the fact, he need not pray again, unless he knows that because of this he failed to concentrate during the blessing of Avos. A chazzan may hold the Sefer Torah on Shabbos while reciting Yekum Purkan, because his intention then is to pray for those who study Torah. He therefore holds the Sefer Torah for that purpose, not merely to guard it, and it is like holding the lulav at its appointed time. It is likewise permitted to hold it while blessing the new month.
(3) Nor a sefer, etc. - The same applies to all of our sefarim.
(4) A full bowl - His mind is on it lest its contents spill. Regarding a knife, he is concerned lest it fall to the ground and pierce his foot; regarding money, lest it scatter. Even if the money is bundled, he is distracted lest it become lost, unless he holds it in a manner in which there is no concern that it will be lost. Birkei Yosef writes that it is likewise forbidden to seat a young child in front of oneself during prayer.
(5) Money, a loaf, etc. - Specifically these objects are forbidden, because if they fall there will be a loss or they may injure him. Other objects may be held. Some say that these examples are not exclusive but merely reflect the usual circumstances, and that one should not hold anything during prayer. Taz agrees with this view.
(6) Because his mind, etc. - It requires study whether one may hold tefillin or a Sefer Torah when he fears thieves. Perhaps the Sages permitted it. Peri Megadim.
(7) It is permitted to hold, etc. - If a sefer fell to the floor and he cannot concentrate, he may lift it after completing the blessing in which he is presently engaged. Otherwise, he should not interrupt. Certainly, one may not gesture with a finger or the like during prayer. If one began the Amidah and became confused, he may walk to a known place to take a siddur from there. Chayei Adam.
