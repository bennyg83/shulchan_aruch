He should not hold tefillin in his hand. It seems straightforward that this is only at the outset; but after the fact one need not return and pray again—even according to Talmudic law, for the Tur wrote in siman 101 that one must return because of lack of concentration in the first blessing; nevertheless, here, even though he held a book in his hand, it is not impossible that he could concentrate, and if it is clear to him that he concentrated he certainly fulfilled his obligation; and if he did not concentrate he did not fulfill it—only that the Sages forbade at the outset to hold a book in his hand, for it is likely he will come to nullify concentration and make a blessing in vain; and this is straightforward in my humble opinion. However, I saw in the Maharach, may his memory be blessed, who wrote that if one transgressed and held a Sefer Torah or tefillin in his hand, he must return and pray again—and this is very astonishing. And what people are accustomed to—that the shaliach tzibbur holds a Sefer Torah in his hand on Shabbat and says Yehi ratzon and other things—even though concentration is not required as in the Shemoneh Esrei, nevertheless we see they are accustomed thus to honor those prayers; and in truth this is not an enhancement per the words of our Sages regarding the Shemoneh Esrei—so why should one do so in other prayers? It seems to me: since the shaliach tzibbur's intention then is to pray for Torah scholars in the wording of Yehi ratzon, therefore he holds the Sefer Torah in his hand—it is like a lulav in its time. And not only that, but according to what appears, if one holds a Sefer Torah in his hand during the Shemoneh Esrei so that awe of Heaven and fear of the Torah be upon him, and thereby he will have more concentration—there is no prohibition in the matter, for perforce they did not forbid in the Gemara except when one holds it for mere guarding, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Terumat HaDeshen—only for guarding and not to perform a mitzvah with them, etc. But nevertheless one should not be lenient in this in the Shemoneh Esrei, since the permission is not found explicit.

Because his heart is on them. In Beit Yosef he wrote that from Rashi's explanation it appears that other things in which there is no loss if they fall from his hand are permitted to pray while still in his hand. And Rabbeinu Yonah wrote in the name of the Yereim that all these are not specifically required but he mentioned them as the usual manner of speech; and likewise one should not take anything in his hand during prayer except a lulav alone, etc.—so far. And proof for this appears from what we say at the end of siman 97, that a load less than four kav must be slung behind him—it implies that any load is forbidden to move before him.
