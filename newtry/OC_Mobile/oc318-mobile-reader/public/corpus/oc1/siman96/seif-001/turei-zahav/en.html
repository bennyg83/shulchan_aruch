He should not hold tefillin in his hand. It appears obvious that this is only an initial requirement, but after the fact he need not pray again. Even according to the law of the Gemara cited by the Tur in siman 101, that one must pray again because of deficient concentration during Avos, holding a sefer does not make concentration impossible. If he is certain that he concentrated, he has certainly fulfilled his obligation; if he did not concentrate, he has not. The Sages merely prohibited holding a sefer initially because it is likely to impair concentration and cause him to recite a blessing in vain. This is obvious in my humble opinion. I saw, however, that my father-in-law, the Bach, writes that if one transgressed and held a Sefer Torah or tefillin, he must pray again. This is exceedingly surprising. Regarding the practice that the chazzan holds the Sefer Torah on Shabbos while reciting Yekum Purkan and other prayers, although these do not require concentration to the degree of the Amidah, we see that the practice is to do so in honor of those prayers. In truth, this is not an enhancement according to the Sages' words concerning the Amidah, and why should it be done during other prayers? It appears to me that since the chazzan's intention then is to pray for those who study Torah in the text of Yekum Purkan, he holds the Sefer Torah for that purpose, and it is like holding the lulav at its appointed time. Moreover, it would appear that if one holds a Sefer Torah during the Amidah so that the fear of Heaven and awe of Torah will rest upon him and thereby improve his concentration, there is no prohibition. The Gemara prohibited only holding it merely for safekeeping, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, that the concern applies only when guarding them and not when performing a mitzvah with them. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient concerning the Amidah, since no explicit permission is found.

Rabbeinu Yonah writes in the name of some commentators that all these examples are not exclusive, but merely reflect the usual circumstances, and that one should hold nothing during prayer except a lulav. It appears that there is proof for this from the end of siman 97, where a burden weighing less than four kav must be shifted behind him. This implies that every burden is forbidden to be carried in front of him.
