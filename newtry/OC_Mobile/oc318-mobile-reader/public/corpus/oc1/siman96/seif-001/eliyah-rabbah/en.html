And let's not be afraid that the tongue will not grasp the meaning precisely to begin with, and the same meaning in all the rulings, and the same is explained in Rambam, Chapter 5 of the Laws of Prayer:
[2] Tefillin, etc. Rabbeinu Yonah writes that these examples are not exclusive, and the same applies to any object: one should not hold anything during prayer except a lulav. Nachalas Tzvi writes that this also appears from Rambam, who writes generally, "and not a utensil." His wording necessarily contains a scribal error, for Rambam does not use this expression. It should read, "and not utensils," as Rambam writes in chapter 5 of Hilchos Tefillah: "and not utensils or money." Nachalas Tzvi apparently infers from the general term "utensils" that one should not hold any object. This is not proof, however, for it may refer to utensils similar to money, about which one is distracted, as Lechem Mishneh writes. This is also implied by Tzeidah LaDerech. Nevertheless, I saw in Sefer Mitzvos Katan, siman 11, that he agrees with Rabbeinu Yonah, and Taz likewise concurs.
