See Taz - Berachos 23b states: The Sages taught that one should not hold a Sefer Torah or tefillin and pray. Shmuel said: The same applies to a knife, money, a bowl, a loaf, and similar items. According to Rashi, these examples are specific; according to Rabbeinu Yonah, everything is forbidden. If so, why does Nimukei Yosef explain that Shmuel's novelty concerning a Sefer Torah and tefillin is that even though it is a mitzvah to guard them, it is forbidden? One may answer that under the law of the Gemara, when lack of concentration required repetition, there were three categories: for these items one must repeat the prayer; other objects are forbidden initially; and a lulav is permitted. According to our present law, however, one certainly does not repeat the prayer, and the passage is resolved. Rambam, chapter 5, explains that eight matters do not invalidate the prayer; see there. This applies even under the law of the Gemara, which raises a difficulty as to why these particular items were listed. The expression "and pray" appears to me to apply likewise to Krias Shema, and nowadays even to Pesukei DeZimrah. See siman 89, seif 3.
