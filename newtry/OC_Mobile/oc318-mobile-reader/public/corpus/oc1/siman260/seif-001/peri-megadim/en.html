It is a mitzvah. See the Taz, whose words are also cited by the Bach. What he writes that one does not wear it on erev Shabbos means that if one launders a garment on erev Shabbos, he should not wear it that same day, because it is difficult to wear immediately after laundering. The closer an act is to Shabbos, the better. Thursday already relates to the coming Shabbos, and if erev Shabbos is impossible, the closer the better. See Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 8, who cites Nazir 5a: Avshalom would cut his hair from erev Shabbos to erev Shabbos in the afternoon, while his brothers would do so in the morning. This implies that doing so in the morning is the mitzvah. See siman 251, Magen Avraham, se'if katan 5. According to the view in Nazir 43 that Avshalom cut his hair in the afternoon to make his nazirite status recognizable, and according to the view in Moed Katan, one may say that after midday is a greater mitzvah, while his brothers' practice was merely their custom. Regarding the Taz's statement that if the nails are cut on Thursday, their growth will occur on Shabbos, see Eliyah Rabbah, se'if katan 5, who writes that since they grow on Shabbos, it is not a preparation for the honor of Shabbos, because they begin emerging again. All the more so would this apply if cut on Wednesday. See there. See also Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3, in the name of Knesset HaGedolah, that hair begins growing immediately after it is cut.
