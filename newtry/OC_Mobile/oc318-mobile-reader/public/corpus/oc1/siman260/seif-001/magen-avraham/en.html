The Bach wrote in the name of Maharshal that one should take a haircut on erev Shabbos and not on Thursday, because it is not evident that he is doing so in honor of Shabbos. Regarding cutting nails, there is a sound reason not to cut them on Thursday. This requires study, for an explicit Mishnah in the second and fourth chapters of Taanis states that on Thursday it is permitted because of the honor of Shabbos. Rashi explains that most people take haircuts on Thursday and not on erev Shabbos because of the burden of preparing for Shabbos. It is possible that nevertheless the optimal fulfillment is on erev Shabbos, but the men of the ma'amad were not free then. In some places they do not take haircuts on Rosh Chodesh even when it falls on Friday, because R' Yehuda HaChasid instructed this. Maggid Meisharim on Mishlei states that one should not cut the fingernails and toenails on the same day, and should cut them only on erev Shabbos or erev Yom Tov. The proper practice is to cut the toenails on Thursday and the fingernails on Friday, and not to cut them at all on other days. Mateh Moshe writes in the name of Sefer HaGan that they should not be cut on Thursday because they grow on Shabbos. One who burns his nails is pious, one who buries them is righteous, and one who throws them away is wicked, Niddah 17. He should not cut them in their regular order. The Arizal mocked this practice and was not concerned for it, as stated in the Kavanot. The Tashbetz, siman 154, likewise writes this in the name of Maharam. Nevertheless, one should be careful l'chatchila.
