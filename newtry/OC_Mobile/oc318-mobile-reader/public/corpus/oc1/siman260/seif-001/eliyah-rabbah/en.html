[1] His entire body, etc. This implies that washing the entire body is a greater mitzvah, and the Tur likewise implies this. In the Remezim he wrote: 'It is a mitzvah to wash his face, hands, and feet, and all the more so his entire body.' This requires study, for Shabbos 25b states: Rav Nachman said in the name of Rav that washing the hands and feet with hot water in the evening is optional, while I say it is a mitzvah. What mitzvah? The practice of R' Yehuda bar Ilai, for on erev Shabbos they would bring him a basin filled with hot water and he would wash his face, hands, and feet. How, then, did R' Yehuda fail to fulfill the mitzvah of washing his entire body? I later found that the Ra'avan, siman 343, writes that the incident concerns a case in which R' Yehuda bar Ilai had no bathhouse available, but this is forced. Furthermore, according to Rav even washing the hands and feet is optional. One cannot say that Rav agrees that washing the entire body is a mitzvah, for if so, why did he contrast lighting the lamp with washing? He should have distinguished within washing itself. Even apart from this, such an explanation is forced. Moreover, the incidents involving Beis Hillel washing the head, Avishai ben Tzeruyah in Perek Chelek, and Rava in the chapter Ein Tzadin all imply that they were not in a bathhouse to wash their entire bodies. Can one say that washing the entire body was impossible in every case? The Mordechai and Agudah in the chapter Bameh Madlikin also write that the mitzvah applies only to the face, hands, and feet. The word mitzvah there means the mitzvah to heed the words of the Sages, as the Ra'avan writes.
