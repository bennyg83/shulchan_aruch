The nails. R' Yehuda HaChasid wrote in his ethical will that one should not cut his nails or take a haircut on Rosh Chodesh. Knesset HaGedolah wrote that even when Rosh Chodesh falls on erev Shabbos, one should not take a haircut because of danger, but should do so on the preceding Thursday. See siman 493, se'if 3. Maggid Meisharim on Mishlei states that one should not cut the fingernails and toenails on the same day, and should cut them only on erev Shabbos or erev Yom Tov. The proper practice is to cut the toenails on Thursday and the fingernails on Friday, and not to cut them at all on other days. Mateh Moshe wrote in the name of Sefer HaGan that they should not be cut on Thursday because they begin growing on Shabbos. The Taz wrote that according to this, one should likewise not cut hair on Thursday; see there. This is not conclusive, for the men of the watch would take haircuts on Thursday. Furthermore, why does a king take a haircut every day, since he would then have no hair? See Yad Aharon and Knesset HaGedolah. One who burns his nails is pious, one who buries them is righteous, and one who throws them away is wicked, Niddah 17. The reason is that before the sin of Adam HaRishon, his garment was like a nail. When he sinned, it was stripped from him and only the nails remained. Since the woman caused that garment to be stripped away, there is concern that a woman may be punished through what remains of it. Tola'as Yaakov.
