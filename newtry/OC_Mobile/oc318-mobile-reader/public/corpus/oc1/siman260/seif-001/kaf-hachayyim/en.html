1) [Seif 1] It is a mitzvah to wash. This was the practice of Rabbi Yehudah bar Ilai on Friday: they would bring him a basin filled with hot water, and he would wash his face, hands, and feet, wrap himself, and sit in fringed linen garments, resembling an angel of Hashem, Master of Legions. Shabbos 25b. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 62d, likewise writes that this was the practice of the Arizal. After cutting and trimming the fingernails and toenails in honor of Shabbos, they would immediately bring him a basin of hot water. He would first wash his face, then his hands, dry them with a cloth, and afterward wash his feet in that hot water and dry them.
1) Shaar HaKavanos writes there that the mystical meaning of cutting the nails on Friday and then washing the feet with hot water alludes to that flame of fire, represented by the hot water, which drives and repels the husk and impurity from the supernal feet and casts them into the abyss. One must intend that all this occurs in the World of Action, because that is where the husks attach and take hold. One must therefore wash the face and hands as well, because all these places are points at which the husks take hold of the holiness of the World of Action. First one must wash the supernal face, causing them to be repelled from there. The hands are then washed, likewise repelling them from there. Finally, the feet, which are below, are washed, completing their separation and total removal from there. It has thus been explained that the hot water embodies the mystical meaning of the flame of fire mentioned in the Zohar, Vayakhel 203b, which drives away the husks. Therefore, while washing with hot water, one must have in mind the mystical intention of 'the flame of Kah,' meaning that this flame issues from the Name Kah to drive away the husks and cast them below. These are his words. The Arizal's words thus make clear that according to the mystical meaning, the washing must specifically use hot water, even during summer, and must specifically follow this order: first the face, then the hands, and then the feet. One must also wash the right hand before the left, and similarly the right foot before the left, as is known. He should not wash one foot over the other, because this is among the acts that cause one to forget his Torah learning, as stated in Horayos 13b. Sefer Kisei Melech writes that he should not wash his feet with the water used to wash his face and hands. Rather, he should pour it away and bring fresh water. Orach Chaim, parashas Lech Lecha, paragraph 15, cites this.
2) There: It is a mitzvah to wash, etc. This applies equally to men and women. Ateres Zekeinim.
