It is a mitzvah, etc. The Gemara implies that even one whose Torah study is his occupation must interrupt it to fulfill the mitzvah of washing with hot water, for the Gemara cites R' Yehuda bar Ilai. In his case, however, only his face, hands, and feet are mentioned.
His entire body. See Eliyah Rabbah, who questions the Tur's ruling. It appears to me that if only a small amount of hot water was available at the bathhouse, it is preferable to use it to wash his face, hands, and feet, for this is stated explicitly in the Gemara.
With hot water. Lukewarm water requires study, since the Gemara specifies hot water, and lukewarm water is not included in that term. This is similar to what Shaarei Teshuvah cites below in siman 326, regarding the prohibition against bathing in hot water, in the name of Noda B'Yehuda. However, that ruling of Noda B'Yehuda itself requires considerable study, as I wrote there. With cold water, he certainly does not fulfill the mitzvah. Yet if, after rinsing himself with cold water, he warms himself thoroughly through the steam and perspiration he produces, it would seemingly be included in washing with hot water. A proof may be brought from siman 326, se'if 4, which states that one may not rinse himself with cold water and then warm himself opposite a fire, etc., for he is thereby considered as though he washed his entire body with hot water. It is possible, however, that this comparison applies there only as a stringency and not as a basis for leniency.
To wash the head. The Bach copied the text as saying 'with hot water,' but hot water is not actually mentioned by the poskim. Anything effective for washing the head is sufficient. In the time of the Gemara, they commonly used natron or something similar, as stated in Shabbos 50b in Rashi, s.v. 'chofef'; see there.
