It is a mitzvah, etc. The Gemara implies that even one whose Torah study is his occupation must interrupt it in order to fulfill the washing with hot water, for the Gemara there cites the conduct of Rabbi Yehudah bar Ilai. Regarding him, however, it mentions only his face, hands, and feet.
His entire body. See Eliyah Rabbah, who questions the ruling of the Tur. It appears to me that if only a small amount of hot water was available at the bathhouse, it is preferable that he wash his face, hands, and feet with it, for this is stated explicitly in the Gemara.
With hot water. The law regarding lukewarm water requires consideration, since the Gemara mentions hot water, and lukewarm water is not included in that term. This is similar to what Shaarei Teshuvah cites below in siman 326 regarding the prohibition against washing with hot water, in the name of Noda BiYehudah. That ruling of Noda BiYehudah itself, however, requires considerable examination, as I wrote there. With cold water, one certainly does not fulfill this mitzvah. Yet if, after rinsing with cold water, he warmed himself thoroughly through the steam and perspiration that he induced, it would seemingly be included in washing with hot water. There is support for this from the ruling below in siman 326, seif 4, that a person may not rinse himself with cold water and then warm himself opposite a fire, because he is thereby considered as one who washed his entire body with hot water. It is possible, however, that the ruling there treats this stringently but cannot serve as grounds for leniency here.
To wash the head. The Bach copied the words ‘with hot water.’ In truth, hot water is not mentioned by the poskim in this regard; anything effective for washing the head is sufficient. In the time of the Gemara, they would use natron or a similar substance, as stated in Shabbos 50b, in Rashi, s.v. chofef; see there.
