(9) Close to nightfall. Meaning, before bein hashmashos, while it is certainly still daytime, with enough time remaining to add from the weekday onto the sacred. He delays asking until then so that they should not be negligent and say that there is still time.
(10) In a gentle manner. So that they will accept his words. A person should never impose excessive fear within his household, for because of fear they may desecrate Shabbos, feed him forbidden food, and come to commit many transgressions.
(11) Have you tithed, etc. Since none of these matters can be done afterward on Shabbos, he should urge them concerning them on erev Shabbos.
(12) Have you prepared the eruv? In places where the practice is to prepare an eruv chatzeros only once each year, from erev Pesach to erev Pesach, and where an eruv techumin is not ordinarily prepared, this question need not be asked.
(13) Have you separated challah? Today people are not accustomed to ask this question. See Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 103, which gives a reason. On erev Pesach, however, it certainly must be asked. If erev Pesach falls on Shabbos, it must be asked on erev Shabbos; see there.
(14) Light the lamps, etc. The hei is vocalized with a patach, as a command, since a question is not applicable because he can see that the lamps have not been lit, as Rashi explains. The same applies that he should warn them to stop performing melacha. If he is then in the study hall or elsewhere, he must send someone to warn them.
