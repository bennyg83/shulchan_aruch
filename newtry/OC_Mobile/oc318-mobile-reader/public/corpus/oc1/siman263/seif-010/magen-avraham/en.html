And the custom, etc. If a man kindles, there is no such custom, and he is permitted to perform melacha. Nevertheless, it is proper for him to stipulate (Bach).
She accepts Shabbos. She should first pray Minchah (Mishnah Berurah).
That she stipulated beforehand. Nevertheless, it appears to me that one should stipulate only when necessary, since some authorities disagree. Otherwise, she should recite the blessing first and make the stipulation; see se’if 5.
But not over the other candles. See what I wrote in se’if 6 in the name of the Maharil, that one nevertheless recites a blessing over them, even when kindling in rooms not used for eating, for this is no worse than Yom Kippur; and see what I wrote in se’if 9. This applies specifically when he has a room designated for him and did not recite a blessing over another candle, as written in se’if 6. Those who kindle in the entrance hall do not recite a blessing, even if they did not bless over another candle, because this mitzvah is not incumbent upon them and the place is not used for eating. In a place used for eating, however, even another person may recite the blessing, as written in se’if 9. It is proper l’chatchila to kindle in the place where one eats, although it appears to me that it is sufficient for one candle to be standing there.
And one must place, etc. See the end of se’if 14. One can say that here the case concerns one who made a stipulation.
And not to kindle, etc. Meaning, because it is not apparent that he is kindling in honor of Shabbos (Levush). Therefore, it appears to me that if the candle remained briefly in its original place, he may afterward place it elsewhere, since it is permitted to use its light. The same applies if he takes it from its place and puts it in a high suspended lamp, because it is evident that he is doing so in honor of Shabbos. The Mordechai explains that the kindling must be performed in its proper place, and this too is considered its place. It is forbidden only when one kindles in a place where the light is not used and afterward places it where it will be used, because we rule that the kindling constitutes the mitzvah, as the Tur wrote. Similarly, regarding the Chanukah light, the Gemara states: if one kindled it indoors and then brought it outside, etc. Moving it outdoors from one place to another, however, is permitted. This is implied by the Yam Shel Shlomo at the end of chapter 6 of Bava Kamma, which states that if one kindled it above twenty cubits and then placed it below twenty cubits, people may say that he took it for his own use. This implies that below twenty cubits this concern does not apply. Mahariv, in the laws of Pesach, wrote that the primary kindling is that of the candles on the table. Therefore, one should not kindle them elsewhere and then place them on the table, because an observer may say that he took them for his own use. End quote. According to us, who follow the Maharil that the entire house is considered their place, this is permitted. Nevertheless, l’chatchila one should be careful to recite the blessing when the candles are on the table. The Agudah wrote: Therefore, one should not kindle the Chanukah, Shabbos, or Yom Tov lights and place them in their proper location after kindling. Rather, one should first place them in their location and then kindle them.
