(42) And the custom, etc. — and if the man kindles — even when he blesses on his kindling, as above in seif 6 — there is no custom and melacha is permitted; nevertheless it is good to stipulate.

(43) She accepts Shabbat — and she should pray Minchah first, for since she already accepted Shabbat she can no longer pray a weekday prayer; and when there is no time for this it is better that she pray Maariv twice, as below in seif 15 — lest one enter, Heaven forbid, into a doubt of chillul Shabbat; and see above siman 262 in s.k. 11.

(44) That she stipulate beforehand — and she may stipulate only for need, since there are those who disagree and hold that a stipulation is not effective, as above.

(45) And the essence of kindling, etc. — meaning one blesses on them and not on the rest of the candles, for l'chatchila the essence of the mitzvah is the candles by which one eats, and the blessing should be on them — and even one candle suffices [Magen Avraham]. However if there are many women and not each has a table to herself — she may bless on the candles standing in the house in other places, such as on the candles burning on the lamp hanging in the middle of the house and the like; for we state a general rule that in every place where one kindles in order to make some use there — there is [reason of] shalom bayit, and one should bless.

(46) The candles in the house — meaning the householder who can kindle on the table and bless on it; but a guest — if he has a special room for himself, even if he does not eat there — he kindles a candle there and blesses on it, as above in seif 6.

(47) And one must, etc. — meaning even if he stipulates explicitly that he does not accept Shabbat until the candles are in their place.

(48) Not, etc. — meaning it is forbidden to kindle in a place where one does not use it and to place the candle in a place where one uses it, for we hold kindling performs the mitzvah; and since he kindled in a place where one does not use it, which is not a place of obligation — his kindling accomplishes nothing even if he places it afterward in a place of obligation; and he must extinguish and kindle again. But if he kindled in the house in a place where one uses it — it is permitted to move them afterward and place them elsewhere, for the whole house is their place. Levush is stringent even on this; and where there is need one may be lenient. Chayei Adam wrote that women who kindle in the sukkah on the festival and move the candles into the house — they do not act properly.
