Young men. This refers to men who have wives but go outside their homes; and even though his wife kindles in his house he is not exempted by his wife’s kindling, since he has a special room where he is lodging; and all the more so, if he is unmarried he is obligated to kindle.
In their room. Even if the room is designated only for sleeping and they eat in another house.
And to bless on it. Because kindling a light is obligatory for the sake of domestic peace, lest one stumble on wood or stone; and the candles must be long so they burn until they come home at night; otherwise the blessing is in vain [Derech HaChaim]. And regarding many householders lodging together in one room at a fair, and the host is not with them in their room; the obligation of kindling with blessing applies to them; even if their wives kindle in their homes, they must therefore all contribute and one kindles and blesses and intends through his blessing to discharge the obligation of them all; and they too should intend to fulfill their obligation through his blessing. And if the host is also with them in their room, they need not participate with him with a perutah, since their wives kindle in their homes, as below in se’if 7.
He need not, etc., and bless on it. But to kindle he must, even if he does not eat there, lest he stumble on wood or stone.
