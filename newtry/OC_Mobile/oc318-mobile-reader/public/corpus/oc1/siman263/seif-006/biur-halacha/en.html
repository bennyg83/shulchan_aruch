Unmarried men etc. — see in Mishna Berurah; so too Olat Shabbat and Tosafos Shabbos and Peri Megadim and Chayei Adam and other Acharonim; and in Derech HaChaim it is implied that he explains unmarried men literally, but not one who has a wife; and the truth is with all the above poskim as one — for thus the wording of Shulchan Aruch is parsed for one who examines it: specifically if he is with his wife he is exempt through her blessing; and furthermore because the source of this law is from Mordechai in the name of Maharam, and this law was brought in Tashbetz Katan [which Rabbenu Shimshon bar Tzadok, disciple of Maharam, compiled] also in the name of Maharam, and there it is explained explicitly like all the above poskim. And now we will explain the reason of this seif and the adjacent seif [and it will be well understood in our words the reason of the words of Magen Avraham in s.k. 14] — namely, when a man has several rooms he must kindle in all the rooms on account of shalom bayit; therefore if he is in his house with his wife and she blesses in one place, it is as if he were the one blessing, and automatically all the rooms that he kindles are exempt through the blessing — like regarding bedikat chametz, where one blesses in one place and on the basis of this he checks all the rooms; not so if he is not in his house and has a special room for himself — how is he exempt through the blessing that his wife blessed elsewhere? And even though he does not eat there — nevertheless on account of shalom bayit he is obligated to kindle and bless; not so in seif 7 where he has no special room — if so the reasoning of shalom bayit does not apply there, for in any event there is light there, and nothing remains upon him except what there is a mitzvah upon every Jewish man — kindling a candle on Shabbat — and this is fulfilled through what his wife kindles there in his house; therefore he need not participate then in the particulars:
