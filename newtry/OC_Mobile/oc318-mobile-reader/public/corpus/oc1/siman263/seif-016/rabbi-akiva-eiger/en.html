Seif 11. With doing labor, and even if he says. In the Tur it is written: Tosafot wrote that it does not depend on lighting the lamp, but on the evening prayer, for one who prays Maariv has Shabbos take effect upon him. The Beit Yosef wrote that one must explain this as referring to the beginning of Maariv, namely Barchu. Afterward the Beit Yosef wrote: What Rabbeinu wrote further, that one who prays Maariv has Shabbos take effect upon him, appears to me to teach that even if the congregation has not prayed Maariv, and an individual preceded them and prayed the Shabbos prayer, Shabbos takes effect upon him. However, if he does not want to accept Shabbos, etc. It is more apparent that since he mentioned the sanctity of the day in the prayer, it is impossible to make it weekday according to all opinions. End quote. It appears that this applies specifically to the actual Maariv prayer, where he mentions the sanctity of the day, but Barchu depends on the opinions of the poskim as to whether a condition helps with lighting. However, the wording of the Shulchan Aruch is difficult. For when it first wrote that if an individual preceded and prayed, acceptance of Shabbos takes effect upon him, that speaks only of Barchu, and regarding that it wrote, even if he said that he does not want to accept Shabbos. But this applies only to actual prayer, not to Barchu. This requires study.
Seif 16. He begins to pray Mincha. He should not wait to pray Mincha until after Barchu in order to answer Barchu, as one must do on other days, since if he answers Barchu he cannot pray Mincha. End quote from Maharai. Perhaps this applies specifically to beginning beforehand, for there is not yet an obligation to answer Barchu. So it also clearly implies in Terumas HaDeshen there: this is where he did not accept Shabbos with them, such as where he was not in the synagogue and did not answer Barchu. This implies that if he is in the synagogue, he is obligated to answer Barchu. But the Agudah wrote in the second chapter of Shabbos that if he came to the synagogue when they are saying Barchu, he should not answer. According to what I wrote above in the gloss to seif 11, that since we rule that with lighting one can make a condition, the same applies to Barchu before prayer. If so, one may say that he must answer Barchu and stipulate that he is not accepting Shabbos, and he may then pray Mincha outside the synagogue.
