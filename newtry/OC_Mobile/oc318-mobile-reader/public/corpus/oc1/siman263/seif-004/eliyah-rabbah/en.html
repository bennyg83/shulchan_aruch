(9) [Levush] All her days, etc.; to add on every mitzvah light more than she was accustomed (Mateh Moshe, Maharil). We learn from this three things: First; that even if she is accustomed to three she lights more; second; even one mitzvah light that is not of Shabbos; third; it implies that only addition is required, such as if she lights a larger light or gives a little more oil, but she need not light another light for the addition; unlike the understanding of Rama and Levush; and it appears to be lenient on this for a poor woman:
(10) [Levush] He must bless, etc. "Ner" or "nerot" of Shabbos (Minhagim); meaning when lighting one light he blesses "ner," and when lighting two he blesses "nerot"; except it implies there that even with two he may bless "ner," and see Yoreh De'ah siman 120 se’if 3. Magen Avraham wrote: if she forgot to bless until it darkened, one may rely on Maharash that she bless again; end quote; and it is not clear that specifically at a permitted time for lighting it is permitted and not at a forbidden time, as Mahari Bruna wrote; and likewise a woman whose time of immersion arrived on Shabbos night should not do so, but as Olat Shabbos wrote; that she light and bless before going, and stipulate in her heart that she does not accept Shabbos until after immersion; however there is room to hesitate, for we say "provided he does not precede"; see there. He further wrote that the husband lights and blesses; and I heard from the house of my ancestors the Gaon Rosh z"l that they go to their house between their hair-washing and immersion, light, and return for immersion; and I further heard that some practice per our elders to light in the immersion house; and this was far in my eyes, for behold specifically "on his table" we require; but I found support in responsa Maharil siman 53 that he permitted to light and bless even in the synagogue adjacent to the mikveh when the lights are not designated for the synagogue; all the more so in the immersion house where there is need for a light near dark; and nevertheless it is correct as I wrote at the beginning:
[Levush] And it is forbidden for her, etc. According to this, on Yom Tov she should recite the blessing and then light, as stated in the introduction of the Prishah to Yoreh De’ah. The Magen Avraham, however, writes that the Sages did not distinguish between cases, as explained above in siman 158.
(12) [Levush] Like Kiddush, etc. And the same law applies to havdalah; and it is simple that if he has one light he should acquire Kiddush or a Chanukah light; and it is possible that the same law applies to other Shabbos needs before a second light. Magen Avraham wrote: a blind woman blesses as he wrote end of siman 69; if she has a seeing husband the husband blesses; however if she eats at a table with others she does not bless, for behold there is no joy with her; end quote; and it requires investigation:
(13) [Levush] Provided, etc. See above end of siman 261; one should not light before Minchah (Magen Avraham in the name of Shiltei HaGibborim); and likewise Raavan ruled; but in Mordechai end of chapter Bameh Madlikin and Shibulei HaLeket that they lightd before Minchah; and one may distinguish that there it speaks of close to dark when one fears desecration of Shabbos:
(14) [Levush] He will extinguish, etc. He wrote in derashot Maharash to warn women in the house not to light Shabbos or Yom Tov while still in her hand, but first place it; like a Chanukah light (Malbushi Yom Tov); and see se’if 10; and this is the language of Agudah: one should not light a Chanukah light or Shabbos and Yom Tov and place them in places after lighting, but first place them in their place and afterward light them; end quote; and see below siman 675 s.k. 4:
