(1) (s.k. 4) What shall he eat, etc.; for certainly his eating precedes, etc.; he wanted to say that one should not interpret "even if he has nothing to eat," etc.; he wanted to say that he has no money for eating needs and not for a light; it is a mistaken view that regarding eating there is no stringency and he need not go door to door for eating needs, for that is included in "make your Shabbos profane" as Magen Avraham wrote s.k. 6 afterwards; and it teaches that Magen Avraham; that nevertheless he must go door to door for a light that precedes bread; it teaches Magen Avraham that the interpretation of Rama's words is not so, for certainly eating precedes the light; rather the interpretation is that he has money enough for eating or to buy a light with the money; and since eating precedes the light and aside from this money he has nothing to eat; perforce he must buy with the money for eating need and no money remains for a light need; nevertheless he must ask at the doors for a light need:
(2) And likewise in Rosh chapter Acharon se’if 17; he asks on Rashbam who holds that they sanctify on bread: we should ask in chapter Bameh Madlikin; his house light and Kiddush of the day, which of them takes precedence; and the conclusion is that his house light takes precedence because of shalom bayis; without this the light takes precedence, for even if he buys a light he will not nullify the mitzvah of Kiddush, for he can sanctify on bread:
And he does not answer, etc.; in a place where he has no bread, etc.; and Teshuvot HaShach challenged: perhaps in any case the light precedes bread. Rather Rosh holds that bread precedes Kiddush; and if so perforce the case deals where he has bread; and if so the challenge is well placed, for behold he can sanctify on bread. And one can answer that perforce this is not so, for it is impossible that Rosh holds bread precedes Kiddush; if we say the light precedes bread, all the more so the light precedes Kiddush; if so there is also difficulty: why does he ask whether his house light or Kiddush of the day takes precedence; it is obvious the light takes precedence; until here Teshuvot HaShach; but its language is garbled. And one can say: how does he say the case deals where he has no bread; behold we hold Kiddush is only in a place of a meal, and if so what will the wine avail him; one can say Magen Avraham follows the Geonim brought siman 473 se’if 5 that a cup of wine on which he is obligated in the after-blessing suffices (see there Taz and Magen Avraham), and this too is called in a place of a meal:
And likewise in the Gemara there folio 101, etc.; Abaye said: when we were with Rabbah, etc.; he said: when they heard from him Kiddush of the night in the house of study they tasted something immediately, lest before he reached the inn the light be extinguished and they not eat and not fulfill Kiddush; for here Kiddush is only in a place of a meal (and Tosafot and Rosh wrote there that Rabbah was not stringent on eating because eating can be completed tomorrow by day, or they could eat elsewhere where there was a light; only in that place there was no wine, and therefore he was not stringent except on Kiddush); until here the Gemara; and on this Magen Avraham wrote that Rabbah was not stringent on the light but on eating; meaning eating in a place of Kiddush; and we challenge Magen Avraham: why was Rabbah stringent on Kiddush and not concerned lest when he arrives at the inn the light already be extinguished; and even if they taste something here and fulfill Kiddush, in any case it is not proper that they sit in darkness at the inn, and it would be better for Rabbah to dismiss them while the light still burns at the inn lest they come there and there be no light; and in all rooms a light is needed even if one does not eat, lest one stumble on a tree or stone as written se’if 6; and one must say he was not concerned except for Kiddush which is more stringent and takes precedence over the light; and even though we say "his house light and Kiddush; his house light takes precedence," that is specifically a light on the table where one eats which is a need of eating; but a light lest one not stumble; Kiddush takes precedence; and it is impossible that the light takes precedence over bread; and if he has money only for one need he buys a light and does not eat; even though then the light avails only lest he not stumble, for he has nothing to eat; nevertheless that light lest he not stumble takes precedence over bread; if so how is it possible to say Kiddush takes precedence over a light lest one not stumble; but a light where one eats with it takes precedence over Kiddush; behold a light lest one not stumble takes precedence over bread, and nevertheless Kiddush takes precedence over that light; and a light where one eats with it, which is only a preparatory for bread, should take precedence over Kiddush; all against Magen Avraham:
