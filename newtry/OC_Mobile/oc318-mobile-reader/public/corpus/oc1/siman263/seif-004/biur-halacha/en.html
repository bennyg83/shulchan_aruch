(א) He may not precede — according to what is explained below in seif 10 in Hagahah that the custom is that a woman when she kindles presumably accepts Shabbat thereby — if so she can properly precede until the time of plag hamincha, unless when she stipulates that she does not accept Shabbat thereby:

(ב) While the day is still great — it is a braita Shabbos 23b that we learned there: provided that he not precede and not delay; and "preceding" is called where one precedes greatly before the time of tosefet, and "delay" is called where one kindles in close proximity to the time of tosefet, lest either way he delay the time; and the time of tosefet depends on great authorities — per Shulchan Aruch above in siman 261 it is a little before the end of sunset, and when one kindles a long time before this it is included in "he may not precede" [and even if it is within the measure of four mil explained above — and even though above in the Mechaber it is explained that one can add until the beginning of four mil, meaning when he accepted Shabbat upon himself from that time and made it tosefet — and this is what the Mechaber wrote there "if he wishes to make all of it tosefet he may" — meaning for all matters of Shabbat and not for kindling alone; and here it does not deal without acceptance]; and per the Geonim that from the beginning of sunset is the time of bein hashemashos — if so the time of tosefet is a short time before this, and the intent of the braita "he may not precede" is a long time before this time; and all this when he did not accept Shabbat upon himself through kindling — meaning he did not cease himself from melacha after kindling; but if he accepted Shabbat upon himself he may precede until plag hamincha:

(ג) And also he may not delay — see in Magen Avraham s.k. 10 in what he wrote from the custom of the ancients, and I did not copy it because today this is not applicable — for they were careful to say Barchu long before bein hashemashos, through which is acceptance of Shabbat, as in our time we are accustomed to say "Lechu neranena" and "Lecha dodi," for even on a weekday their custom was to pray while it was still day, as above siman 233; and the people were careful to kindle before this, and Mincha they preceded before the time of kindling — not so today, where the way of men is to pray Mincha in the synagogue with sunset [see Berachos 29b and above siman 233 in Biur Halacha] — certainly the woman must kindle beforehand and not wait until the people pray Mincha; and it is astonishing on Ba'er Heitev who copied this matter, unless we say that also in his time they were accustomed to pray Barchu while it was still greatly day; and according to what he wrote above siman 261 s.k. 6 — also in this manner his words cannot be conceived:

(ד) Before night — see in Mishna Berurah, and see in siman 261 in Magen Avraham who brought in the name of Bach that one should be concerned for the opinion of R' Eliezer of Metz and separate himself from melacha two hours before night; and it is implied from this that he was not concerned for the opinion of Shulchan Aruch who ruled that one may not precede more than an hour and a quarter; and in practice certainly one should rely on Bach and Magen Avraham that one can accept Shabbat upon himself two hours before night — for even though in this siman Rama was silent and did not disagree with the Mechaber in siman 261 seif 1 in Hagahah he cited Mahari'v's view there — see there — it is implied that he holds that upon two hours before night acceptance falls; and in particular since the opinion of Levush and the Gra above [and other poskim who exist in their method, as written above siman 233 in Biur Halacha] that plag hamincha is an hour and a quarter before sunset. Afterward I found in Peri Megadim in our matter that he also copied the words of Magen Avraham above for practical law:

(ה) While it is still etc. — see in Mishna Berurah, and it is from the chiddushim of R' Eliezer of Metz; and even though the author of Derech HaChaim did not write so, nevertheless the reason is reasonable like the Gaon R' Eliezer of Metz — that "he may not precede" means only l'chatchila, similar to "he may not delay" which is only l'chatchila; and if we wish to be stringent to extinguish and kindle again so that the kindling be at its time and he not transgress "he may not precede" — he loses his reward through his loss, for his first blessing will be void. And all this when the kindling was after plag hamincha; but if he kindled before plag hamincha for the sake of Shabbat, even if he accepted Shabbat upon himself from that time, his kindling accomplishes nothing even b'dieved, and he must extinguish and kindle again and bless — so too in Sefer Olat Shabbat.
