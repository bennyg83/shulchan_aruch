One should not kindle too early. According to what is explained below in se’if 10 in the Rama’s gloss, that the prevailing custom is that a woman ordinarily accepts Shabbos through kindling, she may properly kindle as early as plag haminchah, unless she stipulates that she is not accepting Shabbos through it.
While it is still broad daylight. This is based on the Baraisa in Shabbos 23b: ‘provided that one not kindle too early and not kindle too late.’ Kindling ‘too early’ means kindling a considerable time before the period of tosefes Shabbos, while kindling ‘too late’ means kindling right at the limit of that period, lest one delay beyond the proper time. The time of tosefes Shabbos depends upon a major dispute. According to the Shulchan Aruch above in siman 261, it begins shortly before the end of sunset; therefore, kindling a considerable time before then is included in the prohibition against kindling too early. This is so even if one kindles within the four-mil period discussed there. Although the Mechaber states there that one may add to Shabbos from the beginning of those four mil, that applies when he accepts Shabbos from that time and treats the entire period as tosefes Shabbos for all matters, not merely for candle lighting. Here, however, the discussion concerns one who has not accepted Shabbos. According to the Geonim, bein hashmashos begins at the start of sunset, so tosefes Shabbos is only a short time before that; accordingly, ‘one should not kindle too early’ means not to kindle a considerable time before then. All this applies when one does not accept Shabbos through kindling, meaning that one does not refrain from melacha afterward. If one accepts Shabbos, however, one may kindle as early as plag haminchah.
Nor should one kindle too late. See Magen Avraham, se’if katan 10, regarding the custom of earlier generations. I did not reproduce it because it no longer applies today. They were careful to recite Barechu long before bein hashmashos, thereby accepting Shabbos, just as in our time we recite Lechu Neranenah and Lecha Dodi. Even on weekdays they customarily prayed while it was still daytime, as stated above in siman 233. The people were therefore careful to kindle before Barechu, and they prayed Minchah before the candle-lighting time. Today, by contrast, men commonly pray Minchah in the synagogue close to sunset, as discussed in Berachos 29b and above in the Biur Halacha to siman 233. A woman must therefore certainly kindle beforehand and not wait until the congregation prays Minchah. It is surprising that the Baer Heitev copied this earlier custom, unless in his time they likewise recited Barechu while it was still broad daylight. Yet according to what he writes above in siman 261, se’if katan 6, even in that case his words are difficult to apply.
Before nightfall. See the Mishnah Berurah. In siman 261, the Magen Avraham cites the Bach, who writes that one should be concerned for the opinion of R’ Eliezer of Metz and cease from melacha two hours before nightfall. This implies that he was not concerned for the Shulchan Aruch’s ruling that one should not kindle more than one and one-quarter hours early. In practice, one may certainly rely upon the Bach and Magen Avraham and accept Shabbos two hours before nightfall. Although the Rama does not disagree here with the Mechaber, in siman 261, se’if 1, in his gloss, he cites Mahariv, which implies that acceptance of Shabbos takes effect two hours before nightfall. This is especially so according to the Levush and Gra, and the other poskim who follow their approach, as discussed above in the Biur Halacha to siman 233, who hold that plag haminchah is one and one-quarter hours before sunset. I later found that the Peri Megadim here likewise cites the Magen Avraham’s ruling as practical halacha.
While it is still daytime, etc. See the Mishnah Berurah. This ruling is from the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger. Although the author of Derech HaChaim did not write this way, Rabbi Akiva Eiger’s reasoning is persuasive: ‘one should not kindle too early’ is only a l’chatchilah restriction, just like ‘one should not kindle too late.’ If one were stringent and extinguished the candles in order to kindle them again at the proper time, so as not to violate the instruction not to kindle too early, the loss would outweigh the gain because the first blessing would have been in vain. This applies when the candles were kindled after plag haminchah. If, however, one kindled before plag haminchah for the sake of Shabbos, even if one accepted Shabbos from that time, the kindling is ineffective even b’dieved, and one must extinguish the candles, kindle them again, and recite the blessing. So writes Olas Shabbos. See what we wrote above regarding the time of plag haminchah.
