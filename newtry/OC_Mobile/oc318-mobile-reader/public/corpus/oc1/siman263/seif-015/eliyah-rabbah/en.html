In the rooms, etc. Two wicks are required, according to the laws of Mahariv, because of shalom bayis, so that one not stumble over wood, stone, or anything else, as stated by the Mordechai and Kol Bo. This implies that they do not eat there, and the same is implied by Responsa Maharil, siman 155. From this, Mateh Moshe, citing the Maharshal, learned that one who has several rooms and walks from the house into another room must have one light in every place where there is concern that he might stumble; see there. The same is implied by the Levush and Shulchan Aruch at the end of the se’if: “He need not light in the rooms and recite a blessing,” which follows the language of the Mordechai. This implies that he need not recite a blessing specifically because he is with his wife, but he must nevertheless light. However, I saw that the Kol Bo and Tashbetz write only that he need not light, implying that even lighting is unnecessary because he is with his wife and she lights in the house where they eat. When the Mordechai writes “and recite a blessing,” he means that even if the man wishes to be stringent and light, he should not recite a blessing.
