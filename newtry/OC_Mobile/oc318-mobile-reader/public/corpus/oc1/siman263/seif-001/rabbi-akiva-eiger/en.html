Magen Avraham, seif katan 4. For he teaches that in a case where he does not have enough, in my humble opinion one may say as follows. The Ran in Shabbos asked how we set aside kiddush, which is D'Oraisa, because of the lamp of his home and the Chanukah lamp, and he answered that one recites kiddush over bread, except that the preferred mitzvah is with wine; see there. The Rosh holds that in this there is no novelty, since he can fulfill kiddush over bread and also have the lamp of his home. Yet in this matter the Rosh also holds like the Ran, that we do not set aside a D'Oraisa obligation because of the lamp of his home. Therefore the Rosh holds that if he has no bread, it is certainly preferable to buy bread, for without bread he does not fulfill kiddush, since the Rosh follows his own view that kiddush is only in the place of a meal according to the Torah. Rather, it must be that he has bread and recites kiddush without wine, so that he fulfills the D'Oraisa obligation, though D'Rabbanan he has not fulfilled it. In that case, the lamp of his home takes precedence. For this reason the Rosh asked that if it were so that one recites kiddush over bread, he should also fulfill the D'Rabbanan obligation; rather, perforce, he fulfills the D'Rabbanan obligation only with wine. He therefore teaches that the lamp of his home takes precedence over buying wine for kiddush. Examine this carefully. However, according to what the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 273, seif 11, that types of accompanying foods are called the place of a meal, the question still remains: one could say that the case is where he has types of accompanying foods, so that he fulfills the D'Oraisa obligation, but not the D'Rabbanan kiddush, which requires bread or wine, and the case is where he has neither bread nor wine. The Magen Avraham therefore proved that the Rosh holds that the Shabbos meal, meaning bread, takes precedence over the Shabbos lamp. Examine this carefully.
