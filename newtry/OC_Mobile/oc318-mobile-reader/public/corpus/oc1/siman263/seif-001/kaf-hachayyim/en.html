1) [Seif 1] One should take care to prepare a beautiful lamp. Rav Huna said that one who is accustomed to taking care that his Shabbos lamp is beautiful will have sons who are Torah scholars. Shabbos 23b; Tur. It appears to me that the reason is that Shabbos corresponds to Chochmah. Furthermore, this follows the mystical meaning of the verse, 'For a mitzvah is a lamp and Torah is light.' Therefore, one who is careful with the lamp of the mitzvah will merit the light of Torah. Rashi also explains this way. A woman should therefore pray after kindling that Hashem grant her sons who shine in Torah. See below, paragraph 34.
2) There: One should take care, etc. He should kindle lights in every room through which he walks so that he will not stumble over wood or stone. Mateh Moshe and Maharshal; Magen Avraham, paragraph 1; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 1. The principal blessing, however, is recited over the lamps kindled in the place where one eats.
3) There: Some are particular to prepare two wicks, etc. This means that in the lamp called a kruisel, one kindles two wicks and recites over both of them the blessing, 'to kindle the Shabbos lamp.' Similarly, for wax or tallow lamps, one takes two candles. Bach.
4) There: One corresponding to zachor and one corresponding to shamor. Shaar HaKavanos, folio 67a at the end, writes the same. Some say they correspond to a man and a woman, because the numerical value of two instances of the word ner corresponds to the combined number of limbs in a man and a woman. A woman has four more limbs than the 248 of a man: two hinges and two doors. Another reason is that they allude to the two souls bound together in a person's body. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 2. It further appears to me that they correspond to the two additions to the levels of nefesh, ruach, and neshamah that a person receives on Shabbos, one from the aspect of the night and one from the aspect of the day, as stated above at the end of siman 261; see there. See above, siman 262, paragraph 7.
5) There: One corresponding to zachor, etc. One should not attach two candles to one another, because they then constitute a torch. Rema writes this in siman 671, seif 4 in the gloss. Bach writes that the custom is to bring to the synagogue two wax candles twisted together as a remembrance that zachor and shamor were spoken in one utterance, and that this is a proper custom originating with the great early authorities; see there. It appears that this does not conflict with the gloss. There it discusses lamps in one's home, where twisting them together constitutes a torch. Here it discusses bringing them to the synagogue as a remembrance of shamor and zachor. We are not concerned about a torch, since he already fulfills his obligation with the lamp in his home. Alternatively, here he brings them twisted together but separates them when kindling. This explanation is not compelling. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 3. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, similarly writes that one must kindle two wicks in two separate lamps, one corresponding to zachor and one to shamor, so the distinction is recognizable. This is contrary to the opinion that one should kindle two wicks in a single lamp. What Magen Avraham writes in paragraph 1, with due respect, is incorrect; see there.
6) If one fills a bowl with oil, surrounds it with wicks, and spaces them approximately one fingerbreadth apart, they do not constitute a torch. Tur writes this in siman 671 in the name of Rosh regarding Chanukah lamps. Beis Yosef writes there that the measure of one fingerbreadth applies to medium-sized wicks. The space must be increased proportionally for thick wicks and decreased for thin ones; see there. The same therefore applies to Shabbos. Those who kindle Shabbos lights in a glass vessel containing water, oil, and wicks must space medium-sized wicks approximately one fingerbreadth apart, as stated above. Wherever the width of a finger is mentioned, it means the width of the thumb of an average person, as is known.
7) Wax candles that belonged to idolatry may not be used as Shabbos lamps, even where an ordinary person is permitted to benefit from them. The same applies to every mitzvah article, because they are repulsive, as stated above in siman 154, seif 11, and Yoreh De'ah 139, seif 13; see there. Similarly, one may not make Shabbos candles from wax that dripped from candles in a non-Jewish place of worship, contrary to the permissive opinion, as stated in our words above in siman 154, paragraph 79; see there. Similarly, if a mouse is found in oil, one may not use the oil for Shabbos lamps, even when there is sixty times the mouse's volume, as stated above in siman 154, seif 12, and our words there in paragraphs 89 and 90; see there. Tosefes Shabbos writes the same at the end of paragraph 1 in this siman, as does Zevach Tzedek on Yoreh De'ah, siman 104, paragraphs 6 and 7. Similarly, oil placed beneath a bed, which may not be eaten because of the harmful spirit that rests upon it, as stated in Yoreh De'ah, end of siman 116, seif 5, and Birkei Yosef there, paragraph 10, may not be used for a Shabbos lamp or any mitzvah lamp, because of the principle, 'Present it, if you please, to your governor.' Ben Ish Chai, parashas Noach, paragraph 17, writes the same. Bitter oil unfit for eating, however, does not become subject to a harmful spirit and may be used for Shabbos and Chanukah lamps. Ben Ish Chai there. It appears that all this applies when another oil is available. If no other oil is available, he should kindle with these oils but not recite a blessing.
8) One should not kindle an old earthenware lamp upon the table. Responsa Maharshal, siman 86; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1. A poor person who has no other lamp may certainly use it. Mishbetzos Zahav at the end of the siman. If it was fired in a kiln, or if it is made of glass or glazed earthenware, it is permitted, as stated below in siman 673, seif 3. It is proper for a woman who fears Hashem not to kindle with a wood splinter, but to have a designated wax candle to kindle every Shabbos. Paaneach Raza; Ateres Zekeinim; Eliyah Rabbah there. See below, paragraph 14.
9) There, gloss: They may add, etc. Some have the custom to kindle seven lamps, and some say ten. Shelah; Magen Avraham, paragraph 2. Pious and scrupulous people customarily kindle specifically seven, corresponding to the seven people called to the Torah. They also correspond to the seven days of the week. Furthermore, Shabbos corresponds to Malchus, the seventh of the constructive sefiros, as is known. They therefore kindle seven to allude to this aspect. On Yom Kippur, the custom is to kindle six; on Yom Tov, five; and on Rosh Chodesh, four, corresponding to the number called to the Torah on each of these days. These numbers have an esoteric reason, as stated above in siman 135, paragraph 19; see there. Petach HaDevir at the end of paragraph 1 and Chesed LaAlafim, paragraph 1, write the same.
10) There, gloss: A woman who forgot on one occasion, etc. If she forgot several times, she must add a lamp for each occasion, because the purpose is to provide a reminder that she be careful with the honor of Shabbos in the future. Bach. For this reason, it appears to me that if circumstances beyond her control prevented her from kindling, such as imprisonment or the like, she need not add a lamp. Magen Avraham, paragraph 3; Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 8; Tosefes Shabbos, paragraph 3. It appears that the same applies if she did not kindle because of poverty and lacked money to purchase a lamp; she need not add one.
11) The same applies if she was originally accustomed to kindle three lamps each time. If she forgot, she must add one lamp and kindle four each time thereafter, and so on indefinitely. Machatzis HaShekel. Similarly, if she ordinarily kindles two lamps and once kindled only one, she must add a lamp. If she kindles many lamps and on one occasion forgot and kindled one fewer, she must kindle an additional lamp so that she will be careful. Eshel Avraham, paragraph 3. It is obvious that she must add oil and kindle the additional lamp. She should not take the quantity of oil with which she previously kindled two lamps and distribute it among three, because this does the opposite: the lamps will be extinguished sooner, further diminishing the honor of Shabbos.
12) There, gloss: She kindles throughout her life, etc. She should add to each mitzvah lamp more than she was previously accustomed to use. Mateh Moshe and Maharil. We learn three points from this. First, even if she was accustomed to three lamps, she must add. Second, this applies even to another mitzvah lamp that is not for Shabbos. Third, the wording implies that only an increase is required, such as kindling a larger lamp or adding a little oil, but she need not kindle an additional lamp, contrary to the understanding of Rema and Levush. It appears that one may be lenient in this regard for a poor woman. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 9; Machatzis HaShekel; Eshel Avraham there.
13) There, gloss: Three lamps. It is preferable to provide one poor woman with enough oil to kindle. Maharash Shaar Aryeh in his manuscript glosses; Birkei Yosef in Shiyurei Berachah, paragraph 1. Shaarei Teshuvah, paragraph 3, cites this but writes that the custom is to add a lamp in accordance with Maharil. In light of what we wrote in the preceding paragraph, however, Maharil did not explicitly require an additional lamp. It is therefore preferable to provide the poor with oil when possible.
14) There, gloss: Three lamps. Regarding whether one may kindle one Shabbos lamp from another through an intermediary, see below, siman 674. It states that some permit this when there is no concern that the intermediary will be extinguished before it kindles the Shabbos lamp. According to Maran's unattributed opinion, however, even this is forbidden, unless one lamp is kindled directly from another; see there. See Magen Avraham there, who writes in the name of Mordechai that the custom is to be stringent and not kindle one mitzvah lamp from another. Eshel Avraham there writes that this may refer to kindling through an intermediary, whereas without an intermediary even the custom does not forbid it. He concludes, however, that it is preferable to kindle the Shabbos lamps with a shamash; see there. See above, paragraph 8, and below, paragraph 91.
