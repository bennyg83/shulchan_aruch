(1) (s.k. 1) One should be careful, etc., that he goes there. See seif 6 whether he blesses on them — for it is like a bonfire. And he brought proof from what Rama wrote in siman 671 seif 4, and these are his words there in siman 671: "And they should be careful when making the candles, even of wax, not to attach them together and kindle them, for it becomes like a bonfire — even for Shabbat and Yom Tov candles they should be careful not to do so" — end Rama's words; and Peri Megadim understood that Rama forbids even braided ones like chains. And on this Magen Avraham wrote that he did not look at Darkei Moshe there in siman 671, for from the books of Darkei Moshe his Hagahot in Shulchan Aruch flow, and from Sefer Darkei Moshe it is proven that Rama's intent is not so — Magen Avraham brought there, and these are Darkei Moshe's words: "When one kindles a Shabbat candle or Chanukah candle or Yom Tov candle and attaches the candles adjacent to one another until they warm each other and wax drips from the warmth and they also double and fall — they have not fulfilled the mitzvah" — until here Darkei Moshe's language; and Magen Avraham wrote there that from this it implies it is permitted to make twisted ones; and Magen Avraham brought proof, for on Shabbat and Yom Tov it is a mitzvah to make twisted ones. Afterwards he brought in the name of Mahariv that he forbids on Chanukah even twisted ones, for it is like a bonfire — see there; if so, even though on Chanukah it is forbidden even twisted ones — meaning specifically on Chanukah where a candle is required similar to the miracle; but twisted ones are a bonfire and called an avukah; but regarding Shabbat where we require for light and joy — on the contrary twisted ones are preferable; but from Rama's language in Hagahah siman 671 where he concluded the reason that it becomes like a bonfire — it implies he retracted in Shulchan Aruch and ruled like Mahariv that twisted ones are also forbidden, and this is a general rule including Shabbat and Yom Tov with them there — it implies that on Shabbat and Yom Tov too he forbids twisted ones; and so it appears from Peri Megadim's reference to Rama's Hagahah siman 671 Mahariv.
