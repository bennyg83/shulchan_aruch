(א) Two wicks — see in Mishna Berurah in what he wrote that they kindle from candle to candle. So too in siman 674 in Beit Yosef in the name of Ba'al HaTurim; and in what he wrote that they are accustomed to be stringent by means of a splinter — even though in Magen Avraham in siman 674 he wrote in general that they are accustomed to be stringent also on Shabbat not to kindle from candle to candle — already Peri Megadim wrote there that the custom to be stringent is by means of a splinter alone, but from candle to candle there is no custom; and also in our days we have seen that the custom is to kindle from candle to candle, and so Chayei Adam wrote that on Shabbat they kindle from candle to candle. However in the essence of the law there is room to examine how it is permitted by law to kindle from candle to candle on a Shabbat candle — granted regarding a Chanukah candle, even though the essence of the mitzvah is one candle and the rest are only for hiddur, nevertheless since this hiddur is mentioned in the Gemara, therefore it becomes a candle of a mitzvah and is permitted by law, only that they are accustomed to be stringent on account of this as stated there in Hagahah; but on Shabbat two candles are not mentioned at all in the Gemara, only that the kindling itself is obligatory and we do on our own for a mere hint, and in particular if one kindles more than two — from where do we know that it is permitted to kindle from candle to candle? Perhaps this is like if one wishes to kindle a weekday candle on Chanukah [meaning more than the fixed count] — certainly forbidden by law; and in Sefer HaTerumah who wrote that it is permitted — perhaps it deals with when two householders dwell in one house, that each one has the mitzvah of kindling. But from the conclusion of his wording it does not imply so. And perhaps because for every additional light there is more shalom bayit and extra joy — all of it is included in oneg Shabbat and is called a candle of a mitzvah, similar to what Magen Avraham wrote below s.k. 15; and this is preferable to a Chanukah candle, for they are stringent in it because the rest are only for hiddur — but this cannot be answered, for it is not yet called a candle of a mitzvah as long as one has not blessed upon it; for if her kindling was not for the sake of Shabbat she would need to extinguish it and kindle again, as below seif 6 — that her kindling is for the sake of Shabbat but she has not yet accepted Shabbat upon herself; nevertheless as long as she has not blessed, if so certainly it is included in a candle of a mitzvah, and therefore our answer above; and Eliyah Rabbah wrote in the name of Pa'aneach Raza that it is good to have a special candle with which to always kindle all the Shabbat candles:
