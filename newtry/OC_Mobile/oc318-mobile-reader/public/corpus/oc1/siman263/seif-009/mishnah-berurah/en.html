And they eat, etc. See below, at the end of siman 273, Magen Avraham, se’if katan 15, that eating in the courtyard is permitted only when one suffers greatly inside the house because of flies or the like. Otherwise, one must eat specifically in the place of the candle. See what the Mishnah Berurah writes there.
In the courtyard. This implies that if one eats in the house, it is sufficient even if the candle does not remain lit until night, and the blessing is not in vain. Although it is still daytime, he derives benefit and joy from the candles while eating. The preferred manner of performing the mitzvah is to make long candles that remain lit until night, even if he intends to eat before nightfall.
It is a blessing in vain. Since he cannot make any use of the candles when he later returns home at night, to avoid stumbling over wood or stone, the consideration of domestic peace does not apply. If the house in which he kindled was somewhat dark and he used the candlelight for something connected with the meal, there is no prohibition, even if the candles do not remain lit until night and even if he eats in the courtyard.
