But women do not possess the wisdom to distinguish. That is to say, women generally will not know how to distinguish between Shabbos and Yom Tov. Were we to say that on Yom Tov she should recite the blessing and then light, she might do the same on Shabbos.
As written in siman 158, regarding performing the blessing before the act, the Sages did not distinguish between cases. In siman 158, se’if 1, it is written that one does not recite the blessing before washing, but after washing and before drying. This is because at times one’s hands are unclean, such as after leaving the privy, and one cannot recite the blessing before washing. Therefore, the Sages did not distinguish, and even when one’s hands are clean, one should not recite the blessing before washing. Tosafos similarly write in Pesachim that for this reason one does not recite the blessing over immersion before immersing, even though that would place the blessing before the act. This is because of the immersion of a convert: we rule that he does not become a Jew until he has undergone circumcision and immersion. He therefore cannot recite the blessing before immersing, since before immersion he is not yet a Jew, and how could he say, “Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us,” when he is not yet commanded? Therefore, even a woman immersing from niddah recites the blessing after immersion, because the Sages did not distinguish between cases.
