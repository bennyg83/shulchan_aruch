There, se’if 12: If the majority of the congregation accepted Shabbos, etc. I saw that the Gaon of Lissa, in his siddur in the laws of Shabbos, ruled that even when the congregation accepted Shabbos through prayer while it was still daytime, acts needed for a mitzvah remain permitted, just as during bein hashmashos. In his view, they accepted only the status of tosefes Shabbos and not the full status of Shabbos itself; see there, where he disagrees with the later authorities. At first glance, it appears to me that the later authorities are correct. The Gemara in Nedarim 12 asks whether one who makes a verbal association attaches his statement to the object’s original status or to its present status, and we rule that he attaches it to the original status. Similarly, one who accepts Shabbos would seemingly attach his acceptance to the essential Shabbos itself, rather than merely to its current status as tosefes Shabbos. However, in my work Sefer HaChaim, laws of Rosh Hashanah, siman 600, I challenged the Taz, who writes that accepting Shabbos is comparable to a vow or oath. I objected that this would be an association with something prohibited by its own nature, which does not create a vow. According to that analysis, the ruling of the Gaon of Lissa should be upheld. In the Talmudic case, attaching the statement to the original status would still be an association with something prohibited through a vow. Therefore, since it can be understood as referring to the original status, we do not say that he refers to the present status. Here, by contrast, if one were attaching his acceptance to the essential Shabbos, nothing would take effect, because that would be an association with something prohibited by its own nature. Tosefes Shabbos, however, is not intrinsically recognizable as belonging to the person, and if he does not wish to accept Shabbos, it does not take effect. Only when he accepts Shabbos while it is still daytime does the Torah cause Shabbos to take effect for him. This is therefore comparable to something prohibited through a vow, and he certainly attaches his acceptance to its present status. His law should consequently be only that of bein hashmashos. Just as an act needed for a mitzvah is permitted during bein hashmashos, so too it is permitted after accepting Shabbos while it is still daytime. The same applies to tosefes Shabbos itself: since it is not the essential Shabbos, but rather a period that the Torah obligates a person to add to Shabbos, it is as though it were within the person’s power and is therefore called something prohibited through a vow. One who accepts Shabbos attaches his acceptance to that status. The words of the Gaon of Lissa are therefore correct, in my humble opinion.
