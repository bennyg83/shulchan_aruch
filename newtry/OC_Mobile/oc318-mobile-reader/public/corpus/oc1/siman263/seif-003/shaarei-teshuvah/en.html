That she forgot. See Baer Heitev. Shiyurei Berachah cites Mahar Shmuel Shaar Aryeh, in a handwritten gloss, who writes that it is preferable for a woman who forgot to kindle to provide one poor woman with sufficient oil for candle lighting; see there. The custom, however, is to add an additional candle, following the Maharil. Regarding what was written about a woman after childbirth, Shiyurei Berachah cites Knesset HaGedolah in the name of one sage who questions the practice, because kindling must be performed in its proper place, as stated below in se’if 10; see there. See also above, se’if katan 1, in the name of Beis Yaakov. Responsa Nachalas Shivah, siman 8, discusses a woman who kindled two candles intending to recite the blessing, but because of the pressures of erev Shabbos she became uncertain whether she had done so. He distinguishes according to whether she kindled at the proper time; see there. This does not appear correct to me, and the proof he brings from the law of ‘you shall write them’ is not comparable. The primary ruling is that whenever she kindled from plag haminchah onward, we presume that she kindled for the mitzvah of Shabbos and recited the blessing. Even if she is uncertain, she need not kindle another candle with a blessing. If she is uncertain whether she kindled before plag haminchah or afterward, she should kindle again for the sake of the mitzvah, without a blessing. See what Eliyah Rabbah writes concerning this.
