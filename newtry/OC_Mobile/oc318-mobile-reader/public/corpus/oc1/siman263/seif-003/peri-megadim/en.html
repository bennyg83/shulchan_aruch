And behold from his holy words we learn several mighty halachot: (1) There is no prohibition of amirah unless it is forbidden to all Israel; and even though amirah to a non-Jew is shevut, although the non-Jew acts permissibly — here, since for his fellow Israelite it is permitted, they did not decree amirah in this; and per this it is permitted to tell a priest: your Israelite goes to a place of corpse impurity and brings his objects from there, and the like; and in truth the poskim did not mention this law for prohibition, and now I did not find it; and per Taz's reason it is correct, unlike Peri Megadim's reason — it would not be permission. However one should see: perhaps a person's agent is like himself; and all the more so per the view that where the agent is not liable, the sender is liable — one could say. And via a non-Jew — end of siman 244 in the name of Semag from the verse: all melacha shall not be done for them via others as well. And on Motza'ei Shabbat, darkness before havdalah — there is no prohibition except rabbinically, and it is permitted to tell one's fellow to do melacha since there is no prohibition for the fellow — amirah is permitted like on Erev Shabbat; and it implies: when it is forbidden to all Israel, amirah to a non-Jew is forbidden even in rabbinic matters; and see Yoreh Deah siman 296 seif 69 regarding kilayim hakerem outside Israel — rabbinically permitted to a minor non-Jew, not to an adult; and siman 297 letter 1 from this in the laws of kilayim zera'im, and in 297 laws of kilayim behemah in Shach letter 4; and see below 307 in Magen Avraham letter 29.
