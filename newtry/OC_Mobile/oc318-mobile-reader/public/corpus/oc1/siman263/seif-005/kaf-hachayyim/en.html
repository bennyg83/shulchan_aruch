5) There: One corresponding to zachor, etc. One should not attach two candles to one another, because they then constitute a torch. Rema writes this in siman 671, seif 4 in the gloss. Bach writes that the custom is to bring to the synagogue two wax candles twisted together as a remembrance that zachor and shamor were spoken in one utterance, and that this is a proper custom originating with the great early authorities; see there. It appears that this does not conflict with the gloss. There it discusses lamps in one's home, where twisting them together constitutes a torch. Here it discusses bringing them to the synagogue as a remembrance of shamor and zachor. We are not concerned about a torch, since he already fulfills his obligation with the lamp in his home. Alternatively, here he brings them twisted together but separates them when kindling. This explanation is not compelling. Knesses HaGedolah, Hagahos HaTur, paragraph 3. Mateh Yehudah, paragraph 1, similarly writes that one must kindle two wicks in two separate lamps, one corresponding to zachor and one to shamor, so the distinction is recognizable. This is contrary to the opinion that one should kindle two wicks in a single lamp. What Magen Avraham writes in paragraph 1, with due respect, is incorrect; see there.
