When she kindles. Some authorities write that when there is a wedding on erev Shabbos and they delay there until after sunset, and the woman does not wish to accept Shabbos before the canopy lest she need do something else forbidden on Shabbos; she should therefore kindle the candle without a blessing before the canopy, and afterward in darkness she spreads her hands over the candles and blesses; or while still in bein hashmashos after the canopy she says to a non-Jew to kindle, for they did not decree shevut in bein hashmashos for mitzvah need, as explained above in siman 261, and she blesses. The later authorities questioned on this, for a blessing cannot be recited over a light that is already burning; and especially at that time kindling is already forbidden; how can she say “Who commanded us to kindle”; and regarding the alternative proposal that she say to a non-Jew to kindle; also difficult, for there is no agency for a non-Jew, and thus she is not kindling; how can she bless; and even even when one Jew instructs another Jew to kindle for him; per Derech HaChaim the one who gave the instruction does not recite the blessing; only the person who kindles does; all the more so here, the woman should not recite the blessing. The later authorities therefore propose a different solution: according to our ruling she can bless and stipulate in any case for need that she not accept Shabbos at kindling; here too, this is considered necessary: she kindles and blesses before going to the canopy and stipulates; and likewise a woman whose night of immersion falls on erev Shabbos; she kindles and blesses before going to the mikveh and stipulates she does not accept Shabbos until after washing and combing her hair.
To kindle a candle. And even when kindling several candles, it is preferable to say “candle,” for the main obligation is one candle.
Also on Yom Tov, etc. And one need not recite Shehecheyanu on the kindling; however where the custom is to do so, one should not protest.

Before the kindling. As with all mitzvos, over which the blessing is recited before their performance.
After the kindling. He holds that if she blesses it is as if she accepted Shabbos explicitly and she would then be forbidden to kindle; and thus on Yom Tov where this does not apply, according to all opinions she blesses and afterward kindles. And the Magen Avraham holds that no distinction is made; but many later authorities hold as written initially.
