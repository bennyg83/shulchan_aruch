When she kindles, she should recite the blessing. If she is uncertain whether she recited the blessing, she need not recite it again, because in cases of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently, and also because habit makes it likely that she did recite it, as stated by Nachalas Shivah and Derech HaChaim. If she knows with certainty that she did not recite the blessing, the Eliyah Rabbah implies that she may still recite it as long as it remains a permitted time to kindle. This accords with Magen Avraham, se’if katan 11, who relies b’dieved upon Maharash. Once the prohibited time has begun, however, he disagrees with Maharash.
See below, siman 610. See the Mishnah Berurah. One may also explain that the Mechaber intends to provide a reason for his view that no blessing is recited. In siman 610, se’if 1, the Mechaber writes that in a place where the custom is to kindle on Yom Kippur, one kindles, and where the custom is not to kindle, one does not kindle. Therefore, although our custom is to kindle, no blessing is recited, because one does not recite a blessing over a mere custom. This reason appears in the Biur HaGra.
After kindling. See the Mishnah Berurah. Regarding a woman who stipulates that she is not accepting Shabbos through kindling, or a man, who according to the ruling below does not need to stipulate, Derech HaChaim implies that they should nevertheless kindle, spread their hands over the lights, and then recite the blessing, because no distinction was made, in accordance with the Magen Avraham. The glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger and the Chayei Adam, however, indicate that in such a case they should recite the blessing first and then kindle.
