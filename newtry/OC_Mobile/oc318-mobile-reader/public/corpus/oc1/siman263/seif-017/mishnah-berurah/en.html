(64) Permitted to say — since for his fellow it is permitted, amirah prohibition does not apply here. And this seif speaks when there is much time until bein hashemashos; for if close to bein hashemashos, certainly the majority of people of the city accepted Shabbat and the minority is dragged after them perforce, and as above in seif 12; and this law does not apply. When approaching bein hashemashos they should not wait to say mizmor shir leyom hashabbat [or other statements customary to say for accepting Shabbat] for some people occupied in their homes with their matters and delay coming to the synagogue:
(65) And all the more on Motza'ei Shabbat — meaning for on Erev Shabbat he already accepted Shabbat and cannot return; and nevertheless permitted; and all the more on Motza'ei Shabbat, that every hour if he wishes he can pray and make havdalah — all the more permitted to say:
(66) Prayed and made havdalah — meaning he made havdalah in prayer; for thereby permitted to do melacha, and as below siman 299, even though he did not make havdalah on the cup:
(67) And permitted to benefit — and Levush forbids unless he blessed and removed his mind from Shabbat; and Acharonim agreed with Shulchan Aruch. And if he said hamavdil bein kodesh lechol in the middle of his meal, per all permitted, as written at the end of siman 299, that even he himself is then permitted in melacha; but requires study whether he can afterward say retzeh in Birkat Hamazon, since he made it profane at the start.
