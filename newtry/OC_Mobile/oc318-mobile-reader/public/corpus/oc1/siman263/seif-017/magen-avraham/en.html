(א) Permitted to tell — for if he wished he would not have accepted Shabbat upon himself, and all that he has permission — amirah is permitted, as written siman 307 seif 8: one who did not prepare his food should wait with Barchu; and if he is near synagogue he should not wait; and what is written "better they be inadvertent," etc. — this is in a place of permission; but in synagogue, no (seif 8 siman 262):

(ב) And all the more so in what he wrote — meaning that on Erev Shabbat he already accepted Shabbat and cannot return — nevertheless it is permitted from the reason that if he wished he would not have accepted it; all the more so in what he wrote that every hour and hour if he wishes he prays and distinguishes — all the more so it is permitted to tell:

(ג) He prayed and distinguished — in prayer, as written siman 299:

(ד) Permitted to benefit — and one can say for those who observe two days Yom Kippur who are forbidden to eat at night what was cooked after the day — there according to their opinion they are uncertain lest today be Yom Kippur — if so this melacha was done in prohibition, forbidden for all Israel to do melacha; but here he knows Shabbat already passed, only he did not make havdalah; and one can say for one who did not leave an eruv tavshilin he must transfer his flour to others — but others are forbidden to bake his flour, for we hold his flour was forbidden — they penalized him (Darkei Moshe); and likewise in a responsum; and so Bach — unlike Levush who forbids unless he blessed and diverted his attention from Shabbat — I do not know what Birkat HaMazon avails for melacha; and this is simple that if he said havdalah, etc., all is permitted as written siman 299; however there is room to hesitate whether afterward he can say retzeh in Birkah — one can say specifically when he prayed a weekday prayer he does not say retzeh; but when he said havdalah alone he may say retzeh; and see siman 271 and siman 188:
