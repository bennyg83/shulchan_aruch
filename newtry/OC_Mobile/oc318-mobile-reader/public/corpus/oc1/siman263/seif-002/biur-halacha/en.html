(א) He has nothing to eat — see in Magen Avraham, and I deviated from his explanation for it is very forced in the wording; therefore I explained in Mishna Berurah that the intent is that he is supported from the charity fund; and as a sign that it is so — behold this law and also the law of siman 671 seif 1 regarding a Chanukah candle both come from the wording of Rambam and from one source were hewn, as the Gra explained there; and here and there Rambam wrote explicitly thus — even if he has nothing to eat except from charity, and presumably the same applies in our matter. And what he wrote there "asks" — likewise that he asks at the doorways as in our matter, and as Eliyah Rabbah wrote there — for otherwise he should have used the wording "borrows" as with the four cups — see there. And what he further wrote in Mishna Berurah that the mitzvah of bread precedes everything — see in Peri Megadim that we require bread for satiation and not a kezayit [meaning if he is hungry — for otherwise certainly he fulfills with a small measure, as we say in the Gemara: always a person should set his table on erev Shabbat even though he needs only a kezayit, and see below siman 291 seif 1 and in what we wrote there]; and proof for Peri Megadim's words from that which Magen Avraham brought at the beginning of siman 291 in the name of Shulchan Chai that one may not divide the meal into two so that he not be satiated in order to fulfill the third meal — and from this we learn that the same applies regarding the mitzvah of a candle; but one can reject — for the mitzvah of a candle is stricter than a third meal, for regarding it Chazal said "make your Shabbat like a weekday and do not require [help] from others," and not regarding kindling the candle, as Shulchan Aruch ruled that one asks for it at the doorways — for it is the main oneg Shabbat meal, and this is not included in what they said "make your Shabbat like a weekday," as Magen Avraham wrote. And I saw in Derech HaChaim who copied that bread for satiation of the three meals precedes the candle — and behold his words flow from the words of Taz in siman 678 who wrote there that bread of the three meals which are d'oraisa precedes a Chanukah candle, and from this we learn that the same applies to a Shabbat candle; but in truth the agreement of most poskim is that it is d'rabbanan and asmachta baKra, and Peri Megadim corrected this — for since there is asmachta baKra it is stricter than ordinary d'rabbanan — see there; but nevertheless in the end this requires further study, for we have not seen that the Rabbis considered a Shabbat candle and Chanukah candle on account of shalom bayit and pirsumei nisa more than a third meal — for behold for a Shabbat candle and Chanukah candle one must ask at the doorways in order to attain it, and for a third meal one is not obligated to this, as Magen Avraham wrote above siman 242 — see there; and to this Magen Avraham hinted here in s.k. 5; and in particular what Derech HaChaim wrote that three meals for satiation precede a Shabbat candle more — in my humble opinion it is simple one should not be stringent in this regarding a third meal, for a kebeitzah and a little more suffices [and see siman 291], and the rest one spends for a Shabbat candle:
