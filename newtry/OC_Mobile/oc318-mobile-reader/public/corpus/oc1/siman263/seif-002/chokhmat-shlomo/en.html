There seif 12 — if the majority of the congregation accepted Shabbat upon themselves, etc. Editor's note: behold I saw the Gaon of Lissa in his siddur in the laws of Shabbat that he ruled that even if they accepted Shabbat in prayer while it was still day, it is permitted for mitzvah need like bein hashemashot, for they did not accept upon themselves except as the law of tosefet Shabbat and not as the law of Shabbat itself — see there that he disagrees with the Acharonim. And in my humble opinion it appears like the Acharonim's view, since it is a question in Nedarim daf 12 whether he takes hold at the essence or now he takes hold, and we hold that at the essence he takes hold — and if so here too one who accepts Shabbat certainly takes hold at the essence of Shabbat and not now as the law of tosefet Shabbat he takes hold. So it appeared to me at first glance. However per what I wrote in my work Sefer HaChaim, laws of Rosh Hashanah siman 400, to challenge Taz who wrote there that accepting Shabbat is like a vow and oath — and I wondered at him, for behold it is like taking hold in a forbidden matter which is not a vow — see there; per this one should uphold the words of the Gaon of Lissa z"l cited above, for in the Gemara at the essence as well it is like a vowed matter — therefore since it is possible to say that at the essence he takes hold we do not say now he takes hold; but here if we say at the essence of Shabbat he takes hold it would not apply at all, for it would be like a forbidden matter — only regarding tosefet Shabbat, since this is not recognition for a person and if he wishes he does not accept Shabbat, only if he accepts Shabbat while it is still day the Torah said that Shabbat falls upon him — if so this is like a vow and he takes hold in a vowed matter, and with this certainly that now he takes hold — therefore its law should not be except like the law of bein hashemashot; and therefore just as in bein hashemashot itself it is permitted for mitzvah need, so too in his accepting Shabbat while it is still day it is permitted for mitzvah need [and likewise tosefet Shabbat itself, since it is not the essence of Shabbat but only that the Torah said a person must add tosefet on Shabbat — this is as if it were the person's domain; therefore it is called a vowed matter; and therefore one who accepts Shabbat is like one who takes hold in it; and understand well] — it is found that the words of the Gaon of Lissa z"l are justified as appeared to me in my humble opinion on this; and understand well:
