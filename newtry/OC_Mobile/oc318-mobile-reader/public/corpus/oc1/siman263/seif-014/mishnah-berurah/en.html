(53) They need not — for we do not burden the congregation, even though it was in error.

(54) From plag hamincha and above — for before plag hamincha even the congregation they send back, for it is not the time of Maariv prayer at all. And the time of plag hamincha — see above in siman 233:

(55) He must, etc. — since he mistakenly prayed thinking it had already gotten dark; and likewise on a weekday the law is so, since he is accustomed always to pray Maariv at its time per the Rabbis, and today he mistakenly prayed:

(56) Those who kindled — even if they did not pray; but without kindling, even if they prayed the Shabbat prayer, since it was mistakenly it is not considered acceptance. And the reason of Yesh Omrim who hold that acceptance that is via kindling is preferred, for it has an action — and see in Acharonim who brought in the name of several great poskim who hold that congregation acceptance by mistake, even only via prayer, is considered acceptance; only that in this the minority is not drawn after the majority, since the acceptance was by mistake, and one may not be lenient against those poskim. And in a pressing situation one may rely on the first opinion in Shulchan Aruch:

(57) To touch it, etc. — meaning even before he kindled and did not accept Shabbat; so wrote Eshel Avraham. And Magen Avraham wrote the reason: he goes per his view that congregation acceptance by mistake is considered acceptance; and thus the candle was set aside for its mitzvah; and since it was set aside for the owners it is set aside for all; and also forbidden to touch, lest perhaps he use it. For mitzvah need one may be lenient [Darkhei Chaim]. Peri Megadim wrote: one who kindled a Shabbat candle while the day was still great and stipulated not to accept Shabbat — nevertheless the candle was set aside for its mitzvah and forbidden to use it for weekday use; and even others are forbidden, for set aside for owners is forbidden for all:
