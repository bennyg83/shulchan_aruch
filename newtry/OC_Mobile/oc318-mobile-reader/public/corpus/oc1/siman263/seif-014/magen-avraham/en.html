Those who kindled. Maharam of Lublin wrote that the Mordechai holds that an erroneous communal acceptance of Shabbos is nevertheless a valid acceptance, although those absent are not drawn after the minority. So too wrote the Shiltei HaGibborim and the Maharshal, and so ruled the Darkei Moshe in the name of the Or Zarua. One should not be lenient. The Rokeach, Ra’avan, and Shiltei HaGibborim also wrote this. It appears that the same law applies even on a weekday: an individual must repeat the prayer, whereas a congregation need not repeat it, since they prayed in error. See siman 233. So too wrote the Beit Yosef, unlike the Olas Shabbos.
It is forbidden to touch it. The Or Zarua wrote that even another person who has not yet kindled and has not accepted Shabbos may not touch or move it until Motzei Shabbos, since it was kindled for Shabbos. End quote. This is difficult, because at the end of siman 674 it is written that one may kindle a Chanukah light from a Shabbos light. Some explain that here the case concerns members of his household who fulfill their obligation through his kindling, and they are therefore forbidden to touch it even if they stipulated not to accept Shabbos; there the case concerns others who are not members of his household. End quote. It appears to me, however, that the Or Zarua follows his own view that an erroneous acceptance is nevertheless a valid acceptance. The candle was therefore designated for its mitzvah, as stated in Shabbos, folio 45, and since it was designated with respect to its owners, it was designated with respect to everyone. This is also implied by his words, ‘since it was kindled for Shabbos.’ His prohibition against touching it is a decree lest one use it. The Shiltei HaGibborim explicitly implies that even when the household members do not rely upon his kindling, they may not touch that candle; see there. At the end of siman 674 it is permitted because both lights are used for a mitzvah. Alternatively, one can say that a flame has no substance, as stated at the end of Beitzah regarding a flame belonging to consecrated property. See what I wrote in siman 279, at the end of se’if 1.
