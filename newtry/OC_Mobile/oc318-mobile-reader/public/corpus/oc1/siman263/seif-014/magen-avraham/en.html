(א) Those who kindled — and Maharam Latif wrote that Mordechai holds that acceptance in error by the congregation is acceptance, but the minority is not drawn after them; and likewise wrote Shelah and Rashal; and so Darkei Moshe in the name of Arizal — one may not be lenient; and so Rokeach and Ravan and Shagat Aryeh; and it implies that even on a weekday the law is thus — an individual must repeat and pray, and the congregation need not repeat since they prayed in error (see Ralbag); and so Rabbeinu Yerucham — unlike Peri Megadim:

(ב) Forbidden to touch it — Arizal wrote: even after he has not yet kindled and did not accept Shabbat — nevertheless it is forbidden to touch it and move it until what is written, since it was kindled for Shabbat perforce; and there is difficulty, for in siman 674 it is written that one may kindle a Chanukah candle from a Shabbat candle; and one can say here it deals with members of his household who leave with his kindling — therefore they are forbidden to touch even though they stipulated not to accept Shabbat; and there it deals with others who are not members of the household — until here; and in my humble opinion Arizal follows his view that acceptance in error is acceptance — and if so it was designated for its mitzvah as stated Shabbat daf 45; and since it was designated for the owners it was designated for all, as is implied from what is written "since it was kindled for Shabbat"; and that it is forbidden to touch — we decree lest one use it; and in Shelah it implies explicitly that even though members of the household do not rely on his kindling they are forbidden to touch that candle — see there; and in siman 674 it is permitted since both are of mitzvah; or one can say a flame has no substance as stated end of Beitzah regarding a flame of consecrated property — see siman 299 siman 261:
