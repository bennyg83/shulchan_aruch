So too wrote Maamar Mordechai, os 1, and the Beit Yosef in the name of the Mordechai, who wrote this in the name of Rashi. So too wrote the Bach in explanation of the Tur's words. Rabbeinu Tam, however, ruled that if he made it so thick that rain cannot penetrate it, it is invalid. His words were cited by the Tur and the Beit Yosef. The Levush writes that one should indeed be stringent, and the Bach in siman 635 likewise rules in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam; see there. His words were cited by Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. So too ruled the Taz, siman 635, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 5; Chayei Adam, klal 146, os 18; and Mishna Berurah, os 6. If one removes some of the s'chach in a manner that allows the rain to fall into the sukkah, it is valid, and he need not undo all the s'chach from the beginning. Eliyah Rabbah, ibid.; Eshel Avraham, os 2. Nevertheless, it appears that after the fact, where for some reason it is impossible to remove some of the s'chach, one may rely upon the authorities who validate it. Birkei Yosef, os 2; Shaarei Teshuvah, os 3; Mishna Berurah, ibid. Beit HaShoevah, os 6, however, disputes the aforementioned words of Birkei Yosef and writes that it is invalid even after the fact; see there. I do not know what basis there is to dispute this, since Birkei Yosef writes about a case in which it is impossible to alter it. Is it better that one sit without a sukkah than sit in this sukkah and fulfill the mitzvah according to the opinion that validates it? Rather, he should have said that when alteration is impossible, one should sit in it but not recite a blessing, because of the principle that in cases of doubt concerning blessings we are lenient.
