Se'if 10, and if it was, etc., or he raised, etc. Like Rashi's explanation, and as written in the Yerushalmi there, which the Rosh brought there. However, the beginning of his words, "it had a roof," requires analysis.
And some say, etc. According to Tosafos' explanation there. It is implied from his words that the Rambam mentioned above does not hold this, unlike the Beis Yosef.
And it must have, etc. Like the measure of a sukkah that will be explained in siman 633:8 and 634:1. He teaches us not like the commentators who say that it needs only seven by seven on its floor and that any time it is ten handbreadths high, even if the tenth handbreadth narrows to a width of one handbreadth, that is enough. He teaches that it is not so. See Tosafos there, s.v. ha'oseh, unlike the Rashba, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh, who wrote that the entire slope needs to be only sixteen besides the handbreadth. Rather, everything depends on the height of the wall. For example, if the wall is eighteen handbreadths high, the slope must be twenty-two and a half, and the distance from the wall fourteen and a half; this is if he distanced it one handbreadth from the wall. If he raised it one handbreadth from the ground and the wall is seventeen high, then the slope is twenty-two and a half and more, and the distance is sixteen. So everything depends on the wall and the distance.
It also must, etc. In the two cases mentioned above that I wrote, this refers to the upper ten handbreadths of the slope.
And if all, etc. It is just like s'chach extending from the sukkah there. We also say in chapter 7 of Ohalos, Mishnah 2: all the slopes of tents, etc.; impurity in a tent, etc. The Rosh.
