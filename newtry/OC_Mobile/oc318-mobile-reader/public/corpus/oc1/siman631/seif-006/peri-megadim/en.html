That it is impossible. See Turei Zahav, Sukkah 15b, according to the explanation of Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbeinu Tam: it is impossible to be exact, etc. For we rule that breached space equal to standing material in such a case, with valid and invalid s'chach, is permitted. This is not comparable to se'if 1 regarding equal sun and shade, where it is invalid; see the Rosh there and the Beis Yosef regarding where one makes the valid material exceed and places it and presses it between them until it protrudes over the spits, or places one lengthwise and the other crosswise, and perforce they are like breached and valid. Know that wooden spits are invalid rabbinically, and the long poles of a bed that broke, as worn-out utensils, are also rabbinic. If so, one could say that the issue of impossibility of exactness is a doubt, and in a rabbinic matter one could say it is permitted. See Tosafos there, and Chullin regarding half being like the majority. But with metal spits, and with a bed-pole and two legs, which is invalid by Torah law, this applies. Alternatively, even if they are rabbinic, the Gemara asks properly regarding reeds that have open space; see Beis Yosef. Now he used the language "one who roofs" and not "one who covers with s'chach"; see Chiddushei HaRan, for they are not fit for s'chach, so he used the language "one who roofs." See Rashi, of blessed memory, "as we do," and so wrote Tosafos Yom Tov. See our great master Pnei Yehoshua, of blessed memory, who discussed this at length in his novellae. Apparently, according to the Bach in siman 630, and the Turei Zahav and Magen Avraham there, letter 14, according to the view that a support is forbidden because of a decree regarding something commonly used for s'chach, namely wooden spits, but metal spits, which everyone knows are not used, may be used as a support, as in this case according to the Ran's answer; see Turei Zahav there. If so, one can say that everything follows the support, and this too is rabbinic. Whenever the support is invalid rabbinically, it is a decree upon a decree. If so, the Mishnah specifically teaches us that a ceiling as we make it is permitted. According to Rava, perforce he makes the valid material exceed, since he places the spit lengthwise and the valid s'chach crosswise; but if he placed the spit lengthwise and the valid s'chach between them lengthwise, it is invalid, lest he not make it exceed; see Tosafos there. Perhaps Rashi explains the reverse, and the law teaches us that a support is permitted, unlike Rabbi Yehudah below, for the spits are a support. If you say that iron is not normally used for s'chach, what will you say about bed-poles? And if you say that worn-out utensils are rabbinic and we do not decree regarding a support, so it is a decree upon a decree, the Gemara explains to reject this and establishes it with legs, which are fit to receive impurity by Torah law. We learn from here that a support is permitted in every case. That case of a sukkah among trees on 24b and 22b was explained by the master, of blessed memory, and in Magen Avraham, siman 630, letter 14; see what I wrote there. I have elaborated somewhat merely in the way of analysis.
