Se'if 9: He must remove all the nails, etc. This is the meaning there of 'he loosens them,' as explained by Rambam and Tur.
Even the boards, etc. As stated there: 'This is well according to Rav, etc.; but according to Shmuel, etc.' See Rashi there, s.v. seifa and s.v. b'vatulei, and Tosafos, s.v. Rabbi Meir. We rule like Shmuel, as Rif writes on 14a: Rav Pappa explains Shmuel's statement; moreover, the rule is that between Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah, the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah, as stated in chapter 4 of Eruvin. According to Rav, Rabbi Yehudah validates all boards. Rav Huna disputes Rabbi Chiya and Rabbah bar Rav Huna there, indicating that the halachah follows Shmuel. See Tosafos there, s.v. hafchan.
And there is one who says, etc. Ramban, Rashba, and Ran explain that the Mishnah concerns boards less than four tefachim wide, unlike Rashi and Tosafos, who assume an ordinary ceiling has boards four tefachim wide. Otherwise, why would removing every other board help? Even if, as Rashi explains, the case parallels loosening them and seeks to validate the entire sukkah, invalid sechach four tefachim wide may not be slept beneath; and certainly according to the view that four tefachim in the middle disqualify. Why then does the Gemara challenge Shmuel from the baraisa on 18a rather than from the Mishnah? It is also illogical that a preexisting ceiling should become valid merely by loosening boards four tefachim wide, with no visible indication. Rather, the case is boards under four tefachim. The authority who applies the ceiling decree to boards four tefachim wide applies it here even to narrower boards because, once installed as the house's roof, they resemble a ceiling. Hence the Gemara asks that according to Rav, Rabbi Meir has no such decree, but according to Shmuel all agree to the decree with four-tefach boards and should likewise invalidate even narrower boards here. It answers that the latter clause concerns nullifying the roof: Beis Hillel permit even an old roof through this, while Beis Shammai require removing every other board. This also explains Rabbi Meir according to everyone; all the repairs recreate a roof not originally made as a ceiling. This is Rambam's view. Raavad offers another approach even according to Rashi and Tosafos: he rules like Rav, since the halachah follows Rav in ritual law. Rav Pappa's explanation of Shmuel is not decisive, for Amoraim often explain views not accepted. The sugya invalidates at four because we follow Rabbi Meir in his decrees; here too the issue is the ceiling decree. Both approaches reach the same result. Rashba explains that according to Rav, Rabbi Yehudah makes no decree, so the rule favoring Rav in decrees applies; according to Shmuel, Rabbi Yehudah also decrees and they dispute only the measure, so the ordinary rule favoring Rabbi Yehudah applies. There is no practical difference there, but here there is: according to Rav, the halachah follows Rabbi Meir in the first clause and likewise in the latter. On Raavad's view, even under Rashi's explanation the boards must be less than four tefachim, for Rabbi Meir permits removal of alternate boards only where each is precisely four. Rabbeinu Yonah and Rashba also rule like Rav and Rabbi Meir, but on their explanation only removing alternate boards helps, and only when none is four tefachim wide, as explained.
