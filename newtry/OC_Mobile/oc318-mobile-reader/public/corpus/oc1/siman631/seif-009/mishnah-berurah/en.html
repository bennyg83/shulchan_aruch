(22) That there is no plaster upon them. All the more so if there had been plaster on the boards and he removed the plaster and afterward loosened the boards, it certainly helps, for he performed a significant act for the sake of sukkah [later authorities].
(23) It is enough with this, that he removes, etc. This helps as though he had initially roofed with all the boards for the sake of sukkah.
(24) All the nails, etc. This is the language of the Rosh and the Tur. In Rashi it says that he dismantles all of them and shakes them for the sake of sukkah. In the Rambam's commentary on the Mishnah, it also appears in this language: "loosens" means that he uproots them from their place, from the nails fixed in them. So too Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura, and the Or Zarua also copied like Rashi. From all these it implies that one must shake all the boards.
(25) Even if the boards are four wide. Although if there had not been a ceiling here initially, and now he came to cover with boards of four handbreadths for the sake of sukkah, it would be rabbinically invalid, as above in siman 629:18, because of the decree of ceiling, lest he sit under the beam of his house and say: what difference is there between this and a new one? Nevertheless, now that this was a ceiling, and he comes to perform an act by removing its nails and shaking it for the sake of sukkah, this demonstrates that he knows that the Torah said, "You shall make," and not from what was already made, and there is no longer a decree because of the ceiling of his house. Now this helps only regarding loosening. But regarding removing one from between them, there are views among the poskim, for we rule below in siman 632 that invalid s'chach invalidates in the middle with four handbreadths, and next to the valid s'chach there remains a board in the ceiling that is four handbreadths wide, unless it happens that he places valid s'chach in the place of two boards [which is the measure of a sukkah and more]. Some hold that since he performed an act by removing a board from between them and placing valid s'chach, this act helps to validate even the place of the board beside it.
(26) And there is one who says that they must not be, etc. This view disagrees with the first view regarding loosening, and holds that it does not help when the boards are four handbreadths wide. Regarding removing one from between them, there are differing views among the later authorities. Know that in a case where the boards in the ceiling were not four handbreadths wide, and he removed one board from between every two boards and placed valid s'chach in its place, according to everyone it is permitted to sit even beneath the boards, since once he performed an act, that place too is validated; even if the sukkah is several amos large, that place too is validated. However, according to what is explained in siman 629:18, that nowadays the custom is not to cover with boards at all, even boards less than four, one should be careful not to rely on loosening or on this either, except in pressing circumstances.
