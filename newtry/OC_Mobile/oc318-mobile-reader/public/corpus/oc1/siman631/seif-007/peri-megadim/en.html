In a sukkah. See Turei Zahav. What the Mechaber wrote, "and they do not have four handbreadths," refers to the width of the spit; and also, there is not in one place a width of four handbreadths, meaning that he did not place two spits one after another. For if there is four handbreadths of invalid s'chach, it invalidates with four handbreadths, and in a small sukkah with less than three. See the Tur, Beis Yosef, and Bach; some texts have four handbreadths and some have three handbreadths, referring to a large sukkah and a small sukkah. With Hashem's help, in siman 632:1 and 632:2 the law of when invalid s'chach invalidates will be explained; see there.
