4) [Seif 2] If in most of it the shade is greater, etc. But if in a minority of the sukkah the shade exceeds the sunlight by a great amount, while in most of it the sunlight exceeds the shade by only a slight amount, then even though the total shade throughout the entire sukkah exceeds the total sunlight, it is invalid. Rabbeinu Yeruham, responsum 58, section 1; Darkei Moshe, os 1; Prishah, os 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 1 and 2; Mishbetzos Zahav, os 1; Mishna Berurah, os 2.
5) Ibid. If in most of it the shade exceeds the sunlight by two minute amounts, etc. The explanation is as follows: If we combine all the sunlight and all the shade scattered here and there throughout most of the sukkah, the shade exceeds the sunlight by two minute amounts, meaning above at the s'chach. In the minority of the sukkah, the sunlight exceeds the shade by one minute amount. It is specifically necessary that in the majority the shade exceed by two minute amounts. One minute amount is insufficient, because if the one minute amount of extra shade in the majority were offset against the one minute amount of extra sunlight in the minority, the shade and sunlight would be exactly equal, and we learned that when they are equal the sukkah is invalid. All of this concerns the measurement of shade and sunlight above at the s'chach. Prishah, ibid.
6) Ibid. It is valid. It is permitted to sit even beneath the minority area whose s'chach has more sunlight than shade, because that area is nullified to the majority of the s'chach, whose shade is greater than its sunlight. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 4; Mishna Berurah, os 3.
7) Ibid., Rama's gloss: Some are stringent if the sukkah is large, etc. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran: It is possible that this applies specifically to a sukkah that measures only seven by seven tefachim. But concerning a very large sukkah that contains an area of seven tefachim or more whose sunlight is greater than its shade, it requires further study whether we may validate that area through its combination with the rest of the sukkah, end quote. This means that one should not sit in the portion whose sunlight is greater, but this does not invalidate the remainder, since that remainder contains the minimum dimensions of a valid sukkah. Taz, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 3; Beit HaShoevah, os 2; Mishna Berurah, os 4. If the invalid section extends along the entire length of a wall, so that the area whose shade is greater would have only two walls, it is possible that the validity of the entire sukkah requires further study. Mishna Berurah, os 1, and Mishna Berurah, ibid.
