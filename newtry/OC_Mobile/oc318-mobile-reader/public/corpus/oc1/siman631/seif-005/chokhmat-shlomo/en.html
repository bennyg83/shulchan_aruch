To resolve this, it appears that from here there will be a proof to the view of the Beis Yosef in the laws of Yom Tov, siman 502, that we do not say lavud to be stringent. He noted "as written in the first chapter of Sukkah," and the Magen Avraham wondered about him, for on the contrary, there it proves the opposite, etc. According to the above, this works well, for from here it is proven so. Since Rav Kahana and Rav Ashi asked from a rabbinic beam, perforce they do not hold to distinguish between rabbinic and Torah law. Also, since they hold like Rava, that with less than a handbreadth we do not say chavot rami, since his main proof is from impurity, from this perforce it is proven that they hold one should not distinguish between impurity and prohibition. If so, it is difficult one way or the other: why do we not say lavud with less than a handbreadth? Perforce it is proven that we do not say lavud to be stringent. This is the intent of the Beis Yosef: according to what is written in the first chapter of Sukkah, where it is proven that we do not say lavud in the middle regarding impurity, and according to what we rule, that there is lavud in the middle, while we also rule that with less than a handbreadth we do not say chavot rami, it is proven that Rava, Rav Kahana, and Rav Ashi certainly hold that we do not say lavud to be stringent.
