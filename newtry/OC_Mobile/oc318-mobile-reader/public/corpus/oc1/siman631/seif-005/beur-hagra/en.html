Se'if 5: Provided, etc.; namely, etc. There Rava said, 'From where do I say this?' See Rashi there, s.v. v'tani alah, whose text reads thus and omits 'there is not, etc.' Rashba and Ran, however, write that the texts should not be emended: 'they do not contain an opening of a tefach' refers to the upper beams, while 'there is not a tefach between them' refers to the lower ones; 'impurity beneath one of them' means beneath one of the lower beams. The statement 'evidently, when, etc.' applies only where there is an open tefach below into which it can enter. Some say that if the roof formed by the upper beams is wider than a tefach, the airspace between the lower ones must likewise be of that width, so it can enter there. Ran disagrees: any tefach between the lower beams suffices, as the Gemara says merely 'there is not a tefach between them,' without requiring the measure of the upper beams. This is the meaning of 'namely, etc.' Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch follow the first view; hence, 'provided that, etc.' See Kesef Mishneh.
