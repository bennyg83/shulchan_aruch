Se'if 5 The edge that descends. The Kesef Mishneh inferred from this language, which is the Rambam's language, that the same applies if the upper roof has more than a handbreadth: there must also be in the air of the lower level that same measure, in accordance with the view of the Ran in his novellae in the name of the "some say." Perhaps he comes also to exclude a case where the width of the upper reed is a handbreadth or more, and between the lower reeds there is more than the width of the upper reed; in that case we do not say chavot rami unless the upper reed would enter exactly between the lower ones, but otherwise not. These are his words. In the name of the Ran in his novellae it is written there that the words of the above "some say" do not appear correct in his eyes. Perhaps this is why the Rema was precise to say, "that is, there is a handbreadth in the air between the lower ones," meaning that we do not require the air to be the entire width of the reed above, but it is enough that there be a handbreadth, so that it is possible to judge one handbreadth of the reed above as chavot rami, in accordance with the Ran's view.
