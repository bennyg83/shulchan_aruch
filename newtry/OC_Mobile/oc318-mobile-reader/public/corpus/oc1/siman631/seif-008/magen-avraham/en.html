And there is one, etc. This is the view of the Rambam. It is explicitly implied there that specifically with loosening, he requires that they be less than four, for if they were four wide, one who sees him sitting beneath them will think that it is permitted to cover with them and will also sit under the house. What the Gemara says, that the ceiling is nullified by this, refers to when they are not four wide, for there is no concern because of onlookers: the onlooker assumes that he originally roofed them for the sake of sukkah, but that it is forbidden because of "You shall make," and not from what was already made, comparable to being under the ceiling of the house. Therefore when he loosens it, it is permitted. See the Ran. But when he removes one from between them, even if they are four wide, it is permitted, for the onlooker sees that he needed to place valid s'chach in between, and he will not come to sit under the ceiling of the house. So it also appears in the Gemara, which says: if it is because of "You shall make" and not from what was already made, one is enough; and if it is because of the decree of ceiling, removing one from between them is enough. This implies that where there is no decree of ceiling but only because of "You shall make," etc., loosening also suffices. So it also appears in the Rambam. This appears clear to me according to his view.
