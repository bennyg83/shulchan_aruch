And he came. See Turei Zahav, Sukkah 15a. If there had not been a ceiling here, and he merely came to cover with boards four handbreadths wide, it would be forbidden as in siman 629:18 because of the decree of ceiling; and a house is invalid according to everyone because of "You shall make," and not from what was already made, and because it is not recognizable, since he lives there the whole year, as in simanim 635 and 636. But this person nullifies the ceiling and knows that his house is forbidden; therefore it is permitted even with boards that were four handbreadths wide. [There is a scribal error in some editions and the word "not" is missing.] The "some say" is the view of the Rambam, of blessed memory, that even when nullifying a ceiling, this is specifically when it is not four handbreadths wide. The Rosh wrote that this is astonishing, but did not write why. His reason is that if so, the Gemara would not challenge Shmuel there, since it is speaking where they are not four wide. See Ramban, Beis Yosef, and Bach regarding this. As for what the Mechaber wrote, that he should remove one from between them and place valid s'chach and all is valid, see siman 666 in Turei Zahav, letter 3; and with Hashem's help in Magen Avraham this will be explained further.
