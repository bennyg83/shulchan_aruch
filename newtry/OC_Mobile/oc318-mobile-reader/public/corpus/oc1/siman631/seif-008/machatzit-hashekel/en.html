Seif katan 8, "Magen Avraham wrote: and there are," etc. This is Rambam's view. In order to understand Magen Avraham's intent, it is necessary to set out the Gemara and the explanations of the Rishonim as briefly as possible. The Mishnah, Sukkah 14a, states: R' Yehudah permits roofing a sukkah with wooden boards, while R' Meir prohibits it. Rav explains that they dispute boards 4 handbreadths wide. R' Meir applies the gezeirat tikrah, the decree concerning a house ceiling. Rashi explains that since ordinary ceilings are made from boards 4 handbreadths wide, if such boards were permitted as sechach a person might say: what difference is there between roofing my sukkah with these boards and sitting beneath the beams of my house? If the boards are less than 4 handbreadths wide, however, everyone agrees they are valid. Shmuel says that the dispute concerns boards between 3 and 4 handbreadths wide. R' Meir prohibits them because once they are no longer treated as lavud, we decree lest one use an ordinary ceiling. R' Yehudah permits them. If they are 4 handbreadths wide, R' Yehudah too agrees that they are invalid, because 4 handbreadths constitutes an independent halachic place. If they are less than 3 handbreadths wide, even R' Meir agrees there is no decree, because they are treated like ordinary reeds. Most authorities rule like R' Yehudah according to Shmuel: less than 4 handbreadths is valid; 4 handbreadths is invalid. On 15a the Mishnah discusses a roof that has no plaster coating. R' Yehudah says: Beit Shammai require one to loosen the boards and also remove 1 board from between them; Beit Hillel say that either loosening or removing 1 board from between them suffices. R' Meir says that 1 board must be removed and loosening does not help. The Gemara asks: according to Beit Hillel, the requirement is clearly because of taaseh velo min haasui, and therefore either corrective act suffices. But what is Beit Shammai's reason? If it is taaseh velo min haasui, 1 corrective act should suffice. If it is gezeirat tikrah, removing 1 board from between should suffice. The Gemara answers that Beit Shammai's reason is indeed gezeirat tikrah, and their statement means that even if he loosened the boards, he must still remove 1 from between them. R' Meir holds that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel did not dispute this and that all require removing 1 board. The Gemara then asks that the dispute between R' Meir and R' Yehudah again appears to turn on gezeirat tikrah, although they already disputed that in the earlier Mishnah concerning boards. It answers that in the roof Mishnah R' Yehudah is arguing with R' Meir according to R' Meir's own premises: your gezeirat tikrah follows Beit Shammai, whereas Beit Hillel do not accept such a decree. R' Meir answers that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel did not dispute the decree and both require removal of 1 board. According to Rav, who says that R' Meir and R' Yehudah dispute even boards 4 handbreadths wide, the Mishnah is readily understood. R' Yehudah can hold that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel dispute gezeirat tikrah even regarding boards 4 handbreadths wide, which Rashi says are the ordinary width of ceiling boards. According to Beit Hillel, who do not apply the decree, merely loosening them is enough to correct taaseh velo min haasui. According to Shmuel, however, R' Yehudah himself agrees that boards 4 handbreadths wide are subject to gezeirat tikrah. Why, then, would Beit Hillel permit merely loosening an existing roof? The Gemara answers that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel dispute bittul tikrah, canceling the status of an existing roof. Rashi explains that according to Shmuel the Mishnah indeed concerns boards 4 handbreadths wide. Everyone agrees that if one initially comes to use such boards as sechach they are forbidden because of gezeirat tikrah. Where the boards already constituted a roof and the owner now loosens them, however, he plainly knows that sitting under a house roof is forbidden. Beit Hillel therefore hold that gezeirat tikrah no longer applies and loosening alone suffices. This is Rashi's approach and the first opinion cited by the Shulchan Aruch: even boards 4 handbreadths wide become valid if he loosens them or removes 1 from between them. Rosh and Tur agree. The "some say" brought by the Shulchan Aruch is Rambam's view. The early authorities already questioned Rambam. Bach explains that Rambam was troubled by Rashi's question: how can removing 1 board from between suffice when each remaining board is 4 handbreadths wide, and invalid sechach 4 handbreadths wide in the middle of a sukkah invalidates it? Bach therefore explains Rambam as establishing the case with boards less than 4 handbreadths wide. The roof has 2 defects: taaseh velo min haasui, because it was made as a house ceiling, and gezeirat tikrah. Beit Shammai hold that it leaves its invalid status only through the more substantial act of removing 1 board from between them; once this is done the remaining boards are valid because none is 4 handbreadths wide. Beit Hillel hold that 1 corrective act suffices, even merely loosening. But Rambam himself writes, chapter 5 of Hilchot Sukkah, law 8: "A roof without plaster... if he loosened the boards... it is valid, provided that no board is 4 handbreadths wide. Likewise, if he removed 1 board from between them and placed valid sechach there for the sake of the sukkah, it is valid." Rambam explicitly places the condition that the boards be less than 4 handbreadths wide only after the remedy of loosening, not after the remedy of removing 1 board. This indicates, contrary to Bach, that where 1 board is removed from between them, the sukkah can be valid even when the remaining boards are 4 handbreadths wide. Magen Avraham therefore understands Rambam to distinguish the 2 remedies: loosening works only when the boards are under 4 handbreadths, but removing 1 from between can work even where they are 4 handbreadths. Rambam's reason comes from the Gemara's conclusion that Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel dispute bittul tikrah. The old roof has 2 problems, taaseh velo min haasui and gezeirat tikrah. Beit Shammai require specifically removing 1 from between them, while mere loosening is insufficient. Their reason cannot be that taaseh velo min haasui requires a greater corrective act. Earlier the Gemara explicitly treated it as obvious that for taaseh velo min haasui either loosening or removing 1 board suffices, even according to Beit Shammai. The remaining issue must therefore be gezeirat tikrah. Yet if the owner himself loosened the roof, why should gezeirat tikrah remain? He personally knows that it had been an invalid ceiling and that he had to correct it. Rashi uses exactly this point for Beit Hillel. We must therefore say that Beit Shammai are concerned for observers. Other people do not know that the roof had to be loosened. The case must concern boards under 4 handbreadths. Beit Shammai follow their own position that even such boards remain subject to gezeirat tikrah. Therefore, even if an observer assumes the boards were initially placed as sechach, he may wrongly think such roofing is permitted, when according to Beit Shammai it is not. Beit Hillel, however, permit initially roofing with boards under 4 handbreadths. Thus there is no concern for observers: if they assume these boards were placed initially as sechach, they assume something permitted. Loosening is therefore sufficient. But if the boards are 4 handbreadths wide, even Beit Hillel prohibit them because of gezeirat tikrah. Mere loosening cannot help because of observers. An observer who does not know that the boards were loosened may assume that they were initially placed as sechach, and that would be prohibited. Where 1 board is removed from between them and valid sechach is placed in its position, however, Rambam agrees that even 4-handbreadth boards can become valid. In such a case an observer sees the distinctive correction of alternating existing boards with newly placed valid sechach and will not come to sit under an ordinary house ceiling. This is Magen Avraham's intent. Magen Avraham writes that this is also implied by the Gemara: Beit Shammai's objection cannot be taaseh velo min haasui, because mere loosening admittedly suffices for that. At first this sounds obvious. Its purpose, however, is to prove that Beit Shammai's insistence upon removing 1 board must be based on gezeirat tikrah as perceived by observers, since for the owner himself loosening already demonstrates that he knows a house ceiling is invalid. Chemed Moshe challenges Magen Avraham. Even when 1 board is removed from between, perhaps an observer will not know that any board was removed. He may think the sukkah was initially roofed with a 4-handbreadth invalid board beside 4 handbreadths of valid sechach and will conclude that this arrangement is permitted, although Sukkah 18a says that 4 handbreadths of invalid sechach in the middle invalidates a sukkah. In my humble opinion Rama's wording is exact. He explains gezeirat tikrah as a concern that a person will come to sit beneath the roof of a house. Why did he spell this out rather than simply say "there is gezeirat tikrah"? Because the decree is specifically lest one sit beneath an actual house ceiling, which is invalid by Torah law. We do not impose an additional decree lest someone imitate the corrected sukkah and initially roof another sukkah with alternating 4-handbreadth boards and valid sechach. Such a concern would be a decree upon a decree. By Torah law even wide boards are valid sechach; their invalidity is itself only gezeirat tikrah. Actual house roofing is Torah-invalid because it was installed for dwelling. It is proper to decree lest one sit under an actual roof, but not to add a second decree lest one construct another sukkah in a rabbinically invalid manner. This differs from the concern regarding mere loosening, which is not a decree upon a decree, for the observer may simply think that an ordinary ceiling-like arrangement of wide boards itself is permitted and then sit beneath an actual house roof. Accordingly Magen Avraham's explanation stands. Know also that Bach's original difficulty remains: if the remaining boards are each 4 handbreadths wide, why does removing 1 from between help, since invalid sechach 4 handbreadths wide in the middle ordinarily invalidates the sukkah? Those who disagree with Bach must answer as Bach himself explains the position of Rosh and Tur: by removing 1 board from between and inserting valid sechach, the remaining boards themselves lose the disqualifying status of the old roof and become valid.
