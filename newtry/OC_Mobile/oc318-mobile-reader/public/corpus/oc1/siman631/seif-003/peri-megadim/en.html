A handbreadth. See Turei Zahav. When the upper reed is not a handbreadth wide, then we require that there not be three handbreadths between the upper and the lower, so that it is like lavud, and when the sun is in the middle of the sky the shade will be greater. But when the upper reed is a handbreadth wide, it is considered a tent, and by a law given to Moshe at Sinai we say chavot rami, as though the upper one is placed between the two lower reeds, even though the upper one is higher by more than three handbreadths. This is provided that there is a distance between the two lower reeds of one handbreadth width, the measure within which the upper one could be laid between them. See the Gemara, Sukkah 22a, according to Rashi's explanation there.
