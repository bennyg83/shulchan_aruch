8) [Seif 3] So that the large stars can be seen through it. This refers to stars that are visible during the day. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rambam. So too wrote the Kesef Mishnah, chapter 5, in explaining the Rambam's words, that his reference to the large stars means those visible during the day; see there. So too wrote Biur HaGra. The Bach, however, writes that ideally even the stars visible only at night must be seen. So too wrote the Taz, s.k. 2; Magen Avraham, s.k. 1; and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 5.
9) Ibid. So that the stars can be seen through it, etc. In Derashos Maharil, Hilchos Sukkah, it is written that his teacher, Maharam Shalom, would place the s'chach very thickly, because it is impossible to place so much s'chach that no opening through the roof will be visible from within at any point. Even if one cannot see through it at night, during the day several holes and cracks can nevertheless be seen in many places, end quote. His words were cited by Darkei Moshe, os 2. See Bikkurei Yaakov, who writes that one who is lenient in accordance with this view has not lost. His words were cited by Mishna Berurah, os 5. See below, os 12.
10) Ibid. If it was as thick as a house, etc. This implies that even if he made it extremely thick, so that rain cannot penetrate it, it is valid. Birkei Yosef, os 2. So too wrote Maamar Mordechai, os 1, and the Beit Yosef in the name of the Mordechai, who wrote this in the name of Rashi. So too wrote the Bach in explanation of the Tur's words. Rabbeinu Tam, however, ruled that if he made it so thick that rain cannot penetrate it, it is invalid. His words were cited by the Tur and the Beit Yosef. The Levush writes that one should indeed be stringent, and the Bach in siman 635 likewise rules in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam; see there. His words were cited by Magen Avraham, s.k. 2. So too ruled the Taz, siman 635, s.k. 1; Eliyah Rabbah, os 4; Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 5; Chayei Adam, klal 146, os 18; and Mishna Berurah, os 6. If one removes some of the s'chach in a manner that allows the rain to fall into the sukkah, it is valid, and he need not undo all the s'chach from the beginning. Eliyah Rabbah, ibid.; Eshel Avraham, os 2. Nevertheless, it appears that after the fact, where for some reason it is impossible to remove some of the s'chach, one may rely upon the authorities who validate it. Birkei Yosef, os 2; Shaarei Teshuvah, os 3; Mishna Berurah, ibid. Beit HaShoevah, os 6, however, disputes the aforementioned words of Birkei Yosef and writes that it is invalid even after the fact; see there. I do not know what basis there is to dispute this, since Birkei Yosef writes about a case in which it is impossible to alter it. Is it better that one sit without a sukkah than sit in this sukkah and fulfill the mitzvah according to the opinion that validates it? Rather, he should have said that when alteration is impossible, one should sit in it but not recite a blessing, because of the principle that in cases of doubt concerning blessings we are lenient.
