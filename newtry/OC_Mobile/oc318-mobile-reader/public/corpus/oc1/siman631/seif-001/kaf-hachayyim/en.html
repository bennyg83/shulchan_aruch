1) [Seif 1] A sukkah whose sunlight and shade are equal when measured above is invalid, etc. This means that if the space between each reed and the next is exactly the width of a reed, even precisely so, it is invalid. Rashi, Sukkah 22b, whose words were cited by the Beit Yosef.
2) Ibid. A sukkah whose sunlight and shade are equal when measured above is invalid, etc. This follows Rashi's explanation, with which the Ran and the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchos Sukkah, agree. So too ruled the Rif, and this is also the Rambam's view there in chapter 5, as explained in the Beit Yosef; see there. The Shulchan Aruch therefore ruled accordingly, as did the Levush and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, os 2 and os 3. However, the Baal HaIttur writes the opposite: If they are equal above, it is valid, because below its shade is certainly greater than its sunlight, since less sunlight reaches below. If they are equal below, then above its sunlight is greater, and it is invalid, end quote. His words were cited by the Rosh and the Tur. Rabbeinu Tam explains that if one measures the open space from above and they appear equal, it is valid, because the solid s'chach is actually equal to the open space. But if one stands below on the ground, looks upward, and they appear equal, it is invalid, because an opening that is as broad as a zuz appears to him as small as an istira due to its distance from him; see there. The Rosh and the Ran, however, challenged Rabbeinu Tam's words, as explained in the Beit Yosef; see there. The Bach writes that as a matter of halachah one should be stringent in accordance with both Rashi and the Baal HaIttur. Therefore, whether they are equal above or equal below, it is invalid. One should also follow Rabbeinu Tam's stringency, and therefore, when one stands below and looks upward, even if it appears to him that the shade is greater, it is invalid, end quote. His words were cited by Ateres Zekeinim; Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahos Beit Yosef, os 1; Olas Shabbos, os 1; and Eliyah Rabbah, os 1. See Bigdei Yesha, who writes that Rashi also agrees with Rabbeinu Tam on this point, that one may not stand below and measure above, and this is forbidden according to all opinions; see there. This applies specifically where there is only a slight difference between the s'chach and the open space, in which case one may not stand below and measure above because we are concerned that he may err. But where it is plainly visible to everyone that the s'chach is greater than the open space, it is obvious that he may stand below inside the sukkah and observe whether the s'chach is sufficiently greater than the open space, without any doubt.
3) Ibid. And below its sunlight will be greater, etc. The minority of s'chach is then nullified to the majority of open space, and it is regarded as though he had not covered it with s'chach at all. If the sunlight and shade are equal when measured below, it is valid, because it is known that above, the area occupied by the reeds is wider than the open space. Rashi, Sukkah 22b; Mishna Berurah, os 1.
