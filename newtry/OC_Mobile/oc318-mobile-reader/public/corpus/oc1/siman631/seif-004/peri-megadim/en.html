When the sun. See Turei Zahav, who was precise from Tosafos' language, "if these were lying down," and they did not write that the Mishnah teaches us that we view them as though they are lying together. This indicates that the matter is not definite, for sometimes even if these were lying together, it is possible that the sunlight would be greater than the shade. If so, why does Shmuel not also explain like Rav: one thing is taught, a disordered sukkah, one reed up and one reed down, and the condition is that its shade would be greater if they were lying together; it is valid. See there, page 22a. I did not understand how this could be depicted. If, were they lying evenly, the sunlight would be greater, and if the roofing is in this condition, it is obvious that every sukkah requires its shade to be greater. According to Rav, he teaches us as written in Chiddushei HaRan, so that you should not say perhaps it is too thin and he did not have it in mind. But according to Shmuel, this does not depend on that. Now lavud does not require that the upper reed enter into the air, for it does not need lavud lying on them; that is fine. But with more than three handbreadths, where chavot rami is required, there must be air below so that the upper one could lie below between the air, and if there is more air it is also valid. When the upper one does not have a handbreadth, less than three is required, with the diagonal; understand. See Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura; it implies that he explains Rav as one matter, etc., one law: whenever its shade is greater by any amount it is valid, and Shmuel's case of one reed up also refers to where its shade is greater, and it is a condition, like the question of the Turei Zahav. See there; there is no need to elaborate.
