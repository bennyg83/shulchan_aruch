Seif 2, To repair. For even on Chol HaMoed an unskilled person may sew in his ordinary manner, Moed Katan 8, specifically for the needs of the festival, as stated by Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, siman 541, seif 5.
The same applies to garments. This too is permitted on Chol HaMoed, as stated there on 12a: “Rav Chama permitted,” etc.; and as the Rosh writes there, regarding labors permitted on Chol HaMoed, such as work needed for the festival and work performed in an unskilled manner.
If he is. As stated above.
And he does not receive. As stated above regarding permitting him, etc.; and as stated in siman 542, seif 1.
The same applies to one who. It states there: “One may not write books,” etc., but one may write tefillin and mezuzot for himself, etc. Although at first glance it appears that books may not be written at all, Rambam refers specifically to books that he does not need to read during the festival, such as the Temple scroll. If he needs them, it is permitted, like tefillin and mezuzot, as stated in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 85, and in Shulchan Aruch, siman 545, seif 2.
But it is forbidden. As stated above. Although writing tefillin and mezuzot is permitted, that is only where, according to Rambam, he has nothing to eat, or, according to Rashi and the Rosh, where it is for additional profit.
And one may take a haircut. This requires study, for that which is forbidden on the eve of Pesach is forbidden on Friday, and the Gemara distinguished only between Minchah and midday; yet on the eve of Pesach haircutting is forbidden, as stated regarding the three trades, etc. Kol Bo writes that unskilled work is permitted for haircutting, since it is no worse than an unskilled person sewing in his ordinary manner. But Rama’s language implies that it is permitted in every manner. Moreover, it appears that it is permitted even for payment, whereas even unskilled work is forbidden for payment, as stated above. Kol Bo itself also requires study, since haircutting, even when performed unskillfully, is forbidden. The Gemara says there: “Tailors, because an ordinary person can sew,” etc., and “barbers, because,” etc.; it does not also say regarding barbers that an ordinary person can do it. This is also the meaning of Moed Katan 14a: “All other persons are forbidden. What is the reason?” If only skilled work were forbidden, the Gemara would not have asked the reason, since it would be like every other skilled labor.
