2) There: One who performs labor on Friday, etc. Commerce, however, is permitted. Magen Avraham, paragraph 1. Eliyah Rabbah, paragraph 1, questions these words of Magen Avraham and maintains that labor and commerce are equivalent in this regard; see there. This also appears to be the view of Machazik Berachah and Nehar Shalom, paragraph 1; see there. Tosefes Shabbos and Shulchan Aruch HaRav, paragraph 3, however, agree with Magen Avraham that commerce is permitted; see there. Knesses HaGedolah, siman 696, Hagahos Beis Yosef, paragraph 5, similarly writes that it is an everyday occurrence for people to engage in commerce on Friday and on the day preceding Yom Tov until sunset, and no one objects; see there. Nevertheless, it appears that even according to those who permit it, one should cease while it is still daytime, allowing enough time to prepare the Shabbos needs that must be prepared that evening, to wash and immerse, and to change his clothing, so that he can accept Shabbos while it is still daytime, in purity and fine clothing. If the Sages said that one should reduce his Torah study on Friday in order to prepare the needs of Shabbos, as stated below at the end of seif 2 in the gloss, all the more so must one reduce his commerce in honor of Shabbos. One who trusts in Hashem will be surrounded by kindness. See further below, siman 256, and our words there, with the help of Heaven.
