A Jewish barber. This is specifically unskilled work, for it is no less permissible than Chol HaMoed, when an ordinary person may sew in his usual manner, as stated by Kol Bo. The prevalent custom, however, permits even skilled work. Beit Yosef writes: whenever the law is uncertain, follow the custom. It appears that the basis for the leniency is that, since the haircut is for Shabbat, it is permitted even for payment. Although one may not take payment for repairing garments even for Shabbat, haircutting is different because everyone can see that it is done for Shabbat, whereas on the eve of Pesach even haircutting is forbidden, as stated in siman 468. Shaving a non-Jew, however, is forbidden even when it is unskilled work; see siman 531, seif katan 8, for on Chol HaMoed unskilled labor is permitted only for the needs of the festival, as stated in siman 541. It may nevertheless be argued that Friday is more lenient and unskilled labor is permitted. See siman 468:5, which implies that the prohibition there applies to skilled work; if it were unskilled work, it would be permitted even during the festival when performed for the festival's needs. According to Kol Bo, it is permitted even for payment, as Beit Yosef writes in siman 541. It is strained to say that siman 468 discusses cutting the hair of one who does not need it for the festival, since all the authorities state that it concerns the festival's needs. It follows that all labors permitted on Chol HaMoed are permitted on Friday, and this is reasonable even when payment is taken, for without payment haircutting is obviously permitted because it is a need of Shabbat. Darchei Moshe likewise writes in siman 545:5. The writings of the Arizal state that he was careful not to cut his hair after Minchah even on Friday; see siman 232.

Seder HaYom writes that one who regularly studies a fixed amount, or studies with a group, should not cancel it. A yeshivah session is considered without a fixed limit because participants are accustomed to engage in extended dialectical discussion, unless they are studying Rashi on the weekly portion, which is permitted. If he will later be unable to purchase his needs, he should not establish the study session in the morning. If he will be unable to purchase them after prayer, he should purchase first and pray afterward, because this is a passing mitzvah and is itself a Torah obligation, provided that he first recites Shema. If the congregation is praying, he should not separate from them. If he prepared on Thursday, he may study in his usual manner.
