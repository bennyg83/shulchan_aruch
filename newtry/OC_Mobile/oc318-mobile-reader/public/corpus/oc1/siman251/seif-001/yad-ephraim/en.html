Siman 251, Magen Avraham, seif katan 1 ‘This is also implied at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, etc., when the city was made to cease.’ His intent is to prove that commerce is necessarily not included in the category of labor. The Baraisa states that the first blast caused laborers in the fields to cease, as explained below in seif katan 4, while the second caused the city and its shops to cease. Why was it necessary to mention the shops separately? Had commerce been included in the cessation of labor, the shops would already have been encompassed by the word ‘city,’ just as commerce would be included in the statement concerning one who performs labor and does not see a sign of blessing. Rather, commerce is not included in that category, and the shops therefore had to be mentioned separately. It consequently follows that commerce was forbidden only from the second blast onward. This applied specifically when Israel dwelled in its settled communities and these blasts were sounded. Today, when the blasts have ceased, commerce is forbidden only close to Shabbos. Nor should one refrain from it because it lacks a sign of blessing, since it is not included in the category of ‘one who performs labor.’ This also resolves the apparent contradiction between the Magen Avraham’s words here and those in seif katan 4.
