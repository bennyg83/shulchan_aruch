Minchah Gedolah. This means six and one-half hours into the day. This requires study, for the Gemara on Shabbat 35b states that the second blast was sounded to cause the city to cease labor and the shops to close, while the Tur in siman 256 writes that the first blast was sounded at Minchah. Even assuming that the first blast was at Minchah Gedolah, the second occurred considerably later, showing that labor continued afterward. A further difficulty arises from Choshen Mishpat 331, where the Tur cites the Yerushalmi that a laborer must work for his employer until close enough to sunset to allow time to roast a small fish, namely until the third blast. It appears there that he must remain in the field until then, whereas the Gemara states that at the first blast those working in the fields entered the city. Perhaps the Yerushalmi states the strict law governing the laborer's obligation, but because one who continues working does not see a sign of blessing, the Sages instituted the blasts so that labor would cease earlier. The first difficulty nevertheless remains according to the opinion of Minchah Gedolah. I therefore agree with the view that the relevant time is Minchah Ketanah.
