Melachah, etc. Commercial business is permitted, as appears from the incident of the people of Beishan and from the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, where the second shofar blast caused the city and its shops to cease. Magen Avraham says so. This is surprising, because in seif katan 4 he himself cites that blast as stopping the city from melachah and closing its shops, and Rashi on Shabbos 35 explains the same. Thus the two appear equal.
He does not, etc. Rabbenu Yonah, page 66, says he sees no sign of blessing from it, implying specifically from that melachah. This supports Bach at the beginning of siman 468. Kol Bo at the beginning of siman 31 writes that one who performs melachah then stands under the ban of the Sages, explaining "ban" as a term of death because anyone who violates the words of the Sages is liable to death. This is surprising, because at the beginning of chapter Makom Shenahagu the Gemara says only that he sees no blessing, but we do not place him under a ban, and Rabbenu Yonah says likewise. Perhaps Kol Bo follows the Gemara's first answer there, which understands the period after Minchah as subject to a ban. But the flow of the Gemara does not imply this; even according to the first answer, it appears the rule remains that no ban is imposed, and Agudah implies so. Moreover, at the beginning of siman 58 Kol Bo himself says that one who works sees no blessing, is rebuked, and is forcibly stopped, but is neither flogged with mardut nor placed under nidui; Semag, positive commandment 75, says likewise. It may be that Kol Bo holds that although no formal nidui is imposed, the Sages' declaration that he will see no blessing itself places him under their curse, which explains his precise phrase "stands under the Sages' ban." This requires study. Beit Yosef and the later authorities did not discuss this point.
