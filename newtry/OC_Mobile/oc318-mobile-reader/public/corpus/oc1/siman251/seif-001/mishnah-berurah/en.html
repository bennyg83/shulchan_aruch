(1) One who performs labor. Commerce is permitted. Nevertheless, the Gemara implies that shops must close before the time for lighting the Shabbat lamps. See Biur Halachah and Mishnah Berurah below, siman 256, seif katan 1.
(2) A sign of blessing. From that labor, for even if he profits in one place, he will suffer a loss elsewhere.
(3) Minchah Gedolah. This is six and one-half seasonal hours into the day; Minchah Ketanah is nine and one-half seasonal hours. One who relies upon the lenient opinion has not suffered a loss. All these hours are proportional hours. This applies when one performs his own labor. A worker hired by an employer without a specified end time must work until close to sunset, as stated in Choshen Mishpat 331, leaving only enough time to return home, fill a barrel of water, roast a small fish, and light a lamp, unless there is an established local custom to stop earlier. See Magen Avraham and Tosefet Shabbat.
(4) Casually. Even for payment.
