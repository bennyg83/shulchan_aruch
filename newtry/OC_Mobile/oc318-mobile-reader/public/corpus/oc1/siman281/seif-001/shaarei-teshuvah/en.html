Ba'er Heitev: and it is written in Be'er Heitev in the name of "Michtam l'David" — if he forgot to say Nishmas and said Yishtabach, like weekday he should say Nishmas after Yishtabach and not after the prayer, see there. And in Shulchan Melachim it is written that if he was late coming to the synagogue, he should skip Nishmas and not Pesukei deZimra — only regarding the psalms they add on Shabbat; even though they are also elevated in importance, nevertheless Nishmas is preferable, see there. And see in Shulchan Hatov the precision of words in zemirot — the word "l'yesharim na'eh" — initially the nun is with kamatz, and the aleph is resting; but "l'veitcha na'eh kodesh" — the chet is with patach and the aleph moves with patach. For "na'im na'eh tehillah" — the nun is kamatz and the aleph resting. And it is written in the name of Avudraham in the psalm "Ranenu yir'u laHashem kol ha'aretz" — with two yods at the beginning "yir'u et Heaven k'doshav" — the reish should be sheva and the aleph resting, to distinguish between it and "yir'u" from the root "ra'ah," see Radak. "Kol tov hati'ta" — with cholam; "v'lo yeivoshu" — the aleph with sheva resting and the shin with sheva. "V'yehi no'am Heaven" — in precise old books it is "b'adonai." "Me'adam ad beheimah" — in some books it is written "v'ad" with vav, and it is an error. And similarly in some it is written "v'nasan artzam l'nachalah" — scribal error, and it should be "nachalah"; only below in Kitzur Lechem Chayyim it is "l'nachalah." In the text of Nishmas "u'v'riyosav" — the bet is dagesh and sheva, and the reish with chirik. What is written in siddurim "v'yashuru" — correct to skip it. And "v'alei v'sova" — only "v'sova" — the shin and bet are kamatz, see there. And see in Yad Ephraim that those accustomed to say "v'chol ayin lecha tetzapeh" say "hatyu" with sheva and "hatzaddik" with patach, and it is an error — it should be "hatyu" with chirik and "hatzaddik" with sheva, for it is not from the language "aroch lecha v'etzapeh" which is matter of hope and expectation, only from the language of observation, as "einav baGoyim titzpenah," and many such, see there. And it is written in Mahari Tzach that "da'at v'techuna sovevim hodo" and not "oto." And see in Gan HaMelech siman 52 — there is a version "mal'achei sharat v'ofanim v'chayot hakodesh," and Avudraham ruled so; and he too, may his memory be blessed, sided with this version, for it is gradual from level to level; but in Tikunei Zohar the version is "mal'achei sharat v'chayot v'ofanei hakodesh," and the acceptance of Rashbi will decide, see there. And see in responsa Meir Netivim siman 6 who wrote in the name of Gan HaMelech the opposite — and it is not so, for he concluded "v'kabbalat Rashbi tokhia," see there; and possibly his intent is on what he wrote to side from his own reasoning, see there who wrote that both are words of the holy Ari, and one should not confuse the other version, see there.
