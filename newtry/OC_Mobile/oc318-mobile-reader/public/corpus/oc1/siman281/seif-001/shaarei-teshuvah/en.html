Birkei Yosef writes in the name of Michtam LeDavid that if one forgot to recite Nishmas and said Yishtabach as on a weekday, he should recite Nishmas after Yishtabach, not after the prayer. See there. Shulchan Melachim writes that if one arrived late at shul, he should omit Nishmas before omitting Pesukei DeZimrah. This applies only relative to the additional Tehillim recited on Shabbos. Although those Tehillim are also of elevated importance, Nishmas takes precedence. See there. See Shulchan HaTahor concerning the precise pronunciation of words in the zemiros. In the phrase “laYesharim naavah,” the nun has a kamatz and the alef is quiescent. In “leVeischa naavah kodesh,” the nun has a patach and the alef is sounded with a patach. In “ki naim naavah tehillah,” the nun has a kamatz and the alef is quiescent. He cites Avudraham concerning the chapter of Tehillim Ranenu. The phrase “yir’u meiHashem kol haaretz” is written with two yuds at the beginning. In “yir’u es Hashem kedoshav,” the reish should have a sheva and the alef should be quiescent, to distinguish it from yir’u derived from the root meaning to see. See Radak. In “kol tuv hita,” the tes has a cholam. In “velo ye’ashmu,” the alef has a silent sheva and the shin has a sounded sheva. In carefully written old texts, “Vihi noam Hashem” is written with the Divine Name Adonai. In “me’adam ad behemah,” some texts write ve’ad with a vav, but this is an error. Likewise, some texts read “venasan artzam lenachalah,” but this is a scribal error, and it should read “nachalah.” Only later, in Ki LeOlam Chasdo, does it read “lenachalah.” In the text of Nishmas, the word “uvriyosav” has a dagesh and sheva in the beis, and a chirik in the reish. The word “veyashoreru,” which appears in some siddurim, is properly omitted. One should not say “uvesova,” but “uveseva,” with both the shin and beis bearing a kamatz. See there. Yad Ephraim writes that those accustomed to say “vechol ayin lecha tetzapeh” pronounce the tav with a sheva and the tzadi with a patach, but this is an error. The tav should have a chirik and the tzadi a sheva. It is not from the expression “I will arrange before You and await,” which denotes hope and expectation, but from the language of looking, as in “His eyes behold the nations,” and many similar verses. See there. Mahari Tzemach writes that the words “knowledge and understanding surround” refer to “His glory,” and not to “Him.” Gan HaMelech, siman 52, writes that one version reads “serafim, ofanim, and holy chayos.” Avudraham also rules this way, and he himself favored it because it proceeds gradually from level to level. In Tikkunei Zohar, however, the version is “serafim, chayos, and holy ofanim,” and the received tradition of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai decides the matter. See there. Responsa Meir Nesivim, siman 6, cites Gan HaMelech in the reverse, but this is incorrect, since Gan HaMelech concludes that the received tradition of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai decides the matter. Perhaps Meir Nesivim intended only the version Gan HaMelech favored through his own reasoning. See there, where he writes that both versions are words of the living God and one should not corrupt the alternative text. Shulchan HaTahor further writes in the name of the Rav, of blessed memory, that one should not say “a remembrance of the act of Creation” in Shacharis, but only in Maariv and Mussaf. See there.
