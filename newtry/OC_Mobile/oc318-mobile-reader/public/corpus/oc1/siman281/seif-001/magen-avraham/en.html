(א) There are places where they say "Hu rachum v'chanun," etc., in Pesukei deZimra, except on Yom Kippur when they say it (so is the law, siman 238): From weekday. But nevertheless they hurry to come more than on Yom Tov in order to read Shema at its time — see siman 529:

(ב) And on Shabbat day. The Acharonim raised a difficulty: is not this verse written in the Mussaf parashah, and from where do we derive [reading it] after Shacharit? And they were greatly pressed, and I omitted the words of Tosafos in Berachot daf 28 who wrote that the Mussaf offerings were slaughtered with the tamid and burned with it — see there; and this is what is written regarding Mussaf "all day." R' Shimshon, may his memory be blessed, raised a difficulty, for we say in chapter five of Zevachim "Mussaf at six, in the pans at seven," and you will say this is l'chatchila, but b'dieved every day is also its time; behold in Temurah it implies that the Mussaf offerings were slaughtered with the tamid and burned with it. And Tosafos explain that there it deals with Shabbat, end of his words. And it appears to me that it is a scribal error and it should read "behold in Yoma it implies," etc., and that is there daf 70, for we rule the halacha is like R' Akiva, and he derives it from what is written "on the festivals, besides the morning olah which is the tamid olah," and we derive "yihyu kahem" from them; if so, how did he say Mussaf at six, for the tamid was offered at the latest at four? Rather, perforce he speaks b'dieved that it is valid at six; if so, how did he say all day? And one may say that it deals with Shabbat — meaning, and that which "Mussaf at six" deals with Shabbat when we do not derive from festivals, and the same applies to Rosh Chodesh, and therefore they offer at six; but in truth b'dieved Mussaf is valid all day as is found in chapter 2 of Temurah; and the words of Maharsha here require further study. If so, here too, since it is written in Mussaf "uv'yom" which implies delay, as Rashi wrote in Pesachim chapter 88 — if so, Mussaf at six, and we pray Mussaf after Shacharit; therefore it is a mitzva after Shacharit, as above. And Bach wrote the main reason is because of sleep, which is oneg Shabbat, and they relied on a verse, end of his words; if so, in winter days it is unnecessary:

(ג) They return him. It is written in Hagahot Maimoniyot, and I wonder, for in Toldot Adam he need not return it, end of his words. And it appears to us that even Or Zarua did not say that one should return to Yotzer Or, for from where would he return for something not mentioned in the Talmud, as is written siman 582 seif 5; and furthermore it is only a custom — rather, the intent is that they should still say it after the prayer; unlike other zemirot which are only optional — if he wishes, he need not say them at all. And see siman 60 what he wrote, and siman 59:

(ד) And to make them pleasant in zemirot. Specifically with a melody that has sweetness, as is done in Pesukei deZimra; and the ancients already spoke at length in condemnation of cantors who prolong and separate a letter from its companion and a word from its companion:
