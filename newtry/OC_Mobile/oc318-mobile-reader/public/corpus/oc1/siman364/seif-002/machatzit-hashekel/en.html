(s.k. 2) A public domain, etc. And likewise in Tosafos who say there: the sages taught — how does one make an eruv for a public domain? One makes a doorframe shape from here and a lechi or beam from here, etc.; Chananya says, etc.; and in Beit Hillel: a door from here and a lechi or beam from here; and they challenge: but does one make an eruv in a public domain? Did not Rav Bibi say in the name of R' Yochanan of Jerusalem: were it not for its doors being locked at night, they would be liable for it on account of public domain; R' Yitzchak said: this is what he means — how does one make an eruv for alleyways pass-through to a public domain? One makes a doorframe shape, etc.; and we hold like the first Tanna that a doorframe shape suffices, and the same law is explained in seif 1; and this seif originates from the difficulty of those who ask: but does one make an eruv in a public domain? And Rashi explained that the difficulty is: according to Rav Bibi in the name of R' Yochanan, like Chananya according to Beit Hillel — behold, one door suffices; why did he say 'were its doors locked,' implying that we require doors on both sides? Tosafos wrote: this itself is the reason why we require two doors — since there is one door on one side, behold it has three partitions; why does the fourth side not suffice with a lechi or beam? And they answered: since three partitions are not a complete partition except through a door, it is deficient, and we require also on the second side a door. And to R' Yitzchak it appears that he challenges from the requirement of locking a door, and for Beit Hillel a door suffices even if not locked, for Beit Hillel did not mention that locking is required; and from R' Yitzchak's explanation that the difficulty is from locking a door, Rashi's explanation is not satisfactory; therefore he holds like their difficulty, that in truth one door suffices, as Rashba holds; and that which he said 'were its doors locked,' etc., is not precise — unlike Rashi.
