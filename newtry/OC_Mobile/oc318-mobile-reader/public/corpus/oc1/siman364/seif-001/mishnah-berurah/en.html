(1) A pass-through alleyway, etc. — and it deals with when that alleyway was not in the width of a public domain; and see above siman 345 seif 8 and Taz, and in Mishna Berurah there.

(2) One requires a doorframe shape, etc. — and the reason is that through a doorframe shape it becomes as closed in the partition from that side and is like an alleyway closed on three sides, and it suffices on the second side with a lechi or beam.

(3) And its other side to a courtyard, etc. — for since the courtyard has partitions, the alleyway is not pass-through and one does not need a doorframe shape, but only a lechi or beam in order to separate between the courtyard residents and the alleyway so they do not forbid one another; and some are stringent that specifically a lechi is required on the courtyard side and not a beam; and all this is when the courtyard is not accustomed to this alleyway — such as when it has an opening to another alleyway; but if it is accustomed to it already, even a doorframe shape and doors do not help to permit there unless this alleyway participated with the other courtyards open to it; and if it participated in it, one need not make an eruv at all, as written in siman 387.

(4) But if it is not, etc. — meaning, when the alleyway does not have the conditions explained above, its law is only like a courtyard; and therefore even if it was pass-through on both sides to a public domain and many enter through that courtyard to the public domain, one needs only a courtyard arrangement on both sides — meaning on whichever side, two posts or a post four amot wide.
