Seif 2 — a public domain itself is permitted only with doors, and they are locked at night. N.B. The source of this law is in the first chapter of Eruvin daf 6: R' Yochanan of Jerusalem [said]: were it not for its doors being locked at night, etc. And I saw in Tosafos in Bava Metzia chapter 4 daf 53b s.v. likewise, that the partition fell — and these are his words: the explanation is: most of the partitions — for if part of it fell, even though it is forbidden to carry on Shabbos and Yom Tov, nevertheless it is called a partition for eating maaser and kodshim kalim, as we say in the first chapter of These Matters — that sugya of Hillel needed to erect after breaches opened in it; therefore they could not bring their knives in the ten to slaughter their Pesach offerings — for before they breached they were permitted to carry, as R' Yochanan of Jerusalem [said]: were it not for its doors being locked at night they would be liable on account of public domain; nevertheless at night they ate their Pesach offerings — end of his words. And behold, at first glance I did not merit to understand their words — from where do we know that breaches opened there? Perhaps when even a minority breached, it is forbidden to eat kodshim there; and in Hillel's act one can say that nothing breached, only the doors were removed and were not locked at night — therefore it was forbidden to carry the knives there. And further, what proof from what R' Yochanan of Jerusalem said 'were its doors locked,' etc., 'they would be liable on account of public domain'? One can say: even when its doors are locked it helps to exempt it from the Torah law of public domain; still one can say rabbinically it was forbidden to carry, for we judge it like a karmelit, or because they did not make eruv chatzerot and alleyway partnerships there and were forbidden to carry on that account; and behold there in Pesachim the Gemara establishes it otherwise — regarding shevus d'rabbanan standing before him and they have in their hand to uproot it as if by backhand — if so, one can say that the prohibition of carrying itself was only d'rabbanan and not from the Torah; and even if we strain that from the language of the Gemara in Eruvin it implies that even d'rabbanan it is permitted to carry in Jerusalem, and even if we strain that he holds that if there were no doors, since it is considered public domain for Shabbos — and it is proven that partitions are nullified — it is fit to say that also for kodshim; nevertheless, in the end it is proven that the law of Shabbos and kodshim is not equal, and what is breached for Shabbos is closed for kodshim; still there is no place for their difficulty according to what the poskim wrote here, and brought in Shulchan Aruch for one view, that even if they are not locked at night, since they are fit to lock it is also a private domain; specifically if sunken in dirt so they cannot be locked, it is a public domain. And behold, what is sunken in dirt and not fit to lock is only d'rabbanan — for from the Torah one can remove the dirt also on Shabbos, for there is no d'oraisa melacha here, for behold it is from melacha she'eina tzricha legufa; only to lock the doors, and it is like digging a pit, and one needs only to cover it; and that which they are liable from the Torah is because rape in a rabbinic enactment is not worse than other rape — behold if another rape occurred whereby they could not lock, they would be liable; and if so, here too, since in any case there was rape in eruv chatzerot and we know he has nothing with which to lock, this is not worse than other rape — therefore they are liable from the Torah; and similarly wrote the poskim, and Magen Avraham brought them siman 445 regarding bal yeira'eh — since there is rape in a rabbinic enactment of Chazal that he cannot destroy it, he is exempt from the Torah, see there; and accordingly one can say that in Hillel's days the act was that there were doors there, only they were sunken in dirt and they had nothing with which to lock; therefore regarding bringing the knives, which is before slaughter — since shevus before slaughter we do not push aside on account of Pesach and they are not permitted to move the dirt — therefore it again has the law of public domain and they were forbidden from the Torah to carry the knives; but regarding eating the Pesach, which is after slaughter — and it is explicit in chapter 6 of Pesachim and in Menachot daf 6 that preparatory acts of slaughter after slaughter push aside Shabbos, for slaughter already pushed aside Shabbos, and it is forbidden only preparatory acts of slaughter on erev Pesach — if so, here too regarding eating, which is after slaughter, since slaughter pushed aside Shabbos, all the more so Yom Tov which is lighter than Shabbos — therefore shevus is again pushed aside on account of Pesach, and it is fine to take dirt and lock at night, and there is a partition; therefore even though they did not remove [the dirt], it is considered a partition per the second view here; but to carry the knives, which is before slaughter — for preparatory acts do not push aside Shabbos — it is fine that they were not permitted to carry the knives, since the doors were sunken in dirt; and it comes out well, and examine well; and in particular that the two explicit views here in Shulchan Aruch are the dispute of the Amoraim in the Gemara — one holds we require locked doors and one holds fit to lock suffices. And behold why Tosafos stood on their words in Bava Metzia thus, and did not ask thus in Pesachim — how they ate Pesach there without partitions, and if there were partitions why could they not carry the knives? Perforce in Pesachim it was not difficult for them, for even if partitions fall it is permitted to eat, since in any case there were partitions there at some time, and it suffices for kodshim; and see first chapter of Megillah regarding offering even though there is no house, and in Zevachim, see there, and examine; but according to what they answer in Bava Metzia that the partition fell — and it is proven that when fallen it is forbidden to eat kodshim there — therefore they asked thus: how did they eat Pesach there? And if so, one can say: the one who answers that partitions fell holds like the one who holds that fit to lock suffices; and it deals with when they were sunken in dirt, for the above reason; and the one in Eruvin who holds we require actually locked doors would not understand this sugya in Bava Metzia; rather he holds that even when fallen it is permitted to eat kodshim there, see there, and examine; and it comes out well, there's help. Again I saw in Shitah Mekubetzet chapter 4 of Bava Metzia there explicitly as the words I questioned there — why not eat when partitions fall? Behold we say in chapter 2 of Shevuot that they eat even though there is no wall, and the original sanctity sanctified for the future — and he answered: it follows the view that it did not sanctify for the future, see there; if so, it is explicit as our words; and one can say fine: the one who holds we require actually locked doors holds that it sanctified for the future; and the one who holds it did not sanctify for the future holds that we do not require actually locked doors, as our words above; and examine.
