One requires — Taz: open to a courtyard not combined at all — a beam on both its sides suffices, not like Bach who wrote that on one side we require specifically a lechi — for if so, also pass-through to a combined courtyard; rather it is as closed, only that the uncombined courtyard forbids it — a beam on both its sides suffices; and in Magen Avraham 1 it implies that on the courtyard side one requires specifically a lechi, and a beam on the second side — behold a beam on the courtyard side and a lechi on the public domain side does not help; likewise Bach in the name of Semak, see there; and with God's help it will be explained there; and nevertheless, pass-through to a karpef larger than beit se'atayim not enclosed for residence — all agree one requires on one side a lechi or beam, and on the other side a doorframe shape, even though the karpef is closed on all sides through eruv; and two courtyards pass-through, or more than ten — a lechi or beam does not help, only two posts or a four-amot post; a courtyard is not permitted through an alleyway arrangement, as Rama wrote here; and see siman 372 in Magen Avraham letter yod-dalet, and at the beginning of 363.
