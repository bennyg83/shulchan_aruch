On both sides. A large alleyway going in a straight path, and in its middle there is a kind of large arch like a doorframe shape that interrupts and divides between the lower half of the alleyway and the upper, such that they are like two alleyways open to one another; and furthermore, adjacent to the arch from the side there is a path to another alleyway in which non-Jews and Jews live, and that alleyway divides into two: that which is above the arch exits to a place wider than ten amot; and the complete sage of Salonica asked whether the arch is like an opening for the alleyway, and by making opposite the arch a lechi or beam on one side, this alleyway is corrected with a doorframe shape on one side and a lechi or beam on the other side. However, the other half of the alleyway beyond the arch and above — since a breach wider than ten exits through it, one must close that breach either with building or at least with a doorframe shape, and through this that alleyway too is corrected; but the doorframe shape that permits the large one does not also permit the small one, since the doorframe shape is far from the breach; also the other alleyway that exits on the side of the large one should be permitted by making a doorframe shape in one place and a lechi or beam on the other end; except that the non-Jews forbid them and they must rent from them; see Hasagah on this ruling in Sefer Mishna Berurah 62; and in the end he agreed with the aforementioned sage.
