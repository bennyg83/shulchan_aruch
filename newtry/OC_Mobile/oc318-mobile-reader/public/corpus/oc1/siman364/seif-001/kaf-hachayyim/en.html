1) [Se'if 1] An alleyway open at both ends, etc. This means open to a public domain at both ends when it is not sixteen amos wide. If it is sixteen amos wide, it is itself a complete public domain. Beis Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh, chapter 17. This means that an alleyway open to a public domain at both ends can be rendered permitted by a tzuras hapesach at one end and a lechi or korah at the other only when it is not sixteen amos wide. If it is sixteen amos wide, it requires doors, as stated in the following se'if. The Tur likewise writes that an actual public domain, meaning one sixteen amos wide, extending from gate to gate, with six hundred thousand people passing through it, can be rendered permitted only by doors. Nevertheless, we already wrote above in siman 345, se'if 7, that according to the Shulchan Aruch, it is a public domain even if six hundred thousand people do not pass through it. See there. The Tur writes in the name of Ri, however, that an alleyway thirteen and one-third amos wide whose two ends open to a public domain sixteen amos wide has the law of a public domain and requires doors. Olas Shabbos, subsection 1, and Tosefes Shabbos, subsections 1 and 2, write likewise. We already cited the dispute regarding this above in siman 345, se'ifim 8 and 9. See there. Therefore, initially one should be concerned for the prohibiting authorities, and an alleyway thirteen and one-third amos wide that opens to a public domain should be permitted only by doors, as stated in the following se'if. In pressing circumstances, any alleyway narrower than sixteen amos may be rendered permitted by a tzuras hapesach. The custom to render even an alleyway wider than sixteen amos permitted by a tzuras hapesach relies on the opinion of the Tur and the authorities who agree with him, who maintain that an area is not a public domain unless it is sixteen amos wide and six hundred thousand people pass through it every day. Anything less does not constitute a public domain and can be rendered permitted by a tzuras hapesach, as explained above in siman 345, se'if 7. According to the unqualified ruling of the Shulchan Aruch there, however, any area sixteen amos wide is a public domain even if six hundred thousand people do not pass through it, and it cannot be rendered permitted by a tzuras hapesach, but only by doors, as stated in the following se'if. Therefore, in a place where the custom is to render even an alleyway wider than sixteen amos permitted by a tzuras hapesach because six hundred thousand people do not pass through it, one should not protest, for several great authorities hold this opinion, as explained there. Nevertheless, every person who fears Heaven should be stringent for himself.
2) There. An alleyway open at both ends, etc. Rashba was asked in newly printed responsa, volume 3, siman 271, whether an alleyway containing an arch called a volta has the law of a tzuras hapesach. He answered that if the vertical sides of the arch are ten handbreadths high, all opinions agree that it constitutes a tzuras hapesach, both to obligate affixing a mezuzah and to render the alleyway permitted by virtue of a tzuras hapesach. The author, subsection 1.
3) There. It requires a tzuras hapesach on this side, etc. This is so that the side with the tzuras hapesach is regarded as closed by a partition. It then resembles an alleyway closed on three sides, and a lechi or korah suffices on the other side. Taz, subsection 1.
4) There. And a lechi or korah on this side. Although an alleyway wider than ten amos cannot be rendered permitted by a lechi or korah, as stated in siman 363, se'if 26, Tosafos write that one should make a sufficiently wide lechi to reduce the open space to less than ten amos.
5) There. Into a courtyard that did not establish an eruv, etc. That side is regarded as closed without any rectification. The required rectification serves only as a Rabbinic visual marker, and therefore even a korah on each side suffices. A lechi, which functions as a partition, certainly suffices. Taz, there. All this applies when the courtyard does not regularly use this alleyway, such as when it has an entrance into another alleyway. If it regularly uses this alleyway, however, even a tzuras hapesach and doors do not suffice to permit carrying there unless this alleyway participated in a shituf with the other courtyards that open onto it. If it participated in the shituf, no separate eruv is necessary at all, as stated in siman 387. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1. See our words there, with the help of Heaven.
