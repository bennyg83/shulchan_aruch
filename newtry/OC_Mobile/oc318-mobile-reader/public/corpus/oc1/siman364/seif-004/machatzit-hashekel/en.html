(s.k. 4) Even, etc. — that even when aligned one requires, etc. — meaning, even though the two small alleyways that are aligned have the law of pass-through, for the opening is one opposite the other, nevertheless each of the small ones is like a bent alleyway with the large alleyway.

Not like Beit Yosef — meaning, from what Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote in his long work: the intent of Tosafos who say on daf 8b: it was stated — an alleyway shaped like a nun: Abaye said — one makes a doorframe shape for the large one and all these are permitted with a lechi or beam toward the public domain; Rava said to him: like whom? Like Shmuel, who holds a bent alleyway's law is like closed and does not require arrangement at its bend; if so, why a doorframe shape? And furthermore, behold we hold like Rav that a bent alleyway requires arrangement at its bend; rather Rava said — one makes a doorframe shape for all of them on this side (meaning toward the alleyway side), and the other side is permitted with a lechi or beam; and we hold like Rav. And Rabbeinu Yerucham cited here in Shulchan Aruch the wording of Rambam; and Rabbeinu Yerucham himself inferred from Rambam that Rambam holds that even when aligned one requires a doorframe shape on that side of the large alleyway side; except that he wrote that Tosafos disagree, who wrote, and these are his words: Rabbenu Tam — small alleyways, etc., and they are not one opposite the other, for if so they would be pass-through, etc. — end of his words; and Rabbeinu Yerucham inferred from their wording 'they would be pass-through' that they do not have the law of a bent alleyway — meaning, not like Rambam; but Bach and Magen Avraham do not hold thus: one reason is that it is better to align Tosafos' view with Rambam; and furthermore, otherwise Tosafos with Rambam would be opposite views — for according to Rambam it appears that when aligned it leans more to say that their law is like a bent alleyway, since he wrote the language 'even if the openings are not aligned'; and for Tosafos the law would be the opposite — that when aligned their law is not like bent, but Tosafos' intent is: if aligned, Rava's difficulty is hard — he asks: if like Shmuel, why a doorframe shape? And if when aligned, the large alleyway would not require a doorframe shape, nevertheless the small ones — why does Shmuel not require the law of a bent alleyway? Nevertheless they would be pass-through one opposite the other, and the small ones have the law of a pass-through alleyway and require a doorframe shape; and Rava should not have closed 'why a doorframe shape' but should have said 'why a doorframe shape for the large one' but the small ones require a doorframe shape; or that it deals with when not aligned (and Tosafos concluded that thus are the legs of the nun, not one opposite the other); but for us, who hold like Rava and Rav, who agree that even the small ones when aligned — one can say the law of a bent alleyway.

If there are no paths — meaning, small alleyways.
