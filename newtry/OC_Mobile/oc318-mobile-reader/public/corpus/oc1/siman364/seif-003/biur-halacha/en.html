Its law is as if it were pass-through at the bend — behold in this law there is indeed a dispute among the Rabbis regarding what was said in the Gemara: a bent alleyway — its law is like a pass-through alleyway; the view of Rashi and many other Rishonim [Tosafos, Rabbeinu Yonah, Eshkol, and Or Zarua (I found it somewhat puzzling there that he brought that R' Yitzchak leaned more at the end toward Rashi's view, while in other Rishonim R' Yitzchak's view is cited opposite Rashi's)] and Rosh, Maharam (although in Beit Yosef he wrote that his view is like R' Yitzchak's — one who examines Mordechai will see explicitly that his view is like Rashi's, and Pri Megadim already noted this)] and Ri and Tur] that one requires an arrangement at its bend — whether to make a doorframe shape at the bend and lechayim or a beam on both sides, or doorframe shapes on its sides and a lechi or beam in its middle; and the view of R' Yitzchak and Rashba that one does not require an arrangement at its bend, and its law is like any other pass-through alleyway — one makes a doorframe shape at one head and a lechi or beam at the other end, and at its bend one needs nothing; and Beit Yosef wrote that for halacha we rule like the Tur's unstated view, like Rashi, because Tosafos and Rosh agreed with him; and see in responsum of R' Akiva Eiger siman 33 that the view of R' Yitzchak and Rashba is also not a lone view, for many great ones stand on their view — they are Rambam [also in Or Zarua and Ri it is written that Rambam's view is like R' Yitzchak], Ritva, Rivash, Tashbetz, and Ran [and also from Rosh's girsa on daf 18 that he reads 'they surrounded me' explicitly on its lips, it also implies somewhat that he holds like R' Yitzchak, see there]; see there in R' Akiva Eiger's responsum; and therefore it appears to me that in a time of need, when one cannot make an arrangement at the bend, one can rely on R' Yitzchak's view; and this is even for an alleyway at the time of the Gemara; and especially regarding our alleyway, which has the law of a courtyard because there are no houses and courtyards open into it, as explained above in siman 363 seif 26 in the Rama gloss — certainly one can rely to be lenient per the Acharonim who are lenient [meaning Magen Avraham and other later authorities who stand on his view, as explained above in Shaarei Tzion there, and not like Taz in this siman] that one need not make an arrangement at its bend [only doorframe shapes on both sides, as in the Rama gloss there, and this helps even if the alleyway was wider than ten amot], because even in a complete alleyway that is bent, the view of several Rishonim is that one does not require an arrangement at the bend; and also from the essence of the law, even according to Rashi it appears that that arrangement is only d'rabbanan.

And one requires a doorframe shape at its bend — and it helps even if there at that place it was wider than ten amot, as Tosafos wrote on daf 16 s.v. Rav; however, that which he wrote that at its two heads each one needs a lechi or beam — therefore it deals with when the alleyway there was not wider than ten amot; for if wider than ten, certainly one needs there too a doorframe shape; and likewise in what he concludes that one can make a doorframe shape at its two heads and at its bend a lechi or beam — it also deals with when it was not wider than ten at its bend, for in that case one must make a doorframe shape; and this is only the answer of Rosh in general, etc.; and according to Maharam brought in Mordechai and Beit Yosef, even if at the bend it was more than ten, a lechi or beam suffices; and all this is brought in Darkei Moshe briefly; and it appears one can rely to be lenient according to Maharam, for otherwise the view of many Rishonim is that a bent alleyway's law is entirely like a pass-through alleyway and one requires no arrangement at its bend, as above; and in a straight pass-through alleyway certainly its law is that one need not look at the middle of the alleyway even if it is wider than ten, and one needs only to make a lechi and beam at the end of the alleyway, since there it is not wider than ten; and all the more so in our case, where one made doorframe shapes at its two heads.

And a lechi or beam at the bend — and regarding partnership of alleyways, both must do it together specifically, and they cannot divide one from the other, as explained below in siman 392 seif 6; and only in the first manner, when they make a doorframe shape in the middle, can they divide and each make an eruv for themselves; and as explained there [from Magen Avraham and Beit Me'ir in his conclusion, see there].

And if shaped like a chet, etc. — see in Beit Yosef who wrote that Rashba's view here is that its law is like an alleyway closed on one side, even according to Rav, and one needs only a lechi or beam at its two heads; and for halacha he wrote that we rule like Rashi; and see in responsum of R' Akiva Eiger cited above, who wondered at Beit Yosef for being astonished at Rashba's words; and see there further that he greatly proved that many stand on Rashba's view — they are Rambam and Ritva [and see in Ritva that he brought this explanation in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and it is implied that Rabbeinu Tam also holds thus], Rivash, and Ritzba (cited on daf 10b s.v. one makes); and see there that from his words, when another branch joins, one can rely on these Rabbanan to be lenient; and accordingly, in our alleyways that have the law of courtyards as above [or in a place of need when one cannot make an arrangement at the bend], one can rely to be lenient according to these Rabbanan, and only two lechayim are made at each of its ends like the law of a courtyard breached in its entirety; and nevertheless, because of custom, one must make there a doorframe shape at each of its ends, because sometimes it may happen that there is a width of more than ten amot there, as above in siman 363 seif 26 in the Rama gloss, see there.
