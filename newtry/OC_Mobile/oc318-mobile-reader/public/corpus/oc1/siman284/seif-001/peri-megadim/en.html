(א) We read the haftarah — Taz: see Levush and Bach; and even though the beginning of the enactment was to read haftarah without Torah reading and in a congregation of ten — now that the decree was annulled they did not enact to bless on haftarah except in a congregation of ten adults and after Torah reading as Rama wrote; and nevertheless when they read from a disqualified Torah scroll (without blessing) they may read haftarah afterward with blessings as Bach wrote, see Taz. And the reason may be: now that the decree was annulled, if they read haftarah with blessings without Torah reading it is not honor to the Torah — therefore they enacted the maftir must read in the Torah first, see Megillah 23a; and Magen Avraham and Eliyahu Rabbah — even in another house if unavoidable it is permitted to read haftarah there with blessings with ten. If they began with ten and some left, they finish with blessings — siman 147 and siman 143 and 128 in Eliyahu Rabbah letter 1, see there — meaning they began Torah reading with ten and some left (see 143 whether if majority remain we require them) — they finish Torah reading and haftarah he says without blessings; and if they began haftarah and some left, they finish haftarah with blessings:

(ב) And what Taz wrote that we do not interrupt for us at 'true and just' — in Levush it implies the custom of our fathers in our hands is to interrupt there a little; therefore he wrote we do not interrupt for us. There in Levush: why is it called haftarah? Some say from the language 'they will speak' — that reading a Torah scroll is forbidden even for study, but for haftarah it is permitted. And it requires study in siman 146 seif 3 — it is forbidden to interrupt like in Torah reading; it implies even for study it is forbidden to interrupt even in words of Torah:
