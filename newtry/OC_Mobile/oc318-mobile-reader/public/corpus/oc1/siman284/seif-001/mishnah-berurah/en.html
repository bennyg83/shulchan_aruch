(א) They read the haftarah — Levush wrote: I wondered that I did not see practiced writing the haftarot as a proper book, for it seemed to me one does not fulfill at all with haftarah reading in printed chumashim since they are not written with all the laws of a Torah scroll or megillah; and Taz and Magen Avraham upheld the custom and hold that even though it is through printing and also on paper and not in a scroll it is permitted for this purpose. Nevertheless Magen Avraham's view is that one must read the haftarah from a book of Prophets:
(2) (b) In the Prophet, etc. - and the reason is because the Holy Prophet passed a decree on Israel not to deal with the Torah and called the Prophets seven and blessed them against the seven who had to go up and read the Torah and would not have recited less than 3 verses with each of them, and after all there were 21 verses for that despite the fact that the decree was void from a custom that is not void and the Shu'a decreed that the meftir recite no less than 11 verses from the Prophet and he recites the Torah Because of the honor of the Torah and KMSH in the sign of the Rabbi in siman 18, and the Haftir blesses seven blessings [that is, two blessings that the Haftir blesses on the Torah before and after it and one on the Prophet before it and four after it] against the seventh who read in the Torah:
(ג) But without this — such as they did not have a Torah scroll to read from; and if they read from a Torah scroll and after the reading it was found disqualified, nevertheless they read the haftarah afterward and recite the blessings before and after it; and see Biur Halachah. If there is no eruv so they can bring the Prophets or chumash to the synagogue for the haftarah, ten go and read the haftarah in a house where it rests, and with the haftarah blessing too — for in any case they read in the Torah first even though it was not in the synagogue:
