6) There: A haftarah from the Nevi'im is read, etc. Our Sages state in an aggadic teaching that a deceased person said he would not be saved from his severe punishment until his son recited Kaddish or read the haftarah from the Nevi'im. Rivash writes in Responsa, siman 115, that this is the source of the widespread custom for a deceased person's son to recite the final Kaddish throughout the twelve months and also read the haftarah from the Nevi'im. These are Rivash's words. A further custom has now spread in some places that each year, when the anniversary of a parent's passing arrives, the son reads the haftarah on the preceding Shabbos. Birkei Yosef, paragraph 1. See Yoreh De'ah, siman 376, seif 4 in the gloss. If two people have a yahrzeit during the week, the person whose yahrzeit comes first has priority. Thus, if one is on Sunday and the other on Monday, the person whose yahrzeit is Sunday has the right to maftir on the preceding Shabbos. If both are on Sunday, a father's yahrzeit takes precedence over a mother's. If both commemorate fathers or both mothers, a Torah scholar takes precedence. If both are Torah scholars, the one greater in wisdom takes precedence. If they are equal, they should compromise, but must not quarrel, God forbid, over this. A word is worth one sela, but silence two, and through this silence he will give his father greater satisfaction than by speaking and receiving maftir.
