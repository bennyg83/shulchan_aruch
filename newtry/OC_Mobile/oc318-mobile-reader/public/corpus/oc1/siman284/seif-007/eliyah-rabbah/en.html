[Levush] In a place where there is no sefer Torah, etc. Meaning they did not read from one. If they read from a sefer Torah and it was then removed, they do recite the haftarah afterward. Mordechai, Eruvin chapter 6, writes an even stronger rule: if there is no eruv and therefore a Chumash cannot be brought to the synagogue, ten people may go to a house that contains one and recite the haftarah there. The haftarah may even be read by heart if no other option exists.
[Levush] I have wondered all my days, etc. Malbushei Yom Tov says the Levush overlooked Gittin 60, where the Gemara asks that isolated portions of Neviim were not given to be written, and answers that because a complete set of Neviim is beyond the means of every congregation, necessity permits it. Taz says likewise. In my humble opinion the Levush knew and saw this. His objection is that people read from printed Chumashim containing only the haftarot, thinking printing solves the problem, but it does not if the prophetic book itself is incomplete. They also make two rollers, whereas I instituted in Prague that there be only one, because Bava Batra 13 insists that Neviim be made with only one roller. According to my explanation, writing the haftarot on parchment according to sefer Torah rules is indeed effective, and the fact that the full book is absent is permitted because necessity made a complete book impractical. Magen Avraham writes that now that printing has been discovered and books are, thank God, inexpensive, it is prohibited to write separate haftarot, because printing is considered full writing. He adds that paper and book form are valid for Neviim, as shown by blessings over Shir HaShirim and Eichah. This is no proof, as the Levush writes in siman 559 seif 1. Moreover, some poskim certainly require parchment, as Responsa Rama siman 134 writes. Writing is also certainly superior to printing, and printed divine Names were not produced with sanctifying intent like handwritten ones. See siman 40 seif katan 2. Nevertheless, those without a haftarah written on parchment should not read from a printed Chumash containing only the haftarah section. If printed books count as writing, it is preferable to read from a complete printed book of Neviim, which every congregation can now obtain. Regarding the single roller, Tosafot and the Rosh there say it is placed at the end, while blank parchment is left at the beginning sufficient to wrap around the entire circumference.
[Levush] It all concludes, etc. At the beginning the blessing opens by praising the words of the prophets in their narrative portion, teaching that even that portion warrants praising Hashem Who chose them. When it turns to the prophetic portion, it first mentions Torah and Moshe, from which prophecy is grounded, and precisely says "true and righteous prophets": true excludes false prophets, while righteous either means their prophetic words are justified, or describes the prophets themselves as righteous people who do not become arrogant through prophecy and distort justice. This is Kesef Mishneh, chapter 12 of Hilchot Tefillah. Responsa Rabbi Yom Tov Tzahalon siman 73 explains "prophets of truth and righteousness" as all having warned Israel to observe Torah; this is the meaning of "spoken in truth," that all prophetic words stand on the foundation and true faith of Torah, which itself is called truth.
And he does not know, etc. He overlooked Abudarham's words that I cited in siman 139 seif katan 13. I also have another difficulty: according to the Levush at the beginning of this siman, seven people originally read from Neviim, so the same reason given above would apply, that the middle readers did not bless, just as with sefer Torah. The Rosh in Pesachim 104 does not enter into these explanations; see there.
[Levush] The custom of our fathers, etc. Taz says it is better not to pause there because of those who may misunderstand. This requires study. See Sefer HaManhig siman 37.
[Levush] The order of the haftarah together, etc. All the more so, two people should not recite the blessing simultaneously, because that can constitute an unnecessary blessing. Certainly the blessing should not be translated; if it is translated, the translator should not mention Hashem's Name, as Rivash siman 37 writes.
