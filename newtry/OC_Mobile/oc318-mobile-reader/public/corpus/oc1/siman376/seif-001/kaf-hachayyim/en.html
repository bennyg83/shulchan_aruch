1) [Se'if 1] A pit situated between two courtyards. Half of it is in one courtyard and half in the other. Rashi.
2) There. They may not draw water from it on Shabbos. This is because each person draws water from the other person's domain. Rashi.
3) There. Unless they made a partition, etc. This applies even if the water is not ten handbreadths deep. It is unlike the ruling at the beginning of siman 356, that water less than ten handbreadths deep has the status of the domain in which it is located because it is subordinate to the ground. The present case is different, for since both parties are partners in the pit, each has a share in the entire pit. There, however, the water is within a courtyard and is subordinate to the ground, so its law is that of the courtyard. Tosefes Shabbos, subsection 1.
4) There. Unless they made a partition, etc. This is so that it is recognizable that the water is divided, with half belonging to these residents and half to those residents. Levush. With this partition we apply the principle that it is viewed as descending to the bottom of the pit, as stated in siman 355. Since the partition provides a recognizable reminder, they will not come to carry utensils that began Shabbos in a house from one courtyard to another elsewhere, in a place unrelated to drawing water. Only with respect to water did the Sages rule leniently that a partition is viewed as descending, for the reason explained there. Shulchan Aruch HaRav, subsection 1.
