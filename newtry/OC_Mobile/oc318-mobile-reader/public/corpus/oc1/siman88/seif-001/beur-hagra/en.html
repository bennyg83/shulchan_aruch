Seif 1, they recite, etc. Berachos 22a.
And they recite, etc. This follows from the Mishnah's statement, “A zav who experienced an emission, etc.” This implies that otherwise he is permitted, and likewise after he has immersed for his emission.
Except, etc. Ibid., and there as well. See Beer HaGolah.
In order, etc. Ibid.
And for Krias Shema, etc., and without, etc. This is unlike the Rif's citation in the name of an alternative version, according to which this applies specifically to words of Torah, whereas Krias Shema requires purification, and unlike his citation in the name of Rav Hai Gaon requiring nine kav. Rather, the ruling follows the first opinion there. The proof is from the Yerushalmi at the end of Yoma: “Now that washing is forbidden, is marital relations not certainly forbidden?” Meaning, is prayer not certainly forbidden? It answers that the case is in a place where people do not immerse. This shows that even washing is unnecessary there.
Some say that she is permitted in every respect, etc. As stated in the Mishnah there, “and a niddah, etc.” This is as explained above. Even the first opinion wrote its restriction only as a mere stringency. See Yoreh Deah, siman 282, seif 9, and see Magen Avraham.
On the days, etc., because, etc. As stated in the second chapter of Chagigah, “in order to give satisfaction to the women, etc.” Although there it was close to performing sacrificial service improperly, it is certainly permitted here.
