A baal keri is permitted in Keriat Shema. One seif.
All those who are ritually impure may read from the Torah, recite Keriat Shema, and pray—except for a baal keri, whom Ezra removed from the category of the ritually impure and forbade, both in words of Torah and in Keriat Shema and prayer, until he immerses—so that Torah scholars would not be found constantly with their wives like roosters. And afterwards they annulled that enactment and restored the matter to the law, that even a baal keri is permitted in words of Torah, Keriat Shema, and prayer without immersion and without washing with nine kavim; and such is the practice that spread.There are those who wrote that a niddah woman during the days when she sees [blood] may not enter the synagogue, or pray, or mention the Name, or touch a [Torah] scroll (Hagahot Maimoni chapter 4). And there are those who say she is permitted in all of these, and that) is the main ruling (Rashi, Laws of Niddah). But the custom in these lands is like the first view. And during clean days they practiced leniency; and even in a place where they are stringent during awesome days and the like, when many assemble to go to the synagogue, they are permitted to go to the synagogue like other women—for it is great sorrow for them that everyone assembles and they stand outside (Piskei Mahari'a siman 332).
