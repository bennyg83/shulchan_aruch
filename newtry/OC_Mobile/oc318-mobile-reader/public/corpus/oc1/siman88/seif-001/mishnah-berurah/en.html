(1) In order, etc.—and also because keri comes from lightheadedness, words of Torah must be with awe and fear, trembling and sweating, as at the time the Torah was given.

(2) And afterwards they annulled—nevertheless, one who wishes to conduct himself and immerse should be careful that by this he does not miss the time of Keriat Shema and prayer; and it is possible that even if by this he misses prayer with the congregation, it is also not proper to be stringent in this.

(3) That enactment—which was a decree that most of the congregation could not uphold, and they annulled it because of bitul Torah and because of bitul procreation.

(4) The practice—nevertheless there are men of deeds who practice this enactment and immerse themselves for keri; and if immersion is difficult for them, they wash themselves with nine kavim. And the Acharonim wrote that forty se'ah of drawn water are valid for this immersion; and likewise nine kavim is also in drawn water, even from three vessels—and in a manner that one does not interrupt pouring from one vessel until the second begins; but more than three vessels do not combine for the measure of nine kavim. And those nine kavim must be poured upon him, but not to immerse in them, whether they are in a vessel or on the ground—for whenever it is the way of immersion we require specifically forty se'ah and on the ground like other immersions. Nevertheless, if half his body entered a mikveh and he did not bend his head to immerse the half of his body that is outside, but they poured upon him from above nine kavim, it is effective to purify. It is written in Mateh Ephraim that at the time of pouring the nine kavim upon the person he should stand upright and place his two hands against his heart, and not press them in a tight embrace but with a little space so the water comes there too; and he should warn those pouring upon him to pour directly against his body. And the measure of nine kavim—see in Ba'er Heitev that it is fifteen Polish quarts; see in Mateh Ephraim cited above, who wrote that in our quart one needs twenty-four quarts for pouring nine kavim; and in a time of pressure when vessels do not hold so much, one may be lenient with eighteen quarts. And likewise one should be careful in all this among those who purify on erev Yom Kippur, and similarly by nine kavim. Magen Avraham wrote that even one who practices immersion for keri—specifically when healthy, or a sick person who used his bed; but a sick person who saw keri against his will is exempt from everything and has only to wash that place so it not be soiled from keri; and likewise a healthy person who saw split waters is exempt from everything, for Ezra did not decree this regarding words of Torah and prayer—only regarding terumah he is impure because of split waters.

(5) Niddah—Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote: Some err and say that women who give birth may not enter the synagogue until forty days have passed for a male and eighty for a female; it is a mistaken custom and one must protest against them.

(6) Or to pray, etc.—and in Benjamin Zeev siman 153 he wrote that they practiced only not to enter the synagogue and not to see a Torah scroll; and also when she prays she does not stand before her friends, and they do so because of custom and honor, not because of prohibition—thus he wrote. And likewise the Acharonim agreed that she must pray in her house and bless all blessings—and especially Birkat Hamazon and kiddush, which are from the Torah; see in Magen Avraham.

(7) But the custom, etc.—and in our countries they always practice leniency and bless and pray; nevertheless they should not gaze at the Torah scroll when they raise it to show the people (Chayei Adam). He also wrote that they should not enter a cemetery until they immerse.

(8) Awesome days—from the first day of Selichot onward they are called awesome days for everyone in such a case; and likewise if she marries off her son or daughter, or if she herself gives birth—they go to the synagogue after four weeks, it is permitted even though she is a niddah; and since they are permitted to go to the synagogue, they are also permitted to pray in all this according to everyone.
