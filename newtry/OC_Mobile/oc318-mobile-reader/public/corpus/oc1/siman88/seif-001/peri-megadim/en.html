And likewise Taz: forty se'ah of drawn water on the ground in immersion are valid for baalei keri; and nine kavim specifically by pouring, not immersion on the ground. The measure of nine kavim—in siman 607, sixteen; and in Alef Ra, eleven; and in R' Yehuda HaChasid, eggs—there he wondered on what Magen Avraham wrote from four vessels; and one must say from three they combine, not from four. These nine kavim are explained in Rambam chapter 4 of prayer halakha 8: the widespread custom in Spain is to wash the whole body because of propriety—it implies we require nine kavim on the whole body so there be no place the water did not reach, like immersion; and in Israel now they pour nine kavim and almost part of the body the water did not touch at all—hands and feet one must dab in water beforehand; see Rambam chapter 50 of Mikvaot 4, from three vessels and that it be one after another and without interruption—see all the more at once; only the doubt is if it must be one after another, it must be without interruption; and proof from that mishnah—in the case that one must not interrupt—and he did not stipulate that it be one after another. A mikveh whose appearance changed is valid.

What the Mechaber wrote—the reason that Torah scholars not be found constantly with their wives, etc.—the Peri Megadim wondered at the separation, for in the Gemara the reason is explained as awe and fear, trembling and sweating like at giving of the Torah, and keri is called lightheadedness. And truly in Rambam Laws of Prayer chapter 4 halakha 4 it is explained as the Mechaber wrote (see Netsiv, who wrote chapter 4 Mikvaot, and it is an error in the edition—it must say "in prayer"). And in my novellae on Berachos 22a I wrote that this applies to prayer—a court of their successors enacted that trembling and sweating apply only to words of Torah and Keriat Shema are included; and for prayer the reason is that they not be found constantly, etc. If so, there is a question on the Mechaber, who omitted Keriat Shema and words of Torah—the explicit reasons of Ezra's enactments.
