Seif 1: All the impure, etc.—see in Taz seif 1 from the law of pouring nine kavim. And we learn in chapter 2 of Gittin (16a): Rav Papa asked—half by pouring and half by immersion, what is the law? And Rashi explained: like pouring of nine kavim. For since he does not ask if one poured nine kavim on half and then nine kavim on the second half, it is proven from this that in this case it is obvious to him that it helps, since it is all in pouring of nine kavim; and conversely, what he does not ask—if one poured less than nine kavim on half and then again less than nine kavim on the other half—it appears obvious to him that this does not help, for we require pouring nine kavim together without interruption. And what he does not ask about immersion itself—if one immersed half the body and returned and immersed the other half, whether they combine—it is proven that it is obvious to him that in this they do not combine; only he asks regarding a baal keri, since they were lenient for him that nine kavim suffice—if so, in this case if one poured nine kavim on half the body and again nine kavim on the other half, therefore he asks whether half by immersion works, since it is a change for the better—all the more so it should suffice; or perhaps we require all in immersion or all in pouring. And it is proven from this several laws—see there and understand. Now, as to whether chatzitzah invalidates immersion for keri—we already wrote elsewhere on this, with God's help; and now it appears there is proof that chatzitzah is valid in it, from what we learn in tractate Yoma (31a): Does this immersion interpose or not? He said to him: Every enactment of the rabbis was enacted like Torah law. And it is difficult: why does he not ask regarding drawn water whether drawn water is valid for this immersion or not? And one cannot say that regarding drawn water it is obvious to him that it is valid—for if so, why did he say "every enactment of the rabbis," etc.; drawn water would prove it is valid, and if so, according to this reasoning, he should also have asked whether drawn water is valid. From this it is proven conversely that drawn water is obvious to him as invalid, and he asks only about chatzitzah. And one can say that from this there is proof for those who hold that all drawn water is invalid by Torah law—for if not, why is drawn water more obvious than chatzitzah? Perforce, chatzitzah is only rabbinic where one is particular; and one can say "they said and they said"—in rabbinic law they were lenient, but drawn water is invalid by Torah law and certainly invalid here too. However, in this it was not placed—for he should still have asked regarding mostly drawn water whether it is invalid here; perforce it is proven for some reason that drawn water is worse than chatzitzah, for regarding drawn water it is obvious to him and regarding chatzitzah he is uncertain. And if so, in this immersion drawn water is valid as Beit Yosef wrote in this siman, and Magen Avraham in the laws of Yom Kippur—all the more so chatzitzah is valid here; and this is correct and proper—understand.
