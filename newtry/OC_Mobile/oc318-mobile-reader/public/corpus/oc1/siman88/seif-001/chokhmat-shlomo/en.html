Seif 1 All the impure, etc. NB see Taz s.k. 1 on the law of pouring nine kav. And behold we learn in chapter 2 of Gittin (daf 16a): R' Papa asked — half by pouring and half by immersion — what is the law? Rashi explained: like pouring of nine kav. For since it does not ask if he poured nine kav on half and afterward nine kav on the other half — it is proved from this that in this it is plain to him it helps, since it is all by pouring of nine kav; and conversely, what it does not ask — if he poured less than nine kav on half and afterward again less than nine kav on half — it appears plain to him this does not help, for we require pouring nine kav together and not with interruption. And what it does not ask regarding immersion itself — if he immersed half the body and returned and immersed the other half whether they combine — it is proved plain to him they do not combine; only it asks regarding a baal keri, since they were lenient for him that nine kav suffices — if so, in this nine kav suffice if he poured on half the body nine kav and again on the other half nine kav; therefore it asks whether it is half by immersion, since it is a change for the better — all the more it suffices; or perhaps we require all by immersion or all by pouring. And from this several laws are proved — see there; examine carefully. Behold, if an interposition disqualifies in immersion for keri [we already wrote from this elsewhere, with God's help] — and now it seems a proof that an interposition is valid therein, from what we learn in tractate Yoma (daf 31a): does this immersion interpose or not? He said to him: whatever the Sages enacted similar to Torah enactment is enactment. And it is difficult: why does it not ask regarding drawn water whether drawn water is valid in this immersion or not? And one should not say that regarding drawn water it is plain to him it is valid in this — for if so [why] did he say to him: whatever the Sages enacted similar to Torah enactment is enactment — behold drawn water would prove it is valid therein; and if so, according to the presumption that he asks whether it interposes or not, it should also ask whether drawn water is valid from this — it is proved conversely that drawn water is plain to him invalid, and he asks only regarding interposition. And one may say from this there will be proof for those who hold all drawn water is invalid by Torah law — for otherwise why is drawn water plainer than interposition; and perforce in interposition it is only rabbinic in a minority one is particular. And one may say: they said and they said — in rabbinic matters they were lenient; but in drawn water, which is invalid by Torah law, certainly also here invalid. However, in this it is not placed; still it should ask regarding mostly drawn water whether invalid in this — and perforce it is proved for some reason that drawn water is inferior to interposition — for regarding drawn water it is plain to him, and regarding interposition he is uncertain.
