[1] All those who are impure, etc. Even a zav. They supported this with the verse, “Are not My words like fire?” Just as fire is not susceptible to impurity, so too words of Torah are not susceptible to impurity. Levush. Nachalas Tzvi objects that in the Gemara this verse is used only regarding a baal keri, while the other impure persons are derived from “and you shall make them known to your children.” In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty at all, because the verse “and you shall make them known” is needed for Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi's other teaching, that one who studies with his grandson is considered as though he received the Torah at Mount Sinai, as stated at the end of the chapter Mi Shemeiso, Berachos 21b. This is also stated in Beit Yosef and explicitly in chapter 5 of Hilchos Krias Shema. It is therefore clear that the law concerning all impure persons is derived from “Are not My words like fire?” I likewise found this in Raavan, siman 160. This resolves all the questions and objections that Maadanei Melech raised against the Levush. See there, for I have written briefly.
