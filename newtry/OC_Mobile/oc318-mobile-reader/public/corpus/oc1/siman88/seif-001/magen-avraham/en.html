To mention the Name. It requires study — for some say women in Birkat HaMazon is Torah law; if so, how can they exempt themselves from a Torah positive commandment by a custom that has no basis? Therefore it seems in any case they should hear Birkat HaMazon from others; and if there is no other, she should bless Birkat HaMazon herself quietly. And all the more Kiddush, which is Torah law — for even a bereaved person in the time of the gemara would bless Birkat HaMazon by thought; and in Beit Yosef siman 153 he wrote they practiced only not to enter the synagogue and not to see a Torah scroll; and also when they pray she does not stand before her friends — and on account of custom of honor they do so, not on account of prohibition — so far; and such is the main view:
On the Days of Awe. It appears from the first day of Selichot onward it is called Days of Awe for all such cases (Ma'agalei Tzedek). And it seems to me: since they are permitted to go to the synagogue, they are also permitted to pray — a hint to the matter: for we say at the beginning of Yevamot: since it was permitted for his tzara'at, it was permitted for his keri. And it seems to me the same applies in all similar cases — e.g. she marries off her son or daughter, or she herself gave birth, that they go to the synagogue after four weeks — permitted in every manner, even though she is a niddah.
