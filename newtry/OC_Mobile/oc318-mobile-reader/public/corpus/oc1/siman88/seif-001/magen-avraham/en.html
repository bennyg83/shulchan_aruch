And afterwards they annulled it—because of bitul Torah and because of bitul procreation; and even though a court cannot annul the words of a fellow court unless greater in wisdom and in number—for this enactment did not spread among most of Israel. And even one who practices this immersion—specifically when healthy, or a sick person who used his bed; but a sick person who saw keri against his will is exempt (Beit Yosef; Rambam). And forty se'ah of drawn water are valid for this immersion; and see chapter 1 of Mikvaot—if he saw split waters he is impure because of keri; and it appears to me that this is specifically regarding terumah, but Ezra did not decree this regarding words of Torah and prayer.

To mention the Name—there is doubt, for some say women's Birkat Hamazon is from the Torah; if so, how can they be exempt from a Torah obligation by a custom that has no basis? Therefore it appears that in any case she hears Birkat Hamazon from others; and if there is no other, she blesses Birkat Hamazon herself in a whisper—and all the more kiddush, which is from the Torah; for even a woman in the time of the Gemara would bless Birkat Hamazon by thought. And in Benjamin Zeev siman 153 he wrote that they practiced only not to enter the synagogue and not to see a Torah scroll; and also when they pray she does not stand before her friends, and they do so because of custom and honor, not because of prohibition—thus he wrote, and such is the main ruling.

Awesome days. It appears from the first day of Selichot onward they are called awesome days for everyone in such a case (Ma'agalei Tzedek). And it appears to me: Since they are permitted to go to the synagogue, they are also permitted to pray—a hint to the matter, for they say at the beginning of Yevamos: Since his leprosy was permitted, his keri was permitted. And it appears to me that likewise everything similar to this—such as if she marries off her son or daughter, or if she herself gives birth—they go to the synagogue after four weeks; it is permitted in every case even though she is a niddah. And see at the end of mishnah chapter 3 of Berachos.
