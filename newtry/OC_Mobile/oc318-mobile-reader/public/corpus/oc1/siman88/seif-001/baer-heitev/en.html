The custom. And even one who practices this immersion—specifically when healthy, or when a sick person who used his bed [had an emission]. But a sick person who saw keri against his will is exempt (Beit Yosef; Rambam). And likewise if he saw split waters, Ezra did not decree. And forty se'ah of drawn water are valid for this immersion (Magen Avraham; Peri Chadash). And the Tur in siman 541 wrote that there are pious people and men of deeds who are stringent upon themselves and immerse, and that is an extra stringency—for it is not forty [se'ah] immersion but washing with nine kavim; see there. And it is explained in Beit Yosef that the law of nine kavim applies even in drawn water and even from three or four vessels, in a manner that one does not interrupt pouring from one vessel until the second begins; but from four and above they do not combine at all. And those nine kavim must be poured upon him, but not to immerse in them, whether they are in a vessel or on the ground. And likewise one should conduct himself among those who purify on erev Yom Kippur accordingly, by nine kavim (Taz). And the measure of nine kavim is sixteen Polish quarts; see siman 607 seif 4. And the Rambam wrote that his waters did not annul this immersion.

Niddah. Rav Ovadia wrote: Some err and say that women who give birth may not enter the synagogue until forty days have passed for a male and eighty for a female. It is a mistaken custom and one must protest against them (Or Zarua).

The Name. Birkat Hamazon she hears from others; and if there is no other, she blesses HaMakom herself in a whisper—and all the more kiddush, which is from the Torah. And in Benjamin Zeev siman 153 he wrote that they practiced only not to enter the synagogue and not to see a Torah scroll; and also when they pray she does not stand before her friends, and they do so because of custom and honor, not because of prohibition—thus he wrote, and such is the main ruling (Magen Avraham).

Awesome days. From the first day of Selichot onward they are called awesome days for everyone in such a case (Ma'agalei Tzedek). And Magen Avraham wrote, and it appears to me: Since they are permitted to go to the synagogue, they are also permitted to pray; and likewise if she marries off her son or daughter, or if she herself gives birth—they go to the synagogue after four weeks, it is permitted in every case even though she is a niddah—thus he wrote. And see the responsum of Be'er Eshek siman 36.
