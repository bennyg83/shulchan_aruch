(Se'if 1) For Ne'ilah he says Ashrei, etc. Note: A practical case came before me regarding someone who prayed the Ne'ilah prayer in place of Minchah. It seems that he must go back and pray Minchah, and afterward pray Ne'ilah. This is comparable to what we rule in siman 108 regarding one who prays two prayers on Motza'ei Shabbos: if he prays the make-up prayer first and afterward the current obligatory prayer, he must go back and pray the current obligatory prayer, even if he made havdalah in both of them, or did not make havdalah in either of them, because his intention was for the make-up prayer first, and that is ineffective. So too here: since he intended first for Ne'ilah and afterward for Minchah, while we require the reverse, first Minchah and afterward Ne'ilah, it is ineffective. This case is worse than that one, for here the obligation of Ne'ilah has not yet fallen on him at all so long as he has not prayed Minchah. All the more so in this case, since when he prayed for the sake of Ne'ilah he certainly said 'seal us' instead of 'write us'; if so, he revealed his intent that he was praying for the sake of Ne'ilah, and it is like there, where he made havdalah in the second prayer and not the first. As for what he said afterward, 'You extend a hand,' etc., one may say that this is not considered so much like a distinct indication within the prayer, since after the prayer an individual also says it. But when he said the concluding formula with 'seal us' in place of 'write us,' this is a distinct indication within the prayer itself. If so, he revealed his intent that he is praying for the sake of Ne'ilah, and it is not at its proper time. One may also say that his saying 'seal us,' where he should have said 'write us,' invalidates the prayer. For although it is true that if he did not say 'Remember us' or 'Who is like You,' we do not send him back, this is worse, because he said something improperly. It resembles what the Taz wrote in siman 108, that if one mentioned the occasion of other days in a prayer, we send him back; there he objected to the Shulchan Aruch, which rules that we do not send him back. Even according to the Shulchan Aruch there, which ruled that we do not send him back, it seems that here he would agree, for this resembles what we say at the beginning of Zevachim: that something of its own type destroys it, whereas something not of its type does not destroy it; one who slaughters a chatas for the sake of chullin, the offering is valid. So too, when one mentions the occasion of other days in a prayer, since it is not relevant there at all, it is not considered an invalidating change. But here, this mention is relevant to this prayer, only with 'writing' and not 'sealing,' since it is not yet the time of Ne'ilah. If so, it is of its own type and it destroys it, and mentioning something not relevant now is an interruption. However, from the fact that he did not mention Al Cheit in his prayer, one should not say that he did not fulfill Minchah. This is not so, because in any case he said 'Ashamnu' and 'Aval anachnu chatanu,' and this is the essential confession, as written in the Talmud and the authorities, that one fulfills his obligation with this even in the other prayers. Therefore, if he did not say Al Cheit but did say the above confession, it seems that he has fulfilled his obligation and need not go back and pray. But even if he did not mention the confession at all in the prayer, I am still uncertain whether he must go back and pray or not. Perhaps, since they instituted it within the prayer, it is like an extended prayer, and we require him to say it before 'Yihyu leratzon'; if he did not say it, it is like one who erred in the last three blessings, and if he uprooted his feet he returns to the beginning. Or perhaps it is not like an extended prayer; rather, they instituted it initially to be said immediately after the prayer, and if he uprooted his feet he may say it by itself, or perhaps even if he did not say it at all he has fulfilled his obligation. This requires analysis. I am astonished that I have not seen any authority discuss this. In practice it requires analysis. Even if we say that they considered it obvious that an individual does not repeat, it still remains to determine whether the shaliach tzibbur, if he did not confess, must repeat. Since generally we rule that wherever an individual repeats the prayer, the shaliach tzibbur also repeats, if the individual would say it within the prayer and would repeat, then the shaliach tzibbur too would repeat. This still requires analysis. Examine it carefully.
