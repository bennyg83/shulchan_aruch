And they sound the shofar. See Taz and Levush, who give several reasons. The sounding confounds the accuser so that he cannot prosecute; it expresses freedom from sin; and it commemorates the shofar of the Jubilee, which was sounded on Yom Kippur. Eliyah Rabbah, seif katan 5, cites Tosafos in the chapter Elu Kesherim that the sounding also demonstrates that Motzaei Yom Kippur is treated as a festival. Therefore, on Motzaei Yom Kippur people greet one another with a festival greeting, just as on Shabbos and Yom Tov, in the name of Mateh Moshe. It appears that the shofar is sounded during bein hashmashos as well. See Magen Avraham, seif katan 4, where, with Hashem’s help, this will be explained.
