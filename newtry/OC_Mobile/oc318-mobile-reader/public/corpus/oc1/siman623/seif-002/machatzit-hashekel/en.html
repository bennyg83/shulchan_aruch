(Se'if katan 2) In this, etc. And what the Beis Yosef wrote, that there are those who disagree, etc. This is the language of the Beis Yosef: The Hagahos Mordechai wrote that it appears that if he did not mention Shabbos in the Ne'ilah prayer, we send him back. It appears from his words there that some disagree. End quote from the Beis Yosef. This is the language of the Hagahos Mordechai, chapter Bameh Madlikin: It also appears that if one did not mention Chanukah in Mussaf, or Shabbos in the Ne'ilah prayer, we send him back, for it is the day that obligated him in four prayers. So too, regarding Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos, if he did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Birkas HaMazon, we send him back.
It is a day that obligated him in eating. Meaning: although when Rosh Chodesh falls during the week, if he did not mention Rosh Chodesh in Birkas HaMazon, we do not send him back, because he is not obligated to eat bread on Rosh Chodesh; this differs from Shabbos, where if he forgot to mention Retzeh, we send him back, because on Shabbos he is obligated to eat bread, as above in siman 188. Nevertheless, when Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos, even because he did not mention Rosh Chodesh we send him back, since the day is obligated in eating. Rabbi Eliezer would say that we do not send him back, as it says here, that were it not Shabbos, there would be no haftarah from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh. Meaning, as we say in Shabbos 24: Rav Gidel said in the name of Rav: When Rosh Chodesh falls on Shabbos, the one who reads the haftarah from the Prophets on Shabbos need not mention Rosh Chodesh, for were it not Shabbos, there would be no reading from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh. Therefore one does not mention Rosh Chodesh. So too here, were it not Shabbos there would be no eating today. Although it requires mention, nevertheless we do not send him back. End quote from the Hagahos Mordechai. The Beis Yosef understood that Rabbi Eliezer's statement that we do not send him back does not refer only to Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos, but to everything the Hagahos Mordechai wrote that we send him back; Rabbi Eliezer holds that we do not send him back, and he disagrees also about mentioning Shabbos in Ne'ilah. Although Rabbi Eliezer concluded, 'So too here, were it not Shabbos there would be no eating today,' which implies that he is speaking of mentioning Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos in Birkas HaMazon, nevertheless the Beis Yosef held that he disagrees also about the mention in Ne'ilah. The reason he used language relevant to Birkas HaMazon of Rosh Chodesh is that his proof is from the haftarah on Rosh Chodesh, so he too mentioned Rosh Chodesh; but the same can be said regarding Ne'ilah, that were it not Yom Kippur, there would be no Ne'ilah on Shabbos. Nevertheless, the Magen Avraham disagrees and holds that Rabbi Eliezer disputes only the mention of Rosh Chodesh that falls on Shabbos in Birkas HaMazon, but regarding the mention in Ne'ilah he agrees that if one did not mention Shabbos, we send him back. For Rabbi Eliezer's proof is from Rav Gidel in the name of Rav, etc., the one who reads the haftarah, etc. There the Gemara asks whether one should mention Chanukah in Mussaf: since there is no Mussaf on Chanukah, perhaps one does not mention it, or perhaps when Chanukah falls on Shabbos or Rosh Chodesh, it is the day that obligated him in four prayers, and he should mention it. The Gemara sought to resolve this from Rav Gidel in the name of Rav, etc., the one who reads the haftarah, etc., and so too one should not mention Chanukah in Mussaf. It rejects: Are they comparable? There, there is no reading from the Prophets on Rosh Chodesh at all; but here, on Chanukah, there is mention in the prayers of Ma'ariv, Shacharis, and Minchah. Therefore he mentions it also in Mussaf, and so we rule. If so, regarding Ne'ilah too, Rabbi Eliezer has no proof from Rav Gidel in the name of Rav, etc., for one can also say: since there is mention of Shabbos in the other prayers, Rabbi Eliezer must agree that also in Ne'ilah we send him back, for it is reasonable to say that this is the day that obligated him in four prayers.
