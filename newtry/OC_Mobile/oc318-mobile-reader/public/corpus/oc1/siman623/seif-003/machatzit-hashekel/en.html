(Se'if katan 3) They raise their hands, etc. And even if they delayed, etc. Maharil, Darkei Moshe. This is the language of the Darkei Moshe: The reason is that the limbs and fats of the daily offering were burned at night. End quote. Meaning, although the offering of the sacrifice had to be by day, nevertheless, since the primary act of the sacrifice was by day, it was permitted to finish burning the limbs and fats at night. The same applies here: since the primary prayer was by day, it is permitted to finish by raising their hands at night. And the Beis Shmuel, etc., in Sotah, chapter Eilu Ne'emarim, page 44a; so the text should read.
For in this there is not so much concern, as the Rema wrote above at the end of siman 129.
For in a prayer that is fit for raising hands, we say 'Elokeinu,' etc. And the Minchah prayer on Yom Kippur, although they do not raise hands in it, nevertheless, since if a kohen went up he does not descend, it is therefore called fit for raising hands. The same can be said for us regarding the Ne'ilah prayer: although the custom is not to raise hands, nevertheless certainly if he went up he does not descend. See there in the Beis Yosef in the name of the Ran, who wrote that one says Avinu Malkeinu on Yom Kippur that falls on Shabbos.
Although on Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbos we do not say Avinu Malkeinu, Yom Kippur is different because it is the completion of judgment; if not now, when? These are his words.
Although we do not rule like the Ran, nevertheless at Ne'ilah, which is literally the completion of judgment, one may say according to the Ran's reasoning: if not now, when?
