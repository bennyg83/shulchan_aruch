(Se'if katan 4) And we blow, etc. The day has passed (Tur) in siman 624.
Although, etc. It implies that the stars have not yet emerged, for it teaches 'close to,' and this means half an hour before sunset. So the text should read in the name of the Bach.
Nevertheless, it is bein hashmashos. According to this, one must say that the Magen Avraham holds that the measure of 'close to' is not half an hour.
For bein hashmashos is only approximately a quarter of an hour, as written above in siman 261. His meaning is that since it is bein hashmashos, and we do not decree against a rabbinic prohibition during bein hashmashos in a case of mitzvah, it is permitted. Although in siman 342 the Magen Avraham is uncertain whether they refrained from decreeing on Motza'ei Shabbos against a rabbinic prohibition during bein hashmashos, for the departure of the day may be different, etc., one must say that here they were lenient, because there are several reasons for this blowing. See the end of siman 299. There too the Magen Avraham hinted to see here. His meaning is that although on Motza'ei Shabbos, before one has made havdalah in the prayer, even moving a candle is forbidden, nevertheless they were lenient here.
