(There, se'if 6, in the gloss) Some say that one should blow only one tekiah, etc. Note: Were it not for the words of the commentators, it would seem possible to say that this relates to faith and trust, to show the lot of our trust in Him, may He be blessed, that we have merited in judgment. We may introduce what is written in the Sifri on the verse, 'Hashem gives forth His voice before His army' - this refers to Rosh Hashanah; 'for His camp is very great' - these are Yisrael, etc.; until 'for great is the day of Hashem' - this is Yom Kippur; 'and very awesome, who can endure it' - three books are written. The commentators asked: Why would three books be needed on Yom Kippur, since the righteous and the wicked were already written on Rosh Hashanah, and only the intermediate remain? Why do I need three books? It seems that the matter is as follows. It is known that the world is judged according to its majority. It is also known that the exceptionally elevated people are few, and conversely the completely wicked are also few, while most of the world are intermediate people. Even if the majority are not intermediate, in any case, if we set aside the minority of intermediate people, there is no majority of righteous and no majority of wicked. Therefore, since there is no majority, the righteous and the wicked are judged independently, because the intermediate are not judged then and we do not yet know what their end will be. Thus the righteous are judged according to their own measure, and the wicked according to their own measure. But on Yom Kippur, when the intermediate are judged, if the intermediate merit, they join with the righteous and most of the world becomes meritorious; then even the wicked are granted merit, and their judgment is changed from death to life. If, Heaven forbid, the intermediate do not merit, they join with the wicked, and the wicked become the majority; then the majority is liable, and even the righteous are drawn after them and are seized for the sin of the generation, and their judgment is changed, Heaven forbid, from life to death. Therefore all the books are opened again. With this, the question of the commentators is resolved, namely what we observe, that righteous people die and wicked people live. Therefore this is the idea of the shofar blast. It is known that a tekiah indicates the righteous, while teruah and shevarim indicate the wicked. Therefore on Rosh Hashanah, when the intermediate have not yet merited, there are consequently wicked people in the world who are written for death, and so we blow a tekiah, alluding to the righteous, and we blow teruah and shevarim, alluding to the wicked. But on Yom Kippur we are confident that even the intermediate have merited. If so, since the world is judged according to its majority, the whole is meritorious, and therefore all are righteous. We therefore blow only a tekiah, to allude that now we are all righteous. This shows the greatness of the trust with which we trust in Him, may He be blessed, that 'all your people are righteous'; after Yom Kippur they shall inherit the land forever, speedily in our days, amen.
