It is permitted to slaughter it on Yom Tov. Meaning: after it waited overnight, and it still lacks examination.
Even though a defect has arisen in it. Meaning: and its majority has been impaired. For we say in general that most animals are in a presumption of kashrus; so Rashi explained in chapter HaMevi. And if so, all the more so where treifah animals are as common as kosher ones, it is permitted to slaughter on Yom Tov; so wrote Beis Yosef. And it is difficult for me: why did he permit here where there is a doubt that it will be treifah, and we are not concerned that he may come to a slaughter for nothing, which is certainly forbidden on Yom Tov? And how does this differ from siman 497, se'if 9, regarding dovecote pigeons, etc., where we specifically require designation, lest they be found emaciated and he move them for nothing; and all the more so here that he would slaughter for nothing; and all the more so in a place where a defect has arisen. And it appears to me that there regarding the pigeons, since it is possible to designate from yesterday, and then there will be no doubt on Yom Tov itself, they required that he do so. And there is a parallel in Tosafos, chapter HaMevi, folio 33, according to R' Eliezer: specifically regarding eating, a person fixes a place for his eating and he should have prepared; and here too it is so, since he knew yesterday he should have prepared; which is not so regarding an animal, which is better when slaughtered today than when slaughtered on erev Yom Tov. Therefore they permitted him even where there is a doubt. But Maharshal wrote that we require that the matter not be unresolved, and the Ran wrote that we follow the stringency; and so wrote Or Zarua in a place where treifos are as common as kosher ones; and it is proper to be concerned for this.
