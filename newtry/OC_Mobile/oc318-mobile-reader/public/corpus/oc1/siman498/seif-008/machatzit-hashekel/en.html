(Seif katan 8) Standing, etc. According to Tosafot's explanation, we require, etc. Tosafot on 6b, s.v. muchan, etc., asked why a calf born from a tereifah is permitted, since it is prepared incidentally to its mother for dogs, and that which is prepared for dogs is considered prepared for people, yet a chick born on Yom Tov is forbidden, as written below in siman 513, seif 8, because of muktzeh. When the chick was inside the egg, it was at least prepared for dogs, which should be considered prepared for people. They answered that we require specifically something prepared and standing for dogs. Only a tereifah, which cannot at all become fit for people, is prepared and standing for dogs. But an egg that contains a chick, although now it is fit only for dogs, is not prepared and standing for dogs, since his intent is not to throw it away but to wait until the chick emerges and becomes fit for a person and for himself. These are their words. Nowadays, ordinary tereifos are not standing for dogs, but rather to be sold to a non-Jew. Specifically in the time of the Gemara, when few non-Jews lived among Jews, and even if he sold it to non-Jews the non-Jew would give only a small amount for it, as Tosafot writes in Avodah Zarah 20a, s.v. and Rabbi Meir, it was prepared and standing for dogs. This is not so nowadays, when many non-Jews are found among us and one sells neveilos and tereifos to a non-Jew for their value, or lowers the price somewhat; it is not called prepared and standing for dogs. See what is written in siman 308, seif 31, where Magen Avraham brings many poskim who hold like Tosafot's reasoning, that we require prepared and standing.
According to what we rule like Rabbi Shimon, etc. This is the view of those who permit muktzeh on Yom Tov at the end of siman 495. First, etc. Meaning, do not ask that although he holds muktzeh is permitted on Yom Tov, nevertheless Rama wrote at the end of siman 495 that nolad on Yom Tov is forbidden, and here it is nolad. To this he wrote, first, that the Rosh does not distinguish between muktzeh and nolad, unlike Rama.
Furthermore, with living creatures one cannot say nolad, as Tosafot wrote, etc. Tosafot on 6b wrote: a calf born on Yom Tov is permitted. It is difficult, for nevertheless it should be forbidden because of nolad like an egg. One can say that the statement that nolad can be forbidden applies specifically to food, similar to fruits that fell and liquids that flowed, but not to living creatures. These are Tosafot's words. At first glance their words are difficult. Maharam Lublin wrote: although it is prepared incidentally to its mother and there is no prohibition of muktzeh, nevertheless it should be forbidden like liquids that flowed or fruits that fell, for even by a hen standing for eating, which has no prohibition of muktzeh, its egg is forbidden because of fruits that fell and liquids that flowed, etc. These are Maharam's words. If so, according to this, when Tosafot wrote that nolad does not apply to living creatures, they did not mean nolad forbidden because of muktzeh, but meant that by being born it resembles fruits that fell and liquids that flowed. To this they answered that a living creature is not comparable to fruits that fell and liquids that flowed. But they did not discuss at all nolad that is forbidden because of muktzeh. Maharshal, however, must have understood Tosafot's words simply, that nolad means it should be forbidden because of nolad that is forbidden due to severe muktzeh. To this they answered that with living creatures there is no nolad of muktzeh. But according to this it requires explanation, for what was the question? Even by an egg from a hen standing for eating, we say at the beginning of Beitzah that it is food that separated, for if he wished he could slaughter the hen and eat everything inside it, so its egg has neither muktzeh nor nolad. What disadvantage is there in a calf that is prepared incidentally to its mother, more than an egg, that it should be forbidden because of nolad?
For it is not fit to eat immediately, etc. For it requires slaughter and other food preparations, unlike an egg, which is nolad because it is fit immediately after being laid and one can swallow it raw.
Furthermore, even before, etc. Unlike a chick. Meaning, we rule that a chick born on Yom Tov is forbidden even according to the one who rules like Rabbi Shimon on Yom Tov and does not accept muktzeh. Nevertheless by a chick he agrees, as Tosafot wrote there on 6a, because by a chick even Rabbi Shimon agrees, since it is like dried figs and raisins that he pushed away by hand. According to Maharshal above, the reason is that before the chick was born it was not fit for anything, and therefore it is complete nolad.
