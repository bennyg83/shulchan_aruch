Although it requires inspection, nevertheless, since it had a presumption of kashrus, it is permitted to slaughter it, unlike a calf born on Yom Tov, as I wrote in seif 5, Darchei Moshe. This requires study, for every animal has no presumption of kashrus except by force of the majority, that most animals stand in a presumption of kashrus, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 81. If so, even a calf has this presumption. Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter 4, siman 19, wrote that we rule like Hagahos Ashri, that in a place where tereifos are as common as kosher animals, it is forbidden to slaughter on Yom Tov; all the more so where a defect has been established, see what I wrote in seif 5. Nowadays, in our times, the practice is not to slaughter any animal on Yom Tov, even where tereifos are not common. These are his words. See what I wrote in siman 527. It seems to me that when one needs it on Yom Tov, one may be lenient, and the same applies to a dangerously ill animal.
