With loose earth. Although he makes a pit, we rule that one who digs a pit and needs only its earth is exempt, but it is forbidden; because of the joy of Yom Tov it is permitted. But if he has no spade inserted, there are two prohibitions: one, that it is not prepared, and another, that he makes a pit, Rosh. Yam Shel Shlomo wrote that if he has much loose earth and he does not make a pit, for as soon as he takes the earth, other earth falls underneath it, it is permitted to cover with it if he slaughtered, even if he did not have a spade inserted from before Yom Tov, for one prohibition is permitted, as written in seif 15. These are his words. If he slaughtered on hard ground and there is much blood there, he should take a little of it, place it in loose earth, and cover it, as written in Yoreh Deah, siman 28, seif 7. But if the blood dried somewhat, it seems to me that it is forbidden to scrape it on Yom Tov, for he is leveling holes in the house, and it is a Torah melachah. It is obvious that with prepared earth it is permitted to place earth below, and we are not concerned that perhaps he will reconsider and not slaughter, as stated in the Gemara.
And even if it was, etc. Since he has a spade inserted, it is prepared and standing; see seif 19. The Bach holds that the reason we require a spade inserted is that if he inserts it on Yom Tov there is some indication of digging. Therefore he wrote that it is permitted only with one thrust. The poskim do not hold this way; see the end of this siman in the Tur and Bach.
