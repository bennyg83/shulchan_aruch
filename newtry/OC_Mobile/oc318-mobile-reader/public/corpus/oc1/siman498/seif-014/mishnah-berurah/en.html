Wild animal and bird. For they require covering of the blood.
Prepared earth, etc. The earth must be loose, so that he can cover with it and will not need to crush it, for crushing clods is a full labor, a derivative of grinding, and was not permitted on Yom Tov. The same applies to ash from a fire lit before day, which is presumably prepared for any use, as below in seif 15. When he has prepared earth or ash, he should move it and place it below in the place where he will slaughter, and after slaughter he should cover with it also above, for covering requires loose material below and above, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 28. See there in Yoreh Deah that if he slaughtered on hard ground and there is much blood there, he should take some of it, place it in loose earth, and cover it. But if the blood dried somewhat, it is forbidden to scrape it on Yom Tov, lest he come to level holes, which is a labor according to the Torah.
From before Yom Tov. If he wants to dig on Yom Tov in order to cover, there are two prohibitions: one because of digging a pit, and one because of moving the earth, which is muktzeh. What he wrote, if he has, etc., means that if he does not have it, he should not cover until evening.
A spade inserted from before Yom Tov. Through the spade being inserted in the ground, the ground is considered as already dug. Several poskim hold that it must be inserted explicitly as preparation for the need of tomorrow.
In loose earth. With hard earth he would need to crush it in order to cover, and that is forbidden, as above. Although with loose earth too he now makes a pit by removing the earth, there is no Torah prohibition, for it is a labor not needed for its own sake, and it is also destructive; they permitted it after the fact, when he already slaughtered, because of the mitzvah of covering. It is implied by the poskim that concern for a pit applies only when there was only a little loose earth in the middle and hard ground around it, so that when he takes the earth a pit remains. But when there is much loose earth all around, he does not make a pit by taking it, for as soon as he takes earth, other earth remains in its place. Nevertheless, as to slaughtering initially even relying on such earth, it is forbidden, because inserting a spade is not a complete preparation to remove it from muktzeh, since he did not take the earth from its place of attachment before day. They permitted it through an inserted spade only after the fact, when he already slaughtered.
He covers with it. The later authorities wrote that if there was so much loose earth that no pit was made by taking it, he may cover after the fact, when he already slaughtered, even if there was no spade inserted there from before Yom Tov.
For several thrusts. When he inserted the spade as preparation, presumably his intention was to prepare from the earth according to the amount he would need for covering. Some later authorities hold that the preparation he made yesterday helps only for that spade which he inserted and designated for this, but not to thrust again for this purpose. Certainly according to the poskim who hold that the reason we require a spade inserted before Yom Tov is so there should be no concern of digging on Yom Tov, it is obvious that it is forbidden to thrust anew on Yom Tov.
