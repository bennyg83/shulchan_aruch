He should not slaughter a wild animal, etc. We will briefly copy the sugya. In the Mishnah it is taught: One who slaughters a wild animal or a bird: Beis Shammai say he may dig with a digging-stick and cover; and Beis Hillel say he should not slaughter unless he had prepared earth ready; and they agree that if he [already] slaughtered, he should dig with a digging-stick and cover. And we say in the Gemara on "and they agree that if he slaughtered," etc.: Rav Yehudah said: and that is provided he had a digging-stick stuck in from before Yom Tov. But he is performing crushing! (The Gemara objects: he said "provided he had a digging-stick stuck in," so the Mishnah's reason is not that the positive commandment of "and he shall cover it with earth" overrides the negative commandment against any melacha, but because it is already dug and standing; yet he is performing crushing, for they are still clods and unfit for covering unless he crushes them.) Rav said: with soft earth. But he is making a depression! And it answers: since he needs only its earth, he is exempt for it. And it is further stated there in the first chapter, objecting to this Mishnah that Beis Shammai hold to leniency and Beis Hillel to stringency, yet we hear of them the opposite: R' Yochanan said the attribution of the views is reversed. Rashi explained that that other Mishnah in which Beis Shammai are lenient is reversed, and this one of "one who slaughters" stands; and so the Rif holds for practical law like Beis Hillel toward stringency: one may not slaughter a bird unless there is prepared earth, and after the fact a stuck digging-stick and soft earth avail; and so the Shulchan Aruch ruled here. But Rabbeinu Tam explained that the reversed attribution is of this "one who slaughters": Beis Shammai to stringency and Beis Hillel to leniency, and prepared earth is not required. And therefore Rabbeinu Tam wrote: it is astonishing to me why the world required preparation. And I remember that in my youth they did not require it at all. And Tosafos wrote on this in the name of R' Yitzchak: However this is not so, for according to his explanation as well, at the least Beis Hillel hold what Beis Shammai originally held, and Beis Shammai require a stuck digging-stick, and even with soft earth they require preparation, from what is said here: Beis Shammai say there only where there is a stuck digging-stick, etc.; end quote. And Semag wrote: My teacher wrote that where he has already slaughtered and has no prepared earth, he may cover with what is not prepared, as we say "and they agree that if he slaughtered he should dig with a digging-stick and cover"; and Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote that this is difficult, for the Gemara establishes "and that is provided he had a digging-stick stuck in from before Yom Tov"; end quote. And Beis Yosef wrote on this: And it appears to me that the reason of Semag's teacher was that he relied on Rabbeinu Tam's explanation at least after the fact. And Maharshal wrote in the first chapter against Beis Yosef: and he erred a great error from either side: if he holds Rabbeinu Tam's explanation that Beis Hillel permit l'chatchila even without a stuck digging-stick, and the Gemara required a stuck digging-stick only for the view that permits after the fact, then even l'chatchila, etc.; end quote. And I wondered that he attributed error to our master Beis Yosef in such upright words: for certainly according to Rabbeinu Tam Beis Hillel permit even l'chatchila without a stuck digging-stick; but we are in doubt whether the halacha follows Rabbeinu Tam or Rashi and the Rif. Therefore Semag's teacher held that l'chatchila one should rule like Rashi toward stringency and specifically require prepared earth; but after the fact we rely on Rabbeinu Tam. And what R' Yitzchak wrote on Rabbeinu Tam, that in any case we require a stuck digging-stick as Rav said: it appears that Rabbeinu Tam does not hold so; rather, since Beis Hillel hold to leniency, that even l'chatchila one may slaughter and dig and cover, they hold that the positive commandment of "and he shall cover it with earth" overrides the negative commandment "you shall not do any melacha." For Rashi wrote that specifically according to Rav Yehudah we do not hold this override, since he required a stuck digging-stick. And now R' Yitzchak's words apply to Beis Shammai according to Rabbeinu Tam, who permit only after the fact; then automatically there is no positive overriding a negative, and a stuck digging-stick is needed; but for Beis Hillel according to Rabbeinu Tam, certainly a positive overrides a negative, and it is permitted l'chatchila in every way. And even though on folio 8 we say that it does not override a negative and a positive, Tosafos wrote that Rava does not hold that there is a positive and a negative on Yom Tov, and R' Yitzchak holds like Rava. And what we say, "Beis Shammai say so only where there is a stuck digging-stick," that was before we said the attribution is reversed; but according to the conclusion, whoever is lenient is lenient even in every way for the reason we said. Thus Semag's teacher properly holds like Rabbeinu Tam after the fact, that one may cover even without preparation and needs no stuck digging-stick from before Yom Tov at all, but only as the Mishnah implies: if he slaughtered he should dig with a digging-stick and cover, meaning even without a stick stuck from before Yom Tov. And R' Yitzchak who establishes it as a stuck digging-stick is according to the view that a positive does not override a negative, since he permitted only after the fact; but Beis Hillel truly hold that he digs and covers, as we say in the Gemara according to Beis Shammai before the reversal: one who comes to ask is told, l'chatchila slaughter and dig and cover. But we for practical law do not rule as lenient as Rabbeinu Tam except after the fact, relying on him, as we also find in the Gemara a distinction between l'chatchila and after the fact, as taught "and they agree," etc. So it appears to me to explain Semag's teacher's view according to Rabbeinu Tam, not like the Rosh and Tur according to Rabbeinu Tam who also require a stuck digging-stick and soft earth. I saw other resolutions on this, and for hatred of length I wrote only what appears correct to me. And so it is somewhat implied from Semak, who wrote: if he slaughtered and did not find fine earth that is not prepared, he covers it, etc.; end quote, implying without any stuck digging-stick. And since Semag with his teacher and Semak agreed to this, it appears in a pressing need to rely on them so as not to nullify the joy of Yom Tov, and to take earth that comes to hand even if it is not prepared at all, to cover with it, if he has no stove ash that was heated from erev Yom Tov.
