A firstborn. See Taz. What he wrote in the law of ruling, that whenever the prohibition is clear, such as wet in wet where more was added in a place where chatichah na'asis neveilah does not apply for us, see Yoreh Deah, or dry in dry without a majority of the same kind, and more was added, it is forbidden to rule on Yom Tov. The same applies that if he ruled, it is forbidden to eat, for it was muktzeh at twilight; see siman 495. The same applies to similar cases. Know that those matters that have a remedy by waiting, in Yoreh Deah 57, seif 18, twelve months, twenty-one days, or until it becomes pregnant and gives birth, if their time ended on Yom Tov they are forbidden because of muktzeh; see siman 495. The proof is from what the Maharsha wrote in Beitzah 7 regarding a calf whose eighth day was on Yom Tov, which I brought above. Although at twilight it was only a doubt and not a definite prohibition, nevertheless it was set aside because of doubt. This is unlike a doubtful prohibition that depends on a sage; had a sage been available it could have been clarified at twilight, and he did not set his mind aside from it at twilight. Understand this.
