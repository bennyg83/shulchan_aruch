A sage cannot, etc. Whether the blemish occurred on Yom Tov or before Yom Tov, because it appears like fixing, which is forbidden on Yom Tov. This is not comparable to other rulings, where the prohibition and permission do not depend on the ruling of the one who rules, but on knowledge of the matter. With a firstborn, however, even if it has a complete blemish, as long as a sage or three laymen did not permit it, if he slaughtered it, it is forbidden. Therefore, when he permits it, it is as though he fixes it.
To inspect it, etc. And permit it. But to inspect a firstborn and examine its law, not to rule for others in practice, is like mere Torah study and is not forbidden.
He cannot slaughter it. Although because the sage transgressed the rule that one may not inspect, there is no reason to forbid, nevertheless it is forbidden because of muktzeh. This is obvious if the blemish occurred on Yom Tov, for it certainly was not fit before Yom Tov. Even if the blemish occurred before Yom Tov, one might say that his mind was on it to ask a sage on Yom Tov, and if the sage permits it, he will eat it; nevertheless, since the Sages forbade inspecting blemishes on Yom Tov, he necessarily diverted his mind from it, for he did not expect to find a sage, or even three laymen, who would transgress the prohibition of inspecting blemishes. The poskim wrote that according to all opinions it is forbidden in this case, and even one who permits ordinary muktzeh on Yom Tov agrees that such diversion of mind is forbidden.
But if it was born with its blemish. This means it was born on Yom Tov with the blemish. There is no muktzeh here, because at twilight the offspring was fit to be eaten incidentally to its mother if he slaughtered her. Therefore, although when it was born it was rejected, since it was forbidden as long as a sage had not permitted it, nevertheless when a sage was found and permitted it, it is not forbidden because of muktzeh, since at twilight it was not muktzeh. If it was born unblemished and became blemished that day, see Biur Halacha for its law.
And if he saw the blemish, etc. Meaning, where the main inspection of the firstborn was on Erev Yom Tov, namely that he examined its law whether it is a permanent blemish and he decided that it is a complete blemish, only that he had not yet investigated whether the owner of the firstborn caused the blemish to fall. For any blemish that did not fall by itself but others caused it intentionally is not considered a blemish to slaughter on its basis. In such a case, it is permitted for him to investigate also on Yom Tov, and this is not included in the rule that we do not inspect blemishes on Yom Tov.
It is forbidden to bring it up. Even if it became blemished through its fall, as he concludes, for, etc.; moving not for the need of slaughter is forbidden.
Rather, he provides for it where it is. Although the Mechaber ruled above in siman 497, seif 2, that anything muktzeh may not be given food, here they permitted it so that it should not die.
