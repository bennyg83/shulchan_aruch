(seif katan 11) It is permitted in this case to slaughter for a sick person. And Ran concludes "and see that it is fit for a healthy person in a stew" — Ran needed this, for otherwise what he has no permission except for a sick person — through this it is not considered prepared for himself, for otherwise for an egg and chick one could say he prepared for himself if the sick person needed it; rather it requires that he prepared for himself for a healthy person. Therefore Ran concludes and see for a healthy person in stew — for it is not muktzeh prohibition, for it is prepared via its mother; even though on Shabbat it is forbidden to slaughter from this aspect it is not muktzeh, as Ran wrote at the start of Beitzah that it itself is not muktzeh but a lion cub of prohibition rests on it; if so it is not muktzeh, for perhaps slaughter prohibition can go away, such as if he finds a sick person with danger he may slaughter for him and the remainder is permitted for a healthy person. Even though one who cooks for a sick person is forbidden for a healthy person — that is a decree lest he increase for the healthy — but for slaughter since without slaughter one cannot have an olive-size of meat, the concern lest he increase does not apply; therefore it is permitted for a healthy person in stew without cooking. Therefore it is called on Shabbat he prepared for himself even for a healthy person; unlike egg and chick where there is no preparation via mother and they are muktzeh, even though fit for sick, therefore not considered prepared for himself, since in any case for healthy it is forbidden and not prepared for himself because forbidden for healthy due to muktzeh; also birth, etc. is to raise chicks. And in Rosh he added also that it is not good to eat much when found in its mother's belly.
