(Seif katan 11) It is permitted in this, etc. To slaughter it for a sick person. The Ran concluded: and it is fit for a healthy person as raw meat. The Ran needed this, for if the fact that it has permission only for a sick person were enough to make it considered prepared for itself, then by an egg and a chick we should also say that it was prepared for itself if a sick person needed it. Rather, we require that it be prepared for itself for a healthy person. Therefore he concluded that it is fit for a healthy person as raw meat. For it is not a prohibition of muktzeh, since it is prepared incidentally to its mother. Although on Shabbos it is forbidden to slaughter it, because of this it is not called muktzeh, as the Ran explains several times at the beginning of Beitzah, since it itself is not muktzeh, but the lion of prohibition rests upon it. Therefore it is not muktzeh, since the prohibition of slaughtering could depart, such as if a dangerously ill person is present, for whom it is permitted to slaughter.
