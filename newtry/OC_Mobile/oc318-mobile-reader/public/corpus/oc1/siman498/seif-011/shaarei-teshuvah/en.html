And regarding what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, "and even if he transgressed and saw it," see Chacham Tzvi, siman 88, to resolve the view of those who permit that the Maggid Mishneh cited, who permit even in such a case, which seems against the Gemara, for we hold like R' Shimon that even after the fact it is not [valid]; see there.
(In the Shulchan Aruch se'if 11) An animal that is half, etc. See Eshel Avraham s.k. 2 in the name of Shevut Yaakov; and in Responsa Noda BiYehuda, siman 29, he wrote that there is no difference between a Jew's animal and that of a non-Jewish butcher who sells to Jews, for example where the Jewish slaughterer, because he wants to buy meat from him, the primary slaughter is for the Jew; and also the second concern, that a great noble may happen by and take the whole animal, is a remote concern; and all the more so since a kezayis of meat suffices. And do not say a double doubt: perhaps it will be found treifah, and even if you say it is kosher, perhaps the Jew will not buy of it; that is not so, for the second concern does not enter the category of a safek, and also because one safek is whether the Jew will eat or will not eat, etc. And also Shevut Yaakov wrote that the Geonim before him practiced leniency in the matter, and he of blessed memory innovated this chumra from his own mind; therefore one who is lenient has not lost out; see there.
