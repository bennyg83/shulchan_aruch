They may slaughter it. Because it is impossible to have a kezayis of meat without slaughter; and even if they have two [animals], the Rosh wrote the reason is that they are not equal. And Beis Yosef wrote: even though they are equal in some matter, if they are not equal in every respect it is impossible to divide them. Nevertheless one may wonder, for it implies that if they are completely equal it is forbidden. And why? Even though it is possible to divide them while alive, perhaps he does not want to slaughter for his need a whole animal, since he needs only half of it, and if the whole becomes his he will lose the meat. And perhaps one may say that the Rosh wrote so only when he slaughters both of them on Yom Tov; but if he slaughters one of them, even though they are completely equal, it is permitted for the reason I explained; end quote. And in my humble opinion this leniency does not appear [correct], for it is stated in the Gemara that dough half of which belongs to a non-Jew and half to a Jew is forbidden to bake on Yom Tov, for it is possible to divide it in the kneading; and Tosafos wrote: And if you say, behold we said above that a woman fills an oven with bread even though she needs only one loaf, because bread bakes well when the oven is full, and it is permitted to bake on Yom Tov even l'chatchila. One may answer that that case is different, for all the bread belongs to the Jew, and he has authority to eat each one, this or that; but here half belongs to the non-Jew, and it is forbidden to bake on Yom Tov; end quote. Thus it is explicit that wherever it is fit for division, there is no leniency on account of the benefit that comes to him from the non-Jew's share, since he has no right to eat the non-Jew's share if he wishes. Likewise here, what he gains from the non-Jew's share in that he will not lose his own meat does not avail, since in any case he has no ability to eat the non-Jew's share if he wishes; so he is slaughtering the non-Jew's share in a forbidden way, since division is possible when they are completely equal. This appears clear to me, with Heaven's help.
