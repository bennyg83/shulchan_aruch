Even if they have two. One might have said that if the non-Jew agrees to divide them, they should divide them while alive. It therefore teaches that he is not obligated to divide. This is not only when he needs only half an animal for Yom Tov, for if he divides and must slaughter his animal for the little meat that he needs, the remaining meat will be lost; rather, even if he needs an entire animal and wants to slaughter both, and the non-Jew also agrees to divide, we do not tell him to divide. Even if the two animals are equal in appearance, height, and value, it is possible that the meat of this animal is not as fatty and tasty as the meat of the other, and it is more pleasing to him to eat from the meat of both animals. Some say that if the non-Jew agrees to give him the larger and better one, it is forbidden to slaughter the second. However, if the Jew who slaughters takes for himself only the inner portion and leaves the hindquarter in the skin for the non-Jew partner, all agree that it is permitted to slaughter both in every case, since he has no exertion for the non-Jew's portion, and it is impossible to have an olive-bulk of meat without slaughtering.
