And if it is doubtful, etc. According to Rashi's explanation at the end of Perek Sho'el, that we are concerned, etc., and as written in the fourth chapter of Eiruvin 45b: and if you wish, say, etc. This is what he wrote, that muktzeh, etc., meaning in such a case because of outside the techum. But a doubtful case of prepared status we hold is forbidden, as written in siman 497, seif 3. The Rashba follows his own view, for he rules like Rashi, but we do not hold so. This is the language of the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 2, halacha 6: Our teacher wrote that every doubt of prepared status is forbidden; know that every law of muktzeh and nolad is included in this. The reason is because it is an item that has a permitted status later, or because the Sages wished to be stringent in this doubt. The Rashba disagreed and said that since muktzeh and nolad are from their words, we follow leniency, and they were stringent only in a matter that is close to touching a Torah prohibition, such as a doubt whether it was trapped; but other doubts are permitted. The Maggid Mishneh wrote that his words are astonishing, for why is a doubtful egg forbidden, and our entire sugya in the first chapter treats doubtful nolad stringently. Furthermore, the baraisa teaches plainly: doubtful prepared status, Rabban Gamliel, etc. The words of our teacher, who did not distinguish, appear correct. See the beginning of siman 517, and what I wrote in siman 325, seif 7, and there seif 9, and siman 515, seif 7, and siman 325, seif 16; this requires study.
With muktzeh we follow, etc. It appears to me that he learned this from what is stated in the fourth chapter of Eiruvin 46b: and if you wish, say, it is, etc. Although we hold that a doubt of prepared status is forbidden, the Rashba already wrote in Avodas HaKodesh, 45b: That which we said, that muktzeh and nolad are forbidden on Yom Tov, is not according to the Torah, but from the words of the Sages. Therefore, in their doubts we follow leniency. In what case? In a matter that is not close to coming to a Torah prohibition. But if it is close to touching a Torah prohibition, we follow stringency even in its doubt, as with a Torah matter. How so? If one spread traps or nets for beasts or birds before Yom Tov, and the next day he rose early and found in them a beast or bird, etc., they are forbidden even though they are of doubtful prepared status, because if you permit this, it is close to permitting trapping, which is forbidden according to the Torah. Therefore they were stringent in its doubts. This is the Rashba's approach, and likewise Rashi's approach in Perek Sho'el.
