Animals that go out and graze beyond the techum and come and sleep within the techum are considered prepared, and one may take them and slaughter them on Yom Tov. But animals that graze and sleep beyond the techum — if they came on Yom Tov, one may not slaughter them on Yom Tov since they are muktzeh and the townspeople had no intention regarding them. This applies to animals belonging to Jews, but those belonging to non-Jews have no muktzeh concern since non-Jews require no preparation — unless they were brought for the sake of a Jew, in which case it is forbidden. Therefore if a non-Jew brought animals to the slaughterhouse and it is known they slept beyond the techum, they are forbidden; if there is doubt, they are permitted even if they were brought for a Jew, since muktzeh is treated leniently in cases of doubt.
