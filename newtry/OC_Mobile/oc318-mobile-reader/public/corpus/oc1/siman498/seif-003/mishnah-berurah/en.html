And they come and lodge. Not necessarily every day; even if they come occasionally to lodge, they are also presumed prepared, as below.
And one may take from them. Even if they did not come before day but at night, provided there is no prohibition of techum, such as where they came on their own and the like. Even if he did not think about them at all at twilight, they are permitted, for they are like domestic animals and he need not think about them.
And those that lodge outside the techum. This means that their way is to graze all summer until Cheshvan and then come home. Nevertheless, all the days of the summer the minds of the townspeople are not on them, and they are muktzeh; therefore, if they came, etc.
If they came on Yom Tov. This speaks where there is no prohibition of techum, such as where they came on their own, or he made an eruv in that direction, or with respect to permitting them for another Jew. Otherwise they would in any event be forbidden, for they came from outside the techum.
One may not slaughter, etc. According to the one who permits muktzeh on Yom Tov, they are permitted. Some wrote that according to everyone they are forbidden, for since they do not enter the town's techum all the days of the summer, they are like dried figs and raisins that one raised to the roof to dry, where everyone agrees that he diverted his mind from them until they dry, as explained above in siman 310.
For a non-Jew does not require preparation. As we explained above in siman 497, seif 15. Therefore, even if they were among the animals that lodge outside the house all the days of the summer, we are not concerned.
For then it is forbidden. At least to this Jew for whom it was brought, because of the prohibition of techum; for another Jew it is permitted.
Therefore, etc., if it is known, etc. The language is somewhat difficult, for he does not conclude in the subject with which he began. In truth, the Mechaber now begins a different subject, and some words are missing here, as I explained in Biur Halacha. It should read: specifically when it is known that they lodged outside the techum, but not in a doubtful case; therefore, etc.
A non-Jew who brought an animal to the meat market. In some places non-Jews bring animals to the Jewish meat market, and a Jew slaughters and they sell the meat. If we are in doubt about this animal, whether the non-Jew brought it on Yom Tov from outside the techum, one should not forbid it.
That lodged, etc. It should read: that lodged.
Forbidden. This refers to a town whose majority is not non-Jewish, for we say that presumably they were brought for the need of Jews.
With muktzeh we follow, etc. Meaning, regarding the prohibition of outside the techum, which he also calls muktzeh. But in a doubt of prepared status, we certainly rule stringently, as above in siman 497, seif 3 and 4. The Mechaber here followed the view of the poskim that techumin are lighter and we are not stringent in their doubt. The later authorities already wondered at this, for in several places he ruled like the poskim who are stringent even with a doubt of techumin; see siman 325, seif 9, and siman 515. In seif 4 this will be explained.
For the need of the non-Jew. Meaning, then it is permitted even in a Jewish town.
