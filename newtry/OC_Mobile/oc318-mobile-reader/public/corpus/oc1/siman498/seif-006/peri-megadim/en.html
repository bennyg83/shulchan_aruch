If. See Taz. In the Tur's wording, if he slaughtered it in the field, it seems at first that this refers specifically to a dangerously ill animal; see Beis Yosef. The Mechaber was precise and wrote, if he slaughtered an animal, even a healthy one. What he wrote, in the weekday manner, is difficult, for it implies in the Beis Yosef in the name of the Ran that according to the one who forbids a healthy animal, even with a change it is forbidden; the same applies to a dangerously ill animal, and only limb by limb is permitted. One must distinguish between this and siman 510, seif 8, that slaughter is a matter of greater publicity and a change does not help, only carrying by hand limb by limb. The word pole or carrying bar in Rashi, Beitzah 25b, means two people, and so it is in the Levush. The Shulchan Aruch omitted this; perhaps a pole is carried by two people and is large, while a motah is a small stick. According to what I wrote, one may say as we have written.
